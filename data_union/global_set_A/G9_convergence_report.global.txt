# Global Convergence Report: Cross-Language Analysis of Truth-Domain Semantics

## 1. Scope and Data

### Languages and Analytical Coverage

This report synthesizes findings from a structured cross-linguistic investigation of truth-related vocabulary, morphology, constructions, and oppositions across eight typologically diverse languages: Georgian (ka, South Caucasian), Yoruba (yo, Niger-Congo), Finnish (fi, Finno-Ugric), Basque (eu, language isolate), Tamil (ta, Dravidian), Indonesian (id, Austronesian), Turkish (tr, Turkic), and Korean (ko, Koreanic). The sample spans six distinct genealogical groupings and includes languages with substantially different morphological profiles (agglutinative, isolating, and mixed systems) and different historical contact situations.

For each language, analyses proceeded through six F-phase modules: F1 (vocabulary inventory with usage descriptions and epistemic status ratings), F2a (derivational morphology), F2b (attested constructions), F3 (semantic grouping into usage-based clusters), F4 (construction type inventory), F5 (documented oppositions and contrasts), and F6 (naturalness ratings for targeted expressions). These were followed by eight G-phase comparative steps: G1 (per-language semantic sketches), G2 (cross-language lexical partition comparison), G3 (emergent semantic dimensions), G4 (derivational strategy comparison), G5 (construction comparison), G6 (opposition comparison), G7a (data-derived constraint matrix), G7b (comparison with a theoretical model), and G8 (candidate universals and outliers).

### Data Completeness

Data completeness is uneven across languages. Georgian and Korean produced the largest and most internally differentiated inventories, with extensive derivational families and well-documented semantic groupings. Yoruba and Tamil produced somewhat smaller inventories, particularly for legal and institutional vocabulary. Several dimensions—notably the philosophical/technical truth domain (truth values, truth conditions, truth tables) and the appearance-versus-reality dimension—are more elaborated in some languages (Finnish, Turkish, Korean, Georgian, Basque) than in others (Yoruba, Tamil, Indonesian). Where data are sparse, this is noted in the relevant sections. Absence of documentation for a construction or dimension should not be interpreted as evidence of structural absence; it may reflect data collection scope.

## 2. Core Cross-Language Findings

### 2.1 Semantic Organization: Shared Partitions

Across all eight languages, the truth domain is organized into a set of recurrently distinguished semantic clusters. These clusters are not identical across languages—the specific terms, their register distributions, and their internal boundaries vary—but the same broad partitions appear in all or nearly all languages.

**Propositional correspondence** (whether a specific statement matches what is actually the case) is universally distinguished from **personal honesty and integrity** (whether a person's habitual conduct is free from deception). No language uses identical terms for these two functions, though they may share a root. Georgian uses *simarṭle/č̣ešmariteba* for propositional truth and *gulc̣rpeli* for sincerity; Yoruba uses *òtítọ́* for propositional truth and *ìwà rere* for moral character; Finnish uses *tosi/totuus* for propositional truth and *rehellinen* for honesty; and so on across all eight languages. The distinction is maintained even where the same root underlies both domains.

**Source trustworthiness** (whether a source can be depended upon) is distinguished from **content credibility** (whether a specific claim merits belief) in all eight languages. Georgian distinguishes *sarc̣muno* (inherent trustworthiness of a source) from *damajerebeliˈ* (persuasive effect on a hearer); Yoruba distinguishes *fidagarri* (trustworthy source) from *sinesgarri* (believable content); Finnish distinguishes *luotettava* (reliable over time) from *uskottava* (believable for a specific claim); and parallel distinctions are documented in Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean. The source/content distinction is explicitly noted in the boundary notes of the semantic grouping tables for at least four languages.

**Genuineness of objects versus fraudulent imitation** is universally distinguished from propositional truth. Every language maintains separate vocabulary for evaluating whether a physical item, document, or credential is what it purports to be (genuine/original) versus whether a statement is true. At least six languages (Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean) further distinguish a neutral intermediate—imitation or copy without fraudulent intent—from a fraudulent fake with intent to deceive. Georgian and Yoruba show less elaboration of this three-way contrast.

**Legal/procedural validity** (whether an action or document meets formal legal conditions) is distinguished from **moral/substantive legitimacy** (whether it is rightful in a broader normative sense) in all eight languages. Turkish most explicitly marks this distinction (*yasal* for legal conformity, *meşru* for broader legitimacy), and the construction *yasal ama meşru değil* (legal but not legitimate) is documented as a stable formula. Korean similarly distinguishes *합법* (lawful), *정당* (legitimate/justified), and *유효* (valid/in force). Yoruba shows the least elaboration of this dimension.

**Deliberate deception versus innocent error** is lexically distinguished in all eight languages. Georgian distinguishes *sicrue/ẗq̇uili* (deliberate lie) from *mcdari* (erroneous, without implying intent); Indonesian most explicitly documents this distinction, with *bohong* (deliberate lie) and *keliru* (innocent error) assigned to separate semantic groups with explicit boundary notes. Turkish distinguishes *aldatıcı* (deceptive, implying intent) from *yanıltıcı* (misleading, possibly unintentional) within the falsification domain.

### 2.2 Shared Vocabulary Patterns

Several vocabulary patterns recur across unrelated language families.

**The spatial metaphor of straightness** underlies the primary truth and correctness root in all eight languages: Georgian *marṭl-* (straight/right), Yoruba *tọ́* (straight/correct), Finnish *oikea* (right, also directional), Basque *zuzen* (straight/correct), Tamil *nēr* (straight/direct), Indonesian *benar* (correct), Turkish *doğru* (straight/correct/true), Korean *olbareuda* (upright/correct). The antonym is also spatial in several languages: Turkish *eğri* (crooked/dishonest), Basque *oker* (crooked/wrong). The spatial etymology is more transparent in some languages than others, and the pattern may reflect independent parallel development rather than a single origin, but its consistency across unrelated families is notable.

**No language has a lexicalized single-word agentive nominal for a habitual truth-teller.** All eight languages have lexicalized agentives for habitual liars (Georgian *mt'q̇uari*, Yoruba *aṣírọ́*, Finnish *valehtelija*, Basque *gezurti*, Tamil *poyyaṉ*, Indonesian *pembohong*, Turkish *yalancı*, Korean *거짓말쟁이*) and most have lexicalized agentives for forgers. No language has a parallel single-word form for "truth-teller" or "honest person" as a role noun. The concept is expressed periphrastically in all eight languages. This asymmetry is explicitly noted in the construction tables for all eight languages and is consistent across all language families represented.

**No language has a dedicated lexical item for "half-truth."** All eight languages can express the concept of a partially true statement, but none has a single lexicalized term for it. The concept is expressed through compositional phrases in all languages.

### 2.3 Shared Derivational Patterns

**Abstract property nominalization** is universally productive. Every language produces abstract nouns naming truth-domain qualities through a suffix or circumfix on an adjectival or verbal base: Turkish *-luk/-lik*, Finnish *-uus/-yys*, Basque *-tasun*, Tamil *-mai*, Indonesian *ke-...-an* circumfix, Korean *-seong*, Georgian *-eba/-oba*, Yoruba *ì-* prefix. The specific mechanism varies but the functional outcome is consistent: a nominal naming the property as an abstract entity that participates in evaluative constructions.

**Verbal derivation from truth-domain bases** is documented in seven of eight languages (Yoruba uses light-verb constructions instead). The verbalizing suffix varies: *-la-* (Turkish), *-ta-* (Basque), *-kan* (Indonesian), *-hwa-hada* (Korean), *-ää* (Finnish), preverb + *-eba* (Georgian). The resulting verbs cover verification, falsification, authentication, and related acts.

**Adverbial derivation** is documented in all eight languages, producing manner adverbs and grammaticalized discourse markers from truth-domain bases. The degree of grammaticalization varies from fully compositional (Yoruba hortative construction for frank disclosure) to fully grammaticalized (Indonesian *sebenarnya*, Turkish *aslında*).

**Negative agentive derivation is more productive than positive agentive derivation** in all eight languages. Agentive nominals for liars and forgers are lexicalized; agentive nominals for truth-tellers are absent or marginal. This asymmetry is consistent across all language families.

### 2.4 Shared Constructional Patterns

**Predicative attribution** (subject + copula/predicate asserting a truth-domain property) is universally available across all dimensions and all languages. The copular form varies but the function is unrestricted.

**The intransitive truth-revelation construction** (truth as grammatical subject of an emergence verb) is documented in all eight languages: Georgian *simartle gamovida* (the truth came out), Yoruba *ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* (it became clear that it is the truth), Finnish *totuus tulee ilmi* (the truth comes to light), Basque *egia argitara atera* (truth brought to light), Tamil *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* (the truth came to light), Indonesian *kebenaran akan terungkap* (the truth will come out), Turkish *gerçek ortaya çıktı* (the truth came out), Korean *진실이 드러나다* (the truth emerges). The construction treats truth as an entity that moves from a hidden state to a visible state. The spatial metaphor of truth "coming to light" or "coming to the surface" is consistent across all language families.

**A proverbial or fixed construction asserting the ultimate triumph or indestructibility of truth** is documented in all eight languages: Georgian *simartle gamarjvebs* (truth prevails), Yoruba *òtítọ́ kò ní kú* (truth will not die), Finnish *totuus tulee ilmi* (used in this proverbial sense), Basque *egia beti ateratzen da azalera* (the truth always comes to the surface), Tamil *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail), Indonesian *kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya* (the truth will come out in the end), Turkish *gerçek er ya da geç ortaya çıkar* (the truth comes out sooner or later), Korean *진실은 하나다* (there is only one truth). The specific framing varies (triumph, indestructibility, inevitability, singularity), but the proverbial register is present in all eight languages.

**The discourse-framing construction for frank disclosure** is present in all eight languages, though the degree of grammaticalization varies considerably. At one extreme, Indonesian *sebenarnya* and Turkish *aslında* are fully grammaticalized discourse markers whose compositional meanings are no longer active. At the other extreme, Yoruba uses a hortative construction (*jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́*, let us speak the truth) for the same function. Between these extremes, Georgian *simartle rom vtq̇vat*, Finnish *totta puhuen*, Basque *egia esan behar*, Tamil *uṇmaiyil*, and Korean *사실을 말하자면* all function as sentence-level markers signaling frank disclosure or correction of a prior assumption.

### 2.5 Major Asymmetries

**Positive causation of authenticity is universally blocked; negative causation (forgery) is universally available.** No language documents a natural construction for manufacturing or creating genuine authenticity. All languages document natural constructions for forging, falsifying, or destroying authenticity. Certification and authentication constructions (Georgian *dadastureba*, Finnish *todentaa*, Basque *egiaztatu*, Korean *인증하다*) confirm pre-existing authenticity rather than manufacture it; this distinction is maintained across languages.

**Direct causation of dispositional character traits is universally blocked or strongly resisted.** No language documents a natural construction for directly making someone honest, sincere, or genuinely authentic in character. The concept is expressed indirectly (through encouragement, instruction, or creating conditions) in all eight languages. Some indirect facilitation constructions are attested in a few languages (Basque *zintzotu eragin*, Korean *진정성을 높이다*), but these appear to cause conditions for the dispositional state rather than the state itself.

**Positive emergence of truth is more natural than negative emergence.** Constructions expressing that truth comes to light (positive emergence) are more consistently documented and more natural across all eight languages than constructions expressing that truth becomes false or is destroyed (negative emergence). The asymmetry is consistent, though the data does not systematically test negative emergence constructions.

**The negative agentive derivation is more productive than the positive agentive derivation.** Agentive nominals for liars, forgers, and deceivers are lexicalized in all eight languages; agentive nominals for truth-tellers and honest persons as role nouns are absent or marginal in all eight languages.

## 3. Global Constraint Summary

The G7a constraint matrix identifies the following patterns across twelve semantic dimensions and twelve constructional functions. Confidence ratings reflect the consistency of evidence across languages.

### 3.1 Strongly Supported Constraints

**Predicative attribution is broadly available for all truth-domain dimensions.** The construction type CF1 (subject + copula/predicate asserting a truth-domain property) is rated ALLOWED across all twelve semantic dimensions in all eight languages. No documented restriction was found. This is the most robust positive finding in the dataset.

**Adnominal modification is broadly available for all truth-domain dimensions.** CF3 (truth-domain evaluative term modifying a noun) is rated ALLOWED across all twelve dimensions in all eight languages. The modifier position varies (prenominal in seven languages, postnominal in Yoruba) but the function is unrestricted.

**Abstract nominalization is broadly productive for all truth-domain dimensions.** CF6 (suffix or circumfix producing an abstract noun naming a truth-domain quality) is rated ALLOWED across all twelve dimensions in all eight languages.

**Negative attribution is broadly available for all truth-domain dimensions.** CF2 (negation of a truth-domain predicate) is rated ALLOWED across all twelve dimensions in all eight languages.

**The positive agentive nominalization pathway is blocked across all dimensions and all languages.** CF7 (agentive nominalization) is rated BLOCKED for positive roles (truth-teller, honest person as role noun, authenticator) across all dimensions in all eight languages. The negative pole (liar, forger, deceiver) is ALLOWED. This is the most consistently documented blocked pathway in the dataset, attested across all language families.

**The intransitive truth-revelation construction is broadly available for propositional truth and truth-revelation dimensions.** CF10 (truth as grammatical subject of an emergence verb) is rated ALLOWED for D1 (Propositional Correspondence) and D10 (Truth Revelation and Concealment as Processes) in all eight languages.

**Positive causation of authenticity is broadly blocked; negative causation is broadly available.** CF5 (causative) × D4 (Genuineness of Objects) shows a consistent polarity asymmetry: causing genuine authenticity is unnatural; forging, falsifying, or destroying authenticity is natural. This asymmetry is documented across all eight languages.

**Direct causation of dispositional character traits is broadly blocked or strongly resisted.** CF5 (causative) × D2 (Personal Honesty and Integrity) and D7 (Authenticity of Inner States) is rated BLOCKED for direct causation of the dispositional state in all eight languages. Some indirect facilitation constructions are attested in a few languages, but direct causation of honesty or sincerity is consistently absent.

### 3.2 Moderately Supported Patterns

**The inchoative / change-of-state construction is available for propositional truth and authenticity dimensions.** CF4 (inchoative) is rated ALLOWED for D1, D4, D5, and D10 in at least five languages. The periphrastic inchoative (become X, turn out to be X) is the universal default; morphological inchoatives are restricted to specific roots or bases in specific languages.

**The causative construction is available for verification and truth-revelation dimensions.** CF5 (causative) is rated ALLOWED for D1, D9 (Evidential Grounding and Proof), and D10 in at least five languages. Causative constructions for proving, verifying, revealing, and concealing truth are attested across all eight languages.

**The existential/scalar predication construction is available for abstract quality nominals.** CF9 (existential or scalar predication) is rated ALLOWED for D3 (Source Trustworthiness), D6 (Precision and Exactness), D7 (Authenticity of Inner States), and D9 in at least three languages. Korean shows the most systematic use, where the existential *있다/없다* frame is the only available predication strategy for *-seong* quality nominals.

**Positive emergence of truth is more natural than negative emergence.** The intransitive truth-revelation construction (positive emergence) is documented in all eight languages; negative emergence constructions (truth becoming false) are not documented as natural collocations in any language. The asymmetry is consistent but the data does not systematically test negative emergence across all languages.

**The discourse-framing construction for frank disclosure is broadly available.** CF8 (discourse framing) is rated ALLOWED for D1 in all eight languages, though the degree of grammaticalization varies from fully grammaticalized (Indonesian, Turkish) to fully compositional (Yoruba).

### 3.3 Weak or Uncertain Areas

**The truth-revelation construction is not documented for legal validity, precision, evidential grounding, or reasoning correctness dimensions.** CF10 is rated INSUFFICIENT DATA for D5, D6, D9, and D11. The absence of documentation for these dimensions is consistent across languages but may reflect data collection scope rather than a genuine structural constraint.

**The discourse-framing construction is not documented for source evaluation, object authentication, legal validity, evidential grounding, or the deception/error distinction.** CF8 is rated INSUFFICIENT DATA for D3, D4, D5, D9, and D12. The absence is consistent but may reflect the institutional/technical nature of these dimensions rather than a genuine prohibition.

**The parallel morphological opposition construction is not broadly attested.** CF11 (parallel morphological opposition for verification/falsification) is rated VARIABLE, with the fully productive pattern documented only in Turkish (*doğrulamak/yanlışlamak*). Partial parallels exist in Finnish and Basque. The pattern is not documented in the other five languages.

**The proverbial construction is not documented for the precision/exactness dimension.** CF12 (proverbial/fixed moral assertion) is rated INSUFFICIENT DATA for D6. The absence is consistent across all eight languages and may reflect the technical nature of precision evaluation, but the data does not systematically test this.

**Inchoative and causative constructions for personal honesty, inner-state authenticity, and appearance-versus-reality dimensions are variably attested.** CF4 and CF5 are rated VARIABLE for D2, D7, and D8. Some constructions are attested in some languages, but the pattern is not as consistently documented as for D1 or D4.

### 3.4 Variability Across Languages

Several constraints show consistent directionality but variable strength across languages. The degree of elaboration of the inner-state authenticity dimension (D7) varies considerably: Korean *진정성* is a highly developed public evaluative category with a rich collocation network, while Tamil and Turkish show less elaboration of this as a distinct lexical dimension. The appearance-versus-reality dimension (D8) is expressed through dedicated adjectives in Georgian, Finnish, and Basque, but through discourse markers and constructions in the other five languages. The philosophical/technical truth domain (truth values, truth conditions, truth tables) is elaborated in Finnish, Turkish, and Korean but is absent or marginal in the other five languages.

## 4. Candidate Universals and Outliers

### 4.1 Strong Cross-Language Tendencies

The following patterns are documented in all eight languages across unrelated language families and are rated as strong cross-language tendencies. They are not claimed as absolute universals, as the language sample is limited and data completeness varies.

1. Predicative attribution is broadly available for truth-domain properties. (All 8 languages, all 12 dimensions, no documented restriction.)
2. Adnominal modification is broadly available for truth-domain evaluative terms. (All 8 languages, all 12 dimensions, no documented restriction.)
3. Abstract nominalization of truth-domain properties is broadly productive. (All 8 languages, all 12 dimensions.)
4. Negative attribution is broadly available. (All 8 languages, all 12 dimensions.)
5. The propositional truth / personal honesty distinction is recurrently lexicalized. (All 8 languages; the two functions are never covered by identical terms.)
6. The source-trustworthiness / content-credibility distinction is recurrently lexicalized. (All 8 languages; explicitly documented in at least 4.)
7. The deliberate deception / innocent error distinction is recurrently lexicalized. (All 8 languages; explicitly documented in at least 6.)
8. The intransitive truth-revelation construction is broadly attested. (All 8 languages; truth as grammatical subject of an emergence verb.)
9. A proverbial assertion that truth ultimately prevails is broadly attested. (All 8 languages; specific formulation varies.)
10. Lexicalized single-word agentive nominals for habitual truth-tellers are broadly absent or marginal. (All 8 languages; the concept is expressed periphrastically in all languages.)
11. Direct causation of dispositional character traits is broadly blocked or strongly resisted. (All 8 languages; no natural construction for "make someone honest" is documented.)
12. Positive causation of authenticity is broadly blocked; negative causation (forgery) is broadly available. (All 8 languages; the polarity asymmetry is consistent.)
13. A discourse-framing construction for frank disclosure is broadly attested. (All 8 languages; degree of grammaticalization varies.)
14. A spatial metaphor of straightness underlies truth and correctness vocabulary across unrelated language families. (All 8 languages; transparency of spatial etymology varies.)
15. The legal/procedural validity vs. moral/substantive legitimacy distinction is recurrently maintained. (All 8 languages; degree of lexical differentiation varies.)

### 4.2 Tentative or Weak Candidates

The following patterns are documented in most but not all languages, or are documented in all languages but with weaker evidence.

- The neutral-copy / fraudulent-fake distinction is recurrently lexicalized. (6/8 languages with explicit documentation; Georgian and Yoruba show less elaboration.)
- Positive emergence of truth is more natural than negative emergence. (Consistent across all 8 languages but not systematically tested.)
- No language has a dedicated lexical item for "half-truth." (Consistent across all 8 languages but may reflect data collection scope.)
- The philosophical/technical truth domain (truth values, truth conditions) is elaborated in some languages but not others. (Documented in Finnish, Turkish, Korean; absent or marginal in Yoruba, Tamil, Indonesian.)

### 4.3 Major Outliers

**Yoruba** shows the broadest divergence from the cross-language pattern. The postnominal modifier position is a systematic structural difference in the direction of modification (the only language in the dataset where truth-domain evaluative modifiers follow the head noun). Yoruba also lacks documented dedicated vocabulary for documentary forgery and legal/procedural validity, and uses a hortative construction rather than a grammaticalized adverbial for the frank-disclosure function. The merger of propositional correctness (D1) and procedural/moral correctness (D11) under the *tọ́* root is shared with Tamil but is more complete in Yoruba. Several of these divergences may reflect data collection gaps rather than genuine structural differences.

**Tamil** shares with Yoruba the merger of D1 and D11 under the *cari* root, and shows less elaboration of the precision/exactness and appearance-versus-reality dimensions as distinct lexical categories.

**Korean** diverges in the direction of greater elaboration and more specific constructional constraints. The morphological restriction blocking *-hada* predicative suffixation on *-seong* quality nominals (forcing the existential *있다/없다* frame) is unique in the dataset. The *-하다/-되다* stative/agentive distinction (performed quality vs. inherent/resultant quality) has no parallel in any other language. Korean also documents three distinct inchoative verbs for truth-revelation (*드러나다*, *밝혀지다*, *판명되다*) with different nuances, while other languages typically use a single verb.

**Basque** is unique in documenting a three-way morphological distinction within the verifiability sub-domain (*egiaztagarria* verifiable / *egiaztagaitza* difficult to verify / *egiaztaezina* impossible to verify), and in the documented semantic shift of *ziurrenik* from "certainly" to "probably/most likely."

**Turkish** is unique in documenting a fully productive parallel morphological opposition for verification/falsification (*doğrulamak/yanlışlamak*) with further parallel stacking generating *doğrulanabilirlik/yanlışlanabilirlik* (verifiability/falsifiability).

**Georgian** is unique in the breadth of institutional extension of the *marṭl-* root (orthography, Orthodox Christianity, the justice system, proportionality) and in the documented bifurcation of *sarwmunoeba* into "credibility" (secular) and "faith/religion" (theological) across registers.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

### 5.1 Data Gaps

**Language sample.** The eight languages analyzed represent six genealogical groupings but leave large portions of the world's linguistic diversity unrepresented. The findings should not be generalized beyond the sample without further testing. Languages from Sino-Tibetan, Afroasiatic, Austronesian (beyond Indonesian), Nilo-Saharan, and indigenous American families are absent.

**Domain coverage.** Several dimensions are less thoroughly documented than others. The philosophical/technical truth domain (truth values, truth conditions, truth tables) is elaborated in Finnish, Turkish, and Korean but is absent or marginal in the other five languages; it is unclear whether this reflects genuine structural differences or data collection scope. The appearance-versus-reality dimension (D8) is documented with dedicated adjectives in only three languages (Georgian, Finnish, Basque); the other five languages may express the contrast through constructions not captured in the current data.

**Negative emergence constructions.** The data does not systematically test constructions expressing that truth becomes false or is destroyed (negative emergence). The positive emergence bias is strongly suggested but not definitively established because the negative pole was not systematically elicited.

**Indirect facilitation constructions.** The data does not clearly distinguish direct causation of dispositional character traits from indirect facilitation of conditions for those traits. Some indirect facilitation constructions are attested in Basque and Korean, but the boundary between direct and indirect causation is not consistently drawn across languages.

**Agentive nominalization for positive roles.** The universal absence of lexicalized truth-teller agentives is documented but the mechanism is not established. The data does not distinguish between frequency effects (liars are more salient as social categories), structural constraints (dispositional character traits resist being named as stable role identities), and pragmatic asymmetries (the positive pole is the default and does not require marking).

### 5.2 Methodological Limitations

**Data collection methodology.** The F-phase data was collected through structured elicitation rather than corpus analysis. This means that frequency information is not available, and the naturalness ratings reflect informed judgments rather than corpus-based frequency counts. Some constructions rated as CORE may be less frequent in actual usage than the ratings suggest.

**Epistemic status ratings.** Items rated [UNCERTAIN] or [MARGINAL] in the F-phase data introduce uncertainty into the G-phase analyses. Where the evidence for a pattern rests substantially on marginal or uncertain items, the confidence rating should be treated with caution.

**Single-pass data collection.** The F-phase data for each language was collected through a single analytical pass. Independent replication by native speaker linguists would strengthen the findings, particularly for the less well-documented languages.

**Absence of corpus validation.** The findings have not been validated against large corpora. Corpus validation would be particularly valuable for the naturalness ratings, the frequency of specific constructions, and the degree of grammaticalization of discourse-framing expressions.

### 5.3 Recommended Next Steps

**Additional languages.** The most important next step is expanding the language sample to include languages from underrepresented families. Priority candidates include Mandarin Chinese (Sino-Tibetan), Arabic (Afroasiatic), Swahili (Bantu/Niger-Congo), Quechua (Quechuan), Japanese (Japonic), and a Nilo-Saharan language. This would allow testing of whether the candidate universals hold beyond the current sample.

**Targeted elicitation of blocked constructions.** The positive agentive nominalization block, the positive causation of authenticity block, and the direct causation of dispositional character traits block should be tested through targeted elicitation across all eight languages and additional languages. Specifically: (a) Can a single-word agentive for "truth-teller" be coined and accepted as natural? (b) Can a construction for "manufacturing genuine authenticity" be produced and accepted? (c) What is the boundary between direct causation and indirect facilitation of dispositional character traits?

**Systematic testing of negative emergence.** Constructions expressing that truth becomes false, that a genuine item becomes a fake, or that an honest person becomes dishonest should be systematically elicited across all eight languages to test the positive emergence bias.

**Corpus validation.** The naturalness ratings and construction frequencies should be validated against large corpora for at least a subset of the languages. This would allow frequency-based confirmation of the patterns identified through elicitation.

**Deeper analysis of the D1/D11 merger.** The merger of propositional correctness and procedural/moral correctness in Yoruba and Tamil should be investigated through targeted elicitation to determine whether the two dimensions can be distinguished in context, and whether the merger is a genuine structural property or a data collection artifact.

**Investigation of the neutral-copy intermediate.** The three-way distinction (original/neutral copy/fraudulent fake) should be systematically tested in Georgian and Yoruba to determine whether the neutral intermediate category is genuinely absent or is expressed through constructions not captured in the current data.

**Refined analysis of the agentive asymmetry.** The mechanism behind the universal positive agentive nominalization block should be investigated through targeted elicitation, including testing whether coined positive agentive forms are rejected as unnatural, awkward, or simply unusual. This would help distinguish structural prohibition from frequency effects.

**Cross-architectural validation.** The constraint patterns identified in this report should be tested using data collection methodologies that do not rely on a single analytical system. Independent replication using corpus data, native speaker judgments, and data from additional analytical frameworks would strengthen or qualify the findings.
