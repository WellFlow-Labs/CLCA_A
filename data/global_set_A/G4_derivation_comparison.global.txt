# G4 — Cross-Language Derivational and Morphological Strategies

## Cross-Language Comparison Table

| Strategy Type | Description | Languages Using It | Concrete Examples | Distribution / Notes |
|---------------|-------------|--------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **ABSTRACT QUALITY NOMINALIZATION** | Productive affixation creating abstract nouns denoting the quality or state of being X from adjectival or verbal bases. | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -ობა (სამართლიანობა "justice" from სამართლიანი)<br>**Yoruba**: ì- prefix (ìgbàgbọ́ "belief" from gbàgbọ́), ò- prefix (òtítọ́ "truth" from tọ́)<br>**Finnish**: -uus/-yys (totuus "truth" from tosi, rehellisyys "honesty" from rehellinen)<br>**Basque**: -tasun (egiazkotasuna "truthfulness" from egiazko, zintzotasuna "honesty" from zintzo)<br>**Tamil**: -தன்மை (உண்மைத்தன்மை "truthfulness" from உண்மை)<br>**Indonesian**: ke-...-an circumfix (kebenaran "truth" from benar, kejujuran "honesty" from jujur)<br>**Turkish**: -lık/-lik with vowel harmony (doğruluk "truth" from doğru, gerçeklik "reality" from gerçek)<br>**Korean**: -성 (진실성 "truthfulness" from 진실, 정직성 "honesty" from 정직) | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Universal across sample. Highly productive in all languages. Creates abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives/verbs. Represents core nominalization strategy across typologically diverse languages. |
| **PRIVATIVE/NEGATIVE AFFIXATION** | Productive affixation creating opposites meaning "without X" or "not X," forming systematic morphological oppositions. | **7 of 8 languages** (weak in Yoruba) | **Georgian**: უ- prefix (სამართლო/უსამართლო "just/unjust", მართებულო/უმართებულო "justified/unjustified")<br>**Yoruba**: àì- prefix (tọ́/àìtọ́ "correct/incorrect") [less productive than in other languages]<br>**Finnish**: epä- prefix (tosi/epätosi "true/untrue", rehellinen/epärehellinen "honest/dishonest", todellinen/epätodellinen "real/unreal")<br>**Basque**: -gabe suffix (kள்ளம் கபடம் இல்லாத "without deceit-cunning")<br>**Tamil**: -ஆமை classical privative (பொய்யாமை "non-falsehood" = truthfulness)<br>**Indonesian**: tidak- incorporated in nominalizations (ketidakjujuran "dishonesty" from ke-tidak-jujur-an)<br>**Turkish**: -sız/-siz with vowel harmony (haklı/haksız "justified/unjustified", tutarlı/tutarsız "consistent/inconsistent")<br>**Korean**: 부-/불- prefix (정확/부정확 "accurate/inaccurate", 확실/불확실 "certain/uncertain") | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Present in 7/8 languages. Creates systematic morphological oppositions. Yoruba relies more on lexical oppositions (òtítọ́/irọ́) than morphological negation. Represents strong cross-linguistic tendency toward morphological opposition-marking. |
| **ADJECTIVAL DERIVATION FROM NOUNS** | Affixation creating adjectives meaning "characterized by X" or "having the quality of X" from nominal bases. | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -იანი (სამართლიანი "just" from სამართალი "law"), -ური (ავთენტური "authentic")<br>**Yoruba**: (uses tonal patterns and zero-derivation rather than clear affixation)<br>**Finnish**: -llinen (todellinen "real" from tosi, virheellinen "erroneous" from virhe)<br>**Basque**: -zko/-ezko (egiazkoa "true" from egia, gezurrezko "false" from gezur)<br>**Tamil**: -ஆன (உண்மையான "true" from உண்மை, பொய்யான "false" from பொய்)<br>**Indonesian**: (often uses base form directly or periphrasis; -an suffix less productive for adjectives)<br>**Turkish**: -lı/-li with vowel harmony (haklı "justified" from hak, tutarlı "consistent" from tutar-)<br>**Korean**: -적 (사실적 "realistic" from 사실, 실질적 "substantial" from 실질) | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Present in all languages but with varying productivity and strategies. Basque -zko/-ezko and Finnish -llinen most productive. Indonesian and Yoruba rely more on zero-derivation or periphrastic constructions. |
| **MANNER ADVERBIALIZATION** | Affixation or case marking creating adverbs indicating manner of action from adjectival bases. | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -ად (მართლაც "really" from მართალი, სამართლიანად "justly" from სამართლიანი)<br>**Yoruba**: (uses bare forms or serial verb constructions rather than affixation)<br>**Finnish**: -sti (todennäköisesti "probably" from todennäköinen, rehellisesti "honestly" from rehellinen)<br>**Basque**: -ki (zuzenki "correctly" from zuzen), -ro (egokiro "appropriately" from egoki)<br>**Tamil**: -ஆக (உண்மையாக "truly" from உண்மை, பொய்யாக "falsely" from பொய்)<br>**Indonesian**: dengan + adjective (dengan jujur "honestly", secara + adjective for formal register)<br>**Turkish**: -ca/-ça with harmony (hakça "fairly" from hak), -le/-la instrumental case (kesinlikle "certainly")<br>**Korean**: -게 (정직하게 "honestly" from 정직), -히 (확실히 "certainly" from 확실) | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Universal function but diverse morphological strategies. Suffixation (Finnish -sti, Korean -게/-히, Basque -ki), case marking (Turkish -le, Tamil -ஆக), prepositional phrases (Indonesian dengan), or bare forms (Yoruba). |
| **AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Affixation creating nouns denoting persons characterized by or habitually engaged in X. | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -არა (ტყუარა "liar" from ტყუ-)<br>**Yoruba**: a- prefix (apurọ́ "liar" from purọ́)<br>**Finnish**: -ja (valehtelija "liar" from valehdella)<br>**Basque**: -ti + -a (gezurtia "liar" from gezur), -zale (egiazale "truth-lover" from egia) [marginal]<br>**Tamil**: -அன் (பொய்யன் "liar" from பொய்), -ஆள் (பொய்யாள் "liar"), -வாதி (உண்மைவாதி "truth-advocate") [less common]<br>**Indonesian**: peN- (pembohong "liar" from bohong)<br>**Turkish**: -cı/-ci with harmony (yalancı "liar" from yalan, sahtekâr "fraud" from sahte + Persian -kâr)<br>**Korean**: -쟁이 (거짓말쟁이 "liar" from 거짓말) | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Universal across sample. **CRITICAL ASYMMETRY**: All languages productively lexicalize "liar" but 7/8 lack dedicated "truth-teller" agentive (only Tamil உண்மைவாதி attested, marked as less common in F2a). Represents robust cross-linguistic blocking pattern. |
| **VERBAL NOUN FORMATION** | Affixation creating action/process nouns from verbs (gerunds, masdars, infinitival nouns). | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -ება (გამართლება "justification" from გამართლება verb)<br>**Yoruba**: ì- prefix (ìpurọ́ "lying" from purọ́, ìgbàgbọ́ "belief" from gbàgbọ́)<br>**Finnish**: -minen (todentaminen "verification" from todentaa)<br>**Basque**: -tze (egiaztatze "verification" from egiazta-)<br>**Tamil**: verbal noun forms (multiple strategies)<br>**Indonesian**: peN-...-an (pembenaran "justification" from benar, pembohongan "deception" from bohong)<br>**Turkish**: -me/-ma with harmony (doğrulama "verification" from doğrula-)<br>**Korean**: -기 or -ㅁ/-음 (믿음 "belief" from 믿-, 속임 "deception" from 속이-) | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Universal. Creates action/process nouns distinct from quality nouns. Highly productive in all languages. Represents fundamental nominalization strategy. |
| **DENOMINATIVE VERB FORMATION** | Affixation creating verbs from nominal or adjectival bases, particularly for verification/authentication actions. | **7 of 8 languages** (Yoruba uses serial verbs) | **Georgian**: (uses preverbs + root combinations)<br>**Yoruba**: (uses serial verb constructions instead of affixal derivation)<br>**Finnish**: -ta/-taa (todentaa "verify" from tosi, väärentää "forge" from väärä)<br>**Basque**: -tu (egiazkotu "verify" from egiazko, faltsutu "falsify" from faltsu)<br>**Tamil**: -படுத்து causative (உண்மைப்படுத்து "verify" from உண்மை)<br>**Indonesian**: -kan suffix (membenarkan "verify" from benar, memalsukan "falsify" from palsu)<br>**Turkish**: -la/-le with harmony (doğrulamak "verify" from doğru, yanlışlamak "falsify" from yanlış)<br>**Korean**: -하다 (확인하다 "verify", 왜곡하다 "distort") | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Present in 7/8 languages. Yoruba uses serial verb constructions (ṣe + noun) instead of affixal derivation. Highly productive for creating verification/authentication verbs across languages. |
| **INCHOATIVE DERIVATION** | Affixation creating verbs meaning "to become X" from adjectival bases. | **6 of 8 languages** (weak in Georgian, Yoruba) | **Finnish**: (uses separate verb tulla "become" + translative case rather than affixal inchoative)<br>**Basque**: (uses separate verb izan "become" rather than affixal inchoative)<br>**Tamil**: ஆகு verb (உண்மையாகு "become true")<br>**Indonesian**: menjadi separate verb (menjadi benar "become true") [highly productive]<br>**Turkish**: -leş/-laş with harmony (gerçekleşmek "become real/happen", kesinleşmek "become definite") [highly productive and lexicalized]<br>**Korean**: -아/어지다 (정확해지다 "become accurate") [productive but blocked with abstract truth terms] | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Turkish and Korean show productive affixal inchoatives. Indonesian uses highly productive periphrastic construction. Finnish, Basque, Tamil use periphrastic constructions. Georgian and Yoruba show weaker grammaticalization. **CRITICAL RESTRICTION**: Most languages block or mark inchoatives with abstract truth nouns (진실이 되다 marginal in Korean, requires context in Georgian), preferring revelation verbs instead. |
| **CAUSATIVE DERIVATION** | Affixation creating transitive verbs meaning "to make X" or "to cause to be X" from bases. | **7 of 8 languages** (Yoruba uses serial verbs) | **Georgian**: გა- preverb combinations (გამართლება "justification")<br>**Yoruba**: (uses serial verb constructions: mú kí "cause that" rather than affixal causatives)<br>**Finnish**: (uses separate causative verb tehdä "make" + translative case)<br>**Basque**: -tu verbalizer can have causative sense (egiazkotu "verify" = make true/authentic)<br>**Tamil**: -படுத்து causative suffix (உண்மைப்படுத்து "verify" = make true)<br>**Indonesian**: -kan suffix (membenarkan "confirm/verify" = make true, membohongi "lie to" uses -i applicative)<br>**Turkish**: -tir/-dir with harmony (gerçekleştirmek "realize" from gerçekleş-, kesinleştirmek "finalize" from kesinleş-)<br>**Korean**: -게 하다 periphrastic (믿게 하다 "make believe") | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Present in 7/8 languages. Turkish shows productive morphological stacking (inchoative + causative). Yoruba uses serial verb constructions instead. **SEMANTIC RESTRICTION**: "Make true" in verification sense productive; literal creation sense blocked or marked in most languages. |
| **COMPOUNDING: TRUTH-TERM + NOUN** | Productive compounding where truth-domain terms modify nouns to create specialized concepts. | **All 8 languages** | **Georgian**: ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული "true love", მართლწერა "orthography" (right-writing)<br>**Yoruba**: òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ "true statement", ọ̀nà òdodo "way of righteousness"<br>**Finnish**: tosi tarina "true story", totuudenmukainen "truthful" (truth-according-to)<br>**Basque**: egiazko maitasuna "true love", gezur-sare "web of lies"<br>**Tamil**: உண்மைக்கதை "true story", மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் "true knowledge/enlightenment"<br>**Indonesian**: benar-benar terjadi "really happened", salah paham "misunderstanding"<br>**Turkish**: doğru yol "right path", gerçek hayat "real life", hak yolu "path of truth"<br>**Korean**: 진실게임 "truth game", 사실관계 "facts of case", 거짓뉴스 "fake news" | **WIDELY ATTESTED**: Universal. Varies in productivity: Tamil and Korean show most extensive compounding systems (documented in F2b with dozens of examples). Indonesian shows exceptional productivity with salah- compounds. All languages show this as productive pattern in F2a/F2b tables. |
| **POSSESSIVE ADJECTIVE FORMATION** | Affixation creating adjectives meaning "having X" or "characterized by X" from nominal bases. | **5 of 8 languages** (prominent pattern) | **Georgian**: (uses different strategies; -იანი serves similar function)<br>**Yoruba**: (uses tonal patterns rather than clear affixation)<br>**Finnish**: (uses -llinen or compounds rather than dedicated possessive pattern)<br>**Basque**: -dun "having" (not prominent in truth-domain data)<br>**Tamil**: (uses compounding rather than dedicated possessive affix)<br>**Indonesian**: ber- prefix (berjujur "be honest" marked as marginal in F2a)<br>**Turkish**: -lı/-li with vowel harmony (haklı "having right/justified" from hak, tutarlı "having consistency" from tutar-) [highly productive]<br>**Korean**: (uses different strategies; -적 serves different function) | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Turkish shows most productive use in truth-domain (haklı, tutarlı, tutarsız documented in F2a). Other languages achieve similar meanings through different morphological strategies. Not a universal pattern but recurrent in some language types. |
| **INSTRUMENTAL/ABLATIVE ADVERBIALIZATION** | Case marking or affixation creating emphatic or manner adverbs from abstract nouns, often using instrumental or ablative cases. | **6 of 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -ით instrumental (not prominent in F2 data)<br>**Yoruba**: (uses bare forms or serial verbs)<br>**Finnish**: -lla/-llä instrumental (kesinlikle "certainly" from kesinlik-llä), -sta/-stä ablative (totuudesta "from truth")<br>**Basque**: -z instrumental (itxuraz "apparently" from itxura-z), -tan locative/manner (benetan "really")<br>**Tamil**: -ஆல் instrumental (not prominent in truth-domain F2 data)<br>**Indonesian**: dengan + noun prepositional (dengan jujur "honestly")<br>**Turkish**: -le/-la instrumental (kesinlikle "certainly" from kesinlik-le), -ten/-dan ablative (gerçekten "really" from gerçek-ten, hakikaten "really" from hakikat-en)<br>**Korean**: -으로 instrumental (진실로 "truly" from 진실-으로, 진정으로 "genuinely") | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Present in 6/8 languages. Finnish and Turkish show most productive use documented in F2a tables. Creates emphatic or manner adverbs from abstract nouns. Georgian and Tamil show limited use in truth-domain. |
| **RELATIONAL ADJECTIVE FORMATION** | Affixation creating adjectives meaning "pertaining to X" or "of the nature of X" from nouns. | **6 of 8 languages** | **Georgian**: -ური (ავთენტური "authentic"), -ებრივი (სამართლებრივი "legal/juridical")<br>**Finnish**: -llinen (tosiasiallinen "factual" from tosiasia, virheellinen "erroneous" from virhe)<br>**Basque**: -zko/-ezko (egiazkoa "true" from egia, gezurrezko "false" from gezur) [highly productive]<br>**Tamil**: (uses compounding more than dedicated relational affix)<br>**Indonesian**: (uses base forms or periphrasis; -al borrowed suffix in faktual)<br>**Turkish**: (uses possessive -lı or direct compounding)<br>**Korean**: -적 (사실적 "realistic" from 사실, 실질적 "substantial" from 실질) | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Basque -zko/-ezko most productive in truth-domain (documented extensively in F2a). Finnish -llinen also productive. Other languages use alternative strategies. |
| **REDUPLICATION FOR INTENSIFICATION** | Reduplication of truth-domain terms or related forms to create emphasis, intensification, or distributive meanings. | **5 of 8 languages** | **Georgian**: (limited; not prominent pattern in F2 data)<br>**Yoruba**: tọ́ọ́tọ́ (distributive "each correctly"), uses ideophones (gidigidi, déédé) more than reduplication<br>**Finnish**: (limited; not prominent pattern in F2 data)<br>**Basque**: zuzen-zuzen "very straight/directly" (documented in F2b)<br>**Tamil**: (limited in truth-domain)<br>**Indonesian**: benar-benar "really/truly", sungguh-sungguh "earnestly" (documented extensively in F2a/F2b)<br>**Turkish**: doğru dürüst "proper/decent" (fixed collocation in F2b)<br>**Korean**: 진짜 진짜 "really really" (documented in F2b as colloquial intensification) | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Productive in Indonesian (multiple examples in F2a), Turkish (fixed collocations), Korean (colloquial), Basque (emphatic). Absent or limited in Georgian, Finnish, Tamil. Yoruba uses ideophonic system instead of reduplication. |
| **INCHOATIVE + CAUSATIVE STACKING** | Morphological pattern where inchoative "become X" suffix is followed by causative "make" suffix, creating "make become X" verbs. | **2 of 8 languages** | **Turkish**: -leş (inchoative) + -tir (causative) → gerçekleştirmek "realize/accomplish" (lit. | **COMPOUNDING: ERROR-TYPE SPECIFICATION** | Highly productive compounding where "wrong/error" terms combine with action verbs or nouns to specify particular types of errors or mistakes. | **1 language** (Indonesian exceptional) | **Indonesian**: salah paham "misunderstand", salah guna "misuse", salah kaprah "common error", salah tingkah "awkward", salah langkah "misstep", salah jalan "wrong way", salah alamat "wrong address", salah urus "mismanagement", salah tafsir "misinterpret", salah ucap "slip of tongue", salah tulis "typo", salah hitung "miscalculation", salah lihat "see wrongly", salah dengar "mishear" [all documented in F1 and F2b] | **SINGLE-LANGUAGE OBSERVATION**: Indonesian shows exceptional productivity documented extensively in F1 and F2b tables (14+ distinct compounds). | **COMPOUNDING: INTERNAL OPPOSITION** | Single compounds that internally encode truth/falsehood or reality/appearance opposition within a single lexical item. | **2 of 8 languages** | **Korean**: 진위 "truth-or-falsehood" (진 true + 위 false), 허실 "empty-or-real" (허 empty + 실 real) [documented in F1 and F2b]<br>**Turkish**: doğru yanlış "true-false" (documented in F2b as fixed pair, though not single-word compound) | **WEAKLY ATTESTED**: Korean shows clear lexicalization of oppositional compounds (진위, 허실 documented in F1). | **CLASSICAL PRIVATIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Classical or literary construction defining truth as absence of falsehood rather than positive quality, using privative morphology. | **2 of 8 languages** | **Tamil**: பொய்யாமை (poyyāmai) "truthfulness" = பொய் "lie" + ஆ negative + மை abstract noun [documented in F1 and F2a as classical Tamil ethical literature term]<br>**Basque**: vilpitön "sincere" = vilppi "deceit" + -tön "without" [documented in F1] | **WEAKLY ATTESTED**: Present in Tamil (classical register, marked in F1 as from Tirukkuṟaḷ) and Basque. Other languages define truth positively rather than as absence of falsehood. Represents archaic or literary strategy not productive in modern registers. |
| **BODY-PART METAPHOR COMPOUNDING** | Compounding using body-part terms in truth-related expressions, often metaphorical for sincerity or character. | **5 of 8 languages** | **Georgian**: გულწრფელი "sincere" (გული "heart" + წრფელი "pure") [documented in F1]<br>**Yoruba**: òdodo ọkàn "righteousness of heart" (ọkàn "heart"), tọ́ ọkàn "set heart correctly" [documented in F1 and F2b]<br>**Basque**: (less productive; not prominent in F2 data)<br>**Tamil**: உண்மைக்கண் "eye of truth" [marked as marginal in F1]<br>**Turkish**: içten "heartfelt" (lit. "from inside") [documented in F1]<br>**Korean**: 진심 "true heart/sincerity" (진 true + 심 heart) [documented in F1] | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Present in 5/8 languages. Georgian and Yoruba show most productive use documented in F1/F2b. Often metaphorical rather than literal. Tamil example marked as marginal. Finnish and Indonesian show limited use. |
| **REGISTER-BASED PARALLEL SYSTEMS** | Maintenance of parallel truth-domain vocabularies based on register, formality, or etymological source (native vs. borrowed). | **4 of 8 languages** (highly developed) | **Tamil**: Native (சரி, தப்பு) vs. Sanskrit loans (சத்தியம், நிஜம்) vs. elevated (மெய்) [documented throughout F1]<br>**Korean**: Native (참, 맞다) vs. Sino-Korean formal (진실, 사실, 정확) vs. colloquial hybrid (진짜) [documented throughout F1 with explicit register marking]<br>**Turkish**: Native (doğru, yanlış) vs. Arabic/Persian (hak, hakikat, sahih, meşru) [documented in F1 with etymological notes]<br>**Indonesian**: bohong (colloquial) vs. dusta (formal/literary) [documented in F1] | **MODERATELY ATTESTED**: Tamil, Korean, Turkish show extensive stratification documented in F1 tables. | **DIRECTIONAL/GUIDANCE SEMANTIC EXTENSION** | Semantic extension where "straight/correct" terms extend to directional guidance, proper care, or management domains beyond epistemic correctness. | **2 of 8 languages** | **Yoruba**: tọ́ → tọ́ ẹsẹ̀ "direct steps", tọ́ ọwọ́ "guide hand", tọ́jú "care for properly", tọ́ ọkàn "set heart correctly" [all documented in F1 and F2b]<br>**Georgian**: მართ- → მართვა "management/governance", მართავს "manages/governs/steers" [documented in F1 and F2a] | **WEAKLY ATTESTED**: Yoruba shows most extensive development documented in F1 (tọ́jú, tọ́ ẹsẹ̀, tọ́ ọwọ́, tọ́ ọkàn all listed). | **POLYSEMY: TRUTH/BODY** | Polysemy where a single root means both "truth/reality" and "body/physical," creating systematic ambiguity. | **1 language** | **Tamil**: மெய் (mey) means both "truth/reality" (elevated register) and "body/physical" (மெய்யெழுத்து "consonant" = body-letter, மெய்ப்பாடு "bodily suffering") [documented in F1 with explicit note of polysemy] | **SINGLE-LANGUAGE OBSERVATION**: Unique to Tamil, documented in F1. | **POLYSEMY: RIGHT/TRUTH/DESERVINGNESS/DIVINITY** | Exceptional polysemy where a single root spans truth, moral rightness, legal rights, deservingness, and theological reference to God. | **1 language** | **Turkish**: hak means "truth" (elevated), "right" (legal entitlement), "due" (what is deserved), and "God" (Hak Teala "God Almighty"). ## Narrative Analysis

### Recurring Patterns Across Unrelated Language Families

**1. Abstract quality nominalization (UNIVERSAL)**

All eight languages—spanning six unrelated families (Kartvelian, Niger-Congo, Uralic, isolate, Dravidian, Austronesian, Turkic, Koreanic)—show highly productive affixation for creating abstract quality nouns from truth-domain bases. This is documented in F2a tables for all languages:

- **Suffixation dominant**: Georgian -ობა, Finnish -uus/-yys, Basque -tasun, Tamil -தன்மை, Turkish -lık/-lik, Korean -성
- **Prefixation**: Yoruba ì-, ò-
- **Circumfixation**: Indonesian ke-...-an

**Evidence strength**: F2a tables document multiple examples for each language (Georgian: 15+ forms, Finnish: 10+ forms, Turkish: 20+ forms, Korean: 15+ forms). This represents a **robust cross-linguistic universal**: languages productively derive abstract quality nouns in truth-domain regardless of typological profile.

**2. Privative/negative affixation (NEAR-UNIVERSAL)**

Seven of eight languages show productive privative affixation creating systematic morphological oppositions, documented in F2a and F5 tables:

- **Prefixation**: Georgian უ- (15 examples in F5), Finnish epä- (9 examples in F5), Korean 부-/불- (multiple examples in F2a), Indonesian tidak- (in nominalizations)
- **Suffixation**: Turkish -sız/-siz (multiple examples in F2a and F5), Basque -gabe (documented in F1), Tamil -ஆமை (classical, documented in F2a)

Only Yoruba shows weaker development (àì- prefix exists but F2a notes it as "less productive than in other languages").

**Evidence strength**: F5 opposition tables document systematic morphological oppositions in 7 languages. This represents a **strong cross-linguistic tendency**: languages prefer morphological strategies for creating oppositions in truth-domain, not just lexical antonymy.

**3. Agentive nominalization asymmetry (UNIVERSAL)**

All eight languages show the same striking asymmetry documented in F2a tables and noted in F3 semantic groupings:

- **"Liar" lexicalized**: Georgian ტყუარა (F2a), Yoruba apurọ́ (F2a), Finnish valehtelija (F2a), Basque gezurtia (F2a), Tamil பொய்யன் (F2a), Indonesian pembohong (F2a), Turkish yalancı (F2a), Korean 거짓말쟁이 (F2a)

- **"Truth-teller" absent or periphrastic**: F2a tables explicitly note absence:
  - Georgian: no dedicated term
  - Yoruba: "no common agentive" (F2a note)
  - Finnish: "no *totuuden puhuja" (F2a note)
  - Basque: egiazale "truth-lover" marked as [MARGINAL] in F2a
  - Tamil: உண்மைவாதி "truth-advocate" marked as "less common than liar terms" in F2a
  - Indonesian: "no *penjujur" (F2a note)
  - Turkish: "no *doğrucu" (F2a note)
  - Korean: "no agentive" (F2a note)

**Evidence strength**: F2a tables for all eight languages explicitly document this asymmetry. This represents a **robust cross-linguistic universal**: negative agent roles are more readily lexicalized than positive ones.

**4. Verification/authentication verb derivation (NEAR-UNIVERSAL)**

Seven of eight languages show productive denominative verb formation for verification/authentication actions, documented in F2a tables:

- Georgian: (uses preverb combinations)
- Finnish: -ta/-taa (todentaa "verify", väärentää "forge")
- Basque: -tu (egiazkotu "verify", faltsutu "falsify")
- Tamil: -படுத்து (உண்மைப்படுத்து "verify")
- Indonesian: -kan (membenarkan "verify", memalsukan "falsify")
- Turkish: -la/-le (doğrulamak "verify", yanlışlamak "falsify")
- Korean: -하다 (확인하다 "verify", 왜곡하다 "distort")

Yoruba uses serial verb constructions instead of affixal derivation.

**Evidence strength**: F2a tables document productive patterns in 7 languages. Represents **strong cross-linguistic tendency** toward dedicated verification morphology.

**5. Truth-term + noun compounding (UNIVERSAL)**

All eight languages show productive compounding where truth-domain terms modify nouns, documented extensively in F2b tables:

- Georgian: F2b documents 20+ examples (ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული "true love", მართლწერა "orthography")
- Yoruba: F2b documents 15+ examples (òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ "true statement", ọ̀nà òdodo "way of righteousness")
- Finnish: F2b documents 25+ examples (tosi tarina "true story", totuudenmukainen "truthful")
- Basque: F2b documents 30+ examples (egiazko maitasuna "true love", gezur-sare "web of lies")
- Tamil: F2b documents 40+ examples (உண்மைக்கதை "true story", மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் "true knowledge")
- Indonesian: F2b documents 30+ examples (benar-benar terjadi "really happened", salah paham "misunderstanding")
- Turkish: F2b documents 35+ examples (doğru yol "right path", gerçek hayat "real life")
- Korean: F2b documents 40+ examples (진실게임 "truth game", 사실관계 "facts of case")

**Evidence strength**: F2b tables document extensive productivity in all languages. Represents **universal strategy** for creating specialized truth-related concepts.

### Striking Asymmetries

**1. Inchoative "become true" blocking**

F2a and F6 tables document systematic resistance to simple "become true" constructions with abstract truth nouns across most languages:

- **Blocked or marginal** (documented in F6):
  - Korean: 진실이 되다 rated [MARGINAL] "semantically odd"
  - Tamil: "requires causation" (F6 note)
  - Georgian: "requires specific contexts" (F6 note)
  - Basque: rated [MARGINAL] "marked construction"
  - Finnish: käydä toteen restricted to predictions (F6 note)

- **Natural** (documented in F6):
  - Indonesian: menjadi benar rated [CORE]
  - Turkish: gerçekleşmek rated [CORE] and lexicalized

- **Substitution strategy** (documented in F4 and F6):
  - All languages prefer revelation verbs: Korean 드러나다, Finnish paljastua, Tamil வெளிப்படு, Georgian გამოვლინდა, Basque atera, Indonesian terungkap, Turkish ortaya çıkmak

**Evidence strength**: F6 naturalness tables explicitly rate inchoative constructions. F4 tables document revelation verb preference. Represents **strong cross-linguistic tendency** with clear exceptions.

**Possible explanation**: Abstract truth is conceptualized as pre-existing and discovered rather than as coming into being; inchoatives imply creation or transformation, conflicting with truth's ontological status.

**2. Causative "make true" semantic restrictions**

F2a and F4 tables document semantic split in causative constructions:

- **Verification sense productive** (all languages):
  - Georgian: გამართლება "justification" (F2a)
  - Finnish: todistaa "prove" (F2a)
  - Basque: egiazkotu "verify" (F2a)
  - Tamil: உண்மைப்படுத்து "verify" (F2a)
  - Indonesian: membenarkan "confirm/verify" (F2a)
  - Turkish: doğrulamak "verify" (F2a)
  - Korean: 확인하다 "verify" (F2a)

- **Literal creation sense blocked or marked**:
  - Korean: F4 notes "implies manipulation or philosophical impossibility"
  - Tamil: F4 notes similar restriction
  - Georgian: F4 notes "requires specific contexts"
  - Basque: F4 notes semantic markedness

**Evidence strength**: F2a documents productive verification verbs. F4 construction tables note semantic restrictions on literal "make true" sense. Represents **systematic cross-linguistic distinction**: establishing pre-existing truth is productive; creating truth is blocked or marked.

**3. Passive voice preference in revelation contexts**

F4 and F6 tables document systematic voice preferences:

- **Passive strongly preferred**:
  - Finnish: F4 notes "passive dominant in formal registers"
  - Korean: F4 notes "밝혀지다 'be revealed' preferred over active forms"

- **Intransitive preferred**:
  - Basque: F4 documents egia atera "truth came out" (intransitive)
  - Tamil: F4 documents வெளிப்படு "come out" (intransitive)

- **Active and passive both common**:
  - Georgian, Indonesian, Turkish (F4 notes)

- **Active preferred**:
  - Yoruba: F4 notes "passive less grammaticalized"

**Evidence strength**: F4 construction tables explicitly document voice preferences. Correlates with general typological patterns: languages with highly developed passive systems extend them to truth-domain.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**1. Truth-teller agentive blocking (UNIVERSAL)**

F2a tables for all eight languages document this asymmetry:

- **Productive agentive patterns exist**: All languages have productive agentive nominalization (documented in F2a)
- **These patterns freely create "liar" nouns**: All F2a tables document liar terms
- **The same patterns do NOT create "truth-teller" nouns**: F2a tables explicitly note absence or marginality

**Evidence**:
- Georgian F2a: no entry for truth-teller agentive
- Yoruba F2a: "no common agentive" note
- Finnish F2a: "no *totuuden puhuja" note
- Basque F2a: egiazale marked [MARGINAL]
- Tamil F2a: உண்மைவாதி marked "less common than liar terms"
- Indonesian F2a: "no *penjujur" note
- Turkish F2a: "no *doğrucu" note
- Korean F2a: "no agentive" note

This represents a **robust one-way derivational pathway**: negative → agentive is productive; positive → agentive is blocked or avoided.

**Possible explanation**: Lying is marked behavior requiring characterization; truth-telling is unmarked/expected behavior not requiring special lexicalization.

**2. Inchoative blocking with abstract truth (STRONG TENDENCY)**

F6 naturalness tables document blocking or markedness in 6/8 languages:

- **Inchoative affixation productive in these languages**: Turkish -leş (F2a), Korean -아/어지다 (F2a), Finnish -ua/-yä (F2a)
- **These patterns freely apply to many adjectives**: F2a tables show productivity
- **They are blocked, marginal, or semantically odd with abstract truth terms**:
  - Korean F6: 진실이 되다 rated [MARGINAL] "semantically odd"
  - Tamil F6: "requires causation"
  - Georgian F6: "requires specific contexts"
  - Basque F6: rated [MARGINAL]
  - Finnish F6: käydä toteen "restricted to predictions"

**Substitution strategy**: F4 and F6 tables document that all languages substitute revelation/discovery constructions (passive or intransitive verbs emphasizing emergence from hiddenness).

**Evidence strength**: F6 naturalness ratings explicitly document blocking. F4 tables document substitution strategies. Represents **strong cross-linguistic tendency** with clear exceptions (Indonesian, Turkish allow inchoatives).

**Possible explanation**: Abstract truth is conceptualized as pre-existing and discovered rather than as coming into being.

**3. Causative "make true" restrictions**

F4 construction tables document semantic restrictions:

- **Verification sense productive**: All F2a tables document verification verbs
- **Literal creation sense blocked or implies manipulation**:
  - Korean F4: "implies manipulation or philosophical impossibility"
  - Tamil F4: similar note
  - Georgian F4: "requires specific contexts"
  - Indonesian F4: "semantically marked"

**Distinction**: Verification (establishing pre-existing truth) is productive; creation (bringing truth into existence) is blocked or marked.

**Evidence strength**: F4 construction tables explicitly note this semantic split. Represents **systematic cross-linguistic restriction** on causative semantics.

### Productivity, Restriction, and Lexicalization Patterns

**HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE PATTERNS** (documented as [CORE] in F2a/F2b across multiple languages):

1. **Abstract quality nominalization**: F2a tables show 10-20+ derived forms per language; freely generates new forms
2. **Privative negation**: F2a and F5 tables show systematic oppositions in 7 languages; creates productive paradigms
3. **Verification verb derivation**: F2a tables show multiple verification verbs per language; freely creates authentication terms
4. **Truth-term + noun compounding**: F2b tables document 20-40+ compounds per language; highly productive

**RESTRICTED PATTERNS** (documented with restrictions in F2a/F4/F6):

1. **Agentive nominalization**: F2a tables show restriction to negative pole (liar) in all languages; positive pole (truth-teller) systematically blocked
2. **Inchoative derivation**: F6 tables show blocking or markedness with abstract truth terms in 6/8 languages; productive with concrete or gradable predicates
3. **Causative "make true"**: F4 tables show restriction to verification sense; blocked for literal creation sense in most languages
4. **Possessive adjective formation**: F2a shows high productivity in Turkish (haklı, tutarlı); less productive or absent in truth-domain in other languages

**LEXICALIZED/FOSSILIZED PATTERNS** (marked as [MARGINAL] or noted as non-productive in F2a):

1. **Classical privative constructions**: Tamil பொய்யாமை marked in F1 as "classical Tamil ethical literature term"; F2a notes "not productive pattern in modern Tamil"
2. **Internal oppositional compounds**: Korean 진위, 허실 documented in F1 and F2b; F2a notes "pattern not productive for creating new oppositional compounds"
3. **Postpositional grammaticalization**: Turkish hakkında documented in F1 and F2b; F2a notes "fully grammaticalized; original connection to hak 'right/truth' opaque to speakers"
4. **Fixed collocations**: Turkish doğru dürüst, Georgian მართლაც, Indonesian benar-benar documented in F2b as lexicalized intensifiers; F2a notes "reduplication pattern not freely productive in truth-domain"

**UNCLEAR OR WEAKLY ATTESTED PATTERNS** (marked as [MARGINAL] or [UNCERTAIN] in F2a):

1. **Inchoative + causative stacking**: F2a shows productive in Turkish (gerçekleştirmek, kesinleştirmek); unclear if productive in Korean (periphrastic forms attested but morphological stacking not clear)
2. **Body-part metaphor compounding**: F2a shows productive in Georgian and Yoruba; present but less productive in Turkish, Korean, Tamil; unclear productivity in Finnish, Basque, Indonesian
3. **Relational adjective formation**: F2a shows productive in Basque (-zko/-ezko); present in Finnish, Georgian, Korean; less clear in Indonesian, Tamil, Turkish (use alternative strategies)

4. **Register stratification**: Extreme in Korean, Tamil, Turkish (F1 evidence); minimal in Yoruba, Georgian (F1 evidence)

These patterns suggest **universal cognitive or pragmatic constraints** on truth-domain morphology, not merely language-specific idiosyncrasies. All asymmetries are explicitly documented in F-phase tables with naturalness ratings and usage notes.
