# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 NATURALNESS (Change, Causation, and Stability)
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## SECTION 1: BECOMING TRUE/VERIFIED (Inchoative Constructions)

### 1.1 Basic Inchoative with √bhū

**Pattern:** X + satya/tathya + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + ADJ(NOM) + √bhū (to become)

**Examples:**
- तत् सत्यं भवति / tat satyaṃ bhavati / "that becomes true, proves true"
- एतत् तथ्यं भूतम् / etat tathyaṃ bhūtam / "this became true, proved factual"
- वचनं सत्यं भविष्यति / vacanaṃ satyaṃ bhaviṣyati / "the statement will prove true"

**Usage notes:** Extremely common construction expressing verification or revelation of truth-status. Used across all registers. Past participle bhūta frequently used for completed state change. No restrictions observed.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 Inchoative with Epistemic Terms

**Pattern:** X + pramā/pramāṇa + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + N(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- ज्ञानं प्रमा भवति / jñānaṃ pramā bhavati / "knowledge becomes valid cognition"
- इदं प्रमाणं भूतम् / idaṃ pramāṇaṃ bhūtam / "this became a valid means (of knowledge)"

**Usage notes:** Common in epistemological contexts expressing establishment of epistemic validity. The construction indicates transition from uncertain or unvalidated status to validated status.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.3 Inchoative with yathārtha

**Pattern:** X + yathārtha + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + ADJ(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- ज्ञानं यथार्थं भवति / jñānaṃ yathārthaṃ bhavati / "knowledge becomes accurate, comes to correspond to reality"
- तद् यथार्थं भूतम् / tad yathārthaṃ bhūtam / "that became accurate, proved true to fact"

**Usage notes:** Emphasizes achievement of correspondence between representation and reality. Common in epistemological and ordinary discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.4 Inchoative with dharma

**Pattern:** X + dharma + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + N(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- एतत् धर्मः भवति / etat dharmaḥ bhavati / "this becomes dharma, becomes right/proper"
- कर्म धर्मः भूतम् / karma dharmaḥ bhūtam / "the action became dharma, proved righteous"

**Usage notes:** Less common than with epistemic terms. Used when actions or situations come to be recognized as conforming to dharma. More common in conditional constructions (yadi satyam tadā dharmaḥ bhavati "if true, then it becomes dharma").

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.5 Inchoative with yukta

**Pattern:** X + yukta + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + ADJ(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- तत् युक्तं भवति / tat yuktaṃ bhavati / "that becomes proper/fitting, proves reasonable"
- वचनं युक्तं भूतम् / vacanaṃ yuktaṃ bhūtam / "the statement became proper, proved fitting"

**Usage notes:** Common in contexts where logical propriety or practical fitness is established through reasoning or events. Used across philosophical and ordinary registers.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.6 Revelation/Manifestation with √prakāś

**Pattern:** X + svarūpa/tattva + prakāś
**Structure:** NOM + N(NOM) + √kāś (to shine, appear)

**Examples:**
- स्वरूपं प्रकाशते / svarūpaṃ prakāśate / "the true nature is revealed, manifests"
- तत्त्वं प्रकाशितम् / tattvaṃ prakāśitam / "reality was revealed, truth was manifested"

**Usage notes:** Specifically used for revelation or manifestation of essential nature or reality. Common in philosophical and religious contexts. Emphasizes coming to light rather than simple change of state.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.7 Establishment with √sidh

**Pattern:** X + (instrumental of means) + sidh
**Structure:** NOM + (INST) + √sidh (to be established, accomplished)

**Examples:**
- प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / pramāṇena siddham / "established by valid means, proven"
- युक्त्या सिद्धम् / yuktyā siddham / "established by reasoning, proven logically"
- तत् सत्यं सिद्धम् / tat satyaṃ siddham / "that is established as true, proven true"

**Usage notes:** Extremely common construction for expressing epistemic validation or logical proof. The instrumental typically specifies the means of establishment (pramāṇa, yukti, etc.). Standard in philosophical argumentation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.8 Coming to be known with √jñā (passive)

**Pattern:** X + jñāyate
**Structure:** NOM + √jñā (passive: to become known)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं जञायते / satyaṃ jñāyate / "truth becomes known, is recognized"
- तत्त्वं जञातम् / tattvaṃ jñātam / "reality became known, was understood"

**Usage notes:** Passive construction emphasizing the process of coming to be known rather than active discovery. Common in philosophical contexts for truth-revelation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 2: BECOMING FALSE/INVALIDATED (Negative Inchoative)

### 2.1 Basic Negative Inchoative with √bhū

**Pattern:** X + asatya/mithyā + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + ADJ/ADV + √bhū

**Examples:**
- तत् असत्यं भवति / tat asatyaṃ bhavati / "that becomes false, proves untrue"
- मिथ्या भूतम् / mithyā bhūtam / "became false, proved illusory"
- वचनं मृषा भवति / vacanaṃ mṛṣā bhavati / "the statement becomes false, proves vain"

**Usage notes:** Common construction for falsification or revelation of falsity. Adverbial forms (mithyā, mṛṣā) function without inflection. Used across all registers.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Falling into error with √gam

**Pattern:** X + bhrama/viparyaya + gam
**Structure:** NOM + ACC + √gam (to go, fall into)

**Examples:**
- भ्रमं गच्छति / bhramaṃ gacchati / "falls into error, becomes confused"
- विपर्ययं गतः / viparyayaṃ gataḥ / "fell into error, underwent reversal"

**Usage notes:** Specifically used for entry into cognitive error or confusion. The verb √gam emphasizes movement into an undesirable epistemic state. Common in epistemological contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Falling into error with √pat

**Pattern:** X + bhrama/viparyaya + pat
**Structure:** NOM + LOC + √pat (to fall)

**Examples:**
- भ्रमे पतति / bhrame patati / "falls into error"
- विपर्यये पतितः / viparyaye patitaḥ / "fallen into error, lapsed into confusion"

**Usage notes:** Alternative to √gam construction, emphasizing downward movement into error. Slightly more dramatic or emphatic than √gam. Common in philosophical and narrative contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 Becoming invalidated (epistemic terms)

**Pattern:** X + apramā/apramāṇa + bhū
**Structure:** NOM + N(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- ज्ञानम् अप्रमा भवति / jñānam apramā bhavati / "knowledge becomes invalid cognition"
- इदम् अप्रमाणं भूतम् / idam apramāṇaṃ bhūtam / "this became an invalid means"

**Usage notes:** Used in epistemological contexts for invalidation of previously accepted knowledge or sources. Less common than positive inchoatives but fully productive.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.5 Loss of validity/authority

**Pattern:** prāmāṇya + naś/kṣi
**Structure:** N(NOM) + √naś/√kṣi (to perish, be destroyed)

**Examples:**
- प्रामाण्यं नश्यति / prāmāṇyaṃ naśyati / "validity perishes, authority is lost"
- प्रामाण्यं क्षीयते / prāmāṇyaṃ kṣīyate / "validity diminishes, authority wanes"

**Usage notes:** Specifically used for loss or destruction of epistemic validity or authority. Emphasizes complete loss (naś) or gradual diminishment (kṣi). Common in epistemological debates.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 3: CAUSING TO BE TRUE/VERIFIED (Causative Constructions)

### 3.1 Validation with √kṛ (light verb)

**Pattern:** Y + X + pramāṇa/satya + kṛ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + ACC/INST + √kṛ (to make)

**Examples:**
- प्रमाणं करोति / pramāṇaṃ karoti / "makes (it) authoritative, validates"
- सत्यं करोति / satyaṃ karoti / "makes true, verifies"
- तत् प्रमाणं कृतम् / tat pramāṇaṃ kṛtam / "that was made authoritative, validated"

**Usage notes:** Light verb √kṛ is the primary strategy for causative/factitive meanings in truth-domain. Less grammaticalized than dedicated causatives. Common in technical and administrative contexts for validation/authentication.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (for pramāṇa), [MARGINAL] (for satya - less idiomatic)

---

### 3.2 Authentication with denominative verb

**Pattern:** Y + X + pramāṇīkṛ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + pramāṇī-√kṛ (denominative: to authenticate)

**Examples:**
- प्रमाणीकरोति / pramāṇīkaroti / "authenticates, validates, certifies"
- प्रमाणीकृतम् / pramāṇīkṛtam / "authenticated, validated, certified"

**Usage notes:** Denominative verb formed from pramāṇa + -ī- + √kṛ. More formal/technical than simple pramāṇaṃ karoti. Common in modern administrative and legal usage. Less common in classical philosophical texts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (in modern usage), [MARGINAL] (in classical texts)

---

### 3.3 Establishment/proof with √sidh (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + sādhayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √sidh (causative: to establish, prove)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं साधयति / satyaṃ sādhayati / "establishes as true, proves"
- प्रमाणेन साधयति / pramāṇena sādhayati / "establishes by valid means, proves through evidence"
- तत् साधितम् / tat sādhitam / "that was established, proven"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √sidh is standard construction for proving or establishing truth-claims. Instrumental typically specifies means of proof. Very common in philosophical and logical texts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Demonstration with √darś (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + darśayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √dṛś (causative: to show, demonstrate)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं दर्शयति / satyaṃ darśayati / "shows/demonstrates the truth"
- तत्त्वं दर्शितम् / tattvaṃ darśitam / "reality was shown, truth was demonstrated"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √dṛś (to see) used for demonstrating or showing truth. Emphasizes making visible or evident. Common in philosophical and pedagogical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Revelation/manifestation with √prakāś (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + prakāśayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + pra-√kāś (causative: to reveal, manifest)

**Examples:**
- तत्त्वं प्रकाशयति / tattvaṃ prakāśayati / "reveals reality, manifests truth"
- स्वरूपं प्रकाशितम् / svarūpaṃ prakāśitam / "true nature was revealed"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √kāś used for revealing or bringing to light essential nature or truth. Common in philosophical and religious contexts. Emphasizes active revelation by agent.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.6 Giving proof with √dā

**Pattern:** Y + X + pramāṇa + dā
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + DAT(recipient) + ACC(proof) + √dā (to give)

**Examples:**
- प्रमाणं ददाति / pramāṇaṃ dadāti / "gives proof, provides evidence"
- प्रमाणं दत्तम् / pramāṇaṃ dattam / "proof was given, evidence was provided"

**Usage notes:** Construction with √dā (to give) for providing proof or evidence. Dative typically specifies recipient. Common in argumentative and legal contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.7 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for direct causative of satya

**Pattern:** *Y + X + satyīkaroti (not attested)

**Notes:** Unlike pramāṇa, the term satya does not form a productive denominative causative (*satyīkaroti). The construction satyaṃ karoti exists but is less idiomatic than alternatives. Preferred expressions use √sidh (sādhayati "establishes"), √darś (darśayati "demonstrates"), or passive constructions (satyena sidh "is established as true").

**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT]

---

## SECTION 4: CAUSING TO BE FALSE/INVALIDATED (Negative Causative)

### 4.1 Falsification with √kṛ

**Pattern:** Y + X + mṛṣā/mithyā + kṛ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + ADV + √kṛ

**Examples:**
- मृषा करोति / mṛṣā karoti / "makes false, falsifies"
- मिथ्या कृतम् / mithyā kṛtam / "made false, falsified"

**Usage notes:** Light verb √kṛ with adverbial mṛṣā/mithyā for falsification. Less common than positive causatives. Attested but not highly idiomatic. Alternative expressions often preferred.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 4.2 Refutation with √bādh

**Pattern:** Y + X + bādhate/bādhayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √bādh (to obstruct, contradict, refute)

**Examples:**
- प्रमाणं बाधते / pramāṇaṃ bādhate / "contradicts the valid means, refutes the proof"
- तत् बाधितम् / tat bādhitam / "that was refuted, contradicted"

**Usage notes:** Standard verb for refutation or contradiction in philosophical contexts. Used when one pramāṇa contradicts another (pramāṇabādha). Very common in epistemological debates.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Invalidation with √hā (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + hāpayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √hā (causative: to cause to abandon, invalidate)

**Examples:**
- प्रामाण्यं हापयति / prāmāṇyaṃ hāpayati / "causes validity to be abandoned, invalidates"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √hā (to abandon) used for invalidation. Less common than √bādh. Emphasizes causing something to be given up or abandoned.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 4.4 Destruction of validity with √naś (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + nāśayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √naś (causative: to destroy)

**Examples:**
- प्रामाण्यं नाशयति / prāmāṇyaṃ nāśayati / "destroys validity, annihilates authority"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √naś (to perish) for active destruction of validity or authority. Emphasizes complete destruction. Less common than √bādh for refutation.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 4.5 Causing error with √bhram (causative)

**Pattern:** Y + X + bhrāmayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √bhram (causative: to cause to wander, confuse)

**Examples:**
- बुद्धिं भ्रामयति / buddhiṃ bhrāmayati / "confuses the mind, causes error"
- भ्रामितः / bhrāmitaḥ / "confused, caused to err"

**Usage notes:** Causative of √bhram (to wander, err) for causing confusion or error. Common in contexts of deliberate deception or natural confusion. Emphasizes active causation of error.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.6 Deception with √vañc

**Pattern:** Y + X + vañcayati
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √vañc (to deceive, cheat)

**Examples:**
- वञ्चयति / vañcayati / "deceives, cheats, misleads"
- वञ्चितः / vañcitaḥ / "deceived, cheated"

**Usage notes:** Standard verb for deliberate deception or cheating. Implies moral culpability. Common in ethical and legal contexts. Does not specifically target truth-domain vocabulary but frequently appears in contexts of false speech or fraudulent action.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 5: MAINTAINING/PRESERVING TRUTH (Stability Constructions)

### 5.1 Standing in/by truth with √sthā

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma(LOC) + sthā
**Structure:** NOM + LOC + √sthā (to stand, remain)

**Examples:**
- सत्ये तिष्ठति / satye tiṣṭhati / "stands in truth, remains truthful"
- धर्मे स्थितः / dharme sthitaḥ / "standing in dharma, established in righteousness"
- सत्ये स्थातव्यम् / satye sthātavyam / "one should stand in truth"

**Usage notes:** Extremely common construction for expressing steadfastness or commitment to truth/dharma. Locative indicates the domain or standard in which one stands. Used across ethical, religious, and philosophical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.2 Protecting/upholding with √rakṣ

**Pattern:** Y + satya/dharma + rakṣ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √rakṣ (to protect, guard)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं रक्षति / satyaṃ rakṣati / "protects truth, upholds truthfulness"
- धर्मं रक्षति / dharmaṃ rakṣati / "protects dharma, upholds righteousness"
- धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः / dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ / "dharma protects those who protect it" (famous legal maxim)

**Usage notes:** Standard construction for expressing protection or preservation of truth/dharma. Common in ethical and legal contexts. The reciprocal maxim (dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ) is famous in dharmaśāstra literature.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.3 Maintaining validity with √dhṛ

**Pattern:** Y + prāmāṇya/satya + dhṛ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √dhṛ (to hold, maintain, support)

**Examples:**
- प्रामाण्यं धारयति / prāmāṇyaṃ dhārayati / "maintains validity, upholds authority"
- सत्यं धृतम् / satyaṃ dhṛtam / "truth was maintained, upheld"

**Usage notes:** Verb √dhṛ (to hold, support) used for maintaining or upholding truth/validity. Emphasizes active support or preservation. Common in philosophical and ethical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.4 Not deviating with √cal (negative)

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma(ABL) + na + cal
**Structure:** NOM + ABL + na + √cal (to move, deviate)

**Examples:**
- सत्यात् न चलति / satyāt na calati / "does not deviate from truth"
- धर्मात् न चलितव्यम् / dharmāt na chalitavyam / "one should not deviate from dharma"

**Usage notes:** Ablative of standard + negative + √cal expresses non-deviation or steadfastness. Gerundive form (na chalitavyam) extremely common in prescriptive/ethical texts. Standard expression for moral/epistemic commitment.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.5 Remaining/continuing with √vṛt

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma(LOC) + vṛt
**Structure:** NOM + LOC + √vṛt (to turn, remain, continue)

**Examples:**
- सत्ये वर्तते / satye vartate / "remains in truth, continues in truthfulness"
- धर्मे वर्तमानः / dharme vartamānaḥ / "remaining in dharma, continuing in righteousness"

**Usage notes:** Verb √vṛt with locative for continuing or remaining in a state. Less emphatic than √sthā but equally common. Used across all registers.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.6 Preserving form/nature with √rakṣ

**Pattern:** Y + svarūpa/rūpa + rakṣ
**Structure:** NOM(agent) + ACC(object) + √rakṣ

**Examples:**
- स्वरूपं रक्षति / svarūpaṃ rakṣati / "preserves own nature, maintains essential form"
- रूपं रक्षितम् / rūpaṃ rakṣitam / "form was preserved"

**Usage notes:** Used for preserving essential nature or form. Common in philosophical contexts discussing maintenance of identity or essence. Less common than dharma/satya preservation but fully productive.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 6: LOSS OF TRUTH/VALIDITY (Negative Stability)

### 6.1 Deviation/falling away with √cal

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma(ABL) + cal
**Structure:** NOM + ABL + √cal (to move, deviate)

**Examples:**
- सत्यात् चलति / satyāt calati / "deviates from truth, moves away from truthfulness"
- धर्मात् चलितः / dharmāt chalitaḥ / "deviated from dharma, fallen from righteousness"

**Usage notes:** Ablative of standard + √cal expresses deviation or falling away. Common in contexts of moral/epistemic failure. Often appears in negative constructions (na calati, na chalitavyam).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.2 Abandonment with √hā

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma + hā
**Structure:** NOM + ACC + √hā (to abandon, leave)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं जहाति / satyaṃ jahāti / "abandons truth, forsakes truthfulness"
- धर्मं हीनः / dharmaṃ hīnaḥ / "abandoned dharma, bereft of righteousness"

**Usage notes:** Verb √hā for active abandonment of truth/dharma. Implies deliberate forsaking. Common in ethical condemnation. Past participle hīna "bereft of, deprived of" frequently used.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.3 Turning away with √vṛt (negative prefix)

**Pattern:** X + satya/dharma(ABL) + nivṛt
**Structure:** NOM + ABL + ni-√vṛt (to turn away from)

**Examples:**
- असत्यात् निवृत्तः / asatyāt nivṛttaḥ / "turned away from untruth, abstaining from falsehood"
- अधर्मात् निवर्तते / adharmāt nivartate / "turns away from unrighteousness"

**Usage notes:** Compound verb ni-√vṛt (to turn away) with ablative for renunciation or abstention. Commonly used with negative terms (asatya, adharma) for positive ethical action. Standard in ethical instruction.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.4 Destruction/loss with √naś

**Pattern:** satya/prāmāṇya + naś
**Structure:** N(NOM) + √naś (to perish, be destroyed)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं नश्यति / satyaṃ naśyati / "truth perishes, is destroyed"
- प्रामाण्यं नष्टम् / prāmāṇyaṃ naṣṭam / "validity was lost, authority perished"

**Usage notes:** Intransitive verb √naś for perishing or destruction of truth/validity. Emphasizes complete loss. Common in contexts of epistemic or moral decline.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.5 Diminishment with √kṣi

**Pattern:** prāmāṇya/satya + kṣi
**Structure:** N(NOM) + √kṣi (to diminish, waste away)

**Examples:**
- प्रामाण्यं क्षीयते / prāmāṇyaṃ kṣīyate / "validity diminishes, authority wanes"
- सत्यं क्षीणम् / satyaṃ kṣīṇam / "truth diminished, waned"

**Usage notes:** Intransitive verb √kṣi for gradual diminishment or waning. Less dramatic than √naś. Emphasizes process of decline rather than sudden loss.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 7: SPECIAL DYNAMIC PATTERNS

### 7.1 Reciprocal causation (dharma maxim)

**Pattern:** dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ
**Structure:** N(NOM) + √rakṣ + PAST.PART(NOM)

**Example:**
- धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः / dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ / "dharma protects those who protect it"

**Usage notes:** Famous legal maxim from Manusmṛti expressing reciprocal relationship between dharma and its practitioners. The past participle rakṣitaḥ functions as substantive ("the protected one" = "one who protects it"). Unique construction specific to this maxim but illustrates reciprocal causation pattern.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (as fixed expression)

---

### 7.2 Exclusive triumph (emphatic assertion)

**Pattern:** satyam eva jayate
**Structure:** N(NOM) + eva + √ji (to conquer)

**Example:**
- सत्यमेव जयते / satyam eva jayate / "truth alone conquers, truth alone triumphs"

**Usage notes:** Famous Upaniṣadic motto (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 3.1.6), adopted as national motto of India. Emphatic particle eva stresses exclusivity. While not a causative or inchoative per se, expresses dynamic triumph of truth. Fixed expression but pattern (X eva jayate) is productive.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (as fixed expression)

---

### 7.3 Conditional emergence

**Pattern:** satye sati X bhū
**Structure:** LOC.ABS + N(NOM) + √bhū

**Examples:**
- सत्ये सति धर्मः भवति / satye sati dharmaḥ bhavati / "truth existing, dharma arises"
- प्रमाणे सति प्रमा भवति / pramāṇe sati pramā bhavati / "valid means existing, valid cognition arises"

**Usage notes:** Locative absolute construction expressing conditional relationship. Sati (present participle of √as "to be") indicates existence/presence of condition. Common in philosophical texts for expressing causal or conditional dependencies.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.4 Causal origination

**Pattern:** X(ABL) + Y + utpad/jan
**Structure:** ABL(source) + NOM(result) + √pad/√jan (to arise, be born)

**Examples:**
- भ्रमात् उत्पन्नम् / bhramāt utpannam / "arisen from error, produced by confusion"
- सत्यात् धर्मः जायते / satyāt dharmaḥ jāyate / "from truth, dharma is born"

**Usage notes:** Ablative of source + verb of arising (utpad, jan) for causal origination. Common in philosophical contexts for expressing causal relationships. Emphasizes source or origin of result.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.5 Gradual realization

**Pattern:** X + kramāt/śanaiḥ + jñā/dṛś
**Structure:** ACC + ADV(gradually) + √jñā/√dṛś

**Examples:**
- तत्त्वं क्रमात् जानाति / tattvaṃ kramāt jānāti / "knows reality gradually, understands truth step by step"
- सत्यं शनैः पश्यति / satyaṃ śanaiḥ paśyati / "sees truth gradually"

**Usage notes:** Adverbs kramāt "gradually, step by step" or śanaiḥ "slowly, gradually" with cognitive verbs express gradual realization or understanding. Common in pedagogical and philosophical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.6 Sudden realization

**Pattern:** X + sahasā/ākasmāt + jñā/dṛś
**Structure:** ACC + ADV(suddenly) + √jñā/√dṛś

**Examples:**
- तत्त्वं सहसा जानाति / tattvaṃ sahasā jānāti / "knows reality suddenly, understands truth all at once"
- सत्यम् आकस्मात् प्रकाशते / satyam ākasmāt prakāśate / "truth is revealed suddenly"

**Usage notes:** Adverbs sahasā "suddenly" or ākasmāt "unexpectedly" with cognitive verbs express sudden realization or revelation. Common in narrative and mystical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.7 Transformation of understanding

**Pattern:** X(ABL) + Y(ACC) + pari-√jñā
**Structure:** ABL(from) + ACC(to) + pari-√jñā (to know fully, transform understanding)

**Examples:**
- मिथ्यात् सत्यं परिजानाति / mithyāt satyaṃ parijānāti / "fully knows truth from falsehood, transforms understanding from error to truth"

**Usage notes:** Compound verb pari-√jñā (to know fully, comprehend) with ablative source and accusative goal expresses transformation of understanding. Less common than simple √jñā but attested in philosophical contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 7.8 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "make someone believe X is true"

**Pattern:** *Y + Z + X + satya + manyate (causative) - not standard

**Notes:** Sanskrit lacks a simple, idiomatic causative construction for "make someone believe X is true." Possible expressions include:
- Causative of √man (to think): mānayati "causes to think" - but not specifically used for truth-beliefs
- Causative of √śrad-dhā (to have faith): śraddadhāpayati "causes to have faith" - but more religious than epistemic
- Descriptive constructions: "X speaks such that Y believes" (evaṃ vadati yathā Z manyate)

The concept is typically expressed through indirect means rather than dedicated causative construction.

**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] (as simple causative construction)

---

### 7.9 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "X stops being true"

**Pattern:** *X + satya + sthā (negative) - not idiomatic

**Notes:** Sanskrit lacks a simple, idiomatic expression for "X stops being true" as a neutral change of state. Available expressions emphasize:
- Falsification: mithyā bhavati "becomes false"
- Loss: naśyati "perishes"
- Refutation: bādhyate "is contradicted"

The concept of neutral cessation of truth-status without implication of falsification or loss is not lexicalized in a simple construction.

**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] (as neutral cessation construction)

---

## SECTION 8: ASPECTUAL DISTINCTIONS

### 8.1 Perfective aspect (completed action)

**Pattern:** Past participle constructions
**Structure:** PAST.PART + auxiliary (optional)

**Examples:**
- सत्यं भूतम् (अस्ति) / satyaṃ bhūtam (asti) / "has become true, proved true"
- प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / pramāṇena siddham / "has been established by valid means"
- तत् साधितम् / tat sādhitam / "that has been proven"

**Usage notes:** Past participles express completed action or achieved state. Auxiliary verb often omitted in present tense. Extremely common for expressing established truth-status.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 8.2 Progressive/durative aspect

**Pattern:** Present participle constructions
**Structure:** PRES.PART + auxiliary

**Examples:**
- सत्यं भवत् / satyaṃ bhavat / "becoming true, in process of being verified"
- प्रमाणेन सिध्यत् / pramāṇena sidhyat / "being established by valid means"

**Usage notes:** Present participles express ongoing action or process. Less common than perfective for truth-domain due to nature of verification (typically punctual rather than durative). More common with cognitive verbs (jānat "coming to know").

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (grammatically productive but semantically restricted)

---

### 8.3 Habitual/iterative aspect

**Pattern:** Present tense with habitual adverbs
**Structure:** PRES + sadā/nityam/sarvadā

**Examples:**
- सत्यं सदा वदति / satyaṃ sadā vadati / "always speaks truth, habitually tells the truth"
- नित्यं धर्मे तिष्ठति / nityaṃ dharme tiṣṭhati / "always stands in dharma, constantly remains righteous"

**Usage notes:** Simple present tense with adverbs of frequency (sadā "always," nityam "constantly," sarvadā "at all times") expresses habitual action. Common for characterizing persons or practices.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 8.4 Inceptive aspect (beginning of action)

**Pattern:** ā-√rabh + infinitive
**Structure:** ā-√rabh + INF

**Examples:**
- सत्यं वक्तुम् आरभते / satyaṃ vaktum ārabh ate / "begins to speak truth"
- तत्त्वं ज्ञातुम् आरब्धम् / tattvaṃ jñātum ārabdham / "began to know reality"

**Usage notes:** Compound verb ā-√rabh (to begin) with infinitive expresses inception of action. Less common with truth-domain vocabulary than with action verbs, but fully productive.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] (grammatically productive but less common in truth-domain)

---

### 8.5 Cessative aspect (ending of action)

**Pattern:** √sthā/√vṛt (negative) or √tyaj
**Structure:** na + √sthā/√vṛt or √tyaj + ACC

**Examples:**
- सत्यं वक्तुं न तिष्ठति / satyaṃ vaktuṃ na tiṣṭhati / "ceases to speak truth, no longer tells the truth"
- सत्यं त्यजति / satyaṃ tyajati / "abandons truth, stops being truthful"

**Usage notes:** No dedicated cessative morphology. Cessation expressed through negation of continuative verbs (√sthā, √vṛt) or through verbs of abandonment (√tyaj, √hā). Common in contexts of moral decline.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## SECTION 9: SUMMARY OF ASYMMETRIES AND GAPS

### 9.1 Productive patterns:

1. **Inchoative with √bhū**: Highly productive across all truth-domain terms (satya, tathya, pramā, yukta, dharma, etc.)
2. **Establishment with √sidh**: Standard construction for epistemic validation
3. **Causative with √sidh (sādhayati)**: Standard construction for proving/establishing
4. **Stability with √sthā**: Standard construction for steadfastness in truth/dharma
5. **Protection with √rakṣ**: Standard construction for preserving truth/dharma
6. **Deviation with √cal**: Standard construction for falling away from truth/dharma

### 9.2 Marginal or restricted patterns:

1. **Causative with √kṛ**: Less idiomatic for truth-domain than for other domains
2. **Denominative causatives**: Limited to pramāṇa (pramāṇīkaroti); not productive with other terms
3. **Falsification causatives**: Attested but less common than positive causatives
4. **Transformation constructions**: Grammatically possible but semantically restricted

### 9.3 Absent constructions:

1. **Simple causative "make believe"**: No idiomatic construction for causing belief in truth
2. **Neutral cessation**: No simple expression for "stops being true" without implication of falsification
3. **Dedicated causative morphology**: No morphological causative specifically for truth-domain (unlike some other semantic fields)

### 9.4 Lexical asymmetries:

1. **Positive bias**: Positive terms (satya, pramā, dharma) show richer constructional patterns than negative terms (asatya, apramā, adharma)
2. **Epistemic specialization**: Epistemic terms (pramāṇa, pramā) have more developed causative/validation constructions than moral terms
3. **Vedic archaism**: ṛta and derivatives show limited productivity in dynamic constructions in classical Sanskrit

### 9.5 Register variation:

1. **Philosophical register**: Favors epistemic constructions (pramāṇena sidh, yuktyā sādh)
2. **Ethical register**: Favors moral stability constructions (dharme sthā, satyaṃ rakṣ)
3. **Legal register**: Favors validation and authentication constructions (pramāṇīkṛ, sādh)
4. **Narrative register**: Favors simple inchoatives and revelations (bhū, prakāś)

### 9.6 Semantic constraints:

1. **Punctual vs. durative**: Truth-verification typically conceptualized as punctual (perfective aspect preferred) rather than durative (progressive less common)
2. **Agentive vs. non-agentive**: Some changes (bhū "become") are non-agentive; others (kṛ "make") require agent
3. **Deliberate vs. natural**: Distinction between deliberate causation (√kṛ, √darś) and natural emergence (√bhū, √sidh)
