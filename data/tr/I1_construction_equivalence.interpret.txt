## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for BECOME

Turkish systematically uses the **REVELATION** construction (*çıkmak* light verb) where other languages might use a simple BECOME construction. The *çıkmak* pattern (*doğru çıkmak* "turn out to be true," *sahte çıkmak* "turn out to be fake") emphasizes the **discovery or revelation** of a truth-value that was previously uncertain or hidden, rather than a simple change of state.

The alternative BECOME construction using *dönüşmek* "transform into" (*gerçeğe dönüştü* "it turned into reality") exists but is marked as **[MARGINAL]** in P4b, described as "more formal/literary" and "less common than *çıkmak* pattern." This indicates that Turkish **prefers revelation semantics** over pure inchoative semantics in the truth domain.

**Pattern**: Turkish truth-domain inchoatives encode **epistemic change** (discovery of truth-value) rather than **ontological change** (becoming true).

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION asymmetry

Turkish shows an **asymmetric pattern** for verification vs. disconfirmation:

- **VERIFICATION**: Dedicated morphological derivation *doğrulamak* (doğru + verbalizer *-la-* + infinitive) creates a positive verification verb.
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Two strategies exist:
  1. Negation of verification: *doğrulamamak* "not verify, fail to verify"
  2. Separate lexeme: *yalanlamak* "refute, deny" (from *yalan* "lie")

The asymmetry lies in the fact that **positive verification has dedicated morphology**, while **active disconfirmation** (proving false) requires either a separate lexeme (*yalanlamak*) or circumlocution (*yanlış olduğunu göstermek* "show that it's false"). Simple negation of *doğrulamak* means "fail to verify" rather than "prove false."

### 2.3 CAUSATION (factual) vs. CAUSATION (character)

Turkish shows a clear **structural gap** for CAUSATION (character):

- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Multiple productive light verb constructions exist:
  - *doğru göstermek* "show to be true"
  - *gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak* "bring the truth to light"
  - *doğru kanıtlamak* "prove true"
  
- **CAUSATION (character)**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** exists. P4b notes this as a gap. Circumlocutions like *birini dürüst yapmak* "make someone honest" are grammatically possible but pragmatically awkward and not idiomatic.

**Pattern**: Turkish readily expresses causing factual truth-values but lacks conventional expressions for causing character traits related to truthfulness.

### 2.4 Agentive nominalization asymmetry

Turkish shows an **asymmetric gap** in agentive nominalization:

- **Negative agent** (*yalancı* "liar"): Productive morphological derivation (yalan + agentive *-cı*)
- **Positive agent** ("truth-teller"): **NO MORPHOLOGICAL FORM**; requires periphrastic construction (*doğru söyleyen kişi* "person who tells truth")

P4a explicitly notes: "No dedicated morphological form exists for 'truth-teller.'" This represents a **lexical gap** where the negative agent has a simple lexeme but the positive agent requires a multi-word construction.

### 2.5 Light verb distribution

Turkish distributes semantic operators across **multiple light verb constructions** rather than concentrating them in single dedicated forms:

- **Inchoative/Revelation**: *çıkmak* "emerge, turn out"
- **Causation (factual)**: *göstermek* "show," *etmek* "do/make," *yapmak* "make"
- **Judgment**: *bulmak* "find," *görmek* "see"
- **Acknowledgment**: *vermek* "give," *almak* "take"
- **Speech**: *söylemek* "say/tell"

Each light verb carries specific semantic nuances. For example:
- *Doğru bulmak* "find correct" = subjective judgment
- *Doğru göstermek* "show true" = objective demonstration
- *Hak vermek* "give right" = acknowledge someone else's correctness

This represents a **distributed system** rather than a gap, but it means there is no single unified CAUSATION construction.

### 2.6 Comparative constructions and gradability

Turkish treats truth-value adjectives as **non-gradable** in most contexts:

- Truth-value terms appear in the **standard of comparison** (*gerçekten daha garip* "stranger than reality") but rarely as the **compared element**
- P4b notes: "*Daha doğru* 'more true' is uncommon; truth-values treated as absolute rather than gradable"

This represents a **conceptual constraint** rather than a structural gap: the language can form comparatives with truth-value terms but pragmatically avoids doing so because truth is conceptualized as binary rather than scalar.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

1. **CAUSATION (character)**: No conventional construction for causing character traits related to truthfulness
   - Circumlocution possible but not idiomatic
   - Example gap: "make someone truthful/honest" has no standard expression

2. **Simple BECOME without revelation semantics**: 
   - *Dönüşmek* construction exists but is marginal/literary
   - The language strongly prefers REVELATION (*çıkmak*) over pure inchoative

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization for positive truth-teller**:
   - *Yalancı* "liar" exists as simple lexeme
   - No morphological equivalent for "truth-teller"
   - Requires periphrastic *doğru söyleyen (kişi)*

2. **Active disconfirmation verb**:
   - *Yalanlamak* "refute" exists but is semantically distinct (means "deny, call a lie")
   - No direct morphological counterpart to *doğrulamak* "verify" for proving false
   - Gap filled by negation (*doğrulamamak*) or circumlocution

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

1. **Gradable truth-values**:
   - Comparative constructions grammatically possible
   - Pragmatically avoided because truth conceptualized as absolute
   - Not a structural gap but a usage constraint

2. **Character causation**:
   - May reflect conceptual constraint: character traits not viewed as causable in the same way factual truth-values are

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization**: Negative (*yalancı*) has morphology; positive requires periphrasis

2. **Verification vs. disconfirmation**: Positive verification has dedicated morphology (*doğrulamak*); active disconfirmation requires separate lexeme (*yalanlamak*) or circumlocution

3. **Accusative marking in light verb constructions**:
   - *Doğruyu söylemek* "tell the truth" (with accusative *-yu*)
   - *Yalan söylemek* "tell a lie" (no accusative)
   - P4b notes this as "lexicalized difference"

## 5. Summary of Key Findings

1. **Turkish systematically prefers REVELATION over BECOME** in the truth domain, encoding epistemic change (discovery) rather than ontological change.

2. **CAUSATION is distributed** across multiple light verb constructions rather than concentrated in a single operator.

3. **Agentive nominalization shows asymmetry**: negative agent (*yalancı*) has morphology; positive agent requires periphrasis.

4. **CAUSATION (character) represents a true structural gap**: no conventional way to express causing truthfulness as a character trait.

5. **Truth-values are treated as non-gradable** despite grammatical possibility of comparison.

6. **Light verb constructions are highly productive** and serve as the primary strategy for extending truth-domain vocabulary, especially with loan words.
