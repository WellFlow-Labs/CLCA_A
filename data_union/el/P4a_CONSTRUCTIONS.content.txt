# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Greek (el)
# Layer: P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[Subject NP] + εἶναι/είναι (copula) + [truth-domain adjective (predicative)]

Subject + copula (είναι) + truth-domain adjective (αληθής / αληθινός / σωστός / ορθός / ψευδής / ψεύτικος / έγκυρος / αξιόπιστος / αμφίβολος)

### Examples
- Αυτό είναι αληθινό. / *Aftó íne alithinó.* / "This is true / real."
- Η κατάθεσή του είναι ψευδής. / *I katáthesí tu íne psevdhís.* / "His testimony is false."
- Ο ισχυρισμός είναι αληθής. / *O ishirismós íne alithís.* / "The claim is true."
- Η κατάθεση κρίθηκε ψευδής. / *I katáthesi krítike psevdís.* / "The testimony was judged false."
- Αυτή η δήλωση είναι αληθής. / *Aftí i dílosi íne alithís.* / "This statement is true."
- Η κατάθεσή του ήταν ψευδής. / *I katáthesí tu ítan psevdís.* / "His testimony was false."

### Notes
Copular attribution is the canonical frame. The adjective occupies predicative position and inflects for gender agreement with the subject noun. The copula είναι is invariant; the adjective agrees in gender, number, and case with the subject NP.

Register determines adjective choice: αληθής / ψευδής are formal/legal; αληθινός / ψεύτικος are colloquial. Attributive (prenominal) position is possible but less frequent for αληθής/ψευδής; αληθινός and σωστός are more common attributively (*αληθινή ιστορία*, *σωστή απάντηση*). Both αληθής and ψευδής are used in this pattern primarily in formal and written registers; the colloquial equivalents σωστός and ψεύτικος are more frequent in everyday speech.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[Subject NP] + δεν + είναι + [truth-domain adjective] — OR — [Subject NP] + είναι + [privative-prefixed adjective (αν-/α-)] — OR — [Subject NP] + κρίθηκε/αποδείχθηκε + [negated adjective phrase]

### Examples
- Αυτό δεν είναι αληθινό. / *Aftó dhen íne alithinó.* / "This is not true."
- Η πηγή είναι αναξιόπιστη. / *I pigí íne anaksiópistos.* / "The source is unreliable."
- Δεν είναι αληθινό αυτό που λες. / *Den íne alithinó aftó pu les.* / "What you are saying is not true."
- Ο ισχυρισμός δεν είναι τεκμηριωμένος. / *O ishirismós den íne tekmirio̱ménos.* / "The claim is not substantiated."
- Η πηγή αποδείχθηκε αναξιόπιστη. / *I pigí apodíchthike anaksiópistos.* / "The source proved unreliable."

### Notes
Two strategies coexist: (a) sentential negation with δεν + είναι + positive adjective, and (b) a lexicalized privative adjective (αναληθής, αναξιόπιστος, ανακριβής, αναμφίβολος, άκυρος, ανούσιος) in the predicative position. Strategy (b) is preferred in formal and legal registers; strategy (a) is the default in everyday speech. Not all positive adjectives have a lexicalized privative counterpart.

Sentential negation is formed by preverbal δεν; the adjective itself is not morphologically negated. Privative-prefix forms (αναξιόπιστος, αδιαμφισβήτητος) exist as lexicalized negatives but are not the productive negation strategy for most adjectives in this domain.

Both strategies are productive. Sentential negation (δεν είναι) is universal and register-neutral. Privative-prefixed adjectives (αναληθής, αναξιόπιστος, ανακριβής, άκυρος, ανούσιος) are available for most core items and tend toward formal registers. The two strategies are not always interchangeable: άκυρος ("invalid/null") is the standard legal term, not δεν είναι έγκυρος, though the latter is also grammatical.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
[truth-domain adjective] + [head noun] (prenominal, agreeing in gender/number/case) — OR — [head noun] + [truth-domain adjective] (postnominal, less common, more emphatic) — OR — [head noun] + [genitive NP] for nominal modifiers

### Examples
- ψευδής κατάθεση / *psevdhís katáthesi* / "false testimony"
- αξιόπιστη πηγή / *aksiópistos pigí* / "reliable source"
- αληθινή ιστορία / *alithiní istoría* / "true story"
- πλαστό διαβατήριο / *plastó diavatírio* / "a forged passport"

### Notes
Greek adjectives may appear prenominally or postnominally; in the truth domain, prenominal position is slightly more marked (emphatic or literary), while postnominal is neutral. Prenominal attributive position is the default. The adjective agrees with the head noun in gender, number, and case.

Several collocations have achieved semi-fixed status in legal and journalistic registers: ψευδής κατάθεση, ψευδής δήλωση, έγκυρο έγγραφο, αξιόπιστος μάρτυρας, γνήσιο αντίγραφο, έγκυρη ψήφος, εικονικό τιμολόγιο. These fixed collocations are more frequent than free attributive modification and some carry specific legal definitions.

Nominal modifiers (e.g., τεκμήριο αθωότητας, "presumption of innocence") use a following genitive NP rather than a compound or prefix.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
[Subject NP] + αποδεικνύεται / αποδείχθηκε / κρίνεται / κρίθηκε / αποκαλύπτεται / γίνεται + [truth-domain adjective or NP] — expressing transition to an evaluated state

### Examples
- Αποδείχτηκε ψευδής. / *Apodhíchtike psevdhís.* / "It turned out to be false / was proven false."
- Η πληροφορία αποδείχτηκε αναληθής. / *I pliroforía apodhíchtike analithís.* / "The information turned out to be untrue."
- Ο ισχυρισμός αποδείχθηκε αληθής. / *O ishirismós apodíhthike alithís.* / "The claim proved to be true."
- Αποδείχθηκε αναξιόπιστος. / *Apodíhthike anaksiópistos.* / "He proved to be unreliable."
- Ο ισχυρισμός αποδείχθηκε ψευδής. / *O ischirismós apodíchthike psevdís.* / "The claim proved to be false."
- Η υπογραφή αποκαλύφθηκε ως πλαστή. / *I ipogrfí apokalífthike os plastí.* / "The signature was revealed to be forged."

### Notes
Greek lacks a dedicated inchoative copula equivalent to English "become + adjective" that is idiomatic in this domain. The transition-to-state meaning is expressed by passive or middle forms of αποδεικνύω ("to prove/demonstrate") or κρίνω ("to judge"), which simultaneously encode the process of evaluation and its result. A bare γίνεται ("becomes") construction is not idiomatic here; γίνομαι + adjective is grammatical but less natural for truth-value transitions specifically.

The inchoative meaning is expressed through: γίνεται ("becomes") — general inchoative; αποδεικνύεται ("proves to be," "turns out to be") — with epistemic force, indicating that evidence establishes a new status; αποκαλύπτεται ("is revealed to be") — with a disclosure component.

αποδεικνύεται is the most semantically specific inchoative in this domain: it implies that evidence or argument has established the new truth-value, making it common in legal, journalistic, and scientific contexts. The construction [αποδεικνύεται + adjective] is the closest Greek equivalent to English "turns out to be [false/true/invalid]."

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
[Agent NP] + [causative verb] + [Theme NP] + [result adjective/state]

Causative of truth-domain states is expressed periphrastically through dedicated lexical verbs:
- αποδεικνύω ("to prove")
- επαληθεύω ("to verify")
- διαψεύδω ("to refute / show to be false")
- νοθεύω ("to adulterate / falsify")
- πλαστογραφώ ("to forge")
- ακυρώνω ("to invalidate/cancel")
- νομιμοποιώ ("cause to be legitimate")

Periphrastic alternative: κάνω + [NP] + να + [subjunctive verb] — general causative (not idiomatic in this domain)

### Examples
- Απέδειξε ότι η κατάθεση ήταν ψευδής. / *Apédhikse óti i katáthesi ítan psevdhís.* / "He proved that the testimony was false."
- Διέψευσε τους ισχυρισμούς. / *Dhiépsefse tus ischirismús.* / "He refuted / disproved the claims."
- Νόθευσε τα εκλογικά αποτελέσματα. / *Nóthefse ta eklogika apotelesmata.* / "He falsified the election results."
- Ακύρωσε τη σύμβαση. / *Akíro̱se ti símvasi.* / "He annulled the contract."
- Η έρευνα επαλήθευσε τους ισχυρισμούς. / *I érevna epalíthefse tus ischirismús.* / "The investigation verified the claims."
- Η κυβέρνηση διέψευσε τις φήμες. / *I kivérnisi diépsfefse tis fímes.* / "The government refuted the rumors."

### Notes
Greek has no morphological causative. Causation of truth-states is expressed through dedicated verbs: αποδεικνύω (cause to be proven true), επαληθεύω (cause to be verified), διαψεύδω (cause to be shown false), νοθεύω (cause to be adulterated), πλαστογραφώ (cause a document to be forged). These are lexically distinct rather than derived from the adjectives via a productive causative morpheme.

Each verb encodes a specific causal relationship: επαληθεύω causes something to be established as true; διαψεύδω causes something to be established as false; ακυρώνω causes something to become invalid. A general periphrastic causative with κάνω exists but is not idiomatic in this domain.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[truth-domain adjective stem or verbal stem] + [-εια / -ία / -ότητα / -τητα / -οσύνη / -ση] → abstract noun denoting the property, state, or process

Productive suffixes in this domain:
- **-εια / -ια**: αλήθεια, ακρίβεια, αμφιβολία, νοθεία, αυθεντία
- **-ία**: αξιοπιστία, αμφιβολία, νομιμότητα (via -τητα)
- **-ότητα / -τητα**: αυθεντικότητα, γνησιότητα, αξιοπιστία, εγκυρότητα, ορθότητα, σωστότητα
- **-ση**: επαλήθευση, διάψευση, ακύρωση, αμφισβήτηση, πιστοποίηση, τεκμηρίωση

### Examples
- αλήθεια / *alíthia* / "truth" (← αληθής + -εια)
- αξιοπιστία / *aksiopistía* / "credibility, reliability" (← αξιόπιστος + -ία)
- Αμφισβητήθηκε η εγκυρότητα της σύμβασης. / *Amfisviтíthike i engirotita tis símvasis.* / "The validity of the contract was disputed."
- Η επαλήθευση των στοιχείων διήρκεσε μέρες. / *I epalíthefsi ton stoichíon diírkese méres.* / "The verification of the data took days."

### Notes
Three productive suffixes operate in this domain: -εια (αλήθεια, ακρίβεια), -ία (αξιοπιστία, αμφιβολία, νομιμότητα via -τητα), and -ότητα (αυθεντικότητα, γνησιότητα, ορθότητα, εγκυρότητα). The -ότητα suffix is the most productive for newer or borrowed adjectives and for forming quality nouns from adjectives. The -εια/-ια suffix is older and less regular, appearing in core items (αλήθεια, ακρίβεια). The choice among suffixes is lexically determined, not rule-governed.

Verbal nominalizations in -ση (επαλήθευση, πιστοποίηση, αμφισβήτηση, τεκμηρίωση, διάψευση, ακύρωση) form a parallel set denoting the act or process rather than the abstract quality. Both types are productive and well-attested across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[verbal/nominal base] + [-τής (masc.) / -τρια or -τρα (fem.) / -γράφος] → agent noun; OR compound [ψευδο-] + [role noun]

### Examples
- ψεύτης / ψεύτρα / *psévtis / psévtra* / "liar (masc. / fem.)"
- πλαστογράφος / *plastográfos* / "forger"
- αξιολογητής / *aksioloyitís* / "evaluator, assessor"
- Ο ψεύτης αρνήθηκε τα πάντα. / *O psévtis arníthike ta pánda.* / "The liar denied everything."
- Ο πλαστογράφος συνελήφθη. / *O plastográfos sinelífthи.* / "The forger was arrested."

### Notes
The primary agentive for the liar is ψεύτης (masc.) / ψεύτρα (fem.), derived from the ψευτ- root; it is the standard everyday word for "liar." A truth-teller agentive (*αληθευτής) is not in common use; the concept is expressed periphrastically (αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια, "one who tells the truth"). No common agentive noun meaning "truth-teller" is identified in the data.

πλαστογράφος is the standard legal term for a document forger. αξιολογητής ("evaluator/assessor") is attested but belongs to the evaluation subdomain rather than the core truth domain. Other agentives in the domain include αμφισβητίας (habitual disputant, marginal) and πιστωτής (creditor, financial register only).

The ψευδο- prefix generates pseudo-agent compounds (ψευδοεπιστήμονας, ψευδοπροφήτης) following a productive pattern.

## 8. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
[δήθεν / τάχα] + [NP or clause]
OR: [φερόμενος ως] + [NP]

A distinct construction type in which a particle or participial phrase marks the speaker's non-endorsement of a presented claim or status.

### Examples
- Ο δήθεν ειδικός δεν είχε καμία σχετική εκπαίδευση. / *O díthеn eidikós den íche kamía schetikí ekpédefsi.* / "The so-called expert had no relevant training."
- Ο φερόμενος ως ιδιοκτήτης αρνήθηκε να δώσει στοιχεία. / *O ferómenos os idioktítis arníthike na dósi stoichía.* / "The purported owner refused to provide information."

### Notes
δήθεν and τάχα are particles that scope over a following NP or clause and signal the speaker's ironic or skeptical distance from the attributed status or claim. φερόμενος ως is a participial construction used in formal legal and journalistic registers with a neutral (non-ironic) hedging function. The two sub-patterns differ in pragmatic force: δήθεν/τάχα imply the speaker believes the claim is false; φερόμενος ως implies only that the claim is unconfirmed. Both are productive and well-attested.

## 9. EVIDENTIAL / EPISTEMIC-STATUS MARKING

### Pattern
[evidential adverb or fixed phrase] + [proposition] — marking the speaker's epistemic commitment or the evidential basis of a claim

Adverbs derived from truth-domain adjectives (αποδεδειγμένως, αδιαμφισβήτητα, φαινομενικά) and participial modifiers (εικαζόμενος, τεκμηριωμένος).

### Examples
- Αποδεδειγμένως ψευδής ισχυρισμός. / *Apodedeignménos psevdís ishirismós.* / "A demonstrably false claim."
- Εικαζόμενος δράστης. / *Ikazómenos drástis.* / "The alleged perpetrator."

### Notes
Greek marks evidential and epistemic status through adverbs derived from truth-domain adjectives (αποδεδειγμένως, αδιαμφισβήτητα, φαινομενικά) and through participial modifiers (εικαζόμενος, τεκμηριωμένος). These form a productive paradigm: the adverb or participle precedes or modifies the predicate/NP and signals whether a claim is proven, alleged, apparent, or beyond dispute. This pattern is especially productive in legal and journalistic registers.

## 10. DISCOURSE-FRAMING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
[Fixed NP or clause] + [main proposition] — speaker-oriented frame introducing a frank, corrective, or precision-marking statement

Fixed expressions functioning as discourse-level operators rather than propositional predicates: η αλήθεια είναι ότι, για να λέμε την αλήθεια, αλήθεια να λέμε, αν θέλεις την αλήθεια, για να είμαι ακριβής, στην ουσία, ουσιαστικά.

### Examples
- Η αλήθεια είναι ότι δεν το ήξερα. / *I alíthia íne óti dhen to íksera.* / "The truth is that I didn't know."
- Για να λέμε την αλήθεια, δεν συμφωνώ. / *Ya na léme tin alíthia, dhen simfonó.* / "To tell the truth, I don't agree."
- Η αλήθεια είναι ότι δεν υπάρχουν αποδείξεις. / *I alíthia íne óti den ipárhun apodíksis.* / "The truth is that there is no evidence."
- Για να είμαι ακριβής, δεν το είπε ποτέ. / *Ya na íme akrivís, den to ípe poté.* / "To be precise, he never said it."
- Η αλήθεια είναι ότι δεν ήταν έτοιμοι. / *I alíthia íne óti den ítan étimi.* / "The truth is that they were not ready."
- Για να λέμε την αλήθεια, η κατάσταση είναι σοβαρή. / *Ya na léme tin alíthia, i katástasi íne sovará.* / "To tell the truth, the situation is serious."

### Notes
A productive set of fixed frames uses αλήθεια as a discourse particle to introduce frank or corrective statements. These constructions are pragmatically distinct from propositional attribution (§1): they signal speaker stance (candor, frankness) rather than evaluating a proposition's truth-value.

These are syntactically parenthetical or sentence-initial and signal the speaker's epistemic stance (frankness, precision, correction) toward the following proposition. They do not predicate truth of an external claim but frame the speaker's own utterance. Structurally, they are main clauses whose subject is αλήθεια, but their discourse function is that of hedging or framing devices rather than truth predications.

The frame η αλήθεια είναι ότι… is the most frequent written form; για να λέμε την αλήθεια and αν θέλεις την αλήθεια are characteristic of spoken registers. Both η αλήθεια είναι ότι and για να λέμε την αλήθεια are extremely frequent in everyday speech and are fixed in form.

## 11. VERIFICATION / ESTABLISHMENT CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Verb of verification or establishment (επαληθεύω, διαπιστώνω, αποδεικνύω, τεκμηριώνω) + noun phrase or ότι-clause

### Examples
- Διαπίστωσε ότι τα έγγραφα ήταν πλαστά. / *Dhiapístoce óti ta éngrafa ítan plastá.* / "He established / found that the documents were forged."
- Τεκμηριώνει τη θέση του με στοιχεία. / *Tekmirióni ti thési tu me stoichía.* / "He substantiates his position with evidence."

### Notes
This construction type is structurally distinct from simple attribution: it encodes an epistemic process (checking, establishing, proving) rather than a static property. The verb selects either an ότι-complement (propositional content) or a direct object (the thing verified). Passive forms (επαληθεύτηκε, τεκμηριώθηκε) are common in formal and journalistic registers to background the agent of verification.

## 12. PSEUDO- COMPOUND CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
ψευδο- (prefix) + [domain noun or adjective] → compound noun or adjective denoting a category that has the form but not the substance of the base category

### Examples
- ψευδοεπιστήμη / *psevdhoepistími* / "pseudoscience"
- ψευδοδίλημμα / *psevdhodhílima* / "false dilemma"
- ψευδοδημοκρατία / *psevdodimokratía* / "pseudo-democracy, sham democracy"
- ψευδοεπιστήμονας / *psevdoepistímonas* / (from agentive nominalization pattern)
- ψευδοπροφήτης / *psevdoprofítis* / (from agentive nominalization pattern)

### Notes
ψευδο- is a productive prefix that combines freely with domain nouns across registers (scientific, political, religious, rhetorical, philological, aesthetic) to produce compounds meaning "false/sham X." The resulting compound denotes an entity that presents the appearance of the base category without its defining substance. This is structurally distinct from simple negation (δεν είναι επιστήμη) in that it asserts a positive category of simulacrum rather than mere absence.

ψευδο- is a fully productive combining form in contemporary Greek, freely attaching to nouns and adjectives across domains. The pattern is open-ended; new formations are coined as needed. This is the primary structural resource for expressing the concept of something that simulates a category without instantiating it. The prefix is invariant; the compound follows the inflectional pattern of the base noun.
