# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** Analysis is based on Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties), consistent with P1 scope. Transliteration follows the three-vowel orthography established in P1. Where a form is attested primarily in one variety, this is noted. Forms are drawn from documented lexicographic and grammatical sources (Gonzáles Holguín, Lira, Cusihuamán, Cerrón-Palomino, Parker, and related corpora); no forms are invented or back-translated from English.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**P1 participation:** 5 items (*cheqa*, *cheqanchay*, *cheqaq*, *cheqasqa*, *mana cheqa*)
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding truth, genuineness, and correctness. It anchors the truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua and is the most productive root for deriving expressions of epistemic and moral accuracy.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**P1 participation:** 3 items (*allin*, *allinchay*, *mana allin*)
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding goodness, rightness, and proper functioning. It covers moral, practical, and evaluative correctness broadly, and participates in derivations concerning repair, improvement, and well-being.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**P1 participation:** 2 items (*llulla*, *llullakuy*)
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding falsehood, deception, and dishonesty. It is the direct antonym of *cheqa* in the truth-evaluation domain and is productive in moral and legal characterizations of persons and statements.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: cheqa

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root; unanalyzable
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, correct
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate adjective or nominal to assert the truth or genuineness of a statement, person, or object. Foundational evaluative term across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqam / cheqam kay
**Transliteration:** cheqam / cheqam kay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -m (direct evidential enclitic) / cheqa + -m + kay (to be)
**Natural English translation:** it is true; it is indeed the case; that is the truth
**Context of use:** Used as a standalone assertion or predicate to affirm the truth of a preceding statement, with the speaker's direct evidential commitment. Extremely common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to establish as true, to correct
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts for the act of checking a claim or document against reality and confirming its accuracy. Already in P1; included here as the primary causative derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -q (agentive/adjectival nominalizer)
**Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine; one who is truthful
**Context of use:** Used attributively before nouns to mean 'true' or 'genuine', and nominalized to characterize a person as truthful. Already in P1; included as a primary derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqasqa
**Transliteration:** cheqasqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
**Natural English translation:** verified, confirmed, established as true
**Context of use:** Used to describe something that has undergone a process of verification or confirmation. Already in P1; included as a primary derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**6.**
**Form:** cheqakaय़
**Transliteration:** cheqakay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -ka- (intransitive verbalizer/inceptive) + -y (infinitive)
**Natural English translation:** to become true, to become real, to be realized, to come true
**Context of use:** Used to describe a prediction, hope, or claim that comes to be true or is realized in fact. Attested in narrative and religious discourse, as well as everyday speech about fulfilled expectations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** cheqalla
**Transliteration:** cheqalla
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -lla (limitative/restrictive enclitic)
**Natural English translation:** only the truth, purely genuine, nothing but real
**Context of use:** Used to emphasize that something is purely or exclusively genuine, without admixture of falsehood. Common in moral and testimonial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** cheqallam
**Transliteration:** cheqallam
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -lla (limitative) + -m (direct evidential)
**Natural English translation:** it is purely the truth; it is nothing but genuine
**Context of use:** A reinforced assertion combining the limitative and direct evidential enclitics, used to insist emphatically on the truth of a statement. Common in oral testimony and dispute resolution.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** cheqa simi
**Transliteration:** cheqa simi
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + simi (mouth, word, language)
**Natural English translation:** true word, truthful speech, honest statement
**Context of use:** Used to characterize speech or testimony as honest and accurate. Appears in legal, moral, and religious contexts where the reliability of a speaker's words is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Transliteration:** cheqa sonqo

**Natural English translation:** sincere, honest-hearted, genuine in spirit, true of heart
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person as genuinely sincere or honest in their inner disposition, not merely in outward behavior. Common in moral evaluation and religious discourse.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where their contribution appears to shift or extend beyond the core truth-evaluation domain. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From cheqa:**

- **cheqa kawsay** — the root *cheqa* here applies to a mode of living rather than to a proposition or representation; the evaluative target is an entire life or way of being.

- **cheqa kaynin** — the root *cheqa* here applies to essential nature or ontological constitution rather than to the truth-value of a statement.

- **cheqa ñan** — the root *cheqa* here applies to a course of conduct or a path, where 'right' shades toward moral obligation or proper direction rather than epistemic accuracy.

- **cheqa Dios** — the root *cheqa* here applies to the ontological status of a divine being rather than to the accuracy of a claim.

- **cheqa unanchay** — the root *cheqa* here applies to the interpretation of signs and omens, where correctness involves a specialized hermeneutic competence.

- **cheqa hina** — the root *cheqa* here appears in a similative construction, where the evaluative target is resemblance or likeness rather than identity or truth.

---

**From allin:**

- **allin kawsay** — the root *allin* here applies to a holistic condition of life and social harmony, extending well beyond the evaluation of individual acts or statements.

- **allin p'unchay / allin tuta** — the root *allin* here functions as a social greeting formula, where evaluative content is largely conventionalized and phatic.

- **allin kachun** — the root *allin* here appears in an optative/benedictive construction, where it expresses a wish or blessing rather than an evaluation of an existing state.

- **allin mikhuy / allin puñuy** — the root *allin* here applies to bodily states and activities (eating, sleeping) rather than to conduct, statements, or representations.

- **allin qhaway / allin uyariy** — the root *allin* here applies to perceptual acts (seeing, hearing), where 'good' shades toward attentiveness and accuracy of perception rather than moral or propositional correctness.

- **imaynallan / allinllan** — the root *allin* here is embedded in a conventionalized greeting exchange, where its evaluative force is largely reduced to a social register function.

---

**From llulla:**

- **llulla kawsay** — the root *llulla* here applies to a mode of living rather than to a specific deceptive act or false statement.

- **llulla Dios** — the root *llulla* here applies to the ontological status of a divine being, paralleling the extension noted for *cheqa Dios*.

- **llulla sonqo** — the root *llulla* here applies to an inner disposition or character trait rather than to a specific act of deception or a false statement.
