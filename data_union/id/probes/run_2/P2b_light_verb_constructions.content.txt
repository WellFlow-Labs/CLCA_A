# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the P1 lexical items. The scope includes:
- Fixed and semi-fixed collocations not already documented as morphological derivations in P2a
- Periphrastic constructions (verb + complement, predicate + modifier)
- Idiomatic expressions where the meaning is not fully compositional
- Syntactically productive patterns that are conventionalized for specific items

Constructions already documented in P2a as derivational forms (e.g., *kebenaran*, *membenarkan*, *pemalsuan*) are not repeated here. Collocations documented in P1b root expansion are included here only where they represent conventionalized multi-word units rather than free combinations; borderline cases are noted.

Where no common construction exists for a given P1 item, this is stated explicitly. Absence is treated as data.

Register variation is noted where relevant. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation across multiple sources and registers.

## Item 1: benar

**Base Form:** benar

---

**Construction 1:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Itu benar adanya.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** That is genuinely the case. / That is true as it stands.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed predicate construction used to assert that something is factually and genuinely so, often in formal or emphatic contexts. The element *adanya* (*ada* + *-nya*) adds a sense of existential factuality; the construction as a whole is more emphatic than bare *itu benar*.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Ada benarnya juga.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** There is something to that. / That has a point. / That's not entirely wrong.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed concessive construction used to partially acknowledge the validity of a claim or argument without fully endorsing it. The particle *juga* ("also, too") softens the concession further. Extremely common in conversational and editorial discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Yang benar saja.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Come on, be serious. / Really? / Tell the truth now.
5. **Usage Notes:** A colloquial fixed expression used to challenge someone to be honest or to express mild disbelief at a claim. The construction is pragmatically ambiguous between a challenge to honesty and an expression of incredulity. Restricted to informal spoken registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Memang benar demikian.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** It is indeed the case. / That is indeed so.
5. **Usage Notes:** A concessive or confirmatory construction combining *memang* (indeed, as expected) with *benar* and the demonstrative *demikian* (thus, so). Used in formal and journalistic registers to confirm a claim, often after it has been questioned or disputed.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Benar atau salah, itu pilihannya.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Right or wrong, that is his/her choice.
5. **Usage Notes:** The binary formula *benar atau salah* (true or false / right or wrong) used in a concessive frame. The formula itself is fixed; its embedding in larger constructions is syntactically free. Common in moral and deliberative discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Kalau memang benar, buktikan.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** If it is indeed true, prove it.
5. **Usage Notes:** A conditional challenge construction common in journalistic, legal, and political discourse. The combination *kalau memang benar* is a semi-fixed frame used to demand evidence for a claim.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Tidak ada yang benar-benar tahu.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Nobody really knows. / No one truly knows.
5. **Usage Notes:** A construction combining negation (*tidak ada yang*) with the intensifier *benar-benar* to express that genuine knowledge of something is absent. Common in journalistic and conversational registers when discussing uncertain matters.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Actually, I disagree. / In truth, I don't agree.
5. **Usage Notes:** *Sebenarnya* functioning as a sentence-initial discourse marker introducing a correction or disclosure. This is the canonical use of the form; included here as a construction rather than a derivation because its function is pragmatic-discoursal rather than purely morphological.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Demi kebenaran dan keadilan.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** For the sake of truth and justice.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed formulaic phrase used in legal, political, and moral discourse. The pairing of *kebenaran* (truth) with *keadilan* (justice) is a stable collocation in Indonesian public rhetoric, appearing in court proceedings, political speeches, and activist discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Atas nama kebenaran.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** In the name of truth.
5. **Usage Notes:** A prepositional formula used in moral, religious, and political rhetoric to invoke truth as a justifying principle for an action or stance. Appears in formal speeches, editorials, and religious discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** The truth will come out in the end. / Truth will eventually be revealed.
5. **Usage Notes:** A semi-proverbial construction expressing the belief that truth cannot be permanently concealed. The verb *terungkap* (to be revealed, to come to light) is the standard collocate for *kebenaran* in this construction. Common in journalistic and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12:**
1. **Base Form:** benar
2. **Example:** *Tidak dibenarkan untuk...*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** It is not permitted to... / One is not justified in...
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed regulatory formula using the passive *dibenarkan* in negated form to prohibit or disallow an action. Standard in official regulations, institutional rules, and legal documents. The deontic (permissive/prohibitive) use of *benar* is well established in this construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Item 2: salah

**Base Form:** salah

---

**Construction 1:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah satu dari mereka.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** One of them.
5. **Usage Notes:** The construction *salah satu* (literally "one wrong" or "one of") is a fully grammaticalized quantifier meaning "one of (a set)." This is a peripheral extension of *salah* noted in P1b; included here because it is a fixed multi-word unit. The truth-evaluative meaning of *salah* is absent in this construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah paham.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Misunderstanding. / To misunderstand.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed compound construction (*salah* + *paham*, "understanding") meaning "misunderstanding." Functions as both a noun and a verb (*terjadi salah paham*, "a misunderstanding occurred"; *saya salah paham*, "I misunderstood"). Extremely common across all registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah kaprah.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** A widespread misconception. / A commonly accepted error.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed idiomatic construction meaning a mistaken belief or practice that has become so widespread it is accepted as correct. *Kaprah* means "common, widespread." Used in journalistic, educational, and analytical discourse to identify entrenched errors.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah langkah.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** A misstep. / A wrong move.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *langkah*, "step") meaning a wrong or mistaken action, particularly in strategic or consequential contexts. Used in political, business, and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah sasaran.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Off-target. / Misdirected. / Hitting the wrong target.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *sasaran*, "target") meaning that an action, policy, or criticism is directed at the wrong target or fails to address the right issue. Common in journalistic and policy discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah tafsir.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Misinterpretation. / To misinterpret.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *tafsir*, "interpretation") meaning misinterpretation. Used in legal, religious, and academic contexts to describe incorrect readings of texts, laws, or statements.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Bukan salah saya.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** It's not my fault. / I'm not to blame.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed denial-of-responsibility construction. *Bukan salah* + possessive is a standard formula for disclaiming fault. Extremely common in everyday, legal, and journalistic registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Mengakui kesalahan.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** To admit one's mistake. / To acknowledge wrongdoing.
5. **Usage Notes:** A stable verb-object construction (*mengakui* "to admit/acknowledge" + *kesalahan* "mistake/fault") used in moral, legal, and political discourse. *Mengakui kesalahan* is a standard formula in apologies, confessions, and accountability discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah dalam bertindak.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Wrong in one's actions. / Mistaken in acting.
5. **Usage Notes:** A predicative construction using *salah* as a predicate adjective followed by a prepositional phrase specifying the domain of error. Syntactically productive; the pattern *salah dalam* + verbal noun is common in moral and legal evaluation.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
1. **Base Form:** salah
2. **Example:** *Salah alamat.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Wrong address. / Barking up the wrong tree. / You've got the wrong person/place.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *alamat*, "address") used both literally (wrong address) and figuratively (directing a complaint, accusation, or request to the wrong person or place). The figurative use is common in everyday and journalistic registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Item 3: asli

**Base Form:** asli

---

**Construction 1:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Dijamin asli.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Guaranteed genuine. / Authenticity guaranteed.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed commercial formula used in product marketing and sales to assert that a product is genuine. *Dijamin* (guaranteed) + *asli* is a stable collocation in Indonesian commercial discourse, appearing on product labels, advertisements, and market stalls.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Asli atau palsu?*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Genuine or fake? / Real or counterfeit?
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed binary interrogative formula used in commercial, legal, and journalistic contexts to frame the question of authenticity. The pairing *asli atau palsu* is a stable formula in consumer protection and document verification discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Ini yang asli.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** This is the real one. / This is the genuine article.
5. **Usage Notes:** A demonstrative-predicate construction used to identify the genuine item among alternatives. Common in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts when distinguishing originals from copies or fakes.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Membuktikan keaslian dokumen.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** To prove the authenticity of a document.
5. **Usage Notes:** A stable verb-object construction (*membuktikan* "to prove" + *keaslian* "authenticity" + *dokumen* "document") used in legal and administrative contexts. The pattern *membuktikan keaslian* + noun is productive across domains (documents, artworks, goods).
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Orang asli sini.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** A local native. / Someone originally from here.
5. **Usage Notes:** A colloquial construction used to identify someone as originally from a particular place. *Orang asli* + place name or *sini* ("here") is a common informal formula for asserting local or indigenous origin. Distinct from the formal term *penduduk asli*.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Tampil apa adanya.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** To present oneself as one truly is. / To be authentic.
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed idiomatic construction meaning to present oneself without pretense or artifice. While *asli* does not appear in this construction, it is the closest Indonesian equivalent to "being authentic" in the personal/behavioral sense, and the construction is included here as the standard periphrastic expression for that meaning. *Apa adanya* (literally "as it is") is the key element.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
1. **Base Form:** asli
2. **Example:** *Produk dalam negeri yang asli.*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English Translation:** Genuine domestic product. / Authentic locally-made product.
5. **Usage Notes:** A noun phrase construction combining *produk dalam negeri* (domestic product) with the postposed modifier *yang asli*. Common in nationalist commercial discourse and consumer protection campaigns promoting genuine Indonesian-made goods.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
