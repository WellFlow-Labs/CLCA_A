# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST, consistent with P1, P1b, and P2a.

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This document records attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving the 30 base lexical items identified in P1. Constructions are distinguished from derivational forms (documented in P2a) by the criterion that they consist of syntactically separable elements rather than morphologically fused forms. Compounds that function as fixed lexical units are included only where they operate as phrasal constructions rather than as single lexical items; borderline cases are noted.

Sanskrit presents a particular methodological challenge: the language's extensive compounding capacity means that many expressions that would be multi-word phrases in other languages appear as single orthographic units. Where a compound functions as a frozen, lexicalized unit, it has been documented in P2a. This section focuses on constructions that retain syntactic separability or that function as phrasal idioms across multiple syntactic contexts.

Absence of attested constructions is recorded explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

## Item 1 — सत्य / *satya*

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं वद
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ vada
4. English Translation: speak the truth
5. Usage Notes: A standard imperative formula appearing in Vedic, epic, and legal literature as a moral injunction. The construction *satyaṃ vada* (with *satya* in the accusative as the object of *vad-*) is one of the most widely attested ethical imperatives in Sanskrit literature, appearing in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1) and numerous other texts.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं ब्रूहि
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ brūhi
4. English Translation: tell the truth, speak truly
5. Usage Notes: A near-synonym of *satyaṃ vada* using the root *brū-* (to speak, to say) rather than *vad-*; appears in legal and narrative contexts as an injunction to a witness or interlocutor to speak truthfully. The two constructions are functionally equivalent but *brūhi* is more common in direct address in legal and narrative contexts.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं न परित्यजेत्
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ na parityajet
4. English Translation: one should not abandon truth
5. Usage Notes: A prescriptive construction using the optative (*parityajet*) with negation, appearing in *dharmaśāstra* and epic literature as a moral rule. The construction expresses a normative prohibition against departing from truthfulness.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्येन शपामि / सत्येन शपे
3. Transliteration: satyena śapāmi / satyena śape
4. English Translation: I swear by truth / I take an oath by truth
5. Usage Notes: An oath formula using the instrumental *satyena* (by truth) with the verb *śap-* (to swear, to take an oath); appears in legal and epic literature as a formula for oath-taking. The instrumental marks truth as the instrument or ground of the oath.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं किल / सत्यं खलु
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ kila / satyaṃ khalu
4. English Translation: truly indeed, it is indeed true (that...)
5. Usage Notes: A discourse-marking construction in which *satyam* (neuter accusative used adverbially) combines with affirmative particles (*kila*, *khalu*) to assert the truth of a following proposition; common in narrative and philosophical prose as a marker of affirmation or concession.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं तु / सत्यं तथापि
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ tu / satyaṃ tathāpi
4. English Translation: true, but... / true, yet even so...
5. Usage Notes: A concessive construction in which *satyam* (adverbially used) concedes a point before introducing a qualification or counter-consideration; common in philosophical argumentation and commentary literature as a device for acknowledging an opponent's point before responding.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यं प्रतिजानीते
3. Transliteration: satyaṃ pratijānīte
4. English Translation: one pledges truth, one vows truthfully
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *satya* in the accusative with the verb *pratijñā-* (to pledge, to vow); appears in legal and epic contexts to describe the act of making a truthful pledge or vow.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 8**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Example: सत्यात् न चलति / सत्यात् न विचलति
3. Transliteration: satyāt na calati / satyāt na vicalati
4. English Translation: does not deviate from truth, does not swerve from truth
5. Usage Notes: A construction using the ablative *satyāt* (from truth) with verbs of motion (*cal-*, *vical-*) and negation, expressing steadfast adherence to truth; common in epic literature as a characterization of virtuous heroes.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

## Item 2 — ऋत / *ṛta*

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. Example: ऋतं च सत्यं च
3. Transliteration: ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca
4. English Translation: both *ṛta* and *satya*, cosmic order and truth
5. Usage Notes: A paired formula appearing in Vedic literature (notably Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.1.1: *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhyajāyata*) in which *ṛta* and *satya* are coordinated as complementary values; the pairing is a fixed Vedic formula.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. Example: ऋतस्य पथा
3. Transliteration: ṛtasya pathā
4. English Translation: by the path of *ṛta*, along the way of cosmic order
5. Usage Notes: A Vedic formulaic expression using the genitive *ṛtasya* with *pathā* (instrumental of *path-*, path, way); appears in the Ṛgveda to describe the correct or divinely ordained path of action and ritual. Confined to Vedic poetic register.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. Example: ऋतेन ऋतम् अपिहितम्
3. Transliteration: ṛtena ṛtam apihitam
4. English Translation: truth is covered by truth (Ṛgveda 5.62.1)
5. Usage Notes: A Vedic formulaic phrase in which *ṛta* appears in both instrumental and nominative/accusative, expressing the idea that cosmic order conceals or reveals itself; a fixed Vedic poetic formula with no post-Vedic continuation as a living construction.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** Beyond these Vedic formulaic expressions, *ṛta* does not participate in productive multi-word constructions in post-Vedic Sanskrit. Its usage is largely confined to fixed Vedic formulas.

## Item 3 — यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

**Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Example: यथार्थं वक्ति / यथार्थं कथयति
3. Transliteration: yathārthaṃ vakti / yathārthaṃ kathayati
4. English Translation: speaks accurately, describes correctly, says what is true to the object
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *yathārtha* adverbially (in the accusative) with verbs of speaking (*vac-*, *kath-*) to evaluate whether a statement accurately corresponds to its referent; common in philosophical and grammatical commentary.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Example: नाम यथार्थम् अस्ति
3. Transliteration: nāma yathārtham asti
4. English Translation: the name is accurate (to its referent), the name corresponds to the object
5. Usage Notes: A construction evaluating whether a name (*nāma*) accurately corresponds to the thing it names; appears in philosophical and grammatical literature in discussions of the relationship between words and their referents. The predicate *yathārtham asti* functions as a copular evaluation.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Example: यथार्थज्ञानम् उत्पद्यते
3. Transliteration: yathārthajñānam utpadyate
4. English Translation: accurate knowledge arises, correct cognition is produced
5. Usage Notes: A construction describing the arising or production of accurate cognition; *yathārthajñāna* functions as the subject of *utpad-* (to arise, to be produced), a standard verb in epistemological descriptions of cognitive events.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

## Item 4 — प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: प्रमाणम् अस्ति / प्रमाणं विद्यते
3. Transliteration: pramāṇam asti / pramāṇaṃ vidyate
4. English Translation: there is evidence (for it), a valid means of cognition exists (for it)
5. Usage Notes: A construction asserting the existence of a valid means of cognition or evidence for a claim; *pramāṇam asti* is a standard formula in philosophical argumentation to assert that a position is epistemically grounded.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति
3. Transliteration: na tatra pramāṇam asti
4. English Translation: there is no valid means of cognition for that, there is no evidence for that
5. Usage Notes: The negated form of the above construction, used in philosophical argumentation to deny that a claim has epistemic support; a standard formula for rejecting an opponent's position as lacking evidential grounding.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: किं प्रमाणम्
3. Transliteration: kiṃ pramāṇam
4. English Translation: what is the evidence?, what is the valid means of cognition?
5. Usage Notes: An interrogative construction used in philosophical debate to challenge an interlocutor to produce evidence or a valid means of cognition for their claim; a standard formula in Sanskrit philosophical dialogue.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्
3. Transliteration: pramāṇena siddham
4. English Translation: established by a valid means of cognition, proven by evidence
5. Usage Notes: A construction using the instrumental *pramāṇena* with the past passive participle *siddha* (established, proven); a standard formula in philosophical argumentation to assert that a claim has been validly established.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: प्रमाणविरुद्धम्
3. Transliteration: pramāṇaviruddham
4. English Translation: contrary to valid cognition, contradicted by evidence
5. Usage Notes: A construction (functioning as a compound adjective) characterizing a claim or position as contradicted by valid means of cognition; used in philosophical argumentation to reject a position as epistemically untenable. Borderline between compound and phrasal construction; included here because *pramāṇaviruddha* functions predicatively in syntactic contexts.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: प्रमाणतः सिद्धम् / प्रमाणतो ज्ञायते
3. Transliteration: pramāṇataḥ siddham / pramāṇato jñāyate
4. English Translation: established from/by valid cognition, known through valid means
5. Usage Notes: A construction using the ablative/adverbial *pramāṇataḥ* with verbs of knowing or establishing; appears in epistemological commentary to specify the epistemic ground of a claim.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Example: वेदः प्रमाणम्
3. Transliteration: vedaḥ pramāṇam
4. English Translation: the Veda is the valid authority (for this)
5. Usage Notes: A copular construction asserting that the Veda (or another source) constitutes a valid means of cognition for a given domain; a standard formula in Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta argumentation. The construction pattern *X pramāṇam* (X is the authority) is productive across philosophical schools with different values of X.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

## Item 5 — प्रमा / *pramā*

**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. Example: प्रमा जायते / प्रमा उत्पद्यते
3. Transliteration: pramā jāyate / pramā utpadyate
4. English Translation: valid cognition arises, correct apprehension is produced
5. Usage Notes: A construction describing the arising or production of valid cognition; standard in epistemological literature to describe the outcome of a successful cognitive process. *Pramā* functions as the subject of verbs of arising or production.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. Example: प्रमायाः लक्षणम्
3. Transliteration: pramāyāḥ lakṣaṇam
4. English Translation: the definition of valid cognition
5. Usage Notes: A genitive construction used in epistemological literature to introduce or discuss the definition (*lakṣaṇa*) of valid cognition; a standard formula in the opening of epistemological treatises and commentaries.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Note:** *Pramā* is primarily a technical term and its multi-word constructions are largely confined to epistemological discourse. Beyond the above, no further common multi-word constructions are identified.

## Item 6 — सत् / *sat*

**Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Example: सत् किम् / सत् कतमत्
3. Transliteration: sat kim / sat katamat
4. English Translation: what is real?, which is the real?
5. Usage Notes: Interrogative constructions used in Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic philosophical inquiry to pose the question of what truly exists or is real; appears in dialogic philosophical contexts.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Example: सदेव सोम्येदम् अग्र आसीत्
3. Transliteration: sadeva somyedam agra āsīt
4. English Translation: in the beginning, dear one, this was being alone (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.2.1)
5. Usage Notes: A fixed Upaniṣadic formula asserting the primacy of *sat* (being) as the original state of reality; *sadeva* (*sat* + *eva*, being alone) is the predicate of the copular construction. This is a fixed textual formula rather than a productive construction, but it is the most widely cited usage of *sat* in a syntactic context.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Example: सतः न असतः जायते
3. Transliteration: sataḥ na asataḥ jāyate
4. English Translation: (the world) arises from being, not from non-being
5. Usage Notes: A philosophical formula expressing the ontological principle that existence arises from existence, not from non-existence; appears in Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic argumentation. The ablatives *sataḥ* and *asataḥ* mark the source of arising.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

## Item 7 — असत् / *asat*

**Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: असत् (*asat*)
2. Example: असतः सत् जायते इति न
3. Transliteration: asataḥ sat jāyate iti na
4. English Translation: it is not the case that being arises from non-being
5. Usage Notes: A philosophical formula negating the possibility of existence arising from non-existence; appears in Vedāntic argumentation as the denial of *asatkāryavāda* (the theory that the effect pre-exists in the cause as non-existent). The construction uses the ablative *asataḥ* as source.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: असत् (*asat*)
2. Example: असत् इव प्रतिभाति
3. Transliteration: asat iva pratibhāti
4. English Translation: it appears as if non-existent, it seems unreal
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *asat* with the comparative particle *iva* (as if, like) and a verb of appearing (*pratibhā-*); used in Vedāntic literature to describe the phenomenal world as appearing to be unreal or non-existent from the ultimate perspective.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

## Item 8 — तथ्य / *tathya*

**Base Form:** तथ्य (*tathya*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: तथ्य (*tathya*)
2. Example: तथ्यं वदति / तथ्यं ब्रवीति
3. Transliteration: tathyaṃ vadati / tathyaṃ bravīti
4. English Translation: speaks the truth (of the matter), states the facts
5. Usage Notes: A construction parallel to *satyaṃ vada* but with *tathya* in the accusative; used in legal and narrative contexts to evaluate whether a statement corresponds to the actual facts of a case. *Tathya* here emphasizes factual accuracy in a specific situation rather than the general virtue of truthfulness.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: तथ्य (*tathya*)
2. Example: तथ्यं किम् अत्र
3. Transliteration: tathyaṃ kim atra
4. English Translation: what is the truth of the matter here?, what are the actual facts here?
5. Usage Notes: An interrogative construction used in legal and narrative contexts to inquire into the actual facts of a situation; *tathya* here functions as the predicate of an implicit copula.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** *Tathya* has fewer attested multi-word constructions than *satya*, consistent with its more restricted usage in legal and narrative contexts rather than philosophical or ethical discourse.

## Item 9 — अनृत / *anṛta*

**Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: अनृत (*anṛta*)
2. Example: अनृतं न वदेत्
3. Transliteration: anṛtaṃ na vadet
4. English Translation: one should not speak falsehood
5. Usage Notes: A prescriptive construction using *anṛta* in the accusative with the optative of *vad-* and negation; appears in Vedic and early *dharmaśāstra* literature as a moral prohibition against false speech. Parallel in structure to *satyaṃ vada* but negated.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: अनृत (*anṛta*)
2. Example: अनृतेन जयति न
3. Transliteration: anṛtena jayati na
4. English Translation: one does not conquer by falsehood
5. Usage Notes: A formulaic expression appearing in Vedic and epic literature asserting that falsehood does not lead to ultimate success; the instrumental *anṛtena* marks falsehood as the means. Contrasted with *satyam eva jayate*.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** *Anṛta* is largely confined to Vedic and archaic registers. Its multi-word constructions are correspondingly limited and archaic.

## Item 10 — मिथ्या / *mithyā*

**Base Form:** मिथ्या (*mithyā*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Example: मिथ्या प्रतिभाति / मिथ्या भाति
3. Transliteration: mithyā pratibhāti / mithyā bhāti
4. English Translation: appears falsely, appears as illusory
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *mithyā* adverbially with verbs of appearing (*pratibhā-*, *bhā-*); common in Advaita Vedānta to describe the phenomenal world or a particular cognition as appearing in a false or illusory manner.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Example: मिथ्या इति निश्चयः
3. Transliteration: mithyā iti niścayaḥ
4. English Translation: the determination that (it is) false/illusory
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *mithyā* as a predicate within an *iti*-clause (indirect discourse marker) followed by a noun of determination (*niścaya*); used in Vedāntic literature to describe the cognitive act of determining something to be illusory.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Example: मिथ्या न भवति
3. Transliteration: mithyā na bhavati
4. English Translation: it is not false, it is not illusory
5. Usage Notes: A copular construction using *mithyā* as a predicate adjective/adverb with the negated copula *na bhavati*; used in philosophical argumentation to deny that something is illusory or false.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Example: मिथ्या इति वदन्ति
3. Transliteration: mithyā iti vadanti
4. English Translation: they say it is false/illusory
5. Usage Notes: A construction using *mithyā* within an *iti*-clause as the content of a verb of saying; used in philosophical literature to report or attribute the position that something is illusory, often in the context of inter-school debate.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

## Item 11 — भ्रम / *bhrama*

**Base Form:** भ्रम (*bhrama*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: भ्रम (*bhrama*)
2. Example: भ्रमः जायते / भ्रमः उत्पद्यते
3. Transliteration: bhramaḥ jāyate / bhramaḥ utpadyate
4. English Translation: error arises, mistaken cognition is produced
5. Usage Notes: A construction describing the arising of erroneous cognition; *bhrama* functions as the subject of verbs of arising or production, parallel to the construction with *pramā*. Standard in epistemological literature.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: भ्रम (*bhrama*)
2. Example: रज्जौ सर्पभ्रमः
3. Transliteration: rajjau sarpabhramaḥ
4. English Translation: the error of (seeing) a snake in a rope
5. Usage Notes: The standard illustrative construction for erroneous cognition in Sanskrit epistemology; *rajjau* (locative of *rajju*, rope) marks the locus of the error, and *sarpabhrama* (snake-error) names the type. This construction is a fixed philosophical example (*dṛṣṭānta*) appearing across virtually all epistemological schools.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. Base Form: भ्रम (*bhrama*)
2. Example: भ्रमः निवर्तते / भ्रमः निरस्यते
3. Transliteration: bhramaḥ nivartate / bhramaḥ nirasyate
4. English Translation: error is removed, mistaken cognition is dispelled
5. Usage Notes: A construction describing the removal or dispelling of erroneous cognition; used in Vedāntic and epistemological literature to describe the cognitive effect of correct knowledge or philosophical insight.
6. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. Base Form: भ्रम (*bhrama*)
2. Example: भ्रमेण गृह्यते
3. Transliteration: bhramaṇa gṛhyate
4. English Translation: it is apprehended through error, it is grasped by mistake
5. Usage Notes: A construction using the instrumental *bhramaṇa* (by error) with a verb of apprehension (*grah-*); used in epistemological literature to describe the cognitive mechanism by which an object is misapprehended.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

## Item 12 — विपर्यय / *viparyaya*

**Base Form:** विपर्यय (*viparyaya*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: विपर्यय (*viparyaya*)
2. Example: विपर्ययः न भवति
3. Transliteration: viparyayaḥ na bhavati
4. English Translation: there is no erroneous cognition (here), inversion does not occur
5. Usage Notes: A construction asserting the absence of erroneous cognition in a given case; used in epistemological argumentation to establish that a cognition is not of the inverted type.
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 2**

1. Base Form: विपर्यय (*viparyaya*)
2. Example: विपर्ययात् मोक्षः
3. Transliteration: viparyayāt mokṣaḥ
4. English Translation: liberation from erroneous cognition
5. Usage Notes: A construction using the ablative *viparyayāt* (from erroneous cognition) with *mokṣa* (liberation); appears in Sāṃkhya-Yoga literature to describe liberation as freedom from the five types of erroneous cognition (*viparyaya*).
6. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** *Viparyaya* is a technical term with limited multi-word constructions outside Sāṃkhya-Yoga philosophical discourse. No further common constructions are identified.

## Item 13 — धर्म / *dharma*

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)

---

**Construction 1**

1. Base Form: धर्म (*dharma*)
2. Example: धर्मेण वर्तते / धर्मेण आचरति
3. Transliteration: dharmeṇa vartate / dharmeṇa ācarati
