# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Methodological note:** The following oppositions are extracted exclusively from patterns, contrasts, and explicit statements documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is introduced from external sources. Where the data documents an asymmetry (e.g., one term has a fuller paradigm than its contrast), this is reported without normalization. Where the data does not support a clear opposition, this is stated explicitly.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo / hindi totoo
**English translation:** true/real — not true/not real
**Description of contrast:** *Hindi totoo* is the standard morphosyntactic negation of *totoo*, formed by the general negation particle *hindi* preceding the adjectival predicate. The contrast is between asserting and denying the factual truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** totoo / kasinungalingan
**English translation:** true/real — lie/falsehood
**Description of contrast:** Documented explicitly in P1b A-28 and P2b A-17 as a conventionalized oppositional framing (*katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan* — "truth versus falsehood"). *Totoo* and its nominal *katotohanan* assert factual truth; *kasinungalingan* refers to a deliberate false statement. The contrast is between truth and intentional untruth.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** katotohanan
**Term B:** kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** katotohanan / kasinungalingan
**English translation:** truth — lie/falsehood
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1 item 2 as the primary nominal contrast for *katotohanan*, and in P1b A-28 as a near-formulaic oppositional pair. Both are abstract nominals formed by the *ka-…-an* circumfix; *katotohanan* derives from *totoo* (true/real) and *kasinungalingan* derives from *sinungaling* (liar). The contrast is between truth as an abstract quality and falsehood as an abstract quality.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** huwad
**Transliteration:** tunay / huwad
**English translation:** genuine/authentic — fake/counterfeit

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** tunay / peke
**English translation:** genuine/authentic — fake/phony

The contrast between *tunay* and *peke* is the everyday colloquial version of the *tunay*/*huwad* opposition.

## Opposition 28

**Term A:** totoo (factual truth, root: totoo)
**Term B:** tunay (authenticity/genuineness, root: tunay)
**Transliteration:** totoo / tunay
**English translation:** true/real (factually) — genuine/authentic
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P3 Groups 1 and 3 and in P1b as a distinction between two different dimensions of truth. *Totoo* is used to assert that a proposition or state of affairs corresponds to what is actually the case (factual truth); *tunay* is used to assert that something is what it claims or appears to be (authenticity). The data notes this distinction explicitly in P1 items 1 and 3 and in P1b Step 2. The two roots are not interchangeable in all contexts: *tunay na ginto* (genuine gold) is natural; *totoong ginto* in the same sense is less so; conversely, *totoo ba iyan?* as a truth-check question has no conventionalized *tunay* parallel.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** totoo (factual truth)
**Term B:** tapat (honest/sincere)
**Transliteration:** totoo / tapat
**English translation:** true/real — honest/sincere
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P3 Groups 1 and 5 and in P1b Step 2 as a distinction between two different dimensions of truth. *Totoo* concerns the factual correspondence of a proposition to reality; *tapat* concerns the alignment of a person's outward expression with their inner state (honesty, sincerity, lack of concealment). A statement can be *totoo* (factually true) without the speaker being *tapat* (honest), and vice versa. The data documents these as distinct roots with distinct derivational paradigms and distinct constructional behaviors.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** tunay (authenticity)
**Term B:** tapat (honesty/sincerity)
**Transliteration:** tunay / tapat
**English translation:** genuine/authentic — honest/sincere
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P3 Groups 3 and 5 and in P1b Step 2 as a distinction between authenticity of a thing or state (is this the real instance of its kind?) and honesty of a person's expression (is this person disclosing without concealment?). The data notes that *tunay* is used for objects, emotions, and relationships evaluated as genuine, while *tapat* is used for persons and statements evaluated as honest. The two roots have distinct derivational paradigms: *tunay* generates a causative verbal paradigm (*patunayan*); *tapat* generates a confessional verbal paradigm (*magtapat*, *pagtatapat*).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** pa- + tunay + -an verbal paradigm (causative: to prove/verify)
**Term B:** mag- + tapat verbal paradigm (confessional: to confess/come clean)
**Transliteration:** patunayan / magtapat
**English translation:** to prove/verify (externally demonstrable) — to confess/come clean (internally disclosed)
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P2a Sections 2 and 3, P2b Sections 2 and 3, and P3 Groups 2 and 5 as a contrast between two distinct verbal paradigms associated with two distinct dimensions of truth. *Patunayan* and its paradigm concern the external demonstration of truth through evidence; *magtapat* and its paradigm concern the internal disclosure of truth through honest confession. The data notes this as an asymmetry: *tunay* generates a causative paradigm; *tapat* generates a confessional paradigm; *totoo* generates neither.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** totoo (generates particle-marked epistemic stance constructions)
**Term B:** tunay / tapat (do not generate parallel particle-marked constructions)
**Transliteration:** totoo nga pala / totoo naman / totoo pa rin — (no parallel forms)
**English translation:** "so it really is true" / "it is indeed true" / "it is still true" — (no conventionalized equivalents)
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P4a Section 9 and P4b Section 9 as an asymmetry in constructional behavior. *Totoo* participates in a set of conventionalized particle-cluster constructions (*totoo nga pala*, *totoo naman*, *totoo pa rin*, *totoo ba*) that encode distinct epistemic stances. The data explicitly states that no parallel conventionalized particle-cluster constructions at the same density are attested for *tunay*, *tapat*, or *tama*. This is a constructional asymmetry rather than a semantic opposition between two terms.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 33

**Term A:** hindi + adjective (negation of adjectival predicates)
**Term B:** wala + -ng + nominal (existential negation of abstract nominals)
**Transliteration:** hindi totoo / walang katotohanan; hindi tapat / walang katapatan
**English translation:** not true — there is no truth in it; not honest — there is no honesty
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P4a Section 2 and P4b Section 2 as two structurally distinct negation patterns that are not interchangeable. *Hindi* negates adjectival predicates (*hindi totoo*, *hindi tapat*, *hindi tunay*); *wala* + *-ng* negates existential constructions with abstract nominals (*walang katotohanan*, *walang katapatan*, *walang katunayan*). The data explicitly states these are "structurally distinct and not interchangeable." The contrast is between two different constructional strategies for negating truth-related evaluations.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** tapat na loob / katapatan ng loob (inner honesty, *loob* constructions)
**Term B:** tapat na sagot / tapatang salita (honest speech, output-focused constructions)
**Transliteration:** tapat na loob / katapatan ng loob — tapatang sagot / tapatang salita

The data notes that *loob* is a culturally specific Tagalog moral-psychological concept and that the *loob* constructions are specific to Group 5 and do not appear in other groups. The contrast is between honesty as an inner quality and honesty as a property of speech acts.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 35

**Term A:** nagkatotoo (came true, realization of a prediction)

**English translation:** came true/was realized — is true/is real

The data assigns *nagkatotoo* a [MARGINAL] status and notes it involves "a different semantic trajectory (realization of a prediction or wish)" placing it "at the periphery of the truth-evaluation domain." **Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
