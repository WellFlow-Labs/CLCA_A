## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 CAUSATION (character) substituted by Agentive Nominalization

P4a and P4b document no verbal construction in Basque that encodes the meaning "X causes Y to become a liar" or "X makes Y deceptive" — that is, no construction that treats character causation as a dynamic verbal process with an agent and a causee. Instead, the language addresses character causation through the agentive nominalization system (§7): the *-ti* suffix (*gezurtia*, "habitual liar"), the *-gile* suffix (*iruzurgilea*, "fraudster"), and the *-tzaile* suffix (*zuzentzailea*, "corrector") each name a person by their characteristic or role-based relationship to a truth-domain concept. The *-ti* type specifically encodes stable character traits rather than acts or roles, and is identified in P4b as the primary pattern for moral character evaluation. The result is that character causation is expressed through the nominal endpoint (the characterological label) rather than through a verbal construction encoding the causal process. This is a substitution pattern: CAUSATION (character) → AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION.

### 2.2 DISCONFIRMATION shares construction with NEGATION

P4a and P4b document no construction dedicated to disconfirmation as distinct from negation. The *ez da* construction (§2) is the attested form for both "it is not true" (negation of a truth predicate) and the closest available expression for "this refutes / disproves X." The pejorative *-keria* nominals (§9) encode a pattern of blameworthy conduct that implies the disconfirmation of positive character claims, but they do so nominally rather than through a dedicated verbal disconfirmation construction. No asymmetry between truth-side and falsity-side is documented for this substitution: both *ez da egia* and *ustelkeria* are attested, but neither constitutes a dedicated disconfirmation operator. DISCONFIRMATION is therefore expressed through the NEGATION construction, with no formal distinction between the two operators.

### 2.3 REVELATION is absent; no substitution is documented

P4a and P4b document no construction that encodes the evidential or epistemic meaning "X turns out to be true / X is revealed to be the case." The BECOME construction (*-tu* intransitive; ADJECTIVE + *egon*) encodes change of state and resultant state, but these are not evidential: they describe a change in the world, not a change in the speaker's epistemic access to a fact. The epistemic hedge adverbials (§10: *itxuraz*, *ustez*) mark reduced speaker commitment to a proposition but do not encode revelation. The light-verb constructions (§11) are restricted to speech-act expressions (*esan*) and action expressions (*egin*) and do not fill the revelation role. No substitution pattern is attested: REVELATION is simply absent from the documented construction inventory.

### 2.4 VERIFICATION is nominally attested but verbally underdocumented

The nominal forms *egiaztapen* (result nominal, §6) and *egiaztatze-prozesua* (process nominal, §8) clearly presuppose an underlying verbal stem (*egiazta-*), and the existence of this stem is implied by the morphological analysis in P4a. However, P4a and P4b do not explicitly document a verbal construction of the form "to verify [proposition P]" as a simple predicate. The light-verb construction with *egin* (§11) is attested for action expressions (*iruzurra egin*, "to commit fraud") but not specifically for verification. VERIFICATION is therefore partially available: the nominal domain is well-attested, but the verbal construction is not explicitly documented in the source data.

### 2.5 Asymmetry between analytic and morphological NEGATION

P4b rates syntactic negation (*ez da*) as [CORE] and lexical negation (*ez-* prefix) as [MARGINAL]. The *ez-* prefix forms (*ez-fidagarria*, *ez-sinesgarria*, *ez-zilegia*) are described as restricted to a near-closed set. This creates an asymmetry within the NEGATION operator: the analytic strategy is fully productive and applies to any predicate, while the morphological strategy is available only for a restricted set of adjectives. No parallel restriction is documented for positive attribution. The asymmetry is within NEGATION itself, not between NEGATION and another operator.

### 2.6 BECOME and CAUSATION (factual) share morphology; distinction is syntactic

The *-tu* suffix (§4, §5) is the same morphological form for both BECOME (inchoative, intransitive) and CAUSATION (factual, transitive). P4a and P4b are explicit that no dedicated causative morpheme exists in this domain and that the distinction is determined entirely by argument structure: the presence of an explicit direct object yields the causative reading; its absence yields the inchoative reading. This is not a substitution pattern but a single polyfunctional morphological form covering two CLCA operators.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **REVELATION**: No construction is attested for the meaning "X turns out to be true / X is revealed to be the case." Neither the BECOME construction, the epistemic hedge adverbials, nor the light-verb constructions fill this role. This is a structural gap: the meaning is not expressed by any documented construction, and no substitution is attested.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **VERIFICATION (verbal)**: The nominal forms (*egiaztapen*, *egiaztatze*) are attested and imply an underlying verbal stem, but no dedicated verbal construction meaning "to verify [a proposition]" is explicitly documented in P4a or P4b. This is a lexical gap in the verbal domain specifically; the nominal domain is not gapped.

- **CAUSATION (character) — verbal**: No verb meaning "to make X a liar" or "to cause X to be deceptive" is documented. The gap is in the verbal/causative construction; the characterological meaning is available through agentive nominals.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from P4a and P4b alone. All documented constructions have consistent and interpretable semantic content. The absence of REVELATION does not indicate that the concept is blocked; it indicates only that no dedicated construction for it is documented.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

- **NEGATION — morphological vs. analytic asymmetry**: Lexical *ez-* prefix negation is restricted to a near-closed set of adjectives (rated [MARGINAL]); syntactic *ez da* negation is fully productive (rated [CORE]). The morphological negation strategy is available only on the negative side and only for a restricted set of forms. No parallel morphological positive-attribution prefix is documented.

- **DISCONFIRMATION vs. NEGATION**: DISCONFIRMATION has no dedicated construction and is expressed through the NEGATION construction (*ez da*). The positive counterpart (VERIFICATION) has at least nominal constructions attested. This creates an asymmetry: VERIFICATION is partially available nominally; DISCONFIRMATION has no dedicated form at all and relies entirely on NEGATION.

- **CAUSATION (factual) vs. CAUSATION (character)**: CAUSATION (factual) is directly attested as a verbal construction (*-tu* transitive). CAUSATION (character) is not attested as a verbal construction and is substituted by agentive nominalization. The gap is specific to the character-causation subtype.
