## 1. ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**[NP] είναι + ADJ/N** expressing truth, falsity, correctness, validity, authenticity, reality, sincerity  
### Examples
- **Αυτό είναι αληθές.** — *Aftó eínai alithés.* — “This is true.”
- **Η πηγή είναι έγκυρη.** — *I pigí eínai énkyri.* — “The source is reliable/valid.”
### Notes
Predicative **είναι** is the basic attribution frame. Greek uses adjectives such as **αληθής, σωστός, έγκυρος, αυθεντικός, ψευδής, ειλικρινής** and also nouns like **αλήθεια / ψέμα** in clause patterns such as **είναι αλήθεια/ψέμα ότι...**.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**[NP] δεν είναι + ADJ/N**; also **αποδεικνύεται + ψευδής**, **μακριά από την αλήθεια**
### Examples
- **Αυτό δεν είναι σωστό.** — *Aftó den eínai sostó.* — “This is not correct.”
- **Ο ισχυρισμός αποδεικνύεται ψευδής.** — *O ischyrismós apodeiknýetai psevdís.* — “The claim proves false.”
### Notes
Negation is usually formed analytically with **δεν είναι**. For stronger falsity evaluation, Greek also uses resultative/predicative frames like **αποδεικνύεται ψευδής** and contrastive phrases such as **είναι ψέμα ότι...**.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION
### Pattern
**ADJ + N** with truth-domain adjectives modifying statement, source, document, object, feeling, etc.
### Examples
- **αληθινή ιστορία** — *alithiní istoría* — “true story”
- **πλαστή υπογραφή** — *plastí ypografí* — “forged signature”
### Notes
This is highly productive across semantic subdomains: proposition-truth (**αληθής δήλωση**), correctness (**σωστή απάντηση**), validity (**έγκυρη πηγή**), authenticity (**γνήσιο προϊόν**), and falsification (**παραποιημένο έγγραφο**).

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE
### Pattern
**γίνομαι + ADJ/N**; also lexical verbs such as **αληθεύω** “turn out to be true”
### Examples
- **Η υπογραφή αποδείχθηκε πλαστή.** — *I ypografí apodeíchthike plastí.* — “The signature turned out to be forged.”
- **Αυτό που είπε αληθεύει.** — *Aftó pou eípe alithevi.* — “What he said is true / turns out to be true.”
### Notes
No single dedicated inchoative verb covers the whole domain. Greek commonly uses **γίνομαι**, **αποδεικνύομαι/αποδείχθηκε**, or lexical predicates like **αληθεύω** for emergence or confirmation of truth-status.

## 5. CAUSATIVE (OR ABSENCE)
### Pattern
(no common expression identified)
### Examples
- **παραποιώ τα στοιχεία** — *parapoió ta stoicheía* — “I falsify the data.”
- **πλαστογραφώ την υπογραφή** — *plastografó tin ypografí.* — “I forge the signature.”
### Notes
There is no general productive causative meaning “make true / make false / make authentic” across the domain. Instead Greek uses specific lexical verbs for subtypes, especially **παραποιώ** “falsify, tamper with” and **πλαστογραφώ** “forge.”

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
**ADJ + abstract suffix** (especially **-ότητα, -εια**) → noun of truth/correctness/validity/authenticity/fidelity
### Examples
- **εγκυρότητα** — *enkyrótita* — “validity”
- **ειλικρίνεια** — *eilikríneia* — “sincerity”
### Notes
Abstract nominalization is very common and structurally central: **ορθός → ορθότητα**, **αυθεντικός → αυθεντικότητα**, **γνήσιος → γνησιότητα**, **πιστός → πιστότητα**. These nouns readily enter genitive constructions like **η εγκυρότητα της μεθόδου**.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION (LIAR, TRUTH-TELLER)
### Pattern
Limited lexical agent nouns; no broad productive pattern centered on all truth adjectives
### Examples
- **ψεύτης** — *pséftis* — “liar”
- **(no common expression identified)** for a common everyday single-word equivalent of “truth-teller”
### Notes
Greek has a strong lexicalized agent noun for lying (**ψεύτης**). For “truth-teller,” ordinary usage prefers verbal expressions such as **λέει την αλήθεια** rather than a common parallel agentive noun.

## 8. VERBAL TRUTH / FALSEHOOD PREDICATION
### Pattern
**V + clause/content NP**: **λέω την αλήθεια**, **λέω ψέματα**, **αληθεύει ότι...**
### Examples
- **Λέω την αλήθεια.** — *Léo tin alítheia.* — “I’m telling the truth.”
- **Λέει ψέματα.** — *Léei psémata.* — “He/She is lying.”
### Notes
Greek often encodes truth-domain meanings verbally rather than only adjectivally. The core colloquial opposition is **λέω την αλήθεια** vs. **λέω ψέματα**, while **αληθεύει (ότι...)** is more formal/confirmatory.

## 9. DISCOURSE-FRAMING CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**[Truth/reality/essence noun] + είναι ότι...**; adverbial frames like **στην πραγματικότητα**, **στην ουσία**, **κατ’ ουσίαν**
### Examples
- **Η αλήθεια είναι ότι άργησε.** — *I alítheia eínai óti árghise.* — “The truth is that he/she was late.”
- **Στην πραγματικότητα, δεν συμφωνούν.** — *Stin pragmatikótita, den symfonoún.* — “In reality, they do not agree.”
### Notes
Greek frequently packages truth-domain meanings as clause-introducing discourse frames. These constructions mark correction, candor, or focus on what is really/substantively the case rather than merely apparent or formal.
