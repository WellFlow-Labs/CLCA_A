# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving lexical items identified in P1. Analysis is restricted to Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties), consistent with prior steps. Constructions are drawn from documented grammatical, lexicographic, and textual sources; no constructions are invented or back-translated from English. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where a construction type is not attested for a given base form, this is stated explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

A note on the boundary between derivation and composition: Quechua is a highly agglutinative language in which much of what other languages express through multi-word constructions is expressed through suffixation. This means that the inventory of multi-word constructions in the truth-evaluation domain is smaller relative to what would be found in, for example, a Romance language. This is not an absence of data but a structural feature of the language. Where the expected semantic content is expressed derivationally rather than constructionally, this is noted explicitly.

A note on the boundary between derivation and construction: Quechua noun-noun and adjective-noun collocations documented in P1b (e.g., *cheqa simi*, *allin runa*) are compositional in structure. Where such collocations function as fixed or semi-fixed multi-word units with identifiable usage patterns, they are documented here as constructions. Where they are fully transparent and productive (i.e., any adjective + any noun), they are noted as such without exhaustive listing. Collocations already introduced in P1b as compound expressions are carried forward here only where they appear in syntactic constructions with additional material; bare collocations already documented are not re-listed.

Epistemic statuses are preserved from prior steps where forms were already assessed. New assessments follow the same criteria.

## BASE FORM 1: cheqa

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam kay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m kay
4. **English translation:** It is true. / That is the truth. / It is real. / This is true.
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic predicative truth assertion in Southern Quechua, combining the adjectival root with the direct evidential enclitic *-m* and the copular *kay*. The copula may be omitted in rapid speech, yielding *cheqam* alone as a complete utterance. Used across all registers as a direct affirmation. Widely attested in Southern Quechua discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqachus?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-chus?
4. **English translation:** Is it true? / Is that really so? / Can it be real?
5. **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction combining *cheqa* with the polar question enclitic *-chu*. Used to query the truth or genuineness of a claim or situation. Extremely common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqachus, manachus?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-chus, mana-chus?
4. **English translation:** Is it true or not? / Is it real or isn't it?
5. **Usage notes:** A paired polar question construction using *-chu* on both the positive and negative alternatives. Used to request a definitive answer about the truth or genuineness of something. Common in everyday and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqata rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-ta rimay
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak truthfully. / To speak the truth, to tell the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** Imperative construction with *cheqa* in the accusative/adverbial case (*-ta*) modifying the verb *rimay* ('to speak'). The standard collocation for commanding or requesting truthful speech. *Cheqata rimay* uses the accusative-marked adverbial form of *cheqa* with the verb *rimay*, meaning "to speak truly" or "to speak what is true." This construction is distinct from *cheqanta rimay* (documented in P1), where *cheqan* is a possessed nominal ("its truth"); here *cheqata* functions adverbially. Both constructions are attested and appear to be used in overlapping contexts of moral and legal enjoinment to truthful speech, with *cheqanta rimay* being the more conventionalized fixed expression and *cheqata rimay* the more compositional variant. Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqata rimani
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-ta rimani
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth. / I speak truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** First-person declarative form of the *cheqata rimay* construction. Used in testimony and everyday assertion to claim that one's own speech is truthful. Common in legal and moral contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Mana cheqachu
3. **Transliteration:** Mana cheqa-chu
4. **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not genuine. / That is false. / That is not the case.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard negative predication of falsity, combining the negator *mana* with *cheqa* and the negative polar enclitic *-chu*. The *-chu* is required in negative clauses in Quechua. Used as a direct denial across all registers. Extremely common in discourse as a direct denial.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa hina rikukun
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa hina rikukun
4. **English translation:** It appears to be genuine. / It looks like the real thing. / It seems true.
5. **Usage notes:** Similative construction using *cheqa hina* ('like the genuine/true') with the reflexive perception verb *rikukun* ('it is seen, it appears'). Used to describe something that has the appearance of genuineness without asserting that it is genuine. Relevant in contexts of evaluating representations and imitations.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta yachani
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-ta yachani
4. **English translation:** I know that it is true. / I know it to be genuine. / To know what is truly the case, to know the truth of the matter.
5. **Usage notes:** Nominalized subordinate clause construction: *cheqa kasqanta* ('its being true', accusative nominalization of the predicate) as the object of *yachani* ('I know'). Used to express propositional knowledge of a truth. The nominalized subordinate clause pattern (*X kasqanta yachay*) is the standard Quechua construction for propositional knowledge. Common in epistemic assertion contexts, legal and philosophical discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanman hina
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-man hina
4. **English translation:** As if it were true. / In the manner of its being genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** A complex similative construction combining the nominalized predicate *cheqa kasqan* ('its being true') with the directional/similative postposition *-man hina* ('in the direction of, as if'). Used to describe conduct or representation that proceeds as though something were true, without asserting that it is. Appears in narrative and evaluative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Construction 1.10**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam, Diospa sutimpi
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m, Diospa sutimpi
4. **English translation:** It is true, in God's name. / Truly, I swear by God.
5. **Usage notes:** An oath or solemn affirmation construction combining the evidentially marked truth assertion *cheqam* with the prepositional phrase *Diospa sutimpi* ('in the name of God'). Attested in colonial and contemporary legal and religious Quechua texts as a formula for sworn testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** To speak saying it is true. / To assert truthfully. / To affirm as true while speaking.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using the quotative converb *nispa* ('saying, having said') to embed *cheqa* as a reported or performed assertion within a speech act description. The *nispa* construction is a productive syntactic pattern in Quechua for embedding direct speech or thought. Used in narrative and testimonial contexts when describing how a statement was made.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta willay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-ta willay
4. **English translation:** To report what is truly the case, to tell what is actually true.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *cheqa* (true) with the nominalized stative *kasqan* (the fact of its being, what it actually is) in the accusative, followed by *willay* (to inform, to report). Used in legal and testimonial contexts to enjoin a witness to report the actual facts of a matter. The construction emphasizes the factual state of affairs rather than the speaker's sincerity.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanman hina rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-man hina rimay
4. **English translation:** To speak in accordance with what is actually the case, to speak as things truly are.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *cheqa kasqanman hina* (in accordance with what is truly the case) as an adverbial phrase modifying *rimay* (to speak). The postposition *-man hina* (in accordance with, in the manner of) creates a conformity construction: speech that conforms to the true state of affairs. Attested in formal and legal registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam nispa niy
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m nispa niy
4. **English translation:** To say "it is true," to assert that something is true.
5. **Usage notes:** A reported-speech construction using the quotative converb *nispa* (saying) to embed the assertion *cheqam* within a frame verb *niy* (to say). Used in narrative and testimonial contexts when reporting that someone made a truth claim. The construction is the standard way of reporting direct assertions in Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.15**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa runam kay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa runa-m kay
4. **English translation:** To be a person of integrity, to be a genuine/upright person.
5. **Usage notes:** A predication construction using *cheqa runa* (upright person, documented in P1b) as the predicate nominal with the copular verb *kay* (to be). Used in moral evaluations of persons. The evidential *-m* on *runa* marks the speaker's direct assertion of this characterization. Cross-references the collocation *cheqa runa* documented in P1b.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.16**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanpi iñiy
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-pi iñiy
4. **English translation:** To believe in what is truly the case, to have faith in the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *cheqa kasqanpi* (in the truth of what is the case, locative) with *iñiy* (to believe). Used in religious and philosophical contexts to describe belief grounded in what is actually true. Appears in Christian Quechua catechetical texts and in discussions of epistemically grounded faith.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.17**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanmanta willakuyki.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-manta willakuy-ki.
4. **English translation:** I will tell you about what turned out to be true. / I will tell you of its being true.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominalized construction in which *cheqa* is embedded as a predicate within a nominalized clause (*kasqanmanta*, "about its having been") functioning as the complement of *willakuy* ("to tell"). Illustrates how *cheqa* participates in complex clause embedding in testimonial discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Construction 1.18**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam, manan llullachu
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m, mana-n llulla-chu
4. **English translation:** It is true, it is not a lie; truly, and not deceptive.
5. **Usage notes:** A paired assertive construction combining the positive assertion *cheqam* (it is true) with the negative denial *manan llullachu* (it is not a lie). Used in emphatic affirmations, particularly in testimonial and legal contexts, where the speaker reinforces the truth claim by simultaneously denying its opposite. The pairing of positive and negative poles is a documented rhetorical pattern in Quechua discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 2: cheqam / cheqami

---

**Construction 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqam / cheqami
2. **Example:** Cheqam, paym chaypi karqa
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m, pay-m chay-pi karqa
4. **English translation:** It is true, he/she was there; truly, he/she was indeed there.
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse construction in which *cheqam* functions as a sentence-initial affirmative particle before a further assertion, both marked with the direct evidential *-m/-mi*. This pattern — *cheqam* followed by an evidentially marked proposition — is a documented discourse strategy for emphatic affirmation in testimonial and narrative contexts. The initial *cheqam* validates the following proposition.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqam / cheqami
2. **Example:** Cheqam, arím
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m, arí-m
4. **English translation:** It is true, yes indeed; truly, yes.
5. **Usage notes:** A paired affirmative construction combining *cheqam* (it is true) with *arím* (yes indeed — *arí* "yes" + evidential *-m*). Used in conversational discourse as an emphatic double affirmation. The pairing of two evidentially marked affirmatives is a documented pattern in Southern Quechua conversational interaction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 3: cheqanchay

---

**Construction 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Qelqata cheqanchay
3. **Transliteration:** Qelqa-ta cheqanchay
4. **English translation:** To verify a document. / To confirm the accuracy of a text. / To authenticate a text.
5. **Usage notes:** The standard collocation of *cheqanchay* with a document object in the accusative case. Used in administrative and legal contexts for the act of checking a written record against reality. The accusative *-ta* marks the document as the object of verification.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchay atinichu
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanchay atinichu
4. **English translation:** I cannot verify it. / I am unable to confirm it.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction combining the infinitive *cheqanchay* with the negated ability verb *atinichu* ('I cannot'). Used to express inability to verify or confirm a claim. Common in administrative and testimonial contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchasqam
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanchasqa-m
4. **English translation:** It has been verified. / It is confirmed.
5. **Usage notes:** The participial form *cheqanchasqa* with the direct evidential enclitic *-m*, functioning as a complete predicative assertion of verified status. Used in administrative and legal contexts to declare that something has been officially confirmed. Documented in P2a as a derivational form; included here as an attested predicative construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 4: cheqachiy

---

**Construction 4.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqachiy
2. **Example:** Cheqachiy atiy
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqachiy atiy
4. **English translation:** To be able to verify, to be capable of confirming.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic modal construction combining *cheqachiy* (to verify/confirm) with *atiy* (to be able to, can). Used in legal and administrative contexts when the capacity to verify a claim is at issue. *Atiy* is the standard modal verb for ability in Quechua and combines freely with other infinitives.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqachiy
2. **Example:** Manan cheqachiyta atinichu
3. **Transliteration:** Mana-n cheqachiy-ta atini-chu
4. **English translation:** I cannot verify it, I am unable to confirm it.
5. **Usage notes:** The negated form of the modal construction above, using *mana...chu* negation with *cheqachiyta atiy* (to be able to verify). Used in testimonial contexts when a witness or party cannot confirm a claim. The accusative *-ta* on the infinitive *cheqachiy* is the standard object-marking of infinitives in Quechua modal constructions.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **Usage notes:** Emphatic predicate construction combining *cheqaqpuni* (absolutely true) with the direct evidential clitic and a demonstrative. Used for strong assertion of truth. Attested in Southern Quechua testimonial and everyday discourse.

## BASE FORM 9: cheqanta rimay

---

**Note:** *Cheqanta rimay* is itself a conventionalized multi-word expression documented in P1 and P1b. The constructions below involve this expression in larger syntactic frames.

---

**Construction 9.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanta rimay
2. **Example:** Cheqanta rimayta munay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanta rimay-ta munay
4. **English translation:** To want to tell the truth, to wish to speak truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic volitional construction combining *cheqanta rimay* (to speak the truth) in its nominalized/infinitival accusative form with *munay* (to want, to wish). Used in testimonial and moral contexts when expressing the intention or desire to be truthful. *Munay* is the standard volitional verb in Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanta rimay
2. **Example:** Cheqanta rimanayki tiyan
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanta rima-nayki tiyan
4. **English translation:** You must tell the truth, you are obliged to speak truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic deontic construction using the obligative nominalization *-na* with second-person possessive *-yki* (your obligation to speak the truth) followed by *tiyan* (it exists, it is necessary — the existential/deontic verb). This is the standard construction for expressing obligation in Quechua. Used in legal and moral contexts to enjoin truthful testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
