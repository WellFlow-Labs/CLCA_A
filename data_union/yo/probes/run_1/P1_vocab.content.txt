# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Note on script and transliteration:** Yoruba is written in a Latin-based orthography with diacritics marking tone (acute = high, grave = low, unmarked or macron = mid) and vowel quality (subdot for open vowels). The standard orthography used here follows the Yoruba Studies Association / Akin Oyètádé convention as codified in academic Yoruba linguistics. Tones are marked on the native orthographic form; no separate transliteration column is added where the orthography is self-representing.

---

## Lexical Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** òtítọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalized form; related to *ìtọ́* (straightness, correctness); the prefix *o-* nominalizes
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, accuracy, fact
**Usage:** The most general and widely used term for truth in Yoruba. Used in moral, legal, religious, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether a statement corresponds to reality. Participates in productive constructions such as *sọ òtítọ́* ("speak the truth") and *ọ̀nà òtítọ́* ("the way of truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** irọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; falsehood, lie
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, fabrication
**Usage:** The direct antonym of *òtítọ́*; used to evaluate statements or representations as false or deceptive. Appears in constructions such as *sọ irọ́* ("tell a lie") and is used in moral condemnation of deceptive conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** òdodo
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun/adjective; reduplication of root *do*; associated with rightness and justice
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, fairness, rightness
**Usage:** Used to evaluate conduct, persons, and decisions as morally or procedurally correct. Appears in legal and moral discourse and in proverbs. Distinct from *òtítọ́* in that it foregrounds normative rightness rather than factual accuracy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *mọ̀* ("to know"); prefix *i-* nominalizes
**Typical English translations:** knowledge, understanding, awareness
**Usage:** Used in contexts where the reliability or accuracy of a claim is grounded in genuine knowledge. Evaluates whether a person has the epistemic basis for a statement. Contrasts with *àìmọ̀* (ignorance).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** àìmọ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *àì-* (negation prefix) + *mọ̀* ("to know"); "not-knowing"
**Typical English translations:** ignorance, lack of knowledge, unawareness
**Usage:** Used to evaluate a person's claim or conduct as unreliable or mistaken due to lack of knowledge. Functions as a mitigating or explanatory term in moral and epistemic assessments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *gbàgbọ́* ("to believe, to accept as true"); *i-* nominalizes
**Typical English translations:** belief, faith, trust, conviction
**Usage:** Used in religious, moral, and epistemic contexts to describe acceptance of something as true or reliable. Evaluates the stance of a person toward a claim or entity. Common in Christian and Islamic Yoruba discourse as well as everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* ("to rely on, to trust"); compound involving *gbé* (carry/place) + *kẹ̀lé* (lean on)
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliance, dependability, reliability
**Usage:** Evaluates persons or things as worthy of reliance or trust. Used in interpersonal, commercial, and moral contexts. A person described as having *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* is considered dependable and honest.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; evidence, witness, testimony
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, testimony, witness
**Usage:** Used in legal and evaluative contexts to refer to evidence that supports or undermines the truth of a claim. *Ẹ̀rí ojú* ("eye-witness evidence") is a common compound. Central to legal discourse in Yoruba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ẹ̀rí* (evidence/witness) + *ojú* (eye); "eye-evidence"
**Typical English translations:** eyewitness testimony, direct evidence, firsthand witness
**Usage:** A fixed compound used in legal and evaluative contexts to designate evidence based on direct visual observation. Considered the most reliable form of testimony in Yoruba legal and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization; related to *jẹ́rìí* ("to bear witness, to testify"); possibly from Arabic *shahāda* via Islamic influence, but fully integrated
**Typical English translations:** testimony, attestation, certification, witness
**Usage:** Used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to refer to formal attestation of truth or authenticity. In Islamic Yoruba usage, refers specifically to the declaration of faith. More formal than *ẹ̀rí*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** jẹ́rìí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Verb; to bear witness, to testify, to attest
**Typical English translations:** to testify, to bear witness, to attest, to certify
**Usage:** The verbal base from which *ìjẹ́rìísí* is derived. Used in legal and religious contexts to describe the act of attesting to the truth of something. Evaluates the speaker as a reliable source of confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** gidi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Adjective/adverb; genuine, real, actual
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** Used to evaluate objects, persons, or states as authentic rather than imitation or pretense. Appears in constructions such as *ọrẹ gidi* ("a genuine friend") and *ìfẹ́ gidi* ("real love"). Productive in colloquial and contemporary Yoruba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** òtọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Adjective/noun; correctness, straightness, accuracy
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, measurements, or conduct as correct or accurate. Related morphologically to *òtítọ́* but used more commonly as a predicative adjective. Appears in *ó tọ́* ("it is correct/right").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ni
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *i-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct/straight) + *ni* (linking/directive); "directing toward correctness"
**Typical English translations:** guidance, direction, correction, instruction
**Usage:** Used to describe the act of directing someone toward what is correct or right. Evaluates conduct or information as needing or receiving correction. Common in educational and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** àṣìṣe
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *à-* (nominalizer) + *ṣìṣe* (doing wrongly); "wrong-doing, error"
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing
**Usage:** Used to evaluate actions or statements as incorrect, mistaken, or morally wrong. Applies both to factual errors and to moral transgressions. Contrasts with *òtọ́* and *òdodo*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; sin, offense, guilt, fault
**Typical English translations:** sin, guilt, offense, wrongdoing, fault
**Usage:** Used in moral, religious, and legal contexts to evaluate conduct as a violation of a norm or law. Distinct from *àṣìṣe* in that it more strongly implies moral culpability rather than mere error. Central to Yoruba religious and ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ìwà
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; character, nature, conduct, being
**Typical English translations:** character, conduct, nature, behavior, essence
**Usage:** Used to evaluate what a person fundamentally is, as expressed through their conduct. *Ìwà rere* ("good character") and *ìwà burúkú* ("bad character") are highly productive compounds. Central to Yoruba ethical philosophy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ìwà rere
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ìwà* (character/conduct) + *rere* (good); "good character"
**Typical English translations:** good character, virtue, moral goodness, integrity
**Usage:** A fixed compound that evaluates a person as morally good based on their conduct and nature. Considered a foundational value in Yoruba ethics. Frequently cited in proverbs and moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ìwà burúkú
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ìwà* (character) + *burúkú* (bad/evil); "bad character"
**Typical English translations:** bad character, vice, moral corruption, wickedness
**Usage:** The antonym of *ìwà rere*; evaluates a person's fundamental nature or conduct as morally deficient. Used in moral condemnation and in proverbs contrasting good and bad conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ojúlówó
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ojú* (face/appearance) + *lówó* (in hand / possessing); "having the real face" or "genuine in hand"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, legitimate
**Usage:** Used to evaluate objects, persons, or relationships as authentic and not counterfeit or superficial. Contrasts with imitation or pretense. Common in commercial and interpersonal evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ìtànjẹ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *tànjẹ́* ("to deceive, to defraud"); *i-* nominalizes
**Typical English translations:** deception, fraud, deceit, trickery
**Usage:** Used to evaluate conduct or representations as deliberately misleading. Applied in legal, moral, and commercial contexts. Implies intentional misrepresentation, distinguishing it from mere error (*àṣìṣe*).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** àgàbàgebè
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; pretense, hypocrisy, sham
**Typical English translations:** pretense, hypocrisy, sham, insincerity
**Usage:** Used to evaluate a person's conduct or presentation as insincere or performatively false. Implies that the outward appearance does not correspond to inner reality. Used in moral criticism of hypocritical behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** ìfarahàn
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *i-* (nominalizer) + *farahan* ("to appear, to manifest, to reveal oneself")
**Typical English translations:** appearance, manifestation, revelation, showing
**Usage:** Used in contexts evaluating whether something has been genuinely revealed or made apparent. Can carry the sense of authentic self-disclosure as opposed to concealment. Used in religious and philosophical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** ìmúrasílẹ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *múrasílẹ̀* ("to prepare oneself, to make ready"); compound of *mú* (take/make) + *ara* (body/self) + *sílẹ̀* (down/ready)
**Typical English translations:** preparation, readiness, making ready
**Usage:** Used in evaluative contexts where readiness or proper preparation is a criterion of adequacy or correctness. Less central to truth-evaluation than other items here; included as it appears in assessments of whether something is properly constituted.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ìdánilójú
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *i-* (nominalizer) + *dá* (make/establish) + *ni* (person/one) + *ojú* (eye/certainty); "making one certain / establishing certainty in the eye"
**Typical English translations:** assurance, confirmation, verification, convincing
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether a claim or person has been confirmed or verified to the satisfaction of an observer. Appears in legal and interpersonal contexts where certainty about truth or reliability is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ìdánwò
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *i-* (nominalizer) + *dán* (test/try) + *wò* (look/examine); "testing and examining"
**Typical English translations:** test, trial, examination, proof (by testing)
**Usage:** Used in contexts where the truth, quality, or reliability of something is established through testing or examination. Applies to persons, objects, and claims. Common in educational, religious, and evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization; *fọ̀rọ̀* (question/inquiry) + *wá* (seek) + *ni* (person) + *lẹ́nu* (in mouth); "seeking inquiry from a person's mouth"
**Typical English translations:** interrogation, questioning, interview, inquiry
**Usage:** Used in legal and investigative contexts to describe formal questioning aimed at establishing the truth of a matter. Evaluates the process by which testimony or information is extracted and assessed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** ìdájọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *i-* (nominalizer) + *dá* (establish/make) + *jọ́* (judgment/resemblance); "establishing judgment"
**Typical English translations:** judgment, verdict, ruling, adjudication
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts to refer to the formal evaluation and determination of right and wrong, guilt and innocence. Central to Yoruba legal discourse. Participates in compounds such as *ilé ìdájọ́* ("court of law").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** dọ̀gba
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Verb/adjective; to be equal, to match, to correspond
**Typical English translations:** equal, equivalent, matching, corresponding
**Usage:** Used in evaluative contexts where the criterion is correspondence or equivalence between two things — including resemblance, proportionality, or fair equivalence. Appears in assessments of fairness and in comparisons of representations to originals.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** ìjọra
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalization of *jọra* ("to resemble each other"); *i-* nominalizes; *jọ* (resemble) + *ra* (reflexive/each other)
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used to evaluate the degree to which one thing resembles another — relevant in aesthetic, legal (e.g., identification), and representational contexts. Distinct from *dọ̀gba* in that it concerns likeness rather than strict equality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
