# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: tosi / tode-**
- **Native script:** tosi / tode-
- **Transliteration:** tosi / tode-
- **P1 participation:** 8 items — *tosi*, *totuus*, *epätosi*, *todenmukainen*, *totuudenmukainen*, *todistaa*, *todiste*, *todistus*
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root for truth and factual reality in Finnish. Appears both in its base form *tosi* and in its oblique/derived stem *tode-* (genitive *toden*, partitive *totta*). Anchors the domain of propositional truth, factual accuracy, and evidentiary certification.

---

**Root 2: väärä / vääre-**
- **Native script:** väärä / vääre-
- **Transliteration:** väärä / vääre-
- **P1 participation:** 4 items — *väärä*, *väärentää*, *väärentäminen*, *vääristely*
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root for falsity, wrongness, and deviation from correctness. Covers epistemic falsity, moral wrongness, physical crookedness, and fraudulent production. Highly productive in legal and evaluative contexts.

---

**Root 3: oikea / oikeus-**
- **Native script:** oikea / oikeus-
- **Transliteration:** oikea / oikeus-
- **P1 participation:** 3 items — *oikea*, *oikeellisuus*, *oikeudenmukaisuus*
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root for correctness, rightness, and legitimacy. Covers both epistemic correctness (right answer, correct form) and normative rightness (legal right, justice). Extremely productive across legal, administrative, and moral domains.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in uses where the connection to truth, falsity, or correctness appears to shift or extend into other domains. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From tosi / tode-:**

- *todella* and *tosi* (as in "tosi hyvä") function as degree intensifiers in colloquial Finnish, where the connection to propositional truth is not apparent.
- *todellisuuspako* ("escapism") involves the root in a psychological context concerning avoidance behavior rather than truth evaluation.
- *todellisuudentaju* ("sense of reality") involves the root in a perceptual/psychological capacity context.
- *todentua* ("to come true, to materialize") involves the root in a context of events fulfilling predictions, where the truth-relation is prospective rather than descriptive.
- *totuudenhetki* ("moment of truth") is used in contexts of decisive action or revelation that may not involve propositional truth claims.

---

**From väärä / vääre-:**

- *vääryys* ("injustice, wrong") involves the root in a moral-normative context where the primary sense is injustice rather than falsity.
- *vääräoppinen* / *vääräoppisuus* ("heretical, heresy") involves the root in a religious-doctrinal context.
- *vääristymä* ("distortion, aberration") is used in technical contexts (optics, acoustics, economics) where the primary sense is physical or mathematical deviation rather than falsity.
- *väärinkäyttö* ("misuse, abuse") involves the root in contexts of improper use where the primary sense is normative violation rather than epistemic falsity.
- *väärä hälytys* ("false alarm") involves the root in a context where the falsity is situational rather than propositional.

---

**From oikea / oikeus-:**

- *oikeakielisyys* ("linguistic correctness") involves the root in a prescriptive-normative linguistic context rather than a truth-evaluative one.
- *oikeaoppinen* ("orthodox") involves the root in a doctrinal conformity context.
- *oikeuttaa* ("to justify, to entitle") involves the root in a normative-justificatory context where the primary sense is authorization or entitlement rather than correctness.
- *oikeus* in the sense of "court" involves the root in an institutional context.
- *oikea* in the sense of "right" (direction) involves the root in a spatial orientation context entirely separate from truth or correctness.
- *oikeudenmukaisuus* ("justice, fairness") involves the root in a distributive-moral context where the primary sense is fairness rather than correctness.
