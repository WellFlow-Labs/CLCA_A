## 4. CAUSATIVE: CAUSING ERROR OR MISTAKENNESS

### Pattern
**panta- + causative** or **pantay-man + churay**

### Examples
- **pantachiy** → *pantachiy* → ‘to mislead; to cause to err; to confuse’
- **pantayman churay** → *pantayman churay* → ‘to lead into error; to misdirect’

### Notes
This is the **clearest dynamic pattern** in the dataset. Quechua here encodes change of epistemic state most securely in the **error domain**, not in the truth domain proper.

- **pantachiy** presents the causing event compactly as a derived causative verb.
- **pantayman churay** presents it more transparently as ‘put into error’.

Both describe **Y causing X to enter a state of error**.

---

## 5. RESULTING STATE AFTER CAUSATION

### Pattern
**causative verb / construction → participial result state**

### Examples
- **pantachiy** → *pantachiy* → ‘to mislead; cause to err’
- **pantachisqa** → *pantachisqa* → ‘misled; mistaken through deception or confusion’

### Notes
The materials support a dynamic sequence:

1. **pantachiy** = someone causes error
2. **pantachisqa** = the affected person or judgment is in the resulting state of having been misled

So the language data here clearly encode:
- **causing a change**
- **the resulting changed state**

This is the strongest attested truth-related dynamic opposition in the supplied material.

## 6. DYNAMIC FALSEHOOD: ACT OF LYING

### Pattern
**llulla-root verbal expression of deceptive action**

### Examples
- **llullakuy** → *llullakuy* → ‘to lie; to tell lies; to deceive’

### Notes
Although the dataset does not provide a “cause to become false” construction, it does provide a **dynamic verbal expression for engaging in falsehood**:

- **llullakuy** describes the act/process of lying or deceiving.

This is dynamic in the sense of **performing deception**, but it is **not** specifically a causative pattern like **pantachiy**, and it is **not** an inchoative ‘become false’.

## 7. DYNAMIC TRUTH-ASSERTION IN DISCOURSE

### Pattern
**adverbial truth form used to reinforce a proposition**

### Examples
- **chiqap** → *chiqap* → ‘truly; really; in truth’
- **chiqaqta** → *chiqaqta* → ‘truly; really’

### Notes
These forms do not mean ‘become true’, but they are dynamic at the discourse level: they are used when a speaker **actively asserts or reinforces** truth.

- **chiqap** is the core form.
- **chiqaqta** is less certain.

So in the supplied material, truth is often encoded not as a change-of-state event, but as **strengthened assertion**.

## 8. STABLE PREDICATIVE STATES

### Pattern
**simple attribution / negative attribution**

### Examples
- **allinmi** → *allinmi* → ‘it is right / correct’
- **pantasqa** → *pantasqa* → ‘it is mistaken / wrong’
- **mana allin** → *mana allin* → ‘not right; wrong; improper’
- **chiqaq** → *chiqaq* → ‘true; real; genuine’
- **huchayuq** → *huchayuq* → ‘guilty; culpable; sinful’

### Notes
Much of the supplied truth-related vocabulary is encoded as **stable states or evaluations**, not as transitions into those states.

This includes:
- **truth/reality**: **chiqaq**
- **correctness/propriety**: **allin, allinmi**
- **wrongness/error**: **pantasqa, mana allin**
- **culpability**: **huchayuq**

So the dominant pattern in the dataset is **state attribution**, while explicit change-of-state marking is limited.

## 10. OVERALL SUMMARY

### Best-attested dynamic expressions
- **pantachiy** — ‘to mislead; cause to err’
- **pantayman churay** — ‘to lead into error; misdirect’
- **pantachisqa** — ‘misled; in the state resulting from being led into error’
- **llullakuy** — ‘to lie; to deceive’
- **chiqap** — ‘truly; really’ as dynamic discourse reinforcement

### Weakly or not attested
- ‘become true’ with **chiqaq** — **no common expression identified**
- ‘become false’ with **llulla / llullasqa** — **no common expression identified**
- ‘become right/wrong’ with **allin / mana allin** — **no common expression identified**
- ‘cause something to be true’ — **no common expression identified**
- ‘cause someone to be truthful’ — **no common expression identified**

### Generalization from the supplied material
In the provided Quechua material, **dynamic encoding is strongest in the domain of error and deception**, especially:
- **causing error**: **pantachiy**, **pantayman churay**
- **resulting misled state**: **pantachisqa**
- **performing deception**: **llullakuy**

By contrast, **truth and correctness** are represented mainly as **stable predicative states** or **assertive discourse forms**, not with clearly attested inchoative or causative constructions in the supplied evidence.
