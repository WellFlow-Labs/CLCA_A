# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Methodological note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Constructions already documented in P1b as collocations are cross-referenced where relevant but not re-described at length; the focus here is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven patterns not fully captured in earlier steps. Derivational forms are excluded per protocol. Absence is recorded explicitly. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and natural currency of use.

## Base Form 1: ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti
2. **Example:** ეს არის ჭეშმარიტი
3. **Transliteration:** es aris č̣ešmariti
4. **English translation:** this is true / this is the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative use of the adjective with the copula aris. Used in formal and theological registers to assert the truth of a proposition or state of affairs. Less common in everyday speech than the parallel construction with martalia or namdvilia.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი და ჭეშმარიტი კაცი
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti ɣmerti da č̣ešmariti kaci
4. **English translation:** true God and true man
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed theological formula used in Christological discourse to affirm the dual nature of Christ. Appears in liturgical texts, creeds, and theological writing. A formulaic construction with a specific doctrinal function.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტია, რომ...
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritia, rom...
4. **English translation:** it is true that..., it is a fact that...
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with the copula -ia and the complementizer rom, used to introduce a proposition asserted as true. Found in formal written Georgian and elevated speech. The construction č̣ešmaritia, rom... is more formal than martalia, rom... or namdvilia, rom...
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**No further common multi-word constructions identified for č̣ešmariti beyond those documented above and in P1b.**

## Base Form 2: ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba

---

**Construction 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება გამარჯვებს
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs
4. **English translation:** truth prevails / truth will prevail
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial and rhetorical expression asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. Used in moral and political discourse, in sermons, and in literary contexts. Functions as a standalone assertion or as a closing statement in argument.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება მწარეა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba mc̣area
4. **English translation:** the truth is bitter / truth is hard to hear
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial expression used to acknowledge that truthful statements are often unwelcome or painful. Appears in everyday speech and in literary contexts as a concessive acknowledgment before delivering an unpleasant truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება ითქვა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba itq̇va
4. **English translation:** the truth was spoken / the truth has been told
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction used to assert that a true statement has been made, often in a context of acknowledgment or vindication. Used in formal and literary registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.4**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება გითხრა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba gitxra
4. **English translation:** I will tell you the truth / let me tell you the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A formulaic introductory expression used to signal that what follows is a sincere and accurate statement. The verb gitxra (I will tell you) is in the future/subjunctive form. Used in both spoken and written Georgian to preface a candid disclosure.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.5**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** მთელი ჭეშმარიტება
3. **Transliteration:** mteli č̣ešmariteba
4. **English translation:** the whole truth / the full truth
5. **Usage notes:** Collocation with the quantifier mteli (whole, entire), used in legal and moral contexts to emphasize that nothing has been withheld. Appears in oath formulas and in demands for complete disclosure.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.6**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება და მხოლოდ ჭეშმარიტება
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba da mxolod č̣ešmariteba
4. **English translation:** the truth and nothing but the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A formulaic legal oath expression, parallel to the English courtroom formula. Used in legal proceedings and in rhetorical emphasis outside legal contexts. The construction mirrors international legal oath formulas adapted into Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.7**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება დაიმალა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba daimala
4. **English translation:** the truth was hidden / the truth was concealed
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction used in journalistic, legal, and moral discourse to assert that true information has been suppressed or withheld. Common in investigative journalism and political commentary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Base Form 3: სიმართლე / simartle

---

**Construction 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე გითხრა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle gitxra
4. **English translation:** I will tell you the truth / let me be honest with you
5. **Usage notes:** The most common everyday formula for prefacing a candid statement. More colloquial than the parallel construction with č̣ešmariteba. Used across all registers and age groups in spoken Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე მოგახსენო
3. **Transliteration:** simartle mogaxseno
4. **English translation:** to tell you the truth (polite/formal register) / if I may be honest with you
5. **Usage notes:** A more formal or deferential variant of simartle gitxra, using the polite verb form mogaxseno (I will inform you, respectful). Used in formal speech, addressing elders or superiors, and in written correspondence.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე რომ ვთქვა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle rom vtq̇va
4. **English translation:** to tell the truth / if I'm being honest / truth be told
5. **Usage notes:** A parenthetical or introductory clause meaning "if I am to tell the truth." Used as a discourse marker to signal candor or to introduce a qualification. Very common in spoken Georgian across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.4**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე ნახევარი სიცრუეა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle naxevari sicruea
4. **English translation:** a half-truth is a lie / a partial truth is a falsehood
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial expression used in moral discourse to assert that incomplete or selective truth-telling is equivalent to lying. Used in ethical argument and in everyday speech to challenge misleading statements.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.5**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლეს ვამბობ
3. **Transliteration:** simartles vambob
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth / I speak the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A direct first-person assertion of truthfulness. Used in everyday speech and in legal contexts to assert that one's statement is accurate. The verb ambob (to say, to tell) is in the present tense.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.6**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე გამარჯვებს
3. **Transliteration:** simartle gamarjvebs
4. **English translation:** truth prevails / truth wins out
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to the construction with č̣ešmariteba (2.1). Used in moral and political discourse to assert the ultimate triumph of truth. More common in everyday speech than the č̣ešmariteba variant.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.7**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე თვალში გიყურებს
3. **Transliteration:** simartle tvalshi giq̇urebs
4. **English translation:** the truth is staring you in the face / the truth looks you in the eye
5. **Usage notes:** An idiomatic expression used to assert that the truth is obvious and unavoidable. The construction uses the verb giq̇urebs (it looks at you, it stares at you). Used in everyday speech and in rhetorical argument.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.8**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** მთელი სიმართლე
3. **Transliteration:** mteli simartle
4. **English translation:** the whole truth / the full truth
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to the construction with č̣ešmariteba (2.5). Used in legal and moral contexts to emphasize completeness of disclosure. Very common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.9**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე მწარეა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle mc̣area
4. **English translation:** the truth is bitter / truth hurts
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to the construction with č̣ešmariteba (2.2). More common in everyday speech than the č̣ešmariteba variant. Used as a proverbial acknowledgment before delivering unwelcome information.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.10**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Example:** სიმართლე ძვირი ღირს
3. **Transliteration:** simartle ʒviri ɣirs
4. **English translation:** truth comes at a high price / the truth is costly
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial expression used to acknowledge that telling or upholding the truth may involve personal cost or sacrifice. Used in moral and political discourse.

## Base Form 4: მართალი / martali

---

**Construction 4.1**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალი ხარ
3. **Transliteration:** martali xar
4. **English translation:** you are right / you are correct
5. **Usage notes:** The most common everyday expression for conceding that someone's statement or position is correct. Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 34. Extremely frequent in spoken Georgian across all registers and age groups.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.2**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალია
3. **Transliteration:** martalia
4. **English translation:** it is true / that is right / admittedly
5. **Usage notes:** Used both as a predicate ("it is true/correct") and as a discourse particle meaning "admittedly" or "granted" in concessive constructions. Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 35. Very common in spoken and written Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.3**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალია, მაგრამ...
3. **Transliteration:** martalia, magram...
4. **English translation:** true, but... / admittedly, but... / granted, but...
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed concessive collocation used in argument and everyday speech to acknowledge a point before introducing a counterargument. Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 36. Extremely common in spoken Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.4**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალი ვარ
3. **Transliteration:** martali var
4. **English translation:** I am right / I am correct
5. **Usage notes:** First-person assertion of correctness. Used in everyday speech to assert that one's own position or statement is accurate. The copula var is the first-person singular present form.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.5**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალი კაცი
3. **Transliteration:** martali kaci
4. **English translation:** a righteous man / an honest man / a just person
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation used in moral and religious discourse to describe a person of integrity. Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 29. Used in everyday speech, literature, and religious texts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.6**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** ღმერთი მართალია
3. **Transliteration:** ɣmerti martalia
4. **English translation:** God is just / God is righteous
5. **Usage notes:** A theological and liturgical expression asserting the justice and righteousness of God. Used in religious texts, sermons, and in everyday speech in contexts of moral appeal or consolation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.7**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Example:** მართალი სიტყვა
3. **Transliteration:** martali sitq̇va
4. **English translation:** a true word / a just saying / an honest statement

---

**Construction 4.8**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali

3. **Transliteration:** asi procentit martali xar
4. **English translation:** you are one hundred percent right / you are absolutely correct
5. **Usage notes:** An emphatic colloquial construction using the quantifier asi procentit (one hundred percent) to intensify the assertion of correctness. Common in informal spoken Georgian, particularly among younger speakers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.9**

4. **English translation:** half right / partially correct / half true

## Base Form 6: ყალბი / q̇albi

---

**Construction 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი დოკუმენტით სარგებლობა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi dokumentit sargeblobа
4. **English translation:** use of a forged document / using a false document
5. **Usage notes:** A legal periphrastic construction describing the offense of using a falsified document. The instrumental form dokumentit (with a document) combined with sargeblobа (use, utilization) forms a standard legal phrase in Georgian criminal law.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი ამოიცნო
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi amoicno
4. **English translation:** he/she identified the fake / he/she recognized the forgery
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using the verb amoicno (identified, recognized) to describe the act of detecting something false or counterfeit. Used in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.3**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი გამოდგა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi gamodga
4. **English translation:** it turned out to be fake / it proved to be false / it was found to be counterfeit
5. **Usage notes:** A very common construction using the verb gamodga (turned out, proved to be) to describe the discovery that something is not genuine. Used across all registers in everyday speech and in legal and journalistic contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.4**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი ყოფილა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi q̇opila
4. **English translation:** it turns out it was fake / it was fake all along (evidential)
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using the evidential/reportative copula q̇opila (it turns out to have been, it was — evidential) to express the discovery that something was false, often with a sense of surprise or belated realization. Very common in spoken Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.5**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი ჩვენება მისცა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi čveneba misca
4. **English translation:** he/she gave false testimony / he/she provided false evidence
5. **Usage notes:** A legal construction describing the act of giving false testimony. The verb misca (gave, provided) is in the past tense. Used in criminal proceedings and in journalistic reporting on legal cases.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.6**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Example:** ყალბი ვინაობით მოქმედება
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi vinaobит moq̇medeba
4. **English translation:** acting under a false identity / operating with a false identity
5. **Usage notes:** A legal and administrative periphrastic construction describing the offense of acting under a fabricated identity. The instrumental form vinaobит (with/under an identity) combined with moq̇medeba (action, conduct) forms a standard legal phrase.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
