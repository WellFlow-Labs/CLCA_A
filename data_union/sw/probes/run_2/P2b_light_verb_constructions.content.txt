# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 lexical items. Constructions are recorded only where they are natural and attested. Forms already documented as derivational in P2a are excluded. Forms already documented as collocations in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full, but are listed here for completeness of the construction record. Absence is recorded explicitly where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type.

The following construction types are distinguished where the data supports the distinction:

- **Periphrastic verbal constructions** — multi-word expressions that perform a verbal function not carried by a single derived verb
- **Nominal collocations** — noun + modifier or noun + genitive phrases that function as fixed or semi-fixed nominal expressions
- **Adverbial phrases** — prepositional or other multi-word expressions functioning as adverbials
- **Predicate constructions** — copular or predicative multi-word expressions
- **Idiomatic or proverbial expressions** — fixed expressions where the meaning is not fully compositional or where the expression functions as a culturally recognized formula

Where a construction type is not attested for a given item, this is stated explicitly.

### P1 Item 1: **kweli**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ninasema kweli.
3. **Transliteration:** Ninasema kweli.
4. **English Translation:** I am telling the truth. / I am speaking truthfully.
5. **Usage Notes:** The standard periphrastic verbal construction for truth-telling, combining *sema* (to say/speak) with *kweli* as a direct object or adverbial complement. Used across all registers in moral, legal, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
3. **Transliteration:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
4. **English Translation:** Truly, I don't know. / In truth, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
5. **Usage Notes:** The prepositional adverbial phrase *kwa kweli* functions as a sentence-initial or sentence-medial truth-asserting adverbial. Already documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 4); cross-referenced here.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ni kweli kabisa.
3. **Transliteration:** Ni kweli kabisa.
4. **English Translation:** It is absolutely true. / That is completely true.
5. **Usage Notes:** Copular predicate construction with *kabisa* (absolutely/completely) as an intensifying modifier. Already documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 27); cross-referenced here. Used as an emphatic affirmation across registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Si kweli hiyo.
3. **Transliteration:** Si kweli hiyo.
4. **English Translation:** That is not true. / That is false.
5. **Usage Notes:** Negative copular predicate construction using *si* (negative copula) + *kweli* + demonstrative *hiyo* (that). The standard construction for denying the truth of a specific preceding claim. Already documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 29); cross-referenced here.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli au uongo?
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli au uongo?
4. **English Translation:** True or false? / Truth or lie?
5. **Usage Notes:** A binary evaluative construction using *au* (or) to frame a truth-value question. Already documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 30); cross-referenced here. Used in educational, legal, and evaluative contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 6**

**Construction 7**

### P1 Item 3: **uongo**

---

1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Kusema uongo
3. **Transliteration:** Kusema uongo
4. **English Translation:** To tell lies. / To speak falsely. / To lie.
5. **Usage Notes:** The standard periphrastic verbal construction for the act of lying, using *sema* (to say/speak) + *uongo* as direct object. Used across all registers in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Already documented in P2a (Item 3, Derived Form 3); cross-referenced here.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu.
3. **Transliteration:** Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu.
4. **English Translation:** He/she is a consummate liar. / He/she is a first-class liar.
5. **Usage Notes:** A predicative construction using *muongo* (liar) + the intensifying phrase *wa hali ya juu* (of high level/of the highest order). Used in moral condemnation to characterize habitual or skilled deception. The phrase *wa hali ya juu* is a productive intensifier applicable to various nouns.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 3**

2. **Example:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
3. **Transliteration:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
4. **English Translation:** A lie has short legs. / Lies don't travel far.
5. **Usage Notes:** A proverbial expression asserting that lies are eventually exposed. The construction uses *una* (has, class 11 subject prefix + *na* associative) + *miguu mifupi* (short legs). Used in moral instruction and social commentary.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Kweli au uongo?
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli au uongo?
4. **English Translation:** True or false? / Truth or lie?

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Uongo mtupu
3. **Transliteration:** Uongo mtupu
4. **English Translation:** Pure lies. / Nothing but lies. / Complete falsehood.
5. **Usage Notes:** A nominal phrase using *mtupu* (pure/mere/nothing but) as a post-nominal modifier, parallel to *ukweli mtupu*. Used in moral condemnation to characterize a statement as entirely false.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

2. **Example:** Hii ndiyo hali halisi ya mambo.

3. **Transliteration:** Bidhaa halisi, si bandia.
4. **English Translation:** Genuine goods, not fake. / Authentic products, not counterfeit.
5. **Usage Notes:** A contrastive nominal construction pairing *halisi* with its antonym *bandia* via *si* (not). Used in commercial and consumer protection discourse to assert authenticity. The contrast with *bandia* is the primary evaluative frame for *halisi* in commercial contexts.

1. **Base Form:** halisi
2. **Example:** Upendo halisi hauonyeshwi kwa maneno tu.
3. **Transliteration:** Upendo halisi hauonyeshwi kwa maneno tu.
4. **English Translation:** Genuine love is not shown by words alone.
5. **Usage Notes:** A predicative construction using *upendo halisi* (genuine love) as subject with a negative passive predicate. Used in moral and interpersonal discourse to evaluate the authenticity of affection. The construction is productive with various nouns modified by *halisi*.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**No construction identified:**
