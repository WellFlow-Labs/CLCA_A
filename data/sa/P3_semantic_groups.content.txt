# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Identified Groups

### GROUP 1: Terms for Correspondence to Reality

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions used to characterize statements, cognitions, or representations as corresponding (or failing to correspond) to actual states of affairs. These terms appear in contexts requiring verification, factual accuracy, or assessment of whether something matches objective reality. They are distinguished from moral/ethical evaluations and from metaphysical discussions of ultimate reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- सत्य (satya) — "truth, true, real"
- असत्य (asatya) — "false, untrue"
- तथ्य (tathya) — "true, factual, real"
- यथार्थ (yathārtha) — "true, accurate, corresponding to reality"
- अयथार्थ (ayathārtha) — "untrue, inaccurate, not corresponding to reality"
- सत्यवचन (satyavacana) — "true statement, truthful speech"
- तथ्यं कथयति (tathyaṃ kathayati) — "tells the facts, narrates truthfully"
- यथार्थं भवति (yathārthaṃ bhavati) — "is accurate, corresponds to reality"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- सत्य (satya) — also appears in moral contexts (satyavādin "truth-speaker" as virtue) and ontological contexts (sat "being, reality")
- सत्यार्थ (satyārtha) — "true meaning" — bridges factual correspondence and interpretive accuracy

---

### GROUP 2: Terms for Epistemic Validity and Justification

**Definition:** This group includes expressions denoting valid means of acquiring knowledge, the results of such valid cognition, and the standards by which knowledge claims are evaluated. These terms appear in philosophical discussions of epistemology, debates about proof and evidence, and contexts requiring epistemic warrant. They focus on the instruments and processes of knowing rather than the content of what is known.

**Representative expressions:**
- प्रमाण (pramāṇa) — "valid means of knowledge, proof, authority"
- प्रमा (pramā) — "valid knowledge, true cognition"
- प्रमाता (pramātā) — "knower, cognizer, subject of knowledge"
- प्रमेय (prameya) — "knowable, object of knowledge"
- प्रत्यक्ष (pratyakṣa) — "perception, direct cognition"
- अनुमान (anumāna) — "inference, reasoning"
- शब्द (śabda) — "word, verbal testimony, scriptural authority"
- उपमान (upamāna) — "comparison, analogy (as means of knowledge)"
- प्रमाणेन ज्ञायते (pramāṇena jñāyate) — "is known through valid means"
- प्रमाणं भवति (pramāṇaṃ bhavati) — "becomes/serves as valid means of knowledge"
- किं प्रमाणम् (kiṃ pramāṇam) — "what is the proof?"
- प्रत्यक्षानुमानशब्दाः (pratyakṣānumānaśabdāḥ) — "perception, inference, and testimony"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- प्रमाणाभास (pramāṇābhāsa) — "semblance of valid knowledge, pseudo-proof" — bridges this group and error terms
- प्रमाणीकरण (pramāṇīkaraṇa) — "validation, authentication" — modern administrative usage extends beyond philosophical epistemology

---

### GROUP 3: Terms for Cognitive Error and Illusion

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions denoting mistaken cognition, perceptual error, or failure of knowledge to correspond to reality. These terms appear in epistemological discussions of the distinction between valid and invalid cognition, in analyses of specific types of error (such as mistaking rope for snake), and in philosophical accounts of illusion and misapprehension.

**Representative expressions:**
- भ्रम (bhrama) — "error, illusion, mistake, false cognition"
- भ्रान्ति (bhrānti) — "error, delusion, mistake"
- विपर्यय (viparyaya) — "error, perversion, reversal, mistaken cognition"
- अप्रमा (apramā) — "invalid cognition, erroneous knowledge"
- भ्रमः भवति (bhramaḥ bhavati) — "error occurs, illusion arises"
- भ्रमात् (bhramāt) — "from/due to error"
- मिथ्याप्रत्यय (mithyāpratyaya) — "false cognition, erroneous belief"
- अयथार्थं प्रतीयते (ayathārthaṃ pratīyate) — "is cognized inaccurately"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- मिथ्या (mithyā) — appears both in epistemological contexts (error) and metaphysical contexts (illusoriness, unreality)
- आभास (ābhāsa) — "appearance, semblance" — can denote both perceptual error and metaphysical appearance versus reality

---

### GROUP 4: Terms for Metaphysical Falsity and Illusoriness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used in philosophical discourse to characterize phenomena as lacking ultimate reality, being merely apparent, or possessing only conventional rather than absolute existence. These terms appear particularly in Vedāntic discussions of the relationship between appearance and reality, in Buddhist two-truths doctrine, and in analyses of the ontological status of empirical phenomena.

**Representative expressions:**
- मिथ्या (mithyā) — "false, illusory, unreal"
- मिथ्यात्व (mithyātva) — "falsity, illusoriness, unreality"
- आभास (ābhāsa) — "appearance, semblance, reflection, illusion"
- व्यवहार (vyavahāra) — "conventional usage, practical reality, empirical reality"
- परमार्थ (paramārtha) — "ultimate truth, highest reality, absolute meaning"
- मिथ्या भवति (mithyā bhavati) — "is false, becomes illusory"
- आभासमात्रम् (ābhāsamātram) — "mere appearance, only a semblance"
- व्यवहारे (vyavahāre) — "in conventional usage, at the practical level"
- परमार्थतः (paramārthataḥ) — "from the ultimate standpoint, ultimately"
- व्यवहारपरमार्थयोः (vyavahāraparamārthayoḥ) — "of conventional and ultimate [truth]"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- मिथ्या (mithyā) — also appears in epistemological contexts for cognitive error
- असत् (asat) — "non-being, unreal" — can denote both ontological non-existence and moral evil
- मिथ्यारोप (mithyāropa) — "false superimposition" — technical Vedāntic term bridging epistemology and metaphysics

---

### GROUP 5: Terms for Moral Truthfulness and Deception

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions characterizing persons, speech acts, or conduct as truthful or deceptive from a moral or ethical standpoint. These terms appear in contexts of moral evaluation, ethical instruction, legal testimony, and religious duty. They focus on the virtue or vice of truthfulness in speech and behavior rather than on factual accuracy or metaphysical reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- सत्यवादिन् (satyavādin) — "truth-speaking, truthful person"
- असत्यवादिन् (asatyavādin) — "liar, one who speaks falsely"
- मिथ्यावादिन् (mithyāvādin) — "liar, one who speaks falsely"
- सत्यव्रत (satyavrata) — "vow of truthfulness, committed to truth"
- सत्यसन्ध (satyasandha) — "keeping truth, faithful to promises"
- सत्यनिष्ठ (satyaniṣṭha) — "devoted to truth, firmly established in truth"
- मिथ्याभाषण (mithyābhāṣaṇa) — "false speech, lying"
- मिथ्यावाद (mithyāvāda) — "false doctrine, lying"
- मिथ्यासाक्षिन् (mithyāsākṣin) — "false witness, perjurer"
- सत्यं वद (satyaṃ vada) — "speak truth"
- सत्यं ब्रूयात् (satyaṃ brūyāt) — "one should speak truth"
- असत्यं वदति (asatyaṃ vadati) — "speaks falsely, tells lies"
- सत्यमेव जयते (satyam eva jayate) — "truth alone conquers"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- सत्य (satya) — appears across factual, moral, and ontological contexts
- मिथ्याचार (mithyācāra) — "false conduct, hypocrisy" — bridges moral deception and behavioral authenticity

---

### GROUP 6: Terms for Righteousness and Proper Conduct

**Definition:** This group includes expressions denoting right conduct, moral duty, proper behavior, and their opposites. These terms appear in ethical, legal, and religious discourse about what constitutes appropriate action, social obligation, and virtuous character. They are distinguished from terms specifically about truthfulness by their broader scope encompassing all forms of right and wrong conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- धर्म (dharma) — "duty, righteousness, law, proper conduct"
- अधर्म (adharma) — "unrighteousness, wrong conduct, injustice"
- साधु (sādhu) — "good, virtuous, proper, correct"
- असाधु (asādhu) — "bad, improper, incorrect, wicked"
- सम्यक् (samyak) — "right, correct, proper, complete"
- धार्मिक (dhārmika) — "righteous, religious, relating to dharma"
- धर्मं चर (dharmaṃ cara) — "practice dharma, follow righteousness"
- धर्मात् न चलेत् (dharmāt na calet) — "should not deviate from dharma"
- धर्मः रक्षति रक्षितः (dharmaḥ rakṣati rakṣitaḥ) — "dharma protects those who protect it"
- को धर्मः (ko dharmaḥ) — "what is dharma?, what is the right course?"
- साधु साधु (sādhu sādhu) — "well done! excellent!"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- सत्यधर्म (satyadharma) — "duty of truthfulness" — bridges truthfulness and general righteousness
- साधु (sādhu) — also used for grammatical correctness, extending beyond moral domain
- सम्यग्दृष्टि (samyagdṛṣṭi) — "right view" — Buddhist technical term bridging ethics and epistemology

---

### GROUP 7: Terms for Ignorance and Knowledge

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions denoting the presence or absence of knowledge, particularly in soteriological contexts where ignorance is understood as the root cause of bondage and knowledge as the means to liberation. These terms appear in philosophical discussions of the relationship between ignorance and suffering, in descriptions of spiritual progress, and in analyses of the nature of liberating knowledge.

**Representative expressions:**
- अविद्या (avidyā) — "ignorance, nescience, false knowledge"
- विद्या (vidyā) — "knowledge, learning, wisdom"
- अविद्यया (avidyayā) — "by/through ignorance, due to ignorance"
- विद्यया (vidyayā) — "by/through knowledge"
- अविद्यावशात् (avidyāvaśāt) — "under the power of ignorance"
- अविद्यानिवृत्तौ (avidyānivṛttau) — "upon cessation of ignorance"
- विद्याविद्ययोः (vidyāvidyayoḥ) — "of knowledge and ignorance"
- विद्या ददाति विनयम् (vidyā dadāti vinayam) — "knowledge gives humility"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- मिथ्याज्ञान (mithyājñāna) — "false knowledge" — bridges epistemological error and soteriological ignorance
- यथार्थज्ञानम् (yathārthajñānam) — "accurate knowledge" — bridges epistemological validity and liberating knowledge

---

### GROUP 8: Vedic Cosmic-Moral Order Terms

**Definition:** This group includes archaic Vedic expressions denoting cosmic order, ritual propriety, and the fundamental rightness of things as aligned with universal law. These terms appear primarily in Vedic hymns and early Upaniṣadic texts, where they characterize both cosmic regularity and moral-ritual correctness as aspects of a unified order. They are largely replaced by other terms (satya, dharma) in classical usage.

**Representative expressions:**
- ऋत (ṛta) — "cosmic order, truth, right, law"
- अनृत (anṛta) — "falsehood, untruth, disorder, wrong"
- ऋतस्य पथा (ṛtasya pathā) — "by the path of ṛta, according to cosmic order"
- ऋतं च सत्यं च (ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca) — "both cosmic order and truth"
- ऋतज्ञ (ṛtajña) — "knowing the cosmic order, wise"
- ऋतावन् (ṛtāvan) — "possessing ṛta, righteous"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None significant — these terms form a relatively distinct archaic register

---

### GROUP 9: Terms for Essential Nature and Identity

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions denoting the essential nature, inherent form, or true identity of things as distinguished from accidental properties or superficial appearances. These terms appear in philosophical discussions of substance and attribute, in metaphysical analyses of what things fundamentally are, and in contexts requiring distinction between essence and accident.

**Representative expressions:**
- स्वरूप (svarūpa) — "essential nature, true form, inherent character"
- सत्त्व (sattva) — "being, existence, essence"
- सत्ता (sattā) — "existence, being, reality, entity"
- धर्मता (dharmatā) — "essential nature" (in philosophical contexts)
- स्वरूपेण (svarūpeṇa) — "in its essential nature, by its own form"
- स्वरूपं ज्ञायते (svarūpaṃ jñāyate) — "the essential nature is known"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- सत् (sat) — "being, existing, real" — bridges ontological existence and essential nature
- धर्म (dharma) — in philosophical contexts can mean "essential property" as well as "duty"

---

### GROUP 10: Terms for Similarity and Correspondence

**Definition:** This group includes expressions denoting resemblance, likeness, or correspondence between things. These terms appear in comparative contexts, in discussions of analogy and metaphor, and in epistemological analyses of how cognition relates to its objects. They focus on relationships of similarity rather than identity or difference.

**Representative expressions:**
- सदृश (sadṛśa) — "similar, like, resembling"
- सादृश्य (sādṛśya) — "similarity, resemblance, likeness"
- उपमा (upamā) — "comparison, simile, resemblance"
- उपमान (upamāna) — "comparison, analogy (as means of knowledge)"
- सदृशं भवति (sadṛśaṃ bhavati) — "is similar, becomes like"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- यथार्थ (yathārtha) — "corresponding to reality" — involves correspondence but specifically to objective reality rather than similarity between things

---

## Items Not Clearly Grouped

### Expressions with Multiple Strong Affiliations:

**सत्य (satya)** — Appears prominently in Groups 1 (factual correspondence), 5 (moral truthfulness), and has extensions into Group 9 (ontological being through sat-). Its polysemy makes it central to multiple distinct usage patterns.

**मिथ्या (mithyā)** — Appears in both Group 3 (cognitive error) and Group 4 (metaphysical illusoriness), representing a genuine ambiguity in Sanskrit philosophical usage between epistemological and ontological senses of falsity.

**आभास (ābhāsa)** — Bridges Group 3 (perceptual error) and Group 4 (metaphysical appearance), used both for mistaken appearances and for the appearance-reality distinction at the ontological level.

### Expressions with Specialized or Limited Distribution:

**सत्कार (satkāra)** — "honor, respect, hospitality" — Extends from sat- root but into social domain of respectful treatment, not clearly aligned with truth/reality groups.

**सत्पुरुष (satpuruṣa)** — "good person, virtuous man" — Uses sat- for moral evaluation but also technical grammatical term, spanning multiple registers.

**सत्सङ्ग (satsaṅga)** — "association with the good" — Religious-devotional term using sat- for spiritual quality, distinct from other truth-related usages.

**सत्यलोक (satyaloka)** — "world of truth, highest heaven" — Cosmological-mythological usage extending beyond core truth domain.

**सत्यक्रिया (satyakriyā)** — "act of truth, truth-act" — Ritual-magical usage where truth-statement has performative efficacy, distinct from other truth contexts.

**मिथ्योपचार (mithyopacāra)** — "false metaphor, improper figurative usage" — Grammatical-rhetorical technical term, distinct from philosophical uses of mithyā.

**निर्माण (nirmāṇa)** — "construction, creation" — Derived from √mā but in productive-creative domain rather than epistemic measurement.

**मान (māna)** — "respect, honor, pride" — Extends from √mā into social-psychological domain, distinct from epistemic uses.

**परिमाण (parimāṇa)** — "measure, size, quantity" — From √mā but in quantitative-physical domain rather than epistemological.

**विद्यमान (vidyamāna)** — "existing, being present" — From √vid but denotes existence rather than knowledge, bridging ontological and epistemic domains.

**वैद्य (vaidya)** — "physician, learned person" — Derived from vidyā but professional designation rather than epistemological term.

### Administrative/Modern Extensions:

**प्रमाणीकरण (pramāṇīkaraṇa)** — "validation, authentication, certification"
**प्रमाणित (pramāṇita)** — "validated, authenticated, certified"
**प्रमाणपत्र (pramāṇapatra)** — "certificate, credential"
**सत्यापन (satyāpana)** — "verification, confirmation"

These modern administrative terms extend pramāṇa and satya vocabulary into bureaucratic-legal contexts with specialized meanings somewhat distinct from classical philosophical usage.

## Summary Observations

**Cross-cutting patterns observed:**

1. **Epistemological-Ontological Ambiguity:** Several key terms (mithyā, ābhāsa, sat/satya) operate across both epistemological (how we know) and ontological (what is real) domains, reflecting Sanskrit philosophical traditions' integration of these concerns.

2. **Moral-Factual Overlap:** Terms like satya function both for factual accuracy and moral virtue of truthfulness, with distinct but related usage patterns that resist clean separation.

3. **Register Stratification:** Vedic terms (ṛta/anṛta) form distinct archaic layer; classical philosophical terms dominate middle register; modern administrative extensions create new specialized usages.

4. **Root Productivity:** The √sat and √mā roots show extensive derivational productivity extending into multiple semantic domains (social, moral, epistemic, ontological), creating networks of related but distinct usages.

5. **Two-Truths Framework:** The vyavahāra-paramārtha pair forms a specialized philosophical framework with its own usage patterns, representing a meta-level distinction about levels of truth rather than a simple opposition.

**Grouping challenges:**

- The polysemy of core terms (especially satya, mithyā) creates genuine ambiguity about group membership
- Some derivational extensions move terms into domains (social honor, physical measurement, grammatical correctness) quite distant from core truth-related meanings
- The integration of epistemological and metaphysical concerns in Sanskrit philosophy means many terms resist separation into purely epistemic versus ontological categories
- Modern administrative vocabulary represents recent semantic extensions that partially preserve but also transform classical philosophical meanings
