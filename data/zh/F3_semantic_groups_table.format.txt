# F3 — SEMANTIC GROUPS TABLE

| Group Label | Definition | Representative Expressions | Overlapping / Ambiguous Items | Boundary Notes |
|------------|------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| GROUP 1: AUTHENTICITY AND GENUINENESS EVALUATION | Terms used to evaluate whether objects, products, or representations are genuine versus counterfeit, original versus imitation. These terms commonly appear in commercial contexts (distinguishing real from fake goods), art authentication (original works versus copies), and verification of material objects. They focus on the physical or material authenticity of things rather than the truth of statements. | 真 (zhēn) [when modifying physical objects], 假 (jiǎ), 真品 (zhēnpǐn), 真迹 (zhēnjì), 真丝 (zhēnsī), 真皮 (zhēnpí), 正版 (zhèngbǎn), 正品 (zhèngpǐn), 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí), 真假 (zhēnjiǎ), 真伪 (zhēnwěi), 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn), 辨别真假 (biànbié zhēnjiǎ) | 真 (zhēn) belongs to multiple groups depending on context | Typical contexts: Commerce, art collecting, product authentication, anti-counterfeiting. Key contrasts: Genuine/original vs. counterfeit/imitation; authorized vs. pirated |
| GROUP 2: FACTUAL CORRESPONDENCE AND REALITY | Terms used to evaluate whether statements, descriptions, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs or reality. These terms appear in investigative, journalistic, and analytical contexts where the concern is whether claims match what actually happened or exists. They emphasize correspondence between representation and reality rather than moral or normative correctness. | 真实 (zhēnshí) [of statements/situations], 真相 (zhēnxiàng), 事实 (shìshí), 实际 (shíjì), 实情 (shíqíng), 符合 (fúhé), 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng), 真实度 (zhēnshídù), 揭露真相 (jiēlù zhēnxiàng), 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng), 隐瞒真相 (yǐnmán zhēnxiàng), 还原真相 (huányuán zhēnxiàng), 真相大白 (zhēn xiàng dà bái), 符合事实 (fúhé shìshí), 不符合事实 (bù fúhé shìshí), 事实上 (shìshí shàng), 实际上 (shíjì shàng), 客观事实 (kèguān shìshí), 铁的事实 (tiě de shìshí) | 真 (zhēn) when modifying statements; 实 (shí) in compounds like 真实, 事实; 符合 (fúhé) with 事实, 实际 | Typical contexts: Journalism, investigation, historical research, verification of claims. Key contrasts: What actually happened vs. what is claimed; reality vs. appearance; facts vs. opinions |
| GROUP 3: CORRECTNESS OF ANSWERS AND JUDGMENTS | Terms used to evaluate whether answers, responses, judgments, or solutions are correct versus incorrect. These terms are extremely common in educational contexts (grading, testing), problem-solving situations, and everyday assessments of whether someone's answer or judgment is right or wrong. They focus on binary correctness rather than degrees of accuracy or moral rightness. | 对 (duì), 错 (cuò), 正确 (zhèngquè), 对不对？(duì bu duì?), 你说得对 (nǐ shuō de duì), 你错了 (nǐ cuò le), 哪里错了？(nǎlǐ cuò le?), 对答如流 (duì dá rú liú), 改正 (gǎizhèng), 纠正 (jiūzhèng), 修正 (xiūzhèng) | 正确 (zhèngquè) also belongs to Groups 11 and 13 depending on context | Typical contexts: Education, testing, problem-solving, everyday judgments. Key contrasts: Correct answer vs. wrong answer; right judgment vs. mistaken judgment |
| GROUP 4: PRECISION AND ACCURACY | Terms used to evaluate the degree of exactness or precision in measurements, statements, descriptions, or representations. These terms commonly appear in technical, scientific, and contexts requiring exactness, where the concern is not just whether something is true or false, but how precisely it matches a standard or target. They emphasize gradable accuracy rather than binary correctness. | 准确 (zhǔnquè), 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng), 准确度 (zhǔnquèdù), 精确 (jīngquè), 正确地 (zhèngquè de), 准确地 (zhǔnquè de) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Scientific measurement, technical specifications, data analysis, quality control. Key contrasts: Precise vs. approximate; exact vs. rough; high accuracy vs. low accuracy |
| GROUP 5: HONESTY AND SINCERITY IN CONDUCT | Terms used to evaluate the honesty, sincerity, or truthfulness of persons, their statements, or their conduct. These terms carry strong moral connotations and are central to character evaluation in Chinese culture. They focus on the moral quality of being truthful and sincere rather than the factual accuracy of statements, and often appear in contexts of moral judgment, character assessment, and interpersonal relationships. | 诚 (chéng), 诚实 (chéngshí), 诚实守信 (chéngshí shǒuxìn), 诚实可靠 (chéngshí kěkào), 真诚 (zhēnchéng), 真心 (zhēnxīn), 真挚 (zhēnzhì), 真心实意 (zhēn xīn shí yì), 老实 (lǎoshí), 正直 (zhèngzhí), 正派 (zhèngpài), 开诚布公 (kāi chéng bù gōng), 精诚所至，金石为开 (jīng chéng suǒ zhì, jīn shí wèi kāi), 说实话 (shuō shíhuà), 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō), 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō), 坦白说 (tǎnbái shuō) | 诚实 (chéngshí) and related terms also function as discourse markers (Group 16); 说实话, 老实说, 坦白说 overlap with Group 16 | Typical contexts: Moral evaluation, character assessment, interpersonal relationships, ethical discourse. Key contrasts: Honest vs. deceitful; sincere vs. hypocritical; truthful vs. lying |
| GROUP 6: DECEPTION AND FALSIFICATION | Terms used to describe deliberate acts of deceiving, lying, or creating false representations. These terms carry negative moral connotations and focus on intentional dishonesty rather than mere error or mistake. They commonly appear in contexts of moral condemnation, legal proceedings, and discussions of fraud or deception. | 欺骗 (qīpiàn), 欺骗感情 (qīpiàn gǎnqíng), 谎言 (huǎngyán), 说谎 (shuō huǎng), 撒谎 (sā huǎng), 虚假 (xūjiǎ), 虚张声势 (xū zhāng shēng shì), 弄假成真 (nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn), 把...弄假 (bǎ... nòng jiǎ) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Moral condemnation, legal proceedings, fraud investigation, relationship conflicts. Key contrasts: Deliberate deception vs. honest error; lying vs. truth-telling; fraud vs. authenticity |
| GROUP 7: SUBSTANTIALITY AND CONCRETENESS | Terms used to evaluate whether something has substance, solidity, or concrete reality as opposed to being empty, hollow, or merely theoretical. These terms often contrast the substantial/practical with the insubstantial/theoretical, and commonly appear in contexts evaluating whether plans, content, or work has real substance versus being superficial or empty show. | 实 (shí), 虚 (xū), 实在 (shízài), 实心 (shíxīn), 实体 (shítǐ), 实物 (shíwù), 充实 (chōngshí), 扎实 (zhāshí), 踏实 (tāshí), 殷实 (yīnshí), 朴实 (pǔshí), 平实 (píngshí), 实实在在 (shí shí zài zài), 华而不实 (huá ér bù shí), 虚有其表 (xū yǒu qí biǎo), 脚踏实地 (jiǎo tà shí dì), 虚虚实实 (xū xū shí shí), 真材实料 (zhēn cái shí liào) | 实 (shí) as standalone or in compounds emphasizing solidity; also belongs to Groups 2 and 8 | Typical contexts: Evaluation of work quality, content assessment, character evaluation, strategic analysis. Key contrasts: Substantial vs. empty; concrete vs. abstract; solid vs. hollow; practical vs. theoretical |
| GROUP 8: PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION AND ACTUALIZATION | Terms used to describe the process of making something actual, implementing plans, or putting ideas into practice. These terms focus on the transition from theoretical or planned to actual or realized, and commonly appear in administrative, political, and project management contexts. They emphasize action and actualization rather than mere description or evaluation. | 实现 (shíxiàn), 实施 (shíshī), 实行 (shíxíng), 实践 (shíjiàn), 落实 (luòshí), 坐实 (zuòshí), 从实际出发 (cóng shíjì chūfā), 符合实际 (fúhé shíjì) | 实 (shí) in verbal compounds like 实现, 实施; also belongs to Groups 2 and 7 | Typical contexts: Project management, policy implementation, administrative work, political discourse. Key contrasts: Theory vs. practice; planning vs. implementation; abstract vs. actualized |
| GROUP 9: RELIABILITY AND DEPENDABILITY | Terms used to evaluate whether persons, information, or objects can be depended upon or trusted. These terms focus on trustworthiness and dependability rather than truth per se, and commonly appear in contexts of assessing whether something or someone is reliable enough to be trusted or depended upon. | 可靠 (kěkào), 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng), 可靠度 (kěkàodù), 确实 (quèshí), 确实如此 (quèshí rúcǐ), 确凿的证据 (quèzáo de zhèngjù) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Quality assessment, trust evaluation, technical reliability testing. Key contrasts: Reliable vs. unreliable; dependable vs. undependable; trustworthy vs. untrustworthy |
| GROUP 10: NORMALITY AND REGULARITY | Terms used to evaluate whether something conforms to normal patterns, regular standards, or expected norms. These terms focus on conformity to what is standard or typical rather than correctness in an absolute sense, and commonly appear in medical, social, and administrative contexts where deviation from norms is significant. | 正常 (zhèngcháng), 正常性 (zhèngchángxìng), 不正常 (bù zhèngcháng), 正规 (zhèngguī), 正式 (zhèngshì) | 符合 (fúhé) with standards and norms also relates to this group | Typical contexts: Medical diagnosis, social evaluation, institutional assessment, quality control. Key contrasts: Normal vs. abnormal; regular vs. irregular; standard vs. non-standard |
| GROUP 11: LEGITIMACY AND PROPER AUTHORITY | Terms used to evaluate whether something has legitimate authority, proper status, or justified claim to position or action. These terms carry political and legal connotations and focus on rightful authority or justified action rather than mere factual correctness. They commonly appear in political, legal, and social contexts where questions of legitimacy and proper authority are at stake. | 正当 (zhèngdàng), 正当性 (zhèngdàngxìng), 正统 (zhèngtǒng), 正宗 (zhèngzōng), 正义 (zhèngyì), 正气 (zhèngqì), 名正言顺 (míng zhèng yán shùn), 正大光明 (zhèng dà guāng míng), 理直气壮 (lǐ zhí qì zhuàng), 拨乱反正 (bō luàn fǎn zhèng), 正本清源 (zhèng běn qīng yuán), 不正之风 (bù zhèng zhī fēng) | 正确 (zhèngquè) when used with political/moral implications; 真 (zhēn) as intensifier; 理直气壮 overlaps with Group 13 | Typical contexts: Political discourse, legal proceedings, social criticism, institutional reform. Key contrasts: Legitimate vs. illegitimate; justified vs. unjustified; rightful vs. wrongful; orthodox vs. heterodox |
| GROUP 12: CONSISTENCY BETWEEN APPEARANCE AND REALITY | Terms and expressions used to evaluate whether external appearance or claims match internal reality or actual practice. These terms focus specifically on the correspondence (or lack thereof) between what is shown/claimed and what actually is, and commonly appear in contexts of evaluating sincerity, authenticity of character, or truthfulness of representations. | 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí), 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí), 言行一致 (yán xíng yīzhì), 表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī), 言行不一 (yán xíng bù yī) [implied contrast], 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) [implied contrast] | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Character evaluation, moral judgment, assessment of sincerity, criticism of hypocrisy. Key contrasts: Appearance matching reality vs. appearance deceiving; claims matching practice vs. claims contradicting practice |
| GROUP 13: RATIONALITY AND REASONABLENESS | Terms used to evaluate whether something accords with reason, logic, or rational principles. These terms focus on conformity to rational standards rather than factual truth or moral correctness, and commonly appear in analytical, argumentative, and evaluative contexts where the rationality or reasonableness of positions, decisions, or arrangements is at issue. | 合理 (hélǐ), 合理性 (hélǐxìng), 理直气壮 (lǐ zhí qì zhuàng) [overlaps with Group 11] | 正确 (zhèngquè) when emphasizing logical correctness; 符合 (fúhé) with 理 (reason); 理直气壮 overlaps with Group 11 | Typical contexts: Argumentation, policy evaluation, decision assessment, philosophical discourse. Key contrasts: Reasonable vs. unreasonable; rational vs. irrational; logical vs. illogical |
| GROUP 14: PRACTICAL UTILITY AND FUNCTIONALITY | Terms used to evaluate whether something is practically useful, functional, or serves real purposes. These terms focus on pragmatic value and actual usefulness rather than theoretical correctness or abstract truth, and commonly appear in contexts of product evaluation, policy assessment, and pragmatic decision-making. | 实用 (shíyòng), 实用性 (shíyòngxìng), 实惠 (shíhuì), 实效 (shíxiào) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Product evaluation, policy assessment, pragmatic decision-making, consumer choices. Key contrasts: Practical vs. impractical; useful vs. useless; functional vs. ornamental |
| GROUP 15: EMPIRICAL INVESTIGATION AND VERIFICATION | Terms and expressions used in contexts of empirical investigation, evidence-gathering, and verification of claims through observation or testing. These terms emphasize the process of determining truth through investigation and evidence rather than through authority or reasoning alone. | 实验 (shíyàn), 实证 (shízhèng), 实地 (shídì), 实况 (shíkuàng), 实录 (shílù), 实例 (shílì), 实事求是 (shí shì qiú shì), 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) [overlaps with Group 2], 查无实据 (chá wú shí jù), 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) [overlaps with Group 2], 提供证据 (tígōng zhèngjù) | 查明真相 and 真凭实据 overlap with Group 2 | Typical contexts: Scientific research, legal investigation, journalism, forensic analysis. Key contrasts: Empirical vs. theoretical; evidence-based vs. speculative; verified vs. unverified |
| GROUP 16: DISCOURSE MARKERS OF SINCERITY AND EMPHASIS | Terms and expressions that function as discourse markers to signal speaker sincerity, emphasize truthfulness of statements, or introduce frank/honest remarks. These are pragmatic markers that manage interpersonal communication rather than evaluate truth content per se. | 说真的 (shuō zhēn de), 真的吗？(zhēn de ma?), 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) [overlaps with Group 5], 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō) [overlaps with Group 5], 老实讲 (lǎoshí jiǎng), 坦白说 (tǎnbái shuō) [overlaps with Group 5] | 真 (zhēn) in discourse markers; 诚实 (chéngshí) and related terms from Group 5 also function as discourse markers; 说实话, 老实说, 坦白说 overlap with Group 5 | Typical contexts: Conversational discourse, informal speech, interpersonal communication. Function: Pragmatic marking rather than truth evaluation |
| GROUP 17: SPATIAL AND TEMPORAL PRECISION | Terms using the root 正 (zhèng) to indicate spatial centrality, temporal exactness, or geometric regularity. These terms extend from normative correctness to descriptive precision in space and time, and commonly appear in contexts requiring exact specification of position or time. | 正中 (zhèngzhōng), 正午 (zhèngwǔ), 正点 (zhèngdiǎn), 正面 (zhèngmiàn), 正门 (zhèngmén), 正方形 (zhèng fāngxíng) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Spatial description, time specification, geometric description. Key contrasts: Center vs. periphery; exact time vs. approximate time; front vs. back; main vs. secondary |
| GROUP 18: MATHEMATICAL AND TECHNICAL TERMINOLOGY | Terms using truth-domain roots (especially 正 and 真) in specialized mathematical, scientific, or technical contexts where they have acquired specific technical meanings. These terms represent extensions of truth-domain vocabulary into formal technical systems. | 正数 (zhèngshù), 正比 (zhèngbǐ), 正负 (zhèngfù), 真分数 (zhēn fēnshù), 真命题 (zhēn mìngtí), 真空 (zhēnkōng), 真菌 (zhēnjūn), 真核 (zhēnhé), 实数 (shíshù) | None specifically noted | Typical contexts: Mathematics, physics, biology, logic, formal sciences.
