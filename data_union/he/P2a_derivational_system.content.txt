# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Hebrew (he)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Where a P1 item is itself a derived form, derivations are listed from that item as base, not re-derived from the root. Forms already documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described. Gaps are preserved and noted explicitly. Derivations are listed per P1 item in order; no predefined category labels are imposed. Inflectional forms (conjugated verbs, plural nouns, construct forms) are excluded unless they have acquired independent lexical status. The distinction between derivation and inflection is applied conservatively; borderline cases are noted.

---

## P1 Item 1 — אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)

---

**1.1**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** *ʾamiti*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival suffix *-i*
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** The standard adjectival form of *ʾemet*, used attributively and predicatively across all registers to describe persons, feelings, objects, or states as genuinely what they appear to be. Already documented as P1 item 7; cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitiyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾamiti* (true, adjectival) + abstract noun suffix *-yut*
**Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, truthfulness, realness (as an abstract quality)
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from the adjective *ʾamiti*, denoting the quality of being genuine or real. Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something possesses true or authentic character. Also used in literary discourse when a fully abstract nominal form is required. Less frequent than *ʾemet* itself. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 19).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**1.3**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** לְאַמֵּת
**Transliteration:** *leʾammet*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to authenticate, to confirm
**Usage:** The standard modern Hebrew verb for verifying or authenticating a document, claim, or identity. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 4).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אִמּוּת
**Transliteration:** *immut*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el verbal noun
**Natural English translation:** verification, authentication, validation
**Usage:** The nominal form corresponding to *leʾammet*. Common in legal, administrative, and digital security contexts. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** מְאֻמָּת
**Transliteration:** *meʾummat*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pu'al passive participle
**Natural English translation:** verified, authenticated, confirmed
**Usage:** Adjectival form describing something that has been officially verified or authenticated. Common in legal and administrative Hebrew. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 7).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.6**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** לְאַמִּתוֹ
**Transliteration:** *le-ʾamito*
**Literal Breakdown:** *le-* ("to, in") + *ʾamit-* (construct form of root א-מ-ת) + *-o* (3ms possessive suffix); "to its truth"
**Natural English translation:** in truth, in actual fact, truly
**Usage:** Appears in the fixed phrase *le-ʾamito shel daʾvar* ("in truth, to tell the truth about the matter"), documented in P1b. The construct form *ʾamito* is not used independently outside this fixed phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.7**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitut*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival stem *ʾamit-* + abstract noun suffix *-ut*; "the quality of being true/genuine"
**Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, truthfulness (as a quality)
**Usage:** An abstract noun derived from the adjective *ʾamiti*, used in philosophical and evaluative discourse to refer to the property of being genuine or true. Less common than *ʾemet* itself; appears in academic and literary registers. Overlaps with *ʾamitiyyut*.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**1.8**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** בֶּאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** *be-ʾemet*
**Literal Breakdown:** *be-* (preposition "in/with") + *ʾemet*
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth, honestly
**Usage:** Extremely common adverbial phrase functioning as a sincerity marker or intensifier in everyday speech. Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.9**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֱמֶת הַמִּדָּה
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet ha-middah*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾemet* + *ha-middah* ("the measure/standard")
**Natural English translation:** the standard, the benchmark, the true measure
**Usage:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse to refer to the genuine standard or criterion by which something is judged. Common in journalistic and intellectual writing. Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 7).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.10**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֱמֶת לַאֲמִתָּהּ
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet le-ʾamittah*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾemet* + *le-* ("to/of") + *ʾamittah* (feminine possessive form, "its truth")
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter, the real truth, strictly speaking
**Usage:** A fixed phrase used in rabbinic and philosophical discourse to distinguish genuine or essential truth from a superficial or approximate version; also used in modern Hebrew to mean "strictly speaking." Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *ʾemet*:** The root א-מ-ת generates a productive verbal system via the Pi'el binyan (*leʾammet*, *immut*, *meʾummat*). The adjectival branch (*ʾamiti*, *ʾamitiyyut*) is also attested. The prepositional phrase *be-ʾemet* and the fixed phrase *ʾemet le-ʾamittah* have acquired independent adverbial and discourse-marker status beyond simple compositional use of the noun. No Qal active verb in common modern use has been identified with this root as a standalone form; the Qal infinitive *leʾemot* is marginal (documented in P1b). No causative or reflexive derivations beyond those listed have been identified as commonly used. No verbal derivation directly from *ʾemet* is attested in modern Hebrew; the verbal function is served by forms of the root א-מ-נ (*heʾemin*, etc.).

## P1 Item 2 — אֲמִתִּי (*ʾamiti*)

---

**2.1**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (*ʾamiti*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitiyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾamiti* (true, genuine) + abstract noun suffix *-yut*
**Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, truthfulness, realness (as an abstract quality)
**Usage:** Already documented under P1 item 1 (derivation 1.2) and in P1b. Cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.2**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (*ʾamiti*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּית
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitit*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival suffix *-it* (feminine form of *-i*)
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real (feminine agreement)
**Usage:** The feminine form of *ʾamiti*, used in grammatical agreement with feminine nouns, e.g., *ahavaʾh ʾamitit* ("genuine love"), *beʿayaʾh ʾamitit* ("a real problem"). Not a separate lexical item but a required inflectional form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.3**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (*ʾamiti*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּיִּים / אֲמִתִּיּוֹת
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitiyim* (m.pl.) / *ʾamitiyot* (f.pl.)
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival stem + plural suffixes *-im* (m.) / *-ot* (f.)
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real (plural agreement)
**Usage:** Plural forms of *ʾamiti* used in grammatical agreement with plural nouns, e.g., *reʿim ʾamitiyim* ("true friends"). Standard inflectional forms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *ʾamiti*:** *ʾamiti* is itself a derivation of *ʾemet* (P1 item 1). Its primary further derivation is *ʾamitiyyut*. No additional derivations from *ʾamiti* as a base have been identified beyond those already documented.

## P1 Item 3 — נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)

---

**3.1**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** נְכוֹנוּת
**Transliteration:** *nekhonot*
**Literal Breakdown:** *nakhon* (correct, right) + abstract noun suffix *-ut* (with vowel shift)
**Natural English translation:** correctness, accuracy, rightness; also: readiness, willingness
**Usage:** An abstract noun with two distinct senses: (a) the quality of being correct or accurate, e.g., *nekhonot ha-netunim* ("the accuracy of the data"); and (b) readiness or willingness, e.g., *nekhonot le-shituʾf peʿulaʾh* ("willingness to cooperate"). Used in formal and written Hebrew to describe the correctness of a statement, procedure, or assessment. Less common in everyday speech than *nakhon* itself. Context disambiguates. Both senses are common. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 2).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.2**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** לֹא נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** *lo nakhon*
**Literal Breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *nakhon* (correct)
**Natural English translation:** incorrect, wrong, not right
**Usage:** The standard negated form used in everyday Hebrew to indicate that something is incorrect or false. Extremely common as a response to an inaccurate statement. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.3**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** לְהַכִּין
**Transliteration:** *lehakhin*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ, Hif'il infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to prepare, to set up, to establish
**Usage:** A verbal form from the same root כ-ו-נ, meaning to prepare or establish something. Noted here because it shares the root with *nakhon* but represents a distinct semantic branch of the root (preparation/establishment rather than correctness). Included to document the root's verbal productivity while flagging the semantic divergence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.4**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** נְכוֹנָה
**Transliteration:** *nekhonah*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ + feminine adjectival suffix *-ah*
**Natural English translation:** correct, right, accurate (feminine agreement)
**Usage:** The feminine form of *nakhon*, used in grammatical agreement with feminine nouns, e.g., *teshuvah nekhonah* ("a correct answer"). Standard inflectional form; also used predicatively, e.g., *ze lo nekhonah* ("that is not right").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.5**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** נְכוֹנִים / נְכוֹנוֹת
**Transliteration:** *nekhonim* (m.pl.) / *nekhanot* (f.pl.)
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ + plural suffixes
**Natural English translation:** correct, right (plural agreement)
**Usage:** Plural forms of *nakhon* used in grammatical agreement, e.g., *teshuvot nekhanot* ("correct answers"). Standard inflectional forms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.6**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** לְנַכֵּן
**Transliteration:** *lenakhen*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ; Pi'el infinitive, "to correct, to adjust"
**Natural English translation:** to correct, to adjust, to calibrate
**Usage:** Used in technical and everyday contexts to mean correcting or adjusting something to the proper standard, e.g., *lenakhen sheʾvaʾ* ("to correct an error"), *lenakhen maʿgal* ("to calibrate a device"). Less common than the noun forms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.7**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** כִּיווּן
**Transliteration:** *kivun*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ; Pi'el verbal noun, "direction, aiming, orientation"
**Natural English translation:** direction, orientation, aiming
**Usage:** A highly productive derivation from the same root כ-ו-נ, used in everyday, technical, and military contexts for direction or orientation, e.g., *be-eyzeh kivun?* ("in which direction?"). The connection to *nakhon* is through the shared root rather than direct derivation from the adjective.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.8**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** הֲכָנָה
**Transliteration:** *hakhanah*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ; Hif'il verbal noun, "preparation, making ready"
**Natural English translation:** preparation, readiness
**Usage:** The standard word for preparation or getting ready, e.g., *hakhanah le-baḥinah* ("preparation for an exam"). Shares the root כ-ו-נ with *nakhon* through the sense of "being set, established, ready." A productive and common form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.9**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** נָכוֹן לְהַיּוֹם
**Transliteration:** *nakhon le-hayom*
**Literal Breakdown:** *nakhon* + *le-* + *hayom* ("today")
**Natural English translation:** as of today, correct as of the present date
**Usage:** A fixed administrative and journalistic formula specifying that information is accurate as of the current date. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *nakhon*:** *nakhon* is a Nif'al passive participle from root כ-ו-נ. Its most direct derivational product is the abstract noun *nekhonot*. The root כ-ו-נ is highly productive but generates forms (such as *lehakhin*, *kavvana*, *tikun*) that diverge significantly in meaning from the correctness/accuracy sense of *nakhon*. The broader root כ-ו-נ generates many additional forms documented in P1b; those are not re-listed here as derivations of *nakhon* specifically, since they derive from the root rather than from the *nakhon* form itself.

## P1 Item 4 — מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)

---

**4.1**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** דִּיּוּק
**Transliteration:** *diyyuq*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק, Pi'el verbal noun
**Natural English translation:** precision, accuracy, exactness; punctiliousness
**Usage:** The abstract noun denoting the quality of being precise or exact. Already documented as P1 item 25; cross-referenced here. Used in scientific, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate the degree of accuracy in a statement, measurement, or action. Also used in rabbinic contexts to denote careful textual analysis or punctilious observance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.2**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** בְּדִיּוּק
**Transliteration:** *bediyyuq*
**Literal Breakdown:** *be-* (in, with) + *diyyuq* (precision)
**Natural English translation:** exactly, precisely, accurately
**Usage:** An extremely common adverbial phrase used in everyday Hebrew to mean exactly or precisely. Functions as an intensifier confirming exact correspondence, e.g., *ze bediyyuq ma she-ratsiti* ("that's exactly what I wanted"), *ze be-diyyuq maʾh she-ʾamarti* ("that is exactly what I said"). One of the most frequent adverbs in modern spoken Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.3**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** לְדַיֵּק
**Transliteration:** *ledayyeq*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to be precise, to specify exactly, to examine closely, to be exact
**Usage:** The standard verb meaning to be precise or to specify something exactly. Used in everyday and formal Hebrew when asking for or providing exact information. Common in the phrase *ledayyeq be-* (to be precise about, to examine closely), e.g., *tsarikh ledayeq ba-nisuaḥ* ("one must be precise in the wording"), *ledayeq be-ʿuvdot* ("to be exact about the facts"). Common in academic, journalistic, and legal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.4**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** מְדַיֵּק
**Transliteration:** *medayyeq*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק, Pi'el active participle
**Natural English translation:** precise person, one who is exact; being precise
**Usage:** Used as an adjective or nominal to describe a person who is characteristically precise or meticulous, or to describe the act of being precise in a given moment, e.g., *hu medayeq ba-peratim* ("he is precise about the details"). Common in everyday and professional discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.5**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** אִי-דִּיּוּק
**Transliteration:** *ʾi-diyyuq*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾi-* (negating prefix) + *diyyuq* (precision)
**Natural English translation:** imprecision, inaccuracy, lack of exactness
**Usage:** Used in formal and written Hebrew to describe the absence of precision in a statement, measurement, or representation. Common in academic, legal, and journalistic criticism of inaccurate claims. Also used in scientific and journalistic contexts to evaluate a statement, measurement, or description as lacking the required degree of precision.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.6**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** דַּיְקָן
**Transliteration:** *dayqan*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק + agentive suffix *-an*
**Natural English translation:** a precise person, a stickler for accuracy, a punctilious person
**Usage:** Denotes a person who is characteristically precise, meticulous, or punctilious. Can be used approvingly (a careful, accurate person) or with mild irony (an overly pedantic person). Used in everyday and professional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.7**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** דַּיְקָנוּת
**Transliteration:** *dayqanut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *dayqan* + abstract suffix *-ut*
**Natural English translation:** precision, meticulousness, punctiliousness
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being precise or meticulous. Used in everyday and professional contexts to evaluate a person's or institution's degree of care and exactness. Can carry both positive and mildly negative (overly pedantic) connotations depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *meduyaq*:** *meduyaq* is itself a Pi'el passive participle (or Pu'al form) from root ד-י-ק. The root generates a productive Pi'el verbal system (*ledayyeq*, *diyyuq*, *medayyeq*), the agentive *dayqan* and its abstract *dayqanut*, the adverbial *bediyyuq*, and the negated nominal *ʾi-diyyuq*. No Qal or Hif'il forms of this root in the precision/accuracy sense have been identified as commonly used.

## P1 Item 5 — אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)

---

**5.1**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)
**Derived Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾotentiyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾotenti* (authentic) + abstract noun suffix *-yut*
**Natural English translation:** authenticity
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for authenticity in modern Hebrew. Used in philosophical, aesthetic, cultural, psychological, and commercial discourse to describe the property of being genuine or authentic, e.g., *ʾotentiyut ha-ʾiʃiyut* ("the authenticity of the personality"), *ʾotentiyut tarbutil* ("cultural authenticity"). Common in discussions of art, identity, and cultural heritage. More common in educated registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.2**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)
**Derived Form:** לֹא אוֹתֶנְטִי
**Transliteration:** *lo ʾotenti*
**Literal Breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *ʾotenti* (authentic)
**Natural English translation:** inauthentic, not genuine
**Usage:** The negated form used to describe something evaluated as not genuinely what it claims to be. Used in cultural, aesthetic, and commercial criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.3**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)
**Derived Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִית
**Transliteration:** *ʾotentit*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾotenti-* + feminine suffix *-t*
**Natural English translation:** authentic, genuine (feminine agreement)
**Usage:** The feminine form of *ʾotenti*, used in grammatical agreement with feminine nouns, e.g., *ḥavayaʾh ʾotentit* ("an authentic experience"). Standard inflectional form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *ʾotenti*:** As a loanword, *ʾotenti* has limited morphological integration into the Hebrew derivational system. Its primary attested derivation is the abstract noun *ʾotentiyyut* via the productive *-yut* suffix. No verbal form derived from this loanword has been identified as commonly used in Hebrew. The adverbial function is typically served by the phrase *be-tsura ʾotentit* ("in an authentic manner") rather than a dedicated adverbial derivation.

## P1 Item 6 — מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)

---

**6.1**
**Base Form:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Derived Form:** מְקוֹרִיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *meqoriyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *meqori* (original) + abstract noun suffix *-yut*
**Natural English translation:** originality, genuineness, authenticity
**Usage:** The abstract noun denoting the quality of being original or genuinely creative. Used in aesthetic, legal, intellectual property, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something is a genuine original rather than a copy or imitation, e.g., *meqoriyut ha-yetsirah* ("the originality of the work"), *beḥinat ha-meqoriyut* ("assessment of originality"). Common in evaluations of artistic or intellectual work.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.2**
**Base Form:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Derived Form:** חֹסֶר מְקוֹרִיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ḥoser meqoriyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ḥoser* (lack of) + *meqoriyyut* (originality)
**Natural English translation:** lack of originality, unoriginality
**Usage:** A fixed phrase used in aesthetic and intellectual criticism to describe work that is derivative or unoriginal. Common in literary, artistic, and academic evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.3**
**Base Form:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Derived Form:** מָקוֹר
**Transliteration:** *meqor*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ק-ו-ר; noun, "source, origin"
**Natural English translation:** source, origin
**Usage:** The base noun from which *meqori* is derived, meaning "source" or "origin." Used in journalistic contexts (*meqor ha-yadaʿot*, "the source of the information"), textual scholarship (*ha-meqor ha-ʿivri*, "the Hebrew original"), and everyday speech. Included here to document the derivational direction: *meqor* → *meqori* → *meqoriyyut*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.4**
**Base Form:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Derived Form:** בַּמָּקוֹר
**Transliteration:** *ba-meqor*
**Literal Breakdown:** *be-* ("in") + *ha-meqor* ("the source/original"); "in the original"
**Natural English translation:** in the original, in the source text
**Usage:** A fixed prepositional phrase used in translation, textual scholarship, and legal contexts to refer to the original-language version of a text, e.g., *ba-meqor ha-ʿivri* ("in the Hebrew original"), *ba-meqor ha-yevanit* ("in the Greek original").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *meqori*:** *meqori* is an adjectival form derived from the noun *maqor* (source, origin). Its primary derivational product is the abstract noun *meqoriyyut*. No verbal form derived from *meqori* as a base has been identified as commonly used. The root ק-ו-ר generates a broader family, but most members are not directly relevant to the truth-evaluation domain.

## P1 Item 7 — אֲמִתִּי (*ʾamiti*) [cross-reference]

---
