## OPPOSITION 1

**Term A:** egia  
**Term B:** gezur / gezurra  
**Transliteration:** egia vs. gezur/gezurra  
**Translation:** truth vs. lie/falsehood  
**Description:** Primary lexical opposition between truth and lying/falsehood. These terms function as direct semantic opposites across all contexts, from speech acts to abstract philosophical discourse.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

**Total oppositions identified:** 40 entries (including uncertain/asymmetric cases)

**Clear core oppositions:** 28  
**Uncertain or asymmetric cases:** 12

**Distribution by contrast type:**
- Lexical oppositions: 12 core + 4 uncertain
- Morphological oppositions: 3 core
- Constructional oppositions: 13 core + 1 uncertain
- No clear opposition identified: 7 cases

**Key patterns observed:**

1. **Strong lexical oppositions:** egia/gezur, zuzen/oker, benetako/itxurazko, ziur/zalantza
2. **Productive constructional oppositions:** Parallel modification patterns (egiazko X vs. gezurrezko X), light verb constructions with contrasting verbs, negation constructions
3. **Significant asymmetries:** The "gezur" root shows limited derivational productivity compared to "egia" (no *gezurtu, *gezurtasuna, *gezurki attested)
4. **Morphological negation:** Limited but productive with specific suffixes (-garri/-ezin, -garri/-gaitz)
5. **Gaps:** No dedicated opposites for fidagarria, zintzoa, zuzenean, iruzurra, faltsua in the documented data

**Notable asymmetries:**
- "egia" supports extensive derivation; "gezur" does not
- Some abstract nominals have no documented opposite (*gezurtasuna, *zintzotasunaren kontra)
- Verification verbs exist (egiazkotu) but no falsification verb from "gezur" (*gezurtu)
- Manner adverbs show gaps (*gezurki not attested)

These asymmetries reflect actual patterns in Basque usage rather than analytical gaps.
