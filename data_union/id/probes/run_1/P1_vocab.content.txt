# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** true, correct
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate

Used to affirm that a statement, fact, or claim corresponds to reality or is without error. Also used in moral and evaluative contexts to mean "right" or "proper." Participates in derivation: *kebenaran* (truth, correctness), *membenarkan* (to justify, to confirm), *sebenarnya* (actually, in truth).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** salah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** wrong, mistaken
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false

The primary antonym of *benar*, used to evaluate statements, actions, or attributions as incorrect or erroneous. Appears in moral contexts (wrongdoing) and factual contexts (incorrect information). Productive in derivation: *kesalahan* (error, fault), *menyalahkan* (to blame).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** asli
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** original, native, genuine
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, original, real

Applied to objects, documents, persons, and cultural items to indicate they are not copies, forgeries, or imitations. Commonly used in legal, commercial, and identity contexts. Contrasts with *palsu* (fake) and *tiruan* (imitation).

**[CORE]**

**4.**
**Form:** palsu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** false, fake (from Portuguese *falso*)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged

Used to evaluate objects, documents, currency, or claims as not genuine or deliberately deceptive. Appears in legal contexts (pemalsuan — forgery, falsification) and everyday evaluation of inauthenticity. Productive: *memalsukan* (to falsify, to forge), *pemalsuan* (falsification).

**[CORE]**

**5.**
**Form:** nyata
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** real, manifest, evident
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, evident, concrete

Indicates that something is genuinely present, perceptible, or factually existing rather than imagined or supposed. Used in both philosophical and everyday registers. Related forms: *kenyataan* (reality, fact), *menyatakan* (to state, to declare).

**[CORE]**

**6.**
**Form:** sah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid, lawful (from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*)
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, lawful, official

Primarily used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to indicate that an act, document, or status meets required conditions and is recognized as binding or proper. Productive: *mengesahkan* (to validate, to ratify), *keabsahan* (validity, legitimacy).

**[CORE]**

**7.**
**Form:** absah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid, legitimate (from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*, variant of *sah*)
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, lawful

A near-synonym of *sah*, used in formal and legal registers to evaluate whether something meets the conditions for recognition or legitimacy. Slightly more formal in distribution than *sah*. Related: *keabsahan* (validity).

**Form:** tepat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** precise, exact, fitting
**Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate, appropriate

Evaluates whether something corresponds exactly to a standard, target, or requirement — used for measurements, timing, word choice, and judgments. Productive: *ketepatan* (precision, accuracy), *menepati* (to fulfill, to keep to).

**9.**

**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** accurate (from Dutch *accuraat*)
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise

Used in technical, journalistic, and scientific contexts to evaluate data, reports, or measurements as free from error. More domain-specific than *tepat* and less common in everyday speech. Related: *keakuratan* (accuracy).

**10.**
**Form:** jujur
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** honest, straightforward
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere

Evaluates a person's conduct or statements as free from deception or concealment. Used in moral, interpersonal, and institutional contexts. Productive: *kejujuran* (honesty), *ketidakjujuran* (dishonesty).

**11.**
**Form:** bohong
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** lie, untruth
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, untrue

Used to evaluate a statement or person as deliberately untruthful. Functions as both noun (a lie) and predicate adjective (it is false/a lie). Related: *pembohong* (liar), *membohongi* (to deceive, to lie to someone).

**12.**
**Form:** dusta
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

A near-synonym of *bohong*, somewhat more formal or literary in register. Used in moral and religious discourse to evaluate statements or conduct as deliberately deceptive. Related: *mendustakan* (to deny, to call a liar), *pendusta* (liar).

**14.**
**Form:** tiruan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** imitation, copy (from *tiru* — to imitate + *-an* nominalizer)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, copy, replica, fake

Evaluates an object as a reproduction or copy rather than an original. Used in commercial, artistic, and legal contexts. Less strongly negative than *palsu* (which implies deliberate deception); *tiruan* may be acknowledged openly. Base: *meniru* (to imitate, to copy).

**15.**
**Form:** otentik
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** authentic (from Dutch *authentiek*)
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine

Used in formal, academic, legal, and journalistic registers to evaluate documents, artworks, or statements as genuine and not fabricated. Less common in everyday speech than *asli*. Related: *keaslian* (authenticity) is more commonly used than *keotentikan*.

**16.**
**Form:** valid
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid (from English/Dutch *valid*)
**Typical English translations:** valid

Used in academic, scientific, and administrative contexts to evaluate instruments, arguments, or procedures as meeting required standards. More restricted in distribution than *sah*; primarily found in technical and formal writing. Related: *validitas* (validity), *memvalidasi* (to validate).

**17.**
**Form:** sahih
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** sound, authentic, correct (from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*)
**Typical English translations:** sound, authentic, correct, valid

Used primarily in Islamic religious and scholarly discourse to evaluate hadith (prophetic traditions) or religious rulings as meeting criteria of authenticity and reliability. Also appears in broader formal contexts meaning "correct" or "sound." Related: *kesahihan* (soundness, authenticity).

**18.**
**Form:** keliru
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, in error
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, erroneous, wrong

Evaluates a belief, statement, or action as based on error or misunderstanding, often without implying deliberate deception. Slightly more formal than *salah* in some registers. Related: *kekeliruan* (error, mistake), *mengelirukan* (to mislead, to confuse).

**Form:** sesuai

**Typical English translations:** in accordance with, appropriate, conforming, matching

Evaluates whether something corresponds to, matches, or conforms to a standard, rule, or expectation. Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts. Productive: *kesesuaian* (conformity, correspondence), *menyesuaikan* (to adjust, to conform).

**20.**

**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** natural, reasonable, proper
**Typical English translations:** reasonable, natural, proper, fair

Evaluates conduct, outcomes, or claims as falling within normal, expected, or acceptable bounds. Used in moral, social, and evaluative contexts. Related: *kewajaran* (reasonableness, normality), *tidak wajar* (unreasonable, improper).

**21.**
**Form:** pantas
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** fitting, deserved, appropriate
**Typical English translations:** fitting, appropriate, deserved, proper

Evaluates whether something is suitable, becoming, or merited in a given context — applied to conduct, appearance, or outcomes. Overlaps with *wajar* but carries a stronger sense of social propriety or desert. Related: *kepantasan* (propriety, fittingness).

**Typical English translations:** just, fair, equitable

Evaluates treatment, decisions, or distributions as conforming to principles of fairness or justice. Central in legal, moral, and political discourse. Productive: *keadilan* (justice, fairness), *mengadili* (to judge, to try in court), *ketidakadilan* (injustice).

**23.**
**Form:** terbukti

**Literal meaning:** proven, demonstrated (passive of *membuktikan* — to prove; root *bukti* — evidence)
**Typical English translations:** proven, demonstrated, established

Evaluates a claim or fact as having been established through evidence. Widely used in legal, journalistic, and scientific contexts. Base noun *bukti* (evidence, proof) is independently productive.

Evaluates a person, source, or institution as worthy of trust or reliance. Used in journalistic, institutional, and interpersonal contexts. Related: *kepercayaan* (trust, belief), *tidak terpercaya* (untrustworthy).

**Literal meaning:** resembling, similar
**Typical English translations:** resembling, similar to, like

Evaluates the degree of resemblance between two entities — used in contexts of identification, comparison, and assessment of likeness. Relevant in legal (identification of persons) and aesthetic contexts. Related: *kemiripan* (resemblance, similarity).

**27.**
**Form:** serupa
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** of the same form/kind (from *se-* — same + *rupa* — form, appearance)
**Typical English translations:** similar, alike, resembling

Near-synonym of *mirip*, evaluating resemblance or likeness between entities. Slightly more formal in register. *Rupa* (form, appearance) is independently productive in compounds and derivations.

**28.**
**Form:** hakikat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** essence, fundamental nature (from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*)
**Typical English translations:** essence, true nature, fundamental reality

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance. Appears in evaluative contexts where the question is whether something's true nature has been correctly identified. Related: *pada hakikatnya* (in essence, fundamentally).

**[CORE]**

**29.**
**Form:** esensi
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** essence (from Dutch *essentie*)
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature

Near-synonym of *hakikat* in philosophical and formal registers, used to refer to the fundamental nature of something. More common in academic and secular discourse; *hakikat* is more common in religious and traditional contexts. Related: *esensial* (essential).

**30.**
**Form:** sejati
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine, real (from *se-* — intensifier + *jati* — true, genuine; *jati* also means teak wood, connoting hardness/solidity)
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic

Evaluates persons, qualities, or states as genuinely and fully what they are claimed or appear to be — often with a sense of depth or completeness. Used in moral, philosophical, and literary contexts: *teman sejati* (true friend), *cinta sejati* (true love). Less productive derivationally than *benar* or *asli*.
