# G4 — Cross-Language Derivational and Morphological Strategies in the Truth Domain

---

## 1. Identification of Main Strategies

Working from the documented forms in F2a, F2b, F1, and the G1–G3 analyses, the following recurring word-formation strategies are identifiable across the eight languages. These are described in terms of what the data shows, not imposed from a typological template.

---

## 2. Cross-Language Comparison Table

| Strategy Type | Description | Languages Using It | Concrete Examples | Distribution / Notes |

| **Abstract quality nominalization from adjective/noun base** | A suffix or circumfix derives an abstract noun denoting the quality or property of being true, correct, honest, etc. The resulting noun is used in formal, philosophical, and analytical discourse. | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: სიმართლე (si-mart'l-e) "truth"; -ობა: სანდოობა (sando-oba) "reliability"; yo: ì-gbàgbọ́ "belief," ì-bámu "correspondence"; fi: totuus (tosi + -uus) "truth," rehellisyys (rehellinen + -yys) "honesty"; eu: zuzentasuna (zuzen + -tasun + -a) "correctness/justice," egiazkotasuna (egiazko + -tasun + -a) "truthfulness"; ta: uṇmaittaṉmai (uṇmai + taṉmai) "truthfulness," nērmai (nēr + mai) "honesty"; id: kebenaran (ke- + benar + -an) "truth/correctness," kejujuran (ke- + jujur + -an) "honesty"; tr: doğruluk (doğru + -luk) "truth/correctness," dürüstlük (dürüst + -lük) "honesty"; ko: 정확성 (jeonghwak + -seong) "accuracy," 신빙성 (sinbing + -seong) "credibility" | **Widely attested** (all 8 languages). The specific suffixes differ typologically but the functional pattern is consistent: a dedicated suffix or circumfix creates an abstract noun from a truth-domain adjective or noun base. In ka, two competing patterns exist (si-...-e vs. -oba). In yo, the ì- prefix is the primary nominalizer. In fi, -uus/-yys is the standard pattern. In eu, -tasun is highly productive. In ta, -taṉmai is the formal pattern; classical -mai also attested. In id, ke-...-an is the standard circumfix. In tr, -luk/-lük is universal. In ko, -성 (-seong) is productive with Sino-Korean bases. |
| **Adverbial derivation from truth-domain adjective** | A suffix or case ending derives an adverb of manner or emphasis from a truth-domain adjective. The resulting form is used to modify verbs or entire clauses. | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: სწორად (sts'or + -ad) "correctly," ნამდვილად (namdvil + -ad) "genuinely"; yo: nitootọ́ (ní + tòótọ́) "truly," tọ́ títí "completely correctly"; fi: rehellisesti (rehellise + -sti) "honestly," todennäköisesti (todennäköise + -sti) "probably"; eu: zehazki (zehatz + -ki) "precisely," zintzoki (zintzo + -ki) "honestly," egiazki (egiaz + -ki) "truly"; ta: uṇmaiyāka (uṇmai + āka) "truly/truthfully," cariyāka (cari + āka) "correctly"; id: sebenarnya (se- + benar + -nya) "actually/in truth," benar-benar "really/truly"; tr: gerçekten (gerçek + -ten) "really/truly," kesinlikle (kesinlik + -le) "certainly/definitely"; ko: 정확히 (jeonghwak + -hi) "accurately," 솔직히 (soljik + -hi) "frankly" | **Widely attested** (all 8 languages). In ka, the -ad suffix is the standard adverbial derivation. In yo, adverbials are formed through prepositional phrases (ní + noun) or reduplication. In fi, -sti is the productive adverbial suffix. In eu, -ki is the standard adverbial suffix. In ta, -āka is the productive adverbial suffix. In id, -nya creates adverbial forms from se- + base constructions; reduplication also functions adverbially. In tr, grammaticalized case forms (-ten ablative, -le instrumental) function as adverbs. In ko, -히 (-hi) and -하게 (-hage) are both productive. Several of these forms have grammaticalized into discourse markers (see Strategy 13 below). |
| **Negative prefix creating antonym** | A negative prefix attaches to a truth-domain base to create a systematic antonym. The resulting form denotes absence or negation of the base quality. | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: უ- (u-): უტყუარი (u-t'q'uar-i) "infallible," უსამართლობა (u-samart'l-oba) "injustice"; არა- (ara-): არაადეკვატური (ara-adek'vat'uri) "inadequate"; yo: àì-: àìtọ́ "incorrectness," àìbámu "non-correspondence," àìmọ̀ "ignorance"; fi: epä-: epätosi "untrue," epärehellinen "dishonest," epätodennäköinen "improbable"; eu: (negation expressed through ez + verb rather than prefix; some forms with -ezin: frogaezina "unprovable" [implied]); ta: -aṟṟa: uṇmaiyaṟṟa "without truth/untrue," nērmaiyaṟṟa "dishonest"; id: tidak + adjective (analytic negation, not prefixal); tr: -sız/-siz: haksız "unjust," şüphesiz "doubtless," hatasız "flawless"; ko: 부- (bu-): 부정직 (bu-jeongjik) "dishonesty," 부정확 (bu-jeonghwak) "inaccuracy"; 불- (bul-): 불확실 (bul-hwaksil) "uncertainty" | **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages show prefixal or suffixal negation; id uses analytic negation). The specific form varies: ka uses prefix u- or ara-; yo uses prefix àì-; fi uses prefix epä-; ta uses suffix -aṟṟa (privative); tr uses suffix -sız/-siz (privative); ko uses Sino-Korean prefixes 부- and 불-. eu uses analytic negation (ez) rather than a dedicated negative prefix for most truth-domain terms, though -gaitz and -ezin appear in some implied forms. id uses the analytic negator tidak rather than a prefix. The privative suffix pattern (tr -sız, ta -aṟṟa) and the negative prefix pattern (ka u-, yo àì-, fi epä-, ko 부-/불-) are functionally parallel but formally distinct. |
| **Agentive nominalization: "liar" type** | A suffix or prefix derives a noun denoting a person characterized by lying or deception. This form is consistently lexicalized across languages. | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: მა-...-არა: მატყუარა (ma-t'q'u-ara) "liar"; yo: a-: apurọ́ (a-purọ́) "liar"; fi: -ja/-jä: valehtelija (valehtele + -ja) "liar"; eu: -lari: gezurlari "liar"; ta: -aṉ/-āḷ/-ar: poyyan (poy + aṉ) "liar (m.)," poyyāḷ (poy + āḷ) "liar," poyyar (poy + ar) "liars"; id: peN-: pembohong (peN- + bohong) "liar"; tr: -cı/-ci: yalancı (yalan + -cı) "liar"; ko: -쟁이 (-jjaengi): 거짓말쟁이 (geojinmal + -jjaengi) "liar" | **Widely attested** (all 8 languages). Every language has a dedicated agentive form for "liar." The morphological strategy varies: ka uses a circumfix (ma-...-ara); yo uses a prefix (a-); fi uses an agent suffix (-ja); eu uses a suffix (-lari); ta uses person-marking suffixes (-aṉ, -āḷ, -ar); id uses a prefix (peN-); tr uses an agent suffix (-cı); ko uses a colloquial pejorative suffix (-쟁이). This is one of the most consistent cross-language patterns in the dataset. |
| **Agentive nominalization: "truth-teller" type — absent or periphrastic** | A parallel agentive form for "truth-teller" or "honest speaker" is either absent or requires a periphrastic construction rather than a morphological derivation. | ka, yo, fi, ta, id, ko (documented); eu, tr (implied) | ka: no *სიმართლის მთქმელი as a productive form; მართლმოსაუბრე (mart'l-mosaubre) "truth-teller" is [MARGINAL]; yo: no *atọ́; requires ọmọ òtítọ́ (child of truth) or ẹni òtítọ́ (person of truth); fi: no *totuudella; requires periphrastic expression; ta: no *uṇmaiyaṉ; requires uṇmaiyai colpavar "person who speaks truth"; id: no productive agentive for "truth-teller"; ko: no *진실쟁이; requires 진실을 말하는 사람 (person who speaks truth) | **Widely attested asymmetry** (documented in 6 languages, implied in 2). This is one of the most consistent cross-language asymmetries in the dataset. The "liar" agentive is lexicalized everywhere; the "truth-teller" agentive is either absent or marginal. Where a "truth-teller" form exists, it tends to be a compound or phrase (yo ọmọ òtítọ́, ka მართლმოსაუბრე) rather than a simple morphological derivation. This asymmetry is not explained by morphological constraints alone, since the same suffixes that create "liar" forms could in principle create "truth-teller" forms. |
| **Causative/factitive verb derivation from truth-domain base** | A causative or factitive suffix derives a transitive verb meaning "to make [true/correct/valid]" or "to verify/confirm" from a truth-domain base. | ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko (productive); yo (marginal/reinterpreted) | ka: გა-...-ება: გაასწორა (ga-a-sts'or-a) "corrected," გააყალბა (ga-a-q'alb-a) "falsified"; fi: -staa/-stää: vääristää (vääriste + -ä) "to distort/falsify," todentaa (toden + -taa) "to verify"; eu: -zta- + -tu: egiaztatu (egia + -zta- + -tu) "to verify," ziurtatu (ziur + -ta- + -tu) "to ensure/verify"; ta: -paṭuttu: teḷivupaṭuttu (teḷivu + paṭuttu) "to clarify," uṟutipaṭuttu (uṟuti + paṭuttu) "to confirm"; id: meN-...-kan: membenarkan (meN- + benar + -kan) "to confirm/justify," memalsukan (meN- + palsu + -kan) "to falsify"; tr: -la-: doğrulamak (doğru + -la + -mak) "to verify," yalanlamak (yalan + -la + -mak) "to deny/refute"; ko: (no productive morphological causative; periphrastic -게 하다 used); yo: mú kí construction is pragmatically odd for truth states; reinterpreted as verification | **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages show productive causative derivation; yo and ko show restrictions). In ka, preverb + verb stem constructions function causatively. In fi, -staa/-stää and -taa/-tää are productive causative suffixes. In eu, the -zta- infix creates verification verbs. In ta, the -paṭuttu construction is productive. In id, meN-...-kan is the standard causative circumfix. In tr, -la- is a productive denominative verbalizer. In ko, morphological causatives for truth predicates are absent; the language uses periphrastic constructions (-게 하다). In yo, causing truth states is pragmatically constrained (one cannot "make" truth); the construction is reinterpreted as verification or enabling. |
| **Inchoative/change-of-state verb derivation** | A suffix or construction derives a verb meaning "to become [true/correct/real]" or "to come true/be realized" from a truth-domain base. | fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko (productive); ka (productive); yo (marginal/reinterpreted) | fi: -tua/-tyä: todellistua (todellistu + -a) "to come true/be realized," todentua (todentu + -a) "to be verified"; eu: -tu (intransitive): egiaztatu (intransitive use) "to be verified," okertu (oker + -tu) "to go wrong"; ta: -āku: uṇmai āku "to become true," poy ampalamāku "to be exposed as a lie"; id: menjadi + adjective: menjadi benar "to become true," menjadi sah "to become valid"; tr: -leş-: gerçekleşmek (gerçek + -leş + -mek) "to come true/be realized," kesinleşmek (kesin + -leş + -mek) "to become definite"; ko: -아/어지다 (-a/eojida): 정직해지다 (jeongjik-haejida) "to become honest," 확실해지다 (hwaksil-haejida) "to become certain"; ka: -გახდა (gakhda) construction: მართალი გახდა (mart'ali gakhda) "turned out to be true" | **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages show some inchoative pattern; yo is the most restricted). In fi, the -tua/-tyä reflexive/inchoative suffix is productive. In eu, -tu can function intransitively. In ta, -āku is the standard inchoative. In id, menjadi + adjective is the analytic inchoative. In tr, -leş- is a highly productive inchoative suffix. In ko, -아/어지다 is the standard inchoative for adjectives. In ka, the verb გახდა (gakhda) "became" functions as an inchoative auxiliary. In yo, inchoative constructions for propositional truth are marginal or reinterpreted as discovery/revelation; truth is conceptualized as static rather than emergent. A notable gap across languages: "becoming false/untrue" is less productive than "becoming true" in fi, yo, and ko. |
| **Inchoative/causative pair (intransitive/transitive)** | A language shows a productive morphological pair where the inchoative (intransitive, "become X") and the causative (transitive, "make X") are formally related through a shared base and contrasting suffixes. | fi, eu, tr | fi: todellistua (inchoative) / todellistaa (causative) "come true / realize"; toteutua / toteuttaa "be realized / implement"; todentua / todentaa "be verified / verify"; eu: egiaztatu (intransitive/passive) / egiaztatu (transitive) — same form, different argument structure; okertu (intransitive) / (transitive use marginal); tr: gerçekleşmek (inchoative, -leş-) / gerçekleştirmek (causative, -leştir-) "come true / realize"; kesinleşmek / kesinleştirmek "become definite / finalize"; meşrulaşmak / meşrulaştırmak "become legitimate / legitimize" | **Moderately attested** (clearly productive in fi and tr; present but less systematic in eu; analytic in ka, ta, id, ko). In fi, the -tua/-tyä (inchoative) and -taa/-tää (causative) pair is highly productive across truth-domain bases. In tr, the -leş- (inchoative) and -leştir- (causative) pair is equally productive. In eu, the same -tu suffix can function both transitively and intransitively depending on argument structure. In ka, id, ta, and ko, the inchoative/causative distinction is expressed analytically rather than through a morphological pair. |
| **Pejorative/collective nominalization of deception** | A suffix derives a noun denoting habitual, systematic, or collectively condemned deception, carrying stronger moral condemnation than the base form. | ka, eu, ta, id (partially) | ka: -კერია (-keria): ლიცემერება (lits'emer-eba) "hypocrisy" — note: -eba here; eu: -keria: gezurkeria (gezur + -keria) "habitual lying/deceit," faltsukeria (faltsu + -keria) "falseness/hypocrisy," iruzurkeria (iruzur + -keria) "fraudulence"; ta: -mai: poymai (poy + mai) "falsehood" (classical/literary); id: ke-...-an on deception bases: kebohongan (ke- + bohong + -an) "lie/falsehood" | **Moderately attested** (clearly productive in eu with -keria; present in ka and ta with different suffixes; id uses the standard nominalization circumfix). In eu, the -keria suffix specifically marks habitual or systematically condemned negative behavior and is restricted to deception/moral failing bases (gezurkeria, faltsukeria, iruzurkeria). This suffix is not used for positive qualities. In ta, the classical -mai suffix creates literary abstract nouns (poymai "falsehood," meymai "truth") that are register-restricted. In ka, -ება (-eba) creates verbal nouns from deception verbs. The asymmetry between positive and negative nominalization strategies (neutral -tasun in eu for positive qualities vs. pejorative -keria for negative ones) is notable. |
| **Evaluative adjective suffix (worthy of / able to be)** | A suffix derives an adjective meaning "worthy of [trust/belief/proof]" or "able to be [verified/believed]" from a noun or verb base. | eu, yo, ta (partially) | eu: -garri: fidagarria (fida + -garri + -a) "reliable/trustworthy," sinesgarria (sines + -garri + -a) "believable/credible," zalantzagarria (zalantza + -garri + -a) "doubtful," frogagarria (froga + -garri + -a) "provable/demonstrable"; yo: -ble equivalent expressed through ṣe é ṣe construction (analytic); ta: -garri equivalent expressed through -āṉa + modal constructions (analytic) | **Moderately attested** (clearly productive as a morphological suffix only in eu; functionally present but expressed analytically in yo and ta). In eu, the -garri suffix is highly productive and creates a distinct class of evaluative adjectives in the truth domain. The suffix attaches to both native Basque bases (zalantza "doubt" → zalantzagarria "doubtful") and borrowed bases (fida "trust" → fidagarria "reliable"). No other language in the dataset shows a comparably productive single suffix for this semantic function. In yo and ta, the equivalent meaning is expressed through analytic constructions. |
| **Reduplication for intensification or emphasis** | Full or partial reduplication of a truth-domain base creates an intensified or emphatic form. | ka, yo, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: მართლა (mart'la) → emphatic use; yo: dájúdájú (dájú reduplicated) "very certain"; eu: gezur-gezurra (gezur reduplicated) "complete lie/blatant lie," zuzen-zuzen "very straight/completely correct," berdin-berdin "exactly the same," ziur-ziur "absolutely certain"; ta: (reduplication less common in truth domain specifically); id: benar-benar "really/truly," betul-betul "really/truly," sungguh-sungguh "seriously/earnestly," bohong-bohongan "pretend/fake"; tr: doğru dürüst "proper/decent" (alliterative compound), yalan dolan "lies and deceit" (alliterative compound); ko: (reduplication not productive in truth domain; emphatic particles used instead) | **Moderately attested** (clearly productive in eu and id; present in yo and tr; less prominent in ka, ta, ko). In eu, full reduplication with hyphen (gezur-gezurra, zuzen-zuzen, berdin-berdin) is a productive emphatic strategy. In id, reduplication creates intensifiers (benar-benar, betul-betul) and a colloquial "pretend/fake" form (bohong-bohongan). In yo, reduplication of dájú creates an emphatic certainty form. In tr, alliterative compounding (doğru dürüst, yalan dolan) functions similarly to reduplication. In ko, emphatic particles (-ē, -도) rather than reduplication serve the intensification function. |
| **Compound formation with truth-domain base as first element** | A truth-domain root or word functions as the first element of a compound, generating new lexical items in related semantic domains. | ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: mart'l-: მართლწერა (mart'l-ts'era) "orthography," მართლმეტყველება (mart'l-met'q'veleba) "correct speech," მართლმადიდებელი (mart'l-madidebeli) "Orthodox"; fi: tosi-: tosiasia (tosi-asia) "fact," tosielämä (tosi-elämä) "real life," tositarina (tosi-tarina) "true story," tosi-tv "reality TV"; eu: zuzen-: zuzenbide (zuzen-bide) "law," zuzendari (zuzen-dari) "director," zuzendaritza (zuzen-daritza) "management"; ta: uṇmai-: uṇmai katai "true story," uṇmai nikaḻvu "true event," uṇmai mukam "true face"; id: benar-: sebenar-benarnya "most truthfully," benar-benar "really"; tr: gerçek-: gerçekdışı (gerçek-dışı) "unreal," gerçeküstü (gerçek-üstü) "surreal," gerçek hayat "real life"; ko: 진- (jin-): 진심 (jin-sim) "sincerity," 진상 (jin-sang) "truth of situation," 진품 (jin-pum) "genuine article," 진단 (jin-dan) "diagnosis" | **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages show productive compounding; yo shows less compound formation of this type). In ka, the mart'l- root generates compounds extending into orthography, religious denomination, and modal propriety — the widest single-root extension in the dataset. In fi, tosi- is highly productive in media and everyday compounds. In eu, zuzen- extends into organizational management and live broadcasting. In ko, the jin- morpheme generates compounds across truth/genuineness, medical examination, and advancement domains. In tr, gerçek- generates compounds for virtual/augmented reality. In yo, compound formation with truth-domain bases is less prominent; the language relies more on serial verb constructions and noun phrases. |
| **Compound formation with truth-domain base as second element** | A truth-domain root or word functions as the second element of a compound, where the first element specifies a domain or type. | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: ყალბი ფული (q'albi puli) "counterfeit money," ყალბი დოკუმენტი (q'albi dok'ument'i) "forged document," ტყუილი ფიცი (t'q'uili pits'i) "false oath"; yo: irọ́ compounds: ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ "true word," ìró tọ́ "true report"; fi: väärä-: väärä hälytys "false alarm," väärä todistus "false testimony," väärä vala "false oath"; eu: gezurrezko-: gezurrezko agiri "false document," gezurrezko lekukotza "false testimony," gezurrezko albistea "fake news"; ta: poy-: poy cāṭci "false testimony," poy āvaṇam "false document," poy ceyti "false news"; id: palsu-: uang palsu "counterfeit money," dokumen palsu "forged document," berita palsu "fake news"; tr: sahte-: sahte para "counterfeit money," sahte belge "forged document"; ko: 거짓-: 거짓증언 (geojit-jeungeon) "false testimony," 거짓정보 (geojit-jeongbo) "false information," 거짓뉴스 (geojit-nyuseu) "fake news" | **Widely attested** (all 8 languages). The pattern of [FALSE/FAKE + DOMAIN NOUN] is productive across all languages for generating domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit money, and fake news. The specific false/fake element varies (ka ყალბი, yo irọ́, fi väärä, eu gezurrezko, ta poy, id palsu, tr sahte, ko 거짓), but the compounding strategy is consistent. This is one of the most uniform cross-language patterns in the dataset. |
| **Grammaticalization of truth-domain forms into discourse markers** | A truth-domain word or phrase undergoes grammaticalization, losing compositional meaning and acquiring a discourse-organizational function (introducing corrections, frank statements, or factual clarifications). | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko | ka: მართლაც (mart'lats) "really/indeed," მართლა? (mart'la?) "really?"; yo: nitootọ́ "truly/in truth," òtítọ́ ni "it is true/that's the truth"; fi: totta kai "of course/certainly" (fully grammaticalized), totta puhuen "to tell the truth" (semi-fixed); eu: benetan "truly/really/indeed," egiatan "in truth/truly"; ta: uṇmaiyil "in truth/actually," nijamā? "really?/is that so?"; id: sebenarnya "actually/in fact," ternyata "it turns out/apparently" (grammaticalized evidential); tr: gerçekten "really/truly," hakikaten "really/truly/indeed"; ko: 사실은 (sasireun) "actually/in fact," 솔직히 (soljiki) "frankly" | **Widely attested** (all 8 languages). This is one of the most consistent cross-language patterns. In all languages, at least one truth-domain form has grammaticalized into a discourse marker. The degree of grammaticalization varies: fi totta kai is fully grammaticalized (cannot be decomposed); id ternyata is a grammaticalized evidential; ko 사실은 functions as a topic-marking discourse marker. In several languages (ka, eu, ta), the adverbial form of the truth root has grammaticalized into an emphatic particle. |
| **Privative suffix creating "without X" adjective** | A suffix meaning "without" or "lacking" attaches to a truth-domain noun to create an adjective meaning "without [truth/error/doubt]." | ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, tr | ka: უ- (u-): უტყუარი (u-t'q'uar-i) "infallible/unerring" (without lying); yo: àì-: àìtọ́ "incorrectness," àìbámu "non-correspondence"; fi: -tön/-ttömyys: vilpitön (vilppi + -tön) "sincere" (without deceit); eu: -gaitz/-ezin (implied): frogaezina "unprovable"; tr: -sız/-siz: haksız (hak + -sız) "unjust" (without right), şüphesiz (şüphe + -siz) "doubtless" (without doubt), hatasız (hata + -sız) "flawless" (without error); ta: -aṟṟa: uṇmaiyaṟṟa (uṇmai + aṟṟa) "without truth/untrue," cantēkamaṟṟa (cantēkam + aṟṟa) "without doubt" | **Moderately attested** (clearly productive in ka, yo, fi, tr, ta; present but less systematic in eu). The privative strategy differs from simple negation in that it creates a new lexical item rather than a syntactic negation. In tr, the privative suffix -sız/-siz is highly productive and creates both antonyms (haksız "unjust") and positive-valence forms (şüphesiz "doubtless," hatasız "flawless"). ## 3. Narrative Analysis

### Patterns Recurring Across Unrelated Language Families

**Abstract nominalization is universal but formally diverse.** Every language in the dataset derives abstract quality nouns from truth-domain bases, and these nominalizations are used in formal, philosophical, and analytical discourse. The functional pattern is identical across Georgian (Kartvelian), Yoruba (Niger-Congo), Finnish (Finno-Ugric), Basque (isolate), Tamil (Dravidian), Indonesian (Austronesian), Turkish (Turkic), and Korean (Koreanic), but the formal strategies are typologically diverse: suffixes (fi -uus, eu -tasun, tr -luk, ko -seong), circumfixes (id ke-...-an), prefixes (yo ì-), and suffix + stem alternation (ka si-...-e). The convergence on this functional pattern across unrelated families suggests it reflects a general tendency in how languages organize the truth domain rather than contact or common ancestry.

**The "liar" agentive is universally lexicalized; the "truth-teller" agentive is not.** This asymmetry is documented in all eight languages and is one of the most striking findings in the dataset. Every language has a morphologically derived or lexicalized form for "liar" (ka მატყუარა, yo apurọ́, fi valehtelija, eu gezurlari, ta poyyan/poyyar, id pembohong, tr yalancı, ko 거짓말쟁이), while "truth-teller" either does not exist as a morphological form or is marginal and requires a compound or periphrastic construction. This asymmetry cannot be explained by morphological constraints alone, since the same suffixes that create "liar" forms could in principle create "truth-teller" forms. It appears to reflect a cross-linguistic tendency to lexicalize the negative agent more readily than the positive agent in the deception domain.

**Discourse marker grammaticalization from truth-domain roots is universal.** In all eight languages, at least one truth-domain form has grammaticalized into a discourse marker that introduces corrections, frank statements, or factual clarifications. The forms range from fully grammaticalized (fi totta kai "of course," id ternyata "it turns out") to semi-fixed (ka მართლა? "really?," ko 사실은 "actually"). This pattern appears across unrelated language families and suggests that truth-domain vocabulary is particularly susceptible to pragmatic grammaticalization, likely because truth-evaluation is a fundamental discourse management function.

**Compound formation with [FALSE/FAKE + DOMAIN NOUN] is universal.** All eight languages productively generate domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit money, and fake news through the same structural pattern: a false/fake modifier + a domain noun. The specific modifier varies (ka ყალბი, yo irọ́, fi väärä, eu gezurrezko, ta poy, id palsu, tr sahte, ko 거짓), but the compounding strategy is consistent. This is the most formally uniform cross-language pattern in the dataset.

**Verb + object constructions for truth-telling coexist with dedicated verbs for lying.** In at least four languages (yo, fi, ta, ko), the primary way to express truth-telling is through a verb + object construction (yo sọ òtítọ́, fi puhua totuutta, ta uṇmaiyai col, ko 진실을 말하다), while lying has a dedicated verb (yo purọ́, fi valehdella, ta poy col, ko 거짓말하다). This asymmetry parallels the agentive asymmetry: the negative pole (lying) is more morphologically condensed than the positive pole (truth-telling). In ka, both truth-telling and lying use verb + object constructions, which is the exception rather than the rule.

### Striking Asymmetries

**Positive vs. negative nominalization strategies in Basque.** In Basque, positive moral qualities use the neutral -tasun suffix (zintzotasuna "honesty," egiazkotasuna "truthfulness"), while negative moral qualities use the pejorative -keria suffix (gezurkeria "habitual lying," faltsukeria "falseness," iruzurkeria "fraudulence"). This formal asymmetry encodes a moral asymmetry: the negative forms carry stronger condemnation than simple negation would suggest. No other language in the dataset shows this specific formal asymmetry, though the general tendency to elaborate the negative pole more than the positive pole is cross-linguistically attested.

**Inchoative asymmetry: "becoming true" is more productive than "becoming false."** Across multiple languages, constructions for "becoming true" or "coming true" are more productive than constructions for "becoming false" or "turning out to be a lie." Finnish explicitly lacks a common expression for "becoming untrue." Korean treats truth as a static property that is discovered rather than something that changes. Yoruba treats truth as categorical and non-emergent. Even in languages with productive inchoatives (tr gerçekleşmek, fi toteutua), the "becoming false" direction is less elaborated. This asymmetry may reflect a conceptual tendency to treat truth as a stable state that is revealed rather than a property that changes.

**The causative gap in Yoruba and Korean.** Both Yoruba and Korean show a documented resistance to causative constructions for propositional truth states. In Yoruba, "making something true" (mú kí ó tọ́) is pragmatically odd and reinterpreted as verification or enabling. In Korean, morphological causatives for truth predicates are absent; the language uses periphrastic constructions (-게 하다). This resistance is not found in the other six languages, which show productive causative derivation from truth-domain bases. The constraint appears to reflect a conceptual framing of truth as something discovered or revealed rather than caused or created.

**The Georgian ტყუილი extension to "futile/in vain."** The semantic extension of the "lie/false" root to cover "futile" or "in vain" is documented only in Georgian. This is a single-language observation with no parallel in the other seven languages. The extension is productive in Georgian (ტყუილად "in vain," ტყუილუბრალოდ "pointlessly," ტყუილი იმედი "vain hope") and generates a distinct semantic group (F3 Group 11). Whether this reflects a historical semantic development or a conceptual link between "false" and "purposeless" specific to Georgian cannot be determined from the synchronic data alone.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**Blocked: "truth-teller" agentive.** As noted above, the agentive derivation that creates "liar" forms does not productively create "truth-teller" forms in any of the eight languages. This is a one-way pathway: the deception root → agentive is productive; the truth root → agentive is blocked or marginal.

**Blocked: causative from truth predicates in yo and ko.** The causative derivation that applies to other adjectives does not productively apply to propositional truth predicates in Yoruba and Korean. This is a domain-specific blocking rather than a general morphological constraint.

**One-way: negative compounds are lexicalized; positive counterparts are not.** In Finnish (väärinkäsitys "misunderstanding" but no *oikea käsitys as a lexicalized compound; väärinkäyttö "misuse" but no *oikea käyttö as a lexicalized compound) and Indonesian (similar patterns), the negative compound is lexicalized while the positive counterpart remains compositional. This is a one-way lexicalization pattern: the negative pole is frozen while the positive pole remains productive but unlexicalized.

**One-way: degree modification of truth predicates.** In Yoruba, Korean, Finnish, Basque, and Tamil, core truth predicates resist degree modification (truth is treated as binary rather than scalar). This is a one-way constraint: gradable terms like "credibility" or "similarity" can be modified by degree adverbs, but core truth predicates cannot. The constraint is not absolute across all eight languages (ka, id, tr show more gradable uses) but is attested in enough languages to be noted as a recurring pattern.

### Productivity, Restriction, Lexicalization, and Unclear Status

**Productive patterns:**
- Abstract nominalization (all 8 languages): universally productive with truth-domain bases
- Adverbial derivation (all 8 languages): universally productive
- Negative prefix/suffix (7 of 8 languages): productive, with id using analytic negation instead
- Compound formation with [FALSE/FAKE + DOMAIN NOUN] (all 8 languages): productive
- Inchoative derivation (7 of 8 languages): productive, with yo most restricted
- Causative derivation (6 of 8 languages): productive in ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr; restricted in yo and ko
- Evaluative adjective suffix -garri (eu only): highly productive within Basque

**Restricted patterns:**
- Agentive "truth-teller" nominalization: blocked or marginal in all 8 languages
- Causative from propositional truth predicates: blocked in yo and ko
- Degree modification of core truth predicates: restricted in yo, ko, fi, eu, ta
- Pejorative -keria suffix (eu): restricted to negative moral failing bases
- Inchoative "becoming false": less productive than "becoming true" across multiple languages

**Lexicalized / fossilized patterns:**
- fi totta kai "of course": fully grammaticalized, non-compositional
- id ternyata "it turns out": grammaticalized evidential
- tr hakkında "about/concerning": grammaticalized postposition from hak- root
- ka ტყუილად "in vain": lexicalized extension of "lie" root to "futile"
- yo pa irọ́ "tell a lie": the verb pa (kill/strike) is conventionalized with irọ́ and not compositionally predictable
- ko 옳고 그름 "right and wrong": the archaic 그르다 survives only in this fixed expression
- fi totta puhuen "to tell the truth": semi-fixed adverbial phrase

**Unclear or weakly attested patterns:**
- eu -gaitz/-ezin for "unprovable/unbelievable": implied from the data but not directly attested as productive forms
- ka მართლმოსაუბრე "truth-teller": marked [MARGINAL] in F1; unclear whether this represents a productive pattern or a nonce formation
- id kebenar-benaran "excessive correctness": marked [UNCERTAIN] in F1; limited attestation
- ko 진 (jin) morpheme polysemy: whether the truth/genuineness, advancement/progress, and medical examination uses represent genuine polysemy or homophony of distinct roots cannot be determined from the synchronic data
- yo bá mu láti "to correspond from": marked [MARGINAL] in F1; unclear productivity
