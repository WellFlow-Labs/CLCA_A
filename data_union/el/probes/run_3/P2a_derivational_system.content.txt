# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 843, consistent with P1 and P1b.

---

**Scope note:** This step documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Derivations are listed only where genuine use is supported. Paradigm gaps are preserved. Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The number of derivations varies by item and reflects actual productivity, not assumed symmetry.

## P1 Item 1 — αληθής (alithís)

**Base Form:** αληθής

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** alíthia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + -εια (abstract noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
6. **Usage:** The primary abstract noun derived from αληθής; the central noun for truth across all registers. Fully documented in P1 (item 2) and P1b (Root 1, item 1).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αληθινός
3. **Transliteration:** alithinós
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + -ινός (adjectival suffix indicating nature or material)
5. **Natural English translation:** real, genuine, true
6. **Usage:** A secondary adjective derived from the same root, with a slightly more colloquial register and a stronger connotation of genuineness versus imitation. Fully documented in P1 (item 3).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αληθεύω
3. **Transliteration:** alithévo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + -εύω (verbal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to speak the truth; to be true (of a proposition)
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical and formal written registers. Less common in everyday speech. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 4).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αληθώς
3. **Transliteration:** alithós
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** truly, verily, in truth
6. **Usage:** A formal and liturgical adverb; appears in the Orthodox Easter response Αληθώς ανέστη. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 19).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αναληθής
3. **Transliteration:** analithís
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αν- (privative prefix) + αληθής
5. **Natural English translation:** untrue, false
6. **Usage:** Formal privative negation of αληθής; used in philosophical and written registers. Documented in P1 (item 20) and P1b (Root 1, item 7).
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Derived Form:** αληθοφανής
3. **Transliteration:** alithofanís
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + φαίν- (from φαίνομαι, to appear) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** verisimilar, plausible, lifelike
6. **Usage:** Applied to narratives or accounts that have the appearance of truth. Used in literary criticism and rhetoric. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 10).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The adverb αληθινά (alithiná, "truly, really") is derived from αληθινός rather than directly from αληθής and is documented under item 3 below.*

## P1 Item 2 — αλήθεια (alíthia)

**Base Form:** αλήθεια

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Derived Form:** αναλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** analíthia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αν- (privative) + αλήθεια
5. **Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
6. **Usage:** The privative abstract noun corresponding to αναληθής; used in philosophical and formal written registers. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 8).
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Derived Form:** αληθοφάνεια
3. **Transliteration:** alithofánia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + φαίν- (from φαίνομαι) + -εια (abstract noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, appearance of truth, plausibility
6. **Usage:** Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophy to describe the quality of appearing true without necessarily being true. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 9).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Derived Form:** μετα-αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** meta-alíthia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** μετα- (beyond, after) + αλήθεια
5. **Natural English translation:** post-truth
6. **Usage:** Used in journalistic, political, and academic registers to describe an epistemic condition in which emotional appeals outweigh objective facts. A calque of English "post-truth." Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 30).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: αλήθεια functions primarily as a base noun and derivational source rather than itself being a derived form with further productive suffixation. Its main derivational output is the privative αναλήθεια and the compound αληθοφάνεια. Fixed collocations (λέω την αλήθεια, η αλήθεια είναι ότι, etc.) are documented in P1b as phrasal items rather than morphological derivations.*

## P1 Item 4 — ψευδής (psevdís)

**Base Form:** ψευδής

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** ψευδής
2. **Derived Form:** ψεύδος
3. **Transliteration:** psévdos
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- + -ος (neuter noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** lie, falsehood
6. **Usage:** The primary noun for falsehood; shares the root with ψευδής. Fully documented in P1 (item 5). The relationship between ψευδής and ψεύδος is one of co-derivation from the shared root ψευδ- rather than direct derivation of one from the other.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** ψευδής
2. **Derived Form:** ψευδώς
3. **Transliteration:** psevdós
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** falsely, untruthfully
6. **Usage:** The adverbial form of ψευδής; used in formal, legal, and written registers (e.g., ψευδώς ισχυρίζεται, "falsely claims"). Less common in everyday speech.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The colloquial adjective ψεύτικος and the agent noun ψεύτης are derived from the colloquial stem ψεύτ- (itself a reduction of ψευδ-) rather than directly from ψευδής, and are documented under P1 item 5 (ψεύδος) below, where the shared root is treated as the base.*

## P1 Item 5 — ψεύδος (psévdos)

**Base Form:** ψεύδος

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψεύτης
3. **Transliteration:** psévtis
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- (with regular consonant cluster simplification to ψευτ-) + -ης (agent noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** liar
6. **Usage:** The primary everyday noun for a person who lies; used across all registers and age groups. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 3).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψεύτικος

5. **Natural English translation:** fake, false, phony, counterfeit
6. **Usage:** The most common colloquial adjective for "fake" or "false" in everyday Modern Greek; more informal than ψευδής. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 4).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψεύδομαι
3. **Transliteration:** psévdome
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- + -ομαι (middle/passive verbal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell a lie
6. **Usage:** The primary verb for lying; slightly more formal than the colloquial λέω ψέματα. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 5).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψέμα
3. **Transliteration:** pséma
4. **Literal Breakdown:** colloquial reduction of ψεύδος; ψευδ- + -μα (nominal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** lie, fib
6. **Usage:** The most common colloquial noun for a lie in everyday Modern Greek; plural ψέματα is extremely frequent. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 7).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψευδαίσθηση
3. **Transliteration:** psevdésthisi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδο- (false) + αίσθηση (sensation, perception)
5. **Natural English translation:** illusion, hallucination, false impression
6. **Usage:** Used in psychological, medical, and everyday contexts for a false perception or mistaken impression of reality. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 18).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
1. **Base Form:** ψεύδος
2. **Derived Form:** ψευδώνυμο
3. **Transliteration:** psevdónymo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδο- (false) + όνομα/ώνυμο (name)
5. **Natural English translation:** pseudonym, pen name, alias
6. **Usage:** Used in literary, legal, and everyday contexts for an assumed or false name. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 12).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The ψευδο- prefix is highly productive and generates a large open class of compounds (ψευδοεπιστήμη, ψευδοδιλήμμα, ψευδοπροφήτης, etc.). A representative selection is documented in P1b (Root 2, items 10–30). Productivity of this prefix is not bounded by the P1 item list.*

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** γνήσιος
2. **Derived Form:** νόθος (antonym, not a derivation)
3. **Transliteration:** —
4. **Literal Breakdown:** —
5. **Natural English translation:** —
6. **Usage:** *Not a derivation.* νόθος (P1 item 15) functions as the lexical antonym of γνήσιος in contexts of legitimacy of birth and textual attribution, but is etymologically unrelated. Noted here to prevent false derivational pairing.

## P1 Item 9 — έγκυρος (éngiros)

**Base Form:** έγκυρος

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος
2. **Derived Form:** εγκυρότητα
3. **Transliteration:** engirotita
4. **Literal Breakdown:** έγκυρος + -ότητα (abstract noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** validity, legitimacy, authority
6. **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to έγκυρος; used in legal, administrative, and argumentative contexts to refer to the quality of having legal or logical force (e.g., η εγκυρότητα της σύμβασης, "the validity of the contract").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος
2. **Derived Form:** άκυρος
3. **Transliteration:** ákiros
4. **Literal Breakdown:** α- (privative) + κῦρος (authority, validity)
5. **Natural English translation:** invalid, null, void
6. **Usage:** The lexical antonym of έγκυρος, formed by privative prefixation on the shared base κῦρος rather than directly on έγκυρος. Used in legal and administrative contexts (e.g., η σύμβαση είναι άκυρη, "the contract is null and void"). Noted here as a co-derivative from the same base, not a derivation of έγκυρος itself.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος
2. **Derived Form:** ακυρότητα
3. **Transliteration:** akirótita
4. **Literal Breakdown:** άκυρος + -ότητα (abstract noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** invalidity, nullity
6. **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to άκυρος; used in legal contexts to describe the state of being null or void. Documented here as part of the κῦρος derivational family for completeness.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος
2. **Derived Form:** ακυρώνω
3. **Transliteration:** akiróno
4. **Literal Breakdown:** α- (privative) + κῦρος + -ώνω (verbal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to invalidate, to cancel, to annul, to void
6. **Usage:** The verb meaning to render something invalid or to cancel it; used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts (e.g., ακυρώνω ένα εισιτήριο, "to cancel a ticket"; ακυρώνω μια απόφαση, "to annul a decision").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος
2. **Derived Form:** ακύρωση
3. **Transliteration:** akírosi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ακυρώνω + -ση (verbal noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** cancellation, annulment, invalidation
6. **Usage:** The noun corresponding to ακυρώνω; used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts (e.g., ακύρωση σύμβασης, "contract annulment"; ακύρωση πτήσης, "flight cancellation").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The κῦρος base is moderately productive. The forms άκυρος, ακυρότητα, ακυρώνω, and ακύρωση are all well-attested and form a coherent derivational cluster around the negative pole of the validity domain. No common verbal derivation directly from έγκυρος (as opposed to from the shared base) is identified.*

## P1 Item 10 — ακριβής (akrivís)

**Base Form:** ακριβής

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Derived Form:** ακρίβεια
3. **Transliteration:** akrívia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- + -εια (abstract noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** accuracy, precision, exactness; also: high cost, expensiveness
6. **Usage:** The primary abstract noun from ακριβής. In the epistemic domain, it means accuracy or precision (e.g., η ακρίβεια της μέτρησης, "the accuracy of the measurement"). In everyday usage it also means "high cost" or "expensiveness" — a distinct but well-established polysemy in Modern Greek (e.g., η ακρίβεια των τροφίμων, "the high cost of food").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Derived Form:** ακριβώς
3. **Transliteration:** akrivós
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** exactly, precisely, accurately
6. **Usage:** The adverbial form of ακριβής; extremely common in everyday speech and formal registers. Used both as a modifier (ακριβώς στις 8, "at exactly 8 o'clock") and as a discourse particle affirming agreement ("exactly," "precisely").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Derived Form:** ανακριβής
3. **Transliteration:** anakrivís
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αν- (privative) + ακριβής
5. **Natural English translation:** inaccurate, imprecise, inexact
6. **Usage:** The privative negation of ακριβής; used in formal, journalistic, and technical registers to indicate that a measurement, description, or account is not accurate.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Derived Form:** ανακρίβεια
3. **Transliteration:** anakrívia
4. **Literal Breakdown:** αν- (privative) + ακρίβεια
5. **Natural English translation:** inaccuracy, imprecision
6. **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to ανακριβής; used in formal and technical registers to describe the quality or instance of being inaccurate.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Derived Form:** ακριβός
3. **Transliteration:** akrivós
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- + -ός (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** expensive, costly; (archaic/dialectal) precise
6. **Usage:** A related adjective sharing the root; in Modern Greek the dominant meaning is "expensive" rather than "precise." The semantic split between ακριβής (precise) and ακριβός (expensive) is a well-documented feature of Modern Greek. The two forms coexist and are etymologically related but functionally differentiated.

## Note on Asymmetry

Derivational productivity is markedly uneven across the P1 inventory. The αληθ- and ψευδ- root families are highly productive, generating verbal, nominal, adjectival, and adverbial forms as well as open-class compounding (particularly ψευδο-). Items such as κίβδηλος, δήθεν, and τάχα are derivationally inert in contemporary use. This asymmetry is preserved in the documentation above and reflects actual patterns in the language rather than gaps in analysis.
