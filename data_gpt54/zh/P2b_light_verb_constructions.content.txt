Below are **attested multi-word constructions** built around or extending the P1 items. I avoid simple derivational forms already handled in P2a, and focus on phrase-level, idiomatic, or syntactically conventional constructions.

## 1. Base Form: 真

1. **Example:** 说真的  
   **Transliteration:** shuō zhēn de  
   **English translation:** honestly; seriously speaking  
   **Usage notes:** Very common colloquial discourse marker used to introduce a sincere statement, opinion, or admission. It often signals that the speaker is being candid rather than joking or exaggerating.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **Example:** 当真  
   **Transliteration:** dàngzhēn  
   **English translation:** take seriously; believe to be true  
   **Usage notes:** Common in patterns like 别当真 “don’t take it seriously” or 你还当真了 “you actually believed it.” Although lexicalized, it functions very often in larger syntactic constructions rather than as a simple isolated adjective.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **Example:** 千真万确  
   **Transliteration:** qiān zhēn wàn què  
   **English translation:** absolutely true; unquestionably true  
   **Usage notes:** Fixed idiomatic expression used to strongly affirm truth or certainty. Common in speech and writing when emphasizing that something is beyond doubt.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **Example:** 真有此事  
   **Transliteration:** zhēn yǒu cǐ shì  
   **English translation:** this really happened; such a thing really exists/existed  
   **Usage notes:** Conventional phrase used to confirm that a reported event is real. It appears in both spoken surprise and more formal narrative contexts.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: 假

1. **Example:** 弄虚作假  
   **Transliteration:** nòng xū zuò jiǎ  
   **English translation:** engage in fraud; fake things; falsify  
   **Usage notes:** Very common fixed expression in official, media, and evaluative language for dishonest fabrication or falsification. It is especially frequent in criticism of data, credentials, procedures, or reporting.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **Example:** 以假乱真  
   **Transliteration:** yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn  
   **English translation:** pass off the fake as real; confuse the fake with the genuine  
   **Usage notes:** Common idiom for imitations or deceptions so convincing that they are mistaken for the real thing. Used for goods, art, appearances, and sometimes figurative social deception.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **Example:** 假的真不了，真的假不了  
   **Transliteration:** jiǎ de zhēn bù liǎo, zhēn de jiǎ bù liǎo  
   **English translation:** what is fake cannot become real, and what is real cannot become fake  
   **Usage notes:** Established proverbial saying used to express confidence that truth and falsity will eventually be distinguished. It is rhetorical and somewhat proverbial in tone.  
   **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
