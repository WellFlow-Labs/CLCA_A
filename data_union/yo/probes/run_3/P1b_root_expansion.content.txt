# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **tọ** (tọ)

**Native script:** tọ (with various tone markings depending on context: *tọ*, *tọ́*, *tọ̀*)
**Participation in P1 items:** 6 items — *òtítọ́* (1), *ẹ̀tọ́* (7), *tọ̀nà* (6), *ìtọ́ni* (14), *dájúdájú* (partially, via *dá*-related semantic field), and the verbal base underlying *ìdánwò* (22) in its evaluative sense. More directly: *òtítọ́*, *ẹ̀tọ́*, *tọ̀nà*, *ìtọ́ni* — 4 items with transparent *tọ* participation; *dájú* and *ìdánwò* share the evaluative-correctness semantic field but are not morphologically derived from *tọ*.

**Revised count (morphologically transparent):** 4 items: *òtítọ́*, *ẹ̀tọ́*, *tọ̀nà*, *ìtọ́ni*

**Core meaning contribution:** The root *tọ* carries the meaning of being straight, correct, proper, or fitting — the alignment of something with a standard or norm. It is the primary morphological carrier of correctness and rightness in the Yoruba lexicon, and it underlies the most central truth-term (*òtítọ́*) as well as the term for rights/entitlements (*ẹ̀tọ́*).

---

### Root 2: **ìwà** (ìwà)

**Native script:** ìwà
**Participation in P1 items:** 3 items — *ìwà* (19), *ìwà rere* (20), *ìwà burúkú* (21)

**Core meaning contribution:** The root/nominal *ìwà* (from *wà*, "to exist, to be") denotes character, nature, being, and essential existence. It is the primary vehicle in Yoruba moral and philosophical discourse for evaluating what something or someone fundamentally *is*, as opposed to how they appear. It anchors the domain of essential nature and moral character evaluation.

---

### Root 3: **ẹ̀rí / rí** (ẹ̀rí / rí)

**Native script:** ẹ̀rí; verbal base *rí*
**Participation in P1 items:** 2 items directly — *ẹ̀rí* (12) and *ìjẹ́rìísí* (11, which contains *rí* in its base). Additionally, the semantic cluster of evidence, witnessing, and verification is anchored by this root.

**Note on root selection:** *tọ* is the most morphologically productive root across P1 items in the truth-evaluation domain. *ìwà* is the most semantically central root for the character/essential-nature subdomain. *ẹ̀rí/rí* anchors the evidence and verification subdomain. These three are selected as the primary roots for expansion.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: **tọ** — "straight, correct, proper, fitting"

*Note: This root is highly productive. The following lists the 25 most common and well-attested compounds, collocations, and derived expressions, with a note that additional forms exist in specialized registers.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ò-* (nominalizer) + *tí* (intensifying/aspectual element, analysis debated) + *tọ́* (straight, correct)
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness, accuracy
**Context of use:** The primary Yoruba word for truth across all registers — everyday speech, legal proceedings, religious discourse, proverbs. Used in constructions such as *sọ òtítọ́* ("to speak the truth") and *ènìyàn òtítọ́* ("a truthful person").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct, right, straight)
**Natural English translation:** right, entitlement, what is correct or due
**Context of use:** Used in legal, moral, and social contexts for rights and entitlements, and for what is correct or proper. Appears in human rights discourse (*ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn*, "human rights") and in everyday assertions of correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** Verbal root; bare form
**Natural English translation:** to be straight, to be correct, to be proper, to be fitting
**Context of use:** Used as a stative or evaluative verb: *ọ̀rọ̀ yìí tọ́* ("this matter is correct/right"). Appears across registers as a basic evaluative predicate.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** tọ́ sí
**Transliteration:** tọ́ sí
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct, straight) + *sí* (toward, to)
**Natural English translation:** to be directed toward correctly; to point correctly toward
**Context of use:** Used in directional and advisory contexts to indicate that something is properly oriented or directed. Appears in navigational and moral advisory speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct, straight) + *sọ̀nà* (to direct on a path; *sọ* = to say/direct + *nà* = path/way)
**Natural English translation:** guidance, direction, leading on the right path
**Context of use:** Used in educational, religious, and advisory contexts for the act of guiding someone correctly. Appears in school and church/mosque discourse: *ìtọ́sọ̀nà tó dára* ("good guidance").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** tọ́ sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** tọ́ sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct, straight) + *sọ̀nà* (to direct on a path)
**Natural English translation:** to guide correctly, to direct on the right path
**Context of use:** Verbal expression used in moral and practical advisory contexts. A teacher, elder, or religious leader *tọ́ sọ̀nà* those in their care.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ni
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ni
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct) + *ni* (copula/linker or verbal extension)
**Natural English translation:** correction, guidance, direction toward what is right
**Context of use:** Used in educational and moral contexts for the act of correcting someone or directing them toward accuracy. Appears in school and parenting discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**8.**
**Form:** tọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** tọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ̀* (straight, correct; low-tone variant) + *nà* (path, way)
**Natural English translation:** correct path, right way; correctness of conduct
**Context of use:** Used in moral and advisory contexts, particularly in proverb literature and elder speech, to describe adherence to the proper course of action.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀tọ́* (right, entitlement) + *ènìyàn* (person, human being)
**Natural English translation:** human rights
**Context of use:** Used in legal, political, and NGO discourse as the standard Yoruba rendering of "human rights." Appears in news media, legal documents, and civic education materials.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́ ìdìbò
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀tọ́ ìdìbò
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀tọ́* (right, entitlement) + *ìdìbò* (voting, election)
**Natural English translation:** right to vote, voting rights
**Context of use:** Used in political and civic discourse. Appears in electoral education materials and political speeches in Yoruba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀tọ́* (right, correctness) + *rẹ* (your, 2sg possessive) + *ni* (copula/focus marker)
**Natural English translation:** it is your right; it is correct for you; you are entitled to it
**Context of use:** A fixed assertive expression used in everyday speech to affirm someone's entitlement or to confirm that something is correct or proper for them.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, to speak) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth
**Context of use:** The standard collocation for truth-telling across all registers. Used in legal oaths, moral instruction, everyday assertions, and religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ènìyàn* (person) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** a truthful person, a person of truth
**Context of use:** Used in character evaluation to describe someone who is consistently honest and reliable in their speech. Appears in social and moral assessment contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**Literal breakdown:** *òtítọ́* (truth) + *ni* (copula/focus marker)
**Natural English translation:** it is the truth; that is true
**Context of use:** A fixed assertive expression used to affirm the truth of a statement. Appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and religious affirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìjẹ́* (acknowledgment, admission; from *jẹ*, to eat/consume, here in the sense of accepting) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** acknowledgment of the truth, admission of truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts for the act of admitting or acknowledging that something is true. Appears in court proceedings and in religious confession contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwádìí* (investigation, inquiry; from *wá* = to seek + *dìí* = to press/examine) + *òtítọ́* (truth)

**Context of use:** Used in legal, journalistic, and administrative contexts for the process of investigating what is true. Appears in news media and official inquiry language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** tọ́ (in the sense of "to be correct" applied to accounts/records)
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (to be straight, correct)
**Natural English translation:** to be accurate, to be correct (of a record, account, or statement)
**Context of use:** Used in administrative and accounting contexts to evaluate whether records or statements are accurate. *Àkọsílẹ̀ yìí tọ́* ("this record is correct/accurate").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ìtọ́jú
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́jú
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct, proper) + *jú* (to exceed, surpass; or verbal extension indicating thoroughness)
**Natural English translation:** care, maintenance, proper keeping, preservation
**Context of use:** Used for the proper care and maintenance of persons, objects, or institutions. Appears in healthcare (*ìtọ́jú àrùn*, "care of illness/medical care"), childcare, and archival contexts. Note: the *tọ́* here carries the sense of "proper/correct handling" rather than truth per se.

---

**19.**
**Form:** ìtọ́jú àrùn

**Literal breakdown:** *ìtọ́jú* (proper care, maintenance) + *àrùn* (illness, disease)

**20.**

### ROOT 2: **ìwà** — "character, nature, being, existence"

*Note: ìwà is highly productive in the moral and philosophical domain. The following lists the most common and well-attested expressions.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** ìwà
**Transliteration:** ìwà
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *wà* (to exist, to be)
**Natural English translation:** character, nature, being, conduct, essence
**Context of use:** The base nominal, used across all registers to refer to a person's fundamental character or nature. Central to Yoruba ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ìwà rere
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwà* (character, nature) + *rere* (good, fine, excellent)
**Natural English translation:** good character, virtue, moral goodness
**Context of use:** The foundational expression of Yoruba moral philosophy. Used in proverbs, religious instruction, parenting discourse, and everyday moral evaluation. Treated as the basis of all other virtues.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ìwà burúkú
**Transliteration:** ìwà burúkú
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwà* (character) + *burúkú* (bad, evil, wicked)
**Natural English translation:** bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
**Context of use:** The standard antonym of *ìwà rere*. Used across all registers to evaluate persons whose fundamental nature or conduct is judged morally deficient or harmful.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́
**Transliteration:** ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwà* (character, nature) + *pẹ̀lẹ́* (gentle, soft, careful, tender)
**Natural English translation:** gentle character, gentleness, mild and careful conduct
**Context of use:** Used in moral and social evaluation to describe a person of gentle, careful, and considerate character. Appears in proverbs and in the praise of elders and respected persons. A highly valued quality in Yoruba social ethics.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ìwà ọmọlúwàbí
**Transliteration:** ìwà ọmọlúwàbí
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwà* (character) + *ọmọlúwàbí* (a person of good breeding and character; *ọmọ* = child + *olú* = lord/chief + *wà* = being/character + *bí* = born)
**Natural English translation:** the character of a well-bred, virtuous person; exemplary moral character
**Context of use:** Used in moral, educational, and social discourse to describe the ideal of a person who embodies good character, proper upbringing, and social responsibility. *Ọmọlúwàbí* is itself a key concept in Yoruba ethics, and *ìwà ọmọlúwàbí* specifies the character associated with this ideal.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ọmọlúwàbí
**Transliteration:** ọmọlúwàbí
**Literal breakdown:** *ọmọ* (child, person) + *olú* (lord, chief, head) + *wà* (being, character) + *bí* (born, begotten)
**Natural English translation:** a person of good character and proper upbringing; a virtuous, well-bred person
**Context of use:** One of the most important evaluative terms in Yoruba moral and social discourse. Used to describe the ideal person who combines good character, social responsibility, and proper conduct. Appears in educational policy, proverbs, and everyday character evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**

**Natural English translation:** good character, character that is fine/good
**Context of use:** A descriptive phrase used in everyday moral evaluation, slightly less fixed than *ìwà rere* but functionally similar. Used in parenting, social assessment, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**8.**

### ROOT 3: **ẹ̀rí / rí** — "evidence, proof; to see"

*Note: The verbal root rí ("to see") is extremely productive in Yoruba generally, but its participation in the truth-evaluation domain is anchored primarily through the nominal ẹ̀rí ("evidence, proof") and the compound ìjẹ́rìísí ("testimony"). The following focuses on expressions built from ẹ̀rí and its immediate derivatives in the evidence/verification domain, plus relevant compounds of rí where they bear on truth-evaluation.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀-* (nominalizer) + *rí* (to see)
**Natural English translation:** evidence, proof, witness, sign
**Context of use:** The primary Yoruba term for evidence or proof. Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts to refer to anything that supports or demonstrates the truth of a claim.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, proof) + *ojú* (eye, face)
**Natural English translation:** eyewitness evidence, direct visual evidence
**Context of use:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to refer to evidence based on direct visual observation. *Ẹ̀rí ojú* is considered particularly strong evidence in Yoruba legal and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are expressions where the identified roots appear in contexts where their contribution shifts away from the core truth/evidence/character domain, without interpretation of what that shift means:

---

**From root tọ:**

- *ìtọ́jú* and its compounds (*ìtọ́jú àrùn*, *ìtọ́jú ọmọ*): The root *tọ́* here participates in the sense of "proper/correct handling or maintenance" applied to physical care of persons and objects, rather than to truth-claims or correctness of statements.

- *tọ́* in the sense of physical straightness: *igi tọ́* ("a straight tree"), where the root describes geometric or physical alignment rather than epistemic or moral correctness.

- *ẹ̀tọ́* in the sense of entitlement or rights: The root *tọ* here participates in a legal-normative domain (what one is *due*) that is distinct from the epistemic domain (what is *accurate*), though both derive from the same root.

- *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* in navigational contexts: Used for physical direction-finding and wayfinding, where *tọ́* marks physical orientation rather than epistemic or moral correctness.

---

**From root ìwà:**

- *ìwà* applied to non-human entities: *ìwà omi* ("the nature of water"), *ìwà igi* ("the nature of wood/trees") — the root here describes the essential physical properties of natural substances rather than moral character.

- *wà* as a pure existential/locative verb: *ó wà níbẹ̀* ("it is there") — the base verb functions as a simple existential predicate with no evaluative or character-related meaning.

- *ọmọlúwàbí* in contexts of social class or breeding: The term is sometimes used to mark social status or family background rather than purely moral character, flagging a social-hierarchical dimension alongside the moral one.

- *ìwà* in aesthetic evaluation: *ìwà orin yìí* ("the character/nature of this song") — applied to aesthetic objects to describe their essential qualities or style.

---

**From root ẹ̀rí / rí:**

- *ìrírí* in the sense of sensory or aesthetic experience: *ìrírí orin* ("the experience of music") — the root here marks direct perceptual or aesthetic engagement rather than evidential or testimonial function.

- *rí* in the sense of finding or encountering: *mo rí owó* ("I found money") — the verb here marks discovery of a physical object rather than visual witnessing relevant to truth-evaluation.

- *ìjẹ́rìísí* in the sense of administrative certificates: The term has extended from its core testimonial/attestation meaning to cover official documents (birth certificates, marriage certificates, educational certificates) in administrative contexts, where the "witnessing" function is institutionalized and bureaucratic rather than personal.

- *ẹ̀rí* in the sense of a physical sign or mark: *ẹ̀rí ọgbẹ́* ("the mark/sign of a wound") — the root here marks a physical trace or indicator rather than evidence in a legal or epistemic sense.

- *ẹ̀rí inú* in the sense of emotional or intuitive inner sense: The compound extends the evidential root into the domain of inner feeling or conscience, where "seeing" is metaphorical and the "evidence" is affective rather than perceptual.
