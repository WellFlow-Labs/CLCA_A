# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents how Korean encodes dynamic truth-related meanings: change of state (inchoative), causation of state (causative), and stability or persistence of state. All vocabulary is drawn exclusively from P1–P5. Auxiliary verbs, aspect markers, and function words are added as needed to form grammatical expressions; no new lexical items are introduced beyond what the CONTEXT attests.

Where a given term does not participate in a particular dynamic pattern, this is explicitly stated. Asymmetries across terms are preserved throughout.

The following construction types are examined for each relevant term or group:

- **Inchoative:** X comes to be in a truth-related state (되다, 밝혀지다, 드러나다, 판명되다, 탄로나다)
- **Causative:** Y causes X to be in a truth-related state (-화하다, 밝히다, 드러내다, 만들다)
- **Persistence / stability:** X remains in or continues to hold a truth-related state (여전히 + Adj, -고 있다, 유지하다)
- **Loss / reversal:** X ceases to hold a truth-related state (잃다, 무너지다, 무효가 되다)

## Section 1: Factual States — 사실 (sasil)

---

### 1.1 Inchoative: something comes to be established as fact

**Construction:** 사실로 밝혀지다
**Transliteration:** sasillo balkhyeojida
**English translation:** to be revealed as fact, to turn out to be true
**Source:** P2b (2.6)
**Usage notes:** The -로 frame marks the resultant state; 밝혀지다 (passive of 밝히다) encodes the epistemic process by which the state is established. Common in journalistic discourse when a claim is confirmed after investigation. The subject is the claim or situation that comes to be established as factual.

---

**Construction:** 사실임이 드러나다
**Transliteration:** sasilimi deureonada
**English translation:** it comes to light that something is a fact, the factual nature is revealed
**Source:** Parallel to 허위임이 드러나다 (P2b 10.3), applied to the positive pole
**Usage notes:** Intransitive; the factual nature emerges without a specified agent. Less common than 사실로 밝혀지다 but structurally parallel to the attested 허위임이 드러나다.

---

### 1.2 Causative: someone causes something to be established as fact

**Construction:** 사실을 확인하다
**Transliteration:** sasilеul hwagin hada
**English translation:** to verify the facts, to confirm the facts
**Source:** P2b (2.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively establishes that something is factual. 확인하다 encodes the epistemic action of verification. Common across journalistic, legal, and everyday registers.

---

**Construction:** 사실관계를 파악하다
**Transliteration:** sasil gwangyereul paakada
**English translation:** to establish the facts of the case, to grasp the factual circumstances
**Source:** P2b (2.10)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively determines the factual basis of a situation. Common in legal and journalistic discourse.

---

### 1.3 Causative: someone causes something to be presented as false fact

**Construction:** 사실을 왜곡하다
**Transliteration:** sasilеul waegokada
**English translation:** to distort the facts
**Source:** P2b (2.3)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively alters the factual record. Causative in the sense that the agent produces a distorted state of the facts.

---

**Construction:** 사실을 은폐하다
**Transliteration:** sasilеul eunpye hada
**English translation:** to conceal the facts, to cover up the facts
**Source:** P2b (2.4)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively suppresses factual information. Distinct from 왜곡하다 in that concealment does not alter the facts but prevents their disclosure.

---

### 1.4 Persistence: something continues to be a fact

**No common expression using 사실 + persistence construction is attested in the CONTEXT.** 사실 does not participate in the 여전히 유효하다 type of persistence frame documented for 유효 (see Section 5). The concept of a fact persisting is not lexicalized as a distinct construction in the record.

---

### 1.5 Loss / reversal: something ceases to be treated as fact

**Construction:** 사실이 아닌 것으로 밝혀지다
**Transliteration:** sasiri anin geoseuro balkhyeojida
**English translation:** to be revealed as not being a fact
**Source:** Constructed from attested elements: 사실이 아니다 (P4b 2) + -ㄴ 것으로 밝혀지다 (inchoative passive frame)
**Usage notes:** This is a periphrastic construction combining the negative copula frame (사실이 아니다) with the inchoative passive. No single lexicalized form for "ceasing to be a fact" is attested in the CONTEXT.

---

## Section 2: Truth Disclosure — 진실 (jinsil)

---

### 2.1 Inchoative: truth comes to light

**Construction:** 진실이 드러나다
**Transliteration:** jinsili deureonada
**English translation:** the truth comes to light, the truth is revealed
**Source:** P2b (1.6)
**Usage notes:** Intransitive; 진실 is the subject. The emergence of truth is presented as occurring without a specified agent. Common in journalistic and narrative discourse. P5 (Opposition 23) identifies this as structurally distinct from the transitive 진실을 숨기다.

---

**Construction:** 진실이 밝혀지다
**Transliteration:** jinsili balkhyeojida
**English translation:** the truth is uncovered, the truth comes out
**Source:** P2b (1.7)
**Usage notes:** Passive of 밝히다; implies that the change of state results from an investigative or disclosure process, even if no agent is specified. Slightly more agentive in implication than 드러나다.

---

### 2.2 Causative: someone causes the truth to be known

**Construction:** 진실을 밝히다
**Transliteration:** jinsilеul balkhida
**English translation:** to reveal the truth, to bring the truth to light
**Source:** P2b (1.2), P5 (Opposition 22)
**Usage notes:** Transitive; the agent actively discloses what is true. The most common causative construction for truth disclosure in the CONTEXT.

---

**Construction:** 진실 규명을 하다 / 진실을 규명하다
**Transliteration:** jinsil gyumyeongeul hada / jinsilеul gyumyeonghada
**English translation:** to conduct a truth investigation, to clarify the truth
**Source:** P1b (jin root, item 6), P2b (1.8)
**Usage notes:** The agent undertakes a formal process of establishing what is true. Common in political, legal, and historical discourse.

---

### 2.3 Causative: someone causes the truth to remain hidden

**Construction:** 진실을 숨기다
**Transliteration:** jinsilеul sumgida
**English translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
**Source:** P2b (1.3), P5 (Opposition 22)
**Usage notes:** Transitive; the agent actively prevents disclosure. The direct causative counterpart to 진실이 드러나다.

---

**Construction:** 진실을 외면하다
**Transliteration:** jinsilеul oemyeonhada
**English translation:** to turn away from the truth, to ignore the truth
**Source:** P2b (1.5)
**Usage notes:** The agent deliberately avoids acknowledging what is true. Distinct from 숨기다 in that the agent does not conceal truth from others but refuses to face it themselves.

---

**Construction:** 진실을 왜곡하다
**Transliteration:** jinsilеul waegokada
**English translation:** to distort the truth
**Source:** P2b (1.11)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively alters the representation of what is true. Causative in the sense that the agent produces a distorted state.

---

### 2.4 Persistence: truth remains concealed or unknown

**No single lexicalized persistence construction for 진실 is attested in the CONTEXT.** The concept of truth remaining hidden is expressed through the continued application of 숨기다 or through narrative context rather than a dedicated persistence construction.

---

## Section 3: Genuineness and Sincerity — 진정성 (jinjeonseong), 진심 (jinsim)

---

### 3.1 Inchoative: something comes to have authenticity

**No common inchoative construction for 진정성 is attested in the CONTEXT.** Authenticity is not typically framed as something that comes into being through a change of state; it is evaluated as present or absent. The 있다/없다 frame (P4a Construction 9) treats it as a binary possession rather than a dynamic state.

---

### 3.2 Causative: someone causes authenticity to be perceived or demonstrated

**Construction:** 진정성을 보여주다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong eul boyeojuda
**English translation:** to show sincerity, to demonstrate authenticity
**Source:** P2b (18.3)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively demonstrates genuine authenticity. This is a causative of perception: the agent causes others to perceive their authenticity. Common in political and interpersonal discourse regarding apologies and commitments.

---

**Construction:** 진정성을 확보하다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong eul hwakbo hada
**English translation:** to secure authenticity, to establish genuineness
**Source:** Parallel to 신빙성을 확보하다 (P2b 14.5), applied to 진정성
**Usage notes:** The agent actively establishes or ensures that their authenticity is recognized. Common in public communication contexts.

---

### 3.3 Causative: someone causes authenticity to be doubted or lost

**Construction:** 진정성을 의심하다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong eul uisimhada
**English translation:** to doubt the sincerity of, to question the authenticity of
**Source:** P2b (18.2)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively questions whether authenticity is present. This is a causative of epistemic state: the agent causes doubt about authenticity.

---

### 3.4 Persistence: authenticity continues to be present

**No dedicated persistence construction for 진정성 is attested in the CONTEXT.** The 있다 frame (진정성이 있다) describes a state but does not encode its continuation over time.

---

## Section 4: Credibility and Trust — 신뢰 (sinloe), 신빙성 (sinbyeongseong)

---

### 4.1 Inchoative: trust comes into being

**Construction:** 신뢰 관계가 형성되다
**Transliteration:** sinloe gwangyega hyeongseong doeda
**English translation:** a relationship of trust is formed, a trust-based relationship comes into being
**Source:** P2b (13.5), with 되다 as the passive/inchoative marker
**Usage notes:** The inchoative passive of 형성하다 (to form). Describes the emergence of a trust relationship. Common in organizational and diplomatic discourse.

---

### 4.2 Causative: someone builds or establishes trust

**Construction:** 신뢰를 쌓다
**Transliteration:** sinloereul ssada
**English translation:** to build trust, to accumulate trust
**Source:** P2b (13.1), P5 (Opposition 24)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively accumulates trust over time. 쌓다 (to pile up, to accumulate) encodes the gradual, incremental nature of trust-building. The most common causative construction for trust creation in the CONTEXT.

---

**Construction:** 신뢰 관계를 형성하다
**Transliteration:** sinloe gwangyereul hyeongseong hada
**English translation:** to form a relationship of trust, to establish a trust-based relationship
**Source:** P2b (13.5)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively creates a trust relationship. More formal than 신뢰를 쌓다; common in organizational and diplomatic discourse.

---

### 4.3 Causative: someone restores trust

**Construction:** 신뢰를 회복하다
**Transliteration:** sinloereul hoebokada
**English translation:** to restore trust, to recover credibility
**Source:** P2b (13.3)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively restores trust after it has been damaged. Encodes a return to a prior state rather than the creation of a new one. Common in political and organizational discourse.

---

### 4.4 Loss / reversal: trust is lost or collapses

**Construction:** 신뢰를 잃다
**Transliteration:** sinloereul ilta
**English translation:** to lose trust, to lose credibility
**Source:** P2b (13.2), P5 (Opposition 24)
**Usage notes:** The subject loses trust; the agent of loss may be the same entity (through its own actions) or external circumstances. Common in political and journalistic discourse.

---

**Construction:** 신뢰가 무너지다
**Transliteration:** sinloega muneoijida
**English translation:** trust collapses, credibility crumbles
**Source:** P2b (13.6)
**Usage notes:** Intransitive; trust is the subject. The metaphor of structural collapse (무너지다) presents the loss of trust as a sudden or catastrophic event rather than a gradual process. Contrasts with the gradual accumulation encoded by 쌓다.

---

### 4.5 Causative: someone betrays or destroys trust

**Construction:** 신뢰를 저버리다
**Transliteration:** sinloereul jeobeorida
**English translation:** to betray trust, to abandon trust
**Source:** P2b (13.4)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively betrays the trust placed in them. 저버리다 (to abandon, to betray) encodes a deliberate act of violation. Distinct from 잃다 in that betrayal implies agency and moral responsibility.

---

### 4.6 Causative: someone causes credibility to be established or secured

**Construction:** 신빙성을 확보하다
**Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong eul hwakbo hada
**English translation:** to secure credibility, to establish credibility
**Source:** P2b (14.5)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively ensures that a claim or source is credible. Common in academic, journalistic, and legal discourse.

---

### 4.7 Loss / reversal: credibility is undermined

**Construction:** 신빙성을 의심하다
**Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong eul uisimhada
**English translation:** to doubt the credibility of, to question the reliability of
**Source:** P2b (14.3)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively questions credibility, thereby initiating a process that may lead to its loss. This is a causative of epistemic state rather than a direct causative of loss.

---

## Section 5: Legal Validity — 유효 (yuhyo), 무효 (muhyo)

---

### 5.1 Inchoative: something comes to be valid

**No common inchoative construction for 유효 is attested in the CONTEXT.** Validity is typically asserted as a present state (유효하다, 계약이 유효하다) rather than as something that comes into being through a change of state. The process of making something valid is encoded causatively (see 5.3).

---

### 5.2 Inchoative: something becomes void

**Construction:** 계약이 무효가 되다
**Transliteration:** gyeyagi muhyoga doeda
**English translation:** the contract becomes void
**Source:** P2a (17.3), P2b (17.3), P4a (Construction 4), P5 (Opposition 21)
**Usage notes:** 되다 is the general inchoative verb. The subject (계약, contract) undergoes a change of state from valid to void. Common in legal discourse.

---

**Construction:** 선거가 무효가 되다
**Transliteration:** seongeoga muhyoga doeda
**English translation:** an election becomes void, an election is invalidated
**Source:** P2b (17.3)
**Usage notes:** Same construction applied to elections. Common in electoral and constitutional law discourse.

---

**Construction:** 무효 처리되다
**Transliteration:** muhyo cheoridoeda
**English translation:** to be processed as invalid, to be treated as void
**Source:** P2b (17.4)
**Usage notes:** Passive-administrative construction; the subject is treated as void through an official process. Encodes the institutional dimension of invalidation.

---

### 5.3 Causative: someone renders something void

**Construction:** 무효화하다
**Transliteration:** muhyohwa hada
**English translation:** to invalidate, to nullify, to void
**Source:** P1 (item 17), P2a (17.1), P4a (Construction 5), P5 (Opposition 25)
**Usage notes:** The -화하다 causative suffix encodes the agent's act of rendering something invalid. The most productive single-word causative for invalidation in the CONTEXT.

---

**Construction:** 계약을 무효로 하다
**Transliteration:** gyeyageul muhyoro hada
**English translation:** to render a contract void, to make something invalid
**Source:** P2b (17.2)
**Usage notes:** Periphrastic causative using the -로 frame with 하다. Functionally equivalent to 무효화하다 but more explicitly transitive in structure.

---

**Construction:** 무효를 선언하다
**Transliteration:** muhyoreul seoneonhada
**English translation:** to declare invalid, to declare null and void
**Source:** P2b (17.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent performs a formal speech act of declaration. Distinct from 무효화하다 in that the causation operates through a declarative act rather than a direct action.

---

### 5.4 Causative: someone legalizes something

**Construction:** 합법화하다
**Transliteration:** hapbeophwa hada
**English translation:** to legalize, to make lawful
**Source:** P2a (24.3), P4a (Construction 5), P5 (Opposition 25)
**Usage notes:** The -화하다 causative suffix encodes the agent's act of conferring legal status. Operates in the opposite direction from 무효화하다 within the legal domain.

---

### 5.5 Persistence: something remains valid

**Construction:** 여전히 유효하다
**Transliteration:** yeojeonhi yuhyohada
**English translation:** to still be valid, to remain in force
**Source:** P2b (16.4)
**Usage notes:** The adverb 여전히 (still, as before) encodes persistence of the valid state. Common in legal, diplomatic, and policy discourse when asserting that an agreement or principle has not lapsed. This is the primary persistence construction for 유효 in the CONTEXT.

---

### 5.6 Loss / reversal: validity expires

**Construction:** 유효 기간이 지나다
**Transliteration:** yuhyo gigani jinaeda
**English translation:** for the validity period to expire, to pass the expiration date
**Source:** P2b (16.1), P2a (16.4)
**Usage notes:** The subject is the validity period itself, which passes. This encodes the natural expiration of validity through the passage of time rather than through an agent's action. Distinct from 무효화하다, which encodes deliberate invalidation.

---

## Section 6: Legitimacy — 정당 (jeongdang)

---

### 6.1 Inchoative: something comes to have legitimacy

**No common inchoative construction for 정당 is attested in the CONTEXT.** Legitimacy is typically asserted as a present state (정당하다) or secured through an agent's action (see 6.2).

---

### 6.2 Causative: someone secures or establishes legitimacy

**Construction:** 정당성을 확보하다
**Transliteration:** jeongdangseong eul hwakbo hada
**English translation:** to secure legitimacy, to establish justification
**Source:** P2b (23.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively establishes or ensures legitimacy. Common in political, legal, and organizational discourse.

---

**Construction:** 정당화하다
**Transliteration:** jeongdanghwahada
**English translation:** to justify, to legitimize
**Source:** P1 (item 23), P2a (23.3), P2b (23.4)
**Usage notes:** The -화하다 causative suffix encodes the agent's act of providing justification. Transitive; takes the action or claim being justified as object (폭력을 정당화하다, to justify violence).

---

### 6.3 Loss / reversal: legitimacy is lost

**Construction:** 정당성을 잃다
**Transliteration:** jeongdangseong eul ilta
**English translation:** to lose legitimacy
**Source:** P2b (23.2)
**Usage notes:** The subject loses legitimacy through its own actions or through changed circumstances. Common in political discourse when describing the erosion of a government's or institution's justification.

---

## Section 7: Forgery and Counterfeiting — 위조 (wijo)

---

### 7.1 Inchoative: something comes to be determined as forged

**Construction:** 위조로 판명되다
**Transliteration:** wijoro panmyeong doeda
**English translation:** to be determined to be a forgery, to be found to be counterfeit
**Source:** P2b (21.2)
**Usage notes:** The -로 frame marks the resultant state; 판명되다 encodes the epistemic process of determination. Common in legal and journalistic discourse when the outcome of an authentication investigation is that something is forged.

---

### 7.2 Causative: someone creates a forgery

**Construction:** 위조하다
**Transliteration:** wijo hada
**English translation:** to forge, to counterfeit, to fabricate
**Source:** P1 (item 21), P1b (wi root, item 1), P2a (21.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively creates a false item. The primary causative construction for forgery in the CONTEXT.

---

### 7.3 Causative: someone disguises something as legitimate

**Construction:** 합법을 가장하다
**Transliteration:** hapbeobeul gajanghada
**English translation:** to disguise as legal, to masquerade as lawful
**Source:** P2b (24.4)
**Usage notes:** The agent presents something illegal as if it were legal. 가장하다 (to disguise, to pretend to be) encodes the causative act of creating a false appearance of legitimacy.

---

**Construction:** 위장하다
**Transliteration:** wijang hada
**English translation:** to disguise, to camouflage, to masquerade as
**Source:** P1b (wi root, item 6)
**Usage notes:** The agent conceals their true identity or nature. Broader than 가장하다; applies to identity, purpose, and nature as well as legal status.

---

## Section 8: Distortion and Misrepresentation — 왜곡 (waegok), 허위 (heowi)

---

### 8.1 Inchoative: something comes to be revealed as false

**Construction:** 허위임이 드러나다
**Transliteration:** heowiimi deureonada
**English translation:** it is revealed to be false, the falseness comes to light
**Source:** P2b (10.3)
**Usage notes:** Intransitive; the false nature of something emerges. Parallel in structure to 진실이 드러나다 (Section 2.1) but at the opposite pole.

---

### 8.2 Causative: someone distorts or falsifies

**Construction:** 왜곡하다
**Transliteration:** waegokada
**English translation:** to distort, to misrepresent
**Source:** P1 (item 26), P2b (2.3, 1.11)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively bends or alters something from its true form. Presupposes a prior truth that has been distorted.

---

**Construction:** 허위로 작성하다
**Transliteration:** heowiro jakseong hada
**English translation:** to fill out falsely, to prepare fraudulently
**Source:** P2b (10.2)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively creates a false document or record. The -로 frame encodes the manner (falsely) in which the action is performed.

---

**Construction:** 허위 사실을 유포하다
**Transliteration:** heowi sasilеul yupohada
**English translation:** to spread false information
**Source:** P2b (10.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively disseminates false information. Legally significant in Korean law as 허위 사실 유포.

---

## Section 9: Errors and Incorrectness — 오류 (oryu), 정확 (jeonghwak)

---

### 9.1 Inchoative: an error comes to be identified

**No common inchoative construction for 오류 is attested in the CONTEXT.** Errors are typically identified through the collocation 오류를 범하다 (to commit an error), which encodes the agent's action rather than a change of state.

---

### 9.2 Causative: someone commits an error

**Construction:** 오류를 범하다
**Transliteration:** oryureul beomhada
**English translation:** to commit an error, to commit a fallacy
**Source:** P1 (item 27)
**Usage notes:** The agent commits an error. 범하다 (to commit, to perpetrate) is the standard verb for this collocation. Common in academic, logical, and technical contexts.

---

### 9.3 Causative: someone improves accuracy

**Construction:** 정확성을 높이다
**Transliteration:** jeonghwakseong eul nopida
**English translation:** to improve accuracy, to raise the level of precision
**Source:** P4a (Construction 6), P4b (6)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively increases the degree of accuracy. 높이다 (to raise, to increase) encodes the causative act of improving a scalar property. Common in academic, technical, and administrative discourse.

---

### 9.4 Causative: someone ensures accuracy

**Construction:** 정확성을 기하다 / 정확을 기하다
**Transliteration:** jeonghwakseong eul gihada / jeonghwak eul gihada
**English translation:** to ensure accuracy, to aim for precision
**Source:** P2b (5.3, 5.4)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively aims to achieve or maintain accuracy. 기하다 (to aim for, to ensure) encodes intentional effort toward a standard. Common in journalistic, academic, and administrative discourse as a statement of commitment.

---

## Section 10: Essence and Underlying Reality — 본질 (bonjil), 실체 (silche), 실제 (silje)

---

### 10.1 Inchoative: the true nature comes to be revealed

**Construction:** 실체가 드러나다
**Transliteration:** silchega deureonada
**English translation:** the true nature is revealed, the real substance comes to light
**Source:** Parallel to 진실이 드러나다 (P2b 1.6), applied to 실체
**Usage notes:** Intransitive; the true nature of something emerges. Often carries a critical or accusatory tone in journalistic and political discourse, as in 실체를 드러내다 (see 10.2).

---

### 10.2 Causative: someone reveals the true nature

**Construction:** 실체를 드러내다
**Transliteration:** silchereul deureonaeda
**English translation:** to reveal one's true nature, to expose the true substance
**Source:** P2b (20.1)
**Usage notes:** Transitive; the agent actively exposes what something truly is. Common in journalistic and political discourse, often with a critical tone.

---

**Construction:** 본질을 파악하다
**Transliteration:** bonjileul paakada
**English translation:** to grasp the essence, to understand the essential nature
**Source:** P2b (19.1)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively comprehends what something fundamentally is. Common in philosophical, analytical, and educational discourse.

---

### 10.3 Causative: someone obscures the true nature

**Construction:** 본질을 흐리다
**Transliteration:** bonjileul heurida
**English translation:** to obscure the essence, to muddy the essential issue
**Source:** P2b (19.2)
**Usage notes:** The agent actively obscures or distracts from what is fundamentally at issue. Common in journalistic and political discourse when accusing someone of deflecting from the core matter.

---

### 10.4 Inchoative: something deviates from its essential nature

**Construction:** 본질에서 벗어나다
**Transliteration:** bonjile seo beoseonada
**English translation:** to deviate from the essence, to stray from the essential point
**Source:** P2b (19.3)
**Usage notes:** The subject moves away from what is fundamentally at issue. This is an inchoative of departure rather than of arrival; the change of state is one of increasing distance from the essential. Common in analytical and argumentative discourse.

---

## Section 11: Lies and Deception — 거짓말 (geojitmal)

---

### 11.1 Inchoative: a lie comes to be exposed

**Construction:** 거짓말이 탄로나다
**Transliteration:** geojitmali tallonaeda
**English translation:** a lie is exposed, a lie comes out
**Source:** P2b (11.6)

## Observations on Patterns and Asymmetries

**1. Persistence constructions are sparse.** Across the full vocabulary, only 여전히 유효하다 (to still be valid) and 거짓말을 밥 먹듯 하다 (to lie habitually) are attested as dedicated persistence constructions. Other terms express persistence through the continued application of stative predicates (신빙성이 있다, 진정성이 있다) rather than through constructions that specifically encode continuation over time.

**2. Inchoative constructions cluster at the disclosure pole.** The most productive inchoative constructions involve truth or falseness coming to light: 진실이 드러나다, 사실로 밝혀지다, 허위임이 드러나다, 거짓말이 탄로나다, 위조로 판명되다. The shared verb 드러나다 (to emerge, to come to light) and the passive 밝혀지다 (to be revealed) are the primary inchoative verbs in this domain.

**3. The 드러나다 / 드러내다 pair encodes the intransitive/transitive distinction systematically.** 드러나다 (intransitive: truth emerges) and 드러내다 (transitive: agent reveals truth) form a productive pair across multiple terms: 진실이 드러나다 / 진실을 밝히다, 실체가 드러나다 / 실체를 드러내다, 허위임이 드러나다. This pair is the primary structural means of distinguishing spontaneous disclosure from agentive disclosure.

**4. The -화하다 causative is restricted to Sino-Korean bases and operates in opposing directions.** 무효화하다 (to invalidate) and 합법화하다 (to legalize) both use the -화하다 suffix but produce opposite legal effects. 정당화하다 (to justify) is a third member of this set. No native Korean truth-domain terms participate in this pattern, consistent with the restriction noted in P4a.

**5. Trust constructions form the most elaborated dynamic paradigm.** 신뢰 participates in a fuller set of dynamic constructions than any other term: creation (쌓다), restoration (회복하다), betrayal (저버리다), loss (잃다), and collapse (무너지다). The metaphors are structurally varied: accumulation (쌓다), recovery (회복하다), abandonment (저버리다), and structural collapse (무너지다). No other term in the CONTEXT has an equivalently elaborated dynamic paradigm.

**6. Several terms lack common inchoative constructions.** 진정성, 신빙성, 정당성, 오류, and 정확 do not participate in common inchoative constructions in the CONTEXT. These terms are evaluated as present or absent (있다/없다), as scalar (높다/낮다), or as caused by an agent, but the change of state into the relevant condition is not lexicalized as a distinct construction.
