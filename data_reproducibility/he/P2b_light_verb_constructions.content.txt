# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Multi-Word Constructions Analysis

---

### BASE FORM 1: אמת (emet) "truth"

#### Construction 1.1
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** אמת אחת
**Transliteration:** emet akhat
**English translation:** one truth, a single truth
**Usage notes:** Used to emphasize that there is only one truth in a matter, often in argumentative or philosophical contexts. The numeral "one" modifies emet to stress singularity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** אמת מוחלטת
**Transliteration:** emet mukhлetet
**English translation:** absolute truth
**Usage notes:** Common collocation using the adjective "absolute" to characterize truth as complete and unqualified. Frequent in philosophical and emphatic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.3
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** אמת מרה
**Transliteration:** emet marah
**English translation:** bitter truth
**Usage notes:** Fixed expression using the adjective "bitter" to characterize an unpleasant but true fact. Common in contexts where acknowledging truth is difficult.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.4
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** לומר את האמת
**Transliteration:** lomar et ha-emet
**English translation:** to tell the truth
**Usage notes:** Standard verbal construction with "say/tell" for the act of truth-telling. Very common in all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.5
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** לדבר אמת
**Transliteration:** ledaber emet
**English translation:** to speak truth
**Usage notes:** Alternative verbal construction using "speak" rather than "tell," slightly more formal or literary. Common in moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.6
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** לגלות את האמת
**Transliteration:** legalot et ha-emet
**English translation:** to reveal/discover the truth
**Usage notes:** Construction with "reveal" emphasizing the uncovering or discovery of truth. Common in investigative and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.7
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** האמת היא ש...
**Transliteration:** ha-emet hi she...
**English translation:** the truth is that...
**Usage notes:** Fixed introductory formula using copula "is" followed by complementizer "that." Very common for introducing what speaker considers the real truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.8
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** זאת האמת
**Transliteration:** zot ha-emet
**English translation:** that's the truth, this is the truth
**Usage notes:** Demonstrative construction using "this/that" to point to or emphasize a truth claim. Common in emphatic assertions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.9
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** אמת לאמיתה
**Transliteration:** emet la-amitah
**English translation:** truth in its trueness, the real truth
**Usage notes:** Emphatic construction using emet with prepositional phrase containing related form amitah. Emphasizes genuine or complete truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.10
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** חצי אמת
**Transliteration:** khetsi emet
**English translation:** half-truth
**Usage notes:** Construction with "half" to characterize a statement as partially true but misleading. Common in evaluating deceptive communication.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.11
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** גרעין של אמת
**Transliteration:** gar'in shel emet
**English translation:** kernel/grain of truth
**Usage notes:** Metaphorical construction using "kernel/grain" to indicate a small element of truth in something otherwise false or exaggerated.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.12
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Example:** רגע של אמת
**Transliteration:** rega shel emet
**English translation:** moment of truth
**Usage notes:** Fixed expression borrowed from Spanish/English usage, referring to a decisive or revealing moment. Common in dramatic or evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary Observations

### High-Frequency Construction Types:

1. **Demonstrative constructions** (ze X, zot X): Extremely common across many base forms for pointing to or asserting truth values.

2. **Verbal constructions with common verbs**: Particularly with lomar "say/tell," la'asot "do/make," ledaber "speak" — these create standard ways of expressing truth-related actions.

3. **Intensification with me'od "very"**: Productive pattern for emphasizing degree across many adjectives.

4. **Negation with lo**: Simple and productive for creating antonyms or denials.

5. **Prepositional phrases with be-**: Very productive for creating adverbial expressions (be-emet, be-tsedek, be-yosher, be-khenut, be-vadai, etc.).

6. **Complementizer constructions with she-**: Common for embedding truth-evaluations in clauses (nakhon she-, vadai she-, domeh she-, etc.).

7. **Comparative and superlative constructions**: Standard patterns with yoter "more" and ha-khi "most."

### Notable Patterns:

- **Source evaluation collocations**: Multiple terms (amin, meheyман, mefukpak) commonly collocate with makor "source" for evaluating information credibility.

- **Legal register collocations**: Several terms show specialized legal uses (ed sheker/amin "false/reliable witness," ziyuf mismakhim "document forgery").

- **Personal pronoun constructions**: Very common with epistemic terms (ani batuakh "I'm sure," atah tsodek "you're right," atah mut'eh "you're mistaken").

- **Fixed expressions with biblical resonance**: Several constructions (yosher lev, tsedek tsedek tirdof) carry biblical or liturgical associations.

### Asymmetries:

- Some base forms (emet, nakhon, batuakh, safek) show very high productivity in multi-word constructions.
- Others (ken, lo nakhon, ba-emet) show more limited extension, often because they already function as fixed expressions.
- Loanword otenti shows full integration into Hebrew construction patterns.
- Some semantic domains (doubt/certainty, correctness) show richer constructional elaboration than others.
