1. **òtítọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** òtítọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *títọ́* “straight, correct, true” with nominal prefix *ò-*  
   **Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, accuracy  

   Used for the truth of a statement, the correctness of an account, or the rightness of a matter more generally. It is very common in everyday speech as well as in religious, moral, and philosophical discussion.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **òtọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** òtọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** nominal/adjectival form associated with “truth, correctness, genuineness”  
   **Typical English translations:** truth, true thing, correctness, genuineness  

   This form is used in many contexts for what is true or genuine, and also appears in contrasts with falsity or deception. In actual usage it can overlap strongly with *òtítọ́*, though distributions may vary by speaker and expression.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **òtítọ́ ni**  
   **Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “it is truth / it is true”  
   **Typical English translations:** it is true, that is true, indeed true  

   This fixed expression is used to affirm the truth of a statement or report. It commonly occurs in conversation when agreeing that something said is accurate or correct.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **irọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** irọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** lie, falsehood  
   **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth  

   Used for a false statement, deception, or what is not true. It is one of the basic opposites of *òtítọ́* in ordinary moral and evaluative discourse.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **irọ́ ni**  
   **Transliteration:** irọ́ ni  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “it is a lie / it is falsehood”  
   **Typical English translations:** it is a lie, that is false, that is untrue  

   This expression is used to reject a statement as false or deceptive. It is common in direct conversational evaluation of claims, accusations, and reports.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **jẹ́ òtítọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** jẹ́ òtítọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “be truth / be true”  
   **Typical English translations:** be true, be correct, be genuine  

   This verbal expression is used predicatively for statements, rumors, claims, and sometimes appearances or identities. It is a standard way to say that something turns out to be true or valid.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **kì í ṣe òtítọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** kì í ṣe òtítọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “is not truth”  
   **Typical English translations:** is not true, is incorrect, is false  

   This is a common negative evaluative expression for denying the truth of a statement or report. It is less accusatory than directly calling something *irọ́* in some contexts, since it can simply mark inaccuracy.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **títọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** títọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** straightness; correctness; truth  
   **Typical English translations:** straight, correct, true, proper  

   The form is used both literally for straightness and more abstractly for correctness or truth. This overlap supports uses in evaluating conduct, procedure, and statements as proper or right.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **tọ́**  
   **Transliteration:** tọ́  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** be right, be correct, be proper  
   **Typical English translations:** be right, be correct, be proper  

   This is a common predicate for saying that a person, action, judgment, or statement is right or correct. It is widely used in everyday argument, moral evaluation, and practical decision-making.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **kò tọ́**  
    **Transliteration:** kò tọ́  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “it is not right / not correct”  
    **Typical English translations:** it is not right, it is wrong, it is improper  

    This expression is used for moral wrongness, procedural impropriety, or factual incorrectness depending on context. It is very common in evaluating behavior as well as judgments and decisions.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **dájú**  
    **Transliteration:** dájú  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** be certain, be sure, be definite  
    **Typical English translations:** be certain, be sure, be definite, be reliable  

    This item is used when something is certain, settled, or dependable. It often evaluates the reliability of information, the firmness of evidence, or the trustworthiness of an expectation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **ó dájú**  
    **Transliteration:** ó dájú  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “it is certain / it is sure”  
    **Typical English translations:** it is certain, surely, definitely  

    This fixed expression commonly marks confidence in the truth or likelihood of a statement. It is frequent in both ordinary conversation and more formal persuasive speech.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **àdéhùn**  
    **Transliteration:** àdéhùn  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** agreement, covenant, contract  
    **Typical English translations:** agreement, contract, covenant  

    In legal and social contexts this refers to a binding agreement whose validity and faithfulness can be evaluated. It is relevant where genuineness, enforceability, or correctness of representation is at issue.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **ẹ̀rí**  
    **Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** evidence, testimony, proof  
    **Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, testimony  

    This is used for evidence supporting the truth of a claim or the reality of an event. It is common in legal, religious, and everyday argumentative contexts where reliability and verification matter.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **ẹlẹ́rìí**  
    **Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “owner/bearer of testimony”  
    **Typical English translations:** witness, testifier  

    This refers to a witness whose reliability or credibility may be evaluated. It is especially common in legal, religious, and narrative contexts involving attestation of truth.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **ìdánimọ̀**  
    **Transliteration:** ìdánimọ̀  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** identification, recognition  
    **Typical English translations:** identification, identity verification, recognition  

    This is used for establishing who or what something fundamentally is, especially in administrative or legal settings. It is relevant where authenticity, identity, or correct attribution is being checked.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **ojúlówó**  
    **Transliteration:** ojúlówó  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** conventional lexical item; often associated with genuineness/authenticity  
    **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, original, real  

    This is commonly used for genuine goods, authentic documents, or a real/original item as opposed to an imitation. It is especially natural in commercial and evaluative contexts involving authenticity.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **èké**  
    **Transliteration:** èké  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** falsehood, deceit  
    **Typical English translations:** lie, deceit, falsity, fake  

    This item is used for falsity and deception, and in some contexts can modify things understood as fake or deceptive. It appears in moral and religious discourse as well as ordinary accusation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **òdodo**  
    **Transliteration:** òdodo  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** justice, righteousness, fairness  
    **Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, fairness, rightness  

    This is used in moral, legal, and religious contexts for what is just or righteous. It evaluates conduct, judgment, and social arrangements in terms of rightness rather than mere factual truth.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **olódodo**  
    **Transliteration:** olódodo  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “one possessing justice/righteousness”  
    **Typical English translations:** righteous person, just person, honest person  

    This refers to a person regarded as upright, just, or morally truthful. It is used in moral and religious evaluation of character and conduct.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **ìwà rere**  
    **Transliteration:** ìwà rere  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “good character / good conduct”  
    **Typical English translations:** good character, good conduct, virtue  

    This fixed expression evaluates a person’s conduct and moral quality. While not specifically about factual truth, it is central in Yoruba usage for judging whether behavior is right and commendable.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **ìwà búburú**  
    **Transliteration:** ìwà búburú  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “bad character / bad conduct”  
    **Typical English translations:** bad character, bad conduct, vice  

    This expression is used to condemn conduct or character as morally wrong. It often contrasts with *ìwà rere* in ethical evaluation of persons and actions.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **àṣìṣe**  
    **Transliteration:** àṣìṣe  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** mistake, error  
    **Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault  

    This is used when a statement, action, calculation, or representation is incorrect. It is common in practical, educational, and administrative contexts where accuracy is being assessed.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **péye**  
    **Transliteration:** péye  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** complete, exact, precise  
    **Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate, complete  

    This item is used for precision and exactness in description, measurement, or performance. It is especially relevant when evaluating whether a representation or account matches what it should.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **bá mu**  
    **Transliteration:** bá mu  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “fit / match / correspond”  
    **Typical English translations:** fit, match, correspond, be appropriate  

    This expression is used when one thing matches another, whether in appearance, description, evidence, or suitability. It can evaluate resemblance, consistency, or appropriateness depending on context.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
