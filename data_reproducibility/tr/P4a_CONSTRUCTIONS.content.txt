# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] [ADJ-TRUTH]** or **[NP] [ADJ-TRUTH] olmak** (to be)

Turkish uses simple juxtaposition for predication in nominal sentences (present tense, 3rd person), or the copula *olmak* for other tenses/persons. Truth-domain adjectives function as predicates directly.

### Examples
- Bu doğru. → Bu doğru. → "This is true."
- Hikâye gerçek değil. → Hikâye gerçek değil. → "The story is not real."

### Notes
Turkish allows zero copula in present tense 3rd person equational sentences. The copula *olmak* appears in past tense (*doğruydu* "was true"), future (*doğru olacak* "will be true"), and with non-3rd person subjects.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] [ADJ-TRUTH] değil** or **[NP] [NEG-ADJ-TRUTH]**

Turkish forms predicate negation with the negative particle *değil* following the predicate. Alternatively, privative suffix *-sız/-siz/-suz/-süz* creates negative adjectives.

### Examples
- Bu yanlış değil. → Bu yanlış değil. → "This is not wrong."
- Kaynak güvenilmez. → Kaynak güvenilmez. → "The source is unreliable."

### Notes
*Değil* is the standard predicate negator. Privative *-sız* creates lexicalized negative adjectives (*güvenilir* → *güvenilmez* "unreliable," *hak* → *haksız* "unjust"). Some bases lack productive privative forms.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH] [NOUN]**

Turkish places modifying adjectives directly before the noun they modify, without agreement marking. Truth-domain adjectives freely modify nouns.

### Examples
- doğru bilgi → doğru bilgi → "correct information"
- sahte para → sahte para → "counterfeit money"

### Notes
Turkish adjectives are invariant—no agreement for number, case, or definiteness. Multiple adjectives stack prenominally. Some combinations are lexicalized collocations (*gerçek hayat* "real life," *kesin karar* "final decision").

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH-ROOT] + -leş/-laş + (tense/person)**

The suffix *-leş/-laş* derives inchoative verbs meaning "to become X" from adjectival bases. Productive with quality adjectives in the truth domain.

### Examples
- gerçekleşmek → gerçekleşmek → "to come true, to be realized"
- kesinleşmek → kesinleşmek → "to become definite, to be finalized"

### Notes
Highly productive with quality adjectives (*sağlamlaşmak* "to become solid," *doğallaşmak* "to become natural"). Less productive with relational adjectives. The pattern creates intransitive verbs of change of state.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**[INCHOATIVE-VERB-ROOT] + -tır/-tir/-tur/-tür + (tense/person)**

Turkish forms causatives from inchoative verbs using *-tır/-tir/-tur/-tür*, creating "make X become Y" meanings. Applies to truth-domain inchoatives.

### Examples
- gerçekleştirmek → gerçekleştirmek → "to realize, to make real"
- kesinleştirmek → kesinleştirmek → "to finalize, to make definite"

### Notes
The causative suffix attaches to inchoative stems (*gerçekleş-* + *-tir* → *gerçekleştir-*). Creates transitive verbs. Also applies to other verb types: *doğrulamak* "to verify" uses denominative *-la* + causative semantics.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH-ROOT] + -lık/-lik/-luk/-lük**

The suffix *-lık/-lik/-luk/-lük* derives abstract nouns denoting qualities or states from adjectival bases. Extremely productive in the truth domain.

### Examples
- doğruluk → doğruluk → "truth, correctness, honesty"
- güvenilirlik → güvenilirlik → "reliability, trustworthiness"

### Notes
This is the primary abstract nominalizer in Turkish, showing vowel harmony. Highly productive across all truth-domain adjectives (*gerçeklik* "reality," *kesinlik* "certainty," *tutarlılık* "consistency"). Creates mass nouns referring to qualities.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**[NOUN/ADJ-ROOT] + -cı/-ci/-cu/-cü** (agentive/adherent)
**[VERB-ROOT] + -ıcı/-ici/-ucu/-ücü** (agentive from verbs)

Turkish derives agent nouns using *-cı/-ci* (from nominals) or *-ıcı/-ici* (from verbs). In truth domain, creates "liar," "truth-seeker," etc.

### Examples
- yalancı → yalancı → "liar" (from *yalan* "lie")
- yanıltıcı → yanıltıcı → "misleading (thing/person)" (from *yanılt-* "to mislead")

### Notes
*-cı/-ci* attaches to nouns/adjectives to create agent/adherent nouns (*gerçekçi* "realist," *şüpheci* "skeptic"). *-ıcı/-ici* attaches to verb stems to create agentive adjectives/nouns (*inandırıcı* "convincing"). Productivity varies by semantic class.

---

## 8. VERBAL PHRASE CONSTRUCTIONS (LIGHT VERBS)

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-NOUN/ADJ] + [LIGHT VERB]**

Turkish extensively uses light verbs (*olmak* "be," *etmek* "do," *yapmak* "make," *söylemek* "say") to create verbal phrases from truth-domain nominals and adjectives.

### Examples
- yalan söylemek → yalan söylemek → "to tell a lie, to lie"
- haksızlık etmek → haksızlık etmek → "to wrong someone, to commit injustice"

### Notes
Common light verbs include: *olmak* (stative), *etmek* (action, formal), *yapmak* (action, colloquial), *söylemek* (speech acts), *vermek* (giving), *almak* (taking). Many are semi-lexicalized (*hak vermek* "to agree with," *hak yemek* "to violate rights").

---

## 9. MANNER ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH-ROOT] + -ca/-ce** or **[NOUN-ROOT] + -en/-an**

Turkish derives manner adverbs using *-ca/-ce* (from adjectives) or *-en/-an* (from certain nouns). Also uses ablative case *-dan/-den* lexicalized as adverbs.

### Examples
- dürüstçe → dürüstçe → "honestly, in an honest manner"
- gerçekten → gerçekten → "really, truly" (ablative lexicalized)

### Notes
*-ca/-ce* creates manner adverbs from adjectives (*doğruca* "directly"). Ablative case *-dan/-den* lexicalizes as adverbs (*doğrudan* "directly," *gerçekten* "really"). Instrumental *-la/-le* also lexicalizes (*kesinlikle* "certainly," *yanlışlıkla* "by mistake").

---

## 10. INTENSIFICATION AND EMPHASIS

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH] + [EMPHATIC PARTICLE/REDUPLICATION]**

Turkish uses particles (*çok* "very," *tam* "exactly"), reduplication, and alliterative compounds for emphasis in truth domain.

### Examples
- çok doğru → çok doğru → "very true"
- doğru dürüst → doğru dürüst → "proper, decent" (alliterative compound)

### Notes
Standard intensifiers (*çok*, *tam*, *son derece*) apply freely. Reduplicative/alliterative compounds are lexically specific (*doğru düzgün* "proper," *yalan dolan* "lies and deceit"). Question particle *mu/mı/mu/mü* creates confirmation questions (*doğru mu?* "is it true?").

---

## 11. PRIVATIVE DERIVATION

### Pattern
**[NOUN/ADJ-ROOT] + -sız/-siz/-suz/-süz**

The privative suffix creates adjectives meaning "without X" or "lacking X." Productive in truth domain for creating negative counterparts.

### Examples
- şüphesiz → şüphesiz → "doubtless, without doubt"
- hatasız → hatasız → "flawless, without error"

### Notes
Highly productive for creating negative adjectives (*yanlışsız* "without error," *kuşkusuz* "doubtless"). Some forms lexicalize as adverbs (*şüphesiz* "undoubtedly"). Contrasts with *değil* negation (predicative) and negative adjectives like *güvenilmez* (lexicalized).

---

## 12. RESULTATIVE/OUTCOME CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ-TRUTH] + çıkmak** (to emerge/turn out)

The verb *çıkmak* "to come out, emerge" combines with truth-domain adjectives to express outcomes of verification or revelation.

### Examples
- doğru çıkmak → doğru çıkmak → "to turn out to be true"
- yalan çıkmak → yalan çıkmak → "to turn out to be a lie"

### Notes
*Çıkmak* constructions indicate that something has been revealed or proven to have a particular truth status. Common with evaluative adjectives (*haklı çıkmak* "to be proven right," *sahte çıkmak* "to turn out fake"). Implies prior uncertainty resolved.

---

## 13. DENOMINATIVE VERBALIZATION

### Pattern
**[NOUN/ADJ-ROOT] + -la/-le + (tense/person)**

The suffix *-la/-le* derives verbs from nouns and adjectives. In truth domain, creates verbs of verification, falsification, and related actions.

### Examples
- doğrulamak → doğrulamak → "to verify, to confirm"
- yalanlamak → yalanlamak → "to deny, to refute"

### Notes
Productive denominative verbalizer (*yanlışlamak* "to falsify," *kuşkulanmak* "to doubt"). Creates transitive or intransitive verbs depending on base semantics. Often combines with reflexive *-n* (*şüphelenmek* "to suspect" from *şüphele-n-*).

---

## 14. FIXED COLLOCATIONS WITH BODY/SPATIAL TERMS

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-ADJ] + [BODY/SPATIAL NOUN]**

Turkish uses body part and spatial terms metaphorically in truth-domain expressions, creating semi-fixed collocations.

### Examples
- gerçek yüz → gerçek yüz → "true face, real nature"
- doğru yol → doğru yol → "right path, correct way"

### Notes
Common patterns include *gerçek yüz* "true face" (character), *gerçek kimlik* "true identity," *doğru yol* "right path" (moral/spatial). These are semi-lexicalized metaphorical extensions. *Yüz* "face" and *yol* "path" are productive in such constructions.

---

## 15. EVALUATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *BULMAK* AND *GÖRMEK*

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-ADJ] + bulmak/görmek** (to find/see)

Verbs of perception and judgment (*bulmak* "to find," *görmek* "to see") combine with truth-domain adjectives to express evaluative judgments.

### Examples
- doğru bulmak → doğru bulmak → "to find correct, to deem appropriate"
- şüpheli görünmek → şüpheli görünmek → "to look suspicious"

### Notes
*Bulmak* "to find" expresses considered judgment (*inandırıcı bulmak* "to find convincing"). *Görünmek* "to appear, seem" expresses subjective impression (*doğal görünmek* "to look natural"). These are productive patterns for epistemic evaluation.
