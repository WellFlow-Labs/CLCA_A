## 1. ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**X [egia / gezurra / zuzena / okerra] da**  
‘X is true / a lie / correct / wrong’

### Examples
- **Hori egia da.** → — → “That is true.”
- **Erantzuna okerra da.** → — → “The answer is wrong.”

### Notes
Basque commonly uses a predicate noun/adjective plus the copula **da** for truth/correctness attribution. Definite forms like **zuzena / okerra** are especially common in evaluative contexts (“the correct/wrong one”).

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**X ez da [egia / zuzena / benetakoa]**  
‘X is not true / not correct / not real’

### Examples
- **Hori ez da egia.** → — → “That is not true.”
- **Albiste hori ez da benetakoa.** → — → “That news item is not real/genuine.”

### Notes
Negation is formed with **ez** before the copular form. Basque often prefers simple copular negation rather than a special antonym construction, though antonyms like **faltsu** or **oker** also occur.

### Pattern

‘verify/make true-confirmed’ / ‘correct, make right’

### Examples

## 8. TRUTH-ASSESSMENT CLAUSE
### Pattern
**[proposition] + egia da / egia ote den**

### Examples
- **Egia da berandu iritsi zela.** → — → “It is true that he/she arrived late.”
- **Ez dakit egia ote den.** → — → “I don’t know whether it is true.”

### Notes
Basque has a very common clausal construction for asserting or questioning truth status. **egia da** affirms/concedes truth; **egia ote den** embeds uncertainty or inquiry.

## 9. TRUTH / LIE LIGHT-VERB CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**[egia / gezurra] + verb** such as **esan, bilatu, jakin, aurkitu**

### Examples
- **Egia esan, nekatuta nago.** → — → “To tell the truth, I’m tired.”
- **Gezurra esan du.** → — → “He/she told a lie.”

### Notes
Basque frequently packages truth-domain meanings in noun + verb constructions rather than single verbs. **egia** combines with verbs of saying, seeking, knowing, and finding; **gezurra** most centrally with **esan** “say.”

## 10. EMPHATIC DEGREE CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**[egia / gezur] + hutsa / borobila / erdi**

### Examples
- **Hori egia hutsa da.** → — → “That is pure truth.”
- **Hori gezur borobila da.** → — → “That is an outright lie.”

### Notes
These are compact evaluative constructions for totality or partiality of truth/falsity. **hutsa** intensifies purity, **borobila** marks completeness/emphasis, and **erdi** gives “half-truth/half-lie.”
