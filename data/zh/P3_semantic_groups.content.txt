# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## METHODOLOGY NOTE

This analysis identifies natural groupings based on **observed usage patterns** in the P1 and P2 data. Groupings reflect how terms are actually used, contrasted, and combined in Chinese discourse, not imposed conceptual categories. Some terms appear in multiple groups; some remain ungrouped.

---

## GROUP 1: AUTHENTICITY AND GENUINENESS EVALUATION

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether objects, products, or representations are genuine versus counterfeit, original versus imitation. These terms commonly appear in commercial contexts (distinguishing real from fake goods), art authentication (original works versus copies), and verification of material objects. They focus on the physical or material authenticity of things rather than the truth of statements.

**Representative expressions**:
- 真 (zhēn) - genuine, authentic, real [when modifying physical objects]
- 假 (jiǎ) - fake, counterfeit, artificial
- 真品 (zhēnpǐn) - genuine article, authentic product
- 真迹 (zhēnjì) - authentic work (of art/calligraphy), original
- 真丝 (zhēnsī) - real silk, genuine silk
- 真皮 (zhēnpí) - genuine leather
- 正版 (zhèngbǎn) - legitimate edition, authorized version
- 正品 (zhèngpǐn) - genuine product, authentic goods
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) - genuine goods at fair prices
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) - true or false, genuine or fake, authenticity
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) - authenticity, genuineness vs. falseness
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) - pass off the fake as genuine
- 辨别真假 (biànbié zhēnjiǎ) - to distinguish genuine from fake

**Typical contexts**: Commerce, art collecting, product authentication, anti-counterfeiting

**Key contrasts**: Genuine/original vs. counterfeit/imitation; authorized vs. pirated

## GROUP 2: FACTUAL CORRESPONDENCE AND REALITY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether statements, descriptions, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs or reality. These terms appear in investigative, journalistic, and analytical contexts where the concern is whether claims match what actually happened or exists. They emphasize correspondence between representation and reality rather than moral or normative correctness.

**Representative expressions**:
- 真实 (zhēnshí) - true, real, authentic [of statements/situations]
- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) - truth, the real facts, the actual situation
- 事实 (shìshí) - fact, reality, truth
- 实际 (shíjì) - actual, practical, realistic
- 实情 (shíqíng) - actual situation, true facts, real circumstances
- 符合 (fúhé) - conform to, accord with, correspond to
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) - authenticity, truthfulness, veracity
- 真实度 (zhēnshídù) - degree of authenticity, level of realism
- 揭露真相 (jiēlù zhēnxiàng) - to reveal the truth
- 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) - to ascertain the truth
- 隐瞒真相 (yǐnmán zhēnxiàng) - to conceal the truth
- 还原真相 (huányuán zhēnxiàng) - to restore the truth
- 真相大白 (zhēn xiàng dà bái) - the truth comes to light
- 符合事实 (fúhé shìshí) - conform to facts
- 不符合事实 (bù fúhé shìshí) - not conform to facts
- 事实上 (shìshí shàng) - in fact, actually
- 实际上 (shíjì shàng) - actually, in fact, in reality
- 客观事实 (kèguān shìshí) - objective facts
- 铁的事实 (tiě de shìshí) - hard facts, irrefutable facts

**Typical contexts**: Journalism, investigation, historical research, verification of claims

**Key contrasts**: What actually happened vs. what is claimed; reality vs. appearance; facts vs. opinions

## GROUP 3: CORRECTNESS OF ANSWERS AND JUDGMENTS

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether answers, responses, judgments, or solutions are correct versus incorrect. These terms are extremely common in educational contexts (grading, testing), problem-solving situations, and everyday assessments of whether someone's answer or judgment is right or wrong. They focus on binary correctness rather than degrees of accuracy or moral rightness.

**Representative expressions**:
- 对 (duì) - correct, right, yes
- 错 (cuò) - wrong, incorrect, mistaken, error
- 正确 (zhèngquè) - correct, right, proper
- 对不对？(duì bu duì?) - Right? Is that correct?
- 你说得对 (nǐ shuō de duì) - You're right
- 你错了 (nǐ cuò le) - You're wrong
- 哪里错了？(nǎlǐ cuò le?) - Where is the mistake?
- 对答如流 (duì dá rú liú) - answer fluently
- 改正 (gǎizhèng) - correct, amend
- 纠正 (jiūzhèng) - correct, rectify
- 修正 (xiūzhèng) - revise, amend, correct

**Typical contexts**: Education, testing, problem-solving, everyday judgments

**Key contrasts**: Correct answer vs. wrong answer; right judgment vs. mistaken judgment

---

## GROUP 4: PRECISION AND ACCURACY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate the degree of exactness or precision in measurements, statements, descriptions, or representations. These terms commonly appear in technical, scientific, and contexts requiring exactness, where the concern is not just whether something is true or false, but how precisely it matches a standard or target. They emphasize gradable accuracy rather than binary correctness.

**Representative expressions**:
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) - accurate, precise, exact
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) - accuracy, precision
- 准确度 (zhǔnquèdù) - degree of accuracy, accuracy level
- 精确 (jīngquè) - precise, exact, accurate [more technical than 准确]
- 正确地 (zhèngquè de) - correctly, rightly [manner adverb]
- 准确地 (zhǔnquè de) - accurately, precisely [manner adverb]

**Typical contexts**: Scientific measurement, technical specifications, data analysis, quality control

**Key contrasts**: Precise vs. approximate; exact vs. rough; high accuracy vs. low accuracy

## GROUP 5: HONESTY AND SINCERITY IN CONDUCT

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate the honesty, sincerity, or truthfulness of persons, their statements, or their conduct. These terms carry strong moral connotations and are central to character evaluation in Chinese culture. They focus on the moral quality of being truthful and sincere rather than the factual accuracy of statements, and often appear in contexts of moral judgment, character assessment, and interpersonal relationships.

**Representative expressions**:
- 诚 (chéng) - sincere, honest, true
- 诚实 (chéngshí) - honest, truthful, sincere
- 诚实守信 (chéngshí shǒuxìn) - honest and trustworthy
- 诚实可靠 (chéngshí kěkào) - honest and reliable
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) - sincere, genuine
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) - sincere, wholehearted, genuine intention
- 真挚 (zhēnzhì) - sincere, genuine, heartfelt
- 真心实意 (zhēn xīn shí yì) - wholehearted, sincere, genuine
- 老实 (lǎoshí) - honest, frank; well-behaved
- 正直 (zhèngzhí) - upright, honest, righteous
- 正派 (zhèngpài) - decent, proper, upright
- 开诚布公 (kāi chéng bù gōng) - be frank and open
- 精诚所至，金石为开 (jīng chéng suǒ zhì, jīn shí wèi kāi) - utmost sincerity can work miracles
- 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) - to tell the truth, to be honest
- 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō) - honestly speaking
- 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō) - speak the plain truth
- 坦白说 (tǎnbái shuō) - frankly speaking

**Typical contexts**: Moral evaluation, character assessment, interpersonal relationships, ethical discourse

**Key contrasts**: Honest vs. deceitful; sincere vs. hypocritical; truthful vs. lying

---

## GROUP 6: DECEPTION AND FALSIFICATION

**Definition**: Terms used to describe deliberate acts of deceiving, lying, or creating false representations. These terms carry negative moral connotations and focus on intentional dishonesty rather than mere error or mistake. They commonly appear in contexts of moral condemnation, legal proceedings, and discussions of fraud or deception.

**Representative expressions**:
- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) - deceive, cheat, defraud
- 欺骗感情 (qīpiàn gǎnqíng) - to deceive someone's feelings
- 谎言 (huǎngyán) - lie, falsehood, untruth
- 说谎 (shuō huǎng) - to lie, to tell lies
- 撒谎 (sā huǎng) - to lie, to tell lies
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) - false, fake, phony, fraudulent
- 虚张声势 (xū zhāng shēng shì) - bluff, make an empty show of strength
- 弄假成真 (nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn) - what starts as pretense becomes real
- 把...弄假 (bǎ... nòng jiǎ) - to make... fake; to falsify

**Typical contexts**: Moral condemnation, legal proceedings, fraud investigation, relationship conflicts

**Key contrasts**: Deliberate deception vs. honest error; lying vs. truth-telling; fraud vs. authenticity

## GROUP 7: SUBSTANTIALITY AND CONCRETENESS

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether something has substance, solidity, or concrete reality as opposed to being empty, hollow, or merely theoretical. These terms often contrast the substantial/practical with the insubstantial/theoretical, and commonly appear in contexts evaluating whether plans, content, or work has real substance versus being superficial or empty show.

**Representative expressions**:
- 实 (shí) - solid, real, actual, substantial
- 虚 (xū) - empty, void, false, weak
- 实在 (shízài) - real, true, honest; really, indeed
- 实心 (shíxīn) - solid (not hollow); sincere
- 实体 (shítǐ) - entity, substance; physical (store/business)
- 实物 (shíwù) - material object, physical object
- 充实 (chōngshí) - substantial, rich, fulfilling; enrich
- 扎实 (zhāshí) - solid, sturdy, sound
- 踏实 (tāshí) - steady, dependable; at ease
- 殷实 (yīnshí) - well-off, prosperous
- 朴实 (pǔshí) - simple, plain, honest
- 平实 (píngshí) - plain, simple, down-to-earth
- 实实在在 (shí shí zài zài) - real, genuine, honest, down-to-earth
- 华而不实 (huá ér bù shí) - flashy but lacking substance
- 虚有其表 (xū yǒu qí biǎo) - impressive in appearance only
- 脚踏实地 (jiǎo tà shí dì) - down-to-earth, practical, realistic
- 虚虚实实 (xū xū shí shí) - mix of feint and reality
- 真材实料 (zhēn cái shí liào) - genuine materials, real substance

**Typical contexts**: Evaluation of work quality, content assessment, character evaluation, strategic analysis

**Key contrasts**: Substantial vs. empty; concrete vs. abstract; solid vs. hollow; practical vs. theoretical

## GROUP 8: PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION AND ACTUALIZATION

**Definition**: Terms used to describe the process of making something actual, implementing plans, or putting ideas into practice. These terms focus on the transition from theoretical or planned to actual or realized, and commonly appear in administrative, political, and project management contexts. They emphasize action and actualization rather than mere description or evaluation.

**Representative expressions**:
- 实现 (shíxiàn) - realize, achieve, bring about
- 实施 (shíshī) - implement, carry out
- 实行 (shíxíng) - implement, put into effect, practice
- 实践 (shíjiàn) - practice, put into practice
- 落实 (luòshí) - implement, carry out, put into effect
- 坐实 (zuòshí) - confirm, establish as fact
- 从实际出发 (cóng shíjì chūfā) - proceed from reality
- 符合实际 (fúhé shíjì) - conform to reality; be realistic

**Typical contexts**: Project management, policy implementation, administrative work, political discourse

**Key contrasts**: Theory vs. practice; planning vs. implementation; abstract vs. actualized

---

## GROUP 9: RELIABILITY AND DEPENDABILITY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether persons, information, or objects can be depended upon or trusted. These terms focus on trustworthiness and dependability rather than truth per se, and commonly appear in contexts of assessing whether something or someone is reliable enough to be trusted or depended upon.

**Representative expressions**:
- 可靠 (kěkào) - reliable, dependable, trustworthy
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) - reliability, dependability
- 可靠度 (kěkàodù) - degree of reliability, reliability level
- 确实 (quèshí) - indeed, really, true, authentic
- 确实如此 (quèshí rúcǐ) - indeed so; that's exactly right
- 确凿的证据 (quèzáo de zhèngjù) - conclusive evidence

**Typical contexts**: Quality assessment, trust evaluation, technical reliability testing

**Key contrasts**: Reliable vs. unreliable; dependable vs. undependable; trustworthy vs. untrustworthy

---

## GROUP 10: NORMALITY AND REGULARITY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether something conforms to normal patterns, regular standards, or expected norms. These terms focus on conformity to what is standard or typical rather than correctness in an absolute sense, and commonly appear in medical, social, and administrative contexts where deviation from norms is significant.

**Representative expressions**:
- 正常 (zhèngcháng) - normal, regular, ordinary
- 正常性 (zhèngchángxìng) - normality, normalcy
- 不正常 (bù zhèngcháng) - abnormal, irregular, unusual
- 正规 (zhèngguī) - regular, standard, orthodox
- 正式 (zhèngshì) - formal, official

**Typical contexts**: Medical diagnosis, social evaluation, institutional assessment, quality control

**Key contrasts**: Normal vs. abnormal; regular vs. irregular; standard vs. non-standard

---

## GROUP 11: LEGITIMACY AND PROPER AUTHORITY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether something has legitimate authority, proper status, or justified claim to position or action. These terms carry political and legal connotations and focus on rightful authority or justified action rather than mere factual correctness. They commonly appear in political, legal, and social contexts where questions of legitimacy and proper authority are at stake.

**Representative expressions**:
- 正当 (zhèngdàng) - legitimate, proper, justified, rightful
- 正当性 (zhèngdàngxìng) - legitimacy, justification
- 正统 (zhèngtǒng) - orthodox, legitimate (tradition)
- 正宗 (zhèngzōng) - orthodox, authentic (school/tradition)
- 正义 (zhèngyì) - justice, righteousness
- 正气 (zhèngqì) - righteousness, integrity, moral courage
- 名正言顺 (míng zhèng yán shùn) - proper and logical; justified and reasonable
- 正大光明 (zhèng dà guāng míng) - just and honorable; fair and square
- 理直气壮 (lǐ zhí qì zhuàng) - in the right and self-confident
- 拨乱反正 (bō luàn fǎn zhèng) - bring order out of chaos
- 正本清源 (zhèng běn qīng yuán) - rectify the root cause
- 不正之风 (bù zhèng zhī fēng) - unhealthy trend, corrupt practice

**Typical contexts**: Political discourse, legal proceedings, social criticism, institutional reform

**Key contrasts**: Legitimate vs. illegitimate; justified vs. unjustified; rightful vs. wrongful; orthodox vs. heterodox

---

## GROUP 12: CONSISTENCY BETWEEN APPEARANCE AND REALITY

**Definition**: Terms and expressions used to evaluate whether external appearance or claims match internal reality or actual practice. These terms focus specifically on the correspondence (or lack thereof) between what is shown/claimed and what actually is, and commonly appear in contexts of evaluating sincerity, authenticity of character, or truthfulness of representations.

**Representative expressions**:
- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) - worthy of the name
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) - unworthy of the name
- 言行一致 (yán xíng yīzhì) - words match deeds
- 表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī) - same inside and out
- 言行不一 (yán xíng bù yī) - words don't match deeds [implied contrast]
- 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) - different inside and out [implied contrast]

**Typical contexts**: Character evaluation, moral judgment, assessment of sincerity, criticism of hypocrisy

**Key contrasts**: Appearance matching reality vs. appearance deceiving; claims matching practice vs. claims contradicting practice

---

## GROUP 13: RATIONALITY AND REASONABLENESS

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether something accords with reason, logic, or rational principles. These terms focus on conformity to rational standards rather than factual truth or moral correctness, and commonly appear in analytical, argumentative, and evaluative contexts where the rationality or reasonableness of positions, decisions, or arrangements is at issue.

**Representative expressions**:
- 合理 (hélǐ) - reasonable, rational, logical
- 合理性 (hélǐxìng) - rationality, reasonableness
- 理直气壮 (lǐ zhí qì zhuàng) - in the right and self-confident [overlaps with Group 11]

**Typical contexts**: Argumentation, policy evaluation, decision assessment, philosophical discourse

**Key contrasts**: Reasonable vs. unreasonable; rational vs. irrational; logical vs. illogical

---

## GROUP 14: PRACTICAL UTILITY AND FUNCTIONALITY

**Definition**: Terms used to evaluate whether something is practically useful, functional, or serves real purposes. These terms focus on pragmatic value and actual usefulness rather than theoretical correctness or abstract truth, and commonly appear in contexts of product evaluation, policy assessment, and pragmatic decision-making.

**Representative expressions**:
- 实用 (shíyòng) - practical, pragmatic, functional
- 实用性 (shíyòngxìng) - practicality, utility
- 实惠 (shíhuì) - substantial benefit, real advantage; affordable
- 实效 (shíxiào) - actual effect, practical result

**Typical contexts**: Product evaluation, policy assessment, pragmatic decision-making, consumer choices

**Key contrasts**: Practical vs. impractical; useful vs. useless; functional vs. ornamental

---

## GROUP 15: EMPIRICAL INVESTIGATION AND VERIFICATION

**Definition**: Terms and expressions used in contexts of empirical investigation, evidence-gathering, and verification of claims through observation or testing. These terms emphasize the process of determining truth through investigation and evidence rather than through authority or reasoning alone.

**Representative expressions**:
- 实验 (shíyàn) - experiment, test
- 实证 (shízhèng) - empirical evidence, positive proof
- 实地 (shídì) - on the spot, on-site
- 实况 (shíkuàng) - actual situation, live (broadcast)
- 实录 (shílù) - faithful record, true account
- 实例 (shílì) - actual example, concrete case
- 实事求是 (shí shì qiú shì) - seek truth from facts
- 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) - to ascertain the truth [overlaps with Group 2]
- 查无实据 (chá wú shí jù) - investigation reveals no solid evidence
- 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) - conclusive evidence, solid proof [overlaps with Group 2]
- 提供证据 (tígōng zhèngjù) - to provide evidence

**Typical contexts**: Scientific research, legal investigation, journalism, forensic analysis

**Key contrasts**: Empirical vs. theoretical; evidence-based vs. speculative; verified vs. unverified

---

## GROUP 16: DISCOURSE MARKERS OF SINCERITY AND EMPHASIS

**Definition**: Terms and expressions that function as discourse markers to signal speaker sincerity, emphasize truthfulness of statements, or introduce frank/honest remarks. These are pragmatic markers that manage interpersonal communication rather than evaluate truth content per se.

**Representative expressions**:
- 说真的 (shuō zhēn de) - seriously; to be honest; really
- 真的吗？(zhēn de ma?) - Really? Is that so?
- 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) - to tell the truth; to be honest [overlaps with Group 5]
- 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō) - honestly speaking [overlaps with Group 5]
- 老实讲 (lǎoshí jiǎng) - honestly speaking
- 坦白说 (tǎnbái shuō) - frankly speaking [overlaps with Group 5]

**Typical contexts**: Conversational discourse, informal speech, interpersonal communication

**Function**: Pragmatic marking rather than truth evaluation

---

## GROUP 17: SPATIAL AND TEMPORAL PRECISION

**Definition**: Terms using the root 正 (zhèng) to indicate spatial centrality, temporal exactness, or geometric regularity. These terms extend from normative correctness to descriptive precision in space and time, and commonly appear in contexts requiring exact specification of position or time.

**Representative expressions**:
- 正中 (zhèngzhōng) - right in the center, dead center
- 正午 (zhèngwǔ) - noon, midday
- 正点 (zhèngdiǎn) - on time, punctual; exactly on the hour
- 正面 (zhèngmiàn) - front side; positive, affirmative
- 正门 (zhèngmén) - main entrance, front gate
- 正方形 (zhèng fāngxíng) - square (geometric shape)

**Typical contexts**: Spatial description, time specification, geometric description

**Key contrasts**: Center vs. periphery; exact time vs. approximate time; front vs. back; main vs. secondary

---

## GROUP 18: MATHEMATICAL AND TECHNICAL TERMINOLOGY

**Definition**: Terms using truth-domain roots (especially 正 and 真) in specialized mathematical, scientific, or technical contexts where they have acquired specific technical meanings. These terms represent extensions of truth-domain vocabulary into formal technical systems.

**Representative expressions**:
- 正数 (zhèngshù) - positive number
- 正比 (zhèngbǐ) - direct proportion
- 正负 (zhèngfù) - positive and negative
- 真分数 (zhēn fēnshù) - proper fraction
- 真命题 (zhēn mìngtí) - true proposition
- 真空 (zhēnkōng) - vacuum, void
- 真菌 (zhēnjūn) - true fungi, eumycetes
- 真核 (zhēnhé) - eukaryotic
- 实数 (shíshù) - real number

**Typical contexts**: Mathematics, physics, biology, logic, formal sciences

**Note**: These represent technical conventionalization rather than natural semantic extension

## AMBIGUOUS OR OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items Belonging to Multiple Groups:

**真 (zhēn)**:
- Group 1 (Authenticity) when modifying physical objects
- Group 2 (Factual Correspondence) when modifying statements
- Group 11 (as intensifier: "really, very")
- Group 16 (in discourse markers)

**实 (shí)**:
- Group 2 (Factual Correspondence) in compounds like 真实, 事实
- Group 7 (Substantiality) as standalone or in compounds emphasizing solidity
- Group 8 (Implementation) in verbal compounds like 实现, 实施

**正确 (zhèngquè)**:
- Group 3 (Correctness of Answers) in educational contexts
- Group 11 (Legitimacy) when used with political/moral implications
- Group 13 (Rationality) when emphasizing logical correctness

**诚实 (chéngshí) and related terms**:
- Group 5 (Honesty and Sincerity) as character evaluation
- Group 16 (Discourse Markers) in phrases like 说实话, 老实说

**符合 (fúhé)**:
- Group 2 (Factual Correspondence) with 事实, 实际
- Group 10 (Normality) with standards and norms
- Group 13 (Rationality) with 理 (reason)

### Items Difficult to Classify:

**本质 (běnzhì) - essence, nature**:
- Philosophical/analytical term that doesn't fit cleanly into usage-based groups
- Used across multiple contexts without clear grouping pattern

**真理 (zhēnlǐ) - truth (philosophical/ideological)**:
- More abstract and elevated than other truth terms
- Used in philosophical, religious, and ideological contexts
- Doesn't pattern clearly with other groups

**真传 (zhēnchuán) - authentic teaching/tradition**:
- Specialized to traditional arts and crafts contexts
- Too narrow for clear grouping

**真人 (zhēnrén) - real person; Daoist immortal**:
- Multiple distinct meanings (actual person vs. enlightened being)
- Context-dependent without clear pattern

**认真 (rènzhēn) - serious, earnest, conscientious**:
- Describes attitude/manner rather than truth evaluation
- Doesn't fit truth-domain groupings despite containing 真

**当真 (dàngzhēn) - take seriously, take as true**:
- Meta-level term about treating something as true
- Doesn't evaluate truth itself

### Items with Marginal Status:

**真龙天子 (zhēn lóng tiān zǐ) - true Son of Heaven**:
- Historical/literary term with limited modern usage
- Too specialized for grouping

**真身 (zhēnshēn) - real body, true form**:
- Multiple context-specific meanings (physical presence, Buddhist relics)
- No clear usage pattern

**虚怀若谷 (xū huái ruò gǔ) - extremely modest**:
- Uses 虚 (empty) positively, contrary to typical pattern
- Specialized to character evaluation in literary register
