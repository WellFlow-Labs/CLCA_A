# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** सत् / *sat* (from root *as-*, to be)
**P1 items participating:** सत् (*sat*, #7), असत् (*asat*, #8), सत्य (*satya*, #1), असत्य (*asatya*, #12), तत्त्व (*tattva*, #29, via the copular sense of *tat* + *-tva* as "being of that"), वास्तव (*vāstava*, #13, via *vastu* from *vas-*, cognate domain of being/dwelling)

Note: *sat* directly participates in items #1, #7, #8, #12. Items #13 and #14 (*vastu*, *vāstava*) derive from a distinct but semantically adjacent root (*vas-*); they are counted separately below. The core count for *sat/as-* is **4 direct items** (#1, #7, #8, #12), with #29 tangentially related through the copular structure.

**Core meaning contribution:** Being, existence, reality, genuineness. The root supplies the ontological grounding for truth-evaluation: what is *sat* is real and therefore true; what is *asat* is unreal and therefore false. The root fuses ontological and evaluative senses throughout the domain.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** प्र + मा / *pra-mā-* (prefix *pra-* + root *mā-*, to measure)
**P1 items participating:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*, #4), प्रमा (*pramā*, #5), अप्रमा (*apramā*, #6)
**Count:** **3 direct items** (#4, #5, #6)

**Core meaning contribution:** Correct measuring, valid apprehension. The root *mā-* (to measure) combined with the prefix *pra-* (forth, correctly) generates the central epistemological vocabulary of Sanskrit philosophy: the means of knowing, the act of knowing correctly, and its negation. This cluster is the technical core of the domain in philosophical discourse.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** धृ / *dhṛ-* (to hold, to sustain)
**P1 items participating:** धर्म (*dharma*, #20), अधर्म (*adharma*, #21)
**Count:** **2 direct items** (#20, #21)

Note: *dhṛ-* yields fewer P1 items than *sat* or *mā-*, but the semantic range of *dharma* is exceptionally broad within the truth-evaluation domain (right conduct, property, sustaining order), and the root is highly productive derivationally. It is included as the third central root on the basis of the breadth of its contribution rather than raw item count.

---

**Supplementary note on** ***ṛ-/ṛta-*:**
The root *ṛ-* (to go rightly, to fit) underlies ṛta (#2) and anṛta (#11), giving 2 items. It is a strong candidate for inclusion but is less derivationally productive in post-Vedic Sanskrit than the three roots above. Its compounds are listed under Step 2 for completeness.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: सत् / *sat* (root *as-*, to be; present participle *sat*)

The following lists all common compounds, collocations, derived expressions, and fixed phrases built from *sat* / *satya* and their extensions. The root is highly productive; approximately 25–30 of the most common forms are listed here. Additional forms exist in specialized Vedāntic, Purāṇic, and modern Sanskrit-influenced registers.

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्यता
**Transliteration:** satyatā
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being true"
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, truth (as an abstract property)
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and ethical discourse to name the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from particular true statements. Appears in discussions of the nature of truth and in characterizations of persons of truthful character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*); "one who speaks truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully
**Context of use:** Used in epic and Dharmaśāstra literature to characterize a person whose speech is reliably truthful. Appears as an epithet of virtuous figures and as a category in discussions of reliable testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** सत्यवचन
**Transliteration:** satyavacana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vacana* (speech, utterance, from root *vac-*); "truthful utterance"
**Natural English translation:** truthful speech, speaking the truth
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal contexts to designate the act or practice of speaking truthfully. Appears in Dharmaśāstra enumerations of virtuous conduct and in vow formulas.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** सत्यव्रत
**Transliteration:** satyavrata
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vrata* (vow, observance); "vow of truth"
**Natural English translation:** vow of truthfulness, one who has taken a vow of truth
**Context of use:** Used in epic and Purāṇic literature both as a compound noun naming the vow of always speaking truth and as an epithet of figures who have taken such a vow. Also a proper name.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** सत्यसंध
**Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* (bound, pledged, from root *dhā-* with *sam-*); "bound to truth, true to one's word"
**Natural English translation:** true to one's word, faithful to one's pledge
**Context of use:** Used in epic literature as an epithet of heroes and kings who keep their promises. Evaluates reliability and fidelity to commitments as a form of truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** सत्यनिष्ठ
**Transliteration:** satyanistha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *nistha* (devoted to, grounded in, from root *sthā-*); "grounded in truth"
**Natural English translation:** devoted to truth, steadfast in truthfulness
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness. Appears in enumerations of virtuous qualities.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *āpana* (causing to reach, from causative of root *āp-*); "causing truth to be established"
**Natural English translation:** verification, attestation, confirmation
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for the act of verifying or attesting to the truth of a statement or document. Appears in modern Sanskrit-medium legal and official usage as the standard term for verification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** सत्यापित
**Transliteration:** satyāpita
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *āpita* (past passive participle of causative of *āp-*); "caused to be verified, attested"
**Natural English translation:** verified, attested, certified
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative documents to indicate that a statement or copy has been officially verified. Standard in modern Sanskrit-influenced official language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** सत्यग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyagraha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *graha* (grasping, holding, from root *grah-*); "holding to truth"
**Natural English translation:** holding to truth, truth-force (Gandhian political concept)
**Context of use:** Coined or crystallized by Gandhi as a term for nonviolent resistance grounded in truth. Widely used in political, historical, and ethical discourse in modern Indian languages and internationally. The compound is formed on classical Sanskrit morphological patterns.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** सत्यलोक
**Transliteration:** satyaloka
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *loka* (world, realm); "world of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the realm of truth, the highest world
**Context of use:** Used in Purāṇic cosmology for the highest of the cosmic realms, identified with the abode of Brahmā. Appears in enumerations of the cosmic worlds and in descriptions of the afterlife destinations of virtuous souls.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** सत्ययुग
**Transliteration:** satyayuga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *yuga* (age, era); "the age of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the age of truth, the golden age
**Context of use:** Used in Purāṇic and epic literature for the first and most virtuous of the four cosmic ages (*yugas*), characterized by universal adherence to truth and righteousness. Widely attested in cosmological and ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** सत्यप्रतिज्ञ
**Transliteration:** satyapratijna
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijñā* (pledge, promise, from *prati-* + root *jñā-*); "whose pledge is truth"
**Natural English translation:** one whose promises are true, one who keeps his word
**Context of use:** Used in epic literature as an epithet of figures renowned for keeping their pledges. Evaluates the reliability of a person's commitments as a form of truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** सत्कार
**Transliteration:** satkāra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *kāra* (doing, making, from root *kṛ-*); "doing what is good/real"
**Natural English translation:** honor, respectful treatment, hospitality
**Context of use:** Used widely in social and religious contexts for the act of honoring or showing respect to a person, deity, or guest. The evaluative sense of *sat* (genuine, good) underlies the notion of treating someone as they deserve.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** सत्पुरुष
**Transliteration:** satpuruṣa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *puruṣa* (man, person); "a genuine/good man"
**Natural English translation:** a good man, a man of genuine virtue, a worthy person
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and social discourse to designate a person of genuine virtue and good character. The *sat* component carries both the ontological sense (genuinely what a person should be) and the evaluative sense (morally good).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** सत्संग
**Transliteration:** satsaṅga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *saṅga* (company, association, from root *sañj-*); "company of the good/real"
**Natural English translation:** company of the good, association with virtuous or spiritually realized persons
**Context of use:** Used in devotional and religious contexts for the practice of associating with spiritually advanced or virtuous persons, considered beneficial for one's own development. Widely attested in Bhakti literature and in modern religious usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** सद्गुण
**Transliteration:** sadguṇa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi form *sad-*) + *guṇa* (quality, virtue); "genuine/good quality"
**Natural English translation:** good quality, genuine virtue, positive attribute
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and aesthetic contexts to designate a genuinely good quality or virtue in a person or thing. Appears in enumerations of virtuous characteristics and in literary criticism to evaluate the positive qualities of a work.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** सद्वृत्त
**Transliteration:** sadvṛtta
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *sad-*) + *vṛtta* (conduct, behavior, from root *vṛt-*); "good conduct"
**Natural English translation:** good conduct, virtuous behavior, right behavior
**Context of use:** Used in Dharmaśāstra and ethical literature to designate conduct that conforms to what is genuinely right and good. Appears in prescriptive enumerations of proper behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** असत्कार
**Transliteration:** asatkāra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *kāra* (doing); "doing what is not good, disrespect"
**Natural English translation:** disrespect, dishonor, discourteous treatment
**Context of use:** Used in social and ethical contexts as the negation of *satkāra*, designating disrespectful or dishonoring treatment of a person. Appears in discussions of proper social conduct and its violations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** असत्प्रतिग्रह
**Transliteration:** asatpratigṛha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *pratigṛha* (acceptance, receiving, from *prati-* + root *grah-*); "acceptance of what is not genuine/proper"
**Natural English translation:** improper acceptance (of gifts), acceptance from unworthy sources
**Context of use:** Used in Dharmaśāstra for the improper acceptance of gifts or donations from sources that are not legitimate or from persons whose gifts should not be received. A category of wrongful conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** सत्यमेव जयते
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, nominative) + *eva* (indeed, only) + *jayate* (conquers, from root *ji-*); "truth alone conquers"
**Natural English translation:** Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. Used in philosophical, political, and rhetorical contexts to assert the ultimate triumph of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** सत्यं वद
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, accusative) + *vada* (speak, imperative of root *vad-*); "speak truth"
**Natural English translation:** Speak the truth
**Context of use:** A fixed injunctive phrase from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1), widely cited in ethical and educational contexts as a foundational moral injunction. Used in discussions of the duty of truthful speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** सत्यं ब्रूयात्
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, accusative) + *brūyāt* (should speak, optative of root *brū-*); "one should speak truth"
**Natural English translation:** One should speak the truth
**Context of use:** A fixed prescriptive phrase from Manusmṛti and related Dharmaśāstra literature, used in ethical discourse to state the duty of truthful speech. Often followed by qualifications about when truth-telling may be overridden.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** सत्ताशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** sattāśāstra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sattā* (being, existence, from *sat* + *-tā*) + *śāstra* (treatise, discipline); "treatise on being"
**Natural English translation:** ontology (as a discipline)
**Context of use:** Used in modern Sanskrit philosophical writing as a rendering of "ontology." Less common than *sattāmīmāṃsā* but attested in academic Sanskrit discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**24.**
**Form:** सत्ता
**Transliteration:** sattā
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *-tā* (abstract suffix); "being-ness, existence"
**Natural English translation:** being, existence, ontological status
**Context of use:** Used in Vaiśeṣika and Vedānta as a technical term for the property of existence or being, and in general philosophical discourse for the ontological status of an entity. Appears in discussions of what kinds of things genuinely exist.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** सत्कार्यवाद
**Transliteration:** satkāryavāda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *kārya* (effect, from root *kṛ-*) + *vāda* (doctrine, position); "doctrine that the effect is real (pre-exists in the cause)"
**Natural English translation:** the doctrine of the pre-existence of the effect, the theory that the effect is real in the cause
**Context of use:** A fixed technical term in Sāṃkhya and Vedānta philosophy for the position that the effect genuinely exists in the cause prior to its production, as opposed to *asatkāryavāda* (the effect is new, not pre-existent). Central to debates about causation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** असत्कार्यवाद
**Transliteration:** asatkāryavāda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *kārya* + *vāda*; "doctrine that the effect is not real (prior to production)"
**Natural English translation:** the doctrine that the effect is not pre-existent in the cause
**Context of use:** The opposing position to *satkāryavāda*, associated with Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, holding that the effect is a genuinely new entity not pre-existing in the cause. Used in philosophical debate about causation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** सच्चिदानन्द
**Transliteration:** saccidānanda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (being) + *cit* (consciousness) + *ānanda* (bliss); "being-consciousness-bliss"
**Natural English translation:** being-consciousness-bliss (the triple characterization of Brahman)
**Context of use:** A fixed compound in Advaita Vedānta and related traditions designating the ultimate reality (Brahman) as constituted by pure being, pure consciousness, and pure bliss. Widely used in devotional, philosophical, and liturgical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** सन्मार्ग
**Transliteration:** sanmārga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *san-*) + *mārga* (path, way); "the good/right path"
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the path of virtue
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to designate the path of virtuous conduct and right living. Appears in prescriptive contexts contrasting the right way of life with its opposite.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** सद्धर्म
**Transliteration:** saddharma
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *sad-*) + *dharma*; "genuine dharma, true teaching"
**Natural English translation:** the true teaching, the genuine dharma, the good law
**Context of use:** Used in Buddhist Sanskrit literature (and in Hindu contexts) for the authentic or genuine teaching, as opposed to corrupt or false versions. Appears in the title *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* (Lotus Sūtra) and in discussions of authentic religious doctrine.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** सत्यशोधन
**Transliteration:** satyaśodhana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *śodhana* (purification, investigation, from root *śudh-*); "investigation/purification of truth"
**Natural English translation:** investigation of truth, truth-seeking, verification
**Context of use:** Used in modern Sanskrit and Sanskrit-influenced discourse for the process of investigating or establishing the truth of a matter. Appears in academic and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Root 2: प्र + मा / *pra-mā-* (prefix *pra-* + root *mā-*, to measure)

The root *mā-* (to measure) with the prefix *pra-* is highly productive in epistemological Sanskrit. The following lists the most common derived forms and compounds. Additional forms exist in specialized logical and Mīmāṃsā literature.

---

**1.**
**Form:** प्रमातृ
**Transliteration:** pramātṛ
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pra-* + *mātṛ* (agent noun from root *mā-*); "the one who measures correctly, the knower"
**Natural English translation:** the knower, the knowing subject, the epistemic agent
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature for the subject of valid cognition — the agent who employs a *pramāṇa* to arrive at *pramā*. One of the three elements of the epistemological triad: *pramātṛ* (knower), *pramāṇa* (means), *prameya* (object known).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** प्रमेय
**Transliteration:** prameya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pra-* + *meya* (gerundive/future passive participle of root *mā-*); "that which is to be measured/known"
**Natural English translation:** the object of valid cognition, the knowable, what is to be known
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature for the object that valid cognition apprehends. In Nyāya, *prameya* is one of the sixteen categories (*padārthas*) subject to philosophical investigation. Appears in discussions of what kinds of things can be objects of knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** प्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ya* (abstract adjectival suffix); "the property of being a valid means of knowledge, validity"
**Natural English translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the property of being a genuine means of knowledge
**Context of use:** A central technical term in Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya epistemology for the property that makes a cognition or means of knowledge valid. Debates about *prāmāṇya* — whether it is intrinsic (*svataḥ*) or extrinsic (*parataḥ*) — are among the most important in Sanskrit epistemology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** svataḥprāmāṇya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *svataḥ* (from itself, intrinsically) + *prāmāṇya*; "intrinsic validity"
**Natural English translation:** intrinsic validity, self-certification of knowledge
**Context of use:** A fixed technical term in Mīmāṃsā epistemology for the position that the validity of a cognition is established by the cognition itself, without requiring external verification. Contrasted with *parataḥprāmāṇya* (extrinsic validity, the Nyāya position).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** परतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** parataḥprāmāṇya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *parataḥ* (from another, extrinsically) + *prāmāṇya*; "extrinsic validity"
**Natural English translation:** extrinsic validity, validity established by external means
**Context of use:** The Nyāya position that the validity of a cognition must be established by something external to the cognition itself (e.g., successful action based on it). Contrasted with *svataḥprāmāṇya*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** अप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** aprāmāṇya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *prāmāṇya*; "invalidity, lack of epistemic authority"
**Natural English translation:** invalidity, lack of epistemic authority, the property of not being a genuine means of knowledge
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature for the property of a cognition or means of knowledge that fails to be valid. Appears in discussions of how invalidity is recognized and what causes it.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** प्रमाणशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇaśāstra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *śāstra* (treatise, discipline); "the discipline of valid means of knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** epistemology (as a discipline), the science of valid cognition
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical literature to designate the systematic study of the means of valid knowledge. Appears as a characterization of the subject matter of works like Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya* and Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** प्रमाणवार्त्तिक
**Transliteration:** pramāṇavārttika
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *vārttika* (commentary, gloss, critical note); "critical commentary on valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** Commentary on Valid Cognition (title of Dharmakīrti's work)
**Context of use:** The title of Dharmakīrti's major epistemological treatise, one of the most important works in Buddhist Sanskrit philosophy. Used as a proper title and as a reference point in discussions of Buddhist epistemology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** प्रमाणसमुच्चय
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasamuccaya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *samuccaya* (collection, compendium, from *sam-* + *ud-* + root *ci-*); "compendium of valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** Compendium of Valid Cognition (title of Dignāga's work)
**Context of use:** The title of Dignāga's foundational work in Buddhist epistemology. Used as a proper title and reference point in the history of Sanskrit logic and epistemology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** प्रमाणभूत
**Transliteration:** pramāṇabhūta
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *bhūta* (become, being, past passive participle of root *bhū-*); "having become an authority, being an authority"
**Natural English translation:** authoritative, one who has become an authority, a reliable source
**Context of use:** Used in Buddhist epistemology (notably in Dharmakīrti) as an epithet of the Buddha, characterizing him as one who has become a valid authority — i.e., whose cognitions are reliably valid. Also used more broadly for any authoritative source.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** प्रमाणान्तर
**Transliteration:** pramāṇāntara
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *antara* (other, different); "another means of knowledge, a different valid source"
**Natural English translation:** another means of valid cognition, a different epistemic source
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological debate to refer to a different or additional means of knowledge that might confirm or conflict with a given cognition. Appears in discussions of the relationship between perception, inference, and testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** प्रमाणविरुद्ध
**Transliteration:** pramāṇaviruddha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *viruddha* (opposed, contradicted, from *vi-* + root *rudh-*); "contradicted by a valid means of knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** contradicted by valid cognition, contrary to evidence
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and legal discourse to characterize a claim or position that is contradicted by one or more valid means of knowledge. A standard way of dismissing an opponent's position.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** प्रमाणसिद्ध
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasiddha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *siddha* (established, accomplished, from root *sidh-*); "established by a valid means of knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** established by valid cognition, epistemically warranted
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical discourse to characterize a claim or entity whose existence or truth has been established by a valid means of knowledge. A standard way of asserting the epistemic warrant for a position.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** प्रमाणाभास
**Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāsa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *ābhāsa* (semblance, appearance); "semblance of a valid means of knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-means of knowledge, apparent but invalid epistemic source
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature for something that appears to be a valid means of knowledge but fails to meet the conditions for genuine validity. Parallel in structure to *hetvābhāsa* (fallacy of the reason).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** प्रमाणपत्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇapatra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *patra* (leaf, document); "document of authority/validity"
**Natural English translation:** certificate, credential, official document
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and modern official contexts for a certificate or credential that attests to the validity or authenticity of something. Widely used in modern Indian administrative Sanskrit.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** प्रमाणीकरण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa

**Context of use:** Used in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities associated with cosmic order, particularly Varuṇa. Evaluates divine nature in terms of conformity to *ṛta*.

**Context of use:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities and persons who uphold or embody *ṛta*. Evaluates conformity to cosmic and moral order.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the root's contribution shifting in meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. No interpretation or categorization is provided; these are flagged for attention only.

---

**From Root *sat-*:**

- *satkāra* (#13 above): The root *sat* (real, genuine) appears in a context of social honor and hospitality, where the evaluation concerns appropriate treatment of persons rather than the truth or falsity of propositions or states of affairs.
- *satsaṅga* (#15 above): *sat* appears in a context of spiritual association and religious practice, where the evaluation concerns the quality of persons one associates with rather than the truth of claims.
- *satyayuga* (#11 above): *satya* appears in a cosmological-temporal context, designating an entire cosmic age, where the evaluation concerns the moral quality of an era rather than the truth of particular statements.
- *saccidānanda* (#27 above): *sat* appears as one element of a triple characterization of ultimate reality alongside *cit* (consciousness) and *ānanda* (bliss), where the ontological sense of *sat* is fused with categories that are not obviously truth-related.
- *satyagraha* (#9 above): *satya* appears in a political and activist context, where truth-holding is the basis of a method of political resistance rather than an epistemic or propositional evaluation.
- *sattā* (#24 above): The abstract noun from *sat* appears as a technical term for ontological status or existence as such, where the connection to truth-evaluation is mediated through ontology rather than direct.

---

**From Root *pra-mā-*:**

- *parimāṇa* (#20 above): The root *mā-* (to measure) appears in a context of physical magnitude and quantity, where the evaluation concerns size or extent rather than epistemic validity.
- *māna* (#21 above): The root *mā-* appears in a context of social honor and respect, where "measure" has shifted to the social valuation of persons.
- *apamāna* (#22 above): The root *mā-* appears in a context of social dishonor and insult, the inverse of *māna*, where the measuring sense has shifted entirely to social evaluation.
- *sammāna* (#23 above): As with *māna* and *apamāna*, the root appears in a context of social esteem.
- *nirmāṇa* (#24 above): The root *mā-* appears in a context of construction and fabrication, where the sense of measuring has shifted to making or creating.
- *anumāna* (#18 above): The root *mā-* appears in a context of inferential reasoning, where the measuring sense is extended to the cognitive process of drawing conclusions from evidence — a shift from direct measurement to mediated epistemic procedure.

---

**From Root *dhṛ-*:**

- *dharmin* (#21 above): The root *dhṛ-* appears in a logical context where *dharma* means "property" and *dharmin* means "the subject/locus of a property" — a technical logical use that is distinct from the moral and legal senses of *dharma*.
- *dhāraṇā* (#28 above): The root *dhṛ-* appears in a context of yogic practice and mental concentration, where "holding" refers to the fixing of attention rather than to moral or legal sustaining.
- *dhṛti* (#29 above): The root *dhṛ-* appears in a context of psychological fortitude and resolve, where "holding" refers to mental steadiness rather than to the sustaining of cosmic or moral order.
- *dhāraṇa* (#30 above): The root *dhṛ-* appears in a ritual and social context of wearing or bearing marks and garments, where "holding" refers to the physical bearing of signs of identity.
- *dharmaviśeṣa* (#22 above): *dharma* appears in a Vaiśeṣika philosophical context meaning "specific property" or "particular characteristic" of a substance — a technical ontological use that is distinct from the moral and legal senses.
- *āpaddharma* (#27 above): *dharma* appears in a context of emergency exception, where the evaluation concerns the modification of right conduct under extreme circumstances — a use that foregrounds the contextual and conditional nature of dharmic norms rather than their absolute character.
