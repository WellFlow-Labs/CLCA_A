| Asserting and denying factual correspondence / truth-as-correspondence | These expressions are used in contexts where a speaker asserts, affirms, questions, or denies whether a claim, statement, or situation matches what is actually the case. They appear in predicative constructions (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*, *atu uṇmaiyā?*), in adverbial frames that contrast appearance with reality (*uṇmaiyil*, *uṇmaiyilēyē*), and in fixed evaluative phrases used to characterize statements as factually accurate or inaccurate. The core contrast operative in this group is between what is claimed or appears to be the case and what actually is the case. The central contrast is between *uṇmai* and *poy*, which function as direct antonyms across a wide range of registers. | உண்மை / *uṇmai*; பொய் / *poy*; உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*; பொய்யான / *poyyāna*; உண்மையாக / *uṇmaiyāka*; பொய்யாக / *poyyāka*; உண்மையில் / *uṇmaiyil*; உண்மையிலேயே / *uṇmaiyilēyē*; மெய் / *mey*; மெய்ம்மை / *meymmai*; மெய்யான / *meyyāna*; உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmaitaṉmai*; பொய்மை / *poymai*; உண்மையற்ற / *uṇmaiyaṟṟa*; பொய்யில்லாத / *poyyillāta*; உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāna*; சரி / *cari*; தவறு / *tavaṟu* | *uṇmai* and *mey* are near-synonyms in this function but differ in register; *cari* and *tavaṟu* overlap with this group when used to evaluate the accuracy of statements, but they also participate in Group 8; *meyyē vellum* also participates in Group 9 | *uṇmai* is the default across all registers; *mey* is confined to literary, philosophical, and formal contexts; *cari* and *tavaṟu* overlap with Group 8 (correctness and accuracy in practical contexts) |
| Truth coming to light / revelation of concealed facts | These expressions are used in narrative, journalistic, and legal contexts where the focus is on the movement of information between concealment and disclosure — truth coming to light, being suppressed, being sought, or being admitted. They cluster around verbs of motion (*veḷippaṭu*, *teriya va*), concealment (*maṟai*, *mūṭu*), and admission (*ottukkō*), and they presuppose a gap between what is actually the case and what is publicly known or acknowledged. They appear in intransitive constructions where truth is treated as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility, and in constructions describing the acts of hiding or revealing. | Evaluating persons and conduct for honesty and its absence | These expressions are used specifically to evaluate whether a person's character or conduct is straightforward and free of deception, as opposed to evaluating whether a particular statement is factually accurate. They appear most frequently in moral instruction, interpersonal evaluation, and political discourse. The root *nēr* (straight, direct) underlies the central terms in this group. They appear in character evaluations (*avar nērmaiyāna āḷ*, he/she is an honest person), moral instruction (*nērmaiyāka naṭa*, behave honestly), and condemnation of deceptive conduct. The focus is on the person as an agent rather than on a specific statement or object. | நேர்மை / *nērmai*; நேர்மையான / *nērmaiyāna*; நேர்மையாக / *nērmaiyāka*; நேர்மையற்ற / *nērmaiyaṟṟa*; நேர்மையில்லாத / *nērmaiyillāta*; நேர்மையின்மை / *nērmayiṉmai*; நேர்மையுடன் / *nērmaiyuṭaṉ*; நேர்மையாளர் / *nērmayāḷar*; நேர் / *nēr*; நேரடி / *nēraṭi*; நேரடியாக / *nēraṭiyāka*; நேர்தன்மை / *nēr-taṉmai* [MARGINAL]; தூய்மை / *tūymai*; தூய்மையான / *tūymaiyāna*; தூய்மையற்ற / *tūymaiyaṟṟa*; வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*; வஞ்சனையான / *vañcaṉaiyāka*; உண்மையான மனிதன் / *uṇmaiyāna maṉitaṉ* | *நேர்மை* and related terms can appear in contexts where the honesty of a statement (not just a person) is at issue, creating overlap with Group 1; *பொய்* also participates in this group when applied to persons (*poy āḷ*, a lying person), but its primary membership is in Group 1; *வஞ்சனை* also participates in Group 6 and Group 7; *தூய்மை* also appears in religious and aesthetic contexts outside the truth-evaluation domain; *உண்மையான மனிதன்* overlaps with Group 1 | The dominant usage pattern for *நேர்மை* is evaluation of persons and conduct, while *uṇmai/poy* more typically evaluate propositions and states of affairs; *தூய்மை*'s placement in this group reflects only the subset of its usage documented in P1 and P2 |
| Assessing genuineness versus fakeness or copying | These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a physical object, document, credential, or person is what it claims to be — an original rather than a copy, a real professional rather than an impostor, a genuine article rather than a counterfeit. The operative contrast is between *acal* (original) and *nakal* (copy) or *pōli* (fake), and the evaluation is applied to things and persons rather than to propositions. This group is especially prominent in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts. | அசல் / *acal*; அசலான / *acalāna*; நகல் / *nakal*; போலி / *pōli*; போலியான / *pōliyāna*; போலித்தனம் / *pōlittaṉam*; உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*; உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmait-taṉmai*; மெய்யான / *meyyāna* | *அசல்* also participates in Group 7; *போலி* also participates in Group 7; *உண்மையான* overlaps with Group 1 when applied to propositions and states of affairs | *nakal* is neutral in connotation (a certified copy of a document is a *nakal* without negative implication), while *pōli* is always negative; *uṇmaiyāna* and *meyyāna* participate in both this group and Group 1, depending on whether they are applied to things/persons (genuineness) or to propositions (truth) |
| Providing and evaluating evidential support / Establishing and evidencing claims | These expressions are used in contexts — primarily legal, journalistic, and formal argumentative — where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it can be demonstrated, supported, or established on the basis of evidence or proof. They appear in constructions involving the production of evidence, the act of proving, and the evaluation of whether a claim is well-supported. The operative concern is the relationship between a claim and its evidentiary basis. The typical constructions involve demanding evidence (*ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē*), asserting its absence (*ātāramaṟṟa vatanti*), or establishing its presence (*cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu*). | ஆதாரம் / *ātāram*; ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna*; ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa*; ஆதாரமில்லாத / *ātāramillāta*; ஆதாரப்படுத்து / *ātārappaṭuttu*; சான்று / *cāṉṟu*; சான்றிதழ் / *cāṉṟitaḻ*; சான்றாளர் / *cāṉṟāḷar*; சான்றுமொழி / *cāṉṟumoḻi*; சான்றற்ற / *cāṉṟaṟṟa*; சாட்சி / *cāṭci*; சாட்சியம் / *cāṭciyam*; மெய்ப்பிக்க / *meyppiкka*; மெய்ப்பித்தல் / *meyppittal*; உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டுதல் / *uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭutal*; உண்மைச் சான்று / *uṇmaic cāṉṟu*; உண்மைத்தன்மையை நிரூபி / *uṇmait-taṉmaiyai nirūpi*; நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna*; நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka*; நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*; நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam*; நிரூபிக்கு / *nirūpikku* | *நம்பகமான* and *நம்பத்தக்க* overlap with Group 6; *நிரூபணம்* also participates in Group 2; *சான்றாளர்* also participates in Group 7; *சாட்சி* also participates in Group 7; *சாட்சியம்* also participates in Group 7 | *cāṉṟu* is more frequently used in formal legal contexts while *ātāram* is more common in everyday argumentative contexts; both appear in the same constructions and the distinction is one of register and typical context rather than a sharp semantic boundary; *மெய்ப்பிக்க* is the only verbal form in the entire inventory that directly encodes the act of proving |
| Evaluating trustworthiness and credibility of persons and sources | These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be trusted or relied upon. They appear in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts, and the typical constructions involve asserting or denying credibility (*nampakamāna takavalu*, *nampakattaṉmai kēḷvikkuṟiyākiviṭṭatu*) or describing the relationship of trust between a person and a source (*nampikkai vai*, *nampikkai iḻa*). The operative question is not whether a specific claim is true but whether the agent making the claim or the source providing information is reliable. This group is anchored in the root *nampu* (to trust). | நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*; நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna*; நம்பகம் / *nampakam*; நம்பகத்தன்மை / *nampakattaṉmai*; நம்பகமற்ற / *nampakamāṟṟa*; நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiāna*; நம்பிக்கையற்ற / *nampikkaiāṟṟa*; நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka*; நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*; நம்பமுடியாத / *nampamuṭiyāta*; நம்பிக்கையின்மை / *nampikkaiinmai* | *நம்பகமான* and *நம்பத்தக்க* also participate in Group 5; *வஞ்சனை* also participates in this group (noted in Group 3 as participating in Group 6 and Group 7) | This group is anchored in the root *nampu* (to trust); the operative question is not whether a specific claim is true but whether the agent making the claim or the source providing information is reliable |
