# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Quechua (Southern: Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective (cheqa / llulla / allin / pantasqa) + noun, in prenominal position. No copula required. Evidential enclitic attaches to the predicate or focused element, not to the modifier.

### Examples
- **cheqa qelqa** → *cheqa qelqa* → 'authentic document / genuine text'
- **llulla simi** → *llulla simi* → 'false word / lying speech'

### Notes
Quechua adjectives precede the noun they modify. The attributive construction is structurally identical to a simple juxtaposition; no linking morphology is required. *Cheqaq* (with agentive *-q*) is preferred over bare *cheqa* in some attributive positions (see §7).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**mana** + evaluative adjective + noun (prenominal), or **mana** + predicate + **-chu** (negative polar enclitic) in predicative position. The enclitic *-chu* is obligatory on the predicate in negative clauses.

### Examples
- **mana cheqa qelqa** → *mana cheqa qelqa* → 'inauthentic document / not a genuine text'
- **mana allinchu** → *mana allinchu* → 'it is not good / it is wrong / it is bad'

### Notes
*Mana* is the general negator; *-chu* is required on the predicate in negative declarative clauses. In attributive position (prenominal), *-chu* does not appear; the negation is expressed by *mana* alone preceding the adjective-noun sequence.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective or participial form (*-sqa*, *-q*) precedes the head noun. Possessive nominalization (*X + kay + possessive suffix*) can also function adnominally to express essential nature.

### Examples
- **cheqanchasqa qelqa** → *cheqanchasqa qelqa* → 'a verified/certified document'
- **cheqa kaynin** → *cheqa kaynin* → 'its true nature / what it genuinely is'

### Notes
Participial modifiers (*-sqa*, *-q*) follow the same prenominal order as simple adjectives. The possessive nominalization *cheqa kaynin* ('its being-true') is a more abstract adnominal construction expressing essential nature rather than a simple property; it appears in philosophical and religious registers.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective + **-ka-** (intransitive verbalizer / inceptive) + **-y** (infinitive). Yields an intransitive verb meaning 'to become X' or 'to come to be X'.

### Examples
- **cheqakay** → *cheqakay* → 'to become true / to be realized / to come true'
- **allinkay** → *allinkay* → 'to become good / to get well / to improve / to recover'

### Notes
The *-ka-* pathway is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sumaq*. It contrasts systematically with the transitive *-ncha-* causative (§5): *cheqakay* is intransitive ('it becomes true'), while *cheqanchay* is transitive ('to make something true / to verify'). No *-ka-* inchoative is documented for *llulla*.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Transitive (making/verifying):** Evaluative adjective + **-ncha-** (causative verbalizer) + **-y** (infinitive). Yields a transitive verb meaning 'to make X' or 'to bring X about'.

**Causative on verbal base:** Verbal root + **-chi-** (causative) + **-y**. Yields 'to cause someone to do/be X'.

### Examples
- **cheqanchay** → *cheqanchay* → 'to verify / to confirm / to make true / to correct'
- **pantachiy** → *pantachiy* → 'to mislead / to cause someone to err'

### Notes
Two distinct causative pathways are attested. The *-ncha-* verbalizer applies to adjectival roots (*cheqa*, *allin*, *sut'i*, *sumaq*) and yields transitive verbs of making or establishing. The *-chi-* causative applies to verbal roots (*panta-*, *llulla-*, *yacha-*) and yields verbs of causing an action in another. The root *llulla* uses *-chi-* directly (*llullachiy* 'to cause to lie') rather than *-ncha-*, an asymmetry preserved from the data.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective + **kay** (copular root / nominalizer). Yields an abstract nominal meaning 'the state/quality of being X' or 'truth / goodness / reality'.

### Examples
- **cheqa kay** → *cheqa kay* → 'truth / reality / the state of being true / genuineness'
- **allin kay** → *allin kay* → 'goodness / well-being / the state of being good or right'

### Notes
The copular root *kay* ('to be / being') nominalizes the evaluative adjective to yield an abstract concept. This construction is productive across evaluative roots. The possessive extension *cheqa kaynin* ('its true nature / its genuine being') adds a third-person possessive suffix to specify whose or what's truth/nature is at issue.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Verbal base (or adjectival root) + **-q** (agentive nominalizer). Yields a nominal meaning 'one who does X' or 'one who is X (characteristically)'.

### Examples
- **llullaq** → *llullaq* → 'liar / one who lies / deceiver'
- **cheqanchaq** → *cheqanchaq* → 'one who verifies / a verifier / one who confirms'

### Notes
The *-q* nominalizer applies to verbal bases (*llullaku-q*, *cheqancha-q*, *pantachi-q*) and, in the case of *cheqaq*, directly to the adjectival root. *Cheqaq* ('truthful / genuine') is the standard attributive form of *cheqa* in prenominal position and also nominalizes to mean 'a truthful person'. The double-base form *llullakuq* (from *llullaku-* + *-q*) adds a habitual nuance compared to bare *llullaq*.

---

## 8. EVIDENTIAL MARKING OF TRUTH CLAIMS

### Pattern
Predicate (including truth-evaluation predicates) + evidential enclitic: **-m/-mi** (direct / first-hand), **-s/-si** (reportative / heard from another), **-chá** (conjectural / inferred). The enclitic attaches to the predicate or the focused constituent.

### Examples
- **cheqam** / **cheqas** / **cheqachá** → *cheqam* / *cheqas* / *cheqachá* → 'it is true (I know directly)' / 'it is true (reportedly)' / 'it is probably true'
- **llullachá** → *llullachá* → 'it is probably a lie / I suspect it is false'

### Notes
Evidential enclitics constitute a grammatically obligatory system in Southern Quechua: speakers mark the source and certainty of their claims as a matter of grammar, not optional emphasis. The three-way contrast (*-m/-mi* vs. *-s/-si* vs. *-chá*) applies to all truth-evaluation predicates documented in this inventory and represents a structurally distinct layer of meaning that cross-cuts all other construction types.

---

## 9. REFLEXIVE / SELF-DIRECTED VERIFICATION

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective + **-ncha-** + **-ku-** (reflexive / middle / self-directed) + **-y**. Yields a verb meaning 'to verify / correct / clarify for oneself or on one's own initiative'.

### Examples
- **cheqanchakuy** → *cheqanchakuy* → 'to verify for oneself / to assure oneself of the truth'
- **allinchakuy** → *allinchakuy* → 'to correct oneself / to improve oneself / to set oneself right'

### Notes
The *-ku-* suffix added to the *-ncha-* causative base shifts the action from externally directed (verifying something for another, correcting something on behalf of another) to self-directed or internally motivated. This three-morpheme sequence (*-ncha-ku-y*) is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sut'i* and represents a productive sub-pattern within the causative system.

---

## 10. PROPOSITIONAL KNOWLEDGE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Nominalized subordinate clause: **[predicate + kasqanta]** (accusative nominalization of 'its being X') as object of **yachay** ('to know') or **riqsiy* ('to recognize'). Expresses propositional knowledge of a truth-evaluation state.

### Examples
- **cheqa kasqanta yachani** → *cheqa kasqanta yachani* → 'I know that it is true / I know it to be genuine'
- **pantasqa kasqanta mana yachanichu** → *pantasqa kasqanta mana yachanichu* → 'I did not know that it was wrong / I was unaware of the error'

### Notes
The construction *[X kasqanta] yachay* is the standard Quechua pattern for expressing propositional knowledge ('knowing that'). The nominalized predicate takes the accusative *-ta*, marking it as the object of *yachay*. Negation of the knowledge verb (*mana yachanichu*) expresses ignorance and is relevant in legal contexts of disclaiming responsibility for an error.
