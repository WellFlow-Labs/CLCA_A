# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

## Core Vocabulary Items

### 1.
**Form:** உண்மை  
**Transliteration:** uṇmai  
**Literal meaning:** that which exists/is  
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuineness  
**Usage:** This is the primary term for truth in Tamil, used across all registers from everyday speech to philosophical discourse. It appears in moral contexts (uṇmai pēcu "speak truth"), epistemic contexts (uṇmaiyāṉa "true/real"), and metaphysical discussions about what is real versus illusory.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.
**Form:** பொய்  
**Transliteration:** poy  
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, lie  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, fake  
**Usage:** The direct antonym of uṇmai, used for deliberate falsehoods and lies as well as for things that are fake or counterfeit. Common in moral discourse (poy collu "tell lies") and in evaluating authenticity (poy cāmāṉ "fake goods").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3.
**Form:** மெய்  
**Transliteration:** mey  
**Literal meaning:** body; truth/reality  
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, genuine, actual  
**Usage:** An older, more literary term for truth that emphasizes genuineness and reality. Often used in classical Tamil literature and philosophical contexts, and appears in compounds like meypporuḷ "true meaning/reality."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4.
**Form:** நேர்மை  
**Transliteration:** nērmai  
**Literal meaning:** straightness, directness  
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness, rectitude  
**Usage:** Refers to moral honesty and integrity in conduct and character. Used to evaluate persons rather than statements (nērmaiyāṉa āḷ "honest person"), emphasizing ethical straightforwardness and reliability in behavior.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5.
**Form:** சரி  
**Transliteration:** cari  
**Literal meaning:** equal, level, correct  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper  
**Usage:** Extremely common term for correctness and rightness in everyday speech. Used for factual correctness (cari-yāṉa patiḷ "correct answer"), appropriateness (cari-yāṉa nēram "right time"), and agreement/confirmation (cari "okay/right").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6.
**Form:** தப்பு  
**Transliteration:** tappu  
**Literal meaning:** mistake, error  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, mistake, error, fault  
**Usage:** The direct opposite of cari, used for errors, mistakes, and wrongness in both factual and moral contexts. Common in everyday speech (tappu ceyya "make a mistake") and moral evaluation (tappu-tāṉ "it's wrong").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7.
**Form:** உண்மையான  
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is true/real (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic  
**Usage:** Adjectival form of uṇmai, used attributively to modify nouns. Widely used to evaluate authenticity and truthfulness (uṇmaiyāṉa katai "true story," uṉmaiyāṉa nēcam "genuine love").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8.
**Form:** பொய்யான  
**Transliteration:** poyyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is false (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, untrue, counterfeit  
**Usage:** Adjectival form of poy, used to describe false or fake things. Common in evaluating statements (poyyāṉa cōtaṉai "false test") and authenticity (poyyāṉa nāṇayam "counterfeit currency").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9.
**Form:** நியாயம்  
**Transliteration:** niyāyam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit nyāya) justice, logic, reason  
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, reasonableness  
**Usage:** Used to evaluate fairness and justness of actions, decisions, or situations. Common in moral and legal discourse (niyāyam-āṉa muṭivu "just decision") and in asserting rights (eṉ niyāyam "my right/due").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10.
**Form:** அநியாயம்  
**Transliteration:** aṉiyāyam  
**Literal meaning:** (a- negative prefix + niyāyam) injustice  
**Typical English translations:** injustice, unfairness, wrongness  
**Usage:** The negation of niyāyam, used to condemn unjust or unfair actions and situations. Common in moral protest (itu aṉiyāyam "this is unjust") and legal contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11.
**Form:** சத்தியம்  
**Transliteration:** cattiyam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit satya) truth, reality  
**Typical English translations:** truth, oath, vow  
**Usage:** Used especially in solemn contexts, oaths, and religious discourse. Common in oath-taking (cattiyam colla "speak the truth/swear") and emphasizing absolute truthfulness (cattiyam-āṉa vārttai "truthful words").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12.
**Form:** நம்பகமான  
**Transliteration:** nampakamāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (from nampikkai "trust")  
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable  
**Usage:** Used to evaluate the reliability of persons, sources, or information. Common in assessing credibility (nampakamāṉa ālkaḷ "reliable people," nampakamāṉa tōṟṟam "credible source").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13.
**Form:** தவறு  
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu  
**Literal meaning:** error, mistake, deviation  
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault, wrong  
**Usage:** Similar to tappu but often used in more formal contexts. Refers to errors, mistakes, and deviations from correctness (tavaṟu ceyya "commit an error," tavaṟāṉa karuttu "mistaken opinion").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14.
**Form:** சரியான  
**Transliteration:** cariyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is correct (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, accurate  
**Usage:** Adjectival form of cari, used attributively. Very common in evaluating correctness and appropriateness (cariyāṉa patiḷ "correct answer," cariyāṉa vaḻi "right way").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 15.
**Form:** தவறான  
**Transliteration:** tavaṟāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is wrong/mistaken (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous  
**Usage:** Adjectival form of tavaṟu, used to describe incorrect or mistaken things. Common in formal and informal contexts (tavaṟāṉa tīrppu "wrong decision," tavaṟāṉa kaṇakku "incorrect calculation").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 16.
**Form:** மெய்ப்பொருள்  
**Transliteration:** meypporuḷ  
**Literal meaning:** mey (truth/reality) + poruḷ (meaning/substance)  
**Typical English translations:** true meaning, reality, essence, truth  
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and literary contexts to refer to the true or essential meaning of something. Common in classical Tamil literature and in discussions of deeper significance (meypporuḷ-ai purintu koḷ "understand the true meaning").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17.
**Form:** ஒப்பு  
**Transliteration:** oppu  
**Literal meaning:** agreement, resemblance, comparison  
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, agreement, match  
**Usage:** Used to express resemblance or similarity between things. Common in comparisons (oppu-p pārkkka "compare," oppu-p poruḷ "similar item") and in evaluating whether things match or agree.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18.
**Form:** பொருந்தும்  
**Transliteration:** poruntum  
**Literal meaning:** fits, suits, agrees (verb)  
**Typical English translations:** fits, suits, agrees, is appropriate, matches  
**Usage:** Verb indicating that something fits, suits, or is appropriate to a situation. Used to evaluate appropriateness and correctness of fit (itu poruntātu "this doesn't fit/suit," avar colliyatu poruntum "what he said is appropriate").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 19.
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை  
**Transliteration:** nampikkai  
**Literal meaning:** trust, belief, faith  
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence  
**Usage:** Refers to trust, belief, or faith in persons, statements, or systems. Used in evaluating reliability and credibility (nampikkai vaikka "place trust," nampikkai-yāṉa āḷ "trustworthy person").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 20.
**Form:** அசல்  
**Transliteration:** acal  
**Literal meaning:** (from Arabic/Urdu asl) original, genuine  
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic, real  
**Usage:** Used to distinguish genuine or original items from copies or fakes. Common in commercial and everyday contexts (acal cāmāṉ "genuine goods," acal-āṉa "original/authentic").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21.
**Form:** நகல்  
**Transliteration:** nakal  
**Literal meaning:** copy, imitation  
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, imitation, fake  
**Usage:** Refers to copies or imitations, often with implication of being non-genuine. Used in contrast to acal (nakal cāmāṉ "imitation goods," nakal eṭukka "make a copy").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 22.
**Form:** உண்மையில்  
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil  
**Literal meaning:** in truth, in reality (locative form)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, actually, in fact  
**Usage:** Adverbial expression emphasizing the truth or reality of a statement. Common in discourse to assert what is actually the case (uṇmaiyil avaṉ varala "actually he didn't come," uṇmaiyil colluvēṉ-eṉṟāl "to tell the truth").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 23.
**Form:** மெய்யாக  
**Transliteration:** meyyāka  
**Literal meaning:** truly, genuinely (adverbial form)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, genuinely, really, actually  
**Usage:** Adverbial form emphasizing genuineness or truth. Used in more formal or literary contexts (meyyāka collukiṟēṉ "I speak truly," meyyāka nēcikka "love genuinely").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24.
**Form:** பொய்யாக  
**Transliteration:** poyyāka  
**Literal meaning:** falsely (adverbial form)  
**Typical English translations:** falsely, untruthfully, fakely  
**Usage:** Adverbial form indicating that something is done falsely or untruthfully. Used to describe deceptive actions (poyyāka colla "speak falsely," poyyāka nāṭakam āṭu "act falsely/pretend").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 25.
**Form:** உறுதி  
**Transliteration:** uṟuti  
**Literal meaning:** firmness, certainty, confirmation  
**Typical English translations:** certainty, confirmation, assurance, firmness  
**Usage:** Refers to certainty, confirmation, or firm assurance. Used in epistemic contexts (uṟuti-yāka "certainly," uṟuti ceyya "confirm") and to evaluate the reliability or definiteness of information.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 26.
**Form:** சந்தேகம்  
**Transliteration:** cantēkam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit sandeha) doubt, suspicion  
**Typical English translations:** doubt, suspicion, uncertainty  
**Usage:** Expresses doubt or uncertainty about truth or reliability. Common in epistemic evaluation (cantēkam irukkiṟatu "there is doubt," cantēkam-iṉṟi "without doubt").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 27.
**Form:** ஏமாற்று  
**Transliteration:** ēmāṟṟu  
**Literal meaning:** deception, cheating  
**Typical English translations:** deception, fraud, cheating, trickery  
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception or fraud. Used to evaluate dishonest conduct (ēmāṟṟu ceyya "deceive/cheat," ēmāṟṟu-k-kāraṉ "deceiver/cheat").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 28.
**Form:** நேர்மையான  
**Transliteration:** nērmaiyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is honest/upright (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** honest, upright, straightforward, sincere  
**Usage:** Adjectival form of nērmai, used to describe honest or upright persons and conduct. Common in character evaluation (nērmaiyāṉa āḷ "honest person," nērmaiyāṉa nāṭṭam "honest conduct").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 29.
**Form:** பொருத்தம்  
**Transliteration:** poruttam  
**Literal meaning:** fitness, suitability, appropriateness  
**Typical English translations:** suitability, appropriateness, compatibility, match  
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being suitable, appropriate, or compatible. Used in evaluating fit or match (poruttam-āṉa "suitable/appropriate," poruttam pārkkka "check compatibility").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 30.
**Form:** தகுதி  
**Transliteration:** takuti  
**Literal meaning:** worthiness, qualification, deservingness  
**Typical English translations:** worthiness, qualification, merit, deservingness  
**Usage:** Refers to worthiness, qualification, or merit. Used to evaluate whether someone or something deserves or is qualified for something (takuti-yāṉa āḷ "qualified person," takuti irukkiṟatu "is deserving/worthy").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
