# G7a — Data-Derived Global Constraint Matrix

---

## Preliminary Notes on Procedure

The semantic dimensions and constructional functions below are drawn directly from the G1–G6 synthesis and the underlying F-phase data. No external theoretical framework is assumed. Where the data supports a judgment, the classification is given; where the data is insufficient or contradictory, this is stated explicitly. The matrix covers the eleven semantic dimensions identified in G3 and the thirteen constructional functions identified in G5. Cells are classified as ALLOWED, BLOCKED, VARIABLE, or INSUFFICIENT DATA, with brief supporting notes drawn from attested evidence.

---

## Section 1: Semantic Dimensions (rows) and Constructional Functions (columns)

**Semantic Dimensions (from G3):**
- D1: Propositional truth of statements (whether a claim corresponds to the facts)
- D2: Genuineness of objects, documents, and persons (whether an entity is what it presents itself as)
- D3: Conformity to a standard or norm (whether an answer, procedure, or action meets a relevant standard)
- D4: Reliability and credibility of sources and persons over time
- D5: Sincerity and honesty of inner disposition and speech
- D6: Deliberate deception and intentional falsification
- D7: Legal or institutional validity of documents, procedures, and claims
- D8: Epistemic certainty and source of knowledge
- D9: Appearance of truth or genuineness without assertion of actual truth
- D10: Correspondence between a label or description and the actual nature of the thing
- D11: Ontological status of being (real vs. unreal vs. indeterminate)

**Constructional Functions (from G5):**
- F-A: Attributing a truth-domain property to a subject (copular predication)
- F-B: Negating a truth-domain attribution
- F-C: Modifying a noun with a truth-domain expression (adnominal modification)
- F-D: Describing a change into or out of a truth-domain state (inchoative/change-of-state)
- F-E: Causing a truth-domain state to obtain (causative)
- F-F: Deriving an abstract nominal naming a truth-domain property
- F-G: Naming an agent characterized by a truth-domain property (agentive nominalization)
- F-H: Framing a statement as frank, corrective, or sincere (discourse framing)
- F-I: Embedding a truth-domain evaluation in a subordinate clause (propositional complement)
- F-J: Expressing obligation or prohibition regarding truth-domain conduct (deontic)
- F-K: Marking the degree or intensity of a truth-domain predication
- F-L: Marking the source or certainty of a truth-domain claim (evidential/epistemic-source)
- F-M: Expressing resemblance to truth or genuineness without asserting it (similative/verisimilitude)

## Section 3: Narrative Analysis

### 3.1 Strongly Recurrent Constraints (ALLOWED across most or all languages)

**Copular attribution (F-A) is universally available for all semantic dimensions.** Every language in the corpus uses a copular or zero-copula construction to predicate truth-domain properties of subjects, and this construction is attested for all eleven semantic dimensions wherever the dimension is lexicalized. The copula is overt in zh, el, en, tl, sw; absent by default in he, sa, and qu. In qu, the obligatory evidential enclitic (*-m/-mi*) substitutes for the copula as the primary marker of assertive force. No dimension is blocked from copular predication in any language.

**Negation of attribution (F-B) is universally available for all semantic dimensions.** Every language has a strategy for negating truth-domain attributions, and this strategy applies across all dimensions. The strategies vary (prefixal negation in sa/el/en; syntactic negation in zh/tl/qu; suppletive copula in sw; *lo* + *bilti* + *ʾi-* system in he), but no dimension is blocked from negation in any language. The he *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry (applies to *-ut* nominalizations but not to adjectives) is a language-specific restriction within F-B, not a cross-language blocking.

**Abstract nominalization (F-F) is broadly available for D1–D7.** All eight languages derive abstract property nouns from truth-domain evaluative bases for the core dimensions (D1–D7). The morphological strategy varies (suffix, prefix, circumfix, copular nominalization), but the function is consistently attested. For D8–D11, abstract nominalization is more variable: D8 (certainty/doubt) is nominalized in all 8 languages; D9 (verisimilitude) is nominalized in 5 of 8; D10 and D11 are nominalized primarily in zh and sa.

**Propositional complement embedding (F-I) is broadly available for all dimensions.** Every language can embed a truth-domain predication as the complement of a verb of knowing, believing, proving, or saying. This function is attested for all eleven dimensions wherever the dimension is lexicalized. The complementizer strategy varies (nominalized clause in qu/sa; complementizer particle in he/el/en/tl/sw), but no dimension is blocked from complement embedding.

**Discourse framing (F-H) is broadly available for D1, D5, and D6.** The most consistently documented discourse-framing constructions are those signaling frank or corrective speech (D1) and sincere inner disposition (D5). Framing constructions for D6 (deception) are less grammaticalized but attested. Framing constructions for D7 (legal validity), D9 (verisimilitude), D10, and D11 are less consistently documented.

**Deontic constructions (F-J) are broadly available for D1, D5, and D6.** Constructions enjoining truthful speech (D1/D5) or prohibiting lying and falsification (D6) are attested in all 8 languages. Deontic constructions for D3 (correctness), D4 (reliability), and D7 (legal validity) are attested in most languages. Deontic constructions for D8–D11 are less consistently documented.

**Degree modification (F-K) is broadly available for D1–D6.** Degree modifiers, intensifiers, and emphatic elements are attested for the core truth-domain dimensions in all 8 languages. Reduplication as an intensification strategy is documented in tl, sw, and zh; emphatic suffixes (*-puni*, *-lla*) in qu; degree adverbs in he, el, en, sa. For D7–D11, degree modification is attested but less consistently documented.

**The agentive asymmetry (F-G) is a robust cross-language pattern.** Negative-pole agents (liars, deceivers, forgers) are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-tellers) across all 8 languages and all relevant dimensions (D1, D5, D6). This asymmetry is supported across unrelated language families and is one of the most robust patterns in the corpus. No language in the corpus has a single lexicalized word for "truth-teller" formed by simple suffixation on the truth root; all require either a compound or a periphrastic construction.

### 3.2 Likely Prohibitions or Avoidance Patterns

**Inchoative constructions (F-D) are consistently restricted or avoided for certain dimension-predicate combinations.** The F6 naturalness data across multiple languages converges on the following pattern: inchoative constructions are natural when the change-of-state is the result of an investigation, discovery, or external process (en *proved true*; el *αποδείχτηκε αληθής*; he *hitgalaʾh ke-ʾotenti*; tl *naging totoo*; sw *ulidhihirika*), but are less natural or [MARGINAL] when the change is presented as spontaneous or gradual for categorical truth-value predicates. Specifically:
- zh F6: *他的判断变得正确了* (his judgment became correct) is [MARGINAL]; *这部电影变得越来越真实了* (the film became more and more realistic) is [MARGINAL]
- en F6: *became true* is [MARGINAL]; *proved true* and *turned out to be true* are [CORE]
- el F6: *γίνομαι* + truth-domain adjective is [Non-idiomatic] for propositional truth transitions specifically; *αποδείχτηκε* is [CORE]
- he F6: *ha-mismakh hafakh le-mezuyaf be-yadav* (the document became forged in his hands) is [UNCERTAIN]; causative strongly preferred
- qu: no inchoative form for *llulla* is documented; the gap is attested

This pattern suggests that inchoative constructions for truth-domain predicates are preferred when the change is externally caused or discovered, and are avoided or marked when the change is presented as spontaneous or self-initiated for the negative pole (becoming false, becoming a liar). The positive pole (becoming true, becoming genuine) is more tolerant of inchoative constructions across languages.

**Agentive nominalization of the positive pole (F-G) is consistently avoided via simple suffixation.** No language in the corpus lexicalizes "truth-teller" as a single word formed by simple suffixation on the truth root. This is not a gap in individual languages but a consistent cross-language pattern. The concept requires either a compound (en *truth-teller*; qu *cheqaq runa*; sw *mtu wa kweli*) or a periphrastic construction (he *dover ʾemet*; el *αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια*). The negative pole (liar, deceiver) is consistently lexicalized as a single word in all 8 languages.

**Causative constructions (F-E) for D11 (ontological status) are restricted.** The data shows that causative constructions for bringing something into or out of genuine existence are attested primarily in sa (*asato mā sad gamaya*: lead me from the unreal to the real) and are not consistently documented in other languages for this dimension. In other languages, D11 causative is covered by D1 or D2 causative vocabulary without a dedicated D11-specific causative.

**Similative constructions (F-M) are absent or weakly attested for D7 (legal validity) and D11 (ontological status).** No clear evidence for similative constructions specifically for legal validity is documented across multiple languages. For D11, the *ābhāsa* and *pratibhāsa* elements in sa are the primary similative expressions, but these are not paralleled in other languages. The *εικονική πράξη* (el: sham transaction) is adjacent to D7 but is not a similative construction in the same sense as *parang totoo* (tl) or *cheqa hina* (qu).

**Deontic constructions (F-J) for D9 (verisimilitude) and D11 (ontological status) are not documented.** No evidence for deontic constructions specifically enjoining or prohibiting the creation of false appearances (D9) or the assertion of ontological claims (D11) is documented across multiple languages. This may reflect a genuine gap or a gap in the data.

### 3.3 Important Divergences, Substitutions, and Asymmetries

**The qu evidential system (F-L) substitutes for multiple constructional functions.** The direct evidential *-m/-mi* simultaneously marks the speaker's epistemic commitment (F-L), reinforces the assertive force of the predication (F-A), and signals that the speaker has direct knowledge rather than reported information. In other languages, these three functions are expressed through separate constructions. This substitution compresses into a single morphological marker what other languages express through multiple distinct constructions.

**The sw *-ika/-eka* stative suffix (F-D) substitutes for a periphrastic change-of-state construction** in contexts where no external agent is implied. The semantic distinction between *kudanganyika* (to be taken in, no agent implied) and *kudanganywa* (to be deceived by an agent) is morphologically encoded in sw but expressed through separate constructions or lexical choices in other languages.

**The tl discourse particle system substitutes for several constructional functions.** The particles *ba*, *na*, *pala*, *nga*, *naman* encode epistemic stances toward truth predicates that are expressed through separate adverbs, modal verbs, or discourse markers in other languages. The *totoo na* construction (F-D) uses a grammatical particle to mark a change into a truth-domain state, while other languages use a change-of-state verb or a morphological inchoative.

**The zh four-character expression format** is a constructional strategy with no parallel in the other seven languages. Fixed four-character expressions (*名副其实*, *弄虚作假*, *真金不怕火炼*) function as single evaluative units, and internal negation is a productive strategy for producing antonyms within this format. This format encodes evaluative meanings that in other languages would require a full clause or a periphrastic construction.

**The he *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry** creates a constructional gap: the *ʾi-* prefix negates *-ut* nominalizations but not the corresponding adjectives. This means that the negated abstract nominal (*ʾi-ʾaminut*) and the negated adjective (*lo ʾamin*) are formed through different strategies, creating an asymmetry between the adjectival and nominalized negation systems not paralleled in the other seven languages.

**The qu causative pathway asymmetry** — *-ncha-* for positive-pole adjectival roots vs. *-chi-* for the negative-pole root *llulla* — is a morphological asymmetry within the same language that has no parallel in the other seven languages. The positive pole (cheqa, allin, sut'i) uses one causative pathway; the negative pole (llulla) uses another.

**The sw light-verb asymmetry** (*sema ukweli* for truth-telling vs. *piga uongo* for lying) creates a constructional asymmetry within the same domain: the two poles of the truth/falsehood domain are expressed through different light verbs, with no *piga kweli* construction attested.

**D9 (verisimilitude) is expressed constructionally rather than lexically in tl and qu.** In tl, *parang totoo* and *mukhang totoo* use appearance verbs rather than dedicated truth-domain morphemes. In qu, *cheqa hina* uses the general similative *hina*. In el and sa, D9 is explicitly lexicalized (*αληθοφανής*, *ābhāsa*). This divergence — lexical in some languages, constructional in others — is a consistent pattern for D9.

**D10 and D11 are only clearly lexicalized as distinct groupings in zh and sa respectively.** In most other languages, D10 (label-nature correspondence) is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary, and D11 (ontological status) is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary. The elaboration of D10 in zh (名副其实/名不副实, 本质/实质) and D11 in sa (sat/asat/mithyā, paramārtha/vyāvahārika) reflects language-specific or tradition-specific elaboration rather than a cross-linguistically robust dimension.
