# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## STEP 1 — CENTRAL ROOTS

---

**Root 1**

- **Form:** 실 (實)
- **Transliteration:** sil
- **P1 participation:** 6 items — 진실 (jinsil), 사실 (sasil), 정확 (jeonghwak, via 확實 structure), 진정성 (jinjeonseong), 본질 (bonjil), 실체 (silche)
- **Core meaning contribution:** "Real, actual, substantive; that which has concrete existence or corresponds to reality." This character anchors the domain of factual and ontological reality — the idea that something genuinely exists or obtains, as opposed to being empty, false, or merely apparent.

---

**Root 2**

- **Form:** 진 (眞)
- **Transliteration:** jin
- **P1 participation:** 5 items — 진실 (jinsil), 진짜 (jinjja), 진위 (jinwi), 진정성 (jinjeonseong), and implicitly in the 진/가 contrast structuring the domain
- **Core meaning contribution:** "True, genuine, authentic; that which is what it claims or appears to be." This character marks the positive pole of the genuine/false opposition and anchors authenticity evaluation across registers.

---

**Root 3**

- **Form:** 위 (僞)
- **Transliteration:** wi
- **P1 participation:** 4 items — 허위 (heowi), 진위 (jinwi), 위조 (wijo), and structurally in 가짜 (gajja) as the semantic counterpart
- **Core meaning contribution:** "False, counterfeit, deceptive; that which misrepresents itself or is made to appear as something it is not." This character anchors the negative pole of the authenticity domain, particularly in formal, legal, and evaluative contexts.

---

## STEP 2 — ROOT EXPANSION

---

### ROOT 1: 실 (實) — sil

---

**1.**
- **Form:** 실제
- **Transliteration:** silje
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 際 (occasion/boundary/contact)
- **Natural English translation:** reality, actuality; in reality, actually
- **Context of use:** Used pervasively across all registers to contrast what is actually the case with what is imagined, claimed, or theoretical. Appears both as a noun (실제와 다르다, "differs from reality") and as a prenominal modifier (실제 상황, "actual situation").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** 실제로
- **Transliteration:** siljero
- **Literal breakdown:** 實際 (reality) + 로 (adverbial particle)
- **Natural English translation:** actually, in reality, in practice
- **Context of use:** Adverbial form of 실제, used to assert that something is genuinely the case as opposed to being theoretical, claimed, or apparent. Extremely common in spoken and written Korean across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** 실질
- **Transliteration:** siljil
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 質 (substance/quality)
- **Natural English translation:** substance, substantive reality, actual content
- **Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and analytical discourse to distinguish the substantive content or effect of something from its formal or nominal appearance. Appears in 실질적 (substantive, real in effect) and 실질적으로 (substantively, in substance).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** 실질적
- **Transliteration:** siljiljeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 實質 (substance) + 的 (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** substantive, real, effective, actual
- **Context of use:** Adjectival form used to describe something that has genuine substance or effect, as opposed to being merely formal or nominal. Common in legal, policy, and analytical writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
- **Form:** 실상
- **Transliteration:** silsang
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 狀 (state/condition/appearance)
- **Natural English translation:** the actual state of affairs, the real situation, the truth of the matter
- **Context of use:** Used to refer to the true or actual condition of something, often contrasted with how it appears or is represented. Common in journalistic and analytical discourse: 실상을 파악하다 (to grasp the actual situation).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
- **Form:** 실태
- **Transliteration:** siltae
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 態 (state/condition)
- **Natural English translation:** actual state, real condition, true situation
- **Context of use:** Used in investigative, journalistic, and policy contexts to describe the actual condition of a phenomenon, often implying that the reality may differ from official or expected accounts. Appears in 실태 조사 (survey of actual conditions).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
- **Form:** 실증
- **Transliteration:** siljeung
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 證 (evidence/proof)
- **Natural English translation:** empirical verification, positive proof, demonstration
- **Context of use:** Used in academic and scientific contexts to refer to empirical evidence or the act of demonstrating something through concrete evidence. Participates in 실증주의 (positivism), 실증적 (empirical, evidence-based), 실증하다 (to demonstrate empirically).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
- **Form:** 실증주의
- **Transliteration:** siljeungjuui
- **Literal breakdown:** 實證 (empirical proof) + 主義 (doctrine/ism)
- **Natural English translation:** positivism
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical and social-scientific discourse to refer to the epistemological position that knowledge must be grounded in empirical evidence. Standard academic terminology.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
- **Form:** 실증적
- **Transliteration:** siljeungjeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 實證 (empirical proof) + 的 (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** empirical, evidence-based, positivist
- **Context of use:** Used in academic writing to characterize research or claims that are grounded in concrete evidence rather than speculation or theory alone.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
- **Form:** 실현
- **Transliteration:** silhyeon
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 現 (to appear/manifest)
- **Natural English translation:** realization, actualization, fulfillment
- **Context of use:** Used to describe the process by which something potential or planned becomes actual. Common in everyday, political, and philosophical discourse: 꿈을 실현하다 (to realize a dream), 실현 가능성 (feasibility, literally "possibility of realization").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
- **Form:** 실현 가능성
- **Transliteration:** silhyeon ganeungseong
- **Literal breakdown:** 實現 (realization) + 可能性 (possibility)
- **Natural English translation:** feasibility, practicability
- **Context of use:** Used in planning, policy, and project evaluation to assess whether a proposed course of action can actually be carried out. Standard in administrative and business discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
- **Form:** 실력
- **Transliteration:** sillyeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 力 (power/ability)
- **Natural English translation:** actual ability, real skill, genuine competence
- **Context of use:** Used to refer to a person's genuine, demonstrated ability as opposed to credentials, reputation, or claimed skill. Extremely common in educational and professional contexts: 실력을 발휘하다 (to demonstrate one's real ability).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
- **Form:** 실적
- **Transliteration:** siljeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 績 (achievement/result)
- **Natural English translation:** actual results, performance record, track record
- **Context of use:** Used in business, administrative, and evaluative contexts to refer to concrete, measurable results as opposed to plans or promises. Appears in 실적 평가 (performance evaluation).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
- **Form:** 실용
- **Transliteration:** siryong
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 用 (use/application)
- **Natural English translation:** practical use, utility, pragmatism
- **Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether something has genuine practical application. Participates in 실용적 (practical, utilitarian), 실용주의 (pragmatism), 실용성 (practicality). Common in everyday and policy discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
- **Form:** 실용적
- **Transliteration:** siryongjeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 實用 (practical use) + 的 (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** practical, utilitarian, pragmatic
- **Context of use:** Used to characterize approaches, tools, or solutions that have genuine practical value. Extremely common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
- **Form:** 실용주의
- **Transliteration:** siryongjuui
- **Literal breakdown:** 實用 (practical use) + 主義 (doctrine/ism)
- **Natural English translation:** pragmatism
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical and political discourse to refer to the view that the value of ideas or policies is determined by their practical consequences. Standard academic and journalistic terminology.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
- **Form:** 실명
- **Transliteration:** silmyeong
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 名 (name)
- **Natural English translation:** real name, actual name
- **Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and online contexts to distinguish a person's genuine registered name from a pseudonym or alias. Appears in 실명제 (real-name system, a policy requiring use of real names in financial or online contexts).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
- **Form:** 실명제
- **Transliteration:** silmyeongje
- **Literal breakdown:** 實名 (real name) + 制 (system/institution)
- **Natural English translation:** real-name system, real-name verification system
- **Context of use:** Used in administrative and policy discourse to refer to systems requiring individuals to use their legal names, particularly in financial transactions or online platforms. A well-established term in Korean public discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
- **Form:** 실화
- **Transliteration:** silhwa
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 話 (story/speech)
- **Natural English translation:** true story, based on a true story
- **Context of use:** Used in literary, cinematic, and journalistic contexts to indicate that a narrative is based on actual events. Appears in 실화를 바탕으로 한 (based on a true story).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
- **Form:** 실존
- **Transliteration:** siljon
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 存 (to exist)
- **Natural English translation:** actual existence, real existence; existentialism (in compound)
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical and everyday contexts to assert that something genuinely exists. Participates in 실존주의 (existentialism), 실존적 (existential), 실존 인물 (real person, as opposed to fictional character).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
- **Form:** 실존주의
- **Transliteration:** siljonjuui
- **Literal breakdown:** 實存 (real existence) + 主義 (doctrine/ism)
- **Natural English translation:** existentialism
- **Context of use:** Standard philosophical term for the existentialist movement. Used in academic, literary, and intellectual discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
- **Form:** 실존 인물
- **Transliteration:** siljon inmul
- **Literal breakdown:** 實存 (real existence) + 人物 (person/figure)
- **Natural English translation:** real person, actual historical figure
- **Context of use:** Used in literary, cinematic, and historical contexts to distinguish characters based on real people from fictional ones. Common in media and publishing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
- **Form:** 실정
- **Transliteration:** siljeong
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 情 (situation/circumstances/feeling)
- **Natural English translation:** actual circumstances, real situation, the facts of the matter
- **Context of use:** Used to refer to the actual state of affairs, particularly in administrative or policy contexts: 실정을 모르다 (to be unaware of the actual situation). Also appears in 실정법 (positive law, law as it actually exists).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
- **Form:** 실정법
- **Transliteration:** siljeongbeop
- **Literal breakdown:** 實情 (actual situation) + 法 (law) — or alternatively 實 + 定法 (established law)
- **Natural English translation:** positive law, enacted law
- **Context of use:** Legal term referring to law as it actually exists in a given jurisdiction, as opposed to natural law or moral law. Standard in legal education and discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
- **Form:** 실수
- **Transliteration:** silsu
- **Literal breakdown:** 實 (real/actual) + 數 (number) — in mathematical sense; in everyday sense the etymology is debated
- **Natural English translation:** mistake, error (everyday); real number (mathematical)
- **Context of use:** In everyday usage, refers to an unintentional mistake or error. In mathematics, refers to real numbers (as opposed to imaginary numbers). These are two distinct lexical items sharing the same form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
- **Form:** 실망
- **Transliteration:** silmang
- **Literal breakdown:** 失 (to lose) + 望 (hope/expectation) — note: this 실 is 失, not 實
- **Natural English translation:** disappointment, disillusionment
- **Context of use:** Included here to flag that 실망 contains 失 (to lose), not 實 (real/actual), and therefore does not participate in the 實-root family. Noted to prevent conflation.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN] — excluded from root family; different character

---

**27.**
- **Form:** 사실상
- **Transliteration:** sasilsang
- **Literal breakdown:** 事實 (fact) + 上 (above/on/in terms of)
- **Natural English translation:** in fact, effectively, de facto, practically speaking
- **Context of use:** Used as an adverbial expression to assert that something is effectively or practically the case, even if not formally or officially so. Extremely common across all registers. Equivalent to "de facto" in legal and administrative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
- **Form:** 사실적
- **Transliteration:** sasiljeok
- **Literal breakdown:** 事實 (fact) + 的 (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** factual, realistic, true-to-life

- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
- **Form:** 사실주의
- **Transliteration:** sasiljuui
- **Literal breakdown:** 事實 (fact) + 主義 (doctrine/ism)
- **Natural English translation:** realism (as an artistic or philosophical movement)
- **Context of use:** Used in literary, artistic, and philosophical discourse to refer to the movement or approach that emphasizes faithful representation of reality. Standard academic and critical terminology.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
- **Form:** 사실관계
- **Transliteration:** sasil gwangye
- **Literal breakdown:** 事實 (fact) + 關係 (relationship/connection)
- **Natural English translation:** the facts of the case, factual circumstances

- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The 실 (實) root is highly productive. The above represents the 20–30 most common forms. Additional compounds exist, including 실황 (實況, live broadcast/actual situation), 실감 (實感, real feeling/sense of reality), 실무 (實務, practical affairs/actual work), 실습 (實習, practical training), 실천 (實踐, practice/putting into action), 실행 (實行, execution/implementation), 실효 (實效, actual effect), and others.

### ROOT 2: 진 (眞) — jin

---

**1.**
- **Form:** 진실하다
- **Transliteration:** jinsil hada
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 하다 (to be/do)
- **Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be sincere, to be genuine
- **Context of use:** Predicative form used to characterize a person or their conduct as genuinely honest and sincere. Common in character evaluation and interpersonal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** 진실성
- **Transliteration:** jinsilseong
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 性 (nature/quality)
- **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, sincerity, genuineness as a quality
- **Context of use:** Used to evaluate the quality of being truthful or sincere in a person, statement, or representation. Common in ethical, journalistic, and legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** 진실되다
- **Transliteration:** jinsil doeda
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 되다 (to become/be)
- **Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be genuine, to be sincere
- **Context of use:** Alternative predicative form to 진실하다, with a slightly more passive or resultant nuance. Used in character and conduct evaluation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** 진실을 말하다
- **Transliteration:** jinsilеul malhada
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 을 (object particle) + 말하다 (to speak)
- **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
- **Context of use:** Standard collocation for the act of telling the truth. Used across all registers, from everyday conversation to legal testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
- **Form:** 진실을 밝히다
- **Transliteration:** jinsilеul balkhida
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 을 (object particle) + 밝히다 (to illuminate/reveal)
- **Natural English translation:** to reveal the truth, to bring the truth to light
- **Context of use:** Used in journalistic, legal, and investigative contexts to describe the act of uncovering or disclosing what is actually true. A very common collocation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
- **Form:** 진실 규명
- **Transliteration:** jinsil gyumyeong
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 糾明 (to investigate and clarify)
- **Natural English translation:** truth investigation, clarification of the truth, truth-finding
- **Context of use:** Used in political, legal, and historical contexts to refer to formal processes of investigating and establishing what actually happened, particularly in cases of historical injustice or official misconduct. Appears in institutional names and policy discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
- **Form:** 진실화해위원회

- **Literal breakdown:** 眞實 (truth) + 和解 (reconciliation) + 委員會 (committee/commission)
- **Natural English translation:** Truth and Reconciliation Commission
- **Context of use:** Refers specifically to the Korean Truth and Reconciliation Commission (진실·화해를 위한 과거사정리위원회 in full), established to investigate historical human rights abuses. Used in political and historical discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
- **Form:** 진상
- **Transliteration:** jinsang
- **Literal breakdown:** 眞 (true/genuine) + 相 (appearance/aspect/truth)
- **Natural English translation:** the true facts, the real story, the actual circumstances
- **Context of use:** Used to refer to the true state of affairs behind an event or situation, often implying that the surface account is incomplete or misleading. Common in journalistic and investigative contexts: 진상을 규명하다 (to investigate the true facts), 진상 조사 (investigation of the true facts).

---

**9.**

- **Literal breakdown:** 眞相 (true facts) + 糾明 (to investigate and clarify)
- **Natural English translation:** investigation of the true facts, fact-finding
- **Context of use:** Used in political, legal, and journalistic contexts to describe formal or informal efforts to establish what actually happened in a disputed or unclear situation. Very common in Korean public discourse.

---

**10.**
