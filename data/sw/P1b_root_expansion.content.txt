# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **-kweli** (truth)
- **Root form:** -kweli
- **P1 participation:** 3 items (#1 kweli, #2 ukweli, #5 -a kweli, #9 -enye ukweli)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes truth, veracity, genuineness; the fundamental quality of being true or real as opposed to false or deceptive.

### Root 2: **-ongo** (falsehood/lie)
- **Root form:** -ongo
- **P1 participation:** 2 items (#4 uongo, #13 -a uongo)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes falsehood, deception, lying; the quality of being untrue or deliberately misleading.

### Root 3: **halisi** (genuine/authentic)
- **Root form:** halisi (from Arabic ḫāliṣ)
- **P1 participation:** 2 items (#8 halisi, #23 uhalisia)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes genuineness, authenticity, purity; the quality of being real rather than imitation or counterfeit.

## Step 3 — Note Peripheral Extensions

### Root -kweli (truth)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **Intensity/emphasis marking:** The root appears in expressions like "kweli sana" and "kweli kabisa" where it functions not only to mark truth but also to intensify or emphasize the degree or certainty of a claim, extending beyond simple truth-evaluation into discourse-pragmatic functions of emphasis.

2. **Existential/ontological domains:** Expressions like "ukweli wa dhati" (essential truth) and "ukweli wa maisha" (truth of life) extend the root into philosophical discussions of essence, existence, and the nature of reality, moving beyond propositional truth into metaphysical territory.

3. **Domain-specific truth types:** The productive pattern "ukweli wa [domain]" (ukweli wa kisheria, ukweli wa kisayansi, ukweli wa historia) creates domain-specific truth concepts that may have different epistemic standards or verification procedures, suggesting the root participates in creating specialized epistemic categories.

4. **Spatial metaphors:** "ukweli wa ndani" (inner truth) vs. "ukweli wa nje" (external truth) employs spatial metaphors to distinguish subjective from objective truth, extending the root into psychological and phenomenological domains.

### Root -ongo (falsehood)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **Person-characterization:** The derivation "mwongo/waongo" (liar/liars) extends the root from describing statements to characterizing persons, moving from propositional evaluation to moral character assessment.

2. **Magnitude scaling:** Expressions like "uongo mkubwa" (big lie) and "uongo mdogo" (small lie) use the root with size adjectives to create a scale of moral or practical significance, extending beyond simple true/false binary into gradations of deception.

3. **Behavioral deception:** "uongo wa vitendo" (lies through actions) extends the root from verbal/propositional domain into behavioral and performative domains, suggesting deception through conduct rather than statements.

4. **Modal/rhetorical uses:** "uongo wa ajabu" (strange/remarkable lie) and "uongo wa namna gani" (what kind of lie) show the root participating in expressions of surprise, incredulity, or rhetorical questioning beyond simple falsity marking.

### Root halisi (genuine/authentic)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **Material authenticity:** Applications to physical materials (dhahabu halisi - genuine gold, ngozi halisi - genuine leather) extend the root from truth-evaluation into material verification and commercial authentication, focusing on substance rather than propositional content.

2. **Emotional/psychological authenticity:** Uses with "hisia" (feelings), "mapenzi" (love), "urafiki" (friendship) extend the root into evaluating sincerity and authenticity of emotional states and relationships, moving from factual truth to psychological genuineness.

3. **Aesthetic authentication:** "sanaa halisi" (genuine art) applies the root to artistic evaluation, where authenticity involves questions of authorship, originality, and artistic integrity rather than factual truth.

4. **Character/personality assessment:** "mtu halisi" (genuine person) and "tabia halisi" (genuine character) extend the root to evaluate personal authenticity and integrity, moving from object-authentication to moral-psychological evaluation.

5. **Existential/phenomenological reality:** "uhalisia wa maisha" (reality of life) and "uhalisia wa mambo" (reality of things) extend the root into philosophical discussions of existence and lived experience, beyond simple authenticity verification.
