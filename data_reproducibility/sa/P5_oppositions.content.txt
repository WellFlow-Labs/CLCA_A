# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## OPPOSITION 1

**Term A:** सत्य / satya  
**Term B:** असत्य / asatya  
**Transliteration:** satya vs. asatya  
**Translation:** "true, truth, real" vs. "untrue, false, unreal"  
**Description:** Primary morphological opposition formed by negative prefix a-. Covers factual incorrectness, moral falsehood, and ontological non-existence depending on context.  
**Contrast type:** morphological  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## OPPOSITION 2

**Term A:** सत्य / satya  
**Term B:** मृषा / mṛṣā  
**Transliteration:** satya vs. mṛṣā  
**Translation:** "true, truth" vs. "falsely, in vain"  
**Description:** Lexical opposition between nominal/adjectival truth term and adverbial falsehood term. Mṛṣā specifically denotes false manner of action or speech.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Asymmetries Observed:

1. **Morphological productivity**: Negative prefix a- is highly productive for creating oppositions (satya/asatya, dharma/adharma, yukta/ayukta, etc.), but not all expected negative forms are explicitly attested in the context.

2. **Lexical vs. morphological**: Some semantic domains show lexical opposition (satya vs. mṛṣā, pramā vs. bhrama) while others rely on morphological negation (dharma vs. adharma). Both strategies are [CORE].

3. **Constructional oppositions**: Many oppositions exist only at the constructional level, with the same lexical items appearing in contrasting syntactic frames (e.g., satyaṃ vad vs. asatyaṃ mā vad).

4. **Implicit vs. explicit**: Some constructions imply opposition without explicit contrasting forms being attested (e.g., satyam eva jayate implies contrast with non-truth, but no explicit opposing construction exists).

5. **Domain specificity**: Epistemological oppositions (pramā/apramā, pramāṇa/apramāṇa) are highly developed and technical, while moral oppositions (dharma/adharma) are equally developed but in different registers.

### Patterns Not Forming Clear Oppositions:

1. **Measurement terms**: māna, parimāṇa show morphological negation (aparimāṇa) but do not form systematic oppositions with truth-domain terms.

2. **Seasonal/temporal terms**: ṛtu and derivatives do not participate in truth/falsehood oppositions despite deriving from ṛta.

3. **Social evaluation terms**: satkāra, māna (honor) have opposites (asatkāra) but these function in social rather than epistemic domains.

4. **Vedic archaic terms**: Many ṛta-compounds show limited oppositional productivity in classical usage.
