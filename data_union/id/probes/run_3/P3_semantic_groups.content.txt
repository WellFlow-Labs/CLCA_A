# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed across P1 and P2: which terms appear together in the same contexts, which are contrasted with each other, which share collocational partners, which are restricted to particular domains, and which participate in similar syntactic constructions. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

## GROUP 1: Terms used to affirm that a statement or claim corresponds to fact

**Label:** Affirming factual correspondence

**Definition:** These terms are used to assert or evaluate that a statement, report, or claim accurately reflects what is actually the case. They appear in contexts of verification, correction, and confirmation — in journalism, legal proceedings, and everyday discourse. They contrast with terms in Group 2 (factual error) and Group 3 (deliberate falsehood).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *benar* — the primary term for affirming that something is true or correct; participates in constructions such as *memang benar bahwa...* (it is indeed true that...) and *sudah terbukti benar* (already proven true)
- *tidak benar* — the standard negation used in official denials and journalistic corrections (*informasi itu tidak benar*, that information is not true)
- *akurat* — used specifically for information, data, or reporting that is free from error; collocates with *data*, *laporan*, *informasi*; paired with *reliabel* in research contexts (*data yang valid dan reliabel*)
- *tepat* — used when something corresponds exactly to a standard, target, or requirement; extends to timing (*tepat pada waktunya*), word choice (*kata yang tepat*), and situational fit (*pilihan yang tepat untuk situasi ini*)
- *kebenaran* — the nominalization of *benar*; used to refer to truth as a property or object of pursuit (*mencari kebenaran*, *menegakkan kebenaran*)
- *sebenarnya* — a high-frequency discourse marker introducing what is actually the case, often correcting a prior assumption (*sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju*)
- *benar adanya* — a fixed construction affirming that something is genuinely and factually the case, often with a tone of solemn acknowledgment
- *ada benarnya* — a partial-concession construction acknowledging that a claim has some validity without full endorsement
- *kebenaran ilmiah*, *kebenaran formal*, *kebenaran materiil* — domain-specific collocations distinguishing types of factual correctness in scientific and legal registers

**Notes:** *benar* is the broadest and most frequent term in this group. *akurat* is more restricted to technical and journalistic contexts. *tepat* overlaps with this group but also extends into Group 5 (situational fit). *kebenaran* in philosophical and religious collocations (*kebenaran hakiki*, *kebenaran sejati*, *kebenaran mutlak*) shades into Group 7 (essential or fundamental nature).

## GROUP 2: Terms used to characterize factual error or mistaken belief

**Label:** Characterizing error and mistaken belief

**Definition:** These terms are used to indicate that a belief, judgment, statement, or action is incorrect — typically without implying deliberate deception. They appear in contexts of correction, diagnosis of misunderstanding, and acknowledgment of fault. They contrast with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 1 (factual correctness).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *salah* — the primary term for wrongness or error; used across factual, moral, and evaluative contexts; participates in a large family of compounds
- *keliru* — used specifically for mistaken belief or judgment, often without implying moral fault; *kekeliruan* (error, misconception); *mengelirukan* (to mislead, to cause to err)
- *kesalahan* — the nominalization of *salah*; covers error, mistake, and fault; *mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake), *kesalahan fatal* (fatal error), *kesalahan prosedur* (procedural error)
- *salah paham* — misunderstanding; a very common compound for incorrect interpretation of a message or intention
- *salah kaprah* — a widespread misconception that has become accepted as correct; used in journalistic and educational commentary
- *salah tafsir* — misinterpretation, particularly of texts, laws, or religious passages
- *salah sangka* — mistaken assumption or false suspicion
- *kekeliruan* — error or misconception; *mengakui kekeliruan* (to acknowledge one's error)
- *tidak benar* — also functions in this group when used to characterize a claim as factually incorrect rather than deliberately false
- *salah baca*, *salah dengar*, *salah tulis*, *salah ucap*, *salah cetak* — a productive series of compounds characterizing specific types of perceptual or production error

**Notes:** The distinction between *salah* and *keliru* in practice is one of register and implied agency: *keliru* more often describes innocent error or misunderstanding, while *salah* covers a wider range including moral fault. *salah kaprah* is distinctive in that it describes error that has become socially normalized. The *salah* + [verb/noun] compounding pattern is highly productive and generates new forms as needed.

## GROUP 3: Terms used to characterize deliberate untruth

**Label:** Characterizing deliberate falsehood and lying

**Definition:** These terms are used to describe statements or persons where the untruth is understood to be intentional — where the speaker knows the statement is false and makes it anyway. They appear in moral, interpersonal, legal, and journalistic contexts. They contrast with Group 2 (innocent error) and are often paired with terms from Group 6 (deception and manipulation).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *bohong* — the primary everyday term for lying; functions as noun, adjective, and base for verbal derivations; *berbohong* (to lie), *pembohong* (liar), *kebohongan* (falsehood, lie); *membohongi* (to lie to someone)
- *dusta* — a near-synonym of *bohong* in more formal, literary, and religious registers; *berdusta* (to lie), *pendusta* (liar), *kedustaan* (falsehood as a quality); commonly paired with *tipu daya* in Islamic moral discourse (*jauh dari dusta dan tipu daya*)
- *kebohongan* — the nominalization of *bohong*; refers to a lie or the state of deception; *mengungkap kebohongan* (to expose a lie), *kebohongan publik* (public deception)
- *tidak benar* — in some contexts functions as a euphemistic or formal way of characterizing a deliberate lie without using *bohong* directly
- *bohong demi kebaikan* — a fixed construction framing a lie as morally motivated (a white lie); used in ethical discussions
- *sekali bohong, selamanya tidak dipercaya* — a near-proverbial formula about the consequences of lying
- *ketahuan bohong* — a fixed collocation for the discovery of a lie (*caught lying*)
- *sengaja menyesatkan* — deliberately misleading; the addition of *sengaja* (intentionally) to *menyesatkan* marks the deception as intentional, relevant in legal contexts

**Notes:** *bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms distinguished primarily by register; *bohong* is the everyday term, *dusta* is formal and literary. Both contrast with *keliru* (Group 2) in that they imply intent. *sengaja menyesatkan* bridges this group and Group 6 (deception and manipulation). *palsu* (Group 4) can describe false statements but is more typically applied to objects and documents.

## GROUP 4: Terms used to evaluate whether an object, document, or identity is genuine or counterfeit

**Label:** Evaluating genuineness versus counterfeit

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a physical object, document, credential, or identity is what it claims to be — as opposed to being a forgery, imitation, or fraudulent substitute. They appear heavily in legal, commercial, forensic, and administrative contexts. The central contrast is between *asli* (genuine, original) and *palsu* (fake, counterfeit).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *asli* — the primary term for genuine or original; used of documents, goods, persons, cultural items; contrasts with *palsu* and *tiruan*
- *palsu* — the primary term for fake or counterfeit; used of documents, currency, goods, identities; *memalsukan* (to forge), *pemalsuan* (forgery), *pemalsu* (forger), *kepalsuan* (fakeness)
- *tiruan* — imitation or copy; does not necessarily imply fraudulent intent; contrasts with *asli*; *meniru* (to imitate), *peniruan* (imitation)
- *keaslian* — authenticity as a property; *dijamin keasliannya* (authenticity guaranteed), *diragukan keasliannya* (authenticity questioned), *sertifikat keaslian* (certificate of authenticity), *verifikasi keaslian* (authenticity verification)
- *otentik* — authentic; used in formal, legal, and academic registers; *otentikasi* (authentication), *mengotentikasi* (to authenticate)
- *otentisitas* — authenticity as an abstract property; used in academic registers
- *uang palsu* — counterfeit currency; *dokumen palsu* — forged document; *ijazah palsu* — fake diploma; *identitas palsu* — false identity; *akun palsu* — fake account; *berita palsu* — fake news; *obat palsu* — counterfeit medicine; *produk palsu* — counterfeit product
- *tanda tangan asli* / *tanda tangan palsu* — genuine / forged signature
- *naskah asli* — original manuscript or text
- *foto asli* — original, unedited photograph
- *karya asli* — original creative work
- *asli atau palsu* — a binary evaluation frame (*genuine or fake*) used in commercial and forensic contexts
- *terbukti palsu* — proven false/forged (in legal proceedings)
- *dipalsukan* — to have been forged or falsified
- *mengaku asli padahal palsu* — claiming to be genuine when in fact fake; a deception-characterization construction
- *bukan asli* — not genuine

**Notes:** The *asli* / *palsu* contrast is the organizing axis of this group. *tiruan* occupies an intermediate position: it describes something made to resemble something else without necessarily implying fraud. *otentik* overlaps with *asli* but is more restricted to formal and legal registers. The *[noun] + palsu* compounding pattern is fully open and productive. *berita palsu* (fake news) is a notable extension of this pattern into the domain of information rather than physical objects, which creates overlap with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 6 (deception and manipulation).

## GROUP 5: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate formal validity or legitimacy

**Label:** Evaluating formal legal validity

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether an act, document, decision, or status meets the conditions required for official recognition — whether it is binding, lawful, and procedurally correct. They appear in legal documents, court decisions, administrative procedures, and religious law. They contrast with terms in Group 4 (genuineness of objects) and Group 1 (factual truth).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *sah* — the primary term for legal validity; *mengesahkan* (to ratify, to validate), *pengesahan* (ratification), *disahkan* (ratified), *tidak sah* (invalid), *tidak sah demi hukum* (null and void under the law)
- *absah* — a near-synonym of *sah* in formal and legal registers; *keabsahan* (validity, legitimacy), *mengabsahkan* (to legitimize)
- *keabsahan* — the primary nominalization for legal validity; *mempertanyakan keabsahan* (to question legitimacy), *keabsahan pemilu* (legitimacy of an election)
- *valid* — used in academic, scientific, and technical contexts for validity of arguments, instruments, or data; *validitas* (validity), *memvalidasi* (to validate), *validasi* (validation), *divalidasi* (validated)
- *sahih* — used primarily in Islamic scholarly contexts for the authenticity and soundness of hadith; *kesahihan* (soundness, authenticity of a text)
- *dinyatakan sah* — declared valid (by a court or official body); a fixed legal formula
- *secara hukum dianggap sah* — considered legally valid
- *sah menurut agama tetapi tidak menurut negara* — a contrastive construction common in Indonesian discourse about the dual religious-civil legal system
- *kebenaran formal* / *kebenaran materiil* — formal truth vs. material truth; a distinction in Indonesian criminal procedure
- *benar secara hukum* — legally correct
- *diakui kebenarannya* — whose truth is officially acknowledged

**Notes:** *sah* and *absah* are near-synonyms; *absah* is slightly more formal in some contexts and its *ke-...-an* nominalization (*keabsahan*) is the dominant form for the concept of legitimacy. *valid* is more restricted to academic and technical registers and does not carry the legal-administrative weight of *sah*. *sahih* is the most domain-restricted term in this group, concentrated in Islamic scholarly discourse. The distinction between *sah* (legal validity) and *asli* (genuineness) is maintained in practice: a document can be *asli* (genuine) but *tidak sah* (invalid) if it lacks proper authorization, and vice versa.

## GROUP 6: Terms used to describe the distortion or manipulation of information

**Label:** Describing distortion and manipulation of information

**Definition:** These terms are used to characterize acts or processes by which information, facts, or representations are deliberately altered, skewed, or presented in ways that produce false impressions. They appear in journalistic, legal, political, and academic contexts. They overlap with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) but focus more specifically on the warping or misrepresentation of existing information rather than the creation of outright lies.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *manipulasi* — deliberate distortion of facts, data, or situations; *memanipulasi* (to manipulate), *manipulatif* (manipulative), *dimanipulasi* (manipulated), *manipulator*; *terbukti melakukan manipulasi hasil pemilu* (proven to have manipulated election results)
- *distorsi* — distortion or misrepresentation; *mendistorsi* (to distort); focuses on the warping of an original form or content
- *menyesatkan* — misleading; describes statements or representations that lead others to incorrect conclusions, whether intentionally or not; *informasi yang menyesatkan publik* (information that misleads the public), *berpotensi menyesatkan* (potentially misleading), *sengaja menyesatkan* (deliberately misleading), *dinilai menyesatkan oleh para ahli* (assessed as misleading by experts)
- *penyesatan* — the act or process of misleading; *penyesatan informasi* (information misdirection)
- *tidak berdasar* — unfounded, baseless; used to evaluate claims as lacking evidentiary support; *klaim yang tidak berdasar* (unfounded claim)
- *palsu belaka* — entirely fake, pure fabrication; used for emphatic dismissal of a claim as wholly without genuine basis
- *klaim palsu* — false claim; used in legal and insurance contexts for knowingly untrue claims
- *salah informasi* — misinformation; inaccurate information, not necessarily intentional
- *salah kutip* — misquotation; inaccurate reproduction of someone's words
- *diduga terjadi manipulasi data* — data manipulation is alleged to have occurred; a standard journalistic construction for unverified allegations
- *bebas dari manipulasi* — free from manipulation; used in institutional discourse to assert integrity

**Notes:** *menyesatkan* is distinctive in that it does not require intent — something can be *menyesatkan* without the speaker intending to deceive. *manipulasi* and *distorsi* more strongly imply deliberate action. *salah informasi* (misinformation) contrasts with *manipulasi* in that it does not necessarily imply intent. *tidak berdasar* focuses on the absence of evidentiary support rather than on active distortion. This group overlaps with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) at the intentional end and with Group 2 (factual error) at the unintentional end.

## GROUP 7: Terms used in philosophical, religious, or literary contexts to refer to essential or fundamental nature

**Label:** Referring to essential or fundamental nature

**Definition:** These terms are used to refer to the deep, essential, or fundamental nature of something — as opposed to its surface appearance, conventional form, or partial manifestation. They appear in philosophical, Islamic theological, Sufi literary, and formal academic contexts. They are not primarily used to evaluate factual correctness or legal validity but to point toward a deeper level of reality or truth.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *hakikat* — essential nature or fundamental reality; *pada hakikatnya* (in essence, fundamentally) is the primary multi-word construction; *memahami hakikat kebenaran* (understanding the essential nature of truth); *jauh dari hakikat yang sebenarnya* (far from the true essence)
- *hakiki* — the adjectival form corresponding to *hakikat*; *makna hakiki* (essential meaning), *kebenaran hakiki* (essential truth)
- *esensi* — essence; more neutral in register than *hakikat*; *pada esensinya* (in essence), *menangkap esensi dari argumen* (to grasp the essence of an argument)
- *esensial* — essential, fundamental; *kebutuhan esensial* (essential needs)
- *sejati* — true or genuine in an essential sense; used in formal and literary registers; *cinta sejati* (true love), *pahlawan sejati* (true hero), *kesejatian diri* (the trueness of the self); *mencari jati diri yang sejati* (seeking one's true self)
- *kebenaran sejati* — genuine or essential truth; used in philosophical and Sufi literary contexts
- *kebenaran hakiki* — essential truth; common in Islamic philosophical writing
- *kebenaran mutlak* — absolute truth; used in theological and philosophical discourse
- *kebenaran universal* — universal truth; used in ethical and theological writing
- *sungguh-sungguh dari hati* — truly from the heart; a sincerity-intensification construction in personal and religious discourse

**Notes:** *hakikat* and *esensi* are near-synonyms in many contexts, with *hakikat* carrying stronger philosophical and Islamic connotations and *esensi* being more neutral and academic. *sejati* differs from both in that it is primarily attributive (*cinta sejati*, *pahlawan sejati*) rather than used as a standalone noun. These terms are not typically used in the same contexts as *benar*, *akurat*, or *tepat* (Group 1); they operate at a different level of discourse. *kebenaran* in this group refers to truth as a philosophical or spiritual concept rather than as factual correspondence.

## GROUP 8: Terms used to evaluate the trustworthiness of persons, sources, or institutions

**Label:** Evaluating trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a person, source, or institution can be relied upon to provide accurate information or to act with integrity. They appear in journalistic, political, and institutional contexts. They differ from Group 1 (factual correctness of statements) in that they evaluate the reliability of the producer of information rather than the content of any particular statement.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *terpercaya* — trusted, reliable; *sumber terpercaya* (trusted source), *sumber yang terpercaya dan terverifikasi* (trusted and verified source), *tidak lagi terpercaya di mata publik* (no longer trusted in the eyes of the public)
- *kepercayaan* — trust, belief, confidence; *kepercayaan publik* (public trust), *kepercayaan diri* (self-confidence)
- *mempercayai* — to trust or believe in someone or something
- *kredibel* — credible; *sumber yang kredibel* (credible source), *meragukan apakah lembaga itu masih kredibel* (doubting whether that institution is still credible), *saksi yang kredibel dan independen* (credible and independent witnesses)
- *kredibilitas* — credibility; *kredibilitas sumber berita* (credibility of a news source), *membangun kredibilitas* (to build credibility)
- *tidak kredibel* — not credible
- *jujur* — honest; evaluates the sincerity and truthfulness of a person's conduct; *kejujuran* (honesty), *ketidakjujuran* (dishonesty)
- *jujur saja* — to be honest (discourse marker); *harus jujur pada diri sendiri* (must be honest with oneself)
- *mengaku jujur di hadapan hukum* — to make an honest admission in legal proceedings
- *tidak jujur dalam melaporkan keuangan* — dishonest in financial reporting
- *berkata benar* — to speak the truth; evaluates the truthfulness of a person's statements

**Notes:** *terpercaya* and *kredibel* are near-synonyms in journalistic and institutional contexts; *terpercaya* is the native form and *kredibel* is the loanword, with *kredibel* slightly more restricted to formal and institutional registers. *jujur* evaluates personal character and conduct rather than institutional reliability, but all three terms share the function of evaluating the producer of information rather than the information itself. This group overlaps with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) at the negative end: a person who *berbohong* (lies) is by definition *tidak jujur* and *tidak terpercaya*.

## GROUP 9: Terms used to describe resemblance between things

**Label:** Describing resemblance and similarity

**Definition:** These terms are used to characterize a relationship of resemblance between two things, persons, or representations. They appear in contexts where the degree of similarity between an original and a copy, or between two independent items, is being evaluated. They are relevant to the authenticity domain (Group 4) but are not themselves evaluations of genuineness.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *mirip* — resembling, similar to; *kemiripan* (resemblance, similarity); used when evaluating whether a copy, portrait, or description resembles its subject
- *serupa* — of the same form or appearance; slightly more formal than *mirip*; *keserupaan* (similarity, resemblance); more explicitly grounded in shared form or appearance
- *sulit dibedakan dari yang asli* — difficult to distinguish from the genuine article; a construction used in consumer protection and forensic contexts about high-quality counterfeits

**Notes:** This is a small group. *mirip* and *serupa* are near-synonyms distinguished primarily by register. They are relevant to the authenticity domain because resemblance to an original is a key feature of counterfeits and imitations, but the terms themselves do not evaluate genuineness — something can be *mirip* without being *palsu*, and something *palsu* may or may not be *mirip* to the original. No other P1 or P2 items fit cleanly into this group.

## ITEMS WITH OVERLAPPING OR AMBIGUOUS GROUP MEMBERSHIP

The following items from P1 and P2 participate in more than one group or do not fit cleanly into any single group:

---

**berita palsu** (fake news)
Belongs to both Group 4 (counterfeit objects/documents) and Group 3 (deliberate falsehood). The *[noun] + palsu* pattern places it in Group 4, but *berita palsu* refers to fabricated information rather than a physical counterfeit, which aligns it with Group 3 and Group 6.

---

**menyesatkan** (misleading)
Sits between Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 6 (distortion of information). The term does not require intent, which distinguishes it from *bohong* and *dusta*, but *sengaja menyesatkan* (deliberately misleading) moves it toward Group 3. It is most consistently placed in Group 6 but with acknowledged overlap.

---

**salah** in moral contexts (*salah besar*, *itu bukan salah saya*)
The moral and fault-attribution uses of *salah* (Group 2) shade into a distinct evaluative domain that is not primarily about factual error. *Salah* in *penyalahgunaan wewenang* (abuse of authority) or *tidak ada salahnya* (there is no harm in it) is evaluating moral permissibility rather than factual correctness. These uses do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**kepalsuan** in moral and literary contexts
*Kepalsuan* in collocations such as *kepalsuan hidup modern* (the falseness of modern life) or *kepalsuan dalam hubungan manusia* (falseness in human relationships) extends beyond Group 4 (counterfeit objects) into a broader evaluation of inauthenticity in social existence. This use overlaps with Group 7 (essential nature) in that it implies a contrast between surface appearance and genuine reality.

---

**asli** as a character intensifier
The colloquial use of *asli* as a sincerity or character marker (*dia asli baik*, he is genuinely good) does not fit Group 4 (genuineness of objects) or Group 7 (essential nature) cleanly. It functions more as a general intensifier of positive character evaluation.

---

**sebenarnya** as a pragmatic hedge
The use of *sebenarnya* as a conversational softener (*sebenarnya, saya mau tanya...*) rather than as a correction of false belief does not fit Group 1 (factual correspondence) or any other group. It functions as a discourse particle marking tentativeness.

---

**salah satu / salah seorang**
These partitive constructions (*one of*, *one among*) are grammaticalized uses of *salah* in which the error/wrongness meaning is not present. They do not belong to any of the groups above.

---

**pembenaran** in psychological contexts
*Pembenaran diri* (self-justification) describes a cognitive or emotional process rather than an epistemic act of confirmation. It does not fit Group 1 (factual affirmation) cleanly and does not fit any other group. It is noted here as ungrouped.

---

**sungguh / sungguh-sungguh / kesungguhan**
These terms evaluate the sincerity and earnestness of effort or feeling rather than the truth of a proposition or the genuineness of an object. They overlap with Group 8 (trustworthiness) in that a person who acts *sungguh-sungguh* is implicitly trustworthy, but the primary focus is on degree of commitment rather than on reliability or honesty. They do not fit cleanly into any single group and are noted here as partially overlapping with Groups 3, 7, and 8.

---

**pemalsuan** / **memalsukan** as legal acts
These terms describe criminal acts (forgery, falsification) rather than properties of objects. They belong to Group 4 in that they are derived from *palsu*, but they describe processes and agents rather than the evaluation of genuineness. They are most naturally placed in Group 4 with the note that they describe the act rather than the state.
