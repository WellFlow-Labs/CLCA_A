# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Methodological note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. "Multi-word construction" is understood here as a syntactically-driven, periphrastic, or idiomatic combination of two or more words that functions as a unit in discourse and is not reducible to a single derived form already documented in P2a. Constructions already fully documented as fixed phrases in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full where they add no new information. The constraint against generating constructions to fill expected semantic roles is strictly observed; where no common construction is identified for a given base form or pattern, this is explicitly stated.

Tagalog multi-word constructions frequently involve discourse particles (*na*, *nga*, *pala*, *naman*, *ba*, *pa*) that modulate epistemic stance, evidentiality, and pragmatic force. These particle combinations are documented where they form conventionalized units with P1 items rather than being freely compositional.

## Section 1: Constructions involving **totoo**

---

**P2b-A-01**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Totoo ba iyan?
**Transliteration:** Totoo ba iyan?
**English translation:** Is that true? / Is that real? / Really?
**Usage notes:** The most common interrogative construction for truth-checking in everyday Tagalog. The particle *ba* converts the predicate into a yes/no question; *iyan* (that, proximal-to-addressee) is the standard demonstrative in this frame. Functions both as a genuine information-seeking question and as an expression of surprise or disbelief.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-02**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Totoo nga pala.
**Transliteration:** Totoo nga pala.
**English translation:** So it really is true. / It turns out it's actually true.
**Usage notes:** The combination of *nga* (confirmatory/emphatic) and *pala* (realization, discovery) in a single utterance marks a speaker's arrival at a confirmed realization — something suspected or told to them has now been verified. This particle cluster with *totoo* is a conventionalized expression of epistemic update.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-03**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
**Transliteration:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
**English translation:** The truth is, I don't know. / To tell the truth, I don't know.
**Usage notes:** *Ang totoo* functions as a sentence-initial discourse marker introducing a corrective or candid statement. The construction is highly conventionalized; *ang totoo* is used as a pragmatic frame for honest admission or clarification, not merely as a predicate. Documented in P1b A-11; included here as a multi-word construction pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-04**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Totoo naman ang sinabi ko.
**Transliteration:** Totoo naman ang sinabi ko.
**English translation:** What I said is indeed true. / It really is true, what I said.
**Usage notes:** The particle *naman* here marks a contrastive or responsive affirmation — the speaker is asserting truth in response to an expressed or implied doubt. The construction *totoo naman* is a conventionalized response pattern in conversational Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-05**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Parang totoo ang kanyang kwento.
**Transliteration:** Parang totoo ang kanyang kwento.
**English translation:** His/her story seems true. / His/her story looks real.
**Usage notes:** *Parang totoo* is a fixed evaluative construction meaning "seems real/true" without asserting actual truth. Documented in P1 item 28 and P1b A-05; included here as a multi-word construction. The *parang* + adjective pattern is productive, but *parang totoo* is particularly conventionalized in discourse about appearances versus reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-06**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Totoo man o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
**Transliteration:** Totoo man o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
**English translation:** Whether true or not, we need to find out.
**Usage notes:** The construction *totoo man o hindi* is a conventionalized concessive frame meaning "regardless of whether it is true or not." Documented in P1b A-30; included here as a multi-word construction. The *man o hindi* pattern is productive with other predicates but is particularly attested with *totoo*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-07**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Totoo pa rin iyan.
**Transliteration:** Totoo pa rin iyan.
**English translation:** That is still true. / That remains true nonetheless.
**Usage notes:** The combination *pa rin* (still, nonetheless, even so) with *totoo* forms a construction asserting the persistence of a truth despite contrary expectations or changed circumstances. Common in argumentative and conversational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-08**
**Base Form:** totoo
**Example:** Hindi naman totoo iyon.
**Transliteration:** Hindi naman totoo iyon.
**English translation:** That's not really true. / That isn't actually true.
**Usage notes:** The combination of *hindi* (negation) + *naman* (contrastive/softening particle) + *totoo* forms a conventionalized denial that is softer than bare *hindi totoo*, often used to correct a misconception without direct confrontation. Common in everyday conversational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-09**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Sabihin ang katotohanan.
**Transliteration:** Sabihin ang katotohanan.
**English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak the truth.
**Usage notes:** A fixed imperative construction functioning as a standard moral injunction equivalent to "tell the truth." Documented in P1b A-16; included here as a multi-word construction. Appears in oath-taking, moral instruction, and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-10**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Ang katotohanan ay mapait.
**Transliteration:** Ang katotohanan ay mapait.
**English translation:** The truth is bitter. / Truth is painful.
**Usage notes:** A near-proverbial construction in which *katotohanan* is the subject of a predication about the nature of truth. Functions as a conventional expression acknowledging that true information is often unwelcome. Common in moral, literary, and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-11**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Walang katotohanan ang akusasyon.
**Transliteration:** Walang katotohanan ang akusasyon.
**English translation:** The accusation has no truth to it. / The accusation is groundless.
**Usage notes:** The *wala* + *-ng* + *katotohanan* construction is a conventionalized denial of factual basis. Documented in P1b A-20; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in legal, political, and journalistic denial statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-12**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
**Transliteration:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
**English translation:** There is some truth in what he/she said. / What he/she said has some truth to it.
**Usage notes:** The *may* + *katotohanan* construction is a conventionalized partial concession acknowledging that a claim contains some degree of truth. Documented in P1b A-21; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in argumentative and evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-13**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Kalahating katotohanan lang ang sinabi niya.
**Transliteration:** Kalahating katotohanan lang ang sinabi niya.
**English translation:** He/she only told a half-truth. / What he/she said was only a partial truth.
**Usage notes:** The *kalahating katotohanan* construction (half-truth) is used to evaluate a statement as technically true but misleading through omission. Documented in P1b A-18; included here as a multi-word construction. The particle *lang* (only, merely) is commonly added to emphasize the insufficiency of the partial truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-14**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Ang buong katotohanan ay hindi pa nalalaman.
**Transliteration:** Ang buong katotohanan ay hindi pa nalalaman.
**English translation:** The whole truth is not yet known. / The full truth has not yet been established.
**Usage notes:** The *buong katotohanan* construction (the whole truth) appears in legal, investigative, and moral contexts demanding complete disclosure. Documented in P1b A-19; included here as a multi-word construction. The addition of *hindi pa nalalaman* ("not yet known") is a common extension in investigative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-15**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Sa katotohanan, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
**Transliteration:** Sa katotohanan, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
**English translation:** In truth, the situation is more complicated. / In reality, the situation is more complex.
**Usage notes:** *Sa katotohanan* functions as a sentence-initial discourse marker introducing a corrective statement of what is actually the case. Documented in P1b A-13; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in formal, journalistic, and argumentative registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-16**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Ang katotohanan ay mananatili.
**Transliteration:** Ang katotohanan ay mananatili.
**English translation:** The truth will endure. / Truth will remain.
**Usage notes:** A near-proverbial construction asserting the permanence of truth. Documented in P1b A-29; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in religious, literary, and editorial discourse. Functions as a moral or philosophical assertion rather than a description of a specific state of affairs.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-A-17**
**Base Form:** katotohanan
**Example:** Katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** Katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan
**English translation:** Truth versus falsehood / Truth against lies
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized oppositional framing used as a rhetorical or thematic construction in moral, political, and religious discourse. Documented in P1b A-28; included here as a multi-word construction. Functions as a near-formulaic framing device in editorial and homiletic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Absence note — totoo verbal constructions:** No common periphrastic verbal construction of the form *mag-* + *totoo* or *i-* + *totoo* in the truth-assertion sense is identified. The root *totoo* does not participate in productive periphrastic verbal constructions for asserting truth in the way that *tapat* participates in *magtapat*. This absence is consistent with the derivational gap noted in P2a.

## Section 2: Constructions involving **tunay**

---

**P2b-B-01**
**Base Form:** tunay
**Example:** Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal.
**Transliteration:** Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal.
**English translation:** His/her love is absolutely genuine. / His/her love is truly real.
**Usage notes:** The intensified construction *tunay na tunay* is documented in P1b B-08 and P2a D-B-08; included here as a multi-word construction. Used in everyday speech to emphasize emphatic genuineness, particularly in emotional and relational contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-02**
**Base Form:** tunay
**Example:** Ano ang tunay na nangyari?
**Transliteration:** Ano ang tunay na nangyari?
**English translation:** What really happened? / What truly occurred?
**Usage notes:** The construction *tunay na nangyari* (what really happened) is used in investigative and narrative contexts to seek or assert the factual account of events. Documented in P1b B-17; included here as a multi-word construction. The interrogative frame *ano ang tunay na...* is a conventionalized investigative question pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-03**
**Base Form:** tunay
**Example:** Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?
**Transliteration:** Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?
**English translation:** What is his/her real intention? / What is his/her true purpose?
**Usage notes:** The construction *tunay na layunin* (real intention/true purpose) is used in investigative, political, and moral discourse to distinguish stated purposes from actual motivations. Documented in P1b B-16; included here as a multi-word construction. The interrogative frame is conventionalized in political and journalistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-04**
**Base Form:** tunay
**Example:** Hindi tunay ang kanyang damdamin.
**Transliteration:** Hindi tunay ang kanyang damdamin.
**English translation:** His/her feelings are not genuine. / His/her emotions are not real.
**Usage notes:** The negation construction *hindi tunay* is used to assert inauthenticity of emotions, relationships, or objects. Documented in P1b B-29; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in interpersonal and evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-05**
**Base Form:** katunayan
**Example:** Sa katunayan, mas mataas ang bilang ng mga biktima.
**Transliteration:** Sa katunayan, mas mataas ang bilang ng mga biktima.
**English translation:** In fact, the number of victims is higher. / As a matter of fact, the victim count is higher.
**Usage notes:** *Sa katunayan* functions as a discourse marker meaning "in fact" or "as a matter of fact," introducing a statement that adds factual weight or corrects a prior claim. Documented in P1b B-18; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in formal, journalistic, and argumentative registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-06**
**Base Form:** katunayan
**Example:** Bilang katunayan, ipinakita niya ang dokumento.
**Transliteration:** Bilang katunayan, ipinakita niya ang dokumento.
**English translation:** As proof, he/she showed the document. / By way of evidence, he/she presented the document.
**Usage notes:** *Bilang katunayan* is a conventionalized construction meaning "as proof" or "as evidence," used to introduce a piece of supporting evidence. Documented in P1b B-19; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in legal and formal argumentative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-07**
**Base Form:** katunayan
**Example:** Walang katunayan ang akusasyon.
**Transliteration:** Walang katunayan ang akusasyon.
**English translation:** The accusation is without proof. / The accusation is unsubstantiated.
**Usage notes:** The *walang katunayan* construction is a conventionalized denial of evidentiary support. Documented in P1b B-20; included here as a multi-word construction. Parallel to *walang katotohanan* but focused on the absence of evidence rather than the absence of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-08**
**Base Form:** patunay
**Example:** Ibigay ang patunay.
**Transliteration:** Ibigay ang patunay.
**English translation:** Provide the proof. / Give the evidence.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized imperative construction used in legal, administrative, and argumentative contexts to demand supporting evidence. Documented in P1b B-22; included here as a multi-word construction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-09**
**Base Form:** patunayan
**Example:** Patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi.
**Transliteration:** Patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi.
**English translation:** Prove what you said. / Demonstrate the truth of what you said.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized imperative construction demanding proof of a claim. Documented in P1b B-04; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in argumentative, legal, and everyday confrontational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-10**
**Base Form:** patunayan
**Example:** Hindi mapatunayan ang kanyang sinabi.
**Transliteration:** Hindi mapatunayan ang kanyang sinabi.
**English translation:** What he/she said cannot be proven. / His/her statement is unprovable.
**Usage notes:** The construction *hindi mapatunayan* (cannot be proven) is used to assert the unprovability of a claim. Documented in P1b B-05; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in legal and epistemological discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-B-11**
**Base Form:** pagpapatunay
**Example:** Pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan
**Transliteration:** Pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan
**English translation:** Identity verification / Identity authentication
**Usage notes:** A fixed nominal construction used in administrative, digital security, and legal contexts to describe the process of confirming a person's identity. Documented in P1b B-28; included here as a multi-word construction. Functions as a near-technical term in institutional discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Absence note — tunay interrogative constructions:** No common construction of the form *tunay ba?* functioning as a standalone truth-check question parallel to *totoo ba?* is identified. While *tunay ba iyan?* is grammatically possible, it is not conventionalized as a discourse-level truth-check in the way *totoo ba?* is. The interrogative use of *tunay* tends to occur in the pattern *ano ang tunay na...?* (what is the real...?) rather than as a bare predicate question.

## Section 3: Constructions involving **tapat**

---

**P2b-C-01**
**Base Form:** tapat
**Example:** Maging tapat sa iyong sarili.
**Transliteration:** Maging tapat sa iyong sarili.
**English translation:** Be honest with yourself. / Be true to yourself.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized moral injunction used in psychological, self-help, and moral discourse. Documented in P1b C-19; included here as a multi-word construction. The *maging tapat sa sarili* frame is a near-fixed expression in Tagalog moral and popular discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-C-02**
**Base Form:** tapat
**Example:** Maging tapat ka sa akin.
**Transliteration:** Maging tapat ka sa akin.
**English translation:** Be honest with me. / Be straight with me.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized interpersonal request for honest disclosure. The *maging tapat ka sa* + person construction is a standard formula for requesting candor in a relationship. Common in everyday interpersonal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-C-03**
**Base Form:** tapat
**Example:** Hindi tapat ang kanyang sagot.
**Transliteration:** Hindi tapat ang kanyang sagot.
**English translation:** His/her answer was not honest. / His/her response was not straightforward.
**Usage notes:** The negation construction *hindi tapat* is used to evaluate a person or statement as lacking in honesty or sincerity. Documented in P1b C-17; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in evaluative discourse about testimony, statements, and character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-C-04**
**Base Form:** magtapat
**Example:** Magtapat ka na.
**Transliteration:** Magtapat ka na.
**English translation:** Come clean already. / Be honest now. / Just tell the truth.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized imperative construction used to urge someone to stop concealing and disclose honestly. The particle *na* marks urgency or a change of state (it is time to do so now). Documented in P1b C-22; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in interpersonal confrontation and moral exhortation.

**English translation:** He/she came clean to his/her spouse. / He/she was honest with his/her spouse.
**Usage notes:** The construction *nagtapat sa* + person is a conventionalized expression for the act of honest disclosure to a specific person. Documented in P1b C-24; included here as a multi-word construction. Common in interpersonal, moral, and narrative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-C-07**

**P2b-C-09**

Documented in P1b C-25; included here as a multi-word construction. The *loob* construction is culturally specific and not reducible to a simple honesty predication.

**Absence note — tapat interrogative constructions:** No common construction of the form *tapat ba?* functioning as a standalone truth-check question is identified. *Tapat* does not participate in a conventionalized interrogative truth-check construction parallel to *totoo ba?*. ## Section 4: Constructions involving **tama** and **mali**

---

**P2b-D-01**
**Base Form:** tama
**Example:** Tama ka.
**Transliteration:** Tama ka.
**English translation:** You are right. / You are correct.
**Usage notes:** A minimal but highly conventionalized construction used to affirm the correctness of a person's statement or judgment. The bare predicate + pronoun construction is the standard form for this affirmation in Tagalog. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-02**
**Base Form:** tama
**Example:** Tama ka naman.
**Transliteration:** Tama ka naman.
**English translation:** You are right, actually. / You are correct after all.
**Usage notes:** The addition of *naman* to *tama ka* marks a concessive or responsive affirmation — the speaker is acknowledging correctness in response to an expressed or implied challenge. A conventionalized concessive agreement construction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-03**
**Base Form:** tama
**Example:** Tama ba ang sagot ko?
**Transliteration:** Tama ba ang sagot ko?
**English translation:** Is my answer correct? / Am I right?
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized question construction used to seek confirmation of correctness. Common in educational, conversational, and evaluative contexts. The *tama ba* + noun phrase pattern is a standard correctness-check question.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-04**
**Base Form:** tama o mali
**Example:** Tama o mali ang ginawa niya?
**Transliteration:** Tama o mali ang ginawa niya?
**English translation:** Was what he/she did right or wrong?
**Usage notes:** The paired construction *tama o mali* is a conventionalized binary evaluative frame used in moral reasoning and assessment contexts. Documented in P1 item 8; included here as a multi-word construction. Functions as a standard framing for moral evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-05**
**Base Form:** mali
**Example:** Mali ka.
**Transliteration:** Mali ka.
**English translation:** You are wrong. / You are mistaken.
**Usage notes:** The minimal predicate + pronoun construction for asserting incorrectness, parallel to *tama ka*. Extremely common across all registers. Can be softened with particles (*mali ka naman* — "you are actually wrong") or intensified (*maling-mali ka* — "you are completely wrong").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-06**
**Base Form:** mali
**Example:** Mali ang aking pagkakaunawa.
**Transliteration:** Mali ang aking pagkakaunawa.
**English translation:** My understanding was wrong. / I misunderstood.
**Usage notes:** A conventionalized construction for acknowledging a misunderstanding or error in comprehension. The *mali ang* + nominalized cognitive process pattern is common in formal and everyday discourse for admitting error.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-07**
**Base Form:** mali
**Example:** Nagkamali ako.
**Transliteration:** Nagkamali ako.
**English translation:** I made a mistake. / I was wrong.
**Usage notes:** The verbal construction *nagkamali ako* is the standard first-person admission of error. While *nagkamali* is a derived verbal form (documented in P2a D-P1-02), the full construction with the pronoun *ako* is the conventionalized admission formula. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**P2b-D-08**
**Base Form:** mali
**Example:** Pasensya na, nagkamali ako.
**Transliteration:** Pasensya na, nagkamali ako.
**English translation:** I'm sorry, I made a mistake. / Excuse me, I was wrong.
**Usage notes:** The combination of *pasensya na* (I'm sorry, please forgive me; from Spanish *paciencia*) + *nagkamali ako* is a conventionalized apology-plus-admission construction. Common in everyday interpersonal discourse for acknowledging and apologizing for errors.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary of Constructional Patterns Observed

The following patterns are observed across the documented constructions. These are reported descriptively without theoretical interpretation.

**1. Predicate + *ba* (interrogative truth-check):** Highly conventionalized for *totoo* (*totoo ba?*). Not conventionalized as a standalone discourse-level question for *tunay*, *tapat*, *wasto*, or borrowed adjectives. The asymmetry is noted.

**2. *Ang* + nominal + discourse-initial frame:** Attested for *totoo* (*ang totoo*) and *katotohanan* (*ang katotohanan ay*) as sentence-initial discourse markers introducing corrective or clarifying statements. Not attested as a conventionalized frame for *tunay* or *tapat* nominals.

**3. *Sa* + nominal as discourse marker:** Attested for both *katotohanan* (*sa katotohanan* — "in truth") and *katunayan* (*sa katunayan* — "in fact"). These two constructions are near-synonymous in function but differ in register and emphasis: *sa katotohanan* foregrounds factual truth, *sa katunayan* foregrounds evidential grounding.

**4. *Wala/may* + nominal (existence denial/assertion):** Productive pattern attested for *katotohanan* (*walang katotohanan*, *may katotohanan*), *katunayan* (*walang katunayan*, *may katunayan*), *katapatan* (*walang katapatan*), and *tiwala* (*wala na akong tiwala*). This existential construction is a standard way of asserting or denying the presence of a truth-related quality.

**5. *Huwag* + verbal (negative imperative moral injunction):** Attested for *kasinungalingan* (*huwag magsabi ng kasinungalingan*), *sinungaling* (*huwag kang magsinungaling*), and *linlang* (*huwag mong linlangin*). A standard moral injunction pattern in Tagalog.

**6. Particle combinations marking epistemic stance:** The combinations *totoo nga pala* (confirmed realization), *totoo naman* (responsive affirmation), *totoo pa rin* (persistent truth), and *hindi naman totoo* (softened denial) represent conventionalized epistemic stance constructions specific to *totoo*. Parallel particle constructions are not attested at the same level of conventionalization for *tunay* or *tapat*.

**7. *Maging tapat sa* + person/self:** A conventionalized moral injunction pattern specific to *tapat*, with no parallel construction attested for *totoo* or *tunay*.

**8. *Bilang* + nominal (evidential introduction):** Attested for *katunayan* (*bilang katunayan* — "as proof"). Not attested as a conventionalized construction for *katotohanan* or *katapatan*.

**Constructional absences noted:**
- *totoo* does not participate in a conventionalized periphrastic verbal construction for truth-assertion.
- *tunay* does not participate in a conventionalized standalone interrogative truth-check construction.
- *tapat* does not participate in a conventionalized standalone interrogative truth-check construction.
- Borrowed adjectives (*kredible*, *lehitimo*, *orihinal*, *perpekto*, *eksakto*) do not participate in conventionalized periphrastic verbal or interrogative constructions.
- *dalisay* does not participate in conventionalized verbal or interrogative constructions in the truth-evaluation domain.
