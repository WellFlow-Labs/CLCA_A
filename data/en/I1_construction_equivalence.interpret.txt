## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION → BECOME Merger
English does not distinguish REVELATION from BECOME as separate constructional categories. The construction "Subject + PROVE + truth-adj" ("The rumor proved false") serves both functions: it marks change-of-state AND emphasizes evidential discovery. The notes explicitly state that "prove" emphasizes evidential discovery, while simple "become" is rare with truth-domain adjectives. This suggests REVELATION is the preferred frame, with BECOME semantics embedded within it.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION → CAUSATION (factual) Merger
English does not maintain a constructional distinction between VERIFICATION and CAUSATION in the factual domain. Both meanings are expressed through the same lexicalized causative verbs: "authenticate," "validate," "verify," "falsify," "correct." The construction "Agent + causative-V + object" covers both making something true/authentic (causation) and confirming/checking that it is true/authentic (verification). The notes indicate these are "dedicated causative verbs" used in "formal/technical contexts," suggesting a single construction type.

### 2.3 CAUSATION Asymmetry: Factual vs. Character
A striking asymmetry exists between factual and character causation:
- **Factual domain**: Lexicalized causative verbs available ("authenticate," "validate," "correct")
- **Character domain**: No construction attested; notes explicitly mark "*make him honest" as ungrammatical

This suggests English blocks productive causative constructions in the character domain entirely. There is no common way to express "cause someone to become honest/truthful."

### 2.4 BECOME Lexical Restrictions
The notes indicate that simple "become + truth-adj" is rare, and specific verbs dominate: "prove" (evidential), "get" (informal), "turn out" (discovery), "come true" (fixed expression for predictions). This suggests BECOME is not a productive template but rather a set of lexically specific constructions, each with distinct semantic/register constraints.

### 2.5 DISCONFIRMATION Lexical Restrictions
DISCONFIRMATION patterns ("prove him wrong," "expose it as fake") are limited to specific verbs. The notes explicitly mark general resultatives as ungrammatical: "*paint it true," "*make it authentic." This indicates the construction is not productively available but restricted to a small set of verbs (prove, expose, reveal).

### 2.6 NEGATION Strategy Variation
English employs two negation strategies that coexist with subtle functional differences:
- **Analytic** (not + adj): neutral, freely productive
- **Synthetic** (un-/in-/dis- + adj): often carries stronger implications, lexically restricted

The notes indicate some bases prefer one strategy: "dishonest" is standard while "*not honest" is marked. This suggests lexical gaps constrain the alternation, with some adjectives requiring synthetic forms.

### 2.7 Agentive Nominalization Gaps
Agentive nominalization shows irregular patterns:
- Suppletive forms: "liar" (not *falser)
- Productive -er: "faker," "counterfeiter"
- Compounds fill gaps: "truth-teller" (not *truist), "wrongdoer" (not *wronger)
- Non-standard forms: *truther, *correcter

This indicates agentive formation is not a productive morphological process but rather a lexically specific pattern with systematic gaps.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

**CAUSATION (character)**: No construction exists to express "cause someone to become honest/truthful/trustworthy." The analytic causative pattern "make + object + adjective" is blocked for truth-domain character adjectives. Notes explicitly mark "*make him honest" as ungrammatical.

**Productive BECOME template**: While specific verbs exist (prove, get, turn out, come true), there is no productive "become + truth-adj" construction. Simple "become" is marked as rare.

**Productive analytic CAUSATION (factual)**: The pattern "make + object + adjective" is marginal/awkward ("*make the statement true"). Causative meaning must be expressed through lexicalized verbs.

**General resultative construction**: Patterns like "*paint it true," "*make it authentic" are ungrammatical. Resultatives are restricted to specific verbs (prove, expose, reveal).

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

**Agentive nominals**: Systematic gaps exist:
- *truist (filled by compound "truth-teller")
- *truther (non-standard)
- *correcter (non-standard)
- *wronger (filled by compound "wrongdoer")

**Synthetic negation forms**: Not all adjectives have synthetic negated forms. The notes indicate "*not honest" is marked, suggesting "dishonest" fills a lexical gap, but the inverse pattern (adjectives lacking synthetic forms) is not explicitly documented.

**Simple BECOME verb**: No single general verb for truth-domain state changes; multiple lexically specific verbs required.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

**Character causation**: The blocking of "*make him honest" suggests English conceptually resists the idea of directly causing character traits in the truth domain. This may reflect a folk theory that honesty/truthfulness are intrinsic or volitional rather than externally causable.

**Gradation of absolute terms**: The notes mark "*very true," "*more false" as marginal/marked, though "completely true," "entirely false" are acceptable. This suggests absolute truth-domain terms resist scalar modification, reflecting a conceptual commitment to binary truth values.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Positive vs. negative agentives**: "Liar" (negative) is a core, suppletive form, while "truth-teller" (positive) is a marginal compound. This asymmetry suggests greater lexical elaboration for negative character types.

**Factual vs. character causation**: Lexicalized causative verbs exist for factual domain (authenticate, validate, correct) but not for character domain (no equivalent to "make honest"). This represents a systematic domain-based asymmetry.

**BECOME verb selection**: "Come true" is fixed for positive realization of predictions, with no equivalent "*come false" for negative outcomes. This suggests asymmetric lexicalization of positive vs. negative state changes.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available
- **ATTR**: Subject + BE + truth-adj (unmarked, productive)
- **ADNOM**: Truth-adj + N (productive, prenominal only)
- **NEGATION**: Both analytic (not + adj) and synthetic (un-/in-/dis- + adj) strategies available
- **NOMINALIZATION**: Multiple irregular patterns (-th, -y/-ty, -ness, -ity) with semantic differentiation

### Available with Substitution
- **REVELATION**: Expressed through BECOME construction with evidential verbs (prove, turn out); no distinct construction
- **VERIFICATION**: Expressed through CAUSATION construction with lexicalized verbs (authenticate, validate, verify); no distinct construction
- **BECOME**: Available only through lexically specific verbs (prove, get, turn out, come true); no productive template with simple "become"
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Available through resultative construction with restricted verbs (prove, expose, reveal); not productively available

### Partially Available
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Available only through lexicalized causative verbs (authenticate, validate, correct); productive analytic causative blocked
- **Agentive NOMINALIZATION**: Available through multiple strategies (suppletive forms, -er derivation, compounds) but with systematic lexical gaps

### Missing Entirely
- **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction exists; analytic causative blocked ("*make him honest")
- **Productive resultative**: General pattern unavailable; only verb-specific constructions exist
- **Productive BECOME**: Simple "become + truth-adj" rare/marginal; only lexically specific verbs available

---

## Summary Statement

English truth-domain constructions show a pattern of **functional merger** (REVELATION with BECOME, VERIFICATION with CAUSATION) and **lexical restriction** (BECOME, CAUSATION, DISCONFIRMATION limited to specific verbs rather than productive templates). The most striking gap is **CAUSATION (character)**, which appears to be conceptually blocked. The language relies heavily on **lexicalized verbs** rather than productive syntactic templates for change-of-state and causative meanings, and shows **systematic asymmetries** between factual and character domains, and between positive and negative forms.
