## 1. Base Form: **kweli**

### Construction 1
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** ni kweli  
3. **Transliteration:** ni kweli  
4. **English translation:** it is true  
5. **Usage notes:** Very common clause-level confirmation used to affirm that a statement is accurate. It is frequent in both everyday conversation and more formal discussion.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 2
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kwa kweli  
3. **Transliteration:** kwa kweli  
4. **English translation:** truly; really; in truth; actually  
5. **Usage notes:** Extremely common as a discourse marker or adverbial expression. It can mark sincerity, emphasis, surprise, or a frank assessment.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 3
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kweli kabisa  
3. **Transliteration:** kweli kabisa  
4. **English translation:** absolutely true; really; truly  
5. **Usage notes:** Used for emphatic confirmation or insistence. Common in speech and informal writing.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 4
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kweli ni kwamba  
3. **Transliteration:** kweli ni kwamba  
4. **English translation:** the truth is that  
5. **Usage notes:** Used to introduce what the speaker presents as the real situation or decisive fact. Common in explanatory and argumentative discourse.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 5
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kweli kweli  
3. **Transliteration:** kweli kweli  
4. **English translation:** really truly; genuinely  
5. **Usage notes:** Colloquial reduplicated emphatic expression. It intensifies insistence or surprise rather than simply asserting truth.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 6
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kweli tupu  
3. **Transliteration:** kweli tupu  
4. **English translation:** sheer truth; absolutely true  
5. **Usage notes:** Emphatic expression used to stress that something is nothing but true. It overlaps with *ukweli mtupu* but is somewhat more colloquial.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 7
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** si kweli  
3. **Transliteration:** si kweli  
4. **English translation:** not true; false  
5. **Usage notes:** Very common expression for direct contradiction or denial. Used in everyday rebuttal, argument, and public correction.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 8
1. **Base Form:** kweli  
2. **Example:** kweli au si kweli  
3. **Transliteration:** kweli au si kweli  
4. **English translation:** true or not true  
5. **Usage notes:** Used in debate, questioning, and explicit verification contexts. It frames an issue in terms of factual confirmation.  
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

3. **Transliteration:** sema uongo

3. **Transliteration:** ni uongo

### Construction 4

3. **Transliteration:** uongo mtupu

### Construction 5

3. **Transliteration:** habari za uongo  
4. **English translation:** false news; fake news  
5. **Usage notes:** Used for reports or news judged false or fabricated. Common in media-related and public-information contexts.

### Construction 6
1. **Base Form:** uongo  
2. **Example:** taarifa za uongo  
3. **Transliteration:** taarifa za uongo
