# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## IDENTIFIED SEMANTIC GROUPS

---

### GROUP 1: Truth and Falsity in Speech and Representation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether speech, statements, representations, or claims correspond to reality or deviate from it. These terms are centrally used in contexts of verification, testimony, authentication, and distinguishing genuine from false information. They appear frequently in discourse about honesty, accuracy of reports, and the reliability of claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqaq (true, truthful)
- cheqaq (true, real - dialectal variant)
- chiqay (to be true)
- chiqanchay (to verify, to authenticate)
- chiqaq simi (true word, truthful speech)
- chiqaqta rimay (to speak truthfully)
- chiqaqta willakuy (to tell the truth)
- chiqamanta rimay (to speak from truth)
- llulla (lie, false)
- llullakuy (to lie, to tell lies)
- llulla simi (lying words, false speech)
- llullata rimay (to speak lies)
- llulla willakuy (to tell lies)
- llulla runa (liar, dishonest person)
- chiqaq runa (true person, honest person)
- chiqanchaq (verifier, authenticator)
- chiqanchana (means of verification)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- chiqan (correct, exact) - overlaps with precision/accuracy group but also used for truth evaluation
- sut'i (clear, evident) - overlaps with clarity group but also used to affirm truth
- pantay (to err) - overlaps with error group but can involve false statements

---

### GROUP 2: Moral and Practical Goodness/Badness

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used for general positive or negative evaluation of persons, actions, states, and objects across moral, practical, and social domains. These terms are used to assess whether something is proper, virtuous, beneficial, or conversely improper, harmful, or deficient. They appear in character assessments, conduct evaluation, and judgments about the appropriateness or quality of actions and states.

**Representative expressions:**
- allin (good, well, right)
- allinta ruway (to do well, to do properly)
- allin runa (good person, virtuous person)
- allin kawsay (good life, proper living)
- allin simi (good words, proper speech)
- allin yuyay (good thought, right thinking)
- allin sunqu (good heart, kind-hearted)
- allinyay (to become good, to improve)
- allinyachiy (to make good, to improve, to heal)
- mana allin (not good, bad)
- mana allin runa (bad person)
- mana allin ruway (bad deed, wrongdoing)
- mana allin simi (bad words, improper speech)
- mana allin yuyay (bad thoughts, wrong thinking)
- mana allin sunqu (bad heart, malicious)
- sumaq (beautiful, good, pleasant)
- sumaq kawsay (beautiful life, harmonious life)
- sumaq runa (beautiful person, good person)
- millay (ugly, disgusting, abominable)
- millay runa (disgusting person)
- millay ruway (disgusting deed, abominable act)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- k'uchi (crooked, dishonest) - overlaps with dishonesty but specifically moral corruption
- allinsapa (very good, excellent) - intensified form of general goodness
- sumaq simi (beautiful words, eloquent speech) - overlaps with aesthetic evaluation

---

### GROUP 3: Error, Mistake, and Correctness

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to characterize actions, beliefs, or statements as containing error, being mistaken, or conversely being correct and accurate. These terms focus on deviation from correctness or proper course rather than moral wrongdoing, though they can carry moral implications. They appear in contexts of practical mistakes, incorrect beliefs, misleading, and assessments of accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- pantay (to err, to be mistaken)
- pantasqa (mistaken, erred)
- pantayta ruway (to make a mistake)
- pantay ñan (wrong path, mistaken way)
- pantaypi kay (to be in error)
- pantasqa yuyay (mistaken thought, erroneous belief)
- pantachiy (to cause to err, to mislead)
- pantachiq (deceiver, misleader)
- pantaq (one who errs, mistaken person)
- pantakuy (to err for oneself)
- chiqan (correct, exact)
- chiqan pacha (exact time, right moment)
- paqta (exact, just right)
- paqta chayay (to arrive exactly on time)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- pantachiy (to mislead) - overlaps with deception/dishonesty
- huch'uy pantay (small error) - overlaps with magnitude evaluation
- hatun pantay (big error) - overlaps with magnitude evaluation
- chiqaq (true) - overlaps with truth group but also used for correctness

---

### GROUP 4: Clarity, Obviousness, and Obscurity

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether something is clear, evident, and easily understood versus obscure or lacking in clarity. These terms appear in contexts of explanation, understanding, perception, and the transparency or opacity of information, situations, or meanings.

**Representative expressions:**
- sut'i (clear, evident, obvious)
- sut'i kay (to be clear, to be evident)
- sut'ita rikuy (to see clearly)
- sut'ita yachay (to know clearly)
- sut'ita willakuy (to explain clearly)
- sut'i simi (clear speech, plain language)
- sut'iyachiy (to clarify, to make clear, to explain)
- sut'iyachiq (clarifier, explainer)
- sut'iyay (to become clear, to be clarified)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ch'usaq simi (empty words, meaningless speech) - overlaps with lack of substance but implies lack of clarity
- allinta rikuy (to see well) - overlaps with perception but implies clarity

---

### GROUP 5: Knowledge, Belief, and Thought

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to distinguish different epistemic states and mental activities, including knowing, believing, thinking, remembering, and understanding. These terms appear in contexts where the basis, certainty, or source of knowledge is relevant, and where mental states are characterized or evaluated.

**Representative expressions:**
- yachay (to know, knowledge)
- yachay wasi (house of knowledge, school)
- yachayniyuq kay (to have knowledge, to be knowledgeable)
- allinta yachay (to know well, to understand thoroughly)
- mana yachay (to not know, ignorance)
- yachaq (knower, wise person, expert)
- yachasqa (known, learned)
- yachakuy (to learn, to study)
- yachachiy (to teach, to cause to know)
- yachachiq (teacher, instructor)
- yuyay (to think, to remember, thought)
- yuyaypi kay (to be in thought/memory, to remember)
- allin yuyay (good thought, right thinking)
- mana allin yuyay (bad thoughts, wrong thinking)
- yuyaywan ruway (to do intentionally, to do deliberately)
- mana yuyaywan (without thought, unintentionally)
- yuyaychay (to remind, to cause to remember)
- yuyaykuy (to remember, to recall)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- chiqaq yachay (true knowledge) - overlaps with truth evaluation
- llulla yachay (false knowledge) - overlaps with falsity
- pantasqa yuyay (mistaken thought) - overlaps with error
- hatun yachay (great knowledge) - overlaps with magnitude evaluation

---

### GROUP 6: Perception and Evidence

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to describe direct sensory perception and the evidential basis for knowledge claims. These terms distinguish between seeing, hearing, and other forms of evidence, and are used to characterize the source and reliability of information. They appear in contexts of witnessing, testimony, and distinguishing direct from indirect knowledge.

**Representative expressions:**
- rikuy (to see, to look)
- rikuyta atiy (to be able to see)
- rikuywan yachay (to know by seeing, to know through observation)
- allinta rikuy (to see well, to look carefully)
- sut'ita rikuy (to see clearly)
- rikuy hamuy (come see, come and look)
- rikuchiy (to show, to cause to see)
- rikusqa (seen, visible, what has been seen)
- rikuq (seer, observer, one who sees)
- uyariy (to hear, to listen)
- uyariyta atiy (to be able to hear)
- uyariywan yachay (to know by hearing, to know through report)
- allinta uyariy (to hear well, to listen carefully)
- uyariy hamuy (come hear, come and listen)
- uyarichiy (to cause to hear, to announce)
- uyarisqa (heard, what has been heard)
- uyariq (hearer, listener)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- rikch'ay (to resemble) - involves visual comparison but not direct perception
- allinta uyariy (to obey) - extends from hearing to compliance

---

### GROUP 7: Completeness, Fullness, and Deficiency

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether something has reached its proper measure, contains all necessary parts, or conversely lacks essential elements or proper complement. These terms appear in contexts of sufficiency, wholeness, pairing, and assessments of whether something is complete or deficient.

**Representative expressions:**
- hunt'a (full, complete, whole)
- hunt'a kay (to be full, to be complete)
- hunt'ata ruway (to do completely, to do fully)
- hunt'achiy (to fill, to complete, to make full)
- hunt'ayay (to become full, to fill up)
- tukuy (all, complete, whole)
- tukuy ima (everything, all things)
- tukuy runa (all people, everyone)
- tukuy pacha (all time, always)
- tukuy sunqu (whole heart, with all one's heart)
- tukuchiy (to complete, to finish)
- ch'ulla (single, odd, unpaired)
- ch'ulla kay (to be single/unpaired, to lack complement)
- ch'usaq (empty, void)
- ch'usaq kay (to be empty, to lack substance)
- ch'usaq simi (empty words, meaningless speech)
- ch'usaq yuyay (empty thought, vain thinking)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- wakcha (poor, orphan, lacking) - overlaps with social status but emphasizes lack of support
- hunt'a in health contexts - extends to sufficiency of well-being

---

### GROUP 8: Magnitude and Importance

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate the size, extent, or significance of things, including physical magnitude, social importance, and the seriousness or triviality of matters. These terms appear in contexts where the scale or weight of something is being assessed, particularly in evaluating the gravity of faults, errors, or the status of persons.

**Representative expressions:**
- huch'uy (small, little, minor)
- huch'uy kay (to be small, to be minor)
- huch'uy pantay (small error, minor mistake)
- huch'uy huch'a (small sin, minor fault)
- huch'uyachiy (to make small, to diminish, to minimize)
- hatun (big, large, great)
- hatun kay (to be big, to be great, to be important)
- hatun pantay (big error, serious mistake)
- hatun huch'a (great sin, serious fault)
- hatun yachay (great knowledge, profound learning)
- hatun runa (great person, important person, elder)
- hatunchay (to make big, to enlarge, to magnify)
- hatunyay (to become big, to grow)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- qhapaq (rich, powerful, noble) - overlaps with social status but implies magnitude of resources/power
- wakcha (poor, lacking) - overlaps with deficiency but implies small resources

---

### GROUP 9: Legitimacy, Authority, and Value

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether something has proper standing, legitimate authority, recognized value, or official validity. These terms appear in legal, administrative, and social contexts where the legitimacy of claims, authority of persons, or validity of documents is being assessed or established.

**Representative expressions:**
- chanin (valid, legitimate, having value)
- chanin kay (to be valid, to have legitimacy)
- chanin runa (person of worth, legitimate person, authorized person)
- chanin qillqa (valid document, legitimate writing)
- mana chanin (invalid, illegitimate, without value)
- chaninchay (to validate, to give value to, to legitimize)
- chaninchaq (validator, one who legitimizes, authority figure)
- chaninyuq (having value, possessing legitimacy)
- kamachiy (to order, to command, to establish law)
- kamachiq (commander, ruler, authority)
- kamachina (law, rule, commandment)
- kamachiy simi (command word, order, commandment)
- kamachiy atiy (to have authority to command)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- yupaychay (to honor, to respect) - overlaps with recognition of worth
- qhapaq (rich, powerful, noble) - overlaps with legitimate high status

---

### GROUP 10: Social Status and Resources

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to characterize persons according to their wealth, power, social standing, or lack thereof. These terms appear in contexts of social hierarchy, economic status, and the presence or absence of resources and support. They are used to describe social position and material conditions.

**Representative expressions:**
- qhapaq (rich, powerful, noble)
- qhapaq runa (rich person, powerful person, noble person)
- qhapaq kay (to be rich, to be powerful)
- qhapaqyay (to become rich, to prosper)
- qhapaqchay (to enrich, to make powerful)
- wakcha (poor, orphan, lacking)
- wakcha runa (poor person, orphan, destitute person)
- wakcha kay (to be poor, to be orphaned)
- wakcha wamra (orphan child, poor child)
- wakchayay (to become poor, to lose support)
- wakchachay (to impoverish, to make poor)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- hatun runa (great person, important person) - overlaps with magnitude but indicates social importance
- chanin runa (person of worth) - overlaps with legitimacy but indicates social value

---

### GROUP 11: Aesthetic and Affective Evaluation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate the beauty, pleasantness, or conversely the ugliness and repugnance of things, persons, or situations. These terms carry both aesthetic and affective dimensions, appearing in contexts where sensory appeal, emotional response, or overall pleasantness is being assessed.

**Representative expressions:**
- sumaq (beautiful, good, pleasant)
- sumaq kay (to be beautiful, to be good)
- sumaq kawsay (beautiful life, harmonious life)
- sumaq runa (beautiful person, good person)
- sumaq simi (beautiful words, pleasant speech)
- sumaqyachiy (to beautify, to make beautiful)
- sumaqyay (to become beautiful)
- millay (ugly, disgusting, abominable)
- millay kay (to be ugly, to be disgusting)
- millay runa (disgusting person, abominable person)
- millay ruway (disgusting deed, abominable act)
- millaychay (to make ugly, to disfigure)
- millayyay (to become ugly, to become disgusting)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- allin (good) - overlaps with moral goodness but can have aesthetic dimension
- sumaq in moral contexts - extends from aesthetic to moral evaluation

---

### GROUP 12: Honor, Respect, and Recognition

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used in contexts of showing reverence, giving proper recognition, or valuing persons and things according to their worth. These terms appear in religious, social, and moral contexts where respect, honor, and appropriate recognition are being given or withheld.

**Representative expressions:**
- yupaychay (to honor, to respect, to value)
- yupaychay ruway (to give honor, to show respect)
- yupaychaq (one who honors, respecter, worshipper)
- yupaychana (place of honor, means of honoring, shrine)
- yupaychakuy (to honor for oneself, to worship)
- yupaychasqa (honored, respected, revered)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- chanin (having value) - overlaps with recognition of worth but more administrative
- qhapaq (noble) - overlaps with worthy of respect but emphasizes status

---

### GROUP 13: Physical Shape and Moral Straightness

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that use physical straightness or crookedness to characterize both literal physical shape and metaphorical moral character. These terms appear in contexts where physical deformity or proper form is discussed, and extend metaphorically to moral corruption or integrity.

**Representative expressions:**
- k'uchi (crooked, bent, dishonest)
- k'uchi runa (crooked person, dishonest person, corrupt person)
- k'uchi ñan (crooked path, dishonest way)
- k'uchi ruway (crooked deed, dishonest action)
- k'uchiyay (to become crooked, to become corrupt)
- k'uchichiy (to make crooked, to bend, to corrupt)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- allin ñan (good path) - contrasts with crooked path but emphasizes goodness
- pantay ñan (wrong path) - overlaps with incorrect course but less emphasis on moral corruption

---

### GROUP 14: Resemblance and Comparison

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate similarity, likeness, or correspondence between things. These terms appear in contexts of comparison, matching, and assessing whether something resembles or corresponds to something else.

**Representative expressions:**
- rikch'ay (to resemble, to be similar)
- rikch'achiy (to make resemble, to make similar, to liken)
- rikch'aq (one who resembles, similar one, likeness)
- rikch'akuy (to resemble each other, to be mutually similar)
- rikch'ana (model, pattern, that which is to be resembled)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- paqta (exact, just right) - overlaps with precise correspondence
- chiqan (correct, exact) - overlaps with accurate correspondence

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with unclear or multiple group membership:

1. **allinpi kay** (to be in good health, to be well)
   - Could belong to moral/practical goodness group
   - Could belong to completeness/sufficiency group (being in good condition)
   - Has specialized health meaning that may warrant separate consideration

2. **chiqaqsapa** (very truthful, full of truth)
   - Overlaps truth group and character assessment
   - Intensified form that may function differently from base

3. **pantalla** (just an error, only a mistake)
   - Overlaps error group and magnitude evaluation
   - Limitative form that minimizes significance

4. **allinta** (well, properly) as adverbial modifier
   - Appears across multiple domains (doing, knowing, seeing, hearing)
   - Functions as general manner adverb rather than specific evaluation

5. **yuyaywan/mana yuyaywan** (intentionally/unintentionally)
   - Overlaps thought/belief group and moral evaluation
   - Focuses on intentionality rather than content of thought

### Notes on grouping challenges:

- Many terms show polysemy across physical, moral, and epistemic domains (e.g., k'uchi, allin, sut'i)
- Intensified and diminutive forms sometimes function differently from base forms
- Adverbial uses often span multiple semantic domains
- Cultural concepts like "allin kawsay" and "sumaq kawsay" integrate multiple evaluative dimensions
- Some constructions are highly context-dependent in their grouping

---

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Cross-cutting patterns observed:

1. **Physical-to-moral metaphor**: Several groups show extension from physical properties to moral evaluation (k'uchi: crooked→corrupt; sut'i: clear→evident; ch'usaq: empty→lacking substance)

2. **Perception-to-knowledge**: Strong connection between perception terms (rikuy, uyariy) and epistemic evaluation (yachay)

3. **Size-to-importance**: Magnitude terms (hatun, huch'uy) regularly extend to evaluating significance of moral/epistemic matters

4. **Completeness-to-goodness**: Overlap between completeness/fullness and positive evaluation (hunt'a, tukuy)

5. **Speech as central domain**: Many groups have specialized speech-related expressions (simi constructions), suggesting speech evaluation is a productive domain

### Asymmetries noted:

- Truth/falsity group is more elaborated than error/correctness group
- Positive evaluation (allin, sumaq) shows more derivational productivity than negative evaluation (millay)
- Direct perception (rikuy) has more epistemic constructions than indirect perception (uyariy)
- Social status terms are less elaborated than moral evaluation terms
- No clear group for temporal or spatial evaluation emerged from the data
