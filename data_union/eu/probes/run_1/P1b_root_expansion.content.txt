# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: egia**

- **Root form:** egia (Latin script; no separate transliteration required)
- **P1 items participating:** egia (1), egiazko (2), egiaztatu (3), egiazkotasun (20) — **4 items**
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary nominal root encoding propositional truth and factual correspondence. Serves as the base for derivation of adjectives, verbs, and abstract nouns across the truth-evaluation domain.

---

**Root 2: ziur**

- **Root form:** ziur
- **P1 items participating:** ziur (8), ziurtatu (9), ziurtasun (10) — **3 items**
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes epistemic certainty and reliability. Generates a productive paradigm of adjectives, verbs, and abstract nouns concerned with the state of being sure, confirmed, or dependably established.

---

**Root 3: zuzen**

- **Root form:** zuzen
- **P1 items participating:** zuzen (15), zuzentasun (16) — **2 items**; additionally, *zuzen* functions as a base for a large number of compounds and derivatives not yet listed in P1, making it the most morphologically productive root in the domain of correctness, justice, and rectitude.
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes correctness, straightness, moral rightness, and legal justice. Highly polysemous and productive across epistemic, moral, legal, and spatial domains.

---

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: egia

---

**1.**
- **Form:** egia esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** egia (truth) + esan (to say) — "to say the truth"
- **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth
- **Context of use:** The most common collocation of *egia* in everyday speech; used in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts to describe honest disclosure. Appears in direct commands (*egia esazu*, "tell the truth") and narrative description.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** egia da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** egia (truth) + da (is, 3sg present of *izan*) — "it is truth"
- **Natural English translation:** it is true; that is true
- **Context of use:** The standard predicative construction for asserting the truth of a proposition in everyday and formal speech. Functions as a discourse marker of confirmation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** egia esan behar
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** egia (truth) + esan (to say) + behar (need, must) — "must say the truth"
- **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth (as an obligation); truth be told
- **Context of use:** Used as a discourse-initial phrase equivalent to "to be honest" or "truth be told," signaling that the speaker is about to make a candid or potentially unwelcome statement. Common in spoken and written Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** egiari eutsi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** egiari (truth-DAT) + eutsi (to hold onto, to maintain) — "to hold onto truth"
- **Natural English translation:** to hold to the truth; to maintain the truth
- **Context of use:** Used in moral and rhetorical contexts to describe the act of adhering to or defending the truth under pressure. Appears in literary and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### ROOT 2: ziur

---

**1.**
- **Form:** ziur egon
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** ziur (certain) + egon (to be, stative) — "to be certain"
- **Natural English translation:** to be sure; to be certain
- **Context of use:** The primary predicative construction for expressing epistemic certainty in everyday and formal speech. Used with first-person (*ziur nago*, "I am sure") and third-person subjects.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** ziur nago
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** ziur (certain) + nago (I am, 1sg stative) — "I am certain"
- **Natural English translation:** I am sure; I am certain
- **Context of use:** The most common first-person expression of epistemic certainty in everyday Basque speech. Used to assert confidence in a proposition.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** ziur asko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** ziur (certain) + asko (much, very) — "very certainly"
- **Natural English translation:** most certainly; very likely; almost certainly
- **Context of use:** Adverbial intensifier of certainty, used in everyday speech to express a high degree of confidence in a proposition. Can also function as a hedged assertion ("very probably").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** ziurrenik
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -enik (superlative/adverbial suffix) — "most certainly"
- **Natural English translation:** certainly; most likely; probably; in all likelihood
- **Context of use:** Adverb used to express high probability or near-certainty. One of the most common epistemic adverbs in Basque, used across all registers to hedge or assert the likelihood of a proposition.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**

- **Context of use:** Used in legal, philosophical, and everyday contexts to assert that there is no doubt whatsoever about a proposition or fact.

- **Natural English translation:** in the face of uncertainty; given the lack of certainty
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical, legal, and administrative discourse to frame decision-making or judgment under conditions of uncertainty.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**7.**

- **Context of use:** Deverbal noun used in administrative and legal registers to refer to a formal act of certification or the document resulting from it. Closely related to *egiaztapen* but with emphasis on assurance rather than factual verification.

### ROOT 3: zuzen

---

**1.**
- **Form:** zuzen-zuzen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (straight, correct) + zuzen — reduplicated form
- **Natural English translation:** straight; directly; right away; absolutely correctly
- **Context of use:** Reduplicated intensifier used in everyday speech to emphasize directness of movement or correctness of action. Also used to mean "straight to the point" in discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** zuzendu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (straight, correct) + -du (verbalizing/perfective suffix) — "to make straight/correct"
- **Natural English translation:** to correct; to rectify; to direct; to edit; to address (a letter)
- **Context of use:** Highly productive verb used across multiple domains: correcting errors in writing or speech, rectifying moral or legal wrongs, directing or steering a course, and addressing correspondence. One of the most common verbs derived from *zuzen*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** zuzenketa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (correct) + -keta (deverbal nominal suffix indicating action/process) — "act of correcting"
- **Natural English translation:** correction; amendment; rectification
- **Context of use:** Used in educational, editorial, legal, and administrative contexts to refer to the act or result of correcting an error or amending a text or decision. Common in school and publishing contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** zuzenketa proposamena
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzenketa (correction, amendment) + proposamena (proposal-DEF) — "amendment proposal"
- **Natural English translation:** amendment proposal; proposed correction
- **Context of use:** Used in legislative and administrative contexts to refer to a formal proposal to amend a law, regulation, or document.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
- **Form:** zuzentzaile
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (correct) + -tzaile (agentive suffix) — "one who corrects"
- **Natural English translation:** corrector; editor; proofreader; referee
- **Context of use:** Used in educational and publishing contexts to refer to a person who corrects texts, examinations, or proofs. Also used in sports contexts for a referee or adjudicator.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
- **Form:** zuzendari
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (straight, direct) + -dari (agentive suffix, variant) — "one who directs"
- **Natural English translation:** director; manager; head; principal
- **Context of use:** The standard Basque term for a director, manager, or head of an institution, company, or artistic production. Widely used in administrative, cultural, and everyday contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
- **Form:** zuzendaritza
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzendari (director) + -tza (collective/abstract nominal suffix) — "directorship; body of directors"
- **Natural English translation:** directorship; management; board of directors; direction
- **Context of use:** Used in administrative and organizational contexts to refer to the position of director or the collective body of directors of an institution or company.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
- **Form:** zuzenbide
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** zuzen (right, just) + bide (way, path) — "the way of right/justice"
- **Natural English translation:** law; right; jurisprudence
- **Context of use:** The primary Basque term for law as a discipline or system of rights and justice. Used in legal, academic, and administrative contexts. *Zuzenbide fakultatea* is the Faculty of Law; *zuzenbide zibila* is civil law.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are expressions in which the identified roots appear in contexts where their contribution to the truth-evaluation domain is shifted, extended, or not obviously operative. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**Root: egia**

- *egiazko* appears in *egiazko maitasuna* ("true love") and *egiazko adiskidea* ("true friend"), where the modifier functions to evaluate sincerity and depth of feeling or relationship rather than propositional truth or factual correspondence.
- *egiazko* appears in *egiazko balioa* ("true value") and *egiazko neurria* ("true measure"), where it evaluates intrinsic or essential quantity rather than the truth of a statement.
- *egiazko jainkoa* ("the true God") uses the root in a theological context where "truth" functions to assert ontological uniqueness and ultimate reality rather than propositional accuracy.
- *egia hutsa* uses *hutsa* (literally "empty, pure, mere"), which can in other contexts mean "nothing" or "zero," creating a compound where the intensifier has a range of meanings beyond simple emphasis.

---

**Root: ziur**

- *ziurrenik* functions as a probability adverb ("probably," "most likely") in everyday speech, where it marks epistemic hedging rather than certainty — a use that is in tension with the root's core meaning of "certain."
- *ziurtagiri* (certificate) has extended into technical domains (digital certificates, medical certificates) where the root's contribution is institutional authentication rather than personal epistemic certainty.
- *ziurtasun juridikoa* ("legal certainty") applies the root to a principle of legal predictability and consistency, where "certainty" refers to the stability of legal norms rather than to a cognitive state.

---

**Root: zuzen**

- *zuzendari* ("director") and *zuzendaritza* ("management, directorship") use the root in an organizational and administrative sense where "directing" or "steering" is the operative meaning, with no obvious connection to correctness or justice.
- *zuzeneko* ("live, direct") in broadcasting contexts (*zuzeneko emankizuna*, "live broadcast") uses the root in a temporal and medial sense — immediacy and absence of recording — rather than in any evaluative or truth-related sense.
- *zuzeneko demokrazia* ("direct democracy") uses the root to mean "without intermediary" in a political-structural sense.
- *zuzendu* in the sense of "to address (a letter or communication)" (*gutuna zuzendu*, "to address a letter") uses the root in a directional/spatial sense rather than a correctness or justice sense.
- *zuzentasun linguistikoa* ("linguistic correctness") applies the root to conformity with normative grammatical standards, where "correctness" is defined by institutional prescription rather than by correspondence to reality or moral rightness.
- *zuzentasun politikoa* ("political correctness") applies the root to social norms of acceptable speech, a domain where the evaluative standard is social convention rather than factual accuracy or moral justice in the traditional sense.
