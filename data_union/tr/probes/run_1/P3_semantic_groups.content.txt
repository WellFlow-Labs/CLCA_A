# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms appear in the same collocational environments, which are explicitly contrasted with each other, which share register restrictions, which participate in the same derivational patterns, and which are described as antonyms or near-synonyms in the source data. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

## GROUP 1: Terms used to evaluate whether a statement or claim corresponds to fact

**Label:** Evaluating statements as correct or incorrect

**Definition:** These terms are applied to propositions, claims, statements, and representations to judge whether they correspond to fact or to an accepted standard. They appear in contexts where the question is whether something said or asserted is accurate. They contrast with each other primarily along dimensions of register and degree of moral charge, not along a simple true/false binary.

**Representative expressions:**

- *doğru* — the broadest and most frequent term; applied to statements, beliefs, and actions across all registers
- *yanlış* — the primary antonym of *doğru*; applied to statements and beliefs judged to deviate from correctness
- *doğruluk* — the nominalized property; used when the quality of being true or accurate is itself under discussion
- *doğruluk değeri* — technical use in logic for the truth value of a proposition
- *doğruluk koşulu* — technical use in formal semantics for truth conditions
- *yanlışlık* — the nominalized property of being wrong or incorrect
- *doğrulanabilir* / *doğrulanabilirlik* — used in scientific and philosophical discourse for claims that can in principle be verified
- *yanlışlanabilir* / *yanlışlanabilirlik* — the parallel falsificationist pair; used in philosophy of science
- *doğrulamak* / *doğrulama* — the act or process of verifying a claim
- *yanlışlamak* / *yanlışlama* — the act or process of demonstrating a claim to be false
- *doğru mu yanlış mı?* — the formulaic binary question in educational and evaluative contexts
- *doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek* — the periphrastic construction for discriminating true from false

**Notes:** *Doğru* and *yanlış* are the core pair. The technical terms (*doğruluk değeri*, *doğruluk koşulu*, *yanlışlanabilirlik*) are restricted to academic and philosophical registers but derive from the same base. *Gerçek* (see Group 2) overlaps with *doğru* in some uses but is distinguished in the data by its stronger ontological emphasis.

---

## GROUP 2: Terms used to affirm or question whether something is real or genuine (as opposed to fake, illusory, or fictional)

**Label:** Affirming or questioning ontological genuineness

**Definition:** These terms are applied to objects, persons, identities, situations, and states of affairs to evaluate whether they are what they appear or claim to be — whether they have genuine existence or are counterfeit, simulated, or illusory. The central contrast in this group is between what is real and what is fake, simulated, or constructed. These terms appear frequently in legal contexts involving forgery and fraud, in philosophical contexts involving the nature of reality, and in everyday contexts involving authenticity of persons and feelings.

**Representative expressions:**

- *gerçek* — the primary term; applied to objects, persons, identities, and situations to affirm factual or ontological reality
- *gerçeklik* — the nominalized property of being real; used in philosophical and technical contexts (*sanal gerçeklik*, *artırılmış gerçeklik*)
- *gerçekten* — the adverbial form; used to affirm or emphasize that something is truly the case
- *gerçek dışı* — used to characterize what does not correspond to reality; appears in legal (*gerçek dışı beyan*) and philosophical contexts
- *gerçeğe aykırı* — formal/legal expression for what is contrary to fact
- *gerçeğe uygun* — the positive counterpart; used in legal contexts for declarations that accurately reflect reality
- *gerçek şu ki* — discourse marker introducing a frank or corrective statement
- *sahte* — applied to objects, documents, and identities presented as genuine but not; the primary term for counterfeit
- *sahteci* / *sahtekâr* / *sahtecilik* / *sahtekârlık* — persons and practices of counterfeiting or fraud
- *sahte belge*, *sahte kimlik*, *sahte para* — fixed legal collocations for forged documents, false identity, counterfeit money
- *sahici* — used in everyday and literary contexts to affirm that something is genuinely what it appears to be, without pretense
- *gerçeği sahteden ayırt etmek* — the periphrastic construction for discriminating genuine from fake
- *sahici mi, sahte mi?* — the interrogative construction posing the authenticity question
- *gerçek kişi* — legal term for a natural person (as opposed to a legal entity)
- *gerçekleşmek* / *gerçekleştirmek* — used for events or plans that come into actual existence (see also Group 7)

**Notes:** *Gerçek* and *sahte* are the central antonym pair in this group. *Sahici* overlaps with *gerçek* but is more affective and colloquial, particularly in contexts involving persons and emotions. *Gerçek dışı* and *gerçeğe aykırı* are more formal and legal in register. *Gerçekleşmek* and *gerçekleştirmek* are included here because they derive from *gerçek* and involve the concept of actualization, but they are also noted in Group 7 as peripheral.

## GROUP 3: Terms used to evaluate the authenticity or originality of creative works, documents, and objects

**Label:** Evaluating originality and authenticity of works and objects

**Definition:** These terms are applied specifically to works, documents, and objects to evaluate whether they are the genuine original rather than a copy, imitation, or derivative. They appear in copyright, legal, commercial, and aesthetic contexts. The central contrast is between the original and the copy or imitation.

**Representative expressions:**

- *özgün* — used in aesthetic and intellectual contexts for works or ideas that are not derivative; carries a connotation of intrinsic creative originality
- *özgünlük* — the nominalized property; used in copyright and literary discourse
- *özgün eser* — the standard collocation in copyright law for an original work
- *orijinal* — used in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts to distinguish an authentic item from a copy; tends toward concrete objects
- *orijinallik* — the nominalized property; used in commercial and legal contexts
- *orijinal mi taklit mi?* — the interrogative construction in commercial contexts
- *orijinalinden ayırt edilememek* — the construction for a copy indistinguishable from the original
- *asıl* — used to identify the genuine, foundational, or original instance; *asıl nüsha* (original document)
- *asılsız* — used for claims that lack any evidentiary basis (see also Group 4)
- *sahih* — used in religious and historical scholarship for authentic texts and traditions; *sahih hadis*

**Notes:** *Özgün* and *orijinal* overlap but are distinguished in the data: *özgün* emphasizes creative originality and intrinsic authenticity, while *orijinal* tends toward the concrete distinction between an authentic item and a copy. *Asıl* overlaps with both but also functions as an adverb (*aslında*, actually) and as a modifier meaning "fundamental" or "real" (see Group 6). *Sahih* is restricted to religious and historical registers and does not participate in the broader authenticity discourse of this group.

## GROUP 4: Terms used to characterize claims as lacking evidentiary or factual basis

**Label:** Characterizing claims as unfounded or baseless

**Definition:** These terms are applied to allegations, accusations, and claims to indicate that they lack supporting evidence or factual grounding. They appear primarily in legal and journalistic contexts. The emphasis is not merely that the claim is wrong but that it has no foundation at all.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asılsız* — the primary term; applied to allegations and claims that lack any evidentiary basis; *asılsız iddia*
- *asılsızlık* — the nominalized property; used in legal discourse
- *asılsız olduğu kanıtlanmak* — the construction for formally demonstrating groundlessness
- *asılsız olduğunu öne sürmek* — the construction for alleging groundlessness

**Notes:** This group is small. *Asılsız* is the dominant term and its collocational range is narrow, centering on *asılsız iddia*. The group is distinguished from Group 1 (incorrect statements) by the specific emphasis on absence of foundation rather than mere incorrectness. *Asılsız* does not typically appear in philosophical or scientific discourse about truth values; it is primarily a legal and journalistic term.

---

## GROUP 5: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether something meets required conditions for acceptance or force

**Label:** Evaluating procedural validity and legal force

**Definition:** These terms are applied to contracts, votes, arguments, documents, and procedural acts to evaluate whether they satisfy the conditions required for legal or institutional recognition. They appear in legal, administrative, and electoral contexts. The central question is not whether something is true or genuine but whether it meets formal requirements.

**Representative expressions:**

- *geçerli* — the primary term; used for contracts, arguments, votes, and documents that meet required conditions; *geçerli sözleşme*, *geçerli argüman*
- *geçerlilik* — the nominalized property; *pasaportun geçerlilik süresi*
- *geçerliliğini yitirmek* — the construction for losing validity
- *geçerli sayılmak* — the construction for being institutionally recognized as valid
- *geçersiz* — the antonym; used for what fails to meet required conditions; *geçersiz oy*, *geçersiz sözleşme*
- *geçersizlik* — the nominalized property; *geçersizlik davası*
- *geçersiz kılmak* — the construction for rendering something void
- *geçersiz sayılmak* — the construction for being declared invalid
- *geçersiz ilan etmek* — the construction for formally declaring something void
- *hâlâ geçerli olmak* — the construction for affirming continuing validity
- *geçerli bir neden olmak* — the construction for evaluating whether a reason meets the required standard

**Notes:** *Geçerli* and *geçersiz* are the central antonym pair. This group is distinguished from Group 1 (propositional correctness) and Group 2 (ontological genuineness) by its focus on procedural and institutional conditions rather than factual correspondence. The data notes that *geçerli* carries both procedural and epistemic senses; the epistemic sense (valid argument) overlaps with Group 1.

## GROUP 6: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, decisions, or claims are justified or rightful

**Label:** Evaluating justification and rightfulness

**Definition:** These terms are applied to persons, actions, decisions, and claims to evaluate whether they are justified, rightful, or in accordance with what is owed. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. The central question is whether someone or something has the right on their side.

**Representative expressions:**

- *haklı* — the primary term; used to affirm that a person's position, action, or claim is justified; *haklısın*, *haklı çıkmak*
- *haksız* — the antonym; applied to actions or persons that violate standards of justice or rightful entitlement; *haksız muamele*
- *haklılık* — the nominalized property of being justified or in the right
- *haksızlık* — the nominalized property of being unjust; *haksızlığa uğramak*
- *haklı çıkmak* — the construction for being vindicated by events
- *haklı olduğunu kanıtlamak* — the construction for demonstrating one's justification
- *haksız yere* — the adverbial construction for acting without justification
- *haklı olarak* — the adverbial construction for acting with justification
- *haksız olduğunu kabul etmek* — the construction for acknowledging one was in the wrong
- *hak vermek* / *sana hak veriyorum* — the construction for conceding another's point
- *kim haklı, kim haksız?* — the formulaic interrogative in disputes
- *hak* — the base noun; used for rights, entitlements, and (in elevated register) truth
- *hak etmek* — to deserve or merit something
- *hakkını vermek* — to give someone their due; to do justice to something
- *hak ihlali* / *insan hakları* — legal collocations for rights violations
- *haksız kazanç*, *haksız rekabet*, *haksız fiil* — fixed legal collocations

**Notes:** *Haklı* and *haksız* are the central antonym pair. The data notes that *haklı* carries both epistemic (being correct) and moral (being justified) senses, making it semantically broad. The *hak-* root also underlies *hakikat* (Group 9) and *hakkaniyet* (Group 8), but those terms are grouped separately because their primary usage contexts differ.

---

## GROUP 7: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, processes, or institutions conform to law

**Label:** Evaluating conformity with law

**Definition:** These terms are applied to actions, regulations, rights, and institutions to evaluate whether they conform to written law. They appear in legal, political, and administrative contexts. The data explicitly distinguishes these terms from those evaluating legitimacy (Group 8), noting that legality and legitimacy can diverge.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yasal* — the primary term; refers specifically to conformity with written law; *yasal düzenleme*, *yasal hak*
- *yasallık* — the nominalized property; *yasallık denetimi*
- *yasadışı* — the antonym; used for what is prohibited by law; *yasadışı faaliyet*
- *yasalaşmak* — the process by which a bill becomes law
- *yasalaştırmak* — the act of enacting something as law
- *yasal çerçeve içinde kalmak* — the construction for remaining within the legal framework
- *yasal açıdan değerlendirmek* — the construction for evaluating from a legal standpoint
- *yasal olmakla birlikte meşru olmamak* — the contrastive construction explicitly distinguishing legality from legitimacy

**Notes:** The data explicitly contrasts *yasal* with *meşru* (Group 8), noting that *yasal* refers to conformity with written law while *meşru* can extend to moral or political legitimacy beyond strict legality. The construction *yasal ama meşru değil* (legal but not legitimate) is documented as a productive pattern in political and legal discourse.

---

## GROUP 8: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, authorities, or claims have legitimacy beyond strict legality

**Label:** Evaluating legitimacy and equity

**Definition:** These terms are applied to actions, authorities, decisions, and claims to evaluate whether they are sanctioned by norms, rights, or principles of fairness that may extend beyond written law. They appear in political, legal, and moral contexts. The data distinguishes these terms from those evaluating strict legality (Group 7).

**Representative expressions:**

- *meşru* — the primary term; used for what is sanctioned by law, norm, or right; *meşru müdafaa*
- *meşruiyet* — the nominalized property; *meşruiyet krizi*
- *meşrulaştırmak* — the act of conferring legitimacy
- *meşrulaşmak* — the process of acquiring legitimacy
- *meşruiyet zemini* — the basis of legitimacy
- *gayri meşru* — the antonym; used for what lacks legitimacy
- *meşru yollarla elde etmek* — the construction for obtaining through legitimate means
- *meşru zemine oturmak* — the construction for resting on legitimate grounds
- *hakkaniyet* — the principle of equitable treatment; used in legal and administrative contexts
- *hakkaniyetli* — the adjectival form; *hakkaniyetli karar*
- *hakkaniyetsiz* — the antonym
- *hakkaniyetli bir çözüm bulmak* — the construction for reaching an equitable resolution
- *yasal ama meşru değil* — the cross-cutting construction distinguishing legality from legitimacy

**Notes:** *Meşru* and *hakkaniyet*/*hakkaniyetli* are grouped together because both appear in contexts where the question is whether something meets a standard of rightfulness or fairness that goes beyond procedural legality. The data notes that *hakkaniyetli* is more restricted to formal legal writing than *meşru*. *Adil* (Group 9) overlaps with this group but is distinguished by its broader register and its focus on fairness in processes and outcomes.

---

## GROUP 9: Terms used to evaluate whether processes, decisions, and persons meet standards of fairness and impartiality

**Label:** Evaluating fairness, impartiality, and justice

**Definition:** These terms are applied to persons, processes, decisions, and institutions to evaluate whether they are fair, impartial, and just. They appear across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. The central concerns are whether a process treats parties equally and whether a judgment is free from bias.

**Representative expressions:**

- *adil* — the primary term; applied to persons, processes, and outcomes; *adil yargılama*
- *adalet* — the abstract principle or system of justice; *Adalet Bakanlığı*
- *adillik* — the nominalized quality of being fair
- *adaletsiz* — the antonym; *adaletsiz muamele*
- *adaletsizlik* — the nominalized quality of injustice; *adaletsizliğe karşı mücadele*
- *adil yargılama* — the fixed legal expression for fair trial
- *adalet önünde eşit olmak* — the construction for equality before justice
- *tarafsız* — applied to persons, institutions, and judgments not aligned with any particular interest; *tarafsız hakem*, *tarafsız basın*
- *tarafsızlık* — the nominalized property; *tarafsızlık ilkesi*
- *taraflı* — the antonym; *taraflı habercilik*
- *taraflılık* — the nominalized property of being biased
- *tarafsız kalmak* — the construction for maintaining neutrality
- *tarafsız gözle bakmak* — the construction for viewing without bias
- *tarafsız olduğunu iddia etmek* — the construction for claiming neutrality
- *hakikat* — used in philosophical, religious, and literary contexts for deep or ultimate truth; *hakikati aramak*
- *hakikaten* — the adverbial form; used to affirm truth with a formal or emphatic tone
- *hakikat nedir?* — the philosophical interrogative
- *hakikatin peşinde olmak* — the construction for pursuing truth

**Notes:** *Adil* and *tarafsız* are grouped together because both appear in contexts evaluating whether a process or person is free from bias and treats parties fairly. They are distinguished in the data: *adil* focuses on outcomes and processes meeting standards of justice, while *tarafsız* focuses on the absence of alignment with any particular side. *Hakikat* is included here because the data places it in philosophical and moral discourse alongside questions of justice and truth, and because it derives from the same Arabic root (*ḥaqq*) as *hak* (Group 6). However, *hakikat* is also a candidate for remaining ungrouped (see below), as its primary usage context — elevated philosophical and religious discourse about ultimate truth — does not map cleanly onto the fairness and impartiality concerns of *adil* and *tarafsız*.

---

## GROUP 10: Terms used to characterize statements, representations, or persons as deliberately or effectively misleading

**Label:** Characterizing misleading and deceptive conduct

**Definition:** These terms are applied to statements, representations, actions, and persons to characterize them as causing others to form false beliefs. They appear in legal, regulatory, moral, and everyday contexts. The data distinguishes between terms that imply intent to deceive and those that describe the effect of misleading without necessarily implying intent.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yanıltıcı* — applied to statements and representations that lead others to form false beliefs, even if not outright false; *yanıltıcı reklam*
- *yanıltıcılık* — the nominalized property
- *yanıltıcı biçimde sunmak* — the construction for presenting information in a misleading manner
- *yanıltıcı olmakla birlikte yanlış olmamak* — the construction marking the distinction between misleading and outright false
- *aldatıcı* — stronger than *yanıltıcı*; implies intentional deception; *aldatıcı davranış*
- *aldatıcılık* — the nominalized property
- *aldatıcı bir görünüm sergilemek* — the construction for presenting a deceptive appearance
- *aldatmak* / *aldatma* — the act of deceiving
- *aldanmak* — to be deceived or to deceive oneself
- *aldatmaca* — a deceptive scheme or trick; used to dismiss something as a sham
- *yanıltmak* — the causative form; to cause someone to err or form a false belief
- *yanılgı* — the result of erring; error, fallacy, misconception
- *yanılsama* — illusion, perceptual error; *optik yanılsama*
- *yanılsamak* — to have an illusion
- *yanıltıcı değil, doğrudan aldatıcı* — the contrastive construction distinguishing misleading from outright deceptive

**Notes:** The data explicitly distinguishes *yanıltıcı* (misleading, effect-focused) from *aldatıcı* (deceptive, intent-implied). Both are grouped here because they share the core feature of causing false beliefs in others. *Yanılgı* and *yanılsama* are included because they derive from the same root and appear in related contexts, but they describe the state of being misled rather than the act of misleading.

## GROUP 11: Terms used to characterize deliberate false statements as morally or legally culpable

**Label:** Characterizing deliberate false statements

**Definition:** These terms are applied specifically to statements that are not merely incorrect but are deliberately false, carrying a moral charge of dishonesty. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. The central feature distinguishing this group from Group 1 is the emphasis on deliberate intent and moral culpability.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yalan* — the primary term; refers specifically to a deliberate false statement; *yalan söylemek*
- *yalancı* — a person who tells lies; *yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar*
- *yalancılık* — the habit or practice of lying
- *yalanlamak* / *yalanlama* — to deny or refute a claim; *resmi yalanlama*
- *yalanlanmak* — to be denied or refuted
- *yalan beyan* — false declaration (legal collocation)
- *yalancı tanık* / *yalancı tanıklık* — false witness, perjury
- *yalan dolan* — the fixed binomial for lies and deception
- *yalan dünya* — the fixed construction characterizing the world as illusory or deceptive
- *beyaz yalan* — white lie
- *yalan üstüne yalan* — the intensifying construction for compounding deception
- *yalancı çıkmak* — to be proven a liar
- *yalan söylemek günah* — the formulaic moral statement
- *yalan mı gerçek mi?* — the interrogative construction

**Notes:** *Yalan* is distinguished from *yanlış* (Group 1) in the data by its stronger moral charge and its implication of deliberate intent. *Yalanlamak* (to deny/refute) is included here because it derives from *yalan* and appears in the same discourse contexts, though its meaning has shifted toward official denial rather than the act of lying. *Yalan dünya* is a fixed construction with philosophical and religious resonance that extends beyond the core meaning of deliberate falsehood.

## GROUP 12: Terms used to evaluate the reliability and trustworthiness of sources and persons

**Label:** Evaluating reliability and trustworthiness of sources

**Definition:** These terms are applied to persons, institutions, sources, and information to evaluate whether they can be depended upon to provide accurate and honest information. They appear in journalistic, academic, legal, and everyday contexts. The central question is whether something or someone can be trusted as a source of accurate information.

**Representative expressions:**

- *güvenilir* — the primary term; applied to persons, sources, and information judged to be dependable; *güvenilir kaynak*
- *güvenilirlik* — the nominalized property; *kaynağın güvenilirliği*
- *güvenilir kaynak* — the primary collocation in journalistic and academic discourse
- *güvenilir bir kaynaktan öğrenmek* — the construction for acquiring information from a reliable source
- *güvenilir olduğunu kanıtlamak* — the construction for demonstrating reliability
- *ne kadar güvenilir?* — the interrogative construction
- *güvenilmez* — the antonym; *güvenilmez kaynak*
- *güvenilmezlik* — the nominalized property
- *güvenilmez olduğu ortaya çıkmak* — the construction for being revealed as unreliable
- *güvenmek* — to trust; the base verb
- *güven vermek* — to inspire trust
- *güven duymak* — to feel trust
- *güven bunalımı* — crisis of trust
- *güvenli* — safe, secure (tends toward physical/technical safety rather than epistemic trustworthiness)
- *güvensiz* / *güvensizlik* — unsafe, insecure; distrust
- *muteber* — credible, authoritative; largely displaced by *güvenilir* in modern usage but retained in formal registers; *muteber kaynak*

**Notes:** *Güvenilir* and *güvenilmez* are the central antonym pair. *Muteber* is included because the data explicitly notes it as a near-synonym of *güvenilir* in formal registers. *Güvenli* is included because it derives from the same root, but the data notes that it tends toward physical or technical safety rather than epistemic trustworthiness; it is therefore a peripheral member of this group.

## GROUP 13: Terms used to evaluate whether judgments and claims are objective or subjective

**Label:** Evaluating objectivity and subjectivity of judgments

**Definition:** These terms are applied to judgments, evaluations, and claims to characterize them as either independent of personal perspective (objective) or dependent on it (subjective). They appear primarily in philosophical, scientific, and academic registers. The data notes that both terms are modern coinages associated with academic discourse rather than everyday speech.

**Representative expressions:**

- *nesnel* — objective; used in philosophical, scientific, and evaluative discourse; contrasts with *öznel*
- *nesnellik* — the nominalized property; *bilimsel nesnellik*
- *nesnel bir değerlendirme yapmak* — the construction for making an objective assessment
- *tamamen nesnel olmak mümkün mü?* — the interrogative construction raising the question of full objectivity
- *öznel* — subjective; the antonym of *nesnel*
- *öznellik* — the nominalized property; *estetik yargıların öznelliği*
- *öznel bir yargı olmak* — the construction for characterizing a judgment as subjective
- *öznelden nesnele geçmek* — the construction for moving from subjective to objective perspective
- *nesnel mi, öznel mi?* — the formulaic interrogative

**Notes:** This group is small and register-restricted. Both *nesnel* and *öznel* are described in the data as modern coinages more common in academic and intellectual registers than in everyday speech. They do not appear in the same collocational environments as *doğru*, *yanlış*, or *gerçek*, and they do not participate in the same derivational patterns as the more productive roots.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

The following items were identified as belonging to multiple groups or as not fitting cleanly into any single group.

---

**doğru** — primarily Group 1 (propositional correctness), but also participates in moral discourse (*doğru yol*, the right path) that overlaps with Group 6 (justification and rightfulness), and in spatial/directional uses (*doğrultu*, *doğrudan*) that are peripheral to all groups.

**gerçek** — primarily Group 2 (ontological genuineness), but overlaps with Group 1 in uses where *gerçek* and *doğru* are near-synonymous (e.g., *gerçeği söylemek* vs. *doğruyu söylemek*). The data notes that *gerçek* emphasizes ontological correspondence while *doğru* emphasizes correctness against a standard, but the boundary is not always clear in usage.

**asıl** — participates in Group 3 (originality and authenticity of documents: *asıl nüsha*) and in Group 4 (foundational basis: *asılsız*), but also functions as a discourse marker (*aslında*, actually) and as a prenominal intensifier meaning "the real/fundamental" (*asıl mesele*, *asıl sorun*). The intensifier use does not fit cleanly into any group.

**hak** — the base noun participates in Group 6 (justification and rightfulness) as the foundation of *haklı*/*haksız*, in Group 7/8 through legal collocations (*hak ihlali*, *insan hakları*), and in Group 9 through *hakikat*. As a divine name (*Hak*), it is outside all groups.

**hakikat** — included tentatively in Group 9 (fairness and justice) because of its shared Arabic root with *hak* and its appearance in moral and philosophical discourse, but its primary usage context — elevated philosophical and religious discourse about ultimate or deep truth — is distinct from the fairness and impartiality concerns of *adil* and *tarafsız*. It could equally be placed in Group 1 (propositional correctness) as a more elevated register variant of *doğru* and *gerçek*, or left ungrouped.

**dürüst** / **dürüstlük** — documented in P1b as closely associated with *doğru* in the moral-epistemic field. *Dürüst* characterizes persons and conduct as honest and upright, overlapping with Group 1 (truthfulness of statements) and Group 11 (deliberate false statements, as the positive counterpart of *yalan*). It does not fit cleanly into either group.

**doğru dürüst** — the fixed collocation functions as an adverb of adequacy (*doğru dürüst yapamamak*, unable to do something properly) that is peripheral to all groups in this domain.

**gerçekleşmek** / **gerçekleştirmek** — derive from *gerçek* and are included in Group 2 for completeness, but their primary meaning (to come true, to be realized, to carry out) is about actualization and occurrence rather than truth-evaluation or authenticity. They are peripheral to Group 2.

**aslında** — the adverb derived from *asıl* functions as a corrective discourse marker (actually, in fact) that is used across all groups whenever a correction or clarification is being introduced. It does not belong to any single group.

**doğrusu** — the possessive form used as a discourse marker (honestly, to tell the truth) functions similarly to *aslında* as a sincerity or correction marker across groups.

**yalan dünya** — the fixed construction characterizing the world as illusory or deceptive has philosophical and religious resonance that places it outside the core usage patterns of Group 11 (deliberate false statements). It is noted as a peripheral item.

**güvenlik** — derives from *güven* and is included in Group 12 for completeness, but the data notes that its primary meaning has shifted toward institutional and physical security rather than epistemic trustworthiness. It is peripheral to Group 12.

**doğrusal** — the mathematical/technical term for "linear" derives from *doğru* but has no connection to truth-evaluation in usage. It is not grouped.

**doğrultu** — the spatial/directional term for "direction" or "line of orientation" derives from *doğru* but functions in navigational and discourse-structural contexts (*bu doğrultuda*, along these lines) with no connection to truth-evaluation. It is not grouped.

**sanal gerçeklik** / **artırılmış gerçeklik** — technical terms for virtual and augmented reality derive from *gerçek* but function as names for technological systems. They are not grouped.

**gerçeküstücülük** — the term for surrealism derives from *gerçek* but names an artistic movement. It is not grouped.

**gerçek ötesi** — the recent coinage for "post-truth" derives from *gerçek* but names a political-epistemic condition rather than evaluating individual claims. It is noted as a peripheral item without a clear group placement.

**sahih** — documented in P1 as [MARGINAL] and restricted to Islamic scholarly discourse. It participates in Group 3 (authenticity) within its restricted domain but does not participate in the broader authenticity discourse of that group. It is not grouped with the main body of Group 3.

**muteber** — documented in P1 as [MARGINAL] and largely displaced in modern usage. Included in Group 12 as a peripheral member.
