| 1. General truth / correctness in statements and claims | This grouping centers on expressions used to say that a statement, report, answer, or assumption is true or correct. In usage, these forms are common in confirmation, denial, rebuttal, clarification, and discussion of whether something is in fact the case. | **benar** — ‘true; correct; right’<br>**betul** — ‘correct; right; true; exact’<br>**kebenaran** — ‘truth; correctness; rightness’<br>**kebenarannya** — ‘its truth; the truth of it’<br>**tidak benar** — ‘not true; incorrect’<br>**kurang benar** — ‘not quite correct’<br>**benar bahwa ...** — ‘it is true that ...’<br>**tidak benar bahwa ...** — ‘it is not true that ...’<br>**memang benar** — ‘it is indeed true’<br>**memang betul** — ‘that is indeed correct’<br>**benar atau tidak** — ‘whether true or not’<br>**jawaban yang tepat** — ‘the correct answer’ | **tepat** can join this group when used for answers, diagnoses, or wording, but often extends into precision or appropriateness.<br>**sebenarnya** often introduces what is really the case, but functions partly as a discourse marker rather than a plain truth predicate.<br>**betul** overlaps strongly with **benar**, but is often more conversational in tone. | — |
| 2. Confirming, justifying, and correcting | These expressions are used when speakers do something with a claim or action: confirm it, justify it, or correct it. In actual usage, this grouping mixes truth-confirmation with moral/legal justification and with practical correction of mistakes. | **membenarkan** — ‘to justify; to confirm; to validate; to correct’<br>**pembenaran** — ‘justification’<br>**membenarkan diri** — ‘to justify oneself’<br>**membenarkan anggapan bahwa ...** — ‘to confirm the assumption that ...’<br>**membenarkan kabar / berita** — ‘to confirm news / a report’<br>**tidak ada yang membenarkan ...** — ‘nothing justifies ...’<br>**membetulkan** — ‘to correct; to set right; to fix’<br>**pembetulan** — ‘correction; rectification’ | **membenarkan** belongs both with truth-confirmation and with moral/legal justification.<br>**membetulkan** overlaps with this group only partly, since it also extends to physical fixing or straightening.<br>**pembenaran** is often more about giving reasons than about establishing factual truth. | — |
| 3. Precision, exactness, and getting something right | This grouping covers expressions used for exactness in wording, measurement, identification, timing, and selection. In usage, these forms often evaluate whether something is accurate, precise, or exactly fitting to the situation. | **tepat** — ‘accurate; precise; exact; appropriate’<br>**ketepatan** — ‘accuracy; precision; appropriateness’<br>**akurat** — ‘accurate’<br>**keakuratan** — ‘accuracy’<br>**dengan tepat** — ‘accurately; precisely’<br>**secara tepat** — ‘precisely; accurately’<br>**akurat dalam ...** — ‘accurate in ...’<br>**secara akurat** — ‘accurately’<br>**dengan akurat** — ‘accurately’<br>**tingkat ketepatan** — ‘level of accuracy’<br>**tingkat keakuratan** — ‘level of accuracy’<br>**tepat sekali** — ‘exactly right; very accurate’<br>**kurang tepat** — ‘not quite accurate / somewhat inappropriate’<br>**tidak tepat** — ‘inaccurate; inappropriate’<br>**kurang akurat** — ‘less accurate’<br>**tidak akurat** — ‘inaccurate’ | **tepat** often overlaps with appropriateness, not just factual accuracy.<br>**akurat** is narrower in usage and more strongly tied to factual or representational accuracy.<br>**jawaban yang tepat**, **istilah yang tepat**, and **diagnosis yang tepat** sit between correctness and precision. | — |
| 4. Targeting, timing, and practical fit within the *tepat* family | Within actual Indonesian usage, many *tepat* expressions cluster around whether an action reaches the right target, happens at the right time, or suits a practical purpose. These are related in form and usage, even though they are not all about truth in a narrow sense. | **tepat sasaran** — ‘well-targeted; on target’<br>**ketepatan sasaran** — ‘targeting accuracy’<br>**agar tepat sasaran** — ‘so that it is well-targeted’<br>**belum tepat sasaran** — ‘not yet well-targeted’<br>**kurang tepat sasaran** — ‘insufficiently targeted’<br>**tidak tepat sasaran** — ‘mistargeted’<br>**tepat waktu** — ‘on time; punctual’<br>**ketepatan waktu** — ‘punctuality; timeliness’<br>**tepat pada waktunya** — ‘right on time’<br>**tepat guna** — ‘fit for practical use’<br>**teknologi tepat guna** — ‘appropriate technology’<br>**tepat langkah** — ‘to take the right step’<br>**tepat janji** — ‘true to one’s word’ | **tepat sasaran** can also be grouped with accuracy/evaluation.<br>**tepat waktu** is clearly part of the *tepat* family but is mainly about timing.<br>**tepat janji** shifts toward reliability and character rather than exactness. | — |
| 5. Conformity with rules, facts, standards, or expectations | This grouping includes expressions used when one thing is evaluated against another standard: a rule, procedure, fact, document, expectation, or need. In usage, these forms are especially common in formal, bureaucratic, legal, academic, and evaluative settings. | **sesuai** — ‘appropriate; in accordance; suitable; consistent with’<br>**kesesuaian** — ‘conformity; correspondence; consistency; suitability’<br>**sesuai dengan ...** — ‘in accordance with ...’<br>**sesuai aturan / sesuai prosedur / sesuai fakta** — ‘according to the rules / procedure / facts’<br>**tidak sesuai dengan ...** — ‘not in accordance with ...’<br>**kurang sesuai** — ‘not quite appropriate’<br>**sesuai harapan** — ‘as expected’<br>**sesuai kebutuhan** — ‘according to need; as needed’<br>**kesesuaian antara X dan Y** — ‘correspondence between X and Y’<br>**uji kesesuaian** — ‘conformity test; fit test’<br>**menyesuaikan** — ‘to adjust; to adapt; to make conform’<br>**penyesuaian** — ‘adjustment; adaptation’<br>**bersesuaian** — ‘to correspond; to be in agreement’ | **sesuai** overlaps with **tepat** and **cocok**, but in usage it is often more formal and standard-based.<br>**sesuai fakta** comes close to truth/correctness language.<br>**sesuai harapan** is more about matching expectations than matching facts. | — |
| 6. Everyday fit, match, and compatibility | These expressions are used for whether things go together, suit each other, or match in practice. Compared with **sesuai**, this grouping is often more everyday and less tied to formal standards, though it also appears in technical matching contexts. | **cocok** — ‘suitable; fitting; matching; compatible’<br>**kecocokan** — ‘compatibility; suitability; match’<br>**cocok untuk ...** — ‘suitable for ...’<br>**cocok dengan ...** — ‘to match ...; to be compatible with ...’<br>**tidak cocok dengan ...** — ‘not compatible with ...’<br>**kurang cocok** — ‘not quite a good fit’<br>**cocok sekali** — ‘fits perfectly’<br>**kecocokan antara X dan Y** — ‘compatibility between X and Y’<br>**tingkat kecocokan** — ‘degree of match’<br>**mencocokkan** — ‘to match; to compare; to fit together’<br>**pencocokan** — ‘matching; comparison; alignment’ | **cocok** can refer to personal compatibility, practical suitability, or evidential matching.<br>**mencocokkan** overlaps with verification and comparison, not just suitability.<br>Some uses of **cocok** come close to **mirip**, but **cocok** usually implies fit rather than mere resemblance. | — |
| 7. Authenticity, genuineness, and falsification | This grouping covers expressions used to judge whether an object, document, signature, identity, or work is genuine or fake. In usage, these forms are common in legal, administrative, commercial, artistic, and verification contexts. | **asli** — ‘genuine; authentic; original; real’<br>**keaslian** — ‘authenticity; originality; genuineness’<br>**palsu** — ‘fake; false; counterfeit’<br>**asli atau palsu** — ‘genuine or fake’<br>**palsu atau asli** — ‘fake or genuine’<br>**dipastikan asli** — ‘confirmed to be genuine’<br>**masih asli** — ‘still original’<br>**keaslian dokumen / tanda tangan / karya** — ‘the authenticity of a document / signature / work’<br>**meragukan keaslian ...** — ‘to doubt the authenticity of ...’<br>**memeriksa keaslian ...** — ‘to check the authenticity of ...’<br>**memalsukan** — ‘to falsify; to forge; to counterfeit’<br>**pemalsuan** — ‘falsification; forgery; counterfeiting’<br>**diduga palsu** — ‘suspected to be fake’<br>**terbukti palsu** — ‘proven to be fake’ | **asli** also has uses meaning ‘native’ or ‘original’ beyond verification of genuineness.<br>**sejati** sometimes overlaps with authenticity, but is usually more elevated and less practical than **asli**.<br>**tanda tangan sah** may involve both authenticity and legal validity, so it also overlaps with the validity group. | — |
| 8. In usage, this is a strongly formal cluster, especially common in legal, bureaucratic, academic, and administrative language. | **sah** — ‘valid; legitimate; lawful’<br>**absah** — ‘valid; legitimate’<br>**keabsahan** — ‘validity; legitimacy’<br>**valid** — ‘valid’<br>**validitas** — ‘validity’<br>**validasi** — ‘validation’<br>**kevalidan** — ‘validity’<br>**mengesahkan** — ‘to legalize; to ratify; to validate’<br>**pengesahan** — ‘ratification; legalization; formal approval’<br>**mengabsahkan** — ‘to validate; to legitimize’<br>**pengabsahan** — ‘validation; legitimation’<br>**memvalidasi** — ‘to validate’<br>**tidak sah** — ‘invalid; illegitimate’<br>**tidak absah** — ‘invalid’<br>**tidak valid** — ‘invalid’<br>**belum valid** — ‘not yet valid’<br>**sah | 9. Reliability and dependability | This grouping concerns whether a person, system, method, or source can be relied on consistently. In usage, it is related to evaluation and trust, but it does not directly assert that a particular statement is true. | **andal** — ‘reliable; dependable’<br>**keandalan** — ‘reliability; dependability’<br>**mengandalkan** — ‘to rely on; to depend on’<br>**diandalkan** — ‘can be relied on; dependable’<br>**andal dalam ...** — ‘reliable in ...’<br>**cukup andal** — ‘fairly reliable’<br>**kurang andal** — ‘less reliable’<br>**tidak andal** — ‘unreliable’<br>**keandalan sistem / mesin / sumber data** — ‘the reliability of a system / machine / data source’<br>**tingkat keandalan** — ‘level of reliability’<br>**tepat janji** — ‘true to one’s word; keeps promises’ | **tepat janji** fits here in practice, though formally it belongs to the *tepat* family.<br>**andal** can matter in evaluating evidence or sources, but it is about dependability rather than truth itself.<br>**mengandalkan** is about the act of relying, not the property of being true or valid. | — |
| 10. Resemblance and similarity | These expressions are used when one thing resembles another in appearance, form, pattern, or account. In usage, they are relevant in identification and comparison, but they do not by themselves say that something is correct, valid, or genuine. | **mirip** — ‘similar; resembling’<br>**kemiripan** — ‘similarity; resemblance’<br>**mirip dengan ...** — ‘similar to ...; looks like ...’<br>**sangat mirip** — ‘very similar’<br>**tidak mirip** — ‘not similar’<br>**kemiripan antara X dan Y** — ‘the similarity between X and Y’<br>**tingkat kemiripan** — ‘degree of similarity’<br>**memiripkan** — ‘to liken; to make resemble’ | **mirip** may matter in identification, where resemblance can support a judgment, but resemblance is not the same as authenticity or correctness.<br>**cocok** sometimes overlaps when matching records or descriptions, but usually implies fit rather than resemblance. | — |
| 11. Essence, true identity, and elevated “real/genuine” language | This grouping includes expressions used in reflective, philosophical, religious, literary, and cultural discourse for what something or someone fundamentally is. In usage, these forms are less about checking facts and more about speaking of real nature, authentic identity, or deep genuineness. | **hakikat** — ‘essence; true nature; fundamental nature’<br>**hakikat dari ...** — ‘the essence of ...’<br>**pada hakikatnya** — ‘essentially; in essence’<br>**memahami hakikat ...** — ‘to understand the essence of ...’<br>**jati diri** — ‘true identity; essential self’<br>**mencari jati diri** — ‘to search for one’s true identity’<br>**menemukan jati diri** — ‘to find one’s true identity’<br>**kehilangan jati diri** — ‘to lose one’s identity’<br>**menjaga jati diri** — ‘to preserve one’s identity’<br>**sejati** — ‘true; genuine; real’<br>**cinta sejati** — ‘true love’<br>**sahabat sejati** — ‘true friend’<br>**wajah sejati** — ‘true face; real face’<br>**hakiki** — ‘essential; fundamental; true in essence’<br>**kebenaran hakiki** — ‘essential truth; ultimate truth’<br>**kebenaran sejati** — ‘genuine/true truth’ | **sejati** overlaps with authenticity language, but is usually more elevated and less practical than **asli**.<br>**hakikat** and **jati diri** do not fit neatly with ordinary truth/correctness expressions.<br>**kebenaran hakiki** links this group with the broader **kebenaran** family. | — |
| 12. Propriety, social/moral appropriateness, and warranted judgment | These expressions are used to evaluate whether conduct, treatment, wording, or suspicion is proper, fitting, or warranted. In usage, they are common in moral, social, legal, and formal evaluative contexts rather than in straightforward truth-judgment. | **patut** — ‘proper; appropriate; fitting; deserving’<br>**kepatutan** — ‘propriety; appropriateness; reasonableness’<br>**patut untuk ...** — ‘proper/fitting to ...’<br>**patut diduga ...** — ‘reasonably suspected to ...’<br>**patut dicurigai ...** — ‘reasonably suspected of ...’<br>**tidak patut** — ‘improper; inappropriate’<br>**kurang patut** — ‘not quite appropriate’<br>**asas kepatutan** — implied by P2a usage note for **kepatutan** | **patut** overlaps with **tepat** and **sesuai** in some evaluative contexts, but it is more norm-based in usage.<br>**patut diduga** is especially tied to justified suspicion, not established truth.<br>**kepatutan** is formal and does not always connect directly to factual correctness. | — |
