# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

The following documents derivational forms associated with the P1 lexical items. Each entry begins from a P1 base form and records derived forms that are attested or strongly supported in Basque usage. Forms already appearing in P1 or P1b are noted as such rather than re-documented. The inventory does not assume symmetry across roots or completeness of paradigms. Gaps are preserved and noted where relevant.

- **Natural English Translation:** true, truthful, genuine, real

**D1.9**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Derived Form:** egigabea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egi- (truth root) + -gabe (without, privative suffix) + -a (definite article)
- **Natural English Translation:** untruthful, without truth, false
- **Usage:** Privative form meaning lacking truth or truthfulness. Less common than *gezurra* or *faltsua* as a term for falsehood, but attested in written registers, particularly in philosophical and literary contexts. The *-gabe* privative pattern is productive in Basque, but this particular form occupies a narrower niche than the *balio-* privative *baliogabea*.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**D1.10**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Derived Form:** egiari uko egin
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egiari (to truth, dative) + uko egin (to renounce, to deny)
- **Natural English Translation:** to deny the truth, to renounce the truth
- **Usage:** A verbal collocation rather than a morphological derivation, but attested as a fixed expression in moral, political, and religious discourse. Included here because it represents a grammatically productive pattern (dative + *uko egin*) applied specifically to *egia*.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on egia derivational productivity:** The *egia* root is the most morphologically productive root in the P1 set. The primary derivational pathway runs through the *-zta-* verbalizing infix, which generates *egiaztatu* and its further derivatives (*egiaztatzea*, *egiaztapen*, *egiaztagarria*, *egiaztaezina*). A secondary pathway runs through the *-zko* adjectival suffix (*egiazkoa*, *egiazkotasuna*). The instrumental adverbial *egiaz* represents a third, simpler pathway. No attested causative or reciprocal derivations from *egia* have been identified.

## BASE FORM 2: egiazkoa

---

**D2.1**
- **Base Form:** egiazkoa
- **Derived Form:** egiazkotasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egiazko (true/genuine) + -tasun (abstract noun suffix) + -a (definite article)
- **Natural English Translation:** truthfulness, veracity, genuineness
- **Usage:** Abstract nominal derived from *egiazkoa* via the *-tasun* suffix. This is the standard nominalization of the adjectival base. Documented in P1 item 11.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**D2.2**
- **Base Form:** egiazkoa
- **Derived Form:** egiazko (attributive, indefinite)
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egiazko (true/genuine, without definite article)
- **Natural English Translation:** true, genuine (attributive)
- **Usage:** The indefinite attributive form used before nouns, as in *egiazko lekukotasuna* (true testimony). This is the standard pre-nominal form of *egiazkoa* in Basque, where the definite article is dropped in attributive position. Documented in P1b.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on egiazkoa derivational productivity:** *Egiazkoa* participates in the standard Basque adjectival nominalization pattern via *-tasun*. No additional derivations beyond those already documented under *egia* (D1.1–D1.10) have been identified. The form does not appear to generate verbal derivatives independently of the *egia* root.

## BASE FORM 4: gezurra

- **Natural English Translation:** false, lying, based on lies (attributive)
- **Usage:** Adjectival form used attributively before nouns to describe something as false or based on lies, as in *gezurrezko lekukotasuna* (false testimony). Contrasts with *egiazko* in the same attributive position.

- **Usage:** The definite predicative form of the adjectival derivation, used in predicative position. Contrasts directly with *egiazkoa*.

**D4.3**

- **Usage:** A person noun derived from *gezurra* via the *-ti* suffix, which in Basque marks a person habitually characterized by the root quality. Used in everyday moral evaluation to describe someone who lies habitually. Common across all registers.

---

**D4.4**

---

**D4.7**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Derived Form:** gezur esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie, without article) + esan (to say)
- **Natural English Translation:** to tell a lie, to lie
- **Usage:** The standard verbal collocation for the act of lying. Attested across all registers. Not a morphological derivation but a productive verbal collocation that functions as the primary verbal expression of the concept.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on gezurra derivational productivity:** The *gezur-* root generates an adjectival form (*gezurrezko/a*), a person noun (*gezurti/a*), an abstract nominal (*gezurtasuna*), and an adverbial (*gezurretan*). The *-ti* person-noun suffix is notable as it does not appear in the *egia* or *balio* derivational families within this set. No verbal derivative of *gezurra* via *-tu* or *-zta-* has been identified; the verbal function is covered by the collocation *gezur esan*.

The privative *zuzengabea* is attested but marginal relative to the other forms.

---

**D30.1**
- **Base Form:** ustez
- **Derived Form:** uste
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** uste (belief/opinion/supposition; base nominal form)
- **Natural English Translation:** belief, opinion, supposition, assumption
- **Usage:** The base nominal form from which *ustez* is derived. Used in expressions such as *uste izan* (to think, to believe, to suppose) and *nire ustez* (in my opinion). Very common across all
