# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 Core Vocabulary Collection
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Transliteration standard:** Academic/SBL-style transliteration, applied consistently throughout. Shva na rendered as *e*, tsere as *e*, holem as *o*, qibuts/shureq as *u*. Dagesh forte indicated by consonant doubling where phonologically relevant.

---

**1.**
**Form:** אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet
**Literal meaning:** Derived from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n), related to firmness, reliability
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality, genuineness
**Usage:** The most central and widely used Hebrew term for truth, appearing across biblical, rabbinic, legal, and everyday modern contexts. It can describe a true statement, a genuine object, or a reliable person. In rabbinic literature it is also treated as a foundational moral value.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** אמיתי
**Transliteration:** ʾamitī
**Literal meaning:** Adjectival derivation from ʾemet
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** The standard modern Hebrew adjective meaning "true" or "genuine," used attributively and predicatively across registers. It applies to statements, objects, feelings, and identities. Contrasts with מזויף (mezuyaf, "forged/fake").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** נכון
**Transliteration:** nakhon
**Literal meaning:** From root כ-ו-נ (k-w-n), "to be established, set, correct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage:** Extremely common in everyday speech to evaluate statements, answers, and actions as correct or accurate. It is the default word for "correct" in educational and conversational contexts. Also carries a sense of moral rightness in some collocations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** מדויק
**Transliteration:** meduyaq
**Literal meaning:** Pual/Mefuʿal participial form from root ד-י-ק (d-y-q), related to precision
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, measurements, descriptions, and representations for their degree of precision or exactness. Common in scientific, journalistic, and legal registers. Contrasts with מעורפל (meʿurpal, "vague") or משוער (meshuʿar, "approximate").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** שקר
**Transliteration:** sheqer
**Literal meaning:** Root ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r), falsehood, deception
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, untruth
**Usage:** The primary antonym of ʾemet, used across biblical, legal, and everyday contexts to denote a false or deceptive statement. It appears in legal prohibitions (e.g., against false testimony) and in moral discourse. Also used attributively, as in עד שקר (ʿed sheqer, "false witness").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אותנטי
**Transliteration:** ʾotentī
**Literal meaning:** Loanword from Greek/Latin authenticus via European languages
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** A borrowing used in modern Hebrew, especially in cultural, artistic, and philosophical contexts, to evaluate whether something is genuine or original rather than derivative or imitative. It is more restricted in register than ʾamitī and tends to appear in educated or journalistic discourse. Sometimes interchangeable with מקורי (meqorī) in aesthetic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** מקורי
**Transliteration:** meqorī
**Literal meaning:** Derived from מקור (maqor, "source, origin")
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic, genuine
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether something is an original rather than a copy or imitation, in aesthetic, legal (intellectual property), and everyday contexts. Can describe artworks, documents, ideas, or personal style. Overlaps with ʾotentī in some contexts but emphasizes origin rather than genuineness of character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** מזויף
**Transliteration:** mezuyaf
**Literal meaning:** Pual participial form from root ז-י-פ (z-y-f), "to forge, falsify"
**Typical English translations:** forged, fake, counterfeit, falsified
**Usage:** Applied to documents, currency, artworks, and identities that have been deliberately falsified or counterfeited. Common in legal and journalistic contexts. The root also yields the verb זייף (ziyyef, "to forge/falsify") and the noun זיוף (ziyyuf, "forgery").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** כנות
**Transliteration:** kannut
**Literal meaning:** Abstract noun from כן (ken, "sincere, honest") via root כ-נ-נ
**Typical English translations:** sincerity, honesty, genuineness
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being sincere or honest in one's expressions, intentions, or conduct. Used in moral and interpersonal contexts to evaluate whether a person's behavior or statements reflect their true inner state. The adjective כן (ken) and adverb בכנות (be-kannut, "sincerely, honestly") are closely related.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ישר
**Transliteration:** yashar
**Literal meaning:** Root י-ש-ר (y-sh-r), "straight, upright"
**Typical English translations:** upright, honest, straightforward, just
**Usage:** A moral and evaluative term describing a person or conduct as honest, upright, and morally straight. Appears extensively in biblical and rabbinic literature and remains in use in modern Hebrew in moral and legal discourse. The abstract noun יושר (yosher) denotes uprightness or integrity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** יושר
**Transliteration:** yosher
**Literal meaning:** Abstract noun from root י-ש-ר (y-sh-r)
**Typical English translations:** integrity, uprightness, honesty, fairness
**Usage:** Denotes the quality of moral uprightness or integrity in a person or institution. Used in legal contexts (sometimes approximating "equity" or "fairness") and in everyday moral evaluation. Appears in fixed expressions such as מידת הישר (middat ha-yosher, "the standard of fairness/equity").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** צדק
**Transliteration:** tsedeq
**Literal meaning:** Root צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q), "to be right, just"
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, rightness
**Usage:** A foundational term in biblical, rabbinic, and modern Hebrew for justice and moral rightness. It evaluates conduct, judgments, and social arrangements. The related verb צדק (tsadaq) means "to be right" or "to be justified," and the adjective צדיק (tsaddiq) denotes a righteous person.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** הגון
**Transliteration:** hagun
**Literal meaning:** From root ה-ג-נ or related to הגן (hagen, "to protect, be fitting"); etymology debated
**Typical English translations:** decent, proper, fair, respectable
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, conduct, or offers as decent, fair, and appropriate. Common in everyday speech and in legal or commercial contexts (e.g., מחיר הגון, meḥir hagun, "a fair price"). Also used to evaluate moral character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** תקין
**Transliteration:** taqin
**Literal meaning:** From root ת-ק-נ (t-q-n), "to fix, set right, regulate"
**Typical English translations:** proper, in order, functioning correctly, valid
**Usage:** Evaluates whether something is functioning correctly, is in proper order, or conforms to a standard. Used for objects, procedures, and documents. In legal and bureaucratic contexts it can indicate that a document or process is formally valid and correct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** תקף
**Transliteration:** taqef
**Literal meaning:** From root ת-ק-פ (t-q-p), "to be strong, valid, in force"
**Typical English translations:** valid, in force, legally binding
**Usage:** Used primarily in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a document, contract, law, or decision is currently valid and in force. Contrasts with בטל (batel, "null, void"). The noun תוקף (toqef) means "validity" or "force."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** בטל
**Transliteration:** batel
**Literal meaning:** Root ב-ט-ל (b-t-l), "to be null, cease, be annulled"
**Typical English translations:** null, void, invalid, annulled
**Usage:** The standard legal and everyday term for something that is null, void, or without effect. Used in legal discourse (contracts, rulings) and in rabbinic law. The verb לבטל (levatel) means "to annul, cancel, nullify."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** סביר
**Transliteration:** savir
**Literal meaning:** From root ס-ב-ר (s-b-r), "to think, suppose, consider plausible"
**Typical English translations:** reasonable, plausible, credible
**Usage:** Evaluates claims, conduct, or expectations as reasonable or plausible given the circumstances. Widely used in legal contexts (e.g., האדם הסביר, ha-ʾadam ha-savir, "the reasonable person") and in everyday speech. The noun סבירות (sevirut) denotes reasonableness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** מהימן
**Transliteration:** meheymen
**Literal meaning:** Hifʿil participial form from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n), "to trust, be reliable"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, credible, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, sources, witnesses, and information as trustworthy and credible. Common in legal contexts (עד מהימן, ʿed meheymen, "credible witness") and in journalism. The noun מהימנות (meheymanut) denotes reliability or credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** אמין
**Transliteration:** ʾamin
**Literal meaning:** Adjectival form from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable
**Usage:** Closely related to מהימן but somewhat more colloquial and broadly used. Applied to persons, systems, and information to indicate dependability. The noun אמינות (ʾaminut) is the standard modern Hebrew term for "reliability" or "credibility."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** דומה
**Transliteration:** dome
**Literal meaning:** From root ד-מ-י/ד-מ-ה (d-m-y/d-m-h), "to resemble, be like"
**Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, like
**Usage:** Evaluates resemblance or likeness between two entities. Used across all registers. The related noun דמיון (dimyon) means "resemblance" or "similarity," and the verb לדמות (ledamot) means "to resemble" or "to compare."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** זהה
**Transliteration:** zehe
**Literal meaning:** From root ז-ה-ה or related to זה (ze, "this"); denotes identity/sameness
**Typical English translations:** identical, the same
**Usage:** Evaluates whether two things are identical or the same. Used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts. The noun זהות (zahut) means "identity" (both personal identity and logical identity/sameness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** עקבי
**Transliteration:** ʿaqbī
**Literal meaning:** From עקב (ʿaqev, "heel, track, consequence"); adjectival form suggesting following consistently
**Typical English translations:** consistent, coherent
**Usage:** Evaluates whether a person's statements, positions, or conduct are consistent over time or internally coherent. Used in philosophical, journalistic, and everyday contexts. The noun עקביות (ʿaqbiyut) denotes consistency or coherence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** סותר
**Transliteration:** soter
**Literal meaning:** Qal active participle from root ס-ת-ר (s-t-r), "to contradict, demolish, undermine"
**Typical English translations:** contradicting, contradictory, inconsistent with
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether a statement, piece of evidence, or action contradicts another. Common in legal argumentation and philosophical discourse. The noun סתירה (setira) means "contradiction."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** מוטעה
**Transliteration:** mutʿe
**Literal meaning:** Hofʿal participial form from root ט-ע-י/ט-ע-ה (t-ʿ-y/t-ʿ-h), "to err, mislead"
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, erroneous, misled
**Usage:** Evaluates a belief, statement, or person as having been led into error or as being factually mistaken. Used in everyday and academic discourse. Distinct from שקר (sheqer) in that it does not necessarily imply intentional deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** מטעה
**Transliteration:** matʿe
**Literal meaning:** Hifʿil active participle from root ט-ע-י/ט-ע-ה, "to cause to err, mislead"
**Typical English translations:** misleading, deceptive
**Usage:** Evaluates a statement, representation, or action as misleading, whether or not it is technically false. Common in legal contexts (e.g., פרסום מטעה, pirssum matʿe, "misleading advertisement") and in everyday speech. Distinct from שקר in that a misleading statement may be literally true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** פסול
**Transliteration:** pasul
**Literal meaning:** Qal passive participle from root פ-ס-ל (p-s-l), "to disqualify, invalidate, hew away"
**Typical English translations:** disqualified, invalid, unfit, inadmissible
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, evidence, documents, or ritual objects as disqualified or invalid according to a relevant standard. Widely used in rabbinic law (e.g., a witness who is פסול לעדות, pasul le-ʿedut, "disqualified from testimony") and in modern legal and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** כשר
**Transliteration:** kasher
**Literal meaning:** Root כ-ש-ר (k-sh-r), "to be fit, proper, valid"
**Typical English translations:** fit, valid, proper, kosher
**Usage:** Evaluates whether something meets the relevant standard of fitness or validity. Best known in dietary law but used broadly in rabbinic and legal contexts to mean "valid" or "fit" (e.g., a כשר witness, a כשר document). The noun כשרות (kashrut) denotes fitness or validity as well as the dietary system.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** עדות שקר
**Transliteration:** ʿedut sheqer
**Literal meaning:** ʿedut ("testimony") + sheqer ("falsehood") — "false testimony"
**Typical English translations:** false testimony, perjury
**Usage:** A fixed legal and biblical expression denoting testimony that is false or fabricated. Appears in biblical prohibitions and in modern legal discourse as the equivalent of perjury. Functions as a conventionalized compound rather than a free phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** חזקה
**Transliteration:** ḥazaqah
**Literal meaning:** From root ח-ז-ק (ḥ-z-q), "strong, established"; denotes a presumption
**Typical English translations:** legal presumption, established assumption
**Usage:** A technical term in rabbinic and Jewish law denoting a presumption that something continues in its established state unless proven otherwise. Used to evaluate the status of persons, objects, or situations in the absence of contrary evidence. Functionally analogous to legal presumptions in other systems.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — domain-specific to rabbinic/Jewish legal discourse, though known to educated Hebrew speakers

---

**30.**
**Form:** בדוי
**Transliteration:** baduy
**Literal meaning:** Qal passive participle from root ב-ד-י/ב-ד-א (b-d-y/b-d-ʾ), "to fabricate, invent"
**Typical English translations:** fabricated, invented, fictitious, made up
**Usage:** Evaluates a claim, story, or identity as having been fabricated or invented rather than reflecting reality. Used in everyday and journalistic contexts. Somewhat more colloquial than מזויף and does not necessarily imply criminal intent.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
