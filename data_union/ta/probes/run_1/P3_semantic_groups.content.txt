# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Methodological note:** Groupings below are derived solely from patterns of usage, contrast, and co-occurrence documented in P1 and P1b/P2a/P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Group boundaries are drawn where the data supports them; where usage patterns are ambiguous or overlapping, this is stated explicitly. The number and size of groups reflect the data, not a desire for symmetry.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality

**Label:** Asserting that something is the case / truth-as-correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where a speaker asserts, affirms, or evaluates whether a claim, statement, or situation matches what is actually the case. They appear in predicative constructions (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*, *atu uṇmaiyā?*), in adverbial frames that contrast appearance with reality (*uṇmaiyil*, *uṇmaiyilēyē*), and in fixed evaluative phrases used to characterize statements as factually accurate or inaccurate. The core contrast operative in this group is between what is claimed or appears to be the case and what actually is the case.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை / *uṇmai* — the primary nominal; used predicatively (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*) and in embedded questions (*uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai*)
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — attributive adjective applied to propositions and states of affairs (*uṇmaiyāna taka'l*, true information)
- உண்மையில் / *uṇmaiyil* — adverbial frame contrasting appearance with reality
- உண்மையிலேயே / *uṇmaiyilēyē* — emphatic variant of the above
- மெய் / *mey* — classical/literary counterpart; used in philosophical and poetic assertion of truth (*meyyē colkiṟēṉ*, I am truly telling you)
- மெய்ம்மை / *meymmai* — formal nominal variant of *mey* in the same assertive function
- மெய்யான / *meyyāna* — attributive adjective parallel to *uṇmaiyāna* in literary registers
- உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāna* — the negative pole; used in legal and formal contexts to assert that a statement does not correspond to reality
- பொய் / *poy* — the primary negative counterpart; used predicatively (*atu poytāṉ*) to assert that a claim does not correspond to reality
- பொய்மை / *poymai* — abstract nominal form of *poy* in the same assertive function
- சரி / *cari* — used predicatively (*atu caritāṉ*) to assert correctness or accuracy; overlaps with this group in its epistemic use
- தவறு / *tavaṟu* — used predicatively to assert that a claim or calculation is incorrect; overlaps with this group

**Notes:** *uṇmai* and *mey* are near-synonyms in this function but differ in register: *uṇmai* is the default across all registers; *mey* is confined to literary, philosophical, and formal contexts. *cari* and *tavaṟu* overlap with this group when used to evaluate the accuracy of statements, but they also participate in other groups (see Groups 3 and 5).

## Group 2: Expressions used to characterize objects, persons, or documents as genuine versus not genuine

**Label:** Evaluating genuineness of things and persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a physical object, document, credential, or person is what it claims to be — an original rather than a copy, a real professional rather than an impostor, a genuine article rather than a counterfeit. The operative contrast is between *acal* (original) and *nakal* (copy) or *pōli* (fake), and the evaluation is applied to things and persons rather than to propositions. This group is especially prominent in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- அசல் / *acal* — the primary term for the genuine original; used in *acal āvaṇam* (original document), *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?)
- நகல் / *nakal* — the neutral term for a copy or duplicate; contrasts with *acal* in administrative and legal contexts
- போலி / *pōli* — the strongly negative term for a fake or counterfeit; applied to objects (*pōli poruḷ*, fake goods), documents (*pōli āvaṇam*, forged document), and persons (*pōli ṭākṭar*, fake doctor)
- போலியான / *pōliyāna* — adjectival form of *pōli*; used attributively in the same contexts
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — also used in this group when applied to objects and persons (*uṇmaiyāna poruḷ*, genuine goods; *uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ*, a true friend); overlaps with Group 1
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmait-taṉmai* — used in formal contexts to refer to the property of being genuine (*ataṉ uṇmait-taṉmai cantēkattirku uriyatu*, its authenticity is open to doubt)
- மெய்யான / *meyyāna* — used in literary contexts to characterize persons or things as genuinely what they are; overlaps with Group 1

**Notes:** The *acal/nakal/pōli* triad is the core of this group and is largely confined to it. *uṇmaiyāna* and *meyyāna* participate in both this group and Group 1, depending on whether they are applied to things/persons (genuineness) or to propositions (truth). *nakal* is neutral in connotation (a certified copy of a document is a *nakal* without negative implication), while *pōli* is always negative.

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate a person's character as honest or deceptive

**Label:** Evaluating a person's honesty or deceptiveness

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate persons — their character, conduct, or habitual behavior — as honest, straightforward, or deceptive. They appear in character evaluations (*avar nērmaiyāna āḷ*, he/she is an honest person), moral instruction (*nērmaiyāka naṭa*, behave honestly), and condemnation of deceptive conduct (*vañcaṉai cey*, commit deception). The focus is on the person as an agent rather than on a specific statement or object.

**Representative expressions:**

- நேர்மை / *nērmai* — the primary term for honesty as a character trait; used in *nērmaiyāna āḷ* (an honest person), *nērmaiyāka naṭa* (behave honestly), *nērmaiyē ciṟanta koḷkai* (honesty is the best policy)
- நேர்மையான / *nērmaiyāna* — adjectival form; applied to persons and conduct
- நேர்மையாக / *nērmaiyāka* — adverbial form; used to describe manner of conduct
- நேர்மையில்லாத / *nērmaiyillāta* — negative form; applied to persons or conduct lacking honesty
- நேர் / *nēr* — the base root; used in compounds and attributive phrases to indicate directness and straightforwardness
- வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai* — the primary term for deception as a character-based or intentional act; used in *vañcaṉai cey* (commit deception), *vañcaṉaiyillāta maṉacu* (a guileless heart)
- வஞ்சனையான / *vañcaṉaiyāka* — adverbial form; used to describe deceptive conduct (*vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa*, behave deceitfully)
- உண்மையான மனிதன் / *uṇmaiyāna maṉitaṉ* — a genuine person, a person of integrity; overlaps with Group 2 (genuineness of persons) and this group

**Notes:** *நேர்மை* and *வஞ்சனை* are the clearest members of this group, as they are used almost exclusively in character evaluation of persons. *உண்மையான மனிதன்* sits at the intersection of Groups 2 and 3: it evaluates a person as genuine (Group 2) and as honest/of integrity (Group 3). *பொய்* also participates in this group when applied to persons (*poy āḷ*, a lying person), but its primary membership is in Group 1.

## Group 4: Expressions used in contexts of establishing, proving, or evidencing a claim

**Label:** Establishing and evidencing claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts — primarily legal, journalistic, and formal argumentative — where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it can be demonstrated, supported, or established on the basis of evidence or proof. They appear in constructions involving the production of evidence, the act of proving, and the evaluation of whether a claim is well-supported. The operative concern is the relationship between a claim and its evidentiary basis.

**Representative expressions:**

- ஆதாரம் / *ātāram* — the primary term for evidence or basis; used in *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal etuvum colla vēṇṭām* (don't say anything without reliable evidence), *ātāramāna* (well-founded), *ātāramaṟṟa* (baseless)
- ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna* — adjectival form; applied to claims that are evidentially supported
- ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa* — privative form; applied to claims that lack evidentiary support
- சாட்சி / *cāṭci* — witness or testimony; used in legal contexts as the basis for establishing truth (*poy cāṭci col*, give false testimony)
- சாட்சியம் / *cāṭciyam* — the body of testimony or evidence; used in legal contexts
- மெய்ப்பிக்க / *meyppiкka* — to prove, to establish the truth of a claim; the only morphologically derived verbal form in the truth domain
- மெய்ப்பித்தல் / *meyppittal* — the verbal noun form; proving, verification
- உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டுதல் / *uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭutal* — establishing the truth definitively; used in legal and formal argumentative contexts
- உண்மைச் சான்று / *uṇmaic cāṉṟu* — proof of truth, attestation; used in legal and administrative contexts
- உண்மைத்தன்மையை நிரூபி / *uṇmait-taṉmaiyai nirūpi* — prove the authenticity; used in formal legal contexts
- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* — reliable, credible; used to evaluate sources and evidence (*nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu*, from reliable sources)
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worth trusting, credible; used to evaluate whether testimony or a source is reliable
- நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta* — not credible, not worth trusting; the negative counterpart

**Notes:** This group is defined by the evidential and demonstrative context rather than by a single root. *ஆதாரம்* and *சாட்சி* are the clearest members. *மெய்ப்பிக்க* is the only verbal form in the entire inventory that directly encodes the act of proving, making it a distinctive member of this group. *நம்பகமான* and *நம்பத்தக்க* overlap with Group 5 (trust-based evaluation), but their use in journalistic and legal source evaluation places them here as well.

---

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person or source is worthy of trust

**Label:** Evaluating trustworthiness and credibility of persons and sources

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be trusted or relied upon. They appear in contexts of interpersonal trust, institutional confidence, and journalistic source evaluation. The operative question is not whether a specific claim is true but whether the agent making the claim or the source providing information is reliable. This group is anchored in the root *nampu* (to trust).

**Representative expressions:**

- நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* — the primary nominal; used across interpersonal (*nampikkai vai*, place trust), institutional (*nampikkai vākkeṭuppu*, vote of confidence), and religious (faith) contexts
- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* — reliable, trustworthy; applied to persons, sources, and institutions
- நம்பகம் / *nampakam* — the abstract quality of trustworthiness
- நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiāna* — trustworthy, faithful; applied to persons
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worth trusting, credible
- நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta* — not worth trusting, untrustworthy
- நம்பிக்கையின்மை / *nampikkaiinmai* — lack of trust, distrust
- நம்பிக்கை துரோகம் / *nampikkai turōkam* — betrayal of trust; used in legal and moral contexts
- நம்பிக்கை மோசடி / *nampikkai mōcaṭi* — breach of trust, confidence fraud; used in legal contexts
- நம்பிக்கை மனிதன் / *nampikkai maṉitaṉ* — a trustworthy person
- நம்பிக்கையாளர் / *nampikkaiāḷar* — believers, the faithful; overlaps with religious use

**Notes:** *நம்பிக்கை* is the most internally differentiated item in the inventory, participating in interpersonal trust, institutional confidence, and religious faith. The religious use (*nampikkaiāḷar*, believers; *nampikkai aṟikkai*, creed) is noted in P1b as a peripheral extension where the root shifts from evidential trust to faith-based commitment. This overlap is real and is not resolved here. *நம்பகமான* and *நம்பத்தக்க* also participate in Group 4 (evidencing claims) when applied to sources and testimony.

---

## Group 6: Expressions used in legal and administrative evaluation of validity and entitlement

**Label:** Legal and administrative validity

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in legal, administrative, and institutional contexts to evaluate whether a person, document, action, or claim meets a formal standard — whether it is legally valid, properly qualified, or duly authorized. They appear in constructions involving eligibility, legal procedure, and the formal status of documents and claims. The operative concern is conformity to a formal standard or rule rather than correspondence to reality or personal honesty.

**Representative expressions:**

- தகுதி / *takuti* — qualification, eligibility; used in *takuti illai* (lacks qualification), *takuti nirūpi* (prove your eligibility)
- தகுதியான / *takutiyāna* — qualified, eligible
- தகுதியற்ற / *takutiyaṟṟa* — unqualified, ineligible
- சட்டப்படி / *caṭṭappaṭi* — according to law, legally; used in *caṭṭappaṭi naṭavaṭikkai eṭu* (take legal action), *caṭṭappaṭi avarukku urimai irukkiṟatu* (legally he/she has the right)
- நியாயம் / *niyāyam* — justice, fairness, rightness; used in *niyāyam kēḷ* (seek justice), *niyāyam kiṭaikkum* (justice will be obtained), *itu niyāyamā?* (is this fair?)
- நியாயமான / *niyāyamāna* — just, fair, reasonable
- அநியாயம் / *aniyāyam* — injustice, unfairness; used in *itu aniyāyam* (this is unjust)
- அசல் / *acal* — original document; used in administrative requirements (*acal āvaṇam koṇṭu vā*, bring the original document); overlaps with Group 2
- உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāna* — contrary to fact; used in legal characterization of false statements; overlaps with Group 1
- வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai* — fraud, deception; used in legal condemnation; overlaps with Group 3
- போலி / *pōli* — fake, counterfeit; used in legal condemnation of fraud; overlaps with Group 2

**Notes:** This group is defined by context of use (legal and administrative) rather than by a single semantic property. *நியாயம்* and *தகுதி* are the clearest members, as their primary usage is in formal evaluation of fairness and eligibility. *சட்டப்படி* is a fixed adverbial expression that anchors legal validity. Several items from other groups (*அசல்*, *போலி*, *வஞ்சனை*, *உண்மைக்கு மாறான*) also appear prominently in legal contexts and thus overlap with this group.

## Group 7: Expressions used in narrative and journalistic contexts to describe the revelation of hidden truth

**Label:** Truth coming to light / revelation of concealed facts

**Definition:** These expressions are used in narrative, journalistic, and everyday speech to describe the process by which something that was hidden, unknown, or concealed becomes known or public. They appear in intransitive constructions where truth is treated as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility, and in constructions describing the acts of hiding or revealing. This group is defined by a specific narrative pattern rather than by a single root.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது / *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu* — the truth came out, the truth emerged
- உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது / *uṇmai teriya vantatu* — the truth became known, the truth came to light
- உண்மை வெளிப்படுதல் / *uṇmai veḷippaṭutal* — the coming to light of truth, revelation of truth
- உண்மை தெரிய வருதல் / *uṇmai teriya varutal* — the truth becoming known (verbal noun)
- உண்மையை மறைத்தல் / *uṇmaiyai maṟaittal* — concealment of truth, suppression of facts
- உண்மையை மறை / *uṇmaiyai maṟai* — hide the truth, conceal the truth
- உண்மை தெரியும் வரை காத்திரு / *uṇmai teriyum varai kāttiru* — wait until the truth is known
- பொய் ஒரு நாள் வெளியே வரும் / *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum* — a lie will come out one day
- உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை / *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai* — it is not known what the truth is

**Notes:** This group is defined by a shared narrative structure — truth as something that can be hidden, revealed, or awaited — rather than by a shared root or morphological pattern. The intransitive constructions (*uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu*, *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum*) are particularly characteristic. *உண்மையை மறைத்தல்* represents the concealment pole of this pattern. This group overlaps with Group 1 (truth-as-correspondence) in that the same root *uṇmai* is involved, but the usage pattern here is specifically narrative and processual rather than predicative.

## Group 8: Expressions used in moral instruction and proverbial speech

**Label:** Moral instruction and proverbial assertion

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts of moral teaching, proverbial speech, and normative instruction — enjoining honest conduct, condemning lying, or asserting general moral truths about honesty and deception. They are characterized by their use in imperative, negative imperative, and proverbial constructions, and by their appearance in didactic and literary contexts. Several of these expressions function as fixed moral formulas.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை சொல் / *uṇmai col* — tell the truth (imperative)
- பொய் சொல்லாதே / *poy collāte* — don't lie (negative imperative)
- நேர்மையாக இரு / *nērmaiyāka iru* — be honest (imperative)
- நேர்மையே சிறந்த கொள்கை / *nērmaiyē ciṟanta koḷkai* — honesty is the best policy (proverbial)
- உண்மை கசக்கும் / *uṇmai kacakkum* — the truth is bitter (proverbial)
- உண்மை எப்போதும் வெல்லும் / *uṇmai eppōtum vellum* — truth always prevails (proverbial)
- பொய் ஒரு நாள் வெளியே வரும் / *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum* — a lie will come out one day (proverbial)
- பொய் சொல்வது தவறு / *poy coluvatu tavaṟu* — telling lies is wrong (moral assertion)
- உண்மை என்றாலும் சொல்ல வேண்டாம் / *uṇmai eṉṟālum colla vēṇṭām* — even if true, need not be said (pragmatic moral instruction)
- வஞ்சனையில்லாத மனசு / *vañcaṉaiyillāta maṉacu* — a guileless heart (literary moral ideal)

**Notes:** This group is defined by register and function rather than by a single root. The proverbial expressions (*uṇmai kacakkum*, *uṇmai eppōtum vellum*, *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum*) are the most distinctively characteristic members. Several items here also appear in other groups: *uṇmai col* is in Group 1, *poy collāte* is in Group 1, *nērmaiyāka iru* is in Group 3. The overlap is real; what distinguishes this group is the imperative, proverbial, or didactic framing rather than the lexical content alone.

## Group 9: Expressions used to evaluate fit, appropriateness, or conformity to a standard

**Label:** Evaluating fit and conformity to a standard

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something — a person, action, claim, or object — conforms to or deviates from a relevant standard, norm, or definition. They appear in contexts of assessment, qualification, and evaluation of correctness or appropriateness. The operative concern is not truth in the correspondence sense but fit or conformity.

**Representative expressions:**

- சரி / *cari* — correct, right, okay; used to evaluate statements, actions, and situations as conforming to a standard (*atu caritāṉ*, that is indeed correct; *cariyillai*, something is wrong)
- சரியான / *cariyāna* — correct, exact, proper (*cariyāna nēratil*, at exactly the right time)
- தவறு / *tavaṟu* — mistake, error, wrong; used to evaluate deviation from a standard (*tavaṟu cey*, make a mistake; *tavaṟu tiruttu*, correct a mistake)
- தவறான / *tavaṟāna* — wrong, erroneous, incorrect
- பொருத்தம் / *poruttam* — appropriateness, fit, correspondence; used to evaluate whether something is fitting or appropriate
- பொருத்தமான / *poruttamāna* — appropriate, fitting, suitable
- பொருத்தமற்ற / *poruttamaṟṟa* — inappropriate, unsuitable, ill-fitting
- தகுதி / *takuti* — qualification, fitness; used to evaluate whether a person meets a required standard; overlaps with Group 6
- இலக்கணம் / *ilakkaṇam* — defining characteristic, proper form; used to assess whether something conforms to its proper definition or standard
- இயல்பு / *iyalpu* — natural state, inherent nature; used to evaluate whether something conforms to or deviates from its true nature
- தன்மை / *taṉmai* — inherent quality, nature; used in evaluative contexts about what something fundamentally is

**Notes:** *சரி* and *தவறு* are the most common members of this group in everyday speech. *பொருத்தம்* is more specific to aesthetic and logical evaluation of fit. *இலக்கணம்* and *இயல்பு* are more philosophical in register, used when the question is whether something conforms to its essential definition or nature. *தகுதி* overlaps significantly with Group 6 (legal and administrative validity). *சரி* also functions as a discourse particle (Group 8 overlap) and as a predicative evaluator of truth (Group 1 overlap), making it one of the most cross-group items in the inventory.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items present genuine ambiguity or cross-group membership that cannot be resolved by the available data:

---

**உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna***

Participates in Group 1 (true propositions: *uṇmaiyāna taka'l*, true information), Group 2 (genuine objects and persons: *uṇmaiyāna poruḷ*, genuine goods), and Group 3 (persons of integrity: *uṇmaiyāna maṉitaṉ*, a genuine person). The adjectival suffix *-āna* applies uniformly across all three uses; the grouping depends entirely on the noun being modified. No single group membership is primary.

---

**சரி / *cari***

Participates in Group 1 (predicative truth evaluation: *atu caritāṉ*), Group 8 (discourse particle: *cari, pōkalām*), and Group 9 (correctness and fit: *cariyāna nēratil*). Its use as a discourse particle is structurally distinct from its evaluative uses, but all three uses are attested and common. The item does not fit cleanly into any single group.

---

**நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* and நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka***

Both participate in Group 5 (trustworthiness of persons and sources) and Group 4 (evidencing claims, evaluation of sources). In journalistic contexts (*nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu*, from reliable sources), the concern is evidential reliability; in interpersonal contexts, the concern is personal trustworthiness. The two uses are not always distinguishable.

---

**நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* (religious use)**

The use of *nampikkai* in religious contexts (*nampikkaiāḷar*, believers; *nampikkai aṟikkai*, creed; *nampikkai cōtaṉai*, test of faith) represents a usage pattern that does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. In these contexts, the term refers to religious faith or commitment rather than to trust based on reliability or evidence. This usage is noted in P1b as a peripheral extension. It is left ungrouped here, as the available data does not support identifying a distinct group for religious faith expressions beyond noting that *nampikkai* participates in this use.

---

**கற்பனை / *kaṟpaṉai***

Used in Group 1-adjacent contexts (contrasting fiction with fact: *itu veṟum kaṟpaṉai*, this is mere fabrication) and in neutral literary contexts (fictional narrative: *kaṟpaṉai katai*, fictional story). The negative use (fabrication, dismissal of a claim as invented) places it near Group 1; the neutral use (creative fiction) does not fit any group. The expression *kaṟpaṉaikkum eṭṭāta uṇmai* (a truth beyond imagination) places it in a relationship with Group 7 (revelation of hidden truth). It is listed as ambiguous.

---

**மெய் / *mey* (body sense)**

The uses of *mey* in its body/physical sense (*meypāṭu*, bodily expression of emotion; *mey cilirttal*, the body thrilling; *meykāppu*, bodyguard) do not participate in any of the groups above. These are documented in P1b as a distinct semantic stream of the same root. They are left ungrouped.

---

**தன்மை / *taṉmai* and இயல்பு / *iyalpu***

Both are used in philosophical and evaluative contexts to refer to the inherent nature or essential quality of something. They participate in Group 9 (conformity to a standard) when used to evaluate whether something conforms to its nature, and in Group 2 (genuineness) when used to evaluate whether something is authentically what it is. Neither fits exclusively into either group, and their usage is sufficiently abstract that they resist clear group assignment.

---

## Items Without Clear Group Membership

The following items from P1 do not fit clearly into any of the groups identified above, based on the available usage data:

- **மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் / *meyññāṉam*** — true knowledge, genuine wisdom; used in Shaiva Siddhanta and Vedantic philosophical discourse. Its usage is too specialized and register-specific to assign to a group based on the available data.
- **மெய்யியல் / *meyyiyal*** — philosophy, metaphysics; a terminological item used in academic Tamil. Its primary function is as a disciplinary label rather than an evaluative expression.
- **மெய்ப்பாடு / *meypāṭu*** — bodily expression of emotion; a technical term in classical Tamil poetics. Does not participate in any of the truth-domain groups.
- **நம்பல் / *nampal*** — the act of trusting; a verbal noun form documented as marginal in P1b. Insufficient usage data to assign to a group.
