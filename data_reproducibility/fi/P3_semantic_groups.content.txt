# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

## Methodology Note

This analysis identifies natural groupings based solely on observed usage patterns in Finnish, as documented in P1, P1b, and P2. Groupings reflect how terms are actually used and contrasted in practice, not imposed theoretical categories. Some items appear in multiple groups; some items remain ungrouped.

---

## GROUP 1: FACTUAL CORRESPONDENCE TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, information, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs. These expressions appear in contexts where the primary concern is matching claims to reality, particularly in reporting, verification, and correction contexts. They are commonly used with *tieto* "information," *väite* "claim," and *kertomus* "account."

**Representative expressions:**
- *tosi* "true"
- *totuus* "truth"
- *tosiasia* "fact"
- *tosiasiallinen* "actual, factual"
- *todellinen* "real, actual"
- *paikkansapitävä* "accurate, correct"
- *todenmukainen* "truthful, veracious"
- *todenperäinen* "truthful, veracious"
- *pitää paikkansa* "to be true, to hold"
- *vastata totuutta* "to correspond to truth"
- *ei pidä paikkaansa* "is not true"
- *ei ole totta* "is not true"
- *paikkansapitävä tieto* "accurate information"
- *tosiasioihin perustuva* "based on facts"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Verification and fact-checking
- News reporting and journalism
- Administrative documentation
- Distinguishing actual from hypothetical situations

---

## GROUP 2: CORRECTNESS EVALUATION TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether answers, procedures, methods, or choices are correct or proper according to standards, norms, or expectations. These expressions commonly appear in educational, instructional, and evaluative contexts, often with *vastaus* "answer," *tapa* "way/method," and *ratkaisu* "solution."

**Representative expressions:**
- *oikea* "right, correct"
- *oikeellinen* "correct, accurate"
- *oikein* "correctly, right"
- *väärä* "wrong, incorrect"
- *väärin* "wrongly, incorrectly"
- *virheellinen* "erroneous, faulty"
- *olla oikeassa* "to be right"
- *olla väärässä* "to be wrong"
- *oikea vastaus* "correct answer"
- *oikealla tavalla* "the right way"
- *väärä käsitys* "wrong impression"
- *virheellinen tieto* "erroneous information"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Educational assessment
- Instructions and procedures
- Problem-solving
- Evaluating understanding or interpretation

**Note:** *Oikea* and *väärä* also appear in Group 1 contexts, but with distinct usage patterns (see Ambiguous Items below).

---

## GROUP 3: AUTHENTICITY AND GENUINENESS TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish genuine, authentic, or real things from imitations, fakes, or pretense. These expressions commonly appear with nouns denoting objects (*nahka* "leather," *taide* "art"), persons (*ystävä* "friend"), and emotions (*rakkaus* "love"), emphasizing the contrast between authentic and counterfeit.

**Representative expressions:**
- *aito* "genuine, authentic"
- *aitous* "genuineness, authenticity"
- *aidosti* "genuinely"
- *todellinen* "real, actual" (in some contexts)
- *oikea* "real, genuine" (in some contexts)
- *oikea nimi* "real name"
- *tosimies* "real man"
- *tosiystävä* "true friend"
- *tosirakkauden* "true love"
- *aito nahka* "genuine leather"
- *aito ystävä* "true friend"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Product authentication
- Character evaluation
- Distinguishing sincere from insincere
- Evaluating relationships

---

## GROUP 4: DECEPTION AND FALSIFICATION TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for deliberate acts of creating false representations, lying, or deceiving. These expressions emphasize intentional misrepresentation and commonly appear in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts. They contrast with unintentional error terms.

**Representative expressions:**
- *valhe* "lie, falsehood"
- *valehdella* "to lie"
- *valehtelija* "liar"
- *valheellinen* "false, mendacious"
- *väärentää* "to forge, to counterfeit"
- *väärennös* "forgery, counterfeit"
- *väärennösrikos* "forgery (crime)"
- *petollinen* "deceptive, deceitful"
- *petollisuus* "deceitfulness"
- *vääristää* "to distort, to falsify"
- *vääristely* "distortion, falsification"
- *harhaanjohtava* "misleading, deceptive"
- *puhua valhetta* "tell a lie"
- *elää valheessa* "live a lie"
- *valkoinen valhe* "white lie"
- *vääristää totuutta* "distort the truth"
- *harhaanjohtava mainonta* "misleading advertising"
- *väärä todistus* "false testimony"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Moral evaluation
- Legal proceedings
- Interpersonal trust
- Media criticism

## GROUP 5: HONESTY AND SINCERITY TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to characterize persons, statements, or intentions as honest, truthful, or sincere. These expressions emphasize moral character and genuine intent, commonly appearing in character evaluations and personal communication. They form the positive counterpart to Group 4.

**Representative expressions:**
- *rehellinen* "honest, truthful"
- *rehellisyys* "honesty"
- *rehellisesti* "honestly"
- *vilpitön* "sincere, genuine"
- *vilpittömyys* "sincerity"
- *vilpittömästi* "sincerely"
- *rehellisesti sanottuna* "honestly speaking"
- *olla rehellinen* "to be honest"
- *vilpitön halu* "sincere desire"
- *vilpittömästi pahoillaan* "sincerely sorry"
- *aito ja rehellinen* "genuine and honest"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Character evaluation
- Personal communication
- Moral discourse
- Apologies and admissions

## GROUP 6: EVIDENTIAL AND PROOF TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used in contexts of establishing truth through evidence, demonstration, or testimony. These expressions commonly appear in legal, scientific, and argumentative contexts where claims must be supported or verified through evidence.

**Representative expressions:**
- *todistaa* "to prove, to testify"
- *todiste* "proof, evidence"
- *todistaja* "witness"
- *todistus* "certificate, testimony"
- *todistusaineisto* "body of evidence"
- *todistustaakka* "burden of proof"
- *todistuskappale* "piece of evidence"
- *todistettava* "provable"
- *todistettavasti* "provably, demonstrably"
- *todentaa* "to verify, to authenticate"
- *todentaminen* "verification"
- *esittää todisteita* "present evidence"
- *kiistaton todiste* "indisputable evidence"
- *todistaa syyttömyytensä* "prove one's innocence"
- *todistaa vääräksi* "prove false"
- *todistaa oikeaksi* "prove correct"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Legal proceedings
- Scientific demonstration
- Argumentation
- Administrative verification

## GROUP 7: RELIABILITY AND CREDIBILITY TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether sources, persons, or information can be trusted or believed. These expressions focus on the trustworthiness of information sources and the believability of claims, commonly appearing in contexts of information evaluation and source assessment.

**Representative expressions:**
- *luotettava* "reliable, trustworthy"
- *luotettavuus* "reliability"
- *uskottava* "believable, credible"
- *uskottavuus* "credibility"
- *luotettava lähde* "reliable source"
- *luotettava tieto* "reliable information"
- *uskottava selitys* "credible explanation"
- *ei kuulosta uskottavalta* "doesn't sound credible"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Source evaluation
- Information assessment
- Testimony evaluation
- Media literacy

---

## GROUP 8: PROBABILITY AND LIKELIHOOD TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to express degrees of likelihood or probability that something is true or will occur. These expressions appear in predictive and epistemic contexts where certainty is qualified, commonly with complement clauses.

**Representative expressions:**
- *todennäköinen* "probable, likely"
- *todennäköisyys* "probability, likelihood"
- *todennäköisesti* "probably, likely"
- *on todennäköistä että* "it is probable that"
- *hyvin todennäköinen* "very probable"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Predictions
- Risk assessment
- Epistemic qualification
- Scientific probability

---

## GROUP 9: CLAIM AND ASSERTION TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for making claims or assertions without necessarily committing to their truth. These expressions are neutral regarding truth value and commonly appear in contexts of reported speech, debate, and argumentation.

**Representative expressions:**
- *väittää* "to claim, to assert"
- *väite* "claim, assertion"
- *väittely* "debate, argumentation"
- *väittelijä* "debater"
- *väitös* "dissertation defense"
- *väittää olevansa* "claim to be"
- *väittää että* "claim that"
- *väittää vastaan* "argue against"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Reported speech
- Debate and argumentation
- Academic discourse
- Neutral claim reporting

## GROUP 10: REALIZATION AND ACTUALIZATION TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for processes of making something real or actual, or for things becoming real. These expressions focus on the transition from potential, planned, or hoped-for states to actual existence or occurrence.

**Representative expressions:**
- *todellistaa* "to realize, to actualize"
- *todellistua* "to come true, to be realized"
- *toteutua* "to come true, to be realized"
- *toteuttaa* "to realize, to implement"
- *toteutus* "realization, implementation"
- *toteuttaminen* "realization, implementation"
- *todentua* "to be verified, to be confirmed"
- *todellisuus* "reality"
- *suunnitelman toteuttaminen* "implementation of plan"
- *unelman todellistua* "dream coming true"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Project management
- Goal achievement
- Plans and predictions
- Hopes and aspirations

---

## GROUP 11: OBSERVATION AND STATEMENT TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for stating, noting, or observing facts in a relatively neutral manner. These expressions commonly appear in formal discourse where facts are being established or recorded without strong epistemic commitment.

**Representative expressions:**
- *todeta* "to state, to note, to observe"
- *toteamus* "statement, observation"
- *on todettava että* "it must be noted that"
- *voidaan todeta* "it can be stated"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Formal reporting
- Academic writing
- Official documentation
- Neutral fact-stating

---

## GROUP 12: DISTORTION AND MISREPRESENTATION TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for processes of making something false, inaccurate, or misleading, whether through distortion of existing truth or creation of false representations. These expressions emphasize the transformation or corruption of truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- *vääristää* "to distort, to falsify"
- *vääristyä* "to become distorted"
- *vääristymä* "distortion, deformation"
- *vääristely* "distortion, falsification"
- *väärentää* "to forge, to counterfeit"
- *väärennös* "forgery, counterfeit"
- *harhaanjohtava* "misleading"
- *vääristää totuutta* "distort truth"
- *vääristää tosiasioita* "distort facts"
- *harhaanjohtava tieto* "misleading information"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Media criticism
- Accusation of misrepresentation
- Forensic analysis
- Critical discourse

**Note:** Overlaps with Group 4 but emphasizes transformation/corruption rather than creation of falsehood.

---

## GROUP 13: ERROR AND MISTAKE TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for unintentional incorrectness or mistakes, as opposed to deliberate deception. These expressions commonly appear in contexts of correction and clarification where the focus is on error rather than moral failing.

**Representative expressions:**
- *virheellinen* "erroneous, faulty"
- *virheellisuus* "erroneousness"
- *väärinkäsitys* "misunderstanding, misconception"
- *väärä käsitys* "wrong impression"
- *virheellinen tieto* "erroneous information"
- *virheellinen käsitys* "erroneous understanding"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Corrections
- Clarifications
- Educational feedback
- Technical documentation

**Note:** Contrasts with Group 4 (deliberate deception) and Group 2 (correctness evaluation).

---

## GROUP 14: MISUSE AND ABUSE TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used for improper or harmful use of something, particularly in contexts of power, trust, or resources. These expressions emphasize wrongful application rather than falsity per se.

**Representative expressions:**
- *väärinkäyttö* "misuse, abuse"
- *väärinkäyttää* "to misuse, to abuse"
- *vallan väärinkäyttö* "abuse of power"
- *väärinkäyttää luottamusta* "abuse trust"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Legal discourse
- Ethical evaluation
- Power relations
- Trust violations

**Note:** Peripheral to core truth-domain; included due to *väärä* root but semantically distinct.

---

## GROUP 15: REAL-LIFE AND ACTUALITY TERMS

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish actual lived experience or real-world situations from fiction, simulation, theory, or hypothetical scenarios. These expressions emphasize the contrast between reality and various forms of non-reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tosielämä* "real life"
- *tositarina* "true story"
- *tosi-tv* "reality TV"
- *tosipohjainen* "based on facts, fact-based"
- *tosielämän sankari* "real-life hero"
- *tosielämässä* "in real life"
- *todellisuudessa* "in reality"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Media and entertainment
- Contrasting reality with fiction
- Everyday discourse
- Narrative framing

---

## GROUP 16: EMPHATIC TRUTH EXPRESSIONS

**Definition:** Fixed or semi-fixed expressions used to emphasize or intensify truth claims, often in informal or conversational contexts. These expressions go beyond simple truth assertion to stress certainty or genuineness.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tosi juttu* "true story, for real"
- *ihan tosi* "completely true"
- *totta kai* "of course, certainly"
- *totta tosiaan* "indeed, as a matter of fact"
- *tosiaankin* "indeed, really"
- *puhdas totuus* "pure truth"
- *koko totuus* "whole truth"
- *kiistaton tosiasia* "indisputable fact"

**Typical usage contexts:**
- Conversational emphasis
- Strong assertions
- Confirmations
- Informal speech

## AMBIGUOUS OR OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items appearing in multiple groups:

**1. *todellinen* "real, actual"**
- Appears in Group 1 (factual correspondence) when used with *tilanne* "situation," *ongelma* "problem"
- Appears in Group 3 (authenticity) when used with *ystävä* "friend," *rakkaus* "love"
- Usage context determines which sense is active

**2. *oikea* "right, correct, real, genuine"**
- Appears in Group 2 (correctness) when used with *vastaus* "answer," *tapa* "way"
- Appears in Group 3 (authenticity) when used with *nimi* "name," *nahka* "leather"
- Highly polysemous; context-dependent

**3. *väärä* "wrong, false"**
- Appears in Group 2 (correctness) when used with *vastaus* "answer"
- Appears in Group 1 (factual correspondence) when used with *väite* "claim," *tieto* "information"
- Appears in Group 13 (error) when used with *käsitys* "impression"
- Multiple usage patterns

**4. *vääristää* "to distort, to falsify"**
- Appears in Group 4 (deception) when emphasizing intentional falsification
- Appears in Group 12 (distortion) when emphasizing transformation of truth
- Semantic overlap between groups

**5. *tosiasia* "fact"**
- Appears in Group 1 (factual correspondence) as basic term
- Appears in Group 16 (emphatic expressions) in *kiistaton tosiasia* "indisputable fact"
- Different functional roles

**6. *totuus* "truth"**
- Appears in Group 1 (factual correspondence) as abstract noun
- Appears in Group 16 (emphatic expressions) in *puhdas totuus*, *koko totuus*
- Appears in Group 12 (distortion) as object of *vääristää*
- Multiple functional contexts

### Items that do not fit cleanly into groups:

**1. Spatial/directional uses of *oikea***
- *oikeanpuoleinen* "right-hand, on the right side"
- *oikeakätinen* "right-handed"
- These are completely separate from truth-domain uses; represent homonymy rather than polysemy

**2. Legal/moral uses of *oikeus***
- *oikeus* "right, justice, law, court"
- *oikeudenmukaisuus* "justice, fairness"
- *oikeuttaa* "to justify"
- While derived from *oikea*, these have developed into a distinct semantic domain (legal/moral rights) largely separate from truth-evaluation

**3. Seriousness/earnestness uses**
- *tosissaan* "seriously, in earnest"
- *tosikko* "overly serious person"
- These extend from truth-domain to behavioral/attitudinal domain; peripheral to core truth-evaluation

**4. Religious orthodoxy terms**
- *vääräuskoinen* "heretic"
- *vääräoppisuus* "heresy"
- *oikeaoppinen* "orthodox"
- While using truth-domain roots, these are specialized religious terms with distinct usage patterns

**5. Physical/anatomical terms**
- *vääräleuka* "underbite"
- *väärähammas* "false tooth"
- These use *väärä* for physical abnormality/artificiality; peripheral to truth-domain

**6. Character trait terms**
- *vääräpäinen* "stubborn, wrongheaded"
- *oikeamielinen* "righteous, right-minded"
- These extend from correctness to character evaluation; partially overlapping with truth-domain

**7. Temporal appropriateness**
- *oikea-aikainen* "timely"
- Uses *oikea* for temporal appropriateness; not clearly truth-related

### Items with uncertain grouping:

**1. *epätosi* "untrue, false"**
- Could fit in Group 1 (factual correspondence) as negation of *tosi*
- Less productive than *tosi* or *väärä*; primarily appears as simple predicate
- Lacks the range of constructions that would clearly establish group membership

**2. *epärehellinen* "dishonest"**
- Could fit in Group 5 (honesty) as negation of *rehellinen*
- Or could be separate as negative moral evaluation
- Relatively straightforward negation; less distinctive usage pattern

**3. *oikeakielisyys* "linguistic correctness"**
- Specialized linguistic term
- Could fit in Group 2 (correctness) but highly domain-specific
- Limited to linguistic/educational contexts

**4. *tosiasiallisuus* "factuality, actuality"**
- Abstract nominalization from *tosiasiallinen*
- Could fit in Group 1 but very formal/technical
- Limited usage range

## CROSS-CUTTING OBSERVATIONS

### Register stratification across groups:

**Informal/conversational:**
- Group 16 (emphatic expressions): *tosi juttu*, *ihan tosi*
- Some Group 15 items: *tosielämä*, *tosi-tv*

**Neutral/general:**
- Group 1 (factual correspondence): *tosi*, *totuus*, *tosiasia*
- Group 2 (correctness): *oikea*, *väärä*
- Group 5 (honesty): *rehellinen*

**Formal/technical:**
- Group 1: *paikkansapitävä*, *todenmukainen*
- Group 6 (evidential): *todistusaineisto*, *todistustaakka*
- Group 11 (observation): *todeta*, *voidaan todeta*

### Semantic oppositions within and across groups:

**Within Group 1:**
- *tosi* vs. *epätosi*
- *pitää paikkansa* vs. *ei pidä paikkaansa*

**Within Group 2:**
- *oikea* vs. *väärä*
- *oikeellinen* vs. *virheellinen*
- *olla oikeassa* vs. *olla väärässä*

**Across Groups 4 and 5:**
- Group 4 (deception) vs. Group 5 (honesty)
- *valhe* vs. *totuus*
- *valehdella* vs. *puhua totuutta*
- *petollinen* vs. *rehellinen*

**Across Groups 4 and 13:**
- Group 4 (deliberate deception) vs. Group 13 (unintentional error)
- *valehdella* vs. *erehtyä* (though *erehtyä* not in P1)
- Intentionality distinction

### Functional patterns:

**Verification and establishment:**
- Groups 1, 6, 11 cluster around establishing and verifying facts
- Different aspects: correspondence (Group 1), evidence (Group 6), statement (Group 11)

**Evaluation and judgment:**
- Groups 2, 5, 7 involve evaluative judgments
- Different targets: correctness (Group 2), character (Group 5), trustworthiness (Group 7)

**Falsification and corruption:**
- Groups 4, 12, 13 involve various forms of non-truth
- Different mechanisms: deliberate (Group 4), distortion (Group 12), error (Group 13)

## NOTES ON GROUPING METHODOLOGY

This analysis identifies 16 natural groupings based on observed usage patterns. Key methodological points:

1. **Groups reflect actual usage contexts**, not theoretical categories
2. **Overlaps are preserved** where items genuinely function in multiple contexts
3. **Ambiguous items are explicitly noted** rather than forced into single groups
4. **Peripheral items are identified** where truth-domain connection is weak
5. **Register and formality differences** are noted where they affect grouping
6. **Semantic oppositions** are documented within and across groups
7. **Some items remain ungrouped** where usage patterns don't clearly align with identified groups

The groupings reveal that Finnish truth-domain vocabulary is organized around:
- Factual correspondence and verification
- Correctness and error
- Authenticity and genuineness
- Honesty/deception opposition
- Evidence and proof
- Reliability and credibility
- Realization and actuality

These groupings are not mutually exclusive categories but rather overlapping usage patterns that reflect how Finnish speakers actually deploy truth-domain vocabulary in different contexts.
