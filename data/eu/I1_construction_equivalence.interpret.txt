## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 VERIFICATION Substitutes for CAUSATION (factual)

Basque does not have a productive causative construction meaning "to make X true" or "to cause X to be true." The morphological causative suffix *-arazi* does not combine with truth-domain roots to create causative meanings. Instead, the language uses **verification verbs** (*egiazkotu*, *egiaztatu*, *ziurtatu*) which express establishing, confirming, or ensuring truth rather than causing it. This represents a **conceptual gap**: the language conceptualizes truth-establishment as verification/authentication rather than causation.

### 2.2 BECOME is Marginal; No REVELATION Substitute

The periphrastic inchoative construction with *bihurtu* "become" exists but is **marginal** with abstract truth-domain nouns. Speakers report it sounds less natural than with concrete nouns. Importantly, Basque **lacks a REVELATION construction** (e.g., "turn out to be true," "prove to be false") that might substitute for BECOME. This creates a **structural gap**: the language has limited resources for expressing inchoative change in truth-status. When such meanings are needed, speakers typically use:
- VERIFICATION verbs (*egiazkotu* "verify")
- Light verb constructions (*egia atera* "truth come-out" = "truth emerge")
- Specific verbs for particular contexts (*okertu* "go wrong" for *oker*)

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION Requires Circumlocution

While VERIFICATION has multiple dedicated verbs (*egiazkotu*, *egiaztatu*, *ziurtatu*), **DISCONFIRMATION lacks a simple verb**. There is no Basque equivalent of "disprove," "falsify," or "debunk." To express disconfirmation, speakers must use:
- Negated verification: *ez egiazkotu* "not verify"
- Circumlocution: *gezurra dela frogatu* "prove that it is a lie"
- Light verb constructions: *gezurra atera* "bring out the lie"

This represents a **lexical gap** with an asymmetry favoring positive verification over negative disconfirmation.

### 2.4 "To Lie" Requires Light Verb Construction

A significant **lexical gap** exists: there is no simple verb meaning "to lie" (no *\*gezurtu*). The standard expression is the **light verb construction** *gezurra esan* (lit. "tell a lie"). This is obligatory and fully idiomatic. The pattern extends to other speech acts:
- *egia esan* "tell the truth"
- *gezurra esan* "tell a lie"

This contrasts with the availability of derived verbs for verification (*egiazkotu*) and correction (*zuzendu*), suggesting a **structural preference** for light verb constructions in speech-act contexts.

### 2.5 Multiple Strategies for Manner Adverbials

The language shows **no substitution** but rather **multiple competing strategies** for manner adverbials, all fully productive:
- Instrumental case: *egiaz* "truthfully"
- Adverbial suffix: *egiazki* "truly"
- Locative case: *benetan* "really/truly"

These are not substitutes for each other but rather stylistic variants with subtle semantic differences (instrumental = manner, locative = emphasis).

### 2.6 Asymmetry in Abstract Nominalization

The abstract quality nominalization pattern (*-tasun*) shows an **asymmetry**:
- ✓ *egiazkotasuna* "truthfulness/authenticity"
- ✓ *zintzotasuna* "honesty/sincerity"
- ✗ *\*gezurtasuna* (not attested in standard usage)

For "falsity/mendacity," speakers use other constructions or the bare noun *gezurra* "lie." This represents a **lexical gap** specific to negative truth-values in abstract nominalization.

### 2.7 Negation is Analytic, Not Morphological

Basque NEGATION is **strictly analytic**: the preverbal particle *ez* is obligatory and cannot be replaced by morphological negation. This contrasts with some languages that allow negative affixes. The pattern is:
- Affirmative: *egia da* "it is true"
- Negative: *ez da egia* "it is not true" (never *\*daegia* or similar)

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

1. **REVELATION**: No construction for "turn out to be X," "prove to be X," "reveal itself as X"
   - No equivalent to English *turn out*, *prove* (intransitive), *emerge as*
   - Cannot express discovery of truth-status through dedicated construction

2. **CAUSATION (character)**: No productive causative construction
   - Morphological causative *-arazi* does not combine with truth-domain roots
   - Cannot express "make X true" or "cause X to be true" directly

3. **BECOME (marginal)**: Periphrastic construction exists but is marginal
   - *bihurtu* "become" sounds unnatural with abstract truth-domain nouns
   - Speakers prefer specific verbs or verification constructions

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

1. **DISCONFIRMATION**: No simple verb for "disprove," "falsify," "debunk"
   - Must use negated verification or circumlocution
   - Asymmetry: verification has multiple dedicated verbs, disconfirmation has none

2. **"To lie"**: No simple verb *\*gezurtu*
   - Obligatory light verb construction: *gezurra esan* "tell a lie"
   - Contrasts with availability of *zuzendu* "correct," *egiazkotu* "verify"

3. **Abstract nominalization of "falsity"**: No *\*gezurtasuna*
   - Pattern productive for positive truth-values (*egiazkotasuna*, *zintzotasuna*)
   - Asymmetry in abstract quality nominalization

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

1. **CAUSATION vs. VERIFICATION**: The language conceptualizes truth-establishment as verification/authentication rather than causation
   - No "make true" construction
   - VERIFICATION verbs (*egiazkotu*, *ziurtatu*) serve related but distinct functions

2. **Inchoative change in truth-status**: Limited resources for expressing becoming true/false
   - No REVELATION construction
   - Marginal BECOME construction
   - Preference for verification or specific verbs

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

1. **VERIFICATION (✓) vs. DISCONFIRMATION (✗)**
   - Multiple verification verbs vs. no disconfirmation verb
   - Positive bias in lexicalization

2. **Abstract nominalization**: Productive for positive values, blocked for *gezur*
   - *egiazkotasuna* ✓, *zintzotasuna* ✓
   - *\*gezurtasuna* ✗

3. **Manner adverbs**: Productive for positive values, restricted for negative
   - *egiazki* ✓, *zintzoki* ✓
   - *\*gezurki* ✗ (uses *gezurrez* instead)

## 5. Summary of Constructional Behavior

### Core Operators (Fully Productive)
- ATTR, ADNOM, NEGATION, VERIFICATION, NOMINALIZATION

### Substituted Operators
- CAUSATION → VERIFICATION (conceptual shift)
- BECOME → VERIFICATION or specific verbs (structural preference)

### Gapped Operators
- REVELATION: structural gap (no construction)
- CAUSATION (character): structural gap (no productive morphology)
- DISCONFIRMATION: lexical gap (no dedicated verb)

### Asymmetries
- Verification ≫ Disconfirmation (multiple verbs vs. none)
- Positive abstract nominals ≫ Negative (*egiazkotasuna* vs. *\*gezurtasuna*)
- Positive manner adverbs ≫ Negative (*egiazki* vs. *\*gezurki*)

### Structural Preferences
- Light verb constructions for speech acts (*gezurra esan* "tell a lie")
- Verification over causation for truth-establishment
- Multiple competing strategies for manner adverbials (no single preferred form)
