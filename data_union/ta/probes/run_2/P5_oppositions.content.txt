# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919

---

## Preliminary Note

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented in P1–P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit co-occurrence in contrastive constructions, direct antonym statements, or morphological pairing attested in the data. No opposition is inferred from semantic intuition alone. Where the data supports asymmetry, asymmetry is preserved.

### Opposition 1

**Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Term B:** பொய் / *poy*
**Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *poy*
**English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1 as "the primary antonym of *uṇmai*." The pair co-occurs directly in the binary question construction *itu uṇmaiyā illai poyyā?* (is this true or false?) and in the construction *poyyai uṇmai eṉṟu nampināṉ* (he believed the lie to be true). P3 identifies this as "the central contrast" of Group 1.
**Contrast type:** Lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 2

**Term A:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*
**Term B:** பொய்யான / *poyyāna*
**Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyāna* / *poyyāna*
**English translation:** true, genuine / false, fake
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 4 explicitly states that *uṇmaiyāna* "contrasts with *poyyana* (false)." Both are adjectival *-āna* forms of their respective bases and occupy the same syntactic position as prenominal modifiers.
**Contrast type:** Morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 3

**Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
**Term B:** போலி / *pōli*
**Transliteration:** *acal* / *pōli*
**English translation:** original, genuine / fake, counterfeit
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 14 states *acal* "directly contrasts with *pōli* (fake)." The pair co-occurs in the construction *itu acal illai, pōli* (this is not the original — it's a fake), documented in P2b Construction 3 under *pōli*. P3 Group 4 identifies this as the central contrast of the genuineness-versus-fakeness domain.
**Contrast type:** Lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 4

**Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
**Term B:** நகல் / *nakal*
**Transliteration:** *acal* / *nakal*
**English translation:** original / copy, duplicate
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 14 states *acal* "directly contrasts with... *nakal* (copy)." The pair co-occurs in the binary question construction *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?), documented in P2b Construction 2 under *acal* and in P4b. P3 notes *nakal* is "evaluatively neutral" while *acal* is positively evaluative.
**Contrast type:** Lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 22

**Transliteration:** *uṇmai col* / *poy col*

### Opposition 27

**Term A:** சான்று இல்லாமல் நிரூபிக்க முடியாது
**Term B:** சான்றுடன் நிரூபி
**Transliteration:** *cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu* / *cāṉṟuṭaṉ nirūpi*
**English translation:** without evidence, it cannot be proved / prove it with evidence

The two constructions contrast the absence of evidential support (rendering proof impossible) with the presence of evidential support (enabling proof). **Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 28

**Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Term B:** மெய் / *mey*
**Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *mey*
**English translation:** truth (everyday/modern) / truth, reality (classical/literary)
**Description of contrast:** This is not an antonymic opposition but a register-based contrast between two terms with overlapping meaning. P1 item 23 states *mey* is "less common in everyday modern speech than *uṇmai*." P3 notes both terms appear in Group 1 but with different register distributions. P2b documents parallel constructions: *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (everyday) and *meyyē vellum* (literary). The contrast is between the productive everyday term and its classical/literary equivalent.
**Contrast type:** Lexical
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Opposition 29

**Term B:** உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmaitaṉmai*
**Transliteration:** *poymai* / *uṇmaitaṉmai*
**English translation:** falsehood, the quality of being false / truthfulness, authenticity, genuineness

Both are restricted to formal, philosophical, and literary registers. They name the abstract properties that stand in opposition, paralleling the *uṇmai/poy* lexical opposition at a more formal register level.

### Opposition 34

**Term A:** -āna (positive adjectival suffix)
**Term B:** -aṟṟa / -illāta (privative participial suffixes)
**Transliteration:** *-āna* / *-aṟṟa*, *-illāta*
**English translation:** "having the property of X" / "lacking the property of X"
**Description of contrast:** Across the truth domain, the suffix *-āna* (adjectival copular) consistently produces positive evaluative forms (*uṇmaiyāna*, *nēṟmaiyāna*, *nampakamāna*, *cariyāna*, *niyāyamāna*), while *-aṟṟa* and *-illāta* produce privative forms (*uṇmaiyaṟṟa*, *nēṟmaiyaṟṟa*, *nampakamāṟṟa*, *ātāramaṟṟa*, *kaḷḷamillāta*). P4a documents this as a productive pattern across all truth-domain nominals. P4b notes that *-aṟṟa* tends toward formal registers while *-illāta* is cross-register.
**Contrast type:** Morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Opposition 35

**Term A:** உண்மை தான் வெல்லும் / *uṇmai tāṉ vellum*
**Term B:** பொய் சொல்வது தவறு / *poy coluvatu tavaṟu*
**Transliteration:** *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* / *poy coluvatu tavaṟu*
**English translation:** truth alone will prevail / telling lies is wrong
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as proverbial moral assertions: the first (Construction 8 under *uṇmai*) asserts the ultimate triumph of truth; the second (Construction 4 under *poy*) asserts the wrongness of lying. The two constructions represent opposite poles of the same moral domain — the positive value of truth and the negative evaluation of falsehood — using different construction types (*tāṉ vellum* vs. *V-vatu tavaṟu*).
**Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
