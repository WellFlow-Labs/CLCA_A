# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

---

### BASE: **doğru** (straight, true, correct)

#### Attested Constructions

1. **doğru söylemek**
   - Transliteration: doğru söylemek
   - Translation: to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
   - Usage: Common verbal phrase emphasizing truthful speech. Used across all registers to describe honest communication.
   - [CORE]

2. **doğrusunu söylemek**
   - Transliteration: doğrusunu söylemek
   - Translation: to tell the truth, to be honest (lit. "to say its truth")
   - Usage: Fixed phrase used to introduce honest statements or confessions. Very common in conversational Turkish, often as discourse marker: "Doğrusunu söylemek gerekirse..." (To tell the truth...).
   - [CORE]

3. **doğru mu?**
   - Transliteration: doğru mu
   - Translation: is it true? right? correct?
   - Usage: Common question form used to seek confirmation or verification. Can function as tag question or standalone inquiry.
   - [CORE]

4. **doğru yol**
   - Transliteration: doğru yol
   - Translation: right path, correct way, straight path
   - Usage: Used literally for direct routes and metaphorically for moral or religious righteousness. Common in religious discourse and moral instruction.
   - [CORE]

5. **doğru dürüst**
   - Transliteration: doğru dürüst
   - Translation: proper, decent, respectable, adequate
   - Usage: Colloquial reduplicative expression meaning "proper" or "decent." Often used to describe adequate quality or respectable behavior. Can be used negatively: "doğru dürüst bir iş yok" (there's no proper job).
   - [CORE]

6. **doğru düzgün**
   - Transliteration: doğru düzgün
   - Translation: proper, decent, orderly, respectable
   - Usage: Alliterative compound similar to "doğru dürüst" but emphasizing orderliness and propriety. Common in everyday speech to describe adequate or respectable quality.
   - [CORE]

7. **doğru olmak**
   - Transliteration: doğru olmak
   - Translation: to be true, to be correct, to be right
   - Usage: Copular construction asserting truth or correctness. Essential across all contexts for truth evaluation.
   - [CORE]

8. **doğru çıkmak**
   - Transliteration: doğru çıkmak
   - Translation: to turn out to be true/correct, to prove right
   - Usage: Verbal phrase indicating that something previously uncertain has been verified as true. Common in discussions of predictions or claims.
   - [CORE]

9. **doğru kabul etmek**
   - Transliteration: doğru kabul etmek
   - Translation: to accept as true, to acknowledge as correct
   - Usage: Formal expression for accepting the truth of a statement. Used in academic and formal discourse.
   - [CORE]

10. **doğru bulmak**
    - Transliteration: doğru bulmak
    - Translation: to find correct, to consider right, to deem appropriate
    - Usage: Expression for evaluating something as correct or appropriate. Common in moral and practical judgments.
    - [CORE]

### BASE: **hak** (right, truth, justice)

#### Attested Constructions

1. **hak vermek**
   - Transliteration: hak vermek
   - Translation: to agree with, to side with, to acknowledge someone is right
   - Usage: Very common expression meaning to concede that someone is correct or justified. Essential in arguments and discussions: "sana hak veriyorum" (I agree with you/you're right).
   - [CORE]

2. **hak yemek**
   - Transliteration: hak yemek
   - Translation: to violate someone's rights, to wrong someone (lit. "to eat right")
   - Usage: Idiomatic expression meaning to unjustly take what belongs to another or violate their rights. Strong moral condemnation in Turkish culture.
   - [CORE]

3. **hakkı olmak**
   - Transliteration: hakkı olmak
   - Translation: to have the right, to be entitled
   - Usage: Fixed expression indicating entitlement or rightful claim. Common in legal and everyday contexts: "senin hakkın var" (you have the right/you're entitled).
   - [CORE]

4. **haksızlık etmek**
   - Transliteration: haksızlık etmek
   - Translation: to wrong someone, to treat unjustly
   - Usage: Verbal phrase indicating commission of injustice. Common in accusations and moral judgments.
   - [CORE]

5. **hak iddia etmek**
   - Transliteration: hak iddia etmek
   - Translation: to claim a right, to assert entitlement
   - Usage: Legal and formal expression for asserting one's rights or claims. Used in disputes and legal proceedings.
   - [CORE]

6. **hak aramak**
   - Transliteration: hak aramak
   - Translation: to seek justice, to pursue one's rights
   - Usage: Expression for actively pursuing justice or asserting rights, often through legal channels. Common in discussions of legal recourse.
   - [CORE]

7. **hak ve adalet**
   - Transliteration: hak ve adalet
   - Translation: right and justice, justice and fairness
   - Usage: Fixed phrase common in legal and political discourse, often used rhetorically to invoke principles of justice.
   - [CORE]

8. **hakkını almak**
   - Transliteration: hakkını almak
   - Translation: to get one's due, to obtain justice
   - Usage: Expression meaning to receive what one deserves or is entitled to. Can be positive (getting deserved reward) or negative (getting deserved punishment).
   - [CORE]

9. **hakkını helal etmek**
   - Transliteration: hakkını helal etmek
   - Translation: to forgive someone's debt/wrong, to waive one's right
   - Usage: Religious and cultural expression meaning to forgive someone who has wronged you or to waive a debt. Important in Islamic ethical framework.
   - [CORE]

10. **hakkını vermek**
    - Transliteration: hakkını vermek
    - Translation: to give credit, to do justice to, to acknowledge merit
    - Usage: Expression meaning to properly acknowledge someone's merit or to do something justice. Common in evaluations: "hakkını vermek lazım" (one must give credit where it's due).
    - [CORE]

11. **hak etmek** (Note: This is borderline between derivational and compositional, but included as it functions as a fixed verbal phrase)
    - Transliteration: hak etmek
    - Translation: to deserve, to merit, to earn
    - Usage: Common verbal phrase indicating deservingness or merit. Used in moral judgments: "bunu hak etti" (he/she deserved this).
    - [CORE]

12. **hak ettiği değer**
    - Transliteration: hak ettiği değer
    - Translation: deserved value, due recognition
    - Usage: Expression referring to proper recognition or valuation. Common in discussions of merit and appreciation.
    - [CORE]

13. **Allah hakkı için**
    - Transliteration: Allah hakkı için
    - Translation: for God's sake, in the name of God
    - Usage: Religious oath or emphatic appeal invoking divine truth/right. Used to emphasize sincerity or make urgent requests.
    - [CORE]

14. **hak sahibi**
    - Transliteration: hak sahibi
    - Translation: rightful owner, rights holder, entitled person
    - Usage: Legal and formal term for someone who holds rights to something. Common in property law and intellectual property contexts.
    - [CORE]

15. **hak gözetmek**
    - Transliteration: hak gözetmek
    - Translation: to respect rights, to be mindful of justice
    - Usage: Expression meaning to be careful to respect others' rights and act justly. Used in moral instruction.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productive Patterns Identified

1. **[X] olmak** (to be X): Highly productive copular construction across nearly all adjectival bases. Essential for asserting properties.

2. **[X] çıkmak** (to turn out to be X): Productive pattern for indicating that something has been revealed or proven to have property X. Attested with: doğru, yanlış, sahte, haklı, haksız, yalan.

3. **[X] görünmek** (to appear/look X): Productive pattern for subjective impressions. Attested with: şüpheli, kuşkulu, doğal, yapay, inandırıcı.

4. **[X] söylemek** (to say/tell X): Pattern for verbal expression. Attested with: doğru, yalan, gerçek.

5. **[X] yapmak** (to make/do X): Pattern for performing actions. Attested with: yanlış, hata.

6. **[X] etmek** (light verb construction): Productive with nominal bases. Attested with: haksızlık, taklit, isabet, hak.

7. **[X] davranmak** (to behave X-ly): Pattern for behavioral descriptions. Attested with: dürüst, adil, tutarlı.

8. **[X] kaynak/bilgi/kanıt** (X source/information/evidence): Productive pattern for epistemic evaluation of information. Attested with: güvenilir, sağlam, kesin, yanıltıcı.

### Asymmetries and Gaps

1. Not all bases participate equally in all patterns. For example, "çıkmak" constructions are common with evaluative terms but not with inherent properties.

2. Some bases (hak, gerçek, yalan) show extensive multi-word construction families, while others (özgün, asıl) show minimal compositional productivity.

3. Religious/cultural expressions (Allah hakkı için, hakkını helal etmek) are specific to certain bases with cultural-religious significance.

4. Reduplicative and alliterative compounds (doğru dürüst, doğru düzgün, yalan dolan, yalan yanlış) show limited productivity and are lexically specific.

5. Some semantic domains show rich construction sets (rights/justice with hak, reality with gerçek) while others are more limited.

### Notes on Construction Types

1. **Fixed collocations**: Many constructions represent semi-fixed collocations where the truth-related term combines with specific nouns (gerçek hayat, sahte para, meşru savunma).

2. **Light verb constructions**: Turkish extensively uses light verbs (etmek, olmak, yapmak) to create verbal phrases from nominal and adjectival bases.

3. **Idiomatic expressions**: Some constructions have idiomatic meanings not fully predictable from components (hak yemek, hak vermek).

4. **Register variation**: Some constructions are more formal (hakikate ulaşmak, gerçeğe aykırı) while others are colloquial (doğru dürüst, yalan dolan).

5. **Grammaticalized forms**: Some constructions show grammaticalization (hakkında as postposition, kesinlikle as emphatic adverb).
