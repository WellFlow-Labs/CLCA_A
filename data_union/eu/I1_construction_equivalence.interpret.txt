## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION and BECOME

No dedicated REVELATION construction is attested in the data. The periphrastic *bihurtu da* construction, documented as the general inchoative strategy under BECOME, can carry a reading that overlaps with revelation in certain contexts: *Egia bihurtu da* ("it has become true / it turned out to be true") admits a reading in which a previously unknown truth comes to light. However, P4a and P4b document this construction exclusively under the BECOME/inchoative operator and do not identify a distinct revelation reading as a separate construction type. No dedicated verb or construction meaning "to be revealed as true" or "to turn out to be X" is identified as a separate operator. The BECOME construction therefore covers the semantic space that a REVELATION construction would occupy, but this coverage is incidental to the construction's primary inchoative function and is not documented as a systematic substitution.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and CAUSATION (factual)

The VERIFICATION and CAUSATION (factual) operators share some of the same morphological material. The lexicalized *-tu* transitive verb *egiaztatu* ("to verify") functions as a VERIFICATION verb in most documented uses, but its morphological structure (*egiazta-* + *-tu*) is the same as other *-tu* transitives that carry causative readings (*zuzendu*, "to correct"; *baliogabetu*, "to nullify"). P4b notes that *egiaztatu* carries "an implicit causative meaning" institutionally established rather than compositionally derived. The *-arazi* causative *egiaztarazi* ("to cause to be verified") is documented as a CAUSATION (factual) form built on the same verbal stem. The two operators are therefore formally related but are not documented as substituting for each other: VERIFICATION is expressed by *egiaztatu* (intransitive-like, agent acts on proposition), while CAUSATION (factual) in the verification sub-domain is expressed by *egiaztarazi* (agent causes another to verify).

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION and NEGATION

DISCONFIRMATION as a distinct operator — the act of actively refuting or disproving a claim — is not attested as a separate construction. The data show that the semantic space of disconfirmation is covered by two substitution strategies: (a) NEGATION applied to ATTR (*ez da egia hori*, "that is not true"), which denies the truth of a proposition without encoding the act of refutation as such; and (b) the PRIVATIVE construction (*baliogabea*, "invalid"; *frogaezina*, "unprovable"), which denies that a property holds or that a verification is possible. Neither of these is a dedicated disconfirmation construction: NEGATION + ATTR is a general negation pattern, and the PRIVATIVE construction expresses property-absence or impossibility rather than the speech act of disconfirming. No verb meaning "to disprove" or "to refute" is documented in P4a/P4b.

### 2.4 NEGATION: Analytic vs. Morphological Strategies

NEGATION in this domain is expressed by both analytic and morphological means, and these are not interchangeable. The analytic strategy (preverbal *ez* + finite verb *da*) is fully productive and applies to any predicate in the truth domain without restriction. The morphological strategies are each restricted to specific bases or semantic subtypes:

- The *ez-* prefix on adjectives (*ez-fidagarria*, *ez-sinesgarria*, *ez-zilegia*) is semi-lexicalized and applies to a near-closed set of forms; it is not freely productive.
- The *-gabe* privative applies to nominal and verbal roots to express absence of a property (*baliogabea*); it does not express propositional negation.
- The *ezin* construction applies to verbal roots to express impossibility (*frogaezina*); it is not interchangeable with *-gabe*.
- The *-gaitz* / *-kaitz* suffix is the consistent negative counterpart to *-garri* adjectivals (*fidagaitza* vs. *fidagarria*); it is not used outside this pairing.

The analytic *ez da* strategy is therefore the only fully productive negation construction; the morphological strategies each occupy a restricted and non-overlapping semantic niche.

### 2.5 Asymmetry: Truth-telling vs. Falsehood Agents

A consistent asymmetry is documented across the agentive nominalization domain. The falsehood side has a fully lexicalized, single-word agentive nominal: *gezurtia* ("the liar"), formed with the *-ti* habitual/characterological suffix. No parallel form exists for the truth-telling agent: no *egiati*, *egiazkoti*, or *egiazale* is attested. The truth-telling agent is expressed periphrastically via a relative clause (*egia esaten duena*, "the one who tells the truth") or by character adjectives (*zintzoa*, *jatorra*) that express a property rather than an agentive role. This asymmetry is documented in P4a and P4b as a lexical gap on the truth side of the domain, not as a morphosyntactic gap: the *-ti* suffix is available in principle but no form has been lexicalized for the truth-telling agent.

A parallel asymmetry is noted for the *-keria* pejorative nominalization: all attested *-keria* forms in this domain (*gezurkeria*, *itxurakeria*, *ustelkeria*, *faltsukeria*, *okerkeria*) are on the falsehood/deception side. No *-keria* form on the truth side is documented, which is consistent with the inherently pejorative semantics of the suffix.

### 2.6 CAUSATION (character) and CAUSATION (factual)

No construction specifically targeting the causing of a change in a person's moral character (as distinct from causing a factual state or a belief state) is documented as a separate operator. The *-arazi* causative *sinetsarazi* ("to make someone believe a lie") covers causing a belief state, which is the closest attested form to character causation. The *-tu* transitive use of *zintzotu* ("to make honest") would in principle yield a character-causation reading, but no example with an explicit causee is documented in P4a/P4b. CAUSATION (character) is therefore not attested as a distinct operator and is covered, to the extent it is expressed at all, by CAUSATION (factual) via the *-arazi* causative.

### 2.7 Epistemic Hedging as a Distinct Functional Domain

The epistemic hedge constructions (*ziurrenik*, *antza denez*, *itxuraz*, *ustez*) do not map onto any of the ten CLCA canonical operators listed in the task. They are documented in P4a/P4b as a distinct functional domain (epistemic modulation of propositional commitment) and are noted here for completeness. They are not substitutes for any of the canonical operators but represent an additional functional layer in the truth domain.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **REVELATION** | No dedicated construction encoding the event of a truth being revealed or coming to light is attested. The BECOME construction (*bihurtu da*) may contextually overlap with revelation readings but is not documented as a revelation operator. This is a structural gap: no construction type in P4a/P4b is assigned this function. |
| **DISCONFIRMATION** | No dedicated construction encoding the act of disconfirming or refuting a claim is attested. The semantic space is covered by NEGATION + ATTR and by PRIVATIVE constructions, but neither is a dedicated disconfirmation operator. |
| **CAUSATION (character)** | No construction specifically encoding the causing of a change in a person's moral character (as distinct from factual causation or belief-state causation) is documented as a distinct operator. |

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **Truth-telling agentive nominal** | No single-word agentive nominal for "truth-teller" is attested. The *-ti* suffix is available but no form (*egiati*, *egiazkoti*, or similar) has been lexicalized. The concept is expressed periphrastically (*egia esaten duena*). This is a lexical gap, not a morphosyntactic gap. |
| **Inchoative *-tu* from *egia* or *gezurra*** | No *-tu* inchoative verb derived directly from *egia* ("truth") or *gezurra* ("lie/falsehood") is attested. The *-tu* inchoative pattern is available in the language but has not been lexicalized for these bases. |
| **Dedicated disconfirmation verb** | No verb meaning "to disprove" or "to refute" is documented in P4a/P4b. |

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps — cases where the semantic operation appears to be blocked or unavailable in principle — are identifiable from the data. All gaps documented above are either structural (no construction type assigned) or lexical (no word lexicalized for an available pattern). The data do not support a claim that any operation is conceptually unavailable in Basque.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (Only Negative or Only Positive Forms Missing)

| Asymmetry | Description |
|---|---|
| **Agentive nominalization: falsehood side only** | *Gezurtia* ("liar") is fully lexicalized with the *-ti* suffix. No parallel *-ti* form for the truth-telling agent is attested. The truth side requires periphrasis (*egia esaten duena*) or a property adjective (*zintzoa*, *jatorra*). |
| **Pejorative nominalization (*-keria*): falsehood/deception side only** | All attested *-keria* forms in this domain are on the falsehood or deception side (*gezurkeria*, *itxurakeria*, *ustelkeria*, *faltsukeria*, *okerkeria*). No *-keria* form on the truth side is documented. This is consistent with the inherently pejorative semantics of the suffix and does not constitute a gap in the same sense as the agentive asymmetry. |
| **Lexical *ez-* prefix negation: restricted set** | The *ez-* prefix negation is attested for *fidagarria* → *ez-fidagarria*, *sinesgarria* → *ez-sinesgarria*, *zilegia* → *ez-zilegia*, but is not documented for all adjectives in the domain. The positive forms are all available; the *ez-* negative forms are available only for a near-closed set. |
