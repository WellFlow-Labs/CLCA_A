# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register, and contextual use documented in P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external framework has been imposed. Group boundaries are permeable where the data supports overlap. Items that do not fit cleanly are noted at the end.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert that something corresponds to reality

**Label:** Correspondence-to-reality assertions

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker or writer asserts that a statement, claim, or proposition matches what is actually the case. They appear in contexts where the primary concern is whether something is factually accurate or not — in philosophical argument, legal testimony, journalistic fact-checking, and everyday conversation. They contrast most directly with Group 2 (falsity terms) and are distinguished from Group 3 (genuineness terms) by their focus on propositional content rather than object identity.

**Representative expressions:**
- αληθής / alithís — the base adjective for truth-value; used of statements and propositions
- αλήθεια / alíthia — the primary noun; appears in λέω την αλήθεια, η αλήθεια είναι ότι
- αληθεύω / alithévo — verb: "to speak the truth" or "to be true" (of a proposition)
- αναληθής / analithís — formal negation: "not true" (of a proposition)
- αναλήθεια / analíthia — abstract noun for the state of being untrue
- αληθής ή ψευδής / alithís í psevdís — the binary true/false construction used in logic and testing
- αληθής κατά τεκμήριο / alithís katá tekmírio — legal: "presumed true"
- εν μέρει αληθής / en méri alithís — "partly true"
- επαληθεύω / epalithévo — "to verify": the process of checking correspondence
- επαλήθευση / epalíthefsi — "verification": the act or result of that process
- επαληθεύσιμος / epalithéfsimos — "verifiable": capable of being checked for correspondence
- μη επαληθεύσιμος / mi epalithéfsimos — "unverifiable"

**Notes:** αληθής and αλήθεια are the core items of this group. αναληθής and αναλήθεια are marginal in everyday use but belong here by function. επαληθεύω and its derivatives belong here because they describe the process of establishing correspondence, not the property of genuineness or correctness of procedure.

## Group 2: Terms used to assert or describe falsity, lying, and fabrication

**Label:** Falsity and lying terms

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker asserts that a statement is false, that a person is lying, or that something has been deliberately fabricated. They appear across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. The group includes both propositional falsity (a claim is false) and agentive lying (a person tells lies), as well as the act of officially refuting a false claim. The distinction between deliberate lying and mere falsity is present within the group but does not divide it into separate groups in Greek usage.

**Representative expressions:**
- ψευδής / psevdís — adjective: false, untrue; used of statements, documents, persons
- ψεύδος / psévdos — noun: falsehood, lie (formal/written register)
- ψέμα / pséma — noun: lie (colloquial, most frequent in everyday speech)
- ψεύτης / psévtis — agent noun: liar
- ψεύτικος / psévtikos — adjective: fake, false, phony (colloquial)
- ψεύδομαι / psévdome — verb: to lie (slightly formal)
- λέω ψέματα / léo psémata — verbal construction: to tell lies (most common colloquial form)
- διαψεύδω / diapsévdo — verb: to officially refute or deny a false claim
- διάψευση / diápsfefsi — noun: official denial or refutation
- ψευδής κατάθεση / psevdís katáthesi — fixed legal phrase: false testimony, perjury
- ψευδής δήλωση / psevdís dílosi — fixed legal phrase: false declaration
- ψευδής κατηγορία / psevdís katigoria — fixed legal phrase: false accusation
- ψευδής υπόσχεση / psevdís ipóschesi — false promise
- ψευδής ταυτότητα / psevdís taftótita — false identity
- άσπρο ψέμα / áspro pséma — white lie
- ψέμα για καλό / pséma ya kaló — a lie told with benevolent intent
- μισή αλήθεια / misí alíthia — half-truth (documented under αληθ- root but functions here)
- αναληθής / analithís — formal: untrue (overlaps with Group 1)

**Notes:** διαψεύδω belongs here because its function is to counter a false claim, not to assert truth independently. μισή αλήθεια sits between this group and Group 1: it describes a statement that is partially true but functions deceptively, placing it closer to this group in practice. ψεύτικος overlaps with Group 4 (counterfeit/fake objects) depending on what it modifies.

## Group 3: Terms used to assert that an object, document, or person is what it claims to be

**Label:** Genuineness and authenticity terms

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether a physical object, document, artwork, person, or relationship is the real thing rather than a copy, imitation, or forgery. They appear in legal, commercial, art-market, and everyday contexts. The concern here is identity and origin — is this the genuine article? — rather than whether a proposition is true. They contrast with Group 4 (forgery and counterfeiting terms), which covers the negative pole of the same domain.

**Representative expressions:**
- γνήσιος / gníssios — genuine, legitimate; used of documents, products, signatures, persons
- γνησιότητα / gnissiótita — genuineness, authenticity (abstract noun)
- γνήσιο αντίγραφο / gnísio andígrafo — certified/genuine copy (fixed legal phrase)
- γνήσιο προϊόν / gnísio proïón — genuine product
- γνήσια υπογραφή / gníssia ipogrfí — genuine signature
- έλεγχος γνησιότητας / élegchos gnissiótitas — authenticity check
- αυθεντικός / afthentikós — authentic, original; used of artworks, texts, experiences, persons
- αυθεντικότητα / afthentikótita — authenticity (abstract noun)
- αυθεντικό έργο τέχνης / afthentikó érgo téchnis — authentic work of art
- αυθεντική έκδοση / afthentikí ékdosi — authentic/original edition
- αυθεντικό κείμενο / afthentikó kímeno — authentic text
- αυθεντική εμπειρία / afthentikí embiría — authentic experience

**Notes:** γνήσιος and αυθεντικός overlap substantially but show distributional differences: γνήσιος is more common in legal and administrative contexts (certified copies, signatures, products), while αυθεντικός is more common in cultural, artistic, and experiential contexts (artworks, texts, experiences). Both are used in everyday speech. αληθινός (Group 5) overlaps with this group when applied to objects or relationships (αληθινός φίλος, "a true friend"), but its primary function is different enough to warrant separate treatment.

## Group 4: Terms used to describe forgery, counterfeiting, and adulteration

**Label:** Forgery and counterfeiting terms

**Definition:** These expressions are used when something has been deliberately fabricated, falsified, or adulterated to pass as genuine. They appear primarily in legal, criminal, and regulatory contexts. The emphasis is on deliberate deception through physical or documentary fabrication, as opposed to verbal lying (Group 2) or mere lack of genuineness (Group 3). Several items in this group carry specific legal definitions in Greek law.

**Representative expressions:**
- πλαστός / plastós — forged, fabricated; used of documents, currency, signatures
- πλαστογραφία / plastografía — forgery of documents (primary legal term)
- πλαστογραφώ / plastografó — to forge a document
- πλαστογράφος / plastográfos — forger
- πλαστό διαβατήριο / plastó diavatírio — forged passport
- πλαστό χαρτονόμισμα / plastó chartonómisma — counterfeit banknote
- πλαστή υπογραφή / plastí ipogrfí — forged signature
- πλαστό πτυχίο / plastó ptychío — forged degree
- πλαστή ταυτότητα / plastí taftótita — forged identity card
- πλαστά στοιχεία / plastá stoichía — forged documents, fabricated evidence
- νόθος / nóthos — spurious, adulterated; used of texts, products, persons (of illegitimate birth)
- νοθεία / nothía — adulteration, falsification (abstract noun)
- νοθεύω / nothévo — to adulterate, to falsify
- νοθεία τροφίμων / nothía trofímon — food adulteration (fixed regulatory phrase)
- νόθο κείμενο / nótho kímeno — spurious text (textual criticism)
- κίβδηλος / kívdilos — counterfeit, debased; used of coins, and figuratively of persons or arguments
- κίβδηλο νόμισμα / kívdilo nómisma — counterfeit/debased coin

**Notes:** πλαστός and νόθος cover partially overlapping but distinct areas: πλαστός implies active fabrication (forging), while νόθος implies illegitimacy of origin or adulteration (something mixed or corrupted). κίβδηλος overlaps with both but is restricted to formal and literary registers. The legal collocations under πλαστός are the most productive and frequent items in this group in contemporary use.

## Group 5: Terms used to distinguish what is real or genuine from what is superficial, imitation, or merely nominal

**Label:** Real-versus-nominal or real-versus-imitation distinctions

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker wants to distinguish something that is genuinely what it appears to be from something that merely resembles it, is nominal, or is a simulation. They appear in everyday, legal, financial, and philosophical contexts. Unlike Group 3 (genuineness terms), which focuses on verifying identity and origin, these terms focus on the contrast between substance and appearance, or between the real thing and a stand-in.

**Representative expressions:**
- αληθινός / alithinós — real, genuine; used to contrast with imitation or false versions (αληθινός φίλος, αληθινή αγάπη)
- αληθινά γεγονότα / alithiná yegonóta — true events (as opposed to fictional)
- ο αληθινός εαυτός μου / o alithinós eaftós mu — my true/real self
- εικονικός / eikonikós — virtual, simulated, nominal; used to describe something that resembles but is not the real thing
- εικονική πραγματικότητα / eikonikí pragmatikótita — virtual reality
- εικονική εταιρεία / eikonikí etería — shell company (exists nominally, not genuinely)
- εικονικό τιμολόγιο / eikonikó timológio — fictitious invoice
- εικονική δικαιοπραξία / eikonikí dikeopraksia — simulated legal act
- εικονικός γάμος / eikonikós gámos — sham marriage
- εικονική απασχόληση / eikonikí apascholísi — fictitious employment
- ουσιαστικός / ussiastikós — substantial, real, meaningful (as opposed to formal or superficial)
- ουσία / ussía — substance, essence, gist (as opposed to form)
- ουσιώδης / ussióðis — essential, of the essence
- ανούσιος / anússios — insubstantial, trivial, lacking substance
- ουσιαστική αλλαγή / ussiastikí alagí — real/substantial change (as opposed to cosmetic)
- ουσιαστικό δίκαιο / ussiastikó díkeo — substantive law (as opposed to procedural)

**Notes:** αληθινός belongs here when applied to objects, relationships, and identities, but belongs to Group 1 when used of propositions. εικονικός has a split: in technology contexts (virtual reality, virtual environment) it is neutral or positive; in legal and financial contexts (shell company, fictitious invoice) it is strongly negative. ουσιαστικός and ουσία form a coherent sub-cluster within this group, consistently marking the substance/form distinction across legal, philosophical, and everyday registers.

## Group 6: Terms used to indicate that a claim or status has not been confirmed

**Label:** Unconfirmed-status markers

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker or writer wants to indicate that a claim, identity, or status is being reported or attributed without being confirmed. They appear primarily in legal and journalistic contexts, functioning as epistemic hedges that protect the speaker from asserting something as established fact. They are distinguished from Group 7 (skepticism and irony markers) by their neutral or procedural tone: they do not necessarily imply that the claim is false, only that it has not been verified.

**Representative expressions:**
- φερόμενος ως / ferómenos os — purported, alleged; used of persons presented as having a certain status
- φερόμενος ως ιδιοκτήτης / ferómenos os idioktítis — the purported owner
- φερόμενος ως δράστης / ferómenos os drástis — the alleged perpetrator
- φερόμενος ως εκπρόσωπος / ferómenos os ekprósopος — the purported representative
- εικαζόμενος / eikazómenos — alleged, supposed, putative (slightly more marginal in frequency)
- ο εικαζόμενος δράστης / o eikazómenos drástis — the alleged perpetrator
- αμφισβητήσιμος / amfisvitiísimos — disputable, contestable; used of claims and rights that can be challenged
- αμφισβητούμενη περιοχή / amfisviтúmeni periochí — disputed territory
- αμφισβητούμενη απόφαση / amfisviтúmeni apófasi — contested decision

**Notes:** φερόμενος ως is the most productive and frequent item in this group, particularly in formal legal and journalistic writing. εικαζόμενος is less frequent and more marginal. αμφισβητήσιμος and its verbal base αμφισβητώ belong here when used to describe claims or statuses that are open to challenge, but they also overlap with Group 7 when used to express active skepticism.

## Group 7: Terms used to express skepticism or ironic doubt about a presented claim

**Label:** Skepticism and irony markers

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker wants to signal that they do not believe a claim being made, or that something is presented as true but is doubted. They carry a pragmatic force that goes beyond simple negation: they imply that the claim is being performed or asserted by someone else, and the speaker is distancing themselves from it, often with ironic or dismissive force. They appear primarily in everyday speech and journalistic commentary.

**Representative expressions:**
- δήθεν / díthеn — supposedly, so-called; used to cast ironic doubt on a claim or status
- ο δήθεν ειδικός / o díthеn eidikós — the so-called expert
- δήθεν για το καλό μας / díthеn ya to kaló mas — supposedly for our own good
- δήθεν αθώος / díthеn athóos — supposedly innocent
- η δήθεν αλήθεια / i díthеn alíthia — the so-called truth
- τάχα / tácha — supposedly, as if; used colloquially to express disbelief
- τάχα μου δεν ήξερε / tácha mu den íksere — as if he/she didn't know
- τάχα πως / tácha pos — as if; pretending that
- ο τάχα φίλος μου / o tácha fílos mu — my so-called friend
- αμφίβολος / amfívolos — doubtful, dubious; used of claims, persons, or situations whose status is in question
- αμφίβολης αξίας / amfívolis aksías — of dubious value
- αμφίβολης προέλευσης / amfívolis proéleysis — of dubious origin
- αμφίβολης φήμης / amfívolis fímis — of dubious reputation

**Notes:** δήθεν and τάχα are the most distinctive items in this group: they are the only P1 items whose primary function is to mark the speaker's ironic or skeptical stance toward a claim. They differ in register (τάχα is more colloquial) and in syntactic preference (τάχα more commonly precedes verbal clauses; δήθεν more commonly precedes noun phrases), but both function as scare-quote equivalents in speech. αμφίβολος belongs here when used to express the speaker's doubt, but it is more neutral in tone than δήθεν or τάχα — it describes a state of doubtfulness rather than performing ironic distancing.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate conformity with a standard or norm

**Label:** Correctness and conformity-to-standard terms

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether something conforms to an applicable standard, rule, or norm — whether an answer is right, a procedure is proper, a document is valid, or a decision is sound. They appear in educational, legal, administrative, and everyday contexts. The standard in question may be logical, procedural, legal, or conventional. They are distinguished from Group 1 (truth terms) by their focus on conformity rather than correspondence to reality, and from Group 3 (genuineness terms) by their focus on procedure and norm rather than identity and origin.

**Representative expressions:**
- σωστός / sostós — correct, right, proper; the most common colloquial term
- σωστή απάντηση / sostí apándisi — correct answer
- σωστός άνθρωπος / sostós ánthropos — a decent/sound person
- κάνω το σωστό / káno to sostó — to do the right thing
- είναι σωστό να / íne sostó na — it is right/proper to
- ορθός / orthós — correct, proper; more formal than σωστός
- ορθή απάντηση / orthí apándisi — correct answer (formal)
- ορθή χρήση / orthí chrísi — correct use
- ορθή εφαρμογή του νόμου / orthí efarmoyí tu nómu — correct application of the law
- ορθότητα / orthótita — correctness (abstract noun)
- λανθασμένος / lanthazmenos — wrong, mistaken, erroneous; the standard antonym of σωστός
- λανθασμένη εντύπωση / lanthazmeni endíposi — mistaken impression
- λανθασμένη πεποίθηση / lanthazmeni pepíthisi — mistaken belief
- λανθασμένη ερμηνεία / lanthazmeni erminía — mistaken interpretation
- λάθος / láthos — mistake, error; also used as an indeclinable adjective
- ακριβής / akrivís — accurate, precise, exact
- ακρίβεια / akrívia — accuracy, precision
- ακριβώς / akrivós — exactly, precisely (also used as a discourse particle of agreement)
- ανακριβής / anakrivís — inaccurate, imprecise
- έγκυρος / éngiros — valid, having legal or logical force
- εγκυρότητα / engirotita — validity
- έγκυρο έγγραφο / éngiro éngrafo — valid document
- έγκυρη ψήφος / éngiri psífos — valid vote
- άκυρος / ákiros — invalid, null, void
- ακυρώνω / akiróno — to invalidate, to cancel

**Notes:** σωστός and ορθός overlap substantially but differ in register: σωστός dominates everyday speech, ορθός is more formal and appears in compounds (ορθογραφία, ορθόδοξος, ορθολογισμός). ακριβής belongs here when used of measurements and descriptions (accuracy as conformity to a standard), but it also has a distinct everyday meaning of "expensive" (ακριβός) that is outside this domain entirely. έγκυρος focuses specifically on legal or procedural validity and forms a coherent sub-cluster with άκυρος and ακυρώνω.

## Group 9: Terms used in contexts of image, representation, and resemblance

**Label:** Image, representation, and resemblance terms

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the primary concern is the relationship between a representation and what it represents — whether something is a likeness, a simulation, an image, or a conjecture based on resemblance. They appear in artistic, technological, legal, and philosophical contexts. The group is held together by the shared εικ-/εικον- root and by the common concern with the relationship between appearance and reality, though the specific concern varies by context.

**Representative expressions:**
- εικόνα / ikóna — image, picture, icon; the base noun
- εικονικός / eikonikós — virtual, simulated, image-like (overlaps with Group 5)
- εικαστικές τέχνες / eikastikés téchnes — visual arts (arts concerned with representation)
- εικαστικός καλλιτέχνης / eikastikós kalitéchnis — visual artist
- εικαστικό έργο / eikastikó érgo — work of visual art
- εικάζω / ikázo — to conjecture, to infer (forming a mental image of something not directly seen)
- εικασία / ikasía — conjecture, speculation
- εικαζόμενος / eikazómenos — alleged, supposed (overlaps with Group 6)
- αληθοφάνεια / alithofánia — verisimilitude, appearance of truth
- αληθοφανής / alithofanís — verisimilar, plausible, having the appearance of truth
- εικονογραφία / ikonografía — iconography, illustration
- εικονοκλάστης / ikonokláστis — iconoclast (one who destroys images; figuratively, one who challenges established beliefs)
- αυτοεικόνα / aftoikóna — self-image
- εικόνα του κόσμου / ikóna tu kósmu — image of the world, worldview

**Notes:** This group is less tightly unified than the others: it is held together by the shared root and by the common concern with representation and resemblance, but the individual items serve very different functions. αληθοφάνεια and αληθοφανής belong here because they describe the appearance of truth rather than truth itself — the concern is with how something looks rather than what it is. εικαζόμενος belongs here etymologically and semantically (conjecture as forming a mental image) but functions primarily in Group 6 in contemporary use. εικονικός belongs here etymologically but functions primarily in Group 5 in contemporary use.

## Group 10: Terms used to describe the ψευδο- (pseudo-) pattern

**Label:** Pseudo-X compounds

**Definition:** These expressions are formed with the prefix ψευδο- and describe something that presents itself as belonging to a category (science, democracy, philosophy, etc.) but is judged not to genuinely belong to it. They appear in critical, academic, journalistic, and political registers. The group is defined by a shared morphological and pragmatic pattern rather than a single semantic domain: in each case, the prefix signals that the appearance of category membership is not matched by genuine membership.

**Representative expressions:**
- ψευδοεπιστήμη / psevdoepistími — pseudoscience
- ψευδοεπιστημονικός / psevdoepistimoniikós — pseudoscientific
- ψευδοδιλήμμα / psevdodilíma — false dilemma
- ψευδοπροφήτης / psevdoprofítis — false prophet
- ψευδοδημοκρατία / psevdodimokratía — pseudo-democracy
- ψευδοδιανοούμενος / psevdodianouúmenos — pseudo-intellectual
- ψευδώνυμο / psevdónymo — pseudonym
- ψευδαίσθηση / psevdésthisi — illusion, hallucination (false perception)
- ψευδαργυρος / psevdárgyros — zinc (literally "false silver"; a technical term)
- ψευδοπόδιο / psevdopódio — pseudopod (biological term)

**Notes:** The ψευδο- prefix is highly productive and can combine with virtually any noun. The items listed above are the most attested in P1b. ψευδαργυρος and ψευδοπόδιο are peripheral: in these technical terms, "false" describes morphological or chemical resemblance rather than deceptive presentation, and the evaluative force of the prefix is absent. ψευδαίσθηση sits between this group and Group 2: it describes a false perception rather than a deliberate lie, but the prefix functions similarly to its use in other ψευδο- compounds.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items were identified as belonging to multiple groups or as not fitting cleanly into any single group:

**αληθινός / alithinós**
Belongs to Group 1 when used of propositions ("a true statement"), to Group 3 when used of objects or relationships ("a genuine product," "a true friend"), and to Group 5 when used to contrast with imitation or fiction ("based on true events," "my true self"). The word's distribution across these contexts is well-attested and the overlaps are genuine.

**εικονικός / eikonikós**
Belongs to Group 5 (real-versus-nominal) in legal and financial contexts (shell company, fictitious invoice, sham marriage), to Group 9 (image and representation) etymologically and in technology contexts (virtual reality, virtual environment), and to Group 8 (correctness) in the specific legal sense of εικονική δικαιοπραξία. The word's polysemy is documented and the different uses are contextually distinct.

**αμφίβολος / amfívolos and αμφιβολία / amfivolía**
αμφίβολος belongs to Group 7 (skepticism) when used to describe the speaker's doubt about a claim or person, and to Group 6 (unconfirmed status) when used more neutrally to describe a situation whose outcome is uncertain. αμφιβολία ("doubt") is used both in expressions of personal uncertainty (έχω αμφιβολίες, "I have doubts") and in fixed phrases asserting certainty (χωρίς αμφιβολία, "without doubt"), placing it across Groups 6 and 7.

**αμφισβητήσιμος / amfisvitiísimos and αμφισβητώ / amfisviтó**
Belongs to Group 6 (unconfirmed status) when used of claims or rights that are open to challenge, and to Group 7 (skepticism) when the verb αμφισβητώ is used to express active contestation. The verbal form αμφισβητώ is more active and agentive than the adjective, which is more neutral.

**μισή αλήθεια / misí alíthia**
Belongs to Group 1 (truth terms) morphologically and etymologically, but functions in Group 2 (falsity terms) pragmatically: a half-truth is used to describe a statement that is deceptive by omission, placing it closer to the falsity domain in actual use.

**αξιόπιστος / aksiópistos and αξιοπιστία / aksiopistía**
These terms do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. They describe the trustworthiness of a source, person, or institution — a property that is related to but distinct from truth (Group 1), genuineness (Group 3), and validity (Group 8). The concern is with whether something can be relied upon, which is an evaluative judgment about a source rather than a property of a claim or object. They are noted here as items that form a coherent pair but do not belong to any of the groups identified above.

**αξιόλογος / aksiólogos and αξιολόγηση / aksiolóyisi**
Similarly, these terms describe merit and evaluation rather than truth, genuineness, or correctness in any of the senses documented above. αξιολόγηση in particular has become a general-purpose term for "evaluation" or "assessment" in Greek administrative and educational discourse, extending well beyond the truth-evaluation domain. They are noted as items that do not fit cleanly into any group identified here.

**μετα-αλήθεια / meta-alíthia**
This term belongs to Group 1 etymologically but describes a cultural or political condition rather than a property of individual propositions. It is a calque of English "post-truth" and its usage is restricted to journalistic, academic, and political discourse about the contemporary epistemic environment. It does not fit cleanly into any group.

**κίβδηλος / kívdilos**
Belongs to Group 4 (forgery and counterfeiting) in its historical and numismatic uses (κίβδηλο νόμισμα), and to Group 7 (skepticism) in its figurative uses (κίβδηλος φιλόσοφος, κίβδηλη αρετή). Its marginal status in contemporary use and its restriction to formal and literary registers mean it does not anchor any group.

## Items with No Clear Group Assignment

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit into any of the groups identified above, and no clear grouping pattern was found for them:

- **αξιόπιστος / aksiópistos** and **αξιοπιστία / aksiopistía** — trustworthiness of sources and persons; noted above
- **αξιόλογος / aksiólogos** and **αξιολόγηση / aksiolóyisi** — merit and evaluation; noted above
- **αληθοφάνεια / alithofánia** and **αληθοφανής / alithofanís** — these could form a small group around the concept of appearing true without being true (verisimilitude), but only two items support this and they are documented under Group 9 as the closest fit
- **αληθώς ανέστη / alithós anésti** — a liturgical formula; its function is performative and confessional rather than epistemic, and it does not group with other items
- **αλήθεια ή τόλμη / alíthia í tólmi** — a game name; its use of αλήθεια is social and performative rather than epistemic
