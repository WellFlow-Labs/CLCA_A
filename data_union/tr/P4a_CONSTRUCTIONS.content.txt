# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[Subject] + [truth-domain adjective] + copula (∅ or *-dır/-dir*); or [truth-domain adjective] + [noun] (attributive, prenominal).

### Examples
- Bu iddia doğru. / *Bu iddia doğru.* / "This claim is correct."
- Belge sahte. / *Belge sahte.* / "The document is fake."
- Bu haber doğru. / *Bu haber doğru.* / "This news is correct/true."
- İddia asılsız. / *İddia asılsız.* / "The claim is unfounded/baseless."
- **doğru haber** / doğru haber / "correct news, accurate report"
- **sahte belge** / sahte belge / "forged document, fake document"

### Notes
Turkish uses a zero copula in 3rd-person present affirmative; *değil* is inserted for negation. The adjective precedes the zero copula position. Tense/evidential distinctions are marked on the copular suffix: *doğruydu* (was correct), *doğruymuş* (reportedly correct).

The copula is zero in informal/neutral present tense; *-dır* appears in formal or assertive registers (*Bu haber doğrudur*). The adjective does not inflect for agreement. Both *doğru* and *gerçek* can fill this slot, but *doğru* more typically predicates propositional correctness, *gerçek* ontological realness.

Turkish attributive adjectives are invariant; they precede the noun without case or number agreement. The pattern is fully productive: *doğru*, *yanlış*, *sahte*, *gerçek*, *hakiki*, *özgün*, *geçerli*, *asıl*, *haklı* all occur freely in this slot.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[Subject] + [truth-domain adjective] + *değil* (negation particle); or privative suffix *-sız/-siz* attached to noun base.

### Examples
- Bu bilgi doğru değil. / *Bu bilgi doğru değil.* / "This information is not correct."
- İddia asılsız değil. / *İddia asılsız değil.* / "The allegation is not unfounded."
- Bu belge gerçek değil. / *Bu belge gerçek değil.* / "This document is not real/genuine."
- İddia doğru değil. / *İddia doğru değil.* / "The claim is not correct/true."
- Bu haber doğru değil. / *Bu haber doğru değil.* / "This news is not correct."
- **asılsız iddialar** / asılsız iddialar / "unfounded/baseless claims"

### Notes
*Değil* is the invariant negation particle for nominal/adjectival predicates; it does not inflect for the predicate but takes tense/person suffixes itself (*değildi*, *değilim*). Negation of verbal predicates uses a different strategy (suffix *-me/-ma*).

*Değil* is the sole strategy for negating adjectival predicates; privative suffixes (*-sız*, *-siz*) produce new lexical items (*asılsız*, *kanıtsız*, *geçersiz*) rather than serving as a productive negation construction in the predicate slot. Tense/evidential suffixes attach to *değil*: *doğru değildi* (was not correct), *doğru değilmiş* (reportedly not correct).

Two distinct strategies: (1) predicative negation with invariant *değil* following the adjective; (2) derivational negation via privative *-sız/-siz* on a nominal base (*asıl* → *asılsız*, *hak* → *haksız*, *geçer-* → *geçersiz*). The suffix strategy produces a new lexical item; *değil* negation is syntactic and applies to any predicate.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
[truth-domain adjective] + [noun] (prenominal, no agreement morphology); or [Noun] + *-lı/-li* (possessive-adjectival suffix); or [Noun] + *-sal/-sel* (relational adjectival suffix).

### Examples
- Güvenilir kaynak / *güvenilir kaynak* / "reliable source"
- Asılsız iddia / *asılsız iddia* / "unfounded allegation"
- **Sahte belgeyle yakalandı.** / sahte belgeyle yakalandı / "He/she was caught with a forged document." (*sahte* modifies *belge*, instrumental case on head noun)
- **Güvenilir bir kaynaktan aldım.** / güvenilir bir kaynaktan aldım / "I got it from a reliable source." (*güvenilir* modifies *kaynak*, ablative on head noun)
- **geçerli** (< *geçer-* + *-li*) / geçerli / "valid, having currency/acceptance" — *geçerli pasaport* "valid passport"
- **doğrusal denklem** / doğrusal denklem / "linear equation" (< *doğru* + *-sal*)

### Notes
Turkish adjectives are invariant prenominal modifiers; they carry no case, number, or gender agreement with the head noun. The head noun takes all case suffixes. This is the dominant adnominal pattern across all P1 items.

The modifier is invariant; all inflection falls on the head noun. The indefinite article *bir* (one/a) may intervene between modifier and noun (*güvenilir bir kaynak*) without disrupting the construction. This pattern is fully productive across all truth-domain adjectives in P1: *doğru bilgi*, *yanlış haber*, *hakiki deri*, *özgün eser*, *geçerli pasaport*, etc.

The *-lı/-li* suffix derives adjectives meaning "having/bearing [base property]" (*hak* → *haklı*, *teyit* → *teyitli*, *tasdik* → *tasdikli*); *-sal/-sel* derives adjectives meaning "pertaining to [base]" (*doğru* → *doğrusal*, *öz* → *özsel*). Both are productive within the truth-domain vocabulary.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
[base] + *-laş-/-leş-* (inchoative verbalizer) + *-mak/-mek*; or [adjective] + *ol-* / *olmak* (periphrastic); or [adjective] + *hale gelmek* (periphrastic).

### Examples
- Meşrulaşmak / *meşrulaşmak* / "to become legitimate"
- Geçersiz hale gelmek / *geçersiz hale gelmek* / "to become invalid / to come to be void"
- **Hareket zamanla meşrulaştı.** / hareket zamanla meşrulaştı / "The movement became legitimate over time." (morphological: *meşru* + *-laş-* + past tense)
- **Hayalleri gerçekleşti.** / hayalleri gerçekleşti / "Their dreams came true / were realized." (*gerçek* + *-leş-* + past tense)
- **Hayali gerçekleşti.** / Hayali gerçekleşti. / "His/her dream came true / materialized." (*gerçek* + *-leş-* + *-ti*)

### Notes
Two strategies coexist: morphological (*-laş-* directly on the base, productive for some items: *meşrulaşmak*, *yasalaşmak*) and periphrastic (*[adjective] + hale gelmek*, "to come into the state of," available for all adjectives). Not all P1 bases take *-laş-* productively; *doğrulaşmak* is marginal, *geçersizleşmek* is rare compared to the periphrastic alternative.

The *-leş-* inchoative is productive on adjectives of Arabic and native Turkic origin alike (*meşrulaşmak*, *gerçekleşmek*). For items where *-leş-* is not conventionalized, the periphrastic *[adjective] + olmak* is used (*geçerli olmak*, to become valid; *haklı olmak*, to be/become right). *gerçekleşmek* has shifted toward the meaning "to occur, to take place" in addition to "to become real."

The suffix *-laş-/-leş-* is the primary inchoative/mutative suffix in Turkish, attaching to adjectives and nouns to form intransitive verbs of becoming. Within the truth domain: *meşrulaşmak* (to become legitimate), *gerçekleşmek* (to come true/materialize). The resulting verb is intransitive; the causative counterpart is formed by adding *-tır-/-tir-* (see §5).

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
[base] + *-laştır-/-leştir-* (causative of inchoative) + *-mak/-mek*; or [adjective] + *kılmak* (periphrastic); or [adjective/noun] + *-la-/-le-* + *-mak/-mek* (factitive verbalizer).

### Examples
- Meşrulaştırmak / *meşrulaştırmak* / "to legitimize, to render legitimate"
- Geçersiz kılmak / *geçersiz kılmak* / "to invalidate, to render void"
- **Şiddeti meşrulaştırmak mümkün değil.** / şiddeti meşrulaştırmak mümkün değil / "It is not possible to legitimize violence." (*meşru* + *-laş-* + *-tır-* + infinitive)
- **Bu durum müdahaleyi haklı kılar.** / bu durum müdahaleyi haklı kılar / "This situation justifies the intervention." (*haklı* + *kılmak*, periphrastic)
- **Şiddeti meşrulaştırmak.** / Şiddeti meşrulaştırmak. / "To legitimize violence." (*meşru* + *-laş-* + *-tır-* + *-mak*)
- **Haberi doğrulamak gerekiyor.** / Haberi doğrulamak gerekiyor. / "The news needs to be verified." (*doğru* + *-la-* + *-mak*)

### Notes
Two parallel strategies: morphological (*-laştır-* = inchoative *-laş-* + causative *-tır-*, e.g., *meşrulaştırmak*, *yasalaştırmak*) and periphrastic (*[adjective] + kılmak*, "to make/render," e.g., *geçersiz kılmak*, *geçerli kılmak*). The periphrastic strategy is available for all adjectives; the morphological strategy is lexically restricted.

The morphological causative *-leş-tir-* is available where *-leş-* is already conventionalized (*meşrulaştırmak*, *gerçekleştirmek*). Where it is not, *kılmak* provides the formal causative (*geçersiz kılmak*, to invalidate; *haklı kılmak*, to justify). The factitive *-la-* verbalizer on nouns (*doğrulamak*, to verify; *kanıtlamak*, to prove; *ispatlamak*, to prove formally) constitutes a related but distinct pattern — it creates a verb meaning "to perform the action associated with X" rather than strictly "to cause X to become true."

Two causative/factitive strategies are attested: (1) *-laş-tır-* on adjective/noun bases, producing "to cause to become [base]" (*meşrulaştırmak*, *gerçekleştirmek*); (2) *-la-* directly on an adjective base, producing "to make/treat as [base]" (*doğrulamak* — to verify; *yalanlamak* — to deny/refute). Both are productive. Passive counterparts are formed by inserting *-n-* or *-il-* before the infinitive suffix.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[adjective/verb base] + *-lık/-lik/-luk/-lük* (nominal suffix); or [verb stem] + *-ma/-me* (verbal noun suffix).

### Examples
- Doğruluk / *doğruluk* / "truthfulness, correctness, accuracy"
- Güvenilirlik / *güvenilirlik* / "reliability, trustworthiness"
- **Dürüstlük her zaman ödüllendirilmez.** / dürüstlük her zaman ödüllendirilmez / "Honesty is not always rewarded." (*dürüst* + *-lük*)
- **Kimlik doğrulama zorunludur.** / kimlik doğrulama zorunludur / "Identity verification is mandatory." (*doğrula-* + *-ma*, verbal noun)
- **Dürüstlük en önemli erdemlerden biridir.** / Dürüstlük en önemli erdemlerden biridir. / "Honesty is one of the most important virtues." (*dürüst* + *-lük*)
- **Pasaportunun geçerliliği sona erdi.** / Pasaportunun geçerliliği sona erdi. / "The validity of his/her passport expired." (*geçerli* + *-lik*)

### Notes
The suffix *-lık* (with vowel harmony variants) is the primary nominalizer for abstract properties across all P1 items: *yanlışlık*, *gerçeklik*, *sahtecilik*, *meşruiyet* (Arabic-derived alternative), *tarafsızlık*, *nesnellik*, *öznellik*, *haklılık*, *haksızlık*, *geçerlilik*, *geçersizlik*. The Arabic-derived suffix *-iyet* appears in items of Arabic origin (*meşruiyet*, *hakkaniyet*) as an alternative nominalization strategy.

The *-lık* suffix is the primary strategy for property nominalization across the truth domain: *doğruluk* (truth/correctness), *yanlışlık* (error), *güvenilirlik* (reliability), *meşruiyet* (legitimacy — using the Arabic-pattern *-iyet* instead), *geçerlilik* (validity), *sahtecilik* (forgery), *özgünlük* (originality). The *-ma/-me* verbal noun nominalizes the process (*doğrulama*, verification; *yanlışlama*, falsification; *kanıtlama*, proving). Both types are fully productive.

The suffix *-lık/-lik/-luk/-lük* (with vowel harmony) is the primary abstract nominalizer in Turkish and applies productively across the truth-domain vocabulary: *doğruluk* (truthfulness/accuracy), *yanlışlık* (error), *sahtelik* (fakeness), *özgünlük* (originality), *haklılık* (justifiedness), *haksızlık* (injustice), *geçersizlik* (invalidity), *sahicilik* (genuineness), *meşruluk* (legitimacy), *asılsızlık* (groundlessness). Verbal-stem nominalizations use *-ma/-me* (*doğrulama*, *yalanlama*, *yanlışlama*).

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[base] + *-cı/-ci/-cu/-cü* (agentive suffix); or [verb stem] + *-ıcı/-ici/-yıcı/-yici* (participial agentive).

### Examples
- Yalancı / *yalancı* / "liar" (yalan + *-cı*)
- Yanıltıcı / *yanıltıcı* / "misleader, misleading agent" (yanılt- + *-ıcı*)
- **Sahteci mahkemede yargılandı.** / sahteci mahkemede yargılandı / "The forger was tried in court." (*sahte* + *-ci*)
- **Doğruyu söyleyen biri bulmak zor.** / doğruyu söyleyen biri bulmak zor / "It is difficult to find someone who tells the truth." (participial relative clause, not a lexicalized agentive noun)
- **Yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar.** / Yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar. / "A liar's candle burns only until evening." (*yalan* + *-cı*)
- **Sahteci yakalandı.** / Sahteci yakalandı. / "The forger was caught." (*sahte* + *-ci*)

### Notes
Two agentive strategies: *-cı* attaches to nouns to form habitual/role agents (*yalancı*, liar; *doğrucu*, truth-teller; *sahteci*, forger); *-ıcı* attaches to verb stems to form participial agents that can function as adjectives or nouns (*yanıltıcı*, misleading/misleader; *aldatıcı*, deceptive/deceiver). The *-ıcı* forms are more commonly used adjectivally than as pure agent nouns.

Agentive nouns are lexicalized on the negative/deceptive side of the domain: *sahteci* (forger), *sahtekâr* (fraud/impostor, with Persian *-kâr*), *taklitçi* (imitator/counterfeiter), *yanıltıcı* (misleading agent, adjectival-agentive in *-ıcı*). No single lexicalized noun meaning "truth-teller" or "verifier" as a personal title is in common use; *doğrulayıcı* exists as a technical/adjectival form but not as a standard personal noun. The concept "one who tells the truth" is expressed through participial constructions (*doğruyu söyleyen kişi*) or through character-evaluation adjectives (*dürüst biri*, an honest person).

The suffix *-cı/-ci/-cu/-cü* (with vowel harmony) derives agentive nouns from nominal and verbal bases. Attested truth-domain agentives: *yalancı* (liar), *sahteci* (forger/counterfeiter), *taklitçi* (imitator/counterfeiter), *gerçekçi* (realist), *doğrulayıcı* (verifier — from verbal stem *doğrula-* + *-yıcı*, an alternative agentive suffix for verbal bases). The suffix *-yıcı/-yici* applies to verb stems and is the standard agentive for process-denoting verbs.

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION (VERBAL NOUN)

### Pattern
[verb stem] + *-ma/-me* (verbal noun suffix)

### Examples
- Doğrulama / *doğrulama* / "verification, confirmation" (doğrula- + *-ma*)
- Yalanlama / *yalanlama* / "denial, refutation" (yalanla- + *-ma*)

### Notes
The *-ma/-me* suffix nominalizes the verbal process itself (as distinct from *-lık* which nominalizes the abstract property). This pattern is productive across the domain: *yanlışlama* (falsification), *aldatma* (deception), *yanılma* (erring), *gerçekleşme* (realization). These forms appear frequently in legal and journalistic registers as action nominals.

## 9. ABILITY / POTENTIAL NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[verb stem] + *-(y)abil-* (potential) + *-ir* (aorist) + *-lik* (nominal)

### Examples
- Doğrulanabilirlik / *doğrulanabilirlik* / "verifiability"
- Yanlışlanabilirlik / *yanlışlanabilirlik* / "falsifiability"

### Notes
A layered morphological construction: passive stem + potential suffix + aorist participial + abstract nominalizer. Restricted to philosophical and scientific registers. The parallel pair *doğrulanabilirlik* / *yanlışlanabilirlik* directly encodes the logical positivist / Popperian distinction in Turkish academic discourse.

## 10. SINCERITY / CORRECTION DISCOURSE MARKER

### Pattern
[truth-domain noun in possessive or locative] used sentence-initially as adverbial hedge

### Examples
- Doğrusu, bilmiyorum. / *Doğrusu, bilmiyorum.* / "Honestly, I don't know."
- Aslında öyle değil. / *Aslında öyle değil.* / "Actually, it's not like that."

### Notes
Several truth-domain items grammaticalize as sentence-initial adverbial markers: *doğrusu* (its truth → honestly), *aslında* (in its origin/nature → actually, in fact), *gerçek şu ki* (the truth is that), *hakikaten* (truly). These function as sincerity hedges or corrective frames rather than propositional predicates, and are among the most frequent discourse-structural uses of P1 vocabulary.

## 11. APPROXIMATION CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[noun in dative] + *en yakın* / *uzak* + predicate

### Examples
- Doğruya en yakın cevap / *doğruya en yakın cevap* / "the answer closest to correct"
- Gerçekten uzak bir senaryo / *gerçekten uzak bir senaryo* / "a scenario far from reality"

### Notes
Epistemic approximation is expressed periphrastically via spatial metaphor: *yakın* (near) and *uzak* (far/distant) combine with the dative-marked truth noun to express degrees of correspondence to truth or reality. This pattern is productive and compositional rather than lexicalized.

---

## 12. CONTRASTIVE DISTINCTION CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[term A] + *ama* / *olmakla birlikte* + [term B] + *değil*

### Examples
- Yasal ama meşru değil. / *Yasal ama meşru değil.* / "Legal but not legitimate."
- Yanıltıcı olmakla birlikte yanlış olmamak. / *Yanıltıcı olmakla birlikte yanlış olmamak.* / "To be misleading without being false."

### Notes
A productive syntactic pattern used specifically to mark conceptual distinctions within the truth domain: legal vs. legitimate, misleading vs. false, objective vs. subjective. The concessive connectors *ama* (but) and *olmakla birlikte* (while being) signal that the two terms are not co-extensive. This construction recurs wherever the data documents paired near-synonyms with distinct scope.

## 13. FACTITIVE VERBALIZATION

### Pattern
[truth-domain noun] + *-la-/-le-* + *-mak/-mek* — verbalizer suffix producing a verb meaning "to perform the action associated with the noun" or "to treat as X."

### Examples
- **İddiayı kanıtlamak güçtür.** / iddiayı kanıtlamak güçtür / "It is difficult to prove the claim." (*kanıt* + *-la-* + *-mak*)
- **Hipotezi yanlışlamak mümkün olmalıdır.** / hipotezi yanlışlamak mümkün olmalıdır / "It must be possible to falsify the hypothesis." (*yanlış* + *-la-* + *-mak*)

### Notes
This pattern is highly productive across the truth domain and generates the primary verbal vocabulary for epistemic actions: *doğrulamak* (to verify), *kanıtlamak* (to prove), *ispatlamak* (to prove formally), *yanlışlamak* (to falsify), *meşrulaştırmak* (to legitimize — with additional inchoative morphology). The passive *-n-* and potential *-abil-* stack onto this stem productively: *doğrulanabilir* (verifiable), *yanlışlanabilir* (falsifiable), *kanıtlanmış* (proven).

## 14. CORRESPONDENCE POSTPOSITIONAL CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[truth-domain noun] + dative (*-e/-a*) + postposition (*aykırı* / *uygun*) — produces an adjectival phrase meaning "contrary to X" or "conforming to X," used attributively or predicatively.

### Examples
- **Gerçeğe aykırı beyan suç teşkil eder.** / gerçeğe aykırı beyan suç teşkil eder / "A statement contrary to fact constitutes a crime." (*gerçek* + dative + *aykırı*)
- **Gerçeğe uygun beyanda bulunmak zorundasınız.** / gerçeğe uygun beyanda bulunmak zorundasınız / "You are required to make a statement conforming to fact." (*gerçek* + dative + *uygun*)

### Notes
This construction is particularly productive in legal and administrative registers. *Gerçeğe aykırı* (contrary to reality/truth) and *gerçeğe uygun* (conforming to reality/truth) are fixed legal phrases. The pattern extends to *hakikate aykırı* in literary registers. The dative marks the standard of correspondence; *aykırı* and *uygun* are the primary postpositions in this slot within the truth domain.

---

## 15. LIGHT-VERB CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[Arabic/Persian-origin noun] + *etmek* / *olmak* — periphrastic verb formation; the noun carries the lexical content, the light verb supplies tense, agreement, and voice morphology.

### Examples
- **Bilgiyi teyit etmek gerekiyor.** / Bilgiyi teyit etmek gerekiyor. / "The information needs to be confirmed." (*teyit* + *etmek*)
- **Bunu hak etti.** / Bunu hak etti. / "He/she deserved this." (*hak* + *etmek*)

### Notes
Light-verb constructions with *etmek* (to do) are the standard verbal strategy for Arabic- and Persian-origin nominal bases in Turkish: *tasdik etmek* (to certify), *teyit etmek* (to confirm), *hak etmek* (to deserve), *taklit etmek* (to imitate). Passive voice is formed with *edilmek* (*teyit edilmek* — to be confirmed). This pattern is highly productive and applies to all Arabic/Persian-origin truth-domain nouns that lack a native Turkish verbal derivation.

## 16. PREDICATE OUTCOME CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[truth-domain adjective/noun] + *çıkmak* — "to turn out to be [adjective/noun]"; the subject is evaluated retrospectively as having a particular truth-domain property.

### Examples
- **Tahmini doğru çıktı.** / Tahmini doğru çıktı. / "His/her prediction turned out to be correct."
- **Bu yalan çıktı.** / Bu yalan çıktı. / "This turned out to be a lie."

### Notes
The verb *çıkmak* (to come out, to emerge) functions as a secondary predicate marker in this construction, indicating that the truth-domain evaluation is the result of a process of discovery or confirmation. Attested with *doğru*, *yanlış*, *yalan*, *sahte*, and *haklı* (*haklı çıkmak* — to be proven right). The construction is syntactically regular but semantically specialized to retrospective truth-evaluation.

---

## 17. CONCESSIVE PARTIAL-TRUTH CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**[Locative NP] + bir doğruluk payı var** — existential construction acknowledging partial truth in a claim.

### Examples
- **Bu iddiada bir doğruluk payı var.** / Bu iddiada bir doğruluk payı var. / "There is a grain of truth in this claim."
- **Söylediklerinde doğruluk payı yok değil.** / Söylediklerinde doğruluk payı yok değil. / "There is no denying a grain of truth in what he/she said."

### Notes
The construction uses the existential *var* (there is) with the nominal phrase *doğruluk payı* (share/portion of truth) in a locative frame specifying the claim under evaluation. The double-negative variant (*yok değil* — "it is not absent") is a common hedging form. No parallel construction with *hakikat* or *gerçek* in place of *doğruluk* is attested in this exact frame.

## 18. RIGHTS-DEPRIVATION / RIGHTS-FULFILLMENT CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**hakkını** [verb] — possessed accusative *hakkını* (one's due/right) + verb expressing giving, seeking, eating, or protecting.

### Examples
- **Kimsenin hakkını yemem.** / Kimsenin hakkını yemem. / "I will not deprive anyone of their due." (*hakkını yemek* — lit. "to eat someone's right")
- **İşinin hakkını verdi.** / İşinin hakkını verdi. / "He/she did justice to his/her work." (*hakkını vermek* — lit. "to give its due")

### Notes
The possessed accusative *hakkını* (its/his/her right/due) combines with a small set of verbs to form idiomatic constructions expressing the fulfillment or violation of entitlement: *vermek* (to give — fulfill), *aramak* (to seek — pursue), *yemek* (to eat — deprive), *gözetmek* (to protect — uphold). The possessive marking is obligatory in these idioms; bare *hak* + verb produces different or non-idiomatic meanings.
