# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the P1 lexical items. "Multi-word construction" is understood here as a syntactically compositional or semi-compositional expression of two or more words that functions as a recognizable unit in use — including verb-complement structures, verb-object collocations, coverb phrases, topic-comment frames, and idiomatic clausal patterns. Derivational forms documented in P2a are excluded. Fixed four-character expressions (成语) already documented in P1 and P1b are not re-listed unless they appear as components within a larger construction.

Where no common construction is identified for a base item or a construction type, this is stated explicitly. Absence is recorded as data.

## Constructions by Base Form

---

### Base: 真 (zhēn)

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
2. **Example:** 这是真的吗？
3. **Transliteration:** Zhè shì zhēn de ma?
4. **English translation:** Is this true? / Is this real?
5. **Usage notes:** The copular frame X + 是 + 真的 is the most basic predication of truth or genuineness in everyday speech. The question form with 吗 is extremely common; the affirmative 这是真的 and negative 这不是真的 are equally frequent.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
2. **Example:** 千真万确
3. **Transliteration:** qiān zhēn wàn què
4. **English translation:** Absolutely true, beyond any doubt, one hundred percent certain
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed four-character expression meaning something is completely and unquestionably true. Used for emphatic assertion across everyday and rhetorical registers. Not previously listed in P1 or P1b.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
2. **Example:** 弄假成真
3. **Transliteration:** nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn
4. **English translation:** What started as a pretense becomes real; a fake turns into the genuine article
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed expression describing a situation where something originally false, feigned, or performed ends up becoming genuinely true. Used in narrative and evaluative contexts, often with ironic or cautionary force.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
1. **Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
2. **Example:** 假戏真做
3. **Transliteration:** jiǎ xì zhēn zuò
4. **English translation:** To play a fake role as if it were real; to take a pretense seriously
5. **Usage notes:** Describes a situation where someone performs a role or scenario that was meant to be fictional or performative as though it were genuinely real. Common in everyday and narrative speech, often with ironic or cautionary implications.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: 假 (jiǎ)

3. **Transliteration:** Zhè shì jiǎ de
4. **English translation:** This is fake / This is false
5. **Usage notes:** The copular frame X + 是 + 假的 is the basic predication of falsity or inauthenticity in everyday speech. Directly parallel to 这是真的 and equally common across all registers.

**Construction 6**

3. **Transliteration:** dǎ jiǎ

5. **Usage notes:** A verb-object compound meaning to take action against counterfeit goods or fraudulent practices. Extremely common in commercial, regulatory, and journalistic discourse: 打假行动 (anti-counterfeiting campaign), 打假力度 (intensity of the crackdown on fakes).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 7**

3. **Transliteration:** zào jiǎ

5. **Usage notes:** A verb-object compound meaning to produce false or counterfeit items, data, or documents. Widely used in administrative, journalistic, and legal discourse: 数据造假 (data falsification), 学历造假 (falsification of academic credentials).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 9**

3. **Transliteration:** shípò jiǎxiàng
