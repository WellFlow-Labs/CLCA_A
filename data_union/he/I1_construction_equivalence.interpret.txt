## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituting for BECOME

In P4a and P4b, the construction *nimtsa* + predicate adjective (e.g., הַמִּסְמָךְ נִמְצָא מְזוּיָּף, "the document was found to be forged") is described as serving both a BECOME function (recording a change in assessed status) and a REVELATION function (the act of discovering or establishing that status). The two operators are not formally distinguished by separate constructions in Hebrew: the same *nimtsa* + predicate form covers both "turned out to be" (BECOME) and "was found to be" (REVELATION). P4b notes that *nimtsa* is "particularly frequent in legal discourse for recording a change in assessed status," which places it at the intersection of both operators. No dedicated REVELATION verb is attested; the function is distributed across light-verb + object collocations (*legallot et ha-ʾemet*, *laḥsof et ha-ʾemet*) and the *nimtsa* construction.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION sharing constructions

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not have formally distinct constructions in the attested data. VERIFICATION is expressed through: (a) the Pi'el verbal noun אִמּוּת (*immut*, "verification/authentication"), (b) the verb *levarer* + *ha-ʾemet*, and (c) *nimtsa* + predicate adjective in legal contexts. DISCONFIRMATION is expressed through: (a) verbal periphrasis with verbs such as *lesalef*, *leʿavvet* + *ha-ʾemet*, and (b) predicative negation (*lo* + predicate). The *nimtsa* construction is shared between VERIFICATION and BECOME, as noted above. DISCONFIRMATION and NEGATION share the predicative *lo* + predicate construction, though DISCONFIRMATION of a specific claim more typically uses verbal periphrasis with a definite object (*leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet*), while NEGATION of a property uses *lo* + predicate adjective directly.

### 2.3 NEGATION: analytic, not morphological

Negation in the truth domain is consistently analytic. The particle *lo* (לֹא) is the invariant negator for nominal and adjectival predicates in the present tense and precedes the predicate directly. No productive negative prefix applies to truth-domain adjectives in attributive position. The prefix אִי- is productive but is restricted to *-ut* nominalizations (אִי-אֲמִינוּת, אִי-סְבִירוּת, אִי-תִּקִינוּת) and does not attach to adjectives directly. The nominal construction חוֹסֶר + abstract noun provides an alternative attributive-equivalent negation strategy in formal and written registers. The result is a three-way system: predicative *lo* + adjective (syntactic, unrestricted), אִי- + *-ut* nominalization (morphological, restricted to nominals, formal registers), and חוֹסֶר + abstract noun (periphrastic nominal, formal/written registers).

### 2.4 Positive/negative asymmetry in agentive nominalization

The *-an* agentive suffix is productive for negative or characterological agents in the truth domain: שַׁקְרָן ("habitual liar"), דַּיְקָן ("meticulous/precise person"). No lexicalized *-an* agentive for a positive truth-telling agent is attested; the function is served periphrastically by *dover ʾemet* ("one who speaks truth") or by the participial form נֶאֱמָן (*neʾeman*, "trustworthy one"). P4b explicitly notes this asymmetry: "The -an agentive is productive for negative or characterological evaluations: שַׁקְרָן (liar), בַּטְלָן (idler). A parallel agentive for 'truth-teller' is not lexicalized with -an." This asymmetry is one-directional: negative characterological agents are lexicalized; positive ones are not.

### 2.5 CAUSATION: Hifʿil coverage is root-selective

The Hifʿil binyan is the primary morphological causative strategy in Hebrew, but its coverage across truth-domain roots is uneven. Roots such as צ-ד-ק (לְהַצְדִּיק, "to justify/vindicate") and כ-ש-ר (לְהַכְשִׁיר, "to certify as fit") have productive Hifʿil forms. Roots such as ד-י-ק and ז-י-פ operate primarily in the Pi'el and do not have a productive Hifʿil in this domain. No Hifʿil causative is in common use for *ʾemet* or *sheqer* directly. The causative "to make someone believe" is not available from לְהַאֲמִין (*lehaʾamin*), which has lexicalized as "to believe" rather than "to cause to believe"; the causative sense requires periphrastic expression (*lgaram le-mishehu lehaʾamin*). The Hifʿil causative for CAUSATION (factual) is therefore partially available, with periphrastic strategies filling the gaps.

### 2.6 Grammaticalization within the adverbial set

Within the prepositional adverbial construction (*be-* + abstract noun), the items form a gradient of grammaticalization. בְּדִיּוּק (*bediyyuq*, "exactly, precisely") has grammaticalized as a precision adverb and is no longer restricted to truth-domain contexts; it occurs freely as a quantificational modifier (בְּדִיּוּק עֲשָׂרָה, "exactly ten"). בֶּאֱמֶת (*beʾemet*, "truly, really") has acquired pragmatic functions as a discourse particle and sincerity marker beyond its compositional meaning. Other members of the set (בְּכַנּוּת, בְּיֹשֶׁר) remain closer to compositional manner adverbials. This gradient is noted in P4b but does not constitute a structural gap; it is a difference in degree of entrenchment within a single productive pattern.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **BECOME (morphological):** No dedicated inchoative morphological pattern exists for truth-domain adjectives. The inchoative is expressed periphrastically (*hafakh le-*, *naʿasah*) or through general verbal morphology (Hitpaʿel, intransitive verbs). This is a morphosyntactic gap specific to the truth domain; the periphrastic strategies are productive and fill the slot functionally.

- **CAUSATION (factual) — full morphological coverage:** The Hifʿil causative is root-selective and does not cover all truth-domain roots. Roots without a productive Hifʿil require periphrastic causative (*lgaram she-* + clause). This is a morphosyntactic gap in coverage, not a total absence of the operator.

- **NEGATION (attributive morphological):** No productive negative prefix applies directly to truth-domain adjectives in attributive position. Attributive negation requires either predicative repositioning (*lo* + predicate) or nominalization followed by אִי- prefixation. This is a morphosyntactic gap in the attributive slot specifically.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **REVELATION (dedicated verb):** No single dedicated REVELATION verb is attested. The function is distributed across multiple light-verb + object collocations (*legallot*, *laḥsof*, *levarer* + *ha-ʾemet*) and the *nimtsa* construction. This is a lexical gap; circumlocution or light-verb periphrasis is required.

- **VERIFICATION (dedicated verb):** No single dedicated VERIFICATION verb is attested. The function is covered by *levarer* + *ha-ʾemet*, the Pi'el verbal noun אִמּוּת, and *nimtsa* + predicate adjective. This is a lexical gap; the function is expressed through distributed means.

- **AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION — positive truth-teller:** No lexicalized *-an* agentive for a positive truth-telling agent exists. The function is served periphrastically (*dover ʾemet*, מִי שֶׁאוֹמֵר אֱמֶת) or by the participial form נֶאֱמָן. This is a lexical gap within the agentive nominalization paradigm.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the P4a and P4b data. All CLCA operators are expressible in Hebrew; the gaps noted above are lexical or morphosyntactic, not conceptual. No operator is blocked at the level of conceptual structure.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

- **Agentive nominalization (*-an*):** The *-an* suffix is productive for negative or characterological agents (שַׁקְרָן, "habitual liar"; דַּיְקָן, "meticulous person") but has no lexicalized positive counterpart for truth-telling. The negative pole is lexicalized; the positive pole is not. This is a one-directional asymmetric lexical gap.

- **Hifʿil causative coverage:** The Hifʿil causative is attested for roots associated with justification, certification, and misleading (לְהַצְדִּיק, לְהַכְשִׁיר, לְהַטְעוֹת) but is not productive for the core truth/falsity roots (*ʾemet*, *sheqer*) directly. The causative operator is available for peripheral roots but not for the central lexical items of the domain.

- **Adverbial grammaticalization:** Within the *be-* + abstract noun adverbial set, grammaticalization is asymmetric: בְּדִיּוּק has grammaticalized furthest (quantificational modifier, unrestricted contexts), while other members of the same pattern remain closer to compositional manner adverbials. This is not a gap but a noted asymmetry in entrenchment within a single construction type.
