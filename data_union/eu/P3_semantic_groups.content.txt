# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed in the P1 and P2 documentation. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns support multiple groupings for a single item, this is noted explicitly. Where no clear grouping is supported, items are listed in the ungrouped section with a brief note.

---

## Group 1: Paired truth-assertion and falsity-assertion formulas

**Label:** Paired truth-assertion and falsity-assertion formulas / Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement is true

**Definition:** These expressions function as the primary predicative formulas for asserting that something is true or false in Basque. They appear in direct parallel syntactic environments (*egia da* / *gezurra da*), are explicitly contrasted with each other, and together constitute the baseline binary for propositional evaluation in everyday, legal, and philosophical discourse. They are used in argumentative discourse, testimony, and everyday factual exchange. The pair is so tightly linked that derivational families, collocations, and discourse functions mirror each other systematically.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egia* — "truth" (primary nominal for truth)
- *egia da* — "it is true; that is true" (predicative assertion of propositional truth)
- *egia esan* — "to tell the truth" (verbal collocation asserting honest disclosure)
- *egia hutsa* — "the plain truth"
- *egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik* — "the whole truth and nothing but the truth" (legal oath formula)
- *egia esateko* — "to tell the truth; frankly; to be honest" (purposive discourse marker)
- *egia da, baina...* — "it is true, but..." (concessive construction acknowledging truth while introducing qualification)
- *egia da ala ez?* — binary truth question formula
- *egia da ala gezurra?* — "is it true or false?" (binary interrogative framing)
- *gezurra* — "lie, falsehood" (primary nominal for falsehood)
- *gezurra da* — "it is a lie; it is false" (predicative denial of propositional truth)
- *gezurra esan* — "to tell a lie" (verbal collocation asserting dishonest disclosure)
- *gezurra zabaldu* — "to spread a lie"
- *gezurra eta egia nahastu* — "to mix truth and lies"
- *gezurretan ari da* — "he/she is lying"
- *gezurra txuri* — "white lie"
- *gezurra sinestu* — "to believe a lie"
- *oker egon* — "to be wrong; to be mistaken" (predicative assertion of incorrectness)
- *zuzen egon* — "to be right; to be correct" (predicative assertion of correctness)
- *oker nago* — "I am wrong; I am mistaken" (first-person acknowledgment of error)
- *ziur nago* — "I am sure; I am certain" (first-person assertion of epistemic confidence)
- *ziur ez egon* — "to not be sure; to be uncertain" (negative epistemic state)
- *oker ez banago* — "if I am not mistaken; unless I am wrong" (epistemic hedge)

**Notes on internal structure:** The parallelism is morphological (*egia*/*gezurra* both take *esan*, *da*, *aitortu*, *gorde*/*harrapatu*), derivational (*egiazkoa*/*gezurrezkoa*; *egiatasuna*/*gezurtasuna*), and discursive (both appear in legal oath formulas, both serve as predicative complements). The construction *gezurra eta egia nahastu* presupposes the pair as a contrast set. *Ziur nago* and *ziur ez egon* shade into Group 2 (epistemic certainty expressions) and are listed in both. *Zuzen* and *oker* appear here in their propositional-correctness uses, but both terms have additional uses in other contexts (see Groups 5 and 9).

## Group 2: Adjectival forms used to characterize statements, objects, or persons as true/false / Expressions used to indicate degree of epistemic certainty or uncertainty

**Label:** Adjectival characterization of truth or falsity; epistemic certainty and uncertainty expressions

**Definition:** This group encompasses two related clusters. The first consists of adjectival forms derived from the truth/falsity roots that are used attributively or predicatively to characterize statements, objects, or persons as truthful, genuine, false, or fake. They are distinguished from the nominal forms in Group 1 by their explicitly adjectival function and their range of application. The second cluster consists of expressions used to calibrate how confident a speaker is in a proposition, ranging from full certainty to acknowledged uncertainty. A notable feature is that *ziurrenik*, derived from the root meaning "certain," functions in practice as a probability hedge ("probably," "most likely") rather than as an assertion of full certainty.

**Representative expressions:**

*Adjectival truth/falsity forms:*
- *egiazkoa* — "true, truthful, genuine" (adjectival)
- *egiazko* — indefinite attributive form
- *egiazkotasuna* — "truthfulness, genuineness" (abstract nominal)
- *egiazki* — "truly, genuinely" (adverbial)
- *egiazko informazioa zabaldu* — "to spread true information"
- *egiazko adiskidea* — "a true friend"
- *egiazko arrazoia* — "the real reason"
- *gezurrezkoa* — "false, lying, deceptive" (adjectival)
- *gezurrezko* — indefinite attributive form
- *gezurrezko lekukotasuna* — "false testimony, perjury"
- *gezurrezko informazioa zabaldu* — "to spread false information"
- *gezurrezko agindua* — "a false promise"
- *gezurtasuna* — "falseness, mendacity"
- *gezurrezko agiriak erabili* — "to use false documents; to use forged papers" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko lekukotasuna eman* — "to give false testimony; to provide false witness" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko itxura eman* — "to give a false appearance; to create a false impression" (deception collocation)

*Epistemic certainty/uncertainty expressions:*
- *ziur egon* — "to be sure; to be certain" (predicative certainty)
- *ziur nago* — "I am sure; I am certain" (first-person certainty)
- *ziur asko* — "most certainly; very likely; almost certainly" (intensified certainty or high probability)
- *ziurrenik* — "certainly; most likely; probably" (epistemic adverb, frequently used as probability hedge)
- *ziur-ziur* — "absolutely certain; for sure; definitely" (reduplicated emphatic certainty)
- *ziur samar egon* — "to be fairly sure; to be reasonably certain" (hedged certainty)
- *ziur al zaude?* — "are you sure?" (interrogative of certainty)
- *ziur ez dakit* — "I don't know for sure; I'm not certain" (acknowledged uncertainty)
- *ziurtasun osoz* — "with complete certainty; with full assurance" (adverbial full certainty)
- *ziurtasun eza* — "uncertainty; lack of certainty" (nominal absence of certainty)
- *ziurtasun falta* — "lack of certainty; deficiency of assurance" (nominal absence of certainty)
- *antza du* — "it seems; it appears; it looks like" (hedged epistemic assertion)
- *antza denez* — "apparently; it seems; as it appears" (parenthetical epistemic hedge)

**Notes:** *Egiazkoa* and *gezurrezkoa* are the adjectival counterparts of *egia* and *gezurra* respectively, and their usage patterns mirror each other. The distinction between Group 1 and this group is functional: Group 1 items are used as nominals in predicative formulas; this group's adjectival items are used as adjectives modifying nouns or as predicative adjectives. *Antza du* and *antza denez* appear in both this group and Group 9 (appearance vs. reality). *Ziurrenik* is notable for straddling certainty and probability. *Egiazkoa* also appears in Groups 3, 7, and 8 because the adjective is used across all these domains with somewhat different emphases depending on the noun it modifies.

## Group 3: Expressions used in administrative and legal certification contexts

**Label:** Expressions used in administrative and legal certification contexts; expressions used to assert or question authenticity of objects and identities

**Definition:** These expressions appear in official, administrative, and legal contexts where the formal confirmation, authentication, or validation of documents, facts, qualifications, or identities is at issue. They are associated with institutional procedures rather than with personal epistemic states or interpersonal moral evaluation. They also appear in legal, administrative, and artistic contexts where the question is whether a physical object, document, or work is the genuine article rather than a reproduction or forgery. The most distinctive item is *ziurtagiri* (certificate), which has become the standard term for official certification documents across multiple domains.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egiaztatu* — "to verify; to confirm; to authenticate; to certify" (transitive verb of verification)
- *egiaztapen* — "verification; confirmation; attestation" (result nominal of verification)
- *egiaztatze* — "verification; the process of confirming" (process nominal of verification)
- *egiaztagarri* / *egiaztagarria* — "verifiable; capable of verification" (adjectival form)
- *egiaztaezin* / *egiaztaezina* — "unverifiable; impossible to confirm" (negative potential adjectival form)
- *ziurtatu* — "to certify; to assure; to make sure; to confirm" (transitive verb of certification)
- *ziurtapen* — "certification; assurance; confirmation" (result nominal of certification)
- *ziurtatze* — "certifying; the process of certification" (process nominal)
- *ziurtagiri* — "certificate; certification document" (document noun)
- *ziurtagiri digitala* — "digital certificate" (technical domain extension)
- *ziurtagiri medikua* — "medical certificate" (administrative domain)
- *ziurtagiri akademikoa* — "academic certificate" (educational domain)
- *ziurtatu gabeko albistea* — "unverified news; unconfirmed report" (journalistic use)
- *ziurtatu gabeko* — "uncertified; unconfirmed; unverified" (participial construction)
- *ofizialki ziurtatu* — "to officially certify; to formally confirm" (adverbially specified certification)
- *baliozko agiria* — "a valid document; a legitimate document" (nominal collocation)
- *baliozkotu* — "to validate; to ratify; to authenticate" (verb of validation)
- *baliogabe* — "invalid; null; void; worthless" (negative validity adjective)
- *baliogabetu* — "to invalidate; to annul; to void; to nullify" (verb of invalidation)
- *baliozkotzat jo* — "to consider as valid; to regard as legitimate" (translative construction)
- *egiazko kopiak ziurtatu* — "to certify true copies; to authenticate copies" (combined certification collocation)
- *egiazkotzat jo* — "to consider as true; to take as genuine" (translative construction)
- *egiazkotzat onartu* — "to accept as true; to acknowledge as genuine" (formal acceptance construction)
- *behar bezala egiaztatu* — "to verify properly; to verify as required" (procedural specification)
- *ziurtasun juridikoa* — "legal certainty; juridical certainty" (legal principle)
- *ziurtasun juridikoa bermatu* — "to guarantee legal certainty" (legal obligation construction)
- *faltsifikatu* — "to falsify; to forge; to counterfeit" (verb of document falsification)
- *agiriak faltsifikatu* — "to falsify documents; to forge papers" (most common collocational context)
- *faltsifikazioa* / *faltsifikatze* / *faltsifikazio* — "falsification; forgery" (nominals of falsification)
- *ziurtatze prozesua* — "certification process" (procedural nominal)
- *benetakoa* — "genuine, real, authentic" (overlaps with Groups 1 and 9)
- *benetako* — indefinite attributive form
- *benetakotasuna* — "genuineness, authenticity"
- *benetako izena* — "the real name"
- *benetakoa al da?* — "Is it real? Is it genuine?"
- *autentikoa* — "authentic, genuine"
- *autentikotasuna* — "authenticity"
- *autentifikatu* — "to authenticate"
- *autentifikazioa* — "authentication"
- *autentikoa dela egiaztatu* — "to verify that it is authentic"
- *autentikotasuna zalantzan jarri* — "to call authenticity into question"
- *arte lan autentikoa* — "an authentic work of art"
- *faltsua* — "false, fake, counterfeit" (primary antonym for object-authenticity in this group)
- *dokumentu faltsua* — "a forged document"
- *nortasun faltsua* — "false identity"
- *egiaztatua* — "verified, certified"
- *egiaztagiri* — "certificate of verification"
- *ofizialki egiaztatua* — "officially verified"
- *jatorrizkoa* — "original, source"
- *kopiakoa* — "copy, reproduction"
- *jatorrizko dokumentua aurkeztu* — "to present the original document"
- *kopia eta jatorrizkoa* — "the copy and the original"
- *kopia hutsa da* — "it is a mere copy"
- *kopiatu* — "to copy"
- *egiazko argazkia* — "authentic photograph"
- *egiazko historia* — "true story, genuine account"
- *egiaztatze-agiri* — "verification document"
- *baliozkoa dela egiaztatu* — "to verify that it is valid"
- *benetakoa dela frogatu* — "to prove that it is genuine"

**Notes:** *Egiaztatu* and *ziurtatu* have overlapping but distinct emphases: *egiaztatu* focuses on confirming factual content, while *ziurtatu* emphasizes assurance and formal confirmation. In practice, both appear in administrative certification contexts. *Faltsifikatu* and its nominals appear here as the legal counterpart (falsification of documents). *Benetakoa* and *autentikoa* are near-synonyms but *autentikoa* tends toward formal and legal registers while *benetakoa* is more common in everyday speech. *Faltsua* applied to documents and objects belongs here; when the focus is on the deliberate act of forgery it also belongs to Group 12.

## Group 4: Expressions used in verification and proof contexts

**Label:** Verification and proof expressions; expressions used in contexts of formal or procedural verification

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the establishment of truth or fact through evidence, testing, or official procedure is at issue. They appear in legal, scientific, journalistic, and administrative registers, and they co-occur with terms for evidence, documents, data, and official procedures. They are distinct from Group 3 in that they concern the process of establishing truth rather than the assertion of it, and distinct from Group 10 (legal validity) in that the focus is on the process of establishing truth rather than on the status of the thing.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egiaztatu* — "to verify, to confirm, to certify" (overlaps with Group 3)
- *egiaztatzea* — "verification, verifying"
- *egiaztapen* — "verification, certification, result"
- *egiaztagarria* — "verifiable"
- *egiaztaezina* — "unverifiable"
- *egiaztagiri* — "certificate of verification"
- *froga* — "proof, evidence"
- *frogatu* — "to prove, to demonstrate, to test"
- *frogatzea* — "proving, demonstrating"
- *frogagarria* — "provable, demonstrable"
- *frogaezina* — "unprovable"
- *frogapen* — "proof as result"
- *frogarik gabe* — "without proof, unproven"
- *frogazko* — "evidentiary, relating to proof"
- *frogabidea* — "means of proof"
- *frogatugabea* — "unproven"
- *frogatzeko zama* — "burden of proof"
- *froga bat aurkeztu* — "to present evidence"
- *egiaztatzeko eskatu* — "to request verification"
- *egiaztatu gabe utzi* — "to leave unverified; to leave without verification"
- *fidagarria dela frogatu* — "to prove that it is reliable"
- *faltsua dela frogatu* — "to prove that it is false"
- *autentikoa dela egiaztatu* — "to verify that it is authentic"
- *datuak egiaztatu* — "to verify the data"
- *balio probatorioa* — "probative value"

**Notes:** *Egiaztatu* and *frogatu* are the two primary verbs in this group. *Egiaztatu* tends toward official certification and administrative verification, while *frogatu* tends toward demonstration through evidence in legal and scientific contexts. Both appear in constructions with *dela* subordinate clauses, which is a shared syntactic pattern. *Egiaztagarria* and *frogagarria* are parallel *-garri* forms within this group.

## Group 5: Expressions used to characterize something as false, fake, or spurious

**Label:** Expressions used to characterize something as false, fake, or spurious

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate objects, documents, statements, or representations as not being what they claim to be — as counterfeit, fabricated, or spuriously presented. They appear in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts where the distinction between genuine and fake is at issue. This group is distinct from Group 11 (moral evaluation of persons) in that the focus is on the thing or claim rather than on the person making it, though there is overlap.

**Representative expressions:**
- *faltsu* — "false; fake; counterfeit; insincere" (general falseness adjective)
- *faltsutasun* — "falseness; falsity; inauthenticity" (abstract noun)
- *faltsuki* — "falsely; insincerely; deceptively" (adverb)
- *gezurrezko* — "false; lying; counterfeit; fake" (relational adjective of falsehood)
- *gezurrezko agiriak erabili* — "to use false documents; to use forged papers" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko lekukotasuna eman* — "to give false testimony; to provide false witness" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko itxura eman* — "to give a false appearance; to create a false impression" (deception collocation)
- *sasi-* — "pseudo-; false; spurious; sham" (productive prefix)
- *sasi-jakintsu* — "pseudo-scholar; charlatan; fake expert" (person compound)
- *sasi-zientzia* — "pseudoscience" (discipline compound)
- *sasi-demokrazia* — "pseudo-democracy; sham democracy" (institution compound)
- *sasi-mediku* — "fake doctor; quack" (person compound)
- *sasi-lege* — "pseudo-law; spurious legislation" (legal compound)
- *faltsutzat jo* — "to consider as false; to regard as fake" (translative construction)
- *faltsu agertu* — "to appear false; to turn out to be fake" (revelation construction)
- *gezurtatu* — "to give the lie to; to contradict; to prove false" (verb of refutation)
- *egiazko izena* — "real name; true name" (implicitly contrasting with false name)
- *egiazko sinadura* — "genuine signature; authentic signature" (implicitly contrasting with forged)
- *lekuko faltsua* — "false witness"
- *dokumentu faltsua aurkeztu* — "to present a forged document"

**Notes:** *Sasi-* is distinctive in this group for being a prefix rather than a free form, and for its productivity across nominal categories. *Egiazko izena* and *egiazko sinadura* are included here because their primary function in usage is to distinguish genuine from false identity markers, even though they are formally positive (*egiazko*) rather than negative. *Faltsifikatu* and its nominals appear in Group 3 (administrative/legal certification) rather than here, because their usage is more specifically tied to document forgery procedures. *Faltsu* and *faltsu jokatu* also appear in Group 11 (moral evaluation of persons), but their use in evaluating things' authenticity places them here as well.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate the credibility of sources and testimony

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate the credibility of sources and testimony; expressions used to evaluate whether something can be trusted or believed

**Definition:** These expressions appear in journalistic, legal, and analytical contexts where the question is whether a source, witness, or account deserves to be believed or relied upon. They are used to assess the reliability of information and its origins rather than to evaluate the truth of a specific proposition directly. The contrast between *fidagarri* (trustworthy source) and *sinesgarri* (believable content) is a distinction in usage, though the two overlap. They are distinct from Group 11 (which concerns character) in that they focus on the epistemic relationship between an evaluator and a source or claim rather than on the intrinsic moral quality of a person.

**Representative expressions:**
- *fidagarri* / *fidagarria* — "reliable; trustworthy; dependable; credible" (source trustworthiness adjective)
- *fidagarritasun* / *fidagarritasuna* — "reliability; trustworthiness; dependability" (abstract noun)
- *fidaezin* — "untrustworthy; unreliable" (negative potential form)
- *fidagaitz* / *fidagaitza* — "untrustworthy; hard to trust; unreliable" (difficulty-of-trust form)
- *fidatu* — "to trust, to rely on"
- *fidatu niri* — "trust me"
- *fidagarritasun falta* — "lack of reliability"
- *iturri fidagarria* — "a reliable source; a trustworthy source" (standard journalistic collocation)
- *iturri fidagarrietatik jaso* — "to receive from reliable sources" (sourcing collocation)
- *iturri fidagarrietatik jasotako informazioa* — "information from reliable sources"
- *iturri fidagarrien arabera* — "according to reliable sources"
- *fidagarria ez den informazioa* — "information that is not reliable; unreliable information" (relative clause evaluation)
- *sinesgarri* / *sinesgarria* — "believable; credible; plausible; convincing" (content believability adjective)
- *sinesgarritasun* / *sinesgarritasuna* — "credibility; believability; plausibility" (abstract noun)
- *sinesgaitz* / *sineskaitza* — "unbelievable; hard to believe; implausible" (difficulty form)
- *sinetsi* — "to believe, to trust"
- *sinesmen* — "belief, faith"
- *sinesteko modukoa* — "believable, worth believing"
- *lekuko sinesgarria* — "a credible witness; a believable witness" (legal collocation)
- *sinesgarritasuna galdu* — "to lose credibility" (credibility-loss collocation)
- *sinesgarritasuna irabazi* — "to gain credibility; to earn believability" (credibility-gain collocation)
- *ez da batere sinesgarria* — "it is not at all credible; it is completely unbelievable" (emphatic negative)
- *ez da sinesgarria* — "it is not credible"
- *sinesgarria iruditu* — "to seem credible"
- *sinesgarria iruditzen zait* — "it seems credible to me"
- *sinesgarria ez den istorioa* — "a story that is not credible"
- *egiazko lekukotasuna eman* — "to give true testimony; to provide genuine witness" (legal collocation)
- *egiaztatu ezin den informazioa* — "information that cannot be verified; unverifiable information" (journalistic evaluation)
- *egiaztatu gabe utzi* — "to leave unverified; to leave without verification" (journalistic failure collocation)

**Notes:** *Fidagarri* and *sinesgarri* have a usage distinction: *fidagarri* evaluates the source, *sinesgarri* evaluates the content or claim. Both appear in this group because in practice they co-occur in the same evaluative contexts (legal testimony, journalistic sourcing). *Fidagarri* also appears in Group 11 (moral evaluation of persons) and is listed in both. *Egiazko lekukotasuna eman* appears here because its primary context is legal testimony evaluation. *Sinesmen* also connects this group to religious belief contexts (see ungrouped items).

## Group 7: Expressions used to contrast appearance with underlying reality

**Label:** Appearance-versus-reality expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the outward appearance of something is distinguished from what is actually the case. They appear in moral, analytical, literary, and everyday contexts where the speaker is signaling that surface impressions may be misleading or that something presents itself as other than it is. They are distinguished from Group 8 (hedging) by their focus on the contrast itself rather than on the speaker's epistemic position. The base *itxura* (appearance, semblance) is central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *itxura* — "appearance; semblance; look; form; pretense" (base noun)
- *itxurazko* — "apparent; seeming; superficial; ostensible" (relational adjective)
- *itxurazko adostasuna* — "apparent agreement; superficial consensus" (analytical collocation)
- *itxurazko lasaitasuna* — "apparent calm; seeming tranquility" (literary collocation)
- *itxura eman* — "to give the appearance of; to make it seem; to create the impression of" (deception/impression verb)
- *itxura ona eman* — "to make a good impression; to give a good appearance" (social impression verb)
- *itxurak gorde* — "to keep up appearances; to maintain the facade" (social pretense collocation)
- *itxurak salbu* — "appearances aside; setting aside appearances" (analytical framing)
- *itxura batean* — "at first glance; on the surface; apparently" (adverbial framing)
- *itxura guztien arabera* — "by all appearances; to all appearances" (evidential framing)
- *itxurak engainatzen du* — "appearances deceive; looks are deceiving" (proverbial)
- *itxuratu* — "to disguise; to simulate; to make appear; to feign" (verb of appearance-creation)
- *itxuragabe* — "shapeless; shameless; brazen" (absence-of-appearance form)
- *itxurakeria* — "pretense; affectation; keeping up appearances; hypocrisy" (pejorative practice noun)
- *itxurakoa* — "apparent, seeming, superficial, of mere appearance"
- *itxura egin* — "to pretend, to feign"
- *itxura eta errealitatea* — "appearance and reality"
- *itxurakeria hutsa da* — "it is pure pretense"
- *itxurakeria egiten ari da* — "he/she is being hypocritical"
- *itxura hutsean* — "in mere appearance only"
- *itxuraz besterik ez* — "only in appearance"
- *itxuragilea* — "pretender, impostor, maker of appearances" [MARGINAL]
- *itxura batean ondo dirudi, baina...* — "at first glance it looks fine, but..." (formulaic concessive pattern)
- *antza du* — "it seems; it appears; it looks like" (hedged appearance assertion)
- *antza denez* — "apparently; it seems; as it appears" (parenthetical hedge)
- *egiazko arazoa* — "the real problem; the genuine issue" (implicitly contrasting with apparent problem)
- *benetako arrazoia ezkutatu* — "to hide the real reason; to conceal the genuine motive" (concealment of reality)
- *egia gordea* — "the hidden truth; the concealed truth" (concealed reality noun)
- *egia argitara atera* — "to bring the truth to light; to reveal the truth" (revelation of hidden reality)
- *benetakoa* — used as the positive pole contrasting with mere appearance
- *benetako helburua* — "the real goal" (as opposed to stated goal)
- *egiazko arrazoia* — "the real reason" (as opposed to apparent reason)
- *benetan ala itxuraz?* — "really or just apparently?" (sincerity interrogative)

**Notes:** *Antza du* and *antza denez* appear in both Group 2 (epistemic certainty/uncertainty) and here, because their function as epistemic hedges is related to but distinct from their function as appearance-markers. *Egiazko arazoa* and *benetako arrazoia ezkutatu* appear here because their usage specifically involves the contrast between surface and underlying reality. *Itxurakeria* also appears in Group 11 (moral evaluation of persons). *Itxuragilea* is marked [MARGINAL] in P1. *Egia gordea* and *egia argitara atera* also appear in Group 10 (truth-telling as obligation/practice).

## Group 8: Expressions used to assert genuineness or authenticity of persons, relationships, or feelings

**Label:** Expressions used to assert genuineness or authenticity of persons, relationships, or feelings

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize persons, relationships, feelings, or cultural expressions as sincere, non-artificial, and genuinely what they present themselves as being. They are distinct from Group 5 (false/fake characterizations) in being the positive pole, and distinct from Group 3 (administrative certification) in that they operate in interpersonal, affective, and cultural domains rather than in institutional ones. The *benetako* / *benetan* cluster is central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *benetako* — "genuine; real; authentic; true" (genuineness adjective)
