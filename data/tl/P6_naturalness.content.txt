# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## DYNAMIC EXPRESSIONS IN THE TRUTH DOMAIN

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / REALIZATION

### Pattern
**Verbal constructions with *mag-* or *tum-* prefixes indicating realization or coming true**: The root *totoo* combines with verbal prefixes to express the process of becoming true or being realized. Progressive aspect with reduplication indicates ongoing realization.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Nagtotoo ang panaginip ko.**
  - Transliteration: nagtotoo ang panaginip ko
  - Translation: "My dream is coming true." / "My dream is being realized."
  - Notes: *Nag-* (actor voice) + *totoo* with progressive reduplication. Very common for dreams, predictions, wishes.

#### Example 2
- **Tumutuoo ang hula.**
  - Transliteration: tumutuoo ang hula
  - Translation: "The prediction is coming true." / "The prophecy is being realized."
  - Notes: *Tum-* (intransitive actor voice) + *totoo* with progressive reduplication. Alternative to *nagtotoo*.

#### Example 3
- **Magkatotoo ang iyong pangarap.**
  - Transliteration: magkatotoo ang iyong pangarap
  - Translation: "May your dream come true." / "Your dream will be realized."
  - Notes: *Magka-* (accidental/resultative prefix) + *totoo*. Often used in wishes or hopes for realization.

#### Example 4
- **Nagtotoo na ang sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: nagtotoo na ang sinabi niya
  - Translation: "What he/she said is now coming true." / "His/her statement is being realized."
  - Notes: Addition of *na* (already/now) marks current realization of previously made statement.

#### Example 5
- **Tumuoo ang lahat ng sinabi mo.**
  - Transliteration: tumuoo ang lahat ng sinabi mo
  - Translation: "Everything you said came true."
  - Notes: Completed aspect (no reduplication) indicates full realization has occurred.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - These constructions are very common in everyday speech, particularly for dreams, predictions, and prophecies.

### Notes on Usage
- The realization sense is specific to *totoo*; other truth-domain terms do not productively form "becoming true" expressions
- *Nagtotoo* and *tumutuoo* are largely interchangeable, with *nagtotoo* slightly more common
- *Magkatotoo* often appears in optative/hortative contexts (wishes, hopes)
- The construction emphasizes the dynamic process of manifestation rather than static truth-value

## 2. BECOMING CORRECT / ACHIEVING CORRECTNESS

### Pattern
**Verbal construction with *tum-* prefix indicating achievement of correctness**: The root *tama* combines with *tum-* to express hitting the mark or achieving correctness, particularly in guessing or prediction contexts.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Tumama ang hula ko.**
  - Transliteration: tumama ang hula ko
  - Translation: "My guess was correct." / "I guessed right."
  - Notes: Completed aspect. Common for correct guesses, predictions, or estimates.

#### Example 2
- **Tumama ka sa sagot.**
  - Transliteration: tumama ka sa sagot
  - Translation: "You got the answer right." / "You hit upon the correct answer."
  - Notes: *Sa* marks the goal/target of correctness.

#### Example 3
- **Tumatama ang kanyang palagay.**
  - Transliteration: tumatama ang kanyang palagay
  - Translation: "His/her assumption is proving correct." / "His/her hunch is right."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication indicates ongoing validation.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - Common in contexts of guessing, prediction, and estimation.

### Notes on Usage
- **POLYSEMY WARNING**: *Tumama* also means "to hit/strike" (physical contact). Context disambiguates.
- The correctness sense emphasizes achievement or discovery of the right answer
- Does not extend to moral or normative correctness, only factual/practical correctness
- Other correctness terms (*wasto*, *hustó*) do not productively form "becoming correct" expressions

## 3. MAKING A MISTAKE / BECOMING WRONG

### Pattern
**Verbal construction with *magka-* prefix indicating accidental error**: The root *mali* combines with *magka-* to express the act of making a mistake or becoming wrong/mistaken.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Nagkamali ako.**
  - Transliteration: nagkamali ako
  - Translation: "I made a mistake." / "I was wrong."
  - Notes: Completed aspect. Extremely common expression for acknowledging errors.

#### Example 2
- **Nagkakamali ka.**
  - Transliteration: nagkakamali ka
  - Translation: "You're making a mistake." / "You're being wrong."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication indicates ongoing or habitual error-making.

#### Example 3
- **Baka magkamali ako.**
  - Transliteration: baka magkamali ako
  - Translation: "I might make a mistake." / "I might be wrong."
  - Notes: *Baka* (might/maybe) + contemplated aspect. Common in hedging or expressing uncertainty.

#### Example 4
- **Huwag kang magkamali.**
  - Transliteration: huwag kang magkamali
  - Translation: "Don't make a mistake." / "Don't be wrong."
  - Notes: Negative imperative. Warning against error.

#### Example 5
- **Nagkamali siya sa pagkakaintindi.**
  - Transliteration: nagkamali siya sa pagkakaintindi
  - Translation: "He/she made a mistake in understanding." / "He/she misunderstood."
  - Notes: *Sa* marks the domain of the error.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - *Magkamali* is one of the most frequent verbal expressions in the truth domain.

### Notes on Usage
- The *magka-* prefix suggests accidental or unintentional error (vs. deliberate wrongdoing)
- Highly lexicalized; speakers treat *magkamali* as a unit rather than productive derivation
- No corresponding "become correct" expression with *tama* using *magka-* (*magkatama* exists but means "to happen to be correct by chance" and is much less common)

## 4. CAUSING TO BE TRUE / PROVING

### Pattern
**Causative construction with *pa-* prefix combined with voice marking**: The root *tunay* combines with causative *pa-* and voice suffixes to express causing something to be proven or verified.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo.**
  - Transliteration: patunayan mo ang sinabi mo
  - Translation: "Prove what you said." / "Verify your statement."
  - Notes: Imperative form. *Pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an* (locative voice). Very common directive.

#### Example 2
- **Pinatunayan niya ang katotohanan.**
  - Transliteration: pinatunayan niya ang katotohanan
  - Translation: "He/she proved the truth." / "He/she verified the truth."
  - Notes: Completed aspect, patient voice (*pi-* + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*).

#### Example 3
- **Napatunayan niya ang kanyang punto.**
  - Transliteration: napatunayan niya ang kanyang punto
  - Translation: "He/she was able to prove his/her point." / "He/she successfully verified his/her point."
  - Notes: Abilitative completed aspect (*na-* + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*). Emphasizes successful completion.

#### Example 4
- **Pinapatunayan niya ang kanyang salita.**
  - Transliteration: pinapatunayan niya ang kanyang salita
  - Translation: "He/she is proving his/her word." / "He/she is verifying his/her statement."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication. Ongoing proving action.

#### Example 5
- **Mapatunayan ko ang aking inoseniya.**
  - Transliteration: mapatunayan ko ang aking inoseniya
  - Translation: "I can prove my innocence." / "I am able to verify my innocence."
  - Notes: Abilitative contemplated aspect (*ma-* + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*).

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - *Patunayan* and its inflected forms are very common in legal, investigative, and argumentative contexts.

### Notes on Usage
- Highly lexicalized; *patunayan* functions as a unit meaning "to prove/verify"
- The causative semantics ("cause to be genuine/true") has specialized to evidential/epistemic meaning
- Other truth-domain roots do not productively form causatives with this meaning
- The construction requires appropriate voice marking for grammatical focus

## 5. CAUSING TO BE REALIZED / MAKING COME TRUE

### Pattern
**Causative construction with *totoo* (limited productivity)**: Causative forms with *totoo* are possible but less common than the inchoative forms. The construction emphasizes causing realization rather than natural occurrence.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Pinatotohanan niya ang kanyang pangako.**
  - Transliteration: pinatotohanan niya ang kanyang pangako
  - Translation: "He/she is making his/her promise come true." / "He/she is realizing his/her promise."
  - Notes: Progressive patient voice (*pi-* + *pa-* + *totoo* + reduplication + *-an*). Emphasizes active effort to realize.

#### Example 2
- **Pakatotohanin mo ang sinabi mo.**
  - Transliteration: pakatotohanin mo ang sinabi mo
  - Translation: "Make what you said come true." / "Realize what you said."
  - Notes: Imperative with intensifying *paka-* + causative *pa-* + *totoo* + *-in*. Emphatic directive.

### Epistemic Status
[MARGINAL] - These causative forms are less common than the inchoative forms (*nagtotoo*, *tumutuoo*). They appear in more formal or literary registers.

### Notes on Usage
- Less frequent than inchoative constructions with *totoo*
- Emphasizes deliberate action to cause realization vs. natural occurrence
- The construction is grammatically possible but pragmatically restricted

## 6. CAUSING CORRECTNESS / CORRECTING

### Pattern
**Causative/benefactive constructions with correctness terms**: Limited productivity. Some roots allow causative formations, but these are often lexicalized with specialized meanings.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Itama mo ang sagot.**
  - Transliteration: itama mo ang sagot
  - Translation: "Correct the answer." / "Fix the answer."
  - Notes: *I-* (benefactive/causative voice) + *tama*. Common directive for making corrections.

#### Example 2
- **Tinatama niya ang kanyang pagkakamali.**
  - Transliteration: tinatama niya ang kanyang pagkakamali
  - Translation: "He/she is correcting his/her mistake." / "He/she is fixing his/her error."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication. Ongoing correction action.

#### Example 3
- **Wastuhan mo ang ulat.**
  - Transliteration: wastuhan mo ang ulat
  - Translation: "Correct the report." / "Make the report proper."
  - Notes: *Wasto* + *-han* (locative/benefactive). More formal register than *itama*.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] for *itama*; [MARGINAL] for *wastuhan*

### Notes on Usage
- *Itama* is very common for making corrections or fixing errors
- The construction focuses on the action of correcting rather than the state of being correct
- *Wastuhan* is less common and more formal than *itama*
- Other correctness terms (*hustó*) do not productively form causative constructions

## 7. CAUSING DECEPTION / DECEIVING

### Pattern
**Verbal constructions with *mang-* or *mag-* prefixes indicating causing deception**: Deception roots combine with verbal prefixes to express the act of deceiving or causing false beliefs.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Manlinlang siya ng tao.**
  - Transliteration: manlinlang siya ng tao
  - Translation: "He/she deceives people." / "He/she tricks people."
  - Notes: *Mang-* (actor voice) + *linlang*. Habitual or characteristic action.

#### Example 2
- **Linlangin mo siya.**
  - Transliteration: linlangin mo siya
  - Translation: "Deceive him/her." / "Trick him/her."
  - Notes: *Linlang* + *-in* (patient voice). Directive focusing on the victim.

#### Example 3
- **Nanlilinlang siya sa akin.**
  - Transliteration: nanlilinlang siya sa akin
  - Translation: "He/she is deceiving me." / "He/she is tricking me."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect (*nan-* + *linlang* with reduplication). Ongoing deception.

#### Example 4
- **Mandaya siya sa laro.**
  - Transliteration: mandaya siya sa laro
  - Translation: "He/she cheats in the game." / "He/she commits fraud in the game."
  - Notes: *Mang-* + *daya* (→ *mandaya*). Habitual cheating action.

#### Example 5
- **Dayain mo ang sistema.**
  - Transliteration: dayain mo ang sistema
  - Translation: "Cheat the system." / "Defraud the system."
  - Notes: *Daya* + *-in* (patient voice). Focus on the object being cheated.

#### Example 6
- **Magsinungaling siya.**
  - Transliteration: magsinungaling siya
  - Translation: "He/she lies." / "He/she tells lies."
  - Notes: *Mag-* + *sinungaling*. Common expression for the act of lying.

#### Example 7
- **Bulaan mo siya.**
  - Transliteration: bulaan mo siya
  - Translation: "Lie to him/her." / "Deceive him/her."
  - Notes: *Bula* (root) + *-an* (locative voice). Focus on the person being lied to.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - These verbal constructions are very common in everyday speech for describing dishonest actions.

### Notes on Usage
- Different roots show different preferred verbal patterns
- *Manlinlang* and *mandaya* use *mang-* for habitual/characteristic actions
- *Magsinungaling* is highly lexicalized and extremely frequent
- Patient voice forms (*linlangin*, *dayain*, *bulaan*) focus on the victim or object of deception

## 8. CAUSING PRETENSE / PRETENDING

### Examples

#### Example 1

#### Example 2
- **Nagkukunwari siya.**
  - Transliteration: nagkukunwari siya
  - Translation: "He/she is pretending." / "He/she is feigning."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication. Ongoing pretense.

#### Example 3
- **Magpalabas ng kahinaan.**
  - Transliteration: magpalabas ng kahinaan
  - Translation: "Pretend to be weak." / "Put on a show of weakness."
  - Notes: *Mag-* + *palabas* (show/pretense). Causative sense of creating false appearance.

#### Example 4
- **Nagpapalabas siya ng yaman.**
  - Transliteration: nagpapalabas siya ng yaman
  - Translation: "He/she is pretending to be rich." / "He/she is showing off false wealth."

## 9. CAUSING CONCEALMENT / HIDING

### Pattern
**Causative construction with *i-* prefix indicating causing something to be hidden**: The root *lihim* combines with *i-* (causative/benefactive voice) to express the act of hiding or keeping secret.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Ilihim mo ang totoo.**
  - Transliteration: ilihim mo ang totoo
  - Translation: "Hide the truth." / "Keep the truth secret."
  - Notes: *I-* + *lihim*. Common directive for concealment.

#### Example 2
- **Inililihim niya ang katotohanan.**
  - Transliteration: inililihim niya ang katotohanan
  - Translation: "He/she is hiding the truth." / "He/she is keeping the truth secret."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect with reduplication (*i-* + *ni-* infix + *lihim* + reduplication).

#### Example 3
- **Maglihim ng sikreto.**
  - Transliteration: maglihim ng sikreto
  - Translation: "Keep a secret." / "Hide a secret."
  - Notes: *Mag-* + *lihim*. Alternative construction emphasizing the act of keeping secrets.

#### Example 4
- **Naglilihim siya ng malaking sikreto.**
  - Transliteration: naglilihim siya ng malaking sikreto
  - Translation: "He/she is keeping a big secret." / "He/she is hiding a big secret."
  - Notes: Progressive aspect. Ongoing concealment.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - Very common constructions for describing concealment of information.

### Notes on Usage
- *Ilihim* focuses on the object being hidden (patient voice)
- *Maglihim* focuses on the act of keeping secrets (actor voice)
- Both constructions are productive and frequently used

## 13. REMAINING TRUE / STAYING TRUE

### Pattern
**Continuative constructions with *nananatili* or *mananatili* (remain/stay)**: Truth-domain predicates combine with continuative verbs to express persistence of truth-states.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Nananatiling totoo ang sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: nananatiling totoo ang sinabi niya
  - Translation: "What he/she said remains true." / "His/her statement stays true."
  - Notes: *Nananatili* (progressive of "remain") + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo*. Emphasizes continued truth.

#### Example 2
- **Mananatiling tama ang desisyon.**
  - Transliteration: mananatiling tama ang desisyon
  - Translation: "The decision will remain correct." / "The decision will stay right."
  - Notes: Future aspect of "remain" + *tama*. Prediction of continued correctness.

#### Example 3
- **Nananatiling tunay ang kanyang pag-ibig.**
  - Transliteration: nananatiling tunay ang kanyang pag-ibig
  - Translation: "His/her love remains genuine." / "His/her love stays true."
  - Notes: Emphasizes persistence of authenticity over time.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - Common construction for emphasizing stability or persistence of truth-states.

### Notes on Usage
- Uses general continuative verb *manatili* (to remain/stay) rather than dedicated truth-domain morphology
- Emphasizes temporal persistence and resistance to change
- Productive with all truth-domain predicates

## 14. CEASING TO BE TRUE / BECOMING FALSE

### Pattern
**Negation of continuative or change-of-state descriptions**: No dedicated morphology for "ceasing to be true." Expressed through negation or change-of-state constructions.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Hindi na totoo ang sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: hindi na totoo ang sinabi niya
  - Translation: "What he/she said is no longer true." / "His/her statement is not true anymore."
  - Notes: *Hindi na* (no longer) + *totoo*. Indicates cessation of truth.

#### Example 2
- **Naging mali ang dating tamang sagot.**
  - Transliteration: naging mali ang dating tamang sagot
  - Translation: "The previously correct answer became wrong." / "What was right became incorrect."
  - Notes: *Naging* (became) + *mali*. Explicit change of state from correct to incorrect.

#### Example 3
- **Nawala ang katotohanan sa kanyang salita.**
  - Transliteration: nawala ang katotohanan sa kanyang salita
  - Translation: "The truth disappeared from his/her words." / "Truth was lost in his/her statement."
  - Notes: *Nawala* (disappeared/was lost) + *katotohanan*. Metaphorical loss of truth.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] for *hindi na* constructions; [MARGINAL] for *naging mali* and *nawala* constructions

### Notes on Usage
- No dedicated morphology for "becoming false" or "ceasing to be true"
- Most commonly expressed through *hindi na* (no longer) + truth predicate
- *Naging mali* explicitly marks change from correct to incorrect state
- These constructions are less frequent than affirmative change-of-state expressions

  - Notes: *Nalaman* (found out/learned) + *totoo*. Common for discovery of truth.

#### Example 3
- **Nabunyag ang lihim.**
  - Transliteration: nabunyag ang lihim
  - Translation: "The secret was revealed." / "The secret was uncovered."
  - Notes: *Nabunyag* (was revealed/uncovered) + *lihim*. Often implies dramatic revelation.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - Very common constructions for describing discovery or revelation of truth.

### Notes on Usage
- Uses general discovery verbs (*tuklasan*, *malaman*, *bunyag*) rather than dedicated truth-domain morphology
- *Nabunyag* often implies revelation of something deliberately hidden
- These constructions focus on the event of discovery rather than the state of truth

## 18. DOUBTING / QUESTIONING TRUTH

### Pattern
**Doubt/question verbs with truth-domain objects**: General doubt and questioning verbs combine with truth-domain terms.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Duda ako sa katotohanan nito.**
  - Transliteration: duda ako sa katotohanan nito
  - Translation: "I doubt the truth of this." / "I question the truth of this."
  - Notes: *Duda* (doubt) + *sa katotohanan* (in/of the truth). Expression of skepticism.

#### Example 2
- **Tinatanong ko ang totoo.**
  - Transliteration: tinatanong ko ang totoo
  - Translation: "I'm questioning what is true." / "I'm asking about the truth."
  - Notes: *Tinatanong* (questioning/asking) + *totoo*. Active inquiry.

#### Example 3
- **Hindi ako naniniwala sa sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: hindi ako naniniwala sa sinabi niya
  - Translation: "I don't believe what he/she said." / "I don't trust his/her statement."
  - Notes: *Hindi naniniwala* (don't believe) + statement. Rejection of truth-claim.

### Epistemic Status
[CORE] - Common constructions for expressing doubt or skepticism.

### Notes on Usage
- Uses general doubt/question verbs (*duda*, *tanong*, *naniniwala*) rather than dedicated truth-domain morphology
- These constructions express epistemic attitudes toward truth-claims
- *Naniniwala* (believe) is particularly common in truth-domain contexts

## SUMMARY OF DYNAMIC PATTERNS

### Highly Productive Patterns:

1. **Realization/Coming True** (*nagtotoo*, *tumutuoo*, *magkatotoo*): Specific to *totoo*, very common for dreams, predictions, wishes

2. **Making Mistakes** (*magkamali*, *nagkakamali*): Highly lexicalized and extremely frequent

3. **Proving/Verification** (*patunayan*, *pinatunayan*, *napatunayan*): Very common in legal, investigative, argumentative contexts

4. **Deception Actions** (*manlinlang*, *mandaya*, *magsinungaling*): Productive across multiple deception roots

5. **Concealment/Revelation** (*ilihim*/*ilantad*, *maglihim*, *inililihim*/*inilalantad*): Systematic opposition with productive derivations

6. **Being Deceived** (*malinlang*, *nalinlang*): Common pattern for describing victims of deception

### Limited Productivity:

1. **Causative with *totoo*** (*pinatotohanan*): Grammatically possible but less common than inchoative forms

2. **Correcting** (*itama*, *wastuhan*): *Itama* is common; *wastuhan* is marginal

3. **Becoming Correct** (*tumama*): Common but polysemous with "to hit/strike"

### Gaps and Asymmetries:

1. **No dedicated "becoming false" morphology**: Expressed through negation (*hindi na totoo*) or change-of-state verbs (*naging mali*)

2. **Asymmetry in realization**: *Totoo* productively forms realization verbs; other truth-domain terms do not

3. **Verification uses general verbs**: Most verification/checking/discovery constructions use general verbs (*suriin*, *tingnan*, *alamin*, *tuklasan*) rather than dedicated truth-domain morphology

4. **Maintenance/persistence**: Expressed through general continuative verbs (*manatili*) rather than dedicated truth-domain forms

### Key Observations:

- **Lexical specificity**: Different roots show different productive patterns (e.g., *totoo* for realization, *tunay* for proving, *mali* for making mistakes)

- **Voice system integration**: Dynamic constructions fully integrate with Tagalog voice system (actor, patient, locative, benefactive)

- **Aspect marking**: Progressive aspect with reduplication is very productive for ongoing processes

- **Causative complexity**: Causative *pa-* is productive with some roots (*tunay* → *patunayan*) but not others; often highly lexicalized

- **General verb supplementation**: Many dynamic meanings use general verbs (discovery, maintenance, verification) rather than dedicated truth-domain morphology
