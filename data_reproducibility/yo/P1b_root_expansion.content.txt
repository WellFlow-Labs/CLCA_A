# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

### Root 1: **tọ́**
**Form:** tọ́  
**Participates in:** 5 P1 items (òtítọ́, tọ́, tọ́ sí, nitootọ́, òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, rightness — both factual accuracy and moral propriety. This root establishes the primary axis of truth-evaluation in Yoruba.

### Root 2: **ṣe**
**Form:** ṣe  
**Participates in:** 7 P1 items (ṣe déédé, ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀, ṣe pàtàkì, ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀, ṣe é ṣe, àṣìṣe, ṣìṣe)  
**Core meaning contribution:** To do, to make, to be (copular) — serves as a highly productive auxiliary/copular verb that combines with evaluative predicates to form truth-related assessments.

### Root 3: **bá/bá mu**
**Form:** bá (+ mu)  
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items (bá mu, ìbámu)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Meeting, matching, correspondence — evaluates alignment and agreement between things, central to correspondence-based truth evaluation.

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### Extensions of **tọ́** root:

1. **ọ̀nà tọ́** (straight path) — The root extends from evaluating factual correctness to metaphorical evaluation of moral conduct and proper procedure. The "straightness" becomes a moral/procedural quality rather than purely epistemic.

2. **tọ́ ọkàn / ọkàn tọ́** (straight heart, sincere) — The root shifts from external truth-evaluation to internal sincerity and honesty of intention. This represents a move from propositional truth to character evaluation.

3. **tọ́ lọ́nà** (to be on the right path) — Similar to ọ̀nà tọ́, this extends the root into moral and procedural domains, evaluating life conduct rather than factual accuracy.

4. **àtọ́kùn** (explanation, clarification) — The root appears in a context of making things clear or understandable, which relates to truth but focuses more on comprehensibility than accuracy per se.

### Extensions of **ṣe** root:

1. **ṣe òdodo** (to do justice) — The root extends from truth-evaluation and factual assessment to moral and legal evaluation of just conduct.

2. **ṣe àmì** (to be a sign) — The root appears in evidential/semiotic contexts, where something serves as an indicator rather than being directly evaluated for truth.

3. **ṣe ìdí** (to be the reason) — The root extends into causal/explanatory domains, identifying reasons rather than evaluating truth directly.

4. **ṣe àkíyèsí** (to observe, notice) — The root appears in perceptual/attentional contexts that precede truth-evaluation rather than constituting it.

5. **ṣe àpèjúwe** (to describe) — The root extends to representation and depiction, where accuracy is relevant but the focus is on the act of describing.

### Extensions of **bá/bá mu** root:
