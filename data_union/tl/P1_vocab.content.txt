# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Note on transliteration:** Tagalog uses the Latin-based Filipino alphabet. Native script (Baybayin) is not in active general use. Transliteration column is omitted where the written form is already Roman script; stress marking follows standard Philippine linguistic convention using acute accent where disambiguation is needed.

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Literal meaning:** Root form; possibly a reduplication-derived form; cognate with related Austronesian forms meaning "real, actual"; cognate with Spanish *todo* disputed; morphologically simple root
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Usage:** *Totoo* is the primary everyday term for asserting that a statement or situation corresponds to reality. Used to affirm the truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs, as in *totoo ba iyan?* ("is that true/real?"). Also used to assert that something is not fabricated or pretended. It is used both predicatively (*Totoo iyan* — "That is true") and as a discourse particle affirming sincerity. Participates in productive derivation: *katotohanan* (truth, the abstract noun), *totoong-totoo* (intensified: very real/true).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-an* (nominalizing circumfix forming abstract nouns); "the state or quality of being *totoo*"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the truth of the matter
**Usage:** *Katotohanan* is the standard abstract noun for truth, used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse. Nominal form referring to truth as an abstract property or to a specific true state of affairs. It frequently appears in formal declarations and moral exhortations (*Sabihin ang katotohanan* — "Tell the truth"). It is the expected translation equivalent of "truth" in Bible translations and legal oaths. Also used in formal, moral, and philosophical registers (*Ang katotohanan ay mahalaga* — "Truth is important"). Commonly contrasted with *kasinungalingan* (falsehood). Also used in legal and testimonial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tunay
**Literal meaning:** Root form; etymology not fully established; possibly from Proto-Philippine stock; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Usage:** *Tunay* emphasizes authenticity or genuineness, often applied to persons, objects, or relationships rather than propositions. Used to assert that something is what it claims or appears to be, often with emphasis on authenticity rather than mere factual accuracy. *Tunay na kaibigan* means "a true/genuine friend," *tunay na ginto* means "real gold," and *tunay na pagmamahal* means "real/genuine love." It contrasts with *huwad* (fake) and *peke* (counterfeit) and participates in the abstract nominal *katunayan* (proof, evidence, genuineness). Slightly more evaluative of intrinsic character than *totoo*, which more often concerns factual correspondence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** katunayan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "the state of being *tunay*"; also functions as "proof" or "evidence"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, genuineness, verification, the fact that
**Usage:** *Katunayan* is used both as an abstract noun meaning "genuineness" and, very commonly, as a discourse connector meaning "in fact" or "as a matter of fact" (*Sa katunayan* — "In fact / As a matter of fact"). In legal and formal contexts it carries the sense of evidence or proof that establishes something as real or factually the case. Common in both formal argumentation and everyday speech. Near-synonym of *pruweba* but more formal.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tama
**Literal meaning:** Root form; from Proto-Philippine; core meaning relates to correctness, hitting a mark
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage:** *Tama* is the standard term for correctness of answers, judgments, actions, or measurements. Applied to statements, answers, actions, or judgments to indicate they are correct or appropriate (*Tama ang sagot mo* — "Your answer is correct"). Also used in moral evaluation of conduct (*Tama ba ang ginawa niya?* — "Was what he did right?"). It contrasts with *mali* (wrong, incorrect) and participates in derivations such as *katamaan* and the adverb *nang tama* (correctly), as well as *tama-tama* (just right, exactly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** mali
**Literal meaning:** Root form; Proto-Philippine origin; core meaning is error, wrongness
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
**Usage:** *Mali* is the primary antonym of *tama*, used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong. It applies across factual (*Mali ang datos* — "The data is wrong"), moral (*Mali ang ginawa mo* — "What you did was wrong"), and procedural domains. Derives *pagkakamali* (mistake, error) and *magkamali* (to make a mistake). The abstract noun *kamalian* means "error" or "mistake."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** pagkakamali
**Literal meaning:** *pag-* + *ka-* + *mali* + *-an* (verbal noun of *magkamali*, "to make a mistake"); *pagkaka-* (nominalization of accidental/completed action) + *mali*; "an instance of being wrong"
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault
**Usage:** *Pagkakamali* is the standard nominal form for a mistake or error, used to evaluate actions, statements, or judgments as having gone wrong. Refers to a specific instance of error, whether factual, moral, or practical. Implies an action or judgment that deviated from what was correct. *Aminin ang pagkakamali* means "to admit one's mistake." It is used across factual, moral, and procedural domains and is morphologically transparent from the root *mali*. Commonly used in apology and accountability contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** huwad
**Literal meaning:** Root form; indigenous Tagalog term
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, forged, false, spurious, imitation
**Usage:** *Huwad* denotes something that falsely presents itself as genuine — a counterfeit document, a false identity, or an imitation object. Used to describe objects, documents, or representations that are not genuine but are made to appear so (*huwad na pera* — "counterfeit money"; *huwad na dokumento* — "forged/counterfeit document"). Carries a connotation of intentional deception, distinguishing it from mere error. It is used in legal contexts and in moral evaluation of deceptive representations. It is somewhat more formal or literary than *peke* in contemporary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** peke
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *pique* or via Philippine Spanish, or directly from English *fake*; fully nativized in colloquial Tagalog; root form in Tagalog
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, phony
**Usage:** *Peke* is the colloquial, widely used term for something counterfeit or inauthentic — fake goods, false credentials, or insincere behavior. Colloquial near-synonym of *huwad*, applied to objects, goods, or persons presenting a false identity (*pekeng produkto* — "fake product"; *pekeng doktor* — "fake doctor"). More informal in register than *huwad* and very widely used in everyday speech. *Peke* can also be applied to persons acting inauthentically (*Peke ka* — "You're being fake/phony").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** lehitimo
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *legítimo*
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, genuine, rightful
**Usage:** *Lehitimo* is used in legal, political, and formal contexts to evaluate whether a claim, authority, or status is rightfully held and properly established. *Lehitimong anak* means "legitimate child" (in the legal sense), and *lehitimong pamahalaan* means "legitimate government." Also used in evaluations such as *lehitimong negosyo* ("legitimate business"). Less common in purely factual contexts; primarily evaluative of standing or right. It is a borrowing that has stabilized in formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** wastó
**Literal meaning:** Root form; from Spanish *vasto* or possibly *justo*, or possibly independent development; the exact etymological path is debated; fully integrated into Tagalog
**Typical English translations:** correct, proper, right, appropriate, accurate
**Usage:** *Wasto* overlaps with *tama* in meaning "correct" but tends to emphasize propriety, appropriateness, or conformity to a standard. Often used in more formal or technical registers than *tama*. Commonly used in evaluating grammar (*wastong gamit* — "correct/proper usage"), procedure, and moral conduct (*wastong paraan* — "the proper/correct way"; *wastong pagbigkas* — "correct pronunciation"). Also carries a sense of moral or procedural propriety. Derives *kawastuhan* (correctness, propriety).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** kawastuhan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *wastó* + *-an*; "the state/quality of being correct or proper"
**Typical English translations:** correctness, propriety, rightness
**Usage:** Abstract nominal referring to the quality of being correct or proper, used in formal and evaluative discourse. Less frequent in casual speech than its root *wastó*. Appears in discussions of moral conduct, linguistic correctness, and procedural propriety.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** tapat
**Literal meaning:** Root form; also means "facing, directly opposite" (spatial); extended to moral/epistemic sense
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, straightforward, faithful, frank, loyal
**Usage:** *Tapat* evaluates persons or statements as honest and without concealment. *Maging tapat* means "to be honest/straightforward," *tapatan* means to speak frankly with someone, and *tapatang sagot* means "honest/straightforward answer." The spatial sense of directness ("facing directly") appears to underlie the moral extension and the two senses coexist and are likely related metaphorically. Derives *katapatan* (honesty, sincerity, loyalty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** katapatan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tapat* + *-an*; "the state of being honest/faithful"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty, fidelity
**Usage:** *Katapatan* is used as an abstract noun covering both the epistemic sense (honesty in speech) and the relational-moral sense (faithfulness, loyalty). It appears in formal declarations, vows, and moral discourse, e.g., *katapatan sa trabaho* ("fidelity/dedication to work"), *katapatan ng salita* ("honesty of word"). The semantic range is broader than English "honesty" alone, encompassing fidelity to persons and commitments. The dual sense of sincerity and loyalty is characteristic and both are well attested.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** mapagkakatiwalaan
**Literal meaning:** *ma-* (potential/quality prefix) + *pagkakatiwalaan* (verbal noun from *tiwala*, "trust") + multiple affixes; "able to be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** *Mapagkakatiwalaan* is the standard adjectival form for evaluating persons, sources, or institutions as worthy of trust. Used in contexts of evaluating witnesses, information sources, and persons in positions of responsibility (*mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon* — "reliable source of information"). Morphologically complex but lexically conventionalized. The root *tiwala* (trust, confidence) is highly productive in Tagalog derivation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** tiwala
**Literal meaning:** Root form; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is trust, confidence, reliance
**Typical English translations:** trust, confidence, reliance, faith (in a person or institution)
**Usage:** *Tiwala* is used to evaluate the reliability and trustworthiness of persons, institutions, and claims. *May tiwala ako sa kanya* means "I trust him/her." Used both as a noun (*walang tiwala* — "no trust") and in verbal constructions. It is foundational to evaluations of credibility and is highly productive morphologically, generating *mapagkakatiwalaan*, *pagtitiwalaan*, *tiwalahin*, and others.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** kredible
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *credible* (or Spanish *creíble*); used in journalistic and legal registers
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable
**Usage:** *Kredible* is used in formal, journalistic, and legal contexts to evaluate whether a source, witness, or claim merits belief (*kredibleng saksi* — "credible witness"). It is a relatively recent borrowing and is more common in written and broadcast media than in everyday speech. It coexists with native alternatives such as *mapagkakatiwalaan* but occupies a more specifically epistemic niche. More formal and domain-specific than native equivalents; common in media and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *maka-* (prefix indicating alignment with or capacity for) + *katotohanan* / *totohanan* (from *totoo*); "in accordance with truth/reality"
**Typical English translations:** realistic, truthful, true-to-life, verisimilar
**Usage:** *Makatotohanan* is used in aesthetic and narrative contexts to evaluate whether a representation, portrayal, or depiction corresponds faithfully to reality (*makatotohanang larawan* — "a realistic/truthful portrayal"; *makatotohanang pagkukuwento* — "realistic/truthful storytelling"; *makatotohanang pelikula* — "realistic/true-to-life film"). Also used in moral contexts to mean "truthful." Distinct from *totoo* in that it evaluates resemblance to truth rather than asserting truth directly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** aktuwal
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *actual* / Spanish *actual*
**Typical English translations:** actual, real (as opposed to hypothetical or apparent)
**Usage:** Used to distinguish what is genuinely the case from what is supposed, hypothetical, or apparent (*aktuwal na nangyari* — "what actually happened"). More common in formal and journalistic registers. Overlaps with *tunay* and *totoo* but emphasizes contrast with the hypothetical or supposed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** orihinal
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *original*
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic (not a copy), genuine
**Usage:** *Orihinal* evaluates whether something is the genuine source item rather than a copy, reproduction, or imitation. Used in legal, commercial, and aesthetic contexts (*orihinal na kopya* — "original copy [of a document]"; *orihinal na obra* — "original work"; *orihinal na produkto* — "genuine/original product"). It coexists with *tunay* but specifically foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction. Contrasts with *kopya* (copy) and *huwad/peke* (fake).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** awtentiko
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *auténtico* / English *authentic*
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** *Awtentiko* is used in formal, legal, and commercial contexts to evaluate whether an item or document is genuinely what it purports to be (*awtentikong dokumento* — "authentic document"). It is more formal and less colloquial than *tunay* or *orihinal* and appears frequently in legal certification language. Its distribution is somewhat narrower than *tunay*. Somewhat more formal than *tunay* and more common in written registers. Overlaps substantially with *tunay* and *orihinal*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** paimbabaw
**Literal meaning:** *pa-* + *ibabaw* (surface, top); "on the surface only"
**Typical English translations:** superficial, insincere, hypocritical, surface-level
**Usage:** Evaluates conduct, expressions, or persons as lacking genuine depth or sincerity — performing an appearance without the corresponding reality (*paimbabaw na pagmamahal* — "superficial/insincere love"). Carries a negative evaluative force regarding the gap between appearance and genuine character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** mapanlinlang
**Literal meaning:** *mapa-* + *linlang* (to deceive, to mislead) + agentive/adjectival morphology; "prone to deceiving" / "tending to deceive"
**Typical English translations:** deceptive, misleading, fraudulent
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, statements, or representations as designed or tending to create false impressions (*mapanlinlang na advertisement* — "deceptive advertisement"). Used in moral, legal, and consumer-protection contexts. The root *linlang* (deception) also occurs independently.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** linlang
**Literal meaning:** Root form
**Typical English translations:** deception, deceit, to deceive
**Usage:** Refers to the act or practice of creating false impressions in another's mind. Used as a noun and verbal root (*nillinlang siya* — "he/she was deceived"). Appears in moral condemnation of dishonest conduct and in legal descriptions of fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** pagsisinungaling
**Literal meaning:** *pag-* + *si-* + *sinungaling* (liar, one who lies); verbal noun meaning "the act of lying"
**Typical English translations:** lying, the act of telling a lie, falsehood (as an act)
**Usage:** *Pagsisinungaling* is the verbal noun for the act of lying or deceiving through false statements. Used in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts to characterize conduct as dishonest. Derived from *sinungaling* (liar), which is itself a well-established root. Used in moral evaluation and in legal contexts involving false testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** sinungaling
**Literal meaning:** Root or derived form; agent/quality noun referring to one who lies; etymology debated; functions as both noun (liar) and adjective (lying, false)
**Typical English translations:** liar, lying, false (of a person), deceitful
**Usage:** *Sinungaling* is the standard term for a person who lies or for characterizing someone as dishonest. It is used as a direct predicate (*Sinungaling ka* — "You are a liar") and in nominal constructions (*sinungaling na salita* — "lying/false words"). One of the most most common terms of moral reproach in Tagalog and one of the most common terms in the semantic field of deception and dishonesty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** kasinungalingan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *sinungaling* + *-an*; abstract noun meaning "the quality or instance of lying; a lie; falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** *Kasinungalingan* is the abstract noun for a lie or falsehood, used to evaluate statements as false and deceptive. Refers to a deliberate false statement or to the quality of being false/deceptive, e.g., *huwag magsabi ng kasinungalingan* ("do not tell a lie"). It contrasts directly with *katotohanan* (truth) and appears in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. *Puno ng kasinungalingan* means "full of lies/falsehood." Commonly used in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** pruweba
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *prueba* (proof, test)
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, test
**Usage:** *Pruweba* is used in legal and everyday contexts to refer to evidence or proof that establishes the truth or falsity of a claim (*Nasaan ang pruweba?* — "Where is the proof?"; *Walang pruweba* — "there is no proof/evidence"). It is fully integrated into colloquial and formal Tagalog and coexists with *katunayan* in the sense of evidence. Near-synonym of *katunayan* but more colloquial and direct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** ebidensya
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *evidence* (via Spanish *evidencia*); fully nativized in legal and journalistic registers
**Typical English translations:** evidence
**Usage:** *Ebidensya* is the standard term in legal and journalistic contexts for evidence used to evaluate the truth of claims or the guilt or innocence of persons (*walang ebidensya* — "no evidence"). It is more specifically legal-procedural in connotation than *pruweba* and is the expected term in courtroom and investigative discourse. More domain-specific than *pruweba* and more formal in register.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** akma
**Literal meaning:** Root *akma*; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is fit, suited, matching
**Typical English translations:** fitting, appropriate, accurate (of a match or fit), suitable
**Usage:** *Akma* evaluates whether something corresponds correctly to a standard, requirement, or other item — a translation that is accurate, a size that fits, or a description that matches reality. *Akma ang paglalarawan* means "the description is accurate/fitting." It is used in aesthetic, practical, and evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** angkop
**Literal meaning:** Root *angkop*; indigenous Tagalog; meaning centers on suitability and appropriateness
**Typical English translations:** appropriate, suitable, fitting, proper
**Usage:** *Angkop* evaluates whether something is appropriate or suitable to a context, standard, or purpose. It is used in moral, aesthetic, and practical evaluations (*angkop na pag-uugali* — "appropriate behavior"). It overlaps with *akma* but tends more toward normative appropriateness than physical or descriptive fit.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Form:** tumpak
**Literal meaning:** Root *tumpak*; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is precise, exact, hitting the mark
**Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate, spot-on
**Usage:** *Tumpak* emphasizes precision and exactness, used to evaluate statements, translations, measurements, or descriptions that hit the mark exactly. *Tumpak ang sinabi mo* means "What you said is exactly right/precise." It is stronger than *tama* in implying exactness rather than mere correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**
**Form:** sigurado
**Literal meaning:** From Spanish *seguro*; "sure, certain, secure"
**Typical English translations:** sure, certain, definite, reliable
**Usage:** *Sigurado* is used to evaluate the certainty of a claim or the reliability of a person or thing. *Sigurado ka ba?* means "Are you sure?" It is used both as a predicate adjective and as a discourse marker of epistemic certainty. It is fully integrated into colloquial and formal Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** pagpapatunay
**Literal meaning:** *pag-* (verbal noun prefix) + *pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an* (locative/applicative); "the act of causing something to be verified/proven"
**Typical English translations:** verification, authentication, confirmation, proof
**Usage:** *Pagpapatunay* is the verbal noun for the act of verifying, authenticating, or proving something. Used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts (*pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "authentication/verification of a document"). It is morphologically transparent and productive within the *tunay* family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** lehitimasyon
**Literal meaning:** From Spanish/English *legitimación/legitimation*; the process of establishing legitimacy
**Typical English translations:** legitimation, legitimization
**Usage:** *Lehitimasyon* appears in legal and political discourse to refer to the process by which a claim, status, or authority is formally established as legitimate. It is a domain-specific term found primarily in legal documents and academic writing rather than everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**36.**
**Form:** dalisay
**Literal meaning:** Root form
**Typical English translations:** pure, unadulterated, genuine, uncontaminated
**Usage:** Used to evaluate substances, emotions, or intentions as pure and unmixed, e.g., *dalisay na ginto* ("pure gold"), *dalisay na puso* ("pure heart"). In philosophical and religious discourse it can carry a sense of moral or ontological purity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**37.**
**Form:** perpekto
**Literal meaning:** From Spanish *perfecto*
**Typical English translations:** perfect, flawless, exact
**Usage:** Used to evaluate objects, performances, or reproductions as without flaw or as exactly matching a standard, e.g., *perpektong kopya* ("perfect copy"), *perpektong sagot* ("perfect answer"). Common in everyday and aesthetic evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**38.**
**Form:** eksakto
**Literal meaning:** From Spanish *exacto*
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** Used to evaluate measurements, statements, or reproductions as precisely accurate, e.g., *eksakto ang oras* ("the time is exact/precise"). Common in everyday and technical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**39.**
**Form:** eksaktong-eksakto
**Literal meaning:** Reduplication of *eksakto* (from Spanish *exacto*); intensified "exact"
**Typical English translations:** exactly right, precisely accurate, spot-on
**Usage:** Intensified form used to assert that something matches a standard, measurement, or description with complete precision, e.g., *eksaktong-eksakto ang sukat* ("the measurement is exactly right"). The reduplication pattern is productive in Tagalog for intensification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**40.**
**Form:** tamà o malî
**Literal meaning:** *tama* (correct/right) + *o* (or) + *mali* (wrong/incorrect)
**Typical English translations:** right or wrong, correct or incorrect
**Usage:** A fixed paired expression used in evaluative contexts, particularly in moral reasoning and test/examination discourse, e.g., *tamà o malî ang ginawa niya?* ("Was what he did right or wrong?"). Functions as a conventionalized binary framing for evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**41.**
**Form:** huwad na
**Literal meaning:** *huwad* (fake/counterfeit) + *na* (already; now; marker of changed state)
**Typical English translations:** it turns out to be fake, proven fake, now known to be counterfeit
**Usage:** Fixed predicate expression used to assert that something has been determined or revealed to be fake or counterfeit, e.g., *huwad na ang dokumento* ("the document has been found to be fake"). The particle *na* marks the evaluation as a conclusion reached.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**42.**
**Form:** parang totoo
**Literal meaning:** *parang* (like, as if, seemingly) + *totoo* (true/real); "as if true/real"
**Typical English translations:** seems real, looks genuine, as if true, realistic-seeming
**Usage:** Used to evaluate something as having the appearance of being real or true without asserting that it actually is, e.g., *parang totoo ang kanyang kwento* ("his story seems true/real"). Common in everyday evaluative discourse about appearances versus reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**43.**
**Form:** di-mapagkakatiwalaan
**Literal meaning:** *di-* (negation) + *mapagkakatiwalaan* (trustworthy); "not trustworthy"
**Typical English translations:** untrustworthy, unreliable, not to be trusted
**Usage:** Negative counterpart of *mapagkakatiwalaan*, used to evaluate persons, sources, or institutions as lacking in reliability or credibility, e.g., *di-mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* ("unreliable witness"). The *di-* negation prefix is productive and standard.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**44.**
**Form:** huwag-huwaran
**Literal meaning:** *huwad* + reduplication/derivation pattern; "something made to imitate"
**Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, counterfeit (object)
**Usage:** Refers to an object made to resemble another, with connotations of inauthenticity. Less common than *huwad* alone and more specifically nominal. Marks the object as a deliberate imitation rather than the act of faking.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**45.**
**Form:** di-tunay
**Literal meaning:** *di-* (negation prefix) + *tunay*; "not genuine"
**Typical English translations:** inauthentic, not real, false
**Usage:** Negated form of *tunay*, used to evaluate something as lacking genuine character or authenticity. Attested in written and formal registers but less lexicalized than *huwad* or *peke* for the same evaluative purpose. Functions as a predicate or attributive modifier.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
