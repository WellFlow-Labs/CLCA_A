# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgia (2002), as used in Georgian passports and official romanization, supplemented by standard academic convention for Georgian studies. Applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine substance) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements, beliefs, and propositions. Also applied to persons to mean sincere or honest. Participates in productive derivation: č̣ešmariteba (truth, as abstract noun), č̣ešmaritad (truly, adverb).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in philosophical, theological, and everyday discourse. Frequently appears in legal and moral contexts when the truth of testimony or a claim is at issue. Closely related to č̣ešmariti and shares its derivational family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominalizing circumfix, front part) + martal- (right, correct) + -e (back part of circumfix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, justice
**Usage:** One of the two primary words for truth in Georgian, often more colloquial or concrete than č̣ešmariteba. Used for factual correctness of statements and for moral rightness of conduct. The phrase simartle tkva ("told the truth") is highly frequent in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** root martal- + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, true, just, honest
**Usage:** Applied to persons (a just or honest person), statements (a correct claim), and situations (a rightful state of affairs). Closely related to simartle and participates in the same derivational family. Commonly used in everyday moral and factual evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -e
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, forgery, counterfeit
**Usage:** Denotes the quality of being false or counterfeit, applied to documents, currency, statements, and representations. Frequently appears in legal contexts involving forgery or fraud. The root q̇albi is borrowed from Persian/Arabic and is widely used.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** borrowed root (cf. Persian/Arabic); adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged
**Usage:** The standard adjective for counterfeit or forged items (q̇albi dokumenti, "forged document"; q̇albi fuli, "counterfeit money"). Also used for insincere or false behavior. Very common in legal and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** na- (perfective/resultative prefix) + mdvil- (to place, settle, establish) + -i; roughly "that which has been established/settled"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** The primary adjective for authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, and states of affairs. Namdvili oqro ("real gold"), namdvili megobari ("true friend"). Widely used across registers and participates in productive derivation (namdviloba, "genuineness, authenticity").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, realness
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from namdvili, used in legal, philosophical, and administrative contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in discussions of document authentication and in philosophical discourse about reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (lie, false) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The standard noun for a lie or falsehood in everyday and moral discourse. Contrasts directly with simartle. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in legal contexts involving perjury or deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მცდარი
**Transliteration:** mcdari
**Literal meaning:** m- (subject preverb) + cdar- (to err, to be mistaken) + -i
**Typical English translations:** erroneous, mistaken, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to beliefs, judgments, and statements that are factually incorrect or logically flawed. More neutral in tone than sicrue (it implies error rather than deliberate deception). Common in academic, scientific, and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** სწორი
**Transliteration:** sc̣ori
**Literal meaning:** root sc̣or- (straight, correct) + -i
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, straight
**Usage:** Used for factual correctness of answers, calculations, and statements, as well as for moral uprightness. sc̣ori pasukhi ("correct answer") is a highly frequent collocation. Also used geometrically (straight line) and morally (upright person).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** უზუსტო
**Transliteration:** uzusto
**Literal meaning:** u- (privative prefix) + zusto (exact, precise, from German/Russian borrowing) + privative construction
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, imprecise, inexact
**Usage:** Used to describe statements, measurements, or representations that lack precision or accuracy. The positive form zusti ("exact, precise") is also common. Appears frequently in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** borrowed root (cf. German genau via Russian точный influence, or direct borrowing); adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** The standard adjective for precision and exactness in measurements, translations, statements, and reproductions. zusti targmani ("accurate translation"), zusti monacemebi ("precise data"). Very common across registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarc̣muno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (circumfix front) + rc̣mun- (to believe, to trust) + -o (circumfix back); "worthy of belief/trust"
**Typical English translations:** credible, reliable, trustworthy, believable
**Usage:** Applied to sources, witnesses, evidence, and persons to indicate that they are worthy of belief or trust. Frequently used in legal and journalistic contexts. Participates in the productive sa-...-o pattern for "worthy of X" or "capable of X."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** დამაჯერებელი
**Transliteration:** damajerebeliˈ
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + majer- (to convince, to make believe) + -ebel- (agentive/potential suffix) + -i
**Typical English translations:** convincing, persuasive, plausible, credible
**Usage:** Describes arguments, evidence, persons, or performances that are convincing or persuasive. Slightly more focused on the effect of persuasion than sarc̣muno, which emphasizes inherent trustworthiness. Common in rhetorical, legal, and critical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** გულწრფელი
**Transliteration:** gulc̣rpeli
**Literal meaning:** gul- (heart) + c̣rpel- (sincere, straight) + -i; roughly "straight-hearted"
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt, candid
**Usage:** Applied to persons, expressions, and intentions to indicate sincerity and absence of pretense. gulc̣rpeli bodiši ("sincere apology"), gulc̣rpeli grznoba ("genuine feeling"). Common in interpersonal and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** თვალთმაქცობა
**Transliteration:** tvaltmaqcoba
**Literal meaning:** tval- (eye) + t- (connective) + maqc- (to turn, to deceive the eye) + -oba (nominalizing suffix); roughly "eye-turning, deception of the eye"
**Typical English translations:** deception, trickery, hypocrisy, pretense, illusion
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception, pretense, or the creation of false appearances. Used in moral condemnation of hypocritical or deceptive conduct. Also used for visual illusions or sleight of hand in more literal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** მოჩვენებითი
**Transliteration:** močvenebiti
**Literal meaning:** mo- (preverb) + čven- (to show, to appear) + -ebit- + -i; roughly "of the nature of mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, illusory, ostensible, spurious
**Usage:** Describes something that merely appears to be the case without actually being so. močvenebiti თანხმობა ("ostensible agreement"), močvenebiti safrtkhe ("apparent danger"). Used in philosophical, legal, and analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ფიქტიური
**Transliteration:** piqtiuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin/Russian fictivnyj; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** fictitious, fictional, fabricated, sham
**Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for fictitious entities, sham transactions, or fabricated claims. piqtiuri kompania ("fictitious company"), piqtiuri qidva-gayidva ("sham transaction"). A loanword with a narrower, more formal register than native alternatives.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avtentiuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek/Latin authenticus via European languages; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** A loanword used primarily in formal, legal, archival, and academic contexts to describe documents, texts, or artifacts of verified origin. Coexists with namdvili but tends to appear in more technical or international-facing discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** სიყალბის დადასტურება
**Transliteration:** siq̇albis dadastureba
**Literal meaning:** siq̇albe (falsehood/forgery, genitive) + dadastureba (confirmation, verification)
**Typical English translations:** confirmation of forgery, verification of falsity
**Usage:** A fixed legal and administrative expression used in proceedings to establish that a document or claim is forged or false. Functions as a conventionalized phrase in legal Georgian rather than a free compositional description.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** დადასტურება
**Transliteration:** dadastureba
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + dastur- (confirmation, from Persian dastūr) + -eba (nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, verification, attestation, authentication
**Usage:** The standard noun for the act of confirming, verifying, or authenticating a claim, document, or fact. Widely used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts. The verbal form daadasturebs ("will confirm/verify") is equally common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** გაყალბება
**Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba
**Literal meaning:** ga- (preverb) + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -eba (nominalizing suffix); "making false/counterfeit"
**Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting, fabrication
**Usage:** The standard noun for the act of forging or falsifying documents, evidence, or data. Frequently appears in criminal law contexts. The verbal form gaayalbebs ("will forge/falsify") is productive and common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** მსგავსება
**Transliteration:** msgavseba
**Literal meaning:** msgavs- (similar, resembling) + -eba (nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used to describe the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant in contexts of authentication, identification, and aesthetic evaluation. msgavseba originaltan ("resemblance to the original") is a typical collocation in authentication discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** არსი
**Transliteration:** arsi
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Persian/Arabic asl (origin, essence); nominal form
**Typical English translations:** essence, substance, nature, core
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and analytical discourse to refer to the fundamental nature or essence of something. sakmis arsi ("the essence of the matter"), arsis mixedvit ("according to its essence/substance"). Common in legal reasoning and philosophical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** შინაარსი
**Transliteration:** šinaˈarsi
**Literal meaning:** šina- (inner, inside) + arsi (essence); "inner essence, inner content"
**Typical English translations:** content, substance, meaning, purport
**Usage:** Refers to the actual content or substance of a text, statement, or legal instrument, as opposed to its form or surface appearance. Widely used in legal interpretation, literary analysis, and everyday discussion of what something really means or contains.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- (exact, precise) + -e
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from zusti, used to describe the property of being accurate or precise in measurements, translations, reproductions, and statements. Common in scientific, journalistic, and legal discourse when the degree of accuracy of a representation is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** უტყუარი
**Transliteration:** utyuari
**Literal meaning:** u- (privative) + tyu- (lie, deception) + -ar- + -i; "without lie, incapable of lying"
**Typical English translations:** infallible, indubitable, irrefutable, unerring
**Usage:** Applied to evidence, proofs, and sources to indicate that they are beyond doubt or incapable of deceiving. utyuari mtkicebuleba ("irrefutable evidence") is a common legal collocation. Carries a stronger epistemic force than sarc̣muno.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** მიბაძვა
**Transliteration:** mibadzva
**Literal meaning:** mi- (directional preverb) + badz- (to imitate, to copy) + -va (verbal noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, copying, mimicry
**Usage:** Refers to the act of imitating or copying an original, relevant in aesthetic evaluation (of art, performance) and in contexts where imitation is distinguished from genuine creation or authentic production. Used both neutrally and pejoratively depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** გამართლება
**Transliteration:** gamarṭleba
**Literal meaning:** ga- (preverb) + marṭl- (right, just, correct) + -eba (nominalizing suffix); "making right, making just"
**Typical English translations:** justification, vindication, acquittal, excuse
**Usage:** Used in legal contexts for acquittal or vindication, and in moral and argumentative contexts for the justification of an action or claim. gamarṭleba sasamartloši ("acquittal in court"), qmedebis gamarṭleba ("justification of an action"). Participates in the productive martal- derivational family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
