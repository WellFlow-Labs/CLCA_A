# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

**Note on script and transliteration:** Swahili is standardly written in Latin script; no separate transliteration column is required. Morphological breakdowns are provided where relevant.

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *-kweli*; functions as noun and adverb
**Typical English translations:** truth, truly, really, indeed
**Usage:** Used to assert that a statement corresponds to fact, or to intensify an assertion. Appears in both everyday speech and formal registers. Participates in derivation: *ukweli* (the truth, as abstract noun).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ukweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (noun class 11/14 prefix, abstract) + *kweli*
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity, accuracy
**Usage:** The nominalized abstract form, used when referring to truth as a property or concept. Common in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. Contrasts with *uongo* (falsehood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** uongo
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *-ongo* (root associated with falsehood)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, untruth
**Usage:** Refers to a false or deceptive statement or to the quality of being false. Used in moral condemnation of dishonesty and in legal contexts regarding false testimony. Antonym of *ukweli*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** halisi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *خالص* (khāliṣ), meaning pure, genuine
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, pure
**Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, or qualities to indicate authenticity or lack of adulteration. Used in commercial contexts (e.g., gold, goods) and in evaluations of character. Participates in the nominal form *uhalisi*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** uhalisi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *halisi*
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, reality, realism
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or real. Used in aesthetic and philosophical contexts, including discussions of literary realism (*uhalisi* as a literary movement). Also used in everyday evaluation of authenticity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** sahihi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *صحيح* (ṣaḥīḥ), meaning correct, sound, valid
**Typical English translations:** correct, accurate, right, exact, valid
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, calculations, documents, and procedures as accurate or correct. Extremely common in educational, administrative, and legal contexts. Also used as a confirmation marker meaning "exactly" or "that's right."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** usahihi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *sahihi*
**Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, precision, validity
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from *sahihi*, referring to the property of being accurate or correct. Used in formal and technical registers when evaluating the precision of information, measurements, or claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** haki
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *حق* (ḥaqq), meaning right, truth, due
**Typical English translations:** right, justice, truth, entitlement, fairness
**Usage:** Covers a wide semantic range including moral rightness, legal rights, and factual truth. Used in legal discourse (*haki za binadamu*, human rights), moral evaluation, and everyday assertions of correctness. Participates in *uhaki* and compounds.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** uhakika
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *hakika* (from Arabic *حقيقة*, ḥaqīqa, reality, certainty)
**Typical English translations:** certainty, sureness, reliability, assurance
**Usage:** Refers to the state of being certain or reliable. Used when evaluating whether information or a source can be trusted or confirmed. Contrasts with *shaka* (doubt).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** hakika
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حقيقة* (ḥaqīqa), reality, truth
**Typical English translations:** certainty, sureness; also used adverbially: certainly, for sure
**Usage:** Used both as a noun (a certainty) and as an adverb or discourse particle affirming that something is definitely the case. Common in everyday speech and in formal assertions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** uaminifu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *aminifu* (*-amini-* from Arabic *أمين*, amīn, trustworthy + *-fu* adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** faithfulness, trustworthiness, loyalty, integrity, reliability
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being trustworthy or faithful, applied to persons, institutions, or representations. Used in moral evaluation of character and in assessments of whether a source or account can be relied upon.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** amini
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *أمين* (amīn), trustworthy, faithful
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, faithful, reliable; also used as a verb: to believe, to trust
**Usage:** Functions as an adjective evaluating persons or things as reliable and honest. Also used as a verb (*kuamini*: to believe, to trust). Central to moral and religious discourse on trust and credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** bandia
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly from Arabic *بندي* or related form; meaning fabricated, artificial
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation, forged
**Usage:** Applied to objects, documents, or representations that are not genuine. Common in commercial and legal contexts (e.g., *pesa bandia*, counterfeit money; *bidhaa bandia*, fake goods). Antonym of *halisi*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ghushi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *غش* (ghishsh), fraud, adulteration
**Typical English translations:** fraud, forgery, adulteration, fake, counterfeit
**Usage:** Used in legal and commercial contexts to refer to fraudulent goods, forged documents, or deceptive practices. Overlaps with *bandia* but carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or criminal fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** udanganyifu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *danganya* (to deceive) + *-ifu* (adjectival/nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** deception, deceitfulness, dishonesty, fraud
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality or act of deceiving. Used in moral condemnation and legal contexts. Derived from the verb *kudanganya* (to deceive, to lie), which is itself widely used in evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** kudanganya
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *danganya* (root: to deceive)
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to lie, to mislead, to cheat
**Usage:** Verb used to describe the act of making false representations or misleading someone. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Productive root: *danganyifu* (deceptive), *udanganyifu* (deception).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** kweli kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Reduplication of *kweli*
**Typical English translations:** really truly, genuinely, in actual fact, for real
**Usage:** Reduplicated intensified form of *kweli*, used to emphasize that something is genuinely or emphatically true. Common in spoken Swahili as an intensifier in assertions and evaluations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** asili
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *أصل* (aṣl), origin, root, foundation
**Typical English translations:** origin, nature, original, authentic, indigenous
**Usage:** Refers to the original or fundamental nature of something, or to what something essentially is. Used in evaluations of authenticity (e.g., *lugha ya asili*, native/original language) and in philosophical or cultural discussions of essence and origin.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** wa asili
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *wa* (associative particle) + *asili* (origin, nature)
**Typical English translations:** original, native, natural, authentic, indigenous
**Usage:** Fixed adjectival expression meaning "of the original kind" or "native to." Used to distinguish genuine or original items, persons, or practices from imitations or introduced ones. Common in cultural and commercial evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** haki ya kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *haki* (right, truth) + *ya* (associative particle) + *kweli* (truth)
**Typical English translations:** the real truth, genuine right, true justice
**Usage:** A fixed or semi-fixed expression used for emphasis, asserting that something is genuinely true or genuinely just. Appears in spoken and written discourse when a speaker wishes to stress the authenticity of a claim or the legitimacy of a right.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**21.**
**Form:** uhalali
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *halali* (from Arabic *حلال*, ḥalāl, lawful, permissible)
**Typical English translations:** legitimacy, lawfulness, legality, permissibility
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being lawful or legitimate. Used in legal and moral contexts to evaluate whether an action, claim, or object is rightfully valid. Distinct from but related to *haki* (right, justice).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** halali
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حلال* (ḥalāl), lawful, permitted
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legitimate, permissible, rightful
**Usage:** Used to evaluate actions, goods, or claims as legally or morally permissible. Has both Islamic legal connotations (permitted by religious law) and broader secular uses meaning "legitimate" or "rightfully obtained."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** haramu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حرام* (ḥarām), forbidden, unlawful
**Typical English translations:** forbidden, unlawful, illicit, wrong
**Usage:** Evaluates actions, goods, or conduct as prohibited or morally/legally wrong. Used in religious, moral, and legal registers. Functions as the antonym of *halali*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** sawa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *-sawa*; meaning equal, level, correct
**Typical English translations:** correct, equal, right, okay, same, fair
**Usage:** Used to confirm that something is correct, equal, or in order. In evaluative contexts, functions as an affirmation of accuracy or fairness. Also used in comparisons to indicate equivalence or resemblance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** usawa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *sawa*
**Typical English translations:** equality, balance, fairness, equivalence
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality of being equal, balanced, or fair. Used in moral and legal discourse on justice and equity, and in evaluations of proportionality or correspondence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** shaka
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *شك* (shakk), doubt, uncertainty
**Typical English translations:** doubt, uncertainty, suspicion, hesitation
**Usage:** Refers to a state of uncertainty or lack of confidence in the truth or reliability of something. Used in epistemic evaluations and in legal contexts (e.g., *bila shaka*, without doubt). Contrasts with *uhakika*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** bila shaka
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *bila* (without) + *shaka* (doubt)
**Typical English translations:** without doubt, certainly, undoubtedly, beyond question
**Usage:** Fixed expression used to assert that something is unquestionably true or certain. Common in both spoken and written Swahili as a strong affirmation of reliability or truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** dhahiri
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *ظاهر* (ẓāhir), apparent, manifest, evident
**Typical English translations:** evident, obvious, apparent, manifest, clear
**Usage:** Used to evaluate something as plainly or visibly true, real, or the case. Applied to facts, qualities, or states that are considered self-evident or openly observable. Also used adverbially: *dhahiri kabisa* (quite obviously).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** thibitisha
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *ثبت* (thabbata), to confirm, establish; Swahili causative form
**Typical English translations:** to confirm, to verify, to prove, to authenticate, to establish
**Usage:** Verb used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts to mean the act of confirming or verifying the truth or authenticity of something. Productive: *uthibitisho* (confirmation, verification, proof).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** uthibitisho
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *thibitish-* + *-o* (nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, verification, proof, authentication, attestation
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to the act or result of confirming or verifying something. Used in legal, administrative, and academic contexts when documentary or procedural proof of authenticity or correctness is required.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
