# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

### CONSTRUCTION TYPE 1: COPULAR CONSTRUCTIONS WITH TRUTH PREDICATES

#### Construction 1.1: ni kweli
- **Base Form:** kweli (#1)
- **Example:** Ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** ni kweli
- **English translation:** It's true, that's true
- **Usage notes:** Standard copular affirmation of truth. Most common way to affirm truth of propositions in conversation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2: si kweli
- **Base Form:** kweli (#1)
- **Example:** Si kweli
- **Transliteration:** si kweli
- **English translation:** It's not true, that's false
- **Usage notes:** Standard copular negation of truth. Most common way to deny truth of propositions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.3: ni ukweli
- **Base Form:** ukweli (#2)
- **Example:** Ni ukweli
- **Transliteration:** ni ukweli
- **English translation:** It's the truth, it's a fact
- **Usage notes:** Copular construction emphasizing truth as substantive fact rather than simple affirmation. More emphatic than "ni kweli."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.4: ni sahihi
- **Base Form:** sahihi (#3)
- **Example:** Ni sahihi
- **Transliteration:** ni sahihi
- **English translation:** It's correct, that's right
- **Usage notes:** Copular affirmation of correctness. Common in educational and verification contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.5: si sahihi
- **Base Form:** sahihi (#3)
- **Example:** Si sahihi
- **Transliteration:** si sahihi
- **English translation:** It's not correct, that's wrong
- **Usage notes:** Copular negation of correctness. Common in corrections and evaluations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.6: ni uongo
- **Base Form:** uongo (#4)
- **Example:** Ni uongo
- **Transliteration:** ni uongo
- **English translation:** It's a lie, that's false
- **Usage notes:** Copular construction identifying something as a lie. Stronger accusation than "si kweli."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.7: ni sawa
- **Base Form:** sawa (#7)
- **Example:** Ni sawa
- **Transliteration:** ni sawa
- **English translation:** It's correct, it's okay, that's right
- **Usage notes:** Copular affirmation of correctness or acceptability. Very common in everyday confirmation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.8: ni halisi
- **Base Form:** halisi (#8)
- **Example:** Ni halisi
- **Transliteration:** ni halisi
- **English translation:** It's genuine, it's authentic, it's real
- **Usage notes:** Copular construction affirming authenticity. Common in verification of objects or sincerity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.9: ni batili
- **Base Form:** batili (#12)
- **Example:** Ni batili
- **Transliteration:** ni batili
- **English translation:** It's false, it's invalid, it's null
- **Usage notes:** Copular construction declaring invalidity. Common in legal and formal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.10: ni dhahiri
- **Base Form:** dhahiri (#11)
- **Example:** Ni dhahiri
- **Transliteration:** ni dhahiri
- **English translation:** It's evident, it's obvious, it's clear

### CONSTRUCTION TYPE 2: ADVERBIAL PHRASES WITH kwa

#### Construction 2.1: kwa kweli
- **Base Form:** kweli (#1)

- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli, si-ku-jua
- **English translation:** Truly, I didn't know; Really, I didn't know
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial phrase emphasizing sincerity or truthfulness of statement. Very common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2.2: kwa hakika
- **Base Form:** hakika (#16)
- **Example:** Kwa hakika, atakuja
- **Transliteration:** kwa hakika, a-ta-kuja
- **English translation:** Certainly, he will come; Surely, he will come
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial phrase expressing certainty. Common in emphatic affirmations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2.3: kwa haki

- **English translation:** Truly, he's the one who did it; In truth, he's the one who did it
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial phrase indicating truthfulness or justice of statement. Used in assertions and judgments.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2.4: kwa usahihi
- **Base Form:** usahihi (#24)
- **Example:** Fanya kwa usahihi
- **Transliteration:** fanya kwa usahihi
- **English translation:** Do it accurately, do it correctly
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial phrase indicating manner of doing something with accuracy. Common in instructions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2.5: kwa uadilifu

- **Transliteration:** a-me-fanya kazi kwa uadilifu

## SUMMARY

This analysis has documented **25 construction types** involving truth-domain vocabulary in Swahili, with approximately **150+ individual attested constructions**. The most productive construction types are:

1. **Copular constructions** (ni kweli, si kweli, etc.) - highly productive
2. **Adverbial phrases with kwa** (kwa kweli, kwa hakika, etc.) - highly productive
3. **Associative constructions with -a** (wa kweli, za uongo, etc.) - highly productive
4. **Verbal constructions** (kusema kweli, tafuta ukweli, etc.) - highly productive
5. **Adjectival modification** (jibu sahihi, taarifa kamili, etc.) - highly productive
6. **Possessive constructions** (haki yake, ukweli wa jambo, etc.) - highly productive

Less productive but attested types include:
- Reduplication (limited to emphasis)
- Proverbs and fixed expressions (culturally specific)
- Modal and evidential constructions (using general modals)
- Comparative and degree constructions (limited attestation)

Notable **absences**:
- No dedicated evidential markers (evidentiality expressed through general constructions)
- No specialized temporal constructions for truth
- Limited comparative constructions
- No diminutive/augmentative patterns in this domain
