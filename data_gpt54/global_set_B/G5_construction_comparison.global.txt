## 1. Main recurring constructional functions across the languages

Below are the main constructional functions that recur in the F4 materials, with a brief language-by-language summary of how each language realizes them.

### A. Simple predicative evaluation
This is the basic function of saying that something is true, false, correct, reliable, authentic, invalid, etc.

- **zh**: predicate adjective/noun patterns like **这是真的**, **这个消息很可靠**, with negation by **不**, or lexical negatives like **不实**.
- **he**: zero-copula present-tense predication like **זה נכון**, **המקור מהימן**; negation with **לא** or formal **אינו**.
- **sa**: predicate expressions like **सत्यम् एतत्**, **युक्तं भवति**, **प्रमाणं नास्ति**, **न युक्तम्**.
- **el**: copular predication like **Αυτό είναι αληθές**, **Η πηγή είναι έγκυρη**, negated with **δεν είναι**.
- **en**: copular predication like *The statement is true*, *That explanation is credible*, with lexical or analytic negatives.
- **tl**: predicate-initial evaluation without a copula, e.g. **Totoo iyan**, **Tumpak ang sinabi mo**, **Hindi pa tiyak ang resulta**.
- **sw**: **ni / si** predication, e.g. **Ni kweli**, **Hii ni sahihi**, **Si kweli**.
- **qu**: simple evaluative predicates like **allinmi**, **pantasqa**, **mana allin**.

### B. Negative evaluation / rejection
A recurring function is rejecting a claim, answer, or item as false, wrong, unsupported, fake, or invalid.

- **zh**: **不 + predicate**, plus specialized lexical negatives like **不实**, **失实**.
- **he**: **לא** or **אינו**, plus lexical labels like **כוזב**, **שקרי**.
- **sa**: **na / nāsti**, and lexical negatives such as **असत्य**, **अयथार्थ**, **अप्रामाणिक**.
- **el**: **δεν είναι + adjective/noun**, plus resultative **αποδεικνύεται ψευδής**.
- **en**: *not true*, *untrue*, *false*, *incorrect*, *invalid*, *fake*, etc.
- **tl**: **Hindi + predicate**, and support-status rejection like **Walang saligan ang paratang**.
- **sw**: **si kweli**, **si sahihi**, and stronger noun-predicate rejection like **ni uongo**, **ni batili**.
- **qu**: **mana allin** is the clearest; **mana chiqaq** is only weakly supported.

### C. Noun modification
Another widespread function is classifying a noun as true, false, correct, fake, authentic, reliable, etc.

- **zh**: modifier + noun, often with **的** or as a compound: **虚假的信息**, **名副其实的专家**.
- **he**: postnominal adjective agreement: **סיפור אמיתי**, **מסמך מזויף**.
- **sa**: adjective/compound modification: **तथ्यं वचनम्**, **प्रमाणिकः ग्रन्थः**.
- **el**: adjective + noun: **αληθινή ιστορία**, **πλαστή υπογραφή**.
- **en**: adjective + noun: *a true story*, *an authentic document*.
- **tl**: linker-based attributive forms: **totoong balita**, **pekeng dokumento**, **wastong gamit**.
- **sw**: postnominal modifiers and genitive-like constructions: **taarifa sahihi**, **dawa bandia**, **bidhaa ya asili**.
- **qu**: noun phrases like **llulla runa**, **llulla simi**, **chiqaq simi**.

### D. Speech/reporting framed as truthful or false
Many languages prefer constructions meaning “tell the truth,” “lie,” “answer truthfully,” rather than only static truth labels.

- **zh**: **请如实回答**, **说实话**, **说真的**.
- **he**: **לומר את האמת**, **לספר שקר**.
- **sa**: **सत्यं वद**, **मिथ्या वदति**, **असत्यं वदति**, **अनृतं वदति**.
- **el**: **Λέω την αλήθεια**, **Λέει ψέματα**.
- **en**: *tell the truth*, *tell a lie* is implied by the opposition set, though F4 centers on *tell the truth*.
- **tl**: discourse and truth-reporting expressions like **Sa totoo lang...**; the F4 set does not give a direct “tell the truth” light-verb pattern as centrally as some other languages.
- **sw**: **Sema ukweli**, **Alisema uongo**.
- **qu**: speech-specific noun phrases **chiqaq simi / llulla simi** and the verb **llullakuy** in the wider data, though F4 emphasizes noun-phrase constructions.

### E. Questioning truth/correctness/certainty
Some languages explicitly show a recurring construction for asking whether something is true, correct, or certain.

- **zh**: not highlighted as a separate F4 type, though present elsewhere.
- **he**: not isolated as a separate F4 type.
- **sa**: not isolated as a separate F4 type.
- **el**: not isolated as a separate F4 type.
- **en**: not isolated as a separate F4 type.
- **tl**: clearly attested: **Totoo ba iyan?**, **Sigurado ka ba?**
- **sw**: not given as a separate F4 type, though yes/no questioning exists elsewhere.
- **qu**: not given as a separate F4 type.

So this is **clearly attested as a constructional function mainly in Tagalog** in the F4 tables.

### F. Change into a truth-related state
This function covers “come true,” “turn out true,” “become credible,” etc.

- **zh**: productive change-of-state patterns with **变得 / 变 / 越来越**, but with lexical restrictions; **属实** resists ordinary inchoative use.
- **he**: no common general inchoative construction identified.
- **sa**: limited and domain-specific, e.g. **सत्यतां गच्छति**, **प्रमा जायते**.
- **el**: result/discovery patterns like **αποδείχθηκε πλαστή**, and marginal **αληθεύει**.
- **en**: strong patterns like *proved true*, *came true*.
- **tl**: strong **magkatotoo / nagkatotoo** family.
- **sw**: only clearly with credibility: **inaaminika / hakiaminiki**.
- **qu**: no common inchoative construction identified.

### G. Causing a truth-related result
This includes “mislead,” “authenticate,” “validate,” “correct,” “falsify.”

- **zh**: analytic causatives **使 / 让**, but often replaced by lexical verbs like **伪造**, **发布虚假信息**, **纠正错误**.
- **he**: causative meaning appears through lexical verbs like **להוציא את האמת לאור**, **להצדיק**, **להוכיח**.
- **sa**: weak as a general pattern; mainly technical/lexicalized forms like **प्रमाणीकृतम्**, **प्रमाणीकरणम्**.
- **el**: lexical verbs **παραποιώ**, **πλαστογραφώ**.
- **en**: lexical verbs and limited causative constructions: *proved the claim true*, *authenticated*, *validate/invalidate*, *correct*.
- **tl**: no common general causative pattern; only nearby verbs like **wastuhin**, **beripikahin**.
- **sw**: no general causative for truth/correctness; only nearby **kutia imani**.
- **qu**: strongest in the error domain: **pantachiy**, **pantayman churay**.

### H. Abstract evaluation through nouns
Many languages package truth-related meanings as abstract nouns like “truth,” “accuracy,” “validity,” “authenticity.”

- **zh**: **真实性**, **可信度**.
- **he**: **אמת**, **אמינות**.
- **sa**: **सत्यता**, **यथार्थता**, **प्रामाण्यम्**.
- **el**: **εγκυρότητα**, **ειλικρίνεια**.
- **en**: *accuracy of the report*, *authenticity of the signature*.
- **tl**: **katotohanan**, **saligan**.
- **sw**: **ukweli**, **usahihi wa taarifa**.
- **qu**: **chiqaq kay**, and more marginally **hucha** in a different subdomain.

### I. Person-labeling
Some languages have constructions or lexical forms for “liar,” “truthful person,” “trustworthy person.”

- **zh**: weakly expressed in F4; **伪造者**, **说真话的人**.
- **he**: no common productive pattern identified in F4.
- **sa**: **सत्यवादिन्**, **असत्यवादिन्**.
- **el**: **ψεύτης** is strong; no equally common single-word positive counterpart.
- **en**: *liar* is strong; *truth-teller* is marginal.
- **tl**: no common agentive pattern identified in F4.
- **sw**: **mwongo**, **mkweli**.
- **qu**: **llullaq**, **llulla runa**.

### J. Discourse framing with “truth / reality / essence”
A recurring function is using truth-related expressions to frame what the speaker takes to be the real point or actual situation.

- **zh**: **说实话**, **说真的**, and essence framing with **从本质上看 / 从实质上看**.
- **he**: **למען האמת**, **ליתר דיוק**.
- **sa**: **यथार्थतः**, **तत्त्वतः**.
- **el**: **Η αλήθεια είναι ότι...**, **Στην πραγματικότητα...**
- **en**: *Truth be told...*
- **tl**: **Sa totoo lang...**, **Sa katotohanan...**
- **sw**: less elaborated in F4, but **kwa kweli** and **kweli ni kwamba** are present in F2b rather than F4.
- **qu**: adverbial truth assertion **chiqap**, **chiqaqta**.

### K. Correspondence / fidelity / resemblance
Some languages have dedicated constructions for matching reality, matching facts, or being faithful to an original.

- **zh**: represented in F4 mainly through essence/substance and distortion, less as a dedicated comparison type.
- **he**: **זהה ל־**, **תואם את העובדות**.
- **sa**: less as a dedicated F4 type, though factual/essential adverbials and evaluative constructions exist.
- **el**: fidelity is present in the lexicon, but F4 emphasizes discourse framing and falsification more than a dedicated fidelity type.
- **en**: *faithful to the original*, *true to life*.
- **tl**: basis/support and suitability are clearer than direct correspondence constructions in F4.
- **sw**: no dedicated F4 comparison type, though resemblance exists elsewhere.
- **qu**: not isolated as an F4 type.

### L. Basis/support constructions
A recurring function in some languages is saying that a claim has grounds or lacks them.

- **zh**: partly through verification and reliability constructions.
- **he**: partly through reliability and correspondence constructions, but not isolated as a separate F4 type.
- **sa**: **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**, **ज्ञानस्य प्रामाण्यम्**.
- **el**: partly through validity and substance language.
- **en**: *sound basis* appears in the lexicon, but not as a separate F4 construction type.
- **tl**: clearly attested: **Salig sa ebidensiya ang ulat**, **May saligan ang kanyang reklamo**.
- **sw**: less as a separate F4 type.
- **qu**: not isolated as a separate F4 type.

## 3. Short narrative comparison

### Recurring cross-language constructional patterns
The strongest recurring pattern is **simple predicative evaluation**: all languages in the F4 data have a basic way to say that something is true, false, correct, reliable, fake, or wrong. This is the most widely attested constructional function.

A second strong recurring pattern is **negative evaluation**, usually by analytic negation, though some languages also use stronger lexical rejection forms. This is widespread across all eight languages.

A third strong pattern is **noun modification**, where truth-related meanings classify nouns such as story, document, source, answer, signature, information, or person. This is also broadly shared, though the morphosyntax differs.

Another recurring pattern is that many languages express truth and falsehood in **speech/reporting constructions** rather than only by static predicates. This is especially clear in Hebrew, Sanskrit, Greek, English, and Swahili, and also present in Chinese.

### Important asymmetries, substitutions, and restrictions
A major asymmetry is that **change-of-state constructions are much less evenly distributed than static evaluation**. English and Tagalog have especially clear “come true / turn out true” patterns. Chinese also has productive change-of-state constructions, but with lexical restrictions. Swahili has a clear dynamic pattern mainly in the credibility domain. Hebrew and Quechua explicitly lack a common general inchoative pattern in the supplied F4 data.

Another asymmetry is that **causative meaning is often handled by lexical verbs instead of a general construction**. For example:
- **el** uses **παραποιώ / πλαστογραφώ**
- **en** uses *authenticate, validate, correct*
- **qu** uses **pantachiy**
- **zh** often uses lexical verbs like **伪造** or correction verbs rather than a broad “make true” pattern

So one strategy often replaces another: **specialized lexical verbs replace a general causative construction**.

A further asymmetry is that **positive person-labeling is often weaker than negative person-labeling**. “Liar” is strongly lexicalized in several languages, while “truth-teller” is often absent, marginal, or replaced by descriptive phrases.

### Cases where one strategy replaces another
Several languages prefer **verification/result constructions** instead of ordinary “become true” patterns:
- **zh** uses **属实 / 经查属实**
- **el** uses **αποδείχθηκε πλαστή**, **αποδεικνύεται ψευδής**
- **en** uses *proved true*, *authenticated*
- **tl** uses **beripikahin / beripikadong**
- **sa** uses proof-based constructions like **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**

Likewise, many languages prefer **speech-object constructions** (“tell the truth,” “say a lie”) instead of a simple lexical verb meaning “truth” or “lie” in the truth domain.

### Weakly expressed, indirect, or absent functions
Some functions are only weakly expressed in the F4 data:

- **General inchoative truth-state marking** is weak or absent in **he** and **qu**, and only limited in **sa** and **sw**.
- **General causative truth-state marking** is weak or absent in **tl** and **sw**, and only indirect in several others.
- **Dedicated correspondence/fidelity constructions** are strongly foregrounded only in **he** and **en** in F4, even though related lexical material exists elsewhere.
- **Basis/support constructions** are clearly constructional mainly in **sa** and **tl**.
- **Seriousness shift from truth vocabulary** is a clear single-language observation for **tl**.

Overall, the F4 data show that **static evaluation and noun classification are broadly shared**, while **dynamic change, causation, verification, and support/grounding are more uneven and often handled by specialized constructions rather than general patterns**.
