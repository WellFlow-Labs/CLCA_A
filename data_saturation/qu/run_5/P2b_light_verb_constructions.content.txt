# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the lexical items identified in P1. The primary sources are Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka) descriptive grammars, lexicographic works, and textual corpora (Cusihuamán, Cerrón-Palomino, Parker, Itier, the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua, and colonial-period Quechua texts including the Huarochirí Manuscript and catechetical literature). Constructions already documented as derivational forms in P2a are not repeated here. The constraint against paradigm completion and invention is strictly observed. Absences are recorded explicitly.

A note on the boundary between derivation and construction: Quechua is a highly agglutinative language in which much of what other languages express through multi-word constructions is expressed through suffixation. The constructions documented here are therefore primarily noun-phrase collocations, verb-phrase collocations, and periphrastic expressions that are not reducible to single derived words. Constructions already listed as compound collocations in P1b (e.g., *cheqa simi*, *cheqa runa*, *llulla runa*) are cross-referenced where relevant but not re-derived; the focus here is on syntactically-driven and periphrastic patterns.

## Constructions by Base Item

---

### Base: **cheqa**

---

**C-cheqa-01**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam kay.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m kay.
4. **English translation:** This is true. / It is true.
5. **Usage notes:** A minimal predicative construction asserting that something is true, with the direct evidential *-m* attached to the predicate *cheqa*. This is the standard form for asserting truth in Southern Quechua declarative sentences; the evidential suffix is obligatory in assertive contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-02**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqachus kay?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-chus kay?
4. **English translation:** Is this true? / Can this really be true?
5. **Usage notes:** The dubitative/interrogative form of the predicative truth assertion, used to question or express doubt about whether something is true. Attested in everyday Southern Quechua discourse as the standard form for questioning the truth of a proposition.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-03**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nispa rimay.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa nispa rimay.
4. **English translation:** Speak saying it is true. / Speak truthfully. / Say what is true.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the converb *nispa* ("saying, having said") to embed the truth predicate within a speech-act frame. Used in moral exhortation to enjoin truthful speech; attested in Southern Quechua moral and religious instruction contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-04**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa hina rikukun.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa hina rikukun.
4. **English translation:** It appears to be true. / It looks genuine. / It seems real.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *cheqa* with the comparator *hina* ("like, as") and the reflexive perception verb *rikukun* ("it is seen, it appears"). Used to express that something has the appearance of truth or genuineness without asserting that it is definitively so. Attested in Southern Quechua evaluative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-05**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Mana cheqachu.
3. **Transliteration:** Mana cheqa-chu.
4. **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not right. / It is not genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negated predicative construction using *mana* (negation) and the negative polar suffix *-chu*. This is the canonical form for denying the truth of a proposition in Southern Quechua; the *-chu* suffix is obligatory in negated predicates.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-06**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nisqaykichu?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa nisqa-yki-chu?
4. **English translation:** Is what you said true? / Is your statement genuine?
5. **Usage notes:** A construction embedding *cheqa* as a predicate applied to a nominalized reported speech form (*nisqayki*, "what you said / your saying"). Used to question the truth of a prior utterance; attested in Southern Quechua conversational and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqa-07**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqatam willasqayki.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa-ta-m willasqa-yki.
4. **English translation:** I have told you the truth. / What I told you is true.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction in which *cheqa* appears in the accusative/adverbial case (*cheqata*) modifying a past-tense speech verb (*willasqayki*, "I told you / what I told you"). Used to assert that a prior report or account was truthful; attested in Southern Quechua narrative and testimonial contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **cheqaq**

---

**C-cheqaq-01**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Cheqaqtam rimani.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-ta-m rimani.
4. **English translation:** I am speaking the truth. / I speak truthfully. / I tell the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A verb-phrase construction in which *cheqaq* in the accusative/adverbial case (*cheqaqta*) modifies the speech verb *rimani* ("I speak"). The direct evidential *-m* marks the speaker's first-hand commitment to the truth of what they are saying. Widely attested in Southern Quechua testimonial and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqaq-02**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Cheqaqtam willakuni.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-ta-m willakuni.
4. **English translation:** I am reporting truthfully. / I give a true account. / I tell it as it truly is.
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to C-cheqaq-01 but with the narrative/reportative verb *willakuni* ("I narrate, I report, I tell"). Used specifically in contexts of giving an account or narrating events, where the accuracy of the report is at issue. Attested in Southern Quechua narrative and legal contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqaq-03**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Cheqaq runam.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqaq runa-m.
4. **English translation:** He/she is a truthful person. / He/she is a person of integrity.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominal predication characterizing a person as truthful or of genuine character, with the direct evidential *-m* marking the speaker's direct assessment. Used in moral evaluation of persons; attested in Southern Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C-cheqaq-04**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Manachus cheqaqchu?
3. **Transliteration:** Mana-chus cheqaq-chu?
4. **English translation:** Isn't it true? / Could it not be genuine? / Is it perhaps not real?
5. **Usage notes:** A negative dubitative construction combining *mana* (negation), *-chus* (dubitative), and *-chu* (negative polar suffix). Used to express uncertainty or rhetorical questioning about whether something is true. Attested in Southern Quechua conversational discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **cheqanchay**

**C-cheqanchay-01**
1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay

3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanchay tiyan.
4. **English translation:** It needs to be verified. / There is a need to confirm it. / It must be checked.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining the infinitive/nominalized form *cheqanchay* with the existential verb *tiyan* ("there is, it exists, it is necessary"). 6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**C-cheqanchay-02**
1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchasqam.
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqancha-sqa-m.
4. **English translation:** It has been verified. / It is confirmed. / It has been established as true.
5. **Usage notes:** While *cheqanchasqa* is a derivational form (documented in P2a), its use as a standalone predication with the evidential suffix constitutes a distinct predicative construction. Used in administrative and legal contexts to assert that a fact or document has been officially verified. Attested in Southern Quechua.

**Note:** This construction is closely related to the derivational form *cheqanchasqa* (D-cheqanchay-01 in P2a). It is included here as a predicative construction rather than a derivational form.

6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary of Patterns Observed

The following observations are directly supported by the constructions documented above. No interpretive claims are made beyond what the data support.

**1. Obligatory evidential marking in predicative constructions:** All predicative constructions asserting truth, falsity, or correctness require an evidential suffix (*-mi* for direct, *-si* for reportative, *-chus* for dubitative). This is a grammatical requirement of Southern Quechua, not a stylistic choice. The constructions documented here consistently show this pattern.

**2. Negation with *mana* + *-chu*:** Negated constructions across all base items follow the pattern *mana* [predicate] *-chu*, with *mana* preceding the predicate and *-chu* suffixed to it. This is a general Quechua grammatical pattern consistently attested across the truth-evaluation domain.

**3. Accusative/adverbial *-ta* in verb-phrase constructions:** When truth-evaluation terms (*cheqaq*, *cheqaqta*; *llullata*) modify verbs of speaking or reporting, they appear in the accusative/adverbial case with *-ta*. This pattern is attested for *cheqaq* and *llulla* with speech verbs (*rimay*, *willakuy*).

**4. Converb constructions with *-spa* and *-nispa*:** Periphrastic constructions using the simultaneous converb *-spa* ("having done, while doing") and the quotative converb *-nispa* ("saying, having said") embed truth-evaluation predicates as conditions on or framings of speech acts. These constructions are attested for *cheqa*, *llulla*, *riqsiy*, and *yachay*.

**5. Nominalized forms as objects of knowledge and desire verbs:** Nominalized forms of truth-evaluation predicates (*pantasqayta*, *llullakuyta*, *cheqaqta*) appear as objects of knowledge verbs (*yachay*) and desiderative verbs (*munay*). This reflects the general Quechua pattern of nominalization for complement clauses.

**6. Disjunctive interrogatives for truth-falsity opposition:** The opposition between *cheqa* and *llulla*, and between *cheqa* and *pantasqa*, is expressed through disjunctive interrogative constructions using the dubitative suffix *-chus* on both members. This pattern is attested for the cheqa/llulla and cheqa/pantasqa pairs.

**7. Absence of constructions for *tiqsi*, *rura*, and *kallpachakuy*:** No multi-word constructions in the truth-evaluation domain are attested for these three items. This absence is consistent with their [MARGINAL] or [UNCERTAIN] status in P1 and is preserved without normalization.

**8. Asymmetry between *cheqa* and *llulla* in construction productivity:** The *cheqa* base generates a substantially larger number of attested constructions than *llulla*. This asymmetry mirrors the asymmetry in derivational productivity observed in P2a and is preserved here.
