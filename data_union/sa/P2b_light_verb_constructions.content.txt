# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1, P1b, P2a)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This document records attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving the P1 lexical items. The inventory draws on classical Sanskrit literature, philosophical treatises, grammatical texts, legal literature, epic and Purāṇic texts, and (where noted) modern Sanskrit-medium usage.

The boundary between derivational compounds (handled in P2a) and multi-word constructions is observed throughout. Compounds written as single orthographic units are excluded here even when semantically compositional; the focus is on constructions that remain syntactically separable or that function as fixed multi-word phrases, collocations, or periphrastic expressions. Where a construction is a fixed phrase attested as such in the literature, it is included even if its components could in principle be separated.

Sanskrit presents a particular methodological challenge: the language's extensive compounding (*samāsa*) and derivational morphology means that many semantic functions that other languages express periphrastically are handled in Sanskrit through compounding or suffixation. This means that the inventory of genuinely periphrastic or idiomatic multi-word constructions is smaller relative to the derivational inventory than would be the case in an analytic language. Absence of constructions for certain items reflects this structural fact and is recorded explicitly.

Absence is recorded explicitly for each P1 item where no common construction is identified. Asymmetry across items is preserved and reflects the data.

## P1 Item 1: सत्य / *satya*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं वद
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
4. **English translation:** Speak the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed injunctive phrase from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1), widely cited in ethical and educational contexts as a foundational moral injunction. The construction *satyaṃ vada* (with *satya* in the accusative as the object of *vad-*) is one of the most widely attested ethical imperatives in Sanskrit literature. Functions as a complete imperative sentence and is quoted as a unit across philosophical, didactic, and popular discourse. The phrase appears in the *śikṣāvallī*'s instructions to the graduating student: *satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara* ("Speak truth; walk in *dharma*").
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं ब्रूहि
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūhi
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth; speak truly.
5. **Usage notes:** A near-synonym of *satyaṃ vada* using the root *brū-* (to speak, to say) rather than *vad-*; appears in legal and narrative contexts as an injunction to a witness or interlocutor to speak truthfully. The two constructions are functionally equivalent but *brūhi* is more common in direct address in legal and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं ब्रूयात्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
4. **English translation:** One should speak the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed prescriptive phrase from Manusmṛti and related Dharmaśāstra literature, stating the duty of truthful speech in the optative mood. Often cited as the first half of a longer injunction qualified by conditions under which truth-telling may be overridden. The full verse (*satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt, na brūyāt satyam apriyam*) is a well-known ethical maxim: "One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasant; one should not speak the truth if it is unpleasant."
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं ब्रूयात् प्रियं ब्रूयात् न ब्रूयात् सत्यमप्रियम्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt na brūyāt satyam apriyam
4. **English translation:** One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasing; one should not speak truth that is unpleasant.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed three-part injunctive sequence from Manusmṛti (4.138), widely cited in ethical discourse on the conditions and limits of truthful speech. The full sequence is quoted as a unit in discussions of the relationship between truth and kindness in speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यमेव जयते
3. **Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
4. **English translation:** Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. The *eva* (emphatic particle, "alone, indeed") is integral to the construction and cannot be omitted without changing the meaning. Quoted as a complete sentence across philosophical, political, and rhetorical contexts; functions as a maxim asserting the ultimate triumph of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः
3. **Transliteration:** satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ
4. **English translation:** Established in truth / grounded in truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the locative of *satya* with the past passive participle *pratiṣṭhita* (established, grounded, from *prati-* + root *sthā-*), used in epic and Purāṇic literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness. Appears in Vedāntic and epic literature as a characterization of the sage or the virtuous person. The locative + *pratiṣṭhita* pattern is productive across ethical vocabulary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यात् न प्रचलति
3. **Transliteration:** satyāt na pracalati
4. **English translation:** Does not deviate from truth / does not swerve from truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the ablative of *satya* with the negative particle *na* and the verb *pracalati* (deviates, moves away from, from *pra-* + root *cal-*), used in epic literature to characterize a person of unwavering truthfulness. The ablative + *na pracalati* pattern is a standard way of expressing steadfast adherence to a value.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यात् न चलति / सत्यात् न विचलति
3. **Transliteration:** satyāt na calati / satyāt na vicalati
4. **English translation:** Does not deviate from truth; does not swerve from truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using the ablative *satyāt* (from truth) with verbs of motion (*cal-*, *vical-*) and negation, expressing steadfast adherence to truth; common in epic literature as a characterization of virtuous heroes.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं परं धर्मः
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ
4. **English translation:** Truth is the highest dharma.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed maxim attested in the Mahābhārata (e.g., Ādiparvan) and widely cited in ethical discourse, asserting the primacy of truthfulness among all dharmic obligations. Functions as a complete nominal sentence and is quoted as a unit.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं हि परमो धर्मः
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ
4. **English translation:** For truth is indeed the highest dharma.
5. **Usage notes:** A variant of the preceding maxim with the emphatic particle *hi* (indeed, for), widely attested in the Mahābhārata and in later ethical literature. The two forms (*satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ* and *satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ*) circulate as near-synonymous maxims and are sometimes cited interchangeably.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं नास्ति परो धर्मः
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ
4. **English translation:** There is no higher *dharma* than truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A formulaic maxim attested in the Mahābhārata and widely cited in ethical literature. Functions as a fixed proverbial expression asserting the supremacy of truth among moral values. The construction *nāsti paro X* ("there is no higher X") is itself a formulaic pattern in Sanskrit ethical literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्येन उत्तभिता भूमिः
3. **Transliteration:** satyena uttabhitā bhūmiḥ
4. **English translation:** The earth is upheld by truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed cosmological statement attested in Vedic and epic literature, asserting that truth is the foundational support of the earth and cosmos. Used in hymnic and didactic contexts to express the ontological and cosmological primacy of *satya*. The instrumental *satyena* (by means of truth) with a passive participle is the standard construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं न परित्यजेत्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ na parityajet
4. **English translation:** One should not abandon truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A prescriptive construction using the accusative of *satya* with the negative optative of *parityaj-* (to abandon, from *pari-* + root *tyaj-*), used in Dharmaśāstra and epic literature to state the duty of maintaining truthfulness under all circumstances. The negative optative construction is a standard prescriptive form in Sanskrit ethical literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्येन शपामि / सत्येन शपे
3. **Transliteration:** satyena śapāmi / satyena śape
4. **English translation:** I swear by truth / I take an oath by truth.
5. **Usage notes:** An oath formula using the instrumental *satyena* (by truth) with the verb *śap-* (to swear, to take an oath); appears in legal and epic literature as a formula for oath-taking. The instrumental marks truth as the instrument or ground of the oath.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं किल / सत्यं खलु
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ kila / satyaṃ khalu
4. **English translation:** Truly indeed; it is indeed true (that...).
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse-marking construction in which *satyam* (neuter accusative used adverbially) combines with affirmative particles (*kila*, *khalu*) to assert the truth of a following proposition; common in narrative and philosophical prose as a marker of affirmation or concession.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं तु / सत्यं तथापि
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ tu / satyaṃ tathāpi
4. **English translation:** True, but... / True, yet even so...
5. **Usage notes:** A concessive construction in which *satyam* (adverbially used) concedes a point before introducing a qualification or counter-consideration; common in philosophical argumentation and commentary literature as a device for acknowledging an opponent's point before responding.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम्
3. **Transliteration:** satyāt na pramaditavyam
4. **English translation:** One must not deviate from truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A gerundive construction expressing prohibition, attested in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1) as part of the same passage as *satyaṃ vada*. The gerundive *pramaditavyam* (from root *mad-* with prefix *pra-*, "to be negligent, to deviate") with the ablative of *satya* and the negation *na* forms a fixed injunctive formula.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं प्रतिजानीते
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ pratijānīte
4. **English translation:** One pledges truth; one vows truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using *satya* in the accusative with the verb *pratijñā-* (to pledge, to vow); appears in legal and epic contexts to describe the act of making a truthful pledge or vow.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 19**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** साधु इति मन्यते
3. **Transliteration:** sādhu iti manyate
4. **English translation:** Considers (it) to be correct / regards (it) as proper.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *sādhu* within an *iti*-clause as the content of the verb *man-* (to think, to consider), used in philosophical and grammatical literature to describe the cognitive act of evaluating something as correct or proper.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 2: ऋत / *ṛta*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य पथा
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya pathā
4. **English translation:** By the path of cosmic order / along the way of truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase from Vedic hymns (Ṛgveda), using the genitive of *ṛta* with the instrumental of *path* (path, way), designating the way of cosmic order along which the gods and the righteous travel. Attested as a formulaic expression in ritual and cosmological contexts. Confined to Vedic poetic register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य पन्थाः
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya panthāḥ
4. **English translation:** The path of *ṛta*; the way of cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A genitive construction attested in the Ṛgveda to designate the path or way that accords with cosmic order, often in the context of ritual correctness or moral conduct. The phrase functions as a conventional expression for the right way of acting or proceeding.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतं च सत्यं च
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca
4. **English translation:** Both cosmic order and truth / ṛta and satya.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed dvandva-style pairing attested in Vedic texts (e.g., Ṛgveda 10.190.1: *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhyajāyata*; also Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.1.1), where *ṛta* and *satya* are enumerated together as the foundational principles arising from cosmic heat (*tapas*). The pairing functions as a formulaic expression of the two aspects of cosmic and moral truth. The coordination is not merely additive but marks a conventional pairing that appears repeatedly in Vedic literature; the two terms are treated as complementary aspects of a single domain.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतेन ऋतम् अपिहितम्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtena ṛtam apihitam
4. **English translation:** Truth is covered by truth / cosmic order is veiled by cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed enigmatic phrase from the Ṛgveda (5.62.1), also attested in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (5.15.1) and Īśā Upaniṣad (15), using the instrumental of *ṛta* with the past passive participle *apihita* (covered, concealed, from *api-* + root *dhā-*), expressing the paradoxical concealment of cosmic order within itself. Cited in philosophical and exegetical literature as an example of Vedic enigmatic speech (*brahmodya*). It is a fixed liturgical and philosophical formula rather than a free compositional phrase.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य योनौ
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya yonau
4. **English translation:** In the womb/source of cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed locative phrase from Vedic hymns, using the genitive of *ṛta* with the locative of *yoni* (womb, source, origin), designating the primordial source from which cosmic order arises or in which it is grounded. Attested in Ṛgvedic hymns in cosmological and ritual contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य योनिः
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya yoniḥ
4. **English translation:** The womb/source of *ṛta*; the origin of cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A genitive construction attested in Vedic literature to designate the source or origin of cosmic order, typically applied to a deity (especially Varuṇa or Mitra) or to the sacrificial fire. Functions as a conventional epithet-formula in Vedic hymns.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on further constructions:** The derivational productivity of *ṛta* in multi-word constructions is largely confined to Vedic Sanskrit. Post-Vedic multi-word constructions built on *ṛta* as a free element are not commonly attested. The forms listed above represent the main attested expressions.

## P1 Item 3: यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं वदति
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ vadati
4. **English translation:** Speaks accurately / speaks as the matter is.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb (accusative of manner) with the verb *vadati* (speaks, from root *vad-*), used in philosophical and legal contexts to characterize speech that accurately represents its object. The adverbial use of *yathārtha* with verbs of speaking and knowing is widely attested.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं वक्ति / यथार्थं कथयति
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ vakti / yathārthaṃ kathayati
4. **English translation:** Speaks accurately; describes correctly; says what is true to the object.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using *yathārtha* adverbially (in the accusative) with verbs of speaking (*vac-*, *kath-*) to evaluate whether a statement accurately corresponds to its referent; common in philosophical and grammatical commentary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं ज्ञानम्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ jñānam
4. **English translation:** Knowledge that is as the object is / accurate cognition.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominal phrase using *yathārtha* as an adjective modifying *jñāna* (knowledge), used in epistemological literature to characterize cognition that correctly corresponds to its object. Functionally equivalent to *pramā* but emphasizing the correspondence relation explicitly. Attested across Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā epistemological literature. This is the conventional way of characterizing a cognition as veridical or accurate in the Nyāya tradition; the construction appears in definitional contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं न भवति
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ na bhavati
4. **English translation:** Is not as the object is / does not correspond to the object.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb with the negative copula *na bhavati* (is not), used in epistemological and aesthetic contexts to characterize a representation, description, or cognition that fails to correspond to its object. The negative form is used to identify cases of inaccuracy or misrepresentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थदर्शिन्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthadarśin
4. **English translation:** One who sees things as they are / one who perceives accurately.
5. **Usage notes:** A compound adjective using *yathārtha* with *darśin* (one who sees, from root *dṛś-*), used in philosophical and epic literature to characterize a person who perceives or understands things as they actually are. While morphologically a compound, it functions as a predicate construction in context and is included here as a fixed characterizing expression.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** नाम यथार्थम् अस्ति
3. **Transliteration:** nāma yathārtham asti
4. **English translation:** The name is accurate (to its referent); the name corresponds to the object.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction evaluating whether a name (*nāma*) accurately corresponds to the thing it names; appears in philosophical and grammatical literature in discussions of the relationship between words and their referents. The predicate *yathārtham asti* functions as a copular evaluation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थज्ञानम् उत्पद्यते
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthajñānam utpadyate
4. **English translation:** Accurate knowledge arises; correct cognition is produced.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction describing the arising or production of accurate cognition; *yathārthajñāna* functions as the subject of *utpad-* (to arise, to be produced), a standard verb in epistemological descriptions of cognitive events.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं वक्तुम्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ vaktum
4. **English translation:** To speak accurately; to state things as they are.
5. **Usage notes:** An infinitive construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb with the infinitive of *vac-* (to speak), used in philosophical and legal contexts to characterize the obligation or intention to describe things accurately. Appears in discussions of testimony and witness obligation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** No idiomatic constructions specific to *yathārtha* that are not reducible to its adverbial or adjectival use are identified. The term functions primarily as an adjective and adverb, and its constructions are largely transparent compositional uses.

## P1 Item 4: प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणं किम्?
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇaṃ kim?
4. **English translation:** What is the means of valid knowledge? / What is the authority?
5. **Usage notes:** A standard interrogative formula in Sanskrit philosophical debate, used to challenge an opponent to specify the valid means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*) by which a claim is established. Attested as a formulaic opening move in philosophical disputation across Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Buddhist epistemological literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** किं प्रमाणम्
3. **Transliteration:** kiṃ pramāṇam
4. **English translation:** What is the evidence? / What is the valid means of cognition?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction used in philosophical debate to challenge an interlocutor to produce evidence or a valid means of cognition for their claim; a standard formula in Sanskrit philosophical dialogue.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇena siddham
4. **English translation:** Established by a valid means of knowledge / proven by authority.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the instrumental of *pramāṇa* with the past passive participle *siddha* (established, from root *sidh-*), used in philosophical discourse to assert that a claim has been epistemically warranted. The instrumental + *siddha* pattern is a standard formula for asserting epistemic warrant across philosophical schools.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणाभावात्
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāvāt
4. **English translation:** Due to the absence of a valid means of knowledge / for lack of evidence.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed ablative phrase using the compound *pramāṇābhāva* (absence of a valid means of knowledge) in the ablative of cause, used in philosophical argument to explain why a claim cannot be established or why a position must be rejected. Widely attested as a standard dismissive formula in Sanskrit philosophical debate.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणं नास्ति
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇaṃ nāsti
4. **English translation:** There is no valid means of knowledge / there is no authority for this.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *pramāṇa* as subject with the negative existential *nāsti* (there is not, from *na* + *asti*), used in philosophical and legal discourse to deny that a claim has any epistemic warrant. A standard formula for rejecting an opponent's position.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति
3. **Transliteration:** na tatra pramāṇam asti
4. **English translation:** There is no valid means of cognition for that; there is no evidence for that.
5. **Usage notes:** The negated form of the existential construction, used in philosophical argumentation to deny that a claim has epistemic support; a standard formula for rejecting an opponent's position as lacking evidential grounding.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणम् अस्ति / प्रमाणं विद्
