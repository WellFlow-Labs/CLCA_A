# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions are extracted only where the CONTEXT explicitly documents a contrast, antonym relationship, or constructional asymmetry. Where the CONTEXT notes a contrast without fully elaborating it, that contrast is included with a note on the basis of inclusion. No oppositions have been inferred beyond what the data supports. Where the CONTEXT documents an asymmetry (e.g., one term has a construction that the other lacks), this is recorded as a constructional contrast rather than a lexical one.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** ukweli
**Term B:** uongo
**Transliteration:** ukweli / uongo
**English translation:** truth, truthfulness / lie, falsehood, deception
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly identifies *uongo* as the antonym of *ukweli* (P1: "Antonym of *ukweli*"). Both are abstract nominals in noun class 11/14 formed with the *u-* prefix; they are the primary polar opposites in the propositional truth domain.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** halisi
**Term B:** bandia
**Transliteration:** halisi / bandia
**English translation:** genuine, authentic, real / fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly identifies *bandia* as the antonym of *halisi* (P1: "Antonym of *halisi*"). *Halisi* evaluates an object as genuine; *bandia* evaluates it as not genuine. The contrast is directly illustrated by the paired expressions *bidhaa halisi* (genuine goods) and *bidhaa bandia* (fake goods) documented in P2b.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** Hii ni kweli.
**Term B:** Si kweli.
**Transliteration:** Hii ni kweli. / Si kweli.
**English translation:** "This is true." / "It is not true. / That is false."
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both the affirmative copular construction (*ni* + *kweli*) and its negative counterpart (*si* + *kweli*) as the primary means of propositional assertion and denial respectively (P4a §1–2, P4b). The contrast is between the invariant copula *ni* and its suppletive negative *si*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** Ni sahihi.
**Term B:** Si sahihi.
**Transliteration:** Ni sahihi. / Si sahihi.
**English translation:** "It is correct." / "It is incorrect. / That is not right."
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both the affirmative and negative copular constructions with *sahihi* as predicate (P1b Root 3, P2b §6, P4a §1–2, P4b). The contrast is the same *ni* / *si* opposition as in Opposition 5, applied to the correctness dimension rather than the propositional truth dimension.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** ukweli mzima
**Term B:** nusu ukweli
**Transliteration:** ukweli mzima / nusu ukweli
**English translation:** "the whole truth; the full truth" / "half-truth; partial truth"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both expressions (P1b Root 1, P2b §2). *Ukweli mzima* asserts complete disclosure; *nusu ukweli* describes a statement that is technically accurate but misleading through omission. The contrast is between completeness and partiality of truth.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** kuhalalisha
**Term B:** kuharamisha
**Transliteration:** kuhalalisha / kuharamisha
**English translation:** "to legitimize; to legalize; to authorize; to make lawful" / "to prohibit; to make unlawful; to criminalize; to forbid"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both causative verbal forms derived from *halali* and *haramu* respectively (P2a §22–23). They are morphologically parallel causatives (*-sha* suffix) built on the lexically opposed bases *halali* and *haramu*, expressing opposite acts of legal/moral authorization and prohibition.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** kuthibitisha
**Term B:** kudanganya
**Transliteration:** kuthibitisha / kudanganya
**English translation:** "to confirm; to verify; to prove; to authenticate" / "to deceive; to lie; to mislead; to cheat"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both verbs as central to the truth domain (P1, P2a). *Kuthibitisha* describes the act of establishing truth or authenticity; *kudanganya* describes the act of creating false impressions. They are not morphologically related but occupy opposite functional positions in the domain of acts bearing on truth.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** Sema ukweli.
**Term B:** Anapiga uongo.
**Transliteration:** Sema ukweli. / Anapiga uongo.
**English translation:** "Tell the truth. / Speak the truth." / "He/she is lying. / He/she tells lies."
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both constructions (P1b Root 1, P2b §1, §3, P4a §9, P4b) and explicitly notes the asymmetry: "the 'tell the truth' directive uses *sema ukweli* (light verb *kusema*), not a *piga* construction; *piga* is restricted to *uongo* in this domain" (P4b Summary Notes). The two constructions use different light verbs (*sema* vs. *piga*) for the opposite acts of truth-telling and lying.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** bidhaa halisi
**Term B:** bidhaa bandia
**Transliteration:** bidhaa halisi / bidhaa bandia
**English translation:** "genuine goods; authentic products" / "fake goods; counterfeit products; imitation merchandise"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly documents these as a directly contrasting pair (P2b §4 Construction 2: "Used in commercial contexts to distinguish genuine products from counterfeits. Contrasts with *bidhaa bandia* (fake goods)"). They are the most frequently cited minimal-pair illustration of the *halisi* / *bandia* opposition in commercial discourse.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** pesa bandia
**Term B:** pesa za ghushi
**Transliteration:** pesa bandia / pesa za ghushi
**English translation:** "counterfeit money; fake currency" / "counterfeit money; fraudulent currency"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both expressions as referring to counterfeit currency (P2b §13 Construction 1, §14 Construction 4) and notes that *ghushi* "overlaps with *bandia* but carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or criminal fraud" (P1). The contrast is not one of opposite meaning but of degree of connotation: *bandia* is neutral with respect to intent; *ghushi* implies deliberate criminal fraud.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** ukweli uliofichwa
**Term B:** ukweli unaosimama
**Transliteration:** ukweli uliofichwa / ukweli unaosimama
**English translation:** "hidden truth; concealed truth; suppressed truth" / "truth that stands; enduring truth; truth that holds up"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both relative-clause nominal constructions (P1b Root 1). *Ukweli uliofichwa* describes truth that has been suppressed; *ukweli unaosimama* describes truth that persists and cannot be overturned. The contrast is between truth as vulnerable to concealment and truth as resistant to challenge.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** haki ya kisheria
**Term B:** haki ya kimila
**Transliteration:** haki ya kisheria / haki ya kimila
**English translation:** "legal right; statutory right" / "customary right; traditional right"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both expressions (P1b Root 2, P2b §8) and explicitly notes that *haki ya kisheria* is used "to distinguish rights that are formally recognized by law from moral or customary claims" (P1b Root 2). The contrast is between rights grounded in statutory law and rights grounded in custom or tradition.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** kwa kweli
**Term B:** kwa uaminifu
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli / kwa uaminifu
**English translation:** "truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest" / "faithfully; trustworthily; with integrity; honestly"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both adverbial framing constructions (P1b Root 1, P2b §11 Construction 4, P4a §8). Both use the *kwa* + nominal pattern to frame a statement or action, but *kwa kweli* frames propositional sincerity (what is said is true) while *kwa uaminifu* frames behavioral integrity (how something is done is faithful). The CONTEXT does not explicitly identify these as contrasting, but documents them as parallel constructions with distinct semantic orientations within the same pattern.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
