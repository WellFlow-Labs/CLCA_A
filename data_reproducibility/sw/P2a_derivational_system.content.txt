# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### BASE: **kweli** (truth, true)

#### Derived Form 1: **ukweli**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Derived Form:** ukweli
- **Transliteration:** u-kweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** u- (noun class 11/14 abstract noun prefix) + kweli (truth root)
- **Natural English translation:** truth, reality, fact, the truth
- **Usage description:** Nominalizes the root into an abstract noun denoting truth as a concept or state of affairs. Used extensively in philosophical, legal, and everyday discourse to refer to truth as an entity or quality.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: **ukweli** (no further productive derivation identified)
**Note:** The form ukweli itself does not productively generate further derivational forms through affixation. Compounds and phrases exist (documented in P1b) but these are syntactic rather than morphological derivations.

## SUMMARY OF DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS

### Pattern 1: u- Abstract Nominalization
**Productivity:** Very high
**Examples:** ukweli, uongo, usahihi, uhalisi, uadilifu, udanganyifu, uhakika, usawa, ukamili, udhahiri, ubatili, ubovu, unyofu, utupu

**Description:** The u- prefix (noun class 11/14) productively derives abstract nouns from adjectives and some verb stems, denoting qualities or states. This is the most productive derivational pattern in the truth domain.

### Pattern 2: -ish- Causative Verbalization
**Productivity:** High
**Examples:** hakikisha, sawazisha, kamilisha, dhihirisha, batilisha

**Description:** The -ish- causative suffix productively derives verbs meaning "to cause to be X" or "to make X" from adjectives and nouns. Common in the truth domain for verbs of verification, completion, and invalidation.

### Pattern 3: -ifu Adjectival Derivation
**Productivity:** Moderate
**Examples:** -danganyifu, -adilifu

**Description:** The -ifu suffix derives adjectives indicating characteristic qualities, particularly from verb roots. Less productive than nominalization but well-attested.

### Pattern 4: Reciprocal -ana Verbalization
**Productivity:** Low (in this domain)
**Examples:** hojiana

**Description:** The -ana reciprocal suffix can verbalize nouns in the argument/debate domain, but this is not highly productive in the truth domain generally.

### Pattern 5: Agent/Result Nominalization (m- and other class prefixes)
**Productivity:** Moderate
**Examples:** mdanganyifu, mthibitisho

**Description:** Various noun class prefixes derive agent nouns (m- class 1) or result/instrument nouns (m- class 3) from verbal or adjectival bases.

## NOTES ON GAPS AND ASYMMETRIES

1. **Arabic loans show limited derivation:** Forms borrowed from Arabic (haki, hoja, dhati, busara) generally do not participate in productive Bantu derivational morphology, functioning primarily as invariant nouns.

2. **Causative gap:** Not all adjectives productively form causatives. For example, *sahihisha is not commonly attested, despite the theoretical possibility.

3. **Verbal base asymmetry:** Some roots appear primarily as adjectives (sahihi, halisi) while others appear primarily as verbs (thibitisha, danganya), affecting their derivational possibilities.

4. **Reciprocal limitation:** The reciprocal -ana pattern is productive mainly with verbs of communication and interaction (hojiana) but does not extend to most truth-domain vocabulary.

5. **No diminutive/augmentative patterns:** Unlike some semantic domains, truth vocabulary does not productively use diminutive or augmentative derivation.
