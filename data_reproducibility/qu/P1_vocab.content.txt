# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## Lexical Items

### 1. chiqaq
**Form:** chiqaq  
**Literal meaning:** true, truthful (from root *chiqay* "to be true")  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic

This term is used to affirm the accuracy or genuineness of statements, objects, or persons. It appears in both everyday discourse to validate claims and in more formal contexts to distinguish authentic from false representations.

**[CORE]**

---

### 2. cheqaq
**Form:** cheqaq  
**Literal meaning:** variant of *chiqaq* (dialectal variation)  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, correct

This is a dialectal variant of *chiqaq* used in some Quechua varieties. It functions identically to affirm truth or accuracy of statements and situations.

**[CORE]**

---

### 3. llulla
**Form:** llulla  
**Literal meaning:** lie, false  
**Typical English translations:** false, lie, untrue, deceptive

Used to characterize statements or representations as false or deceptive. This term can function as both noun (a lie) and adjective (false), and is commonly used in moral evaluations of dishonest speech or behavior.

**[CORE]**

---

### 4. allin
**Form:** allin  
**Literal meaning:** good, well  
**Typical English translations:** good, right, correct, proper, well

This is a general evaluative term used for positive assessment of conduct, objects, states, and actions. It appears in moral contexts (good behavior), practical contexts (done correctly), and aesthetic contexts (pleasing, beautiful).

**[CORE]**

---

### 5. mana allin
**Form:** mana allin  
**Literal meaning:** not good  
**Typical English translations:** bad, wrong, improper, incorrect

A fixed negative expression used to evaluate conduct, states, or objects as deficient or improper. This is the standard way to express negative moral or practical evaluation in many Quechua varieties.

**[CORE]**

---

### 6. chanin
**Form:** chanin  
**Literal meaning:** having value, valid  
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, worthy, valuable

Used to affirm the legitimacy or validity of claims, documents, or authority. It appears in legal and administrative contexts to indicate something has proper standing or recognition.

**[CORE]**

---

### 7. rikch'ay
**Form:** rikch'ay  
**Literal meaning:** to resemble, to be similar  
**Typical English translations:** to resemble, to look like, to be similar to

This verb is used to express resemblance or similarity between objects, persons, or representations. It is commonly used when evaluating whether something matches or corresponds to something else.

**[CORE]**

---

### 8. chiqan
**Form:** chiqan  
**Literal meaning:** correct, exact  
**Typical English translations:** correct, exact, precise, accurate

Used to evaluate the accuracy or precision of statements, measurements, or representations. It emphasizes exactness and correspondence to reality or standards.

**[CORE]**

---

### 9. pantay
**Form:** pantay  
**Literal meaning:** to err, to be mistaken  
**Typical English translations:** to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong

This verb describes making errors or being mistaken in judgment, action, or statement. It is used in evaluating incorrect conduct or false beliefs without necessarily implying moral fault.

**[CORE]**

---

### 10. pantasqa
**Form:** pantasqa  
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, erred (past participle of *pantay*)  
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error

The participial form used to characterize something as containing error or being mistaken. It applies to statements, beliefs, or actions that have gone astray from correctness.

**[CORE]**

---

### 11. sut'i
**Form:** sut'i  
**Literal meaning:** clear, evident  
**Typical English translations:** clear, evident, obvious, manifest

Used to evaluate the clarity or obviousness of statements, situations, or truths. It indicates that something is plainly visible or understandable without obscurity.

**[CORE]**

---

### 12. paqta
**Form:** paqta  
**Literal meaning:** exact, just right  
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, just right, fitting

This term evaluates correspondence or fit, indicating that something matches exactly or is precisely appropriate. It is used for measurements, timing, and appropriateness of actions or objects.

**[CORE]**

---

### 13. yachay
**Form:** yachay  
**Literal meaning:** to know, knowledge  
**Typical English translations:** to know, knowledge, to understand

While primarily meaning "to know," this term is relevant in epistemic contexts where knowledge is distinguished from belief or error. It appears in evaluations of whether someone truly knows versus merely thinks they know.

**[CORE]**

---

### 14. yuyay
**Form:** yuyay  
**Literal meaning:** to think, to remember, thought  
**Typical English translations:** to think, to believe, opinion, thought

Used to express belief or opinion as distinct from certain knowledge. It appears in contexts evaluating subjective versus objective claims.

**[CORE]**

---

### 15. ch'ulla
**Form:** ch'ulla  
**Literal meaning:** single, odd (not paired)  
**Typical English translations:** single, odd, unpaired, incomplete

Used to characterize something as incomplete or lacking its proper complement. It can evaluate objects or situations as deficient in their essential pairing or completeness.

**[CORE]**

---

### 16. hunt'a
**Form:** hunt'a  
**Literal meaning:** full, complete  
**Typical English translations:** full, complete, whole, sufficient

Used to evaluate completeness or sufficiency of objects, actions, or states. It indicates that something has reached its proper measure or fulfillment.

**[CORE]**

---

### 17. k'uchi
**Form:** k'uchi  
**Literal meaning:** crooked, bent  
**Typical English translations:** crooked, bent, dishonest, corrupt

Beyond its literal meaning, this term is used metaphorically to evaluate conduct or persons as dishonest or morally corrupt. It appears in moral and legal contexts to characterize improper behavior.

**[CORE]**

---

### 18. chiqamanta
**Form:** chiqamanta  
**Literal meaning:** from truth, truly (ablative of *chiqaq*)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, in truth, genuinely

An adverbial expression used to emphasize the truth or genuineness of a statement. It functions to strengthen assertions and affirm their correspondence to reality.

**[CORE]**

---

### 19. rikuy
**Form:** rikuy  
**Literal meaning:** to see, to look  
**Typical English translations:** to see, to perceive, to witness

While primarily a perception verb, it is used in epistemic contexts to indicate direct evidence or witnessing as a basis for knowledge claims, distinguishing seen from merely heard or believed information.

**[CORE]**

---

### 20. uyariy
**Form:** uyariy  
**Literal meaning:** to hear, to listen  
**Typical English translations:** to hear, to listen, to obey

Used in contexts involving reported information or hearsay, distinguishing indirect knowledge from direct perception. Also used for obedience, linking hearing to proper conduct.

**[CORE]**

---

### 21. kamachiy
**Form:** kamachiy  
**Literal meaning:** to order, to command, to establish law  
**Typical English translations:** to command, to order, to legislate, to establish

Used in legal and moral contexts to describe the establishment of rules or proper order. It appears in evaluations of legitimate authority and rightful conduct.

**[CORE]**

---

### 22. huch'uy
**Form:** huch'uy  
**Literal meaning:** small, little  
**Typical English translations:** small, little, minor, insignificant

Used to evaluate the magnitude or importance of faults, errors, or matters. It can minimize the significance of mistakes or wrongdoing in moral evaluations.

**[CORE]**

---

### 23. hatun
**Form:** hatun  
**Literal meaning:** big, large, great  
**Typical English translations:** big, large, great, important, serious

Used to evaluate the magnitude or seriousness of matters, including moral faults or errors. It emphasizes the weight or significance of conduct or situations.

**[CORE]**

---

### 24. sumaq
**Form:** sumaq  
**Literal meaning:** beautiful, good, pleasant  
**Typical English translations:** beautiful, good, pleasant, excellent

An aesthetic and moral evaluative term indicating beauty, goodness, or excellence. It is used for positive evaluation of objects, persons, conduct, and situations across multiple domains.

**[CORE]**

---

### 25. millay
**Form:** millay  
**Literal meaning:** ugly, disgusting, abominable  
**Typical English translations:** ugly, disgusting, abominable, hateful

A strong negative evaluative term used for aesthetic, moral, and general disapproval. It characterizes objects, conduct, or persons as repugnant or morally reprehensible.

**[CORE]**

---

### 26. yupaychay
**Form:** yupaychay  
**Literal meaning:** to honor, to respect, to value  
**Typical English translations:** to honor, to respect, to revere, to value

Used in contexts evaluating the worthiness of persons or things for respect and honor. It appears in moral and social contexts regarding proper recognition of value or status.

**[CORE]**

---

### 27. qhapaq
**Form:** qhapaq  
**Literal meaning:** rich, powerful, noble  
**Typical English translations:** rich, powerful, noble, magnificent

Used to evaluate high status, wealth, or excellence. Historically used for nobility and continues to indicate superior quality or standing in various contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

### 28. wakcha
**Form:** wakcha  
**Literal meaning:** poor, orphan, lacking  
**Typical English translations:** poor, orphan, destitute, lacking

Used to evaluate deficiency in resources, status, or support. It appears in social and moral contexts regarding those who lack proper means or protection.

**[CORE]**

---

### 29. tukuy
**Form:** tukuy  
**Literal meaning:** all, complete, whole  
**Typical English translations:** all, complete, whole, entire

Used to evaluate completeness or totality. It appears in contexts assessing whether something encompasses all necessary parts or aspects.

**[CORE]**

---

### 30. ch'usaq
**Form:** ch'usaq  
**Literal meaning:** empty, void  
**Typical English translations:** empty, void, hollow, lacking substance

Used to evaluate absence or lack of content, substance, or validity. It can characterize statements as empty or lacking genuine meaning, or objects as devoid of their proper contents.

**[CORE]**
