# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo / hindi totoo
**English translation:** true / not true
**Description of contrast:** *Hindi totoo* is the standard negation of *totoo*, used to deny the truth or reality of a statement or situation. The contrast is between asserting and denying factual correspondence with reality, as in *Hindi totoo ang balita* ("The news is not true").
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** totoo / peke
**English translation:** real / fake
**Description of contrast:** These two terms are used in direct contrast within the same construction, as attested in *Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala* ("The product looked real but turned out to be fake"). *Totoo* asserts factual reality or genuineness; *peke* asserts deliberate inauthenticity of an object or performance.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** tama
**Term B:** mali
**Transliteration:** tama / mali
**English translation:** correct / wrong
**Description of contrast:** The P1 data explicitly identifies *mali* as the direct antonym of *tama*, applied to statements, beliefs, actions, or persons. The contrast is between conformity to fact or standard (*Tama ang sagot mo* "Your answer is correct") and deviation from it (*Mali ang datos* "The data is wrong").
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** tama ka
**Term B:** mali ka
**Transliteration:** tama ka / mali ka
**English translation:** you are right / you are wrong
**Description of contrast:** These are the standard minimal predicate constructions for affirming and denying a person's correctness respectively, documented in P2b as the most frequent utterances for agreement and disagreement in Tagalog conversation.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** di-tunay
**Transliteration:** tunay / di-tunay
**English translation:** genuine / inauthentic
**Description of contrast:** *Di-tunay* is formed by prefixing the negation clitic *di-* to *tunay*, producing an evaluative term for something that lacks genuine character or authenticity. The P1 data notes that *di-tunay* is less lexicalized than *huwad* or *peke* for the same evaluative purpose.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** huwad
**Transliteration:** tunay / huwad
**English translation:** genuine / counterfeit
**Description of contrast:** *Tunay* evaluates something as intrinsically what it claims to be; *huwad* evaluates something as deliberately made to appear genuine when it is not. The P1 data contrasts *orihinal* and *awtentiko* with *huwad* and *peke*, and the same contrast applies to *tunay* as the native authenticity term.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** may katotohanan
**Term B:** walang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** may katotohanan / walang katotohanan
**English translation:** there is truth to it / there is no truth to it
**Description of contrast:** These are contrasting existential constructions using *may* ("there is") and *wala* ("there is none") with *katotohanan*. *May katotohanan* partially affirms a claim; *walang katotohanan* dismisses it as entirely unfounded, as in *Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon* ("The allegation has no truth to it").
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** huwad
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** huwad / peke
**English translation:** counterfeit/forged / fake/phony
**Description of contrast:** These two terms are near-synonyms rather than opposites, but the P1 data consistently distinguishes them by register and domain: *huwad* is more formal and legal, applied to documents and currency; *peke* is colloquial and extends more readily to emotional performances and persons. They are not in opposition to each other but occupy overlapping rather than identical semantic space. This is noted here because the data supports a contrast in register and range of application rather than in truth-domain value.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** This entry documents a register and domain distinction between near-synonyms rather than a truth-domain opposition. It is included because the P3 and P1 data explicitly distinguish the two items on these grounds.

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** tama
**Term B:** tama na
**Transliteration:** tama / tama na
**English translation:** correct / enough (stop)
**Description of contrast:** The P2b data documents *tama na* ("enough, stop") as a distinct semantic extension of *tama* toward sufficiency or cessation rather than correctness. The addition of the change-of-state particle *na* shifts the meaning from an evaluation of correctness to a directive to cease. The P3 data explicitly notes that *tama na* does not fit the correctness-evaluation function of the group to which *tama* belongs.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** This is not a truth-domain opposition but a contrast in meaning produced by the addition of *na* to *tama*. It is included because the data explicitly marks it as a semantic shift within the same root.

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi
**Term B:** kung mali man ako
**Transliteration:** kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi / kung mali man ako
**English translation:** if my understanding is correct / even if I am wrong
**Description of contrast:** Both are documented in P2b as conventionalized hedging constructions used to introduce a claim while acknowledging the possibility of error. They are structurally parallel conditional constructions using *tama* and *mali* respectively, and they contrast in the direction of the epistemic hedge: *kung tama* assumes correctness as the default; *kung mali man* concedes the possibility of error with the concessive particle *man*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
