# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b CONSTRUCTION USAGE & CONSTRAINTS
**Language: English (en)**

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copular predication: Subject + BE + truth-domain adjective | *The account is accurate.* | N/A | The account is accurate. | [CORE] | Occurs across all registers (spoken, written, formal, informal); no observable restriction on which truth-domain adjective enters this frame. |
| Copular predication: Subject + BE + truth-domain adjective | *His testimony was truthful.* | N/A | His testimony was truthful. | [CORE] | Equally natural in past tense; legal and journalistic contexts are typical environments for *truthful* specifically. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** Predicative use with *seem*, *appear*, *prove* as copula substitutes (*the account seems accurate*; *his testimony proved truthful*) — these add evidential nuance but share the same basic frame.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prefixed negative adjective | *The document is inauthentic.* | N/A | The document is inauthentic. | [CORE] | Formal and written registers preferred; not all adjectives in the domain accept prefixation, so availability must be checked item by item. |
| Periphrastic negation: NOT + adjective | *The claim is not genuine.* | N/A | The claim is not genuine. | [CORE] | Available for all adjectives in the domain without exception; the universal fallback strategy when prefixed forms are unavailable or unnatural. |
| Lexically distinct antonym | *The claim is fake.* | N/A | The claim is fake. | [CORE] | Informal to neutral register; *fake*, *bogus*, *counterfeit* are not derivationally related to their positive counterparts and carry additional connotations (deliberate deception, material forgery) absent from simple negation. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** Double negation for rhetorical softening (*not inaccurate*) is attested in formal prose but is a distinct pragmatic strategy, not a third negation type.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prenominal attributive adjective + noun | *a credible witness* | N/A | a credible witness | [CORE] | The dominant and unmarked order in English; attested across all registers and with all truth-domain adjectives documented in P2a. |
| Prenominal attributive adjective + noun (lexicalized collocation) | *fraudulent misrepresentation* | N/A | fraudulent misrepresentation | [CORE] | Strongly lexicalized in legal register; *fraudulent* collocates with a restricted set of nouns (*misrepresentation*, *claim*, *activity*, *intent*) more tightly than other adjectives in the domain. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** Postnominal predicative construction (*the witness was credible*) is the only available alternative and is structurally distinct (predicative, not attributive); no productive postnominal attributive order is attested for this domain in English.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PROVE + adjective (evidential inchoative) | *The rumor proved true.* | N/A | The rumor proved true. | [CORE] | Common in written and formal spoken registers; encodes retrospective confirmation of a truth-domain property; most natural with *true*, *false*, *correct*, *accurate*, *valid*. |
| TURN OUT + TO BE + adjective (discovery inchoative) | *The document turned out to be fraudulent.* | N/A | The document turned out to be fraudulent. | [CORE] | Neutral to informal register; encodes discovery, often with an implication of surprise; available with a wider range of truth-domain adjectives than *prove*. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *become true/valid/authentic* is grammatical but [MARGINAL] — uncommon in natural usage. *Come true* is attested but restricted to the fixed idiom documented in §9 and is not a productive inchoative frame.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Derivational verbal causative | *The expert authenticated the painting.* | N/A | The expert established the painting as authentic. | [CORE] | Formal and professional registers (legal, archival, scientific); the derivational causative verbs (*authenticate*, *validate*, *verify*, *legitimize*) are not available for all adjectives in the domain — lexical gaps are documented. |
| Periphrastic causative: MAKE + NP + adjective / confirmation paraphrase | *New evidence validated the hypothesis.* | N/A | New evidence made the hypothesis valid / confirmed its validity. | [CORE] | *Validate* is [CORE]; the periphrastic *make X valid* is [MARGINAL] in most registers, sounding formal or stilted; the nominalization paraphrase (*confirmed its validity*) is a common written alternative. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *confirm*, *establish*, *demonstrate* + NP function as near-synonymous causative-evidential verbs in context but are not morphologically derived from truth-domain adjectives.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adjective + *-ity* suffix | *The authenticity of the document was disputed.* | N/A | The authenticity of the document was disputed. | [CORE] | Formal and written registers; *-ity* forms (*validity*, *credibility*, *authenticity*, *sincerity*, *plausibility*) are well-established and show no register restriction within formal usage. |
| Verbal base + *-tion* suffix | *The verification of the claim took three days.* | N/A | The verification of the claim took three days. | [CORE] | Formal and procedural registers; *-tion* nominalizations (*verification*, *authentication*, *validation*, *correction*) denote processes or events rather than properties, distinguishing them semantically from *-ity* forms. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *-ness* forms (*correctness*, *soundness*, *genuineness*) are [CORE] but carry a slightly more neutral or descriptive register than *-ity* equivalents where both exist. *-cy* forms (*accuracy*, *legitimacy*) and *-ude* forms (*exactitude*, *verisimilitude*) are [CORE] but lexically restricted to specific bases. *-th* (*truth*) is [CORE] but synchronically non-productive — no new *-th* nominalizations are formed in the domain.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compound agentive: *truth-teller* | *She is a truth-teller who refuses to flatter the powerful.* | N/A | She is a person who tells the truth. | [CORE] | Neutral to formal register; preferred over any suffixed form for the positive honest-agent role; carries evaluative (often admiring) connotation in context. |
| Suffixed agentive: *-er*/*-ster* on deception/fraud base | *The counterfeiter was convicted on three counts.* | N/A | The person who produces counterfeits was convicted. | [CORE] | Legal and journalistic registers typical; the deception/fraud cluster (*faker*, *counterfeiter*, *fraudster*, *con man*) shows richer and more productive agentive morphology than the positive cluster, consistent with the asymmetry noted in P2a and P4a §7. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *authenticator*, *validator*, *verifier* (suffixed agentives for the verification cluster) are [CORE] in technical and procedural registers. *Con man* is a compound form parallel to *truth-teller* but on the deceptive side; it is [CORE] in informal and journalistic registers.

## 8. EVIDENTIAL AND DISCOURSE-MARKING CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence-initial fixed phrase: *truth be told* | *Truth be told, the plan was never viable.* | N/A | To tell the truth, the plan was never viable. | [CORE] | Informal to neutral spoken and written register; signals departure from a polite or expected formulation; the subjunctive form (*be told* rather than *is told*) is a frozen feature of the idiom. |
| Prepositional phrase stance marker: *in truth* | *In truth, the situation was far more complex than reported.* | N/A | In reality, the situation was far more complex than reported. | [CORE] | Formal and written registers; functions as a corrective or contrastive marker; more formal in tone than *truth be told* or *to tell the truth*. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *to tell the truth*, *if truth be told*, *frankly*, *honestly* are all [CORE] members of the same functional class, varying primarily by register (more to less formal in the order listed). These are fixed phrases, not productive derivations, as noted in P4a §8.

## 9. VERBAL IDIOMS WITH TRUTH-DOMAIN PREDICATES

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *ring true/false* | *His explanation didn't quite ring true.* | N/A | His explanation did not seem genuine or plausible. | [CORE] | Neutral to informal register; typically used with perceptual or evaluative subjects (explanations, stories, excuses); the negative (*didn't ring true*) may be slightly more frequent than the positive. |
| *hold true* | *The same principle holds true across all cases.* | N/A | The same principle remains valid across all cases. | [CORE] | Formal and academic registers; used with abstract subjects (principles, rules, generalizations); signals continued validity across contexts or time. |

**Attested alternate constructions:** *come true* [CORE but restricted] — fixed idiom limited to wishes, predictions, and fears as noted in P4a §4 and §9; not available as a general inchoative. *Stand corrected* [CORE] — used in first person to acknowledge an error (*I stand corrected*); no parallel idiom exists for other truth-domain adjectives. *Prove true* [CORE] — overlaps with the inchoative frame in §4 and the idiom set here; listed in both categories in P4a.
