# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / BECOMING REAL

### Pattern
The inchoative suffix **-ya-** attaches to **chiqan** to create **chiqanyay**, meaning "to become true" or "to be verified/confirmed." This is used when something previously uncertain or disputed becomes established as true.

### Examples
- **chiqanyay** → chiqanyay → "to become true, to be verified, to turn out to be true"
  - Usage: When a claim or prediction is confirmed by events
  - Context: "Willasqayki chiqanyan" (What I told you has become true / turned out to be true)

### Notes
This form is less common than the causative **chiqanchay** "to verify/authenticate." The inchoative emphasizes the natural emergence or confirmation of truth rather than active verification. The form takes standard verbal inflection and may combine with evidentials.

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / BEING REVEALED AS FALSE

### Pattern
**NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "to become false" as an inchoative process. The language does not have an attested form *llullayay with inchoative meaning.

### Alternatives
The concept is expressed through:
- **llulla kasqan rikurin** → llulla kasqan rikurin → "its being false is revealed/appears"
  - Literal: "its false-being appears"
  - Uses nominalized **llulla kasqan** + appearance verb **rikuriy**

- **llulla kaq sutichakun** → llulla kaq sutichakun → "it becomes clear/evident as false"
  - Uses substantivizer **llulla kaq** + **sutichakuy** "to become clear"

### Notes
Quechua does not treat "becoming false" as a simple state change. Instead, it conceptualizes this as revelation or recognition of pre-existing falsity. The falsity itself is not seen as emerging but as being discovered.

---

## 3. BECOMING GOOD / IMPROVING

### Pattern
The inchoative suffix **-ya-** attaches to **allin** to create **allinyay**, meaning "to become good, to improve, to get better."

### Examples
- **allinyay** → allinyay → "to become good, to improve, to get better"
  - Usage: For health improvement, moral improvement, or general betterment
  - Context: "Unqusqayki allinyashan" (Your illness is getting better)
  - Context: "Runaqa allinyapun" (The person has improved/reformed)

### Notes
This is a highly productive and frequent form. It applies across physical, moral, and functional domains. The form takes full verbal inflection including progressive **-sha-** (allinyashan "is improving") and perfective **-pu-** (allinyapun "has improved").

---

## 4. BECOMING BAD / DETERIORATING

### Pattern
**NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "to become bad" as a simple inchoative derivation. There is no attested form *mana allinyay.

### Alternatives
The concept is expressed through:
- **mana allinman tukuy** → mana allinman tukuy → "to turn into bad, to become bad"
  - Uses directional **mana allinman** + **tukuy** "to become/turn into"
  - Context: "Mikhuyqa mana allinman tukupun" (The food has gone bad)

- **wañuy** → wañuy → "to die, to spoil, to go bad"
  - Used for deterioration of organic matter
  - Context: "Mikhuyqa wañupun" (The food has spoiled)

- **ismuy** → ismuy → "to rot, to decay"
  - Specific to organic decay
  - Context: "Ruruqa ismupun" (The fruit has rotted)

### Notes
Quechua lacks a general inchoative for deterioration parallel to **allinyay**. Instead, it uses specific verbs for different types of deterioration or the periphrastic construction with **tukuy** "become."

---

## 5. BECOMING MISTAKEN / FALLING INTO ERROR

### Pattern
The verb **pantay** "to err, to make a mistake" itself encodes the entry into error. There is no separate inchoative form.

### Examples
- **pantay** → pantay → "to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong"
  - Usage: Describes the act/event of making an error
  - Context: "Pantarqani" (I made a mistake / I was wrong)

### Notes
The base verb **pantay** already has inchoative semantics—it describes the transition into error rather than a state of being in error. The resultative participle **pantasqa** describes the resulting state: "mistaken, in error."

---

## 6. BECOMING COMPLETE / BEING COMPLETED

### Pattern
The verb **hunt'achiy** (causative of **hunt'a**) can be used intransitively with middle/reflexive semantics to mean "to become complete."

### Examples
- **hunt'achikuy** → hunt'achikuy → "to become complete, to be completed"
  - Literal: hunt'a (complete) + -chi- (causative) + -ku- (reflexive/middle)
  - Usage: When something reaches completion
  - Context: "Rurayqa hunt'achikupun" (The work has been completed)

### Alternatives
- **hunt'ay** → hunt'ay → "to fill, to become full"
  - Used for containers and spaces
  - Context: "Mankaqa hunt'ashan" (The pot is filling up)

### Notes
The causative-reflexive **hunt'achikuy** emphasizes the process of completion. The simple **hunt'ay** is more common for physical filling but can extend to abstract completion.

---

## 7. BECOMING EMPTY / BEING EMPTIED

### Pattern
The verb **ch'usaqyay** (inchoative of **ch'usaq**) means "to become empty, to be emptied."

### Examples
- **ch'usaqyay** → ch'usaqyay → "to become empty, to be emptied"
  - Usage: For containers, spaces, or metaphorically for becoming meaningless
  - Context: "Mankaqa ch'usaqyapun" (The pot has become empty)

### Alternatives
- **ch'usaqchakuy** → ch'usaqchakuy → "to empty oneself, to become empty"
  - Causative-reflexive form
  - Context: "Plazaqa ch'usaqchakupun" (The plaza has emptied out)

### Notes
Both forms are attested. The simple inchoative **ch'usaqyay** is more common for passive emptying; the causative-reflexive **ch'usaqchakuy** suggests more active or agentive emptying.

---

## 8. CAUSING TO BE TRUE / VERIFICATION

### Pattern
The causative suffix **-cha-** attaches to **chiqan** to create **chiqanchay**, meaning "to verify, to authenticate, to make true, to confirm."

### Examples
- **chiqanchay** → chiqanchay → "to verify, to authenticate, to make true, to confirm"
  - Usage: Active verification or authentication of claims, documents, or facts
  - Context: "Willakuyta chiqanchay" (to verify the account)
  - Context: "Qillqata chiqanchay" (to authenticate the document)

### Notes
This is a highly productive causative form. It is common in legal, administrative, and investigative contexts. The form emphasizes human agency in establishing truth rather than truth emerging naturally.

---

## 9. CAUSING TO LIE / MAKING SOMEONE DECEIVE

### Pattern
The causative suffix **-chi-** attaches to **llulla** to create **llullachiy**, meaning "to cause to lie, to make someone lie, to deceive."

### Examples
- **llullachiy** → llullachiy → "to cause to lie, to make someone lie, to deceive"
  - Usage: When someone causes another to lie or when someone deceives another
  - Context: "Paymi llullachiwaran" (He made me lie / He deceived me)

### Notes
This causative has two related meanings: (1) causing someone else to lie, often through coercion or manipulation; (2) deceiving someone (making them believe a lie). Context disambiguates which reading is intended.

---

## 10. CAUSING TO BE GOOD / IMPROVEMENT

### Pattern
The causative suffix **-cha-** attaches to **allin** to create **allinchay**, meaning "to make good, to improve, to fix, to correct."

### Examples
- **allinchay** → allinchay → "to make good, to improve, to fix, to correct"
  - Usage: Active improvement or repair of objects, situations, or conduct
  - Context: "Wasita allinchay" (to fix the house)
  - Context: "Runakunata allinchay" (to improve/reform people)

### Notes
This is an extremely productive and frequent causative. It applies across physical repair, moral improvement, and social correction. It is one of the most common verbs in everyday speech for fixing or improving things.

---

## 11. CAUSING TO BE BAD / DAMAGING

### Pattern
**NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** for a simple causative "to make bad" parallel to **allinchay**. There is no attested form *mana allinchay or *millaychay.

### Alternatives
The concept is expressed through specific verbs:
- **waqllichiy** → waqllichiy → "to damage, to harm, to ruin"
  - Not derived from **mana allin** but a separate lexical item
  - Context: "Runaqa wasita waqllichirqan" (The person damaged the house)

- **wañuchiy** → wañuchiy → "to kill, to cause to die, to spoil"
  - Causative of **wañuy** "to die"
  - Context: "Mikhuyqa wañuchipun" (The food has spoiled / gone bad)

- **ismuchiy** → ismuchiy → "to cause to rot, to let decay"
  - Causative of **ismuy** "to rot"
  - Context: "Rurukunata ismuchiy" (to let the fruits rot)

### Notes
Quechua lacks a general causative for "making bad" that parallels **allinchay** "to make good." Instead, it uses specific verbs for different types of damage or harm.

---

## 12. CAUSING SOMEONE TO ERR / MISLEADING

### Pattern
The causative **pantachiy** (from **pantay** "to err") means "to cause to err, to mislead, to lead astray."

### Examples
- **pantachiy** → pantachiy → "to cause to err, to mislead, to lead astray"
  - Usage: When someone causes another to make a mistake or holds a false belief
  - Context: "Paymi pantachiwaran" (He misled me / He caused me to err)

### Notes
This causative is common in contexts of giving wrong directions, providing false information, or leading someone into error. It differs from **llullachiy** in that it may not involve intentional deception—the error may be unintentional.

---

## 13. SELF-DIRECTED CAUSATION: VERIFICATION

### Pattern
The causative-reflexive **chiqanchakuy** (chiqan + -cha- + -ku-) means "to verify for oneself, to confirm personally, to ascertain."

### Examples
- **chiqanchakuy** → chiqanchakuy → "to verify for oneself, to confirm, to ascertain"
  - Usage: When someone personally verifies or confirms something
  - Context: "Chiqanchakuy willakuyta" (Verify the account for yourself)

### Notes
The reflexive **-ku-** adds a sense of personal involvement or benefit. This form emphasizes that the verification is done by and for the subject themselves, not for others.

---

## 14. SELF-DIRECTED CAUSATION: SELF-DECEPTION

### Pattern
The causative-reflexive **llullachakuy** (llulla + -cha- + -ku-) means "to deceive oneself, to be self-deceived."

### Examples
- **llullachakuy** → llullachakuy → "to deceive oneself, to be self-deceived"
  - Usage: When someone deceives themselves or refuses to acknowledge truth
  - Context: "Ama llullachakuychu" (Don't deceive yourself)

### Notes
This form describes psychological self-deception or willful ignorance. It is used in moral and philosophical discourse to describe people who refuse to face reality.

---

## 15. SELF-DIRECTED CAUSATION: SELF-IMPROVEMENT

### Pattern
The causative-reflexive **allinchakuy** (allin + -cha- + -ku-) means "to improve oneself, to make oneself better, to prepare."

### Examples
- **allinchakuy** → allinchakuy → "to improve oneself, to make oneself better, to prepare"
  - Usage: Self-improvement, moral reform, or preparation
  - Context: "Allinchakuy examenpa" (Prepare yourself for the exam)
  - Context: "Runaqa allinchakupun" (The person has reformed themselves)

### Notes
This form is very common in contexts of self-improvement, preparation, and moral reform. It emphasizes personal agency and responsibility in the process of improvement.

---

## 16. MAINTAINING TRUTH / STAYING TRUE

### Pattern
**NO SIMPLE EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "to remain true" or "to stay true." The concept is expressed periphrastically.

### Alternatives
- **chiqanpi qhipakuy** → chiqanpi qhipakuy → "to remain in truth, to stay in truth"
  - Uses locative **chiqanpi** + **qhipakuy** "to remain/stay"
  - Context: "Chiqanpi qhipakuy" (Remain in truth / Stay truthful)

- **chiqan kayta qatiy** → chiqan kayta qatiy → "to continue being true, to keep being true"
  - Uses nominalized **chiqan kay** + **qatiy** "to follow/continue"
  - Context: "Chiqan kayta qatiy" (Continue being true)

### Notes
Quechua does not have a simple verb for "remaining true." The concept requires periphrastic constructions using locative + "remain" or nominalized state + "continue."

---

## 17. MAINTAINING GOODNESS / STAYING GOOD

### Pattern
**NO SIMPLE EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "to remain good" or "to stay good." The concept is expressed periphrastically.

### Alternatives
- **allin kayta qatiy** → allin kayta qatiy → "to continue being good, to keep being good"
  - Uses nominalized **allin kay** + **qatiy** "to follow/continue"
  - Context: "Allin kayta qatiy" (Continue being good / Stay good)

- **allinlla kay** → allinlla kay → "to be only good, to be simply good"
  - Uses limitative **-lla** to emphasize unchanging goodness
  - Context: "Allinlla kay" (Just be good / Stay good)

### Notes
Like with truth, Quechua lacks a simple verb for maintaining goodness. The limitative **-lla** can suggest stability or unchanging state when used with **kay** "to be."

---

## 18. PREVENTING ERROR / AVOIDING MISTAKES

### Pattern
The prohibitive construction **ama pantaychu** "don't err" is the standard way to express prevention of error.

### Examples
- **ama pantaychu** → ama pantaychu → "don't make a mistake, don't err"
  - Standard prohibitive: ama + verb + -chu
  - Context: "Ama pantaychu ñanta akllaykuspa" (Don't make a mistake choosing the path)

### Alternatives
- **pantaymanta qhispiy** → pantaymanta qhispiy → "to escape from error, to avoid error"
  - Uses ablative **pantaymanta** + **qhispiy** "to escape/be saved"
  - Context: "Pantaymanta qhispirqani" (I escaped from error / I avoided the mistake)

### Notes
The prohibitive is the most direct and common expression. The construction with **qhispiy** "escape" is used when someone narrowly avoids or is saved from error.

---

## 19. PREVENTING DECEPTION / AVOIDING LIES

### Pattern
The prohibitive construction **ama llullakychu** "don't lie" prevents the act of lying. For avoiding being deceived, different constructions are used.

### Examples
- **ama llullakychu** → ama llullakychu → "don't lie, do not deceive"
  - Standard prohibitive for preventing one's own lying
  - Context: "Ama llullakychu" (Don't lie)

- **llullamanta qhispiy** → llullamanta qhispiy → "to escape from deception, to avoid being deceived"
  - Uses ablative **llullamanta** + **qhispiy** "to escape"
  - Context: "Llullamanta qhispirani" (I escaped from deception / I avoided being deceived)

- **ama llullachiwankichu** → ama llullachiwankichu → "don't deceive me, don't make me lie"
  - Prohibitive with causative verb
  - Context: Direct address preventing someone from deceiving the speaker

### Notes
Different constructions distinguish between preventing one's own lying (**ama llullakychu**), avoiding being deceived (**llullamanta qhispiy**), and preventing someone from deceiving you (**ama llullachiwankichu**).

---

## 20. GRADUAL CHANGE: PROGRESSIVE ASPECT

### Pattern
The progressive suffix **-sha-** combines with inchoative and causative verbs to indicate ongoing change processes.

### Examples
- **allinyashan** → allinyashan → "is improving, is getting better"
  - allin-ya-sha-n (good-INCH-PROG-3)
  - Context: "Unqusqayki allinyashan" (Your illness is improving)

- **chiqanyashan** → chiqanyashan → "is becoming true, is being verified"
  - chiqan-ya-sha-n (true-INCH-PROG-3)
  - Context: "Willakuyqa chiqanyashan" (The account is being verified / is turning out to be true)

- **allinchaykashan** → allinchaykashan → "is improving (something), is fixing"
  - allin-cha-yka-sha-n (good-CAUS-PROG-PROG-3)
  - Context: "Wasita allinchaykashan" (He/she is fixing the house)

### Notes
The progressive **-sha-** is highly productive with all dynamic verbs. It may combine with the additional progressive marker **-yka-** for emphasis on ongoing process. This creates forms like **allinchaykashan** with double progressive marking.

---

## 21. COMPLETED CHANGE: PERFECTIVE ASPECT

### Pattern
The perfective suffix **-pu-** combines with inchoative and causative verbs to indicate completed change or sudden/definitive change.

### Examples
- **allinyapun** → allinyapun → "has improved, got better (completely)"
  - allin-ya-pu-n (good-INCH-PERF-3)
  - Context: "Runaqa allinyapun" (The person has improved / reformed)

- **chiqanyapun** → chiqanyapun → "has become true, turned out to be true (definitively)"
  - chiqan-ya-pu-n (true-INCH-PERF-3)
  - Context: "Willasqayki chiqanyapun" (What I told you has turned out to be true)

- **allinchapun** → allinchapun → "has fixed, has improved (completely)"
  - allin-cha-pu-n (good-CAUS-PERF-3)
  - Context: "Wasita allinchapun" (He/she has fixed the house)

### Notes
The perfective **-pu-** adds a sense of completion, suddenness, or definitiveness to the change. It is extremely common with both inchoative and causative verbs in the truth/goodness domain.

---

## 22. ITERATIVE CHANGE: REPETITION

### Pattern
The repetitive suffix **-paya-** can combine with causative verbs to indicate repeated action.

### Examples
- **allinchapayay** → allinchapayay → "to keep fixing, to fix repeatedly"
  - allin-cha-paya-y (good-CAUS-REPET-INF)
  - Context: "Wasita allinchapayayta munan" (He/she wants to keep fixing the house)

### Notes
The repetitive **-paya-** is less common than progressive or perfective marking but is attested with causative verbs. It suggests repeated or habitual action rather than a single event.

---

## 23. RECIPROCAL VERIFICATION

### Pattern
The reciprocal suffix **-naku-** combines with **chiqanchay** to create **chiqanchanakuy** "to verify each other, to verify mutually."

### Examples
- **chiqanchanakuy** → chiqanchanakuy → "to verify each other, to verify mutually"
  - chiqan-cha-naku-y (true-CAUS-RECIP-INF)
  - Context: "Willakuykunata chiqanchanakuy" (to verify each other's accounts)

### Notes
The reciprocal **-naku-** is productive with transitive verbs including causatives. It indicates mutual or reciprocal action between multiple participants.

---

## 24. RECIPROCAL DECEPTION

### Pattern
The reciprocal suffix **-naku-** combines with **llullachiy** to create **llullachinakuy** "to deceive each other, to lie to each other."

### Examples
- **llullachinakuy** → llullachinakuy → "to deceive each other, to lie to each other"
  - llulla-chi-naku-y (false-CAUS-RECIP-INF)
  - Context: "Runakunaqa llullachinakunku" (The people are deceiving each other)

### Notes
The reciprocal form describes mutual deception or a situation where multiple parties are lying to each other. It is used in contexts of mutual distrust or conflict.

---

## 25. STABILITY: UNCHANGING TRUTH

### Pattern
**NO DEDICATED EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "unchanging truth" or "eternal truth" as a single lexical item. The concept is expressed through modification.

### Alternatives
- **wiñaypaq chiqan** → wiñaypaq chiqan → "eternal truth, truth for always"
  - Uses **wiñaypaq** "forever, eternally" + **chiqan** "true"
  - Context: "Wiñaypaq chiqan kay" (to be eternally true)

- **mana tikrakuq chiqan** → mana tikrakuq chiqan → "unchanging truth, truth that doesn't change"
  - Uses **mana tikrakuq** "not changing" + **chiqan** "true"
  - Literal: "not-changing true"

### Notes
Quechua expresses stability through temporal modification (**wiñaypaq** "forever") or negative participles (**mana tikrakuq** "not changing") rather than dedicated lexical items for unchanging or eternal truth.

---

## 26. INSTABILITY: CHANGEABLE TRUTH

### Pattern
**NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** for "changeable truth" or "unstable truth." The concept would be expressed periphrastically if needed.

### Alternatives
- **tikrakuq** → tikrakuq → "changing, changeable"
  - Could modify truth terms: **tikrakuq chiqan** (hypothetical: "changeable truth")
  - Not attested in materials but structurally possible

### Notes
The concept of "changeable truth" appears not to be lexicalized or commonly expressed in Quechua. If needed, it would likely use the participle **tikrakuq** "changing" as a modifier.

## SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

### Productive Patterns:
1. **Inchoative -ya-**: Productive with quality terms (chiqanyay, allinyay, ch'usaqyay)
2. **Causative -cha-/-chi-**: Highly productive (chiqanchay, allinchay, llullachiy, pantachiy)
3. **Causative-Reflexive -cha-ku-**: Productive for self-directed action (chiqanchakuy, allinchakuy, llullachakuy)
4. **Progressive -sha-**: Combines freely with dynamic verbs
5. **Perfective -pu-**: Combines freely with dynamic verbs
6. **Reciprocal -naku-**: Productive with transitive/causative verbs

### Asymmetries and Gaps:
1. **No inchoative for "becoming false"**: Concept expressed through revelation/recognition constructions
2. **No inchoative for "becoming bad"**: Concept expressed through specific deterioration verbs or periphrastic **tukuy** "become"
3. **No causative for "making bad"**: Concept expressed through specific damage verbs (waqllichiy, wañuchiy)
4. **No simple verbs for "remaining/staying"**: Concept requires periphrastic constructions
5. **No lexicalized "unchanging/eternal truth"**: Concept expressed through temporal or participial modification

### Key Observations:
1. **Positive bias**: Inchoative and causative derivations are more productive with positive terms (chiqan, allin) than negative terms (llulla, mana allin)
2. **Aspectual richness**: Progressive and perfective marking is highly productive with all dynamic verbs
3. **Self-directed emphasis**: Causative-reflexive constructions are common and emphasize personal agency
4. **Lexical specificity**: Negative changes (deterioration, damage) use specific verbs rather than general derivations
