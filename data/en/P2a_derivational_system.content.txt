# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: English (en)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### Methodological Note
This analysis documents only attested derivational forms found in actual usage. English derivational morphology for this semantic domain shows significant irregularity, with some roots highly productive and others limited. Many apparent "gaps" reflect genuine lexical patterns rather than analytical incompleteness.

## BASE: true

### Derived Form 1: truth
- **Base Form**: true
- **Derived Form**: truth
- **Transliteration**: /truːθ/
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + -th (nominal suffix, archaic productive pattern)
- **Translation**: truth, verity
- **Usage**: Nominalization denoting the quality of being true, true statements collectively, or reality itself. Extremely common across all registers.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Derived Form 2: truly
- **Base Form**: true
- **Derived Form**: truly
- **Transliteration**: /ˈtruːli/
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + -ly (adverbial suffix)
- **Translation**: truly, genuinely
- **Usage**: Adverbial form marking manner (genuinely) or serving as intensifier/emphasizer. Very high frequency across registers.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Derived Form 3: untrue
- **Base Form**: true
- **Derived Form**: untrue
- **Transliteration**: /ʌnˈtruː/
- **Literal Breakdown**: un- (negative prefix) + true
- **Translation**: untrue, false
- **Usage**: Negated adjectival form marking lack of correspondence to facts. Common in formal and informal contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Derived Form 4: untruly
- **Base Form**: true
- **Derived Form**: untruly
- **Transliteration**: /ʌnˈtruːli/
- **Literal Breakdown**: un- + true + -ly
- **Translation**: untruly, falsely
- **Usage**: Negated adverbial form. Attested but much less frequent than "untrue" or "falsely."
- **Status**: [MARGINAL]

### Derived Form 5: untruth
- **Base Form**: true (via truth)
- **Derived Form**: untruth
- **Transliteration**: /ʌnˈtruːθ/
- **Literal Breakdown**: un- + truth
- **Translation**: untruth, falsehood
- **Usage**: Nominalization of negated form, denoting false statement. Somewhat formal or euphemistic register.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Derived Form 6: truism
- **Base Form**: true
- **Derived Form**: truism
- **Transliteration**: /ˈtruːɪzəm/
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + -ism (nominal suffix)
- **Translation**: truism, obvious truth
- **Usage**: Denotes a self-evident or obvious truth, often with implication of banality. Common in intellectual discourse.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Note on *trueness
The form "trueness" is occasionally attested but rare, with "truth" serving as the standard nominalization. When "trueness" appears, it typically emphasizes quality or degree rather than abstract concept.
**Status**: [MARGINAL]

### Note on *truer, *truest
Comparative and superlative forms exist ("truer," "truest") but show restricted distribution, often in philosophical or literary contexts questioning degrees of truth.
**Status**: [CORE] (for comparative/superlative morphology, though semantically complex)
