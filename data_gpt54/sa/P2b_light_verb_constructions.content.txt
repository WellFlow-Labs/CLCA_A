## 1. Base Form: **सत्य**

### Example
**सत्यं वद**
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada  
**English translation:** Speak the truth.  
**Usage notes:** A very common imperative expression in ethical, didactic, and scriptural contexts. It is especially well known from normative instruction on truthful speech.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Example
**सत्यं ब्रूते / सत्यं ब्रवीति**
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūte / satyaṃ bravīti  
**English translation:** He/she speaks the truth.  
**Usage notes:** A standard verbal construction with *satyaṃ* as the object of “say/speak.” It is widely natural in narrative, ethical, and philosophical prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Example
**सत्येन जयते**
**Transliteration:** satyena jayate  
**English translation:** One triumphs by truth.  
**Usage notes:** An established instrumental construction expressing truth as the means of victory or success. It belongs especially to moral and laudatory discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Example
**सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम्**
**Transliteration:** satyāt na pramaditavyam  
**English translation:** One must not deviate from truth.  
**Usage notes:** A well-known normative construction in learned and scriptural style. It presents truth as a standard from which one should not lapse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Example
**सत्ये स्थितः / सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः**
**Transliteration:** satye sthitaḥ / satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ  
**English translation:** established in truth; standing in truth  
**Usage notes:** This locative construction is used in ethical, religious, and philosophical registers for a person grounded in truth. It often has a moral or spiritual nuance beyond mere factual correctness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
