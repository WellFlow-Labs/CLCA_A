# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. The inventory covers periphrastic verbal constructions, nominal collocations, idiomatic expressions, and syntactically-driven patterns. Where no common construction exists for a given base form, this is stated explicitly. Constructions already introduced in P1b as collocations are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full; new constructions not previously documented are given full entries.

A construction is included only where there is positive reason to believe it is attested and natural. Absence is recorded explicitly and is treated as data.

## 1. Base Form: **egia**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** The most common verbal collocation of *egia* in everyday speech. Used in imperative (*egia esazu*, "tell the truth"), declarative, and nominalized (*egia esatea*) forms across all registers. Used in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts. Cross-referenced from P1b (Root 1, item 1). Also functions as a sentence-initial framing device signaling candor or frankness, syntactically detached from the main clause as a discourse marker.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** it is true; that is true
- **Usage notes:** Standard predicative construction for asserting the truth of a proposition, with *egia* as predicate nominal and *da* (3sg present of *izan*) as copula. Functions as a discourse marker of confirmation in both spoken and written Basque. Cross-referenced from P1b (Root 1, item 2).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia esan behar
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to tell the truth (as obligation); truth be told
- **Usage notes:** Discourse-initial phrase signaling candid or potentially unwelcome disclosure, equivalent to "to be honest" or "truth be told." Cross-referenced from P1b (Root 1, item 3).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esan behar da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** One must tell the truth; the truth must be told
- **Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *egia esan* with the modal *behar* ("must, need"). Used in moral and legal contexts to assert an obligation of truthfulness. The impersonal *behar da* construction is standard Basque for expressing obligation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** Legal oath formula used in testimonial contexts, paralleling the English legal formula. Attested in Basque legal and judicial language. Extends the collocation *egia osoa* (P1b Root 1, item 20) with the additional clause. Used in court proceedings and formal legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egiari eutsi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to hold to the truth; to maintain the truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in moral and rhetorical contexts to describe adherence to or defense of the truth under pressure. Cross-referenced from P1b (Root 1, item 4).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egiaren bila ibili
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to go about seeking the truth; to be in search of the truth
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *egiaren bila* (in search of truth, P1b Root 1, item 5) with the motion verb *ibili* (to go about, to be engaged in). The *ibili* verb marks ongoing or habitual activity. Used in philosophical, journalistic, and literary contexts to describe the ongoing pursuit of truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia aitortu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to admit the truth; to confess the truth; to acknowledge the truth
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *egia* with *aitortu* (to confess, to acknowledge, to admit). Used in legal, moral, and interpersonal contexts where truth-telling involves an element of confession or acknowledgment of something previously concealed or denied. Distinct from *egia esan* in its connotation of acknowledgment after resistance.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia ukatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to deny the truth; to refuse to acknowledge the truth
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *egia* with *ukatu* (to deny, to refuse). Used in moral and legal contexts to describe the act of refusing to acknowledge or admit a true state of affairs. Distinct from *egia gorde* (concealing) in that *ukatu* implies active denial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia ezkutatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to hide the truth; to conceal the truth
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *egia* with *ezkutatu* (to hide, to conceal). Used in moral, legal, and journalistic contexts to describe deliberate concealment of true information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia gorde
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to hide the truth; to conceal the truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *gorde* ("to keep, to hide, to conceal"). Used in moral and legal contexts to describe the withholding of truth. Functions as a partial antonym of *egia esan* in contexts of concealment rather than active lying.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia argitara atera
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to bring the truth to light; to reveal the truth
- **Usage notes:** Idiomatic verbal construction combining *egia* with *argitara atera* (to bring to light, literally "to take out into the light"). Used in journalistic, investigative, legal, and moral contexts to describe the exposure of hidden truth. The light metaphor is productive in Basque as in other languages.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia mingarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the painful truth; the bitter truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal collocation combining *egia* with *mingarria* (painful, hurtful). Used in everyday and literary contexts to characterize truths that are difficult to hear or accept. Reflects the common conceptualization of truth as potentially painful. Common in spoken and written Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia gordea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the hidden truth; the concealed truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal collocation combining *egia* with *gordea* (hidden, concealed, kept). Used in literary, journalistic, and everyday contexts to refer to truth that has been deliberately kept from view.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia eta gezurra nahastu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to mix truth and lies; to confuse truth and falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining the paired nouns *egia* (truth) and *gezurra* (the lie) with *nahastu* (to mix, to confuse). Used in moral and analytical contexts to describe the deliberate or careless blending of true and false information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia da ala gezurra?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Is it true or false? Is it truth or lie?
- **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction using the disjunctive particle *ala* (or) to pose the binary question of truth versus falsehood. Common in everyday speech and in pedagogical and evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia ala gezurra?
- **Transliteration:** Egia ala gezurra?
- **English translation:** True or false? Truth or lie?
- **Usage notes:** A fixed binary interrogative construction used in educational, game, and rhetorical contexts to pose a truth-value question. The *ala* conjunction marks the exclusive disjunction. Common in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia esateko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to tell the truth; frankly; to be honest
- **Usage notes:** Purposive/discourse-marker construction using the purposive form *esateko* (for saying, in order to say). Functions as a parenthetical equivalent to "to be honest" or "frankly speaking" when used sentence-initially. Distinct from *egia esan behar* in that it is less obligative in tone. More colloquial than the fuller periphrastic variant. Common in both spoken and written Basque as a hedging or sincerity marker.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia zati bat
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** a part of the truth; a portion of the truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal construction combining *egia* with *zati bat* (a part, a piece). Used in analytical and moral contexts to describe partial disclosure of truth, often implying that the remainder is withheld. Related in meaning to *egia erdi* (half-truth, P1b) but less lexicalized.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia jakiteko eskubidea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the right to know the truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal construction using the purposive verbal noun *jakiteko* (in order to know) and *eskubidea* (the right). Used in legal, journalistic, and political discourse to assert a right to truthful information, particularly in contexts of institutional accountability, freedom of information, press freedom, transparency, and victims' rights.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia beti ateratzen da azalera
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the truth always comes to the surface; truth will out
- **Usage notes:** Proverbial construction asserting the inevitable emergence of truth. Uses *azalera atera* (to come to the surface) with *beti* (always). Attested as a common saying in Basque moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egiari begira
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** looking at the truth; facing the truth; in view of the truth
- **Usage notes:** Postpositional construction using *begira* (looking at, facing) with the dative *egiari*. Used in rhetorical and analytical contexts to frame an argument as grounded in honest assessment of reality.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 23**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia da, baina...
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** it is true, but...; true, but...
- **Usage notes:** Concessive construction using *egia da* followed by *baina* (but) to acknowledge a truth while introducing a qualification or counterpoint. Extremely common in spoken and written argumentative discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esaten ausartu zen.
- **Transliteration:** Egia esaten ausartu zen.
- **English translation:** He/she dared to tell the truth.
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *egia esan* with the verb *ausartu* (to dare), used to frame truth-telling as an act of courage. Common in narrative and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia eta justizia
- **Transliteration:** Egia eta justizia
- **English translation:** Truth and justice
- **Usage notes:** A fixed paired nominal construction used in political, legal, and transitional justice discourse. Common in the context of truth commissions and post-conflict reconciliation. Attested in Basque political and journalistic writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia baino egia handiagoa
- **Transliteration:** Egia baino egia handiagoa
- **English translation:** A truth greater than truth / truer than true
- **Usage notes:** A comparative intensification construction using the comparative marker *baino* and the comparative suffix *-ago*. Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to assert that something is emphatically or paradoxically true. Attested in literary and oratorical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 27**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egiaren bila dabil.
- **Transliteration:** Egiaren bila dabil.
- **English translation:** He/she is in search of the truth.
- **Usage notes:** The construction *egiaren bila ibili/dabil* uses the postposition *bila* (in search of) with the genitive of *egia*. Documented in P1b item 14 as a collocation; noted here as a productive syntactic construction with the motion verb *ibili*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia da ala ez?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Is it true or not?
- **Usage notes:** A fixed interrogative formula using the disjunctive particle *ala* ("or") to pose a binary truth question. Common in everyday speech and formal argumentation. The *ala ez* ("or not") construction is a standard Basque pattern for yes/no alternatives.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Hori egia bada
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** If that is true; if that be true
- **Usage notes:** A conditional construction using *bada* (conditional form of *da*, "is"). Used in argumentation and everyday speech to introduce a conditional premise. Standard Basque conditional syntax applied to truth-assertion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 30**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esan nahi badut
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** If I want to tell the truth; if I'm being honest
- **Usage notes:** A longer periphrastic discourse marker combining *egia esan* with the conditional *nahi badut* ("if I want to"). Used in spoken registers to introduce a candid or frank statement, functioning similarly to English "if I'm being honest." More colloquial than *egia esateko*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia dirudien arren
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Although it seems true; even though it appears to be true
- **Usage notes:** A concessive construction combining *egia* with *dirudien arren* ("although it seems," from *dirudi*, "it seems/appears"). Used to introduce a qualification of apparent truth. The *arren* concessive construction is standard in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 32**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia baino egiagoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Truer than true; more true than truth itself
- **Usage notes:** A comparative intensification construction using the comparative *baino...ago* ("more...than"). Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to emphasize the certainty or self-evidence of a claim. Functions as a hyperbolic intensifier.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 33**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia hutsa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The plain truth; the bare truth; pure truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *hutsa* ("empty, mere, pure, bare"). Used to emphasize that a statement is nothing but the truth, without embellishment or qualification. Common in everyday and rhetorical speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 34**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia erdiak esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell half-truths
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia erdiak* ("half-truths") with *esan* ("to say/tell"). Used in moral and political discourse to describe misleading partial disclosure. Already documented in P1b 1.14.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **egiazko** / **egiazkoa**

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** egiazko
- **Example:** egiazko lekukotasuna eman
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to give true testimony; to provide genuine witness
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining the nominal collocation *egiazko lekukotasuna* (true testimony, P1b Root 1, item 11) with *eman* (to give). Used in legal contexts to describe the act of providing accurate and genuine testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** egiazko

- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to certify true copies; to authenticate copies
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *egiazko kopiak* (certified true copies, P1b Root 1, item 26) with *ziurtatu* (to certify). Used in administrative and legal contexts for the formal certification of document copies.

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** egiazko
- **Example:** egiazkotzat jo

- **English translation:** to consider as true; to take as genuine; to regard as authentic
- **Usage notes:** Construction using the translative case suffix *-tzat* (as, for) with *jo* (to consider, to take). Used in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts to describe the act of treating something as true or genuine, without necessarily asserting that it is so.

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** egiazko
- **Example:** egiazkotzat onartu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to accept as true; to acknowledge as genuine
- **Usage notes:** Construction using the translative *-tzat* with *onartu* (to accept, to acknowledge). Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe formal acceptance of something as authentic or valid.

**Construction 5**

- **Transliteration:** Hori egiazkoa da.
- **English translation:** That is genuine / That is true.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction with *egiazkoa* as predicate adjective. Used to assert that something is genuine or truthful. Common across all registers.

**Construction 6**

- **Usage notes:** A subordinate interrogative construction using the relative *den* and the disjunction *ala ez*. Used in evaluative and investigative contexts to frame the question of genuineness. Common in spoken and written registers.

**Construction 7**

- **Transliteration:** Egiazkoa dela ziurtatu
- **English translation:** To confirm that it is genuine
- **Usage notes:** A complement clause construction with *egiazkoa dela* (that it is genuine) as the embedded clause complement of *ziurtatu* (to confirm, to certify). Used in legal and administrative contexts.

**Construction 8**

- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining the attributive *egiazko* with *informazioa* ("information") and *zabaldu* ("to spread, to disseminate"). Used in journalistic and media contexts, particularly in discussions of misinformation and fact-checking.

## 5. Base Form: **gezur** / **gezurra**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to tell a lie; to say a falsehood
- **Usage notes:** The primary verbal collocation of *gezur* in everyday speech, directly parallel to *egia esan* (to tell the truth). Used across all registers in moral, interpersonal, and legal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** it is a lie; it is false; that is untrue
- **Usage notes:** Predicative construction asserting that a proposition or statement is false, directly parallel to *egia da* (it is true). Common in everyday speech and in argumentative discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra asmatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to make up a lie; to invent a falsehood; to fabricate
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *gezurra* with *asmatu* (to invent, to devise, to make up). Used in moral and interpersonal contexts to describe the deliberate fabrication of false information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra zabaldu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to spread a lie; to disseminate falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *gezurra* with *zabaldu* (to spread, to disseminate, to extend). Used in journalistic and political contexts to describe the propagation of false information. Common in media and political commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra harrapatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to catch someone in a lie; to catch out a liar
- **Usage notes:** Verbal construction combining *gezurra* with *harrapatu* (to catch, to trap). Used in everyday and legal contexts to describe the act of detecting or exposing a lie or a liar.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurretan harrapatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to catch in lies; to catch out lying
- **Usage notes:** Construction using the locative plural *gezurretan* (in lies, in the act of lying) with *harrapatu* (to catch). Slightly more emphatic than *gezurra harrapatu*, implying repeated or habitual lying. Common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra sinetsarazi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to make someone believe a lie; to get someone to believe a falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Causative construction combining *gezurra* with *sinetsarazi* (to cause to believe, causative of *sinetsi*). Used in moral and rhetorical contexts to describe the act of deceiving someone into believing something false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezurra eta egia nahastu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** to mix lie and truth; to confuse falsehood and truth
- **Usage notes:** Paired nominal construction parallel to *egia eta gezurra nahastu* (Construction 15 under *egia*). The ordering with *gezurra* first may carry a slightly more pejorative framing. Used in moral and analytical contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Example:** gezur handia
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** a big lie; a great falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Nominal collocation combining *gezur* with *handia* (big, great). Used in moral and political discourse to characterize a particularly egregious or consequential falsehood. Common in journalistic and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** gezur
