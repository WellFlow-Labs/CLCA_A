1. **αλήθεια**  
   **Transliteration:** alítheia  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** ancient formation; conventionally understood as “truth”  
   **Typical English translations:** truth, reality

   **Usage:** Used for the truth of a statement, but also more broadly for what is real or genuine. It appears in everyday speech, journalism, religion, and philosophy, including contrasts such as *αλήθεια ή ψέμα* “truth or lie.”  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **αληθινός**  
   **Transliteration:** alithinós  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *αλήθεια*; “true, real”  
   **Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine

   **Usage:** Commonly describes something as real or genuine rather than fake, as in a person’s feelings, a story, or an object. It is also used evaluatively for authenticity in personal and aesthetic contexts, e.g. *αληθινός φίλος* “a true friend.”  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **αληθής**  
   **Transliteration:** alithís  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** learned/adjectival form related to *αλήθεια*  
   **Typical English translations:** true, truthful, correct

   **Usage:** More formal and learned than *αληθινός*, often used for propositions, claims, or statements. It is common in formal writing, logic, law, and academic contexts, especially in phrases like *αληθής δήλωση* or *αληθές/ψευδές*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **σωστός**  
   **Transliteration:** sostós  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “right, correct”  
   **Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper

   **Usage:** Very common in everyday Greek for correctness, appropriateness, or rightness. It can evaluate answers, behavior, decisions, procedures, and even moral conduct, depending on context.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **ορθός**  
   **Transliteration:** orthós  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “straight, upright”  
   **Typical English translations:** correct, proper, right, orthodox

   **Usage:** A more formal or elevated term than *σωστός*, often used in technical, academic, legal, and institutional language. It can refer to correct reasoning, proper procedure, or right form, and survives in many fixed expressions.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **ορθότητα**  
   **Transliteration:** orthótita  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *ορθός* + abstract suffix; “correctness”  
   **Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, propriety

   **Usage:** Used for the correctness of language, reasoning, procedure, or representation. It is frequent in formal discussion, especially where one evaluates whether something conforms to accepted standards.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **ακρίβεια**  
   **Transliteration:** akríveia  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “precision, exactness”  
   **Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness

   **Usage:** Refers to factual or technical accuracy, as in measurements, descriptions, reporting, or reproduction. It is common in scientific, journalistic, and practical contexts where exact correspondence matters.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **ακριβής**  
   **Transliteration:** akrivís  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjectival form of *ακρίβεια*  
   **Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact

   **Usage:** Describes statements, data, instruments, depictions, or descriptions that are exact or accurate. It is widely used in both technical and ordinary contexts, often where factual fidelity is being assessed.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **έγκυρος**  
   **Transliteration:** énkyros  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** “valid, authoritative”  
   **Typical English translations:** valid, authoritative, reliable

   **Usage:** Used for sources, information, documents, arguments, and institutions that are recognized as valid or trustworthy. In legal and administrative contexts it often means formally valid; in public discourse it can mean reputable or authoritative.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **εγκυρότητα**  
    **Transliteration:** enkyrótita  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *έγκυρος* + abstract suffix; “validity”  
    **Typical English translations:** validity, soundness, reliability

    **Usage:** Common in legal, academic, and technical language for the validity of a claim, method, document, or inference. It often concerns whether something holds up under recognized criteria rather than merely seeming plausible.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **έγκριτος**  
    **Transliteration:** énkritos  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** historically “approved/distinguished”  
    **Typical English translations:** reputable, distinguished, respected

    **Usage:** Typically used for persons, institutions, or publications regarded as reputable or authoritative. It does not directly mean “true,” but often supports evaluations of reliability or credibility in public and professional contexts.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **αυθεντικός**  
    **Transliteration:** afthentikós  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *αυθεντία/αυθεντικός* tradition; “authentic”  
    **Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine

    **Usage:** Used for objects, works, styles, experiences, and persons judged to be genuine rather than imitation or artificial. It is common in art, commerce, food, tourism, and personal evaluation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **αυθεντικότητα**  
    **Transliteration:** afthentikótita  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *αυθεντικός* + abstract suffix; “authenticity”  
    **Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness

    **Usage:** Refers to the quality of being genuine, original, or true to itself. It appears in discussions of art, identity, heritage, and interpersonal sincerity.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **γνήσιος**  
    **Transliteration:** gnísios  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “genuine, legitimate by origin”  
    **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, true

    **Usage:** Used for genuine products, documents, emotions, or character, often with emphasis on legitimacy or non-counterfeit status. It can also describe a person as sincere or a trait as truly representative.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **γνησιότητα**  
    **Transliteration:** gnisiótita  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *γνήσιος* + abstract suffix; “genuineness”  
    **Typical English translations:** genuineness, authenticity

    **Usage:** Used especially in formal or evaluative contexts concerning whether something is genuine, original, or not forged. It is common in discussions of documents, artifacts, signatures, and also personal sincerity.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **πραγματικός**  
    **Transliteration:** pragmatikós  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *πράγμα* “thing”; “real, actual”  
    **Typical English translations:** real, actual, genuine

    **Usage:** Used to distinguish what is real or actual from what is apparent, fictional, nominal, or merely alleged. It is common in everyday speech and also in philosophical or legal contrasts involving what something fundamentally is.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **πραγματικότητα**  
    **Transliteration:** pragmatikótita  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *πραγματικός* + abstract suffix; “reality”  
    **Typical English translations:** reality, actuality

    **Usage:** Refers to reality as opposed to appearance, illusion, or representation. It is frequent in ordinary speech and in philosophical, political, and media discourse when discussing what is actually the case.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **ουσία**  
    **Transliteration:** ousía  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “essence, substance”  
    **Typical English translations:** essence, substance, core nature

    **Usage:** Used for what something fundamentally is, especially in philosophical, theological, and reflective contexts. In broader usage it can also mean the essential point or substance of a matter, as opposed to superficial form.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **ουσιώδης**  
    **Transliteration:** ousiódis  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *ουσία*; “essential”  
    **Typical English translations:** essential, substantial, fundamental

    **Usage:** Describes features, distinctions, or elements that belong to the essence or are fundamentally important. It is common in formal writing, law, philosophy, and serious public discourse.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **πιστός**  
    **Transliteration:** pistós  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** related to *πίστη* “faith, trust”  
    **Typical English translations:** faithful, loyal; accurate

    **Usage:** Most commonly refers to loyalty or faithfulness, but it is also used for faithful reproduction or accurate rendering, as in translation or depiction. In that latter use it evaluates resemblance and fidelity to an original.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **πιστότητα**  
    **Transliteration:** pistótita  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *πιστός* + abstract suffix; “fidelity”  
    **Typical English translations:** fidelity, faithfulness, accuracy

    **Usage:** Used for fidelity in representation, reproduction, translation, or sound/image quality, as well as in interpersonal loyalty. It is especially relevant where one judges how closely something corresponds to an original.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **απαράλλακτος**  
    **Transliteration:** aparállaktos  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “unchanged, unaltered”  
    **Typical English translations:** identical, exact, unaltered

    **Usage:** Used when something is reproduced or repeated exactly, without variation. It appears in formal and literary contexts, often to stress exact sameness or strict fidelity.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **παραποιημένος**  
    **Transliteration:** parapoïiménos  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** participial form from *παραποιώ* “to falsify, alter”  
    **Typical English translations:** falsified, tampered with, forged

    **Usage:** Used for documents, evidence, data, products, or representations that have been altered in a deceptive way. It is common in legal, journalistic, and administrative contexts involving authenticity and reliability.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **πλαστός**  
    **Transliteration:** plastós  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “molded, fabricated”  
    **Typical English translations:** fake, forged, counterfeit

    **Usage:** Commonly used for forged documents, fake signatures, counterfeit goods, or fabricated evidence. It strongly marks lack of genuineness and is especially frequent in legal and everyday evaluative speech.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **ψευδής**  
    **Transliteration:** psevdís  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “false”  
    **Typical English translations:** false, untrue

    **Usage:** A formal and widely used adjective for false statements, allegations, impressions, or indications. It is especially common in legal, academic, journalistic, and logical contexts.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **ψέμα**  
    **Transliteration:** pséma  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “lie”  
    **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood

    **Usage:** The ordinary everyday word for a lie or falsehood. It can refer both to a specific false statement and more generally to deception or untruth in moral and interpersonal evaluation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

27. **ειλικρινής**  
    **Transliteration:** eilikrinís  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “sincere, frank”  
    **Typical English translations:** sincere, honest, candid

    **Usage:** Used mainly for persons, speech, feelings, or intentions judged to be genuine and not deceptive. It is central in moral and interpersonal evaluation rather than factual accuracy as such.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

28. **ειλικρίνεια**  
    **Transliteration:** eilikríneia  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *ειλικρινής*; “sincerity”  
    **Typical English translations:** sincerity, honesty, frankness

    **Usage:** Refers to sincerity or honesty in speech, conduct, and emotional expression. It is common in everyday and ethical discourse where genuineness of intention or expression is being assessed.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

29. **κατά γράμμα**  
    **Transliteration:** katá grámma  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “according to the letter”  
    **Typical English translations:** literally, to the letter, verbatim

    **Usage:** A fixed expression used when something follows wording exactly or is interpreted in a strictly literal way. It is common in legal, interpretive, and everyday contexts involving exact correspondence to a text or instruction.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

30. **κατ’ ουσίαν**  
    **Transliteration:** kat’ ousían  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “according to essence / in essence”  
    **Typical English translations:** essentially, in substance, fundamentally

    **Usage:** A fixed expression used to indicate what something really amounts to at the substantive level, as opposed to form or appearance. It is frequent in formal speech, law, philosophy, and argumentation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
