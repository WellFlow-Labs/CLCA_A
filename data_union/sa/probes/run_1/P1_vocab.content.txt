# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST (International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्य
**Transliteration:** satya
**Literal meaning:** from *sat* (being, existing, real) + adjectival suffix *-ya*
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality, the real
**Usage:** Used across Vedic, epic, and philosophical literature to denote both the property of a statement corresponding to reality and the ontological status of what genuinely exists. Appears in moral contexts as a virtue of speech and conduct, and in metaphysical contexts as a name for ultimate reality. Participates in extensive derivation: *satyatā*, *satyavādin*, *satyavrata*, etc.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ऋत
**Transliteration:** ṛta
**Literal meaning:** from root *ṛ-* (to go, to fit, to be in order); "that which has gone rightly," cosmic order
**Typical English translations:** cosmic order, truth, right, the fitting
**Usage:** A Vedic term designating the ordered functioning of the cosmos and the moral-ritual rightness aligned with it; statements and acts conforming to this order are *ṛta*. Closely related to *satya* in early texts but with stronger cosmological and ritual connotations. Less productive in post-Vedic derivation than *satya*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** यथार्थ
**Transliteration:** yathārtha
**Literal meaning:** *yathā* (as, according to) + *artha* (meaning, object, purpose); "as the object/meaning is"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, true to the object, literal, correct, realistic
**Usage:** Applied to descriptions, representations, or cognitions that correspond to their object as it actually is. Common in epistemological and aesthetic contexts to evaluate whether a word, image, or account faithfully represents its referent. Appears as both adjective and adverb.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** प्रमाण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa
**Literal meaning:** *pra-* (forth, correctly) + *māṇa* (measuring, from root *mā-*, to measure); "correct measure"
**Typical English translations:** valid cognition, means of knowledge, authority, standard, criterion
**Usage:** The central epistemological term across nearly all Sanskrit philosophical schools, denoting a reliable means of arriving at true cognition (perception, inference, testimony, etc.). Also used in legal and normative contexts for an authoritative standard or text. Generates a large derivational family: *pramātṛ*, *prameya*, *pramā*, *apramāṇa*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** प्रमा
**Transliteration:** pramā
**Literal meaning:** *pra-* + *mā-* (to measure); "correct measuring/cognition"
**Typical English translations:** valid cognition, true knowledge, correct apprehension
**Usage:** The result or product of a *pramāṇa*; a cognition that accurately apprehends its object as it is. Distinguished in epistemological literature from *apramā* (erroneous cognition) and from mere belief. Primarily a technical philosophical term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** अप्रमा
**Transliteration:** apramā
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *pramā*; "non-valid cognition"
**Typical English translations:** erroneous cognition, invalid apprehension, false knowledge
**Usage:** The counterpart to *pramā*, designating cognition that fails to apprehend its object correctly, including illusion, doubt, and memory in some classifications. Used in epistemological analysis to demarcate the boundary of reliable knowledge. Primarily technical.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** सत्
**Transliteration:** sat
**Literal meaning:** present participle of root *as-* (to be); "being, existing, real"
**Typical English translations:** being, real, existent, good, true, genuine
**Usage:** Used both ontologically (what truly exists) and evaluatively (what is genuine or good, as in *sat-puruṣa*, a genuine/good man). In Vedāntic contexts it names the ultimate reality. The evaluative and ontological senses are not always sharply distinguished in usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** असत्
**Transliteration:** asat
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *sat*; "non-being, unreal"
**Typical English translations:** unreal, non-existent, false, bad, spurious
**Usage:** The negation of *sat*, used both ontologically for what lacks genuine existence and evaluatively for what is false, spurious, or morally bad. Appears in philosophical debate about the status of the phenomenal world and in ethical contexts contrasting genuine virtue with its absence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** तथ्य
**Transliteration:** tathya
**Literal meaning:** from *tathā* (thus, so) + *-ya*; "that which is as it is said to be"
**Typical English translations:** true, factual, real, actual fact
**Usage:** Used to characterize statements or accounts that correspond to how things actually are; closer to "factual" or "accurate" than to the broader metaphysical resonances of *satya*. Appears in legal and narrative contexts to evaluate testimony or report. Less philosophically loaded than *satya*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** मिथ्या
**Transliteration:** mithyā
**Literal meaning:** from *mithā* (wrongly, alternately, mutually) + adverbial/adjectival use; "wrongly, falsely"
**Typical English translations:** false, erroneous, illusory, wrong, spurious
**Usage:** Applied to statements, cognitions, and objects that are false, mistaken, or illusory. In Advaita Vedānta, *mithyā* has a specific technical sense: neither absolutely real (*sat*) nor absolutely unreal (*asat*), but of indeterminate ontological status. Also used in everyday contexts for "falsely" or "in vain."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** अनृत
**Transliteration:** anṛta
**Literal meaning:** *an-* (negation) + *ṛta*; "contrary to cosmic order/right"
**Typical English translations:** untruth, falsehood, wrong, lie
**Usage:** The negation of *ṛta*, used in Vedic and epic literature for falsehood or moral wrongness, especially in speech. Appears in Dharmaśāstra contexts as a category of wrongful speech. Less common than *asatya* in post-Vedic prose but attested in verse and legal literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** असत्य
**Transliteration:** asatya
**Literal meaning:** *a-* + *satya*; "non-truth"
**Typical English translations:** untruth, falsehood, lie, false
**Usage:** The direct negation of *satya*, used widely in ethical, legal, and philosophical contexts for false statements or the quality of being untrue. Common in Dharmaśāstra as a category of wrongful speech and in philosophical debate as the opposite of truth. More straightforwardly evaluative than *mithyā*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** वास्तव
**Transliteration:** vāstava
**Literal meaning:** from *vastu* (thing, real object, substance) + *-va* (adjectival); "pertaining to the real object"
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, genuine, factual
**Usage:** Used to characterize what pertains to or corresponds with actual things or states of affairs, as opposed to what is imagined, conventional, or merely apparent. Appears in philosophical and literary contexts to distinguish genuine properties from attributed or conventional ones. Also used in later Sanskrit and in Sanskrit-influenced registers of modern Indian languages.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** वस्तु
**Transliteration:** vastu
**Literal meaning:** from root *vas-* (to dwell, to be); "thing, real entity, substance"
**Typical English translations:** real thing, object, substance, the actual matter
**Usage:** Denotes a real, existing thing or the actual substance of a matter, as opposed to mere appearance or verbal designation. In philosophical contexts, *vastu* is what cognition must correspond to for the cognition to be valid. In legal and practical contexts, it refers to the actual matter at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** आभास
**Transliteration:** ābhāsa
**Literal meaning:** *ā-* + *bhāsa* (shining, appearance, from root *bhās-*, to shine); "semblance, mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** semblance, appearance, fallacy, pseudo-, spurious instance
**Usage:** Used in epistemological and logical contexts for something that merely appears to be what it is not — a fallacious inference (*hetvābhāsa*, fallacy of the reason), a spurious instance, or an illusory appearance. In Kashmiri Śaiva philosophy, *ābhāsa* has a more specific technical sense for the manifestation of appearances within consciousness. Marks the gap between genuine and merely apparent instances of a kind.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** हेत्वाभास
**Transliteration:** hetvābhāsa
**Literal meaning:** *hetu* (reason, logical ground) + *ābhāsa* (semblance); "semblance of a reason"
**Typical English translations:** fallacy, pseudo-reason, spurious logical ground
**Usage:** A fixed technical expression in Sanskrit logic (Nyāya and related schools) for a reason in an inference that appears valid but fails to meet the conditions for genuine probative force. Several subtypes are enumerated in logical literature (*savyabhicāra*, *viruddha*, *satpratipakṣa*, etc.). Widely attested across logical treatises.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** भ्रम
**Transliteration:** bhrama
**Literal meaning:** from root *bhram-* (to wander, to be confused); "wandering, confusion, error"
**Typical English translations:** error, illusion, mistaken cognition, confusion
**Usage:** Denotes erroneous cognition in which something is apprehended as other than it is, the classic example being the rope mistaken for a snake. Central to epistemological discussions of illusion and error across Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Vedānta. Also used in non-technical contexts for confusion or dizziness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** विपर्यय
**Transliteration:** viparyaya
**Literal meaning:** *vi-* (apart, wrongly) + *paryaya* (going around, reversal); "reversal, inversion"
**Typical English translations:** erroneous cognition, misapprehension, wrong knowledge, inversion
**Usage:** Used in Sāṃkhya and other philosophical systems for a category of false or inverted cognition, one of the five types of mental modification (*vṛtti*) in Patañjali's Yogasūtra. Denotes cognition that takes something to be what it is not, the inversion of correct apprehension. Primarily a technical philosophical term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** औचित्य
**Transliteration:** aucitya
**Literal meaning:** from *ucita* (fitting, appropriate, from root *vac-/uc-*) + *-ya*; "fittingness, appropriateness"
**Typical English translations:** propriety, fittingness, appropriateness, decorum
**Usage:** A key term in Sanskrit aesthetics, especially in Kṣemendra's *Aucityavicāracarcā*, where it designates the quality of being fitting or appropriate in a literary composition — the right word, image, or sentiment in the right place. Also used in ethical and social contexts for conduct that is appropriate to one's role or situation. Evaluates rightness as a matter of fit rather than correspondence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** धर्म
**Transliteration:** dharma
**Literal meaning:** from root *dhṛ-* (to hold, to sustain) + *-ma*; "that which holds/sustains"
**Typical English translations:** duty, right conduct, law, righteousness, moral order, nature/property
**Usage:** One of the most semantically broad terms in Sanskrit, covering right conduct, moral and legal duty, the sustaining order of society and cosmos, and (in philosophical logic) the property or characteristic of a thing. Evaluations of conduct as right or wrong, and of things as having certain properties, both invoke *dharma*. The legal sense (as in Dharmaśāstra) and the philosophical sense (property of a subject in inference) are both widely attested.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** अधर्म
**Transliteration:** adharma
**Literal meaning:** *a-* + *dharma*; "contrary to dharma"
**Typical English translations:** unrighteousness, wrong conduct, violation of duty, moral wrong
**Usage:** The negation of *dharma* in its moral and legal senses, designating conduct that violates right order, duty, or law. Widely attested in epic, Purāṇic, and Dharmaśāstra literature as the evaluative opposite of *dharma*. Also used in Vaiśeṣika as a category of unseen moral force (*adṛṣṭa*) generated by wrong action.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** साधु
**Transliteration:** sādhu
**Literal meaning:** from root *sādh-* (to accomplish, to go straight to a goal); "going straight, accomplishing"
**Typical English translations:** correct, proper, good, virtuous, well-formed (of speech)
**Usage:** Used in grammatical contexts to evaluate whether a linguistic form is correct or well-formed (*sādhu śabda*, a correct word), and in ethical contexts for a person of good character or conduct. Pāṇinian grammar uses *sādhu* as the standard for grammatical correctness. The evaluative range covers both linguistic and moral rightness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** अपशब्द
**Transliteration:** apaśabda
**Literal meaning:** *apa-* (away from, deviant) + *śabda* (word, sound); "deviant word"
**Typical English translations:** incorrect word, solecism, ungrammatical form, corrupt usage
**Usage:** Used in grammatical literature for a word or form that deviates from the standard of correct Sanskrit usage (*sādhu*), i.e., a form not sanctioned by grammar. Evaluates linguistic forms with respect to correctness. Contrasted with *sādhu śabda* or *śiṣṭaprayoga* (usage of the learned).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** सादृश्य
**Transliteration:** sādṛśya
**Literal meaning:** from *sadṛśa* (similar, resembling) + *-ya*; "similarity, resemblance"
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used in epistemological contexts (as the basis of analogical cognition, *upamāna*) and in aesthetic contexts to evaluate how closely a representation or description resembles its object. In discussions of portraiture, metaphor, and analogy, *sādṛśya* is the criterion by which the accuracy of a likeness is judged. Participates in derivation: *sādṛśyavāda*, etc.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** औपचारिक
**Transliteration:** aupacārika
**Literal meaning:** from *upacāra* (approach, figurative use, courtesy) + *-ika*; "pertaining to figurative/secondary use"
**Typical English translations:** figurative, secondary, metaphorical, conventional (as opposed to primary/literal)
**Usage:** Used in Mīmāṃsā and grammatical-philosophical literature to characterize a use of a term that is secondary or figurative rather than primary and literal (*mukhya*). Evaluates whether a predication or designation is genuine/direct or merely conventional/transferred. Contrasted with *mukhya* (primary, literal).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**26.**
**Form:** मुख्य
**Transliteration:** mukhya
**Literal meaning:** from *mukha* (face, front, primary) + *-ya*; "primary, foremost"
**Typical English translations:** primary, literal, principal, direct
**Usage:** In Mīmāṃsā and philosophical grammar, *mukhya* designates the primary or literal meaning of a term, as opposed to secondary (*aupacārika*, *gauṇa*, or *lakṣaṇā*) uses. Evaluating whether a term is used in its *mukhya* sense is central to Mīmāṃsā interpretation of Vedic injunctions. Also used more broadly for what is primary or chief.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** गौण
**Transliteration:** gauṇa
**Literal meaning:** from *guṇa* (quality, secondary element) + *-a*; "pertaining to a secondary quality"
**Typical English translations:** secondary, figurative, subordinate, non-primary
**Usage:** Designates a secondary or figurative use of a term, based on some quality (*guṇa*) shared between the primary referent and the secondary one. In grammatical and Mīmāṃsā analysis, *gauṇa* use is distinguished from *mukhya* (primary) use as a mode of non-literal predication. Relevant to evaluating whether a statement is literally or only figuratively true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** स्वरूप
**Transliteration:** svarūpa
**Literal meaning:** *sva* (own, self) + *rūpa* (form, appearance); "own form, intrinsic nature"
**Typical English translations:** own nature, intrinsic form, essential character, what something really is
**Usage:** Used across philosophical, grammatical, and legal contexts to refer to the intrinsic or essential nature of something, as opposed to what it merely appears to be or is called by convention. Evaluating something *svarūpataḥ* (according to its own nature) is a mode of assessment that bypasses conventional or relational characterizations. Widely attested and derivationally productive.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** तत्त्व
**Transliteration:** tattva
**Literal meaning:** *tat* (that) + *-tva* (abstract suffix); "that-ness, the being of that"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, true nature, principle, essence, what something really is
**Usage:** Denotes the true or essential nature of something — what it fundamentally is. Used in philosophical inquiry (*tattvajijñāsā*, inquiry into the real nature of things) and in evaluative contexts where one asks what something truly is as opposed to how it appears. Also used as a technical term for the fundamental categories or principles of reality in Sāṃkhya, Śaiva, and other systems.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** निर्णय
**Transliteration:** nirṇaya
**Literal meaning:** *nis-* (out, completely) + *naya* (leading, from root *nī-*, to lead); "leading out to a conclusion, determination"
**Typical English translations:** determination, ascertainment, conclusion, verdict, decision
**Usage:** Used in epistemological contexts for the definitive ascertainment of a matter after inquiry or deliberation, and in legal contexts for a verdict or judicial determination. In Nyāya, *nirṇaya* is the outcome of debate (*kathā*) when doubt is resolved. Evaluates the epistemic status of a conclusion as settled and determined rather than merely probable or disputed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
