# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] + [ADJ/STATIVE]** — Simple juxtaposition of noun phrase and truth-domain adjective/stative predicate. No copula required in present tense affirmative statements.

### Examples
- **cerita itu benar**
  - cerita itu benar
  - "that story is true"

- **dokumen ini palsu**
  - dokumen ini palsu
  - "this document is fake"

### Notes
Indonesian uses zero copula for present tense predication with adjectives. The truth-domain adjectives function as predicates without additional verbal material. Negation requires **tidak**: *cerita itu tidak benar* "that story is not true."

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] + tidak/bukan + [ADJ/STATIVE]** — Negation with **tidak** for adjectival predicates, **bukan** for nominal predicates.

### Examples
- **informasi itu tidak akurat**
  - informasi itu tidak akurat
  - "that information is not accurate"

- **dia bukan pembohong**
  - dia bukan pembohong
  - "he/she is not a liar"

### Notes
**Tidak** negates adjectival/stative predicates (truth-domain adjectives). **Bukan** negates nominal predicates (agentive nouns like *pembohong* "liar"). Hedged negation uses **kurang**: *kurang benar* "not quite correct."

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + [N]** — Truth-domain adjective precedes and directly modifies noun without intervening particle.

### Examples
- **kebenaran hakiki**
  - kebenaran hakiki
  - "essential truth, fundamental truth"

- **bukti nyata**
  - bukti nyata
  - "tangible evidence, concrete proof"

### Notes
Adjectives precede nouns in Indonesian. Very productive pattern for truth-domain terms. Can be intensified with degree modifiers: *sangat* "very," *paling* "most," *lebih* "more."

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**menjadi + [ADJ/N]** — Verb *menjadi* "to become" combines with truth-domain adjectives or nouns to express change of state.

### Examples
- **menjadi nyata**
  - menjadi nyata
  - "to become real, to be realized"

- **menjadi kenyataan**
  - menjadi kenyataan
  - "to become reality, to come true"

### Notes
*Menjadi* is the standard inchoative/change-of-state verb. Can combine with both adjectival forms (*nyata* "real") and nominalizations (*kenyataan* "reality"). The pattern emphasizes transition into a truth-state.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**meN- + [ROOT] + -kan** — Active voice causative with prefix *meN-* and suffix *-kan*. Highly productive for truth-domain roots.

### Examples
- **membenarkan**
  - meN- + benar + -kan
  - "to confirm, to verify, to validate"

- **menyalahkan**
  - meN- + salah + -kan
  - "to blame, to fault, to accuse"

### Notes
The *meN-...-kan* causative is extremely productive across truth-domain vocabulary. Means "to make X" or "to cause to be X." Passive formed with *di-...-kan*: *dibenarkan* "to be confirmed." Some roots also take applicative *-i*: *membohongi* "to lie to (someone)."

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ke- + [ROOT] + -an** — Circumfix creating abstract nouns denoting qualities or states.

### Examples
- **kebenaran**
  - ke- + benar + -an
  - "truth, correctness, veracity"

- **kejujuran**
  - ke- + jujur + -an
  - "honesty, truthfulness, integrity"

### Notes
The *ke-...-an* pattern is highly productive for creating abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives. Denotes the quality or state of being X. Can be possessed: *kebenarannya* "its truth, the truth of it."

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**peN- + [ROOT]** (without *-an*) — Prefix *peN-* creates agent nouns referring to persons characterized by the action/quality.

### Examples
- **pembohong**
  - peN- + bohong
  - "liar, deceiver"

- **pendusta**
  - peN- + dusta
  - "liar, one who tells falsehoods" (formal/literary)

### Notes
The *peN-* prefix (without *-an*) creates agentive nouns for persons who habitually perform the action or embody the quality. Carries negative moral judgment for deception-related terms. Compare with *peN-...-an* which creates process nominalizations: *pembohongan* "the act of deceiving."

---

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**peN- + [ROOT] + -an** — Circumfix creating nouns denoting processes or acts.

### Examples
- **pembenaran**
  - peN- + benar + -an
  - "justification, validation, confirmation"

- **pemalsuan**
  - peN- + palsu + -an
  - "forgery, counterfeiting, falsification"

### Notes
The *peN-...-an* pattern creates nouns referring to the process or act of doing X, typically derived from *meN-...-kan* causative verbs. Denotes the action rather than the agent or abstract quality. Very productive in formal and legal registers.

---

## 9. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**se- + [ROOT] + -nya** — Circumfix creating adverbial forms meaning "in the manner of X" or "actually X."

### Examples
- **sebenarnya**
  - se- + benar + -nya
  - "actually, in fact, in truth"

- **sesungguhnya**
  - se- + sungguh + -nya
  - "truly, verily, in truth"

### Notes
The *se-...-nya* pattern creates adverbials that function as discourse markers introducing corrections, clarifications, or revelations of actual facts. Extremely common in conversational and narrative contexts. Can also form superlatives with reduplicated bases: *sebenar-benarnya* "most truly."

---

## 10. INTENSIFICATION BY REDUPLICATION

### Pattern
**[ROOT] + [ROOT]** — Full reduplication of truth-domain adjectives/adverbs for intensification or emphasis.

### Examples
- **benar-benar**
  - benar + benar
  - "really, truly, genuinely, completely"

- **sungguh-sungguh**
  - sungguh + sungguh
  - "seriously, earnestly, genuinely"

### Notes
Full reduplication is highly productive for intensifying truth-domain terms. Creates emphatic forms meaning "very X" or "truly X." Can combine with verbal prefixes: *bersungguh-sungguh* "to be earnest." Also used in warnings: *salah-salah* "if by any chance wrong, lest."

---

## 11. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**lebih + [ADJ] + (daripada [NP])** — Comparative using *lebih* "more" before adjective, optionally followed by *daripada* "than" and comparison standard.

### Examples
- **lebih benar**
  - lebih benar (daripada yang lain)
  - "more correct (than the other)"

- **lebih jujur**
  - lebih jujur
  - "more honest, more truthful"

### Notes
Standard comparative construction with *lebih* preceding the adjective. The comparison standard introduced by *daripada* is often omitted when contextually clear. Productive across all truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 12. SUPERLATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**paling + [ADJ]** — Superlative using *paling* "most" before adjective.

### Examples
- **paling benar**
  - paling benar
  - "most correct, truest"

- **paling tepat**
  - paling tepat
  - "most accurate, most appropriate"

### Notes
Standard superlative construction with *paling* preceding the adjective. Identifies the highest degree among alternatives. Productive across all truth-domain adjectives. Alternative emphatic superlative uses *se-...-nya* with reduplication: *sebenar-benarnya* "most truly."

---

## 13. INTERROGATIVE VERIFICATION

### Pattern
**apa + [ADJ]** — Question particle *apa* precedes truth-domain adjective to form yes/no question seeking verification.

### Examples
- **apa benar**
  - apa benar
  - "is it true, is it correct"

- **apa sungguh**
  - apa sungguh
  - "is it really, are you serious"

### Notes
*Apa* functions as yes/no question marker before predicates. Very common formula for verifying truth claims in conversational Indonesian. Can be intensified with particles: *apa benar ya* "is it really true?" Alternative uses *apakah* in more formal registers.

---

## 14. CONDITIONAL TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**kalau/jika + [ADJ]** — Conditional marker *kalau/jika* "if" introduces hypothetical truth conditions.

### Examples
- **kalau benar**
  - kalau benar
  - "if it's true, if correct"

- **kalau tidak salah**
  - kalau tidak salah
  - "if not mistaken, if I'm not wrong"

### Notes
Standard conditional construction introducing hypothetical truth states. *Kalau* is more colloquial, *jika* more formal. Common in reasoning and argumentation. The pattern *kalau tidak salah* functions as epistemic hedge meaning "if I'm not mistaken."

---

## 15. BINARY OPPOSITION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ₁] + atau + [ADJ₂]** — Coordination of antonymous truth-domain terms with *atau* "or" to express binary alternatives.

### Examples
- **benar atau salah**
  - benar atau salah
  - "true or false, right or wrong"

- **asli atau palsu**
  - asli atau palsu
  - "genuine or fake, authentic or counterfeit"

### Notes
Fixed phrases coordinating antonymous pairs with *atau* "or." Very common in tests, logical reasoning, and authentication contexts. The pattern emphasizes binary choice between truth-values or authenticity states.

---

## 16. PREPOSITIONAL MANNER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**dengan/secara + [ADJ/N]** — Preposition *dengan* "with" or *secara* "in a...manner" combines with truth-domain terms to express manner.

### Examples
- **dengan jujur**
  - dengan jujur
  - "honestly, with honesty, truthfully"

- **secara faktual**
  - secara faktual
  - "factually, in a factual manner"

### Notes
*Dengan* is more colloquial, *secara* more formal. Both create adverbial expressions of manner. *Secara* is particularly common with borrowed/technical terms (*faktual*, *objektif*). Very productive pattern across truth-domain vocabulary.

---

## 17. EVIDENTIAL RESULT CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**ter- + [ROOT]** — Prefix *ter-* marks accidental occurrence or resultative state, including evidential discovery.

### Examples
- **ternyata**
  - ter- + nyata
  - "it turns out, apparently, evidently"

- **terbukti bohong**
  - ter- + bukti + bohong
  - "proven false, proven to be a lie"

### Notes
*Ter-* has multiple functions; with truth-domain terms it often marks evidential discovery or resultative state. *Ternyata* is extremely common as discourse marker introducing new information that contradicts prior belief. *Terbukti* "proven" combines with truth-predicates to mark demonstrated truth-value.

---

## 18. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH TRUTH NOUNS

### Pattern
**[VERB] + [TRUTH-NOUN]** — Common verbs combine with truth-domain nominalizations to form semi-fixed expressions.

### Examples
- **mencari kebenaran**
  - mencari kebenaran
  - "to seek truth, to search for truth"

- **menghadapi kenyataan**
  - menghadapi kenyataan
  - "to face reality, to confront facts"

### Notes
Productive pattern where verbs of seeking (*mencari*), revealing (*mengungkap*), upholding (*menegakkan*), hiding (*menyembunyikan*), facing (*menghadapi*), accepting (*menerima*) combine with truth-nominalizations. Common in investigative, philosophical, and psychological discourse.

---

## 19. COMPOUND ERROR CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**salah + [NOUN/VERB]** — *Salah* "wrong" combines with nouns or verb roots to specify type of error.

### Examples
- **salah paham**
  - salah paham
  - "to misunderstand, misunderstanding"

- **salah hitung**
  - salah hitung
  - "miscalculation, calculation error"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern where *salah* specifies the domain of error: *salah lihat* "to see wrongly," *salah dengar* "to mishear," *salah ucap* "slip of the tongue," *salah tulis* "typo," *salah tafsir* "misinterpretation." Functions as both verb and noun. Distinct from *salah satu* "one of" where *salah* is grammaticalized as selector.

---

## 20. EMPHATIC CONFIRMATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + sekali/belaka** — Intensifier *sekali* "very" or *belaka* "entirely" follows truth-domain adjective for emphasis.

### Examples
- **benar sekali**
  - benar sekali
  - "very true, absolutely correct, exactly right"

- **bohong belaka**
  - bohong belaka
  - "nothing but lies, entirely false"

### Notes
*Sekali* following adjectives creates emphatic affirmation, very common in conversational responses. *Belaka* "entirely, merely" emphasizes totality, often with negative connotation (*bohong belaka* "nothing but lies"). Both patterns are productive but *sekali* is more frequent.

---

## 21. RELATIVIZATION WITH TRUTH PREDICATES

### Pattern
**yang + [ADJ/VERB]** — Relativizer *yang* nominalizes truth-predicates or creates relative clauses.

### Examples
- **yang benar**
  - yang benar
  - "the correct one, what is right"

- **yang sesungguhnya**
  - yang sesungguhnya
  - "the real one, what is truly, the actual"

### Notes
*Yang* is the general relativizer/nominalizer. With truth-domain adjectives, creates expressions identifying correct/true alternatives or emphasizing actual reality. Can function as exclamation: *Yang benar saja!* "Really?!/You must be joking!" Very productive pattern.

---

## 22. PASSIVE APPLICATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**di- + [ROOT] + -i** — Passive prefix *di-* with applicative suffix *-i* for patient-focused deception/error.

### Examples
- **dibohongi**
  - di- + bohong + -i
  - "to be lied to, to be deceived"

- **disalahkan**
  - di- + salah + -kan
  - "to be blamed, to be faulted"

### Notes
Passive applicative marks the patient/victim of deception or blame attribution. *-i* applicative appears with roots like *bohong* (lie), while *-kan* causative appears with *salah* (wrong). Both patterns are productive for expressing who receives the action.

---

## 23. HEDGED NEGATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**kurang + [ADJ]** — *Kurang* "less, lacking" creates polite or hedged negation.

### Examples
- **kurang benar**
  - kurang benar
  - "not quite correct, somewhat inaccurate"

- **kurang jujur**
  - kurang jujur
  - "not quite honest, somewhat dishonest"

### Notes
*Kurang* softens negation, common in formal and polite discourse to avoid direct contradiction. Implies partial rather than complete absence of the quality. Very productive across truth-domain adjectives as face-saving strategy.

---

## 24. EXISTENTIAL TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + adanya** — Combination of truth-adjective with *adanya* (nominalized "exist") affirms actual existence.

### Examples
- **benar adanya**
  - benar adanya
  - "it is true, it truly exists, it is indeed the case"

- (no common expression identified for other roots)

### Notes
*Benar adanya* is a fixed phrase confirming that something truly exists or is the case. Common in formal statements and testimony. The pattern is not productive beyond this specific expression with *benar*.

---

## 25. VALIDATION AND AUTHORIZATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**meN- + [ROOT] + -kan** (specialized for legal/official validity) — Causative verbs specifically for conferring legal/official validity.

### Examples
- **mengesahkan**
  - meN- + sah + -kan
  - "to validate, to legalize, to authorize, to ratify"

- **memvalidasi**
  - meN- + valid + -asi
  - "to validate" (borrowed pattern)

### Notes
*Mengesahkan* (from *sah* "valid") is the primary verb for legal/official validation. *Memvalidasi* uses borrowed suffix *-asi* (from -ation) with borrowed root *valid*. Both common in legal and administrative contexts. Passive forms *disahkan*, *divalidasi* are frequent.
