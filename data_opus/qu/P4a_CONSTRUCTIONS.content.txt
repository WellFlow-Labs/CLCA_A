## 1. ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
Quality predicated of a subject via the copula *kay* "to be," or by simple juxtaposition with an evidential enclitic (e.g. *-mi*) marking assertion. The bare quality word functions predicatively.
### Examples
- chiqaqmi → chiqaqmi → "it's true (I affirm)"
- allinmi → allinmi → "it's good / it's fine"
### Notes
Truth/value attribution is typically carried by the bare quality root plus an evidential enclitic; the copula *kay* surfaces overtly mainly in nominalized abstractions (*chiqap kay*). Adjective and predicate share the same form.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
Preposed negator *mana* "not" + quality word (+ optional agentive *-q*), forming a fixed negated evaluative expression.
### Examples
- mana allin → mana allin → "bad, not good, improper"
- mana llullaq → mana llullaq → "honest, one who does not lie"
### Notes
*mana* + ADJ yields negative polarity (*mana allin* = bad), while *mana* + AGENTIVE yields positive polarity by negating a negative trait (*mana llullaq* = honest). Both are conventionalized multi-word units.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION
### Pattern
Quality word directly precedes the head noun (modifier + noun), with no linker. Recurrent frames include [QUALITY + simi] "word," [QUALITY + sunqu] "heart," [QUALITY + kawsay] "life."
### Examples
- chiqaq simi → chiqaq simi → "true word, truthful speech"
- llulla simi → llulla simi → "false word, falsehood"
### Notes
Modifier-head order is fixed (quality precedes noun). The frame is productive across truth-domain roots and yields the clearest minimal contrast pair (*chiqaq simi* vs. *llulla simi*).

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE
### Pattern
(no common expression identified)
### Examples
- (none attested)
### Notes
No dedicated inchoative ("become true/good/false") construction is documented in P1–P3. The causative derivations (*-cha-*) cover change-of-state transitively, but no intransitive "become X" form is attested for these roots.

## 5. CAUSATIVE
### Pattern
Quality root + causative *-cha-* (+ infinitive *-y*) forms a verb "to make/render X." A second causative *-chi-* derives "to cause someone to (do) X."
### Examples
- allinchay → allinchay → "to make good, improve, repair"
- llullachiy → llullachiy → "to cause to believe a falsehood, deceive"
### Notes
*-cha-* is the dominant, broadly productive causative across the domain (chiqachay, chiqanchay, allinchay, sut'inchay, kikinchay). *-chi-* is attested but narrower, marking external causation of a third party.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
Quality word + copular noun *kay* "being" → "the state/abstraction of X." A recurrent [X + kay] frame spans truth, goodness, and falsehood poles.
### Examples
- chiqap kay → chiqap kay → "truth, reality"
- llulla kay → llulla kay → "falseness, mendacity"
### Notes
[X + kay] is the most cross-cutting nominalizing template (also *allin kay* "goodness"). Word order/form is somewhat variable, bordering between compounding and free combination.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
Verb stem + agentive *-q* forms a person noun ("one who Xs"); negated agentives use *mana* + STEM + *-q*. Augmentative *-sapa* marks a habitual/intensive trait.
### Examples
- llullakuq → llullakuq → "liar, deceiver"
- mana llullaq → mana llullaq → "truthful person, one who does not lie"
### Notes
Agentive *-q* is productive (llullakuq, rikch'akuq, iñiq); a true-teller is expressed negatively (*mana llullaq*) rather than by a dedicated positive agentive. *-sapa* (*llullasapa* "habitual liar") intensifies the trait.

## 8. ADVERBIAL (MANNER / TRUTH-INTENSIFICATION)
### Pattern
Quality word + accusative/adverbial *-ta* forms a manner adverb ("X-ly, truly, properly"); certainty enclitic *-puni* intensifies assertion strength.
### Examples
- chiqaqta → chiqaqta → "truly, really, rightly"
- allinta → allinta → "well, properly, thoroughly"
### Notes
*-ta* derives adverbs of manner/affirmation from evaluative roots; *-puni* (chiqaqpuni "definitely true") marks heightened certainty and is borderline grammatical.

## 9. INTERROGATIVE TRUTH-CONFIRMATION
### Pattern
Quality word + interrogative *-chu* forms a conventionalized request to confirm a claim's truth.
### Examples
- chiqaqchu → chiqaqchu → "is it true? really?"
- chiqaptachu → chiqaptachu → "is it true?"
### Notes
The interrogative enclitic *-chu* attaches to the truth root to form fixed conversational confirmation formulas; borderline between inflected word and fixed phrase.

## 10. COMPARISON / RESEMBLANCE
### Pattern
Standard of comparison + particle *hina* "like/as" (postposed), or standard + allative *-man* + verb *rikch'akuy* "to resemble."
### Examples
- kikin hina → kikin hina → "just the same, exactly alike"
- X-man rikch'akuy → X-man rikch'akuy → "to resemble X, look like X"
### Notes
Two distinct strategies: a postposed particle (*hina*) for equivalence/likeness, and a case-marked verbal construction (*-man rikch'akuy*) for resemblance of appearance. Both are productive rather than idiomatic.
