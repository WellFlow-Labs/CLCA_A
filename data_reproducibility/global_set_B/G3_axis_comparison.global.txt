# G3 — Emergent Cross-Language Semantic Dimensions

---

## Methodology Note

The dimensions below are inferred from patterns of co-occurrence, grouping, and contrast visible in the F1–F3 tables and the G1–G2 summaries. Each dimension is defined by what the data shows, not by prior theoretical categories. Where a dimension is attested in only one or two languages, this is noted explicitly.

---

## Proposed Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Correspondence to Fact / Reality

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether a statement, claim, or representation matches an actual state of affairs in the world. The primary contrast is between what is the case and what is asserted or represented. These terms appear in contexts of verification, fact-checking, and distinguishing claims from reality.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 真实 (zhēnshí), 事实 (shìshí), 符合事实 (fú hé shì shí), 属实 (shǔshí)
- he: אמת (emet), עובדה (uvdah), מציאות (metsi'ut), נכון (nakhon)
- sa: satya, tathya, yathārtha, vastutaḥ
- el: αλήθεια (alítheia), αληθής (alithís), πραγματικός (pragmatikós)
- en: true, accurate, factual, correspond to reality
- tl: totoo, katotohanan, makatotohanan
- sw: kweli, ukweli, ni kweli
- qu: chiqaq, chiqaqta rimay

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all 8 languages. This is the most consistently attested dimension in the corpus. In every language, there is at least one core term whose primary function is to affirm or deny correspondence between a claim and reality.

---

### Dimension 2: Authenticity / Genuineness of Entities

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether an object, person, document, or relationship is what it purports to be — original, genuine, or authentic — as opposed to counterfeit, forged, or imitation. The contrast is not between true and false statements but between genuine and fake instances of a category.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 真品 (zhēnpǐn), 真迹 (zhēnjì), 真金 (zhēnjīn), 假冒 (jiǎmào), 假造 (jiǎzào)
- he: אמיתי (amiti), אותנטי (otenti), מזויף (mezuyaf), זיוף (ziyuf)
- sa: halisi (borrowed into sw), vāstava, vāstavika
- el: αυθεντικός (afthentikós), γνήσιος (gnísios), πλαστός (plastós), μζοιφ (mezuyaf parallel)
- en: genuine, authentic, counterfeit, fake, bona fide
- tl: tunay, peke, huwad, katunayan
- sw: halisi, -a kweli (in object contexts), batili
- qu: chanin (valid/legitimate), chiqaq (in object contexts)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all 8 languages, though the degree of elaboration varies. In Quechua, the distinction between propositional truth and material authenticity is less lexically enforced than in other languages — chiqaq covers both. In Swahili, halisi (from Arabic) handles material authenticity while kweli handles propositional truth.

---

### Dimension 3: Correctness of Judgment, Answer, or Action

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether a judgment, answer, decision, or action is correct or incorrect according to some standard, rule, or expected outcome. These terms appear prominently in educational, practical, and evaluative contexts. They are distinct from propositional truth in that they assess conformity to a norm or standard rather than correspondence to a state of affairs.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 对/错 (duì/cuò), 正确 (zhèngquè), 错误 (cuòwù), 说对 (shuōduì), 做错 (zuòcuò)
- he: נכון (nakhon), לא נכון (lo nakhon), תשובה נכונה (tshuvah nkhonah)
- sa: samyak, sādhu, yukta/ayukta
- el: σωστός (sostós), λάθος (láthos), σωστή απάντηση (sostí apántisi)
- en: correct, incorrect, right answer, wrong answer
- tl: tama, mali, tamang sagot, itama
- sw: sahihi, si sahihi, jibu sahihi
- qu: chiqan, pantay, pantasqa

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all 8 languages. In several languages (Hebrew nakhon, Swahili sahihi, Tagalog tama), this dimension overlaps with Dimension 1 (factual correspondence), and the same term serves both functions. In Chinese, 对/错 is more specifically tied to correctness of judgment and action, while 真实 handles factual correspondence more directly.

---

### Dimension 4: Sincerity / Honesty of Persons

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether a person's speech, expressions, or conduct genuinely reflect their inner states and intentions, or whether they are pretending, dissembling, or being insincere. The contrast is between alignment and misalignment between inner state and outward expression. These terms appear in interpersonal and moral evaluation contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 诚实 (chéngshí), 真诚 (zhēnchéng), 真心 (zhēnxīn), 虚情假意 (xū qíng jiǎ yì)
- he: כנה (khen), כנות (khenut), יושר (yosher), ביושר (be-yosher)
- sa: satyadharma, satyavādin, satyavacana
- el: ειλικρινής (ilikrinís), ειλικρίνεια (ilikríneia)
- en: honest, sincere, truthful (of persons), dishonest, insincere
- tl: tapat, matapat, katapatan, magtapat
- sw: -nyofu, unyofu, -adilifu (in honesty sense), mtu wa kweli
- qu: (expressed through allin in moral sense; no dedicated term)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 7 of 8 languages. Quechua shows the weakest lexical elaboration of this dimension as a distinct cluster; honesty of persons is expressed through the general goodness term allin rather than a dedicated sincerity vocabulary. In Tagalog, tapat covers both sincerity in speech and faithfulness/loyalty in conduct without a clear internal boundary.

---

### Dimension 5: Deliberate Deception / Lying

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension describe intentional acts of creating false impressions in others through speech or behavior. The distinguishing feature is the presence of deliberate intent to deceive, which separates these terms from unintentional error. These terms appear in moral condemnation, legal contexts, and warnings about dishonest behavior.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 谎言 (huǎngyán), 欺骗 (qīpiàn), 假话 (jiǎhuà), 撒谎 (sā huǎng)
- he: שקר (sheker), שקרן (shakran), שיקר (shiker), עד שקר (ed sheker)
- sa: mṛṣā, kūṭa, chala, mṛṣāvādin
- el: ψέμα (pséma), ψεύτης (pséftis), ψεύδομαι (psévdome), ψευδής (psevdís)
- en: lie, liar, deceive, falsehood, mendacious
- tl: kasinungalingan, sinungaling, magsinungaling
- sw: uongo, -danganyifu, mdanganyifu, kusema uongo
- qu: llulla, llullakuy, llullaq, pantachiy (to mislead)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all 8 languages. The distinction between deliberate deception (this dimension) and unintentional error (Dimension 6) is maintained in all 8 languages, though the lexical items differ. In Quechua, pantachiy (causative of pantay "to err") covers the sense of causing someone to err or mislead, bridging Dimensions 5 and 6.

---

### Dimension 6: Unintentional Error / Mistake

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension characterize statements, beliefs, or actions as containing error or being mistaken, without necessarily implying moral fault or deliberate deception. The contrast is with correctness, not with honesty. These terms appear in corrections, apologies, and evaluations that distinguish error from falsehood.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 错误 (cuòwù), 误 (wù in compounds), 失真 (shīzhēn, loss of fidelity)
- he: טעות (ta'ut), מוטעה (mut'eh), טועה (to'eh), בטעות (be-ta'ut)
- sa: bhrama, bhrānti, viparyaya, apramā
- el: λάθος (láthos), λαθεμένος (latheménos), από λάθος (apó láthos)
- en: mistake, error, erroneous, mistaken, by mistake
- tl: pagkakamali, nagkamali, mali (in error sense)
- sw: (implied in pantay parallel; less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- qu: pantay, pantasqa, pantakuy, pagkakamali (tl parallel)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 7 of 8 languages. Swahili shows the least elaborated dedicated vocabulary for unintentional error as a distinct cluster; the distinction is maintained but less lexically marked than in other languages. In Chinese, 错 covers both "wrong" (correctness evaluation) and "error" (mistake), making the boundary with Dimension 3 less sharp.

---

### Dimension 7: Reliability / Trustworthiness of Sources

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether persons, sources, or information can be depended upon to produce accurate results or honest reports consistently over time. The focus is on dependability and track record rather than the truth-value of any single claim. These terms appear in contexts of source evaluation, credibility assessment, and trust judgments.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 可靠 (kěkào), 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng), 可靠度 (kěkàodù), 不可靠 (bù kěkào)
- he: אמין (amin), מהימן (meheyман), אמינות (aminut), מהימנות (meheymanut)
- el: αξιόπιστος (axiópistos), αξιοπιστία (axiopistía), αμφίβολος (amfívolos)
- en: reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible, unreliable
- tl: mapagkakatiwalaan, magtiwala, pagtitiwala
- sw: -a busara (in reliability sense), mtu wa kweli (as reliable person)
- sa: (less elaborated as distinct cluster; prāmāṇika covers authoritative/reliable)
- qu: chanin (in legitimate/reliable sense)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 5–6 languages (Chinese, Hebrew, Greek, English, Tagalog most clearly). Sanskrit and Quechua show less elaboration of reliability as a distinct cluster separate from validity of knowledge. Swahili integrates reliability into the moral character cluster (-adilifu, -nyofu) rather than maintaining a separate reliability vocabulary.

---

### Dimension 8: Epistemic Certainty / Doubt

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension express or evaluate the degree of confidence or uncertainty a speaker or subject has about a proposition. These terms function as epistemic modifiers indicating how strongly something is believed or known, ranging from certainty to doubt. They appear in hedging, emphatic assertion, and expressions of confidence.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 确实 (quèshí), 的确 (díquè), 千真万确 (qiān zhēn wàn què)
- he: ודאי (vadai), בטוח (batuakh), ספק (safek), אין ספק (ein safek)
- sa: hakika (borrowed into sw), ṛtajña (knowing order)
- el: βέβαιος (véveos), βεβαιότητα (veveótita), αμφίβολος (amfívolos), αμφιβολία (amfivolía)
- en: certain, sure, definite, doubt, uncertain
- tl: sigurado, hindi sigurado, talaga (as emphatic reality marker)
- sw: hakika, uhakika, kwa hakika, bila shaka
- qu: -mi/-si/-cha evidential suffixes (grammaticalized); chiqapuni (emphatic truth)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 7 of 8 languages. Quechua is unique in grammaticalizing this dimension as obligatory morphological suffixes (-mi for direct knowledge, -si for hearsay, -cha for conjecture) rather than expressing it through lexical items. Chinese shows less elaboration of certainty/doubt as a distinct cluster compared to other languages; certainty is expressed through intensifiers (确实, 千真万确) rather than a dedicated certainty vocabulary.

---

### Dimension 9: Validity / Legitimacy (Formal/Institutional)

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether documents, claims, authority, or legal instruments have official force and recognition within institutional systems. The contrast is between what is formally recognized and what lacks such recognition. These terms appear predominantly in legal, administrative, and bureaucratic contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 有效 (yǒuxiào, valid — implied), 合法 (héfǎ, legal — implied), 证实 (zhèngshí, verified)
- he: תוקף (tokef, validity — implied), מוצדק (mutsдak, justified)
- el: έγκυρος (énkyros), άκυρος (ákyros), εγκυρότητα (enkyrótita), ακυρώνω (akyróno)
- en: valid, invalid, legitimate, illegitimate, lawful
- tl: chanin (valid/legitimate), chanin qillqa (valid document)
- sw: batili (invalid), batilisha (to invalidate), hati sahihi (valid document), saini sahihi
- sa: chanin (in Quechua parallel; Sanskrit: prāmāṇika, prāmāṇya)
- qu: chanin, chaninchay (to validate), mana chanin (invalid)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 6–7 languages. Greek shows the most elaborated vocabulary for this dimension (έγκυρος/άκυρος pair with productive derivation). Swahili shows elaboration particularly in document authentication vocabulary. Chinese shows less dedicated vocabulary for formal validity as a distinct cluster; the concept is expressed through compounds (有效, 合法) that are less central to the truth domain as documented.

---

### Dimension 10: Moral Rightness / Justice

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether actions, judgments, or distributions conform to principles of moral rightness, fairness, or justice. These terms assess ethical correctness in treatment and judgment rather than factual accuracy. They appear in moral, legal, and social contexts where fairness is at stake.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 公正 (gōngzhèng, just — implied), 正义 (zhèngyì, justice — implied)
- he: צדק (tsedek), צדיק (tsadik), דין צדק (din tsedek), בצדק (be-tsedek)
- sa: dharma, nyāya (in justice sense), ṛta (cosmic rightness)
- el: δίκαιος (díkaios), δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosýni), άδικος (ádikos), αδικία (adikía)
- en: just, justice, righteous, righteousness, fair, unfair
- tl: (less elaborated as distinct cluster; tapat covers some moral rightness)
- sw: haki (in justice sense), -adilifu, uadilifu, kwa haki
- qu: allin (in moral goodness sense), yupaychay (honor/respect)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 6 of 8 languages. Tagalog shows the least elaborated dedicated vocabulary for justice/moral rightness as a distinct cluster. Chinese shows moral rightness vocabulary but it is less central to the truth domain as documented in F1–F3. In Hebrew and Swahili, this dimension merges with epistemic rightness (tsodek = "you're right"; haki = "truth/right/justice"), creating a merged cluster not seen in other languages.

---

### Dimension 11: Substance vs. Appearance / Essence vs. Surface

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension contrast what something fundamentally is (its essential nature, substance, or reality) with how it appears, is named, or is represented on the surface. These terms appear in philosophical, critical, and analytical contexts where the gap between surface and depth is at issue.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 实/虚 (shí/xū), 本质 (běnzhì), 实质 (shízhì), 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí), 华而不实 (huá ér bù shí)
- he: מהות (mahut), מהותי (mahuti)
- sa: tattva, svarūpa, vastu vs. ābhāsa, rūpa (in appearance sense)
- el: αληθοφανής (alithofanís), αληθοφάνεια (alithofáneia), απατηλός (apatilós)
- en: real vs. apparent, substance vs. form, true self, true colors
- tl: diwa (essence), tunay na kalikasan (true nature)
- sw: -a dhati (essential/inherent), ukweli halisi (genuine truth)
- qu: chiqaq ñan (true path, metaphorical), sut'i (clear/evident)

**Distribution:** Attested in all 8 languages but with varying degrees of lexical elaboration. Chinese shows the most elaborated vocabulary for this dimension, with a dedicated semantic group (Group 9: Substance vs. Appearance) and numerous fixed expressions. Sanskrit shows elaboration through the tattva/ābhāsa opposition. Greek shows it through αληθοφανής (verisimilitude). In Tagalog and Swahili, this dimension is less lexically elaborated as a distinct cluster.

---

### Dimension 12: Precision / Accuracy of Measurement or Description

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate the degree of exactness, precision, or fine-grained correspondence between a representation and its target. These terms appear in technical, scientific, and informational contexts where the degree of correspondence matters, not just its presence or absence.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 准确 (zhǔnquè), 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng), 准确度 (zhǔnquèdù), 精确 (jīngquè)
- he: מדויק (meduyak), דיוק (diyuk), בדיוק (be-diyuk)
- sa: paqta (in Quechua parallel; Sanskrit: parimāṇa, māna)
- el: ακριβής (akrivís), ακρίβεια (akríveia), ακριβώς (akrivós)
- en: accurate, precise, exact, accuracy, precision
- tl: wasto (in precision sense), hustó (exact, just right)
- sw: usahihi (in precision sense), kwa usahihi, hesabu sahihi
- qu: chiqan (exact/correct), paqta (exact, just right), paqtachiy

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in 7 of 8 languages. Sanskrit shows less elaboration of precision as a distinct cluster separate from general correctness; the measurement vocabulary (māna, parimāṇa) is present but less integrated into the truth domain as documented. In several languages (Swahili sahihi, Hebrew nakhon), precision overlaps with general correctness (Dimension 3) without a sharp boundary.

---

### Dimension 13: Resemblance / Similarity

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether one thing resembles, looks like, or is similar to another. In several languages, these terms also serve an epistemic hedging function, indicating that something appears or seems to be the case without full certainty. The dual function — comparative and epistemic — is attested in multiple languages.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 像 (xiàng), 似 (sì), 逼真 (bīzhēn, lifelike), 失真 (shīzhēn, loss of fidelity)
- he: דומה (domeh), דמיון (dimyon), כנראה (ken-nir'eh, apparently)
- sa: sadṛśa, sādṛśya, upamāna (comparison as epistemic instrument)
- el: αληθοφανής (alithofanís, apparently true), δομεί (domeh parallel)
- en: resemble, similar, seem, appear, verisimilar
- tl: katulad, kamukha, kawangis
- sw: (less elaborated; rikch'ay in Quechua parallel)
- qu: rikch'ay (to resemble), rikch'aq (similar one), rikch'ana (model/pattern)

**Distribution:** Attested in 7 of 8 languages, but with varying integration into the truth domain. Hebrew shows the strongest integration of resemblance into epistemic hedging (domeh li she- = "it seems to me that"). Sanskrit uniquely treats comparison (upamāna) as a distinct valid means of knowledge. Swahili shows the least elaboration of resemblance vocabulary within the truth domain. In Tagalog, resemblance terms (katulad, kamukha) form a distinct semantic group but are less integrated with epistemic hedging than in Hebrew.

---

### Dimension 14: Epistemic Source / Evidence Type

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension mark or evaluate the source or type of evidence on which a knowledge claim is based, distinguishing for example between direct observation, hearsay, inference, and conjecture. This dimension is expressed grammatically in one language and lexically or constructionally in others.

**Representative terms:**
- qu: -mi (direct evidence), -si (hearsay/reportative), -cha (conjecture/inference) — grammaticalized
- sa: pratyakṣa-pramāṇa (perception), anumāna-pramāṇa (inference), śabda-pramāṇa (testimony), upamāna-pramāṇa (comparison) — lexicalized technical vocabulary
- he: rikuywan yachay (know by seeing) vs. uyariywan yachay (know by hearing) — constructional
- zh: 耳听为虚，眼见为实 (hearing is uncertain, seeing is believing) — proverbial/constructional
- el: (less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- en: (expressed through evidential adverbs: apparently, reportedly, allegedly)
- tl: (less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- sw: (expressed through verbal collocations: kusema kweli vs. uyariywan parallel)

**Distribution:** Attested in all 8 languages but with very different degrees of grammatical vs. lexical expression. Quechua is unique in grammaticalizing this dimension as obligatory morphology. Sanskrit shows the most elaborate lexical differentiation of epistemic source types. Other languages express this dimension constructionally or through isolated lexical items rather than as a systematic cluster.

---

### Dimension 15: Completeness / Sufficiency

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate whether something has reached its proper measure, contains all necessary parts, or is sufficient for its purpose. These terms appear in contexts of assessing whether something is whole, full, or adequate. In at least one language, this dimension shows a semantic extension to cessation/sufficiency markers.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 充实 (chōngshí), 完整 (wánzhěng), 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí, worthy of the name)
- he: שלם (shalem, complete — implied), תיקון (tikun, correction/completion)
- sa: kamili (borrowed into sw), hunt'a (in Quechua parallel), tukuy
- el: (less elaborated as distinct cluster within truth domain)
- en: complete, whole, full, sufficient
- tl: hunt'a (Quechua parallel), tama na (sufficiency/cessation extension)
- sw: kamili, ukamili, kamilisha, taarifa kamili
- qu: hunt'a, hunt'achiy, tukuy, tukuchiy, ch'usaq (empty, as opposite)

**Distribution:** Attested in 6–7 languages but with varying integration into the truth domain. Quechua shows the most elaborated vocabulary for completeness/fullness as a distinct cluster within the truth domain. Tagalog shows a unique semantic extension where tama na (from correctness) and hustó na (from exactness) shift to sufficiency/cessation markers. In Chinese, completeness vocabulary (充实, 翔实) is present but less central to the truth domain.

---

### Dimension 16: Objectivity vs. Subjectivity

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension distinguish perspectives, judgments, or claims based on whether they are independent of personal bias (objective) or influenced by personal viewpoint (subjective). These terms appear in academic, analytical, and methodological contexts to qualify the epistemic status of claims.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 客观 (kèguān), 主观 (zhǔguān), 客观性 (kèguānxìng), 主观性 (zhǔguānxìng), 客观化 (kèguānhuà)
- el: (less elaborated; implied in αμερόληπτος, impartial — not in data)
- en: objective, subjective, objectivity, subjectivity, impartial, biased
- he: (less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- sa: (less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- tl: (not attested as distinct cluster)
- sw: (not attested as distinct cluster)
- qu: (not attested as distinct cluster)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in Chinese and English. Weakly attested or absent as a distinct cluster in Hebrew, Sanskrit, Greek, Tagalog, Swahili, and Quechua. This is the most language-specific dimension in the corpus: Chinese shows the most elaborated vocabulary (with productive derivation), and English shows a parallel elaboration, but other languages do not show this as a distinct semantic cluster within the truth domain.

---

### Dimension 17: Rights / Entitlements

**Definition:** Terms in this dimension evaluate what persons are legally or morally entitled to, what is owed to them, or what constitutes a legitimate claim. These terms appear in legal, political, and advocacy contexts. In at least one language, this dimension merges with propositional truth in a single term.

**Representative terms:**
- sw: haki (rights/justice/truth), haki za binadamu, haki ya kura, linda haki, vunja haki
- he: צדק (tsedek), צדקה (tsedakah), משפט צדק (mishpat tsedek), בצדק (be-tsedek)
- sa: dharma (in legal/rights sense), nyāya (in justice sense)
- el: δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosýni), δίκαιος (díkaios)
- en: right, rights, legitimate, entitlement, just
- zh: (less elaborated as distinct cluster within truth domain)
- tl: (less elaborated as distinct cluster)
- qu: chanin (in legitimate entitlement sense)

**Distribution:** Attested in 6–7 languages but with very different degrees of elaboration. Swahili shows the most elaborated vocabulary for this dimension, with haki generating an extensive set of compounds and verbal collocations. Hebrew shows secondary elaboration through the ts-d-k root. In Swahili, this dimension uniquely merges with propositional truth in the single term haki (which covers both "the truth is that..." and "human rights"). Other languages maintain a clearer separation between truth and rights/entitlements.

## Strength Assessment

### Strongly Supported Cross-Linguistically (6–8 languages, clearly lexicalized)

- **Dimension 1: Correspondence to Fact/Reality** — all 8 languages, clearly lexicalized, the most consistently attested dimension
- **Dimension 2: Authenticity/Genuineness of Entities** — all 8 languages, clearly lexicalized; Quechua shows least differentiation from Dim. 1
- **Dimension 3: Correctness of Judgment/Action** — all 8 languages, clearly lexicalized; overlaps with Dim. 1 in several languages
- **Dimension 5: Deliberate Deception/Lying** — all 8 languages, clearly lexicalized
- **Dimension 6: Unintentional Error/Mistake** — 7 of 8 languages; Swahili least elaborated
- **Dimension 8: Epistemic Certainty/Doubt** — all 8 languages; expression varies from lexical to grammaticalized
- **Dimension 4: Sincerity/Honesty of Persons** — 7 of 8 languages clearly lexicalized; Quechua weakest

### Moderately Supported (4–7 languages, with variation in elaboration)

- **Dimension 7: Reliability/Trustworthiness of Sources** — 5–6 languages clearly lexicalized
- **Dimension 9: Validity/Legitimacy (Formal/Institutional)** — 6–7 languages; Greek and Swahili most elaborated
- **Dimension 10: Moral Rightness/Justice** — 6 of 8 languages; merges with Dim. 1 in Hebrew and Swahili
- **Dimension 11: Substance vs. Appearance** — all 8 languages but varying elaboration; Chinese most elaborated
- **Dimension 12: Precision/Accuracy of Measurement** — 7 of 8 languages; overlaps with Dim. 3 in several
- **Dimension 13: Resemblance/Similarity** — 7 of 8 languages; epistemic hedging function varies by language
- **Dimension 15: Completeness/Sufficiency** — 6–7 languages; Quechua most elaborated; Tagalog shows unique extension

### Tentative or Weakly Attested (1–3 languages as distinct clusters)

- **Dimension 14: Epistemic Source/Evidence Type** — attested in all 8 but only grammaticalized in Quechua; only Sanskrit shows systematic lexical differentiation of source types; other languages express this constructionally or through isolated items
- **Dimension 16: Objectivity vs. Subjectivity** — clearly lexicalized only in Chinese and English; absent or marginal as a distinct cluster in 6 other languages
- **Dimension 17: Rights/Entitlements** — attested in 6 languages but only Swahili shows it as a primary distinct cluster within the truth domain; in most languages it merges with Dim. 10 (Moral Rightness)

## Notes on Splits, Mergers, and Language-Specific Variation

**Dimensions 1 and 2 merge in Quechua:** chiqaq covers both propositional truth and material genuineness without a dedicated separate term for the latter. All other languages maintain a clearer lexical separation.

**Dimensions 1 and 10 merge in Hebrew and Swahili:** Hebrew tsodek covers both "you're right" (epistemic) and "just" (moral); Swahili haki covers both "truth" (propositional) and "rights/justice" (moral/legal). This merger is not found in the other six languages.

**Dimensions 3 and 4 merge in Tagalog:** tapat covers both correctness/faithfulness in conduct and honesty in speech without a clear internal boundary. Other languages maintain a clearer separation between correctness evaluation and sincerity evaluation.

**Dimension 8 is grammaticalized in Quechua** (obligatory evidential suffixes -mi/-si/-cha) while expressed lexically or constructionally in all other languages. This is the most structurally distinctive feature of Quechua's truth domain organization.

**Dimension 14 is lexically differentiated in Sanskrit** (the pramāṇa system) to a degree not found in any other language. Other languages express epistemic source distinctions but do not generate a systematic technical vocabulary for multiple distinct source types.

**Dimension 16 is language-specific to Chinese and English** as a distinct elaborated cluster. The objectivity/subjectivity distinction exists conceptually in other languages but does not generate a dedicated semantic cluster within the truth domain.

**Dimension 17 is most elaborated in Swahili** (haki compounds and verbal collocations) and secondarily in Hebrew (ts-d-k root). In Swahili, this dimension uniquely merges with Dimension 1 in the single term haki.

**Chinese 假 uniquely merges Dimensions 2 and 5 with hypothetical framing:** the same morpheme covers material inauthenticity (假冒), deliberate falsehood (假话), and hypothetical/suppositional framing (假如, 假设). No other language in the corpus shows this three-way merger in a single morpheme.

**Greek ψευδο- prefix extends Dimension 5 into technical/scientific domains** (ψευδοεπιστήμη, ψευδοπόδιο) in a way not paralleled in other languages. The ορθο- prefix similarly extends Dimension 3 across physical, religious, linguistic, and logical domains.

**Tagalog tama shows a unique extension of Dimension 3 into sufficiency/cessation** (tama na = "that's enough"), creating a semantic overlap between correctness evaluation and discourse-level cessation marking not found in other languages.
