## OPPOSITION 1

**Term A:** benar  
**Term B:** salah  
**Transliteration:** benar / salah  
**Translation:** true, correct / wrong, incorrect  

**Description of contrast:** Primary lexical opposition for evaluating correctness of statements, actions, or judgments. "Benar" indicates correspondence to fact or standard; "salah" indicates deviation from fact or standard. This is the most productive and frequent opposition in the truth domain.

**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Lexical Oppositions (Direct Antonym Pairs)
The data shows **4 robust lexical oppositions** with dedicated antonym pairs:
1. benar/salah (true/wrong)
2. asli/palsu (genuine/fake)
3. jujur/bohong (honest/lie)
4. sah/batal (valid/void)

These represent the core lexicalized oppositions in the Indonesian truth domain.

### Morphological Oppositions (Negation Strategy)
The **dominant pattern** for creating oppositions is morphological negation with "tidak":
- This applies to virtually all truth-domain terms
- No lexical antonyms exist for: sama, mirip, cocok, tepat, akurat, wajar, pantas, valid, faktual, keliru, sungguh, absah
- Negation with "tidak" is the productive, default strategy

### Constructional Oppositions
Several oppositions are encoded constructionally rather than lexically:
- Hedged negation with "kurang" vs. direct assertion
- Binary evaluation frames "X atau Y"
- Evidential/epistemic contrasts (sebenarnya, ternyata)
- Emphatic affirmation/negation constructions

### Asymmetries and Gaps
- **Agent nominalization asymmetry**: Negative agents (pembohong, pemalsu, pendusta) are lexicalized; positive agents use descriptive phrases
- **Register stratification**: Some terms (dusta, sejati, hakikat) lack clear oppositions in their register
- **Semantic specialization**: Terms like tulen, khilaf show restricted domains without clear opposites
- **Borrowed terms**: valid, faktual, akurat follow Indonesian negation patterns but lack lexical antonyms

### Productivity Patterns
- Morphological negation with "tidak" is **fully productive**
- Lexical antonym pairs are **restricted to 4 core oppositions**
- Constructional oppositions show **moderate productivity** in specific pragmatic contexts
