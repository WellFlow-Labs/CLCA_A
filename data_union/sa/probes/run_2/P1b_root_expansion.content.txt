# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1

**Form:** सत् / *sat* (from root *as-*, to be)

**P1 items participating:** सत् (*sat*, item 6), असत् (*asat*, item 7), सत्य (*satya*, item 1), तत्त्व (*tattva*, item 28 — via the copular element *tat* + *-tva*, but more directly *sat* underlies the truth-as-being cluster)

**Count:** 3 direct participants (*sat*, *asat*, *satya*); *sat* is the etymological base of the most semantically central cluster in the domain.

**Core meaning contribution:** *Sat* contributes the equation of truth with being or existence — to be true is to be real, to exist. This root generates the largest and most philosophically loaded cluster in the Sanskrit truth-evaluation domain, spanning ontology, ethics, and epistemology.

---

### Root 2

**Form:** मा / *mā-* (root *mā-*, to measure)

**P1 items participating:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*, item 4), प्रमा (*pramā*, item 5)

**Count:** 2 direct participants, but the root is exceptionally productive in the epistemological subdomain, generating the entire *pramāṇa*-theory vocabulary across all Sanskrit philosophical schools.

**Core meaning contribution:** *Mā-* contributes the idea of truth as correct measurement or calibration — a cognition or standard is valid insofar as it accurately measures or delimits its object. This root generates the technical vocabulary of Sanskrit epistemology.

---

### Root 3

**Form:** अर्थ / *artha* (stem, not a verbal root; meaning: object, referent, purpose, meaning)

**P1 items participating:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*, item 3), परमार्थ (*paramārtha*, item 23), व्यावहारिक (*vyāvahārika*, item 24 — via *vyavahāra*, which is semantically paired with *paramārtha*)

**Count:** 2 direct participants (*yathārtha*, *paramārtha*); *artha* is the pivot of the correspondence-and-levels-of-truth cluster.

**Core meaning contribution:** *Artha* contributes the referential or objective pole of truth — truth is evaluated as the relation between a word, cognition, or claim and its object or intended meaning. This stem generates expressions that evaluate correspondence, appropriateness, and the distinction between levels of reality.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Expansion of Root 1: सत् / *sat*

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्ता
**Transliteration:** sattā
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "being-ness"
**Natural English translation:** existence, being, reality
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical literature (Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Vedānta) to denote the property of existence as such, the abstract quality of being real. Appears in debates about whether existence is a universal property or a specific relation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** सत्त्व
**Transliteration:** sattva
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *-tva* (abstract suffix); "the state of being, being-ness"
**Natural English translation:** being, existence, essence; also: the quality of goodness/clarity (in Sāṃkhya)
**Context of use:** In Sāṃkhya philosophy, *sattva* is the first of the three *guṇas*, associated with clarity, purity, and illumination. In general philosophical usage, it also denotes the quality of genuine existence or substantiality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** सत्यता
**Transliteration:** satyatā
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā* (abstract suffix); "the state of being true"
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, truth (as an abstract property)
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and ethical literature to denote the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from a particular true statement. Appears in discussions of the nature of truth as a property.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** सत्यत्व
**Transliteration:** satyatva
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *-tva* (abstract suffix); "truth-ness"
**Natural English translation:** the property of being true, truthfulness
**Context of use:** A more technical philosophical variant of *satyatā*, used in logical and epistemological discussions to refer to the property of truth as a formal category. Appears in Navya-Nyāya literature.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**5.**
**Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*); "one who speaks truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully
**Context of use:** Used in epic, Purāṇic, and legal literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks the truth. A standard epithet of virtuous characters in narrative literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** सत्यसंध
**Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* (bound by, pledged to, from root *dhā-*); "bound to truth"
**Natural English translation:** true to one's word, faithful to one's pledge
**Context of use:** Used in epic literature (Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa) as an epithet for heroes who keep their oaths and promises. Evaluates a person's reliability and integrity in fulfilling commitments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** सत्यप्रतिज्ञ
**Transliteration:** satyapratijna
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijñā* (promise, vow); "one whose vow is true"
**Natural English translation:** one who keeps true promises, faithful to one's vow
**Context of use:** An epithet in epic and Purāṇic literature for a person whose pledges are always honored. Closely related to *satyasaṃdha* and used in similar narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
**Form:** सत्याग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyāgraha
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence, from root *grah-*); "holding firmly to truth"
**Natural English translation:** insistence on truth, truth-force
**Context of use:** Coined or systematized by Mohandas Gandhi to denote nonviolent resistance grounded in the force of truth. Used in political, ethical, and historical discourse in modern Sanskrit and in discussions of Gandhi's philosophy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** सत्यनारायण
**Transliteration:** satyānārāyaṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *Nārāyaṇa* (a name of Viṣṇu); "the true/real Nārāyaṇa"
**Natural English translation:** Satyanārāyaṇa (a form of Viṣṇu worshipped as the embodiment of truth)
**Context of use:** A devotional and ritual name used in the *Satyanārāyaṇa vrata* (a widely performed Hindu votive rite). The name characterizes the deity as the ultimate reality or truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** सत्यलोक
**Transliteration:** satyaloka
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *loka* (world, realm); "the realm of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the world of truth, the highest cosmic realm
**Context of use:** In Purāṇic cosmology, *Satyaloka* is the highest of the cosmic realms, identified with the abode of Brahmā and associated with ultimate reality. Used in cosmological and devotional literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** सत्ययुग
**Transliteration:** satyayuga
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *yuga* (age, era); "the age of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the age of truth, the golden age
**Context of use:** In Purāṇic chronology, the first and most virtuous of the four cosmic ages (*yugas*), characterized by universal adherence to truth and righteousness. Used in cosmological, ethical, and nostalgic literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *āpana* (causing to obtain, from causative of root *āp-*); "causing truth to be established"
**Natural English translation:** verification, confirmation, attestation
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to denote the act of verifying or confirming the truth of a statement or document. Also used in modern Sanskrit-influenced administrative language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** सत्यापित
**Transliteration:** satyāpita
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *āpita* (past passive participle of causative of *āp-*); "caused to be true, verified"
**Natural English translation:** verified, confirmed, attested
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts as the past participle form corresponding to *satyāpana*. Appears on official documents to indicate that a statement has been verified.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** सत्यम् शिवम् सुन्दरम्
**Transliteration:** satyam śivam sundaram
**Literal breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, neuter accusative) + *śivam* (auspiciousness, goodness) + *sundaram* (beauty); "truth, goodness, beauty"
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, beauty (a triad of ultimate values)
**Context of use:** A well-known philosophical and aesthetic formula identifying the three highest values. Used in philosophical, devotional, and aesthetic discourse; also the title of a well-known Hindi film. Corresponds loosely to the Western *verum, bonum, pulchrum*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** सत्यमेव जयते
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**Literal breakdown:** *satyam* (truth) + *eva* (indeed, alone) + *jayate* (conquers, from root *ji-*); "truth alone conquers"
**Natural English translation:** truth alone conquers
**Context of use:** A phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. Used in philosophical, political, and ceremonial contexts to assert the ultimate triumph of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** सत्पुरुष
**Transliteration:** sat-puruṣa
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *puruṣa* (man, person); "a real/genuine man"
**Natural English translation:** a good man, a genuine person, a man of integrity
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and narrative literature to characterize a person of genuine virtue and integrity. The *sat-* prefix marks authenticity and moral worth rather than mere appearance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** सत्कार
**Transliteration:** satkāra
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *kāra* (doing, making, from root *kṛ-*); "doing what is good/right"
**Natural English translation:** hospitality, honor, respectful treatment, good reception
**Context of use:** Used in social and ethical contexts to denote the proper and respectful treatment of a guest, elder, or honored person. Common in narrative and prescriptive literature describing social norms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** सत्कर्मन्
**Transliteration:** sat-karman
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *karman* (action, deed); "right/good action"
**Natural English translation:** good deed, right action, virtuous conduct
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to denote actions that are morally correct and in accordance with *dharma*. Contrasted with *asat-karman* (wrong or evil action).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** सत्संग
**Transliteration:** satsaṃga
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *saṃga* (company, association, from root *sañj-*); "company of the good/real"
**Natural English translation:** association with the good, company of the virtuous, spiritual fellowship
**Context of use:** Used in devotional and religious literature to denote the practice of associating with virtuous or spiritually realized persons. A central concept in bhakti traditions; also the name of many religious gatherings.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** असत्य
**Transliteration:** asatya
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *satya*; "not true"
**Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood, lie
**Context of use:** Used across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as the direct negation of *satya*. Denotes a false statement or the quality of being untrue. Common in moral prescriptions against lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** असत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** asatyavādin
**Literal breakdown:** *asatya* + *vādin* (speaker); "one who speaks untruth"
**Natural English translation:** liar, one who speaks falsely
**Context of use:** Used in legal and ethical literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks falsehoods. Appears in legal texts as a category of unreliable witness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** सदसत्
**Transliteration:** sadasat
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *asat*; "being and non-being"
**Natural English translation:** the real and the unreal, being and non-being, the existent and the non-existent
**Context of use:** A dvandva compound used in philosophical literature to denote the totality of existence or to pose the question of the ontological status of the world. Appears in Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic discussions of the nature of reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** सन्मार्ग
**Transliteration:** sanmārga
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi form *san-*) + *mārga* (path, way); "the right/true path"
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the path of virtue
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and devotional literature to denote the path of correct conduct and virtue. Contrasted with *kumārga* (wrong path). Common in prescriptive and narrative texts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** सच्चिदानन्द
**Transliteration:** saccidānanda
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (being) + *cit* (consciousness) + *ānanda* (bliss); "being-consciousness-bliss"
**Natural English translation:** being-consciousness-bliss (a characterization of ultimate reality in Advaita Vedānta)
**Context of use:** A central formula in Advaita Vedānta and related traditions characterizing the nature of *Brahman* (ultimate reality) as the unity of pure being, pure consciousness, and pure bliss. Used in philosophical and devotional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** सत्प्रतिपक्ष
**Transliteration:** satpratipakṣa
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *pratipakṣa* (counter-position, from *prati-* + *pakṣa*); "a genuine counter-position"
**Natural English translation:** a real counter-argument, a genuine opposing thesis
**Context of use:** Used in Navya-Nyāya logical literature to denote a counter-argument that genuinely undermines a thesis, as opposed to a merely apparent or fallacious objection.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**26.**
**Form:** सत्कविता
**Transliteration:** sat-kavitā
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *kavitā* (poetry); "genuine/good poetry"
**Natural English translation:** genuine poetry, good poetry
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic and literary critical contexts to evaluate poetry as genuinely excellent or authentic, as opposed to mediocre or imitative verse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**27.**
**Form:** असत्कल्पना
**Transliteration:** asat-kalpanā
**Literal breakdown:** *asat* + *kalpanā* (imagination, construction, from root *kḷp-*); "construction of the unreal"
**Natural English translation:** fabrication of the non-existent, false imagination
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical literature to characterize the cognitive act of constructing or imagining something that has no real basis. Appears in Vedāntic and Buddhist discussions of erroneous cognition.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**28.**
**Form:** असत्ख्याति
**Transliteration:** asatkhyāti
**Literal breakdown:** *asat* + *khyāti* (cognition, appearance, from root *khyā-*); "appearance of the non-existent"
**Natural English translation:** the theory that erroneous cognition presents the non-existent as real
**Context of use:** A technical term in Sanskrit epistemological debate about the nature of error (*khyātivāda*). The *asatkhyāti* theory holds that in erroneous cognition, a non-existent object is presented as real. Associated with the Mādhyamika Buddhist position in this debate.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** सत्यशोधन
**Transliteration:** satyaśodhana
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *śodhana* (purification, investigation, from root *śudh-*); "investigation/purification of truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-finding, investigation of truth, truth inquiry
**Context of use:** Used in modern Sanskrit-influenced discourse (including Gandhi's autobiography, *Satya-śodhana* / *My Experiments with Truth*) to denote the systematic inquiry into or pursuit of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** सत्यभामा
**Transliteration:** satyabhāmā
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *bhāmā* (splendor, radiance); "she whose splendor is true/real"
**Natural English translation:** Satyabhāmā (a proper name; one of Kṛṣṇa's wives in the Mahābhārata)
**Context of use:** A proper name in epic literature. The *satya-* element marks genuine or true radiance, functioning as an honorific qualifier in the name.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

*Note: The root *sat-/satya-* is highly productive; additional compounds exist (e.g., *satyaśīla*, *satyavrata*, *satyabhāṣaṇa*, *satyagrāhin*, *satyaparāyaṇa*). The 30 items above represent the most common and well-attested forms.*

### Expansion of Root 2: मा / *mā-* (to measure)

---

**1.**
**Form:** मान
**Transliteration:** māna
**Literal breakdown:** root *mā-* + action-noun suffix *-na*; "measuring, measure"
**Natural English translation:** measure, standard, respect, honor
**Context of use:** Used in multiple domains: as a measure or standard in technical and mathematical contexts; as honor or respect in social and ethical contexts. The semantic range reflects the idea that proper measure confers worth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** प्रमातृ
**Transliteration:** pramātṛ
**Literal breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* + agent suffix *-tṛ*; "one who correctly measures"
**Natural English translation:** the knower, the subject of valid cognition
**Context of use:** A technical epistemological term denoting the knowing subject — the one who employs a *pramāṇa* to arrive at *pramā*. Part of the standard triadic analysis: *pramātṛ* (knower), *pramāṇa* (means), *prameya* (object).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** प्रमेय
**Transliteration:** prameya
**Literal breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* + gerundive suffix *-ya*; "that which is to be correctly measured"
**Natural English translation:** the object of valid cognition, the knowable
**Context of use:** A technical epistemological term denoting the object that is apprehended by a valid cognition (*pramā*). In Nyāya, the *prameyas* are the sixteen categories of inquiry. Part of the standard triadic analysis.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** अप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** apramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramāṇa*; "not a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** invalid means of cognition, non-authority, lacking evidential force
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to characterize a source or means of cognition that fails to yield valid knowledge. Also used in legal contexts to denote evidence that lacks authority or probative force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** प्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ya* (adjectival/abstract suffix); "the property of being a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the quality of being a *pramāṇa*
**Context of use:** A central technical term in Sanskrit epistemology denoting the property of validity or authority that a cognition or source possesses. Debates about *prāmāṇya* (whether validity is intrinsic or extrinsic — *svataḥ* or *parataḥ*) are a major topic in Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** svataḥprāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *svataḥ* (from itself, intrinsically) + *prāmāṇya*; "intrinsic validity"
**Natural English translation:** intrinsic validity, self-certifying authority
**Context of use:** The Mīmāṃsā doctrine that the validity of a cognition is established by the cognition itself, without requiring external verification. A central position in Sanskrit epistemological debate.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** परतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** parataḥprāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *parataḥ* (from another, extrinsically) + *prāmāṇya*; "extrinsic validity"
**Natural English translation:** extrinsic validity, externally established authority
**Context of use:** The Nyāya doctrine that the validity of a cognition must be established by an external means, not by the cognition itself. The opposing position to *svataḥprāmāṇya* in Sanskrit epistemological debate.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** अप्रमा
**Transliteration:** apramā
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramā*; "not valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** erroneous cognition, invalid apprehension
**Context of use:** A technical epistemological term denoting a cognition that fails to accurately apprehend its object. Contrasted with *pramā* (valid cognition). Includes *bhrama* (error), *saṃśaya* (doubt), and *tarka* (hypothetical reasoning) in some classifications.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** प्रत्यक्षप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** pratyakṣapramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *pratyakṣa* (perception, direct sense-contact) + *pramāṇa*; "perception as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** perceptual evidence, the *pramāṇa* of direct perception
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to denote direct sensory perception as one of the primary means of valid cognition. Accepted by virtually all Sanskrit philosophical schools as a *pramāṇa*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** अनुमानप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** anumānapramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *anumāna* (inference, from *anu-* + *mā-*) + *pramāṇa*; "inference as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** inferential evidence, the *pramāṇa* of inference
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to denote inference as a means of valid cognition. Accepted by all major Sanskrit philosophical schools. The compound *anumāna* itself contains the root *mā-* (measuring after/following evidence).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** अनुमान
**Transliteration:** anumāna
**Literal breakdown:** *anu-* (after, following) + *mā-* + *-na*; "measuring after, inferring"
**Natural English translation:** inference, reasoning from evidence
**Context of use:** A central epistemological term denoting the process of inferring a conclusion from a reason (*hetu*) and an example (*dṛṣṭānta*). Used across all Sanskrit philosophical schools and in legal reasoning.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** शब्दप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** śabdapramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *śabda* (word, verbal testimony) + *pramāṇa*; "verbal testimony as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** testimonial evidence, the *pramāṇa* of verbal testimony
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to denote verbal testimony (especially Vedic testimony in Mīmāṃsā) as a means of valid cognition. Accepted by most schools; debated as to its scope and reliability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** उपमानप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** upamānapramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *upamāna* (comparison, analogy, from *upa-* + *mā-*) + *pramāṇa*; "comparison as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** analogical evidence, the *pramāṇa* of comparison
**Context of use:** Used in Nyāya epistemology to denote comparison or analogy as a distinct means of valid cognition. The compound *upamāna* itself contains the root *mā-*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** उपमान
**Transliteration:** upamāna
**Literal breakdown:** *upa-* (near, toward) + *mā-* + *-na*; "measuring toward, comparing"
**Natural English translation:** comparison, analogy, the standard of comparison
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature as a *pramāṇa* (Nyāya) and in aesthetic and grammatical literature to denote the standard or vehicle of a comparison. A central term in Sanskrit poetics (*alaṃkāraśāstra*) for the comparandum in a simile.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** अर्थापत्तिप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** arthāpattipramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *arthāpatti* (presumption, postulation) + *pramāṇa*; "postulation as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** the *pramāṇa* of postulation or presumption
**Context of use:** Used in Mīmāṃsā and Advaita Vedānta epistemology to denote *arthāpatti* (postulation of an unobserved fact to explain an observed one) as a distinct *pramāṇa*.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**16.**
**Form:** अभावप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** abhāvapramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *abhāva* (absence, non-existence) + *pramāṇa*; "absence as a valid means of cognition"
**Natural English translation:** the *pramāṇa* of non-perception or absence
**Context of use:** Used in Mīmāṃsā and Advaita Vedānta epistemology to denote the cognition of absence (*anupalabdhi* or *abhāva*) as a distinct means of valid knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**17.**
**Form:** प्रमाणशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇaśāstra
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *śāstra* (treatise, science); "the science of valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** epistemology, the science of valid means of knowledge
**Context of use:** Used to denote the branch of Sanskrit philosophy concerned with the theory of valid cognition. Particularly associated with Buddhist epistemology (Dignāga, Dharmakīrti) and Nyāya.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** प्रमाणवार्तिक
**Transliteration:** pramāṇavārtika
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *vārtika* (commentary, gloss); "commentary on valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** Pramāṇavārttika (title of Dharmakīrti's major epistemological work)
**Context of use:** The title of Dharmakīrti's foundational Buddhist epistemological treatise. Used in scholarly and philosophical contexts to refer to this specific text and its tradition.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** प्रमाणसमुच्चय
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasamuccaya
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *samuccaya* (collection, compendium); "compendium of valid cognition"
**Natural English translation:** Pramāṇasamuccaya (title of Dignāga's foundational epistemological work)

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where the core truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other domains. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

### From Root 1: सत् / *sat*

- **सत्कार** (*satkāra*): The *sat-* element, which in the truth domain marks genuine existence or authenticity, here functions in a context of social hospitality and honor.

- **सत्संग** (*satsaṃga*): The *sat-* element marks association with the virtuous; the expression is used primarily in devotional and communal religious contexts rather than in truth-evaluation contexts.

- **सत्त्व** (*sattva*): In Sāṃkhya, *sattva* is one of three fundamental constituents of matter (*guṇas*), associated with luminosity and clarity; the connection to truth or being is present but the term functions primarily as a cosmological and psychological category.

- **सत्यलोक** (*satyaloka*): The *satya-* element marks the highest cosmic realm in Purāṇic cosmology; the expression functions in a cosmographical rather than epistemological context.

- **सत्ययुग** (*satyayuga*): The *satya-* element marks a temporal era characterized by virtue; the expression functions in a chronological and moral-historical context.

- **सत्यभामा** (*satyabhāmā*): The *satya-* element functions as an honorific qualifier in a proper name, with no direct epistemological or truth-evaluative function.

- **सत्कविता** (*sat-kavitā*): The *sat-* element marks aesthetic quality in poetry; the expression functions in a literary-evaluative context.

- **सच्चिदानन्द** (*saccidānanda*): The *sat* element is one of three terms in a theological formula characterizing ultimate reality; it functions as a metaphysical characterization rather than a truth-evaluative predicate.

---

### From Root 2: मा / *mā-*

- **मान** (*māna*) in the sense of honor/respect: The root *mā-* (to measure) generates *māna* in the sense of social honor or esteem, where the measuring function has shifted into a domain of social valuation.

- **सम्मान** (*sammāna*) and **अवमान** (*avamāna*): These expressions use the *māna* stem in contexts of social honor and dishonor, where the measuring function operates in a social-evaluative rather than epistemological domain.

- **निर्मान/निर्माण** (*nirmāṇa*): The root *mā-* (to measure/fashion) here generates a term for creation or construction, including the Buddhist *nirmāṇakāya* (a supernaturally fashioned body); the measuring function has shifted into a domain of creative production.

- **उपमान** (*upamāna*) in poetics: While *upamāna* is an epistemological *pramāṇa*, it also functions as a technical term in Sanskrit poetics for the standard of comparison in a simile, operating in an aesthetic rather than epistemological context.

- **परिमाण** (*parimāṇa*): The root *mā-* generates a term for physical quantity or magnitude, functioning in mathematical and natural-philosophical contexts rather than epistemological ones.

---

### From Root 3: अर्थ / *artha*

- **अर्थशास्त्र** (*arthaśāstra*): The *artha* element here carries the sense of "wealth" and "statecraft" rather than "meaning" or "referent"; the expression functions in a political-economic domain.

- **अनर्थ** (*anartha*) in the sense of harm or misfortune: The *artha* element, which in the truth domain marks meaning or referent, here generates a term for harm or calamity through the "purpose/benefit" sense of *artha*.

- **अर्थालंकार** (*arthālaṃkāra*): The *artha* element marks a category of figures of speech in Sanskrit poetics; the expression functions in an aesthetic-literary domain.

- **अर्थान्तरन्यास** (*arthāntaranyāsa*): A technical term in Sanskrit poetics for a specific figure of speech; the *artha* element functions in a literary-rhetorical rather than epistemological context.

- **अर्थक्रिया** (*arthakriyā*): The *artha* element here carries the sense of "purposive action" or "causal efficacy" rather than "meaning"; the expression functions as a criterion of ontological reality in Buddhist philosophy.

- **अर्थसिद्धि** (*arthasiddhi*): The *artha* element carries the sense of "purpose" or "goal" in addition to "meaning"; the expression functions in contexts of practical achievement and aesthetic success as well as semantic accomplishment.

- **सार्थक** (*sārthaka*) and **निरर्थक** (*nirarthaka*): These expressions evaluate meaningfulness and purposefulness; in everyday usage they frequently function as general evaluative terms (worthwhile / pointless) without specific epistemological content.

- **अर्थवाद** (*arthavāda*): In Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics, this term functions as a category of Vedic textual genre (laudatory or explanatory passages) rather than as a truth-evaluative predicate; the *artha* element marks the function of such passages in relation to injunctions.
