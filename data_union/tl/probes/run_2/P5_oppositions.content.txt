# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

The oppositions below are extracted directly from contrasts attested in P1–P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit statements in the context data. Where the context notes asymmetry, partial overlap, or register difference between terms, this is preserved in the description. No oppositions have been constructed by inference beyond what the data states.

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** totoo
2. **Term B:** hindi totoo
3. **Transliteration:** totoo / hindi totoo
4. **English translation:** true / not true, false
5. **Description of contrast:** *Totoo* is the primary predicate for asserting that a statement corresponds to reality; *hindi totoo* is its standard negation, formed by prefixing the negator *hindi*, and is described in P2b as the standard formula for denying the truth of a proposition or the reality of a situation.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** totoo
2. **Term B:** kasinungalingan
3. **Transliteration:** totoo / kasinungalingan
4. **English translation:** true / lie, falsehood, untruth
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *kasinungalingan* "contrasts directly with *katotohanan* (truth)"; by extension, *totoo* (the root of *katotohanan*) and *kasinungalingan* occupy opposite poles of the truth/falsehood axis. *Totoo* asserts correspondence with reality; *kasinungalingan* denotes a false and deceptive statement.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** katotohanan
2. **Term B:** kasinungalingan
3. **Transliteration:** katotohanan / kasinungalingan
4. **English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood, untruth
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *kasinungalingan* "contrasts directly with *katotohanan*." Both are abstract nouns formed by the *ka-…-an* circumfix; *katotohanan* denotes the abstract quality or instance of truth, *kasinungalingan* denotes the abstract quality or instance of lying or falsehood.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** tama
2. **Term B:** mali
3. **Transliteration:** tama / mali
4. **English translation:** correct, right / wrong, incorrect, mistaken
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *mali* "is the primary antonym of *tama*." Both are used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments; *tama* asserts correctness and *mali* asserts incorrectness or wrongness across factual, moral, and procedural domains.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** tunay
2. **Term B:** huwad
3. **Transliteration:** tunay / huwad
4. **English translation:** genuine, real, authentic / fake, counterfeit, false
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *tunay* "contrasts with *huwad* (fake)." *Tunay* evaluates something as authentically what it is; *huwad* denotes something that falsely presents itself as genuine. Both are used in legal and evaluative contexts, though *huwad* is described as more formal or literary.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** tunay
2. **Term B:** peke
3. **Transliteration:** tunay / peke
4. **English translation:** genuine, real, authentic / fake, counterfeit, phony
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *tunay* "contrasts with… *peke* (counterfeit)." *Tunay* and *peke* occupy the same genuine/fake axis as *tunay*/*huwad* (Opposition 5), but *peke* is colloquial while *tunay* is neutral across registers. P1 notes *peke* is "more informal" than *huwad*.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** huwad
2. **Term B:** peke
3. **Transliteration:** huwad / peke
4. **English translation:** fake, counterfeit, false (formal/literary) / fake, counterfeit, phony (colloquial)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *huwad* as "somewhat more formal or literary than *peke* in contemporary usage." Both denote inauthenticity or counterfeiting, but they differ in register rather than in core meaning. P3 explicitly notes: "*huwad* is formal/literary, *peke* is colloquial."
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** tapat
2. **Term B:** sinungaling
3. **Transliteration:** tapat / sinungaling
4. **English translation:** honest, sincere, straightforward / liar, lying, false (of a person)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *tapat* as evaluating persons or statements as "honest and without concealment" and *sinungaling* as "the standard term for a person who lies." These terms occupy opposite poles in the evaluation of personal honesty in speech, though no single passage in the context states the contrast as explicitly as the *tama*/*mali* or *tunay*/*huwad* pairs.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** may katotohanan
2. **Term B:** walang katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** may katotohanan / walang katotohanan
4. **English translation:** there is truth in it, partly true / without truth, having no truth, baseless
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both constructions as fixed evaluative formulas: *may katotohanan* is used "to concede partial truth in a claim" and *walang katotohanan* is used "to characterize a claim, rumor, or statement as entirely without factual basis." The contrast is between the existential *may* (presence) and *wala* (absence) applied to the same nominal.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 10

1. **Term A:** may katunayan
2. **Term B:** walang katunayan
3. **Transliteration:** may katunayan / walang katunayan
4. **English translation:** with proof, supported by evidence / without proof, unproven, lacking evidence
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as fixed formulas in legal and argumentative discourse: *may katunayan* asserts evidentiary support and *walang katunayan* asserts the absence of evidentiary support. The structural contrast is the same *may*/*wala* opposition applied to *katunayan* (proof/evidence).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** may pruweba
2. **Term B:** walang pruweba
3. **Transliteration:** may pruweba / walang pruweba
4. **English translation:** there is proof / there is no proof
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents *Walang pruweba* as "the standard formula for asserting the absence of proof or evidence" and *May pruweba ka ba?* as "the standard formula for demanding evidence." The same *may*/*wala* opposition applies to the Spanish-derived *pruweba* as to the native *katunayan* (Opposition 10).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** may tiwala
2. **Term B:** wala(ng) tiwala / kawalan ng tiwala
3. **Transliteration:** may tiwala / walang tiwala / kawalan ng tiwala
4. **English translation:** trust, confidence (present) / no trust, distrust, loss of confidence
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents *May tiwala ako sa kanya* as the standard formula for expressing trust and *Wala akong tiwala sa kanya* as the standard formula for expressing distrust. P2a additionally documents *kawalan ng tiwala* as the nominalized form meaning "distrust / lack of trust / loss of confidence." The *may*/*wala* opposition is again the structural mechanism, with *kawalan ng tiwala* as a lexicalized nominal for the negative pole.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 13

1. **Term A:** napatunayan
2. **Term B:** hindi mapatunayan
3. **Transliteration:** napatunayan / hindi mapatunayan
4. **English translation:** has been proven, was verified / cannot be proven, is unprovable
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents *napatunayan* as the completed-aspect form meaning "has been successfully proven or verified" and *mapatunayan* as the potential form; P2b cites *Hindi mapatunayan* ("cannot be proven") as an attested formula. The contrast is between achieved proof and the impossibility or failure of proof, expressed through aspect and negation on the same verbal stem.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** sigurado
2. **Term B:** hindi sigurado
3. **Transliteration:** sigurado / hindi sigurado
4. **English translation:** sure, certain / not sure, uncertain
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents *Sigurado ka ba?* ("Are you sure?") and *Hindi ako sigurado* ("I'm not sure / I'm not certain") as standard formulas. The latter is described as "the standard formula for hedging or admitting uncertainty across all registers." The contrast is between epistemic certainty and its negation.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

1. **Term A:** tama
2. **Term B:** tama na
3. **Transliteration:** tama / tama na
4. **English translation:** correct, right / that's enough, stop
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents *Tama na* as having "a pragmatically specialized meaning distinct from simple correctness-predication," signaling that a sufficient point has been reached and further action or speech should cease. P3 notes this construction as "sufficiently divergent from correctness-evaluation" that it does not fit cleanly into the correctness group. The contrast is between the base correctness predication and a pragmatically reanalyzed construction with the particle *na*.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** akma
2. **Term B:** angkop
3. **Transliteration:** akma / angkop
4. **English translation:** fitting, accurate (of a match or physical fit) / appropriate, suitable (normative appropriateness)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *akma* as centering on whether something "corresponds correctly to a standard, requirement, or other item" with a physical or descriptive fit sense, while *angkop* "tends more toward normative appropriateness than physical or descriptive fit." P3 restates: "*akma* tends toward physical or descriptive fit… while *angkop* tends toward normative appropriateness." The two terms overlap but are distinguished by the type of correspondence evaluated.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** tama
2. **Term B:** wasto
3. **Transliteration:** tama / wasto
4. **English translation:** correct, right (neutral) / correct, proper (with emphasis on propriety and conformity to standard)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *wasto* "overlaps with *tama* in meaning 'correct' but tends to emphasize propriety, appropriateness, or conformity to a standard." P3 restates: "*tama* is more neutral in its correctness evaluation" while "*wasto* carries a stronger sense of propriety and conformity to standard." The contrast is one of semantic nuance within the correctness domain rather than opposition.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** tumpak
2. **Term B:** tama
3. **Transliteration:** tumpak / tama
4. **English translation:** precise, exact, spot-on / correct, right
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *tumpak* "is stronger than *tama* in implying exactness rather than mere correctness." P3 restates: "*tumpak* is stronger than both [*akma* and *angkop*], implying exactness rather than mere fit or appropriateness." The contrast is one of degree and specificity: *tama* asserts correctness, *tumpak* asserts exactness.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** katunayan
2. **Term B:** pruweba
3. **Transliteration:** katunayan / pruweba
4. **English translation:** proof, evidence, genuineness (native form) / proof, evidence (Spanish borrowing)
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 states: "*katunayan* is morphologically native (from *tunay*) and also functions as a discourse connector (*sa katunayan*), while *pruweba* is a Spanish borrowing with no discourse-connector use." Both denote proof or evidence, but *katunayan* has a broader functional range including the grammaticalized discourse connector *sa katunayan*, which *pruweba* lacks.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** pruweba
2. **Term B:** ebidensya
3. **Transliteration:** pruweba / ebidensya
4. **English translation:** proof, evidence (general) / evidence (specifically legal-procedural)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *ebidensya* "is more specifically legal-procedural in connotation than *pruweba* and is the expected term in courtroom and investigative discourse." P3 restates: "*ebidensya* is more narrowly legal-procedural than *pruweba*." Both denote evidence, but *ebidensya* is register-restricted to legal-procedural contexts while *pruweba* is used more broadly.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** kredible
2. **Term B:** mapagkakatiwalaan
3. **Transliteration:** kredible / mapagkakatiwalaan
4. **English translation:** credible, believable (formal/journalistic borrowing) / trustworthy, reliable, dependable (native form)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *kredible* "is a relatively recent borrowing" more common in "written and broadcast media" and "occupies a more specifically epistemic niche," while *mapagkakatiwalaan* is the native alternative. P3 restates: "*kredible* is more formal and specifically epistemic… while *mapagkakatiwalaan* is more broadly applicable and morphologically native." Both evaluate trustworthiness but differ in register and morphological origin.

## Opposition 22

1. **Term A:** tunay
2. **Term B:** awtentiko
3. **Transliteration:** tunay / awtentiko
4. **English translation:** genuine, real, authentic (broad use) / authentic, genuine (formal/legal, narrower)

P3 notes *awtentiko* appears "primarily in legal certification language." Both evaluate genuineness, but *awtentiko* is register-restricted and narrower in distribution.

## Opposition 23

1. **Term A:** orihinal
2. **Term B:** awtentiko
3. **Transliteration:** orihinal / awtentiko
4. **English translation:** original (as opposed to copy) / authentic (as opposed to fraudulent)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *orihinal* "coexists with *tunay* but specifically foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction," while *awtentiko* foregrounds "the genuine-versus-fraudulent distinction." P3 restates this: "*orihinal* foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction while *awtentiko* foregrounds the genuine-versus-fraudulent distinction." Both concern authenticity but along different axes.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

1. **Term A:** katapatan (honesty/sincerity in speech)
2. **Term B:** katapatan (faithfulness/loyalty in relationships)
3. **Transliteration:** katapatan / katapatan
4. **English translation:** honesty, sincerity / faithfulness, loyalty
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *katapatan* covers "both the epistemic sense (honesty in speech) and the relational-moral sense (faithfulness, loyalty)" and that "its semantic range is broader than English 'honesty' alone." P3 notes the two dimensions are "not separated in Tagalog usage." This is an internal semantic contrast within a single form rather than an opposition between two forms; it is included because the context explicitly identifies the two distinct senses as co-present.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

1. **Term A:** *i-* causative (itama, iwasto, iakma)
2. **Term B:** *pa-…-an* causative (patunayan)
3. **Transliteration:** itama / iwasto / iakma vs. patunayan
4. **English translation:** to correct / to set right / to make fit vs. to prove to someone / to cause to be verified
5. **Description of contrast:** P4a explicitly states that the *i-* causative "is productive for correctness adjectives (*tama*, *wasto*, *akma*) but is **not attested** for truth-predicate adjectives (*totoo*, *tunay*)," for which the causative is formed by the distinct pattern *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* → *patunayan*. The two causative patterns are morphologically distinct and apply to non-overlapping subsets of the semantic field.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

1. **Term A:** *ka-…-an* nominalization (katotohanan, kamalian, katapatan…)
2. **Term B:** *pag-* action nominal (pagpapatunay, pagsisinungaling, pagwawasto…)
3. **Transliteration:** katotohanan / pagpapatunay (representative examples)
4. **English translation:** truth / the act of verifying (representative examples)
5. **Description of contrast:** P4a documents two distinct nominalization patterns: the *ka-…-an* circumfix produces "abstract nouns denoting the state, quality, or instance of the root meaning," while *pag-* + verbal stem produces "action nominals" denoting the act or event. P4b confirms these are "distinct pattern[s] with a different semantic output."
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 27

1. **Term A:** sinungaling (agentive: liar)
2. **Term B:** (no parallel single-word agentive for truth-teller)
3. **Transliteration:** sinungaling / —
4. **English translation:** liar / (no lexicalized equivalent)
5. **Description of contrast:** P4a explicitly notes that "no parallel lexicalized agentive for 'truth-teller'… is attested in the data" and that the concept is expressed periphrastically (*ang nagsasabi ng katotohanan*). The opposition is asymmetric: the negative pole (liar) is lexicalized as a single stable word; the positive pole (truth-teller) is not.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 28

1. **Term A:** makatotohanan / totoo sa buhay
2. **Term B:** totoo (propositional)
3. **Transliteration:** makatotohanan / totoo sa buhay vs. totoo
4. **English translation:** realistic, true-to-life (representational/aesthetic) / true (propositional)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 states that *makatotohanan* is "less common in purely propositional contexts than *totoo*" and is applied to "literary, cinematic, and artistic works." P3 places *makatotohanan* and *totoo sa buhay* in Group 10 (accuracy of fit/correspondence) rather than Group 1 (propositional truth-assertion), explicitly distinguishing their "primary use" as "aesthetic/representational evaluation rather than propositional truth-assertion." Both derive from the *totoo* root but apply to different types of evaluation.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

1. **Term A:** sa katotohanan (discourse connector)
2. **Term B:** katotohanan (abstract nominal)
3. **Transliteration:** sa katotohanan / katotohanan
4. **English translation:** in truth, actually (discourse marker) / truth, the truth (abstract noun)
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents *sa katotohanan* as having a "pragmatic function as a sentence-initial or clause-initial marker" that "is distinct from its compositional meaning." P4a describes it as a "grammaticalized discourse connector" whose "pragmatic force… is not fully predictable from their compositional meanings." The same surface material functions either as a compositional prepositional phrase or as a grammaticalized pragmatic marker depending on position and use.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 30

1. **Term A:** sa katunayan (discourse connector)
2. **Term B:** katunayan (abstract nominal: proof, evidence)
3. **Transliteration:** sa katunayan / katunayan
4. **English translation:** in fact, as a matter of fact (discourse marker) / proof, evidence, genuineness (abstract noun)
5. **Description of contrast:** The same pattern as Opposition 29, applied to *katunayan*. P2a states that the discourse-connector use of *sa katunayan* is "grammaticalized and distinct from the base nominal meaning of 'proof/genuineness.'" P4a groups both *sa katotohanan* and *sa katunayan* under the same grammaticalized discourse connector construction.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
