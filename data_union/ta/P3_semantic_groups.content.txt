# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which items appear together in the same constructions, which items are contrasted with each other, which items share typical contexts of use (legal, moral instruction, everyday speech, formal/literary, administrative), and which items participate in the same productive patterns. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented. Where usage patterns are ambiguous or overlapping, this is stated explicitly.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality

**Label:** Asserting and denying factual correspondence / truth-as-correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where a speaker asserts, affirms, questions, or denies whether a claim, statement, or situation matches what is actually the case. They appear in predicative constructions (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*, *atu uṇmaiyā?*), in adverbial frames that contrast appearance with reality (*uṇmaiyil*, *uṇmaiyilēyē*), and in fixed evaluative phrases used to characterize statements as factually accurate or inaccurate. The core contrast operative in this group is between what is claimed or appears to be the case and what actually is the case. The central contrast is between *uṇmai* and *poy*, which function as direct antonyms across a wide range of registers.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை / *uṇmai* — the primary nominal for truth/what is actually the case; used as predicate (*atu uṇmai*), as object (*uṇmai col*, *uṇmai pēcu*), and as subject (*uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu*)
- பொய் / *poy* — the primary nominal for falsehood/what is not the case; appears in direct contrast with *uṇmai* and in parallel constructions (*poy collāte*, *poy pēcukiṟāṉ*)
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — adjectival: true, genuine, real; applied to propositions and states of affairs (*uṇmaiyāna takaḷ*, true information); contrasts with *poyyāna* in attributive position
- பொய்யான / *poyyāna* — adjectival: false, untrue; the direct antonym of *uṇmaiyāna*
- உண்மையாக / *uṇmaiyāka* — adverbial/predicative form; *itu uṇmaiyāka irukku* (this is genuinely the case)
- பொய்யாக / *poyyāka* — adverbial/predicative form; *poyyāka colluvāṉ* (he will speak falsely)
- உண்மையில் / *uṇmaiyil* — discourse marker/adverbial frame: in truth, actually; contrasting appearance with reality; sentence-frame adverbial introducing corrections or clarifications
- உண்மையிலேயே / *uṇmaiyilēyē* — emphatic variant of the above; truly, really; reinforcing the sincerity or factual accuracy of a claim
- மெய் / *mey* — classical/literary counterpart; used in philosophical and poetic assertion of truth (*meyyē colkiṟēṉ*, I am truly telling you); confined to literary, philosophical, and formal contexts
- மெய்ம்மை / *meymmai* — formal nominal variant of *mey* in the same assertive function; classical abstract nominal for truth/reality
- மெய்யான / *meyyāna* — attributive adjective parallel to *uṇmaiyāna* in literary registers
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmaitaṉmai* — formal: truthfulness, authenticity; used in formal contexts to refer to the property of being genuine (*ataṉ uṇmait-taṉmai cantēkattirku uriyatu*, its authenticity is open to doubt)
- பொய்மை / *poymai* — abstract nominal form of *poy*; formal: the quality of being false; appears in literary and philosophical contexts contrasting *uṇmai* and *poymai* as opposing qualities
- உண்மையற்ற / *uṇmaiyaṟṟa* — privative: lacking truth, false
- பொய்யில்லாத / *poyyillāta* — privative: without falsehood, truthful
- உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāna* — the negative pole; used in legal and formal contexts to assert that a statement does not correspond to reality
- சரி / *cari* — used predicatively (*atu caritāṉ*) to assert correctness or accuracy; overlaps with this group in its epistemic use; also functions as a discourse particle (see Group 8 overlap) and as an evaluator of correctness (see Group 8)
- தவறு / *tavaṟu* — used predicatively to assert that a claim or calculation is incorrect; overlaps with this group and with Group 8

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail)
- *itu poyyā illai uṇmaiyā?* (is this a lie or the truth?)
- *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟāl...* (the truth is that...)
- *itil uṇmai illai* (there is no truth in this)
- *poy colli ēmāṟṟiṉāṉ* (he deceived by lying)
- *meyyē vellum* (truth alone will prevail — literary)
- *atu uṇmaiyā?* (is that true?)
- *atu uṇmaitāṉ* (that is indeed true)
- *atu poyyillai* (that is not a lie)
- *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai* (it is not known what the truth is)

**Notes:** *uṇmai* and *mey* are near-synonyms in this function but differ in register: *uṇmai* is the default across all registers; *mey* is confined to literary, philosophical, and formal contexts. *cari* and *tavaṟu* overlap with this group when used to evaluate the accuracy of statements, but they also participate in Group 8 (correctness and accuracy in practical contexts). *meyyē vellum* also participates in Group 9 (moral instruction and proverbial discourse).

## Group 2: Expressions used to describe the disclosure or concealment of what is actually the case

**Label:** Truth coming to light / revelation of concealed facts

**Definition:** These expressions are used in narrative, journalistic, and legal contexts where the focus is on the movement of information between concealment and disclosure — truth coming to light, being suppressed, being sought, or being admitted. They cluster around verbs of motion (*veḷippaṭu*, *teriya va*), concealment (*maṟai*, *mūṭu*), and admission (*ottukkō*), and they presuppose a gap between what is actually the case and what is publicly known or acknowledged. They appear in intransitive constructions where truth is treated as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility, and in constructions describing the acts of hiding or revealing. This group is defined by a specific narrative and processual pattern rather than by a single root, and is distinct from Group 1 in that these expressions are not primarily about evaluating whether something is true, but about the social and epistemic process of truth becoming known or being hidden.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது / *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu* — the truth came out, the truth emerged
- உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது / *uṇmai teriya vantatu* — the truth became known, the truth came to light (slightly more colloquial)
- உண்மை வெளிப்படுதல் / *uṇmai veḷippaṭutal* — the coming to light of truth, revelation of truth (verbal noun)
- உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது / *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* — the truth came to light
- உண்மை தெரிய வருதல் / *uṇmai teriya varutal* — the truth becoming known (verbal noun)
- உண்மையை மறைத்தல் / *uṇmaiyai maṟaittal* — concealment of truth, suppression of facts (verbal noun; legal and journalistic)
- உண்மையை மறை / *uṇmaiyai maṟai* — hide the truth, conceal the truth
- உண்மை மறைக்கப்பட்டது / *uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu* — the truth was concealed (passive)
- உண்மையை மூடி மறைத்தார்கள் / *uṇmaiyai mūṭi maṟaittārkaḷ* — they covered up the truth (compound verb, emphasizing thoroughness)
- உண்மை தெரியும் வரை காத்திரு / *uṇmai teriyum varai kāttiru* — wait until the truth is known
- பொய் ஒரு நாள் வெளியே வரும் / *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum* — a lie will come out one day (also participates in Group 9)
- உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை / *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai* — it is not known what the truth is
- உண்மை தேடுதல் / *uṇmai tēṭutal* — seeking the truth (philosophical, journalistic, legal)
- உண்மையை அறிய வேண்டும் / *uṇmaiyai aṟiya vēṇṭum* — one must know the truth (deontic)
- உண்மையை எதிர்கொள்ள வேண்டும் / *uṇmaiyai etiṟkoḷḷa vēṇṭum* — one must face the truth
- உண்மையை ஒத்துக்கொண்டான் / *uṇmaiyai ottukkōṇṭāṉ* — he admitted the truth (implies prior concealment or denial)
- உண்மை நிலை என்ன? / *uṇmai nilai eṉṉa?* — what is the actual state of affairs? (presupposes gap between appearance and reality)
- உண்மை விளக்கம் / *uṇmai viḷakkam* — a true/accurate explanation (journalistic and administrative)
- நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam* — proof, demonstration (the act of establishing something conclusively; legal and formal; also participates in Group 5)
- நிரூபிக்கப்படாத / *nirūpikkap-paṭāta* — unproven (passive negative; legal)
- ஆதாரம் இல்லாமல் குற்றம் சாட்டினான் / *ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭiṉāṉ* — he accused without evidence

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 1 (truth-as-correspondence) in that the same root *uṇmai* is involved, but the usage pattern here is specifically narrative and processual rather than predicative. The intransitive constructions (*uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu*, *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum*) are particularly characteristic. *உண்மையை மறைத்தல்* represents the concealment pole of this pattern. *நிரூபணம்* participates in both this group and Group 5, reflecting genuine overlap in its usage contexts.

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate a person's character as honest or deceptive

**Label:** Evaluating persons and conduct for honesty and its absence

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate whether a person's character or conduct is straightforward and free of deception, as opposed to evaluating whether a particular statement is factually accurate. They appear most frequently in moral instruction, interpersonal evaluation, and political discourse. The root *nēr* (straight, direct) underlies the central terms in this group. They appear in character evaluations (*avar nērmaiyāna āḷ*, he/she is an honest person), moral instruction (*nērmaiyāka naṭa*, behave honestly), and condemnation of deceptive conduct. The focus is on the person as an agent rather than on a specific statement or object.

**Representative expressions:**

- நேர்மை / *nērmai* — the primary term for honesty as a character trait; used in *nērmaiyāna āḷ* (an honest person), *nērmaiyāka naṭa* (behave honestly), *nērmaiyē ciṟanta koḷkai* (honesty is the best policy)
- நேர்மையான / *nērmaiyāna* — adjectival form; applied to persons and conduct; extremely common
- நேர்மையாக / *nērmaiyāka* — adverbial form; used to describe manner of conduct
- நேர்மையற்ற / *nērmaiyaṟṟa* — negative/privative form; applied to persons or conduct lacking honesty; dishonest, lacking integrity
- நேர்மையில்லாத / *nērmaiyillāta* — negative form; applied to persons or conduct lacking honesty
- நேர்மையின்மை / *nērmayiṉmai* — dishonesty, absence of honesty (abstract nominal; formal)
- நேர்மையுடன் / *nērmaiyuṭaṉ* — with honesty, with integrity
- நேர்மையாளர் / *nērmayāḷar* — person(s) of integrity (agentive; formal and political)
- நேர் / *nēr* — the base root; used in compounds and attributive phrases to indicate directness and straightforwardness
- நேரடி / *nēraṭi* — direct, straightforward, unmediated
- நேரடியாக / *nēraṭiyāka* — directly, straightforwardly
- நேர்தன்மை / *nēr-taṉmai* — straightforwardness, directness (more literary/philosophical; overlaps with *nērmai*) [MARGINAL]
- தூய்மை / *tūymai* — purity, moral integrity (in evaluative contexts of persons and conduct)
- தூய்மையான / *tūymaiyāna* — pure, morally unblemished
- தூய்மையற்ற / *tūymaiyaṟṟa* — impure, morally tainted, corrupt
- வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai* — the primary term for deception as a character-based or intentional act; used in *vañcaṉai cey* (commit deception), *vañcaṉaiyillāta maṉacu* (a guileless heart); also participates in Group 6 and Group 7
- வஞ்சனையான / *vañcaṉaiyāka* — adverbial form; used to describe deceptive conduct (*vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa*, behave deceitfully)
- உண்மையான மனிதன் / *uṇmaiyāna maṉitaṉ* — a genuine person, a person of integrity; overlaps with Group 1 (genuineness of persons) and this group

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *nērmaiyāka iru* (be honest)
- *nērmai tāṉ ciṟanta koḷkai* (honesty is the best policy)
- *nērmaiyāna vaḻiyil campāti* (earn by honest means)
- *nērmaiyāna atikāri kiṭaippatu aritu* (an honest official is hard to find)
- *maṉat tūymai mukkiyam* (purity of mind is important)
- *tūymai illāta arациyal* (politics without integrity)
- *nērmaiyāka pēcu* (speak honestly)
- *nērmai kāṭṭiṉāṉ* (he showed honesty)
- *nērmai kaṭaippiṭikkiṟār* (he/she adheres to honesty)
- *nērmayuṭaṉ paṇi ceykiṟār* (he/she works with integrity)
- *avaṟiṭam nērmai illai* (there is no honesty in him/her)
- *nērmayāṉa maṉitaṉ* (an honest person)
- *nērmayāṉa viṭai* (an honest/straightforward answer)

**Notes:** *நேர்மை* and *வஞ்சனை* are the clearest members of this group, as they are used almost exclusively in character evaluation of persons. *தூய்மை* also appears in religious and aesthetic contexts (physical purity, ritual cleanliness) that are outside the truth-evaluation domain; its placement in this group reflects only the subset of its usage documented in P1 and P2. *நேர்மை* and related terms can appear in contexts where the honesty of a statement (not just a person) is at issue, creating overlap with Group 1. However, the dominant usage pattern for *நேர்மை* is evaluation of persons and conduct, while *uṇmai/poy* more typically evaluate propositions and states of affairs. *பொய்* also participates in this group when applied to persons (*poy āḷ*, a lying person), but its primary membership is in Group 1. *வஞ்சனை* also participates in Group 6 (deliberate deception) and Group 7 (legal and administrative validity).

## Group 4: Expressions used to characterize objects, persons, or documents as genuine versus not genuine

**Label:** Assessing genuineness versus fakeness or copying

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a physical object, document, credential, or person is what it claims to be — an original rather than a copy, a real professional rather than an impostor, a genuine article rather than a counterfeit. The operative contrast is between *acal* (original) and *nakal* (copy) or *pōli* (fake), and the evaluation is applied to things and persons rather than to propositions. This group is especially prominent in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- அசல் / *acal* — the primary term for the genuine original; used in *acal āvaṇam* (original document), *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?); also participates in Group 7
- அசலான / *acalāna* — adjectival: original, genuine
- நகல் / *nakal* — the neutral term for a copy or duplicate; contrasts with *acal* in administrative and legal contexts; not itself negatively evaluative — a *nakal* is not a fake, merely a reproduction
- போலி / *pōli* — the strongly negative term for a fake or counterfeit; applied to objects (*pōli poruḷ*, fake goods), documents (*pōli āvaṇam*, forged document), and persons (*pōli ṭākṭar*, fake doctor); also participates in Group 7
- போலியான / *pōliyāna* — adjectival form of *pōli*; used attributively in the same contexts; fake, spurious
- போலித்தனம் / *pōlittaṉam* — fakeness, the quality of being counterfeit
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — also used in this group when applied to objects and persons (*uṇmaiyāna poruḷ*, genuine goods; *uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ*, a true friend); overlaps with Group 1
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmait-taṉmai* — used in formal contexts to refer to the property of being genuine (*ataṉ uṇmait-taṉmai cantēkattirku uriyatu*, its authenticity is open to doubt); also used in document/object evaluation
- மெய்யான / *meyyāna* — used in literary contexts to characterize persons or things as genuinely what they are; overlaps with Group 1

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?)
- *itu acal illai, pōli* (this is not the original — it's a fake)
- *pōli eṉṟu kaṇṭupiṭikkappaṭṭatu* (it was found to be fake)
- *acal āvaṇam koṇṭu vā* (bring the original document)
- *acal eṉṟu nampi vāṅkiṉāṉ* (he bought it believing it to be genuine)
- *pōli āvaṇam vaittiruntāṉ* (he was in possession of a forged document)
- *inta āvaṇattiṉ uṇmaitaṉmai cantēkattirku uriyatu* (the authenticity of this document is open to doubt)
- *atu pōliyā, acalā?* (is that fake or genuine?)

**Notes:** The *acal/nakal/pōli* triad is the core of this group and is largely confined to it. *uṇmaiyāna* and *meyyāna* participate in both this group and Group 1, depending on whether they are applied to things/persons (genuineness) or to propositions (truth). *nakal* is neutral in connotation (a certified copy of a document is a *nakal* without negative implication), while *pōli* is always negative. *acal* and *pōli* also appear prominently in Group 7 (legal and administrative contexts).

## Group 5: Expressions used in providing, demanding, or evaluating evidential support for claims

**Label:** Providing and evaluating evidential support / Establishing and evidencing claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts — primarily legal, journalistic, and formal argumentative — where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it can be demonstrated, supported, or established on the basis of evidence or proof. They appear in constructions involving the production of evidence, the act of proving, and the evaluation of whether a claim is well-supported. The operative concern is the relationship between a claim and its evidentiary basis. The typical constructions involve demanding evidence (*ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē*), asserting its absence (*ātāramaṟṟa vatanti*), or establishing its presence (*cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu*).

**Representative expressions:**

- ஆதாரம் / *ātāram* — the primary term for evidence or basis; used in *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal etuvum colla vēṇṭām* (don't say anything without reliable evidence), *ātāramāna* (well-founded), *ātāramaṟṟa* (baseless); more common in everyday argumentative contexts
- ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna* — adjectival form; applied to claims that are evidentially supported; well-founded, evidence-based
- ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa* — privative form; applied to claims that lack evidentiary support; without basis, unsubstantiated
- ஆதாரமில்லாத / *ātāramillāta* — without evidence, baseless
- ஆதாரப்படுத்து / *ātārappaṭuttu* — to substantiate, to support with evidence
- சான்று / *cāṉṟu* — evidence, proof, testimony, certificate; more frequently used in formal legal contexts (testimony, certification, witnessing)
- சான்றிதழ் / *cāṉṟitaḻ* — certificate, credential
- சான்றாளர் / *cāṉṟāḷar* — witness, attester; also participates in Group 7
- சான்றுமொழி / *cāṉṟumoḻi* — testimony, sworn statement
- சான்றற்ற / *cāṉṟaṟṟa* — without evidence, unattested
- சாட்சி / *cāṭci* — witness or testimony; used in legal contexts as the basis for establishing truth (*poy cāṭci col*, give false testimony); also participates in Group 7
- சாட்சியம் / *cāṭciyam* — the body of testimony or evidence; used in legal contexts; also participates in Group 7
- மெய்ப்பிக்க / *meyppiкka* — to prove, to establish the truth of a claim; the only morphologically derived verbal form in the truth domain directly encoding the act of proving
- மெய்ப்பித்தல் / *meyppittal* — the verbal noun form; proving, verification
- உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டுதல் / *uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭutal* — establishing the truth definitively; used in legal and formal argumentative contexts
- உண்மைச் சான்று / *uṇmaic cāṉṟu* — proof of truth, attestation; used in legal and administrative contexts
- உண்மைத்தன்மையை நிரூபி / *uṇmait-taṉmaiyai nirūpi* — prove the authenticity; used in formal legal contexts
- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* — reliable, credible; used to evaluate sources and evidence (*nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu*, from reliable sources); also participates in Group 6
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worth trusting, credible; used to evaluate whether testimony or a source is reliable; also participates in Group 6
- நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta* — not credible, not worth trusting; the negative counterpart; also participates in Group 6
- நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam* — proof, demonstration (formal and legal); also participates in Group 2
- நிரூபிக்கு / *nirūpikku* — to prove, demonstrate (verb; legal and formal)

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu* (without evidence, it cannot be proved)
- *cāṉṟukaḷai maṟaittārkaḷ* (they concealed the evidence)
- *itarku eṉṉa cāṉṟu irukkiṟatu?* (what evidence is there for this?)
- *ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē* (don't make accusations without evidence)
- *itu ātāramaṟṟa vatanti* (this is an unsubstantiated rumor)
- *ātārattūṭaṉ nirūpi* (prove it with evidence)
- *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal collātē* (don't assert without reliable evidence)
- *cāṉṟu camarppikkāṇum* (evidence/documentation must be submitted)
- *cāṭci illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu* (it cannot be proven without a witness)

**Notes:** *ஆதாரம்* and *சாட்சி* are the clearest members. *மெய்ப்பிக்க* is the only verbal form in the entire inventory that directly encodes the act of proving, making it a distinctive member of this group. *நம்பகமான* and *நம்பத்தக்க* overlap with Group 6 (trust-based evaluation), but their use in journalistic and legal source evaluation places them here as well. Usage patterns suggest that *cāṉṟu* is more frequently used in formal legal contexts while *ātāram* is more common in everyday argumentative contexts; both appear in the same constructions, however, and the distinction is one of register and typical context rather than a sharp semantic boundary.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person or source is worthy of trust

**Label:** Evaluating trustworthiness and credibility of persons and sources

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be trusted or relied upon. They appear in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts, and the typical constructions involve asserting or denying credibility (*nampakamāna takavalu*, *nampakattaṉmai kēḷvikkuṟiyākiviṭṭatu*) or describing the relationship of trust between a person and a source (*nampikkai vai*, *nampikkai iḻa*). The operative question is not whether a specific claim is true but whether the agent making the claim or the source providing information is reliable. This group is anchored in the root *nampu* (to trust).

**Representative expressions:**

- நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* — the primary nominal; used across interpersonal (*nampikkai vai*, place trust), institutional (*nampikkai vākkeṭuppu*, vote of confidence), and religious (faith) contexts
- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* — reliable, trustworthy, credible; applied to persons, sources, and institutions; also participates in Group 5
- நம்பகம் / *nampakam* — the abstract quality of trustworthiness
- நம்பகத்தன்மை / *nampakattaṉmai* — credibility, reliability (abstract nominal; formal written Tamil)
- நம்பகமற்ற / *nampakamāṟṟa* — unreliable, not credible
- நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiāna* — trustworthy, faithful; applied to persons; reliable
- நம்பிக்கையற்ற / *nampikkaiāṟṟa* — faithless, without trust; untrustworthy
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worth trusting, credible; also participates in Group 5
- நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta* — not worth trusting, untrustworthy; also participates in Group 5
- நம்பமுடியாத / *nampamuṭiyāta* — unbelievable, not credible; not to be trusted (everyday)
- நம்பிக்கையின்மை / *nampikkaiinmai* — lack of trust, distrust
