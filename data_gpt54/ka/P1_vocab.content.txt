1. **სიმართლე**  
   **Transliteration:** *simartle*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **მართალი** “true; right; just”  
   **Typical English translations:** truth; correctness; justice  

   Used for the truth of a statement, but also for moral or social rightness in many contexts. It is very common in everyday speech, public discourse, religion, and literature, and can refer both to factual truth and to what is just or fair.  
   **[CORE]**

2. **მართალი**  
   **Transliteration:** *martali*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic adjective; also used substantivally  
   **Typical English translations:** true; right; correct; just  

   This is a central adjective for saying that someone is right, a statement is true, or a person is morally in the right. It is also widely used in legal, ethical, and conversational contexts, including expressions meaning “you are right.”  
   **[CORE]**

3. **ჭეშმარიტება**  
   **Transliteration:** *č’ešmarit’eba*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **ჭეშმარიტი**  
   **Typical English translations:** truth; verity  

   This word is common in elevated, religious, philosophical, and literary usage for “truth” in a fuller or more absolute sense. It can refer to doctrinal truth, metaphysical truth, or the genuine nature of something, and is more formal than **სიმართლე** in many contexts.  
   **[CORE]**

4. **ჭეშმარიტი**  
   **Transliteration:** *č’ešmarit’i*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
   **Typical English translations:** true; genuine; real; authentic  

   Often used for statements, beliefs, teachings, or identities judged to be truly what they claim to be. It is especially frequent in formal, religious, philosophical, and literary styles, though still broadly recognizable.  
   **[CORE]**

5. **სწორი**  
   **Transliteration:** *sts’ori*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
   **Typical English translations:** correct; right; proper; straight  

   This is a very common word for correctness in answers, actions, procedures, and judgments. Depending on context, it can refer to factual correctness, proper conduct, or the proper form or alignment of something.  
   **[CORE]**

6. **სისწორე**  
   **Transliteration:** *sists’ore*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **სწორი**  
   **Typical English translations:** correctness; rightness; accuracy  

   Used for the correctness or accuracy of information, reasoning, performance, or form. It appears in educational, technical, and evaluative contexts, and can also refer to propriety or rightness more broadly.  
   **[CORE]**

7. **ნამდვილი**  
   **Transliteration:** *namdvili*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
   **Typical English translations:** real; genuine; authentic; true  

   A very common adjective for distinguishing what is genuine from what is fake, apparent, or merely nominal. It is used for objects, emotions, persons, identity, and sometimes for “real” or “true” in a more emphatic everyday sense.  
   **[CORE]**

8. **ნამდვილად**  
   **Transliteration:** *namdvilad*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** adverb from **ნამდვილი**  
   **Typical English translations:** really; truly; indeed; genuinely  

   This adverb is frequent in speech and writing to affirm that something is genuinely the case. It often strengthens assertions and can indicate certainty, sincerity, or actual reality rather than appearance.  
   **[CORE]**

9. **ნამდვილობა**  
   **Transliteration:** *namdviloba*  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **ნამდვილი**  
   **Typical English translations:** genuineness; authenticity; realness  

   Used for the quality of being genuine or authentic, especially in relation to documents, objects, claims, or identity. It is less common than **ნამდვილი**, but clearly attested in contexts where authenticity is under evaluation.  
   **[CORE]**

10. **ყალბი**  
    **Transliteration:** *q’albi*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
    **Typical English translations:** false; fake; counterfeit  

    This is a common term for forged money, fake documents, insincere behavior, or false representations. It applies both to material counterfeits and to figurative falseness, such as artificial emotion or deceptive appearance.  
    **[CORE]**

11. **სიყალბე**  
    **Transliteration:** *siq’albe*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **ყალბი**  
    **Typical English translations:** falseness; counterfeit nature; insincerity  

    Refers to the quality of being false, fake, or insincere. It is used for forged or inauthentic things as well as for hypocrisy, artificiality, or deceptive representation in personal and aesthetic contexts.  
    **[CORE]**

12. **უტყუარი**  
    **Transliteration:** *ut’q’uari*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** negative/prefixal formation; “not deceptive / not failing” in established usage  
    **Typical English translations:** reliable; unfailing; certain; trustworthy  

    This adjective is used for evidence, signs, methods, or sources that can be trusted and do not mislead. It often appears in formal or written language and conveys strong reliability or certainty.  
    **[CORE]**

13. **სარწმუნო**  
    **Transliteration:** *sarč’muno*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective; historically related to belief/trust  
    **Typical English translations:** credible; trustworthy; reliable  

    Used for testimony, information, sources, or evidence judged worthy of belief. It is common in formal, journalistic, legal, and scholarly contexts where credibility or trustworthiness is being assessed.  
    **[CORE]**

14. **სარწმუნოება**  
    **Transliteration:** *sarč’munoeba*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **სარწმუნო**  
    **Typical English translations:** religion; creed; belief  

    In modern Georgian this usually means “religion” or “confession,” but it is relevant because it concerns what is held as true or authoritative in doctrinal contexts. Its evaluative connection to truth is indirect in ordinary use, so it is less central for this semantic area.  
    **[MARGINAL]**

15. **დამაჯერებელი**  
    **Transliteration:** *damajerebeli*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “causing belief / convincing”  
    **Typical English translations:** convincing; credible; persuasive  

    This adjective is used when an argument, explanation, performance, or representation seems believable or persuasive. It evaluates whether something appears sufficiently supported or lifelike to be accepted as true or plausible.  
    **[CORE]**

16. **სარწმუნოდ**  
    **Transliteration:** *sarč’munod*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adverb from **სარწმუნო**  
    **Typical English translations:** credibly; reliably  

    Used adverbially to describe how something is presented, established, or attested in a believable and trustworthy way. It is more common in formal written usage than in casual conversation.  
    **[MARGINAL]**

17. **ზუსტი**  
    **Transliteration:** *zust’i*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
    **Typical English translations:** exact; precise; accurate  

    A very common adjective for factual, numerical, descriptive, or technical accuracy. It is used for measurements, statements, descriptions, and reproductions when closeness to the actual facts or form is being evaluated.  
    **[CORE]**

18. **სიზუსტე**  
    **Transliteration:** *sizust’e*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **ზუსტი**  
    **Typical English translations:** accuracy; precision; exactness  

    Refers to the degree of exactness in information, representation, measurement, or execution. It is frequent in education, science, administration, and any context where correctness of detail matters.  
    **[CORE]**

19. **მსგავსი**  
    **Transliteration:** *msgavsi*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective  
    **Typical English translations:** similar; resembling; like  

    This word is used when evaluating resemblance between persons, objects, images, or situations. It does not itself mean “true” or “correct,” but it is central in judgments about whether a representation or thing matches another in appearance or character.  
    **[CORE]**

20. **მსგავსება**  
    **Transliteration:** *msgavseba*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **მსგავსი**  
    **Typical English translations:** resemblance; similarity; likeness  

    Used for the relation of likeness between originals and copies, people and portraits, or one case and another. It is common in ordinary and literary usage when discussing how closely something corresponds in form or appearance.  
    **[CORE]**

21. **არსი**  
    **Transliteration:** *arsi*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** essence; being  
    **Typical English translations:** essence; core nature; substance  

    This noun is used in philosophical, literary, and reflective contexts for what something fundamentally is. It is relevant where evaluation concerns not surface appearance but underlying nature or essential character.  
    **[CORE]**

22. **არსებითი**  
    **Transliteration:** *arsebiti*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective from **არსი / არსებ-**  
    **Typical English translations:** essential; substantial; fundamental  

    Used for features, distinctions, or properties considered basic rather than accidental. It appears in philosophical, legal, and formal explanatory contexts when identifying what is fundamentally important or constitutive.  
    **[CORE]**

23. **მართლმსაჯულება**  
    **Transliteration:** *martlmsajuleba*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** compound involving **მართალ-** “just/right” + judging  
    **Typical English translations:** justice; administration of justice  

    This is a standard legal and institutional term for justice in the judicial sense. It belongs especially to legal and political discourse, where rightness and proper judgment are evaluated in relation to law and fairness.  
    **[CORE]**

24. **მართებულობა**  
    **Transliteration:** *martebuloba*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** abstract noun from **მართებული**  
    **Typical English translations:** validity; appropriateness; correctness; justification  

    Used in formal and argumentative contexts for whether a claim, action, interpretation, or decision is justified or proper. It often appears in academic, legal, and critical writing rather than in casual speech.  
    **[CORE]**

25. **მართებული**  
    **Transliteration:** *martebuli*  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** adjective related to **მართ-**  
    **Typical English translations:** proper; justified; valid; appropriate  

    This adjective is used when assessing whether an action, argument, wording, or decision is appropriate or well-founded. It is especially common in formal discussion, criticism, and institutional language.  
    **[CORE]**
