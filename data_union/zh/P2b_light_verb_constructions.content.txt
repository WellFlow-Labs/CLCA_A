# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve P1 base forms. The boundary between "construction" and "compound" is not always sharp in Chinese; the criterion applied here is that the construction must involve at least two syntactically separable elements where the P1 item functions as a component within a larger, conventionalized multi-word unit, rather than as a fused lexical item. Fixed four-character expressions (成语) already documented in P1 and P1b are not re-listed here unless they appear as components within a larger construction pattern. Derivational forms documented in P2a are excluded.

Where no common construction is identified for a P1 item or for a particular construction type, this is stated explicitly. Absence is preserved as data.

## Constructions by Base Form

---

### Base Form: 真 (zhēn)

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 这是真的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** This is true / This is real / This is genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The copular construction X + 是 + 真的 is the most basic predication of truth or genuineness in Chinese. The particle 的 nominalizes or substantivizes 真, producing a predicative structure. Ubiquitous across all registers and contexts. Functions as the default construction for asserting that something is not fake or not false.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 是真是假
**Transliteration:** shì zhēn shì jiǎ
**English Translation:** Whether it is true or false; is it real or fake?
**Usage Notes:** The parallel structure 是X是Y presents two alternatives for evaluation. This construction frames the authenticity question as an open binary and is common in investigative, commercial, and everyday discourse. Used in everyday speech and in contexts of evaluating claims, objects, or situations ("说不清是真是假" — can't tell whether it's true or false). Closely related to 真假难辨.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 说真的
**Transliteration:** shuō zhēn de
**English Translation:** To be honest; speaking truthfully; honestly speaking.
**Usage Notes:** A discourse-initial or parenthetical expression used to signal that the speaker is about to make a frank or sincere statement, or to assert that one is speaking seriously rather than jokingly. Parallel in function to 说实话 and 老实说. Common in conversational registers. Functions as a discourse marker across all registers of spoken Chinese.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 来真的
**Transliteration:** lái zhēn de
**English Translation:** To do it for real; to get serious; to mean business.
**Usage Notes:** A colloquial construction using the verb 来 (to come/do) with 真的 to assert that something is being done genuinely rather than as a pretense or game. Contrasts with 来假的 (to go through the motions). Used in everyday speech ("他是来真的" — he's serious about this / he means it for real). Common in informal spoken registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 不是真的
**Transliteration:** bù shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** It is not true / It is not real / It is not genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The negated copular construction, parallel to Construction 1. The most basic predication of non-truth or inauthenticity. Ubiquitous across all registers. The negated construction has distinct pragmatic uses (denial, correction, reassurance) that differ from the affirmative.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 当真吗？
**Transliteration:** dàng zhēn ma?
**English Translation:** Are you serious? Do you really mean it? Is it for real?
**Usage Notes:** A common conversational question used to ask whether a statement or situation should be taken as genuine rather than as a joke or exaggeration. Distinct from the lexicalized 当真 (P1b) in that it functions as a full interrogative construction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 半真半假
**Transliteration:** bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
**English Translation:** Half true, half false; partly genuine, partly fabricated.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed periphrastic construction using the 半X半Y pattern to describe something that is a mixture of genuine and false elements. Used in evaluative discourse about accounts, claims, or representations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 说真话
**Transliteration:** shuō zhēnhuà
**English Translation:** To tell the truth; to speak truthfully.
**Usage Notes:** A common verb-object collocation meaning to speak truthfully. Contrasts directly with 说假话 (to tell lies). Used across moral, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 真有其事
**Transliteration:** zhēn yǒu qí shì
**English Translation:** It really happened; the matter is real; it's a true story.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed predicate construction asserting that a reported event or matter actually occurred. Used in journalistic and everyday contexts to confirm the factual basis of a report or rumor ("此事真有其事" — this matter really did happen).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 假戏真做
**Transliteration:** jiǎ xì zhēn zuò
**English Translation:** To play a fake role as if it were real; to act out a fiction as though it were genuine.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed expression describing the situation in which someone performs a pretense with genuine commitment, so that the boundary between fiction and reality blurs. Used in dramatic, interpersonal, and political contexts. Distinct from 弄假成真 in that the performative frame is maintained even as the execution becomes genuine.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base Form: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 这是假的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì jiǎ de
**English Translation:** This is fake / This is false / This is not genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The copular construction X + 是 + 假的, parallel to Construction 1 for 真. The most basic predication of inauthenticity or falseness. Ubiquitous across all registers, including commercial, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 装假
**Transliteration:** zhuāng jiǎ
**English Translation:** To put on a false front; to pretend; to fake it.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 装 (to pretend, to put on) combined with 假 produces a colloquial expression for performing inauthenticity. Common in everyday speech. Overlaps with 假装 but 装假 foregrounds the performance aspect more explicitly. Slightly more colloquial than 假装 (P1b).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 打假
**Transliteration:** dǎ jiǎ
**English Translation:** To crack down on counterfeits; to combat fakes; anti-counterfeiting.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 打 (to strike, to combat) combined with 假 produces a widely used expression in commercial, regulatory, and legal discourse for efforts to suppress counterfeit goods or fraudulent practices. 打假行动 (anti-counterfeiting campaign) and 打假力度 (intensity of anti-fake enforcement) are standard collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 造假
**Transliteration:** zào jiǎ
**English Translation:** To fabricate; to falsify; to manufacture fakes.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 造 (to make, to manufacture) combined with 假 produces a common expression for the act of creating false documents, data, or objects. Used in legal, academic, and journalistic contexts: 数据造假 (data fabrication), 学术造假 (academic fraud). Closely related to 伪造 but more colloquial and broader in application.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 掺假
**Transliteration:** chān jiǎ
**English Translation:** To adulterate; to mix in fake or inferior material.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 掺 (to mix in, to adulterate) combined with 假 describes the practice of mixing counterfeit or inferior material into genuine goods. Common in food safety, commercial, and regulatory discourse: 食品掺假 (food adulteration).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 作假
**Transliteration:** zuò jiǎ
**English Translation:** To falsify; to cheat; to engage in deception.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 作 (to do, to engage in) combined with 假 produces a common expression for deliberate falsification or cheating. Used in academic, commercial, and administrative contexts: 考试作假 (cheating on an exam), 账目作假 (falsifying accounts). Overlaps with 造假 but 作假 more often implies procedural or documentary falsification.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 说假话
**Transliteration:** shuō jiǎhuà
**English Translation:** To tell a lie; to speak falsely.
**Usage Notes:** The standard verb-object collocation for lying or making false statements. Directly contrasts with 说真话. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 辨别真假
**Transliteration:** biànbié zhēnjiǎ
**English Translation:** To distinguish the genuine from the fake; to tell real from false.
**Usage Notes:** A common verb-object collocation used in authentication, commercial, and analytical contexts. The object 真假 functions as a binomial noun denoting the evaluative dimension of authenticity.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 辨真假
**Transliteration:** biàn zhēnjiǎ
**English Translation:** To distinguish the genuine from the fake; to tell real from false.
**Usage Notes:** A verb-object construction using 辨 (to distinguish) + 真假, meaning to evaluate and determine whether something is authentic or counterfeit. Related to 辨别真假 but more colloquial and shorter.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** zhuīqiú zhēnshí

**Construction 24**

**Transliteration:** jīyú zhēnshí shìjiàn
**English Translation:** Based on true events; based on a true story.
**Usage Notes:** A prepositional phrase construction extremely common in film, literature, and media to indicate that a narrative is grounded in actual events. Functions as a fixed attributive or adverbial frame.

**Construction 25**

**Construction 42**
**Base Form:** 正确 (zhèngquè)
**Example:** 答对了 / 答错了
**Transliteration:** dá duì le / dá cuò le

**Transliteration:** jiūzhèng cuòwù

**Usage Notes:** The verb 纠正 (to correct, to rectify) combined with 错误 is a standard collocation in educational, political, and everyday discourse. Appears frequently in political self-criticism and administrative language. Common in self-criticism and policy-correction discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 45**
**Base Form:** 错误 (cuòwù)
**Example:** 承认错误
**Transliteration:** chéngrèn cuòwù

**Transliteration:** yánzhòng cuòwù

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 48**

**Transliteration:** biǎoshù zhǔnquè
**English Translation:** The formulation is accurate; the expression is precise.
**Usage Notes:** A predicate construction used in academic, legal, and editorial contexts to evaluate whether a statement or formulation is precisely worded. 准确 functions as a predicate adjective. The word order (noun + 准确) is the standard Chinese predicate adjective frame.
