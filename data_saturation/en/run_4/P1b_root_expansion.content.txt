# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: TRUE / TRUTH (Old English *trēowe* / *trēowth*)**

- **Root form:** true / truth
- **P1 items participating:** *true* (1), *truth* (2), *truthful* (29) — plus etymological relatives *faithful* (20) and *fidelity* (21) via Latin *fides*, which shares the semantic core of trustworthiness but is morphologically distinct in English. Direct morphological participation: **3 items** (*true*, *truth*, *truthful*).
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary English root for propositional correspondence with reality and personal reliability. Generates the most productive derivational family in the domain, spanning adjective, noun, adverb, and compound forms.

---

**Root 2: VER- (Latin *verus*, true)**

- **Root form:** ver-
- **P1 items participating:** *verify* (17), *veracious* (18), *veracity* (19) — **3 items**
- **Core meaning contribution:** The Latin-derived root for truth and truthfulness, entering English through legal, scholarly, and formal registers. Less frequent in everyday speech than *true/truth* but highly productive in technical and institutional vocabulary.

---

**Root 3: GENUINE / AUTHENTIC (Latin *genuinus* / Greek *authentikos*)**

- **Root form:** genuine / authentic
- **P1 items participating:** *genuine* (4), *authentic* (5), *bona fide* (28) — **3 items** (with *bona fide* as a fixed Latin phrase semantically aligned with this cluster)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The domain of originality, non-imitation, and correspondence to an original source or identity. Productive in aesthetic, legal, and philosophical registers. These two roots are morphologically distinct but functionally parallel; both are included because together they constitute the primary English resources for the authenticity sub-domain, and each is independently productive.

**1.1**
- **Form:** truly
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *true* + *-ly* (adverb-forming suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** in a true manner; genuinely; used as an intensifier
- **Context of use:** Used as a general adverb of manner ("she truly believed it") and as a formulaic intensifier in correspondence ("yours truly"). Appears across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *un-* (negation) + *true*
- **Natural English translation:** not true; false; unfaithful
- **Context of use:** Applied to statements and claims to indicate falsity, and to persons or conduct to indicate disloyalty. Slightly more formal than *false* in some contexts; also carries a connotation of betrayal.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 2: VER- (Latin *verus*)

---

**2.1**
- **Form:** verification
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ver-* + *-ific-* + *-ation* (Latin *verificatio*)
- **Natural English translation:** the process or result of establishing the truth or accuracy of something
- **Context of use:** Used across legal, scientific, administrative, and technical contexts. Central to scientific methodology (verification of hypotheses) and identity management (identity verification).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.2**
- **Form:** verifiable
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *verif(y)* + *-able*
- **Natural English translation:** capable of being verified or confirmed
- **Context of use:** Used in scientific, philosophical, and journalistic contexts to assess whether a claim can in principle be tested or confirmed. Key term in logical positivism's verificationism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.3**
- **Form:** verifiability
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *verif(y)* + *-abil-* + *-ity*
- **Natural English translation:** the property of being capable of verification
- **Context of use:** Technical term in philosophy of science and epistemology. Central to the verificationist criterion of meaning associated with logical positivism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.4**
- **Form:** unverified
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *un-* + *verif(y)* + *-ed*
- **Natural English translation:** not yet confirmed or established as true
- **Context of use:** Used in journalism, science, and intelligence contexts to flag claims that have not been independently confirmed ("unverified reports").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.5**
- **Form:** unverifiable
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *un-* + *verif(y)* + *-able*
- **Natural English translation:** incapable of being verified; not subject to confirmation
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophy of science, epistemology, and journalism to characterize claims that cannot in principle be tested or confirmed.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.6**
- **Form:** verism
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ver-* (Latin *verus*, true) + *-ism*
- **Natural English translation:** a style or doctrine in art and literature emphasizing faithful, unidealized depiction of reality
- **Context of use:** Used in art history and literary criticism to describe a movement, particularly in Italian opera and visual art of the late 19th century, committed to realistic and often harsh depictions of ordinary life.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.7**
- **Form:** verist
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ver-* + *-ist*
- **Natural English translation:** an adherent of verism; an artist committed to realistic depiction
- **Context of use:** Used in art history and music criticism, particularly in discussions of Italian *verismo* opera. Less common than *verismo* itself in English-language scholarship.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.8**
- **Form:** verismo
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Italian: *ver-* + *-ismo* (borrowed into English)
- **Natural English translation:** a late 19th-century Italian operatic and literary movement emphasizing realistic, often lower-class subject matter
- **Context of use:** Used as a borrowed term in English-language music criticism and art history. Associated with composers such as Puccini, Mascagni, and Leoncavallo.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within music and art criticism)

---

**2.9**
- **Form:** veridical
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Latin *veridicus*: *ver-* (*verus*, true) + *-dic-* (*dicere*, to say) + *-al*
- **Natural English translation:** truthful; corresponding to facts; (of a perception or dream) accurately representing reality
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophy, psychology, and cognitive science. A *veridical* perception is one that accurately represents the external world; contrasts with *illusory* or *hallucinatory*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within philosophy and psychology)

---

**2.10**
- **Form:** veridicality
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *veridical* + *-ity*
- **Natural English translation:** the property of accurately representing reality
- **Context of use:** Technical term in philosophy of perception and cognitive science. Used to assess whether perceptual states or representations correspond to the actual world.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.11**
- **Form:** verisimilitude
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Latin *verisimilitudo*: *veri-* (genitive of *verus*, true) + *similis* (like) + *-tude*
- **Natural English translation:** the appearance of being true or real; the quality of seeming probable or lifelike
- **Context of use:** Used in literary criticism, philosophy of science, and aesthetics. In literary contexts, refers to the quality of a narrative that makes it seem realistic. In philosophy of science (Popper), refers to the degree to which a theory approximates truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.12**
- **Form:** verdict
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Medieval Latin *veredictum*: *ver-* (*verus*, true) + *dict-* (*dicere*, to say) + *-um*
- **Natural English translation:** a formal decision or judgment, especially by a jury
- **Context of use:** Central legal term for the formal finding of a jury or judge. Also used more broadly for any authoritative judgment or decision ("the verdict of history").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.13**
- **Form:** verity
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Latin *veritas*: *ver-* + *-itas* (abstract noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** a true principle or belief; a fundamental truth
- **Context of use:** Used in formal, philosophical, and literary registers, often in the plural ("eternal verities"). Less common in everyday speech; carries a somewhat archaic or elevated tone.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.14**
- **Form:** aver
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Old French *averer*, from Medieval Latin *adverare*: *ad-* + *ver-* (*verus*)
- **Natural English translation:** to assert or affirm with confidence; to declare to be true
- **Context of use:** Used in legal and formal registers to indicate a confident, formal assertion. In law, to *aver* is to allege as a fact in a pleading.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within legal and formal registers)

---

**2.15**
- **Form:** averment
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *aver* + *-ment*
- **Natural English translation:** a formal assertion or allegation; in law, a positive statement of fact in a pleading
- **Context of use:** Legal term for a formal allegation of fact. Used in pleadings and legal documents to distinguish asserted facts from argument.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within legal register)

---

**2.16**
- **Form:** verify against
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *verify* + *against* (prepositional collocation)
- **Natural English translation:** to check the accuracy of something by comparing it with a reference standard
- **Context of use:** Used in technical, scientific, and administrative contexts ("verify the data against the original records"). Standard procedural phrasing in quality control and auditing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.17**
- **Form:** cross-verify
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *cross-* + *verify*
- **Natural English translation:** to verify by checking against multiple independent sources
- **Context of use:** Used in journalism, intelligence, and scientific contexts. Less common than *cross-check* but attested in technical and journalistic writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.18**
- **Form:** self-verifying
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *self-* + *verify* + *-ing*
- **Natural English translation:** capable of confirming its own truth or accuracy without external reference
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophy, logic, and computer science. A *self-verifying* system or statement is one that contains within itself the means of confirming its own validity.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.19**
- **Form:** verificationism
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *verification* + *-ism*
- **Natural English translation:** the philosophical doctrine that a statement is meaningful only if it is empirically verifiable or analytically true
- **Context of use:** Technical term in philosophy of language and philosophy of science. Associated with the Vienna Circle and logical positivism. Contrasts with falsificationism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within philosophy)

---

**2.20**
- **Form:** verificationist
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *verification* + *-ist*
- **Natural English translation:** an adherent of verificationism; relating to verificationism
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical discourse as both noun and adjective ("a verificationist criterion of meaning").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within philosophy)

---

> **Note:** The *ver-* root is moderately productive in English. Additional attested but less common forms include *veraciously*, *veridicalism*, and the archaic *veridically*. The items above represent the most common and broadly attested forms.

### ROOT 3: GENUINE / AUTHENTIC

*Note: These are two morphologically distinct roots that are treated together here because they constitute the primary English resources for the authenticity sub-domain and are functionally parallel. Each is expanded separately below.*

#### ROOT 3a: GENUINE

---

**3a.1**
- **Form:** genuinely
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in a genuine manner; sincerely; actually
- **Context of use:** Used as an adverb of manner across all registers ("she was genuinely surprised"). Also functions as an intensifier to assert sincerity or authenticity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.2**
- **Form:** genuineness
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being genuine; authenticity; sincerity
- **Context of use:** Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something is what it purports to be. Less common than *authenticity* in formal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.3**
- **Form:** genuine article
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *article* (fixed phrase)
- **Natural English translation:** the real thing; the authentic version of something, as opposed to an imitation
- **Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to assert that something or someone is authentic and not a substitute or imitation ("he's the genuine article"). Common in informal and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.4**
- **Form:** genuine interest
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *interest* (collocation)
- **Natural English translation:** sincere, unfeigned interest
- **Context of use:** Common collocation in everyday and professional speech to distinguish sincere engagement from performed or strategic interest. Frequently appears in descriptions of interpersonal and professional conduct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.5**
- **Form:** genuine concern
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *concern* (collocation)
- **Natural English translation:** sincere, unfeigned concern or worry
- **Context of use:** Common collocation in political, journalistic, and everyday speech. Used to assert that a concern is sincerely held rather than strategically expressed.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.6**
- **Form:** genuine effort
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *effort* (collocation)
- **Natural English translation:** a sincere, real attempt
- **Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts to distinguish sincere attempts from token or performative ones ("the court found no genuine effort to comply").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3a.7**
- **Form:** genuine issue
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *issue* (legal collocation)
- **Natural English translation:** a real, substantive dispute of fact or law
- **Context of use:** Legal term of art, particularly in the phrase *genuine issue of material fact*, which is the standard for defeating a motion for summary judgment in U.S. civil procedure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within legal register)

---

**3a.8**
- **Form:** genuine issue of material fact
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *genuine* + *issue* + *of* + *material* + *fact* (fixed legal phrase)
- **Natural English translation:** a real dispute about a fact that is legally significant to the outcome of a case
- **Context of use:** Standard legal phrase in U.S. civil procedure. A court grants summary judgment only when there is no genuine issue of material fact and the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE] (within legal register)

- **Context of use:** Used across legal, aesthetic, philosophical, and everyday contexts. In existentialist philosophy, refers to living in accordance with one's true self. In art and law, refers to the verified origin of an object.

- **Morpheme breakdown:** *authentic* + *-ate* (verb-forming suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** to establish or confirm the authenticity of something
- **Context of use:** Used in legal, archival, art-historical, and digital security contexts. Documents, artworks, and digital identities are *authenticated* by authorized persons or processes.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3b.3**
- **Form:** authentication
- **Transliteration:** N/A

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots identified above but appear in contexts where the contribution of the root seems to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From ROOT 1 (TRUE / TRUTH):**

- **true north** — used in leadership and motivational discourse to mean a guiding principle or core value, independent of any propositional truth claim
- **true-blue** — used to evaluate loyalty and political allegiance rather than factual accuracy
- **true love** — applied to the quality or intensity of an emotional bond rather than to the accuracy of a statement
- **come true** — describes the actualization of a wish or prediction; the *true* here relates to realization rather than correspondence
- **truism** — the *true* root is present, but the term is used to criticize a statement for being trivially obvious rather than to affirm its accuracy
- **ring true** — evaluates the perceived credibility of a claim based on intuitive impression rather than verified correspondence
- **true-born** — applies *true* to identity and origin rather than to propositional content
- **gospel truth** — the intensifying function of *truth* here emphasizes certainty and commitment rather than the process of verification
- **home truth** — *truth* here carries a connotation of interpersonal confrontation and discomfort rather than abstract correspondence

**From ROOT 2 (VER-):**

- **verismo / verism** — the *ver-* root appears in an aesthetic movement concerned with realistic depiction; the truth-relation is to artistic representation rather than to propositional accuracy
- **verdict** — the *ver-* root appears in a legal judgment context where the emphasis is on authoritative decision rather than on the epistemic process of truth-finding
- **verisimilitude** — the root contributes a sense of *apparent* or *seeming* truth rather than confirmed truth; the *similis* (like) component introduces a resemblance relation that may not entail actual correspondence
- **veridical** — in perceptual psychology, *veridical* applies to sensory experience rather than to propositional content

**From ROOT 3b (AUTHENTIC):**

- **authentic cadence** — the *authentic* root appears in music theory to designate a specific harmonic formula (V–I); the connection to genuineness or originality is not transparent in current usage
- **authentic self** — the root is applied to personal identity and psychological wholeness rather than to the accuracy of a representation or the origin of an object
- **authentic experience** — in tourism and cultural discourse, *authentic* evaluates the quality of an experience against an implied original or unmediated version; the standard of comparison may be culturally constructed rather than factually determined
- **authentic leadership** — the root is applied to a style of conduct and self-presentation; the evaluative standard involves consistency and transparency rather than correspondence to external facts
