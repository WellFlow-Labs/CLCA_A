# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: English (en)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Subject + BE + truth-adj | That statement is true. | /ðæt ˈsteɪtmənt ɪz truː/ | "That statement corresponds to facts." | [CORE] | Unmarked construction for all truth-domain predication. Used across all registers. Subject type constrains adjective selection (propositions: true/false; objects: genuine/fake; persons: honest/trustworthy). |
| Subject + BE + truth-adj | The document is authentic. | /ðə ˈdɒkjumənt ɪz ɔːˈθɛntɪk/ | "The document is of genuine origin." | [CORE] | Same construction, object-oriented adjective. Common in verification contexts (documents, artifacts, credentials). |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested beyond basic pattern variation.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Subject + BE + NEG + truth-adj | His claim is not true. | /hɪz kleɪm ɪz nɒt truː/ | "His claim does not correspond to facts." | [CORE] | Analytic negation, neutral register. Freely productive with all truth-domain adjectives. |
| Subject + BE + negated-adj | The signature is inauthentic. | /ðə ˈsɪɡnətʃər ɪz ˌɪnɔːˈθɛntɪk/ | "The signature is not genuine." | [CORE] | Synthetic negation via prefix (un-/in-/dis-). Often carries stronger implication than analytic form. Lexically restricted (some bases lack synthetic forms). |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested. Note indicates "dishonest" is standard while "*not honest" is marked, suggesting lexical gaps constrain alternation.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Truth-adj + N | a true story | /ə truː ˈstɔːri/ | "a factual narrative" | [CORE] | Fixed prenominal position. Highly productive. Shows semantic specialization in conventionalized combinations ("true friend" = loyal, not factual). |
| Truth-adj + N | an honest answer | /ən ˈɒnɪst ˈɑːnsər/ | "a truthful response" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in all registers. Some combinations highly conventionalized ("false alarm," "genuine article," "honest mistake"). |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested. English does not permit postnominal adjective placement for this domain.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Subject + PROVE + truth-adj | The rumor proved false. | /ðə ˈruːmər pruːvd fɔːls/ | "The rumor was revealed to be untrue." | [CORE] | Emphasizes evidential discovery. Common in formal/written registers. Implies verification process. |
| Subject + COME + truth-adj | His predictions came true. | /hɪz prɪˈdɪkʃənz keɪm truː/ | "His predictions were realized." | [CORE] | Fixed expression "come true" for realization of predictions/hopes. Not productive with other adjectives (*came false, *came honest). |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes mention "become" (rare), "get" (informal), "turn out" (common with infinitive). Simple "become + truth-adj" marked as rare, suggesting verb selection is highly constrained.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Agent + causative-V + object | The lab authenticated the painting. | /ðə læb ɔːˈθɛntɪkeɪtɪd ðə ˈpeɪntɪŋ/ | "The lab verified the painting's authenticity." | [CORE] | Dedicated causative verbs (authenticate, validate, verify, falsify, correct) are standard. Used in formal/technical contexts. |
| Agent + causative-V + object | He corrected the error. | /hiː kəˈrɛktɪd ði ˈɛrər/ | "He made the error correct/fixed the error." | [CORE] | Same pattern. "Correct" functions as causative verb. Common across registers. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes explicitly mark analytic causative "make + object + adjective" as marginal ("*make the statement true" is awkward). Causative meaning is lexicalized, not syntactically productive.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Derived nominal | The truth emerged slowly. | /ðə truːθ ɪˈmɜːdʒd ˈsləʊli/ | "The factual reality became known." | [CORE] | Irregular formation patterns (-th, -y/-ty, -ness, -ity). "Truth" can denote abstract quality or concrete reality. Common in all registers. |
| Derived nominal | We value honesty. | /wiː ˈvæljuː ˈɒnɪsti/ | "We value the quality of being truthful." | [CORE] | Character trait nominalization. Frequent in evaluative contexts. Multiple nominalizations per base show semantic differentiation (truth/truthfulness/truism). |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes indicate multiple nominalization strategies coexist with semantic specialization (falsity/falsehood/falseness), suggesting lexical rather than productive morphology.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Agentive nominal | He's a compulsive liar. | /hiːz ə kəmˈpʌlsɪv ˈlaɪər/ | "He habitually tells untruths." | [CORE] | Suppletive form (not regular derivation). Highly frequent, often with evaluative modifiers (compulsive, pathological). Negative social evaluation. |
| Agentive nominal (compound) | She's a truth-teller. | /ʃiːz ə ˈtruːθˌtɛlər/ | "She habitually speaks truthfully." | [MARGINAL] | Compound formation fills lexical gap (*truist). Less frequent than "liar." Positive evaluation, somewhat formal/literary. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes indicate productive -er formation for some bases (faker, counterfeiter) but gaps for others (*truther, *correcter non-standard). Compounds fill gaps (truth-teller, wrongdoer).

**Alternate constructions:** None attested. Notes indicate some adverbs function as discourse markers beyond manner modification, suggesting polysemy rather than distinct construction.

## 9. DEGREE MODIFICATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Degree-mod + truth-adj | The report is completely accurate. | /ðə rɪˈpɔːt ɪz kəmˈpliːtli ˈækjərət/ | "The report is entirely precise." | [CORE] | Gradable adjectives accept degree modification freely. "Completely/entirely" with completive semantics. Common in formal assessment contexts. |
| Comparative | She's more honest than him. | /ʃiːz mɔːr ˈɒnɪst ðən hɪm/ | "She has a greater degree of honesty." | [CORE] | Standard comparative with gradable adjectives. Common in contrastive contexts. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes indicate absolute terms (true/false) resist gradation ("*very true," "*more false" marked), while gradable terms accept comparative/superlative. "Truer" exists but philosophically contentious.

## 10. EVIDENTIAL/EPISTEMIC CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| It + BE + truth-adj + that-clause | It is true that he resigned. | /ɪt ɪz truː ðæt hiː rɪˈzaɪnd/ | "The proposition of his resignation corresponds to facts." | [CORE] | Extraposition for propositional evaluation. Common in formal/written registers. Allows focus on proposition. |
| Subject + SEEM + truth-adj | The story seems authentic. | /ðə ˈstɔːri siːmz ɔːˈθɛntɪk/ | "The story appears to be genuine (based on evidence)." | [CORE] | Evidential uncertainty marking. Common across registers. "Seem/appear" indicate inference from evidence. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes mention fixed expression "ring true/false" for intuitive authenticity judgments, suggesting idiomatic extension of pattern.

## 11. FIXED DISCOURSE MARKERS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| To be honest + clause | To be honest, I don't like it. | /tə biː ˈɒnɪst aɪ dəʊnt laɪk ɪt/ | "Frankly speaking (discourse frame), I don't like it." | [CORE] | Grammaticalized discourse marker. Signals speaker stance (frankness), not truth-evaluation. Highly frequent in conversational discourse. |
| To tell the truth + clause | To tell the truth, I forgot. | /tə tɛl ðə truːθ aɪ fəˈɡɒt/ | "In truth (discourse frame), I forgot." | [CORE] | Same function as above. Frames utterance pragmatically. Common in informal registers. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes list additional markers ("honestly," "truthfully," "in truth," "if truth be told") suggesting paradigm of functionally equivalent expressions.

## 12. RESULTATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| V + object + truth-adj | They proved him wrong. | /ðeɪ pruːvd hɪm rɒŋ/ | "They demonstrated that he was incorrect." | [CORE] | "Prove + object + right/wrong/false" is productive pattern. Common in argumentative contexts. |
| V + object + as + truth-adj | She exposed it as fake. | /ʃiː ɪkˈspəʊzd ɪt əz feɪk/ | "She revealed its counterfeit nature." | [CORE] | "Expose/reveal as fake/counterfeit" marks unmasking. Common in investigative/journalistic contexts. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes explicitly mark general resultatives as ungrammatical ("*paint it true," "*make it authentic"), indicating construction is lexically restricted to specific verbs.

## 14. METALINGUISTIC NEGATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Not adj₁ but adj₂ | It's not false, just incomplete. | /ɪts nɒt fɔːls dʒʌst ˌɪnkəmˈpliːt/ | "The characterization as false is rejected; incomplete is more accurate." | [CORE] | Corrective/clarificatory function. Common in argumentative discourse. Second term typically weakens or reframes first. |
| Not adj₁ but adj₂ | He's not dishonest, merely tactful. | /hiːz nɒt dɪsˈɒnɪst ˈmɪəli ˈtæktfəl/ | "The characterization as dishonest is rejected; tactful is more accurate." | [CORE] | Same pattern. Rejects negative characterization in favor of neutral/positive one. Common in face-saving contexts. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes mention pattern "not wrong, just different" and "not fake, just reproduction," suggesting productive template for nuanced correction.

## 15. INTENSIFICATION CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Intensifier + truth-adj | That's absolutely true. | /ðæts ˈæbsəluːtli truː/ | "That corresponds completely to facts." | [CORE] | Intensifiers with absolute terms (absolutely/completely/entirely). Common in emphatic assertion. |
| Truth-adj + intensifying phrase | He's honest to a fault. | /hiːz ˈɒnɪst tə ə fɔːlt/ | "He is excessively honest." | [MARGINAL] | Fixed intensifying phrase indicating excess. Less frequent, somewhat literary. Implies negative consequence of excessive quality. |

**Alternate constructions:** Notes list additional fixed phrases ("honest to God," "dead right," "spot on," "gospel truth"), suggesting domain includes both productive intensification and fixed idiomatic expressions. Intensifier selection varies by adjective type (absolute vs. gradable).

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**CORE constructions (highly natural, frequent):**
- Basic attribution (1)
- Both negation strategies (2)
- Adnominal modification (3)
- Inchoative with specific verbs (4)
- Lexicalized causatives (5)
- Abstract nominalizations (6)
- "Liar" agentive (7)
- Manner adverbials (8)
- Degree modification with gradable adjectives (9)
- Evidential/epistemic patterns (10)
- Discourse markers (11)
- Resultatives with specific verbs (12)
- Standard comparatives (13)
- Metalinguistic negation (14)
- Basic intensification (15)

**MARGINAL constructions (less frequent, restricted):**
- "Truth-teller" compound agentive (7)
- "Honest to a fault" intensifying phrase (15)

**UNCERTAIN constructions:**
- None explicitly marked as uncertain in data

**Key constraints observed:**
- Lexical restrictions on negation strategy (synthetic forms not available for all bases)
- Subject-type constraints on adjective selection in attribution
- Verb-specific patterns in inchoatives and causatives (not productive templates)
- Gradability constraints on degree modification and comparison
- Fixed expressions and idioms throughout domain
