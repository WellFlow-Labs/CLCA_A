## 1. Candidate Universals / Strong Tendencies

1. **Simple attribution is broadly available for core truth-related evaluations.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages provide a straightforward predicative way to say that something is true, correct, real, valid, or comparable core statuses.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **All 8 languages** show strong simple attribution for at least central truth-related domains:  
       - **ka** ეს მართალია / ეს სწორია-type patterns  
       - **yo** òtítọ́ ni, ó jẹ́ òtítọ́, ó tọ́  
       - **fi** Se on totta, Väite on epätosi  
       - **eu** Hori egia da  
       - **ta** இது உண்மை, அது சரி  
       - **id** Informasi itu benar, Dokumen ini sah  
       - **tr** Bu bilgi doğru, Bu belge geçerli  
       - **ko** 그것은 사실이다, 네 말이 옳다  
     - This holds across multiple dimensions: factual truth, correctness, authenticity, validity, reliability.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - The exact lexical partition differs greatly by language.  
     - Some languages merge truth/correctness/rightness more than others.  
     - This is strongest for **state attribution**, not for dynamic meanings.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

2. **Negative attribution is also broadly available and often highly productive.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages readily express “not true / not correct / not valid / not genuine,” either by analytic negation, lexical negatives, or both.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **All 8 languages** show robust negative attribution:  
       - **ka** სწორი არ არის, სიმართლეს არ შეესაბამება  
       - **yo** kì í ṣe òtítọ́, kò tọ́, irọ́ ni  
       - **fi** ei ole tosi, ei pidä paikkaansa  
       - **eu** ez da egia, ez da benetakoa  
       - **ta** உண்மையில்லை, சரியல்ல / சரியில்லை  
       - **id** tidak benar, tidak valid, kurang tepat, belum valid  
       - **tr** doğru değil, geçerli değil, asılsız  
       - **ko** 사실이 아니다, 사실과 다르다  
     - The recurrence is both constructional and lexical.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Some languages prefer lexical negatives in some domains (e.g. fake/baseless/invalid) rather than simple negation.  
     - Indonesian stands out for richer scalar negatives (**tidak / kurang / belum**).  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

3. **Adnominal modification is a highly recurrent strategy in the domain.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that truth-related distinctions are commonly realized as noun modifiers: “true story,” “fake document,” “accurate information,” “reliable source,” etc.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly attested in **ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**, and present though less richly represented in **yo**.  
     - Examples include:  
       - **ka** ნამდვილი დოკუმენტი, ყალბი დოკუმენტი, სწორი პასუხი  
       - **fi** aito nahka, virheellinen tieto  
       - **eu** egiazko istorioa, gezurrezko agiria  
       - **ta** உண்மையான தகவல், பொய்யான குற்றச்சாட்டு  
       - **id** jawaban yang tepat, bukti yang sah  
       - **tr** güvenilir kaynak, sahte belge  
       - **ko** 진정한 사랑, 정확한 정보  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Yoruba has weaker F4 representation here, though the broader lexical data supports noun modification.  
     - The modifier systems differ structurally.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

4. **Truth/fact as the object of a verb is a major recurrent constructional pattern.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages often encode truth dynamically through constructions like “tell the truth,” “hide the truth,” “reveal the truth,” “seek the truth,” or “check the facts.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly attested in **all 8 languages**:  
       - **ka** სიმართლის თქმა  
       - **yo** sọ òtítọ́, mọ̀ òtítọ́, fi òtítọ́ hàn  
       - **fi** puhua totta, kertoa totuus, etsiä totuutta  
       - **eu** egia esan, egia bilatu  
       - **ta** உண்மையைச் சொல், உண்மையை மறை  
       - **id** mencari kebenaran, mengungkap kebenaran  
       - **tr** doğruyu söylemek, gerçeği söylemek, hakikati gizlemek  
       - **ko** 진실을 말하다, 진실을 숨기다, 사실을 확인하다  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - The exact objects differ: truth, fact, reality, essence, etc.  
     - Some languages use these more centrally than simple derived verbs.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

5. **Verification/checking is broadly lexicalized and constructionally prominent.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that the truth-related domain includes dedicated ways to check, verify, confirm, establish, or test truth, authenticity, correctness, or validity.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strong in **ka, yo, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**, and also clearly present in **fi**.  
     - Examples:  
       - **ka** სიმართლის დადგენა, ნამდვილობის დადგენა  
       - **yo** rí i dájú, fi ẹ̀rí hàn  
       - **eu** egiaztatu  
       - **ta** சரிபார், உறுதி செய், உறுதிப்படுத்து  
       - **id** memvalidasi, memeriksa keaslian, membenarkan  
       - **tr** doğrulamak  
       - **ko** 사실을 확인하다, 정품 여부를 확인하다  
       - **fi** todistaa, pitää paikkansa, validity-testing constructions  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - The semantic scope varies: some verbs mean verify, others confirm, others establish or prove.  
     - Some languages rely more on nominal constructions than on a single verb.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

6. **Revelation/exposure is a recurrent preferred strategy for truth dynamics.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages often express truth dynamically as something that is revealed, comes out, is brought to light, or is exposed, rather than simply “becomes true.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly attested in **ka, yo, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**.  
       - **ka** სიმართლე გაირკვა / დადგინდა  
       - **yo** fi òtítọ́ hàn  
       - **fi** totuus tulee ilmi  
       - **eu** egia agerian jarri / azaleratu  
       - **ta** உண்மை வெளிவரு / தெரியவரு  
       - **id** terbukti benar / palsu, dipastikan asli  
       - **tr** gerçek ortaya çıkmak, gerçeği yansıtmak/çarpıtmak  
       - **ko** 진실이 드러나다, 실체가 드러나다  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - In some languages this is more central for truth than for correctness or reliability.  
     - Indonesian often uses evidential-result forms rather than a single revelation verb.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

7. **A broad general causative meaning ‘make X true’ is recurrently absent or strongly disfavored.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages do not generally provide a broad, ordinary, productive construction meaning “make a proposition true”; instead they use verify, confirm, reveal, establish, justify, validate, or correct.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Explicitly weak/absent in **ka, yo, eu, ta, ko**.  
     - Also effectively replaced by other strategies in **fi, id, tr**.  
     - G7a marks **factual truth × causative** as **BLOCKED**.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Some languages have root-specific verbs that come close in restricted domains, e.g. “confirm as true,” “prove,” “justify.”  
     - This is about a **broad general causative**, not about all truth-related causative verbs.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

8. **A broad general inchoative meaning ‘become true’ is often weak, restricted, or replaced by other strategies.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that ordinary proposition truth is often not expressed by a simple general “become true” construction; revelation, confirmation, or result-discovery patterns are often preferred.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Explicitly weak or absent as a broad pattern in **ka, yo, eu, ko**.  
     - More available in **fi, id, tr**, and partly **ta**, but still often not the preferred strategy.  
     - G7a marks **factual truth × become/inchoative** as **VARIABLE**.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Finnish is a notable positive case with **tulla todeksi**.  
     - Indonesian and Turkish allow general “become” strategies more readily than several others.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

9. **Authenticity/genuineness is recurrently lexicalized separately from factual truth.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that many languages distinguish truth of statements from genuineness/authenticity of objects, identities, feelings, or relations.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Clear in **ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**, and present in **yo**.  
     - Representative contrasts:  
       - **ka** ნამდვილი vs truth terms  
       - **fi** aito/autenttinen vs totuus/totta  
       - **eu** benetako/egiazko vs egia  
       - **ta** அசல் / நிஜமான vs உண்மை  
       - **id** asli vs benar  
       - **tr** hakiki/sahici/özgün vs doğru/gerçek/hakikat  
       - **ko** 진짜/정품 vs 진실/사실  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Some languages partially merge these domains in certain lexemes, especially **yo**, **eu**, **ta**, **tr**.  
     - The distinction is not equally sharp in every language.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

10. **A fake/forged/counterfeit domain is recurrently lexicalized.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages have dedicated vocabulary for fake, forged, counterfeit, fabricated, or bogus things, especially documents, goods, identities, and reports.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strong in **ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**, and present in **yo**.  
     - Examples: ყალბი, väärennös, gezurrezko/faltsu, போலி, palsu, sahte/taklit/uydurma, 가짜/위작.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Some languages split everyday fake vs formal falsity more strongly than others.  
     - Yoruba’s authenticity/fake domain is less elaborated than in some others.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

11. **Formal validity is often distinguished from ordinary truth.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that many languages distinguish factual truth from legal/procedural/technical validity.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Especially strong in **fi, id, tr, ko**, and also present in **ka**.  
       - **fi** laillinen, lainmukainen, valid-related terms  
       - **id** sah, absah, valid  
       - **tr** geçerli, meşru  
       - **ko** 유효하다, 무효  
       - **ka** მართებული, მართლზომიერი  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - This subsystem is less sharply developed in the supplied Yoruba and Basque materials.  
     - Tamil has procedural confirmation and justice vocabulary, but not as centralized a validity subsystem in the supplied truth-domain set.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

12. **Institutional validity changes are more readily encoded than truth-state changes.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that “become invalid / be declared valid / invalidate” is often more natural and better lexicalized than “become true / make true.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strong in **id, tr, ko, fi**, and partly adjacent in **ta**.  
       - **ko** 무효가 되다 / 무효로 하다  
       - **id** menjadi tidak valid, mengesahkan, mengabsahkan, memvalidasi  
       - **tr** geçerli olmak / geçersiz olmak / geçerlilik kazanmak  
       - **fi** validity/resultative patterns are clearer than broad truth-causatives  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - This is strongest in formal/legal/procedural domains.  
     - Not all languages show equally rich validity dynamics.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

13. **Reliability/trustworthiness is recurrently distinguished from truth itself.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that languages often lexicalize reliability/trustworthiness as a distinct evaluative domain, separate from whether a proposition is true.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Clear in **ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko**; weaker but still present in **yo**.  
     - Examples: სარწმუნო/უტყუარი, luotettava, fidagarri, நம்பகமான, andal, güvenilir, 신뢰성/믿을 만하다.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Yoruba’s lexical split between reliability and other evidential notions is weaker.  
     - Some languages merge reliability and credibility more than others.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

14. **Direct causative or inchoative patterns for reliability are often weak or absent.**  
   - **Statement:** A probable strong tendency is that reliability is usually expressed as a state, an assessment, or something gained/lost/secured, rather than by a simple productive “become reliable” or “make reliable” construction.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Explicitly weak or absent for direct causative/inchoative patterns in **id, tr, ko**, and partly **ta, yo, ka**.  
     - G7a marks **reliability × become** and **reliability × causative** as **BLOCKED**.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - This depends partly on how broadly “reliability” is defined.  
     - Some languages allow periphrastic expressions like “gain trust,” “secure reliability,” “become more reliable.”  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

15. **Essence / underlying nature is recurrently lexicalized as distinct from ordinary truth.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that many languages have a separate lexical area for essence, underlying nature, or true identity, distinct from ordinary proposition truth.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Clear in **ka, fi, ta, id, tr, ko**, and present in **eu**.  
       - **ka** არსი  
       - **fi** olemus  
       - **ta** மெய்ப்பொருள், சாரம், தத்துவம்  
       - **id** hakikat, jati diri  
       - **tr** öz, asıl, hakikat  
       - **ko** 본질, 실체  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - The exact boundary with reality/authenticity varies.  
     - Yoruba does not show as strong a separate essence subsystem in the supplied material.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

16. **Essence is often expressed through revelation/understanding, not direct causation.**  
   - **Statement:** A probable strong tendency is that essence/underlying nature is commonly something revealed, grasped, obscured, or returned to, rather than something directly “made” by a truth-domain causative.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly supported in **ta, tr, ko**, and compatible with **ka, fi, id, eu**.  
     - G7a marks **essence × causative** as **BLOCKED**.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Inchoative behavior is less clear: G7a gives **VARIABLE** for essence × become.  
     - Some languages may allow broader “become” paraphrases outside the core truth-domain evidence.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

17. **Appearance-vs-reality framing is recurrent, but unevenly lexicalized.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that many languages distinguish seeming/appearance from revealed reality, often through constructions like “looks real,” “seems fake,” “the truth came out,” or “the real nature was revealed.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Especially clear in **ko, id, tr, eu, ta**, and also present in **ka, fi**.  
     - Examples:  
       - **ko** 진짜처럼 보이다 / 실체가 드러나다  
       - **id** diduga palsu / terbukti palsu / dipastikan asli  
       - **tr** gerçeğe uygun / hakikatten uzak / gerçek yüzü  
       - **eu** itxurazko vs benetako  
       - **ta** உண்மை வெளிவரு  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Not equally explicit in all languages.  
     - Often constructional rather than lexical.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

18. **Positive and negative sides are often structurally asymmetric.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that negative-side dynamics such as falsify, forge, invalidate, expose as false, or prove fake are often better lexicalized than positive-side dynamics such as make true, make genuine, or make reliable.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly visible in **id, ko, ta, fi, ka**, and also compatible with **tr**.  
     - Examples:  
       - **id** memalsukan strong, but no equally strong “make asli”  
       - **ko** 무효로 하다 strong, but no broad “make true”  
       - **ta** பொய்யாகி போ strong, but no broad “make true”  
       - **fi** paljastua väärennökseksi strong, but no broad positive dynamic for all authenticity adjectives  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - This is a tendency, not a categorical universal.  
     - Some positive-side verbs exist, but often in narrower domains (validate, confirm, correct).  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

19. **A compact lexical ‘liar’ is more common than a compact lexical ‘truth-teller’.**  
   - **Statement:** A probable cross-language tendency is that negative person-denoting forms (“liar”) are more likely to be lexicalized than equally central positive counterparts (“truth-teller”), which are often descriptive or periphrastic.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Clear asymmetry in **eu, ko, ta**, and noted in **ka, fi, yo, id, tr**.  
       - **eu** gezurti  
       - **ko** 거짓말쟁이  
       - **ta** descriptive positive forms rather than compact lexicalized counterpart  
     - G4/G5/G6 repeatedly note this asymmetry.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - The evidence is uneven because agentive vocabulary was not equally rich in all languages.  
     - Some languages have “honest person” or “witness,” but these are not exact truth-teller counterparts.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

20. **Truth, authenticity, correctness, validity, and reliability are often distinct enough to support separate lexicalization, but the degree of separation varies.**  
   - **Statement:** A strong cross-language tendency is that the truth-related domain is often internally partitioned into multiple recurrent subdomains rather than a single undifferentiated “truth” field.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Strongly visible in **fi, id, tr, ka, ko**, and still present in **eu, ta, yo**.  
     - Recurrent subdomains include:
       - factual truth
       - authenticity/genuineness
       - correctness/propriety
       - validity
       - reliability/credibility
       - essence  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:**  
     - Yoruba is a major partial exception because truth/correctness/rightness are more merged.  
     - Tamil has a split system rather than a simple fine-grained partition.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

## 2. Major Outliers

### A. Language-level outliers

1. **Yoruba as a broad merger outlier**
   - **What diverges:** The *tọ́ / òtítọ́* family merges truth, correctness, rightness, and straightness more strongly than in most other languages.  
   - **How strongly:** Strong divergence in the core lexical organization.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Broad; it affects the central truth/correctness field.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely a real structural difference, though more data could refine the boundaries.

2. **Tamil as a split-system outlier**
   - **What diverges:** Tamil shows a strong split between everyday truth vocabulary (**உண்மை / நிஜம்**) and a large literary-philosophical-religious **மெய்** subsystem.  
   - **How strongly:** Strong divergence in internal organization.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Broad; it affects register, semantics, and derivational structure.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely a real structural difference.

3. **Finnish as a dynamic-resultative outlier**
   - **What diverges:** Finnish has especially clear lexicalized resultative patterns like **tulla todeksi** and **osoittautua epätodeksi**, more explicitly than most other languages.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate to strong divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow-to-medium; mainly constructional in the dynamic domain.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real, though still limited to certain subdomains.

4. **Korean as a revelation/procedural outlier**
   - **What diverges:** Korean strongly favors revelation, disclosure, checking, and institutional status change (**드러나다, 밝히다, 확인하다, 무효가 되다**) over broad “become true” patterns.  
   - **How strongly:** Strong divergence in dynamic encoding.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Broad within dynamic constructions.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

5. **Indonesian as a scalar-negative outlier**
   - **What diverges:** Indonesian shows unusually rich and systematic scalar negative contrasts with **tidak / kurang / belum** across many evaluative domains.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Broad across multiple dimensions.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

6. **Georgian as a register-split outlier**
   - **What diverges:** Strong split between everyday truth (**სიმართლე**) and elevated/religious/philosophical truth (**ჭეშმარიტება**), plus a separate strong authenticity axis (**ნამდვილი**).  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate to strong divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Broad in lexical organization and register.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

---

### B. Construction-level outliers

1. **General “become true” constructions**
   - **What diverges:**  
     - More available: **fi, id, tr**, partly **ta**  
     - Weak/absent: **ka, yo, eu, ko**
   - **How strongly:** Strong divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow to one construction type, but cross-cutting.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Probably real, though some languages may allow broader paraphrases outside the supplied evidence.

2. **General “make true” constructions**
   - **What diverges:** Broadly absent across the sample; this is an outlier pattern in the sense of a shared gap rather than a language-specific exception.  
   - **How strongly:** Strong.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow constructionally, but broad cross-linguistically.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real within the current evidence.

3. **Institutional invalidation constructions**
   - **What diverges:** Especially strong and natural in **ko, id, tr**, and to a lesser extent **fi**.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow to formal validity/status domains.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

4. **Appearance-vs-reality constructions**
   - **What diverges:** Especially explicit in **ko** and **id**, clearer than in several others.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate divergence.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow-to-medium; concentrated in one constructional zone.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could partly reflect evidence density, but likely also real.

5. **Agentive asymmetry**
   - **What diverges:** Some languages have strong lexicalized “liar” terms but no equally central “truth-teller” term.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate divergence, but also a broad tendency.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow to person-denoting constructions.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could partly reflect limited evidence, but the asymmetry itself looks real.

---

### C. Dimension-specific outliers

1. **Reliability as a weakly dynamic domain**
   - **What diverges:** Reliability often resists direct “become” and “make” constructions more than validity or correctness do.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow to one semantic dimension.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real, but more evidence would help.

2. **Authenticity as a strongly checked-not-caused domain**
   - **What diverges:** Authenticity is often checked, certified, doubted, or exposed, but not straightforwardly “made” or “become” genuine.  
   - **How strongly:** Strong.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Narrow to one semantic dimension, but cross-linguistically broad.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

3. **Precision/accuracy as an under-modeled but robust dimension**
   - **What diverges:** Some languages sharply lexicalize precision separately from correctness/truth (**ka, id, ko, fi**), while others merge it more.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Dimension-specific.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real, but the cross-language boundaries need more evidence.

4. **Credibility/plausibility as unevenly separated from reliability**
   - **What diverges:** Strongly distinct in **ka, fi, eu, tr**; less sharply separated in **yo, ta, id, ko**.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Dimension-specific.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could be partly evidence-related, but likely reflects real lexical differences.

5. **Essence/underlying nature as variably integrated**
   - **What diverges:** Strongly lexicalized in many languages, but not equally central everywhere, and dynamic behavior is inconsistent.  
   - **How strongly:** Moderate.  
   - **Narrow or broad:** Dimension-specific.  
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Mixed; likely real but under-specified.

## 3. Open Questions

1. **How secure is the candidate tendency that “make true” is broadly blocked?**  
   The current evidence strongly suggests this, but more languages and more targeted elicitation would help confirm whether this is a robust cross-language constraint or partly an artifact of the sampled lexicons.

2. **How secure is the weaker tendency against broad “become true” constructions?**  
   The sample is split: some languages support them, others strongly prefer revelation/verification. More evidence is needed to determine whether the real generalization is “restricted” rather than “rare.”

3. **Should precision/accuracy be treated as a separate cross-language dimension from truth/correctness?**  
   The evidence suggests yes in several languages, but not all. More targeted comparison is needed.

4. **Should formal validity be treated as a distinct semantic dimension rather than a subcase of correctness or legitimacy?**  
   The evidence from Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, Finnish, and Georgian suggests a robust separate subsystem, but broader coverage would help.

5. **How stable is the distinction between reliability and credibility/plausibility?**  
   It is strong in some languages and weak in others. More evidence is needed to determine whether this is a recurrent universal distinction or a language-family-skewed pattern.

6. **How widespread is the asymmetry between lexicalized “liar” and non-lexicalized “truth-teller”?**  
   The current sample suggests a real tendency, but the evidence is uneven and often indirect.

7. **How should appearance-vs-reality be modeled cross-linguistically?**  
   It is clearly important, but sometimes lexical, sometimes constructional, and sometimes distributed across authenticity, truth, and resemblance domains.

8. **How distinct is faithfulness-to-original from resemblance and authenticity?**  
   Turkish shows this especially clearly, but in many languages it is merged with neighboring domains. More evidence is needed.

9. **How should essence/underlying nature be related to truth proper?**  
   The evidence shows recurrent lexicalization, but the exact relation to truth, reality, and authenticity varies considerably.

10. **Are the weak or absent dynamic patterns for authenticity and reliability true structural gaps, or just gaps in the current evidence?**  
    This remains open, especially for languages where the data is rich lexically but thinner constructionally.
