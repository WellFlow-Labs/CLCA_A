# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** प्रमा / *pramā*
2. **Term B:** अप्रमा / *apramā*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramā* / *apramā*
4. **English translation:** valid cognition / non-valid cognition
5. **Description of contrast:** *Pramā* designates a cognition that correctly apprehends its object as it is; *apramā* is its direct morphological negation, designating a cognition that fails to do so. The two terms are explicitly contrasted as the positive and negative poles of the same epistemological evaluative dimension in Nyāya and related literature.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*
2. **Term B:** प्रमाणाभास / *pramāṇābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramāṇa* / *pramāṇābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** valid means of knowledge / semblance of a valid means of knowledge
5. **Description of contrast:** *Pramāṇa* designates a genuinely reliable epistemic source; *pramāṇābhāsa* designates something that merely appears to be such a source but fails to meet the conditions for genuine validity. The contrast is between the genuine instance and its spurious appearance.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological (via *ābhāsa* suffix)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** हेतु / *hetu*
2. **Term B:** हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *hetu* / *hetvābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** genuine logical reason / semblance of a logical reason (fallacy)
5. **Description of contrast:** *Hetu* is a probative logical ground that meets the conditions for valid inference; *hetvābhāsa* is a reason that appears valid but fails to be genuinely probative. The contrast is stated explicitly in the fixed formula *hetvābhāsaḥ na hetuḥ* ("a pseudo-reason is not a genuine reason").
6. **Contrast type:** morphological (via *ābhāsa* suffix)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** प्रामाण्य / *prāmāṇya*
2. **Term B:** अप्रामाण्य / *aprāmāṇya*
3. **Transliteration:** *prāmāṇya* / *aprāmāṇya*
4. **English translation:** validity (the property of being a genuine means of knowledge) / invalidity
5. **Description of contrast:** *Prāmāṇya* names the abstract property that makes a cognition or epistemic source valid; *aprāmāṇya* is its direct morphological negation, naming the property of failing to be valid. Both terms appear in epistemological debates about how validity is established and recognized.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥprāmāṇya*
2. **Term B:** परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
3. **Transliteration:** *svataḥprāmāṇya* / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
4. **English translation:** intrinsic validity (validity established by the cognition itself) / extrinsic validity (validity established by something external)
5. **Description of contrast:** These two terms name opposing positions in a specific intra-school debate: the Mīmāṃsā position that a cognition's validity is self-certifying versus the Nyāya position that validity must be established by external means such as successful action. The two terms are explicitly paired as contrasting positions in epistemological literature.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical (opposing adverbial prefixes *svataḥ* vs. *parataḥ* within the same compound frame)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** सत् / *sat*
2. **Term B:** असत् / *asat*
3. **Transliteration:** *sat* / *asat*
4. **English translation:** being, the real, what genuinely exists / non-being, the unreal, what lacks genuine existence
5. **Description of contrast:** *Sat* and *asat* are the primary ontological binary in Sanskrit philosophical literature. They are explicitly paired in fixed formulas such as *nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ* / *nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ* (Bhagavadgītā 2.16) and in the causation debate (*satkāryavāda* vs. *asatkāryavāda*). The contrast covers both ontological status and evaluative quality (genuine vs. spurious).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** असत्य / *asatya*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *asatya*
4. **English translation:** truth, truthfulness / untruth, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** *Satya* and *asatya* are the primary pair for evaluating speech and conduct as truthful or false in post-Vedic Sanskrit ethical, legal, and philosophical literature. They are regularly contrasted in prescriptive formulas (*satyaṃ brūyāt* / *asatyaṃ na vadet*) and in characterizations of persons (*satyavādin* / *asatyavādin*).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** ऋत / *ṛta*
2. **Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
3. **Transliteration:** *ṛta* / *anṛta*
4. **English translation:** cosmic order, right / falsehood, violation of right order
5. **Description of contrast:** *Ṛta* designates the ordered functioning of the cosmos and the moral-ritual rightness aligned with it; *anṛta* is its negation, designating falsehood and moral wrongness in Vedic and early classical literature. The pair is the older Vedic counterpart to the *satya/asatya* opposition and carries stronger cosmological connotations.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** सत्यवादिन् / *satyavādin*
2. **Term B:** असत्यवादिन् / *asatyavādin*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyavādin* / *asatyavādin*
4. **English translation:** one who speaks truth / one who speaks falsely
5. **Description of contrast:** These agentive compounds characterize persons as reliable or unreliable speakers respectively. They are structurally parallel and are used in ethical and legal literature to evaluate the trustworthiness of testimony and the character of speakers.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

1. **Term A:** सत्कार / *satkāra*
2. **Term B:** असत्कार / *asatkāra*
3. **Transliteration:** *satkāra* / *asatkāra*
4. **English translation:** honor, respectful treatment / disrespect, dishonoring treatment
5. **Description of contrast:** *Satkāra* designates the act of treating someone as they genuinely deserve (honoring); *asatkāra* is its negation, designating disrespectful or dishonoring treatment. The contrast operates in social and ethical contexts rather than epistemological ones, with the *sat/asat* root contributing an evaluative sense of genuine worth.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** सत्कार्यवाद / *satkāryavāda*
2. **Term B:** असत्कार्यवाद / *asatkāryavāda*
3. **Transliteration:** *satkāryavāda* / *asatkāryavāda*
4. **English translation:** the doctrine that the effect pre-exists (as real) in the cause / the doctrine that the effect does not pre-exist in the cause
5. **Description of contrast:** These two terms name directly opposing positions in the Sanskrit philosophical debate about causation: the Sāṃkhya-Vedānta position that the effect is genuinely real in the cause prior to production, versus the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika position that the effect is a new entity not pre-existing in the cause. They are explicitly paired as contrasting doctrinal positions.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** धर्म / *dharma*
2. **Term B:** अधर्म / *adharma*
3. **Transliteration:** *dharma* / *adharma*
4. **English translation:** right conduct, duty, the sustaining order / wrong conduct, violation of duty
5. **Description of contrast:** *Dharma* and *adharma* are the primary pair for evaluating conduct as conforming to or violating right order in Sanskrit ethical, legal, and epic literature. They are regularly contrasted in prescriptive and narrative contexts, and in Vaiśeṣika philosophy they name opposing categories of unseen moral force.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

1. **Term A:** साधु / *sādhu* (in grammatical usage)
2. **Term B:** अपशब्द / *apaśabda*
3. **Transliteration:** *sādhu* / *apaśabda*
4. **English translation:** correct, well-formed word / deviant, incorrect word (solecism)
5. **Description of contrast:** In Sanskrit grammatical literature, *sādhu* designates a word or form sanctioned by Pāṇinian grammar and the usage of the learned, while *apaśabda* designates a form that deviates from that standard. The two terms are the standard contrast pair for grammatical correctness evaluation and are explicitly opposed in grammatical literature.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** मुख्य / *mukhya*
2. **Term B:** गौण / *gauṇa*
3. **Transliteration:** *mukhya* / *gauṇa*
4. **English translation:** primary, literal, direct (use of a term) / secondary, figurative, based on a shared quality
5. **Description of contrast:** In Mīmāṃsā and philosophical grammar, *mukhya* designates the primary or literal sense of a term, while *gauṇa* designates a secondary or figurative use licensed by a quality (*guṇa*) shared between the primary and secondary referents. The two terms are the standard contrast pair for evaluating whether a predication is literal or figurative.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 15

1. **Term A:** मुख्य / *mukhya*
2. **Term B:** औपचारिक / *aupacārika*
3. **Transliteration:** *mukhya* / *aupacārika*
4. **English translation:** primary, literal / figurative, secondary, conventional
5. **Description of contrast:** *Mukhya* and *aupacārika* are contrasted in Mīmāṃsā and philosophical grammar as the primary/literal versus the secondary/conventional mode of linguistic reference. *Aupacārika* emphasizes the conventional or courtesy dimension of secondary use, distinguishing it from *gauṇa* which emphasizes the quality-based dimension, though both contrast with *mukhya*.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** वस्तु / *vastu*
2. **Term B:** आभास / *ābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *vastu* / *ābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** real thing, genuine entity / mere semblance, appearance without genuine reality
5. **Description of contrast:** *Vastu* designates a genuinely existing thing that cognition must correspond to; *ābhāsa* designates something that merely appears to be what it is not. The contrast is stated explicitly in the formula *ābhāso na vastu* ("a semblance is not a real thing") attested in epistemological and Vedāntic literature.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / *pramāṇena siddham*
2. **Term B:** प्रमाणाभावात् / *pramāṇābhāvāt*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramāṇena siddham* / *pramāṇābhāvāt*
4. **English translation:** established by a valid means of knowledge / due to the absence of a valid means of knowledge
5. **Description of contrast:** These two constructions are the standard opposing formulas in Sanskrit philosophical debate for asserting and denying epistemic warrant respectively. *Pramāṇena siddham* asserts that a claim has been established; *pramāṇābhāvāt* dismisses a claim by citing the absence of any valid epistemic ground for it.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** सत्यं ब्रूयात् / *satyaṃ brūyāt*
2. **Term B:** असत्यं न वदेत् / *asatyaṃ na vadet*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyaṃ brūyāt* / *asatyaṃ na vadet*
4. **English translation:** one should speak the truth / one should not speak untruth
5. **Description of contrast:** These two prescriptive constructions express the same ethical obligation from opposite directions — the positive injunction to speak truth and the prohibitive injunction against speaking falsehood. They are structurally parallel (accusative + optative of a speech verb) and are attested as complementary formulas in Dharmaśāstra literature.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** सत्यात् न प्रचलति / *satyāt na pracalati*
2. **Term B:** औचित्यात् च्युतम् / *aucityāt cyutam*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyāt na pracalati* / *aucityāt cyutam*
4. **English translation:** does not deviate from truth / fallen from propriety
5. **Description of contrast:** These two constructions share the same ablative-deviation frame but express opposite outcomes: the first negates departure from a normative standard (steadfast adherence), the second asserts departure from a normative standard (deviation). They are the positive and negative poles of the ablative deviation construction pattern identified in P4a §9.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** प्रमा जायते / *pramā jāyate*
2. **Term B:** मिथ्याज्ञानं निवर्तते / *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramā jāyate* / *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate*
4. **English translation:** valid cognition arises / false knowledge ceases
5. **Description of contrast:** These two constructions represent opposing inchoative transitions in the epistemological-soteriological domain: the arising of valid cognition and the cessation of erroneous cognition. They use different verbs (*jāyate* for arising, *nivartate* for cessation) and are attested in different but related philosophical contexts (Nyāya epistemology and Vedāntic soteriology respectively).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** नासतो विद्यते भावः / *nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ*
2. **Term B:** नाभावो विद्यते सतः / *nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ*
3. **Transliteration:** *nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ* / *nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ*
4. **English translation:** of non-being there is no existence / of being there is no non-existence
5. **Description of contrast:** These two sentences from Bhagavadgītā 2.16 are explicitly paired as a complementary pair of ontological assertions, one about *asat* and one about *sat*. They are regularly cited together as a unit and constitute a constructional opposition in which the subject and predicate terms are inverted across the two sentences.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

1. **Term A:** सत् / *sat*
2. **Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
3. **Transliteration:** *sat* / *mithyā*
4. **English translation:** genuine being, the absolutely real / neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal
5. **Description of contrast:** In Advaita Vedānta, *mithyā* is explicitly defined as neither *sat* nor *asat* (*mithyā na sat na asat*), making it a third ontological category that stands in contrast to *sat* (absolute reality) without being its simple negation. This contrast is specific to the Advaita framework and is not the same as the *sat/asat* binary.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

1. **Term A:** भ्रम / *bhrama*
2. **Term B:** विपर्यय / *viparyaya*
3. **Transliteration:** *bhrama* / *viparyaya*
4. **English translation:** erroneous cognition (Nyāya term) / inverted cognition (Sāṃkhya-Yoga term)
5. **Description of contrast:** Both terms designate the same evaluative category — cognition that apprehends its object as other than it is — but belong to different school vocabularies. The context data explicitly notes that *bhrama* is the standard Nyāya term and *viparyaya* the standard Sāṃkhya-Yoga term for the same evaluative dimension. This is a school-register contrast between near-synonyms rather than a semantic opposition.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

1. **Term A:** सत्यता / *satyatā*
2. **Term B:** सत्यत्व / *satyatva*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyatā* / *satyatva*
4. **English translation:** truthfulness (abstract property, *-tā* form) / truthfulness (abstract property, *-tva* form)
5. **Description of contrast:** Both forms name the same abstract property of being true, derived from the same base with different abstract nominal suffixes. The context data notes that *satyatā* is somewhat more common in ethical contexts and *satyatva* more common in technical philosophical argument, but explicitly states that no consistent semantic distinction between the two suffixes is supported by the data. This is a morphological alternation rather than a semantic opposition.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 25

1. **Term A:** यथार्थ / *yathārtha*
2. **Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
3. **Transliteration:** *yathārtha* / *mithyā*
4. **English translation:** as the object actually is, accurate / false, erroneous, illusory
5. **Description of contrast:** *Yathārtha* evaluates a cognition or description as corresponding to its object as it actually is; *mithyā* evaluates a cognition or object as false, erroneous, or illusory. The two terms are used in overlapping epistemological contexts to evaluate the accuracy of cognition, and their contrast is implicit in the P3 grouping that places both in the cognition-evaluating domain with opposite valences.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 27

1. **Term A:** सत्यमेव जयते / *satyam eva jayate*
2. **Term B:** अनृतेन न जयति / *anṛtena na jayati*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyam eva jayate* / *anṛtena na jayati*
4. **English translation:** truth alone conquers / does not conquer by falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** These two fixed phrases assert the same evaluative conclusion from opposite directions: the ultimate triumph of truth and the ultimate failure of falsehood. The context data explicitly notes that *anṛtena na jayati* is attested as a counterpart to *satyam eva jayate*. They use different syntactic structures (nominative subject + verb vs. instrumental + negative verb) but express a complementary pair of claims.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
