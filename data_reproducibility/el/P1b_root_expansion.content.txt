# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## STEP 1: IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

### Root 1: ἀληθ- (alith-)
**Native form:** αληθ-  
**Transliteration:** alith-  
**P1 participation:** 4 items (αλήθεια, αληθινός, αληθεύω, αναληθής)  
**Core meaning contribution:** "un-hidden, true, real" — the primary positive marker of truth and reality in Greek, built on privative α- + λήθω "to hide"

### Root 2: ψευδ- (pseud-)
**Native form:** ψευδ-  
**Transliteration:** pseud-  
**P1 participation:** 5 items (ψέμα, ψεύτης, ψευδής, ψευδώνυμο, ψευδολογία)  
**Core meaning contribution:** "false, lying, deceptive" — the primary negative marker of falsehood and deception

### Root 3: ὀρθ- (orth-)
**Native form:** ορθ-  
**Transliteration:** orth-  
**P1 participation:** 2 items (ορθός, ορθότητα)  
**Core meaning contribution:** "straight, upright, correct" — indicates correctness, rightness, and proper alignment with standards

## STEP 2: EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1 EXPANSION: ἀληθ- (alith-)

#### 1. αληθώς
**Transliteration:** alithós  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)  
**Translation:** truly, really, indeed  
**Context:** Adverb used to emphasize the truth or reality of a statement in both everyday speech and formal writing. Common in confirmations and emphatic assertions.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. αλήθω
**Transliteration:** alítho  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + -ω (verb ending)  
**Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully  
**Context:** Verb form used in everyday speech and writing to indicate truthful speaking. Less common than αληθεύω but still in regular use.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. αληθοφανής
**Transliteration:** alithofanís  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + φαίνομαι (to appear)  
**Translation:** apparently true, plausible, verisimilar  
**Context:** Used in formal and literary contexts to describe something that appears true or has the semblance of truth, though actual truth may be uncertain. Common in critical analysis and rhetoric.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. αληθολογώ
**Transliteration:** alithológo  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + λόγος (speech) + -ω (verb)  
**Translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth  
**Context:** Formal verb used in literary and elevated discourse to emphasize truthful speech. More emphatic than simple αληθεύω.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 5. αληθοφιλής
**Transliteration:** alithofilís  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + φιλ- (love)  
**Translation:** truth-loving, devoted to truth  
**Context:** Describes persons who value or love truth. Used in philosophical, moral, and character descriptions, particularly in formal or literary contexts.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 6. αλήθεια μου
**Transliteration:** alítheia mou  
**Literal breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + μου (my)  
**Translation:** my truth, honestly, truly (exclamation)  
**Context:** Colloquial expression used as an interjection to emphasize sincerity or truthfulness in everyday speech. Functions as "honestly!" or "really!"  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. στην αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** stin alítheia  
**Literal breakdown:** στην (in the) + αλήθεια (truth)  
**Translation:** in truth, actually, in fact  
**Context:** Common prepositional phrase used in everyday and formal speech to introduce a truthful statement or correction. Equivalent to "actually" or "in fact."  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. η αλήθεια είναι
**Transliteration:** i alítheia íne  
**Literal breakdown:** η (the) + αλήθεια (truth) + είναι (is)  
**Translation:** the truth is, actually  
**Context:** Fixed phrase used to introduce a frank or honest statement, often a correction or clarification. Very common in everyday discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 9. αλήθεια;
**Transliteration:** alítheia?  
**Literal breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) [interrogative intonation]  
**Translation:** really? is that true? seriously?  
**Context:** Single-word question used colloquially to express surprise or seek confirmation. Extremely common in everyday conversation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 10. αληθινά
**Transliteration:** alithiná  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθιν- (true) + -ά (neuter plural/adverbial)  
**Translation:** truly, really, genuinely  
**Context:** Adverb used to emphasize genuineness or reality in everyday and formal speech. Interchangeable with αληθώς in many contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 11. αληθινότητα
**Transliteration:** alithinótita  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθιν- (true) + -ότητα (abstract noun suffix)  
**Translation:** genuineness, authenticity, truthfulness  
**Context:** Abstract noun referring to the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in formal discourse, particularly in discussions of art, documents, or character.  
**[CORE]**

#### 12. αληθοφάνεια
**Transliteration:** alithofáneia  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -εια (noun suffix)  
**Translation:** verisimilitude, plausibility, appearance of truth  
**Context:** Formal noun used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophical discourse to refer to the quality of appearing true or realistic.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 13. αληθολόγος
**Transliteration:** alithológos  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + λόγος (speech)  
**Translation:** truthful person, truth-teller  
**Context:** Formal or literary term for a person who speaks truth. Less common in everyday speech, more typical of elevated or archaic register.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 14. αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + -ής (adjective suffix)  
**Translation:** true, truthful, veracious  
**Context:** Adjective form used in formal and literary contexts, somewhat more elevated than αληθινός. Common in philosophical and formal writing.  
**[CORE]**

#### 15. αληθεύομαι
**Transliteration:** alithévome  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (true) + -εύομαι (passive/middle verb)  
**Translation:** to be verified, to prove true, to turn out to be true  
**Context:** Passive/middle voice form indicating that something is verified or proves to be true. Used in formal and everyday contexts when predictions or claims are confirmed.  
**[CORE]**

### ROOT 2 EXPANSION: ψευδ- (pseud-)

#### 1. ψεύδομαι
**Transliteration:** psévdome  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + -ομαι (middle/passive verb)  
**Translation:** to lie, to tell lies, to deceive  
**Context:** Primary verb for lying in Greek. Used in all registers from everyday conversation to formal discourse. The middle voice emphasizes the speaker's agency in deception.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. ψευδώς
**Transliteration:** psevdós  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)  
**Translation:** falsely, untruthfully, mendaciously  
**Context:** Adverb used in formal and legal contexts to indicate that something is done or stated falsely. Common in accusations and formal denunciations.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. ψευδομαρτυρία
**Transliteration:** psevdomartyría  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + μαρτυρία (testimony)  
**Translation:** false testimony, perjury  
**Context:** Legal term for giving false testimony under oath. Used in judicial and legal contexts, both ancient and modern.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. ψευδομάρτυρας
**Transliteration:** psevdomártyras  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + μάρτυρας (witness)  
**Translation:** false witness, perjurer  
**Context:** Legal term for a person who gives false testimony. Used in judicial contexts and moral condemnation of dishonest witnesses.  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. ψευδεπίγραφος
**Transliteration:** psevdepígrafos  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ἐπιγραφή (inscription, title)  
**Translation:** falsely attributed, pseudepigraphical  
**Context:** Technical term used in literary and biblical scholarship to describe texts falsely attributed to a particular author. Common in academic discourse.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 6. ψευδαίσθηση
**Transliteration:** psevdésthisi  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + αἴσθηση (perception, sensation)  
**Translation:** illusion, false perception, hallucination  
**Context:** Used in psychological, medical, and everyday contexts to describe false perceptions or illusions. Common in discussions of sensory deception.  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. ψευδοεπιστήμη
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistími  
**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ἐπιστήμη (science, knowledge)

**Context:** Used in academic and critical discourse to describe practices or beliefs that falsely claim scientific status. Common in skeptical and scientific writing.  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. ψευδοϊστορία

#### 9. ψευδοφιλόσοφος

**Translation:** liar, mendacious person  
**Context:** Formal or literary term for a habitual liar. More elevated register than ψεύτης, used in moral condemnation and formal discourse.

#### 11. ψευδορκία

**Literal breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ὅρκος (oath)

## STEP 3: PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### Root ἀληθ- (alith-) extensions:

**Exclamatory/interjectional uses:** The root appears in purely phatic or emphatic functions (αλήθεια; αλήθεια μου) where truth-value assessment is secondary to expressing surprise, seeking confirmation, or emphasizing sincerity. The semantic contribution shifts from propositional truth to discourse management.

**Appearance vs. reality:** In αληθοφανής and αληθοφάνεια, the root combines with "appearance" to create terms specifically about verisimilitude rather than actual truth, marking a domain where truth and appearance are explicitly distinguished.

### Root ψευδ- (pseud-) extensions:

**Scientific taxonomy:** The root appears extensively in technical scientific terminology (ψευδοπόδιο, ψευδομεταμόρφωση, ψευδοκυοφορία, ψευδάργυρος) where it marks resemblance or superficial similarity rather than deliberate deception. The semantic contribution shifts from moral/epistemic falsity to morphological or functional similarity.

**Aesthetic/stylistic judgment:** In ψευδοκλασικός, the root marks stylistic inauthenticity in art rather than propositional falsity, extending into aesthetic evaluation.

**Medical/perceptual domains:** Terms like ψευδαίσθηση, ψευδοδιάγνωση, and ψευδοκύηση apply the root to perceptual errors and medical conditions where falsity is not intentional but phenomenological or diagnostic.

### Root ὀρθ- (orth-) extensions:

**Physical/spatial orientation:** The root frequently marks literal physical straightness or uprightness (ορθοστασία, ορθόπνοια, ορθογώνιος, ορθοποδώ) rather than correctness in truth-value. The spatial meaning appears to be primary or co-equal with the correctness meaning.

**Medical/anatomical terminology:** Extensive use in medical contexts (ορθοπεδικός, ορθόπνοια, ορθοστασία) where the root marks physical correction or alignment rather than epistemic correctness.

**Geometric/mathematical usage:** In ορθογώνιος and ορθογώνιο, the root marks right angles, a purely geometric property without obvious connection to truth or correctness beyond mathematical precision.

**Religious/ideological orthodoxy:** In ορθόδοξος, ορθοδοξία, ορθοπραξία, the root marks conformity to established doctrine or practice rather than correspondence to reality, shifting from epistemic to social/institutional correctness.

**Rational/logical domains:** In ορθολογισμός and ορθολογικός, the root marks rationality and logical thinking, extending from correctness to a broader cognitive domain of reason.
