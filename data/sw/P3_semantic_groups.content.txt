# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## IDENTIFIED USAGE-BASED GROUPINGS

---

### GROUP 1: Factual Accuracy and Correctness

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions used when assessing whether statements, information, or representations correspond to facts or meet standards of accuracy. These terms appear in contexts of verification, confirmation, and correction, particularly in administrative, educational, and informational discourse. They contrast with terms indicating error or inaccuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- **ukweli** (truth, reality, fact) — used for factual accuracy
- **sahihi** (correct, accurate, right, proper, authentic)
- **kweli** (true, truth, real, really, indeed)
- **-a kweli** (of truth, true, genuine, real)
- **madhubuti** (reliable, accurate, precise, firm, trustworthy)
- **thibitisha** (to confirm, to verify, to establish, to prove)
- **ushahidi** (evidence, testimony, witness, proof)
- **hoja** (argument, proof, evidence, reason)
- **dhahiri** (clear, evident, obvious, apparent, manifest)
- **hakika** (certain, sure, definite, indeed, really)
- **sawa** (correct, right, okay, equal, same, appropriate)
- **kamili** (complete, perfect, whole, entire, accurate)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **ni sahihi** (it is correct)
- **ukweli wa mambo** (the truth of the matter)
- **thibitisha ukweli** (confirm the truth)
- **ushahidi madhubuti** (reliable evidence)
- **kwa hakika** (certainly, surely)
- **ni dhahiri kwamba** (it is clear that)

---

### GROUP 2: Falsehood and Deception

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to characterize statements, representations, or behaviors as deliberately false or misleading. These terms appear prominently in moral condemnation, accusations of dishonesty, and warnings about deception. They often involve intentionality or moral judgment beyond simple factual incorrectness.

**Representative expressions:**
- **uongo** (lie, falsehood, untruth)
- **-a uongo** (of falsehood, false, lying, deceptive)
- **mwongo/waongo** (liar/liars)
- **-danganya** (to deceive, to lie, to trick, to cheat, to mislead)
- **mdanganyifu/wadanganyifu** (deceiver/deceivers, liar/liars, trickster/tricksters)
- **udanganyifu** (deception, deceitfulness, trickery, dishonesty)
- **bandia** (fake, artificial, counterfeit, false, imitation)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **ni uongo** (it is a lie)
- **sema uongo** (tell a lie)
- **mtu wa uongo** (lying person)
- **habari za uongo** (false news)
- **uongo mtupu** (pure lie, outright falsehood)
- **danganya kwa maneno** (deceive with words)

---

### GROUP 3: Genuineness and Authenticity

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to distinguish real, authentic, or original items from imitations, counterfeits, or artificial substitutes. These terms are particularly common in commercial contexts, material verification, and assessments of sincerity in relationships or emotions. The focus is on substance and origin rather than propositional truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- **halisi** (genuine, authentic, real, pure, sincere)
- **uhalisia** (authenticity, genuineness, reality)
- **bandia** (fake, artificial, counterfeit, false, imitation) — [overlaps with Group 2]
- **ubandia** (artificiality, fakeness, counterfeit nature)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **dhahabu halisi** (genuine gold)
- **ngozi halisi** (genuine leather)
- **mtu halisi** (genuine person)
- **mapenzi halisi** (genuine love)
- **halisi au bandia** (genuine or fake)
- **kwa uhalisia** (in reality, actually)
- **bidhaa halisi** (genuine products)

---

### GROUP 4: Rights, Justice, and Moral Correctness

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions used in discussions of entitlements, fairness, and moral rightness. These terms appear in legal, political, and ethical discourse where the focus is on what is just, fair, or morally appropriate rather than simply factually accurate. They often involve normative judgments about proper conduct and legitimate claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- **haki** (right, justice, truth, entitlement, fairness)
- **-a haki** (of right/justice, rightful, just, legitimate)
- **adili** (just, fair, equitable, righteous)
- **uadilifu** (justice, fairness, equity, righteousness)
- **hukumu** (judgment, ruling, verdict, sentence)
- **hukumia** (to judge, to pass judgment on, to sentence)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **haki yake** (his/her right)
- **haki za binadamu** (human rights)
- **kwa haki** (justly, rightly, by right)
- **haki na batili** (right and wrong)
- **ni adili** (it is just)
- **kwa uadilifu** (justly, fairly)
- **toa hukumu** (pass judgment)
- **hukumu ya kifo** (death sentence)

---

### GROUP 5: Error and Deviation

**Definition:** This group includes expressions referring to mistakes, errors, or deviations from correct paths or standards. Unlike Group 2 (falsehood and deception), these terms do not necessarily imply intentional wrongdoing but can indicate both factual mistakes and moral transgressions. They appear in contexts of correction, criticism, and moral instruction.

**Representative expressions:**
- **makosa** (mistakes, errors, faults, wrongs)
- **kosa** (error, mistake, fault, wrong)
- **kosea** (to make a mistake, to err, to be wrong, to sin)
- **-potoka** (to go astray, to deviate, to err, to be mistaken)
- **upotovu** (error, deviation, wrongness, perversion)
- **potoa/potosha** (to lead astray, to cause to err, to mislead)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **fanya makosa** (make mistakes)
- **makosa yake** (his/her mistakes)
- **bila makosa** (without mistakes)
- **amepotoka njia** (has gone astray from the path)

---

### GROUP 6: Completeness and Perfection

**Definition:** This group contains expressions emphasizing wholeness, completeness, or perfection in representation or execution. These terms appear when assessing whether something fully meets standards or requirements, often implying accuracy through completeness. They are common in quality assessments and evaluations of thoroughness.

**Representative expressions:**
- **kamili** (complete, perfect, whole, entire, accurate)
- **ukamili** (completeness, perfection, wholeness)
- **kamilisha** (to complete, to perfect, to finish, to make whole)
- **safi** (clean, pure, clear, genuine, perfect)
- **usafi** (cleanliness, purity, clarity)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **ukweli kamili** (complete truth)
- **kwa ukamili** (completely, perfectly)
- **ni kamili** (it is complete)
- **ni safi** (it is clean/pure/good)
- **safi kabisa** (perfectly clean/excellent)

---

### GROUP 7: Clarity and Obviousness

**Definition:** This group comprises expressions indicating that something is clear, evident, or manifest to observation or understanding. These terms appear when the truth or reality of something is plainly visible or easily demonstrable, often used to strengthen arguments by appealing to what should be obvious to all.

**Representative expressions:**
- **dhahiri** (clear, evident, obvious, apparent, manifest)
- **udhahiri** (evidence, manifestation, clarity, obviousness)
- **dhihirisha** (to show, to demonstrate, to manifest, to make evident)
- **ukweli ulio wazi** (obvious truth, clear truth)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **ni dhahiri** (it is clear)
- **ni dhahiri kwamba** (it is clear that)
- **kwa udhahiri** (clearly, evidently)
- **uongo wa dhahiri** (obvious lie)

---

### GROUP 8: Certainty and Confidence

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to convey or assess the degree of certainty, confidence, or definiteness about claims or situations. These terms appear when speakers express or evaluate epistemic confidence, ranging from absolute certainty to doubt. They are common in assertions, confirmations, and hedging.

**Representative expressions:**
- **hakika** (certain, sure, definite, indeed, really)
- **uhakika** (certainty, sureness, definiteness)
- **hakikisha** (to make certain, to ensure, to verify, to confirm)
- **ukweli usio na shaka** (undoubted truth, indisputable truth)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **kwa hakika** (certainly, surely)
- **nina hakika** (I am certain)
- **sina hakika** (I am not certain)
- **bila ya hakika** (without certainty)

---

### GROUP 9: Purity and Cleanliness (Extended to Truth)

**Definition:** This group centers on expressions of cleanliness and purity that extend metaphorically to indicate genuineness, clarity, or freedom from problems in non-physical domains. These terms appear both in literal contexts of physical cleanliness and in figurative uses indicating moral purity, clarity of truth, or quality of correctness.

**Representative expressions:**
- **safi** (clean, pure, clear, genuine, perfect) — [overlaps with Group 6]
- **usafi** (cleanliness, purity, clarity)
- **safisha** (to clean, to purify, to clarify, to make clear)
- **ukweli mtupu** (plain truth, bare truth, naked truth)

**Phrasal patterns:**
- **ni safi** (it is clean/pure/good/fine)
- **kwa usafi** (cleanly, purely, clearly)
- **safi sana** (very clean/very good)

---

## AMBIGUOUS OR OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items belonging to multiple groups:

1. **bandia** (fake, artificial, counterfeit)
   - Appears in Group 2 (Falsehood and Deception) when referring to false representations
   - Appears in Group 3 (Genuineness and Authenticity) when contrasting with genuine materials
   - Usage context determines which aspect is foregrounded

2. **safi** (clean, pure, clear, genuine, perfect)
   - Appears in Group 6 (Completeness and Perfection) for quality assessment
   - Appears in Group 9 (Purity and Cleanliness) for its core meaning
   - Shows extension from physical to abstract domains

3. **kamili** (complete, perfect, whole, entire, accurate)
   - Primarily in Group 6 (Completeness and Perfection)
   - Can overlap with Group 1 (Factual Accuracy) when "kamili" implies accuracy through completeness
   - The connection is through thoroughness rather than direct synonymy

4. **haki** (right, justice, truth, entitlement, fairness)
   - Primarily in Group 4 (Rights, Justice, and Moral Correctness)
   - The "truth" sense occasionally overlaps with Group 1, but usage patterns show this is secondary to the justice/rights meaning

5. **-potoka** (to go astray, to deviate, to err)
   - Primarily in Group 5 (Error and Deviation)
   - Can involve moral deviation (overlapping with Group 4 concerns) or factual error (overlapping with Group 1)
   - The term itself is neutral regarding intentionality

### Items with uncertain or weak grouping:

1. **-enye ukweli** (having truth, truthful, veracious)
   - Could fit in Group 1 (Factual Accuracy) as it describes truthfulness
   - However, it's primarily a grammatical construction rather than a distinct semantic unit
   - Its usage is largely determined by the noun "ukweli" it contains

2. **Phrasal constructions with "kwa + abstract noun"**
   - These (kwa kweli, kwa ukweli, kwa usahihi, etc.) function primarily as adverbial modifiers
   - They inherit their grouping from their base nouns but function differently in discourse
   - They serve more as discourse markers than as independent semantic units

3. **Demonstrative and possessive constructions**
   - Forms like "ukweli huu" (this truth), "haki yake" (his/her right)
   - These are grammatical variations rather than semantically distinct items
   - They inherit grouping from their base forms

---

## ITEMS NOT CLEARLY GROUPED

The following items from P1/P2 do not fit cleanly into the identified groups based on usage patterns:

1. **Copular constructions (ni/si + term)**
   - These are syntactic frames rather than semantic units
   - They appear across all groups as a grammatical pattern
   - Example: "ni kweli," "ni sahihi," "ni uongo," "ni halisi," etc.

2. **Emphatic particles and intensifiers**
   - **kabisa** (completely, entirely)
   - **sana** (very, much)
   - **tu** (only, just)
   - These modify terms from various groups but don't constitute a semantic group themselves

3. **Generic verb-object constructions**
   - Patterns like "sema X" (speak X), "toa X" (give X), "fanya X" (do X)
   - These are productive syntactic patterns rather than fixed semantic units
   - The semantic content comes from the object noun, not the construction itself

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Cross-cutting distinctions observed:

1. **Intentionality**: Group 2 (Falsehood and Deception) strongly implies intentional wrongdoing, while Group 5 (Error and Deviation) can be either intentional or unintentional. Group 1 (Factual Accuracy) is typically neutral regarding intention.

2. **Domain of application**: 
   - Groups 1, 2, 5 apply primarily to propositional content (statements, claims, information)
   - Group 3 applies primarily to material objects and personal sincerity
   - Group 4 applies to normative/moral domains
   - Groups 6, 7, 8, 9 can apply across domains

3. **Evaluative vs. descriptive**: Most groups contain evaluative terms, but Group 7 (Clarity and Obviousness) is more descriptive of epistemic accessibility rather than truth value itself.

4. **Arabic vs. Bantu origins**: Arabic-derived terms (sahihi, haki, adili, hakika, dhahiri, halisi, hukumu, hoja, ushahidi) are distributed across multiple groups, showing full integration into Swahili semantic patterns. Bantu roots (kweli, ongo, safi, kosa, potoka) similarly distribute across groups.

### Asymmetries noted:

1. **Positive vs. negative terms**: There are more distinct terms for varieties of truth and correctness (Groups 1, 3, 6, 7, 8) than for falsehood (primarily Group 2). However, Group 5 (Error and Deviation) provides additional negative evaluation.

2. **Abstract vs. concrete**: Group 3 (Genuineness and Authenticity) shows the strongest connection to material/concrete assessment, while other groups operate more abstractly.

3. **Gradability**: Terms in Groups 6, 7, and 8 show strong gradability (more/less complete, more/less clear, more/less certain), while Groups 2 and 4 show less gradability in practice.

### Patterns not forming distinct groups:

1. **Temporal aspects of truth**: While some constructions reference historical truth (ukweli wa historia) or final judgment (hukumu ya mwisho), these don't form a coherent usage-based group.

2. **Source or authority of truth**: References to divine truth (ukweli wa Mungu), legal truth (ukweli wa kisheria), or scientific truth (ukweli wa kisayansi) appear as modifiers but don't constitute a distinct semantic grouping.

3. **Subjective vs. objective truth**: While "ukweli wa ndani" (inner truth) and "ukweli wa nje" (external truth) exist, this distinction is not productive enough to form a separate group.
