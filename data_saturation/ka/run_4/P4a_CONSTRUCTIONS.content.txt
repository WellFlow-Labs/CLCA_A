# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**NP + ADJ-i (copula optional in present tense)**

The predicate adjective agrees in case with the subject; the present-tense copula aris is frequently omitted in informal speech. The adjective carries the nominative suffix -i.

### Examples
- ეს ამბავი მართალია → es ambavi martalia → "This story is true / correct"
- ეს დოკუმენტი ნამდვილია → es dokumenti namdvilia → "This document is genuine"

### Notes
The copula -ia (3sg enclitic) fuses with the predicate adjective in informal and written registers. Both martali and namdvili function freely in this pattern; č̣ešmariti is also used but is more formal. The pattern is identical across the truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**NP + ara-ADJ-i (copula optional) OR NP + ADJ + ar aris**

Negation is expressed either by the prefix ara- attached to the adjective (lexicalized negation) or by the negative particle ar before the copula aris (syntactic negation). Both strategies are attested.

### Examples
- ეს ინფორმაცია არასანდოა → es inpormacia arasandoa → "This information is unreliable"
- ეს ამბავი მართალი არ არის → es ambavi martali ar aris → "This story is not true / not correct"

### Notes
Lexicalized ara- negation (arasando, arazusti, arасarc̣muno) is productive for adjectives denoting stable properties. Syntactic negation with ar aris is the general strategy and applies to all adjectives. The two strategies are largely interchangeable in everyday speech, though ara- forms carry a slightly more categorical or permanent sense.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**ADJ-i + NOUN (prenominal, uninflected for case in attributive position)**

Georgian attributive adjectives precede the noun and do not inflect for case when modifying a noun; the noun carries the case suffix. The adjective retains its nominative form -i in all attributive positions.

### Examples
- ყალბი დოკუმენტი → q̇albi dokumenti → "a forged document"
- ნამდვილი მეგობარი → namdvili megobari → "a genuine / true friend"

### Notes
All truth-domain adjectives (martali, namdvili, q̇albi, zusti, mcdari, gulc̣rpeli, etc.) follow this pattern without exception. The adjective is invariant in attributive position; only the head noun inflects. This is the dominant pattern for adnominal modification in Georgian.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**NP + ADJ-i + gamodga (discovery/resultative) OR NP + ADJ-i + q̇opila (evidential/retrospective)**

Georgian lacks a dedicated inchoative copula equivalent to "become true/false." The nearest functional equivalents use the resultative verb gamodga ("turned out to be") or the evidential copula q̇opila ("it turns out it was / it was all along"). These express the discovery or retrospective recognition of a state rather than a transition into it.

### Examples
- ეს ამბავი ყალბი გამოდგა → es ambavi q̇albi gamodga → "This story turned out to be false"
- ეს ყალბი ყოფილა → es q̇albi q̇opila → "This was fake all along (evidential, often with surprise)"

### Notes
gamodga (past of gamodis, "comes out, turns out") is the standard verb for the discovery that something has a property. q̇opila is the evidential/reportative past of q̇opa ("to be"), marking that the speaker has inferred or belatedly recognized a state. Both constructions are extremely common in the truth domain. A true inchoative ("become true") is not a natural category in Georgian truth-domain expression.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**შეცდომაში შეყვანა / šecodomashi šeq̇vana ("leading into error") — periphrastic causative of falsity**
**გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba ("falsification") — verbal noun of the causative act of making false**

Georgian has no single morphological causative applied productively to truth-domain adjectives (no form meaning "make-true" or "make-false" built by a causative suffix on martali or q̇albi). The causative meanings are expressed periphrastically.

### Examples
- შეცდომაში შეიყვანა → šecodomashi šeiq̇vana → "He/she misled (someone) / led (someone) into error"
- დოკუმენტების გაყალბება → dokumentebis gaq̇albeba → "falsification of documents / making documents false"

### Notes
šecodomashi šeq̇vana (lit. "leading into mistake") is the standard periphrastic expression for causing someone to hold a false belief. gaq̇albeba (preverb ga- + q̇alb- + -eba) is a verbal noun expressing the completed act of making something false or counterfeit; it is the closest Georgian comes to a morphological causative in this domain, but it is root-specific to q̇alb- and does not generalize across truth-domain adjectives.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**si-ROOT-e (nominal frame) OR ROOT/ADJ + -eba OR ADJ + -oba**

Georgian has three productive nominalization patterns attested in the truth domain:
- **si-...-e** frame: simartle, siq̇albe, sicrue, sizuste
- **-eba** suffix: č̣ešmariteba, sarc̣munoeba, namdviloba (via -oba)
- **-oba** suffix: namdviloba, sandooba, gulc̣rpeloba, avṭenturoba, martebuloba

### Examples
- სიმართლე → simartle → "truth, correctness" (si-marṭ-al-e)
- ნამდვილობა → namdviloba → "genuineness, authenticity" (namdvili + -oba)

### Notes
The si-...-e frame and the -oba suffix are the two most productive patterns in the truth domain. The si-...-e frame tends to produce nouns with a more concrete or situational sense (the truth of a matter, a lie, a falsehood); -oba nominalizations tend to denote stable abstract properties (genuineness, reliability, sincerity). The -eba suffix in this domain (č̣ešmariteba, sarc̣munoeba) produces nouns that are more elevated in register. All three patterns are fully productive.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Dedicated compound or participial form; no single productive agentive suffix applies uniformly**

Georgian agentive nouns in the truth domain are formed by:
- Lexicalized compounds: pirmoṭne (hypocrite), tvaltmaq̇ci (deceiver/fraud)
- cru + ROLE NOUN: cru moq̇me (false witness), cru c̣inasq̇armetq̇veli (false prophet)
- Periphrastic description for "truth-teller" (no single lexicalized form)

### Examples
- ცრუ მოწმე → cru moq̇me → "false witness, perjurer" (cru + witness)
- თვალთმაქცი → tvaltmaq̇ci → "deceiver, fraud, impostor" (lit. "eye-tricker")

### Notes
Georgian does not have a single productive agentive suffix that applies across truth-domain roots to yield forms like "truth-teller" or "liar" as a morphological class. pirmoṭne and tvaltmaq̇ci are lexicalized compounds. The concept "liar" is expressed by the verbal form cruobs (he/she lies) or periphrastically (sicrueze mosaubre — "one who speaks falsehood"); no single agentive noun for "liar" parallel to English "liar" is in common use as a lexicalized item. The concept "truth-teller" has no common single-word expression.

## 8. EVIDENTIAL / RETROSPECTIVE DISCOVERY CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**NP + ADJ-i + q̇opila**

This construction, using the evidential/reportative past copula q̇opila, deserves separate treatment as it is a structurally distinctive Georgian pattern with no direct parallel in the attribution or inchoative patterns above. It expresses the speaker's retrospective discovery or inference that something has a property, typically with a connotation of surprise, disillusionment, or belated recognition.

### Examples
- ყალბი ყოფილა → q̇albi q̇opila → "It was fake all along / it turns out it was fake"
- პირმოთნე ყოფილა → pirmoṭne q̇opila → "He/she turned out to be a hypocrite (evidential)"

### Notes
q̇opila is the evidential/reportative form of the copula, grammatically distinct from the simple past iyo ("was"). It marks that the speaker did not directly witness or previously know the state, but has now inferred or been informed of it. This construction is extremely frequent in the truth domain precisely because the discovery of falsity or inauthenticity is a paradigmatic context for evidential marking in Georgian.

## 9. FORMULAIC CANDOR INTRODUCTION

### Pattern
**NOUN-i rom vtq̇va / NOUN-i gitxra — parenthetical clause introducing a candid statement**

A structurally distinctive pattern in which a truth-domain noun appears in a subordinate or parenthetical clause that frames the following statement as sincere or accurate. This is not a predicative or attributive construction but a discourse-level framing device.

### Examples
- სიმართლე რომ ვთქვა → simartle rom vtq̇va → "To tell the truth / if I'm being honest / truth be told"
- გულწრფელად გეუბნები → gulc̣rpelaد geubnebi → "I'm telling you sincerely / frankly speaking"

### Notes
simartle rom vtq̇va (lit. "if I am to say the truth") is a parenthetical subordinate clause functioning as a discourse marker of candor. It is structurally a conditional/subjunctive clause but functions pragmatically as a hedging or sincerity-signaling device. gulc̣rpelaد geubnebi uses the adverbial form of gulc̣rpeli with a speech verb. Both patterns are extremely common in spoken Georgian and have no single-word equivalent.
