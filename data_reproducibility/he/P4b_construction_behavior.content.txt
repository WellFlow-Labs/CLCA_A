# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## CONSTRUCTION NATURALNESS & USAGE ANALYSIS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | הסיפור אמת | ha-sipur emet | "the story is truth/true" | [CORE] | Extremely common in all registers. Zero copula in present tense is the default pattern. Demonstrative subjects (*ze*, *zot*) are particularly frequent in evaluative contexts. |
| | זה שקר | ze sheker | "that/this is a lie" | [CORE] | Demonstrative + truth-term is the most natural way to evaluate propositions in conversation. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | זה לא נכון | ze lo nakhon | "that's not correct" | [CORE] | *Lo* negation is standard in all spoken contexts. Most frequent pattern for denying truth-value claims. |
| | הדבר אינו אמת | ha-davar eino emet | "the thing is not true" | [MARGINAL] | *Ein/eino* is formal/literary. Rare in conversation but appears in written/formal discourse. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | סיפור אמיתי | sipur amiti | "true story" | [CORE] | Standard N-Adj order with full agreement. Productive across all truth-domain adjectives. |
| | עד שקר | ed sheker | "false witness" | [CORE] | Construct state formations are lexicalized fixed expressions. Not productive—limited to established compounds. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | הדבר נעשה ברור | ha-davar ne'esah barur | "the thing became clear" | [MARGINAL] | True inchoative with truth-terms is uncommon. Change-of-state more naturally expressed through revelation/discovery verbs. |
| | האמת התגלתה | ha-emet hitgaltah | "the truth was revealed" | [CORE] | Verbs of revelation (*hitgalah*, *hitbarar*) are the natural way to express truth emerging. More idiomatic than direct "become" constructions. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | הוא הוכיח שזה אמת | hu hokhiakh she-ze emet | "he proved that it's true" | [CORE] | Periphrastic causatives with *hokhiakh* "prove" are standard. Direct causative stems limited to specific roots. |
| | היא הטעתה אותי | hi hit'atah oti | "she misled me" | [CORE] | Hif'il causatives productive for roots with truth-domain meanings (*hit'ah* "cause to err," *hitsdik* "justify"). |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | אמיתות | amitiyut | "authenticity" | [CORE] | *-ut* suffix highly productive for creating abstract nouns from adjectives. Standard pattern across registers. |
| | אמת | emet | "truth" | [CORE] | Root nouns (*emet*, *sheker*, *tsedek*) are basic vocabulary, not derived forms. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | שקרן | shakran | "liar" | [CORE] | *-an* suffix for habitual agents is productive and common. Often carries negative connotation. |
| | משקר | meshaker | "liar" | [CORE] | Participles as agentive nouns are standard. Slightly more neutral/descriptive than *-an* forms. |
| **8. PREPOSITIONAL ADVERBIALS** | באמת | be-emet | "truly, really" | [CORE] | Extremely frequent in conversation. *be-* + truth-noun is fully productive and highly idiomatic. |
| | בוודאי | be-vadai | "certainly" | [CORE] | Among the most common epistemic adverbials in Hebrew. Used constantly in spoken discourse. |
| **9. COMPLEMENTIZER CONSTRUCTIONS** | נכון ש... | nakhon she-... | "it's correct that..." | [CORE] | Standard pattern for propositional evaluation. Extremely common in all registers. |
| | האמת היא ש... | ha-emet hi she-... | "the truth is that..." | [CORE] | Fixed expression with copula + complementizer. Very frequent discourse marker for introducing corrections or clarifications. |
| **10. VERBAL COLLOCATIONS** | לומר את האמת | lomar et ha-emet | "to tell the truth" | [CORE] | Fixed collocation. *Lomar/ledaber* + *emet/sheker* are the standard verbs for truth-telling/lying. |
| | להטיל ספק | lehatil safek | "to cast doubt" | [CORE] | Semi-fixed collocation. *Lehatil* "cast" specifically collocates with *safek* "doubt." |
| **11. INTENSIFICATION** | נכון מאוד | nakhon me'od | "very correct" | [CORE] | Standard intensification pattern. *Me'od* is the default intensifier in all contexts. |
| | בטוח לגמרי | batuakh le-gamrei | "completely sure" | [CORE] | *Le-gamrei* and *le-khlutin* used for absolute/complete intensification. Very common in epistemic contexts. |
| **12. COMPARISON** | נכון יותר | nakhon yoter | "more correct" | [CORE] | Analytic comparison is standard Hebrew pattern. Fully productive with all truth-domain adjectives. |
| | הכי נכון | ha-khi nakhon | "most correct" | [CORE] | Standard superlative construction. Productive and frequent. |
| **13. EXISTENTIAL CONSTRUCTIONS** | יש ספק | yesh safek | "there is doubt" | [CORE] | Particularly common with *safek* "doubt." Standard way to express uncertainty. |
| | אין ספק | ein safek | "there is no doubt" | [CORE] | Fixed expression for certainty. Extremely frequent in argumentative/assertive discourse. |
| **14. PERSONAL EPISTEMIC CONSTRUCTIONS** | אני בטוח | ani batuakh | "I'm sure" | [CORE] | Among the most frequent constructions in conversational Hebrew. Standard way to express personal epistemic states. |
| | אתה צודק | atah tsodek | "you're right" | [CORE] | Extremely common in dialogue for agreement/disagreement. Core conversational formula. |
| **15. DATIVE EXPERIENCER CONSTRUCTIONS** | דומה לי ש... | domeh li she-... | "it seems to me that..." | [CORE] | Standard hedging construction. Very frequent in polite/tentative assertions. |
| | נראה לי ש... | nir'eh li she-... | "it appears to me that..." | [CORE] | Parallel to *domeh li*. Both are core hedging strategies in Hebrew discourse. |

## ALTERNATE CONSTRUCTIONS ATTESTED

### Attribution Variants
- **Predicate-Subject order**: *emet ha-sipur* "true (is) the story" — [MARGINAL] — Used for emphasis or in poetic/formal contexts
- **Copula in present tense**: *ha-sipur hu emet* "the story it-is truth" — [MARGINAL] — Emphatic or contrastive contexts only

### Negation Variants
- **Prefix negation**: *bilti amin* "unreliable" (lit. "un-reliable") — [CORE] — Lexicalized for certain adjectives; not productive with all terms
- **Lo + participle**: *lo meshaker* "not lying" — [CORE] — Standard negation of verbal forms

### Adverbial Variants
- **le- + abstract noun**: *le-emet* "truly" — [MARGINAL] — Less common than *be-emet*; more formal/archaic
- **Bare noun as adverb**: *vadai* "certainly" (without preposition) — [CORE] — Some terms function as adverbs without preposition

### Complementizer Variants
- **Zero complementizer**: *ani batuakh (Ø) ze nakhon* "I'm sure (that) it's correct" — [MARGINAL] — Possible but less common than overt *she-*
- **ki complementizer**: *ha-emet hi ki...* "the truth is that..." — [MARGINAL] — *Ki* alternates with *she-* in formal/biblical style

## GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

1. **Register variation**: Formal/literary Hebrew shows more use of *ein/eino* negation, *le-* prepositions, and *ki* complementizer. Colloquial Hebrew strongly prefers *lo* negation, *be-* prepositions, and *she-* complementizer.

2. **Demonstrative constructions**: *Ze/zot* + truth-term is the unmarked way to evaluate propositions in conversation, far more common than full NP subjects.

3. **Epistemic constructions**: Personal epistemic constructions (#14) and dative experiencer constructions (#15) are among the most frequent patterns in conversational Hebrew, used constantly for hedging and perspective-marking.

4. **Lexicalized patterns**: Construct state formations (#3), verbal collocations (#10), and prepositional adverbials (#8) show varying degrees of fixedness. Some are fully productive, others are limited to established expressions.

5. **Inchoative gap**: Hebrew lacks a natural "become true/false" construction. Change-of-state in the truth domain is expressed through verbs of revelation, discovery, or clarification rather than direct inchoative marking.
