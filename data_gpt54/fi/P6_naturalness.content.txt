## 1. BECOMING TRUE / REAL / VALID

### Pattern
**tulla todeksi**  
‘become true; come true’

### Examples
- **Uni tuli todeksi.** → “The dream came true.”
- **Pelko tuli todeksi.** → “The fear came true.”

### Notes
This is the clearest common Finnish change-of-state expression in the material for movement into a truth-related state. It is especially natural with hopes, fears, dreams, and predictions. The result state is encoded with the translative **todeksi**.

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / UNTRUE

### Pattern
**osoittautua epätodeksi**  
‘turn out to be false’

### Examples
- **Väite osoittautui epätodeksi.** → “The claim turned out to be false.”
- **Tieto osoittautui epätodeksi.** → “The information turned out to be false.”

### Notes
This pattern is used when later checking, evidence, or events show that something was not true. It is retrospective and verification-oriented. The result state is encoded with the translative **epätodeksi**.

---

## 3. BEING REVEALED AS A FORGERY / IMITATION

### Pattern
**paljastua väärennökseksi**  
‘be revealed as a forgery’

### Examples
- **Asiakirja paljastui väärennökseksi.** → “The document was revealed to be a forgery.”
- **Väärennös paljastui.** → “The forgery was exposed.”

### Notes
This is a dynamic authenticity expression rather than a simple truth-value expression. It is used when an object’s status changes in public knowledge: not that the object itself changes, but that it comes to be identified as false or forged.

---

## 4. TRUTH COMING OUT / BECOMING KNOWN

### Pattern
**totuus tulee ilmi**  
‘the truth comes out’

### Examples
- **Totuus tulee ilmi.** → “The truth comes out.”
- **Lopulta totuus tuli ilmi.** → “In the end the truth came out.”

### Notes
This does not mean that truth itself changes state from false to true. Rather, hidden truth becomes known or visible. It is one of the main dynamic expressions involving **totuus** in the material.

---

## 5. REVEALING / STATING TRUTH

### Pattern
**kertoa totuus / paljastaa totuus / puhua totta**  
‘tell the truth / reveal the truth / speak truthfully’

### Examples
- **Hän kertoi totuuden.** → “He/she told the truth.”
- **Lehti paljasti totuuden.** → “The newspaper revealed the truth.”
- **Lapsi puhui totta.** → “The child told the truth.”

### Notes
These are dynamic event expressions involving truth as content. They do not mean “make something true,” but rather “state” or “disclose” what is true.

---

## 6. SEEKING / ESTABLISHING TRUTH

### Pattern
**etsiä totuutta / totuudenetsintä**  
‘seek the truth / search for truth’

### Examples
- **Toimittaja etsii totuutta.** → “The journalist seeks the truth.”
- **Totuudenetsintä on tärkeää.** → “The search for truth is important.”

### Notes
These expressions encode an ongoing process directed toward truth. They are dynamic in the sense of inquiry, not in the sense of changing an object into a truth-state.

---

## 7. PROVING / DEMONSTRATING

### Pattern
**todistaa jokin**  
‘prove something; demonstrate something’

### Examples
- **Todiste todistaa väitteen.** → “The evidence proves the claim.”
- **Todistusaineisto todistaa asian.** → “The evidentiary material proves the matter.”

### Notes
This is the main lexical verb in the material for causing or establishing acceptance of truth. It does not literally “make” a proposition true; rather, it shows or demonstrates that it is true.

---

## 8. CAUSING SOMETHING TO SEEM CREDIBLE

### Pattern
**tehdä jokin uskottavaksi**  
‘make something believable’

### Examples
- **Hän teki tarinan uskottavaksi.** → “He/she made the story believable.”
- **Selitys tehtiin uskottavaksi.** → “The explanation was made believable.”

### Notes
This is a causative pattern with **tehdä** + translative. It concerns **uskottava** ‘credible, believable’, not truth itself. A thing can be made believable without being true.

---

## 9. JUSTIFYING / ENTITLING

### Pattern
**oikeuttaa jokin**  
‘justify something; entitle’

### Examples
- **Tämä ei oikeuta tekoa.** → “This does not justify the act.”
- **Laki oikeuttaa päätöksen.** → “The law justifies the decision.”

### Notes
This is dynamic in the normative domain. It causes something to count as justified or entitled, not true. It belongs to the **oikeus/oikea** family, but not to factual truth in the narrow sense.

---

## 10. CONSIDERING SOMETHING TRUE / RELIABLE

### Pattern
**pitää jokin totena / pitää jotakuta luotettavana**  
‘consider something true / consider someone reliable’

### Examples
- **Monet pitävät väitettä totena.** → “Many consider the claim true.”
- **Pidän häntä luotettavana.** → “I consider him/her reliable.”

### Notes
This is an evaluative change or stance construction only in a weak sense: it encodes a judgment, not necessarily a real change in the object. It is important because Finnish often uses this pattern instead of a direct causative “make true.”

---

## 11. LOSING TOUCH WITH REALITY

### Pattern
**irtautua todellisuudesta**  
‘detach from reality; lose touch with reality’

### Examples
- **Hän irtautui todellisuudesta.** → “He/she lost touch with reality.”
- **Keskustelu irtautui todellisuudesta.** → “The discussion lost touch with reality.”

### Notes
This is a dynamic movement away from the reality domain. It is one of the clearest “becomes non-real / disconnected from reality” expressions in the material.

---

## 12. CORRESPONDING TO REALITY

### Pattern
**vastata todellisuutta / perustua todellisuuteen**  
‘correspond to reality / be based on reality’

### Examples
- **Kuvaus vastaa todellisuutta.** → “The description corresponds to reality.”
- **Tarina perustuu todellisuuteen.** → “The story is based on reality.”

### Notes
These are not inchoative by themselves, but they encode stable relation to reality. In dynamic contexts they can describe whether something comes to align with reality, but the common expressions in the material are stative/evaluative.

---

## 13. BECOMING LEGALLY OR FACTUALLY ACCEPTABLE

### Pattern
**NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED** for a general pattern meaning  
‘become legal / become reliable / become authentic / become correct’

### Notes
The material does include:
- **tulla todeksi**
- **osoittautua epätodeksi**
- **paljastua väärennökseksi**
- **tehdä jokin uskottavaksi**

But it does **not** provide a similarly clear common dynamic expression for each truth-related adjective such as:
- **laillinen**
- **lainmukainen**
- **luotettava**
- **aito**
- **autenttinen**
- **oikea**
- **paikkansapitävä**

So these should not be generalized beyond the attested patterns.

---

## 14. STABILITY OF TRUTH / FACTUAL CORRECTNESS

### Pattern
**pitää paikkansa / olla totta / olla tosi / olla epätosi**  
‘be true / hold true / be false’

### Examples
- **Se pitää paikkansa.** → “That is true.”
- **Se on totta.** → “That is true.”
- **Väite on epätosi.** → “The claim is false.”

### Notes
These are primarily stable-state expressions rather than change expressions. They are important in this section because Finnish often encodes truth as a state to be assessed, while change is expressed only in a smaller set of specialized constructions.

---

## 15. STABILITY OF AUTHENTICITY / RELIABILITY / CREDIBILITY

### Pattern
**olla aito / olla epäaito / olla luotettava / olla uskottava / olla alkuperäinen / olla identtinen**  
‘be genuine / inauthentic / reliable / credible / original / identical’

### Examples
- **Hymy on aito.** → “The smile is genuine.”
- **Käytös on epäaito.** → “The behavior is inauthentic.”
- **Lähde on luotettava.** → “The source is reliable.”
- **Selitys on uskottava.** → “The explanation is credible.”

### Notes
These are stable evaluative states. The material supports them well as attribution patterns, but does not equally support productive “become X” or “make X” patterns for all of them.

---

## 16. OVERALL BEHAVIOR

### Summary
Finnish, in the provided material, encodes truth-related dynamics mainly through a **small number of lexicalized change/result constructions**, especially:

- **tulla todeksi**
- **osoittautua epätodeksi**
- **paljastua väärennökseksi**
- **totuus tulee ilmi**
- **tehdä jokin uskottavaksi**
- **todistaa jokin**

### Notes
The system is **not uniform** across the whole semantic field. Different subdomains behave differently:

- **truth/falsity**: clear dynamic patterns exist
- **authenticity/forgery**: revelation patterns exist
- **credibility**: causative pattern exists
- **reality**: detachment/correspondence patterns exist
- **legality, reliability, correctness, authenticity in general**: no single common productive “become/make X” pattern is established in the provided material

So the Finnish material favors **specific lexical constructions** over one broad general dynamic template.
