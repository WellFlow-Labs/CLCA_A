# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested derivational morphology for the 28 base forms identified in P1. The P1b root expansion has already generated many derived forms; those are cross-referenced here rather than duplicated. The present step focuses on derivations directly from the P1 base forms themselves. Where P1b has already documented a derivation, this is noted and the entry is not repeated in full. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved without normalization.

---

### 1. Base Form: **true**

**Derived Form: truly**
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ly* (adverbial suffix)
- Natural English translation: in a true manner; genuinely; really
- Usage: Functions as adverb of manner (*truly believe*), intensifier (*truly remarkable*), and epistolary closing (*yours truly*). Documented in P1b Root 2, item 1.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

**Derived Form: truth**
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: OE *trēowth*; *tru-* + *-th* (abstract noun suffix)
- Natural English translation: the property of being true; a true statement or fact
- Usage: The primary nominalization of *true*; independently listed as a P1 base form. Highly productive in further compounds (see base form *truth* below).
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

**Derived Form: trueness**
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ness*
- Natural English translation: the quality of being true or accurate; exactness of alignment
- Usage: Used in technical and engineering contexts (*trueness of a surface*) and occasionally in abstract contexts; less common than *truth* for propositional accuracy. Attested but lower frequency than other *true*-derivatives.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

**Derived Form: truism**
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ism*
- Natural English translation: a statement so obviously true as to be not worth stating; a platitude
- Usage: Documented in P1b Root 2, item 25. Carries a mildly dismissive connotation.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

> **Gap noted:** No attested *\*truer* or *\*truest* functioning as epistemic comparatives in standard usage; *more true* and *most true* are used instead, or *truer* appears only in informal registers. No *\*truify*, *\*truable*, or *\*truation* attested.

- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

**Derived Form: truthiness**
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *truth* + *-i-* (linking vowel) + *-ness*

- Epistemic status: **[CORE]** (within contemporary media and political discourse)

> **Gap noted:** No attested *\*truther* as a neutral agent noun; *truther* exists in informal usage but with specific conspiratorial connotations (e.g., *9/11 truther*) rather than as a general derivation meaning "one who tells the truth" (for which *truth-teller* is used instead). This asymmetry is preserved.

## Summary of Gaps and Asymmetries

The following gaps are noted across the P1 base forms, preserved without normalization:

- **bogus**: no productive derivational morphology attested in standard English
- **counterfeit**: limited productivity; no abstract noun *\*counterfeitness* or adverb *\*counterfeitly* attested
- **spurious**: limited to adverb and abstract noun; no verbal forms attested
- **fake**: no *\*fakeness* or *\*fakely* attested; negation expressed lexically rather than morphologically
- **verisimilitude**: minimal derivational productivity from the English noun form
- **veracity**: minimal derivational productivity; *inveracity* is marginal
- **veracious**: narrow paradigm; no standard negated form
- **bona fide**: no English derivational morphology beyond the Latin nominative *bona fides*
- **apocryphal**: narrow paradigm; *Apocrypha* is a related form but not a derivation from the English adjective
- **genuine**: standard negation is periphrastic (*not genuine*) or uses the semantically adjacent *disingenuous* rather than a prefixed form

These asymmetries reflect genuine patterns in English derivational morphology and are not artifacts of incomplete documentation.
