## 1. Language Inventory

- **ka — Georgian**  
  Richly differentiated field covering everyday truth, elevated truth, genuineness/authenticity, correctness, precision, credibility, justification, justice, resemblance, and essence.

- **yo — Yoruba**  
  Strong overlap between truth, correctness, and rightness around the *tọ́ / òtítọ́* family, alongside separate evidence/witness vocabulary and a distinct moral-justice set around *òdodo*.

- **fi — Finnish**  
  Broadly differentiated system separating truth, reality, genuineness, correctness, error, justice, legality, reliability, credibility, proof, and essence, with many lexicalized derivations.

- **eu — Basque**  
  Core contrasts among truth, genuineness, correctness, falsity, credibility, resemblance, and verification, with many productive derivations from *egia*, *zuzen*, and *gezur*.

- **ta — Tamil**  
  Large field with everyday truth/reality terms, literary-philosophical *மெய்* forms, correctness and error vocabulary, reliability/confirmation terms, authenticity/fake terms, justice/fairness terms, and essence/principle vocabulary.

- **id — Indonesian**  
  Strongly differentiated system for truth/correctness, precision, conformity, fit, authenticity, validity, reliability, resemblance, essence, and propriety, with many productive affixal families.

- **tr — Turkish**  
  Broad lexical spread across truth, reality, authenticity, validity, legitimacy, rightness, aptness, reliability, resemblance, essence, honesty, and falsity, with many derivational families and lexicalized contrasts.

- **ko — Korean**  
  Distinct clusters for truth/fact revelation, falsehood, everyday real/fake, product authenticity, accuracy, correctness, justification, formal validity, trustworthiness, sincerity, essence, and resemblance.

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### Georgian (ka)
Main distinctions are especially clear between **everyday truth/rightness** (*სიმართლე, მართალი*), **elevated/religious/philosophical truth** (*ჭეშმარიტება, ჭეშმარიტი*), **genuineness/authenticity** (*ნამდვილი*), **fake/forged/insincere** (*ყალბი*), **practical correctness** (*სწორი*), **precision** (*ზუსტი*), **credibility/reliability** (*სარწმუნო, უტყუარი, დამაჯერებელი*), **formal validity/appropriateness** (*მართებული*), **justice/lawfulness** (*მართლმსაჯულება, მართლზომიერი*), and separate but related areas for **resemblance** and **essence**. Overlap is frequent: *სიმართლე* also reaches fairness/justice; *მართალი* spans true/right/just; *ნამდვილი* can approach “real nature.”

Derivationally, Georgian shows several strong families: *მართალი → სიმართლე, მართლა, მართებული, მართლმსაჯულება*; *ჭეშმარიტი → ჭეშმარიტება*; *ნამდვილი → ნამდვილად, ნამდვილობა*; *სწორი → სისწორე*; *ზუსტი → სიზუსტე*; *ყალბი → სიყალბე*. A notable asymmetry is that the *მართ-* family extends far beyond truth into governance and law, but those extensions are marked as peripheral in this domain.

Constructionally, Georgian strongly favors **truth-telling expressions**, **discourse markers** (“to tell the truth”), **correspondence-to-truth constructions**, and **verification/establishment nouns** such as “establishing truth/authenticity/correctness/accuracy.” The data explicitly notes no strong general “become true/correct” pattern; instead, dynamic meanings are often expressed through “became clear,” “was established,” or checking/verification expressions.

Oppositions are especially strong for **ნამდვილი vs ყალბი**, **ნამდვილობა vs სიყალბე**, **სწორი vs სწორი არ არის**, **მართებული vs მართებული არ არის**, and scalar contrasts such as **უტყუარი vs სარწმუნო / დამაჯერებელი** and **სისწორე vs სიზუსტე**. There are also repeated register contrasts, especially **სიმართლე** vs **ჭეშმარიტება**.

### Yoruba (yo)
The main lexical center is the *tọ́ / òtítọ́ / òtọ́ / títọ́* cluster, which covers **truth, correctness, rightness, and even straightness**. This creates broad overlap between factual truth and practical/moral rightness. Separate clusters appear for **falsehood** (*irọ́, èké*), **evidence and witness** (*ẹ̀rí, ẹlẹ́rìí*), **certainty/assurance** (*dájú*), **authenticity/documentary verification** (*ojúlówó, ìdánimọ̀*), **justice/righteousness** (*òdodo, olódodo*), and **accuracy/error/matching** (*péye, àṣìṣe, bá mu*). A major visible distinction is between **truth/correctness** and **moral righteousness**, with *òdodo* not simply functioning as factual truth.

Derivationally, the strongest family is the *tọ́* family, producing *òtítọ́, òtọ́, títọ́, ìtọ́sọ́nà,* and several directional/reference forms. Another clear family is *rí → ẹ̀rí → ẹlẹ́rìí*. But many core items such as *irọ́, èké, ojúlówó, péye* do not show rich derivational expansion in the supplied material. This creates a noticeable asymmetry: some semantic areas are morphologically dense, others are mostly lexical.

Constructionally, Yoruba strongly prefers **predicative truth/falsehood expressions** (*jẹ́ òtítọ́, kì í ṣe òtítọ́, irọ́ ni*), **truth as object of verbs** (*sọ òtítọ́, mọ̀ òtítọ́, fẹ́ òtítọ́, fi òtítọ́ hàn*), and **evidential constructions** (*ní ẹ̀rí, kò sí ẹ̀rí, fi ẹ̀rí hàn*). The data repeatedly notes the absence of a clearly supported dedicated “become true/false/right/authentic” pattern; dynamic meanings are instead handled by showing, evidencing, ensuring, or predicating states.

Oppositions are strongest for **òtítọ́ vs irọ́**, **òtítọ́ ni vs irọ́ ni**, **jẹ́ òtítọ́ vs kì í ṣe òtítọ́**, **tọ́ vs kò tọ́**, **ojúlówó vs èké**, **péye vs àṣìṣe**, and **ìwà rere vs ìwà búburú**. There is also a recurring contrast between **truth** and **justice/righteousness**: *òtítọ́* vs *òdodo*.

### Finnish (fi)
Finnish shows clear lexical separation among **truth of statements** (*totta, tosi, totuus, epätosi, pitää paikkansa*), **reality/actuality** (*todellinen, todellisuus*), **genuineness/sincerity** (*aito, autenttinen, epäaito*), **originality/authenticity of objects** (*alkuperäinen, väärennös, jäljitelmä*), **correctness/properness** (*oikea, oikein*), **error/incorrectness** (*väärä, virheellinen*), **justice/fairness/rights** (*oikeus, oikeudenmukainen*), **legal conformity** (*laillinen, lainmukainen, oikeudellinen*), **reliability** (*luotettava*), **credibility/plausibility** (*uskottava*), **proof/evidence** (*todistaa, todiste, todistaja*), **technical truth terms** (*totuusarvo, totuusehto*), **identity/sameness** (*identtinen*), and **essence** (*olemus*). Overlap exists, but the lexical divisions are relatively fine-grained.

Derivationally, Finnish is especially rich. Major families include *tosi/totta → totuus, todellinen, todellisuus, todella, epätosi, todistaa, todiste, todistaja*; *aito → aidosti, aitous, epäaito*; *oikea → oikein, oikeus, oikeudenmukainen, oikeutettu, oikeuttaa, oikeutus*; *luotettava → luotettavuus*; *uskottava → uskottavuus*. A notable asymmetry is that some domains have extensive derivational networks, while others rely more on separate lexical items.

Constructionally, Finnish strongly supports **predicative attribution**, **truth-as-object constructions** (*puhua totta, kertoa totuus, etsiä totuutta*), **clausal truth-evaluation** (*On totta, että ...*), **essive complement constructions** (*pitää jokin totena*), and several clear **dynamic/resultative patterns**: *tulla todeksi*, *osoittautua epätodeksi*, *paljastua väärennökseksi*, *tehdä jokin uskottavaksi*. The data also notes that dynamic behavior is not uniform across the field: some subdomains have clear change-of-state patterns, others do not.

Oppositions are strong for **totta/tosi vs epätosi**, **aito vs epäaito**, **oikea vs väärä**, **alkuperäinen vs jäljitelmä**, **aito vs väärennös/jäljitelmä**, and **luotettava vs uskottava**. There are also usage contrasts such as **laillinen vs lainmukainen** and **väärä vs virheellinen**.

### Basque (eu)
Basque distinguishes **truth as statement status/disclosed fact** (*egia* and its constructions), **genuine/real/authentic things** (*egiazko, benetako, autentiko*), **adverbial truth/sincerity markers** (*egiaz, benetan, zinez*), **correctness/propriety** (*zuzen, egoki*), **error/falsity/lying** (*oker, faltsu, gezur*), **fabricated/bogus public objects** (*gezurrezko* constructions), **reliability/credibility** (*fidagarri, sinesgarri, sinetsi*), **sameness/resemblance** (*antzeko, berdin, identiko*), and **verification** (*egiaztatu* family). There is also a separate area for **appearance vs reality** through *itxurazko*.

Derivationally, Basque shows strong productive families around *egia* (*egiazko, egiaz, egiazkotasun, egiaztatu, egiaztapen, egiaztatze, egiaztatagiri*), *zuzen* (*zuzentasun, zuzendu, zuzentze, zuzenketa, zuzentzaile, zuzenbide*), and *gezur* (*gezurrezko, gezurretan, gezurti*). A notable asymmetry is that some very common items such as *benetako, fidagarri, sinesgarri* do not show equally rich derivational expansion in the supplied data.

Constructionally, Basque strongly favors **copular attribution**, **truth/lie light-verb constructions** (*egia esan, gezurra esan*), **truth-assessment clauses** (*egia da ...; egia ote den*), **emphatic degree constructions** (*egia hutsa, gezur borobila*), and **lexical verbs for verification/correction** (*egiaztatu, zuzendu*). The data explicitly says no common dedicated inchoative “become true/false” pattern was securely identified; dynamic meanings are instead expressed through verification, correction, revelation, or truth-seeking.

Oppositions are especially clear for **egia vs gezur**, **egiazko vs gezurrezko**, **egiazko/benetako vs itxurazko**, **zuzen vs oker**, **egia esan vs gezurra esan**, and the parallel emphatic pairs **egia hutsa vs gezur hutsa**, **egia borobila vs gezur borobila**, **egia erdi vs gezur erdi**.

### Tamil (ta)
Tamil has a broad distinction between **everyday truth/reality** centered on *உண்மை* and *நிஜம்*, **literary/philosophical/religious truth/reality** centered on *மெய்*, **falsehood/lying** (*பொய்*), **correctness/propriety/okayness** (*சரி*), **error/fault** (*தவறு*), **certainty/confirmation/reliability** (*உறுதி, நம்பகமான*), **matching/correspondence** (*ஒத்த, ஒற்றுமை*), **authentic vs fake objects/documents** (*அசல், போலி, மூலம்*), **justice/fairness/righteousness** (*நீதி, நியாயம், தர்மம்*), and **essence/principle** (*தத்துவம், சாரம், மெய்ப்பொருள்*). A major internal split is between ordinary *உண்மை* vocabulary and elevated *மெய்* vocabulary.

Derivationally, Tamil shows strong adjective and abstract-noun formation: *உண்மை → உண்மையான, உண்மைத்தன்மை, உண்மையில், உண்மையாக, உண்மையில்லை, உண்மையற்ற*; *மெய் → மெய்யான, மெய்மை, மெய்ப்பொருள், மெய்யியல், மெய்ஞ்ஞானம், மெய்யுணர்வு*; *சரி → சரியான, சரியாக, சரியில்லை, சரிபார், சரிசெய்*; *தவறு → தவறான*; *நம்பகமான → நம்பகத்தன்மை*. A notable asymmetry is the very large *மெய்* family, much of which extends into poetics, spirituality, grammar, and technical vocabulary beyond ordinary truth evaluation.

Constructionally, Tamil strongly favors **bare attribution without overt copula**, **truth/falsity speech constructions** (*உண்மையைச் சொல், பொய் சொல்*), **emergence/revelation constructions** (*உண்மை வெளிவரு, உண்மை தெரியவரு*), **correction/fixing constructions** (*சரிசெய்*), and **confirmation constructions** (*உறுதி செய், உறுதிப்படுத்து*). The data repeatedly notes that Tamil often handles truth dynamics through **emergence, concealment, acknowledgment, acceptance, and knowledge change**, rather than through a single general “become true” verb. For correctness, *சரி ஆகு* is especially strong.

Oppositions are strong for **உண்மை vs பொய்**, **உண்மையான vs பொய்யான**, **சரி vs தவறு**, **சரியான vs தவறான**, **அசல் vs போலி**, and several negated forms such as **உண்மையில்லை**, **சரியில்லை**, **சரியல்ல**. There is also a notable contrast between **truth revelation** and **truth concealment**.

### Indonesian (id)
Indonesian distinguishes **general truth/correctness** (*benar, betul, kebenaran*), **confirming/justifying/correcting** (*membenarkan, membetulkan*), **precision/accuracy** (*tepat, akurat*), **targeting/timing/practical fit** (*tepat sasaran, tepat waktu*), **conformity** (*sesuai*), **everyday fit/match** (*cocok*), **authenticity/genuineness** (*asli, keaslian*), **fake/falsified** (*palsu*), **validity/legitimacy** (*sah, absah, valid*), **reliability** (*andal*), **resemblance** (*mirip*), **essence/identity** (*hakikat, jati diri, sejati*), and **propriety/warrantedness** (*patut*). Overlap is present, but the field is strongly differentiated.

Derivationally, Indonesian shows extensive affixal productivity: *benar → kebenaran, membenarkan, pembenaran, sebenarnya*; *betul → membetulkan, pembetulan*; *tepat → ketepatan*; *sesuai → kesesuaian, menyesuaikan, penyesuaian*; *cocok → kecocokan, mencocokkan, pencocokan*; *asli → keaslian*; *palsu → memalsukan, pemalsuan*; *sah → mengesahkan, pengesahan*; *absah → keabsahan, mengabsahkan, pengabsahan*; *valid → validitas, validasi, kevalidan*; *andal → keandalan, mengandalkan*; *mirip → kemiripan*. A notable asymmetry is that some expected causatives are explicitly marked as uncertain or absent, such as a common “make accurate” from *akurat*.

Constructionally, Indonesian strongly favors **bare predicate attribution**, **negated/scalar predicate patterns** (*tidak, kurang, belum*), **propositional complement frames** (*benar bahwa ...*), **conformity/comparison frames** (*sesuai dengan, cocok dengan, mirip dengan*), **evidential/evaluative passives** (*dianggap, dinyatakan, dipastikan, diduga, terbukti + ADJ*), and **menjadi + predicate** for change of state. Dynamic meanings are especially clear for validity, conformity, targeting, and falsification; authenticity is often handled by checking/confirming rather than “becoming genuine.”

Oppositions are strong for **benar vs tidak benar / kurang benar**, **tepat vs tidak/kurang tepat**, **tepat sasaran vs tidak/kurang/belum tepat sasaran**, **sesuai vs tidak/kurang sesuai**, **cocok vs tidak/kurang cocok**, **asli vs palsu**, **sah vs tidak sah**, **absah vs tidak absah**, **valid vs tidak/belum valid**, **akurat vs tidak/kurang akurat**, **andal vs tidak/kurang andal**, **mirip vs tidak mirip**, and **patut vs tidak/kurang patut**.

### Turkish (tr)
Turkish distinguishes **factual truth/correctness** (*doğru, gerçek, hakikat*), **verification/checkability** (*doğrulamak, doğrulanmak*), **reliability/credibility** (*güvenilir, inandırıcı*), **validity/applicability** (*geçerli*), **legitimacy/justification** (*meşru, haklı*), **appropriateness/aptness** (*yerinde, isabetli*), **genuineness/authenticity** (*hakiki, sahici, özgün, gerçek*), **fake/fabricated/imitation** (*sahte, taklit, uydurma, asılsız*), **faithfulness to original/source** (*sadık, aslına uygun*), **essence/core point** (*asıl, öz*), **realism** (*gerçekçi*), and **honesty/frankness** (*dürüst*). There is substantial overlap, especially around *doğru*, *gerçek*, and *hakikat*.

Derivationally, Turkish shows many productive families: *doğru → doğruluk, doğrulamak, doğrulama, doğrulanmak*; *gerçek → gerçekten, gerçeklik, gerçekçi, gerçekçilik, gerçek dışı*; *hakikat → hakiki, hakikaten*; *sahici → sahicilik*; *özgün → özgünlük, özgünleşmek*; *geçerli → geçerlilik, geçersiz*; *meşru → meşruiyet, gayrimeşru*; *haklı → haklılık, haksız, haksızlık*; *isabetli → isabetsiz*; *dürüst → dürüstlük, dürüstçe*. A notable asymmetry is that some change/causative forms exist only for a few items (*sahteleşmek, özgünleşmek, dürüstleşmek*) and are not general across the field.

Constructionally, Turkish strongly favors **zero-copula attribution**, **değil** negation, **truth-object verb constructions** (*gerçeği söylemek, çarpıtmak, gizlemek*), **partial-truth constructions** (*gerçek payı, doğruluk payı*), **conformity-to-reality constructions** (*gerçeğe uygun, hakikatten uzak*), and several dynamic strategies: **olmak**, **çıkmak**, **doğrulamak/doğrulanmak**, and **kazanmak/yitirmek** with abstract nouns like *meşruiyet* or *inandırıcılık*. The data explicitly notes that Turkish often uses lexical verbs and periphrastic patterns rather than one uniform causative template.

Oppositions are strong for **doğru vs yanlış**, **haklı vs haksız**, **geçerli vs geçersiz**, **meşru vs gayrimeşru**, **isabetli vs isabetsiz**, **asıl vs asılsız**, **gerçek vs sahte**, **özgün vs taklit**, **sahici vs sahte**, and **gerçeği söylemek vs gerçeği çarpıtmak**. There are also repeated contrasts between **appearance** and **revealed reality**.

### Korean (ko)
Korean distinguishes **truth/fact revelation and concealment** (*진실, 사실, 실체* constructions), **falsehood/lying/false reporting** (*거짓, 허위*), **everyday real vs fake** (*진짜, 가짜*), **product/artifact authenticity** (*정품, 위작, 모사*), **accuracy/exactness** (*정확하다*), **correctness/rightness/properness** (*올바르다, 옳다*), **justification/legitimacy/soundness** (*정당하다, 타당하다*), **formal validity/invalidity** (*유효하다, 무효*), **trustworthiness** (*신뢰하다, 믿을 만하다*), **sincerity/genuineness of feeling or stance** (*진실하다, 진실성, 진정성, 참되다*), **essence/underlying nature** (*본질, 실체*), and **resemblance/copying** (*닮다, 흡사하다, 모사*). A notable split is between everyday *진짜/가짜* and more formal authenticity terms like *정품, 위작*.

Derivationally, Korean shows many Sino-Korean noun/adjective families: *진실 → 진실하다, 진실성*; *참 → 참되다, 참됨*; *진정하다 → 진정한, 진정성*; *사실 → 사실상, 사실적, 사실성, 사실무근*; *정확하다 → 정확성, 정확도, 정확히*; *정당하다 → 정당성*; *타당하다 → 타당성*; *유효하다 → 유효성, 유효기간*; *무효 → 무효하다, 무효화*; *신뢰하다 → 신뢰성, 신뢰도*; *본질 → 본질적, 본질적으로*; *실체 → 실체적*. A notable asymmetry is that some very common items like *진짜* and *믿을 만하다* do not participate in equally rich derivational families.

Constructionally, Korean strongly favors **copular attribution**, **negative attribution with 아니다 or 다르다**, **truth/fact object constructions** (*진실을 밝히다, 사실을 확인하다*), **revelation constructions** (*드러나다*), **binary-status checking with 여부를 확인하다**, **appearance vs reality constructions** (*~처럼 보이다, ~같다*), and **institutional status change with 되다 / ~로 하다**, especially for *무효*. The data explicitly notes that Korean does not strongly support a simple common “become true” or “make true” pattern; instead it prefers revelation, disclosure, verification, and institutional recognition.

Oppositions are strong for **진짜 vs 가짜**, **진실 vs 거짓**, **참 vs 거짓**, **허위 vs 사실**, **사실이다 vs 사실이 아니다**, **유효하다 vs 무효**, **정당하다 vs 타당하다** as a domain contrast, **올바르다 vs 옳다** as a usage contrast, **진실을 밝히다 vs 진실을 숨기다**, and **appearance predicates** vs **실체가 드러나다**.

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring convergences
- Many languages show a recurring distinction between **truth/factuality**, **genuineness/authenticity**, and **correctness/propriety**, even when some words overlap across these areas. This is very clear in Georgian, Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, Tamil, and Basque, and present in Yoruba though with heavier overlap.

- Many languages distinguish **reliability/credibility** from plain truth. This is especially clear in Georgian (*სარწმუნო, უტყუარი, დამაჯერებელი*), Finnish (*luotettava, uskottava*), Basque (*fidagarri, sinesgarri*), Tamil (*நம்பகமான*), Indonesian (*andal* and related forms), Turkish (*güvenilir, inandırıcı*), and Korean (*신뢰성, 믿을 만하다*).

- Many languages have a separate area for **authentic vs fake** objects, documents, or identities. Strong examples appear in Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean; Yoruba also has this area through *ojúlówó* and documentary/evidential vocabulary.

- Many languages prefer **truth as something revealed, checked, confirmed, or established** rather than a simple general “become true” verb. This is especially explicit in Georgian, Yoruba, Basque, Tamil, Turkish, and Korean, and also visible in Finnish and Indonesian through specialized dynamic constructions.

- Many languages have strong **truth-telling constructions** built with “say/tell truth” or equivalent object constructions: Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Turkish, and Korean all show this clearly.

- Many languages distinguish **formal/legal validity** from ordinary truth or correctness. This is especially strong in Georgian, Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean, and also present in Tamil through confirmation/justice/legal-style expressions.

- Many languages include a separate cluster for **essence / true nature / underlying reality**, distinct from ordinary factual truth. This is clear in Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean.

### Major divergences
- Yoruba stands out for the unusually broad *tọ́ / òtítọ́* cluster, where **truth, correctness, rightness, and straightness** are tightly linked, more so than in the other languages.

- Tamil stands out for the strong internal split between **everyday truth vocabulary** (*உண்மை, நிஜம்*) and a large **literary-philosophical-religious *மெய்* system**, which extends far beyond ordinary truth evaluation.

- Finnish stands out for the number of clearly established **dynamic/resultative constructions** such as *tulla todeksi*, *osoittautua epätodeksi*, and *paljastua väärennökseksi*, more explicitly than most other languages.

- Korean stands out for the strong role of **revelation and exposure** constructions (*드러나다, 밝히다, 확인하다*) and for the sharp distinction between **everyday real/fake** (*진짜/가짜*) and more formal authenticity/validity vocabulary.

- Turkish stands out for the breadth of separate lexical areas for **truth, reality, authenticity, validity, legitimacy, aptness, honesty, and faithfulness to an original**, with many partially overlapping but distinct roots.

- Georgian stands out for the especially clear register split between **everyday truth** (*სიმართლე*) and **elevated/religious/philosophical truth** (*ჭეშმარიტება*), plus a separate strong authenticity axis (*ნამდვილი* vs *ყალბი*).

- Basque stands out for the strong productivity of a few central derivational families (*egia, zuzen, gezur*) combined with the explicit absence of a securely identified common “become true/false” pattern.

### Asymmetries, gaps, or irregularities
- In several languages, there is a strong lexical or constructional pattern for **revealing/checking truth**, but no equally strong general pattern for **making something true**. This is explicitly noted for Georgian, Yoruba, Basque, Tamil, Turkish, and Korean, and partly for Indonesian.

- Positive and negative sides are often uneven. Many languages have strong everyday words for **fake/false/lie**, but no equally neat positive counterpart in every subdomain, or vice versa. Examples include Korean’s strong *거짓말쟁이* without a parallel simple “truth-teller,” Georgian’s lack of a clear agentive truth-teller, and Yoruba’s lack of many derivational expansions for falsehood items.

- Some languages show rich derivation in only part of the field. This is especially visible in Yoruba, Basque, Tamil, and Korean, where a few roots generate large families while other equally central meanings remain mostly lexical.

- Several languages show that **appearance-based judgments** (“looks real,” “seems fake,” “seems convincing”) are kept distinct from confirmed truth or authenticity. This is especially clear in Georgian, Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean.

- Some roots extend far outside the truth domain and create peripheral families. Clear cases include Georgian *მართ-* into governance, Yoruba *tọ́* into guidance/reference/care, Tamil *மெய்* into body/grammar/virtuality, Turkish *doğru* into technical terms like direct current, and Korean *사실* into de facto/legal compounds.

- Register splits are strong in a few languages: Georgian (*სიმართლე* vs *ჭეშმარიტება*), Tamil (*உண்மை* vs *மெய்*), Turkish (*gerçek/doğru* vs *hakikat/hakiki*), and Korean (*진짜/가짜* vs formal *허위, 정품, 위작*).

- A few languages show especially strong scalar or softened negative patterns rather than simple binary opposites. This is very visible in Indonesian (*kurang*, *belum*), and also in Finnish and Georgian where contrastive alternatives often mark degree, register, or evidential strength rather than strict contradiction.
