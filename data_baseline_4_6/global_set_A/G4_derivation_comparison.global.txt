# G4 — Cross-Language Derivational and Morphological Strategies in the Truth Domain

---

## 1. Identification of Main Strategies

Working from the documented forms across all eight languages, the following recurring strategies are identifiable in the truth-related domain. These are described in terms of what the data actually shows, without imposing a predetermined typological framework.

**Strategies observed:**
1. Abstract nominalization from adjectival or verbal bases
2. Agentive nominalization (person who lies / person who is truthful)
3. Privative / negative prefix derivation
4. Adverbial derivation from truth-domain adjectives
5. Verbal derivation from nominal or adjectival bases (denominative verbing)
6. Inchoative / change-of-state derivation
7. Causative derivation
8. Compounding: truth root + content noun
9. Compounding: negative element + content noun (falsehood compounds)
10. Reduplication for intensification
11. Relational / adjectival suffix derivation
12. Process / action nominalization (distinct from abstract nominalization)
13. Grammaticalization of truth roots into discourse markers and postpositions
14. Productive prefix systems for reality vs. counterfeit marking

## 3. Narrative Analysis

### Patterns Recurring Across Unrelated Language Families

**Abstract nominalization is universal but morphologically diverse.** Every language in the dataset derives abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives or verbs. The suffixes involved are entirely language-specific: Georgian uses -oba/-eba, Finnish uses -uus/-yys, Basque uses -tasun, Tamil uses -mai/-taṉmai, Indonesian uses ke-...-an, Turkish uses -luk/-lık, Korean uses -seong, and Yoruba uses the base noun directly. Despite this morphological diversity, the functional outcome is consistent: all languages produce abstract nouns denoting the quality of being truthful, honest, correct, or genuine. This pattern is productive in all eight languages and shows no blocked pathways.

**Adverbial derivation from truth-domain adjectives is universal.** All eight languages derive manner adverbials from truth-domain adjectives (meaning "truly," "honestly," "correctly," etc.). Again, the morphological strategies differ: Finnish uses -sti, Basque uses -ki, Tamil uses -āka, Indonesian uses se-...-nya, Turkish uses -ca or ablative -dan, Korean uses -age/-hage, Georgian uses the adverbial case -ad, and Yoruba uses prepositional phrases (ní òtítọ́ "in truth"). The functional pattern is consistent across unrelated families.

**Grammaticalization of truth roots into discourse markers is universal.** All eight languages show at least one truth-domain form that has grammaticalized into a discourse marker introducing corrections, frank statements, or factual information. The forms involved are: Georgian mart'la, Finnish totta puhuen, Basque egia esan, Tamil uṇmaiyil, Indonesian sebenarnya/sejujurnya, Turkish doğrusu/aslında, Korean sasireun, and Yoruba ní òtítọ́. This grammaticalization path—truth root → adverbial → discourse marker—appears to be a cross-linguistically robust tendency, attested across Kartvelian, Niger-Congo, Uralic, language isolate, Dravidian, Austronesian, Turkic, and Koreanic families.

**Light verb constructions for truth-telling and lying are universal.** All eight languages express the acts of telling the truth and lying through light verb constructions combining a truth/falsehood noun with a speech verb, rather than through a single dedicated verb. This pattern holds across all language families represented in the dataset. The specific verbs differ (Georgian t'qva "say," Yoruba sọ "say," Finnish puhua "speak," Basque esan "say," Tamil col "say," Indonesian mengatakan "say," Turkish söylemek "say," Korean malhada "say"), but the structural pattern is consistent.

**Compounding of truth/falsehood roots with content nouns is universal.** All eight languages produce compounds of the form "true X" and "false X" by combining a truth or falsehood root with a content noun. The compounding strategies differ (Finnish uses prefixal compounding with tosi-/vale-, Korean uses Sino-Korean compounds, Georgian uses attributive modification, Yoruba uses N+ADJ order), but the productive pattern of creating domain-specific truth/falsehood compounds is attested in all languages.

### Striking Asymmetries

**The liar/truth-teller agentive asymmetry is the most consistent cross-linguistic finding.** In all eight languages, agentive forms for habitual liars are more lexicalized, more productive, or use more dedicated morphology than corresponding forms for habitual truth-tellers. Georgian maṭq'uara "liar" is a productive agentive; no standard agentive for "truth-teller" is documented. Yoruba apurọ́ "liar" is productive; olótítọ́ "truthful person" uses a different, less agentive morphology. Finnish has no dedicated agentive suffix for either, but the liar concept is more lexicalized. Basque gezurtia "liar" is productive; egiazale "truth-lover" uses a different suffix (-zale "enthusiast") and is less common. Tamil poyyan "liar" uses a masculine agentive suffix; uṇmaiyāḷar "truthful person" uses an honorific/plural suffix. Indonesian pembohong "liar" is productive; no standard agentive for "truth-teller" is documented. Turkish yalancı "liar" is lexicalized; no standard agentive for "truth-teller" is documented. Korean geojinmaljaengi "liar" is productive; no attested *jinsiljaengi "truth-teller" is documented.

This asymmetry is consistent across all eight languages and all language families represented. It suggests that habitual deviation from expected truthfulness is universally marked, while habitual conformity to expected truthfulness is unmarked or expressed through different morphological strategies.

**Saturation and totality expressions are asymmetric.** Several languages show productive expressions for "full of lies" or "riddled with falsehood" (Korean geojittuseongi, Indonesian penuh bohong, Georgian ṭq'uilubralo) but no corresponding expressions for "full of truth." This asymmetry parallels the agentive asymmetry: deceptive behavior is marked as an excess, while truthful behavior is the unmarked default.

**Privative constructions for "without lies" are more productive than constructions for "without truth."** Georgian uṭq'uari "without lie" (= truthful) is productive; no corresponding "without truth" form is documented. Basque gezurrezko "false" is productive; no *egiazko-gabe "without truth" is attested. Korean geojiseobsi "without lies" is attested; *jinsilseobsi "without truth" is not. This asymmetry is consistent with the broader pattern: absence of deception is marked, while absence of truth is not.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**The causative pathway for "making X true" is universally blocked.** No language in the dataset shows a productive morphological or light verb causative construction meaning "to cause a proposition to acquire truth value" (i.e., "to make X true" in the sense of changing its truth-value from false to true). All causative constructions in the truth domain mean "to verify," "to realize/implement," "to confirm," or "to authenticate"—not "to make true." This gap is explicitly noted in the F6 naturalness data for Georgian, Basque, Finnish, Tamil, and Indonesian, and is consistent with the patterns documented for the remaining languages. The gap appears to reflect a conceptual constraint: truth is treated as something discovered or established, not caused.

**The morphological causative is blocked on truth roots in Basque.** The Basque morphological causative suffix -arazi does not productively combine with truth-domain roots. The F6 data explicitly marks *egiaarazi, *gezurarazi, *zuzendarazi, and *okerarazi as uncertain or unattested. This is a language-specific blocking that is not found in Turkish or Finnish, which show productive morphological causatives on truth roots (gerçekleştirmek, toteuttaa).

**Simple verbal derivation for "to lie" is blocked in Basque.** Basque lacks a simple verb *gezurtu "to lie" derived from the falsehood root. The act of lying requires the light verb construction gezurra esan "tell a lie." This blocking is language-specific: Georgian has moṭq'ueba "deception" (verbal noun), Finnish has valehdella "to lie," Indonesian has berbohong "to lie," Turkish has yalan söylemek (light verb, but also yalanlamak "to deny/refute"), and Korean has geojinmareul hada "to lie" (light verb). The Basque blocking of simple verbal derivation from the falsehood root is a single-language observation.

**Inchoative derivation on core truth terms is restricted in most languages.** While Turkish and Finnish show productive morphological inchoatives on truth roots (gerçekleşmek, toteutua), most other languages treat truth as a categorical state that is revealed or established rather than one that is gradually acquired. The F6 naturalness data for Finnish explicitly notes that expressions for propositions "becoming true" as state changes are unnatural; Finnish prefers revelation/verification constructions. Georgian, Basque, Tamil, and Korean show similar restrictions. This suggests that the inchoative pathway on core truth terms (as opposed to reality/actualization terms) is restricted cross-linguistically.

**Gradable / comparative derivation on core truth terms is restricted across all languages.** All eight languages treat core truth terms as categorical rather than gradable. Expressions like "more true," "very true," or "half true" are either unattested, marked as marginal, or expressed through constructions rather than through productive morphological gradation. The F6 data for Finnish, Korean, and Basque explicitly notes this restriction. Precision terms (Georgian zusti, Finnish tarkka, Indonesian akurat) show some gradability, but core truth terms (true/false, real/fake, honest/dishonest) resist degree modification across all languages.

### Productivity, Restriction, Lexicalization, and Unclear Cases

**Productive patterns:**
- Abstract nominalization from truth-domain adjectives (all 8 languages)
- Adverbial derivation from truth-domain adjectives (all 8 languages)
- Compounding of truth/falsehood roots with content nouns (all 8 languages)
- Light verb constructions for truth-telling and lying (all 8 languages)
- Grammaticalization of truth roots into discourse markers (all 8 languages)
- Privative prefix derivation in Georgian (u-), Finnish (epä-), Turkish (-sız), and Yoruba (àì-)
- Inchoative derivation in Turkish (-leş-) and Finnish (-utua, -stua)
- Causative derivation in Turkish (-leştir-), Finnish (-uttaa), and Indonesian (meN-...-kan)
- Falsehood compounding in Korean (geojit+N) and Finnish (vale-)
- Reality compounding in Finnish (tosi+N) and Korean (sil+N)

**Restricted patterns:**
- Morphological causative on truth roots (blocked in Basque; restricted in most languages)
- Inchoative derivation on core truth terms (restricted; most languages prefer revelation constructions)
- Gradable/comparative derivation on core truth terms (restricted across all 8 languages)
- Simple verbal derivation for "to lie" (blocked in Basque; productive in Finnish, Georgian, Indonesian)
- Reflexive self-deception formation (productive in Georgian; constructional in others)

**Lexicalized / fossilized patterns:**
- Turkish hakkında "about/concerning" (postposition grammaticalized from hak "right/truth")
- Turkish doğru as postposition "toward" (grammaticalized from "straight/correct")
- Georgian mart'lmadidebeli "Orthodox Christian" (compound with mart'l- "right/correct")
- Georgian mart'va "management" (semantic extension of mart'l- "correct/direct")
- Indonesian salah satu "one of" (quantifier grammaticalized from salah "wrong")
- Finnish totta kai "of course" (grammaticalized from totta "true")
- Korean sasireun "in fact, actually" (grammaticalized discourse marker from sasil "fact")
- Tamil uṇmaiyil "in truth, actually" (grammaticalized discourse marker from uṇmai "truth")
- Turkish alliterative pairs doğru dürüst, yalan dolan (fixed collocations, not productive)

**Unclear or weakly attested patterns:**
- Saturation suffix in Korean (-tuseongi): productive for geojittuseongi "full of lies" but unclear whether it extends to other truth-domain terms
- Emphatic authenticity suffix in Korean (-baegi): productive for jinjjabaegi/gajjabaegi but limited to this pair in the data
- Reflexive self-deception in languages other than Georgian: constructional expressions are attested but morphological forms are not clearly documented
- Productive prefix system for reality vs. counterfeit in languages other than Finnish: Korean geojit- and eu egiazko- function as modifiers but their prefix status is unclear
- Consistency/coherence vocabulary in Basque, Tamil, and Korean: the data shows limited or no dedicated vocabulary for this dimension, but it is unclear whether this reflects a genuine gap or a documentation gap
