# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
## Language: English (en)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
Copular predication: SUBJECT + *be* + truth-domain ADJ (predicate adjective). Also nominal attribution with abstract noun: SUBJECT + *be* + (the) NOUN.
### Examples
- That is true. → *that is true* → "that is true"
- Her story rings true. → *her story rings true* → "her story seems genuine/credible"
### Notes
Attribution of statement-truth is overwhelmingly copular with *be*; a small set of perception-copula verbs (*ring true*, *seem true*, *sound right*) substitute for *be* to mark apparent rather than asserted truth.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
Two strategies: (a) copular *be* + lexically-prefixed negative ADJ (*untrue*, *false*, *incorrect*, *inaccurate*); (b) copular *be* + sentential negator *not* + positive ADJ.
### Examples
- That is untrue / That is false. → *that is untrue* → "that is not true"
- That is not correct. → *that is not correct* → "that is not correct"
### Notes
The negating prefix is lexically unpredictable (*un-*, *in-*, *im-*, *il-*, *dis-*); independent suppletive antonyms (*false* for *true*, *fake* for *genuine/real*) compete with prefixed forms, and *false/fake* are often preferred over *untrue/*unreal in predication.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION
### Pattern
Attributive ADJ + NOUN (prenominal). Postnominal *proper* exists as a delimiter. Truth-domain adjectives are predominantly prenominal.
### Examples
- a true friend / a false claim → *a true friend* → "a genuine friend / an untrue claim"
- a reliable witness → *a reliable witness* → "a dependable witness"
### Notes
Modification is canonically prenominal; the only attested postnominal use is delimiting *proper* ("the city proper"). Latinate items (*accurate, authentic, valid, credible*) appear freely in ordinary ADJ + N collocations but form no fixed idiomatic units.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE
### Pattern
*come* / *become* / *get* + truth-domain ADJ, expressing entry into a truth-state. Realization sense uses fixed *come true*.
### Examples
- A dream come true. → *a dream come true* → "a wish that has been realized"
- Get real! → *get real* → "become realistic"
### Notes
Inchoative formation is restricted and largely idiomatic: *come true* (actualization, a peripheral sense), *get real* (informal imperative). General *become true/false* is compositional but uncommon; no dedicated inchoative morphology exists.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE (OR ABSENCE)
### Pattern
Causative is expressed by denominal/deadjectival verbs in *-ify*, *-ate*, *-ize*, or by periphrastic *make* + ADJ: "render X true/valid/right."
### Examples
- to falsify the records → *to falsify the records* → "to make the records false"
- to validate / rectify → *to validate* → "to make valid / to put right"
### Notes
A productive causative layer exists via suffixation (*falsify, verify, validate, authenticate, rectify, legitimize, justify*), supplemented by periphrastic *make/render* + ADJ. Notably, native core adjectives (*true, real, right*) lack a simple causative verb and use periphrasis (*make true*) or particle constructions (*true up*).

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
ADJ + abstract-noun suffix: *-th* (*truth*), *-ity* (*validity, authenticity, sincerity, credibility*), *-cy* (*accuracy, legitimacy*), *-ness* (*correctness, falseness*), *-hood* (*falsehood*).
### Examples
- true → truth → *truth* → "the quality of being true"
- valid → validity → *validity* → "the quality of being valid"
### Notes
Nominalization is highly productive but the suffix is lexically selected and unpredictable (*-ity/-cy/-ness/-th/-hood*); several bases support two nominals with different nuance (*falsity* vs *falsehood*; *trueness* [metrology] vs *truth* [factual]).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
(a) Compound *NOUN + -teller* / agentive *-er* on a verb stem; (b) suffixal *-er/-or* on fabricator verbs (*faker, forger, counterfeiter*, *corrector*).
### Examples
- truth-teller → *truth-teller* → "one who tells the truth"
- forger / counterfeiter → *forger* → "one who forges / counterfeits"
### Notes
Positive agentives are compounds (*truth-teller*, also *liar* as the suppletive negative agent); deception agentives are suffixal on fabricator verbs (*faker, forger, counterfeiter*). Most evaluative adjectives (*true, honest, valid*) have no direct agentive noun and require periphrasis ("an honest person").

---

## 8. EVIDENTIAL / BELIEF-WORTHINESS PREDICATION
### Pattern
Copular predication with adjectives marking belief-worthiness without asserting truth: SUBJECT + *be* + (*plausible / credible / reliable / convincing*); also ADJ + N collocations (*credible threat*, *plausible explanation*).
### Examples
- His explanation is plausible. → *his explanation is plausible* → "his explanation is believable"
- a credible witness → *a credible witness* → "a witness worthy of belief"
### Notes
This pattern (drawn from P3 Group 8) is structurally identical to attribution but semantically distinct: it predicates deservedness-of-belief rather than truth. Lexically Latinate and forms no idioms except the fixed term *plausible deniability*.

---

## 9. CANDOR-MARKING DISCOURSE FRAMES
### Pattern
Fixed clause-level parentheticals built on truth/honesty/precision lexemes: *to be honest*, *truth be told*, *to tell the truth*, *to be exact*, *correct me if I'm wrong*.
### Examples
- Truth be told, I forgot. → *truth be told* → "to be honest, I forgot"
- Five, to be exact. → *to be exact* → "five, speaking precisely"
### Notes
These behave as sentence adverbials/hedges, not as predicates, and form a distinct construction class (P3 Group 10). They cluster lexically on the native core (*honest, truth*) plus precision (*exact*).

---

## 10. AUTHENTICATION NOMINAL FRAME
### Pattern
Fixed definite NP asserting genuineness: *the* + ADJ/N + (*article / thing / deal / McCoy*).
### Examples
- the genuine article → *the genuine article* → "the real thing, not an imitation"
- the real McCoy / the real deal → *the real McCoy* → "the genuine item"
### Notes
A near-synonymous cluster of fixed NPs (P3 Group 3) concentrated on *real/genuine*, used to assert that an object/person is not counterfeit. Notable lexical redundancy; no productive equivalent exists for the negative pole (no fixed "*the fake article").
