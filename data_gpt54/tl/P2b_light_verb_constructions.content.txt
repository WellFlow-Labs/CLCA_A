Below is a construction inventory restricted to **multi-word constructions** that are supported by the P1/P1b/P2a material and that **involve a P1 base form directly**. I avoid listing single-word derivations already handled in P2a.

---

## 1. Base Form: **totoo**
**Example:** Totoo ba iyan?  
**Transliteration:** Totoo ba iyan?  
**English translation:** Is that true?  
**Usage notes:** Very common interrogative construction for checking the truth of a statement, report, or claim. Natural in everyday conversation and also usable in more serious discussion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **totoo**
**Example:** Totoo iyan.  
**Transliteration:** Totoo iyan.  
**English translation:** That is true.  
**Usage notes:** Basic predicative construction affirming truth or reality. It is one of the most ordinary ways to confirm a claim in Tagalog.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: **totoo**
**Example:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ako kumbinsido.  
**Transliteration:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ako kumbinsido.  
**English translation:** To tell the truth, I’m not convinced.  
**Usage notes:** Fixed discourse-marking expression used to introduce a candid opinion, admission, or correction. Extremely common in speech and informal writing.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. Base Form: **totoo**
**Example:** Sa katotohanan, mas mahirap ang nangyari kaysa sa inaakala nila.  
**Transliteration:** Sa katotohanan, mas mahirap ang nangyari kaysa sa inaakala nila.  
**English translation:** In fact, what happened was more difficult than they thought.  
**Usage notes:** Formal to semi-formal discourse marker used to introduce clarification, correction, or emphasis. More formal than *sa totoo lang*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: **totoo**
