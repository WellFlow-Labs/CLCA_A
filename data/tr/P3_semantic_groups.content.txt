# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis identifies natural groupings based solely on observed usage patterns in Turkish. Groupings reflect how terms are actually used, contrasted, and distributed across contexts in the language data from P1 and P2. No theoretical framework or universal categories have been imposed.

---

## GROUP 1: Factual Correspondence Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, information, or representations correspond to facts or reality. These terms are primarily used in epistemic contexts to assess the accuracy of claims, the correctness of information, or the truth-value of propositions. They contrast with terms indicating error or falsehood and are commonly used in verification, confirmation, and factual assessment contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- doğru (true, correct)
- doğruluk (truth, correctness)
- doğrulamak (to verify, to confirm)
- doğrulama (verification, confirmation)
- yanlış (wrong, false, incorrect)
- yanlışlık (wrongness, error)
- yanlışlamak (to falsify, to refute)
- doğru bilgi (correct information)
- yanlış bilgi (wrong information)
- doğru çıkmak (to turn out to be true)
- yanlış çıkmak (to turn out to be wrong)
- doğru mu yanlış mı (true or false?)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- doğru: Also used for moral rightness and spatial direction, not just factual correctness
- yanlış anlamak (to misunderstand): Involves cognitive error rather than simple factual incorrectness
- doğru kabul etmek (to accept as true): Involves epistemic stance, not just factual assessment

---

## GROUP 2: Reality and Existence Terms

**Definition:** Terms that distinguish what is real, actual, or genuinely existing from what is imaginary, simulated, fake, or non-existent. These terms focus on ontological status rather than truth-value of statements. They are used to evaluate the authenticity of objects, the reality of situations, and the genuineness of phenomena, often contrasting real with unreal, genuine with counterfeit, or actual with virtual.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçek (real, true, genuine)
- gerçeklik (reality, realness)
- gerçekten (really, truly)
- gerçek olmak (to be real)
- gerçek hayat (real life)
- sahte (fake, counterfeit, false)
- sahtelik (fakeness, falseness)
- sahte olmak (to be fake)
- sahte çıkmak (to turn out to be fake)
- taklit (imitation, counterfeit)
- asıl (original, real, actual)
- özgün (original, authentic, genuine)
- özgünlük (originality, authenticity)
- gerçek mi hayal mi (real or fantasy?)
- gerçek gibi (like real, realistic)
- gerçek dışı (unreal, unrealistic)
- gerçek üstü / gerçeküstü (surreal, surrealistic)
- sanal gerçeklik (virtual reality)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- gerçek: Also used for truth of statements, overlapping with Group 1
- sahte para, sahte belge, sahte imza: Specific to forgery and counterfeiting, may form subgroup
- gerçekçi (realistic): Evaluates representations or attitudes rather than ontological status
- gerçekleşmek (to come true, be realized): Process of becoming real rather than state of being real

---

## GROUP 3: Actualization and Realization Terms

**Definition:** Terms focused on the process of something becoming real or being accomplished, transforming from potential, planned, or imaginary state into actual existence or completion. These terms emphasize the dynamic transition from non-actual to actual rather than static evaluation of reality or truth. Common in contexts of planning, prediction, and accomplishment.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçekleşmek (to come true, to be realized, to materialize)
- gerçekleştirmek (to realize, to accomplish, to carry out)
- gerçekleşme (realization, occurrence)
- gerçekleştirme (realization, implementation)
- gerçeğe dönüşmek (to become reality, to turn into reality)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These terms are derived from *gerçek* (real) but function distinctly from static reality assessment
- May overlap with accomplishment vocabulary beyond truth/reality domain

---

## GROUP 4: Rightness and Justice Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating moral rightness, legal entitlement, justice, and what is due or deserved. These terms combine notions of correctness with moral and legal dimensions, used to assess whether actions are just, whether claims are justified, and whether people receive what they deserve. Central to moral argumentation, legal discourse, and disputes about fairness.

**Representative expressions:**
- hak (right, truth, justice, what is due)
- haklı (right, justified, in the right)
- haksız (wrong, unjust, unjustified)
- haklılık (rightness, justification)
- haksızlık (injustice, wrong, unfairness)
- hak etmek (to deserve, to merit)
- hak vermek (to agree with, to acknowledge someone is right)
- hakkını vermek (to give credit, to do justice to)
- hakkını aramak (to seek one's rights)
- hakkını yemek (to usurp rights, to wrong someone)
- haklı olmak (to be right, to be justified)
- haksız yere (unjustly, wrongly)
- adil (just, fair)
- adaletli (just, fair)
- adalet (justice, fairness)
- hak ve adalet (right and justice)
- haklı çıkmak (to be proven right)
- haksız çıkmak (to be proven wrong)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- hak: Polysemous, also used for legal rights and entitlements beyond moral evaluation
- haklı/haksız: Can evaluate both moral positions and factual correctness, overlapping with Group 1
- hakkaniyet (fairness, equity): More abstract, focuses on fairness principle

---

## GROUP 5: Fundamental Truth and Ultimate Reality Terms

**Definition:** Terms used in elevated, philosophical, or religious discourse to refer to fundamental, ultimate, or absolute truth and reality. These terms carry connotations of depth, essence, and transcendence beyond everyday factual truth or ordinary reality. Common in philosophical, religious, and literary contexts where truth is treated as profound or ultimate.

**Representative expressions:**
- hakikat (truth, reality, essence)
- hakikaten (truly, really, indeed)
- hakiki (real, genuine, true, authentic)
- hakikat payı (grain of truth, element of truth)
- hakikate ermek (to attain truth, to reach reality)
- hakikatin özü (the essence of truth)
- mutlak hakikat (absolute truth)
- mutlak doğru (absolute truth)
- mutlak gerçek (absolute reality)
- Hak (God, The Truth - as divine name)
- Hak Teala (God the Most High)
- hak yolu (the path of truth/righteousness)
- acı gerçek (harsh truth, bitter reality)
- çıplak gerçek (naked truth, bare truth)
- katıksız gerçek (pure truth, unadulterated truth)
- katıksız doğru (pure truth, absolute truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- mutlak (absolute): Modifier that can apply to various concepts, not inherently about truth
- hakikat: Can be used in non-elevated contexts, though typically formal
- Overlap with religious vocabulary where truth becomes theological concept

---

## GROUP 6: Reliability and Trustworthiness Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether persons, sources, information, or objects can be trusted, relied upon, or depended on. These terms assess credibility and dependability rather than direct truth-value or reality. Used for evaluating sources of information, assessing character, and determining whether something merits confidence or trust.

**Representative expressions:**
- güvenilir (reliable, trustworthy, dependable)
- güvenilirlik (reliability, trustworthiness)
- güvenilir olmak (to be reliable)
- güvenilir kaynak (reliable source)
- güvenilir bilgi (reliable information)
- sağlam (sound, solid, reliable, robust)
- sağlamlık (soundness, solidity)
- sağlam kanıt (solid evidence)
- sağlam delil (solid evidence)
- sağlam temel (solid foundation)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- sağlam: Also used for physical solidity, extends metaphorically to reliability
- May overlap with terms for validity and soundness of arguments

---

## GROUP 7: Certainty and Definiteness Terms

**Definition:** Terms indicating the degree of certainty, definiteness, or finality of knowledge, information, or decisions. These terms mark epistemic confidence and the settled or unsettled status of matters. Used to distinguish what is certain from what is uncertain, what is definite from what is provisional, and what is final from what is subject to change.

**Representative expressions:**
- kesin (certain, definite, absolute)
- kesinlik (certainty, definiteness)
- kesinlikle (certainly, definitely, absolutely)
- kesinleşmek (to become definite, to be finalized)
- kesinleştirmek (to finalize, to confirm)
- kesin olmak (to be certain)
- kesin bilgi (certain information, definite knowledge)
- kesin karar (final decision)
- kesin hüküm (final judgment)
- mutlak (absolute, unconditional)
- mutlaka (absolutely, certainly, definitely)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- mutlak: Used for absoluteness in various domains, not just epistemic certainty
- kesin: Can indicate precision/exactness as well as certainty
- Overlap with finality in legal/administrative contexts

---

## GROUP 8: Doubt and Suspicion Terms

**Definition:** Terms indicating doubt, suspicion, or questionable status. These terms mark epistemic uncertainty or grounds for suspicion, used to evaluate claims, persons, or situations as warranting doubt or raising questions. Common in contexts where credibility is questioned or where evidence is insufficient.

**Representative expressions:**
- şüpheli (doubtful, suspicious, dubious, suspect)
- şüphelilik (dubiousness, suspiciousness)
- şüpheli olmak (to be suspicious)
- kuşkulu (doubtful, dubious, questionable)
- kuşkuluk (doubtfulness)
- şüphe (doubt, suspicion)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- şüpheli: Can refer to suspected persons (noun) as well as doubtful quality (adjective)
- Overlap with terms for uncertainty vs. terms for moral suspicion

---

## GROUP 9: Consistency and Coherence Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating internal consistency, logical coherence, or freedom from contradiction. These terms assess whether statements, arguments, or behaviors hold together logically or contain contradictions. Used in logical analysis, argumentation, and evaluation of reliability over time.

**Representative expressions:**
- tutarlı (consistent, coherent)
- tutarlılık (consistency, coherence)
- tutarlı olmak (to be consistent)
- tutarsız (inconsistent, incoherent)
- tutarsızlık (inconsistency, incoherence)
- çelişkili (contradictory, inconsistent)
- çelişkililik (contradictoriness)
- çelişki (contradiction)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- tutarlı: Can refer to behavioral consistency as well as logical consistency
- Overlap with reliability terms (Group 6) when consistency indicates dependability

---

## GROUP 10: Validity and Legitimacy Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether something is valid, in effect, legitimate, or lawful according to established rules, norms, or legal standards. These terms assess conformity to standards of validity (for documents, arguments) or legitimacy (for authority, actions). Common in legal, administrative, and formal contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- geçerli (valid, current, applicable, in effect)
- geçerlilik (validity, effectiveness)
- geçerli olmak (to be valid)
- geçersiz (invalid, ineffective, void)
- geçersizlik (invalidity)
- geçerli sebep (valid reason)
- geçerli mazeret (valid excuse)
- meşru (legitimate, lawful, rightful)
- meşruiyet (legitimacy, lawfulness)
- meşrulaşmak (to become legitimate)
- meşrulaştırmak (to legitimize)
- meşru olmak (to be legitimate)
- meşru müdafaa (legitimate defense, self-defense)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- geçerli: Can indicate temporal validity (currently in effect) or logical validity
- meşru: Specifically legal/political legitimacy, distinct from other validity types
- Overlap with appropriateness terms when validity involves conformity to norms

---

## GROUP 11: Accuracy and Aptness Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether judgments, observations, comments, or actions hit the mark, are appropriate to the situation, or accurately capture what they aim at. These terms assess precision of fit between assessment and reality, or appropriateness of response to context. Used for evaluating the quality of observations, decisions, and responses.

**Representative expressions:**
- isabetli (accurate, apt, appropriate, on-target)
- isabetlilik (accuracy, aptness)
- isabetli olmak (to be accurate)
- isabetli tespit (accurate observation)
- isabetli yorum (apt comment)
- isabetli karar (sound decision)
- isabetsiz (inaccurate, inappropriate, off-target)
- isabetsizlik (inaccuracy, inappropriateness)
- yerinde (appropriate, apt, well-placed, justified)
- yerinde olmak (to be appropriate)
- yerinde bir tespit (an apt observation)
- yerinde bir eleştiri (a justified criticism)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- yerinde: Literally "in its place," extends to appropriateness and justification
- Overlap with correctness terms (Group 1) when accuracy is about factual correctness
- Overlap with appropriateness terms (Group 12) when aptness involves situational fit

---

## GROUP 12: Appropriateness and Conformity Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, statements, or objects are appropriate, suitable, proper, or conforming to standards, norms, or expectations. These terms assess fitness for purpose, conformity to social or technical standards, and propriety of conduct. Used across social, technical, and moral contexts for evaluating suitability.

**Representative expressions:**
- uygun (appropriate, suitable, fitting, conforming)
- uygunluk (appropriateness, suitability, conformity)
- uygun olmak (to be appropriate)
- uygunsuz (inappropriate, unsuitable, improper)
- uygunsuzluk (inappropriateness, unsuitability)
- uygun görmek (to deem appropriate)
- uygun bulmak (to find appropriate)
- düzgün (proper, correct, orderly, decent)
- düzgünlük (propriety, orderliness)
- düzgün olmak (to be proper)
- doğru düzgün (proper, decent, respectable)
- doğru dürüst (proper, decent, honest)
- düzgün dürüst (proper and honest)
- gerçeğe uygun (in accordance with reality, truthful)
- gerçeğe aykırı (contrary to reality, false)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- düzgün: Can indicate physical orderliness as well as social propriety
- doğru düzgün, doğru dürüst: Fixed collocations emphasizing adequacy/propriety
- Overlap with correctness (Group 1) when appropriateness involves being right
- Overlap with aptness (Group 11) when appropriateness involves situational fit

---

## GROUP 13: Deception and Misleading Terms

**Definition:** Terms indicating deliberate deception, misleading representation, or tendency to cause false belief. These terms evaluate intentions to deceive or properties that mislead, distinguishing intentional falsehood from mere error. Used in contexts of fraud, dishonesty, and critical evaluation of potentially deceptive information or appearances.

**Representative expressions:**
- yalan (lie, falsehood, untruth)
- yalan söylemek (to tell a lie, to lie)
- yalancı (liar)
- yalanlamak (to deny, to refute)
- yalan yanlış (false and wrong, nonsense)
- yalan dolan (full of lies)
- yalandan (falsely, pretending)
- yanıltıcı (misleading, deceptive, delusive)
- yanıltıcılık (misleadingness, deceptiveness)
- yanıltıcı olmak (to be misleading)
- yanıltıcı bilgi (misleading information)
- yanıltıcı reklam (misleading advertisement)
- aldatıcı (deceptive, deceitful, misleading)
- sahtekâr (fraud, swindler, counterfeiter)
- sahtekârlık (fraud, swindling)
- sahtecilik (counterfeiting, forgery)
- gerçeği gizlemek (to hide the truth)
- gerçeği çarpıtmak (to distort the truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- yanıltıcı: Can indicate unintentional misleading properties as well as deliberate deception
- sahte: Overlaps with Group 2 (reality terms) but emphasizes deliberate falsification
- yalan vs. yanlış: Distinguishes intentional falsehood from error
- Overlap with fakeness (Group 2) when deception involves counterfeit objects

---

## GROUP 14: Convincingness and Credibility Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating the persuasive power or believability of arguments, evidence, or presentations, focusing on capacity to produce belief rather than objective truth. These terms assess whether something is convincing, credible, or plausible to an audience, regardless of actual truth-value. Used in rhetorical, argumentative, and evaluative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- inandırıcı (convincing, credible, believable, plausible)
- inandırıcılık (convincingness, credibility)
- inandırıcı olmak (to be convincing)
- inandırıcı gelmek (to seem convincing)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- Overlap with reliability (Group 6) when credibility involves trustworthiness
- Overlap with plausibility (Group 15) when convincingness involves reasonableness
- Focus on subjective impression rather than objective assessment

---

## GROUP 15: Reasonableness and Plausibility Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether claims, proposals, or situations are reasonable, sensible, or plausible according to common sense or rational standards. These terms assess conformity to reason and acceptability within normal expectations. Used for evaluating the rationality of proposals, the sensibility of actions, and the plausibility of explanations.

**Representative expressions:**
- makul (reasonable, rational, sensible, plausible)
- makuliyet (reasonableness, rationality)
- makul olmak (to be reasonable)
- makul sebep (reasonable reason)
- makul şüphe (reasonable doubt)
- mantıklı (logical, reasonable, rational)
- mantıklılık (logicality, rationality)
- mantıklı olmak (to be logical)
- mantıksız (illogical, irrational)
- mantıksızlık (illogicality)
- akla yakın (plausible, reasonable, likely)
- akla yakın olmak (to be plausible)
- akla yakın gelmek (to seem plausible)
- gerçekçi (realistic, practical)
- gerçekçilik (realism, realistic approach)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- mantıklı: Specifically logical coherence, may overlap with consistency (Group 9)
- gerçekçi: Can indicate artistic realism (Group 2) or practical realism
- akla yakın: Literally "near to reason/mind," emphasizes plausibility
- Overlap with appropriateness (Group 12) when reasonableness involves suitability

## GROUP 16: Completeness and Exactness Terms

**Definition:** Terms indicating completeness, wholeness, exactness, or precision. These terms evaluate whether something is complete without deficiency, exact without approximation, or perfect without flaw. Used for assessing completeness of information, precision of measurement, and perfection of execution.

**Representative expressions:**
- tam (complete, exact, full, precise)
- tam olmak (to be complete, to be exact)
- tam olarak (exactly, precisely, completely)
- tam anlamıyla (in the full sense, completely)
- tamlık (completeness, exactness)
- tamam (complete, finished, okay)
- tamamlamak (to complete, to finish)
- tamamen (completely, entirely, totally)
- kusursuz (flawless, perfect, faultless)
- kusursuzluk (flawlessness, perfection)
- kusursuz olmak (to be flawless)
- katıksız (pure, unadulterated, absolute)
- katıksızlık (purity, unadulteratedness)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- tam: Can indicate temporal completeness (finished) or spatial/quantitative completeness
- kusursuz: Evaluates perfection, which may be moral or technical
- katıksız: Literally "without additive," extends to purity and absoluteness
- Overlap with absoluteness when completeness involves totality

---

## GROUP 17: Sincerity and Genuineness of Expression Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether expressions, emotions, or behaviors are sincere, heartfelt, or genuinely felt rather than pretended or insincere. These terms assess authenticity of sentiment and honesty of expression, focusing on the relationship between inner state and outer expression. Used for evaluating emotional authenticity and sincerity of communication.

**Representative expressions:**
- samimi (sincere, genuine, heartfelt)
- içten (sincere, heartfelt, genuine)
- sahici (genuine, authentic, real)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- sahici: Colloquial term, overlaps with reality/genuineness (Group 2)
- Limited attestation in corpus, may not form robust independent group
- Overlap with authenticity terms (Group 2) when genuineness involves being real

---

## GROUP 18: Truth Revelation and Discovery Terms

**Definition:** Multi-word constructions focused on the process of truth being revealed, discovered, or brought to light. These expressions emphasize the dynamic emergence of truth from concealment or the active pursuit of truth. Common in investigative, narrative, and explanatory contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçek ortaya çıkmak (the truth comes out, reality emerges)
- gerçek ortaya çıkarmak (to reveal the truth, to uncover reality)
- gerçeğin peşinde olmak (to be in pursuit of truth)
- gerçeğin peşinden koşmak (to chase after the truth)
- hakikat ortaya çıkmak (the truth comes to light)
- gerçeği öğrenmek (to learn the truth)
- gerçeği göstermek (to show the truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These are multi-word constructions rather than single lexical items
- May overlap with verification terms (Group 1) when revelation involves confirmation
- Focus on discovery process rather than static truth assessment

---

## GROUP 19: Truth Concealment and Distortion Terms

**Definition:** Multi-word constructions focused on hiding, concealing, distorting, or denying truth. These expressions indicate active suppression or manipulation of truth rather than simple falsehood. Used in contexts of cover-up, propaganda, and deliberate misrepresentation.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçeği gizlemek (to hide the truth)
- gerçeği saklamak (to conceal the truth)
- gerçeği çarpıtmak (to distort the truth)
- gerçeği inkâr etmek (to deny the truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These are multi-word constructions rather than single lexical items
- Overlap with deception terms (Group 13) when concealment involves deliberate misleading
- Focus on active suppression rather than passive falsity

---

## GROUP 20: Truth Acceptance and Acknowledgment Terms

**Definition:** Multi-word constructions focused on accepting, acknowledging, or coming to terms with truth or reality. These expressions emphasize the epistemic or psychological act of accepting what is true, often in contexts where truth is difficult or unwelcome. Used in contexts of acknowledgment, reconciliation with reality, and epistemic responsibility.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçeği kabul etmek (to accept the truth)
- doğru kabul etmek (to accept as true)
- gerçeği söylemek (to tell the truth)
- gerçeği yansıtmak (to reflect reality)
- hakikati söylemek (to tell the truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These are multi-word constructions rather than single lexical items
- Overlap with truthful speech (Group 21) when acceptance involves verbal acknowledgment
- Focus on epistemic stance rather than simple statement

---

## GROUP 21: Truthful Speech and Communication Terms

**Definition:** Multi-word constructions specifically about speaking truthfully, telling the truth, or communicating honestly. These expressions focus on the speech act of truthful communication rather than the truth-value of statements themselves. Used in contexts of moral instruction, confession, and honest communication.

**Representative expressions:**
- doğru söylemek (to tell the truth)
- doğruyu söylemek (to tell the truth)
- doğrusunu söylemek (to tell the truth, to be frank)
- yalan söylemek (to tell a lie)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These are multi-word constructions rather than single lexical items
- Overlap with deception (Group 13) when speech involves lying
- Focus on speech act rather than content evaluation

## ITEMS NOT CLEARLY GROUPED

The following items do not fit cleanly into the groups above or show significant ambiguity:

### Items with Multiple Group Membership:
- **doğru**: Functions in Group 1 (factual correspondence), Group 4 (moral rightness), and spatial/directional contexts not captured by truth-evaluation groups
- **gerçek**: Functions in Group 2 (reality/existence) and Group 1 (truth of statements)
- **hak**: Functions in Group 4 (rightness/justice) and in legal entitlement contexts beyond moral evaluation
- **sağlam**: Functions in Group 6 (reliability) and in physical solidity contexts

### Grammaticalized or Highly Specialized Items:
- **hakkında** (about, concerning): Grammaticalized postposition, no longer functions in truth-evaluation
- **doğrudan** (directly): Primarily directional/manner adverb, peripheral to truth-evaluation
- **doğrultu** (direction, course): Spatial/directional term, peripheral to truth-evaluation
- **doğru akım** (direct current): Technical electrical term
- **doğru açı** (right angle): Geometric term
- **doğru orantı** (direct proportion): Mathematical term

### Religious/Theological Extensions:
- **Hak** (as divine name): Theological use distinct from truth-evaluation
- **hakkın rahmetine kavuşmak** (to pass away): Death euphemism
- **hak getire** (God forbid): Religious exclamation

### Constructions Requiring Context:
- **gerçek bir** (a real, a true): Intensifying construction, meaning varies by context
- **gerçek sandım** (I thought it was real): Specific to mistaken belief contexts
- **yanlışlıkla** (by mistake, accidentally): Manner adverb indicating unintentionality

### Items with Limited Attestation:
- **uydurma** (fabricated, made-up): Limited attestation in corpus
- **sahici** (genuine, authentic): Colloquial, limited attestation

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Overlapping Domains:
Several groups show systematic overlap:
- Groups 1 (factual correspondence) and 2 (reality/existence) overlap when truth involves correspondence to reality
- Groups 4 (rightness/justice) and 1 (factual correspondence) overlap when being right involves being factually correct
- Groups 11 (accuracy/aptness) and 12 (appropriateness) overlap when accuracy involves situational fit
- Groups 6 (reliability) and 9 (consistency) overlap when reliability involves consistent behavior

### Register and Context Variation:
- Group 5 (fundamental truth) represents elevated/formal register
- Groups 1-2 represent everyday epistemic vocabulary
- Group 10 (validity/legitimacy) is concentrated in legal/administrative contexts
- Group 4 (rightness/justice) spans moral, legal, and everyday argumentative contexts

### Morphological Families:
Several groups are built around morphological families:
- **doğru** family: Primarily in Groups 1, 12, 21
- **gerçek** family: Primarily in Groups 2, 3, 18-20
- **hak** family: Primarily in Groups 4, 5
- **yalan** family: Primarily in Groups 13, 21

### Asymmetries:
- Extensive vocabulary for truth/correctness (Groups 1-2) vs. limited vocabulary for simple falsehood
- Rich vocabulary for deception and misleading (Group 13) vs. limited vocabulary for unintentional error
- Multiple groups for different aspects of appropriateness/correctness (Groups 11-12) showing fine-grained distinctions
- Certainty terms (Group 7) relatively limited compared to truth-value terms

### Multi-word Construction Groups:
Groups 18-21 consist primarily of multi-word constructions rather than single lexical items, suggesting these semantic domains are expressed compositionally rather than through dedicated lexical items.
