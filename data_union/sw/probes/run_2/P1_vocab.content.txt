# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Lexical Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** truth; from Arabic *ḥaqīqī* via borrowing pathway, though direct etymology debated
**Typical English translations:** truth, true, truly, really

Used to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality. Functions both as a noun (*kweli ni kwamba…* "the truth is that…") and as an adverb or intensifier. Appears in legal, moral, and everyday evaluative contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** ukweli
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (noun class 11/abstract) + *kweli*; abstract nominalization of *kweli*
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, accuracy, veracity

The abstract noun form referring to the property or state of being true or accurate. Used in philosophical, legal, and moral discourse (*ukweli wa jambo* "the truth of a matter"). Participates in the same derivational family as *kweli*.

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** uongo
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *ongo* (falsehood, deception)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, untruth

The standard term for a false or deceptive statement. Used in moral condemnation of dishonesty and in legal contexts involving false testimony. Contrasts directly with *ukweli*.

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** halisi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *khāliṣ* (pure, genuine, unmixed)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, pure

Applied to objects, persons, or representations evaluated as authentic rather than counterfeit or adulterated. Common in commercial, aesthetic, and moral contexts (*dhahabu halisi* "genuine gold"). Also used of sincere or authentic character.

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** asili
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *aṣl* (origin, root, foundation)
**Typical English translations:** origin, nature, original, authentic

Refers to the fundamental nature or original form of something. Used to evaluate whether something is in its original or natural state, and in philosophical contexts concerning essential nature (*asili ya binadamu* "the nature/essence of humanity").

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** bandia
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** artificial, fabricated; possibly from *banda* (shed, makeshift structure) with suffix, though etymology uncertain
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation, false

Used to describe objects, documents, or representations that are not genuine. Appears in legal contexts (counterfeit currency, forged documents) and in evaluations of inauthenticity more broadly.

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** sahihi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ* (correct, sound, valid)
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, valid, exact

Applied to statements, calculations, documents, and procedures evaluated for correctness or accuracy. Also used as a verb (*kusahihisha*, to correct) and in legal contexts to mean valid or properly executed.

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** makosa
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *ma-* (plural prefix, class 6) + *kosa* (error, fault, mistake)
**Typical English translations:** errors, mistakes, faults, wrongs

Plural of *kosa*; used in evaluations of incorrect statements, faulty conduct, or moral wrongdoing. Appears in legal, moral, and epistemic contexts (*bila makosa* "without errors/faults").

**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** kosa
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** error, fault, sin; from Arabic *qaṣwa* or related root, though direct etymology debated
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fault, wrong, sin

The singular form; used to evaluate a single act, statement, or judgment as incorrect or morally wrong. Participates in productive derivation: *kukosa* (to err, to lack), *makosa* (plural), *kukosea* (to wrong someone).

**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** haki
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqq* (right, truth, due)
**Typical English translations:** right, justice, truth, entitlement, due

A semantically broad term covering both the sense of what is correct or true and the sense of a legitimate entitlement or right. Used in legal, moral, and philosophical contexts (*haki yake* "his/her right"; *haki ni haki* "right is right/truth is truth").

**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** haki ya
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *haki* (right/truth) + *ya* (of, genitive linker)
**Typical English translations:** the right of, the truth of, it is true that

A fixed genitive construction used to assert the truth or legitimacy of something. Functions in evaluative assertions (*haki ya Mungu* "by God's truth"; used as an oath or strong assertion of truth).

**12.**
**Form:** kweli kweli
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** reduplication of *kweli* (truth)
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in truth

A reduplicated intensified form of *kweli*, used to emphasize the truth or genuineness of an assertion. Common in spoken Swahili as an emphatic marker of sincerity or accuracy.

**[CORE]**

**13.**
**Form:** dhahiri
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ẓāhir* (apparent, manifest, evident)
**Typical English translations:** evident, manifest, clear, obvious

Used to evaluate a claim or situation as self-evidently true or plainly visible. Appears in argumentative and legal contexts where evidence is assessed as unambiguous (*ni dhahiri kwamba…* "it is evident that…").

**[CORE]**

**14.**
**Form:** thibitisha
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *thubūt* (confirmation, proof) + Swahili causative *-isha*
**Typical English translations:** to confirm, to verify, to prove, to establish

A verb used in contexts of verification, confirmation, or proof of a claim or fact. Appears in legal, scientific, and everyday evaluative contexts. Related nominal: *uthibitisho* (confirmation, proof).

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** uthibitisho
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *thibiti* + *-sho* (nominalizing suffix); from same Arabic root as *thibitisha*
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, verification, proof, attestation

The abstract noun derived from *thibitisha*; refers to the act or result of confirming or verifying. Used in legal and administrative contexts for official attestation or proof of a claim.

**[CORE]**

**16.**
**Form:** ushahidi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *shahidi* (witness); from Arabic *shāhid* (witness)
**Typical English translations:** evidence, testimony, witness, proof

Refers to evidence or testimony used to evaluate the truth of a claim. Central to legal discourse in Swahili-speaking contexts; also used in religious and moral evaluations of conduct.

**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** shahidi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *shāhid* (witness, one who testifies)
**Typical English translations:** witness, testifier

A person who provides testimony or evidence regarding the truth of events or claims. Used in legal proceedings and in religious contexts. Participates in derivation with *ushahidi*.

**[CORE]**

**18.**
**Form:** uaminifu
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *amini* (to trust, to believe; from Arabic *amīn*, trustworthy) + *-fu* (adjectival/nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** faithfulness, trustworthiness, reliability, honesty, fidelity

Refers to the quality of being reliable, honest, or faithful in conduct or representation. Used in moral evaluations of persons and in assessments of the reliability of information or sources.

**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** amini
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *amīn* (trustworthy, faithful); functions as verb *kuamini* (to believe, to trust)
**Typical English translations:** to believe, to trust, to have faith in; trustworthy (adjectival use)

Used to evaluate whether something is credible or whether a person is trustworthy. Appears in religious, moral, and epistemic contexts. Participates in productive derivation: *uaminifu*, *mwaminifu* (faithful person).

**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** mwaminifu
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *m-* (class 1 person prefix) + *amini* + *-fu*; one who is trustworthy
**Typical English translations:** faithful person, trustworthy person, reliable person

Refers to a person evaluated as reliably honest or faithful. Used in moral and relational contexts to commend character. Contrasts with *msaliti* (traitor, betrayer).

**[CORE]**

**21.**
**Form:** udanganyifu
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *danganya* (to deceive) + *-ifu* (suffix); abstract noun of deception
**Typical English translations:** deception, deceitfulness, fraudulence, trickery

Refers to the quality or practice of deceiving. Used in moral and legal evaluations of dishonest conduct or misrepresentation. Related verb: *kudanganya* (to deceive, to mislead).

**[CORE]**

**22.**
**Form:** kudanganya
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *danganya* (to deceive); etymology of root uncertain
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to mislead, to lie, to cheat

The verb for deliberate deception or misrepresentation. Used in moral condemnation and legal contexts. Participates in productive derivation: *udanganyifu*, *mdanganyifu* (deceiver).

**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, transcript, reproduction

Used in evaluations of whether a document or object is an original or a reproduction. Relevant in legal and aesthetic contexts where authenticity is assessed (*nakala ya hati* "copy of a document").

**Typical English translations:** the actual situation, the real state of affairs, reality

A fixed nominal phrase used to evaluate or describe what is genuinely the case, as opposed to appearances or claims. Common in journalistic and analytical discourse.

**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *halisi* (genuine, real)
**Typical English translations:** reality, genuineness, authenticity, realism

The abstract noun derived from *halisi*; refers to the quality of being real or genuine, or to reality itself. Used in philosophical and aesthetic contexts, including literary criticism (*uhalisi wa kisanaa* "artistic realism").

**Typical English translations:** correct, right, equal, okay, same, fair

Used to evaluate statements, measurements, or situations as correct, equal, or appropriate. Functions as an adjective, adverb, and discourse particle confirming accuracy or agreement (*sawa sawa* "exactly right, precisely").

**Literal meaning:** reduplication of *sawa* (equal, correct)
**Typical English translations:** exactly, precisely, correctly, just right

An intensified reduplicated form of *sawa*, used to assert exact correctness or precise equivalence. Common in evaluations of measurements, statements, and representations for accuracy.

Used in moral evaluations of persons and conduct with respect to whether proper regard is shown. Relevant in assessments of rightness of behavior in social and ethical contexts (*kukosa heshima* "to lack respect/to be disrespectful").

**Typical English translations:** legality, legitimacy, lawfulness, permissibility

Refers to the quality of being legally or morally legitimate. Used in legal and religious evaluations of acts, contracts, or claims (*uhalali wa mkataba* "the legality/validity of a contract").

**30.**
**Form:** halali
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥalāl* (permitted, lawful)
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legitimate, permissible, rightful

Used to evaluate acts, goods, or claims as legally or morally permissible. Appears in religious, legal, and moral contexts. Contrasts with *haramu* (forbidden, illicit). Participates in derivation with *uhalali*.
