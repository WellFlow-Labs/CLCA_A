# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** Analysis is based on Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties), consistent with P1 scope. Transliteration follows the three-vowel orthography established in P1. Where a form is attested primarily in one variety, this is noted. Forms are drawn from documented lexicographic and grammatical sources (Gonzáles Holguín, Lira, Cusihuamán, Cerrón-Palomino, Parker, Soto Ruiz, Itier, the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua, and related corpora); no forms are invented or back-translated from English.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**P1 participation:** attested across multiple P1 items including *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *cheqanchay* / *cheqachiy*, *cheqasqa*, *mana cheqa*, *cheqanta rimay*, *cheqan*, and related forms
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding truth, genuineness, correctness, and authenticity. It anchors the truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua and is the most productive root for deriving expressions of epistemic and moral accuracy. It participates in nominal, adjectival, verbal, and evidential constructions, and anchors the semantic field of accurate correspondence between representation and reality, and of moral integrity in persons.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**P1 participation:** attested across multiple P1 items including *llulla*, *llullakuy*, *llullaq*
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding falsehood, deception, and dishonesty. It is the direct antonym of *cheqa* in the truth-evaluation domain and is productive in moral and legal characterizations of persons and statements. It participates in derivations covering the act of lying, the character of a liar, and the quality of being false or counterfeit.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**P1 participation:** attested across multiple P1 items including *allin*, *allinchay*, *mana allin*
**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding goodness, rightness, and proper functioning. It covers moral, practical, and evaluative correctness broadly, and participates in derivations concerning repair, improvement, and well-being.

---

**Root 4**

**Form:** sut'i
**Transliteration:** sut'i
**P1 participation:** attested in P1 items including *sut'i*, *sut'ichay*
**Core meaning contribution:** Encodes clarity, plainness, explicitness, and distinctness. While not a direct synonym of *cheqa*, it contributes a related dimension of the truth-evaluation domain — the quality of being unambiguous, transparent, and evident. It participates in evaluations of communicative accuracy and representational precision, and appears in identity and naming contexts.

---

**Root 5**

**Form:** chiqan
**Transliteration:** chiqan
**P1 participation:** attested in P1 items including *chiqan*, *chiqanchay*, *chiqap*
**Core meaning contribution:** Encodes straightness, correctness, exactness, and justice. It contributes a meaning of alignment with what is right or proper — spatial, moral, and procedural — and is productive in legal and ethical discourse.

---

**Note on root selection:** *Cheqa*, *llulla*, *allin*, *sut'i*, and *chiqan* are the morphemes most specifically and productively associated with the truth-evaluation and correctness domain in the P1 data. Other P1 items (*yachay*, *iñiy*, *pantay*) are semantically relevant but appear in P1 primarily as roots with fewer documented derivatives specifically within the truth-evaluation domain, or their derivational productivity is more general. They are not excluded from consideration but do not meet the threshold for full root expansion here.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: cheqa

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root; unanalyzable
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, correct, right
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate adjective, adverb, or nominal to assert the truth or genuineness of a statement, person, or object. Foundational evaluative term across all registers. The base form from which all derivatives below are built.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqam / cheqami
**Transliteration:** cheqam / cheqami
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -m/-mi (direct evidential enclitic); "it is true (I assert this with direct knowledge)"
**Natural English translation:** it is true; it is indeed the case; that is the truth; certainly; indeed
**Context of use:** Fixed assertive expression used as a standalone assertion or predicate to affirm the truth of a preceding statement, with the speaker's direct evidential commitment. Extremely common in both everyday conversation and formal testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to establish as true, to correct
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts for the act of checking a claim or document against reality and confirming its accuracy. Primary causative derivative of *cheqa*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqachiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + causative -chi- + infinitive -y; "to cause to be true/correct"
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts for the act of establishing accuracy or correcting error. Appears in testimony and document-verification contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + -q (agentive/adjectival nominalizer); "one who is true, that which is true"
**Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine, authentic; one who is truthful
**Context of use:** Used attributively before nouns to mean 'true' or 'genuine', and nominalized to characterize a person as truthful. Used in moral discourse and in descriptions of authentic objects or persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** cheqaqta
**Transliteration:** cheqaqta
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + -ta (accusative/adverbial case)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, honestly, in truth
**Context of use:** Adverbial form used to reinforce the speaker's commitment to the truth of a statement. Appears in everyday speech and in testimonial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**

**Transliteration:** cheqaqchay

**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to prove true, to authenticate
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts where a claim or document is being confirmed as accurate. Appears in testimonial and procedural discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**

**Transliteration:** cheqaqsiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + -siy (reflexive/reciprocal verbalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to be confirmed, to turn out to be true, to prove oneself genuine
**Context of use:** Used when a claim or person's character is tested and found to be authentic. Less frequent than *cheqaqchay*.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**9.**
**Form:** cheqaqniyuq
**Transliteration:** cheqaqniyuq
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + -niyuq (possessive/having suffix); "one who has truth, one who possesses truthfulness"
**Natural English translation:** truthful person, one who possesses honesty
**Context of use:** Used in moral characterization of persons as genuinely honest or as possessing the quality of truthfulness. Appears in ethical and religious discourse in Southern Quechua.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + -manta (ablative case); "from truth, about truth, concerning what is true"

**12.**

**Transliteration:** cheqaqtam

**Natural English translation:** truly, it is really true (with direct evidential force)
**Context of use:** The adverbial form with the direct evidential clitic attached, used to assert that the speaker has direct knowledge that something is true. A common construction in Southern Quechua testimony and narrative.
