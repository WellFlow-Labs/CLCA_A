## Construction Usage Table

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | *Kabar itu benar.* | N/A | "That news is true." | [CORE] | Extremely common across all registers; the copula-less predicate adjective pattern is the unmarked form in Indonesian. No register restriction observed. |
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | *Pernikahan mereka sah.* | N/A | "Their marriage is valid." | [CORE] | Frequent in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts; *sah* in particular is high-frequency in both formal and informal speech. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | *Informasi itu tidak akurat.* | N/A | "That information is inaccurate." | [CORE] | Standard negation pattern; *tidak* + adjective is the default form across all registers. No restrictions observed for truth-domain adjectives. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | *Tindakan itu tidak sah menurut hukum yang berlaku.* | N/A | "That action is not valid under applicable law." | [CORE] | Frequent in legal and journalistic registers; the appended *menurut* phrase is a common extension in formal contexts. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | *Data yang akurat sangat diperlukan untuk pengambilan keputusan.* | N/A | "Accurate data is essential for decision-making." | [CORE] | The *yang*-relativizer construction is the fully productive and unmarked adnominal pattern; found across all registers and all truth-domain adjectives. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | *Alasan yang sah harus disertakan dalam permohonan.* | N/A | "A valid reason must be included in the application." | [CORE] | Common in administrative and legal writing; *yang sah* is a high-frequency collocation in formal documents and regulations. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | *Dusta yang diulang-ulang bisa menjadi kebenaran yang diterima.* | N/A | "A lie repeated many times can become an accepted truth." | [MARGINAL] | The *menjadi* + truth-domain adjective/noun pattern is grammatically available but less frequent than resultative passives; more typical of rhetorical, journalistic, or philosophical prose than everyday speech. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | *Kontrak itu dinyatakan sah oleh pengadilan.* | N/A | "The contract was declared valid by the court." | [CORE] | Resultative passives (*dinyatakan*, *terbukti*) are the dominant inchoative strategy in legal and institutional registers; *dinyatakan sah* is a near-formulaic legal expression. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu.* | N/A | "The authorities confirmed that report." | [CORE] | *meN-...-kan* causatives are fully productive and common in formal, journalistic, and legal registers; *membenarkan* in the sense of "to confirm" is a standard journalistic verb. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | *Ia memalsukan tanda tangan pada dokumen itu.* | N/A | "He forged the signature on that document." | [CORE] | *Memalsukan* is the standard verb for forgery/falsification; frequent in legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. The passive *dipalsukan* is equally common in formal writing. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *Mencari kebenaran adalah tugas jurnalis.* | N/A | "Seeking the truth is the journalist's task." | [CORE] | *Ke-...-an* nominalizations are fully productive and extremely frequent; *kebenaran* is one of the highest-frequency nominalizations in the domain, appearing across all registers. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *Keabsahan kontrak itu masih dipertanyakan.* | N/A | "The validity of that contract is still being questioned." | [CORE] | *Keabsahan* is standard in legal and administrative registers; the construction *masih dipertanyakan* ("still being questioned") is a frequent collocate in journalistic prose. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *Ia dikenal sebagai pembohong ulung.* | N/A | "He is known as a skilled liar." | [CORE] | *Pembohong* is the everyday agentive form for "liar" and is unrestricted by register; the collocation *pembohong ulung* ("skilled/accomplished liar") is a fixed evaluative phrase. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi.* | N/A | "The currency counterfeiter was finally arrested by police." | [CORE] | *Pemalsu* is the standard agentive for forger/counterfeiter; frequent in legal and journalistic registers. The compound *pemalsu uang* is a lexicalized collocation. |
| **8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION** | *Beban pembuktian ada pada pihak penuntut.* | N/A | "The burden of proof lies with the prosecuting party." | [CORE] | *Pembuktian* is a core legal term; *beban pembuktian* is a lexicalized legal formula. Restricted to formal, legal, and academic registers; not typical of casual speech. |
| **8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION** | *Pengesahan undang-undang itu memakan waktu berbulan-bulan.* | N/A | "The ratification of that law took many months." | [CORE] | *Pengesahan* is standard in legal, legislative, and journalistic registers; the *peN-...-an* process nominalization pattern is productive and frequent in formal Indonesian. |
| **9. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *Kalau saya tidak salah, pertemuan itu dijadwalkan hari Kamis.* | N/A | "If I am not mistaken, the meeting is scheduled for Thursday." | [CORE] | Extremely frequent in spoken Indonesian and informal written registers; *kalau saya tidak salah* is the most colloquial variant. The more formal *jika saya tidak keliru* is restricted to formal speech and writing. |
| **9. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *Sejauh yang saya tahu, hal itu benar adanya.* | N/A | "As far as I know, that is indeed the case." | [CORE] | Common across spoken and written registers; *benar adanya* is a fixed emphatic phrase ("is indeed the case") that frequently co-occurs with epistemic hedges. |
| **10. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *Memang benar bahwa angka kemiskinan menurun, tetapi ketimpangan masih tinggi.* | N/A | "It is indeed true that poverty rates have declined, but inequality remains high." | [CORE] | *Memang benar bahwa* is a high-frequency concessive opener in journalistic and argumentative writing; the *memang* particle adds an emphatic or concessive force not present in bare *benar bahwa*. |
| **10. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *Ada benarnya pendapat itu, meski tidak sepenuhnya tepat.* | N/A | "There is something to that view, though it is not entirely accurate." | [CORE] | *Ada benarnya* is a common partial-concession formula in both spoken and written Indonesian; the pairing with *meski tidak sepenuhnya* ("though not entirely") is a frequent collocation in opinion and editorial registers. |
| **11. DOMAIN-RESTRICTING VALIDITY** | *Pernikahan mereka sah secara hukum maupun agama.* | N/A | "Their marriage is valid both legally and religiously." | [CORE] | Frequent in legal, administrative, and journalistic registers; the dual *secara hukum maupun agama* framing reflects the Indonesian context of parallel civil and religious legal systems and is a near-formulaic pairing. |
| **11. DOMAIN-RESTRICTING VALIDITY** | *Keputusan itu sah secara prosedural tetapi dipertanyakan secara moral.* | N/A | "The decision is procedurally valid but morally questionable." | [CORE] | Common in journalistic and legal commentary; the *sah secara X tetapi dipertanyakan secara Y* frame is a productive argumentative structure in editorial and analytical writing. Restricted to the *sah/absah/valid* cluster as noted in P4a. |

## Attested Alternate Constructions

| Construction Type | Alternate Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | *Benarkah kabar itu?* | Interrogative inversion with enclitic *-kah*; attested in P4a notes. Common in formal questions and rhetorical contexts. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | *Ini bukan produk asli.* | *Bukan* negation for noun predicates; attested in P4a notes. Not interchangeable with *tidak*; applies when the predicate is nominal rather than adjectival. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | *berita palsu*, *uang palsu*, *hadis sahih* | Prenominal order without *yang*; attested in P4a notes as lexicalized collocations only, not a productive pattern. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | *terbukti* + adjective | Resultative passive functioning inchoatively; attested in P4a notes alongside *dinyatakan*. Frequent in legal and journalistic registers. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *pembenaran*, *pemalsuan*, *pembuktian*, *pengesahan*, *pernyataan* | *peN-...-an* process/result nominalizations; attested in P4a §6 notes as a second nominalization pattern distinct from *ke-...-an* abstract nominalizations. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *pendusta* | More formal/literary and Islamic-register variant of *pembohong*; attested in P4a notes. Register-restricted; not interchangeable with *pembohong* in everyday speech. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *orang yang jujur*, *saksi yang terpercaya* | Periphrastic alternatives for the truth-telling side of the domain where agentive *peN-* forms are not established; attested in P4a notes. |
| **9. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *jika saya tidak keliru* | More formal variant of *kalau saya tidak salah*; attested in P4a notes. Restricted to formal speech and writing. |
| **9. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *jujur saja* / *untuk jujur* | Honesty-framing hedges; attested in P4a notes. Frequent in spoken Indonesian and registers approximating spoken discourse. |
| **10. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *pada dasarnya benar* | Partial concession form ("essentially correct"); attested in P4a notes. Occupies a middle position on the concession scale between *memang benar bahwa* and *benar, tapi*. |
| **10. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | *benar, tapi* | Minimal concession form; attested in P4a notes. More common in spoken and informal written registers than *memang benar bahwa*. |
| **8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION** | *pembuktian terbalik* | Lexicalized legal compound ("reversal of burden of proof"); attested in P4a notes. Restricted to legal register. |
