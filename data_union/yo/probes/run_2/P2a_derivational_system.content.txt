# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note on Yoruba Derivational Morphology

Yoruba derivational morphology operates primarily through **prefixation** (especially nominalizing prefixes *ì-*, *ò-*, *à-*, *ẹ̀-*) and **compounding**. Suffixation is limited. Verbal roots are typically bare; nominalization is the most productive derivational process. The negating prefix *àì-* (combining *à-* + *ì-*) is productive across nominal forms. Not all roots participate equally in all processes. Gaps are preserved below without completion.

## 1. Base Form: òtítọ́

---

**Derived Form 1**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** àìtọ́
- **Transliteration:** àìtọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (negating prefix) + *ì-* (nominalizing) + *tọ́* (correct, straight); "the state of not being correct"
- **Natural English Translation:** incorrectness, impropriety, wrongness
- **Usage Description:** Used in moral and evaluative contexts to refer to the quality of being incorrect or improper. Appears in everyday speech and moral condemnation as a general negation of correctness, though less frequent than *irọ́* for the specific sense of falsehood.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** òtítọ́ pátápátá
- **Transliteration:** òtítọ́ pátápátá
- **Literal Breakdown:** *òtítọ́* (truth) + *pátápátá* (completely, entirely); "complete truth"
- **Natural English Translation:** the whole truth, complete truth, nothing but the truth
- **Usage Description:** Used in legal and moral contexts to assert that a statement is entirely truthful without omission or distortion. Appears in oath-taking and formal testimony contexts, parallel to the English legal formula.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *ọ̀rọ̀* (word, speech, matter) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "word/matter of truth"
- **Natural English Translation:** true statement, truthful speech, words of truth
- **Usage Description:** A productive nominal compound used to evaluate a statement or account as truthful. Appears in everyday speech, preaching, and legal contexts where the truthfulness of an utterance is being affirmed.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** sọ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, speak) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "to speak truth"
- **Natural English Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
- **Usage Description:** The most productive verbal collocation with *òtítọ́*, used across all registers to describe the act of making a truthful statement. Appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and moral instruction.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** ìsọ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** ìsọ òtítọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizing prefix) + *sọ* (to say) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "the act of speaking truth"
- **Natural English Translation:** truth-telling, the act of speaking truthfully
- **Usage Description:** The nominalized form of *sọ òtítọ́*, used to refer to truth-telling as a practice or virtue. Appears in moral and educational discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Derived Form:** olòtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** olòtítọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *olú-* / *eni tó ní* contracted to *ol-* + *òtítọ́* (truth); "one who has/possesses truth, a truthful person"
- **Natural English Translation:** truthful person, one who speaks truth, honest person
- **Usage Description:** Used as a nominal to describe a person characterized by truthfulness. Appears in moral evaluation and in proverbs contrasting truthful and deceptive persons.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on gap:** No common suffixed or verbal derivation from *òtítọ́* itself (as distinct from its root *tọ́*) is identified. The word functions primarily as a nominal base for collocations rather than as a morphological base for further affixation.

## 2. Base Form: irọ́

---

**Derived Form 1**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Derived Form:** sọ irọ́
- **Transliteration:** sọ irọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, speak) + *irọ́* (lie, falsehood); "to speak a lie"
- **Natural English Translation:** to tell a lie, to lie, to speak falsely
- **Usage Description:** The primary verbal collocation with *irọ́*, highly productive across all registers. Used in moral condemnation, legal contexts, and everyday speech to describe the act of making a false statement.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Derived Form:** ìsọ irọ́
- **Transliteration:** ìsọ irọ́

- **Natural English Translation:** lying, the act of telling lies, deception through speech
- **Usage Description:** The nominalized form of *sọ irọ́*, used to refer to lying as a practice or act. Appears in moral and legal discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Derived Form:** aláìrọ́
- **Transliteration:** aláìrọ́
- **Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (agentive prefix, from *eni tó ní*) + *àì-* (negating) + *irọ́* (lie); "one who does not lie, one without falsehood"
- **Natural English Translation:** truthful person, one who does not lie, honest person
- **Usage Description:** Used as a nominal to describe a person who does not lie. Appears in moral commendation and in proverbs. Less common than *olòtítọ́* but attested in evaluative discourse.

**Derived Form 6**

**Derived Form 7**

- **Transliteration:** olóirọ́

- **Natural English Translation:** liar, one who tells lies, deceiver
- **Usage Description:** The standard agentive nominal for a habitual liar. Used in moral condemnation and in legal contexts to characterize a witness or party as unreliable. Appears in proverbs and everyday speech.

- **Usage Description:** Used in everyday and legal speech to characterize a falsehood as particularly serious or consequential. Appears in moral condemnation and in legal argument about the gravity of perjury.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Note on gap:** The morphological base of *òdodo* is debated (see P1), and this limits confident identification of further derivations. The forms above are supported by attested usage patterns; additional derivations are not identified with confidence.
