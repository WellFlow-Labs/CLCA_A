# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step analyzes how Basque encodes dynamic processes — becoming, causing, and maintaining — involving truth-related vocabulary documented in P1–P5. All forms and constructions are drawn exclusively from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents a form but does not attest a specific dynamic construction, this is stated explicitly. Where a dynamic construction is not attested for a given item, no construction is invented.

The analysis is organized by construction type rather than by lexical item, since the CONTEXT reveals that dynamic encoding is largely determined by morphological class (which suffix a root takes) rather than by individual lexical items. Cross-references to P1–P5 are provided throughout.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE (*-tu* INTRANSITIVE / INCHOATIVE)

### Pattern
Root adjective or noun + *-tu* verbal suffix, used intransitively (no explicit agent).
Documented in P4a §4 and P4b.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**1.1**
- **Form:** *okertu*
- **Example:** *Gauzak okertu dira*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Things have gone wrong / Things have become crooked
- **Source:** P1b 3.22; P2a 7.2; P4a §4; P4b
- **Notes:** The inchoative reading ("become wrong/crooked") is attested for both physical and moral/situational senses. The CONTEXT documents *gauzak okertu dira* as the example. The auxiliary *dira* (3pl of *izan*) marks the perfective resultant state.

---

**1.2**
- **Form:** *usteldu*
- **Example:** *Sistema usteldu da*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The system has become corrupt / The system has rotted
- **Source:** P2a 22.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT explicitly documents both physical ("to rot") and moral/political ("to become corrupt") inchoative readings. The example *sistema usteldu da* is given in P2a 22.2. The auxiliary *da* (3sg of *izan*) marks the perfective resultant state.

---

**1.3**
- **Form:** *okertu* (applied to physical objects)
- **Example:** *Egurra okertu da*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The wood has warped / The wood has bent
- **Source:** P1b Step 3 Peripheral Extensions (from *zuzen-*)
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT explicitly notes this as a peripheral use of the wrongness root applied to physical deformation. Documented as a peripheral extension in P1b, not as a core truth-domain use.

---

**1.4**
- **Form:** *zuzendu* (intransitive reading)
- **Example:** (no intransitive example explicitly given in CONTEXT)
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To become straight / To straighten (intransitive)
- **Source:** P1b 3.4; P2a 6.2; P4a §4–5
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *zuzendu* as polyfunctional (inchoative or causative depending on argument structure, per P4a §5), but does not provide an explicit intransitive example sentence. The causative reading (*akatsak zuzendu*, "to correct the errors") is the primary attested use. The inchoative reading is documented as structurally possible (P4a §4) but no specific intransitive example sentence is given in the CONTEXT.

---

**1.5**
- **Form:** *egokitu* (intransitive reading)
- **Example:** *Baldintzetara egokitu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To adapt to the conditions / To adjust to the conditions
- **Source:** P2a 19.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *egokitu* as used in constructions such as *baldintzetara egokitu* ("to adapt to the conditions"). The allative *-ra* on *baldintzetara* ("to the conditions") marks the target state or context of adaptation. This is an inchoative-like construction where the subject undergoes a change of fit relative to a context.

---

### Items for which no inchoative *-tu* form is attested in CONTEXT

The following P1 items do not have an attested inchoative *-tu* form in the CONTEXT:

- *egia*, *egiazkoa*: No verb meaning "to become true" built on *egia-* is documented. The CONTEXT documents *egiaztatu* (to verify) but this is a causative/transitive form, not an inchoative.
- *gezurra*, *gezurrezkoa*: No verb meaning "to become false" built on *gezurr-* is documented. The CONTEXT explicitly notes (P2a item 4 note) that no verbal derivation parallel to *egiaztatu* is identified as a common form for the *gezurr-* root.
- *zehatza*: *Zehaztu* is documented (P2a 8.2) but its primary meaning is "to specify, to determine precisely" — a causative/transitive action, not an inchoative. No construction meaning "to become precise" is attested.
- *fidagarria*, *sinesgarria*: No inchoative *-tu* forms are documented. The CONTEXT explicitly notes (P2a items 9 and 10) that no verbal derivation directly from these adjectives is identified as a common form.
- *zintzoa*, *leiala*: No inchoative *-tu* forms are documented. The CONTEXT explicitly notes (P2a item 20) that no verbal derivation directly from *zintzoa* is identified as a common form.
- *baliozko*: *Baliozkotu* is documented (P2a 11.3) but as a causative ("to validate"), not an inchoative. *Baliogabetu* (P2a 11.5) is similarly causative ("to invalidate").

## 2. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE (*-tu* TRANSITIVE / CAUSATIVE)

### Pattern
Root adjective or noun + *-tu* verbal suffix, used transitively with an explicit object.
Documented in P4a §5 and P4b. The causative and inchoative readings are morphologically identical; transitivity is determined by argument structure.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**2.1**
- **Form:** *zuzendu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Akatsak zuzendu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To correct the errors
- **Source:** P1b 3.4; P2a 6.2; P4a §5; P4b
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as the primary causative use: an agent acts on an object (errors, texts, injustices) to make them correct or straight. Additional examples from CONTEXT: *bidegabekeria zuzendu* ("to rectify an injustice"), *gutuna zuzendu* ("to address a letter"). The object bears absolutive case.

---

**2.2**
- **Form:** *baliogabetu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Epaia baliogabetu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To nullify the judgment / To invalidate the judgment
- **Source:** P2a 11.5; P4a §5; P4b
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as a causative in legal and administrative contexts: an agent causes a judgment, document, or act to become invalid. The example *epaia baliogabetu* is given in P2a 11.5.

---

**2.3**
- **Form:** *baliozkotu* (transitive)
- **Example:** (no specific example sentence given in CONTEXT beyond the verb itself)
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To validate / To make valid / To legitimize
- **Source:** P2a 11.3
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *baliozkotu* as a verb meaning "to validate or confer validity upon something," used in administrative, legal, and institutional contexts. No specific example sentence is provided in the CONTEXT beyond the verb description.

---

**2.4**
- **Form:** *legitimatu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Boterea legitimatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To legitimize power
- **Source:** P2a 12.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *boterea legitimatu* as the example. An agent causes power or authority to acquire the status of legitimacy. Used in political and legal discourse.

---

**2.5**
- **Form:** *zilegitu* (transitive)
- **Example:** (no specific example sentence given in CONTEXT)
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To make lawful / To legitimize / To permit
- **Source:** P2a 13.3
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *zilegitu* as a verb meaning "to make something lawful or permissible," used in legal and ethical contexts. Epistemic status is [MARGINAL] in P2a. No specific example sentence is provided.

---

**2.6**
- **Form:** *egiaztatu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Datuak egiaztatu* / *Sinadura egiaztatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To verify the data / To certify a signature
- **Source:** P1b 1.6; P2a 1.5–1.6; P4a §11 (note)
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *egiaztatu* as the primary verb for official verification and certification. An agent causes a fact, document, or signature to acquire the status of being verified. The examples *datuak egiaztatu* and *sinadura egiaztatu* are given in P1b 1.6.

---

**2.7**
- **Form:** *autentifikatu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Sinadura autentifikatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To authenticate a signature
- **Source:** P2a 15.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *sinadura autentifikatu* as the example. An agent causes a signature, document, or object to acquire the status of being authenticated. Used in legal, administrative, and digital security contexts.

---

**2.8**
- **Form:** *faltsifikatu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Dokumentua faltsifikatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To forge a document / To falsify a document
- **Source:** P2a 14.3; P2b 14.4
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *dokumentua faltsifikatu* as the example. An agent causes a document to acquire the status of being forged or falsified. This is the causative of inauthenticity, contrasting with *autentifikatu* (Opposition 15 in P5).

---

**2.9**
- **Form:** *zehaztu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Data zehaztu* / *Baldintza zehaztu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To specify the date / To define the conditions precisely
- **Source:** P2a 8.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents both examples. An agent causes a date, condition, or fact to acquire the status of being precisely specified. Used in scientific, legal, and administrative contexts.

---

**2.10**
- **Form:** *frogatu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Fidagarria dela frogatu* / *Faltsua dela frogatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To prove that it is reliable / To prove that it is false
- **Source:** P1 item 26; P2b 9.3; P2b 14.3; P4a §11
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *frogatu* as the primary verb for demonstrating truth or fact through evidence. The examples with embedded *dela* clauses are given in P2b 9.3 and 14.3. An agent causes a claim or fact to acquire the status of being proven.

---

**2.11**
- **Form:** *egokitu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Testua egokitu* / *Baldintzetara egokitu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To adapt the text / To adapt to the conditions
- **Source:** P2a 19.2; P2b 19.3
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents both examples. An agent causes a text, material, or situation to become appropriate for a given context. The transitive reading (*testua egokitu*, "to adapt the text") involves an explicit object; the reflexive-like reading (*baldintzetara egokitu*, "to adapt to the conditions") involves an allative goal.

---

**2.12**
- **Form:** *berdindu* (transitive)
- **Example:** *Aukerak berdindu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To equalize opportunities
- **Source:** P2a 18.3
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *aukerak berdindu* as the example. An agent causes opportunities or things to become equal. Used in social and political discourse.

---

**2.13**
- **Form:** *usteldu* (transitive reading)
- **Example:** (no explicit transitive example given in CONTEXT)
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To corrupt / To cause to rot
- **Source:** P2a 22.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *usteldu* as covering both physical and moral/political senses, and notes it can be used transitively (causing corruption) as well as intransitively (becoming corrupt). However, no explicit transitive example sentence is provided in the CONTEXT; the documented example (*sistema usteldu da*) is intransitive.

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE THROUGH LIGHT-VERB CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth-domain nominal + light verb *egin* ("to do/make") or *esan* ("to say/tell").
Documented in P4a §11 and P4b. These constructions encode caused events where the nominal names the act and the light verb carries inflection.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**3.1**
- **Form:** *iruzurra egin*
- **Example:** *Iruzurra egin*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To commit fraud / To cheat / To deceive
- **Source:** P1 item 23; P2a 23.3; P2b 23.1; P4a §11
- **Notes:** An agent causes a state of fraud or deception to come about. The CONTEXT documents this as the primary verbal strategy for *iruzurra* since no dedicated *-tu* verb is attested for this root.

---

**3.2**
- **Form:** *itxura egin*
- **Example:** *Itxura egin*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To pretend / To put on an appearance / To feign
- **Source:** P1b 2.4; P2b 16.1; P4a §11
- **Notes:** An agent causes a false appearance to come about. The CONTEXT documents this as one of the most common expressions for pretense in Basque.

---

**3.3**
- **Form:** *gezurra esan*
- **Example:** *Gezurra esan*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell a lie
- **Source:** P1b 1.21; P2b 4.1; P4a §11
- **Notes:** An agent causes a falsehood to be communicated. The CONTEXT documents this as the primary verbal collocation for the act of lying.

---

**3.4**
- **Form:** *egia esan*
- **Example:** *Egia esan*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell the truth
- **Source:** P1b 1.1; P2b 1.1; P4a §11
- **Notes:** An agent causes truth to be communicated. The CONTEXT documents this as the most basic and frequent collocation of *egia* in everyday speech.

---

**3.5**
- **Form:** *egia gorde*
- **Example:** *Egia gorde*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To hide the truth / To conceal the truth
- **Source:** P2b 1.8
- **Notes:** An agent causes truth to remain concealed. The CONTEXT documents *gorde* ("to keep, to hide, to conceal") as the verb in this collocation. Documented as a partial antonym of *egia esan* in P2b 1.8.

---

**3.6**
- **Form:** *egia ukatu*
- **Example:** *Egia ukatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To deny the truth
- **Source:** P2b 1.20
- **Notes:** An agent causes truth to be actively denied. The CONTEXT distinguishes this from *egia gorde* (concealment) by the implication of active denial in *ukatu*.

---

**3.7**
- **Form:** *egia aitortu*
- **Example:** *Egia aitortu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To admit the truth / To confess the truth
- **Source:** P2b 1.7
- **Notes:** An agent causes truth to be acknowledged after prior concealment or denial. The CONTEXT notes the connotation of acknowledgment after resistance, distinguishing this from *egia esan*.

---

**3.8**
- **Form:** *gezurra zabaldu*
- **Example:** *Gezurra zabaldu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To spread a lie / To disseminate a falsehood
- **Source:** P2b 4.10
- **Notes:** An agent causes a falsehood to circulate. The CONTEXT documents this in journalistic and political contexts relating to misinformation.

---

**3.9**
- **Form:** *egia argitara atera*
- **Example:** *Egia argitara atera*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To bring the truth to light
- **Source:** P2b 1.9
- **Notes:** An agent causes hidden truth to become known. The CONTEXT documents *argitara atera* ("to bring out into the light") as a productive collocation in Basque used in journalistic, legal, and literary contexts.

---

**3.10**
- **Form:** *ustelkeria salatu*
- **Example:** *Ustelkeria salatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To denounce corruption / To report corruption
- **Source:** P2b 22.2
- **Notes:** An agent causes corruption to become publicly known or officially reported. The CONTEXT documents *salatu* ("to denounce, to report, to accuse") as the verb in this collocation.

---

**3.11**
- **Form:** *iruzurra salatu*
- **Example:** *Iruzurra salatu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To denounce fraud / To report fraud
- **Source:** P2b 23.2
- **Notes:** Parallel to *ustelkeria salatu* (3.10 above). The CONTEXT documents both as parallel constructions in P2b 22.2 and 23.2.

---

**3.12**
- **Form:** *legitimotasuna zalantzan jarri*
- **Example:** *Legitimotasuna zalantzan jarri*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To call legitimacy into question / To challenge legitimacy
- **Source:** P2b 12.3
- **Notes:** An agent causes legitimacy to enter a state of doubt. The CONTEXT documents *zalantzan jarri* ("to put into doubt") as a fixed phrase. The same construction applies to *autentikotasuna zalantzan jarri* (P2b 15.3) and *sinesgarritasuna galdu* (P2b 10.3) as related destabilizing actions.

---

**3.13**
- **Form:** *autentikotasuna zalantzan jarri*
- **Example:** *Autentikotasuna zalantzan jarri*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To call authenticity into question
- **Source:** P2b 15.3
- **Notes:** Parallel to *legitimotasuna zalantzan jarri* (3.12 above). The CONTEXT explicitly notes this parallel in P2b 15.3.

---

**3.14**
- **Form:** *zuzen jarri*
- **Example:** *Zuzen jarri*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To set straight / To put right / To correct
- **Source:** P1b 3.26
- **Notes:** An agent causes something to become straight or correct. The CONTEXT documents this in both physical and figurative senses. The verb *jarri* ("to put, to place, to set") functions as a light verb here with the adverbial *zuzen* ("straight, correct").

## 4. MAINTAINING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE (STABILITY EXPRESSIONS)

### Pattern
Stative *egon* ("to be," contingent) or *leial egon* / *leial izan* constructions.
Documented in P4a §4 and P5 Opposition 32.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**4.1**
- **Form:** *oker nago* / *oker egon*
- **Example:** *Oker nago*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** I am wrong / I am mistaken (stative, contingent)
- **Source:** P1 item 7; P2b 7.1; P4a §4; P5 Opposition 32
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *egon* (stative copula) as marking a contingent, reversible state of being mistaken, contrasting with *izan* for inherent properties (P5 Opposition 32). The state is maintained as long as the *egon* construction holds; it can be exited (e.g., by being corrected).

---

**4.2**
- **Form:** *leial egon*
- **Example:** *Leial egon*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To remain loyal / To stay faithful
- **Source:** P2b 21.1
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *leial egon* as expressing the maintenance of loyalty over time, using *egon* to mark a sustained state. The CONTEXT explicitly notes that *egon* marks a sustained state in this construction.

---

**4.3**
- **Form:** *leial izan*
- **Example:** *Leial izan*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To be loyal / To be faithful (inherent property)
- **Source:** P2b 21.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *leial izan* as expressing loyalty as an inherent property, contrasting with *leial egon* (sustained contingent state). The CONTEXT explicitly notes the *egon*/*izan* distinction in P2b 21.2 and P4a §4.

---

**4.4**
- **Form:** *itxurak gorde*
- **Example:** *Itxurak gorde*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To keep up appearances / To maintain appearances
- **Source:** P1b 2.6; P2b 16.2
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as describing the social practice of maintaining outward appearances regardless of inner reality. The verb *gorde* ("to keep, to preserve") encodes the maintenance of a state (appearances) over time.

---

**4.5**
- **Form:** *gezurra bizi da*
- **Example:** *Gezurra bizi da*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The lie lives on / The lie persists
- **Source:** P2b 4.9
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as a rhetorical or literary construction describing a falsehood that continues to circulate or be believed. The verb *bizi* ("lives, is alive") encodes persistence of a state. Epistemic status is [MARGINAL] in P2b.

---

**4.6**
- **Form:** *zintzoa izatea*
- **Example:** *Zintzoa izatea*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Being honest / Being upright
- **Source:** P2b 20.3
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this nominalized construction as referring to the quality or practice of honesty as an ongoing state or disposition. The *izatea* (verbal noun of *izan*) encodes the state as an inherent property rather than a contingent one.

---

**4.7**
- **Form:** *zuzena izatea*
- **Example:** *Zuzena izatea*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Being correct / Being right / Being just
- **Source:** P1b 3.19; P2b 6.4
- **Notes:** Parallel to *zintzoa izatea* (4.6 above). The CONTEXT documents this in philosophical and ethical discourse to refer to the state or quality of being correct or just as an ongoing disposition.

## 5. LOSS OF A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### Pattern
Truth-domain abstract nominal + *galdu* ("to lose").
Documented in P2b for credibility and loyalty.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**5.1**
- **Form:** *sinesgarritasuna galdu*
- **Example:** *Sinesgarritasuna galdu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To lose credibility
- **Source:** P2b 10.3; P5 Opposition 18
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as describing the erosion of credibility of a person, institution, or source. The verb *galdu* ("to lose") encodes the transition from a state of credibility to its absence.

---

**5.2**
- **Form:** *leialatasuna galdu*
- **Example:** *Leialatasuna galdu*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To lose loyalty / To forfeit faithfulness
- **Source:** P2b 21.4; P5 Opposition 19
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as parallel to *sinesgarritasuna galdu* and explicitly notes the structural parallel in P2b 21.4.

---

**5.3**
- **Form:** *usteldu* (intransitive, as documented in §1.2)
- **Example:** *Sistema usteldu da*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The system has become corrupt / The system has decayed
- **Source:** P2a 22.2
- **Notes:** Also classifiable as a loss-of-integrity expression. The CONTEXT documents this as covering the transition from a state of integrity to corruption.

---

## 6. ACQUISITION OF A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### Pattern
Truth-domain abstract nominal + *irabazi* ("to earn/gain") or *erakutsi* ("to show/demonstrate").
Documented in P2b for credibility and loyalty.

### Attested forms from CONTEXT

**6.1**
- **Form:** *sinesgarritasuna irabazi*
- **Example:** *Sinesgarritasuna irabazi*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To gain credibility / To earn credibility
- **Source:** P2b 10.4; P5 Opposition 18
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as describing the building of credibility over time. The verb *irabazi* ("to earn, to win, to gain") encodes the acquisition of a state. Documented as the antonym of *sinesgarritasuna galdu* in P2b 10.4.

---

**6.2**
- **Form:** *leialatasuna erakutsi*
- **Example:** *Leialatasuna erakutsi*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To show loyalty / To demonstrate faithfulness
- **Source:** P2b 21.3; P5 Opposition 19
- **Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as describing the demonstration of loyalty through action. The verb *erakutsi* ("to show, to demonstrate") encodes the making-visible of a state rather than its acquisition, but functions in the same semantic slot as *irabazi* in the loyalty domain.

## 8. STRUCTURAL OBSERVATIONS

The following observations are drawn from the patterns documented above. They report what the CONTEXT data shows; no claims are made beyond what is attested.

**8.1 Asymmetry between truth and falsity roots in dynamic encoding**

The CONTEXT documents a systematic asymmetry: the *egia-* root generates a productive inchoative/causative verb (*egiaztatu*, "to verify/certify") while the *gezurr-* root does not. The CONTEXT explicitly notes this gap in P2a item 4: "No verbal derivation parallel to *egiaztatu* is identified as a common form" for *gezurr-*. Dynamic expressions involving *gezurra* are encoded through light-verb constructions (*gezurra esan*, *gezurra zabaldu*) rather than through a dedicated *-tu* verb.

**8.2 The *-tu* verb as the primary dynamic morpheme**

Across the domain, the *-tu* suffix is the primary morphological device for encoding both inchoative ("become X") and causative ("cause X to become Y") meanings. The CONTEXT documents in P4a §4–5 that these two readings are not morphologically distinguished; transitivity (and therefore the causative vs. inchoative reading) is determined by argument structure alone.

**8.3 The *egon*/*izan* distinction encodes contingency vs. inherence**

The CONTEXT documents in P4a §4, P4b, and P5 Opposition 32 that *egon* (stative copula) marks contingent, reversible states (e.g., *oker nago*, "I am [currently] wrong") while *izan* (equative copula) marks inherent properties (e.g., *zuzena da*, "it is [inherently] correct"). This distinction is the primary grammatical device for encoding whether a truth-related state is stable or contingent.

**8.4 *Galdu* and *irabazi* as the primary verbs for loss and acquisition of abstract truth-related properties**

The CONTEXT documents *galdu* ("to lose") and *irabazi* ("to earn/gain") as the verbs used with abstract *-tasun* nominals (*sinesgarritasuna*, *leialatasuna*) to encode loss and acquisition of truth-related properties. This pattern is attested for credibility and loyalty; it is not attested in the CONTEXT for other *-tasun* nominals in this domain (e.g., *egiazkotasuna galdu*, "to lose truthfulness," is not documented).

**8.5 *Zalantzan jarri* as a fixed destabilizing construction**

The CONTEXT documents *zalantzan jarri* ("to put into doubt") as a fixed phrase that combines with truth-related abstract nominals (*legitimotasuna*, *autentikotasuna*) to encode the destabilization of a truth-related status. This construction is attested for legitimacy and authenticity; it is not attested in the CONTEXT for other nominals in the domain.

**8.6 No dynamic constructions attested for several P1 items**

The following P1 items have no dynamic (becoming, causing, losing, gaining) constructions documented in the CONTEXT: *antzekoa*, *berdintsua*, *egokia* (beyond *egokitu*), *zintzoa* (beyond nominalized *zintzoa izatea*), *itxurakoa* (beyond *itxura egin* and *itxurak gorde*). For these items, the CONTEXT documents only static attributive, predicative, or adverbial uses.
