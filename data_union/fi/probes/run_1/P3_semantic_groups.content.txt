# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in the P1 and P2 data: which terms co-occur, which are contrasted with which, which appear in the same discourse contexts, and which share functional roles in Finnish usage. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries are determined by the data. Items that do not fit cleanly are noted at the end.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that a statement holds

**Label:** Expressions for asserting or denying that a claim is the case

**Definition:** These expressions are used in Finnish to evaluate whether a proposition, statement, or claim corresponds to how things are. They appear in contexts where a speaker is affirming, denying, or questioning the truth of something said or believed. The core contrast in this group is between *tosi* / *totta* / *pitää paikkansa* on one side and *epätosi* / *väärä* / *ei pidä paikkaansa* on the other.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *tosi* — used predicatively (*se on tosi* — "it is true") and as the base of the cluster
- *totuus* — the nominal form; appears in *kertoa totuus*, *koko totuus ja vain totuus*
- *epätosi* — the direct negation; used in formal and philosophical discourse
- *epätotuus* — "untruth"; used in journalistic and formal contexts without necessarily implying deliberate deception
- *pitää paikkansa* — "to hold/be true"; the standard colloquial construction for asserting a claim is correct
- *ei pidä paikkaansa* — "it is not true/does not hold"; the standard colloquial negation
- *totta puhuen* — "to tell the truth / truthfully speaking"; parenthetical adverbial
- *puhua totta* — "to speak the truth"
- *totuusarvo* — "truth value"; technical use in logic and formal semantics
- *totuudenmukainen* / *totuudenmukaisuus* — "truthful / truthfulness"; used to evaluate whether an account corresponds to the facts
- *totuudenvastainen* / *totuudenvastaisuus* — "contrary to truth / untruthfulness"; formal and legal register
- *todella* — "really, truly, indeed"; adverb asserting that something is genuinely the case
- *todennäköinen* / *todennäköisyys* / *todennäköisesti* — "probable / probability / probably"; the probabilistic extension of the truth-assertion cluster

**Observations:** The contrast between *tosi* and *epätosi* is more formal and explicitly propositional than the contrast between *oikea* and *väärä*. The construction *pitää paikkansa* / *ei pidä paikkaansa* is the most frequent everyday equivalent. *Todennäköinen* and its derivatives belong here because they are built on the same root and function in the same evaluative space, but they introduce degree rather than binary assertion.

---

## Group 2: Terms used to evaluate correctness relative to a standard or norm

**Label:** Expressions for evaluating whether something meets a standard or norm

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether an answer, action, procedure, or person conforms to what is required, expected, or proper. The standard or norm may be explicit (a rule, a qualification requirement, a prescribed procedure) or implicit (common usage, professional expectation). The key contrast is between *oikea* / *oikein* and *väärä* / *väärin* in this evaluative role.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *oikea* — "right, correct"; used for answers, persons, words, times (*oikea vastaus*, *oikea sana*, *oikea henkilö*)
- *oikein* — "correctly"; adverb (*vastata oikein*, *ymmärtää oikein*)
- *olla oikeassa* — "to be right/correct"; the standard periphrastic construction
- *oikeellisuus* — "correctness"; formal and administrative evaluation of conformity to standard
- *oikeakielisyys* / *oikeakielinen* — "linguistic correctness"; prescriptive language evaluation
- *oikeaoppinen* / *oikeaoppisuus* — "orthodox / orthodoxy"; doctrinal correctness
- *oikaista* / *oikaisu* — "to correct / correction"; the act of setting something right
- *väärä* — "wrong, incorrect"; the primary antonym (*väärä vastaus*, *olla väärässä*)
- *väärin* — "wrongly, incorrectly"; adverb (*vastata väärin*, *ymmärtää väärin*)
- *väärinkäsitys* — "misunderstanding"; incorrect understanding
- *väärintulkinta* / *väärintulkita* — "misinterpretation / to misinterpret"
- *asianmukainen* / *asianmukaisuus* / *asianmukaisesti* — "proper, appropriate"; conformity to what the matter requires
- *poliittinen oikeellisuus* — "political correctness"; a specific application of the correctness-to-norm pattern

**Observations:** This group overlaps with Group 1 in that *väärä* and *oikea* can also be used in propositional truth contexts. The distinguishing feature here is that the evaluation is against a norm, standard, or expectation rather than against a state of affairs in the world. *Asianmukainen* belongs here rather than in Group 1 because it evaluates procedural and normative conformity, not propositional truth. *Oikeaoppinen* is included because it follows the same pattern of norm-conformity evaluation, though it can carry ironic force.

## Group 3: Terms used in evaluating genuineness or non-counterfeit status

**Label:** Expressions for asserting or denying that something is genuine rather than a copy or fake

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether an object, document, person, or emotion is the real thing as opposed to an imitation, forgery, or performance. They appear in contexts of art authentication, commercial labelling, legal document evaluation, and personal identity. The core contrast is between *aito* / *alkuperäinen* on one side and *väärennös* / *epäaito* on the other.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *aito* — "genuine, authentic"; the primary term (*aito nahka*, *aito tunne*, *aito suomalainen*)
- *aitous* — "authenticity, genuineness"; the nominal form
- *aidosti* — "genuinely, authentically"; adverb
- *aito ja alkuperäinen* — the formulaic pairing asserting both authenticity and originality
- *epäaito* / *epäaitous* — "inauthentic / inauthenticity"; the direct negation
- *alkuperäinen* — "original"; asserts temporal and genealogical priority (*alkuperäinen teos*, *alkuperäinen sopimus*)
- *alkuperäisyys* — "originality, original character"
- *alkuperäiskappale* — "original copy/document"
- *alkuperäisversio* — "original version"
- *väärennös* — "forgery, counterfeit"; the product of deliberate falsification
- *väärentää* — "to forge, to falsify, to counterfeit"
- *väärentäjä* — "forger, counterfeiter"
- *väärennösepäily* — "suspicion of forgery"
- *paljastaa väärennös* — "to expose a forgery"
- *epäillä väärennöstä* — "to suspect forgery"

**Observations:** *Aito* and *alkuperäinen* overlap but are not identical: *aito* asserts non-counterfeit status, while *alkuperäinen* asserts temporal priority (being the first version). The P2 data shows these are frequently paired (*aito ja alkuperäinen*) precisely because they cover adjacent but distinct concerns. *Väärennös* and *väärentää* belong here rather than in Group 1 because they specifically concern the production of false objects, not the making of false statements.

## Group 4: Terms used in legal and investigative contexts for establishing what happened

**Label:** Expressions used in legal and investigative discourse for establishing facts and assigning proof

**Definition:** These expressions cluster in legal, judicial, and investigative contexts where the task is to establish what actually occurred, who is responsible, and what evidence supports a conclusion. They appear in court proceedings, criminal investigations, and formal legal texts. The cluster is organized around the *todistaa* / *todistus* / *todiste* family and the associated procedural vocabulary.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *todistaa* — "to prove, to testify, to witness"; central to both legal testimony and logical proof
- *todistus* — "proof, certificate, testimony"
- *todistaja* — "witness"
- *todiste* — "piece of evidence"
- *todistusaineisto* — "body of evidence"
- *todistustaakka* — "burden of proof"
- *todistusvoima* — "probative force, evidentiary weight"
- *todistetusti* — "demonstrably, as has been proven"
- *todentaa* / *todennus* — "to verify / verification, authentication"
- *tarkistaa* / *tarkistus* — "to check, to verify / check, verification"
- *tarkistaja* — "checker, verifier, inspector"
- *väärä todistus* — "false testimony"
- *väärä vala* — "false oath, perjury"
- *valheellinen* / *valheellisuus* — "mendacious / mendacity"; used in legal evaluation of testimony
- *valheellisesti todistaa* — "to testify falsely"
- *perusteeton* — "unfounded, groundless"; used to evaluate accusations and claims in legal contexts
- *perusteltu* — "justified, well-founded"; used to evaluate decisions and suspicions
- *perusteltu epäilys* — "a well-founded suspicion"
- *oikeuden edessä* — "before the court"
- *viedä oikeuteen* — "to take to court"
- *väärennysrikos* — "forgery offence"
- *totuus tulee ilmi* — "the truth comes to light"

**Observations:** This group is large because Finnish legal discourse draws heavily on the *tode-* root for proof and evidence, the *oikeus* root for the institutional apparatus, and the *väärä* root for falsification offences. *Tarkistaa* and *todentaa* appear here because they function in verification roles within legal and administrative proceedings, though they also appear in non-legal contexts (see Group 5). *Valheellinen* belongs here rather than in Group 1 because its primary register is legal and its primary referent is testimony.

## Group 5: Terms used in administrative and procedural contexts for checking and confirming

**Label:** Expressions used in administrative contexts for checking, verifying, and confirming that things are in order

**Definition:** These expressions appear in administrative, bureaucratic, and technical contexts where the task is to confirm that data, documents, credentials, or procedures meet required standards. They are used by institutions, officials, and systems rather than primarily in interpersonal or philosophical discourse. The cluster is organized around *tarkistaa*, *todentaa*, and *pätevyys*.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *tarkistaa* / *tarkistus* / *tarkistaja* — "to check / check, verification / checker, inspector"
- *tarkistaa tiedot* — "to verify the information"
- *tarkistaa passi* — "to check the passport"
- *tarkistaminen* — "the act of checking/verifying"
- *todentaa* / *todennus* — "to verify, to authenticate / verification, authentication"
- *todentaa henkilöllisyys* — "to verify identity"
- *kaksivaiheinen todennus* — "two-factor authentication"
- *pätevä* / *pätevyys* — "valid, competent / validity, competence, qualification"
- *pätevyysvaatimus* — "qualification requirement"
- *pätevyystodistus* — "certificate of competence"
- *täyttää pätevyysvaatimukset* — "to meet the qualification requirements"
- *pätemätön* / *pätemättömyys* — "invalid, not applicable / invalidity"
- *oikeellisuustarkistus* — "correctness check, validation"
- *tietojen oikeellisuus* — "the correctness of the information"
- *asianmukainen* / *asianmukaisesti* — "proper, appropriate / properly"; procedural conformity
- *asianmukainen menettely* — "proper procedure"
- *asianmukainen dokumentaatio* — "proper documentation"
- *luotettavuusarvio* — "reliability assessment"
- *tarkkuusvaatimus* — "accuracy requirement"

**Observations:** This group overlaps with Group 4 (legal contexts) and Group 2 (norm-conformity) but is distinguished by its specifically administrative and procedural character: the evaluation is performed by an institution or system against formal requirements, and the outcome is typically a binary pass/fail determination. *Pätevyys* is central here because it covers both the validity of credentials and the competence of persons in formal administrative evaluation.

## Group 6: Terms used to evaluate the reliability or credibility of sources and persons

**Label:** Expressions for evaluating whether a source, person, or claim can be trusted or believed

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether a source of information, a witness, an institution, or a claim is such that it can be relied upon or believed. They appear in journalistic, academic, legal, and everyday discourse. The cluster is organized around the contrast between *luotettava* (reliable, dependable) and *uskottava* (credible, believable), which are distinct in Finnish usage.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *luotettava* — "reliable, trustworthy, dependable"; emphasizes that something can be depended upon
- *luotettavuus* — "reliability, trustworthiness"
- *luotettavasti* — "reliably, dependably"
- *epäluotettava* / *epäluotettavuus* — "unreliable / unreliability"
- *luotettava lähde* — "a reliable source"
- *luotettava todistaja* — "a reliable witness"
- *tutkimuksen luotettavuus* — "the reliability of the research"
- *lähteen luotettavuus* — "the reliability of the source"
- *luotettavuusongelma* — "a credibility/reliability problem"
- *kyseenalaistaa luotettavuus* — "to call reliability into question"
- *uskottava* — "credible, believable, plausible"; emphasizes that something can or should be believed
- *uskottavuus* — "credibility, believability, plausibility"
- *uskottavasti* — "credibly, convincingly"
- *epäuskottava* / *epäuskottavuus* — "incredible, implausible / implausibility"
- *uskottava selitys* — "a credible explanation"
- *uskottava todistaja* — "a credible witness"
- *uskottava uhka* — "a credible threat"
- *tehdä uskottavaksi* — "to make credible, to lend credibility to"
- *pitää luotettavana* / *pitää uskottavana* — "to consider reliable / to consider credible"
- *osoittautua luotettavaksi* — "to prove to be reliable"

**Observations:** The P1 and P2 data explicitly distinguish *luotettava* (reliability/dependability) from *uskottava* (believability/credibility), and Finnish usage maintains this distinction: *luotettava lähde* concerns whether a source can be depended upon over time, while *uskottava selitys* concerns whether a specific explanation is believable. Both terms appear with *todistaja* (witness) in legal contexts, but with different evaluative emphases. *Luotettavuusongelma* is particularly common in journalistic discourse about institutional credibility.

## Group 7: Terms used to characterize deliberate misrepresentation or distortion

**Label:** Expressions for characterizing deliberate falsification, distortion, or misrepresentation

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers characterize acts or products of deliberate falsification, distortion, or misrepresentation. They appear in legal, journalistic, political, and moral discourse. The cluster is organized around *väärentää* / *väärennös* (forgery of objects/documents) and *vääristää* / *vääristely* (distortion of facts or representations), with *valheellinen* covering the domain of mendacious statements.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *väärentää* — "to forge, to falsify, to counterfeit"; deliberate production of a false object
- *väärennös* — "forgery, counterfeit"; the product of deliberate falsification
- *väärentäjä* — "forger, counterfeiter"
- *väärentää asiakirja* — "to forge a document"
- *väärentää historiaa* — "to falsify history"
- *väärentää allekirjoitus* — "to forge a signature"
- *väärentää rahaa* — "to counterfeit money"
- *vääristää* — "to distort, to falsify, to misrepresent, to skew"
- *vääristely* / *vääristelty* — "distortion, misrepresentation (as a practice) / distorted"
- *vääristää tosiasioita* — "to distort the facts"
- *historian vääristely* — "distortion of history"
- *valheellinen* / *valheellisuus* — "mendacious / mendacity"; deliberate falsehood in statements
- *valheellinen todistus* — "false testimony"
- *valheellinen väite* — "a mendacious claim"
- *valheellisesti todistaa* — "to testify falsely"
- *väärä henkilöllisyys* — "false identity"
- *väärä vala* — "false oath, perjury"

**Observations:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (truth/falsity of statements) by the element of deliberate intent: these expressions characterize acts of making something false, not merely the state of being false. *Vääristää* and *vääristely* are distinguished from *väärentää* in that they typically concern the distortion of facts, data, or representations rather than the production of counterfeit objects. *Valheellinen* belongs here because it specifically implies deliberate deception, unlike *epätosi* or *väärä* which can describe unintentional error.

---

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate whether a claim or decision is adequately supported

**Label:** Expressions for evaluating whether a claim, decision, or belief has adequate grounds

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether a claim, accusation, decision, or belief is supported by sufficient reasons or evidence. They appear in legal, academic, and argumentative discourse. The core contrast is between *perusteltu* (well-founded, justified) and *perusteeton* (unfounded, groundless).

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *perusteltu* — "justified, well-founded, substantiated"; supported by adequate grounds
- *perusteeton* — "unfounded, groundless, unsubstantiated"; lacking adequate grounds
- *perusteltu päätös* — "a justified decision"
- *perusteltu epäilys* — "a well-founded suspicion"
- *perusteeton syytös* — "an unfounded accusation"
- *perusteeton väite* — "an unsubstantiated claim"
- *pätevä peruste* — "a valid ground/reason"
- *pätevä* (in argumentative use) — "valid, sound"; applied to arguments and grounds
- *oikeutettu* — "justified, warranted, legitimate"; applied to actions, claims, and criticism
- *oikeuttaa* / *oikeutus* — "to justify / justification, warrant"
- *oikeutettu kritiikki* — "justified criticism"
- *todistusvoima* — "probative force, evidentiary weight"
- *todistustaakka* — "burden of proof"

**Observations:** This group overlaps with Group 4 (legal contexts) in that *todistustaakka* and *todistusvoima* appear in both. The distinguishing feature here is the focus on the adequacy of grounds or reasons, which extends beyond legal contexts into academic and everyday argumentative discourse. *Oikeutettu* and *oikeutus* belong here because they evaluate whether something is warranted by sufficient grounds, though they also appear in moral and legal contexts (see overlap note below).

---

## Group 9: Terms used to signal a gap between appearance and reality

**Label:** Expressions for signalling that something appears to be the case but may not be

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers signal that there is or may be a discrepancy between how something appears and how it actually is. They appear in analytical, journalistic, philosophical, and everyday discourse. The cluster is organized around *näennäinen* and the contrast between *todellinen* / *todellisuus* and what is merely apparent.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *näennäinen* — "apparent, seeming, ostensible"; has the surface form without the substance
- *näennäisesti* — "apparently, seemingly, ostensibly"
- *näennäisyys* — "apparentness, seeming quality"
- *näennäistiede* / *näennäistieteellinen* — "pseudoscience / pseudoscientific"
- *näennäinen yksimielisyys* — "apparent consensus"
- *näennäinen rehellisyys* — "ostensible honesty"
- *todellinen* — "real, actual"; contrasts with *näennäinen* (*todellinen syy* — "the real reason")
- *todellisuudessa* — "in reality, in actual fact"; the standard corrective adverb
- *todellisuus* — "reality"; the domain against which apparent states are evaluated
- *todellisuudentaju* — "sense of reality"
- *irti todellisuudesta* — "out of touch with reality"
- *vastata todellisuutta* — "to correspond to reality"
- *todellisuuspako* — "escapism, escape from reality"
- *epätodellinen* — "unreal, surreal"
- *totuus on toisenlainen* — "the truth is different"; corrective framing device
- *osoittautua todelliseksi* — "to turn out to be real"

**Observations:** This group is defined by the appearance/reality contrast rather than by the truth/falsity contrast of Group 1. *Näennäinen* is the primary term for the appearance side; *todellinen* and *todellisuus* anchor the reality side. The construction *todellisuudessa* functions as a discourse marker introducing a correction of an apparent state of affairs. *Todellisuuspako* and *todellisuudentaju* belong here because they concern a person's relationship to reality rather than the truth of specific propositions.

## Group 10: Terms used in accuracy and precision evaluation

**Label:** Expressions for evaluating the degree of correspondence between a representation and its object

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate how closely a measurement, description, translation, or observation corresponds to its referent or standard. Unlike the binary true/false contrast of Group 1, these expressions admit of degrees. They appear in scientific, technical, journalistic, and administrative discourse.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *tarkka* — "accurate, precise, exact, careful"
- *tarkkuus* — "accuracy, precision, exactness"
- *tarkasti* — "accurately, precisely, exactly"
- *tarkasti ottaen* — "strictly speaking, to be precise"
- *epätarkka* / *epätarkkuus* — "inaccurate / inaccuracy"
- *tarkka mittaus* — "an accurate measurement"
- *tarkka kuvaus* — "an accurate description"
- *mittauksen tarkkuus* — "the accuracy of the measurement"
- *millimetrin tarkkuudella* — "with millimetre precision"
- *tarkkuusvaatimus* — "accuracy requirement"
- *tarkentaa* / *tarkennus* — "to specify, to refine, to clarify / specification, clarification"
- *tarkkailla* / *tarkkailu* / *tarkkailija* — "to observe, to monitor / observation, surveillance / observer"
- *totuudenmukaisuus* — "truthfulness, fidelity to truth, accuracy"; overlaps with this group in journalistic evaluation of accounts
- *vääristymä* — "distortion, bias, skew"; the negative pole in statistical and scientific contexts

**Observations:** *Tarkka* and its derivatives form a coherent cluster around the concept of precision and close correspondence. *Tarkkailla* and *tarkkailu* are included because they derive from the same base and function in the related domain of careful observation, though they extend into surveillance contexts. *Vääristymä* belongs here as the negative pole (systematic deviation from accurate representation) rather than in Group 7 (deliberate distortion), because it does not necessarily imply intent.

## Group 11: Terms used in honesty evaluation of persons and conduct

**Label:** Expressions for evaluating persons and conduct as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether a person or their conduct is characterized by honesty, straightforwardness, and non-deception. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday discourse and are applied to persons and their statements or actions rather than to propositions or objects.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *rehellinen* — "honest, truthful, straightforward"
- *rehellisyys* — "honesty, truthfulness, integrity"
- *rehellisesti* — "honestly, truthfully"
- *rehellisesti sanottuna* — "honestly speaking"
- *rehellinen vastaus* — "an honest answer"
- *rehellinen ihminen* — "an honest person"
- *epärehellinen* / *epärehellisyys* — "dishonest / dishonesty"
- *valheellinen* — "mendacious, false"; overlaps with Group 7 but also evaluates character
- *valheellisuus* — "mendacity"

**Observations:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (propositional truth) by its focus on persons and conduct rather than statements. *Rehellinen* evaluates a person's character or a specific act as honest; *tosi* evaluates a proposition as true. *Valheellinen* appears in both this group and Group 7 because it can characterize both a person's character (mendacious) and a specific statement (false/mendacious). The overlap is genuine and reflects Finnish usage.

---

## Group 12: Terms used in justice and rights discourse

**Label:** Expressions used in discourse about justice, fairness, and legal rights

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether persons, decisions, and systems conform to principles of justice or fairness, or when they refer to the institutional apparatus of rights and law. They derive primarily from the *oikeus* root and appear in moral, legal, and political discourse.

**Representative expressions from P1 and P2:**

- *oikeudenmukainen* / *oikeudenmukaisuus* / *oikeudenmukaisesti* — "just, fair / justice, fairness / justly, fairly"
- *epäoikeudenmukainen* / *epäoikeudenmukaisuus* — "unjust / injustice"
- *oikeudenmukainen tuomio* — "a just verdict"
- *oikeudenmukainen kohtelu* — "fair treatment"
- *oikeus* — "right, justice, court, law"
- *oikeuslaitos* — "the judiciary, the justice system"
- *oikeusturva* — "legal protection, due process"
- *oikeushenkilö* — "legal person, legal entity"
- *oikeusvoima* — "legal force, res judicata"
- *oikeusvarmuus* — "legal certainty"
- *oikeustiede* — "jurisprudence, legal science"
- *oikeutettu* / *oikeuttaa* / *oikeutus* — "justified, entitled / to justify / justification, entitlement"
- *oikeus on sokea* — "justice is blind"
- *viedä oikeuteen* — "to take to court"

**Observations:** This group is large because the *oikeus* root is extremely productive in Finnish and covers both the abstract value of justice and the institutional apparatus of law. The group overlaps with Group 4 (legal/investigative discourse) in that both involve legal institutions, but Group 12 is distinguished by its focus on justice as a value and on rights as entitlements, rather than on the establishment of facts. *Oikeutettu* and *oikeutus* appear in both Group 8 (adequate grounds) and here, because they cover both epistemic justification and moral/legal entitlement.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 present genuine overlap across multiple groups and are noted here without forcing them into a single group:

**väärä / väärin**
Appears in Group 1 (propositional falsity: *epätosi* / *väärä*), Group 2 (norm-violation: *väärä vastaus*, *olla väärässä*), Group 7 (deliberate misrepresentation: *väärä henkilöllisyys*, *väärä vala*), and Group 3 (non-genuine: *väärennös* derives from it). Finnish usage does not consistently distinguish these senses; *väärä* is the general-purpose negative evaluative term across all these domains.

**oikeutettu / oikeuttaa / oikeutus**
Appears in Group 8 (adequate grounds: *oikeutettu kritiikki*, *episteeminen oikeutus*) and Group 12 (justice and rights: *oikeutettu vaatimus*, *oikeuttaa toiminta*). The overlap reflects genuine polysemy in Finnish usage.

**valheellinen / valheellisuus**
Appears in Group 7 (deliberate misrepresentation) and Group 11 (honesty evaluation of persons). Finnish usage applies it to both statements (*valheellinen väite*) and persons/conduct (*valheellinen todistaja*).

**tarkistaa / todentaa**
Appears in Group 4 (legal/investigative: *tarkistaa passi*, *todentaa henkilöllisyys*) and Group 5 (administrative/procedural: *tarkistaa tiedot*, *kaksivaiheinen todennus*). The overlap reflects the fact that verification procedures occur in both legal and administrative contexts.

**totuudenmukaisuus / totuudenmukainen**
Appears in Group 1 (truth-assertion: evaluating whether an account corresponds to the facts) and Group 10 (accuracy evaluation: journalistic standards of accuracy). Finnish usage applies these terms in both propositional and degree-of-correspondence contexts.

**pätevä / pätevyys** (in argumentative use)
Appears in Group 5 (administrative/procedural: qualification requirements) and Group 8 (adequate grounds: *pätevä peruste*). The overlap reflects the dual use of *pätevä* for both professional competence and logical validity.

## Items That Do Not Fit Cleanly into Any Group

**totuuskomissio** ("truth commission")
