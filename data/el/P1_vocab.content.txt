# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Greek (el)

## Lexical Items

1. **αλήθεια** (alítheia)  
   Literal: un-forgetting, un-hiddenness (α- privative + λήθη "forgetting")  
   Typical translations: truth, reality, truthfulness  
   Used to denote correspondence with facts, genuine state of affairs, or sincerity in speech and conduct. Common in everyday discourse, philosophical texts, and legal contexts to mark what is factually correct or authentic.  
   [CORE]

2. **ψέμα** (pséma)  
   Literal: lie, falsehood  
   Typical translations: lie, untruth  
   Everyday term for a false statement made with intent to deceive. Used in informal and formal contexts to identify deliberate misrepresentation of facts.  
   [CORE]

3. **αληθινός** (alithinós)  
   Literal: true, genuine (derived from αλήθεια)  
   Typical translations: true, genuine, real, authentic  
   Adjective marking something as conforming to reality or being authentic rather than counterfeit. Applied to objects, statements, and persons to indicate genuineness.  
   [CORE]

4. **ψεύτης** (pséftis)  
   Literal: liar (agent noun from ψεύδομαι "to lie")  
   Typical translations: liar, deceiver  
   Denotes a person who habitually or characteristically tells lies. Used in moral evaluation of character and trustworthiness.  
   [CORE]

5. **ψευδής** (pseudís)  
   Literal: false, lying  
   Typical translations: false, mendacious, deceptive  
   Adjective applied to statements, claims, or persons to mark them as not corresponding to truth. Common in both ancient and modern Greek across registers.  
   [CORE]

6. **σωστός** (sostós)  
   Literal: saved, correct (from σώζω "to save")  
   Typical translations: correct, right, proper, accurate  
   Marks conformity to standards, rules, or facts. Used for correct answers, proper conduct, accurate information, and things functioning as they should.  
   [CORE]

7. **λάθος** (láthos)  
   Literal: error, mistake  
   Typical translations: wrong, mistake, error, incorrect  
   Denotes deviation from correctness, whether in statements, calculations, actions, or judgments. Functions as both noun and adjective in modern Greek.  
   [CORE]

8. **ορθός** (orthós)  
   Literal: straight, upright  
   Typical translations: correct, right, proper, orthodox  
   Indicates conformity to standard, correctness in opinion or practice. Common in formal registers and compounds (e.g., ορθογραφία "correct writing, orthography").  
   [CORE]

9. **γνήσιος** (gnísios)  
   Literal: legitimate, born in wedlock  
   Typical translations: genuine, authentic, legitimate, true  
   Marks authenticity and legitimacy of objects, documents, or descent. Used to distinguish real from counterfeit or spurious.  
   [CORE]

10. **πλαστός** (plastós)  
    Literal: molded, formed, fabricated  
    Typical translations: fake, forged, counterfeit, false  
    Denotes something artificially made to deceive, especially forged documents or counterfeit objects. Carries connotation of deliberate fabrication.  
    [CORE]

11. **κίβδηλος** (kívdilos)  
    Literal: adulterated, debased (originally of coins)  
    Typical translations: counterfeit, spurious, adulterated, fake  
    Applied to objects or claims that are not genuine, especially those mixed with inferior material. More formal or literary than πλαστός.  
    [MARGINAL]

12. **αυθεντικός** (afthentikós)  
    Literal: authentic, original (from αυθέντης "master, author")  
    Typical translations: authentic, genuine, original  
    Marks something as original rather than copied, or as having verified provenance. Common in discussions of art, documents, and historical artifacts.  
    [CORE]

13. **ακριβής** (akrivís)  
    Literal: exact, precise  
    Typical translations: accurate, exact, precise, correct  
    Denotes precision and exactness in measurement, description, or representation. Used when correspondence to detail is emphasized.  
    [CORE]

14. **εικονικός** (eikonikós)  
    Literal: pertaining to images (from εικόνα "image")  
    Typical translations: virtual, notional, apparent, seeming  
    Marks something as having appearance without substance, or existing in representation rather than reality. Used in modern contexts for virtual reality and apparent phenomena.  
    [CORE]

15. **πραγματικός** (pragmatikós)  
    Literal: pertaining to things/facts (from πράγμα "thing, fact")  
    Typical translations: real, actual, true, genuine  
    Denotes what actually exists or occurred as opposed to what is imagined, claimed, or apparent. Common across all registers.  
    [CORE]

16. **δίκαιος** (díkaios)  
    Literal: just, righteous  
    Typical translations: just, fair, righteous, right  
    Marks conformity to justice, fairness, or moral rightness. Applied to persons, actions, and judgments in moral and legal evaluation.  
    [CORE]

17. **άδικος** (ádikos)  
    Literal: unjust (α- privative + δίκη "justice")  
    Typical translations: unjust, unfair, wrong  
    Denotes violation of justice or fairness. Used in moral and legal contexts to evaluate actions and persons negatively.  
    [CORE]

18. **ειλικρινής** (eilikrinís)  
    Literal: judged in sunlight, pure (disputed etymology)  
    Typical translations: sincere, honest, genuine, frank  
    Marks sincerity in speech and conduct, absence of deception or hidden motives. Applied to persons, statements, and emotions.  
    [CORE]

19. **υποκριτής** (ipokritís)  
    Literal: actor, one who answers (from υποκρίνομαι)  
    Typical translations: hypocrite, pretender  
    Denotes someone who pretends to have qualities or beliefs they lack. Used in moral evaluation of inconsistency between profession and practice.  
    [CORE]

20. **νόθος** (nóthos)  
    Literal: bastard, illegitimate  
    Typical translations: spurious, illegitimate, bastard, counterfeit  
    Marks illegitimate birth or spurious origin. Extended to denote anything not genuine or of questionable provenance.  
    [MARGINAL]

21. **έγκυρος** (énkyros)  
    Literal: valid, having authority  
    Typical translations: valid, legitimate, authoritative  
    Denotes legal or logical validity, proper authorization. Common in legal and administrative contexts for valid documents and arguments.  
    [CORE]

22. **άκυρος** (ákyros)  
    Literal: invalid, without authority (α- privative + κύρος "authority")  
    Typical translations: invalid, null, void  
    Marks lack of legal or logical validity. Used for invalidated documents, void contracts, and fallacious arguments.  
    [CORE]

23. **ψευδώνυμο** (psevdónymo)  
    Literal: false name (ψευδής + όνομα)  
    Typical translations: pseudonym, pen name, alias  
    Denotes a name used instead of one's real name. Fixed expression for assumed names in literary and other contexts.  
    [CORE]

24. **απατηλός** (apatilós)  
    Literal: deceptive (from απάτη "deception")  
    Typical translations: deceptive, misleading, deceitful  
    Marks something as likely to deceive or mislead. Applied to appearances, arguments, and persons who create false impressions.  
    [CORE]

25. **βέβαιος** (vévaios)  
    Literal: firm, steady, certain  
    Typical translations: certain, sure, reliable, confirmed  
    Denotes certainty, reliability, or confirmation. Used for verified information and trustworthy sources.  
    [CORE]

26. **αμφίβολος** (amfívolos)  
    Literal: thrown both ways, ambiguous  
    Typical translations: doubtful, dubious, ambiguous, questionable  
    Marks uncertainty, ambiguity, or questionable status. Applied to claims, evidence, and situations lacking clarity or reliability.  
    [CORE]

27. **καταχρηστικός** (katachristikós)  
    Literal: pertaining to misuse (from καταχρώμαι "to misuse")  
    Typical translations: improper, abusive, catachrestic  
    Denotes improper or abusive use, especially of language or authority. More technical or formal register.  
    [MARGINAL]

28. **ουσιαστικός** (ousiastikós)  
    Literal: pertaining to essence (from ουσία "essence, substance")  
    Typical translations: substantial, essential, real, actual  
    Marks what is essential or substantial rather than superficial or nominal. Used to distinguish real from apparent significance.  
    [CORE]
