# G8 — Candidate Universals, Outliers, and Open Questions

---

## Preliminary Notes

All claims below are derived from the G1–G7 synthesis and the underlying F-phase data. The evidence base is eight languages: Georgian (ka), Yoruba (yo), Finnish (fi), Basque (eu), Tamil (ta), Indonesian (id), Turkish (tr), Korean (ko). These span six language families (Kartvelian, Niger-Congo, Finno-Ugric, language isolate, Dravidian, Austronesian, Turkic, Koreanic) and represent diverse morphological types. Claims are graded by confidence and caveated where evidence is incomplete or potentially confounded.

---

## 1. Candidate Universals and Strong Cross-Language Tendencies

---

### CU-1. Lexical separation of deliberate deception from unintentional error

**Statement.** All eight languages maintain a lexical distinction between terms marking intentional false representation (lying, deception) and terms marking unintentional error or mistake. The two clusters show different moral valence, different collocational patterns, and different derivational behavior.

**Evidence.** Documented in all 8 languages: ka ტყუილი (lie) vs. მცდარი (erroneous); yo irọ́/purọ́ (lie/to lie) vs. àṣìṣe (error); fi valhe (lie) vs. virheellinen (erroneous); eu gezurra (lie) vs. okerra (wrong/erroneous); ta poy (lie) vs. tappu/tavaṟu (mistake/error); id bohong (lie) vs. keliru (mistaken); tr yalan (lie) vs. hatalı/yanılmak (erroneous/to err); ko 거짓말 (lie) vs. 틀리다 (to be wrong/incorrect). The deception terms consistently carry stronger moral valence and generate more agentive nominalizations. The distinction is maintained even when a single root covers both domains in some contexts.

**Caveats.** The boundary between deliberate and unintentional falsity is not always sharp in usage, and some terms (e.g., ka ყალბი, id salah) can shade between the two. The distinction is lexical and semantic, not always morphologically encoded. The eight languages are not a random sample; languages with less elaborated moral vocabulary might show less clear separation.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-2. Agentive nominalization asymmetry: "liar" lexicalized; "truth-teller" absent or periphrastic

**Statement.** In all eight languages, a dedicated agentive noun for "liar" or "habitual deceiver" is lexicalized through morphological derivation. No language productively derives a parallel agentive noun for "truth-teller" through the same morphological strategy. Where a "truth-teller" form exists, it is either marginal or requires a compound or periphrastic construction.

**Evidence.** Liar agentives: ka მატყუარა (ma-...-ara), yo apurọ́ (a- prefix), fi valehtelija (-ja suffix), eu gezurlari (-lari suffix), ta poyyan/poyyāḷ/poyyar (person-marking suffixes), id pembohong (peN- prefix), tr yalancı (-cı suffix), ko 거짓말쟁이 (-쟁이 suffix). Truth-teller forms: ka მართლმოსაუბრე [MARGINAL], tr doğrucu [MARGINAL], yo ọmọ òtítọ́ (compound "child of truth"), ko 진실을 말하는 사람 (periphrastic "person who speaks truth"). The asymmetry is documented in all 8 languages and is not explained by morphological constraints, since the same suffixes that create "liar" forms could in principle create "truth-teller" forms.

**Caveats.** The evidence is strong but the explanation is unclear. It is possible that some languages outside this sample have productive "truth-teller" agentives. The marginal forms in ka and tr show the pathway is not completely blocked, only strongly dispreferred. The asymmetry may reflect a general tendency to lexicalize negative agents more readily than positive agents in morally charged domains, but this broader claim is not tested here.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-3. Compound formation [DECEPTION MODIFIER + DOMAIN NOUN] is universally productive

**Statement.** All eight languages productively generate domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit money, fake news, and related concepts through the structural pattern [deception/falsity modifier + domain noun]. The specific modifier varies across languages but the compounding strategy is consistent.

**Evidence.** ka: ყალბი ფული (counterfeit money), ყალბი დოკუმენტი (forged document), ტყუილი ფიცი (false oath); yo: irọ́ compounds (ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ "true word," ìró tọ́ "true report"); fi: väärä hälytys (false alarm), väärä todistus (false testimony), väärä vala (false oath); eu: gezurrezko agiri (false document), gezurrezko lekukotza (false testimony), gezurrezko albistea (fake news); ta: poy cāṭci (false testimony), poy āvaṇam (false document), poy ceyti (false news); id: uang palsu (counterfeit money), dokumen palsu (forged document), berita palsu (fake news); tr: sahte para (counterfeit money), sahte belge (forged document); ko: 거짓증언 (false testimony), 거짓정보 (false information), 거짓뉴스 (fake news). The pattern is productive and generates new compounds as new domains emerge (e.g., fake news terms in all languages).

**Caveats.** This is a morphological/lexical pattern rather than a syntactic universal. The specific modifier (ka ყალბი, yo irọ́, fi väärä, eu gezurrezko, ta poy, id palsu, tr sahte, ko 거짓) varies, and the compounding strategy differs typologically (some languages use attributive adjectives, others use relational suffixes or bare nouns). The pattern may reflect the general productivity of nominal modification rather than something specific to the truth domain.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-4. Discourse marker grammaticalization from truth-domain roots

**Statement.** In all eight languages, at least one truth-domain form has grammaticalized into a discourse marker that introduces corrections, frank statements, or factual clarifications. This grammaticalization pattern appears across unrelated language families.

**Evidence.** ka: მართლაც (mart'lats) "really/indeed," მართლა? (mart'la?) "really?"; yo: nitootọ́ "truly/in truth," òtítọ́ ni "it is true/that's the truth"; fi: totta kai "of course/certainly" (fully grammaticalized), totta puhuen "to tell the truth"; eu: benetan "truly/really/indeed," egiatan "in truth/truly"; ta: uṇmaiyil "in truth/actually," nijamā? "really?/is that so?"; id: sebenarnya "actually/in fact," ternyata "it turns out/apparently" (grammaticalized evidential); tr: gerçekten "really/truly," hakikaten "really/truly/indeed"; ko: 사실은 (sasireun) "actually/in fact," 솔직히 (soljiki) "frankly." The degree of grammaticalization varies from fully opaque (fi totta kai, id ternyata) to semi-transparent (ka მართლა?, ko 사실은).

**Caveats.** The specific forms and their degree of grammaticalization vary considerably. Some (fi totta kai) are fully lexicalized; others (ko 사실은) retain transparent compositional structure. The pattern may reflect a general tendency for epistemic/evaluative vocabulary to grammaticalize into discourse markers, not something specific to truth-domain roots. The eight languages may not be representative of all possible grammaticalization paths.

**Confidence: High.**

### CU-5. Abstract nominalization from truth-domain bases is universally productive

**Statement.** All eight languages derive abstract quality nouns from truth-domain bases through dedicated suffixes or circumfixes. These nominalizations are used in formal, philosophical, and analytical discourse.

**Evidence.** ka: სიმართლე (si-mart'l-e) "truth," სანდოობა (sando-oba) "reliability"; yo: ìgbàgbọ́ (ì- prefix) "belief," ìbámu (ì- prefix) "correspondence"; fi: totuus (tosi + -uus) "truth," rehellisyys (rehellinen + -yys) "honesty"; eu: zuzentasuna (zuzen + -tasun + -a) "correctness/justice," egiazkotasuna (egiazko + -tasun + -a) "truthfulness"; ta: uṇmaittaṉmai (uṇmai + taṉmai) "truthfulness," nērmai (nēr + mai) "honesty"; id: kebenaran (ke- + benar + -an) "truth/correctness," kejujuran (ke- + jujur + -an) "honesty"; tr: doğruluk (doğru + -luk) "truth/correctness," dürüstlük (dürüst + -lük) "honesty"; ko: 정확성 (jeonghwak + -seong) "accuracy," 신빙성 (sinbing + -seong) "credibility." The specific suffixes differ typologically but the functional pattern is consistent.

**Caveats.** Abstract nominalization is a general morphological process not specific to the truth domain. The finding that it applies to truth-domain bases is expected given its general productivity. The more specific claim — that truth-domain bases are among the most productive sources of abstract nominalizations in all eight languages — is supported by the data but not quantified.

**Confidence: High.**

### CU-6. Proverbial or idiomatic expressions asserting truth's ultimate triumph or difficulty

**Statement.** All eight languages show fixed or semi-fixed expressions asserting that truth will eventually be revealed, that lies are short-lived, or that truth is difficult but persistent. These expressions reflect a shared cultural framing of truth as something that persists or emerges despite concealment.

**Evidence.** ka: "ტყუილს მოკლე ფეხები აქვს" (lies have short legs); yo: "òtítọ́ á kọjá irọ́" (truth will surpass lies), "òtítọ́ korò" (truth is bitter); fi: "totuus paljastuu lopulta" (truth will be revealed eventually); eu: egia eta gezurra binomial, "egia bilatu" (seek truth); ta: "uṇmai iṟutiyil vellum" (truth will triumph in the end); id: "ternyata benar" (it turns out to be true), "kebenaran akan terungkap" (truth will be revealed); tr: "acı gerçek" (bitter truth), "gerçek ortaya çıkar" (truth comes out); ko: "진실은 언제나 밝혀진다" (the truth always comes to light). The specific metaphors vary (legs, bitterness, light, triumph) but the underlying framing is consistent.

**Caveats.** The specific expressions vary considerably in form and metaphor. Some are fully lexicalized proverbs; others are semi-fixed collocations. The claim that this reflects a "shared cultural framing" goes beyond what the linguistic data alone can establish. The pattern may reflect universal pragmatic tendencies (truth-telling is valued; deception is condemned) rather than a specifically linguistic universal.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** The pattern is consistent across all 8 languages but the interpretation requires caution.

### CU-7. Separation of authenticity/genuineness from propositional truth

**Statement.** All eight languages maintain a lexical distinction between terms evaluating the truth-value of statements and terms evaluating the genuineness or authenticity of objects, persons, or emotions. The two domains show different collocational patterns and different constructional behavior.

**Evidence.** ka: ჭეშმარიტი (true statements) vs. ნამდვილი (genuine things); yo: tọ́ (correct) vs. gidi (genuine); fi: tosi (true) vs. aito (genuine); eu: egia (truth) vs. benetakoa (genuine); ta: uṇmai (truth) vs. acal (original/genuine); id: benar (correct) vs. asli (authentic); tr: doğru (correct) vs. gerçek (real/genuine) or asıl (original); ko: 진실 (truth) vs. 진짜 (genuine/real). The distinction is maintained even when some terms participate in both domains (ka ნამდვილი, tr gerçek).

**Caveats.** The boundary between propositional truth and object authenticity is not always sharp. Several terms (ka ნამდვილი, tr gerçek, ta uṇmai) participate in both domains. The distinction is a tendency rather than a strict separation. Some languages (tr) show more overlap between the two domains than others (fi, yo).

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-8. Reliability and credibility terms are freely gradable; core propositional truth predicates show resistance to degree modification in a majority of languages

**Statement.** In all eight languages, reliability and credibility terms are freely gradable (can be modified by degree adverbs, can increase or decrease). In contrast, core propositional truth predicates show resistance to scalar degree modification in at least five of the eight languages, where truth is treated as binary rather than scalar.

**Evidence.** Reliability freely gradable: ka უფრო სანდო (more reliable), yo dájúdájú (very certain), fi erittäin luotettava (very reliable), eu oso fidagarria (very reliable), ta nampakamāka (reliably), id sangat terpercaya (very trustworthy), tr çok güvenilir (very reliable), ko 신빙성이 높다 (credibility is high). Propositional truth resisted: yo tọ́ jù ú lọ [MARGINAL] (more correct than), ko (no attested degree modification of 진실), fi (truth-becoming not gradual), eu (no degree modification attested for egia), ta muḻu uṇmai [MARGINAL] (whole truth). Propositional truth allowed: ka ძალიან მართალი (very true), id sangat benar (very true), tr çok doğru (very true).

**Caveats.** The resistance is not universal: ka, id, and tr allow degree modification of propositional truth predicates. The pattern is a strong tendency (5 of 8 languages) rather than a universal. The distinction between "truth is binary" and "truth predicates resist degree modification for pragmatic reasons" is not always clear from the data. Some languages may allow degree modification in specific constructions (e.g., "more true than") while resisting it in others.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** Strong tendency, not universal.

### CU-9. Positive inchoative constructions for propositional truth are more productive than negative inchoative constructions

**Statement.** Across all eight languages, constructions expressing "becoming true" or "coming true" are more productive and more naturally expressed than constructions expressing "becoming false" or "turning out to be a lie." The negative direction is consistently expressed through discovery-of-falsity constructions rather than true inchoatives.

**Evidence.** Positive direction: ka მართალი გახდა [CORE], fi toteutua [CORE], tr gerçekleşmek [CORE], id menjadi benar [CORE], ta uṇmai āku [NATURAL]. Negative direction: fi (no common expression; [UNCERTAIN] gap documented in F6), yo (no attested construction for either direction), eu (both marginal), ko (both marginal; discovery preferred). All languages express "becoming false" through discovery constructions (fi osoittautua vääräksi, tr yanlış çıkmak, ko 거짓으로 판명되다, id ternyata palsu) rather than simple inchoatives. This asymmetry is documented in G5, G6, and G7a as one of the most consistent cross-language patterns.

**Caveats.** The asymmetry is strong but not absolute. Some languages (tr, id) have constructions that approach "becoming false" through resultative discovery verbs. The distinction between a true inchoative and a discovery construction is not always clear. Yoruba lacks constructions for both directions, which is a different pattern from the asymmetry.

**Confidence: High.**

### CU-10. Verification verbs substitute for direct causatives of propositional truth across languages

**Statement.** In all eight languages, the preferred way to express causing something to be true is through lexical verification verbs (prove, confirm, verify, reveal) rather than through direct causatives of truth adjectives. No language has a productive morphological causative of a truth adjective meaning "make true" in everyday use.

**Evidence.** ka: დაამტკიცა (proved), გადაამოწმა (verified) — lexical verbs preferred; yo: mú kí ó tọ́ [MARGINAL], reinterpreted as verification; fi: todistaa oikeaksi (prove correct), todentaa (verify/authenticate); eu: egiaztatu (verify), frogatu (prove); ta: uṟutipaṭuttu (confirm), cāṭci (witness/evidence); id: membenarkan (confirm/justify), mengesahkan (validate); tr: doğrulamak (verify/confirm), yanlışlamak (deny/refute); ko: 진실을 밝히다 (reveal the truth), 사실을 확인하다 (verify the facts). The substitution is most complete in yo and ko, where direct causatives of propositional truth predicates are absent or marginal.

**Caveats.** The distinction between "verify" and "make true" is conceptually clear but may not always be grammatically distinct. Some verification verbs (id membenarkan, tr doğrulamak) can be glossed as either "confirm" or "make correct." The claim that no language has a productive "make true" causative is strong but based on absence of evidence, which is weaker than positive evidence.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-11. Causing deception is more morphologically productive than causing propositional truth

**Statement.** Across all eight languages, causative constructions for deception and falsification are more morphologically productive and more naturally expressed than causative constructions for propositional truth. The asymmetry parallels the agentive asymmetry (CU-2) and the inchoative asymmetry (CU-9).

**Evidence.** Deception causatives natural: ka გააყალბა (falsified), მოატყუა (deceived); yo wọ́n mú kí ó pa irọ́ (they made him lie) [CORE]; fi vääristää (distort/falsify), väärentää (forge/counterfeit); eu norbaitek dokumentua faltsutu du (someone falsified the document); ta poy kaṭṭu (fabricate lies), ēmāṟṟu (deceive); id memalsu (forge), memalsukan (falsify), membohongi (lie to someone); tr yalanlamak (deny/refute), yanıltmak (mislead/deceive); ko 조작하다 (fabricate), 왜곡하다 (distort). Truth causatives constrained: see CU-10. The asymmetry is documented in G5, G6, and G7a.

**Caveats.** The asymmetry is strong but the explanation is unclear. It may reflect the general principle that negative/destructive causation is more natural than positive/constructive causation for epistemic properties (parallel to R3 in AOML). The asymmetry is less absolute for some languages (ka, fi, eu show productive causatives for both directions) than for others (yo, ko show strong asymmetry).

**Confidence: High.**

---

### CU-12. Reliability and credibility form a distinct semantic cluster, consistently separated from propositional truth

**Statement.** All eight languages maintain vocabulary for evaluating whether sources, persons, or information can be trusted, and this cluster is consistently kept separate from vocabulary for evaluating the truth-value of specific statements. Reliability terms are consistently gradable (unlike core truth predicates in several languages) and show different constructional behavior.

**Evidence.** Distinct reliability clusters: ka სანდო/საეჭვო (reliable/doubtful); yo dájú/gbàgbọ́/ìgbàgbọ́ (certain/believe/belief); fi luotettava/uskottava (reliable/credible); eu fidagarria/sinesgarria/zalantzagarria (reliable/credible/doubtful); ta nampakam/nampikkai/cantēkam (reliability/trust/doubt); id (partially: sah, terpercaya); tr güvenilir/inandırıcı (reliable/convincing); ko 신빙성/그럴듯하다 (credibility/plausible). The distinction between "this source is reliable" and "this claim is true" is maintained across all eight languages.

**Caveats.** The boundary between reliability and truth is not always sharp. Some terms (ka სანდო, yo dájú) participate in both domains. The claim that reliability is "consistently separated" from propositional truth is a tendency rather than an absolute separation. Indonesian's reliability vocabulary is less elaborated in the P1 data than other languages.

**Confidence: High.**

### CU-13. Metalinguistic truth-telling constructions show asymmetric verb selection across languages

**Statement.** In at least five of the eight languages, the speech act of lying is expressed through a more morphologically condensed construction (dedicated verb or compound verb) than the speech act of truth-telling (verb + object construction). The asymmetry parallels CU-2 and CU-11.

**Evidence.** Asymmetric: yo pa irọ́ (kill/strike + lie, conventionalized) vs. sọ òtítọ́ (speak + truth); fi valehdella (dedicated verb) vs. puhua totuutta (speak + truth); ta poy col (lie + say, no case marking) vs. uṇmaiyai col (truth-ACC + say, with accusative); id berbohong (dedicated verb) vs. berkata jujur (speak + honestly); ko 거짓말하다 (compound verb) vs. 진실을 말하다 (truth-ACC + say). Symmetric: ka სიმართლეს ამბობს / ტყუილს ამბობს (both verb + dative object); eu egia esan / gezurra esan (both verb + object); tr doğru söylemek / yalan söylemek (both verb + object).

**Caveats.** The asymmetry is present in 5 of 8 languages, not universal. Three languages (ka, eu, tr) show symmetric constructions. The asymmetry may reflect historical lexicalization patterns rather than a synchronic constraint. The Yoruba case (pa irọ́ with conventionalized verb pa "kill/strike") is particularly striking but may be a language-specific idiom.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** Strong tendency in majority of languages, not universal.

### CU-14. Moral rightness and justice vocabulary is present in all languages but shows variable integration with propositional truth vocabulary

**Statement.** All eight languages have vocabulary for moral rightness, justice, or fairness that is related to but distinct from factual truth vocabulary. However, the degree of lexical integration between the two domains varies considerably across languages.

**Evidence.** All languages have justice/moral rightness vocabulary: ka სამართლიანი/მართლმსაჯულება, yo òdodo, fi oikeudenmukaisuus, eu zuzentasuna/zuzenbide, ta niyāyam/aṟam, id adil/pantas, tr hak/haklı/haksız/adil, ko 정당하다/타당하다. Integration varies: ka mart'l- and tr hak- merge factual correctness with moral rightness within a single root; eu zuzen- similarly spans correctness, moral rightness, and legal concepts; yo, ko, and fi keep the domains more separate.

**Caveats.** The degree of integration is a matter of degree, not a binary distinction. The merger of factual correctness with moral rightness within a single root (ka, tr, eu) may reflect historical semantic development rather than a synchronic constraint. The claim that all languages have this vocabulary is expected given the universality of moral evaluation.

**Confidence: High** (for presence of the vocabulary); **Medium** (for the claim about variable integration).

### CU-15. Epistemic certainty and doubt form a distinct cluster in all languages, consistently gradable

**Statement.** All eight languages have vocabulary for expressing degrees of confidence or certainty regarding claims, and these terms are consistently gradable (unlike core propositional truth predicates in several languages). The certainty/doubt cluster is consistently kept separate from the propositional truth cluster.

**Evidence.** Certainty/doubt vocabulary: ka ეჭვი/უტყუარი (doubt/infallible); yo ìdájú/dájú (certainty/to be certain); fi todennäköinen/todennäköisyys (probable/probability); eu ziurra/ziurtasuna (certain/certainty); ta uṟuti/cantēkam/teḷivu (certainty/doubt/clarity); id (partially: yakin, ragu); tr kesin/şüpheli/kuşkulu (certain/doubtful); ko 확실하다/불확실성 (certain/uncertainty). All are freely gradable: yo dájúdájú (very certain), fi hyvin todennäköinen (very probable), eu oso ziurra (very certain), tr kesinlikle (certainly), ko 매우 확실하다 (very certain).

**Caveats.** Indonesian's certainty/doubt vocabulary is less elaborated in the P1 data. The distinction between certainty (epistemic stance) and truth (propositional content) is conceptually clear but may not always be grammatically distinct. Some terms (ka ეჭვი, eu zalantzagarria) participate in both reliability and certainty clusters.

**Confidence: High.**

## 2. Major Outliers

---

### 2a. Language-Level Outliers

**Yoruba (yo): Strongest resistance to inchoative and causative constructions for propositional truth.**

Yoruba shows the most extreme version of the pattern documented in CU-9 and CU-10. While other languages show restricted or variable inchoative and causative constructions for propositional truth, Yoruba lacks attested constructions for both directions of the inchoative (neither "becoming true" nor "becoming false" is attested). Causative constructions for truth states are pragmatically odd and reinterpreted as verification or enabling. This is not merely a matter of degree: Yoruba's F6 data explicitly marks these as [MISSING] rather than [MARGINAL]. The divergence is broad, affecting multiple constructional functions (CF4, CF5, CF6 for D1). It may reflect a genuine conceptual framing of truth as a static property to be discovered rather than a state that changes or is caused, but this interpretation goes beyond what the data alone establishes. The divergence is not narrow (it affects multiple constructions) and is strongly documented.

**Korean (ko): Parallel resistance to inchoative and causative constructions, plus archaic antonym.**

Korean shows a pattern similar to Yoruba for inchoative and causative constructions (both marginal for propositional truth), but with additional features: the native Korean antonym 그르다 (wrong) is archaic and survives only in the fixed expression 옳고 그름 (right and wrong), and morphological causatives for truth predicates are absent (periphrastic -게 하다 used instead). Korean also shows the most elaborated practical reality vs. theory cluster (실- compounds) of any language in the dataset, which is a positive outlier rather than a gap. The divergence from other languages on inchoative/causative constructions is broad and strongly documented.

**Georgian (ka): Widest single-root semantic range and unique "futile/in vain" extension.**

Georgian's mart'l- root generates the widest single-root semantic range in the dataset, spanning factual correctness, moral rightness, legal justice, orthography (მართლწერა), religious denomination (მართლმადიდებელი), and modal propriety (მართებს). This is a language-level outlier in the breadth of semantic extension from a single truth-related root. Additionally, Georgian is the only language where the "lie/false" root (ტყუილი) extends to cover "futile/in vain" (ტყუილად "in vain," ტყუილუბრალოდ "pointlessly"). This extension is documented only in Georgian and has no parallel in the other seven languages. Both divergences are narrow (specific to Georgian) but strongly documented.

**Tamil (ta): Three-way register stratification of truth vocabulary.**

Tamil is the only language with a three-way register stratification of truth vocabulary: uṇmai (general, all registers), mey (literary/classical/spiritual), and nijam (colloquial/borrowed). The mey root generates an extensive cluster of philosophical and spiritual compounds (meyññāṉam, meyyanpu, mey teyvam, mey pakti) that are absent for uṇmai. No other language in the dataset shows this degree of register-based lexical differentiation within the truth domain. The divergence is narrow (specific to Tamil) but strongly documented and reflects a genuine structural feature of Tamil's lexical organization.

**Indonesian (id): Analytic negation strategy for all truth-domain oppositions.**

Indonesian uses the analytic negator tidak for all truth-domain negations rather than a prefix or suffix. This means Indonesian lacks the morphological antonym pairs found in tr (-lı/-sız), ta (-āṉa/-aṟṟa), fi (epä-), ko (부-/불-), and eu (-garri on contrasting bases). Indonesian's oppositions are consistently constructional rather than morphological in the truth domain. This is a language-level outlier in negation strategy, though it reflects Indonesian's general typological preference for analytic over synthetic morphology rather than something specific to the truth domain.

### 2b. Construction-Level Outliers

**Yoruba: Asymmetric verb selection in metalinguistic truth predicates (pa irọ́).**

The Yoruba construction pa irọ́ (tell a lie, literally "kill/strike + lie") uses a verb (pa "kill/strike") that is not used for truth-telling (sọ "speak") and is not compositionally predictable. This conventionalized pairing is a construction-level outlier: no other language in the dataset uses a "kill/destroy" verb for the speech act of lying. The divergence is narrow (specific to this construction) but strongly documented.

**Basque: Pejorative nominalization asymmetry (-tasun vs. -keria).**

Basque uses the neutral -tasun suffix for positive moral qualities (zintzotasuna "honesty") and the pejorative -keria suffix for negative moral qualities (gezurkeria "habitual lying," faltsukeria "falseness," iruzurkeria "fraudulence"). This formal asymmetry encoding a moral asymmetry is not found in other languages, where both positive and negative nominalizations use the same suffix pattern. The divergence is narrow (specific to Basque's suffix system) but strongly documented and reflects a genuine structural feature.

**Finnish: Productive inchoative/causative morphological pair for truth-domain terms.**

Finnish shows the most productive inchoative/causative morphological pair in the dataset: todellistua/todellistaa (come true/realize), toteutua/toteuttaa (be realized/implement), todentua/todentaa (be verified/verify). Turkish shows a parallel pattern (gerçekleşmek/gerçekleştirmek, kesinleşmek/kesinleştirmek). No other language shows this degree of morphological productivity for inchoative/causative pairs in the truth domain. This is a positive outlier (more productive than other languages) rather than a gap.

**Korean: Advancement/progress morpheme sharing with truth morpheme.**

Korean's 진 (jin) morpheme appears in truth/genuineness compounds (진실, 진짜, 진심), advancement/progress compounds (진보, 진화, 진행, 진출), and medical examination terms (진단, 진찰). Whether this represents genuine polysemy or homophony of distinct roots cannot be determined from the synchronic data. No other language in the dataset shows this degree of morphological overlap between truth-related and advancement-related vocabulary. The divergence is narrow (specific to Korean's morphological system) and its interpretation is uncertain.

### 2c. Dimension-Specific Outliers

**D1 × CF4/CF5 (Inchoative for propositional truth): Yoruba and Korean as outliers.**

While most languages show variable but attested inchoative constructions for propositional truth (positive direction more productive than negative), Yoruba lacks attested constructions for both directions and Korean treats both as marginal. These two languages represent the extreme end of the resistance pattern documented in CU-9. The divergence is broad (affecting multiple constructions) and strongly documented.

**D10 × CF6/CF7 (Validity causatives): Indonesian and Turkish as positive outliers.**

Indonesian and Turkish show the most elaborated legal validity causative vocabulary in the dataset (id mengesahkan/membatalkan, tr meşrulaştırmak/geçersiz kılmak). Both directions of validity causation are ALLOWED and productive. This contrasts with the general pattern for propositional truth causatives (CU-10) and suggests that institutional/procedural validity behaves differently from propositional truth under causation. The divergence is dimension-specific and reflects genuine structural differences in how validity is conceptualized.

**D11 (Similarity and Correspondence): Yoruba as positive outlier.**

Yoruba shows the most elaborated correspondence/matching cluster of any language in the dataset (bá mu, ìbámu, jọ, ìjọra, àìbámu, bá mu pọ̀, ìbámu pípé). This is a positive outlier: Yoruba has developed more explicit vocabulary for the correspondence relation between representations and reality than any other language in the sample. The divergence is dimension-specific and strongly documented.

**D12 (Practical Reality vs. Theory): Korean as positive outlier.**

Korean shows the most extensive vocabulary for distinguishing actual practice from theory or nominal status, with a large cluster of 실- (sil-) compounds (실제, 실질, 실천, 실행, 실현, 실력, 실적, 실효, 실무, 실전, 실습, 실험, 실시간, etc.). No other language in the dataset shows this degree of lexical elaboration for the theory/practice distinction. The divergence is dimension-specific and strongly documented.

**D5 × CF4 (Character change toward honesty): More productive than AOML predicts.**

Across all languages with F6 data, inchoative constructions for character change toward honesty are natural and productive (fi hän muuttui rehellisemmäksi, ko 정직해졌다, id menjadi jujur, tr dürüst olmak). This contrasts with the restricted pattern for propositional truth inchoatives (CU-9) and suggests that character is treated as more changeable than propositional truth across languages. This is not an outlier within the empirical data but represents a divergence from AOML's prediction (BECOME × SINC = restricted).

## 3. Open Questions

---

**OQ-1. What explains the agentive asymmetry (CU-2)?**

The universal absence of productive "truth-teller" agentives alongside universal presence of "liar" agentives is one of the strongest findings in the dataset. The morphological resources exist in every language; the forms are simply not produced. Is this a grammatical constraint, a lexicalization tendency, a pragmatic pattern (liars are more salient as social types), or something else? The current data establishes the pattern but does not explain it. Testing additional languages, particularly those with very different morphological systems, would help determine whether the pattern is truly universal or reflects properties of the eight languages sampled.

**OQ-2. Is the resistance to degree modification of propositional truth predicates a grammatical constraint or a pragmatic tendency?**

Five of eight languages show documented resistance to scalar degree modification of core truth predicates (yo, ko, fi, eu, ta). Three languages allow it (ka, id, tr). The variation is not random: languages that treat truth as binary resist degree modification; languages that treat truth as more scalar allow it. But it is unclear whether this reflects a grammatical constraint (truth predicates are categorically non-gradable in these languages) or a pragmatic tendency (degree modification is dispreferred but not ungrammatical). Elicitation studies targeting the grammaticality vs. pragmatic oddness distinction would help clarify this.

**OQ-3. Should institutional validity (D10) be treated as a distinct dimension from moral rightness (D6)?**

The empirical data shows that D10 × CF6/CF7 (validity causatives) are ALLOWED while D6 × CF6 (moral rightness causatives) is VARIABLE. This suggests that institutional/procedural validity and moral rightness behave differently under causation. AOML's single NORM axis conflates these two sub-domains. Whether this difference reflects a genuine semantic distinction (institutional properties can be conferred by agents; moral properties cannot) or a language-specific pattern requires testing across more languages and more targeted elicitation.

**OQ-4. Is the Yoruba correspondence/matching cluster (D11) a genuine cross-linguistic elaboration or a sampling artifact?**

Yoruba shows the most elaborated correspondence/matching vocabulary of any language in the dataset (bá mu, ìbámu, jọ, ìjọra, àìbámu). This may reflect a genuine typological feature of Yoruba (or Niger-Congo languages more broadly) or may reflect the depth of elicitation in the Yoruba F-phase data. Testing other Niger-Congo languages and comparing the depth of correspondence vocabulary would help determine whether this is a language-family feature or a sampling artifact.

**OQ-5. What is the scope of the causative constraint for propositional truth (CU-10)?**

The finding that "make true" causatives are absent or marginal while verification verbs are natural raises a question about the scope of the constraint. Is the constraint specific to propositional truth (D1), or does it extend to other truth-related dimensions? The empirical data shows that D10 × CF6 (validity causatives) is ALLOWED, suggesting the constraint is specific to propositional truth rather than a general property of the truth domain. But the boundary between D1 and D10 is not always clear, and more targeted testing is needed.

**OQ-6. Is the Georgian ტყუილი extension to "futile/in vain" a historical accident or a cross-linguistically available semantic pathway?**

The extension of the "lie/false" root to cover "futile/in vain" is documented only in Georgian. It is possible that this extension is available in other languages but has not been lexicalized, or that it reflects a historical semantic development specific to Georgian. Testing whether speakers of other languages find the "lie → futile" extension semantically natural (even if not lexicalized) would help determine whether this is a language-specific accident or a cross-linguistically available pathway.

**OQ-7. Does the Tamil register stratification (CU-14 caveat) reflect a typological feature of Dravidian languages or a language-specific development?**

Tamil's three-way register stratification (uṇmai/mey/nijam) is unique in the dataset. Whether this reflects a general Dravidian feature (other Dravidian languages may show similar stratification), a feature of Tamil's specific literary and religious history, or a more general tendency in languages with long literary traditions is unclear. Testing other Dravidian languages (Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam) would help determine the scope of this pattern.

**OQ-8. How robust is the positive emergence bias (CU-9) across a larger language sample?**

The asymmetry between positive and negative inchoative constructions for propositional truth is one of the most consistent findings in the dataset. But the sample of eight languages may not be representative. Languages with different aspectual systems, different conceptualizations of truth, or different discourse traditions might show different patterns. The finding is strong within the current sample but requires broader testing.

**OQ-9. What is the relationship between the agentive asymmetry (CU-2), the metalinguistic predicate asymmetry (CU-13), and the causative asymmetry (CU-11)?**

Three distinct asymmetries all point in the same direction: the negative pole of the truth domain (lying, deception) is more morphologically condensed and lexically elaborated than the positive pole (truth-telling, honesty). The agentive asymmetry (liar lexicalized; truth-teller not), the metalinguistic predicate asymmetry (lying has a dedicated verb; truth-telling uses verb + object), and the causative asymmetry (causing deception more productive than causing truth) may all reflect a single underlying tendency. Whether these three patterns have a common explanation — and what that explanation might be — is an open question that the current data motivates but does not resolve.

**OQ-10. Are there languages where the authenticity/genuineness domain (D4) and the propositional truth domain (D1) are more thoroughly merged than in the current sample?**

In the current sample, all eight languages maintain some distinction between D1 and D4, though some terms (ka ნამდვილი, tr gerçek) participate in both. It is possible that some languages outside the sample show a more thorough merger of the two domains, treating "genuine" and "true" as a single undifferentiated concept. Testing languages from different typological and cultural backgrounds would help determine whether the D1/D4 distinction is universal or a strong tendency.

**OQ-11. Is the discourse marker grammaticalization pattern (CU-4) specific to truth-domain roots, or is it a general property of epistemic/evaluative vocabulary?**

The universal grammaticalization of truth-domain forms into discourse markers is one of the most consistent findings in the dataset. But it is unclear whether this reflects something specific to truth-domain roots or a general tendency for epistemic/evaluative vocabulary to grammaticalize into discourse markers. Testing whether other epistemic domains (e.g., knowledge, belief, possibility) show comparable grammaticalization rates would help determine the scope of this pattern.

**OQ-12. What is the status of the AOML LIFE axis in light of the empirical data on similarity (D11)?**

The empirical data shows that similarity/resemblance nominalizations are natural across languages (yo ìjọra, id kemiripan, eu antzetasuna, ko 유사성), which conflicts with AOML's prediction that ABSTR × LIFE = blocked. If similarity belongs to LIFE, this is a genuine conflict. If similarity should be placed outside LIFE (in a separate axis or sub-axis), the conflict dissolves. Resolving this requires a clearer theoretical specification of what LIFE covers and whether natural similarity between objects belongs to the same axis as constructed representational fidelity.
