# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

The following documents derivational forms associated with each P1 base item. Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The focus here is on derivations not already captured in P1b, plus systematic documentation of the derivational relationship between P1 items themselves. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and currency of use.

---

## 1. Base Form: αληθής (*alithís*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** alíthia
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εια (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
- **Usage:** The primary abstract noun derived from αληθής. Already documented as a P1 core item. The derivational relationship is transparent and productive.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθινός
- **Transliteration:** alithinós
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ινός (adjectival suffix indicating material or characteristic nature)
- **Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
- **Usage:** Already documented as a P1 core item. The suffix -ινός shifts the adjective from a formal/philosophical register (αληθής) toward a more colloquial and applied register.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθεύω
- **Transliteration:** alithévo
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to be truthful
- **Usage:** Already documented in P1b. The verbal form is used in philosophical and literary registers; less common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθώς
- **Transliteration:** alithós
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, verily
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of αληθής. Used primarily in formal, liturgical, and literary registers. Appears in the Easter liturgical exchange "Χριστός Ανέστη / Αληθώς Ανέστη" (Christ is risen / Truly He is risen). Less common in everyday speech than αληθινά.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αναληθής
- **Transliteration:** analithís
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative prefix) + αληθής (true)
- **Natural English translation:** untrue, false
- **Usage:** Already documented in P1b as [MARGINAL]. The privative negation of αληθής; used in formal philosophical and logical contexts to describe a proposition that does not correspond to reality.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθοφανής
- **Transliteration:** alithofanís
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ο- (linking vowel) + φαν- (from φαίνομαι, to appear) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** plausible, verisimilar, truth-seeming
- **Usage:** Already documented in P1b. Used in rhetorical, literary, and philosophical contexts to describe something that has the appearance of truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθοφάνεια
- **Transliteration:** alithofánia
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθοφανής (truth-seeming) + -εια (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, plausibility
- **Usage:** Already documented in P1b. The abstract noun corresponding to αληθοφανής; used in literary criticism and rhetoric.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The αληθ- root is moderately productive in derivation. The primary derivational paths are: (a) the abstract noun αλήθεια, (b) the colloquial adjective αληθινός with its adverb αληθινά, (c) the formal adverb αληθώς, (d) the verb αληθεύω, and (e) the compound stem αληθοφαν- for the appearance-of-truth cluster. The privative form αναληθής exists but is marginal.

## 2. Base Form: αλήθεια (*alíthia*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αλήθεια
- **Derived:** αναλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** analíthia
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αλήθεια (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
- **Usage:** Already documented in P1b. A formal or literary term for falsehood, used as a more elevated alternative to ψέμα.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** αλήθεια itself is a derived form of αληθής (see above). Its own derivational productivity is limited: the primary form it generates is the privative αναλήθεια. The adverbial and phrasal uses documented in P1b (στ' αλήθεια, αλήθεια ή ψέμα, etc.) are syntactic rather than morphological derivations and are not repeated here.

## 4. Base Form: γνήσιος (*gníssios*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησιότητα
- **Transliteration:** gnissiótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνήσιος (genuine) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix indicating quality)
- **Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, legitimacy
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in legal, aesthetic, and everyday contexts: "η γνησιότητα ενός έργου τέχνης" (the authenticity of a work of art). Appears in the collocation πιστοποίηση γνησιότητας (certification of authenticity), documented in P1b.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησίως
- **Transliteration:** gnissíos
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνήσιος (genuine) + -ως (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** genuinely, legitimately
- **Usage:** The formal adverbial form of γνήσιος. Used in legal and official documents to indicate that something is done or presented in a genuine or legitimate manner. Less common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** γνήσιος does not appear to generate a common verbal derivation. The derivational family is limited to the adjective, its abstract noun γνησιότητα, and the marginal adverb γνησίως.

## 5. Base Form: αυθεντικός (*afthentikós*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντικότητα
- **Transliteration:** afthentikótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** αυθεντικός (authentic) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** authenticity
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for authenticity. Used in aesthetic, philosophical, psychological, and legal contexts: "η αυθεντικότητα της υπογραφής" (the authenticity of the signature); "η αυθεντικότητα του εαυτού" (the authenticity of the self). Highly productive in contemporary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντικά
- **Transliteration:** afthentika
- **Literal Breakdown:** αυθεντικός (authentic) + -α (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** authentically, genuinely
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of αυθεντικός. Used to describe behavior or expression that is genuinely consistent with one's character: "συμπεριφέρεται αυθεντικά" (he/she behaves authentically). Common in psychological and self-help registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντία
- **Transliteration:** afthentía
- **Literal Breakdown:** αυθέντης (one who acts on their own authority) + -ία (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** authority, authenticity (archaic/formal)
- **Usage:** An older or more formal nominal form related to the same root as αυθεντικός. In modern Greek, αυθεντία primarily means "authority" in the sense of recognized expertise or power, rather than "authenticity" in the aesthetic sense. The semantic split between αυθεντία (authority) and αυθεντικότητα (authenticity) is well established in contemporary usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The base αυθέντης (the underlying noun) is archaic in modern Greek; αυθεντικός and its derivatives are the productive modern forms. The derivational family is: αυθεντικός → αυθεντικότητα (abstract noun), αυθεντικά (adverb). αυθεντία is a parallel nominal form from the same root but with a distinct semantic trajectory.

## 6. Base Form: ακριβής (*akrivís*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ακρίβεια
- **Transliteration:** akrívia
- **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- (precise, exact) + -εια (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** accuracy, precision, exactness; also: high cost, expensiveness
- **Usage:** The primary abstract noun from ακριβής. Carries two distinct senses in modern Greek: (a) precision or accuracy ("η ακρίβεια της μέτρησης" = the accuracy of the measurement) and (b) high cost or expensiveness ("η ακρίβεια των τροφίμων" = the high cost of food). Both senses are fully current and [CORE]. The semantic duality reflects the historical development of the word.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ακριβώς
- **Transliteration:** akrivós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- (precise) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** exactly, precisely, accurately
- **Usage:** The standard adverb of ακριβής. Extremely common in everyday speech as a response particle meaning "exactly" or "precisely" (equivalent to "exactly!" in English), and in descriptive use: "ακριβώς στις 8" (exactly at 8). One of the most frequent adverbs in spoken Greek.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ακριβολόγος
- **Transliteration:** akrivológos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- (precise) + -ο- (linking vowel) + λόγος (word, speech)
- **Natural English translation:** precise in speech, meticulous, exact
- **Usage:** Used to describe a person who is careful and precise in their use of language or in their statements. Applied in contexts of scholarly, legal, or professional precision. Less common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ακριβολογία
- **Transliteration:** akrivología
- **Literal Breakdown:** ακριβ- (precise) + -ο- (linking vowel) + λογία (discourse, study)
- **Natural English translation:** precision of language, exactness of expression
- **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to ακριβολόγος. Refers to the quality or practice of precise, exact expression. Used in scholarly and critical contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ανακριβής
- **Transliteration:** anakrivís
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + ακριβής (accurate)
- **Natural English translation:** inaccurate, imprecise, inexact
- **Usage:** The privative negation of ακριβής. Used in formal, journalistic, and academic contexts to characterize a statement, measurement, or account as not accurate. "Η περιγραφή είναι ανακριβής" (The description is inaccurate).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base:** ακριβής
- **Derived:** ανακρίβεια
- **Transliteration:** anakrívia
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + ακρίβεια (accuracy)
- **Natural English translation:** inaccuracy, imprecision
- **Usage:** The abstract noun for inaccuracy. Used in journalistic, legal, and academic contexts to refer to errors or imprecisions in a statement or account: "η ανακρίβεια της αναφοράς" (the inaccuracy of the report).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The ακριβ- root is moderately productive. The core derivational family is: ακριβής (adjective) → ακρίβεια (abstract noun, with semantic split) → ακριβώς (adverb). The privative pair ανακριβής / ανακρίβεια is well attested. The compound forms ακριβολόγος / ακριβολογία are marginal.

## 7. Base Form: ορθός (*orthós*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθότητα
- **Transliteration:** orthótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (correct, straight) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** correctness, rightness, propriety
- **Usage:** The abstract noun for correctness or rightness. Used in philosophical, linguistic, and evaluative contexts: "η ορθότητα μιας απόφασης" (the correctness of a decision). Less common in everyday speech than σωστό.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθά
- **Transliteration:** orthá
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθός (correct) + -α (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** correctly, rightly, properly
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of ορθός. Used to describe correct performance of an action or correct understanding: "το κατάλαβες ορθά" (you understood it correctly). Common in formal and educational registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθογραφία
- **Transliteration:** orthografía
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (correct) + -ο- (linking vowel) + γραφία (writing)
- **Natural English translation:** orthography, correct spelling
- **Usage:** The standard term for correct spelling or the system of spelling in a language. Used in educational, linguistic, and everyday contexts: "κάνει λάθη ορθογραφίας" (he/she makes spelling mistakes). Extremely common.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθογραφικός
- **Transliteration:** orthografikós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθογραφία (orthography) + -ικός (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** orthographic, spelling (adj.)
- **Usage:** The adjective derived from ορθογραφία. Used in educational and linguistic contexts: "ορθογραφικό λάθος" (spelling error), "ορθογραφικός έλεγχος" (spell check).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθόδοξος
- **Transliteration:** orthódhoksos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (correct, right) + -ο- (linking vowel) + δόξα (belief, opinion)
- **Natural English translation:** orthodox; Orthodox (Christian)
- **Usage:** Used both as a general adjective meaning "holding correct beliefs" or "conforming to established doctrine," and specifically as a proper adjective for the Eastern Orthodox Christian tradition. "Ορθόδοξη Εκκλησία" (Orthodox Church). Extremely common in both senses.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθοδοξία
- **Transliteration:** orthodhoksía
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθόδοξος (orthodox) + -ία (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** orthodoxy; Orthodoxy (religious)
- **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to ορθόδοξος. Used in religious, political, and intellectual contexts to refer to adherence to established or correct doctrine. "Θρησκευτική ορθοδοξία" (religious orthodoxy).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ανορθόδοξος
- **Transliteration:** anorthódhoksos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + ορθόδοξος (orthodox)
- **Natural English translation:** unorthodox, heterodox
- **Usage:** Used to describe approaches, methods, or beliefs that deviate from established norms or doctrine. "Ανορθόδοξες μέθοδοι" (unorthodox methods). Common in both religious and secular contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθολογισμός
- **Transliteration:** ortholoyismós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (correct, right) + -ο- (linking vowel) + λογισμός (reasoning)
- **Natural English translation:** rationalism, rational thinking
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical and intellectual discourse to refer to the approach that privileges reason as the basis for knowledge and action. "Ο ορθολογισμός του Διαφωτισμού" (the rationalism of the Enlightenment).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθολογικός
- **Transliteration:** ortholoyikós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (correct) + -ο- (linking vowel) + λογικός (rational, logical)
- **Natural English translation:** rational, rationalistic
- **Usage:** Used to describe approaches, decisions, or arguments that are based on reason and logic. "Ορθολογική σκέψη" (rational thinking). Common in academic and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10**
- **Base:** ορθός
- **Derived:** ορθοπεδικός
- **Transliteration:** orthopedikós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ορθ- (straight, correct) + -ο- (linking vowel) + παιδ- (child) + -ικός (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** orthopedic
- **Usage:** The standard medical adjective for the branch of medicine dealing with the musculoskeletal system. "Ορθοπεδικός χειρουργός" (orthopedic surgeon). The connection to the truth-evaluation domain is etymological only; current usage is purely medical.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The ορθ- root is highly productive in Greek, generating compounds across multiple domains: spelling (ορθογραφία), religion (ορθόδοξος), philosophy (ορθολογισμός), and medicine (ορθοπεδικός). The core truth-evaluation derivations are ορθότητα and ορθά. The compound forms represent domain-specific extensions of the "correct/straight" meaning.

## 8. Base Form: σωστός (*sostós*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** σωστός
- **Derived:** σωστά
- **Transliteration:** sostá
- **Literal Breakdown:** σωστός (correct) + -α (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** correctly, rightly, properly
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of σωστός. Extremely common in everyday speech: "το έκανες σωστά" (you did it correctly). Also used as a response particle meaning "right!" or "correct!" in conversation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** σωστός
- **Derived:** σωστότητα
- **Transliteration:** sostótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** σωστ- (correct) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** correctness, propriety
- **Usage:** The abstract noun for correctness or propriety. Less common than σωστά in everyday speech; used in more formal evaluative contexts: "η σωστότητα της απόφασης" (the correctness of the decision). Somewhat less frequent than ορθότητα in formal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** σωστός does not appear to generate productive compound forms or verbal derivations. Its derivational family is limited to the adverb σωστά and the marginal abstract noun σωστότητα. The underlying verb σώζω (to save) has its own extensive derivational family, but this is not directly connected to the truth-evaluation function of σωστός.

## 9. Base Form: έγκυρος (*éngiros*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** εγκυρότητα
- **Transliteration:** engirotita
- **Literal Breakdown:** έγκυρ- (valid, authoritative) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** validity, authority, credibility
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for validity or authority. Used in legal, administrative, and epistemic contexts: "η εγκυρότητα ενός εγγράφου" (the validity of a document); "η εγκυρότητα μιας πηγής" (the credibility of a source). Common in formal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** άκυρος
- **Transliteration:** ákiros
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀ- (privative) + κῦρος (authority, validity)
- **Natural English translation:** invalid, void, null
- **Usage:** The privative counterpart of έγκυρος, formed directly from the shared root κῦρος rather than from έγκυρος itself. Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe a document, contract, or decision that has no legal force: "η σύμβαση είναι άκυρη" (the contract is void). Extremely common in legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** ακυρότητα
- **Transliteration:** akirótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἄκυρος (invalid) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** invalidity, nullity, voidness
- **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to άκυρος. Used in legal contexts to refer to the state of being legally void or invalid: "η ακυρότητα της σύμβασης" (the nullity of the contract).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** ακυρώνω
- **Transliteration:** akiróno
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἄκυρος (invalid) + -ώνω (verbal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** to cancel, to annul, to invalidate, to void
- **Usage:** The verb for canceling or annulling something. Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts: "ακύρωσε την κράτηση" (he/she cancelled the reservation); "ακυρώνω μια σύμβαση" (to annul a contract). Very common across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** ακύρωση
- **Transliteration:** akírosi
- **Literal Breakdown:** ακυρώνω (to cancel) + -ση (nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** cancellation, annulment, invalidation
- **Usage:** The nominal form of ακυρώνω. Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts: "ακύρωση εισιτηρίου" (ticket cancellation); "ακύρωση γάμου" (annulment of marriage).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The κῦρος root underlying έγκυρος is itself productive, generating the cluster άκυρος / ακυρότητα / ακυρώνω / ακύρωση on the negative side. The positive έγκυρος generates primarily εγκυρότητα. The root κῦρος also appears in κυρίαρχος (sovereign) and related political terms, but these are outside the truth-evaluation domain.

## 11. Base Form: αξιόλογος (*aksiólogos*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

- **Transliteration:** aksioloyó
