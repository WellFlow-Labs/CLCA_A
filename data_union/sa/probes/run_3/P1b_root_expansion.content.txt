# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** सत् / *sat* (from root *as-*, to be)
**P1 items participating:** सत् (*sat*, #11), असत् (*asat*, #12), सत्य (*satya*, #1), असत्य (*asatya*, #4), वास्तव (*vāstava*, #13), वस्तु (*vastu*, #14)
**Count:** 6 items (directly); the *sat/satya* family is the most pervasive cluster in the P1 set.
**Core meaning contribution:** Being, existence, and genuine reality as the ground of truth. The root supplies both the ontological sense (what truly exists) and the evaluative sense (what is genuine, good, or correct). It is the primary morphological source for the Sanskrit truth-domain.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** मा / *mā-* (root *mā-*, to measure)
**P1 items participating:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*, #5), प्रमा (*pramā*, #6)
**Count:** 2 items directly, but the root is extraordinarily productive in the epistemological subdomain and generates the largest single cluster of technical terms for valid cognition and its failures.
**Core meaning contribution:** Measuring, gauging, and thereby knowing correctly. The root supplies the framework for epistemological evaluation: what counts as a valid means of knowing, what the result of such knowing is, and what falls short of it.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ऋत / *ṛta* (from root *ṛ-*, to go, to fit, to be ordered)
**P1 items participating:** ऋत (*ṛta*, #2), अनृत (*anṛta*, #3)
**Count:** 2 items directly, but the root underlies a significant cluster of terms for cosmic, moral, and ritual order, and is the etymological ancestor of the *ṛta/ṛtu/ṛju* family.
**Core meaning contribution:** Proper ordering, fittingness, and the regularity that underlies both natural law and moral rightness. Distinct from *sat* in emphasizing order and fit rather than sheer being.

---

*Note on scope:* The root *mā-* is selected over *dhṛ-* (underlying *dharma*) because, while *dharma* is extremely productive, its primary domain is moral and legal duty rather than truth-evaluation per se. The *mā-* root is more tightly focused on the epistemological core of the domain. The *ṛta* root is included because it represents the oldest and cosmologically most fundamental stratum of Sanskrit truth-vocabulary. Where *dharma* compounds appear in the expansion below, they are included under peripheral extensions.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Root 1: सत् / *sat* (root *as-*, to be; also the stem *satya* and the nominal *vastu*)

*Note:* This root is highly productive. The following covers the 25–30 most common and well-attested compounds and derived expressions, organized by sub-stem (*sat*, *satya*, *vastu/vāstava*). Additional forms exist beyond those listed.

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्पुरुष
**Transliteration:** sat-puruṣa
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (genuine, good, real) + *puruṣa* (man, person)
**Natural English translation:** a genuine or virtuous person; a man of integrity
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and social literature to designate a person of genuine moral worth, as opposed to one who merely appears virtuous. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra*, epic, and didactic literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** सत्कार
**Transliteration:** sat-kāra
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, genuine) + *kāra* (doing, making, from root *kṛ-*)
**Natural English translation:** proper treatment, honor, hospitality; doing what is right
**Context of use:** Used in social and ethical contexts to designate the proper or fitting treatment of a guest, elder, or deity. Appears in epic, *Purāṇic*, and *Dharmaśāstra* literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** सत्कर्मन्
**Transliteration:** sat-karman
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, right) + *karman* (action, deed)
**Natural English translation:** right action, virtuous deed, good work
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and ritual literature to designate actions that are genuinely meritorious or morally correct. Contrasted with *asat-karman* or *duṣkarman*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** सत्संग
**Transliteration:** sat-saṅga
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, genuine, real) + *saṅga* (association, company, from root *sañj-*)
**Natural English translation:** association with the good; company of the virtuous or spiritually realized
**Context of use:** Widely used in devotional (*bhakti*), Vedāntic, and popular religious literature to designate the practice of keeping company with spiritually genuine or virtuous persons. Remains in active use in modern Indian religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** सत्त्व
**Transliteration:** sattva
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (being, real) + abstract suffix *-tva*; "the state of being, being-ness"
**Natural English translation:** being, existence; the quality of goodness/purity (in Sāṃkhya); courage, vitality
**Context of use:** In *Sāṃkhya* philosophy, one of the three *guṇas* (fundamental qualities of *prakṛti*), associated with clarity, lightness, and goodness. In general usage, also means courage, strength of character, or vitality. Appears across philosophical, epic, and medical literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** सत्ता
**Transliteration:** sattā
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (being) + abstract suffix *-tā*; "being-ness, existence"
**Natural English translation:** existence, being, ontological status
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical literature (particularly *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* and Vedāntic traditions) to designate the property of existence or being as such. Distinguished from *sattva* in being a more abstract ontological term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** असत्त्व
**Transliteration:** asattva
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *sattva* (being, the quality of goodness)
**Natural English translation:** non-existence; absence of the quality of goodness
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical contexts as the negation of *sattva* in its ontological sense, and in ethical contexts as the absence of the *sattva* quality. Less frequent than *sattva* but attested in commentarial literature.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
**Form:** सत्यता
**Transliteration:** satyatā
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth, real) + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the property of being true"
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, the quality of being true, truth-hood
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological and ethical literature to designate the abstract property of being true or truthful, as predicated of statements, persons, or cognitions. Appears in *Nyāya* commentaries and *Dharmaśāstra*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*)
**Natural English translation:** one who speaks the truth, a truth-teller
**Context of use:** Used in ethical, legal, and epic literature to characterize a person whose speech is reliably truthful. Appears as a term of praise in *Dharmaśāstra* witness evaluation and in epic characterization.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** सत्यवाक्य
**Transliteration:** satya-vākya
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *vākya* (sentence, utterance, from root *vac-*)
**Natural English translation:** a true statement, a truthful utterance
**Context of use:** Used in logical, legal, and ethical contexts to designate an utterance that corresponds to the facts. Appears in discussions of testimony (*śabda-pramāṇa*) and in legal evaluation of witness statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** सत्यव्रत
**Transliteration:** satya-vrata
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *vrata* (vow, observance, from root *vṛ-*)
**Natural English translation:** one who has taken a vow of truthfulness; the vow of truth
**Context of use:** Used in epic and *Purāṇic* literature to designate a person who has committed to speaking only truth as a religious or ethical observance. Also used as a proper name.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** सत्यप्रतिज्ञ
**Transliteration:** satya-pratijña
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *pratijñā* (promise, assertion, from *prati-* + root *jñā-*)
**Natural English translation:** one whose promise is true; one who keeps his word
**Context of use:** Used in epic literature to characterize a hero or king whose pledges are reliably fulfilled. Appears as an epithet in the *Mahābhārata* and *Rāmāyaṇa*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + causative/denominative suffix *-āpana*; "causing to be true, verification"
**Natural English translation:** verification, confirmation, authentication
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to designate the act of verifying or confirming that something is true or genuine. Appears in modern Sanskrit-derived administrative usage and in traditional legal literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** सत्याग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyāgraha
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence, from *ā-* + root *grah-*)
**Natural English translation:** insistence on truth; truth-force (Gandhi's coinage for nonviolent resistance)
**Context of use:** Coined by Mohandas Gandhi as a political and ethical term designating nonviolent resistance grounded in commitment to truth. Widely used in modern Indian political discourse and internationally recognized.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** सत्यनारायण
**Transliteration:** satya-nārāyaṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (true, real) + *Nārāyaṇa* (a name of Viṣṇu)
**Natural English translation:** the True Nārāyaṇa; an epithet of Viṣṇu
**Context of use:** Used in devotional and ritual contexts, particularly in the *Satyanārāyaṇa-vrata* (a popular Hindu ritual). The compound designates Viṣṇu as the deity who embodies or is identical with truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** सत्यलोक
**Transliteration:** satya-loka
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth, real) + *loka* (world, realm)
**Natural English translation:** the world of truth; the highest realm (in cosmological schemes)
**Context of use:** Used in *Purāṇic* cosmology to designate the highest of the cosmic realms, associated with Brahmā and with ultimate reality. Appears in lists of the seven *lokas*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** सत्यमेव जयते
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**Literal breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, nominative) + *eva* (indeed, only) + *jayate* (conquers, from root *ji-*)
**Natural English translation:** Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails
**Context of use:** A maxim from the *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* (3.1.6); adopted as the national motto of the Republic of India. Used in philosophical, political, and popular discourse to assert the ultimate triumph of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** असत्यभाषण
**Transliteration:** asatya-bhāṣaṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *asatya* (untruth, false) + *bhāṣaṇa* (speaking, from root *bhāṣ-*)
**Natural English translation:** speaking falsehood, false speech, lying
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal literature to designate the act of uttering falsehoods. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* lists of prohibited conduct and in discussions of witness credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** वस्तुतः
**Transliteration:** vastutaḥ
**Literal breakdown:** *vastu* (real thing, actual state) + ablative/adverbial suffix *-taḥ*; "from the real thing, in reality"
**Natural English translation:** in reality, actually, in truth, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Used as an adverb in philosophical, legal, and general prose to assert that what follows corresponds to the actual state of affairs, as opposed to what is merely apparent or conventionally said. Widely used in Sanskrit commentarial literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** वस्तुस्थिति
**Transliteration:** vastu-sthiti
**Literal breakdown:** *vastu* (real thing, actual state) + *sthiti* (standing, condition, from root *sthā-*)
**Natural English translation:** the actual state of affairs, the real situation, the facts of the matter
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and philosophical contexts to designate the actual or factual condition of something, as opposed to what is alleged, imagined, or conventionally described. Common in Sanskrit legal commentary and modern Sanskrit prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** वस्तुतन्त्र
**Transliteration:** vastu-tantra
**Literal breakdown:** *vastu* (real thing, object) + *tantra* (dependent on, governed by, from root *tan-*)
**Natural English translation:** object-dependent, governed by the real thing (as opposed to subject-dependent)
**Context of use:** Used in *Mīmāṃsā* and Vedāntic epistemology to characterize cognitions or judgments that are determined by the nature of the object rather than by the subject's will or convention. Contrasted with *puruṣa-tantra* (subject-dependent).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** वस्तुसत्
**Transliteration:** vastu-sat
**Literal breakdown:** *vastu* (real thing) + *sat* (being, real)
**Natural English translation:** genuinely real, real as an actual entity
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* and Buddhist philosophical literature to distinguish what is real as an actual entity from what is merely conventionally or nominally real (*saṃvṛti-sat*). A technical ontological distinction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** वास्तविक
**Transliteration:** vāstavika
**Literal breakdown:** *vāstava* (real, pertaining to the real object) + adjectival suffix *-ika*
**Natural English translation:** real, actual, genuine, factual
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and general prose to characterize what is genuinely real or factual as opposed to apparent, conventional, or imagined. Common in modern Sanskrit and in Sanskrit-derived vocabulary in Hindi and other Indian languages.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** सद्भाव
**Transliteration:** sad-bhāva
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, genuine, real) + *bhāva* (state, being, feeling, from root *bhū-*)
**Natural English translation:** genuine feeling, good faith, sincerity; real existence
**Context of use:** Used in ethical contexts to designate sincerity or good faith in conduct or speech. Also used in ontological contexts to designate genuine existence or being. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra*, philosophical, and devotional literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** सदसत्
**Transliteration:** sad-asat
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (being, real) + *asat* (non-being, unreal); a dvandva compound
**Natural English translation:** being and non-being; the real and the unreal
**Context of use:** Used in Vedāntic and *Upaniṣadic* literature to designate the pair of being and non-being, often in contexts where the ultimate reality is said to transcend both. Appears in the *Bhagavad Gītā* and *Upaniṣads*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** सन्मार्ग
**Transliteration:** san-mārga
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, right) + *mārga* (path, way)
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the path of virtue, the correct way
**Context of use:** Used in ethical, religious, and didactic literature to designate the morally or spiritually correct course of conduct. Contrasted with *kumārga* or *asat-mārga* (wrong path).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** सच्चिदानन्द
**Transliteration:** sac-cid-ānanda
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (being) + *cit* (consciousness) + *ānanda* (bliss); a compound epithet
**Natural English translation:** being-consciousness-bliss; the triple nature of ultimate reality (Brahman)
**Context of use:** Central compound in Advaita Vedānta designating the three essential attributes of *Brahman* (ultimate reality): pure being, pure consciousness, and pure bliss. Widely used in philosophical and devotional literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** सत्कविता
**Transliteration:** sat-kavitā
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (good, genuine) + *kavitā* (poetry, from *kavi*, poet)
**Natural English translation:** genuine poetry, good poetry, poetry of real merit
**Context of use:** Used in Sanskrit aesthetic and literary critical literature to designate poetry that is genuinely meritorious, as opposed to verse that merely has the external form of poetry without its essential qualities.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** सत्प्रतिपक्ष
**Transliteration:** sat-pratipakṣa
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (real, genuine) + *pratipakṣa* (counter-party, opposing reason)
**Natural English translation:** a genuine counter-reason; a real opposing argument
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* logic to designate a genuine counter-argument or counter-evidence that actually undermines a proposed inference, as opposed to a merely apparent or spurious one.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** सत्कार्यवाद
**Transliteration:** sat-kārya-vāda
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* (real, pre-existing) + *kārya* (effect) + *vāda* (doctrine, theory)
**Natural English translation:** the doctrine that the effect pre-exists in the cause
**Context of use:** A technical term in *Sāṃkhya* philosophy designating the theory that effects are not genuinely new but are already present in their causes in a latent form. Contrasted with *asat-kārya-vāda* (the doctrine that effects are genuinely new).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 2: मा / *mā-* (root *mā-*, to measure; prefix *pra-* + *mā-*)

*Note:* This root is highly productive in the epistemological domain. The following covers the most common and well-attested compounds and derived expressions. Additional technical forms exist in the commentarial literature.

---

**1.**
**Form:** अप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** apramāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramāṇa* (valid means of knowledge)
**Natural English translation:** invalid means of knowledge; not authoritative; lacking epistemic warrant
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to designate a source or means of cognition that fails to meet the criteria for validity. Also used in general contexts to characterize a person or text as lacking authority.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** प्रमाणता
**Transliteration:** pramāṇatā
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means) + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the property of being a valid means"
**Natural English translation:** the property of being a valid means of knowledge; epistemic validity; authoritativeness
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*, and Buddhist epistemological literature to designate the abstract property that a source or means of cognition must possess in order to count as genuinely knowledge-yielding.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** प्रमाणभूत
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-bhūta
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means, authority) + *bhūta* (become, being, from root *bhū-*)
**Natural English translation:** one who has become an authority; a recognized standard or authority
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person, text, or tradition that has come to be recognized as an authoritative standard. Appears in philosophical and legal literature to designate recognized epistemic authorities.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** प्रमाणवार्तिक
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-vārttika
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means of knowledge) + *vārttika* (commentary, critical gloss)
**Natural English translation:** Commentary on Valid Cognition (title of Dharmakīrti's work)
**Context of use:** The title of Dharmakīrti's major work in Buddhist epistemology, a commentary on Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya*. Used as a proper title and as a reference point in discussions of Buddhist logic and epistemology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** प्रमाणसमुच्चय
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-samuccaya
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means) + *samuccaya* (collection, compendium, from *sam-* + *ud-* + root *ci-*)
**Natural English translation:** Compendium of Valid Cognition (title of Dignāga's work)
**Context of use:** The title of Dignāga's foundational work in Buddhist epistemology. Used as a proper title and as a reference in discussions of the *pramāṇa* tradition.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** पramāṇābhāsa
**Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāsa
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means) + *ābhāsa* (semblance, pseudo-); "pseudo-valid means of knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** a pseudo-valid means of knowledge; an apparent but not genuine epistemic source
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to designate something that resembles a valid means of knowledge but fails to meet the criteria for genuine validity. Parallel to *hetvābhāsa* (fallacious reason).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** अप्रमा
**Transliteration:** apramā
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramā* (valid cognition, correct apprehension)
**Natural English translation:** erroneous cognition, invalid cognition, false apprehension
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* epistemological literature as the general term for any cognition that fails to accurately represent its object. Covers *bhrānti*, *saṃśaya* (doubt), and *tarka* (hypothetical reasoning) as species.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** प्रमातृ
**Transliteration:** pramātṛ
**Literal breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* (to measure, to know) + agent suffix *-tṛ*; "the one who knows validly"
**Natural English translation:** the knowing subject, the cognizer, the epistemic agent
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature to designate the subject who undergoes valid cognition, as one of the three components of the knowledge-situation (*pramātṛ*, *pramāṇa*, *prameya*).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** प्रमेय
**Transliteration:** prameya
**Literal breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* (to measure, to know) + gerundive suffix *-ya*; "that which is to be known/measured"
**Natural English translation:** the object of valid cognition; the knowable; what is to be known
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* and related epistemological literature to designate the object of valid cognition, as one of the three components of the knowledge-situation. The *Nyāyasūtra* lists twelve *prameyas* as the objects whose knowledge leads to liberation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** प्रमाणशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-śāstra
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means of knowledge) + *śāstra* (treatise, discipline, from root *śās-*)
**Natural English translation:** the science of valid cognition; epistemology as a discipline
**Context of use:** Used to designate the discipline or body of literature concerned with the theory of valid means of knowledge. Covers *Nyāya*, Buddhist logic, *Mīmāṃsā* epistemology, and related traditions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** svataḥ-prāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *svataḥ* (from itself, intrinsically) + *prāmāṇya* (the property of being a valid means, from *pramāṇa* + *-ya*)
**Natural English translation:** intrinsic validity; the view that cognitions are self-validating
**Context of use:** A central technical term in *Mīmāṃsā* epistemology, designating the doctrine that valid cognitions carry their validity intrinsically rather than requiring external confirmation. Contrasted with *parataḥ-prāmāṇya* (extrinsic validity, the *Nyāya* view).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** परतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** parataḥ-prāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *parataḥ* (from another, extrinsically) + *prāmāṇya* (validity)
**Natural English translation:** extrinsic validity; the view that cognitions require external confirmation of their validity
**Context of use:** The *Nyāya* position on epistemic validity, contrasted with the *Mīmāṃsā* doctrine of *svataḥ-prāmāṇya*. Used in debates about the source and criterion of epistemic validity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** प्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + secondary derivational suffix *-ya*; "the property of being a *pramāṇa*"
**Natural English translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the status of being a valid means of knowledge
**Context of use:** Used as the abstract noun for the property that makes something a *pramāṇa*. Central to debates in *Mīmāṃsā* and *Nyāya* about how validity is established and recognized.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** अनुमान
**Transliteration:** anumāna
**Literal breakdown:** *anu-* (after, following) + *māna* (measuring, cognizing, from root *mā-*); "measuring/cognizing after (a mark)"
**Natural English translation:** inference; inferential cognition
**Context of use:** One of the primary *pramāṇas* in Indian epistemology, designating the valid means of knowledge that proceeds from a mark (*liṅga*) to a conclusion. Analyzed in detail in *Nyāya*, Buddhist logic, and *Mīmāṃsā* literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** अनुमिति
**Transliteration:** anumiti
**Literal breakdown:** *anu-* (after) + *miti* (cognition, from root *mā-*); "cognition following (a mark)"
**Natural English translation:** inferential cognition; the result of inference
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* epistemology to designate the cognition that results from a successful inference (*anumāna*), as distinct from the inferential process itself. Parallel to *pramā* as the result of *pramāṇa*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** उपमान
**Transliteration:** upamāna
**Literal breakdown:** *upa-* (near, resembling) + *māna* (measuring, cognizing); "cognizing by resemblance"
**Natural English translation:** comparison; analogical cognition; the valid means of knowledge by analogy
**Context of use:** One of the *pramāṇas* recognized in *Nyāya* epistemology, designating the means of knowing the meaning of an unfamiliar term through comparison with a known object. Analyzed in the *Nyāyasūtra* and its commentaries.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** उपमिति
**Transliteration:** upamiti
**Literal breakdown:** *upa-* (near, resembling) + *miti* (cognition, from root *mā-*)
**Natural English translation:** analogical cognition; the result of comparison
**Context of use:** The cognition that results from *upamāna* (comparison). Used in *Nyāya* epistemological literature to designate the specific type of cognition produced by the analogical means of knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**18.**
**Form:** मान
**Transliteration:** māna
**Literal breakdown:** root *mā-* (to measure) + action noun suffix *-na*; "measuring, cognizing"
**Natural English translation:** measure, standard, cognition; (in some contexts) pride, self-regard
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological contexts as a general term for cognition or measurement. Also used in social and ethical contexts to mean honor, self-regard, or pride. The epistemological and social senses are distinct but both derive from the root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** मिति
**Transliteration:** miti
**Literal breakdown:** root *mā-* (to measure) + action noun suffix *-ti*; "measuring, cognition"
**Natural English translation:** cognition, measurement, valid apprehension
**Context of use:** Used in epistemological literature as a general term for the act or result of valid cognition. Appears in compounds such as *anumiti* and *upamiti* and in general discussions of the knowledge-situation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** प्रमाणपत्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-patra
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (authority, valid standard) + *patra* (document, leaf, letter)
**Natural English translation:** certificate, credential, document of authority
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to designate an official document that serves as authoritative evidence or certification. Common in Sanskrit legal literature and in modern Sanskrit-derived administrative vocabulary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** प्रमाणीकरण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ī-* (denominative) + *karaṇa* (making, from root *kṛ-*); "making into a *pramāṇa*"
**Natural English translation:** authentication, certification, validation
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to designate the act of authenticating or certifying something as valid or authoritative. Appears in modern Sanskrit administrative usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** प्रमाणसिद्ध
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-siddha
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* (valid means) + *siddha* (established, proven, from root *sidh-*)
**Natural English translation:** established by valid means of knowledge; proven; epistemically warranted
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and legal literature to characterize a claim or fact that has been established through a recognized valid means of knowledge, as opposed to what is merely asserted or assumed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** बाधित
**Transliteration:** bādhita
**Literal breakdown:** past passive participle of root *bādh-* (to obstruct, to contradict); "contradicted, sublated"
**Natural English translation:** contradicted, sublated, overridden (by a stronger cognition or evidence)
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* and Vedāntic epistemology to characterize a cognition that has been overridden or shown to be erroneous by a subsequent, stronger cognition. The classic example is the sublation of the rope-snake illusion upon closer inspection. Closely associated with the *pramāṇa* framework.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** बाधा
**Transliteration:** bādhā
**Literal breakdown:** from root *bādh-* (to obstruct, to contradict) + action noun suffix *-ā*
**Natural English translation:** contradiction, sublation, obstruction (of a cognition by contrary evidence)
**Context of use:** Used in *Nyāya* epistemology to designate the process or fact of a cognition being overridden by contrary evidence. Also used in logic to designate a condition that defeats an inference. Related to but distinct from the *mā-* root; included here because of its functional role in the *pramāṇa* framework.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note:* Items 23–24 (*bādhita*, *bādhā*) are from root *bādh-*, not *mā-*, but are included because they are functionally integral to the *pramāṇa* framework and regularly co-occur with *pramāṇa* vocabulary in epistemological literature.

### Root 3: ऋत / *ṛta* (root *ṛ-*, to go, to fit, to be ordered)

*Note:* This root is less productive in terms of compound formation than *sat* or *mā-*, but generates a significant cluster of terms in the Vedic, cosmological, and moral-order domains. The following covers all well-attested compounds and derived expressions.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ऋतधामन्
**Transliteration:** ṛta-dhāman
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛta* (cosmic order, truth) + *dhāman* (abode, seat, from root *dhā-*)
**Natural English translation:** whose abode is *ṛta*; dwelling in cosmic order (epithet of Varuṇa and other Vedic deities)
**Context of use:** Used in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities, particularly Varuṇa, who are regarded as guardians or embodiments of cosmic order. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ऋतावन्
**Transliteration:** ṛtāvan
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛta* (cosmic order) + possessive suffix *-van*; "possessing *ṛta*, righteous"
**Natural English translation:** righteous, one who possesses or upholds cosmic order
**Context of use:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities and of persons who uphold cosmic and moral order. Appears in the *Ṛgveda* and *Atharvaveda*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ऋतज्ञ
**Transliteration:** ṛta-jña
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛta* (cosmic order, truth) + *jña* (knowing, from root *jñā-*)
**Natural English translation:** one who knows *ṛta*; knower of cosmic order
**Context of use:** Used in Vedic literature to designate one who has knowledge of the cosmic order, typically a deity or a sage. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ऋतस्पृश्
**Transliteration:** ṛta-spṛś
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛta* (cosmic order) + *spṛś* (touching, from root *spṛś-*)
**Natural English translation:** touching or in contact with *ṛta*; one who touches cosmic order
**Context of use:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities or of ritual acts that are in accord with or touch upon cosmic order. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ऋतु
**Transliteration:** ṛtu
**Literal breakdown:** from root *ṛ-* (to go, to be ordered) + suffix *-tu*; "the proper time, the ordered time"
**Natural English translation:** season, the proper time, the right time; menstrual period (in medical usage)
**Context of use:** Designates the seasons as the properly ordered divisions of the year. Used in Vedic ritual literature to specify the correct time for ritual performance, in astronomical and agricultural literature for the seasons, and in medical literature for the menstrual cycle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ऋतुकाल
**Transliteration:** ṛtu-kāla
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛtu* (proper time, season) + *kāla* (time)
**Natural English translation:** the proper season, the right time; the fertile period
**Context of use:** Used in ritual literature to designate the correct time for a ritual act, and in medical and legal literature (*Dharmaśāstra*) to designate the fertile period in a woman's cycle, which is the proper time for conjugal relations according to traditional norms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ऋजु
**Transliteration:** ṛju
**Literal breakdown:** from root *ṛ-* (to go straight, to be ordered); "straight, direct, honest"
**Natural English translation:** straight, direct, honest, upright
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and descriptive contexts to characterize persons, speech, or conduct as straightforward, honest, or direct. Appears in epic and *Dharmaśāstra* literature. Etymologically related to *ṛta* through the root *ṛ-*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ऋजुता
**Transliteration:** ṛjutā
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛju* (straight, honest) + abstract suffix *-tā*
**Natural English translation:** straightforwardness, honesty, directness, uprightness
**Context of use:** Used in ethical literature to designate the quality of being straightforward or honest in conduct and speech. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature as a virtue term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** अनृतभाषिन्
**Transliteration:** anṛta-bhāṣin
**Literal breakdown:** *anṛta* (untruth, falsehood) + *bhāṣin* (speaking, from root *bhāṣ-*)
**Natural English translation:** one who speaks falsehood, a liar
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks falsely. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* discussions of witness disqualification and in epic characterization of villains.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** अनृतवचन
**Transliteration:** anṛta-vacana
**Literal breakdown:** *anṛta* (untruth) + *vacana* (speech, utterance, from root *vac-*)
**Natural English translation:** false speech, lying utterance
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal literature to designate an utterance that is false or contrary to the facts. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* lists of prohibited conduct and in discussions of the sin of lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ऋतंभर
**Transliteration:** ṛtaṃbharā
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛtam* (accusative of *ṛta*, cosmic order/truth) + *bharā* (bearing, carrying, from root *bhṛ-*); "bearing truth/order"
**Natural English translation:** truth-bearing; an epithet of a special type of wisdom (*prajñā*) in Yoga philosophy
**Context of use:** Used in *Yoga* philosophy (specifically *Yogasūtra* 1.48) to designate a special, highest form of wisdom (*prajñā*) that directly bears or carries truth. Also used as an epithet of Vedic speech or of deities who uphold cosmic order.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ऋतचित्
**Transliteration:** ṛta-cit
**Literal breakdown:** *ṛta* (cosmic order, truth) + *cit* (consciousness, knowing, from root *cit-*)
**Natural English translation:** whose consciousness is *ṛta*; one whose mind is attuned to cosmic order
**Context of use:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities, particularly those associated with cosmic order and truth. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**13.**
**Form:** सत्यानृत
**Transliteration:** satyānṛta
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *anṛta* (falsehood); a dvandva compound
**Natural English translation:** truth and falsehood; a mixture of truth and falsehood
**Context of use:** Used in *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature to designate a category of speech or livelihood that mixes truth and falsehood, such as trade (which involves some deception). One of the categories of livelihood (*vṛtti*) in *Manusmṛti*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the identified roots operating in contexts where the connection to truth-evaluation is not straightforward. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From root *sat*:**

- **सत्त्व** (*sattva*): In *Sāṃkhya* philosophy, designates one of the three *guṇas* (fundamental qualities of matter/nature), associated with lightness, clarity, and goodness — a cosmological and psychological category that does not straightforwardly involve truth or falsity.

- **सत्कार** (*sat-kāra*): Used to mean "hospitality" or "honor shown to a guest," where the *sat-* component contributes a sense of "proper" or "fitting" rather than "true."

- **सत्कविता** (*sat-kavitā*): Applied to literary merit in poetry, where *sat-* marks aesthetic genuineness rather than propositional truth.

- **सत्संग** (*sat-saṅga*): Used in devotional contexts to mean "company of the spiritually realized," where *sat-* marks spiritual authenticity rather than factual accuracy.

- **सच्चिदानन्द** (*sac-cid-ānanda*): Used as a theological epithet of *Brahman*, where *sat* functions as an ontological category (pure being) rather than as a truth-predicate.

- **सत्कार्यवाद** (*sat-kārya-vāda*): A metaphysical doctrine about causation (the pre-existence of effects in causes), where *sat* marks ontological status rather than truth of a statement.

- **सन्मार्ग** (*san-mārga*): Used to mean "the right path" in a moral or spiritual sense, where *sat-* marks normative correctness rather than factual truth.

---

**From root *mā-*:**

- **मान** (*māna*): In social and ethical contexts, used to mean "honor," "self-regard," or "pride" — a social-evaluative sense entirely distinct from the epistemological sense of "measuring" or "cognizing."

- **उपमान** (*upamāna*) and **उपमा** (*upamā*): In Sanskrit aesthetics (*alaṃkāraśāstra*), *upamā* designates the figure of simile — a literary device evaluated for aesthetic aptness rather than epistemic validity.

- **अनुमान** (*anumāna*): In everyday usage, can mean "estimate" or "approximation" (e.g., of quantity or distance), where the epistemological sense of "valid inference" shades into a more general sense of approximate reckoning.

- **प्रमाणपत्र** (*pramāṇa-patra*): In administrative usage, functions as "certificate" or "credential" — a bureaucratic document whose authority is institutional rather than epistemological.

---

**From root *ṛta*:**

- **ऋतु** (*ṛtu*): Designates the seasons of the year and (in medical usage) the menstrual cycle — natural temporal regularities where the connection to truth or moral order is indirect.

- **ऋतुकाल** (*ṛtu-kāla*): In *Dharmaśāstra*, used to specify the proper time for conjugal relations — a normative-temporal usage where *ṛtu* marks biological and ritual propriety rather than truth.

- **ṛju** (*ṛju*) and **ṛjutā** (*ṛjutā*): Used to mean "straight" or "direct" in physical and moral senses, where the connection to cosmic order (*ṛta*) is etymological but the primary sense is directness or honesty rather than truth of a proposition.

- **ऋतंभरा** (*ṛtaṃbharā*): In *Yoga* philosophy, designates a special type of meditative wisdom — a soteriological and contemplative usage where *ṛta* marks a mode of direct insight rather than propositional truth.
