# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / marṭ-
**Transliteration:** mart-
**P1 participation:** 4 items — martali (#4), simartle (#3), gamarṭleba (#30), and the derivational base underlying further items. Directly: martali, simartle, gamarṭleba, and the adverbial form marṭla. The root marṭ- (right, correct, straight, just) is the most productive native root in the truth-evaluation domain.
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, correctness, and justice as aligned with a standard — covering factual accuracy of statements, moral uprightness of persons, and legal vindication. The root spans epistemic, moral, and legal registers with high productivity.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ყალბ- / q̇alb-
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 participation:** Items including q̇albi (#6), siq̇albe (#5), gaq̇albeba (#23), siq̇albis dadastureba (#19), siq̇albis damadasturebeli (#30).
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, forgery, and counterfeit — covering the production and attribution of false or inauthentic items, documents, currency, and representations. The root is borrowed but fully integrated and highly productive in legal and everyday registers.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ჭეშმარ- / č̣ešmar-
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmar-
**P1 participation:** 2 items directly — č̣ešmariti (#1), č̣ešmariteba (#2). The root is the primary philosophical and theological vehicle for truth as an abstract property, and its derivational family is well-attested across registers.
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as genuine quality or authentic correspondence to reality — the philosophically and theologically marked root for truth in its deepest or most absolute sense. Less productive morphologically than mart-, but semantically central to the domain.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: mart- (მართ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართალია
**Transliteration:** martalia
**Literal breakdown:** martali + copula -a; "is right/correct"
**Natural English translation:** he/she/it is right; that is correct; you are right
**Context of use:** Extremely frequent in everyday conversation as a response particle or predicate, used to concede that an interlocutor's claim is correct or that a state of affairs is as described. Functions almost as a discourse marker in informal speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** მართალი ხარ
**Transliteration:** martali xar
**Literal breakdown:** martali (right/correct) + xar (2sg copula "you are")
**Natural English translation:** you are right
**Context of use:** Standard everyday expression for conceding correctness to an interlocutor. Ubiquitous in informal and semi-formal conversation across all registers and age groups.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** marṭla
**Literal breakdown:** marṭ- + adverbial suffix -la; "truly, really, in truth"
**Natural English translation:** really, truly, indeed, in fact
**Context of use:** High-frequency adverb and discourse particle used in everyday speech to assert or emphasize that something is genuinely the case. marṭla moxda ("it really happened"), marṭla ar vici ("I truly don't know"). Also used as a question tag or challenge ("martla?" — "really?"). Functions as an intensifier and truth-asserting particle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** marṭlac
**Literal breakdown:** marṭla (truly) + emphatic particle -c; "truly indeed"
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of marṭla, used to confirm or underscore the truth of a statement, often in response to a question or in conceding a point. Very common in both spoken and written Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** მართლაც რომ
**Transliteration:** marṭlac rom
**Literal breakdown:** marṭlac (indeed) + rom (subordinating conjunction); "it being truly the case that"
**Natural English translation:** it is indeed the case that; truly, the fact is that; if it is indeed the case that
**Context of use:** Used in written and formal spoken Georgian to introduce a concession or confirmation of a fact, or a concessive/conditional clause whose truth is being granted for the sake of argument. Common in journalistic and analytical prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** სიმართლის თქმა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis tqma
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + tqma (saying, verbal noun of tkva); "the saying of truth"
**Natural English translation:** telling the truth, truth-telling
**Context of use:** Used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to describe the act of speaking truthfully. simarṭlis tqma valdebulebaa ("telling the truth is an obligation") is a typical moral formulation. Appears in oath formulas and moral exhortation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დადგენა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis dadgena
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + dadgena (establishment, determination); "determination of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** establishing the truth, determination of the facts
**Context of use:** Standard legal and investigative expression for the process of determining what actually occurred. Appears in court proceedings, journalistic investigations, and official inquiries.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დამალვა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis damalva
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + damalva (concealment, hiding); "concealment of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** concealment of the truth, hiding the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral discourse to describe deliberate suppression of true information. Appears in criminal law contexts and in moral condemnation of dishonest behavior. Contrasts with simartlis tqma.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Form:** მართლმეტყველება
**Transliteration:** martlmet̕q̇veleba
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + met̕q̇vel- (speaker, from t̕q̇ven-, "to speak") + -eba; "right-speaking"
**Natural English translation:** correct speech, orthoepy; also: truthful speaking
**Context of use:** Used in linguistic and rhetorical contexts to refer to correct or proper speech, including pronunciation norms. Also appears in older texts with the sense of truthful or righteous speaking.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**52.**
**Form:** მართლზომიერი
**Transliteration:** martlzomieri
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + zom- (measure) + -ieri (adjectival suffix); "rightly measured"
**Natural English translation:** proportionate, measured, appropriate in degree
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a measure or action is proportionate to its purpose. Appears in constitutional law discussions of proportionality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
