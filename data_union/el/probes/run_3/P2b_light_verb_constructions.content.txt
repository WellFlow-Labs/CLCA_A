# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 843, consistent with P1, P1b, and P2a.

**Scope note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Fixed collocations already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The number of constructions varies by item and reflects actual productivity. Absence is recorded explicitly.

---

## P1 Item 1 — αληθής (alithís)

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** αληθής ως προς τα πράγματα
3. **Transliteration:** alithís os pros ta prágmata
4. **English translation:** true with respect to the facts; accurate as regards the facts
5. **Usage notes:** A formal prepositional construction used in philosophical and legal registers to specify the domain or object of a truth-claim. The prepositional phrase ως προς (with respect to) restricts the scope of the predication.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** αληθής κατά τεκμήριο
3. **Transliteration:** alithís katá tekmírio
4. **English translation:** presumed true; true by presumption
5. **Usage notes:** A legal construction used to describe a claim or fact that is treated as true in the absence of contrary evidence. Appears in legal texts and judicial reasoning.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** αληθής ή ψευδής
3. **Transliteration:** alithís í psevdís
4. **English translation:** true or false
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed binary construction used in logic, philosophy, and everyday contexts to present the two possible truth-values of a proposition. Appears in educational, philosophical, and quiz contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** εν μέρει αληθής
3. **Transliteration:** en méri alithís
4. **English translation:** partly true, partially true
5. **Usage notes:** A formal construction used in legal, journalistic, and philosophical registers to indicate that a claim is true in some respects but not entirely. The prepositional phrase εν μέρει (in part) is formal in register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 2 — αλήθεια (alíthia)

*Note: Many fixed collocations involving αλήθεια were documented in P1b (Root 1, items 12–29). The constructions below are additional multi-word patterns not fully treated there.*

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** λέω την αλήθεια στα μούτρα κάποιου
3. **Transliteration:** léo tin alíthia sta mútra kápiu
4. **English translation:** to tell someone the truth to their face; to say something bluntly to someone's face
5. **Usage notes:** A colloquial idiomatic construction combining truth-telling with the idiom στα μούτρα (literally "in the face/snout"), indicating directness or bluntness. Used in everyday speech to describe frank, unsparing honesty.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** η αλήθεια βγαίνει στο φως
3. **Transliteration:** i alíthia vgéni sto fos
4. **English translation:** the truth comes to light; the truth comes out
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed idiomatic construction using the verb βγαίνω (to come out) and the noun φως (light). Used in everyday, journalistic, and literary registers to describe the eventual revelation of a hidden truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** η αλήθεια είναι στη μέση
3. **Transliteration:** i alíthia íne sti mési
4. **English translation:** the truth is in the middle; the truth lies somewhere between the two positions
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction expressing the view that truth is found between two extreme positions. Used in everyday and journalistic registers when mediating between conflicting accounts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** κρύβω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** krívo tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A common verbal construction used across all registers to describe the deliberate concealment of true information. Contrasts with λέω την αλήθεια.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** αποκαλύπτω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** apokalípto tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to reveal the truth, to uncover the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A common verbal construction used in journalistic, legal, and everyday registers to describe the act of bringing hidden truth to light.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** παραμορφώνω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** paramorfonó tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to distort the truth, to twist the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Used in journalistic and political registers to describe the deliberate misrepresentation of facts while maintaining a surface appearance of truth-telling.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** στρεβλώνω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** strevlóno tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to distort the truth, to warp the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Functionally similar to παραμορφώνω την αλήθεια but with a slightly stronger connotation of deliberate warping or bending. Used in formal and journalistic registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** υπηρετώ την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** ipiretó tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to serve the truth; to be in the service of truth
5. **Usage notes:** A formal construction used in journalistic, academic, and moral discourse to describe a commitment to truth as a value. Carries an elevated or idealistic register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** αντιμετωπίζω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** andimetopízo tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to face the truth, to confront the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Used in everyday and psychological registers to describe the act of accepting or acknowledging a difficult reality. Common in therapeutic and self-help discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** για να λέμε την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** ya na léme tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** to tell the truth; to be honest; if we're being honest
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed discourse marker used to introduce a candid or qualifying statement, often one that contradicts or complicates what has just been said. Extremely common in everyday speech as a hedging or framing device.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** αν θέλεις την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** an thélis tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** if you want the truth; if you want my honest opinion
5. **Usage notes:** A colloquial conditional construction used to preface a frank or potentially unwelcome statement. Functions as a softening or framing device in everyday conversation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** χωρίς να κρύβω την αλήθεια
3. **Transliteration:** chorís na krívo tin alíthia
4. **English translation:** without hiding the truth; frankly; honestly
5. **Usage notes:** A participial construction used as a discourse marker to signal candor. Used in formal and everyday registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** η αλήθεια πονάει
3. **Transliteration:** i alíthia ponái
4. **English translation:** the truth hurts
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed proverbial construction used in everyday speech to acknowledge that honest statements can be painful. Functionally equivalent to the English "the truth hurts."

**Construction 14**
1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** η αλήθεια είναι σχετική
3. **Transliteration:** i alíthia íne schetikí
4. **English translation:** truth is relative
5. **Usage notes:** A philosophical and everyday construction used to express relativism about truth. Common in informal philosophical discussion and everyday argumentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
