# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

## Methodology Statement

This analysis identifies natural groupings based solely on **observed usage patterns** in the P1 and P2 data. Groupings reflect how Finnish speakers actually use and contrast these expressions, not imposed theoretical categories. Groups may overlap, vary in size, and some items may remain ungrouped.

---

## GROUP 1: Predicative Truth Evaluation

**Definition:** Expressions used to affirm or deny that a statement, claim, or proposition corresponds to reality. These forms appear in predicative constructions (typically with *olla* "to be") and are used to evaluate the truth-value of assertions. Distinguished from other truth-domain expressions by their primary function in direct truth-assessment rather than describing properties of objects or persons.

**Representative expressions:**
- tosi (true)
- totta (partitive: "is true")
- totuus (truth)
- se on totta (that is true)
- onko se totta? (is that true?)
- ei ole totta (is not true)
- pitää paikkansa (to hold true, to be accurate)
- ei pidä paikkaansa (does not hold true)
- epätosi (untrue, false)
- paikkansapitävä (accurate, correct, valid)

**Typical contexts:** Confirming or questioning statements, evaluating claims, asserting or denying correspondence to facts.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from *oikea/väärä* which evaluate correctness more broadly (including moral and practical domains), and from *aito* which evaluates authenticity of objects/materials rather than truth of statements.

---

## GROUP 2: Correctness and Error

**Definition:** Expressions evaluating whether something is correct, right, or proper versus incorrect, wrong, or mistaken. Used across factual, procedural, and practical domains (answers, methods, directions, choices). Distinguished from pure truth-evaluation by applicability to non-propositional contexts (e.g., "correct answer," "wrong direction") and emphasis on conformity to standards or norms rather than correspondence to reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- oikea (right, correct)
- oikein (correctly, rightly)
- väärä (wrong, false, incorrect)
- väärin (wrongly, incorrectly)
- virhe (error, mistake)
- virheellinen (erroneous, faulty)
- olla oikeassa (to be right)
- olla väärässä (to be wrong)
- oikea vastaus (correct answer)
- väärä vastaus (wrong answer)
- tehdä virhe (to make a mistake)
- korjata virhe (to correct an error)
- oikeellisuus (correctness, accuracy)
- virheellisyys (erroneousness)

**Typical contexts:** Educational settings (answers, solutions), procedural contexts (methods, steps), navigation (directions), decision-making (choices).

**Contrasts observed:** While *oikea/väärä* can evaluate truth of statements, they more commonly evaluate correctness in broader domains. *Virhe* specifically denotes unintentional error, contrasting with *valhe* (intentional falsehood).

---

## GROUP 3: Deliberate Falsehood and Deception

**Definition:** Expressions denoting intentional misrepresentation, lying, or deception. Characterized by element of intent to mislead or deceive, distinguishing them from mere error or incorrectness. Used in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts where truthfulness and honesty are at issue.

**Representative expressions:**
- valhe (lie, falsehood)
- valehdella (to lie, to tell lies)
- puhua valhetta (to tell a lie)
- vale- (prefix: false-, fake-, pseudo-)
- valepassi (fake passport)
- valenimi (pseudonym, false name)
- petollinen (deceptive, deceitful)
- petollisuus (deceitfulness)
- väärä todistus (false testimony, perjury)
- väärä vala (false oath, perjury)
- väärentää (to forge, to falsify)
- väärennös (forgery, counterfeit)
- harhaanjohtava (misleading, deceptive)
- johtaa harhaan (to mislead, to lead astray)

**Typical contexts:** Moral discourse about honesty, legal contexts (perjury, forgery), interpersonal trust, authentication of documents/objects.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from *virhe* (unintentional error) by element of intent. *Petollinen* and *harhaanjohtava* may or may not involve conscious intent, while *valhe* and *valehdella* explicitly denote intentional falsehood.

---

## GROUP 4: Authenticity and Genuineness

**Definition:** Expressions evaluating whether objects, materials, emotions, or persons are genuine, authentic, or real versus fake, counterfeit, or artificial. Applied primarily to physical objects (materials, documents) and to character/emotions (genuine feelings, true friendship). Distinguished from truth-evaluation by focus on authenticity of entities rather than truth of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- aito (genuine, authentic, real)
- aitous (genuineness, authenticity)
- aito nahka (genuine leather)
- aito kulta (genuine gold)
- aito ystävä (true friend)
- aito tunne (genuine feeling)
- todellinen (real, actual)
- todellisuus (reality)
- oikea nimi (real name)
- oikea henkilöllisyys (true identity)
- väärennös (forgery, counterfeit)
- väärentää (to forge, to counterfeit)
- vale- (prefix in compounds: fake-)

**Typical contexts:** Commercial authentication (materials, products), identity verification, evaluation of emotions and relationships, distinguishing reality from fiction.

**Contrasts observed:** *Aito* focuses on material/emotional authenticity, while *todellinen* emphasizes actual existence versus imagination. *Oikea* in this domain means "real/genuine" rather than "correct."

---

## GROUP 5: Factuality and Actual Occurrence

**Definition:** Expressions marking that something actually exists, occurred, or is the case in reality, as opposed to being imagined, theoretical, fictional, or merely possible. Used to distinguish actual states of affairs from appearances, beliefs, plans, or hypotheticals. Includes both adjectival forms describing factual status and adverbial expressions emphasizing actuality.

**Representative expressions:**
- tosiasia (fact)
- tosiasiallinen (factual, actual, de facto)
- tosiasiassa (in fact, actually)
- todellinen (real, actual)
- todellisuus (reality)
- todellisuudessa (in reality, actually)
- todella (really, truly, actually)
- tositarina (true story)
- tositapahtumat (real events, actual events)
- tosielämä (real life)
- tosielämässä (in real life)
- perustua tosiasioihin (to be based on facts)
- perustua tositapahtumiin (to be based on real events)

**Typical contexts:** Distinguishing fact from opinion, reality from fiction, actual occurrence from possibility, practical reality from theory.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from truth-evaluation (which assesses correspondence of statements) by focus on actual existence or occurrence. *Tosiasiallinen* emphasizes de facto status versus nominal or legal status.

---

## GROUP 6: Proof, Evidence, and Demonstration

**Definition:** Expressions related to establishing truth through evidence, testimony, or demonstration. Includes both the evidence itself and the processes of proving, testifying, or verifying. Concentrated in legal, scientific, and argumentative contexts where establishing truth requires evidential support.

**Representative expressions:**
- todistaa (to prove, to testify, to demonstrate)
- todiste (proof, evidence)
- todistus (certificate, testimony, proof)
- todistaja (witness)
- todistaa oikeaksi (to prove right, to vindicate)
- todistaa vääräksi (to prove wrong, to disprove)
- esittää todisteita (to present evidence)
- todistaa oikeudessa (to testify in court)
- todistusaineisto (body of evidence)
- todistustaakka (burden of proof)
- todentaa (to verify, to authenticate)
- todentaa henkilöllisyys (to verify identity)
- näyttää toteen (to demonstrate, to prove)

**Typical contexts:** Legal proceedings (testimony, evidence), scientific demonstration, argumentative discourse, administrative verification.

**Contrasts observed:** *Todistaa* can mean both "to prove" (establish truth) and "to testify" (give evidence). *Todiste* refers to evidence itself, while *todistus* can mean both evidence and official certificates.

---

## GROUP 7: Reliability and Trustworthiness

**Definition:** Expressions evaluating whether sources, persons, or information can be trusted or relied upon. Focus on dependability and consistency over time rather than single-instance truth. Used in contexts of source evaluation, character assessment, and information quality judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- luotettava (reliable, trustworthy, dependable)
- luotettavuus (reliability, trustworthiness)
- luotettava lähde (reliable source)
- luotettava tieto (reliable information)
- uskottava (believable, credible, plausible)
- uskottavuus (credibility, believability)
- uskottava selitys (credible explanation)
- uskottava todistaja (credible witness)

**Typical contexts:** Source evaluation (journalism, academia), witness credibility (legal), information quality assessment, character evaluation.

**Contrasts observed:** *Luotettava* emphasizes dependability and consistency, while *uskottava* emphasizes subjective believability or plausibility. Both differ from truth-evaluation by focusing on trustworthiness rather than correspondence to facts.

---

## GROUP 8: Honesty and Sincerity

**Definition:** Expressions characterizing persons, statements, or conduct as honest, truthful, and sincere versus dishonest or insincere. Focus on moral character and communicative integrity rather than factual accuracy. Used in moral evaluation of persons and assessment of sincerity in communication.

**Representative expressions:**
- rehellinen (honest, truthful)
- rehellisyys (honesty, truthfulness)
- rehellisesti sanottuna (honestly speaking, to be honest)
- olla rehellinen (to be honest)
- epärehellinen (dishonest, untruthful)
- vilpitön (sincere, genuine, without deceit)
- vilpittömyys (sincerity, genuineness)
- vilpitön anteeksipyyntö (sincere apology)
- tosissaan (seriously, in earnest)
- olla tosissaan (to be serious)
- ottaa tosissaan (to take seriously)

**Typical contexts:** Character evaluation, moral discourse, interpersonal communication, assessment of sincerity in apologies or statements.

**Contrasts observed:** *Rehellinen* focuses on truthfulness as moral virtue, while *vilpitön* emphasizes absence of deceptive intent. *Tosissaan* marks seriousness/earnestness rather than honesty per se.

---

## GROUP 9: Distortion and Misrepresentation

**Definition:** Expressions denoting alteration, distortion, or misrepresentation of facts, truth, or information. Includes both intentional manipulation and unintentional distortion. Used in critical discourse about information quality, media criticism, and analytical contexts where accuracy of representation is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**
- vääristää (to distort, to falsify, to misrepresent)
- vääristää totuutta (to distort the truth)
- vääristää tosiasioita (to distort facts)
- vääristyä (to become distorted)
- vääristymä (distortion, aberration)
- vääristely (distortion, misrepresentation)
- harhaanjohtava (misleading, deceptive)
- harhaanjohtava tieto (misleading information)
- johtaa harhaan (to mislead, to lead astray)
- antaa väärä kuva (to give a false impression)
- luoda väärä mielikuva (to create a false impression)

**Typical contexts:** Media criticism, analytical discourse about information quality, legal contexts (misrepresentation), critical evaluation of claims.

**Contrasts observed:** *Vääristää* can apply to both intentional manipulation and unintentional distortion. *Harhaanjohtava* may or may not involve conscious intent to deceive.

---

## GROUP 10: Misunderstanding and Error in Comprehension

**Definition:** Expressions denoting incorrect understanding, interpretation, or comprehension. Focus on cognitive errors in processing information rather than errors in production or action. Used in communicative contexts where understanding has gone wrong.

**Representative expressions:**
- väärinkäsitys (misunderstanding, misconception)
- väärinymmärrys (misunderstanding)
- ymmärtää väärin (to misunderstand)
- väärä käsitys (wrong impression, misconception)
- saada väärä käsitys (to get the wrong impression)
- väärintulkinta (misinterpretation)
- väärinarviointi (misjudgment, miscalculation)
- väärinarvioida (to misjudge, to miscalculate)

**Typical contexts:** Interpersonal communication, clarification of misunderstandings, explanatory discourse, conflict resolution.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from general error (*virhe*) by focus on comprehension/interpretation rather than action or production. Distinguished from deliberate deception by lack of intent.

---

## GROUP 11: Justice and Rights

**Definition:** Expressions related to justice, fairness, legal rights, and moral rightness. Derived from *oikea* (right) but specialized to legal and moral domains. Used in legal, political, and moral discourse about entitlements, fairness, and justice.

**Representative expressions:**
- oikeus (right, justice, law, court)
- oikeudenmukainen (just, fair)
- oikeudenmukaisuus (justice, fairness)
- oikeutettu (justified, entitled, warranted)
- oikeuttaa (to justify, to entitle)
- saada oikeutta (to get justice)
- vaatia oikeutta (to demand justice)
- puolustaa oikeuksiaan (to defend one's rights)
- loukata oikeuksia (to violate rights)
- vääryys (injustice, wrong)
- kärsiä vääryyttä (to suffer injustice)
- oikeuslaitos (judicial system)

**Typical contexts:** Legal proceedings, political discourse, moral philosophy, human rights discourse, discussions of fairness.

**Contrasts observed:** While derived from *oikea* (correct/right), this group has specialized to legal/moral domains. Distinguished from correctness evaluation by focus on entitlements and justice rather than factual accuracy.

---

## GROUP 12: Realization and Actualization

**Definition:** Expressions denoting the process of making something real or actual, or of something becoming real. Focus on transition from possibility, plan, or prediction to actual reality. Used in contexts of planning, achievement, and fulfillment of predictions.

**Representative expressions:**
- toteutua (to come true, to be realized, to materialize)
- toteuttaa (to realize, to implement, to carry out)
- toteutus (realization, implementation, execution)
- toteutuminen (realization, coming true)
- todellistua (to come true, to be realized)
- todellistaa (to realize, to actualize, to make real)
- käydä toteen (to come true, to be realized)
- tulla todeksi (to come true)

**Typical contexts:** Project management (implementation), achievement discourse, fulfillment of predictions or dreams, planning contexts.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from truth-evaluation by focus on making/becoming real rather than assessing correspondence to reality. Specialized use of truth-domain roots for actualization processes.

---

## GROUP 13: Probability and Likelihood

**Definition:** Expressions marking probability, likelihood, or appearance of truth. Used for epistemic hedging and probability assessment. Distinguished from categorical truth-evaluation by marking degrees of likelihood rather than definite truth or falsity.

**Representative expressions:**
- todennäköinen (probable, likely)
- todennäköisesti (probably, likely)
- todennäköisyys (probability, likelihood)
- vaikuttaa todelta (to seem true, to appear true)
- kuulostaa todelta (to sound true)
- näyttää todelta (to look true)
- tuntua todelta (to feel true)

**Typical contexts:** Epistemic hedging, probability assessment, expressing uncertainty, scientific/statistical discourse.

**Contrasts observed:** Distinguished from categorical truth-evaluation by marking likelihood rather than definite truth. *Todennäköinen* literally means "truth-appearing" but functions as probability marker.

---

## GROUP 14: Seriousness and Earnestness

**Definition:** Expressions marking seriousness of intent, earnestness, or non-joking attitude. Semantic extension from truth domain (being "truly" serious) to marking gravity or sincerity of manner. Used to distinguish serious intent from joking or casual attitude.

**Representative expressions:**
- tosissaan (seriously, in earnest)
- olla tosissaan (to be serious, to be in earnest)
- ottaa tosissaan (to take seriously)
- puhua tosissaan (to speak seriously)
- tositarkoituksella (seriously, with serious intent)
- tosimielessä (seriously, in earnest)
- totinen (serious, earnest, solemn)

**Typical contexts:** Marking seriousness of intent, distinguishing serious from joking, emphasizing gravity of situation, requesting to be taken seriously.

**Contrasts observed:** Semantic extension from truth/reality domain to seriousness domain. *Tosissaan* marks earnestness rather than factual truth. Distinguished from honesty (*rehellinen*) by focus on seriousness rather than truthfulness.

---

## GROUP 15: Directional and Spatial (Right/Left)

**Definition:** Expressions using *oikea* and *väärä* in directional/spatial senses (right vs. left, correct side vs. wrong side). Polysemous with correctness meanings but functionally distinct in spatial/navigational contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- oikea (right [direction])
- oikealla (on the right)
- kääntyä oikealle (to turn right)
- mennä oikealle (to go right)
- oikealla puolella (on the right side)
- oikeakätinen (right-handed)
- oikeanpuoleinen (right-side)
- väärä suunta (wrong direction)
- mennä väärään suuntaan (to go in the wrong direction)

**Typical contexts:** Navigation, spatial description, giving directions, describing location.

**Contrasts observed:** Same lexical forms as correctness domain but functionally distinct in spatial contexts. *Oikea* = "right" (direction) vs. "correct" (evaluation). *Väärä suunta* can be literal (wrong direction) or figurative (wrong course of action).

---

## GROUP 16: Intensification (Colloquial)

**Definition:** Use of *tosi* as colloquial intensifier meaning "really" or "very." Grammaticalized from truth-domain adjective to degree modifier. Restricted to informal registers.

**Representative expressions:**
- tosi hyvä (really good)
- tosi iso (really big)
- tosi paljon (really much, a lot)
- tosi + adjective (really + adjective)

**Typical contexts:** Informal speech, colloquial emphasis, degree modification in casual conversation.

**Contrasts observed:** Functional shift from truth-evaluation to degree modification. Restricted to informal registers; not used in formal writing. Parallel to *oikein* which can also function as intensifier but is more accepted in formal contexts.

---

## GROUP 17: Observation and Statement

**Definition:** Expressions for stating, noting, or observing facts without necessarily proving them. Focus on declarative act of stating what is observed or known. Distinguished from proof/demonstration by lack of evidential emphasis.

**Representative expressions:**
- todeta (to state, to note, to observe, to ascertain)
- toteamus (statement, observation, finding)
- toteava (stating, observational, declarative)

**Typical contexts:** Formal statements, medical/professional findings, neutral observation, declarative discourse.

**Contrasts observed:** *Todeta* emphasizes stating/observing rather than proving. Less evidentially strong than *todistaa*. Common in professional contexts for stating findings or observations.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items belonging to multiple groups:

**oikea**
- GROUP 2 (Correctness): oikea vastaus (correct answer)
- GROUP 4 (Authenticity): oikea nimi (real name)
- GROUP 11 (Justice): oikeus (right, justice)
- GROUP 15 (Directional): oikealla (on the right)

**väärä**
- GROUP 2 (Correctness): väärä vastaus (wrong answer)
- GROUP 3 (Deception): väärä todistus (false testimony)
- GROUP 9 (Distortion): vääristää (to distort)
- GROUP 10 (Misunderstanding): väärinkäsitys (misunderstanding)
- GROUP 15 (Directional): väärä suunta (wrong direction)

**todellinen**
- GROUP 4 (Authenticity): todellinen (real, actual)
- GROUP 5 (Factuality): todellinen tilanne (actual situation)

**tosissaan**
- GROUP 8 (Honesty): marking sincerity
- GROUP 14 (Seriousness): marking earnestness

**vale-** (prefix)
- GROUP 3 (Deception): valepassi (fake passport)
- GROUP 4 (Authenticity): marking inauthenticity

### Items with unclear grouping:

**totta kai** (of course, certainly)
- Functions as discourse particle/affirmative marker
- Grammaticalized from truth domain but no longer evaluates truth
- Does not fit cleanly into any identified group

**totta puhuen** (to tell the truth, truthfully speaking)
- Functions as discourse marker introducing frank statement
- Overlaps with GROUP 8 (Honesty) but more grammaticalized
- Borderline between truth-evaluation and discourse marking

**oikeaoppinen** (orthodox)
- Derived from *oikea* but specialized to doctrinal correctness
- Limited to religious/ideological contexts
- Does not fit cleanly into identified groups

**paikkansapitävä** (accurate, correct, valid)
- Overlaps with GROUP 1 (Truth Evaluation) and GROUP 2 (Correctness)
- Formal register equivalent to both truth and correctness evaluation
- Idiomatic construction *pitää paikkansa* is fixed expression

### Items not clearly grouped:

**tosikko** (overly serious person)
- Pejorative person noun derived from *tosi*
- Marginal status, limited to informal contexts
- Does not participate in productive patterns

**tosipoika** (real boy, genuine boy)
- Colloquial compound with limited distribution
- Marginal status
- Does not fit into identified groups

**oikotie** (shortcut)
- Derived from *oikea* but semantically distant
- Literal meaning "straight road"
- Does not participate in truth/correctness domains

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Observed asymmetries:

1. **Legal concentration**: Groups 3, 6, and 11 show heavy concentration in legal contexts, while other groups are more evenly distributed across registers.

2. **Register stratification**: Group 16 (Intensification) is restricted to informal registers, while Groups 6 and 11 are concentrated in formal/technical registers.

3. **Polysemy patterns**: *Oikea* and *väärä* show systematic polysemy across multiple groups (correctness, authenticity, direction, justice), while *tosi* shows more concentrated semantic range.

4. **Derivational productivity**: The *tosi/tode-* root shows extensive derivation across multiple groups (1, 5, 6, 12, 13, 14, 17), while *aito* shows limited derivation concentrated in Group 4.

5. **Intentionality distinction**: Groups 2 (Error) and 10 (Misunderstanding) mark unintentional incorrectness, while Group 3 (Deception) marks intentional falsehood. This distinction is lexically encoded in Finnish.

### Gaps and absences:

- No clear grouping for temporal/timing expressions beyond *oikea-aikainen* (timely)
- No distinct group for epistemic modality beyond probability (Group 13)
- No clear grouping for orthodoxy/doctrinal correctness beyond marginal *oikeaoppinen*
- Limited grouping for authentication processes beyond scattered items in Groups 4 and 6

### Cross-cutting patterns:

- **Formal vs. informal**: Some groups (6, 11, 17) are concentrated in formal registers, while others (16) are restricted to informal registers
- **Propositional vs. non-propositional**: Groups 1, 5, 6 primarily evaluate propositions, while Groups 2, 4 evaluate non-propositional entities
- **Intentionality**: Groups 3, 8, 9 involve intentional states, while Groups 2, 10 involve unintentional errors
- **Process vs. state**: Group 12 focuses on processes of becoming real, while most other groups evaluate states
