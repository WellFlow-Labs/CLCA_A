## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### BASE: ჭეშმარიტი (ch'eshmarit'i) — "true, genuine"

#### Derived Form 1: ჭეშმარიტება (ch'eshmarit'eba)
**Transliteration:** ch'eshmarit'eba
**Literal breakdown:** ch'eshmarit- (true) + -eba (abstract noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truth, veracity, truthfulness
**Usage description:** Abstract noun referring to the quality or state of being true. Used in formal and philosophical discourse to discuss truth as a concept, often interchangeable with სიმართლე but with slightly more elevated register.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: ჭეშმარიტად (ch'eshmarit'ad)
**Transliteration:** ch'eshmarit'ad
**Literal breakdown:** ch'eshmarit- (true) + -ad (adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, really
**Usage description:** Adverb used to emphasize that something is genuinely or truly the case. Common in both formal and informal speech to affirm the reality or authenticity of statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OF DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS

### Productive Suffixes Observed:

1. **-ad (adverbial)**: Extremely productive across nearly all adjectives in the truth domain
2. **-eba (verbal/abstract noun)**: Very productive for creating action nouns and abstract concepts
3. **-oba (abstract noun)**: Highly productive for quality/state nominalizations
4. **-ebuli (participle)**: Regular participial formation from verbs
5. **si- ... -e (abstract noun frame)**: Productive nominalizing pattern

### Productive Prefixes Observed:

1. **ga- (preverb)**: Common in perfective verb formation and related nominalizations
2. **da- (preverb)**: Frequent in verb formation with completive/resultative aspect
3. **mo- (preverb)**: Appears in verbs of deception and appearance
4. **u- (negative)**: Productive for negation
5. **ara- (negative)**: Alternative negation strategy, especially with borrowed terms

### Asymmetries and Gaps:

- Borrowed terms (ადეკვატური, ობიექტური, რეალური, etc.) show regular Georgian derivational patterns despite foreign origin
- Some native roots (სანდო) show limited productivity
- The root ტყუ- shows semantic extension from "lie" to "vain/futile" in certain derivations
- Not all adjectives form abstract nouns with -ობა; some use other patterns or lack such forms
