## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### REVELATION → BECOME Merger
The language does not distinguish between "becoming true" and "being revealed as true." Construction 4 (√bhū + truth-domain adjective) covers both meanings. The context determines whether the interpretation is ontological change or epistemic revelation. Example: *tat satyaṃ bhavati* can mean either "that becomes true" or "that proves true/is revealed as true."

### CAUSATION → VERIFICATION Substitution
Active causative constructions for truth-domain meanings (Construction 5) are marked as [MARGINAL] in P4b. The language strongly prefers passive evidential constructions (Construction 18) with instrumental case marking the means/agent. Rather than saying "X makes Y true," Sanskrit prefers "Y is established as true by means of X" (*pramāṇena siddham*). This represents a systematic preference for evidential over agentive framing.

### DISCONFIRMATION Strategies
Disconfirmation lacks a dedicated high-frequency construction. Two strategies exist:
1. The marginal causative construction (*mṛṣā karoti* "makes false")
2. Negative existential reasoning (*pramāṇābhāvāt* "due to absence of proof")

The second strategy is more common in philosophical contexts, expressing refutation through absence rather than active falsification.

### Morphological vs. Analytic Negation
Construction 2 shows two parallel strategies with different semantic profiles:
- **Analytic negation** (*na satya*): Simple denial, neutral
- **Morphological negation** (*asatya*): Categorical/definitional negation

P4b notes that morphological negation is "categorical/definitional rather than simple denial" and that "copula more frequently explicit with negative-prefixed adjectives." This suggests the two strategies are not fully equivalent.

### Asymmetries
No systematic asymmetries between positive and negative forms are observed. Truth-domain and falsity-domain terms show parallel productivity across constructions (e.g., *satyavādin* "truth-speaker" vs. *mṛṣāvādin* "liar").

### Evidential Preference
Multiple constructions (10, 18) emphasize evidential basis and means of knowledge rather than simple assertion. The instrumental case is extremely productive for expressing "by what means" something is known or established. This reflects a systematic preference for specifying epistemic grounds.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
**None identified.** All core CLCA operators have at least one attested construction.

### Lexical Gaps
**None identified.** The language has rich vocabulary across truth/falsity domains (satya, asatya, mṛṣā, mithyā, pramāṇa, dharma, etc.).

### Conceptual Gaps
**None identified.** All truth-domain operations can be expressed, though some require circumlocution or substitution.

### Partial/Marginal Gaps

1. **CAUSATION (factual) - Marginal**
   - Construction 5 marked as [MARGINAL] in P4b
   - "Less grammaticalized than inchoative"
   - "Restricted primarily to technical/philosophical contexts"
   - **Functional substitution available**: Passive evidential constructions (Construction 18) serve this function more commonly

2. **DISCONFIRMATION - Marginal**
   - Same construction as factual causation (Construction 5)
   - "Attested but less common than other strategies"
   - **Functional substitution available**: Negative existential reasoning (Construction 20) more common in philosophical refutation

3. **Comparative Truth-Domain Adjectives - Marginal**
   - Construction 17 with -tara suffix marked as [MARGINAL]
   - "Less commonly applied to truth-domain adjectives than to scalar adjectives"
   - "Context-dependent acceptability"
   - **Functional substitution available**: Ablative comparison with explicit degree terms (*alpa*, *adhika*) fully productive

### Asymmetric Gaps
**None identified.** Positive and negative forms show parallel productivity.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available (Core Status)
- **ATTR**: Construction 1 - Attribution with copula √as/√bhū
- **ADNOM**: Construction 3 - Adnominal modification with agreement
- **BECOME**: Construction 4 - Inchoative with √bhū
- **CAUSATION (character)**: Construction 7 - Agentive nominalization with -vādin
- **VERIFICATION**: Construction 18 - Resultative/evidential with instrumental + passive participle
- **NEGATION**: Construction 2 - Both analytic (particle na) and morphological (prefix a-)

### Available with Substitution
- **REVELATION**: Expressed through BECOME (Construction 4)
  - No dedicated construction; √bhū covers both meanings
  - Context disambiguates ontological vs. epistemic reading

- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Primarily expressed through VERIFICATION (Construction 18)
  - Direct causative (Construction 5) marginal
  - Passive evidential construction preferred
  - Represents systematic preference for evidential over agentive framing

- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Primarily expressed through negative existential reasoning (Construction 20)
  - Direct causative (Construction 5) marginal
  - Ablative of abhāva-compounds more common in philosophical contexts

### Partially Available (Marginal Status)
- **CAUSATION (factual) - direct expression**: Construction 5 with √kṛ
  - Attested but marked [MARGINAL]
  - "Less grammaticalized than inchoative"
  - Restricted to technical/philosophical contexts
  - Functional alternatives strongly preferred

- **DISCONFIRMATION - direct expression**: Construction 5 with √kṛ
  - Same marginal status as factual causation
  - "Attested but less common than other strategies"
  - Functional alternatives strongly preferred

### Missing Entirely
**None.** All CLCA operators have at least one expression strategy, though some require substitution or circumlocution.
