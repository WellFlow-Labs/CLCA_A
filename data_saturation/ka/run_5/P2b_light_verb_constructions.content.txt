# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving the 30 P1 base items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a and collocational patterns already documented in P1b are not re-documented here unless they participate in a distinct constructional pattern not previously captured. Where no common multi-word construction exists for a given item, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are organized by P1 base item.

**Note on the distinction between collocation and construction:** P1b documented many genitive collocations (e.g., č̣ešmaritebis dzieba, "the search for truth"). These are not re-documented here. The present step focuses on constructions with distinct syntactic or pragmatic structure: predicative constructions, periphrastic verbal constructions, idiomatic expressions with non-compositional meaning, and fixed syntactic frames that are not simple noun-phrase collocations.

## Item 1: ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ეს არის ჭეშმარიტი ამბავი
3. **Transliteration:** es aris č̣ešmariti ambavi
4. **English translation:** this is a true story / this is a true account
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction using the copula aris (is, 3sg present of qopna). Used to assert that a narrative or account corresponds to actual events. Common in literary, journalistic, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი გაგებით
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti gagebit
4. **English translation:** in the true sense (of the word); in the genuine meaning
5. **Usage notes:** Instrumental construction (gagebit = "in the sense/understanding of") used to assert that a term or concept is being used in its genuine or proper meaning rather than loosely or metaphorically. Appears in philosophical, legal, and analytical discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** სიტყვის ჭეშმარიტი გაგებით
3. **Transliteration:** sit̕q̇vis č̣ešmariti gagebit
4. **English translation:** in the true sense of the word
5. **Usage notes:** Extended form of Construction 2 with the genitive sit̕q̇vis (of the word) added. A fixed rhetorical frame used to assert that a predication is being made in the strictest or most genuine sense. Widely used in formal and academic registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 2: ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება ითქვა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba itqva
4. **English translation:** the truth was told / the truth has been spoken
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction (itqva = was said, 3sg aorist passive of tqva). Used in formal, rhetorical, and religious registers to assert that a true statement has been made. Carries a somewhat elevated or solemn register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება თვალში გიყურებს
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba tvalši giq̇urebs
4. **English translation:** the truth is staring you in the face (literally: truth looks you in the eye)
5. **Usage notes:** Idiomatic construction in which truth is personified as an agent that confronts the addressee directly. Used in rhetorical and moral discourse to assert that a fact is obvious and unavoidable. The construction is not fully compositional.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)
2. **Example:** ეს ჭეშმარიტებასთან შეუსაბამოა
3. **Transliteration:** es č̣ešmaritebastан šeusabamoа
4. **English translation:** this is inconsistent with the truth / this does not accord with the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction using the postposition -stan (with, in relation to) and the predicative form šeusabamoа (is inconsistent). Used in legal, journalistic, and analytical contexts to assert a mismatch between a claim and reality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)
2. **Example:** მთელი ჭეშმარიტება
3. **Transliteration:** mteli č̣ešmariteba
4. **English translation:** the whole truth
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed nominal construction with mteli (whole, entire). Used in legal oaths, moral discourse, and everyday speech to assert that nothing true is being withheld. Appears in the Georgian rendering of oath formulas.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)
2. **Example:** მთელი ჭეშმარიტება და მხოლოდ ჭეშმარიტება
3. **Transliteration:** mteli č̣ešmariteba da mxolod č̣ešmariteba
4. **English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Extended oath formula, a calque of the legal oath formula. Used in legal and formal contexts. The construction mxolod (only/nothing but) + č̣ešmariteba is the key idiomatic element.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 3: სიმართლე (simartle)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე გითხრა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle gitxra
4. **English translation:** to tell you the truth / let me tell you the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Periphrastic construction using the verb gitxra (I will tell you, 1sg future/subjunctive of tqva with 2sg dative). Functions as a discourse marker introducing a candid or frank statement. Extremely common in spoken Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე რომ ვთქვა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle rom vtqva
4. **English translation:** to tell the truth; if I'm being honest; truth be told
5. **Usage notes:** Conditional/concessive construction using the subordinator rom (that/if) and the subjunctive vtqva (I say/were to say). Functions as a hedging or candor-marking discourse frame. Very common in spoken and informal written Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლეს ვამბობ
3. **Transliteration:** simartle s vambob
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth / I'm speaking the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Periphrastic verbal construction using vambob (I say/am saying, 1sg present of amboba). Used to assert sincerity or truthfulness of one's own statement. Common in everyday speech and in contexts where one's honesty is being questioned.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე მოვიდა გარეთ
3. **Transliteration:** simartle movida garet
4. **English translation:** the truth came out
5. **Usage notes:** Idiomatic construction using movida (came, 3sg aorist of mosvla) and garet (outside/out). Used to report that a previously concealed truth has been revealed. The spatial metaphor (truth coming outside) is idiomatic.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლეში ვიყო
3. **Transliteration:** simartleši viq̇o
4. **English translation:** to be honest; in truth; frankly
5. **Usage notes:** Locative construction (simartleši = in the truth) used as a discourse marker to introduce a frank or candid statement. Functions similarly to "to be honest" in English. Common in spoken Georgian.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** ნახევარი სიმართლე
3. **Transliteration:** naxevari simartle
4. **English translation:** a half-truth
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed nominal construction with naxevari (half). Used in moral and journalistic discourse to characterize a statement that is partially true but misleading through omission or selective presentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 4: მართალი (martali)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალი ხარ
3. **Transliteration:** martali xar
4. **English translation:** you are right
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction using xar (you are, 2sg present copula). The most common way to concede a point or validate an interlocutor's claim in spoken Georgian. Extremely high frequency.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალი ვარ
3. **Transliteration:** martali var
4. **English translation:** I am right
5. **Usage notes:** First-person predicative construction using var (I am, 1sg present copula). Used to assert the correctness of one's own position. Common in argument and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალი კაცი
3. **Transliteration:** martali kaci
4. **English translation:** an honest man; a righteous man; a just man
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed nominal construction with kaci (man/person). A culturally salient collocation used in moral and literary discourse to characterize a person of integrity. Appears in proverbs and moral narratives.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალს ვამბობ
3. **Transliteration:** martals vambob
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth / I am saying what is right
5. **Usage notes:** Periphrastic verbal construction using the accusative martals and vambob (I am saying). Used to assert the truthfulness of one's own statement. Parallel to simartle s vambob but with a slightly different nuance — martals emphasizes correctness and rightness rather than factual truth alone.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალია, მაგრამ...
3. **Transliteration:** martalia, magram...
4. **English translation:** it is true, but... / that's right, but... / granted, but...
5. **Usage notes:** Concessive construction using martalia (it is true/right) followed by the adversative conjunction magram (but). Functions as a concession frame in argument, acknowledging a point before introducing a counter-consideration. Very common in spoken and written discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალი სიტყვა
3. **Transliteration:** martali sit̕q̇va
4. **English translation:** a true word; a word of truth; a just word
5. **Usage notes:** Fixed nominal construction with sit̕q̇va (word). Used in moral and rhetorical discourse to characterize a statement as true and just. Appears in proverbial and literary registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Item 5: სიყალბე (siq̇albe)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბე გამოაშკარავდა
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albe gamoaškaravda
4. **English translation:** the falseness/fraud was exposed / the deception came to light
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using gamoaškaravda (was exposed/revealed, 3sg aorist of gamoaškaraveba). Used to report that a deception or forgery has been uncovered. Common in journalistic and legal narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბეში დაიჭირეს
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albеši daič̣ires
4. **English translation:** was caught in a deception / was caught in the act of falsification
5. **Usage notes:** Locative construction (siq̇albeši = in the falseness/act of falsification) with daič̣ires (was caught, 3sg aorist passive of dač̣era). Used in legal and journalistic contexts to report that someone was apprehended in the act of fraud or deception.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბის ნიშნები
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albis nišnebi
4. **English translation:** signs of falsification / indicators of fraud
5. **Usage notes:** Genitive construction with nišnebi (signs, plural of nišani). Used in legal, forensic, and investigative contexts to refer to the observable markers that indicate a document or item has been falsified.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 6: ყალბი (q̇albi)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი (q̇albi)
2. **Example:** ყალბი აღმოჩნდა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi aɣmočnda
4. **English translation:** turned out to be fake / proved to be false
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction using aɣmočnda (turned out to be, 3sg aorist of aɣmočena). Used to report that something previously accepted as genuine has been revealed to be counterfeit or false. Common in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი (q̇albi)
2. **Example:** ყალბად გამოვიდა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albad gamovida
4. **English translation:** turned out to be false / came out as fake
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial predicative construction using the adverbial q̇albad (falsely/as false) and gamovida (came out, 3sg aorist of gamosvla). Functionally equivalent to Construction 1 but with a slightly more colloquial register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი (q̇albi)
2. **Example:** ყალბი ამოიცნო
3. **Transliteration:** q̇albi amoicno
4. **English translation:** identified/recognized the fake; detected the forgery
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using amoicno (recognized/identified, 3sg aorist of amocnoba). Used in investigative, forensic, and everyday contexts to report the detection of a counterfeit item or false claim.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
