# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction documented in P1 and P1b/P2a/P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect usage patterns as documented. Items that appear in multiple groups are listed under each relevant group and flagged in the overlapping items section. Items for which no clear grouping is supported by the data are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a statement or claim is correct or true

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a proposition, answer, or claim corresponds to reality or meets a standard of factual correctness. They appear in educational contexts (evaluating answers), in everyday conversation (affirming or denying claims), and in journalistic and analytical writing. They are typically applied to statements and propositions rather than to persons or objects.

**Representative expressions:**
- נָכוֹן / nakhon — "correct, right" (the default everyday term for evaluating a statement as correct)
- לֹא נָכוֹן / lo nakhon — "incorrect, wrong"
- אֱמֶת / ʾemet — "truth" (used predicatively: "זה אמת," "that is true")
- בֶּאֱמֶת / be-ʾemet — "truly, really" (sincerity/truth marker on statements)
- אֱמֶת לַאֲמִתָּהּ / ʾemet le-ʾamittah — "the real truth, strictly speaking"
- מְדֻיָּק / meduyaq — "accurate, precise" (evaluates degree of exactness)
- לֹא מְדֻיָּק / lo meduyaq — "inaccurate, imprecise"
- מְדֻיָּק יוֹתֵר / meduyaq yoter — "more precisely" (discourse marker for self-correction)
- נָכוֹן שֶׁ... / nakhon she-... — "it is true that..., admittedly..." (concessive construction)
- מוּטְעֶה / mutʿe — "mistaken, erroneous" (applied to beliefs or persons who hold incorrect views)
- טָעוּת / taʿut — "mistake, error"
- זֶה שֶׁקֶר / ze sheqer — "that is a lie, that is false" (strong negation of a claim's truth)
- הוּא צָדַק / hu tsadak — "he was right, he was correct" (verbal evaluation of correctness)
- אִם אֲנִי נָכוֹן / ʾim ʾani nakhon — "if I am correct" (epistemic hedging)

**Notes:** נָכוֹן is the unmarked, highest-frequency term in this group. אֱמֶת overlaps with Group 2 (moral/interpersonal honesty) and Group 5 (foundational value discourse). מְדֻיָּק is distinguished from נָכוֹן by its emphasis on precision and degree of exactness rather than simple correctness; it belongs more naturally to Group 3 (precision evaluation). מוּטְעֶה is distinguished from שֶׁקֶר in that it does not imply intentional deception.

## Group 2 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a person is honest or sincere in their conduct and speech

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate persons (and sometimes their statements or actions) as genuinely honest, sincere, or straightforward in their dealings with others. They appear in interpersonal, moral, and character-evaluation contexts. The focus is on the person's inner disposition and its expression in behavior, rather than on the factual correctness of a specific claim.

**Representative expressions:**
- כַּנּוּת / kannut — "sincerity, honesty" (the quality of being genuine in expression)
- בְּכַנּוּת / be-kannut — "sincerely, honestly, frankly" (adverbial sincerity marker)
- בְּכַנּוּת רַבָּה / be-kannut rabbah — "in all sincerity, very honestly"
- כֵּן / ken — "sincere, honest" (adjectival evaluation of a person)
- יָשָׁר / yashar — "upright, honest, straightforward" (moral character evaluation)
- יְשָׁרוּת / yesharut — "straightforwardness, honesty, directness"
- בִּישָׁרוּת / be-yesharut — "straightforwardly, honestly"
- יֹשֶׁר / yosher — "integrity, uprightness" (more formal/abstract than yesharut)
- אָדָם יָשָׁר / ʾadam yashar — "an upright person, an honest person"
- לְדַבֵּר יָשָׁר / ledabber yashar — "to speak straight, to speak honestly"
- יָשָׁר מִן הַכָּתֵף / yashar min ha-katef — "straight from the shoulder, bluntly"
- נֶאֱמָן / neʾeman — "faithful, loyal, trustworthy" (overlaps with Group 4)
- נֶאֱמָן לְעַצְמוֹ / neʾeman le-ʿatsmo — "true to oneself"
- לְדַבֵּר אֱמֶת / ledabber ʾemet — "to speak the truth"
- חוֹסֶר כַּנּוּת / ḥoser kannut — "lack of sincerity, insincerity"

**Notes:** כַּנּוּת and יָשָׁר/יֹשֶׁר are closely related but distinguishable in usage: כַּנּוּת tends to emphasize the genuineness of inner feeling as expressed outwardly (sincerity), while יָשָׁר/יֹשֶׁר emphasizes moral uprightness and directness of conduct. Both are used in interpersonal and moral evaluation. הָגוּן (Group 6) overlaps with this group when applied to persons. אֱמֶת overlaps with Group 1 and Group 5.

## Group 3 — Expressions used to evaluate precision and exactness

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a statement, measurement, description, or representation achieves the required degree of exactness or specificity. They are particularly common in scientific, journalistic, legal, and technical registers, and they contrast with terms meaning "vague" or "approximate." The evaluative dimension concerns degree of fit between a representation and its object, not simply whether something is true or false.

**Representative expressions:**
- מְדֻיָּק / meduyaq — "accurate, precise, exact"
- דִּיּוּק / diyyuq — "precision, exactness, accuracy"
- בְּדִיּוּק / be-diyyuq — "exactly, precisely" (extremely common adverbial)
- לְדַיֵּק / ledayyeq — "to be precise, to specify precisely"
- דַּיְקָן / dayqan — "a precise person, a stickler for accuracy"
- דַּיְקָנוּת / dayqanut — "precision, meticulousness"
- אִי-דִּיּוּק / ʾi-diyyuq — "inaccuracy, imprecision"
- תֵּאוּר מְדֻיָּק / teʾur meduyaq — "an accurate description"
- מֵידָע מְדֻיָּק / meydaʿ meduyaq — "accurate information"
- מְדֻיָּק יוֹתֵר / meduyaq yoter — "more precisely" (self-correction marker)

**Notes:** This group is relatively cohesive and internally consistent. The root ד-י-ק generates the entire cluster. בְּדִיּוּק is among the most frequent items in this group in everyday speech. The group overlaps with Group 1 (correctness evaluation) in that precision is a dimension of correctness, but the precision cluster is distinguished by its emphasis on degree and exactness rather than binary true/false evaluation.

## Group 4 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a person, source, or information can be trusted or relied upon

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a person, source, witness, or piece of information is dependable and worthy of trust. They are common in legal contexts (evaluating witnesses and evidence), journalistic contexts (evaluating sources), and interpersonal contexts (evaluating relationships). The evaluative dimension concerns the reliability of the entity over time or across situations, not merely the truth of a single statement.

**Representative expressions:**
- מְהֵימָן / meheymen — "reliable, credible, trustworthy" (somewhat formal; common in legal discourse)
- מְהֵימָנוּת / meheymanut — "reliability, credibility, trustworthiness"
- עֵד מְהֵימָן / ʿed meheymen — "a credible witness" (fixed legal expression)
- מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן / maqor meheymen — "a reliable source"
- אָמִין / ʾamin — "reliable, trustworthy, dependable" (somewhat more colloquial than meheymen)
- אֲמִינוּת / ʾaminut — "reliability, credibility"
- אִי-אֲמִינוּת / ʾi-ʾaminut — "unreliability, lack of credibility"
- נֶאֱמָן / neʾeman — "faithful, loyal, trustworthy" (also used for fidelity to a text or facts)
- נֶאֱמָנוּת / neʾemanut — "loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity"
- נֶאֱמָן לָעוּבְדוֹת / neʾeman la-ʿuvdot — "faithful to the facts"
- נֶאֱמָן לַטֶּקְסְט / neʾeman la-teqst — "faithful to the text"
- אֱמוּנָה / ʾemunah — "faith, trust" (overlaps with Group 7)
- אָמוֹן / ʾamon — "trust, confidence"
- בְּנִיַּת אָמוֹן / binyan ʾamon — "trust-building"
- אִי-אָמוּן / ʾi-ʾamon — "distrust, no-confidence"
- הַצְבָּעַת אִי-אָמוּן / hatsbaʿat ʾi-ʾamon — "vote of no confidence"
- לֹא מְהֵימָן / lo meheymen — "not reliable, not credible"

**Notes:** מְהֵימָן and אָמִין are near-synonyms with register differences (meheymen more formal/legal, ʾamin more colloquial). נֶאֱמָן overlaps with Group 2 (personal honesty) when applied to persons, but belongs here when applied to fidelity to facts or texts. אֱמוּנָה in its religious sense belongs to Group 7; in its interpersonal sense (trust) it belongs here.

## Group 5 — Expressions used in discourse about truth and falsehood as values or principles

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth and falsehood are treated as moral, social, or philosophical values rather than as properties of individual statements. They are common in religious, ethical, legal-moral, and rhetorical discourse. They often appear in fixed phrases, biblical citations, and formal declarations.

**Representative expressions:**
- אֱמֶת / ʾemet — "truth" (as a foundational value)
- שֶׁקֶר / sheqer — "falsehood, lying" (as a moral category)
- לְדַבֵּר שֶׁקֶר / ledabber sheqer — "to speak falsehood" (biblical/formal register)
- מִדְּבַר שֶׁקֶר תִּרְחָק / midvar sheqer tirḥaq — "distance yourself from falsehood" (biblical injunction)
- אֱמֶת וְשָׁלוֹם / ʾemet ve-shalom — "truth and peace" (biblical paired value)
- בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים / be-ʾemet u-ve-tamim — "in truth and integrity" (formal oath formula)
- כָּל הָאֱמֶת וְכְלוּם חוּץ מֵהָאֱמֶת / kol ha-ʾemet ve-klum ḥuts min ha-ʾemet — "the whole truth and nothing but the truth" (legal oath)
- חֲצִי אֱמֶת / ḥatsi ʾemet — "a half-truth"
- גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת / garʿin shel ʾemet — "a kernel of truth"
- אֱמֶת מָרָּה / ʾemet marah — "a bitter truth"
- לְגַלּוֹת אֶת הָאֱמֶת / legallot ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to reveal the truth"
- לְהַסְתִּיר אֶת הָאֱמֶת / lehastir ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to hide the truth"
- לְחַפֵּשׂ אֶת הָאֱמֶת / leḥappes ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to search for the truth"
- לְעַוֵּת אֶת הָאֱמֶת / leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to distort the truth"
- לְסַלֵּף אֶת הָאֱמֶת / lesallef ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to falsify/distort the truth"
- לְהִתְעַמֵּת עִם הָאֱמֶת / lehitʿammet ʿim ha-ʾemet — "to confront the truth"
- חוֹתֶמֶת שֶׁל הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא אֱמֶת / ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh hu ʾemet — "the seal of the Holy One is truth" (rabbinic dictum)
- שֶׁקֶר מוּחְלָט / sheqer muḥlat — "an outright lie"
- שֶׁקֶר וָכָזָב / sheqer va-khazav — "lies and falsehoods" (emphatic paired expression)

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (correctness of statements) by its focus on truth and falsehood as objects of moral and social concern rather than as properties of individual propositions. The expressions here tend to appear in contexts of moral evaluation, formal declarations, and rhetorical discourse. אֱמֶת participates in all three groups (1, 2, 5) depending on context.

## Group 6 — Expressions used to evaluate whether conduct, offers, or persons meet a standard of basic decency and fairness

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a person's behavior, an offer, or a situation meets a basic standard of what is considered fair, proper, and decent in social and commercial interaction. They are common in everyday speech, commercial contexts, and labor relations. The evaluative standard is social and relational rather than strictly legal or epistemic.

**Representative expressions:**
- הָגוּן / hagun — "decent, fair, proper, respectable"
- הֲגוּנוּת / hagunot — "decency, fairness, propriety"
- לֹא הָגוּן / lo hagun — "unfair, indecent, not right"
- זֶה לֹא הָגוּן / ze lo hagun — "that is not fair" (extremely common in everyday speech)
- מְחִיר הָגוּן / meḥir hagun — "a fair price"
- אָדָם הָגוּן / ʾadam hagun — "a decent person"
- הֶסְכֵּם הָגוּן / heskem hagun — "a fair agreement"
- יַחַס הָגוּן / yaḥas hagun — "fair treatment"
- מִידַּת הַיֹּשֶׁר / middat ha-yosher — "the standard of fairness/equity" (more formal; legal register)
- עֲשֵׂה הַיָּשָׁר וְהַטּוֹב / ʿase ha-yashar ve-ha-tov — "do what is right and good" (biblical-legal principle)
- צֶדֶק חֶבְרָתִי / tsedeq ḥevrati — "social justice" (overlaps with Group 8)
- לְמַעַן הַצֶּדֶק / lemaʿan ha-tsedeq — "for the sake of justice"

**Notes:** הָגוּן is the most colloquial and everyday term in this group, with a lower threshold of formality than יֹשֶׁר or צֶדֶק. מִידַּת הַיֹּשֶׁר and עֲשֵׂה הַיָּשָׁר וְהַטּוֹב belong to a more formal legal-ethical register. צֶדֶק overlaps with Group 8 (justice and righteousness). The group is unified by the shared evaluative context of fairness in social and commercial interaction.

## Group 7 — Expressions used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate the formal validity of documents, contracts, and procedures

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a document, contract, decision, or procedure is formally valid, in force, and compliant with the relevant legal or administrative standard. They are primarily used in legal, bureaucratic, and official contexts. The evaluative dimension concerns formal status and compliance rather than moral quality or factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- תָּקֵף / taqef — "valid, in force, legally binding"
- תֹּקֶף / toqef — "validity, legal force"
- אִי-תֹּקֶף / ʾi-toqef — "invalidity, lack of legal force"
- בְּתֹקֶף / be-toqef — "by virtue of, pursuant to" (fixed legal formula)
- תָּקֵף עַד / taqef ʿad — "valid until" (expiry formula)
- לֹא תָּקֵף / lo taqef — "not valid, expired"
- חוֹזֶה תָּקֵף / ḥoze taqef — "a valid contract"
- דַּרְכּוֹן תָּקֵף / darkon taqef — "a valid passport"
- בָּטֵל / batel — "null, void, invalid"
- בִּטּוּל / bittul — "cancellation, annulment, nullification"
- לְבַטֵּל / levatel — "to cancel, to annul, to nullify"
- בָּטֵל וּמְבֻטָּל / batel u-mevuttal — "null and void" (emphatic legal formula)
- בָּטֵל מֵעִיקָּרוֹ / batel me-ʿiqqaro — "void from the outset, null ab initio"
- תָּקִין / taqin — "proper, in order, functioning correctly"
- תִּקִינוּת / taqinut — "proper functioning, regularity, compliance"
- אִי-תִּקִינוּת / ʾi-taqinut — "improper functioning, irregularity"
- נוֹהַל תָּקִין / nohal taqin — "proper procedure"
- הַכֹּל תָּקִין / ha-kol taqin — "everything is in order"
- פָּסוּל / pasul — "disqualified, invalid, inadmissible"
- כָּשֵׁר / kasher — "fit, valid, proper" (in legal/formal sense)
- עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר / ʿedut sheqer — "false testimony, perjury" (overlaps with Group 5)
- עֵד שֶׁקֶר / ʿed sheqer — "false witness" (overlaps with Group 5)
- חֲזָקָה / ḥazaqah — "legal presumption" (domain-specific)

**Notes:** תָּקֵף and בָּטֵל are direct antonyms within this group. תָּקִין is distinguished from תָּקֵף in that it evaluates proper functioning and procedural compliance rather than legal validity per se. פָּסוּל and כָּשֵׁר form an antonymous pair primarily in rabbinic and legal-religious contexts. עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר belongs to both this group and Group 5. חֲזָקָה is domain-specific to rabbinic/Jewish legal discourse.

## Group 8 — Expressions used to evaluate moral righteousness and justice

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate persons, actions, social arrangements, or judgments as morally righteous or just. They appear in biblical, rabbinic, religious, and modern political discourse. The evaluative dimension concerns moral and social rightness rather than factual correctness or procedural validity.

**Representative expressions:**
- צֶדֶק / tsedeq — "justice, righteousness"
- צַדִּיק / tsaddiq — "righteous, just; a righteous person"
- צְדָקָה / tsedaqah — "charity, righteousness" (with semantic narrowing to charitable giving in modern usage)
- לִצְדֹּק / litsdog — "to be right, to be justified"
- הִצְדִּיק / hitsdiq — "to justify, to vindicate"
- הַצְדָּקָה / hatsdaqah — "justification, vindication"
- זֶה לֹא מַצְדִּיק / ze lo matsdiq — "this does not justify"
- צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט / tsedaqah u-mishpat — "righteousness and justice" (biblical paired value)
- צֶדֶק חֶבְרָתִי / tsedeq ḥevrati — "social justice"
- לַעֲשׂוֹת צֶדֶק / laʿasot tsedeq — "to do justice"
- מִשְׁפַּט צֶדֶק / mishpat tsedeq — "a just judgment"
- יֹשֶׁר / yosher — "integrity, uprightness, fairness" (overlaps with Groups 2 and 6)
- תִּיקּוּן עוֹלָם / tiqqun ʿolam — "repair of the world, social justice"

**Notes:** צֶדֶק is the central term in this group. The group overlaps with Group 6 (fairness in social interaction) at the level of יֹשֶׁר and with Group 2 (personal honesty) at the level of the צַדִּיק evaluation. The distinction between this group and Group 6 is one of register and scope: Group 8 expressions tend to appear in more formal, religious, or political discourse and concern systemic or moral rightness, while Group 6 expressions are more colloquial and concern immediate social fairness.

## Group 9 — Expressions used to evaluate genuineness versus fabrication or imitation

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether something is what it claims or appears to be — a genuine original rather than a forgery, fabrication, or imitation. They are common in legal, commercial, artistic, and everyday contexts. The evaluative dimension concerns the relationship between an object or claim and its purported origin or identity.

**Representative expressions:**
- אֲמִתִּי / ʾamitī — "true, genuine, real, authentic"
- אֲמִתִּיּוּת / ʾamitiyut — "genuineness, authenticity, realness"
- אוֹתֶנְטִי / ʾotentī — "authentic, genuine" (more restricted register; cultural/artistic contexts)
- אוֹתֶנְטִיּוּת / ʾotentiyut — "authenticity"
- מְקוֹרִי / meqorī — "original, genuine" (emphasizes origin rather than character)
- מְקוֹרִיּוּת / meqoriyut — "originality, authenticity"
- מְזֻיָּף / mezuyaf — "forged, fake, counterfeit, falsified"
- זִיּוּף / ziyyuf — "forgery, falsification"
- לְזַיֵּף / lezayyef — "to forge, to falsify"
- בָּדוּי / baduy — "fabricated, invented, fictitious, made up"
- מָסְמָךְ מְזֻיָּף / mismakh mezuyaf — "a forged document"
- חֲתִימָה מְזֻיֶּפֶת / ḥatimah mezuyefet — "a forged signature"
- כֶּסֶף מְזֻיָּף / kesef mezuyaf — "counterfeit money"
- זֶהוּת מְזֻיֶּפֶת / zehut mezuyefet — "a false identity"
- יְצִירָה מְקוֹרִית / yetsira meqorit — "an original work"
- מָסְמָךְ מְקוֹרִי / mismakh meqorī — "the original document"
- חָבֵר אֲמִתִּי / ḥaver ʾamitī — "a true friend, a genuine friend"
- הַסִּיבָּה הָאֲמִתִּית / ha-sibba ha-ʾamitit — "the real reason"
- לֹא אֲמִתִּי / lo ʾamitī — "not genuine, not real"
- קוֹל אוֹתֶנְטִי / qol ʾotentī — "an authentic voice"

**Notes:** This group contains an internal distinction between terms that evaluate genuineness positively (אֲמִתִּי, אוֹתֶנְטִי, מְקוֹרִי) and terms that evaluate fabrication negatively (מְזֻיָּף, בָּדוּי). אֲמִתִּי is the broadest and most colloquial positive term; אוֹתֶנְטִי is more restricted to cultural and artistic registers; מְקוֹרִי emphasizes origin and is common in intellectual property and aesthetic contexts. מְזֻיָּף implies deliberate criminal falsification; בָּדוּי implies fabrication without necessarily implying criminal intent.

## Group 10 — Expressions used to evaluate consistency and contradiction

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a person's positions, a set of statements, or a body of evidence are internally consistent or whether they contradict one another. They are common in legal argumentation, philosophical discourse, and journalistic analysis. The evaluative dimension concerns the logical relationship between multiple claims or positions over time.

**Representative expressions:**
- עַקְבִּי / ʿaqbī — "consistent, coherent"
- עַקְבִּיּוּת / ʿaqbiyut — "consistency, coherence"
- אִי-עַקְבִּיּוּת / ʾi-ʿaqbiyut — "inconsistency, incoherence"
- סוֹתֵר / soter — "contradicting, contradictory, inconsistent with"
- סְתִירָה / setira — "contradiction, inconsistency"
- לִסְתּוֹר / listor — "to contradict, to undermine"
- סְתִירָה עַצְמִית / setira ʿatsmit — "self-contradiction, internal contradiction"
- הָרָאָיוֹת סוֹתְרוֹת אֶת הַטַּעֲנָה / ha-raʾayot sotrot ʾet ha-taʿanah — "the evidence contradicts the claim" (typical usage pattern)

**Notes:** This group is relatively small and cohesive. עַקְבִּי and סוֹתֵר are near-antonyms in usage: עַקְבִּי evaluates consistency positively, while סוֹתֵר evaluates contradiction negatively. The group is most active in legal and analytical discourse.

---

## Group 11 — Expressions used to evaluate reasonableness and plausibility

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a claim, inference, decision, or course of conduct is reasonable or plausible given the available information and circumstances. They are particularly prominent in legal discourse (especially administrative and tort law) and in everyday evaluation of claims and behavior. The evaluative dimension concerns whether something falls within the range of what a reasonable person would accept or do.

**Representative expressions:**
- סָבִיר / savir — "reasonable, plausible, credible"
- סְבִירוּת / sevirut — "reasonableness, plausibility"
- אִי-סְבִירוּת / ʾi-sevirut — "unreasonableness"
- הָאָדָם הַסָּבִיר / ha-ʾadam ha-savir — "the reasonable person" (fixed legal standard)
- בְּאֹפֶן סָבִיר / be-ʾofen savir — "in a reasonable manner, reasonably"
- לֹא סָבִיר / lo savir — "unreasonable"
- זֶה לֹא סָבִיר / ze lo savir — "that is not reasonable"
- סָבִיר לְהָנִיחַ / savir lehaniʾaḥ — "it is reasonable to assume"
- סָבִיר שֶׁ... / savir she-... — "it is reasonable that..."
- מִבְחַן הַסְּבִירוּת / mivḥan ha-sevirut — "the reasonableness test" (fixed legal expression)

**Notes:** This group is cohesive and centered on the root ס-ב-ר. The legal expressions (הָאָדָם הַסָּבִיר, מִבְחַן הַסְּבִירוּת, אִי-סְבִירוּת) are particularly prominent in Israeli administrative law. The everyday expressions (זֶה לֹא סָבִיר, סָבִיר שֶׁ...) are common across all registers.

---

## Group 12 — Expressions used to evaluate misleading or deceptive conduct

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a statement, representation, or course of conduct is misleading or deceptive, with particular attention to cases where the misleading effect may be achieved without outright falsehood. They are common in legal (especially consumer protection and advertising law) and journalistic contexts. The evaluative dimension concerns the effect of a communication on its recipient rather than its strict truth value.

**Representative expressions:**
- מַטְעֶה / matʿe — "misleading, deceptive"
- הַטְעָיָה / hatʿayah — "misleading, deception, misrepresentation"
- לְהַטְעוֹת / lehatʿot — "to mislead, to deceive"
- פִּרְסוּם מַטְעֶה / pirssum matʿe — "misleading advertisement" (fixed legal expression)
- מוּטְעֶה / mutʿe — "misled, mistaken" (the recipient of misleading conduct)
- לְעַוֵּת אֶת הָאֱמֶת / leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to distort the truth"
- לְסַלֵּף אֶת הָאֱמֶת / lesallef ʾet ha-ʾemet — "to falsify/distort the truth"
- חֲצִי אֱמֶת / ḥatsi ʾemet — "a half-truth" (a form of misleading by omission)

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 5 (truth and falsehood as values) by its focus on the mechanism and effect of deception rather than on falsehood as a moral category. It is distinguished from Group 9 (genuineness vs. fabrication) by its focus on communicative acts rather than on the status of objects or documents. מַטְעֶה and מוּטְעֶה are related but occupy different positions: מַטְעֶה evaluates the misleading act or agent, while מוּטְעֶה evaluates the person who has been misled. חֲצִי אֱמֶת belongs to both this group and Group 5.

---

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups or resist clean assignment:

**אֱמֶת / ʾemet:** Participates in Group 1 (correctness of statements), Group 2 (honest conduct), Group 5 (truth as a value), and Group 9 (genuineness). Its distribution across groups reflects its status as the most semantically central and contextually flexible term in the domain.

**יֹשֶׁר / yosher:** Participates in Group 2 (personal honesty/integrity), Group 6 (fairness in social interaction), and Group 8 (moral righteousness). The legal expression מִידַּת הַיֹּשֶׁר places it in Group 6/7; its use in moral character evaluation places it in Group 2; its use in formal ethical discourse places it in Group 8.

**נֶאֱמָן / neʾeman:** Participates in Group 2 (personal honesty) when evaluating a person's character, and in Group 4 (reliability/trustworthiness) when evaluating fidelity to facts, texts, or commitments. In its legal sense (trustee), it belongs to Group 7.

**אֱמוּנָה / ʾemunah:** Participates in Group 4 (trust/reliability) in its interpersonal sense, and in Group 7 (religious/traditional discourse) in its religious sense. The expression אֱמוּנָה תְּמִימָה can be used approvingly (sincere faith) or critically (blind credulity), placing it at the boundary between Groups 4 and 5.

**צֶדֶק / tsedeq:** Participates in Group 6 (fairness in social interaction) and Group 8 (moral righteousness and justice). The expression צֶדֶק חֶבְרָתִי is primarily political and belongs to Group 8; the expression מִשְׁפַּט צֶדֶק is primarily legal and belongs to Group 7/8.

**עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר / ʿedut sheqer and עֵד שֶׁקֶר / ʿed sheqer:** Participate in Group 5 (truth and falsehood as values, especially in biblical-moral discourse) and Group 7 (legal validity and formal status of testimony).

**חֲצִי אֱמֶת / ḥatsi ʾemet:** Participates in Group 5 (truth as a value) and Group 12 (misleading conduct), since a half-truth is a form of misleading by selective disclosure.

**הָגוּן / hagun:** Primarily in Group 6 (fairness/decency), but when applied to persons (אָדָם הָגוּן) it overlaps with Group 2 (personal honesty and moral character).

**כָּשֵׁר / kasher and פָּסוּל / pasul:** Primarily in Group 7 (legal/formal validity), but in their broader rabbinic usage they also function as general evaluations of fitness and propriety that overlap with Group 6.

---

## Items that do not fit cleanly into any group

**זָהֶה / zehe ("identical, the same") and זְהוּת / zahut ("identity, sameness"):** These evaluate identity and sameness rather than truth, correctness, genuineness, or any of the other dimensions covered by the groups above. Their usage in logical and legal contexts (identity of persons, sameness of objects) does not align clearly with any of the groups identified. They are noted here without assignment.

**דּוֹמֶה / dome ("similar, resembling") and דִּמְיוֹן / dimyon ("resemblance; imagination"):** These evaluate resemblance rather than truth or genuineness. The resemblance dimension is distinct from the genuineness dimension (Group 9) and from the correctness dimension (Group 1). The dual sense of dimyon (resemblance and imagination) further complicates assignment. These items are noted without assignment.

**תִּיקּוּן עוֹלָם / tiqqun ʿolam ("repair of the world"):** This expression is used in political and social justice discourse and overlaps with Group 8, but its primary semantic content concerns repair and rectification rather than evaluation of justice or righteousness per se. It is noted as a borderline item.

**יִישַׁר כֹּחַ / yishar koaḥ ("well done, congratulations"):** This is a conventional congratulatory expression derived from the root י-ש-ר but no longer functioning as an evaluation of uprightness or honesty in current usage. It does not fit any of the groups identified and is noted without assignment.

**אָמֵן / ʾamen:** Functions as a liturgical affirmation particle and colloquial agreement marker. Its evaluative function (affirming the truth of a statement) is present but highly conventionalized and context-restricted. It does not fit cleanly into any group.

**אָמָן / ʾomman ("craftsman, artist") and אָמָנוּת / omanut ("art"):** These derive from the root א-מ-נ but their usage is entirely in the domain of aesthetic production and skilled craft. No truth-evaluation function is present in current usage. They are noted without assignment.
