# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Tamil (ta)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X + copula (zero or இருக்கு irukku)** for equational predication of truth-domain properties. Tamil typically uses zero copula in present tense with third person subjects, or explicit இருக்கு (irukku) "be/exist" for emphasis or non-present contexts.

### Examples
- அது உண்மை (atu uṇmai) → "that truth" → "That is true/the truth"
- இது பொய் (itu poy) → "this lie" → "This is false/a lie"

### Notes
Zero copula is standard in present tense affirmative statements. The construction is extremely frequent in everyday evaluative speech. Negative uses இல்லை (illai): அது உண்மை இல்லை (atu uṇmai illai) "that is not true."

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X + TERM + இல்லை (illai)** where இல்லை is the negative existential/copular verb. This is the standard negation strategy for equational sentences.

### Examples
- அது சரி இல்லை (atu cari illai) → "that correct not" → "That is not correct"
- இது நியாயம் இல்லை (itu niyāyam illai) → "this justice not" → "This is not fair/just"

### Notes
இல்லை (illai) functions as both negative copula and negative existential. Extremely common in everyday speech for denial or correction. Can also use negative adjectival forms: உண்மையற்ற (uṇmaiyaṟṟa) "without truth, untrue."

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆன (āṉa)** produces adjectival forms that modify following nouns. The suffix -ஆன (-āṉa) is the standard adjectival participle, highly productive across the truth-domain vocabulary.

### Examples
- உண்மையான கதை (uṇmaiyāṉa katai) → "true story" → "a true story"
- பொய்யான சாட்சி (poyyāṉa cāṭci) → "false witness" → "false testimony"

### Notes
This is the most productive adjectival derivation pattern in Tamil. Nearly all truth-domain nominals take -ஆன. Alternative: bare noun + noun compounds without derivational morphology (உண்மை கதை uṇmai katai "true story").

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆகு (āku)** "become" expresses change of state into truth-domain properties. ஆகு is the standard inchoative/resultative verb in Tamil.

### Examples
- பொய் அம்பலமாகு (poy ampalamāku) → "lie public-become" → "The lie is exposed" (lit. "becomes public")
- உண்மை தெரிய வரு (uṇmai teriya varu) → "truth known come" → "The truth comes to be known"

### Notes
ஆகு (āku) "become" is highly productive with truth-domain terms. Alternative construction uses வரு (varu) "come" for emergence: உண்மை வெளிச்சத்திற்கு வரு (uṇmai veḷiccattiṟku varu) "truth comes to light."

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**TERM + accusative -ஐ (-ai) + VERB-causative** where causative is formed with -உத்து (-uttu) suffix on verb stems. Used for causing truth to be revealed, made clear, etc.

### Examples
- உண்மையை வெளிப்படுத்து (uṇmaiyai veḷippaṭuttu) → "truth-ACC reveal-CAUS" → "reveal the truth, cause truth to be revealed"
- தெளிவுபடுத்து (teḷivupaṭuttu) → "clarity-become-CAUS" → "make clear, clarify"

### Notes
Causative formation is productive with verbs of revelation and clarification. The pattern NOUN + படுத்து (paṭuttu) "cause to become" is particularly common: தெளிவுபடுத்து (teḷivupaṭuttu) "clarify," உறுதிப்படுத்து (uṟutipaṭuttu) "confirm."

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + மை (-mai)** or **ROOT + தன்மை (-taṉmai)** produce abstract nouns denoting qualities or properties. -மை is classical Tamil; -தன்மை is more formal/analytical.

### Examples
- பொய்மை (poymai) → "lie-ABSTR" → "falsehood, falsity" (classical)
- உண்மைத்தன்மை (uṇmaittaṉmai) → "truth-quality" → "truthfulness, veracity"

### Notes
-மை (-mai) is productive in classical/literary Tamil but less so in modern colloquial. -தன்மை (-taṉmai) is preferred in formal modern Tamil for abstract quality nouns. Both patterns create philosophical/analytical terms.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**பொய் (poy) + person marker** produces agent nouns for "liar." Person markers include -அன் (-aṉ) masculine, -ஆள் (-āḷ) neutral/feminine, -அர் (-ar) plural/honorific. This pattern is **restricted** to பொய் in the truth domain.

### Examples
- பொய்யன் (poyyan) → "lie-person.MASC" → "liar (male)"
- பொய்யாள் (poyyāḷ) → "lie-person" → "liar (person)"

### Notes
Agentive derivation is **not productive** across truth-domain vocabulary. Only பொய் (poy) "lie" readily forms agent nouns. உண்மை (uṇmai) "truth" does not form *உண்மையன் for "truth-teller" — instead, periphrastic expressions are used: உண்மை பேசுபவர் (uṇmai pēcupavar) "truth-speaking person."

---

## 8. VERB + OBJECT COLLOCATIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + accusative marker + VERB** where specific verbs collocate with truth-domain nouns. Common verbs: சொல்லு (collu) "say," பேசு (pēcu) "speak," மறை (maṟai) "hide," தேடு (tēṭu) "seek."

### Examples
- உண்மையைச் சொல்லு (uṇmaiyaic collu) → "truth-ACC say" → "tell the truth"
- பொய் சொல்லு (poy collu) → "lie say" → "tell lies" (no accusative marker)

### Notes
Asymmetry: உண்மை takes accusative -ஐ (-ai), but பொய் typically appears without case marking in this construction. Both collocate with சொல்லு (collu) and பேசு (pēcu). This is the most common construction for truth-telling/lying actions.

---

## 9. NOUN + NOUN COMPOUNDS

### Pattern
**TERM + NOUN** forms compounds without overt derivational morphology. Highly productive, especially with பொய் (poy) and மெய் (mey), creating specialized domain terms.

### Examples
- பொய் சாட்சி (poy cāṭci) → "false witness" → "false testimony, perjury"
- மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் (meyññāṉam) → "truth-knowledge" → "true knowledge, enlightenment"

### Notes
Extremely productive pattern. பொய் forms legal/commercial compounds (பொய் வழக்கு poy vaḻakku "false case"). மெய் forms philosophical/spiritual compounds (மெய் பக்தி mey pakti "true devotion"). உண்மை forms general descriptive compounds (உண்மை கதை uṇmai katai "true story").

---

## 10. INTERROGATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆ (-ā)** where -ஆ is the interrogative particle, forming yes/no questions. Extremely common in conversational Tamil for confirmation requests.

### Examples
- உண்மையா? (uṇmaiyā?) → "truth-Q" → "Is it true? Really?"
- நிஜமா? (nijamā?) → "truth-Q" → "Really? Is that so?"

### Notes
This is one of the most frequent constructions in everyday speech. நிஜமா? (nijamā?) may be even more common colloquially than உண்மையா?. Also used with சரி: சரியா? (cariyā?) "Is it correct? Okay?"

---

## 11. ADVERBIAL MANNER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆக (-āka)** produces manner adverbs meaning "in a X manner, as X." Highly productive across truth-domain vocabulary.

### Examples
- உண்மையாக (uṇmaiyāka) → "truth-as" → "truly, truthfully"
- தெளிவாக (teḷivāka) → "clarity-as" → "clearly"

### Notes
Parallel productivity to -ஆன (-āṉa) adjectival. Nearly all truth-domain nominals take -ஆக for adverbial derivation. Can be further intensified with emphatic -ஏ: உண்மையாகவே (uṇmaiyākavē) "truly indeed."

---

## 12. LOCATIVE/ABSTRACT STATE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + இல் (-il)** locative case marker functions derivationally to create adverbial/discourse markers, particularly with உண்மை and பொய்.

### Examples
- உண்மையில் (uṇmaiyil) → "truth-in" → "in truth, actually, in reality"
- பொய்யில் (poyyil) → "lie-in" → "in falsehood" (less common, literary)

### Notes
உண்மையில் is extremely common as discourse marker and adverbial emphasizer in everyday speech. Functions both as "actually" (epistemic) and "in reality" (ontological). Less productive than -ஆக pattern.

---

## 13. NEGATIVE ADJECTIVAL DERIVATION

### Pattern
**TERM + அற்ற (-aṟṟa)** "without, lacking" produces negative adjectives. Productive pattern for creating antonyms or expressing absence of quality.

### Examples
- உண்மையற்ற (uṇmaiyaṟṟa) → "truth-without" → "without truth, untrue, false"
- சந்தேகமற்ற (cantēkamaṟṟa) → "doubt-without" → "without doubt, certain"

### Notes
Productive negative adjectival pattern. Alternative to using பொய்யான (poyyāṉa) "false" for negation. Particularly common with abstract nouns: நம்பிக்கையற்ற (nampikkaiyaṟṟa) "faithless," தெளிவற்ற (teḷivaṟṟa) "unclear."

---

## 14. EMPHATIC/INTENSIFYING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + locative/adverbial + emphatic particles** where multiple particles stack for emphasis. Common pattern: -இல் + -ஏ + -ய் + -ஏ (-il-ē-y-ē).

### Examples
- உண்மையிலேயே (uṇmaiyi-l-ē-y-ē) → "truth-in-EMPH-EUPH-EMPH" → "truly indeed, really and truly"
- நிஜமாகவே (nijamāka-v-ē) → "truth-as-EUPH-EMPH" → "really indeed"

### Notes
Characteristic Tamil emphatic construction with particle stacking. -ஏ (-ē) is primary emphatic particle; -ய்/-வ் are euphonic connectors. Very common in colloquial speech for strong emphasis or insistence.

---

## 15. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + accusative + விட (viṭa)** "than, compared to" forms comparative constructions, though less common with truth-domain terms than with gradable adjectives.

### Examples
- உண்மையை விட (uṇmaiyai viṭa) → "truth-ACC than" → "more than truth, beyond truth"
- பொய்யை விட (poyyai viṭa) → "lie-ACC than" → "more than a lie, worse than a lie"

### Notes
Less productive with truth-domain vocabulary than with scalar adjectives. Used primarily in emphatic or hyperbolic contexts. Truth-domain terms are generally treated as categorical rather than gradable.

---

## 16. CONDITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆக இருந்தால் (-āka iruntāl)** "if it is X" forms conditional protases. Standard conditional pattern in Tamil.

### Examples
- உண்மையாக இருந்தால் (uṇmaiyāka iruntāl) → "truth-as be-if" → "if (it is) true"
- சரியாக இருந்தால் (cariyāka iruntāl) → "correct-as be-if" → "if (it is) correct"

### Notes
Standard conditional formation. இருந்தால் (iruntāl) is conditional form of இருக்கு (irukku) "be." Common in reasoning and argumentation contexts. Can be shortened colloquially: உண்மைன்னா (uṇmaiṉṉā) "if true."

---

## 17. MODAL NECESSITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆக இருக்க வேண்டும் (-āka irukka vēṇṭum)** "must be X" expresses deontic or epistemic necessity. வேண்டும் (vēṇṭum) is the modal auxiliary "must, should."

### Examples
- உண்மையாக இருக்க வேண்டும் (uṇmaiyāka irukka vēṇṭum) → "truth-as be-INF must" → "must be true, should be true"
- சரியாக இருக்க வேண்டும் (cariyāka irukka vēṇṭum) → "correct-as be-INF must" → "must be correct"

### Notes
Standard modal construction for necessity/obligation. வேண்டும் (vēṇṭum) expresses both deontic ("should") and epistemic ("must") modality depending on context. Common in instructions and requirements.

---

## 18. MODAL POSSIBILITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + ஆக இருக்கலாம் (-āka irukkalām)** "may be X" expresses epistemic possibility. -லாம் (-lām) is the possibility modal suffix.

### Examples
- உண்மையாக இருக்கலாம் (uṇmaiyāka irukkalām) → "truth-as be-may" → "may be true, might be true"
- சாத்தியம் இல்லை (cāttiyam illai) → "possibility not" → "(it is) not possible, impossible"

### Notes
-லாம் (-lām) is the standard possibility modal. For impossibility, negative existential with சாத்தியம் (cāttiyam) "possibility" is extremely common: சாத்தியம் இல்லை "not possible."

---

## 19. EVIDENTIAL/APPEARANCE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + என்று தெரிகிறது (eṉṟu terikiṟatu)** "it appears/seems that X" expresses evidential inference. தெரிகிறது (terikiṟatu) "appears, is visible" is the standard evidential verb.

### Examples
- உண்மை என்று தெரிகிறது (uṇmai eṉṟu terikiṟatu) → "truth COMP appears" → "it appears to be true"
- தெளிவாக தெரியவில்லை (teḷivāka teriyavillai) → "clearly appear-NEG" → "(it) doesn't appear clearly, (it's) not clear"

### Notes
என்று (eṉṟu) is the quotative/complementizer. தெரி (teri) "appear, be visible, seem" is the primary evidential verb. Negative: தெரியவில்லை (teriyavillai) "doesn't appear, isn't clear."

---

## 20. CONTRASTIVE DISJUNCTION

### Pattern
**TERM₁ + ஆ (-ā) + TERM₂ + ஆ (-ā)** forms "X or Y?" questions presenting alternatives. Both terms take interrogative particle.

### Examples
- உண்மையா பொய்யா (uṇmaiyā poyyā) → "truth-Q lie-Q" → "true or false? truth or lie?"
- சரியா தப்பா (cariyā tappā) → "correct-Q wrong-Q" → "right or wrong?"

### Notes
Common construction for presenting binary alternatives in questions. Both elements take -ஆ interrogative particle. Extremely frequent in everyday evaluative speech and decision-making contexts.

---

## 21. RESULTATIVE VERB CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + VERB-resultative** where verbs of emergence/revelation combine with truth-domain nouns. Common verbs: வெளிப்படு (veḷippaṭu) "emerge," தெரிய வரு (teriya varu) "come to be known."

### Examples
- உண்மை வெளிப்படு (uṇmai veḷippaṭu) → "truth emerge" → "truth comes out, is revealed"
- பொய் வெளியாகு (poy veḷiyāku) → "lie become-exposed" → "lie becomes exposed"

### Notes
Resultative constructions emphasize change of state or emergence. வெளிப்படு (veḷippaṭu) "emerge, be revealed" is particularly common with உண்மை. These are intransitive; causative counterparts use -உத்து (-uttu): வெளிப்படுத்து (veḷippaṭuttu) "reveal."

---

## 22. IDIOMATIC METAPHORICAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + metaphorical noun + VERB** where truth/lies are conceptualized through spatial or physical metaphors. Common: "truth comes to light," "lie is caught."

### Examples
- உண்மை வெளிச்சத்திற்கு வரு (uṇmai veḷiccattiṟku varu) → "truth light-to come" → "truth comes to light"
- பொய் பிடிபடு (poy piṭipaṭu) → "lie catch-become" → "lie is caught, discovered"

### Notes
These are semi-fixed idiomatic expressions. வெளிச்சம் (veḷiccam) "light" metaphor for revelation is common. பிடிபடு (piṭipaṭu) "be caught" treats lies as physical objects that can be captured. Frequent in narrative contexts.

---

## 23. IMPERATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TERM + accusative + VERB-imperative** for commands related to truth-telling. Imperative is bare verb stem in Tamil.

### Examples
- உண்மையைச் சொல்லு (uṇmaiyaic collu) → "truth-ACC say-IMP" → "Tell the truth!"
- சரி பார் (cari pār) → "correct see-IMP" → "Check (it)! Verify!"

### Notes
Imperative is unmarked (bare stem). Common in interrogation contexts (உண்மையைச் சொல்லு "tell the truth!") and instructions (சரி பார் "check it"). Polite imperatives add -உங்கள் (-uṅkaḷ) or use modal வேண்டும் (vēṇṭum).

---

## 24. DEGREE/EXTENT MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**Degree modifier + TERM** where modifiers like முழு (muḻu) "whole, complete" or பகுதி (pakuti) "partial" modify truth-domain nouns.

### Examples
- முழு உண்மை (muḻu uṇmai) → "whole truth" → "the whole truth, complete truth"
- பகுதி உண்மை (pakuti uṇmai) → "partial truth" → "partial truth, part of the truth"

### Notes
Limited productivity. முழு (muḻu) "whole" and பகுதி (pakuti) "partial" are the main degree modifiers attested with truth-domain terms. Truth is generally treated as categorical rather than gradable, limiting degree modification.

---

## 25. ABSTRACT QUALITY NOMINALIZATION WITH -இன்மை

### Pattern
**TERM + இன்மை (-iṉmai)** "absence, non-existence" creates abstract nouns denoting absence of quality. Formal/literary pattern.

### Examples
- உண்மையின்மை (uṇmaiyiṉmai) → "truth-absence" → "absence of truth, untruth"
- (no common second example; pattern has limited attestation)

### Notes
Less productive than -அற்ற (-aṟṟa) negative adjectival pattern. இன்மை (iṉmai) "absence, non-existence" is formal/literary. More common to use negative adjectives or simple negation than this abstract nominalization.

---
