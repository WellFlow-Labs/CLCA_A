# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## GROUP 1: Truth-Telling and Lying as Speech Acts

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that mark the act of speaking truthfully or falsely. These terms appear in constructions with verbs of saying (esan, mintzatu) and focus on the communicative act itself rather than the content's properties. They are used when evaluating whether someone is speaking honestly or dishonestly.

**Representative expressions:**
- egia esan (to tell the truth)
- gezurra esan (to tell a lie)
- egia (truth - as object of speech verbs)
- gezurra (lie - as object of speech verbs)
- gezurti/gezurtia (liar - agent of lying speech)
- zintzo/zintzoa (honest - characterizing truthful speakers)
- zintzoki (honestly - manner of speaking)

**Typical contexts:**
- Discourse markers: "egia esan" introducing candid statements
- Moral evaluation of speakers: "gezurtia da" (he/she is a liar)
- Manner adverbials: "zintzoki mintzatu" (to speak honestly)

## GROUP 2: Verification and Authentication Processes

**Definition:** This group contains expressions focused on establishing, confirming, or proving truth through investigation or formal procedures. These terms appear in administrative, legal, and investigative contexts where truth must be actively established rather than simply stated. They involve processes and agents of verification.

**Representative expressions:**
- egiazkotu (to verify, authenticate)
- egiaztatu (to verify, prove)
- egiaztatze (verification - process)
- egiaztapen (verification - result)
- egiaztatzailea (verifier - agent)
- ziurtatu (to make certain, ensure)
- frogatu (to prove, test)
- froga egin (to make proof)
- egiaztagiri (certificate - verification document)
- ziurtagiri (certificate)

**Typical contexts:**
- Administrative procedures: document authentication
- Legal proceedings: establishing facts
- Scientific contexts: testing and demonstration
- Quality control: verification processes

---

## GROUP 3: Genuine vs. Counterfeit Distinction

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that distinguish authentic objects, documents, or products from false or counterfeit ones. These terms appear primarily in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts where the authenticity of physical objects or official documents is at issue, rather than the truth of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- egiazko dokumentua (authentic document)
- gezurrezko dokumentua (false document)
- egiazko produktua (genuine product)
- gezurrezko produktua (counterfeit product)
- egiazko sinadura (authentic signature)
- gezurrezko sinadura (forged signature)
- faltsua (false, counterfeit)
- faltsutu (to falsify, forge)
- iruzurra (fraud)
- gezurrezko dirua (counterfeit money)

**Typical contexts:**
- Commercial transactions: distinguishing genuine from fake goods
- Legal proceedings: forgery and fraud cases
- Document authentication: verifying official papers
- Consumer protection: identifying counterfeits

## GROUP 4: Correctness and Error Evaluation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether statements, answers, actions, or procedures are correct or incorrect. These terms appear in educational, technical, and procedural contexts where accuracy and proper execution are assessed. They focus on conformity to standards or rules rather than moral truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- zuzena (correct, right)
- okerra (wrong, incorrect)
- zuzendu (to correct)
- zuzenketa (correction)
- zuzentzailea (corrector)
- zuzen egin (to do correctly)
- zuzen erantzun (correct answer)
- okertu (to go wrong)
- zuzen ulertu (to understand correctly)
- zuzen aplikatu (to apply correctly)

**Typical contexts:**
- Educational settings: grading answers
- Technical procedures: following instructions
- Editorial work: correcting texts
- Problem-solving: identifying and fixing errors

## GROUP 5: Appearance vs. Reality Distinction

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that mark the contrast between how things appear or seem and how they actually are. These terms are used when surface appearances may be misleading or when distinguishing superficial from underlying truth. They often function as epistemic hedges or qualifications.

**Representative expressions:**
- itxura (appearance)
- itxurazko (apparent, seeming)
- itxuraz (apparently, seemingly)
- benetako (real, genuine)
- benetakoa (the real one)
- benetan (really, truly)
- egiazko izaera (true nature)
- egiazko aurpegia (true face)
- gezurrezko itxura (false appearance)
- itxura eman (to give appearance)

**Typical contexts:**
- Epistemic qualification: "itxuraz" marking claims based on appearance
- Revealing hidden reality: "egiazko izaera" (true nature)
- Distinguishing surface from substance
- Unmasking deception: revealing "egiazko aurpegia"

---

## GROUP 6: Certainty and Doubt Expressions

**Definition:** This group contains expressions marking the speaker's epistemic stance regarding certainty or uncertainty. These terms indicate confidence levels in knowledge or belief rather than evaluating truth itself. They appear in contexts where speakers express or question their degree of certainty.

**Representative expressions:**
- ziurra (certain, sure)
- ziur egon (to be certain)
- ziurtasuna (certainty)
- ziurki (certainly)
- zalantza (doubt)
- zalantza izan (to have doubt)
- zalantzagarria (doubtful, questionable)
- zalantzan jarri (to put in doubt)
- zalantzarik gabe (without doubt)
- ziur asko (very sure)

**Typical contexts:**
- Expressing confidence: "ziur nago" (I am certain)
- Expressing uncertainty: "zalantza izan" (to have doubts)
- Questioning reliability: "zalantzagarria da" (it is questionable)
- Emphatic assertions: "zalantzarik gabe" (undoubtedly)

---

## GROUP 7: Reliability and Trustworthiness Evaluation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions evaluating whether persons, sources, or information are worthy of trust or belief. These terms assess credibility and dependability rather than truth per se. They appear in contexts where the reliability of sources or the plausibility of claims is being evaluated.

**Representative expressions:**
- fidagarria (reliable, trustworthy)
- sinesgarria (believable, credible)
- fede (faith, trust)
- fede izan (to have faith)
- fede eman (to give credence)
- sinestu (to believe)
- fidatu (to trust)

**Typical contexts:**
- Evaluating sources: "iturburu fidagarria" (reliable source)
- Assessing credibility: "sinesgarria da" (it is believable)
- Trust relationships: "fede izan" (to have faith)
- Plausibility judgments: determining what merits belief

---

## GROUP 8: Direct vs. Mediated Connection

**Definition:** This group contains expressions marking whether connections, relationships, or transmissions are direct and unmediated or involve intermediaries. These terms appear in technical, media, political, and commercial contexts where the presence or absence of intermediation is significant. The root "zuzen" extends from physical straightness to lack of mediation.

**Representative expressions:**
- zuzenean (directly, live)
- zuzeneko (direct, live)
- zuzeneko emisioa (live broadcast)
- zuzeneko konexioa (direct connection)
- zuzeneko demokrazia (direct democracy)
- zuzeneko salmentak (direct sales)
- zuzeneko harremana (direct relationship)
- zuzeneko eragina (direct influence)
- zuzen-zuzenean (very directly)

**Typical contexts:**
- Broadcasting: live vs. recorded transmission
- Political organization: direct vs. representative democracy
- Commerce: direct vs. intermediated sales
- Causal analysis: direct vs. indirect effects
- Telecommunications: unmediated connections

---

## GROUP 9: Moral Honesty and Deception

**Definition:** This group contains expressions evaluating the moral character of persons or actions regarding honesty and deception. These terms focus on sincerity, integrity, and moral uprightness rather than factual accuracy. They appear in contexts of character evaluation and moral judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- zintzo/zintzoa (honest, sincere)
- zintzotasuna (honesty, integrity)
- zintzoki (honestly)
- zintzo ibili (to behave honestly)
- iruzurra (fraud, deceit)
- iruzurtia (fraudulent person)
- engainua (deception)
- engainatu (to deceive)
- gezurrezko modestia (false modesty)
- gezurrezko adiskidea (false friend)

**Typical contexts:**
- Character evaluation: "zintzo da" (he/she is honest)
- Moral condemnation: "iruzurtia da" (he/she is a fraudster)
- Evaluating sincerity: distinguishing genuine from feigned behavior
- Interpersonal trust: identifying false friends

## GROUP 10: Legal and Juridical Truth

**Definition:** This group contains expressions specific to legal and judicial contexts where truth is established through formal procedures, testimony, and evidence. These terms appear in courtroom settings, legal documents, and discussions of justice and law. They often involve specialized legal meanings.

**Representative expressions:**
- gezurrezko lekukotza (false testimony, perjury)
- gezurrezko salaketa (false accusation)
- gezurrezko aitorpena (false confession)
- egiazko lekukoa (true witness)
- zuzenbide (law, jurisprudence)
- zuzenbidea (the law)
- zuzenbide zibila (civil law)
- zuzenbide penala (criminal law)
- zuzentasun juridikoa (legal correctness)
- fede eman (to certify - legal sense)

**Typical contexts:**
- Courtroom proceedings: testimony and evidence
- Legal charges: accusations and confessions
- Legal systems: branches and principles of law
- Judicial evaluation: determining legal truth

---

## GROUP 11: Emphatic Truth Markers and Intensifiers

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that intensify or emphasize the truth, genuineness, or completeness of something. These terms function as intensifiers or emphatic markers rather than making basic truth claims. They appear when speakers want to stress the absolute or unqualified nature of truth or falsehood.

**Representative expressions:**
- egia hutsa (pure truth, nothing but truth)
- gezur hutsa (pure lie, complete lie)
- egia gordina (raw truth, harsh truth)
- egia garbiak (plain truths)
- egia sakona (deep truth)
- gezur-gezurra (complete lie - reduplicated)
- zuzen-zuzen (very straight, exactly)
- benetan (really, truly - emphatic)
- egiatan (in truth - emphatic)
- ziur asko (very sure)

**Typical contexts:**
- Emphatic assertions: stressing absolute truth
- Dismissive statements: "gezur hutsa!" (complete lie!)
- Philosophical discourse: "egia sakona" (deep truth)
- Intensifying correctness: "zuzen-zuzen" (exactly right)

---

## GROUP 12: Authentic vs. False Identity and Nature

**Definition:** This group contains expressions distinguishing the true or essential nature, identity, or character of persons and things from false or assumed ones. These terms appear when discussing what something or someone fundamentally is, as opposed to what they appear to be or claim to be. They focus on essential identity rather than factual statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- egiazko izaera (true nature)
- egiazko identitatea (true identity)
- gezurrezko identitatea (false identity)
- egiazko aurpegia (true face)
- egiazko jatorria (true origin)
- benetako (real, genuine - identity sense)
- egiazko artista (true artist)
- gezurrezko profeta (false prophet)
- egiazko fedea (true faith)

**Typical contexts:**
- Revealing character: "egiazko izaera" (true nature)
- Identity verification: true vs. false identity
- Authenticity judgments: genuine vs. pretender artists
- Religious discourse: true vs. false faith/prophets

---

## GROUP 13: Actual vs. Stated Reasons and Intentions

**Definition:** This group contains expressions distinguishing actual motivations, reasons, and purposes from stated or apparent ones. These terms appear in explanatory and analytical contexts where speakers identify hidden or underlying causes that differ from surface explanations. They focus on uncovering real motivations.

**Representative expressions:**
- egiazko arrazoia (true reason, real reason)
- egiazko asmoa (true intention)
- egiazko helburua (true goal)
- gezurrezko aitzakia (false excuse)
- gezurrezko promesa (false promise)

**Typical contexts:**
- Explanatory discourse: identifying actual causes
- Strategic analysis: uncovering real objectives
- Moral evaluation: distinguishing genuine from stated intentions
- Political criticism: exposing false promises

---

## GROUP 14: Factual vs. Fictional Narratives

**Definition:** This group contains expressions marking whether stories, accounts, or events are factual or fictional. These terms appear in narrative, journalistic, and historical contexts where the factual status of accounts must be indicated. They distinguish what actually happened from what is imagined or fabricated.

**Representative expressions:**
- egiazko historia (true story)
- egiazko gertaera (real event)
- gezurrezko albistea (false news)
- gezurrezko informazioa (false information)
- egiazko istorioa (true story)

**Typical contexts:**
- Narrative framing: marking stories as factual
- Journalism: distinguishing news from misinformation
- Historical discourse: actual vs. legendary events
- Media criticism: identifying fake news

---

## GROUP 15: Straightness and Physical Alignment

**Definition:** This group contains expressions describing physical straightness, alignment, or posture. These terms use the root "zuzen" in its literal spatial sense rather than its extended epistemic or moral senses. They appear in contexts of physical description, instruction, and adjustment.

**Representative expressions:**
- zuzen jarri (to straighten physically)
- zuzen egon (to stand/sit straight)
- lerro zuzena (straight line)
- zuzen-lerro (straight line)
- zuzen-angelua (right angle)
- zuzen-bidea (straight path - literal sense)

**Typical contexts:**
- Posture instructions: "zuzen egon" (sit up straight)
- Geometric descriptions: straight lines and right angles
- Physical adjustments: straightening objects
- Spatial navigation: direct routes

## AMBIGUOUS OR OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items belonging to multiple groups:

**zuzen/zuzena:**
- Belongs to GROUP 4 (Correctness) in epistemic/procedural contexts
- Belongs to GROUP 15 (Straightness) in physical/spatial contexts
- Belongs to GROUP 8 (Direct Connection) when extended to "zuzeneko"
- The polysemy is fundamental to the term's usage

**egiazko:**
- Belongs to GROUP 3 (Genuine vs. Counterfeit) with physical objects
- Belongs to GROUP 12 (Identity and Nature) with persons and character
- Belongs to GROUP 13 (Reasons and Intentions) with motivations
- Belongs to GROUP 14 (Factual Narratives) with stories
- The term is highly productive across multiple domains

**benetan/benetako:**
- Belongs to GROUP 5 (Appearance vs. Reality) as reality marker
- Belongs to GROUP 11 (Emphatic Markers) as intensifier
- Functions both substantively and as emphasis

**fede:**
- Belongs to GROUP 7 (Reliability) in trust contexts
- Belongs to GROUP 12 (Identity) in religious contexts ("egiazko fedea")
- Spans interpersonal trust and religious faith

**gezurrezko:**
- Belongs to GROUP 3 (Counterfeit) with objects
- Belongs to GROUP 9 (Moral Deception) with character
- Belongs to GROUP 10 (Legal) in judicial contexts
- Belongs to GROUP 14 (Fictional) with information
- Parallel productivity to "egiazko" across domains

## ITEMS THAT DO NOT FIT CLEANLY

**antzekoa (similar, resembling):**
- Indicates resemblance rather than truth or falsity
- Does not clearly belong to any identified group
- Functions in comparative rather than evaluative contexts
- May form its own micro-domain of similarity/resemblance

**egokia (appropriate, suitable):**
- Evaluates fitness or appropriateness rather than truth
- Does not clearly belong to any identified group
- Functions in normative evaluation of suitability
- Distinct from truth-evaluation proper

**ageria (evident, obvious):**
- Marks visibility or manifestness of truth
- Overlaps partially with GROUP 5 (Appearance vs. Reality)
- But focuses on evidential clarity rather than appearance/reality contrast
- May represent a distinct evidential category

**erreala/errealitatea (real/reality):**
- Borrowed term with limited integration
- Overlaps with GROUP 5 (Appearance vs. Reality)
- But functions more as philosophical/ontological category
- Less integrated into native semantic patterns

**zuzentasuna (correctness, justice, rectitude):**
- Abstract nominal spanning multiple domains
- Overlaps with GROUP 4 (Correctness) in epistemic sense
- Overlaps with GROUP 10 (Legal) in juridical sense
- Extends to moral rectitude beyond both
- The polysemy makes single group assignment problematic

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Observed asymmetries:

1. **egia** shows much broader productivity across groups than **gezur**
   - egia appears in 8+ groups
   - gezur appears primarily in 3-4 groups
   - This reflects actual usage asymmetry in the language

2. **zuzen** shows unique polysemy bridging physical, epistemic, and moral domains
   - No other root shows this three-way extension
   - The physical-to-abstract extension is highly productive

3. **Legal/juridical contexts** form a distinct specialized domain
   - Terms take on specialized meanings in legal contexts
   - Some terms appear almost exclusively in legal usage

4. **Commercial/authentication contexts** form another specialized domain
   - Genuine vs. counterfeit distinction is highly elaborated
   - Specialized vocabulary for fraud and forgery

5. **Media/broadcasting contexts** show recent semantic extension
   - "zuzeneko" has specialized in live transmission contexts
   - This represents a modern extension of the directness concept

### Groups not formed:

No clear grouping emerged for:
- Temporal aspects of truth (no temporal marking patterns found)
- Degrees or gradations of truth (no scalar patterns beyond intensifiers)
- Sources or origins of truth (no specialized source-marking)
- Consequences of truth/falsehood (no dedicated consequence-marking)

These absences reflect actual gaps in the semantic organization of the domain in Basque, not analytical oversight.
