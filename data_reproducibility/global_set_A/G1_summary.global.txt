# G1 — Cross-Language Summary of Observed Semantic Organization

---

## 1. Language Inventory

- **Georgian (ka)** — Agglutinative South Caucasian language with a rich derivational system; shows extensive morphological productivity around roots for truth (mart'l-, namdvil-, ts'eshmarit-) and falsehood (t'q'uil-).
- **Yoruba (yo)** — Tonal Niger-Congo language; uses a small set of core roots (tọ́, irọ́, bá mu) with nominalization prefixes and serial verb constructions; truth predicates are stative and resist degree modification.
- **Finnish (fi)** — Agglutinative Finno-Ugric language; shows highly productive derivational morphology from tosi- and väärä- roots; extensive compound formation; grammaticalized case forms as adverbs.
- **Basque (eu)** — Language isolate; uses relational suffixes (-zko, -ko), abstract noun suffix (-tasun), and verbalizing infixes (-zta-, -tu) productively; shows reduplication for emphasis.
- **Tamil (ta)** — Dravidian language with two major truth roots (uṇmai, mey) distributed across registers; highly productive -āṉa adjectival and -āka adverbial suffixes; negative adjectival suffix -aṟṟa.
- **Indonesian (id)** — Austronesian language with a rich prefix/suffix system (meN-, di-, ke-...-an, peN-...-an); truth-domain vocabulary includes many Arabic, Sanskrit, and Dutch/English loans; zero copula in present tense.
- **Turkish (tr)** — Agglutinative Turkic language with extensive suffix-based derivation; many Arabic and Persian loans in the truth domain; productive inchoative (-leş-) and causative (-tir-) morphology.
- **Korean (ko)** — Agglutinative language with Sino-Korean and native Korean strata; Sino-Korean negative prefixes (부-, 불-) productive; truth predicates resist simple inchoative and causative formation; agentive nominalization asymmetry.

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### Georgian (ka)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Georgian distinguishes factual correctness (სწორი sts'ori, მართალი mart'ali), authenticity/genuineness (ნამდვილი namdvili, ჭეშმარიტი ch'eshmarit'i), deliberate lying (ტყუილი t'q'uili, მოტყუება mot'q'ueba), sincerity (გულწრფელი gults'rpeli), reliability (სანდო sando), moral justice (სამართლიანი samart'liani), and appearance vs. reality (მოჩვენებითი moch'venebiti, ზედაპირული zedap'iruli). The root mart'l- is notably polysemous, spanning factual correctness, moral rightness, legal justice, and orthographic correctness. ნამდვილი namdvili participates in at least four semantic groups (authenticity, emotional genuineness, reality vs. appearance, fiction vs. fact). ტყუილი t'q'uili has an unusual semantic extension from "lie" to "in vain/futile."

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** The mart'l- root generates an exceptionally large derivational family including legal terms (სამართალი samart'ali "law"), directional terms (მართლწერა mart'lts'era "orthography"), religious terms (მართლმადიდებელი mart'lmadidebeli "Orthodox"), and modal verbs (მართებს mart'ebs "it befits"). The negative prefix უ- (u-) creates privative forms (უტყუარი ut'q'uari "infallible," უსამართლობა usamart'loba "injustice"). The abstract noun suffixes სი-...-ე (si-...-e) and -ობა (-oba) are both productive.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Georgian shows a rich set of evidential particles (თურმე turme, ხომ khom, მეთქი metki) that mark epistemic stance. The contrastive frame არა X, არამედ Y (ara X, aramed Y) is a fixed correction construction. Concessive constructions (მართალია, მაგრამ... mart'alia, magram...) are well-attested. Causative morphology for truth predicates is marginal; lexical verbs (დაამტკიცა daamt'k'itsa "proved") are preferred.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Georgian shows both lexical oppositions (სიმართლე/ტყუილი, ნამდვილი/ყალბი, ბუნებრივი/ხელოვნური) and morphological oppositions via negative prefixes (უ-, არა-). Several constructional oppositions use the same noun modified by contrasting adjectives (სანდო წყარო vs. საეჭვო წყარო). Asymmetries include the absence of a fixed lexical opposite for ნაღდი ფული "cash" and the absence of a symmetric lexicalization for "unconvincing evidence."

### Yoruba (yo)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Yoruba organizes the truth domain around a small set of core roots: tọ́ (to be straight/correct), irọ́/purọ́ (lie/to lie), bá mu (to correspond/match), jọ (to resemble), dájú (to be certain), gbàgbọ́ (to believe), yẹ (to be fitting), and gidi (genuine). The root tọ́ spans factual correctness, moral character, appropriateness, and directional metaphors (ọ̀nà tọ́ "straight path"). Correspondence and matching (bá mu, ìbámu) form a distinct cluster. Genuineness (gidi) lacks a specific lexical antonym; negation is used instead. The domain of investigation and verification is expressed through a large set of ṣe + noun constructions.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** The nominalization prefix ì- is highly productive (ìgbàgbọ́, ìdájú, ìbámu, ìmọ̀). The negative nominalizer àì- creates privative abstract nouns (àìtọ́, àìmọ̀, àìbámu, àìyẹ, àìpé). The agentive prefix a- creates apurọ́ "liar" but does not productively apply to tọ́ (*atọ́ is not attested). "Truthful person" requires the compound ọmọ òtítọ́ (child of truth) rather than a morphological agentive. The gerundive prefix gbí-/pí-/ṣí- creates action nouns.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Yoruba treats truth as a static, categorical property: inchoative constructions ("becoming true") are marginal or reinterpreted as discovery/revelation. Causative constructions for truth states are pragmatically odd (one cannot "make" truth); causing belief (mú kí n gbàgbọ́) is natural, but causing truth (mú kí ó tọ́) is reinterpreted as verification. Serial verb constructions (fi...hàn, bá...mu) are central. The focus construction with ni is highly productive for emphatic truth assertion. Proverbial constructions (Noun + á + Verb + Noun) carry cultural authority.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** The primary opposition is òtítọ́/irọ́ (truth/lie) and tọ́/purọ́ (be correct/lie). Morphological negation with kò is the standard strategy for most oppositions. A notable constructional asymmetry: sọ òtítọ́ (speak truth) uses sọ while pa irọ́ (tell a lie) uses pa (conventionalized), creating an asymmetric verb pairing. The ọmọ òtítọ́/apurọ́ opposition shows agentive asymmetry (compound vs. prefix). Degree modification of truth predicates is resisted (truth is conceptualized as binary).

### Finnish (fi)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Finnish distinguishes factual correspondence (tosi, totuus, paikkansapitävä, todenmukainen), correctness evaluation (oikea, oikeellinen, väärä, virheellinen), authenticity (aito, aitous), deception and falsification (valhe, valehdella, väärentää, vääristää, harhaanjohtava), honesty and sincerity (rehellinen, vilpitön), evidential and proof terms (todistaa, todiste, todistaja), reliability (luotettava, uskottava), probability (todennäköinen), realization and actualization (toteutua, todellistua, toteuttaa), and real-life versus fiction (tosielämä, tositarina). The tosi- root is exceptionally productive, generating compounds (tosiasia, tosielämä, tosi-tv) and grammaticalized adverbials (totta kai, totta puhuen). The väärä- root spans incorrectness, falsification, forgery, misuse, and physical abnormality.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** Finnish shows highly productive abstract nominalization (-uus/-yys, -suus/-syys), agentive nominalization (-ja/-jä), gerund formation (-minen), result noun formation (-e, -us, -os), and causative/inchoative pairs (todellistaa/todellistua, toteuttaa/toteutua). The privative suffix -tön/-ttömyys is productive (vilpitön "sincere," literally "without deceit"). The negative prefix epä- creates antonyms (epätosi, epärehellinen, epätodennäköinen). Spatial/directional uses of oikea (right-hand) and väärä (physical abnormality) represent semantic divergence from the truth domain.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Finnish shows a notable gap: no simple inchoative for "becoming false/untrue" — the concept is expressed through discovery constructions (osoittautua vääräksi "turn out to be false," paljastua valheeksi "be revealed as a lie"). The fixed inessive constructions olla oikeassa/olla väärässä (to be right/wrong) are grammaticalized spatial metaphors. Translative case constructions (todistettiin vääräksi "was proven false") are productive. The adverb totta kai "of course" is fully grammaticalized. Finnish lacks a simple causative "make genuine" (*aitouttaa not attested).

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Finnish shows both lexical (tosi/väärä, totuus/valhe, aito/väärennös) and morphological (epä- prefix, -tön privative) oppositions. Several asymmetries are noted: väärinkäsitys "misunderstanding" is lexicalized but oikea käsitys "correct understanding" is not; väärinkäyttö "misuse" is lexicalized but oikea käyttö "proper use" is not. The opposition valehdella/puhua totuutta is asymmetric (dedicated verb for lying vs. verb+object construction for truth-telling). Several implied oppositions (tosielämä/fiktio, tositarina/satutarina) lack lexicalized antonyms in the data.

### Basque (eu)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Basque distinguishes truth-stating and verification (egia, egiazkoa, egiaztatu, ziurra), authenticity and genuineness (benetakoa, egiazko maitasuna), falsehood and deception (gezurra, gezurrezko, iruzurra, faltsua), correctness and error (zuzena, okerra, zehatza), reliability and credibility (fidagarria, sinesgarria, zalantzagarria), moral honesty (zintzoa), similarity and identity (berdina, antzekoa), appearance and manifestation (itxurazko, ageria), directness (zuzenean, zuzeneko), and legal/administrative validation (zuzenbide, egiaztagiri, ziurtagiri). The zuzen- root spans physical straightness, factual correctness, moral rightness, legal concepts (zuzenbide "law"), organizational direction (zuzendari "director"), and live broadcasting (zuzenean "live").

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** The -tasun abstract noun suffix is highly productive (egiazkotasuna, zuzentasuna, berdintasuna, zehaztasuna, zintzotasuna). The verbalizing suffixes -tu and -zta- create causative/factitive verbs (egiaztatu, ziurtatu, zuzendu, zehaztu). The -garri suffix creates evaluative adjectives (fidagarria, sinesgarria, zalantzagarria, frogagarria). The -keria suffix creates pejorative collective nouns (gezurkeria, faltsukeria, iruzurkeria). Reduplication is used for emphasis (gezur-gezurra, zuzen-zuzen, berdin-berdin, ziur-ziur).

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Basque uses bihurtu (become) for inchoative constructions, but specialized verbs (egiaztatu, okertu, agertu) are preferred. The locative constructions agerian jarri/egon (expose/be exposed) and zalantzan jarri/egon (call into question/be in doubt) are highly productive parallel patterns. Modal necessity with behar (must) is common. The -garri suffix creates a productive class of evaluative adjectives. Binomial expressions (egia eta gezurra, zuzen eta oker) are fixed with positive term first.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Basque shows lexical (egia/gezurra, zuzena/okerra, zintzoa/faltsua), morphological (egiaztatu/gezurtatu, zuzendu/okertu, zuzentasuna/okertasuna), and constructional (egia esan/gezurra esan, zuzen egin/oker egin, agerian jarri/ezkutatu) oppositions. Moral asymmetry in nominalization: zintzotasuna uses neutral -tasun while faltsukeria uses pejorative -keria. The implied oppositions sinesgarria/sinesgaitza and frogagarria/frogaezina use different suffixes (-garri vs. -gaitz/-ezin) rather than negation.

### Tamil (ta)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Tamil has two major truth roots distributed by register: uṇmai (general, all registers) and mey (literary, classical, philosophical/spiritual). Both contrast with poy (lie/falsehood). Additional distinctions include factual correctness (cari, tappu/tavaṟu), moral rightness (nērmai, niyāyam, aṟam, nīti), authenticity (pōli "fake," acal "original"), reliability (nampakam, nampikkai), possibility (cāttiyam), doubt (cantēkam), certainty (uṟuti), clarity (teḷivu), appropriateness (poruttam), and natural state/essence (iyalpu). The mey root generates an extensive cluster of philosophical and spiritual compounds (meyññāṉam, meyyanpu, mey teyvam, mey pakti) that are absent for uṇmai.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** The -āṉa adjectival suffix and -āka adverbial suffix are universally productive across all truth-domain bases. The negative adjectival suffix -aṟṟa (without) creates privative adjectives (uṇmaiyaṟṟa, nērmaiyaṟṟa, niyāyamaṟṟa, poruttamaṟṟa). The abstract noun suffix -taṉmai (quality/nature) creates formal quality nouns (uṇmaittaṉmai, poyttaṉmai). Agentive nominalization is restricted: poyyan/poyyāḷ/poyyar (liar) exists for poy but no parallel *uṇmaiyaṉ (truth-teller) is attested; truth-tellers require periphrastic expression.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Tamil shows a strong preference for verb+object collocations (uṇmaiyai col, poy col) with an asymmetry: uṇmai takes the accusative marker -ai while poy appears without case marking in the standard collocation. Inchoative constructions are productive (uṇmai āku, poy ampalamāku). Causative constructions use -paṭuttu (teḷivupaṭuttu "clarify," uṟutipaṭuttu "confirm"). Emphatic particle stacking (-il-ē-y-ē) is characteristic of Tamil. Degree modification of truth predicates is marginal (truth treated as categorical). Reciprocal, benefactive, and instrumental constructions with truth-domain terms are absent or unattested.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** The primary lexical opposition is uṇmai/poy and mey/poy. The -aṟṟa suffix creates systematic morphological oppositions (uṇmaiyāṉa/uṇmaiyaṟṟa, nērmaiyāṉa/nērmaiyaṟṟa). Contrastive disjunction constructions (uṇmaiyā poyyā "true or false?", cariyā tappā "right or wrong?") are highly productive. Register stratification creates a three-way opposition: uṇmai (general), mey (literary/classical), nijam (colloquial). The agentive asymmetry (poyyar "liars" vs. no lexicalized "truth-teller") parallels patterns in other languages.

### Indonesian (id)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Indonesian distinguishes factual correctness (benar, betul, salah, keliru), authenticity of objects (asli, palsu, tulen), legal/procedural validity (sah, absah, batal, valid, sahih), existence and reality (nyata, kenyataan, sejati, sungguh, hakikat), deliberate deception (bohong, dusta), honesty (jujur), precision (tepat, akurat, pas), similarity (sama, mirip), appropriateness (cocok, wajar, pantas), and error attribution (salah, kesalahan, menyalahkan). The benar root is exceptionally broad, spanning factual truth, moral rightness, procedural correctness, and general affirmation. The salah root spans incorrectness, moral wrongness, blame attribution, and grammaticalized uses (salah satu "one of").

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** Indonesian shows highly productive prefix/suffix combinations: meN-...-kan (causative transitive), di-...-kan (passive), ke-...-an (abstract nominalization), peN-...-an (action nominalization), ber- (stative/intransitive), ter- (accidental/resultative). The ter- prefix creates evidential/discovery forms (ternyata "it turns out," tersalah "accidentally wrong"). Reduplication creates intensifiers (benar-benar, betul-betul, sungguh-sungguh) and colloquial forms (bohong-bohongan "pretend/fake"). The compound penyalahgunaan "abuse/misuse" shows productive compounding of salah with guna.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Indonesian shows a notable gap: no dedicated construction for "prove false" — this requires circumlocution. Positive character causation ("make someone honest") is also absent as a productive pattern. The evidential ternyata is fully grammaticalized. The discourse markers sebenarnya, sesungguhnya, sebetulnya function as truth-framing adverbials. The hedging construction kurang + adjective (kurang tepat "not quite right") is a productive politeness strategy. Inchoative menjadi constructions are natural for most truth-domain terms.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Indonesian shows lexical (benar/salah, asli/palsu, jujur/bohong, sah/batal), morphological (tidak + adjective as universal negation strategy), and constructional oppositions. Several asymmetries: väärinkäsitys-type compounds (väärinkäsitys, väärinkäyttö) are lexicalized while their positive counterparts are not. sejati and tulen lack clear lexical antonyms. The opposition hakikat vs. (surface appearance) is conceptual rather than lexically encoded.

### Turkish (tr)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Turkish distinguishes factual correctness (doğru, yanlış, hatalı, isabetli), reality and authenticity (gerçek, sahte, asıl, özgün, taklit, doğal, yapay), trustworthiness (güvenilir, sağlam, tutarlı, inandırıcı), honesty and moral character (dürüst, yalancı), deception (yanıltıcı, yalan), doubt and suspicion (şüpheli, kuşkulu), certainty (kesin, şüphesiz), rights, justice, and fairness (hak, haklı, haksız, adil, adalet, meşru), validity and legitimacy (geçerli, meşru), and fundamental/elevated truth (hakikat, hakiki). The hak root (from Arabic ḥaqq) is notably polysemous, spanning rights, justice, truth, and religious reference (Allah hakkı için). The doğru root spans factual truth, spatial directness, and moral rightness.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** Turkish shows highly productive inchoative (-leş-) and causative (-leştir-) pairs (gerçekleşmek/gerçekleştirmek, kesinleşmek/kesinleştirmek, meşrulaşmak/meşrulaştırmak). The abstract nominalizer -luk/-lük is universally productive. The privative suffix -sız/-siz creates antonyms (haksız, şüphesiz, hatasız, güvenilmez). The agentive suffix -cı/-ci creates adherent/practitioner nouns (gerçekçi "realist," yalancı "liar," sahtekâr "fraud"). The Arabic-origin suffix -kâr (sahtekâr) and -iyet (meşruiyet, hakkaniyet) appear in formal/elevated vocabulary.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Turkish shows productive resultative constructions with çıkmak (doğru çıkmak "turn out to be true," yanlış çıkmak "turn out to be wrong," haklı çıkmak "be proven right"). The light verb constructions with etmek, olmak, and söylemek are central. Idiomatic expressions using body/spatial metaphors (gerçek yüz "true face," doğru yol "right path") are well-attested. The hak-based idioms (hak vermek, hak yemek, hakkını vermek) form a rich cluster. Causative morphology for truth predicates is productive (doğrulamak, yanlışlamak, yalanlamak).

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Turkish shows lexical (doğru/yanlış, gerçek/sahte, haklı/haksız, dürüst/yalancı), morphological (privative -sız: şüphesiz, hatasız; negative prefix: güvenilmez), and constructional (doğru çıkmak/yanlış çıkmak, haklı çıkmak/haksız çıkmak) oppositions. The acı gerçek/tatlı yalan (bitter truth/sweet lie) opposition uses taste metaphors. Several items lack clear lexical antonyms (sejati-type: hakikat lacks a simple opposite). The hakkında postposition is fully grammaticalized from the hak root.

### Korean (ko)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):** Korean distinguishes factual correspondence (진실 jinsil, 사실 sasil, 실제 silje), deliberate deception (거짓 geojit, 거짓말 geojinmal, 허위 heowi), authenticity of objects (진짜 jinjja, 가짜 gajja, 진본 jinbon, 모조 mojo), honest character (정직 jeongjik, 솔직 soljik, 진심 jinsim), correctness evaluation (맞다 matda, 틀리다 teullida, 옳다 olda), legitimacy and justification (정당하다 jeongdanghada, 타당하다 tadanghada), credibility (신빙성 sinbingseong, 그럴듯하다 geureoldeuthada), certainty (확실하다 hwaksilhada, 명백하다 myeongbaekhada), and practical reality (실제 silje, 실질 siljil, 실천 silcheon, 실현 silhyeon). The 진 (jin) morpheme appears in truth/genuineness compounds, advancement/progress compounds, medical examination terms, and calming contexts — representing either polysemy or homophony. The 실 (sil) morpheme generates an extensive cluster of practical reality terms.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries (F2a):** Sino-Korean negative prefixes 부- (bu-) and 불- (bul-) are productive for formal negation (부정직, 부정확, 불확실). The abstract quality suffix -성 (-seong) is highly productive with Sino-Korean bases (정확성, 타당성, 신빙성, 확실성). The adverbial suffix -히 (-hi) and the adverbial construction -하게 (-hage) are both productive. Agentive nominalization is asymmetric: 거짓말쟁이 "liar" exists but no parallel "truth-teller" agentive is attested; truth-tellers require periphrastic expression.

**Constructional patterns and absences (F4):** Korean shows a notable resistance to simple inchoative and causative constructions for truth predicates: "becoming true/false" for propositions is marginal, and "making something true" has no productive pattern. Truth emergence is expressed through specialized verbs (드러나다 deureonada "come to light," 밝혀지다 balkhyeojida "be brought to light"). Causation of truth states is expressed periphrastically (-게 하다 construction). The discourse markers 사실은 (sasireun) "actually" and 솔직히 (soljiki) "frankly" are grammaticalized. Evidential marking with 것 같다 (geot gatda) is highly productive.

**Oppositional structures (F5):** Korean shows lexical (진실/거짓, 진짜/가짜, 맞다/틀리다, 옳다/그르다), morphological (부-/불- prefixes, -지 않다 negation), and constructional (진실을 밝히다/진실을 숨기다, 신빙성이 있다/없다) oppositions. The agentive asymmetry (거짓말쟁이 "liar" vs. no lexicalized "truth-teller") is notable. 그르다 "to be wrong" is archaic, surviving mainly in the fixed expression 옳고 그름. Several items (명백하다, 그럴듯하다, 사실상) lack clear lexical antonyms in the data.

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring Convergences (patterns observed across many languages)

- **Lexical separation of deliberate lying from factual error.** All eight languages maintain a distinction between terms for deliberate falsehood/deception (Georgian ტყუილი, Yoruba irọ́/purọ́, Finnish valhe, Basque gezurra, Tamil poy, Indonesian bohong, Turkish yalan, Korean 거짓말) and terms for unintentional error or incorrectness (Georgian მცდარი, Yoruba àṣìṣe, Finnish virheellinen, Basque okerra, Tamil tappu/tavaṟu, Indonesian keliru, Turkish hatalı, Korean 틀리다). The deception terms consistently carry stronger moral valence.

- **Agentive nominalization asymmetry: "liar" is lexicalized; "truth-teller" is not.** Across at least six languages, a dedicated agentive noun for "liar" or "habitual liar" exists (Georgian მატყუარა, Yoruba apurọ́, Finnish valehtelija, Basque gezurlari, Tamil poyyan/poyyar, Indonesian pembohong, Turkish yalancı, Korean 거짓말쟁이), while a parallel agentive for "truth-teller" is either absent or requires periphrastic expression. This asymmetry is observed in Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Tamil, Indonesian, and Korean explicitly; it is implied in Basque and Turkish.

- **Productive abstract nominalization from truth-domain adjectives.** All languages show productive patterns for deriving abstract quality nouns from truth-domain bases: Georgian -ობა/-ება, Yoruba ì- prefix, Finnish -uus/-yys, Basque -tasun, Tamil -taṉmai, Indonesian ke-...-an, Turkish -luk/-lük, Korean -성 (-seong). These nominalizations are used in formal, philosophical, and analytical discourse.

- **Discourse markers derived from truth-domain roots.** In all eight languages, truth-domain items have grammaticalized into discourse markers that introduce corrections, frank statements, or factual clarifications: Georgian მართლაც (mart'lats) "really, indeed," Yoruba nitootọ́ "truly," Finnish sebenarnya/totta puhuen, Basque benetan "truly, really," Tamil uṇmaiyil "in truth, actually," Indonesian sebenarnya "actually, in fact," Turkish gerçekten "really, truly," Korean 사실은 (sasireun) "actually, in fact" and 솔직히 (soljiki) "frankly." This grammaticalization pattern is cross-linguistically robust.

- **Resistance to degree modification of core truth predicates.** Multiple languages show that core truth predicates resist scalar or degree modification. Yoruba explicitly treats tọ́ as binary and non-gradable; Korean data notes that truth predicates resist degree modification; Finnish shows that truth-becoming constructions are conceptualized as discrete events rather than gradual processes; Basque and Tamil show similar patterns. This contrasts with related terms like "credibility" or "similarity," which are freely gradable in all languages.

- **Separation of authenticity/genuineness from factual truth.** All languages maintain a distinction between terms evaluating the truth-value of statements and terms evaluating the genuineness or authenticity of objects, persons, or emotions. Georgian distinguishes ჭეშმარიტი (true statements) from ნამდვილი (genuine things); Yoruba distinguishes tọ́ (correct) from gidi (genuine); Finnish distinguishes tosi (true) from aito (genuine); Basque distinguishes egia (truth) from benetakoa (genuine); Tamil distinguishes uṇmai (truth) from acal (original/genuine); Indonesian distinguishes benar (correct) from asli (authentic); Turkish distinguishes doğru (correct) from gerçek (real/genuine); Korean distinguishes 진실 (truth) from 진짜 (genuine/real).

- **Proverbial or idiomatic expressions asserting the ultimate triumph or difficulty of truth.** All eight languages show fixed or semi-fixed expressions asserting that truth will eventually be revealed or that lies are short-lived: Georgian "ტყუილს მოკლე ფეხები აქვს" (lies have short legs), Yoruba "òtítọ́ á kọjá irọ́" (truth will surpass lies), Finnish "totuus paljastuu lopulta" (truth will be revealed eventually), Basque "egia eta gezurra" binomial, Tamil "uṇmai iṟutiyil vellum" (truth will triumph in the end), Indonesian "ternyata benar" (it turns out to be true), Turkish "acı gerçek" (bitter truth), Korean "진실은 언제나 밝혀진다" (the truth always comes to light). These expressions reflect a shared cultural framing of truth as something that persists or emerges despite concealment.

### Major Divergences (patterns observed in only a few languages or showing strong contrast)

- **Extent of a single root's semantic range.** Georgian mart'l- and Turkish hak- show unusually wide semantic extension: mart'l- covers factual correctness, moral rightness, legal justice, orthography, religious denomination, and modal propriety; hak- covers rights, justice, truth, religious reference, and grammaticalized postposition. By contrast, Yoruba tọ́ and Korean 맞다 (matda) have narrower, more focused ranges. Finnish tosi- and Indonesian benar show intermediate breadth.

- **Presence of a distinct register-stratified truth vocabulary.** Tamil is the most clearly documented case of register stratification within the truth domain: uṇmai (general), mey (literary/classical/spiritual), and nijam (colloquial/borrowed) are distributed across registers with different collocational patterns. Korean shows a Sino-Korean vs. native Korean stratum (진실 vs. 참) but less elaborately. Georgian, Finnish, and Turkish show formal/informal variation but not a three-way register split of this kind.

- **Semantic extension of "lie/false" to "futile/in vain."** Georgian ტყუილი (t'q'uili) has a well-documented extension from "lie" to "in vain, for nothing" (ტყუილად "in vain," ტყუილუბრალოდ "pointlessly"). This semantic extension is not documented in the other seven languages, where "lie" terms remain within the deception domain.

- **Elaboration of a practical reality vs. theory distinction.** Korean shows the most extensive vocabulary for distinguishing actual practice from theory or nominal status, with a large cluster of 실- (sil-) compounds (실제, 실질, 실천, 실행, 실현, 실력, 실적, 실효, 실무, 실전, 실습, 실험, 실시간, etc.). Indonesian shows a similar but less extensive pattern (nyata, kenyataan, faktual). The other languages do not show this degree of lexical elaboration in this specific domain.

- **Elaboration of rights and justice vocabulary within the truth domain.** Turkish shows the most extensive development of hak-based vocabulary (haklı, haksız, hakkaniyet, hakketmek, hak vermek, hak yemek, hakkını almak, hakkını helal etmek, hak sahibi, hak iddia etmek, hak aramak, hak ve adalet, etc.) as part of the broader truth/correctness domain. Georgian also shows significant development of the mart'l-/samart'l- cluster for justice and law. Yoruba òdodo (justice/righteousness) and Korean 정당하다 (legitimacy) participate in this domain but with less elaboration.

- **Resistance to inchoative and causative constructions for truth predicates.** Yoruba and Korean show the strongest documented resistance to inchoative ("become true/false") and causative ("make true/false") constructions for propositional truth. Both languages reinterpret such constructions as discovery/revelation or verification rather than change in truth-value. Finnish shows a partial gap (no common expression for "become untrue"). Georgian, Turkish, and Indonesian show more productive inchoative and causative patterns in this domain.

- **Elaboration of legal and administrative validation vocabulary.** Finnish shows the most extensive cluster of evidential and proof terms (todistaa, todiste, todistaja, todistus, todistusaineisto, todistustaakka, todistuskappale, todistettava, todistettavasti, todentaa, todentaminen). Basque shows a parallel cluster (egiaztagiri, ziurtagiri, egiaztapen, egiaztatze). Georgian and Korean show legal vocabulary but less systematically organized. Yoruba and Tamil show less elaboration in this specific sub-domain.

### Asymmetries, Gaps, and Irregularities

- **Asymmetric lexicalization of negative vs. positive poles.** Across multiple languages, the negative pole (falsehood, error, inauthenticity) is more lexically elaborated or differently structured than the positive pole. Finnish väärinkäsitys "misunderstanding" and väärinkäyttö "misuse" are lexicalized while their positive counterparts are not. Indonesian shows the same pattern. Korean 거짓말쟁이 "liar" is lexicalized while "truth-teller" is not. Yoruba apurọ́ "liar" uses the agentive prefix while ọmọ òtítọ́ "truthful person" requires a compound. This asymmetry is observed in at least six of the eight languages.

- **Morphological homophony or polysemy creating cross-domain extension.** Several languages show a single morpheme spanning the truth domain and unrelated domains. Georgian mart'l- extends to orthography and religious denomination. Turkish hak- extends to rights and religious reference. Korean 진 (jin) appears in truth/genuineness, advancement/progress, medical examination, and calming contexts. Basque zuzen- extends to organizational direction and live broadcasting. Finnish oikea extends to spatial direction (right-hand side). These extensions are language-specific and do not show a common cross-linguistic pattern.

- **Absence of a simple verb meaning "to tell the truth" parallel to "to lie."** In several languages, lying has a dedicated verb while truth-telling requires a verb+object construction. Finnish has valehdella "to lie" but no *totuudella; Yoruba has purọ́ "to lie" but sọ òtítọ́ (speak + truth) for truth-telling; Korean has 거짓말하다 (geojinmalhada) "to lie" but 진실을 말하다 (jinsireul malhada) for truth-telling; Tamil has poy col (lie + say) vs. uṇmaiyai col (truth + say). This asymmetry is observed in at least four languages and implied in others.

- **Variation in how "correspondence" or "matching" is lexicalized.** Yoruba has a dedicated cluster for correspondence and matching (bá mu, ìbámu, jọ, ìjọra) that is more elaborated than in other languages. Georgian has ადეკვატური (adek'vat'uri) and მსგავსი (msgavsi) but these are borrowed or less central. Finnish, Turkish, and Indonesian have terms for similarity (mirip, sama, mirip) but not a dedicated correspondence cluster. This suggests Yoruba has developed a more explicit vocabulary for the correspondence relation between representations and reality.

- **Variation in the treatment of objectivity/subjectivity as a distinct semantic domain.** Georgian explicitly includes ობიექტური (obiek't'uri) and სუბიექტური (subiek't'uri) as a distinct semantic group (Group 8 in F3). These are borrowed terms present in all languages but only Georgian's F3 analysis identifies them as forming a distinct semantic cluster. This may reflect analytical choices in the F3 tables rather than a genuine cross-linguistic difference, but it is the only language where this distinction is explicitly grouped.

- **Variation in the elaboration of "appearance vs. reality" vocabulary.** Georgian, Basque, and Turkish show the most explicit vocabulary for distinguishing surface appearance from underlying reality (Georgian მოჩვენებითი moch'venebiti, ზედაპირული zedap'iruli, ნამდვილი სახე namdvili sakhe; Basque itxurazko, ageria, funtsean; Turkish gerçek yüz, gerçeğe aykırı, gerçekdışı). Korean 진면목 (jinmyeonmok) "true character" and Tamil uṇmai mukam "true face" show parallel metaphors but less systematic elaboration. Yoruba and Indonesian show this distinction but with less dedicated vocabulary.

- **Presence of taste or color metaphors for truth and lies.** Turkish acı gerçek (bitter truth) and tatlı yalan (sweet lie) use taste metaphors. Korean 새빨간 거짓말 (bright red lie) uses a color metaphor. Georgian ტყუილი (t'q'uili) has the proverb "ტყუილს მოკლე ფეხები აქვს" (lies have short legs) using a body metaphor. Yoruba "òtítọ́ korò" (truth is bitter) also uses a taste metaphor. These metaphorical extensions are present in multiple languages but the specific metaphors differ; taste metaphors for truth/lies appear in at least three languages (Turkish, Yoruba, and implied in Korean's white lie calque).
