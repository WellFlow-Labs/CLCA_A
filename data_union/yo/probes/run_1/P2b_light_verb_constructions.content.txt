# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note:** This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. Derivational forms documented in P2a are excluded. Where no common construction is identified for a given item, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are drawn from attested usage in Yoruba oral tradition, legal discourse, religious texts, and documented literary and journalistic sources. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and naturalness.

---

## Constructions by Base Item

---

### Base: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** speak the truth; tell the truth
**Usage Notes:** The most basic and widely attested verbal construction with *òtítọ́*. The verb *sọ* (to say, to speak) combines with *òtítọ́* as its object to produce the standard expression for truth-telling. Appears in everyday speech, legal contexts, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ọ̀nà òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ọ̀nà òtítọ́
**English Translation:** the way of truth; the path of truth
**Usage Notes:** A fixed nominal phrase used in moral and religious discourse to describe the course of conduct characterized by truthfulness and integrity. Appears in proverbs, preaching, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Dúró sí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** dúró sí òtítọ́
**English Translation:** stand by the truth; hold to the truth; remain committed to the truth
**Usage Notes:** A verbal phrase using *dúró* (to stand, to remain) with the directional *sí* (toward/by) and *òtítọ́*. Used in moral and legal contexts to describe steadfast commitment to truthfulness under pressure.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Gbà òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** gbà òtítọ́
**English Translation:** accept the truth; receive the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbà* (to accept, to receive) with *òtítọ́* as object. Describes the act of acknowledging or accepting a true statement, particularly when it is unwelcome or contrary to one's prior belief. Common in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**English Translation:** it is the truth; that is true (emphatic)
**Usage Notes:** A copular-focus construction using the focus marker *ni* to assert emphatically that something is true. The focus marker *ni* follows the predicate nominal to produce an emphatic identificational assertion. Extremely common in everyday and formal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ kò ní kú
**English Translation:** truth will not die; truth is imperishable
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial expression asserting the indestructibility of truth. *Kò ní* is the negative future construction ("will not"); *kú* means "die." Appears in moral discourse and in legal contexts to assert that the truth will eventually emerge.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ló ga
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ló ga
**English Translation:** truth is what is supreme; truth is the highest
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* = *ni* + *o*, focus marker on subject) asserting the supremacy of truth over other values. Appears in proverbs and in moral and legal discourse. The verb *ga* means "to be high, to be supreme."
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀
**Transliteration:** fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀
**English Translation:** speak with truth; speak truthfully
**Usage Notes:** An instrumental construction using *fi* (to use, to employ) + *òtítọ́* + *sọ̀rọ̀* (to speak/talk). Describes the manner of speaking as characterized by truthfulness. Appears in moral instruction and in assessments of testimony.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ẹni tó bá sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó bá sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** whoever speaks the truth; the one who tells the truth
**Usage Notes:** A relative clause construction using *ẹni* (person/one) + *tó bá* (who/whoever, conditional relative) + *sọ òtítọ́*. Used in proverbial and moral discourse to introduce a general statement about truth-tellers. The *bá* element marks the conditional/generic reading.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**English Translation:** truth saves one; truth rescues a person
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction in which *òtítọ́* is the subject of the verb *gbani lọ́wọ́* (to save/rescue a person; *gbà* + *ni* + *lọ́wọ́* "from hand/trouble"). Asserts the protective or salvific function of truth. Appears in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ
**Transliteration:** jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ
**English Translation:** let truth be your guide; allow truth to lead you
**Usage Notes:** A hortative construction using *jẹ́ kí* (let/allow) + *òtítọ́* as subject + *jẹ́* (be/become) + *olùdarí* (guide/leader) + *rẹ* (your). Used in moral instruction and in religious preaching to urge commitment to truth as a guiding principle.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood, untruth)

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** tell a lie; speak a falsehood
**Usage Notes:** The direct parallel to *sọ òtítọ́*; the verb *sọ* (to say/speak) takes *irọ́* as its object. The most basic and widely attested verbal construction with *irọ́*. Appears in everyday speech, legal contexts, and moral condemnation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ò ní gbádùn
**Transliteration:** irọ́ ò ní gbádùn
**English Translation:** falsehood will not prosper; a lie will not succeed
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial expression asserting that lies ultimately fail. *Ò ní* is the negative future; *gbádùn* means "to enjoy, to prosper, to succeed." Appears in moral discourse and in legal contexts to assert the ultimate failure of deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúrú
**Transliteration:** irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúrú
**English Translation:** a lie has short legs; falsehood has short legs
**Usage Notes:** A well-known Yoruba proverb asserting that lies cannot travel far — they are eventually exposed. *Ẹsẹ̀* means "leg/foot"; *kúrú* means "short." Appears in moral instruction and in legal discourse about the eventual exposure of false testimony.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Má sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** má sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** do not tell a lie; do not speak falsehood
**Usage Notes:** The negative imperative construction using *má* (do not) + *sọ irọ́*. The most direct moral injunction against lying. Appears in moral instruction, religious teaching, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ kò ní dúró
**Transliteration:** irọ́ kò ní dúró
**English Translation:** a lie will not stand; falsehood cannot endure
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the impermanence of falsehood. *Dúró* means "to stand, to remain, to endure." Contrasts with the permanence of truth (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú*). Appears in moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Gbọ́ irọ́
**Transliteration:** gbọ́ irọ́
**English Translation:** believe a lie; accept a falsehood; be taken in by a lie
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbọ́* (to hear, to believe, to accept) with *irọ́* as object. Describes the act of being deceived or of accepting a false statement as true. Appears in moral warnings and in assessments of gullibility or deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**English Translation:** whoever tells a lie will bear guilt/sin; one who lies will be guilty
**Usage Notes:** A conditional moral statement linking the act of lying (*sọ irọ́*) to moral culpability (*jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*, "bear sin/guilt"). Uses the conditional relative *tó bá* and the future marker *á*. Appears in moral and religious instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **òdodo** (justice, righteousness, fairness)

---

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Example:** Ṣe òdodo
**Transliteration:** ṣe òdodo
**English Translation:** do justice; act righteously; practice fairness
**Usage Notes:** The verb *ṣe* (to do, to practice) takes *òdodo* as its object. The standard verbal construction for the practice of justice or righteousness. Appears in legal, moral, and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Example:** Ìdájọ́ òdodo
**Transliteration:** ìdájọ́ òdodo
**English Translation:** just judgment; righteous verdict; fair ruling
**Usage Notes:** A nominal phrase combining *ìdájọ́* (judgment) with *òdodo* (justice/righteousness) as a modifier. Documented in P2a (item 53) as a derivational compound; included here because it also functions as a productive syntactic collocation in legal discourse. Appears in assessments of judicial decisions.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Example:** Òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú
**Transliteration:** òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú
**English Translation:** justice must not die; righteousness must not perish
**Usage Notes:** A normative assertion using *kò gbọdọ̀* (must not, prohibition) + *kú* (die). Asserts the imperative preservation of justice. Appears in legal and moral discourse, particularly in contexts of advocacy for fair treatment.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Example:** Wà ní òdodo
**Transliteration:** wà ní òdodo
**English Translation:** be in righteousness; live in justice; exist in rightness
**Usage Notes:** A locative-predicative construction using *wà* (to be, to exist) + *ní* (in/at) + *òdodo*. Describes the state of living or acting within the domain of justice and righteousness. Appears in religious and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **ìmọ̀** (knowledge, understanding, awareness)

---

**Construction 23**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Ní ìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** ní ìmọ̀
**English Translation:** have knowledge; possess understanding; be knowledgeable
**Usage Notes:** The possessive-existential construction *ní* (have/possess) + *ìmọ̀*. The most basic verbal construction for predicting knowledge of a person. Used in assessments of competence and expertise across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Gbà ìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** gbà ìmọ̀
**English Translation:** acquire knowledge; receive knowledge; gain understanding
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbà* (to receive, to acquire) with *ìmọ̀* as object. Describes the process of acquiring knowledge through learning or experience. Common in educational and moral discourse about the value of learning.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Pín ìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** pín ìmọ̀
**English Translation:** share knowledge; distribute understanding
**Usage Notes:** Uses *pín* (to share, to distribute) with *ìmọ̀* as object. Describes the act of transmitting knowledge to others. Appears in educational and community discourse about the social responsibility of the knowledgeable.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là
**English Translation:** it is knowledge that saves/enriches a person; knowledge is what delivers one
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* marks focus on *ìmọ̀*) with the habitual marker *ń* and the verb *gbani là* (to save/deliver/enrich a person). A proverbial assertion of the value of knowledge. Appears in educational and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Kọ́ ìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** kọ́ ìmọ̀
**English Translation:** learn knowledge; study; acquire learning
**Usage Notes:** Uses *kọ́* (to learn, to study) with *ìmọ̀* as object. Describes the deliberate acquisition of knowledge through study. Common in educational discourse and in moral instruction about the importance of learning.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Ìmọ̀ kò ní pé
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ kò ní pé
**English Translation:** knowledge is never complete; learning is never finished
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the inexhaustibility of knowledge. *Pé* here means "to be complete, to be sufficient, to be finished." Appears in educational and philosophical discourse to encourage continued learning.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Example:** Ẹni tó ní ìmọ̀ kò ní sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó ní ìmọ̀ kò ní sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** one who has knowledge will not tell lies; a knowledgeable person does not lie
**Usage Notes:** A complex construction linking *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) to *sọ irọ́* (telling lies) through a negative future assertion. Appears in moral discourse connecting epistemic virtue to moral virtue. The construction uses a relative clause (*tó ní ìmọ̀*) as subject.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

### Base: **àìmọ̀** (ignorance, lack of knowledge)

---

**Construction 30**
**Base Form:** àìmọ̀
**Example:** Nítorí àìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** nítorí àìmọ̀
**English Translation:** because of ignorance; on account of not knowing; due to lack of knowledge
**Usage Notes:** A causal prepositional phrase using *nítorí* (because of, on account of) + *àìmọ̀*. Used in legal and moral contexts to introduce ignorance as a mitigating factor in assessments of culpability. Extremely common in explanatory and exculpatory discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**
**Base Form:** àìmọ̀
**Example:** Àìmọ̀ kò ní gbani là
**Transliteration:** àìmọ̀ kò ní gbani là
**English Translation:** ignorance will not save/enrich a person; ignorance does not deliver one
**Usage Notes:** The negative counterpart of *ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là* (construction 26). Asserts that ignorance provides no benefit or protection. Appears in moral and educational discourse as a warning against complacency.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 32**
**Base Form:** àìmọ̀
**Example:** Jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀
**English Translation:** come out of ignorance; emerge from not-knowing; leave ignorance behind
**Usage Notes:** A directional-motion construction using *jáde kúrò nínú* (come out from within) + *àìmọ̀*. Used in educational and religious discourse to describe the transition from ignorance to knowledge. Appears in preaching and in motivational educational discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **ìgbàgbọ́** (belief, faith, trust, conviction)

---

**Construction 33**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Example:** Ní ìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ní ìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** have faith; hold belief; possess conviction
**Usage Notes:** The possessive-existential construction *ní* + *ìgbàgbọ́*. The standard construction for predicting faith or belief of a person. Used in religious and moral discourse across Christian, Islamic, and traditional Yoruba contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 34**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Example:** Dúró nínú ìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** dúró nínú ìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** stand firm in faith; remain in belief; hold fast to conviction
**Usage Notes:** A locative-motion construction using *dúró* (stand/remain) + *nínú* (in/within) + *ìgbàgbọ́*. Used in religious discourse to urge steadfastness in faith. Common in Christian and Islamic Yoruba preaching.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 35**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ ló gbani là
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ ló gbani là
**English Translation:** your faith is what saves you; it is your belief that delivers you
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* marks focus on *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ*) asserting that faith is the instrument of salvation or deliverance. Appears in Christian and Islamic Yoruba religious discourse, often in the context of healing or spiritual deliverance.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 36**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Example:** Padà sí ìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** padà sí ìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** return to faith; come back to belief
**Usage Notes:** A directional construction using *padà* (return, go back) + *sí* (to/toward) + *ìgbàgbọ́*. Used in religious discourse to describe the act of returning to faith after a period of doubt or apostasy. Common in pastoral and evangelistic contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 37**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú
**English Translation:** faith without works is dead
**Usage Notes:** A nominal predication construction (*jẹ́* = "is/becomes") asserting that *ìgbàgbọ́* (faith) without *ìṣe* (works/action) is *òkú* (dead). A direct Yoruba rendering of a well-known biblical formulation (James 2:17), widely attested in Yoruba Christian discourse and in general moral discourse about the relationship between belief and action.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé** (trust, reliance, dependability)

---

**Construction 38**
**Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Example:** Gbẹ́kẹ̀lé ẹni
**Transliteration:** gbẹ́kẹ̀lé ẹni
**English Translation:** rely on a person; trust someone; depend on one
**Usage Notes:** The verbal base *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* takes a person (*ẹni*) as its object. The most basic verbal construction expressing reliance or trust. Used in interpersonal, commercial, and religious contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 39**
**Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Example:** Ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Transliteration:** ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**English Translation:** a person who can be relied upon; someone trustworthy; a dependable person
**Usage Notes:** A relative clause construction using *ẹni* (person) + *tí a lè* (who one can, relative + modal) + *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (rely on). Used in interpersonal and commercial assessments of trustworthiness. The modal *lè* (can/be able to) marks the possibility of reliance.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 40**
**Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Example:** Ní ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé nínú
**Transliteration:** ní ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé nínú
**English Translation:** have trust in; place reliance in; have confidence in
**Usage Notes:** A possessive-locative construction *ní* (have) + *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* + *nínú* (in/within). Used to describe the placement of trust in a specific person or institution. Appears in interpersonal and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **ẹ̀rí** (evidence, proof, testimony, witness)

---

**Construction 41**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Mú ẹ̀rí wá
**Transliteration:** mú ẹ̀rí wá
**English Translation:** bring evidence; produce proof; present testimony
**Usage Notes:** Uses *mú* (to bring, to take) + *ẹ̀rí* + *wá* (come, directional). The standard legal construction for the requirement to produce evidence. Appears in court proceedings and in everyday demands for proof.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 42**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Ẹ̀rí rẹ kò tó
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí rẹ kò tó
**English Translation:** your evidence is insufficient; your proof does not meet the standard
**Usage Notes:** A predicative construction in which *ẹ̀rí rẹ* (your evidence) is the subject and *kò tó* (does not suffice/reach) is the predicate. *Tó* here means "to be sufficient, to reach a standard." Used in legal and evaluative contexts to reject inadequate proof.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 43**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Ẹ̀rí tó péye wà
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí tó péye wà
**English Translation:** there is sufficient evidence; adequate proof exists
**Usage Notes:** An existential construction using *wà* (exist/there is) with *ẹ̀rí tó péye* (evidence that is sufficient) as subject. Used in legal proceedings to assert that the evidentiary threshold has been met.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 44**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Ẹ̀rí kò sí
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí kò sí
**English Translation:** there is no evidence; proof is absent; no testimony exists
**Usage Notes:** A negative existential construction using *kò sí* (does not exist/there is no) with *ẹ̀rí* as subject. The standard legal expression for the absence of evidence. Appears in court proceedings and in everyday assessments of unsubstantiated claims.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 45**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Gbà ẹ̀rí
**Transliteration:** gbà ẹ̀rí
**English Translation:** accept evidence; receive testimony; admit proof
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbà* (to accept, to receive) with *ẹ̀rí* as object. Describes the act of admitting or accepting evidence in a legal or evaluative proceeding. Appears in court discourse and in everyday assessments of claims.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 46**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Example:** Ẹ̀rí ojú ló gbádùn
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú ló gbádùn
**English Translation:** it is eyewitness evidence that prevails/is accepted; direct evidence is what succeeds
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* marks focus on *ẹ̀rí ojú*) asserting the primacy of eyewitness testimony. *Gbádùn* here carries the sense of "to succeed, to be accepted, to prevail." Appears in legal discourse about the hierarchy of evidence types.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
