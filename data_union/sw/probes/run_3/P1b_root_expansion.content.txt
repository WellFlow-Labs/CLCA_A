# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **kweli**

**Root form:** kweli
**P1 items participating:** 4 (*kweli*, *ukweli*, *kweli kweli*, *haki ya kweli*)
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth, genuine assertion, factual correctness. This root anchors the domain of propositional truth — the evaluation of statements and claims as corresponding to reality. It is the most semantically central root in the Swahili truth-evaluation domain.

---

### Root 2: **halisi / asili** (treated as a cluster: authenticity/genuineness roots)

After review, these two roots are distinct and should be treated separately. Substituting:

### Root 2: **-danganya / udanganyifu** cluster → replaced by the more morphologically productive root:

### Root 2: **sahihi**

**Root form:** sahihi
**P1 items participating:** 3 (*sahihi*, *usahihi*, and participates in *thibitisha/uthibitisho* collocations)
**Core meaning contribution:** Correctness, accuracy, validity. This root evaluates whether something conforms to a standard — factual, procedural, or formal. It is highly productive in administrative, legal, educational, and technical registers.

---

### Root 3: **halisi**

**Root form:** halisi
**P1 items participating:** 3 (*halisi*, *uhalisi*, *uhalisia*)
**Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, authenticity, reality. This root evaluates whether something is what it purports to be — not counterfeit, not imitative, not artificial. It bridges truth-evaluation and ontological/aesthetic judgment.

---

> **Note:** The roots *kweli*, *sahihi*, and *halisi* were selected over *danganya* and *haki* because they are the most morphologically productive and semantically central to the truth-evaluation domain as a whole. *Haki* is also highly productive but its primary semantic weight falls on rights/justice rather than truth per se; it is noted in Step 3 where relevant.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: **kweli**

---

**1.**
- **Form:** ukweli halisi
- **Transliteration:** ukweli halisi
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *kweli* (truth) + *halisi* (genuine/authentic)
- **Natural English translation:** the genuine truth, the real truth
- **Context of use:** Used in assertive and argumentative discourse to emphasize that what is being stated is not merely technically accurate but genuinely and fully true. Common in spoken debate and formal testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** ukweli mzima
- **Transliteration:** ukweli mzima
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *kweli* (truth) + *mzima* (whole, complete, full)
- **Natural English translation:** the whole truth, the full truth
- **Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to demand or assert complete disclosure, parallel to the English legal formula "the whole truth." Appears in courtroom discourse and moral exhortation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** ukweli uchi
- **Transliteration:** ukweli uchi
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *kweli* (truth) + *uchi* (nakedness, bare state)
- **Natural English translation:** the naked truth, the bare truth
- **Context of use:** Used rhetorically to assert that something is the unvarnished, unembellished truth. Appears in journalistic, literary, and argumentative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** kusema ukweli
- **Transliteration:** kusema ukweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *sema* (to speak/say) + *ukweli* (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, to speak the truth
- **Context of use:** The standard verbal phrase for the act of truth-telling, used across moral, legal, religious, and everyday contexts. Appears in oaths, moral instruction, and narrative.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
- **Form:** kuambia ukweli
- **Transliteration:** kuambia ukweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *ambia* (to tell someone) + *ukweli* (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** to tell someone the truth
- **Context of use:** Distinguishes the act of truth-telling directed at a specific person from general truth-speaking. Used in interpersonal, moral, and narrative contexts (*niambie ukweli*, "tell me the truth").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
- **Form:** ukweli wa jambo
- **Transliteration:** ukweli wa jambo
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* (truth) + *wa* (of) + *jambo* (matter, thing, affair)
- **Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter, the facts of the case
- **Context of use:** Used in legal, investigative, and deliberative contexts to refer to the actual facts underlying a situation. Common in formal discourse and journalism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
- **Form:** kwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* (by/with/in, prepositional particle) + *kweli* (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, honestly, indeed
- **Context of use:** An adverbial phrase used to assert sincerity or genuine agreement. Appears across registers from everyday speech to formal writing (*kwa kweli, hii ni tatizo kubwa*, "truly, this is a serious problem").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
- **Form:** kwa kweli kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli kweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* (prepositional particle) + *kweli kweli* (reduplicated truth, intensified)
- **Natural English translation:** really and truly, genuinely, absolutely
- **Context of use:** An intensified form of *kwa kweli*, used in spoken Swahili to emphasize sincerity or the genuine nature of a claim. More emphatic than *kwa kweli*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
- **Form:** ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** ni kweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ni* (copula, "is/are") + *kweli* (truth/true)
- **Natural English translation:** it is true, that is true, indeed
- **Context of use:** The basic copular assertion of truth, used in response to claims or as an affirmation. Extremely frequent in spoken and written Swahili across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
- **Form:** si kweli
- **Transliteration:** si kweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *si* (negative copula, "is not") + *kweli* (truth/true)
- **Natural English translation:** it is not true, that is false, untrue
- **Context of use:** The standard negation of a truth claim, used in denial and refutation across all registers. Contrasts directly with *ni kweli*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
- **Form:** kweli kabisa
- **Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* (truth/true) + *kabisa* (completely, absolutely, entirely)
- **Natural English translation:** absolutely true, completely true, certainly
- **Context of use:** Used to affirm a claim with maximum force. *Kabisa* is a high-frequency intensifier in Swahili; the combination is common in spoken discourse and informal writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
- **Form:** ukweli unauma
- **Transliteration:** ukweli unauma
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* (truth) + *u-na-uma* (it-PRES-hurt, "it hurts")
- **Natural English translation:** the truth hurts
- **Context of use:** A fixed proverbial expression used to acknowledge that honest statements can be painful to hear. Appears in moral discourse, proverbs, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**

- **Transliteration:** tafuta ukweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *tafuta* (seek, search for) + *ukweli* (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** seek the truth, search for the truth
- **Context of use:** Used in investigative, journalistic, philosophical, and religious contexts to describe the pursuit of truth. Appears as an imperative and in descriptive statements.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**

- **Natural English translation:** the truth is known, the truth comes out

- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**

- **Transliteration:** ukweli daima hushinda

- **Natural English translation:** truth always prevails, truth always wins
- **Context of use:** A proverbial expression used in moral and religious discourse to assert the ultimate triumph of truth over falsehood. Appears in sermons, moral instruction, and political rhetoric.

**16.**

- **Transliteration:** mwambie ukweli
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *m-ambie* (tell him/her, object-marked imperative) + *ukweli* (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** tell him/her the truth
- **Context of use:** A directed imperative form of truth-telling, used in interpersonal and narrative contexts. Illustrates the object-marking morphology of Swahili verbs.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
- **Form:** ukweli ni silaha
- **Transliteration:** ukweli ni silaha
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* (truth) + *ni* (is) + *silaha* (weapon)
- **Natural English translation:** truth is a weapon
- **Context of use:** A metaphorical expression used in political, activist, and rhetorical discourse to characterize truth-telling as a means of power or resistance.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**18.**
- **Form:** kweli ya Mungu
- **Transliteration:** kweli ya Mungu
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* (truth) + *ya* (of) + *Mungu* (God)
- **Natural English translation:** God's truth, the truth of God
- **Context of use:** Used in religious discourse, particularly in Christian and Islamic contexts, to refer to divine truth or scripture as the ultimate standard of truth. Appears in sermons and religious texts.

> **Note:** The root *kweli* is highly productive. The 30 items above represent the most common and well-attested expressions. Additional forms exist, particularly in the pattern *X wa kweli* (genuine/true X) applied to virtually any noun, and in the pattern *ukweli wa ki-Y* (truth of domain Y).

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots in contexts where their contribution appears to shift from core truth-evaluation toward other domains. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

### From root **kweli**:

- **upendo wa kweli** (true love): *kweli* here evaluates the authenticity of an emotional state rather than the truth-value of a proposition.

- **rafiki wa kweli** (true friend): *kweli* evaluates the quality of a relationship rather than the accuracy of a claim.

- **mtu wa kweli** (a genuine person): *kweli* evaluates character or identity rather than a statement.

- **kweli ya Mungu** (God's truth): *kweli* operates within a theological frame where truth is grounded in divine authority rather than empirical correspondence.

- **ukweli unauma** (the truth hurts): *ukweli* appears in a proverbial frame where truth is characterized by its emotional or social effects rather than its epistemic properties.

- **ukweli ni silaha** (truth is a weapon): *ukweli* appears in a political/rhetorical frame where truth is characterized by its instrumental power.

- **nusu kweli** (half-truth): *kweli* is modified by a quantity term (*nusu*, half), treating truth as a scalar or gradable property.

---

### From root **sahihi**:

- **sahihi** (signature): The root has developed a distinct lexical meaning — "signature" — in administrative and legal contexts, where it refers to a mark that authenticates a document rather than to the property of correctness itself.

- **weka sahihi** (to sign): The verbal use of *sahihi* in the sense of "to sign" extends the root into procedural/administrative action rather than evaluation.

- **sahihi ya kidijitali** (digital signature): The "signature" meaning of *sahihi* has extended into technology discourse.

- **njia sahihi** (the right way): *sahihi* evaluates a course of action or method rather than a statement or representation.

- **wakati sahihi** (the right time): *sahihi* evaluates temporal appropriateness rather than factual accuracy.

- **lugha sahihi** (correct language): *sahihi* evaluates conformity to a prescriptive standard rather than correspondence to external reality.

---

### From root **halisi**:

- **uhalisi pepe** (virtual reality): *uhalisi* (reality) is combined with *pepe* (virtual/electronic) to name a technologically constructed environment, where "reality" is used to describe something that is explicitly not physical reality.

- **uhalisi ulioboreshwa** (augmented reality): Similarly, *uhalisi* names a technologically mediated perceptual experience that supplements rather than constitutes unmediated reality.

- **uhalisia wa kichawi** (magical realism): *uhalisia* (realism) is applied to a literary genre that deliberately incorporates non-realistic elements, creating a tension with the root's core meaning of faithful representation.

- **thamani halisi** (real value): *halisi* evaluates economic or philosophical worth rather than the authenticity of a physical object.

- **Kiswahili halisi** (genuine/pure Swahili): *halisi* evaluates a language variety against a standard of purity or authenticity, entering the domain of language ideology and cultural identity.

- **utamaduni halisi** (genuine culture): *halisi* evaluates cultural practices or heritage against a standard of authenticity, entering the domain of cultural nationalism.

- **uhalisi mgumu** (harsh reality): *uhalisi* takes on an evaluative-affective dimension, characterizing reality by its difficulty rather than simply its existence.
