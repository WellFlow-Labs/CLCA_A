# G3 — Emergent Cross-Language Semantic Dimensions

---

## 1. Methodology and Scope

The dimensions below are inferred directly from the F3 semantic grouping tables and supporting F1–F2 evidence across all eight languages. Each dimension is defined by patterns of co-occurrence, lexical opposition, derivational clustering, and constructional parallelism visible in the data. No dimension is proposed unless it is supported by evidence from at least two languages; single-language observations are noted as such. Dimensions are ordered from most to least cross-linguistically robust.

---

## 2. Proposed Dimensions

---

### DIMENSION A: Propositional Correspondence

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether a statement, claim, or piece of information corresponds to actual states of affairs. Terms in this dimension are used when the question is whether what is said or represented matches what is the case. It is the most basic truth-evaluation dimension and is present in all eight languages.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: simart'le (truth), č'ešmarit'i (true), ṭq'uili (lie), mcdari (erroneous)
- Yoruba: òtítọ́ (truth), tọ́ (to be correct/true), irọ́ (lie/falsehood)
- Finnish: tosi (true), totuus (truth), valhe (lie), epätosi (untrue)
- Basque: egia (truth), egiazkoa (true), gezurra (lie), gezurrezko (false)
- Tamil: uṇmai (truth), poy (falsehood), uṇmaiyāna (true), poyyāna (false)
- Indonesian: benar (true/correct), kebenaran (truth), bohong (lie), kebohongan (falsehood)
- Turkish: doğru (true/correct), doğruluk (truth/correctness), yanlış (wrong/false), yalan (lie)
- Korean: jinsil (truth), sasil (fact), geojit (falsehood), geojinmal (lie)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In all languages this dimension generates the primary lexical opposition (truth term vs. falsehood term) and the primary light verb constructions for truth-telling and lying. The dimension is categorical rather than scalar in all languages: core truth terms resist degree modification.

---

### DIMENSION B: Moral Honesty / Integrity of Character

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of persons or conduct as characterized by truthfulness, integrity, and absence of deception as a habitual or dispositional quality. Terms in this dimension evaluate character and communicative ethics rather than the truth-value of specific propositions. The focus is on the speaker or agent rather than on the content of statements.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: uṭq'uari (truthful, honest, infallible), uṭq'uaroba (truthfulness), gulsindeli (sincere)
- Yoruba: olótítọ́ (truthful person), ìwà rere (good character), òdodo ọkàn (uprightness of heart)
- Finnish: rehellinen (honest), rehellisyys (honesty), vilpitön (sincere, without deceit), vilpittömyys (sincerity)
- Basque: zintzo/zintzoa (honest, sincere), zintzotasuna (honesty), zintzoki (honestly)
- Tamil: nērmai (honesty, integrity), nērmaiyāna (honest), kaḷḷam (deceit, as negative pole)
- Indonesian: jujur (honest), kejujuran (honesty), sejujurnya (honestly speaking)
- Turkish: dürüst (honest), dürüstlük (honesty), samimi (sincere), içten (heartfelt)
- Korean: jeongjik (honest), jeongjikada (to be honest), soljik (frank), soljikada (to be frank)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In all languages this dimension is distinct from Dimension A: the moral honesty terms evaluate persons and habitual conduct, while propositional truth terms evaluate specific statements. The degree of lexical separation varies: Finnish and Indonesian show the clearest separation; Yoruba tọ́ and Turkish doğru partially merge this dimension with Dimension A. Agentive nominalization is asymmetric in all languages: terms for habitual liars are more productive than terms for habitual truth-tellers.

---

### DIMENSION C: Ontological Reality / Actuality

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether something actually exists, occurs, or is the case, as opposed to being imagined, fictional, theoretical, or merely apparent. Terms in this dimension are used when the question is not whether a statement is true but whether a referent or situation is real. The contrast is between actual existence and non-existence, appearance, or fiction.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: namdvili (real, genuine, actual), namdviloba (reality, genuineness), sinarmdvile (reality)
- Yoruba: gidi (genuine, real, authentic), jẹ́jẹ́ (genuine, veritable), pàtàkì (genuine, authentic)
- Finnish: todellinen (real, actual), todellisuus (reality), todella (really, truly), tosielämä (real life)
- Basque: benetako (real, genuine), erreala (real), errealitatea (reality), itxurazko (apparent, seeming)
- Tamil: silje (reality, actuality), silche (substance, entity), uṇmainilai (true state, actual situation)
- Indonesian: nyata (real, actual), kenyataan (reality, fact), ternyata (it turns out, evidently)
- Turkish: gerçek (real, genuine), gerçeklik (reality), gerçekten (really, truly), gerçek dışı (unreal)
- Korean: silje (reality, actuality), silche (substance, entity), jinjja (real, genuine), sasil (fact, reality)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In most languages this dimension is partially distinct from Dimension A (propositional correspondence), though the boundary is not always sharp. Finnish maintains the clearest separation (tosi for propositional truth, todellinen for ontological reality). Turkish gerçek and Korean jinsil/sasil span both dimensions. The dimension consistently generates a contrast between what is real and what is apparent, imagined, or fictional.

---

### DIMENSION D: Material Authenticity / Genuineness of Objects

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether physical objects, documents, goods, or representations are genuine originals as opposed to counterfeits, forgeries, or imitations. Terms in this dimension are used primarily in commercial, legal, and authentication contexts where the question is whether an item is what it purports to be. The focus is on physical or documentary authenticity rather than propositional truth or personal honesty.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: namdvili (genuine, authentic), q'albi (fake, counterfeit), gaq'albeba (falsification, forgery)
- Yoruba: gidi (genuine, real), kì í ṣe gidi (not genuine, not real)
- Finnish: aito (genuine, authentic), aitous (genuineness), väärennös (forgery, counterfeit), väärentää (to forge)
- Basque: egiazko dokumentua (authentic document), gezurrezko dokumentua (false document), faltsua (false, fake), faltsutu (to falsify)
- Tamil: acal (original, genuine), poyp poruḷ (fake object), uṇmaip poruḷ (genuine object), poyeḻuttu (forged document)
- Indonesian: asli (genuine, original), palsu (fake, counterfeit), keaslian (authenticity), pemalsuan (forgery)
- Turkish: asıl (original, genuine), sahte (fake, counterfeit), özgün (original, authentic), sahtekâr (forger)
- Korean: jinpum (genuine article), jinbon (authentic copy), wijo (forgery), gajja (fake, counterfeit)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In most languages this dimension has dedicated vocabulary distinct from propositional truth terms, though the same adjectives sometimes cover both (Georgian namdvili, Tamil uṇmaiyāna, Turkish gerçek). The dimension consistently generates a genuine/counterfeit opposition and vocabulary for forgery as a legal category. Finnish and Indonesian show the clearest separation from Dimension A.

---

### DIMENSION E: Factual Correctness / Error in Statements or Actions

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether statements, answers, calculations, or actions are correct or incorrect according to established standards, rules, or norms. Terms in this dimension are used in educational, procedural, and evaluative contexts where correctness is determined by conformity to a standard rather than by correspondence to an independently existing state of affairs. The dimension overlaps with Dimension A but is distinguishable by its focus on conformity to criteria rather than correspondence to facts.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: sc'ori (correct, right, straight), mcdari (erroneous, mistaken), gaasc'oreba (correction)
- Yoruba: tọ́ (to be correct), àṣìṣe (error, mistake), ṣe déédé (to do correctly), déédé (correctly, exactly)
- Finnish: oikea (right, correct), väärä (wrong, incorrect), virhe (error, mistake), oikein (correctly)
- Basque: zuzena (correct, right), okerra (wrong, incorrect), zuzendu (to correct), zuzenketa (correction)
- Tamil: cari (correct, right), tappu (mistake, wrong), tavaṟu (mistake, error), cariyāna (correct)
- Indonesian: benar (correct, right), salah (wrong, mistaken), kesalahan (mistake, error), betul (correct)
- Turkish: doğru (correct, right), yanlış (wrong, incorrect), hata (error, mistake), düzeltmek (to correct)
- Korean: matda (to be correct), teullida (to be wrong), teullimeobsi (certainly, without mistake)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In most languages the same terms cover both Dimension A (propositional correspondence) and this dimension, but Tamil shows the clearest separation (cari/tappu for everyday correctness vs. uṇmai/poy for truth/falsehood). The dimension consistently generates correction vocabulary and is associated with educational and procedural contexts.

### DIMENSION F: Reliability / Trustworthiness of Sources

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether persons, sources, or information can be depended upon to provide accurate information consistently over time. Terms in this dimension assess credibility and dependability rather than the truth-value of specific propositions. The focus is on the track record or character of a source rather than on any single statement.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: sando (reliable, trustworthy, credible), sandooba (reliability), uṭq'uari (infallible, unerring)
- Yoruba: fidagarria (reliable, trustworthy), sinesgarria (believable, credible)
- Finnish: luotettava (reliable, trustworthy), luotettavuus (reliability), uskottava (believable, credible)
- Basque: fidagarria (reliable, trustworthy), sinesgarria (believable, credible), zalantzagarria (doubtful)
- Tamil: nampakam (reliability, trustworthiness), sinbingseong (credibility)
- Indonesian: dapat dipercaya (trustworthy), terpercaya (trusted), kredibel (credible)
- Turkish: güvenilir (reliable, trustworthy), güvenilirlik (reliability), inandırıcı (convincing, credible)
- Korean: sinbingseong (credibility, reliability), mideumanada (to be trustworthy), hwaksilhada (to be certain)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, though the degree of elaboration varies. Basque shows the most elaborate vocabulary for this dimension, with productive -garri suffix creating evaluative adjectives (fidagarria, sinesgarria, zalantzagarria). Finnish also shows high elaboration (luotettava, uskottava, paikkansapitävä). The dimension is consistently distinct from Dimension A: reliability concerns consistent accuracy over time, not the truth of a specific statement.

---

### DIMENSION G: Verification / Authentication Processes

**Definition.** This dimension covers the processes and acts of establishing, confirming, or certifying the truth, accuracy, or authenticity of claims, documents, or identities through investigation, evidence, or formal procedures. Terms in this dimension are used in legal, administrative, scientific, and investigative contexts where truth must be actively established rather than simply asserted.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: damoc'meba (verification, attestation), daadast'ura (confirmed, verified), egiazkotu (to verify)
- Yoruba: ìfẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀ (confirmation, verification), fẹsẹ̀ múlẹ̀ (to confirm, verify), ẹ̀rí (witness, evidence)
- Finnish: todistaa (to prove, testify), todiste (proof, evidence), todentaa (to verify, authenticate), todistustaakka (burden of proof)
- Basque: egiazkotu (to verify, authenticate), egiaztatu (to verify, prove), frogatu (to prove, test), egiaztagiri (certificate)
- Tamil: meyppu (proof, verification), uṇmaippaṭuttu (to verify, authenticate), uṇmaippramāṇam (authoritative proof)
- Indonesian: membenarkan (to confirm, verify), pembenaran (justification, confirmation), memvalidasi (to validate)
- Turkish: doğrulamak (to verify, confirm), doğrulama (verification), kanıtlamak (to prove)
- Korean: sasil hwagin (fact-checking), jinsilgyumyeong (truth clarification), jinwireul garida (to determine authenticity)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. Finnish shows the most elaborate vocabulary for this dimension, with a large family derived from tode- (todistaa, todentaa, todeta, todistus, todistaja, todistustaakka, todistusaineisto). The dimension consistently generates vocabulary for evidence, proof, witnesses, and formal certification. All languages show a gap: no productive causative construction for "making X true" is attested; verification constructions establish that something is true rather than causing it to be true.

---

### DIMENSION H: Revelation / Concealment of Truth

**Definition.** This dimension covers the processes of making hidden truth visible or keeping known truth hidden. Terms in this dimension describe the dynamics of truth disclosure and concealment, focusing on the transition between hidden and known states. The dimension is consistently expressed through constructional pairs (reveal vs. conceal) rather than through dedicated lexical items.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: gamovlinda (was revealed), gamoač'ina (revealed), damala (hid), dap'ara (covered)
- Yoruba: fi òtítọ́ hàn (to show/reveal truth), ṣe àṣírí (to keep secret), irọ́ jáde (lie comes to light)
- Finnish: paljastaa totuus (reveal truth), salata totuus (conceal truth), totuus paljastui (truth was revealed)
- Basque: egia atera (bring out truth), egia ezkutatu (hide truth), gezurra atera (expose a lie)
- Tamil: uṇmai veḷippaṭu (truth comes out), uṇmaiyai maṟai (hide truth), uṇmaiyai veḷippaṭuttu (reveal truth)
- Indonesian: mengungkap kebenaran (reveal truth), menyembunyikan kebenaran (hide truth), ternyata (it turns out)
- Turkish: gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak (bring truth to light), gerçeği gizlemek (hide truth), gerçek ortaya çıkmak (truth comes out)
- Korean: jinsireul balkhida (reveal truth), jinsireul sumgida (hide truth), jinsiri balkhyeojida (truth is revealed)

**Distribution:** Clearly attested in all eight languages, primarily through constructional pairs rather than dedicated lexical items. The dimension is consistently expressed through light verb constructions combining a truth noun with opposing verbs of disclosure and concealment. The intransitive revelation construction (truth reveals itself) is attested in all languages alongside the transitive causative (agent reveals truth). This dimension is closely related to Dimension G (verification) but distinct: verification establishes truth through evidence, while revelation makes hidden truth visible.

### DIMENSION I: Epistemic Certainty / Doubt

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of the speaker's or subject's degree of confidence or uncertainty about the truth of claims. Terms in this dimension mark epistemic stance rather than objective truth status, indicating how certain or uncertain a knower is about whether something is true. The dimension is distinct from Dimension A (propositional correspondence) by focusing on the knower's confidence rather than the claim's truth-value.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: ṭq'uili ar aris (it's not a lie, emphatic), mart'laa? (really? question), vitom (supposedly, allegedly)
- Yoruba: dájú (to be certain, sure), ìdájú (certainty, assurance), kò dájú (not certain)
- Finnish: kesin (certain, definite), kesinlikle (certainly), todennäköinen (probable, likely), todennäköisyys (probability)
- Basque: ziurra (certain, sure), ziurtasuna (certainty), zalantza (doubt), zalantzagarria (doubtful)
- Tamil: uṟuti (certainty), cantēkam (doubt), uṟutippaṭuttu (to confirm, verify)
- Indonesian: yakin (certain, sure), tidak yakin (uncertain), mungkin (possible, perhaps)
- Turkish: kesin (certain, definite), kesinlikle (certainly), şüpheli (doubtful, suspicious), mutlak (absolute)
- Korean: hwaksilhada (to be certain), hwaksilhi (certainly), uisimhada (to doubt), uisimseureopda (doubtful)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. Basque shows the most elaborate vocabulary for this dimension, with dedicated terms for certainty (ziurra), doubt (zalantza), and evaluative adjectives for each (zalantzagarria "doubtful," frogagarria "provable"). Finnish uniquely derives a probability term (todennäköinen "probable") from the truth root, creating a scalar epistemic dimension. The dimension is consistently distinct from Dimension A: certainty concerns the knower's confidence, not the claim's truth-value.

---

### DIMENSION J: Moral Righteousness / Justice

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of conduct, judgments, or persons as conforming to moral or legal standards of rightness, fairness, and justice. Terms in this dimension go beyond personal honesty (Dimension B) to evaluate whether actions and outcomes are just, fair, and in accordance with moral law. The dimension is most clearly elaborated in Yoruba and Georgian.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: samart'liani (just, fair), samart'lianoba (justice, fairness), usaamt'lo (unjust), samart'ali (law, justice)
- Yoruba: òdodo (righteousness, justice), olódodo (righteous person), ṣe òdodo (to act justly), ọ̀nà òdodo (path of righteousness)
- Finnish: oikeudenmukainen (just, fair), oikeudenmukaisuus (justice, fairness), vääryys (injustice, wrong)
- Basque: zuzentasun morala (moral rectitude), zuzentasun juridikoa (legal correctness), egia eta zuzentasuna (truth and justice)
- Tamil: niyāyam (justice, fairness, rightness), niyāyamāna (just, fair)
- Indonesian: adil (just, fair), keadilan (justice, fairness, equity)
- Turkish: adil (just, fair), adalet (justice), hakça (fairly, justly), hakkaniyet (fairness, equity)
- Korean: jeongdanghada (to be legitimate, justified), jeongdangseong (legitimacy, justification)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, but the degree of elaboration varies substantially. Yoruba shows the most elaborate vocabulary for this dimension, with òdodo generating a large family of compounds and fixed expressions. Georgian shows high elaboration through the samart'- family. Finnish, Basque, and Turkish show moderate elaboration. Tamil, Indonesian, and Korean show this dimension but with less dedicated vocabulary. The dimension is consistently distinct from Dimension B (moral honesty): righteousness/justice concerns conformity to moral law and fair treatment of others, while honesty concerns truthfulness in communication.

---

### DIMENSION K: Legal / Formal Validity

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether documents, procedures, marriages, arguments, or official acts have legal force or formal recognition according to institutional standards. Terms in this dimension assess conformity to procedural or legal requirements rather than factual truth or moral rightness. The dimension is most clearly elaborated in languages with extensive legal vocabulary.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: samart'ali (law, justice, legal system), samart'lebrivi (legal, juridical), gasaamt'leba (trial, court proceeding)
- Yoruba: ìdájọ́ (judgment, verdict), dájọ́ (to judge), adájọ́ (judge)
- Finnish: sah (valid, legitimate, legal), mengesahkan (to legalize, validate), pengesahan (legalization, ratification)
- Basque: zuzenbide (law, jurisprudence), zuzenbide zibila (civil law), zuzenbide penala (criminal law)
- Tamil: sattiyam (truth, oath, vow), poyccāṭci (false testimony, perjury), poyvaḻakku (false case, fraudulent lawsuit)
- Indonesian: sah (valid, legitimate, legal), valid (valid), absah (valid, legitimate), mengesahkan (to legalize)
- Turkish: geçerli (valid, effective, in force), meşru (legitimate, lawful), meşruiyet (legitimacy)
- Korean: jinsul (statement, testimony, deposition), jinwi (truth or falsehood, authenticity), sasilhon (common-law marriage)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, though the degree of elaboration varies. Georgian shows the most elaborate legal vocabulary through the samart'- family. Finnish and Indonesian show high elaboration. Yoruba shows this dimension through judgment vocabulary. The dimension is consistently distinct from Dimension A: legal validity concerns conformity to institutional requirements, not correspondence to facts.

### DIMENSION L: Precision / Exactness

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether measurements, statements, or representations meet exact specifications or achieve a high degree of accuracy in detail. Terms in this dimension emphasize degree of accuracy and specificity rather than simple correctness. The dimension is most clearly elaborated in languages with technical or scientific vocabulary.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: zusti (precise, accurate, exact), sizuste (precision, exactness), zustad (precisely, exactly)
- Yoruba: déédé (correctly, exactly, precisely), ṣe déédé (to do correctly/precisely)
- Finnish: tarkka (precise, exact), tarkkuus (precision, accuracy), täsmällinen (precise, exact)
- Basque: zehatza (precise, exact), zehaztasuna (precision, exactness)
- Tamil: tuḷḷiyam (accuracy, precision, exactness)
- Indonesian: tepat (precise, exact, accurate), akurat (accurate), ketepatan (precision, accuracy)
- Turkish: kesin (certain, definite, precise), tam (exact, complete, full), isabetli (accurate, apt)
- Korean: jeonghwakhada (to be accurate, precise), jeonghwakhi (accurately, precisely)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, though the degree of elaboration varies. Georgian shows the most dedicated vocabulary for this dimension (zusti and its derivatives). Finnish and Indonesian show high elaboration. The dimension is consistently distinct from Dimension E (correctness): precision concerns degree of accuracy and specificity, while correctness concerns conformity to a standard. The dimension is most prominent in technical, scientific, and measurement contexts.

### DIMENSION M: Sincerity / Authenticity of Expression

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether emotions, expressions, or relationships are genuinely felt or authentic rather than performed or insincere. Terms in this dimension assess the alignment between inner states and outward expression, focusing on emotional authenticity rather than factual accuracy or moral character. The dimension is distinct from Dimension B (moral honesty) by focusing on emotional genuineness rather than habitual truthfulness.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: gulsindeli (sincere, honest, frank), gulsindeloba (sincerity), namdvili siq'varuli (true love), č'ešmarit'i siq'varuli (true love)
- Yoruba: òdodo ọkàn (sincerity, uprightness of heart), tọ́ lọ́kàn (to be sincere, true in heart), ìgbàgbọ́ ọkàn (heartfelt belief)
- Finnish: vilpitön (sincere, genuine, without deceit), vilpittömyys (sincerity), aito tunne (genuine feeling)
- Basque: zintzoa (honest, sincere), zintzotasuna (honesty, sincerity), egiazko sena (genuine feeling)
- Tamil: jinsim (sincerity, true heart), jinsimeuro (sincerely, from the heart), meyyanpu (true love, genuine affection)
- Indonesian: tulus (sincere, genuine), ketulusan (sincerity), sungguh-sungguh (earnestly, sincerely)
- Turkish: samimi (sincere), samimiyet (sincerity), içten (heartfelt, sincere), içtenlik (sincerity)
- Korean: jinsim (sincerity, true heart), jinsimeuro (sincerely), jinjeong (genuine, sincere), jinjeongseonng (authenticity, genuineness)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. The dimension is consistently associated with emotional and relational contexts (love, friendship, faith) rather than with propositional or procedural contexts. In several languages, the same terms that cover material authenticity (Dimension D) also cover emotional sincerity (Georgian namdvili, Finnish aito, Basque benetako), suggesting partial merger of these two dimensions. The dimension is most clearly separated from Dimension B in Finnish (vilpitön "sincere" vs. rehellinen "honest") and Korean (jinsim "sincere heart" vs. jeongjik "honest").

---

### DIMENSION N: Plausibility / Surface Appearance of Truth

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether something appears true or believable on the surface, without necessarily being verified as actually true. Terms in this dimension mark epistemic hedging or the gap between appearance and reality, indicating that something seems true but may require further investigation. The dimension is distinct from Dimension I (certainty/doubt) by focusing on the appearance of truth rather than the knower's confidence.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: vitom (supposedly, allegedly), vitom mart'alia (supposedly it's true), akla yakın (plausible)
- Yoruba: (limited dedicated vocabulary; expressed through constructions)
- Finnish: uskottava (believable, credible, plausible), uskottavuus (credibility, believability), vaikuttaa todelta (to seem true)
- Basque: sinesgarria (believable, credible), itxurazko (apparent, seeming), itxuraz (apparently, seemingly)
- Tamil: uṇmai pōl (like truth, as if true), poy pōl (like a lie, as if false)
- Indonesian: ternyata (it turns out, evidently), tampaknya (apparently, seemingly), seolah-olah (as if)
- Turkish: görünüşe göre (apparently), sanki (as if), akla yakın (plausible, reasonable)
- Korean: geureoldeuthada (to be plausible, to seem likely), geureoldeutan iyagi (plausible story)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages but variably lexicalized. Finnish and Basque show the most dedicated vocabulary for this dimension. Yoruba and Tamil express it primarily through constructions rather than dedicated lexical items. The dimension is consistently associated with epistemic hedging and the appearance/reality contrast. It overlaps with Dimension I (certainty/doubt) but is distinct: plausibility concerns how something appears, while certainty concerns the knower's confidence.

---

### DIMENSION O: Actual vs. Nominal / Formal Status

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether something is actually the case in practice as opposed to being merely nominal, theoretical, or formally designated. Terms in this dimension contrast what actually happens or exists with what is officially claimed, planned, or theoretically supposed to be the case. The dimension is most elaborated in Korean and Indonesian.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: namdvili (real, actual, as opposed to apparent), namdvilad (really, actually)
- Yoruba: (expressed through constructions; limited dedicated vocabulary)
- Finnish: tosiasiallinen (actual, factual, de facto), tosiasiassa (in fact, actually), tosiasiallisesti (actually)
- Basque: benetako (real, genuine, as opposed to apparent), benetan (really, truly)
- Tamil: naṭappu (actual, current, real, happening), silje (actual reality)
- Indonesian: sebenarnya (actually, in fact), nyatanya (in reality), de facto (de facto)
- Turkish: aslında (actually, in fact, in reality), fiilen (actually, in practice), de facto (de facto)
- Korean: siljiljeok (substantial, actual), sasilsang (in fact, de facto), silje (actual reality), sillyeok (actual ability)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages but variably elaborated. Korean shows the most systematic elaboration through the sil- (實) compound family. Finnish shows elaboration through tosiasiallinen and related forms. The dimension is consistently associated with contrasting formal/nominal status with actual practice. It overlaps with Dimension C (ontological reality) but is distinct: the actual/nominal dimension concerns whether something has real-world effect or is merely formal, while the reality dimension concerns whether something exists at all.

---

### DIMENSION P: Consistency / Coherence

**Definition.** This dimension covers the evaluation of whether statements, arguments, or behaviors maintain internal consistency or logical coherence across instances. Terms in this dimension assess whether elements fit together without contradiction, rather than whether they correspond to external facts. The dimension is most clearly elaborated in Turkish and Finnish.

**Representative terms:**
- Georgian: (limited dedicated vocabulary; expressed through constructions)
- Yoruba: ìbámu (agreement, correspondence, harmony), bá mu (to correspond, match, agree with)
- Finnish: johdonmukainen (consistent, logical), johdonmukaisuus (consistency), ristiriitainen (contradictory)
- Basque: (limited dedicated vocabulary)
- Tamil: (limited dedicated vocabulary; expressed through constructions)
- Indonesian: konsisten (consistent), konsistensi (consistency), sesuai (in accordance with, appropriate)

## 4. Assessment of Dimensional Support

### Strongly Supported Cross-Linguistically (all 8 languages, clearly lexicalized)

**Dimensions A through M** are all strongly supported, with evidence from all eight languages. Each of these dimensions generates dedicated vocabulary in every language, appears in productive derivational families, and shows consistent constructional patterns. The following observations apply:

- **Dimensions A, B, C, D, E** form the core of the truth-evaluation domain and are present in all languages with the highest degree of lexical elaboration.
- **Dimensions F, G, H** are consistently present and generate productive vocabulary, though the degree of elaboration varies.
- **Dimensions I, J, K, L, M** are present in all languages but show more variation in elaboration.

### Moderately Supported (present in all or most languages, but variably lexicalized)

**Dimension N (Plausibility / Surface Appearance of Truth)** is present in all eight languages but is clearly lexicalized in only about half of them. In Yoruba and Tamil it is expressed primarily through constructions rather than dedicated lexical items. The dimension is real but not universally elaborated.

**Dimension O (Actual vs. Nominal / Formal Status)** is present in all eight languages but the degree of systematic elaboration varies substantially. Korean shows the most systematic vocabulary (sil- family); other languages express this dimension through adverbials and discourse markers rather than through a productive derivational family.

### Weakly Supported or Language-Specific

**Dimension P (Consistency / Coherence)** is clearly lexicalized in Turkish and Finnish, present but less elaborated in Yoruba and Indonesian, and weakly attested or expressed through constructions in the remaining four languages. This dimension may be a language-specific elaboration rather than a universal semantic partition in the truth domain.

---

### Dimensions That Split, Merge, or Vary Across Languages

**Dimensions A and E (Propositional Correspondence and Factual Correctness)** are merged in most languages (the same terms cover both) but split in Tamil (uṇmai/poy vs. cari/tappu) and partially in Yoruba (òtítọ́ vs. àṣìṣe).

**Dimensions B and J (Moral Honesty and Moral Righteousness)** are merged in Yoruba (òdodo covers both) and partially in Georgian (mart'ali covers both), but split in Finnish (rehellinen vs. oikeudenmukainen), Indonesian (jujur vs. adil), and Turkish (dürüst vs. adil).

**Dimensions C and D (Ontological Reality and Material Authenticity)** are merged in Georgian (namdvili covers both), Tamil (uṇmaiyāna covers both), and Turkish (gerçek covers both), but split in Finnish (todellinen vs. aito), Indonesian (nyata vs. asli), and Korean (silje vs. jinjja).

**Dimensions C and O (Ontological Reality and Actual vs. Nominal Status)** overlap substantially but are most clearly separated in Korean (silje "reality" vs. siljiljeok "substantial/actual") and Finnish (todellinen "real" vs. tosiasiallinen "de facto/actual").

**Dimensions D and M (Material Authenticity and Sincerity of Expression)** are merged in several languages where the same terms cover both physical genuineness and emotional authenticity (Georgian namdvili, Finnish aito, Basque benetako). They are most clearly separated in Korean (jinpum "genuine article" vs. jinsim "sincere heart") and Turkish (asıl "original" vs. samimi "sincere").

**Dimensions I and F (Epistemic Certainty and Reliability)** partially overlap in Korean (hwaksilhada covers both certainty and reliability) and Turkish (güvenilir "reliable" vs. kesin "certain" are distinct but related). They are most clearly separated in Basque (ziurra "certain" vs. fidagarria "reliable").

**Dimension N (Plausibility)** merges with Dimension I (Certainty) in some languages (Turkish makul covers both plausibility and reasonableness) but is separated in Finnish (uskottava "believable/plausible" vs. varma "certain") and Basque (sinesgarria "believable" vs. ziurra "certain").
