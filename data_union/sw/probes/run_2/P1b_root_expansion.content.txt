# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **-kweli** / **kweli**

**Participation in P1:** 3 items (*kweli*, *ukweli*, *kweli kweli*)

**Core meaning contribution:** The primary Swahili root for truth as correspondence to reality. Functions as noun, adverb, and intensifier. Generates abstract nominalizations and reduplicated emphatic forms. Serves as the baseline against which false or uncertain claims are measured.

---

### Root 2: **halisi** / **hali-** (and the broader *ḥalāl/khāliṣ* Arabic-derived cluster: *halisi*, *halali*, *uhalisi*, *uhalali*, *hali halisi*)

**Participation in P1:** 5 items (*halisi*, *hali halisi*, *uhalisi*, *halali*, *uhalali*)

**Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, purity, and legitimacy — covering both the sense of being unmixed/authentic (from Arabic *khāliṣ*) and the sense of being lawfully permitted (from Arabic *ḥalāl*). These two Arabic-derived strands converge in Swahili around the evaluation of whether something is real, pure, or rightfully valid.

---

### Root 3: **-amini** / **amini**

**Participation in P1:** 3 items (*amini*, *uaminifu*, *mwaminifu*)

**Core meaning contribution:** Trust, belief, and faithfulness — the epistemic and moral evaluation of whether a person or claim is credible and reliable. Generates a productive derivational family covering persons, qualities, and acts of believing or trusting.

---

**Note on scope:** The root *-danganya* (deceive) and the Arabic-derived cluster around *haki*, *sahihi*, and *ushahidi* are also productive, but each participates in only 2–3 P1 items and generates fewer derived forms than the three roots above. They are noted here but not selected for full Step 2 expansion. The three roots selected above show the broadest derivational productivity in the truth-evaluation domain.

### ROOT 1: **kweli**

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli
**Literal breakdown:** root noun/adverb: truth, truly
**Natural English translation:** truth; truly; really
**Context of use:** Used as a standalone assertion of truth or as an adverb modifying a claim. Appears in everyday speech, moral discourse, and legal testimony (*Ninasema kweli* "I am speaking the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Form:** ukweli
**Transliteration:** ukweli
**Literal breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/abstract prefix) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; accuracy; veracity
**Context of use:** The abstract nominalization; used in philosophical, legal, and moral discourse to refer to the property of being true or the truth of a matter (*ukweli wa jambo* "the truth of the matter"). Already in P1; included here as the base of further derivation.

**Transliteration:** kweli kweli
**Literal breakdown:** reduplication of *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truly; really; genuinely; in all honesty

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots identified above but appear in contexts where the root's contribution shifts from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**Root: kweli**

- *kweli* and *kweli kweli* function as degree intensifiers in spoken Swahili (*Alikimbia kweli kweli* "He really ran / He ran very fast"), where the truth-assertion function appears to shade into a general intensification function.

- *ukweli wa hali ya hewa* (the truth/reality of the weather) — *ukweli* appears in meteorological and environmental discourse to mean simply "the actual state" of physical conditions, without evaluative or moral content.

- *ukweli wa kisanaa* (artistic truth) — used in aesthetic criticism to evaluate whether a work of art captures something essential about human experience, where "truth" operates as an aesthetic rather than factual criterion.

- *ukweli wa ndani* (inner truth) — used in psychological and spiritual discourse to refer to subjective or personal truth, where the correspondence-to-external-reality sense is attenuated.

---

**Root: halisi / halali**

- *uhalisi* in the compounds *uhalisi pepe* (virtual reality) and *uhalisi ulioboreshwa* (augmented reality) — the root appears in technology discourse where "reality" refers to a simulated or enhanced perceptual environment rather than to the genuine as opposed to the counterfeit.

- *halali* in the phrase *halali yake* ("it is his/her due / he/she deserves it") — the root appears in contexts of desert and entitlement that are not straightforwardly about lawfulness or religious permissibility but about what is fitting or merited.

- *thamani halisi* (real/genuine value) — in economic contexts, *halisi* appears in discussions of intrinsic versus market value, where the genuine/authentic sense extends into questions of worth and valuation.

- *nakala halisi* (genuine/certified copy) — the combination of *nakala* (copy) and *halisi* (genuine) produces an expression where authenticity is attributed to a reproduction rather than an original, representing a shift in what the root's genuineness criterion is applied to.

---

**Root: amini / imani**

- *imani* in the phrase *imani ya nchi* (patriotism, lit. "faith of/in the country") — the root appears in nationalist and civic discourse where "faith" refers to political loyalty rather than religious or epistemic belief.

- *kuamini* in commercial contexts (*Amini bidhaa zetu* "Trust our products") — the root appears in advertising discourse where trust-evaluation is directed toward commercial goods rather than persons or propositions.

- *amana* in the sense of a financial deposit (*kuweka amana benki* "to make a deposit at the bank") — the trust-related root appears in banking and commercial contexts where the primary sense is custodial rather than evaluative of truthfulness.

- *mwaminifu* in the phrase *mwaminifu wa nchi* (a patriot, a loyal citizen) — the root appears in civic and political discourse where faithfulness is directed toward a political entity rather than toward a person or truth-claim.

- *imani thabiti* — the collocation with *thabiti* (firm, stable) shifts the focus from the epistemic content of belief to its psychological or dispositional stability, where degree of conviction rather than accuracy of belief is being evaluated.
