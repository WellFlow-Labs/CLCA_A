# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4. Each opposition is supported by explicit textual evidence from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents an opposition explicitly (e.g., "the direct semantic opposite of"), this is noted. Where an opposition is inferred from co-occurrence in contrastive constructions or from documented antonym relationships, the source is identified. No opposition has been constructed by inference from English semantic categories or from general linguistic knowledge not grounded in the CONTEXT.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *egia*
**Term B:** *gezur* / *gezurra*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** truth / lie; falsehood
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly describes *gezur* as "the direct semantic opposite of *egia*," and the two terms appear as the primary binary poles of propositional evaluation in everyday Basque speech. The construction *egia da ala gezurra?* ("is it true or false?") and the paired collocation *egia eta gezurra nahastu* ("to mix truth and lies") both document this as a lexically established binary opposition.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *egiazko*
**Term B:** *gezurrezko*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true, genuine, authentic / false, lying, counterfeit, fake
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *egiazko* "contrasts with *faltsuko* or *gezurrezko* in evaluative contexts." The two relational adjectives are documented as adnominal modifiers in parallel legal collocations: *egiazko lekukotasuna* ("true testimony") vs. *gezurrezko lekukotasuna* ("false testimony"), and *egiazko kopiak* ("certified true copies") vs. *gezurrezko agiriak* ("false documents"). Both are formed with the same relational suffix pattern (*-zko* / *-rezko*) on opposing base nouns.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** *egiazko*
**Term B:** *faltsuko* / *faltsu*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true, genuine / false, fake, insincere
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly lists *faltsuko* as one of the contrasting terms for *egiazko* in evaluative contexts. *Faltsu* is documented as covering falseness of objects, documents, persons, and statements, overlapping with *gezurrezko* but extending to insincerity of persons. The contrast is documented in both adjectival and predicative uses.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** *zuzen*
**Term B:** *oker*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** correct, right, straight / wrong, mistaken, crooked
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly describes *oker* as "the primary antonym of *zuzen*." The two terms appear as a documented antonym pair across epistemic (propositional correctness), moral (rightness of conduct), and spatial (straight vs. crooked) domains. Parallel constructions are attested: *zuzen egon* ("to be right") vs. *oker egon* ("to be wrong"), *zuzen ibili* ("to behave correctly") vs. *oker ibili* ("to go wrong"), *zuzen esan* ("to say correctly") vs. *oker esan* ("to say incorrectly"), *zuzen ulertu* ("to understand correctly") vs. *oker ulertu* ("to misunderstand").
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term B:** *gezurrezko lekukotasuna eman*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to give true testimony / to give false testimony
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b as verbal collocations in legal contexts, explicitly described as counterparts. The opposition is constructional in that the contrast is realized through substitution of the adnominal modifier (*egiazko* vs. *gezurrezko*) within an otherwise identical frame.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** *egia da*
**Term B:** *gezurra da*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** it is true / it is a lie; it is false
**Description of contrast:** Both predicative constructions are documented in P2b as "the primary binary poles of propositional truth attribution in everyday Basque speech" and are explicitly described as parallel and contrasting. They share the same syntactic frame (nominal predicate + copula *da*) and function as the standard affirmative and negative poles of propositional evaluation.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** *ziur egon* / *ziur nago*
**Term B:** *ziur ez egon* / *ziur ez dakit*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to be sure; I am certain / to not be sure; I don't know for certain
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b as expressing opposite epistemic states. *Ziur egon* and *ziur nago* assert epistemic certainty; *ziur ez egon* and *ziur ez dakit* assert its absence. The contrast is realized through the negation particle *ez* within the same constructional frame.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** *ziurtasun*
**Term B:** *ziurtasun eza* / *ziurtasun falta*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** certainty / uncertainty; lack of certainty
**Description of contrast:** P1b and P2b document *ziurtasun eza* ("lack of certainty") and *ziurtasun falta* ("deficiency of assurance") as nominal constructions expressing the absence of the state named by *ziurtasun*. The opposition is morphological in that *eza* (lack, absence) and *falta* (lack, deficiency) negate the base nominal, and constructional in that the contrast is realized through postposed negating elements rather than through a prefixed or suffixed form.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term B:** *egiaztatu gabeko* / *egiaztatu gabe utzi*
**Transliteration:** N/A

**Description of contrast:** P1b and P2b document *egiaztatu gabeko* ("lacking verification") and *egiaztatu gabe utzi* ("to leave without verification") as participial constructions expressing the absence of the action named by *egiaztatu*. The contrast is morphological in that *gabe* (without, lacking) negates the participial form, and constructional in that the negation is postposed rather than prefixed.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** *baliozkotu*
**Term B:** *baliogabetu*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to validate; to ratify / to invalidate; to annul; to void; to nullify
**Description of contrast:** P2a explicitly describes *baliogabetu* as "the semantic counterpart of *baliozkotu*." Both are transitive verbs used in legal and administrative contexts; *baliozkotu* confers validity and *baliogabetu* removes it. The opposition is lexical, realized through different bases (*baliozko-* vs. *baliogabe-*) with the same verbalizing suffix *-tu*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** *egia osoa*
**Term B:** *egia erdi*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** the whole truth / half-truth
**Description of contrast:** P1b documents both collocations as attested expressions within the *egia* paradigm. *Egia osoa* ("the whole truth") appears in legal oath formulas asserting complete disclosure; *egia erdi* ("half-truth") is documented as used in moral and rhetorical contexts to describe a statement that is partially true but misleading by omission. The opposition is lexical, realized through the contrasting modifiers *osoa* (whole, complete) and *erdi* (half).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** *itxura* / *itxurazko*
**Term B:** *benetako* / *egiazko*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** appearance; seeming; ostensible / genuine; real; authentic
**Description of contrast:** P3 documents this as the central contrast in Group 7 (appearance vs. reality), with *itxura* and *itxurazko* characterizing surface appearance and *benetako* and *egiazko* characterizing underlying reality. Collocations such as *benetako arrazoia ezkutatu* ("to hide the real reason") and *itxurazko adostasuna* ("apparent agreement") instantiate this contrast. P1b notes that *itxurazko* is "used to characterize something as having the appearance or semblance of a quality without necessarily possessing it genuinely."
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 27

**Term A:** *egiazkotasun*

**Transliteration:** N/A

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** *ziur* (certain, full certainty)
**Term B:** *ziurrenik* (probably, hedged probability)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** certain; sure / probably; most likely; in all likelihood
**Description of contrast:** P1b, P2a, and P4a all document the semantic shift between *ziur* (the base adjective expressing full certainty) and *ziurrenik* (the derived adverb that "functions in practice as a probability marker rather than a certainty marker"). P4a explicitly flags this as "notable." The opposition is morphological in form (base adjective vs. derived adverb with *-enik*) but semantic in nature: the derived form has shifted away from the meaning of its base.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 38

**Term A:** *-tasun* (abstract quality nominalization)
**Term B:** *-keria* (pejorative habitual practice nominalization)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** -ness; -ity (quality) / habitual wrongdoing; reprehensible practice

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
