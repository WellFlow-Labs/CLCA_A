# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** The following oppositions are extracted directly from patterns documented across P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition has been constructed or inferred beyond what the data explicitly supports. Where the data notes a contrast, antonym pair, or negative counterpart, it is recorded here. Where asymmetry exists between Term A and Term B (in productivity, register, or semantic weight), this is noted rather than smoothed over. Oppositions are listed in the order in which they emerge from the data, not grouped by type.

---

**Opposition 1**

**Term A:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Term B:** שֶׁקֶר (*sheqer*)
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet* / *sheqer*
**English translation:** truth / falsehood, lie
**Description of contrast:** *ʾemet* is the primary Hebrew term for truth across moral, legal, religious, and everyday contexts; *sheqer* is explicitly identified in P1 as "the primary Hebrew term for falsehood or lying" and as defining "the negative pole against which *ʾemet* is measured." The opposition is stated directly in P1 and recurs across P1b, P2b, and P3 (Groups 2 and 12).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 2**

**Term A:** עֵד אֱמֶת (*ʿed ʾemet*)
**Term B:** עֵד שֶׁקֶר (*ʿed sheqer*)
**Transliteration:** *ʿed ʾemet* / *ʿed sheqer*
**English translation:** truthful witness / false witness, perjurer
**Description of contrast:** These are fixed phrases forming a direct antonym pair in legal and religious discourse, documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 8–9) and P2b (items 1.9 and 11.1). The contrast is between a witness who testifies truthfully and one who gives false testimony; the pair appears in biblical prohibition (*lo taʿane be-reʿakha ʿed sheqer*) and in modern legal Hebrew.
**Contrast type:** lexical (constructional fixed phrases)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 3**

**Term A:** דּוֹבֵר אֱמֶת (*dover ʾemet*)
**Term B:** דּוֹבֵר שֶׁקֶר (*dover sheqer*)
**Transliteration:** *dover ʾemet* / *dover sheqer*
**English translation:** one who speaks the truth / one who speaks falsehood, liar
**Description of contrast:** These are parallel participial constructions documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 10–11) and P2b (items 1.10 and 11.2) as explicit counterparts. Both describe a person by their habitual relationship to truth or falsehood; the contrast is between moral characterizations of a truth-teller and a liar.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 4**

**Term A:** נָכוֹן (*nakhon*)
**Term B:** לֹא נָכוֹן (*lo nakhon*)
**Transliteration:** *nakhon* / *lo nakhon*
**English translation:** correct / not correct, wrong
**Description of contrast:** *lo nakhon* is documented in P2a (item 3.2) as "the standard negated form used in everyday Hebrew to indicate that something is incorrect or false." The opposition is between affirmation and denial of correctness in everyday conversational exchange; P3 (Group 1) lists both *ze nakhon* and *ze lo nakhon* as the primary tools for this function.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 5**

**Term A:** זֶה נָכוֹן (*ze nakhon*)
**Term B:** זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן (*ze lo nakhon*)
**Transliteration:** *ze nakhon* / *ze lo nakhon*
**English translation:** that is correct / that is not correct
**Description of contrast:** These are the standard constructional pair for affirming and denying correctness in everyday spoken Hebrew, documented in P2b (items 3.1–3.2) and P3 (Group 1). P4b notes that negative attribution via *lo* is "fully parallel to affirmative attribution" with no additional morphology required.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 6**

**Term A:** זֶה אֱמֶת (*ze ʾemet*)
**Term B:** זֶה לֹא אֱמֶת (*ze lo ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** *ze ʾemet* / *ze lo ʾemet*
**English translation:** that is true / that is not true
**Description of contrast:** These are documented in P2b (items 1.29–1.30) as "the standard everyday construction for affirming the truth of a statement" and "the standard everyday construction for denying the truth of a statement" respectively. They form the most direct constructional pair for truth affirmation and denial in spoken Hebrew.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 7**

**Term A:** זֶה אֱמֶת (*ze ʾemet*)
**Term B:** זֶה שֶׁקֶר (*ze sheqer*)
**Transliteration:** *ze ʾemet* / *ze sheqer*
**English translation:** that is true / that is a lie
**Description of contrast:** P2b (item 11.3) notes that *ze sheqer* is "stronger in register than *ze lo nakhon* (that is not correct); implies deliberate falsehood." This opposition differs from Opposition 6 in that *ze sheqer* does not merely deny truth but positively characterizes the statement as a lie, adding a moral dimension absent from the simple negation *ze lo ʾemet*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 8**

**Term A:** אָמִין (*ʾamin*)
**Term B:** לֹא אָמִין (*lo ʾamin*)
**Transliteration:** *ʾamin* / *lo ʾamin*
**English translation:** reliable, credible / unreliable, untrustworthy
**Description of contrast:** *lo ʾamin* is documented in P1 (item 2) as the direct contrast to *ʾamin*, and in P2a (item 2.2) as "the standard negated form used to describe a person, source, or system that cannot be trusted." P1 notes that *ʾamin* "contrasts with *lo ʾamin* (unreliable)."
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 9**

**Term A:** מְהֵימָן (*mehiman*)
**Term B:** לֹא מְהֵימָן (*lo mehiman*)
**Transliteration:** *mehiman* / *lo mehiman*
**English translation:** trustworthy, credible / untrustworthy, not credible
**Description of contrast:** *lo mehiman* is documented in P2a (item 9.2) as the negated form used "in legal and formal contexts to describe a witness or source whose testimony or information is not considered credible." The pair is particularly prominent in legal discourse, where the judicial formulas *nimtsa mehiman* and *nimtsa lo mehiman* (Opposition 10 below) are standard.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 10**

**Term A:** נִמְצָא מְהֵימָן (*nimtsa mehiman*)
**Term B:** נִמְצָא לֹא מְהֵימָן (*nimtsa lo mehiman*)
**Transliteration:** *nimtsa mehiman* / *nimtsa lo mehiman*
**English translation:** found to be credible / found not to be credible
**Description of contrast:** These are documented in P2b (items 9.4–9.5) as "a standard formula in Israeli legal judgments" for recording the court's finding on witness credibility. The opposition is constructional and institutionally fixed: both formulas use the same verb *nimtsa* (was found/determined) with the predicate adjective affirmed or negated.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 11**

**Term A:** נֶאֱמָן (*neʾeman*)
**Term B:** לֹא נֶאֱמָן (*lo neʾeman*)
**Transliteration:** *neʾeman* / *lo neʾeman*
**English translation:** faithful, trustworthy / untrustworthy, unreliable, disloyal
**Description of contrast:** *lo neʾeman* is documented in P1b (Root א-מ-נ, item 15) as used "in everyday and legal contexts to describe a person or source that cannot be trusted." The opposition covers both the personal loyalty dimension (disloyal) and the epistemic dimension (testimony not to be relied upon).
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 12**

**Term A:** כָּשֵׁר (*kasher*)
**Term B:** פָּסוּל (*pasul*)
**Transliteration:** *kasher* / *pasul*
**English translation:** fit, valid, proper / disqualified, invalid, inadmissible
**Description of contrast:** P1 (items 28–29) explicitly identifies these as contrasting terms: *kasher* evaluates something as meeting required standards of fitness, while *pasul* evaluates something or someone as "failing to meet required standards and therefore invalid or inadmissible." P3 (Group 3) lists them as a "direct antonym pair." The opposition operates in both religious law (ritual fitness) and legal/administrative contexts (admissibility, validity).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 13**

**Term A:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)
**Term B:** לֹא אוֹתֶנְטִי (*lo ʾotenti*)
**Transliteration:** *ʾotenti* / *lo ʾotenti*
**English translation:** authentic, genuine / inauthentic, not genuine
**Description of contrast:** *lo ʾotenti* is documented in P2a (item 5.2) as "the negated form used to describe something evaluated as not genuinely what it claims to be." As a loanword, *ʾotenti* does not generate a lexical antonym through Hebrew morphology; negation via *lo* is the only attested strategy.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 14**

**Term A:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Term B:** מְזוּיָּף (*mezuyaf*)
**Transliteration:** *meqori* / *mezuyaf*
**English translation:** original, genuine / forged, falsified, counterfeit
**Description of contrast:** P1 (item 13) explicitly states that *mezuyaf* "directly contrasts with *meqori* (original) and *ʾotenti* (authentic)." The opposition is between something that is the genuine original and something that has been deliberately falsified to appear genuine.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 15**

**Term A:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (*ʾotenti*)
**Term B:** מְזוּיָּף (*mezuyaf*)
**Transliteration:** *ʾotenti* / *mezuyaf*
**English translation:** authentic / forged, counterfeit
**Description of contrast:** P1 (item 13) explicitly lists *ʾotenti* as one of the terms that *mezuyaf* "directly contrasts with." The opposition is between genuineness and deliberate falsification; unlike the *meqori* / *mezuyaf* pair (Opposition 14), this pair foregrounds the question of genuineness rather than originality.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 16**

**Term A:** מְקוֹרִי (*meqori*)
**Term B:** לֹא מְקוֹרִי (*lo meqori*)
**Transliteration:** *meqori* / *lo meqori*
**English translation:** original / not original, unoriginal, derivative
**Description of contrast:** *lo meqori* is documented in P2b (item 6.4) as "the standard negated form used to describe something that lacks originality or is a copy or imitation." This opposition differs from Opposition 14 in that *lo meqori* does not imply deliberate falsification; something can be *lo meqori* (derivative, unoriginal) without being *mezuyaf* (forged).
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 17**

**Term A:** מוּצְדָּק (*mutsdddaq*)

**Transliteration:** *mutsdddaq* / *lo mutsdddaq*
**English translation:** justified, warranted / unjustified, unwarranted
**Description of contrast:** *lo mutsdddaq* is documented in P1b (Root צ-ד-ק, item 7) as "the negative form, used to evaluate actions, claims, or decisions as lacking justification" and described as "extremely common in everyday moral and legal evaluation." P2a (item 14.5) cross-references both forms as a productive pair.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)

**Opposition 18**

**Term A:** חוּקִי (*ḥuqi*)
**Term B:** בִּלְתִּי חוּקִי (*bilti ḥuqi*)
**Transliteration:** *ḥuqi* / *bilti ḥuqi*
**English translation:** legal, lawful / illegal, unlawful
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 19.3) documents *bilti ḥuqi* as "the standard adjectival phrase for illegal or unlawful in modern Hebrew" and as "the direct antonym of *ḥuqi*." The negator here is *bilti* rather than *lo*, which is the standard pattern for adjectival negation in formal and written registers.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 19**

**Term A:** חוּקִי (*ḥuqi*)
**Term B:** אִי-חוּקִיּוּת (*ʾi-ḥuqiyyut*)
**Transliteration:** *ḥuqi* / *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut*
**English translation:** legal, lawful / illegality, unlawfulness
**Description of contrast:** This opposition is asymmetric: *ḥuqi* is an adjective while *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut* is an abstract noun formed by the *ʾi-* prefix on the *-ut* nominalization. P2a (item 19.2) documents *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut* as used "in legal and administrative discourse to describe the quality of being illegal or unlawful." The *ʾi-* prefix on *-ut* nominals is documented in P4b as productive specifically in formal and legal registers.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation via *ʾi-* prefix on nominalization)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 20**

**Term A:** לֶגִיטִימִי (*legitimi*)
**Term B:** בִּלְתִּי לֶגִיטִימִי (*bilti legitimi*)
**Transliteration:** *legitimi* / *bilti legitimi*
**English translation:** legitimate / illegitimate, not legitimate
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 20.3) documents *bilti legitimi* as "the adjectival negation used to describe something as lacking legitimacy" and as "the direct antonym of *legitimi*." The *bilti* negator is used here rather than *lo*, consistent with the formal register of the loanword.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 21**

**Term A:** לֶגִיטִימִיּוּת (*legitimiyyut*)
**Term B:** אִי-לֶגִיטִימִיּוּת (*ʾi-legitimiyyut*)
**Transliteration:** *legitimiyyut* / *ʾi-legitimiyyut*
**English translation:** legitimacy / illegitimacy, lack of legitimacy
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 20.2) documents *ʾi-legitimiyyut* as used "in political and legal discourse to describe the absence of legitimacy in a claim, authority, or action." This is a nominalized opposition, parallel to the *ḥuqiyyut* / *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut* pair (Opposition 19), using the productive *ʾi-* prefix on the *-ut* abstract noun.
**Contrast type:** morphological (negation via *ʾi-* prefix)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 26**

This nominalized opposition has acquired particular institutional salience beyond the adjectival pair.

**Opposition 30**

**Term A:** אֱמֶת אוֹבְּיֶקְטִיבִית (*ʾemet obyektivit*)
**Term B:** אֱמֶת סוּבְּיֶקְטִיבִית (*ʾemet subyektivit*)
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet obyektivit* / *ʾemet subyektivit*
**English translation:** objective truth / subjective truth
**Description of contrast:** These are documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 29–30) as contrasting collocations: *ʾemet subyektivit* describes "truth that holds from a particular perspective or for a particular subject," while *ʾemet obyektivit* describes "truth that holds independently of any particular perspective or subject." P1b explicitly states that the two "contrast" with each other.
**Contrast type:** lexical (constructional collocations)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 31**

**Term A:** אֱמוּנָה עִוֶּרֶת (*ʾemuna ʿiveret*)

**Transliteration:** *ʾemuna ʿiveret* / *ʾemuna ʾamitit*
**English translation:** blind faith / genuine faith

The opposition is between faith held without examination and faith that is genuinely grounded; the contrast is stated in P1b.
**Contrast type:** lexical (constructional collocations)
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**Opposition 33**

**Term A:** חֲצִי אֱמֶת (*ḥatsi ʾemet*)
**Term B:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** *ḥatsi ʾemet* / *ʾemet*
**English translation:** half-truth / truth
**Description of contrast:** P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 20) documents *ḥatsi ʾemet* as describing "a statement that is technically accurate but misleading because it omits relevant information" and notes it "carries a negative connotation, implying deliberate deception through selective truth-telling." The opposition is between full, unqualified truth and a partial representation that functions deceptively; the contrast is implicit in the construction *ḥatsi* (half) modifying *ʾemet*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 34**

**Term A:** גִּלּוּי הָאֱמֶת (*gilluy ha-ʾemet*)
**Term B:** הַסְתָּרַת הָאֱמֶת (*hastaʾrat ha-ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** *gilluy ha-ʾemet* / *hastaʾrat ha-ʾemet*
**English translation:** revelation of the truth / concealment of the truth
**Description of contrast:** *gilluy ha-ʾemet* (disclosure of truth) is documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 27) and P2b (item 1.12); *lehastir et ha-ʾemet* (to hide the truth) is documented in P2b (item 1.26) as its counterpart action. The opposition is between the act of bringing truth to light and the act of deliberately concealing it; both constructions use *ha-ʾemet* as the shared object.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 35**

**Opposition 36**

**Term A:** לְדַבֵּר אֱמֶת (*ledabber ʾemet*)
**Term B:** לְשַׁקֵּר (*leshaqer*)
**Transliteration:** *ledabber ʾemet* / *leshaqer*
**English translation:** to speak the truth / to lie
**Description of contrast:** *ledabber ʾemet* is documented in P2b (item 1.23) as "functionally equivalent to 'to tell the truth' in English"; *leshaqer* is documented in P2a (item 11.4) as "the standard verb for lying in modern Hebrew." The opposition is between the two primary verbal expressions for truth-telling and lying, though they are morphologically asymmetric: *ledabber ʾemet* is a verb-plus-object construction while *leshaqer* is a single Pi'el verb.
**Contrast type:** constructional (asymmetric)
