# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Oppositions below are extracted directly from patterns documented across P1–P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit attestation in the prior steps. No opposition is inferred beyond what the data state. Where prior steps noted asymmetries between terms (e.g., a construction attested for one term but not its apparent counterpart), those asymmetries are preserved and reported. Epistemic statuses are carried forward from prior steps; where both terms in a pair carry the same status, that status is assigned to the pair; where they differ, the lower status is assigned.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** cheqa
**Term B:** llulla
**Transliteration:** cheqa / llulla
**English translation:** "true, genuine, real" / "false, lie, deceptive"
**Description of contrast:** *Cheqa* is the primary term for truth, genuineness, and correctness in Southern Quechua; *llulla* is the primary term for falsehood and deception. They are used as direct predicative antonyms in the same construction type (e.g., *Cheqam kay* "This is true" vs. *Llullam chay* "That is a lie") and appear as contrasting modifiers in parallel nominal phrases (*cheqa simi* "true word" vs. *llulla simi* "false speech"; *cheqa sonqo* "honest at heart" vs. *llulla sonqo* "false-hearted").
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** cheqaq
**Term B:** llullaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq / llullaq
**English translation:** "truthful, honest (person or statement)" / "liar, deceiver (habitual)"
**Description of contrast:** Both are formed by the agentive/adjectivizing suffix *-q* applied to their respective roots (*cheqa + -q*; *llulla + -q*), producing parallel agentive nominals used in moral characterization of persons. They appear in directly parallel constructions: *Cheqaq runám pay* ("He/she is an honest person") vs. *Llulla runám pay* ("He/she is a liar"), and are explicitly described in P3 as antonyms in evaluative discourse.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** cheqa sonqo
**Term B:** llulla sonqo
**Transliteration:** cheqa sonqo / llulla sonqo
**English translation:** "genuine of heart, sincere, honest at heart" / "false-hearted, insincere, deceitful at heart"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations combine a truth-domain root with *sonqo* ("heart, inner self") to locate honesty or deception specifically in a person's inner disposition rather than in their outward conduct or statements. P2a explicitly describes *llulla sonqo* as the antonym of *cheqa sonqo* in ethical and literary discourse.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** cheqa kawsay
**Term B:** llulla kawsay
**Transliteration:** cheqa kawsay / llulla kawsay
**English translation:** "true life, genuine way of living, authentic existence" / "false way of life, deceptive existence, inauthentic living"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations apply their respective roots to *kawsay* ("life, living, existence") in philosophical, religious, and ethical discourse about modes of existence. P1b and P2a describe them as antonyms in Andean religious and ethical texts. *Llulla kawsay* carries lower attestation than *cheqa kawsay*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** cheqa ñan
**Term B:** chiqan ñan
**Transliteration:** cheqa ñan / chiqan ñan
**English translation:** "the right path, the correct way, the true road" / "the straight path, the correct way, the just road"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations combine a truth-domain root with *ñan* ("road, path") in moral and religious discourse about the correct course of conduct or belief. P1b notes they appear "sometimes in parallel" in Andean ethical and religious texts, suggesting they are used in overlapping rather than strictly contrasting contexts. The contrast here is between two near-synonymous expressions rather than antonyms.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** cheqa
**Term B:** mana cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa / mana cheqa
**English translation:** "true, genuine" / "untrue, false, not genuine, inauthentic"
**Description of contrast:** *Mana cheqa* is the standard morphological negation of *cheqa* using the negator *mana*, producing the primary negated form used to evaluate statements, persons, or things as false or inauthentic. P1b and P2a describe it as "the standard negated form of *cheqa*" and "widely attested in Southern Quechua."
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** cheqaq
**Term B:** mana cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq / mana cheqaq
**English translation:** "truthful, genuine, honest" / "untruthful, dishonest, not genuine"
**Description of contrast:** *Mana cheqaq* is the negated form of *cheqaq*, described in P1b as "a direct antonym of *cheqaq* in evaluative discourse" used in moral evaluation of persons or statements as dishonest or inaccurate. It follows the same *mana* + predicate negation pattern as *mana cheqa*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** allin
**Term B:** mana allin
**Transliteration:** allin / mana allin
**English translation:** "good, right, proper, correct" / "bad, wrong, improper, not right"
**Description of contrast:** *Mana allin* is described in P1 as "the standard negated form" and "the primary antonym of *allin* in evaluative contexts," functioning as a conventionalized fixed expression. Both appear in the same predicate-plus-evidential construction (*Allinmi chay ruway* vs. *Mana allinmi chay*).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** cheqaqchay
**Term B:** llullachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqaqchay / llullachiy
**English translation:** "to verify, to confirm, to prove true, to authenticate" / "to deceive, to cause to lie, to mislead"
**Description of contrast:** Both are morphological causatives formed by applying a causative suffix to their respective roots (*cheqaq + -chay*; *llulla + -chiy*), but they describe causation in opposite directions: *cheqaqchay* causes something to be established as true, while *llullachiy* causes someone to believe something false or to make a false statement. The contrast is between causation toward truth and causation toward falsehood.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** cheqaqta rimay
**Term B:** llulla rimay
**Transliteration:** cheqaqta rimay / llulla rimay
**English translation:** "to speak the truth, to tell the truth" / "to speak falsely, to lie in speech, false talk"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations combine a truth-domain modifier with *rimay* ("to speak, speech") to evaluate speech acts as truthful or false. They appear in parallel moral and legal contexts: *cheqaqta rimay* is attested in imperative constructions enjoining truthful speech; *llulla rimay* is attested in moral evaluation of speech as dishonest. P2a and P3 document them as contrasting expressions in the same speech-evaluation domain.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** cheqa yuyay
**Term B:** llulla yuyay
**Transliteration:** cheqa yuyay / llulla yuyay
**English translation:** "correct understanding, true opinion, sound judgment" / "false belief, mistaken opinion, erroneous thought"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations combine a truth-domain root with *yuyay* ("thought, mind, opinion, belief") to evaluate cognitive states as correct or false. P1b documents *cheqa yuyay* as used in philosophical and educational discourse; *llulla yuyay* is documented in philosophical and religious discourse about incorrect beliefs. *Llulla yuyay* carries lower attestation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** cheqaqpuni
**Term B:** cheqaqchus
**Transliteration:** cheqaqpuni / cheqaqchus
**English translation:** "absolutely true, certainly genuine, without doubt true" / "Is it true? Is it really so? Can it be genuine?"
**Description of contrast:** Both are formed from *cheqaq* with different clitics: *-puni* (emphatic/absolute) produces a strong positive assertion of truth; *-chus* (interrogative/dubitative) produces a question or expression of doubt about whether something is genuinely the case. They represent opposite epistemic stances — maximum certainty vs. doubt — expressed through the same base form with different clitics.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** cheqaqtam (cheqaqta + direct evidential *-mi*)
**Term B:** cheqaqchus (cheqaq + dubitative *-chus*)
**Transliteration:** cheqaqtam / cheqaqchus
**English translation:** "truly [I know this directly]" / "Is it true? Can it be genuine?"
**Description of contrast:** Both attach different epistemic clitics to forms of *cheqaq*: the direct evidential *-mi/-m* signals that the speaker has direct knowledge that something is true; the dubitative *-chus* signals uncertainty or doubt about whether something is the case. P2a and P4a document both as attested constructions in Southern Quechua, representing contrasting epistemic commitments expressed through the same root.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** Cheqam kay. (positive predicate assertion with direct evidential)
**Term B:** Mana cheqachu. (negative predicate with *mana … -chu*)
**Transliteration:** Cheqa-m kay. / Mana cheqa-chu.
**English translation:** "This is true." / "It is not true. / That is not the case."
**Description of contrast:** These are the two standard constructional poles for asserting and denying truth in Southern Quechua. The positive construction uses the direct evidential clitic *-mi/-m* attached to the predicate; the negative construction uses the *mana … -chu* frame. P4a and P4b document both as the standard forms for truth assertion and denial across Southern Quechua registers.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** Llullam chay. (positive predicate assertion of falsehood)
**Term B:** Mana llullachu. (negative predicate denying falsehood)
**Transliteration:** Llulla-m chay. / Mana llulla-chu.
**English translation:** "That is a lie. / That is false." / "It is not a lie. / That is not false."
**Description of contrast:** Parallel to Opposition 16 but for the *llulla* predicate: the positive construction asserts that something is a lie or false; the negative construction denies it. Both are documented in P2b as attested constructions in Southern Quechua.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** cheqa rimay / cheqaqta rimay (imperative: enjoining truthful speech)
**Term B:** ama llullakuychu (prohibitive: forbidding lying)
**Transliteration:** cheqa rimay / cheqaqta rimay / ama llullakuy-chu
**English translation:** "Speak the truth. / Tell the truth." / "Do not lie."
**Description of contrast:** These are the two attested constructional strategies for directing speech toward truthfulness: one is a positive imperative enjoining truthful speech (*cheqaqta rimay*); the other is a prohibitive construction forbidding lying (*ama llullakuychu*). P4a and P4b note that the prohibitive construction is attested exclusively with *llulla* and *llullakuy*, while the positive imperative is attested with *cheqaqta*; no parallel prohibitive for *cheqa* and no parallel positive imperative for *llulla* are documented.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** pantay / pantasqa (error, mistake — without implication of intent)
**Term B:** llullakuy / llullaq (lying, deception — with implication of deliberate conduct)
**Transliteration:** pantay / pantasqa / llullakuy / llullaq
**English translation:** "to err, to make a mistake, mistaken, erroneous" / "to lie, to deceive, liar, deceiver"
**Description of contrast:** P3 explicitly distinguishes these two sets: *pantay* appears in contexts of acknowledgment and attribution of error without the moral condemnation associated with *llulla*, and no prohibitive construction (*ama pantaychu*) is attested for *pantay* in available documentation, unlike *ama llullakuychu* for *llullakuy*. The contrast is between unintentional deviation from correctness and deliberate deception, as supported by their distinct usage contexts.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** cheqa hina (resembling truth, as if genuine)
**Term B:** llulla hina (resembling falsehood, as if deceptive)
**Transliteration:** cheqa hina / llulla hina
**English translation:** "as if true, like the real thing, resembling the genuine" / "like a lie, as if deceptive, resembling falsehood"
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations use the comparative particle *hina* ("like, as, similar to") with their respective roots to describe resemblance to truth or falsehood without asserting either. P1b, P2a, and P3 document them as parallel comparative constructions used when something has the appearance of being true or false without the speaker committing to a definitive assertion. Both carry lower attestation than the non-*hina* forms.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** llullaq runa (person characterized as a liar)
**Term B:** cheqaq runa (person characterized as honest)
**Transliteration:** llullaq runa / cheqaq runa
**English translation:** "liar, dishonest person" / "honest person, truthful person"
**Description of contrast:** Both are nominal phrases combining a truth-domain modifier with *runa* ("person, human being") in the same adnominal modification construction. P2b documents them as directly parallel constructions used in moral characterization of persons: *Llulla runám pay* ("He/she is a liar") vs. *Cheqaq runám pay* ("He/she is an honest person").
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** qelqasqa (written, documented, on record — authentic documentary status)
**Term B:** llulla qelqa (false document, forged writing, fraudulent record)
**Transliteration:** qelqasqa / llulla qelqa
**English translation:** "written, documented, recorded, inscribed" / "false document, forged writing, fraudulent record"
**Description of contrast:** Both expressions are attested in legal and administrative contexts where the authenticity of written documents is at issue. *Qelqasqa* asserts that something has the status of an official written record; *llulla qelqa* characterizes a document as falsified or fraudulent. P2a and P3 document them in the same documentary-authenticity domain.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** iñiy (to believe, to trust)
**Term B:** iñinichu chay simita (I do not believe that statement — negated construction)
**Transliteration:** iñiy / iñi-ni-chu chay simi-ta
**English translation:** "to believe, to trust" / "I do not believe that statement. / I don't trust those words."
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents the negated first-person construction *iñinichu chay simita* as the attested form for expressing disbelief or distrust of a claim, contrasting with the positive *iñiy* in contexts of credibility evaluation. This is a constructional contrast between trust and distrust of a statement, expressed through the same verbal root with and without negation.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** cheqaqniyuq (one who possesses truthfulness — *-niyuq* possessive-agentive)
**Term B:** llullaq / llullakuq (one who lies — *-q* agentive)
**Transliteration:** cheqaqniyuq / llullaq / llullakuq
**English translation:** "truthful person, one who possesses honesty" / "liar, one who lies, deceiver"
**Description of contrast:** These are the attested agentive nominals for "honest person" and "liar" respectively, but they are formed by different morphological strategies: *cheqaqniyuq* uses the possessive suffix *-niyuq* ("one who has/possesses"), while *llullaq* and *llullakuq* use the agentive suffix *-q* ("one who does"). P4b notes this asymmetry explicitly, observing that the bare agentive *-q* is not documented as freely applicable to *cheqaq* in the same way it is to *llulla*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
