## Narrative Analysis

### Universally Attested Patterns (All 8 Languages)

**1. Abstract Nominalization**

Every language shows productive means of deriving abstract quality nouns from truth-domain terms:

- **Suffixation**: Chinese -性, Hebrew -וּת, Sanskrit -त्व/-ता, Greek -ότητα/-ία, English -ness/-ity
- **Circumfixation**: Tagalog ka-...-an, Swahili u-...-a
- **Periphrasis**: Quechua nominalized copula (chiqan kay)

This represents a universal derivational need: all languages require ways to refer to truth, honesty, accuracy, etc. as abstract concepts rather than just predicating them of subjects. The formal strategies vary (affixation vs. circumfixation vs. periphrasis), but the functional requirement is constant.

**Productivity**: Highly productive in all languages, especially in formal and philosophical registers. No restrictions observed on which truth-domain terms can undergo nominalization.

**2. Morphological Negation**

All eight languages show highly productive morphological negation of truth-domain terms:

- **Prefixal**: Sanskrit अ-/अन-, Greek α-, English un-/in-/dis-
- **Proclitic**: Chinese 不, Hebrew לא, Tagalog hindi/di-, Swahili mana, Quechua mana

This creates systematic oppositions (true/untrue, correct/incorrect, honest/dishonest) across all languages. The strategy (prefix vs. proclitic) varies, but productivity is universal.

**Asymmetry**: Morphological negation coexists with lexical antonyms (true/false, good/bad) in all languages. Pattern: morphological negation more productive for derived/abstract terms; lexical antonyms more common for basic oppositions.

**3. Light Verb Constructions**

All languages show highly conventionalized verb-object constructions for "speak truth/lies":

- Chinese 说实话/说谎
- Hebrew לומר את האמת/לספר שקרים
- Sanskrit सत्यं वद/असत्यं वदति
- Greek λέω την αλήθεια/λέω ψέματα
- English tell the truth/tell lies
- Tagalog magsabi ng totoo/magsinungaling
- Swahili sema ukweli/sema uongo
- Quechua chiqanta rimay/llullata rimay

Despite structural differences (accusative marking, definiteness, verb choice), all show high conventionalization of this specific collocation. This suggests universal pragmatic importance of the "truth-telling" speech act.

**Extension**: The pattern extends to "make mistake/error" in most languages (Chinese 犯错误, Greek κάνω λάθος, English make a mistake, Tagalog gumawa ng pagkakamali, Swahili fanya makosa).

**4. Compounding Strategies**

All languages show productive compounding of truth-domain terms with nouns to create specialized concepts:

- **Direct compounding**: Chinese 真相, Sanskrit सत्यवचन, English truth-teller
- **Associative constructions**: Hebrew construct state, Tagalog linker, Swahili associative, Quechua juxtaposition
- **Attributive modification**: Greek, English

The formal strategies vary by language type (isolating, fusional, agglutinative), but all show high productivity in creating domain-specific terms (true friend, false witness, genuine love, etc.).

### Widely Attested Patterns (6-7 Languages)

**1. Causative Derivation for Verification**

Six of eight languages show dedicated causative morphology for "make true/verify/confirm":

- **Affixal causatives**: Hebrew hif'il, Greek -ώνω, Tagalog pa-, Swahili -isha, Quechua -cha-
- **Alternative strategies**: Chinese resultative compounds (证实), Sanskrit uses separate roots

**English exception**: Uses separate roots from Latin (verify, prove, authenticate) rather than causative derivation from true/truth.

**Productivity**: Extremely productive in the six languages with causative morphology. Common in legal, administrative, and investigative contexts.

**Asymmetry**: Causatives for "make true/verify" are more common and productive than causatives for "make false/falsify." Several languages lack or rarely use causatives for falsification, suggesting truth-establishment is conceptualized as active process while falsity may be seen as default or passive state.

**2. Discourse Marker Development**

Seven languages (all except Sanskrit) show grammaticalization of truth vocabulary into discourse markers:

- **Introducing frank statements**: Chinese 说真的, Hebrew באמת, Greek αλήθεια?, English to be honest, Tagalog totoo lang, Swahili kwa kweli, Quechua chiqap
- **Seeking confirmation**: Hebrew נכון?, Greek ορθός?, Tagalog totoo ba?
- **Expressing surprise**: Hebrew באמת?, Greek αλήθεια?, English really?

**Sanskrit exception**: As a classical language with limited colloquial development, Sanskrit lacks pragmatic grammaticalization into discourse markers.

This represents a universal pragmatic pathway: truth-asserting terms grammaticalize into markers of speaker stance, sincerity, or epistemic attitude. The pathway appears active in all living languages sampled.

**3. Passive Derivation**

Six languages show productive passive derivation from causative/transitive truth-domain verbs:

- **Morphological passives**: Hebrew passive binyanim, Greek -ομαι, Tagalog -in/-an, Swahili -wa, Quechua -sqa
- **Periphrastic passives**: English be + past participle

**Chinese exception**: Uses 被 bèi construction rather than morphological passive.

**Sanskrit**: Shows passive forms but less systematically in truth domain.

Common in formal/legal registers for verification statements ("was proven," "has been confirmed").

### Moderately Attested Patterns (3-5 Languages)

**1. Inchoative Derivation**

Four languages show productive inchoative morphology for "become X":

- **Dedicated morphology**: Tagalog mag-ka-, Swahili -ika, Quechua -ya-, Sanskrit (context-dependent)
- **Periphrastic**: Chinese 变 biàn + adjective, Hebrew להפוך le-hafokh, Greek γίνομαι gínomai, English become

**Restrictions**: Even in productive languages, inchoatives show lexical restrictions. English "come true" is fixed but *come false is ungrammatical. This suggests "becoming true" is conceptualized differently from "becoming false" (revelation vs. exposure).

**2. Reflexive/Middle Marking**

Four languages show productive reflexive marking in truth domain:

- **Hebrew**: reflexive binyanim (התברר hitbarer "became clear")
- **Tagalog**: -ku- reflexive (from llullakuy pattern)
- **Swahili**: -ji- reflexive (rare in truth domain)
- **Quechua**: -ku- reflexive (llullakuy "to lie", chiqanchakuy "verify for oneself")

**Functions vary**:
- Self-directed action (verify for oneself)
- Moral self-affection (lying affects one's character)
- Middle voice semantics (spontaneous change)

Other languages show less systematic reflexive marking in truth domain.

**3. Reduplication for Emphasis**

Five languages show reduplication patterns, but with varying productivity:

- **Systematic**: Chinese AABB pattern (实实在在), Tagalog with linker (totoong-totoo)
- **Marginal**: Greek (pragmatic), Swahili (informal), Quechua (limited)
- **Absent**: Hebrew, Sanskrit, English

This appears to be a feature of isolating (Chinese) and agglutinative (Tagalog) languages rather than a universal pattern. Functions primarily for emphasis in informal registers.

**4. Prepositional/Case Adverbials**

Six languages create adverbials through prepositional phrases or case marking:

- **Prepositional**: Hebrew באמת, Greek στην αλήθεια, Tagalog sa totoo lang, Swahili kwa kweli
- **Case-marked**: Sanskrit ablative -तः, Quechua ablative -manta

Chinese and English rely more on suffixal adverbs (-ly, -地) but also have some prepositional phrases (in truth, for real).

Highly productive across languages with rich case or prepositional systems.

### Weakly Attested or Language-Specific Patterns

**1. Pseudo-X Compounding (Greek)**

Greek shows uniquely extensive ψευδ- (pseud-) compounding for creating critical/polemical terms:

- Academic: ψευδοεπιστήμη "pseudoscience", ψευδοφιλόσοφος "pseudo-philosopher"
- Political: ψευδοδημοκρατία "pseudo-democracy"
- Media: ψευδοπληροφορία "misinformation"

**English** borrows this pattern (pseudoscience, pseudo-intellectual) but shows less productivity.

**Other languages** lack equivalent systematic pattern. This represents Greek-specific elaboration for critical discourse, possibly reflecting its role in academic and intellectual contexts.

**2. Resultative Verb Compounds (Chinese)**

Chinese uniquely shows extensive resultative verb compounding as preferred strategy over periphrastic causatives:

- 改正 gǎizhèng "change-correct = to correct"
- 落实 luòshí "fall-solid = to implement"
- 证实 zhèngshí "prove-solid = to verify"

Other languages use affixal causatives (Hebrew, Greek, Tagalog, Swahili, Quechua) or periphrastic constructions (English, Sanskrit). This represents characteristic Chinese morphological strategy not paralleled elsewhere.

**3. Evidential Integration (Quechua)**

Quechua uniquely shows obligatory grammatical evidential marking integrated with truth vocabulary:

- chiqapmi "it is truly so (I know directly)"
- chiqapsi "it is reportedly true"
- chiqapcha "it seems to be true"

No other language shows grammatical evidentials, though all have optional epistemic modals or adverbs. This represents typologically rare feature specific to Quechua and related languages.

**4. Ablative Standpoint Marking (Sanskrit)**

Sanskrit uniquely uses ablative case systematically for marking philosophical standpoint:

- परमार्थतः paramārthataḥ "from ultimate standpoint"
- व्यवहारतः vyavahārataḥ "from conventional standpoint"

Quechua has ablative case but doesn't use it systematically for standpoint marking. Other languages lack equivalent grammatical resource. This represents Sanskrit-specific philosophical elaboration.

**5. Binyan/Voice Alternations (Hebrew)**

Hebrew uniquely shows Semitic root-and-pattern morphology creating systematic voice alternations:

- pa'al → pi'el → hif'il → huf'al
- Example: צדק → הצדיק (causative) → מוצדק (passive causative)

No equivalent in other languages, which use affixation or separate lexemes. This represents Semitic morphological typology.

**6. Dual Number for Oppositions (Sanskrit, Hebrew)**

Only Sanskrit and Hebrew productively use grammatical dual number for binary oppositions:

- Sanskrit: सत्यासत्ययोः satyāsatyayoḥ "of truth and falsehood"
- Hebrew: similar dual constructions

Other languages use coordination (and/or) instead. This represents grammatical resource available only in languages with dual number.

### Systematic Asymmetries and Blocked Pathways

**1. Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry**

**Pattern**: Most languages show productive agentive formation for "liar" but gaps or circumlocution for "truth-teller."

**Evidence**:
- **Productive for liar**: Hebrew שקרן, Sanskrit मिथ्यावादिन्, Greek ψεύτης, Tagalog sinungaling, Swahili mwongo, Quechua llullakuq
- **Gaps for truth-teller**: English (compound truth-teller, not *truther), Chinese (circumlocution 说真话的人), Hebrew (participial phrase), Tagalog (no dedicated form), Swahili (possessive mwenye ukweli)
- **Productive for both**: Sanskrit सत्यवादिन्/मिथ्यावादिन्, Quechua chiqan rimaq/llullakuq

**Interpretation**: Negative moral evaluation (lying) drives lexicalization more strongly than positive evaluation (truth-telling). This suggests a universal cognitive or social bias toward marking and naming negative moral agents.

**2. Causative Verification vs. Falsification Asymmetry**

**Pattern**: Causatives for "make true/verify" are more common and productive than causatives for "make false/falsify."

**Evidence**:
- **Productive verification causatives**: Hebrew אימת, Greek βεβαιώνω, Tagalog patunayan, Swahili thibitisha, Quechua chiqanchay
- **Limited falsification causatives**: Most languages lack or rarely use causatives for "make false." Greek shows some symmetry (διαψεύδω "disprove") but others don't.
- **Alternative strategies**: Languages use separate roots for falsification (English falsify from different root) or describe falsification through deception verbs rather than direct causatives.

**Interpretation**: Truth-establishment is conceptualized as active process requiring causative morphology, while falsification may be seen as passive state or achieved through deception rather than direct causation. This suggests asymmetric conceptualization of how truth and falsehood come about.

**3. Inchoative Restrictions**

**Pattern**: Even in languages with productive inchoative morphology, "become true" shows restrictions not seen with "become good/better."

**Evidence**:
- **English**: come true (fixed) but *come false, *become true (marked)
- **Chinese**: 变好 biàn hǎo "become good" productive, but 变真 biàn zhēn "become true" marginal
- **Hebrew**: להשתפר "improve" productive, but truth inchoatives use different constructions (התברר "became clear")

**Interpretation**: "Becoming true" is conceptualized differently from general improvement. Languages prefer revelation/discovery constructions (truth emerging, coming to light) over simple inchoative "become true." This suggests truth is seen as pre-existing and revealed rather than coming into being.

**4. Positive vs. Negative Agentive Elaboration**

**Pattern**: Across languages, vocabulary for negative agents (liar, deceiver, fraud) is more elaborate than for positive agents (truth-teller, honest person).

**Evidence**:
- **Chinese**: Multiple deception terms (骗子 piànzi "fraud", 说谎者 shuōhuǎng zhě "liar") but no simple truth-teller
- **Greek**: ψεύτης "liar", απατεώνας "swindler", υποκριτής "hypocrite" but no single truth-teller
- **English**: liar (suppletive), deceiver, fraud but truth-teller (marginal compound)
- **Tagalog**: sinungaling "liar", mandaya "cheater" but no dedicated truth-teller

**Interpretation**: Negative moral evaluation drives lexicalization more than positive. This may reflect universal cognitive bias toward marking threats or violations more than virtues.

### Recurrent Patterns Across Unrelated Families

**1. Causative Morphology for Verification (6/8 Languages)**

Six languages from four unrelated families show productive causative derivation for verification:

- **Semitic**: Hebrew hif'il causative
- **Indo-European**: Greek -ώνω causative, Sanskrit (limited)
- **Austronesian**: Tagalog pa- causative
- **Niger-Congo**: Swahili -isha causative
- **Quechuan**: Quechua -cha- causative

This represents convergent evolution: unrelated languages independently develop causative morphology and apply it to truth-domain. The function (establishing truth through active process) appears universal even though formal realization varies.

**Exceptions**: Chinese uses resultative compounds instead. English uses separate borrowed roots (verify < Latin).

**2. Discourse Marker Grammaticalization (7/8 Languages)**

Seven languages from all families except classical Sanskrit show grammaticalization of truth terms into discourse markers:

- **Sino-Tibetan**: Chinese 说真的
- **Semitic**: Hebrew באמת
- **Indo-European**: Greek αλήθεια?, English to be honest
- **Austronesian**: Tagalog totoo lang
- **Niger-Congo**: Swahili kwa kweli
- **Quechuan**: Quechua chiqap

This represents a universal pragmatic pathway: truth-asserting terms → markers of speaker stance/sincerity. The pathway appears active in all living languages, suggesting ongoing grammaticalization process.

**Sanskrit exception**: Classical language with limited colloquial development lacks this pragmatic grammaticalization.

**3. Abstract Nominalization Productivity (8/8 Languages)**

All languages show productive abstract nominalization, though strategies vary:

- **Suffixation**: Chinese, Hebrew, Sanskrit, Greek, English (most common cross-linguistically)
- **Circumfixation**: Tagalog, Swahili (Austronesian and Bantu strategy)
- **Periphrasis**: Quechua (agglutinative strategy)

Despite formal variation, all show high productivity and no restrictions on which truth-domain terms can nominalize. This suggests universal need for abstract quality nouns in philosophical and formal discourse.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**1. Agentive Nominalization: Productive for Negative, Blocked for Positive**

**Pattern**: Most languages productively form agentive nouns for "liar/deceiver" but block or lack productive formation for "truth-teller."

**Evidence**:
- **Productive negative**: Hebrew שקרן (productive -an suffix), Greek ψεύτης (productive -της), Swahili mwongo (productive m- prefix), Quechua llullakuq (productive -q suffix)
- **Blocked positive**: English *truther (unattested; uses compound truth-teller), Chinese (no 真者; uses circumlocution), Hebrew (no *אמתן; uses participial phrase), Tagalog (no dedicated form)

**Exceptions**: Sanskrit सत्यवादिन् satyavādin shows productive formation for both poles.

**Interpretation**: This represents a systematic cross-linguistic asymmetry. Negative moral agents are more readily lexicalized than positive ones. This may reflect:
- Cognitive salience of threats/violations
- Social need to identify and warn about dishonest persons
- Positive behavior seen as default or less marked

**2. Causative Falsification: Rare or Uses Different Strategies**

**Pattern**: While causatives for "make true/verify" are productive, causatives for "make false/falsify" are rare or use different roots.

**Evidence**:
- **Verification causatives productive**: Hebrew אימת, Greek βεβαιώνω, Tagalog patunayan, Swahili thibitisha, Quechua chiqanchay
- **Falsification causatives rare**: Most languages lack productive causative "make false" from false/lie roots
- **Alternative strategies**: English falsify (from different root), Greek uses deception verbs, others describe falsification through deception rather than direct causation

**Interpretation**: Truth-establishment is conceptualized as active process requiring causative morphology. Falsification is conceptualized differently—either as passive state, as achieved through deception (different verb class), or as corruption/destruction rather than direct causation.

**3. Inchoative "Become True": Restricted or Uses Different Constructions**

**Pattern**: Even in languages with productive inchoative morphology, "become true" shows restrictions.

**Evidence**:
- **English**: come true (lexically restricted to this phrase), *become true (marked), prove true (evidential, not pure inchoative)
- **Chinese**: 变真 biàn zhēn (marginal), prefers 成为现实 chéngwéi xiànshí "become reality"
- **Hebrew**: Uses התברר hitbarer "became clear" (different root) rather than inchoative of אמת
- **Productive languages**: Tagalog, Swahili, Quechua allow inchoatives but often prefer revelation/discovery constructions

**Interpretation**: "Becoming true" is conceptualized as revelation or discovery of pre-existing truth rather than truth coming into being. Languages prefer:
- Revelation metaphors (truth emerging, coming to light)
- Evidential constructions (proved true, turned out to be true)
- Realization of predictions (dreams came true)

This suggests truth is seen as pre-existing and discovered rather than created or achieved.

**4. Positive Evaluation Terms: Broader Than Truth-Specific**

**Pattern**: Languages often lack dedicated "truth-teller" or "honest person" agentives but have general positive evaluation terms that include honesty.

**Evidence**:
- **Quechua**: allin runa "good person" (includes honest) rather than dedicated *chiqan runa agentive
- **Swahili**: Uses mtu mwema "good person" or possessive mwenye ukweli rather than dedicated agentive
- **English**: honest person, truthful person (adjective + noun) rather than dedicated agentive *honester

**Interpretation**: Positive moral qualities may be less individuated in agentive formation. Languages prefer general "good person" terms or adjectival modification over dedicated agentives for specific virtues. This contrasts with negative agents (liar, thief, murderer) which are more readily individuated.

### Productivity Assessments

**Highly Productive Across Languages**:

1. **Abstract nominalization**: No restrictions observed; applies to all truth-domain terms in all languages
2. **Morphological negation**: Extremely productive; creates systematic oppositions across all derived forms
3. **Light verb constructions**: Highly conventionalized but productive with new truth-domain terms
4. **Adverbial derivation**: Productive across all languages; unrestricted application
5. **Causative verification**: Productive in 6/8 languages with causative morphology; common in formal registers

**Moderately Productive**:

1. **Inchoative derivation**: Productive in 4/8 languages but shows lexical restrictions even where available
2. **Passive derivation**: Productive in 6/8 languages but applies primarily to transitive/causative verbs
3. **Compounding**: Productive across all languages but with language-specific formal strategies
4. **Discourse marker grammaticalization**: Active process in 7/8 languages but creates fixed expressions rather than productive pattern

**Restricted or Lexicalized**:

1. **Agentive nominalization (positive)**: Blocked or uses circumlocution in 6/8 languages
2. **Causative falsification**: Rare or uses different strategies across languages
3. **Reduplication**: Productive only in Chinese and Tagalog; marginal or absent in others
4. **Reflexive marking**: Productive in 4/8 languages but with varying functions
5. **Reciprocal derivation**: Productive in 3/8 languages; applies mainly to argumentative verbs

**Fossilized or Fixed Expressions**:

1. **English come true**: Fixed expression, not productive (*come false ungrammatical)
2. **Greek proverbs**: το ψέμα έχει κοντά ποδάρια "lies have short legs" (fixed)
3. **Chinese four-character idioms**: 名副其实 míng fù qí shí (fixed, not productive pattern)
4. **Hebrew biblical phrases**: אמת ויציב emet veyatsiv (liturgical, fixed)

### Summary of Major Findings

**Universal or Near-Universal Strategies**:

1. Abstract nominalization (8/8 languages)
2. Morphological negation (8/8 languages)
3. Light verb constructions (8/8 languages)
4. Adverbial derivation (8/8 languages)
5. Compounding (8/8 languages, varying strategies)

**Widely Attested Strategies**:

1. Causative derivation for verification (6/8 languages)
2. Discourse marker grammaticalization (7/8 languages)
3. Passive derivation (6/8 languages)
4. Prepositional/case adverbials (6/8 languages)

**Systematic Asymmetries**:

1. Agentive nominalization more productive for negative (liar) than positive (truth-teller)
2. Causative verification more productive than causative falsification
3. Inchoative "become true" restricted compared to "become good/better"
4. Negative evaluation vocabulary generally more elaborate than positive

**Language-Specific Elaborations**:

1. Greek pseudo-X compounding (critical/polemical discourse)
2. Chinese resultative verb compounds (characteristic morphological strategy)
3. Sanskrit ablative standpoint marking (philosophical framework)
4. Quechua evidential integration (typologically rare)
5. Hebrew binyan alternations (Semitic morphology)

**Typological Observations**:

- **Isolating languages** (Chinese) favor compounding and resultative constructions
- **Fusional languages** (Greek, Sanskrit) favor suffixation and templatic morphology
- **Agglutinative languages** (Tagalog, Swahili, Quechua) favor affixation and circumfixation
- **All types** show convergent evolution in causative verification and discourse marker development

These patterns suggest both universal cognitive/pragmatic pressures (need for abstract nouns, verification processes, discourse management) and language-specific morphological resources (compounding, affixation, templatic morphology) that shape how truth-domain vocabulary is elaborated.
