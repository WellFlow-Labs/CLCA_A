# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Where a P1 item is itself a derived form (e.g., *ʾaminut* from *ʾamin*, *ʾamiti* from *ʾemet*), derivations are listed from that item as base, not re-derived from the root. Forms already documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described. Gaps are preserved and noted explicitly. Derivations are listed per P1 item in order; no predefined category labels are imposed.

---

## P1 Item 1 — אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)

---

**1.1**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** *ʾamiti*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival suffix *-i*
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
**Usage:** The standard adjectival form of *ʾemet*, used to describe persons, feelings, or objects as genuinely what they appear to be. Already documented as P1 item 7; cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּיּוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾamitiyyut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾamiti* (true, adjectival) + abstract noun suffix *-yut*
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, genuineness, authenticity as an abstract quality
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and literary discourse when a fully abstract nominal form is required. Less frequent than *ʾemet* itself. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 19).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**1.3**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** לְאַמֵּת
**Transliteration:** *leʾammet*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to authenticate, to confirm
**Usage:** The standard modern Hebrew verb for verifying or authenticating a document, claim, or identity. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 4).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** אִמּוּת
**Transliteration:** *immut*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el verbal noun
**Natural English translation:** verification, authentication, validation
**Usage:** The nominal form corresponding to *leʾammet*. Common in legal, administrative, and digital security contexts. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Derived Form:** מְאֻמָּת
**Transliteration:** *meʾummat*
**Literal Breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pu'al passive participle
**Natural English translation:** verified, authenticated, confirmed
**Usage:** Adjectival form describing something that has been officially verified or authenticated. Common in legal and administrative Hebrew. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 7).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *ʾemet*:** The root א-מ-ת generates a productive verbal system via the Pi'el binyan (*leʾammet*, *immut*, *meʾummat*). The adjectival branch (*ʾamiti*, *ʾamitiyyut*) is also attested. No Qal active verb in common modern use has been identified with this root as a standalone form; the Qal infinitive *leʾemot* is marginal (documented in P1b). No causative or reflexive derivations beyond those listed have been identified as commonly used.

## P1 Item 2 — אמין (*ʾamin*)

---

**2.1**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִינוּת
**Transliteration:** *ʾaminut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾamin* (reliable) + abstract noun suffix *-ut*
**Natural English translation:** reliability, credibility, trustworthiness
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for the property of being reliable or credible. Already documented as P1 item 8; cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.2**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Derived Form:** לֹא אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *lo ʾamin*
**Literal Breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *ʾamin* (reliable)
**Natural English translation:** unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
**Usage:** The standard negated form used to describe a person, source, or system that cannot be trusted. Functions as a fixed evaluative phrase in everyday and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *ʾamin*:** *ʾamin* is itself an adjectival form from root א-מ-נ. Its primary derivational product in common use is the abstract noun *ʾaminut*. No verbal form derived directly from *ʾamin* as a base (rather than from the root) has been identified. The negation *lo ʾamin* is listed as it functions as a conventionalized evaluative unit.

## P1 Item 3 — נכון (*nakhon*)

---

**3.1**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** נְכוֹנוּת
**Transliteration:** *nekhonot*
**Literal Breakdown:** *nakhon* (correct, right) + abstract noun suffix *-ut*
**Natural English translation:** correctness, accuracy, rightness
**Usage:** The abstract noun denoting the quality of being correct or accurate. Used in formal and written Hebrew to describe the correctness of a statement, procedure, or assessment. Less common in everyday speech than *nakhon* itself.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.2**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** לֹא נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** *lo nakhon*
**Literal Breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *nakhon* (correct)
**Natural English translation:** incorrect, wrong, not right
**Usage:** The standard negated form used in everyday Hebrew to indicate that something is incorrect or false. Extremely common as a response to an inaccurate statement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.3**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Derived Form:** לְהַכִּין
**Transliteration:** *lehakhin*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-ו-נ, Hif'il infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to prepare, to set up, to establish
**Usage:** A verbal form from the same root כ-ו-נ, meaning to prepare or establish something. Noted here because it shares the root with *nakhon* but represents a distinct semantic branch of the root (preparation/establishment rather than correctness). Included to document the root's verbal productivity while flagging the semantic divergence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *nakhon*:** *nakhon* is a Nif'al passive participle from root כ-ו-נ. Its most direct derivational product is the abstract noun *nekhonot*. The root כ-ו-נ is highly productive but generates forms (such as *lehakhin*, *kavvana*, *tikun*) that diverge significantly in meaning from the correctness/accuracy sense of *nakhon*. Those forms are not listed here as derivations of *nakhon* the P1 item, only the abstract noun directly formed from it.

**Transliteration:** *diyyuq*

**Natural English translation:** precision, accuracy, exactness

---

**4.2**
**Base Form:** מדויק (*meduyaq*)

**Transliteration:** *ledayyeq*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to be precise, to specify exactly, to examine closely
**Usage:** The standard verb meaning to be precise or to specify something exactly. Used in everyday and formal Hebrew when asking for or providing exact information. Common in the phrase *ledayyeq be-* (to be precise about, to examine closely).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.3**
**Base Form:** מדויק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** מְדַיֵּק
**Transliteration:** *medayyeq*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ד-י-ק, Pi'el active participle
**Natural English translation:** precise person, one who is exact; being precise
**Usage:** Used as an adjective or nominal to describe a person who is characteristically precise or meticulous, or to describe the act of being precise in a given moment. Common in everyday and professional discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.4**
**Base Form:** מדויק (*meduyaq*)
**Derived Form:** אִי-דִּיּוּק
**Transliteration:** *ʾi-diyyuq*
**Literal Breakdown:** *ʾi-* (negating prefix) + *diyyuq* (precision)
**Natural English translation:** imprecision, inaccuracy, lack of exactness
**Usage:** Used in formal and written Hebrew to describe the absence of precision in a statement, measurement, or representation. Common in academic, legal, and journalistic criticism of inaccurate claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *meduyaq*:** *meduyaq* is itself a Pi'el passive participle (or Pu'al form) from root ד-י-ק. The root generates a productive Pi'el verbal system (*ledayyeq*, *diyyuq*, *medayyeq*) and the negated nominal *ʾi-diyyuq*. No Qal or Hif'il forms of this root in the precision/accuracy sense have been identified as commonly used.

## P1 Item 10 — כנות (*kanut*)

---

**10.1**
**Base Form:** כנות (*kanut*)
**Derived Form:** כָּן
**Transliteration:** *kan*
**Literal Breakdown:** root כ-נ-נ or כ-ו-נ, adjectival base form
**Natural English translation:** sincere, honest, genuine
**Usage:** The adjectival base from which *kanut* is derived. Used to describe a person or expression as sincere and honest. Less common in everyday modern Hebrew than *kanut* itself but attested in formal and literary registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *kanut*:** *kanut* is itself an abstract noun (the *-ut* nominalization of *kan*). The relationship here is the reverse of the typical base-to-derivation direction: *kan* is the adjectival base and *kanut* is its nominalization. No further derivations from *kanut* as a base have been identified. The verbal form *lehitkanen* (to be sincere, to mean sincerely) is occasionally attested but is not common in everyday usage.

**10.2**
**Base Form:** כנות (*kanut*)
**Derived Form:** בְּכַנּוּת
**Transliteration:** *bekhannot*
**Literal Breakdown:** *be-* (in, with) + *kanut* (sincerity)
**Natural English translation:** sincerely, honestly, genuinely
**Usage:** An adverbial phrase used to mark a statement as sincere or honest. Common in everyday speech as a sincerity marker, parallel to *beʾemet* but with a stronger interpersonal/moral connotation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**11.3**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (*sheqer*)
**Derived Form:** שַׁקְרָנוּת
**Transliteration:** *shaqranut*
**Literal Breakdown:** *shaqran* (liar) + abstract noun suffix *-ut*
**Natural English translation:** habitual lying, mendacity, dishonesty
**Usage:** The abstract noun denoting the quality or habit of lying. Used in moral and legal discourse to describe a pattern of dishonest behavior rather than a single act of lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.4**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (*sheqer*)
**Derived Form:** לְשַׁקֵּר
**Transliteration:** *leshaqer*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ש-ק-ר, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell falsehoods
**Usage:** The standard verb for lying in modern Hebrew. Used across all registers and contexts. The Pi'el participle *meshaqer* (one who is lying) and perfect *shiqer* are equally common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.5**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (*sheqer*)
**Derived Form:** שִׁקְרִי
**Transliteration:** *shiqri*
**Literal Breakdown:** root ש-ק-ר + adjectival suffix *-i*
**Natural English translation:** false, mendacious, lying (adjective)
**Usage:** An adjectival form used to describe something as false or mendacious — a false claim, a lying account. Used in formal and written Hebrew; less common in everyday speech than the phrase *sheqer* used predicatively.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *sheqer*:** The root ש-ק-ר is productive, generating a verbal system (*leshaqer*), agentive nouns (*shaqran*, *shaqranit*), an abstract noun of habitual quality (*shaqranut*), and an adjective (*shiqri*). This is one of the more morphologically productive roots in the P1 set.

## Summary of Derivational Productivity

The following observations are directly supported by the data above and are offered without interpretive categorization:

- **Highest derivational productivity** is observed for roots ש-ק-ר (*sheqer*), כ-ש-ר (*kasher*), צ-ד-ק (*tsedeq*), and ס-ב-ר (*savir*), each generating verbal systems, agentive or abstract nouns, and adjectival forms.

- **The *-ut* suffix** (abstract noun formation) is the most consistently productive derivational process across P1 items, attested for nearly every adjectival base form in the set.

- **The *ʾi-* negating prefix** is productive with abstract nouns (*ʾi-diyyuq*, *ʾi-teqinut*, *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut*, *ʾi-legitimiyyut*, *ʾi-sevirut*, *ʾi-teqifut*), particularly in legal and administrative registers.

- **Loanwords** (*ʾotenti*, *legitimi*) show limited morphological integration, generating abstract nouns via *-yut* but no verbal forms.

- **Items that are themselves abstract nouns** (*ʾaminut*, *yosher*, *ziyyuf*, *diyyuq*) show reduced further derivational productivity, consistent with the general pattern that abstract nominals are endpoints rather than bases in Hebrew derivational chains.
