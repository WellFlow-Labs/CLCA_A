# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

The groupings below are derived from observed patterns in the P1 and P2 data: which items co-occur, which are explicitly contrasted with each other, which appear in the same institutional contexts, and which share characteristic verb collocates or syntactic frames. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. Items that do not fit cleanly into any group, or that fit into multiple groups, are identified explicitly at the end.

---

## GROUP 1 — Expressions used to assert or deny that a specific thing is what it appears or claims to be

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a particular object, document, person, or product is genuinely what it presents itself as, rather than a substitute, imitation, or counterfeit. They appear characteristically in authentication contexts — art appraisal, document verification, commercial fraud investigation, and customs enforcement — and their typical verbs are 辨别, 鉴定, 鉴别, and 识别. The central question framed by these expressions is not "is this statement true?" but "is this thing the genuine article?"

**Representative expressions:**
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — frames the question of genuine vs. counterfeit as a binary to be resolved
- 辨伪 (biànwěi) — the scholarly/philological practice of detecting forgeries
- 伪造 (wěizào) — the act of fabricating a false artifact
- 仿冒 (fǎngmào) — fraudulent imitation of a genuine product or mark
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — passing off a counterfeit as genuine
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎ mào wěi liè) — the fixed regulatory phrase for counterfeit and substandard goods
- 原版 (yuánbǎn) — the original, unaltered version against which copies are measured
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — the everyday colloquial framing of genuine vs. fake
- 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn) — the process of discarding false elements and retaining genuine ones
- 难辨真伪 — the fixed phrase for situations where genuine and counterfeit are hard to distinguish
- 以假乱真 — passing off fakes as genuine by mixing them with real items
- 经鉴定系伪造 — the standard administrative finding of forgery

**Notes:** 真伪 and 真假 overlap substantially but 真伪 is more formal and associated with expert authentication, while 真假 is more colloquial. 辨伪 is domain-specific to philological and archival practice. 伪造者 (D-22) and 伪造罪 (D-23) are institutional extensions of this group into legal categorization of agents and offenses.

---

## GROUP 2 — Expressions used to evaluate whether an account, report, or statement corresponds to the facts

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a verbal or written representation — a news report, a witness statement, a historical record, an advertisement — accurately reflects what actually happened or what is actually the case. They appear characteristically in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts, and their typical verbs include 核实, 查实, 属实, 反映, 陈述, and 保证. The central question is not about the genuineness of a physical object but about the accuracy of a claim or account.

**Representative expressions:**
- 真实 (zhēnshí) — the general evaluative term for whether an account is factually accurate
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — the abstract property of being factually accurate, used in formal verification contexts
- 如实 (rúshí) — the adverbial instruction to report in accordance with the facts
- 失实 (shīshí) — the evaluation that a report has deviated from the facts
- 失真 (shīzhēn) — deviation from accurate representation, used in both media and technical contexts
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — the formal/legal evaluation of a statement or representation as false
- 相符 (xiāngfú) — the evaluation that a claim and the facts are in correspondence
- 属实 (shǔshí) — the administrative confirmation that a reported matter is factually accurate
- 核实 (héshí) — the process of checking whether information is accurate
- 查实 (cháshí) — verification through investigation
- 不真实 (bù zhēnshí) — the direct negation used in legal and journalistic evaluation
- 报道失实 — the standard phrase for inaccurate reporting
- 如实陈述 — the legal instruction to state truthfully
- 与事实相符 / 与事实不符 — the standard legal frames for correspondence or non-correspondence with facts
- 真实性审查 (D-31) — formal review process for factual accuracy

**Notes:** 失实 and 失真 overlap in media criticism contexts but 失真 extends into technical domains (audio distortion) where 失实 does not appear. 虚假 is more formal and carries stronger negative connotation than 不真实; it is the preferred term in regulatory and legal language (虚假广告, 虚假陈述).

---

## GROUP 3 — Expressions used in contexts of seeking, revealing, or concealing what actually happened

**Definition:** These expressions cluster around the activity of uncovering or suppressing the actual facts of a situation, particularly where there is an assumption that surface appearances differ from underlying reality. They appear characteristically in investigative journalism, legal proceedings, and political discourse, and their typical verbs are 揭露, 查明, 掩盖, 了解, and 还原. The framing is dynamic — something hidden is being brought to light or kept from view.

**Representative expressions:**
- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — the actual state of affairs behind appearances; the central noun of this group
- 揭露真相 — to expose the truth (implies prior concealment)
- 查明真相 — to establish the truth through investigation
- 掩盖真相 — to cover up the truth
- 真相大白 — the moment when concealed facts become known
- 不明真相的群众 — people who have been kept from knowing the true situation
- 了解真相 — to come to know the actual facts
- 还原真实情况 — to reconstruct the true situation from distorted accounts
- 真实情况 (zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — the actual circumstances, as opposed to reported or alleged versions
- 实情 (shíqíng) — the true situation, often used when truth has been concealed
- 说出实情 — to tell the true situation

**Notes:** 真相 is the dominant noun in this group and is strongly associated with the verb 揭露 ("expose"). 实情 overlaps with 真相 but is slightly less dramatic in connotation and more commonly used in administrative contexts. 真实情况 is a more neutral noun phrase that can appear in this group or in Group 2 depending on context.

---

## GROUP 4 — Expressions used to evaluate the precision or exactness of measurements, descriptions, and translations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess the degree to which a measurement, description, translation, or prediction matches its target with precision. They appear characteristically in scientific, technical, journalistic, and quality-control contexts, and they treat accuracy as a scalar property that can be more or less sufficient. The typical syntactic frames involve degree adverbs (不够准确, 足够准确) and degree nominalizations (-性, -度).

**Representative expressions:**
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — the primary evaluative term for precision and exactness
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — the abstract property of being precise, used in formal evaluation
- 不准确 (bù zhǔnquè) — the negation, used to evaluate measurements or descriptions as falling short
- 准确无误 — the fixed phrase asserting complete accuracy without error
- 准确地描述事实 — the standard adverbial construction for accurate description
- 数据不够准确 — the scalar evaluation of insufficient precision

**Notes:** 准确 is distinguished from 正确 in the data by its emphasis on degree of correspondence and its association with measurement and technical precision, whereas 正确 is used for binary correctness (right vs. wrong). This distinction is supported by the different contexts in which they appear: 准确 collocates with 数据, 翻译, 测量; 正确 collocates with 答案, 方法, 道路. However, the boundary is not absolute and the two terms overlap in some evaluative contexts.

---

## GROUP 5 — Expressions used to evaluate whether an action, claim, or status conforms to legal or institutional requirements

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether something meets the formal requirements of law, regulation, or institutional procedure. They appear characteristically in administrative, legal, and commercial contexts, and their typical verbs are 保护, 维护, 通过, and 质疑. The evaluation is against an external standard (law, regulation) rather than against factual reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- 合法 (héfǎ) — the primary evaluative term for conformity with law
- 合法性 (héfǎxìng) — the abstract property of legality or legitimacy
- 合法化 (héfǎhuà) — the process of making something legal
- 正当 (zhèngdàng) — evaluation of an action as morally and legally justified
- 正当性 (zhèngdàngxìng) — the abstract property of legitimacy or justifiability
- 正当防卫 — the fixed legal term for legitimate self-defense
- 合法权益 — the standard phrase for lawful rights and interests
- 通过合法途径 — the standard phrase for acting through legal channels
- 通过正当手段 — the standard phrase for acting through legitimate means
- 非合法 (fēi héfǎ) — the more technical negation, distinguishing non-legal from illegal

**Notes:** 合法 and 正当 are distinguished in the data by register and scope: 合法 is more narrowly tied to legal conformity, while 正当 encompasses moral justifiability as well. 正当防卫 is a fixed legal term that anchors 正当 in criminal law. 合法化 is a productive derivation that does not have a parallel 正当化 in the data.

---

## GROUP 6 — Expressions used to evaluate the sincerity or genuineness of a person's intentions and feelings

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a person's expressed feelings, intentions, or conduct reflect their actual inner state, rather than being performed for effect. They appear characteristically in interpersonal, moral, and political contexts, and their typical verbs are 表达, 道歉, 感谢, and 对待. The evaluation is about the relationship between outward expression and inward state, not about correspondence with external facts.

**Representative expressions:**
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — the primary evaluative term for sincerity of intention and expression
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — sincerity of feeling, slightly more colloquial than 真诚
- 真心实意 (zhēn xīn shí yì) — the four-character expression emphasizing both genuine feeling and genuine intent
- 虚伪 (xūwěi) — the moral evaluation of outward presentation that conceals a contrary inner state
- 虚伪性 (xūwěixìng) — the abstract property of hypocrisy
- 伪善 (wěishàn) — false virtue, the performance of goodness without genuine moral commitment
- 撕下虚伪的面具 — the metaphorical construction for exposing hypocrisy
- 真诚地道歉 — the standard construction for sincere apology
- 缺乏真诚 — the evaluative construction for absence of sincerity

**Notes:** 真诚 and 真心 overlap substantially but 真心 is more colloquial and more focused on emotional sincerity, while 真诚 extends to sincerity of conduct and commitment. 虚伪 and 伪善 are both negative moral evaluations but 伪善 is more specifically about the performance of virtue, while 虚伪 is broader. This group is distinct from Group 1 (authentication of objects) and Group 2 (accuracy of accounts) in that the evaluation is of a person's inner state rather than of a thing or a claim.

---

## GROUP 7 — Expressions used in philosophical and ideological discourse about truth as a principle or value

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth is discussed not as a property of specific claims or objects but as an abstract principle, a goal of inquiry, or a fundamental value. They appear characteristically in philosophical, educational, and political-ideological discourse, and they are associated with verbs like 追求, 坚持, and 标准. They are not typically used to evaluate specific claims or objects.

**Representative expressions:**
- 真理 (zhēnlǐ) — truth as a universal principle or fundamental proposition
- 真理性 (zhēnlǐxìng) — the property of being a genuine truth, used in philosophical and ideological evaluation
- 真善美 (zhēn shàn měi) — the philosophical triad of truth, goodness, and beauty
- 求真 (qiúzhēn) — the pursuit of truth as an epistemic goal
- 求真务实 (qiú zhēn wù shí) — the political-administrative slogan combining truth-seeking with pragmatism
- 实事求是 (shí shì qiú shì) — the foundational Maoist epistemological principle of seeking truth from facts
- 真知 (zhēnzhī) — genuine knowledge as opposed to superficial or mistaken understanding
- 真知灼见 (zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn) — penetrating insight grounded in genuine understanding

**Notes:** 求真务实 and 实事求是 overlap in political-administrative usage and are often cited together, but 实事求是 has a more specific ideological provenance (Maoist epistemology) while 求真务实 is more broadly used in contemporary administrative discourse. 真理 is distinct from 真相 (Group 3) in that 真理 refers to abstract or universal propositions while 真相 refers to the specific facts of a particular situation.

---

## GROUP 8 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a name, title, or reputation corresponds to actual substance

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a label, designation, or reputation accurately reflects the actual nature or quality of what it names. They appear in evaluative contexts across journalism, commerce, and political commentary, and they frame the question as one of correspondence between a nominal claim and an underlying reality. The typical syntactic pattern is attributive (名副其实的 + N) or predicative (可以说是名副其实).

**Representative expressions:**
- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — the name lives up to the reality; genuinely what it claims to be
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — the name does not live up to the reality
- 名实 (míngshí) — the classical binomial framing the name-reality relationship
- 名实相符 — name and reality correspond
- 名实不符 — name and reality do not correspond
- 伪命题 (wěi mìngtí) — a proposition or question that is spurious because its framing is false
- 伪科学 (wěi kēxué) — a field that presents itself as science but lacks genuine scientific character

**Notes:** 名副其实 and 名实相符 overlap in meaning but differ in register: 名副其实 is a common 成语 used across registers, while 名实相符 is more classical and literary. 伪命题 and 伪科学 extend the name-reality concern into categorical and disciplinary boundary-marking, where the question is whether something genuinely belongs to the category it claims.

---

## GROUP 9 — Expressions used in contexts of verifying or confirming information through investigation

**Definition:** These expressions describe or evaluate the process of checking whether information, claims, or reports are factually accurate through active investigation or examination. They appear characteristically in administrative, legal, and journalistic contexts, and they are associated with procedural language about what has been done to establish accuracy. The focus is on the epistemic process of verification rather than on the property of being accurate.

**Representative expressions:**
- 核实 (héshí) — to verify, to check the accuracy of information
- 查实 (cháshí) — to confirm through investigation
- 属实 (shǔshí) — the result of verification: confirmed as accurate
- 经查属实 — the standard administrative phrase for "verified as true upon investigation"
- 真实性审查 (D-31) — formal review process for factual accuracy
- 鉴定真伪 — to determine through expert examination whether something is genuine or forged
- 经鉴定系伪造 — the standard finding of forgery through expert examination
- 证据的可靠性 — the reliability of evidence as an object of assessment

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 2 (accuracy of accounts) in that verification processes are typically directed at claims and reports. The distinction is that Group 2 contains expressions that evaluate the property of accuracy, while this group contains expressions that describe the process of establishing accuracy. 核实 and 查实 are process verbs; 属实 is a result predicate. 鉴定真伪 bridges this group and Group 1 (authentication of objects).

---

## GROUP 10 — Expressions used to evaluate the reliability or credibility of sources, evidence, and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a source of information, a piece of evidence, or a witness's testimony can be depended upon or merits belief. They appear characteristically in legal, journalistic, and intelligence contexts, and they treat reliability and credibility as assessable properties that can be questioned, established, or measured. The evaluation is about the source or medium of information rather than about a specific claim's content.

**Representative expressions:**
- 可靠 (kěkào) — reliable, dependable; used for sources, systems, and persons
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) — the abstract property of reliability
- 不可靠 (bù kěkào) — unreliable
- 可信 (kěxìn) — credible, believable; used specifically for testimony and claims
- 可信性 (kěxìnxìng) — the abstract property of credibility
- 可信度 (kěxìndù) — credibility as a scalar, measurable property
- 不可信 (bù kěxìn) — not credible
- 来源可靠 — the standard evaluative phrase for a reliable source
- 证词是否可信 — the standard legal framing of credibility as an open question
- 令人可信的解释 — a credible explanation

**Notes:** 可靠 and 可信 are distinguished in the data by their typical objects: 可靠 applies to sources, systems, channels, and persons as dependable entities, while 可信 applies more specifically to statements, testimony, and claims as meriting belief. This distinction is supported by their different collocates: 可靠 collocates with 来源, 渠道, 系统; 可信 collocates with 证词, 陈述, 解释. 可信度 is more common than 可信性 in contemporary usage, suggesting that credibility is more often treated as a scalar property than as a binary one.

---

## GROUP 11 — Expressions used in aesthetic and representational evaluation of how closely something approximates reality

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate the degree to which a representation — a painting, a performance, a photograph, a literary work — faithfully or vividly approximates the reality it depicts. They appear characteristically in artistic, literary, and critical contexts, and they treat fidelity to reality as an aesthetic value. The evaluation is not about whether a claim is true but about whether a representation is lifelike or realistic.

**Representative expressions:**
- 逼真 (bīzhēn) — lifelike, true to life; used in aesthetic evaluation of representations
- 写真 (xiězhēn) — realistic depiction; in modern usage, a photographic portrait session
- 写实 (xiěshí) — realistic in artistic style; the aesthetic doctrine of representing reality faithfully
- 写实主义 (xiěshí zhǔyì) — realism as an artistic or literary movement
- 真实地再现 — to faithfully recreate (used in film and historical representation)
- 真实记录 (zhēnshí jìlù) — a faithful record or accurate documentation
- 实录 (shílù) — a veritable record; faithful historical documentation

**Notes:** 逼真 and 写实 both concern fidelity to reality in representation but differ in emphasis: 逼真 is a scalar degree term ("how close to real") used in evaluating specific representations, while 写实 names an aesthetic approach or doctrine. 写真 in modern usage has shifted toward a commercial photographic genre, with the truth-correspondence dimension receding (noted in P1b Step 3). 实录 is more specifically associated with official historical documentation.

---

## GROUP 12 — Expressions used in administrative and political discourse to describe fact-based, pragmatic conduct

**Definition:** These expressions appear in administrative, political, and organizational contexts to describe an approach to work or governance that is grounded in actual facts and practical realities rather than in formalism, ideology, or empty rhetoric. They are associated with positive evaluation of conduct and are often used in official discourse to characterize a desirable work style.

**Representative expressions:**
- 实事求是 (shí shì qiú shì) — seek truth from facts; the foundational epistemological-political principle
- 求真务实 (qiú zhēn wù shí) — seek truth and be pragmatic; a widely used administrative slogan
- 务实 (wùshí) — pragmatic, practical, realistic in approach
- 切实 (qièshí) — genuinely effective, concretely grounded
- 落实 (luòshí) — to implement concretely, to follow through
- 扎实 (zhāshí) — thorough, well-grounded (of work or skills)
- 切实可行 — genuinely feasible
- 切实有效 — concretely effective

**Notes:** This group is distinct from Group 7 (philosophical discourse about truth as a principle) in that these expressions are oriented toward practical conduct and administrative evaluation rather than toward abstract epistemological questions. 实事求是 appears in both groups because it has both a philosophical provenance and a practical administrative application. 落实, 切实, and 扎实 are peripheral to the truth domain (noted in P1b Step 3 as extensions where the root's truth-domain contribution is indirect), but they cluster together in administrative evaluation contexts.

## SECTION — Ambiguous or Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 present clear overlap between two or more groups, or resist clean placement.

---

**真实 (zhēnshí)**
Belongs to both Group 2 (accuracy of accounts) and Group 11 (aesthetic fidelity). In journalistic and legal contexts it evaluates factual accuracy of claims; in literary and film criticism it evaluates the fidelity of artistic representation. The same word is used in both domains without apparent distinction in form.

---

**真相 (zhēnxiàng)**
Belongs primarily to Group 3 (revealing/concealing what happened) but overlaps with Group 2 (accuracy of accounts). The distinction is that 真相 implies a dynamic of concealment and revelation, while 真实 and 失实 evaluate accuracy without necessarily implying prior concealment.

---

**客观 (kèguān) / 客观性 (kèguānxìng) / 客观化 (kèguānhuà)**
These items do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. 客观 evaluates whether an assessment is based on external facts rather than personal bias, which is related to but distinct from the accuracy evaluations in Group 2 and the reliability evaluations in Group 10. It appears most consistently in journalistic and scholarly evaluation of method and perspective. It could be placed in a group with 公正 (see below) as expressions evaluating the impartiality of a judgment or process, but the data does not show strong enough clustering to warrant a separate group for just these two items.

---

**公正 (gōngzhèng) / 公正性 (gōngzhèngxìng)**
Overlaps with Group 5 (legal/institutional conformity) in legal contexts (公正的审判, 主持公正) but also functions as a moral evaluation of fairness independent of legal standards. The compound 客观公正 (Construction 22.3) suggests that 客观 and 公正 are treated as a natural pair in evaluative discourse, but neither fits cleanly into the groups defined above.

---

**正确 (zhèngquè) / 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) / 不正确 (bù zhèngquè)**
These items evaluate conformity to a standard (correctness), which overlaps with Group 2 (accuracy of accounts) in some contexts and with Group 5 (legal/institutional conformity) in others, but neither is a precise fit. 正确 is used most distinctively in educational and logical contexts (correct answer, correct method, correct path) where the standard is not factual reality but a normative criterion. This usage pattern is sufficiently distinct that 正确 does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**错误 (cuòwù)**
Functions as the direct antonym of 正确 and shares its ambiguity. It is used in factual, moral, political, and procedural evaluation. It does not fit cleanly into any single group.

---

**本质 (běnzhì) / 实质 (shízhì)**
Both appear in Group 3-adjacent contexts (揭示本质, 认清本质) but their primary function is to distinguish fundamental or substantive reality from surface or formal appearance, which is a distinct evaluative operation not well captured by any of the groups above. They are most consistently used in analytical and argumentative discourse to assert that despite surface differences, the underlying nature is X. They could form a small group together, but the data does not show enough additional items clustering with them to warrant a full group entry.

---

**真金不怕火炼 (zhēn jīn bù pà huǒ liàn)**
This proverb uses 真 in a metaphorical extension to evaluate genuine quality or moral integrity under testing conditions. It does not fit cleanly into any group; it is closest to Group 1 (authentication) in its use of the genuine/counterfeit contrast, but its application is to character and worth rather than to objects.

---

**认真 (rènzhēn)**
Noted in P1b Step 3 as a peripheral extension of 真. It describes an attitude of earnest engagement with a task and does not participate in any of the truth-evaluation groups above. It is not grouped here.

---

**天真 (tiānzhēn) / 纯真 (chúnzhēn)**
Both describe qualities of natural innocence or purity of character. They are noted in P1b Step 3 as peripheral extensions of 真. They do not participate in any of the truth-evaluation groups above and are not grouped here.

---

**假设 (jiǎshè) / 假如 (jiǎrú)**
These use 假 in a conditional/hypothetical function entirely distinct from its deceptive or inauthentic uses. They do not belong to any of the groups above and are not grouped here.

---

## SECTION — Items That Do Not Fit Any Group

The following items from P1 and P2 were examined and no clear grouping was identified based on the usage patterns in the data.

- **充实 (chōngshí)** — evaluates substantive fullness or richness; the truth-domain contribution is indirect
- **踏实 (tāshí)** — psychological steadiness or careful work; the truth-domain contribution is indirect
- **实力 (shílì)** — actual capability or strength; 实 functions as an intensifier of authenticity rather than a truth predicate
- **现实 (xiànshí)** — reality or realistic; the boundary between ontological and normative uses is fluid and the item does not cluster with other items in the data
- **实用 (shíyòng)** — practical or utilitarian; the evaluative dimension concerns usefulness rather than truth
- **名实 (míngshí)** — placed in Group 8 but noted as marginal; its classical register separates it from the more common items in that group
