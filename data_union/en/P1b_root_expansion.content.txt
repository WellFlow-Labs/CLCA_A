# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: English (en)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: TRUE / TRĒOWE (OE *trēowe*, *trēowþ*)**

- **Root form:** *true* / *tru-* (Old English *trēowe*, faithful, trustworthy; Modern English reflex *tru-*)
- **P1 items participating:** *true*, *truth*, *truthful*, *trustworthy*, *truly* — plus the derivational families cited under these headwords (*untruth*, *truism*, *untrue*, *truthfully*, *truthfulness*, *untruthful*). The related root *trust* (from Old Norse *traust*, cognate with *true*) is treated as a closely related stem and included where it participates in the same semantic field. Direct participation: **true**, **truth**, **truthful**, **trustworthy**, **truly** as headwords, with the largest single derivational cluster in the domain.
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary native-English carrier of truth-predication. Supplies both propositional truth (correspondence of statements to facts) and relational fidelity (loyalty, genuineness to type). Highly productive in compounding and derivation. The most productive root in everyday English for the domain.

---

**Root 2: VERUS (Latin *ver-*, "true")**

- **Root form:** *ver-* (Latin *verus*, true; no native script distinction in English)
- **P1 items participating:** *veracious*, *veracity*, *verisimilitude*, *verifiable*, *veridical* — plus indirect participation through the semantic field anchored by *verus* that underlies the entire domain. The root also underlies *verify*, *veracity*, *verification* cited as derivational forms across multiple entries. Direct participation: **veracious**, **veracity**, **verisimilitude**, **verifiable**, **veridical** as headwords.
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes the property of correspondence to fact or reality as an inherent quality; supplies the semantic nucleus of correspondence-to-fact, truth-telling, and truthfulness. The most etymologically concentrated "truth" root in the Latinate stratum of English. Supplies the formal and learned stratum of the English truth-evaluation vocabulary. Moderately productive in formal, legal, and academic registers.

---

**Root 3: FALSUS (Latin *fals-* / *fall-*, from *fallere*, to deceive)**

- **Root form:** *fals-* (Latin *falsus*, past participle of *fallere*, to deceive)
- **P1 items participating:** *false*, *falsehood* — plus the derivational family *falsity*, *falsify*, *falsification*, *falsely* cited under *false*; also structurally related to the evaluative polarity established by the negative pole of the domain. Direct participation: **false**, **falsehood** as headwords, with the most morphologically productive negative-truth stem in the set.
- **Core meaning contribution:** Supplies the primary negative pole of truth-evaluation: inaccuracy, inauthenticity, deceptive appearance, and misrepresentation. Covers both propositional falsity and moral duplicity. Highly productive in derivation and compounding across legal, scientific, logical, psychological, and everyday registers.

---

**Root 4: FIDES (Latin *fid-* / *fide-*, "faith, trust")**

- **Root form:** *fid-* / *fide-* (Latin *fides*, faith, trust)
- **P1 items participating:** *faithful*, *bona fide*, *credible* (via *credere*, semantically adjacent), *fraudulent* (as negative counterpart in the trust domain)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes trust, good faith, and fidelity to commitment or original; links the truth domain to interpersonal reliability and legal good-faith concepts. Participates in the widest range of cross-register expressions — legal, moral, aesthetic, and interpersonal. Highly productive in English through Latin and French pathways.

---

> **Note on selection rationale:** *TRUE/TRĒOWE* is the most productive in everyday English; *VERUS* supplies the formal and technical stratum; *FALSUS* anchors the negative pole. The root *GENUINE/GENUINUS* and *VALID/VALIDUS* were considered but each participates in only one or two P1 items and generate smaller families. The root *CRED-* (Latin *credere*) was also considered given *credible* and *credibility* in P1, but its expansion belongs more centrally to the belief/trust domain than the truth-evaluation domain proper. *Ver-*, *true/truth*, and *fals-* are the three roots that most directly and recurrently build the propositional truth-evaluation vocabulary in English. The *fid-* root is selected over alternatives (e.g., *leg-* "law," *cred-* "believe") because it participates in the widest range of cross-register expressions that the protocol requires expanding.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: TRUE / TRĒOWE

---

**1.1**
- **Form:** *true*
- **Morphological breakdown:** Old English *trēowe* (faithful, trustworthy)
- **Natural English translation:** true
- **Context of use:** The base adjective, used predicatively and attributively across all registers to assert factual correctness, genuineness, or faithfulness. Already in P1; listed here as the anchor form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
- **Form:** *truth*
- **Morphological breakdown:** OE *trēowþ*, from *trēowe* "faithful" + *-þ* (abstract noun suffix); also analysed as *true* + Old English *-th* (abstract noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** the quality or state of being true; that which is true
- **Context of use:** Used across all registers as the primary noun for the concept of correspondence to fact or reality. The nominalization of *true*; refers to the property of being true or to a true statement. Already in P1; listed here as anchor form. "Tell me the truth." "The truth of the matter is disputed."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
- **Form:** *truly*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *true* + *-ly* (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, really; in a true manner; used as an intensifier
- **Context of use:** Used as an adverb to intensify or affirm the genuineness of a predicate (*truly remarkable*, *truly sorry*) and in formal letter closings (*Yours truly*). Also used as an adverb of manner (*truly believe*). Appears across all registers. "I truly believe this is the right decision."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
- **Form:** *untrue*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *un-* (negation) + *true*
- **Natural English translation:** untrue, false, unfaithful; not true
- **Context of use:** Applied to statements and representations to indicate that they do not correspond to fact. Also used of persons to indicate unfaithfulness or disloyalty, particularly in older or literary usage. Used in general speech as a somewhat softer alternative to *false* or *wrong*. "That statement is simply untrue."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
- **Form:** *untruth*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *un-* (negation) + *truth*
- **Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood, lie; a false statement (often used as a euphemism)
- **Context of use:** Refers to a false statement, often with a softer connotation than *lie*, implying that the falsity may be partial or unintentional. Used in formal, journalistic, political, and polite registers as a euphemism for *lie*. "He was accused of telling untruths."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.6**
- **Form:** *trueness*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *true* + *-ness* (abstract noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** trueness, accuracy, fidelity
- **Context of use:** Refers to the quality of being true or accurate, particularly in technical contexts such as engineering (the *trueness* of a surface or measurement). Less common than *truth* in philosophical contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**1.7**
- **Form:** *true-blue*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *true* + *blue* (historically associated with constancy and loyalty; color symbolism for constancy)
- **Natural English translation:** genuinely loyal, staunchly faithful, thoroughly committed; staunchly faithful to a cause or party
- **Context of use:** Used to describe persons or things that are thoroughly genuine, loyal, or committed to a cause or identity. Common in informal and political registers to describe unwavering loyalty. "She was a true-blue supporter of the cause." (*a true-blue conservative*)
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.8**
- **Form:** *true-life*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *true* + *life*
- **Natural English translation:** based on real events, factual
- **Context of use:** Used attributively to describe narratives, films, or accounts that are based on actual events rather than invented (*a true-life story*). Common in popular media and publishing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.9**
- **Form:** *true north*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *true* + *north* (geographic direction)
- **Natural English translation:** geographic north (as opposed to magnetic north); also: one's guiding principle or authentic direction
- **Context of use:** In navigation, refers to the direction of the geographic North Pole as distinct from magnetic north. Metaphorically used in leadership and self-help discourse to mean one's authentic values or guiding purpose. "The organization's values serve as its true north."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

- **Natural English translation:** characteristically, as expected, consistent with past behavior; behaving in the way that is expected or typical
- **Context of use:** Used to indicate that someone or something is behaving in a manner consistent with their established character or pattern. Common in journalism and informal commentary, often with a slightly ironic tone. "True to form, he arrived an hour late."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**1.22**
- **Form:** *truth value*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *truth* + *value* (logical or semantic value)
- **Natural English translation:** the truth or falsity of a proposition; the logical status of a proposition as true or false
- **Context of use:** A technical term in formal logic and philosophy of language referring to whether a proposition is true or false. In classical logic, every proposition has exactly one truth value: true or false (in non-classical logics, multiple values may be admitted). Used in logic, linguistics, and philosophy of language. "In classical logic, every proposition has exactly one truth value: true or false."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**1.23**
- **Form:** *truth condition*
- **Morphological breakdown:** *truth* + *condition* (requirement for obtaining)
- **Natural English translation:** the condition under which a statement is true
- **Context of use:** A technical term in formal semantics and philosophy of language referring to the circumstances that must obtain for a sentence to be true. Central to truth-conditional semantics. "Knowing the meaning of a sentence is knowing its truth conditions."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**1.24**

- **Natural English translation:** of or relating to truth conditions; describing a theory of meaning based on truth conditions
- **Context of use:** Used in formal semantics and philosophy of language. *Truth-conditional semantics* identifies the meaning of a sentence with its truth conditions; contrasts with use-theoretic or inferentialist approaches. "Truth-conditional semantics identifies the meaning of a sentence with its truth conditions."
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

- **Context of use:** Used both literally and as a discourse marker. "To tell the truth, I have no idea what happened."
