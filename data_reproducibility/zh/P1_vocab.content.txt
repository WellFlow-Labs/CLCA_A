# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

## Lexical Items

1. **真 (zhēn)**
   - Literal meaning: true, real, genuine
   - Typical English translations: true, real, genuine, authentic
   - Usage: Used to describe statements that correspond to reality, objects that are authentic rather than fake, and genuine emotions or intentions. Appears in both everyday speech and formal contexts to mark truthfulness or authenticity.
   - [CORE]

2. **假 (jiǎ)**
   - Literal meaning: false, fake, artificial
   - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
   - Usage: Describes statements that do not correspond to reality, counterfeit objects, or pretended behavior. Commonly used in opposition to 真 across various contexts from factual claims to product authenticity.
   - [CORE]

3. **对 (duì)**
   - Literal meaning: correct, right, facing/toward
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, yes
   - Usage: Indicates correctness of statements, answers, or actions, often in response to questions or when evaluating accuracy. Widely used in everyday conversation and educational contexts to confirm or validate.
   - [CORE]

4. **错 (cuò)**
   - Literal meaning: wrong, mistaken, error
   - Typical English translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
   - Usage: Marks statements, judgments, or actions as incorrect or mistaken. Used across contexts from simple factual errors to moral wrongdoing, often paired with 对 as its antonym.
   - [CORE]

5. **实 (shí)**
   - Literal meaning: solid, real, actual
   - Typical English translations: real, actual, solid, substantial
   - Usage: Emphasizes reality, actuality, or substantiality of things, often contrasted with appearance or theory. Frequently appears in compounds related to truthfulness and concrete reality.
   - [CORE]

6. **虚 (xū)**
   - Literal meaning: empty, void, false, unreal
   - Typical English translations: false, empty, unreal, nominal
   - Usage: Describes things lacking substance, reality, or truthfulness, including false statements and insubstantial claims. Often contrasted with 实 in philosophical and everyday discourse.
   - [CORE]

7. **诚 (chéng)**
   - Literal meaning: sincere, honest, truthful
   - Typical English translations: sincere, honest, truthful, genuine
   - Usage: Describes sincerity and honesty in speech and conduct, emphasizing alignment between inner state and outward expression. Central to moral and interpersonal evaluation in Chinese culture.
   - [CORE]

8. **准确 (zhǔnquè)**
   - Literal meaning: standard + exact = accurate
   - Typical English translations: accurate, precise, exact
   - Usage: Evaluates the precision and accuracy of statements, measurements, or representations. Commonly used in technical, scientific, and informational contexts to assess correspondence with facts.
   - [CORE]

9. **正确 (zhèngquè)**
   - Literal meaning: upright/correct + exact = correct
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, proper
   - Usage: Indicates correctness of statements, decisions, or actions, often with normative implications beyond mere factual accuracy. Used in political, moral, and practical contexts to mark approved or proper positions.
   - [CORE]

10. **事实 (shìshí)**
    - Literal meaning: matter/affair + actual = fact
    - Typical English translations: fact, reality, truth
    - Usage: Refers to actual states of affairs or factual reality as opposed to opinion or fiction. Commonly used to ground claims in objective reality or to distinguish facts from interpretations.
    - [CORE]

11. **真实 (zhēnshí)**
    - Literal meaning: true + actual = real, true
    - Typical English translations: real, true, authentic, genuine
    - Usage: Emphasizes both truthfulness and actuality, describing things that are both genuine and correspond to reality. Used for evaluating representations, accounts, and the nature of things.
    - [CORE]

12. **真相 (zhēnxiàng)**
    - Literal meaning: true + appearance/aspect = truth
    - Typical English translations: truth, the real facts, actual situation
    - Usage: Refers to the true state of affairs or facts behind appearances, often implying something hidden or obscured. Commonly used when revealing or seeking what really happened.
    - [CORE]

13. **可靠 (kěkào)**
    - Literal meaning: can + rely on = reliable
    - Typical English translations: reliable, dependable, trustworthy
    - Usage: Evaluates the reliability and trustworthiness of sources, people, or information. Used to assess whether something can be depended upon for accuracy or consistency.
    - [CORE]

14. **诚实 (chéngshí)**
    - Literal meaning: sincere + actual = honest
    - Typical English translations: honest, truthful, sincere
    - Usage: Describes honest character and truthful speech, emphasizing moral virtue of not deceiving. Central to evaluations of personal integrity and trustworthiness in social relations.
    - [CORE]

15. **虚假 (xūjiǎ)**
    - Literal meaning: empty/false + false = false, fake
    - Typical English translations: false, fake, phony, fraudulent
    - Usage: Describes deliberately false or deceptive statements, representations, or things. Stronger than simple 假, often implying intentional deception or fraudulence.
    - [CORE]

16. **符合 (fúhé)**
    - Literal meaning: tally/符 + join/合 = conform to, accord with
    - Typical English translations: conform to, accord with, correspond to, match
    - Usage: Indicates correspondence or conformity between things, such as statements matching facts or actions conforming to standards. Used to evaluate alignment or agreement.
    - [CORE]

17. **本质 (běnzhì)**
    - Literal meaning: root/origin + quality = essence, nature
    - Typical English translations: essence, nature, essential quality
    - Usage: Refers to the fundamental nature or essential quality of something as opposed to superficial attributes. Used in philosophical and analytical contexts to identify what something fundamentally is.
    - [CORE]

18. **客观 (kèguān)**
    - Literal meaning: guest/object + observe = objective
    - Typical English translations: objective, impartial
    - Usage: Describes objectivity in statements, judgments, or perspectives, emphasizing independence from subjective bias. Contrasted with 主观 (subjective) in evaluating reliability of claims.
    - [CORE]

19. **主观 (zhǔguān)**
    - Literal meaning: master/subject + observe = subjective
    - Typical English translations: subjective, personal
    - Usage: Describes subjective perspectives or judgments influenced by personal views rather than objective reality. Used to qualify the epistemic status of claims or to critique bias.
    - [CORE]

20. **真理 (zhēnlǐ)**
    - Literal meaning: true + principle/reason = truth
    - Typical English translations: truth, verity
    - Usage: Refers to fundamental truths or principles, often in philosophical, scientific, or ideological contexts. Carries weight of universal or objective truth rather than mere factual accuracy.
    - [CORE]

21. **谎言 (huǎngyán)**
    - Literal meaning: lie/deceive + speech = lie
    - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
    - Usage: Refers to deliberately false statements intended to deceive. Clearly marks intentional dishonesty in speech, distinguished from mere error or mistake.
    - [CORE]

22. **欺骗 (qīpiàn)**
    - Literal meaning: deceive + cheat = deceive, cheat
    - Typical English translations: deceive, cheat, defraud
    - Usage: Describes acts of deception or fraud, emphasizing intentional misleading of others. Used for both verbal deception and deceptive actions across moral and legal contexts.
    - [CORE]

23. **准 (zhǔn)**
    - Literal meaning: standard, accurate, allow
    - Typical English translations: accurate, standard, correct
    - Usage: Indicates accuracy or correctness, often in informal speech as a shortened affirmation. Also used to mark something as meeting a standard or being permitted.
    - [CORE]

24. **像 (xiàng)**
    - Literal meaning: resemble, be like, image
    - Typical English translations: resemble, be like, seem
    - Usage: Indicates resemblance or similarity between things, used to evaluate how well something matches or represents something else. Common in comparisons and assessments of likeness.
    - [CORE]

25. **似 (sì)**
    - Literal meaning: resemble, seem, similar
    - Typical English translations: resemble, seem, similar to
    - Usage: Marks resemblance or apparent similarity, often with nuance of seeming or appearing to be something. Used in both concrete comparisons and epistemic qualifications.
    - [CORE]

26. **确实 (quèshí)**
    - Literal meaning: firm/certain + actual = indeed, really
    - Typical English translations: indeed, really, truly, certainly
    - Usage: Affirms the reality or truth of statements, emphasizing certainty and actuality. Used to strengthen claims or confirm that something is genuinely the case.
    - [CORE]

27. **的确 (díquè)**
    - Literal meaning: [possessive marker] + exact/certain = indeed, really
    - Typical English translations: indeed, really, certainly
    - Usage: Confirms or affirms the truth of statements, similar to 确实 but often used for emphasis or acknowledgment. Common in both spoken and written discourse to validate claims.
    - [CORE]

28. **真正 (zhēnzhèng)**
    - Literal meaning: true + upright/correct = genuine, real
    - Typical English translations: genuine, real, true, authentic
    - Usage: Emphasizes genuine or authentic nature of something, distinguishing it from imitations or superficial appearances. Used to mark what is truly or properly the case.
    - [CORE]

29. **名副其实 (míng fù qí shí)**
    - Literal meaning: name + match + its + reality = live up to one's name
    - Typical English translations: worthy of the name, live up to one's reputation
    - Usage: Fixed expression indicating that something's reality matches its name or reputation, evaluating correspondence between designation and actuality. Used when reality confirms expectations or claims.
    - [CORE]

30. **名不副实 (míng bù fù shí)**
    - Literal meaning: name + not + match + reality = not live up to one's name
    - Typical English translations: unworthy of the name, not live up to reputation
    - Usage: Fixed expression indicating mismatch between name/reputation and reality, marking failure of correspondence. Used critically when reality falls short of claims or expectations.
    - [CORE]
