# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (single morpheme; functions as root in many compounds)
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that something corresponds to fact or is not counterfeit. Functions as a free morpheme and as a bound root in compounds (e.g., 真实, 真相). Can modify persons, objects, statements, and emotions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** false, fake, borrowed (single morpheme; antonym of 真 in most contexts)
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
**Usage:** Contrasts directly with 真 in evaluative contexts; applied to goods, documents, identities, and statements. Also appears in compounds such as 假冒 (to counterfeit) and 虚假 (false, deceptive). Widely used in commercial, legal, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal meaning:** true + solid/real (compound adjective/noun)
**Typical English translations:** truthful, authentic, real, factual
**Usage:** Describes the quality of being in accordance with fact or reality, applied to accounts, feelings, representations, and situations. More explicitly nominal and adjectival than bare 真; commonly used in journalism, law, and literary criticism. Appears in constructions like 真实情况 (the actual situation) and 真实性 (authenticity, veracity).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal meaning:** true + appearance/aspect (the true face of something)
**Typical English translations:** the truth, the true facts, the real situation
**Usage:** Refers specifically to the actual state of affairs behind appearances, often in contexts of investigation, disclosure, or concealment. Frequently collocates with 大白 (come to light) and 揭露 (expose). Common in legal, journalistic, and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 虚假
**Transliteration:** xūjiǎ
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** false, fraudulent, fictitious, deceptive
**Usage:** Carries a stronger evaluative weight than bare 假, often implying deliberate misrepresentation. Frequently used in legal and regulatory language, e.g., 虚假广告 (false advertising) and 虚假陈述 (false statement). Less common in casual speech than 假.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 正确
**Transliteration:** zhèngquè
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + certain/precise (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Evaluates statements, answers, methods, or conduct as conforming to a standard, rule, or fact. Widely used in educational, political, and everyday contexts. Contrasts with 错误 (wrong, erroneous) and does not typically carry strong moral weight on its own.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 错误
**Transliteration:** cuòwù
**Literal meaning:** wrong/mistaken + confused/mistaken (compound noun/adjective)
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Denotes deviation from correctness in statements, reasoning, conduct, or belief. Functions both as a noun (an error) and as an attributive adjective (an erroneous view). Used across moral, factual, and procedural evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 准确
**Transliteration:** zhǔnquè
**Literal meaning:** standard/precise + certain/precise (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Emphasizes precision and exactness, particularly of measurements, descriptions, translations, and predictions. Distinguishable from 正确 in that it foregrounds degree of fit rather than binary correctness. Common in scientific, technical, and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 可靠
**Transliteration:** kěkào
**Literal meaning:** can + lean on/rely on (potential compound)
**Typical English translations:** reliable, dependable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates sources, persons, information, and equipment as worthy of reliance. Commonly used in assessments of evidence quality, character, and technical performance. Appears in collocations such as 可靠来源 (reliable source) and 可靠性 (reliability).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** true + false/spurious (binary compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity vs. forgery, genuine vs. fake, truth vs. falsehood
**Usage:** Functions as a paired nominal referring to the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit. Typically appears in constructions like 辨别真伪 (distinguish the genuine from the fake) and 真伪难辨 (hard to tell genuine from fake). Common in legal, art authentication, and documentary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 伪造
**Transliteration:** wěizào
**Literal meaning:** false/spurious + to make/manufacture (verb compound)
**Typical English translations:** to forge, to fabricate, to counterfeit
**Usage:** Refers to the act of producing a false version of a document, signature, currency, or identity. Strongly associated with legal and criminal contexts. Contrasts with 仿造 (to imitate/copy) in implying fraudulent intent.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 仿冒
**Transliteration:** fǎngmào
**Literal meaning:** to imitate + to falsely claim/counterfeit (verb/noun compound)
**Typical English translations:** to counterfeit, to pass off as, imitation goods
**Usage:** Used primarily in commercial and legal contexts to describe the production or sale of goods falsely presented as a genuine brand. Closely related to 假冒 but with a slight emphasis on imitation as the mechanism. Common in intellectual property and consumer protection discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 假冒
**Transliteration:** jiǎmào
**Literal meaning:** false/fake + to falsely claim/impersonate (verb/noun compound)
**Typical English translations:** to counterfeit, to impersonate, fake (goods)
**Usage:** Covers both counterfeit goods and impersonation of persons or institutions. Appears in fixed expressions such as 假冒伪劣 (fake and inferior goods) and 假冒品牌 (counterfeit brand). Widely used in everyday, commercial, and legal speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal meaning:** true + principle/reason (compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** truth (as a principle), universal truth
**Usage:** Refers to truth as an abstract or universal principle rather than the truth of a particular statement; common in philosophical, ideological, and rhetorical contexts. Contrasts with 事实 (fact) in being more abstract and normative. Frequently appears in political and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 事实
**Transliteration:** shìshí
**Literal meaning:** matter/affair + solid/real (compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** fact, the facts, reality
**Usage:** Denotes a state of affairs that actually obtains, used to ground claims in evidence or actuality. Commonly contrasted with 观点 (opinion/viewpoint) and 谎言 (lie). Central to legal, journalistic, and argumentative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 谎言
**Transliteration:** huǎngyán
**Literal meaning:** lie + speech/word (compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood
**Usage:** Refers to a deliberate false statement; implies intentional deception on the part of the speaker. Contrasts with 错误 (unintentional error) in attributing intent. Common across everyday, literary, and political registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 欺骗
**Transliteration:** qīpiàn
**Literal meaning:** to deceive + to cheat/trick (verb compound)
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to cheat, deception, fraud
**Usage:** Describes the act of causing someone to hold a false belief through deliberate action or misrepresentation. Broader than 谎言 in that it encompasses non-verbal deception. Used in moral, legal, and interpersonal evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 诚实
**Transliteration:** chéngshí
**Literal meaning:** sincere/honest + solid/real (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere
**Usage:** Evaluates persons or conduct as characterized by truthfulness and absence of deception. Commonly used in moral assessments of character and in educational contexts. Contrasts with 虚伪 (hypocritical, insincere) and 欺骗 (deceptive).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 虚伪
**Transliteration:** xūwěi
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false/spurious (compound adjective/noun)
**Typical English translations:** hypocritical, insincere, false, two-faced
**Usage:** Evaluates persons or conduct as outwardly presenting a false appearance of virtue or sincerity. Carries strong moral disapproval. Contrasts with 诚实 and 真诚, and is frequently used in character assessments and social criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal meaning:** true/genuine + sincere (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Usage:** Describes the quality of being genuinely sincere in feeling, intention, or expression, as opposed to performing sincerity. Applied to persons, apologies, emotions, and relationships. Closely related to 诚实 but with greater emphasis on inner genuineness rather than factual truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 名副其实
**Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
**Literal meaning:** name + matches/corresponds to + its + reality (four-character fixed expression, chéngyǔ)
**Typical English translations:** living up to one's name, worthy of the title, as good as its reputation
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression (成语) used to evaluate whether a label, title, or reputation accurately corresponds to the actual qualities of a person or thing. Its negation 名不副实 (name does not match reality) is equally common. Used in formal, journalistic, and evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 如实
**Transliteration:** rúshí
**Literal meaning:** as + real/actual (adverbial compound)
**Typical English translations:** truthfully, as it actually is, accurately
**Usage:** Functions as an adverb meaning to report or represent something exactly as it is, without distortion or omission. Commonly collocates with 反映 (reflect), 陈述 (state), and 汇报 (report). Frequent in legal testimony, journalistic, and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 失实
**Transliteration:** shīshí
**Literal meaning:** lose/deviate from + reality/fact (verb-object compound)
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, not in accordance with the facts, distorted
**Usage:** Describes a statement, report, or account that deviates from the facts, whether through error or distortion. Commonly used in legal and journalistic contexts to characterize defamatory or inaccurate reporting. Contrasts with 如实 and 属实.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 属实
**Transliteration:** shǔshí
**Literal meaning:** belong to/be + reality/fact (verb-object compound)
**Typical English translations:** to be true, to be confirmed as factual, to check out
**Usage:** Used in formal and official contexts to confirm that a reported matter has been verified as factual. Frequently appears in administrative, legal, and investigative language, e.g., 经查属实 (verified upon investigation to be true). More formal than 是真的.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 本质
**Transliteration:** běnzhì
**Literal meaning:** root/origin + substance/nature (compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, fundamental character
**Usage:** Refers to the intrinsic or fundamental nature of something, as opposed to its surface appearance. Used in philosophical, political, and analytical discourse to evaluate what something truly is beneath appearances. Appears in constructions like 本质上 (essentially, in essence) and 揭示本质 (reveal the true nature).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 实质
**Transliteration:** shízhì
**Literal meaning:** real/actual + substance/nature (compound noun)
**Typical English translations:** substance, essence, real nature
**Usage:** Closely related to 本质 but more commonly used in legal and analytical contexts to distinguish the substantive reality of a matter from its formal or procedural surface. Appears in 实质上 (in substance, essentially) and 实质性 (substantive). Slightly more technical than 本质.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 原形毕露
**Transliteration:** yuánxíng bì lù
**Literal meaning:** original form + completely + exposed (four-character fixed expression, chéngyǔ)
**Typical English translations:** to show one's true colors, to be exposed for what one really is
**Usage:** A fixed expression used when a person's or thing's true (typically negative) nature is revealed after having been concealed. Carries a strong evaluative and often moral tone. Used in narrative, journalistic, and rhetorical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 鉴定
**Transliteration:** jiàndìng
**Literal meaning:** to examine/appraise + to determine/fix (verb/noun compound)
**Typical English translations:** to authenticate, to appraise, authentication, expert assessment
**Usage:** Refers to the formal process of expert evaluation to determine the authenticity, quality, or nature of an object, document, or substance. Common in legal, forensic, art authentication, and scientific contexts. Produces a 鉴定结论 (authentication conclusion) or 鉴定报告 (appraisal report).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 辨伪
**Transliteration:** biànwěi
**Literal meaning:** to distinguish/discriminate + false/spurious (verb-object compound)
**Typical English translations:** to detect forgeries, to distinguish the genuine from the fake, textual criticism (of spurious works)
**Usage:** Refers to the practice of identifying false, forged, or spuriously attributed works, particularly in the context of historical documents, classical texts, and artworks. Has a long history in Chinese philological and bibliographic scholarship. Less common in everyday speech; primarily a scholarly and connoisseurial term.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 信实
**Transliteration:** xìnshí
**Literal meaning:** trustworthy/faithful + solid/real (compound adjective)
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, faithful (to fact)
**Usage:** An older or more literary term for reliability and truthfulness, used to evaluate persons or accounts as both honest and accurate. Less frequent in contemporary vernacular than 可靠 or 诚实, but attested in classical and formal written Chinese. Occasionally appears in legal and contractual language.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
