# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, collocational behavior, register distribution, and contextual deployment observed across P1 and P2 data. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Group boundaries are drawn where the data shows consistent clustering; where items straddle groups or resist grouping, this is noted explicitly.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a proposition or state of affairs corresponds to what is actually the case

**Label:** Factual truth assertion and denial

**Definition:** These expressions are used in everyday and formal discourse to affirm or deny that something is true or real as a matter of fact — that it actually happened, actually exists, or actually is the case. They appear in contexts of confirming reports, correcting misconceptions, and responding to doubt. The core items in this group participate in interrogative truth-checks (*totoo ba?*), discourse-initial framing (*ang totoo*, *sa katotohanan*), and particle-marked epistemic stance constructions (*totoo nga pala*, *totoo naman*). They are applied to propositions, events, and states of affairs.

**Representative expressions:**

- *totoo* — base predicate for asserting that a statement is true or a situation is real; "Is this true?" (*Totoo ba ito?*); "The news is not true" (*Hindi totoo ang balita*)
- *katotohanan* — nominal form; "the truth," "the true state of affairs"; "Tell the whole truth" (*Sabihin ang buong katotohanan*); "The allegation has no truth to it" (*Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon*)
- *totoong-totoo* — intensified: "absolutely true/real"; "This is absolutely real, not a joke" (*Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro*)
- *hindi totoo* — standard negation formula for denying a claim
- *totoo ba?* — conventionalized interrogative truth-check
- *totoo naman* — responsive affirmation against doubt; concessive acknowledgment in dialogue ("it's true, though")
- *totoo nga* — confirmatory: "indeed true"
- *totoo pala* — realization: "it turns out to be true"
- *totoo nga pala* — confirmed realization after update
- *totoo pa rin* — persistent truth: "still true"
- *hindi naman totoo* — softened denial
- *totoo man o hindi* — concessive frame: "whether true or not"; "True or not, we need to find out" (*Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman*)
- *totoo na* — marks that something previously doubted has been confirmed or has come to pass; "It's real now / It came true" (*Totoo na*)
- *totoo o hindi* — "True or not, we need to find out" (*Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman*)
- *ang totoo* — discourse-initial corrective formula: "The truth is, he has no money" (*Ang totoo, wala siyang pera*)
- *ang totoo nito* — "the truth of this matter"; "The truth of this matter is more complicated" (*Ang totoo nito ay mas kumplikado*)
- *sa katotohanan* — discourse marker: "in truth, in reality, actually"; introduces a corrective or clarifying statement
- *ang katotohanan ay* — formal sentence-initial frame: "the truth is that..."
- *walang katotohanan* — formula for characterizing a claim as entirely without factual basis; "The allegation has no truth to it" (*Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon*)
- *may katotohanan* — formula for conceding partial truth in a claim; "There is some truth in what he said" (*May katotohanan sa sinabi niya*)
- *totoo ang lahat* — "it's all true"; used in confessional or testimonial contexts
- *totoo sa buhay* — "true to life, realistic"
- *totoong kwento* — "true story"; characterizes a narrative as based on actual events
- *totoong pangyayari* — "real event"; used in journalistic and testimonial contexts
- *naniniwala akong totoo ito* — "I believe this is true"
- *kung totoo man o hindi* — "Whether true or not..."
- *aktuwal* — formal/journalistic: "actual, as opposed to hypothetical"; "What actually happened" (*aktuwal na nangyari*)
- *aktuwal na nangyari* — "what actually happened"
- *makatotohanan* — "realistic, true-to-life, truthful"; evaluates representations as faithfully corresponding to reality; "a realistic/truthful portrayal" (*makatotohanang larawan*)
- *hindi makatotohanan* — "unrealistic, not true-to-life" (*Hindi makatotohanan ang kwento*)
- *makatotohanang-makatotohanan* — "extremely realistic" [MARGINAL]
- *makatotohanang pelikula* — "realistic film"

**Notes:** *Totoo* is the dominant item in this group and is the only one that participates in the full range of particle-marked epistemic stance constructions documented in P2b. *Aktuwal* belongs here primarily through its use in contrasting what is genuinely the case with what is supposed or hypothetical, though it is more restricted in register than *totoo*. *Sa katotohanan* and *ang totoo* function as discourse-framing devices rather than simple predicates, but their function is to introduce a claim of factual correspondence. *Sa katotohanan* overlaps with Group 2 (proof and verification) in that it can introduce evidentially grounded statements, but its primary function here is as a factual-truth framing device. *Makatotohanan* and *totoo sa buhay* participate in this group but their primary use is aesthetic/representational evaluation rather than propositional truth-assertion; they also appear in Group 10. *Katotohanan* also appears in Group 2 (proof and verification) and Group 10 (general assertions about the nature of truth). *Totoo naman* and *totoo na* are pragmatically specialized and could be considered a sub-pattern within this group rather than fully independent items.

## Group 2: Expressions used in contexts of establishing, demanding, or denying proof and evidence

**Label:** Proof and verification

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it can be demonstrated, authenticated, or substantiated through evidence or a formal process. They are concentrated in legal, administrative, investigative, and formal argumentative contexts. The cluster centers on the root *tunay* and its derivations, particularly the *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* verbal paradigm, as well as borrowings *pruweba* and *ebidensya*.

**Representative expressions:**

- *katunayan* — "proof, evidence, genuineness"; used both as abstract noun and as discourse connector; "The proof of this can be seen in the data" (*Ang katunayan nito ay makikita sa datos*)
- *patunay* — "a piece of proof, evidence, attestation"; "show proof of payment" (*Ipakita ang patunay ng pagbabayad*)
- *patunayan* — verbal: "to prove, to verify, to demonstrate"; "prove what you are saying" (*Patunayan mo ang iyong sinasabi*)
- *patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi* — "prove what you said"
- *mapatunayan* — potential: "to be able to prove, provable"; "cannot be proven" (*Hindi mapatunayan*)
- *hindi mapatunayan* — "cannot be proven"
- *napatunayan* — completed: "has been proven, was verified"
- *pinatunayan* — completed actor-focus: "proved, verified"
- *pagpapatunay* — nominal: "the act/process of verification, authentication, proving"
- *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "document authentication"; fixed technical formula
- *pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan* — "identity verification"; fixed technical formula
- *patunay ng pagkakakilanlan* — "proof of identity"
- *katunayan ng pagkakakilanlan* — "proof of identity"
- *katunayan ng pagmamay-ari* — "proof of ownership"
- *katunayan sa hukuman* — "evidence in court"
- *walang katunayan* — "without proof, unsubstantiated"; "the accusation has no proof"
- *may katunayan* — "there is evidence, it is substantiated"; "his/her statement has proof to back it up"
- *sa katunayan* — discourse marker: "in fact, as a matter of fact"; introduces a factual clarification or emphasis
- *bilang katunayan* — "as proof, as evidence"; "as proof, here is the document"
- *ito ang nagsisilbing katunayan* — "this serves as proof"
- *ang katunayan ay nagsasalita para sa sarili nito* — "the evidence speaks for itself"
- *pruweba* — "proof, evidence" (from Spanish *prueba*); used in legal and everyday contexts; "Where is the proof?" (*Nasaan ang pruweba?*)
- *Walang pruweba* — "There is no proof"; standard formula
- *May pruweba ka ba?* — "Do you have proof?"; standard demand for evidence
- *pruweba ng pagkakasala* — "proof of guilt"
- *ebidensya* — "evidence"; specifically legal-procedural in connotation
- *walang ebidensya* — "There is no evidence"; standard legal formula
- *ebidensyang pisikal* — "physical evidence"; fixed legal term
- *ebidensyang sirkumstansyal* — "circumstantial evidence"; fixed legal term
- *hanapin ang katotohanan* — "to seek the truth" (overlaps with Group 1)
- *paghahanap ng katotohanan* — "the search for truth" (overlaps with Group 1)

**Notes:** *Katunayan* and *pruweba* overlap substantially in meaning but *katunayan* is morphologically native (from *tunay*) and also functions as a discourse connector (*sa katunayan*), while *pruweba* is a Spanish borrowing with no discourse-connector use. *Ebidensya* is more narrowly legal-procedural than *pruweba*. The *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* verbal paradigm (*patunayan*, *mapatunayan*, *napatunayan*, *pinatunayan*) is the most fully developed verbal paradigm for the act of proving in the dataset. *Sa katunayan* also appears in Group 7 (discourse connectors and pragmatic markers). *Katunayan* appears in Group 1 (asserting factual correspondence) and Group 3 (evaluating genuineness) as well; the evidentiary function documented here is the most consistently attested usage in formal discourse. Several items in this group also appear in Group 8 (legal and administrative contexts); the distinguishing feature of this group is the broader proof/verification function across registers, while Group 8 is restricted to institutional-procedural contexts.

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate whether something is what it claims or appears to be

**Label:** Authenticity and genuineness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate objects, persons, documents, emotions, relationships, or representations as either genuinely what they appear to be or as imitations, counterfeits, or pretenses. They appear in commercial, material, relational, aesthetic, and identity contexts. Unlike Group 1, the question here is not whether a proposition is factually true but whether a thing is the real instance of its kind. The core items center on *tunay* (genuine, authentic) and its contrasts with *huwad* (counterfeit) and *peke* (fake).

**Representative expressions:**

- *tunay* — "genuine, real, authentic"; evaluates persons, objects, or relationships as genuine rather than false or imitation; "a true/genuine friend" (*tunay na kaibigan*); "true love" (*tunay na pagmamahal*); "what truly happened" (*tunay na nangyari*)
- *tunay na tunay* — intensified: "absolutely genuine"; "His love is absolutely genuine" (*Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal*)
- *tunay na ginto* — "genuine gold"
- *tunay na pagmamahal* — "genuine/real love"
- *tunay na kaibigan* — "true/genuine friend"; the most common and stable collocation of *tunay* in relational discourse
- *tunay na halaga* — "true/real value"
- *tunay na kahulugan* — "true/real meaning"; "the true meaning of freedom" (*Ang tunay na kahulugan ng kalayaan*)
- *tunay na layunin* — "real intention, true purpose"; "What is his/her real intention?" (*Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?*)
- *tunay na nangyari* — "what really happened"
- *tunay na kalagayan* — "the true/real state of affairs"
- *tunay na pagkatao* — "true/authentic character"; "I came to know his/her true character" (*Nakilala ko ang tunay na pagkatao niya*)
- *tunay na mukha* — metaphorical: a person's genuine character as opposed to a performed persona; "Reveal the true face of the government" (*Ipakita ang tunay na mukha ng gobyerno*)
- *tunay na bayani* — "true hero"; evaluates whether a person genuinely merits a social designation
- *tunay na Pilipino* — "true/genuine Filipino"; used in identity and nationalist discourse
- *tunay na pagbabago* — "genuine change"
- *ito ang tunay na suliranin* — "This is the real problem"
- *hindi tunay* — "not genuine, inauthentic"
- *di-tunay* — "inauthentic, not genuine" [MARGINAL]
- *huwad* — "fake, counterfeit, spurious"; more formal and legal; "counterfeit money" (*huwad na pera*); "forged document" (*huwad na dokumento*)
- *huwad na pera* — "counterfeit money"
- *huwad na dokumento* — "fake/forged document"
- *huwad na* — "has been found to be fake"
- *huwad na huwad* — intensified: "completely fake" [MARGINAL]
- *paghuhuwad* — "counterfeiting, forgery"; "The forgery of documents is a crime" (*Ang paghuhuwad ng dokumento ay isang krimen*)
- *maghuwad* — verbal: "to forge, to counterfeit"
- *humuwad* — verbal: "to counterfeit, to forge"
- *naghuwad* — "forged (by an agent)"
- *natuklasang huwad* — "discovered to be fake/forged"; "Discovered to be fake was his diploma" (*Natuklasang huwad ang kanyang diploma*)
- *nahuli siyang may hawak na huwad na pera* — "caught holding counterfeit money"
- *huwad na pagkakakilanlan* — "false identity"
- *peke* — colloquial: "fake, counterfeit, phony"; applied to objects and persons; "fake product" (*pekeng produkto*); "fake doctor" (*pekeng doktor*)
- *pekeng produkto* — "fake product"
- *peke lang* — dismissive: "it's just a fake"
- *peke o hindi* — binary frame: "fake or not"
- *peke pala* — "it turns out it's fake"
- *mukhang peke* — "looks fake"
- *peke-peke* — "totally fake" [MARGINAL]
- *pekein* — "to fake (a specific item)"
- *orihinal* — "original, genuine, not a copy"; evaluates something as the genuine source item rather than a copy; "original work" (*orihinal na obra*)
- *orihinal na kopya* — "original copy (of a document)"
- *awtentiko* — formal/legal evaluation of a document or item as genuinely what it purports to be; "authentic document" (*awtentikong dokumento*)
- *awtentikong dokumento* — fixed legal formula for a genuine document
- *dalisay* — "pure, unadulterated, genuine"
- *dalisay na ginto* — "pure gold"
- *dalisay na puso* — "pure heart"
- *kadalisayan* — "purity" [MARGINAL]
- *lehitimo* — "legitimate, lawful, genuine"; "legitimate government" (*lehitimong pamahalaán*); "legitimate claim" (*lehitimong paghahabol*)
- *lehitimong negosyo* — "legitimate business"
- *lehitimidad* — "legitimacy"
- *hindi lehitimo* — "illegitimate"
- *iligitimo* — "illegitimate child" (*iligitimong anak*)
- *di-lehitimo* — "illegitimate government" (*Di-lehitimong pamahalaán*)
- *itinuturing na lehitimo* — "considered legitimate"
- *di-lehitimong pamamaraan* — "illegitimate method"
- *huwad-huwaran* — "imitation, replica" [UNCERTAIN]

**Notes:** *Tunay* is the central native item in this group and is applied to a very wide range of nouns — persons, emotions, political concepts, artistic works — wherever the question is whether something is the genuine instance of its category. *Huwad* and *peke* are its primary contrasts; *huwad* is more formal and legal, *peke* is colloquial and very widely used. *Orihinal* foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction while *awtentiko* foregrounds the genuine-versus-fraudulent distinction; both are more restricted to formal registers and to objects/documents rather than persons or abstract qualities. *Dalisay* is used primarily for substances and moral purity. *Lehitimo* is used primarily for legal standing and institutional authority; it is the clearest member of Group 8 that also participates here. *Tunay na nangyari* and *tunay na kalagayan* also overlap with Group 1 (factual truth) and Group 2 (verification), since asserting what "really happened" involves both authenticity and factual accuracy. *Awtentiko* and *huwad* also appear in Group 8 (legal and administrative contexts). *Peke* in emotional performance contexts overlaps with Group 4. *Tunay na Diyos*, *tunay na relihiyon*, *tunay na bayani*, and *tunay na Pilipino* use *tunay* in identity, theological, and political discourse where "genuine" is defined by ideological, cultural, or theological criteria rather than material or factual ones; they sit at the edge of this group.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate performances, displays, or representations as lacking genuine feeling or sincerity

**Label:** Marking insincerity in social and emotional performance

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person's expressed emotions, social displays, or interpersonal conduct as not reflecting genuine inner states — as performed rather than felt. They appear in interpersonal, psychological, and critical discourse and are applied to smiles, love, crying, intentions, and similar expressions of inner states.

**Representative expressions:**

- *peke ang kanyang ngiti* — "his/her smile is fake"
- *nagpeke ng sakit* — "faked an illness"
- *nagpepeke ng pagmamahal* — "faking love"
- *huwag magpeke ng emosyon* — "don't fake your emotions"
- *pagpepeke* — "the act of faking (emotions)"
- *paimbabaw* — "superficial/insincere love" (*paimbabaw na pagmamahal*); "surface-level, without genuine depth"; evaluates conduct or expression as lacking genuine depth, performing an appearance without the corresponding inner reality
- *totoo ang nararamdaman* — "what I feel is real/genuine" (used to assert the opposite of this group's concern)
- *tapat ang kanyang intensyon* — "his/her intentions are sincere" (used to assert the opposite)

**Notes:** *Paimbabaw* belongs primarily here: it evaluates conduct or expression as lacking genuine depth, performing an appearance without the corresponding inner reality. *Peke* in this group overlaps with Group 3 (deliberate inauthenticity of objects) but the application to emotional performance is a distinct and well-attested usage pattern. *Totoo ang nararamdaman* and *tapat ang kanyang intensyon* are included as expressions used specifically to counter the concern this group addresses — they assert genuine feeling or sincere intention in contexts where inauthenticity is at issue. *Paimbabaw* also overlaps with Group 11 (deliberate deception), where it can describe conduct that is deliberately misleading about one's true character or intentions.

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person's speech or conduct is honest and without concealment

**Label:** Evaluating honesty and directness in persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons, their speech, or their conduct as honest, frank, and without concealment — or as lacking these qualities. They appear in moral instruction, interpersonal requests for candor, and evaluations of character. The contrast they mark is between honest disclosure and concealment or deception, rather than between genuine and fake objects. A distinctive feature of this group is the emphasis on the alignment between inner state and outward expression, including the culturally specific *loob* (inner self) constructions.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tapat* — "honest, sincere, straightforward, frank"; evaluates persons or statements as without concealment; "an honest person" (*tapatang tao*); "speak honestly" (*magsalita nang tapat*)
- *katapatan* — abstract noun: "honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty"; "Honesty is important" (*Ang katapatan ay mahalaga*)
- *tapatang-tapat* — "completely honest"
- *tapat na tapat* — "truly honest"
- *maging tapat sa akin* — "be honest with me"
- *maging tapat ka sa akin* — "be honest with me"
- *maging tapat sa iyong sarili* — "be honest with yourself"
- *tapat sa sarili* — "honest with oneself"
- *magtapat ka na* — "come clean already"
- *tapatan mo na siya* — "be honest with him/her already"
- *nagtapat* — completed: "confessed, came clean"
- *nagtapat siya sa kanyang asawa* — "he came clean to his spouse"
- *nagtatapat* — progressive: "is confessing, is disclosing frankly"
- *magtapat* — verbal: "to be honest, to confess, to come clean"
- *pagtapat* — "the act of honest disclosure"
- *pagtatapat* — nominal: "confession, honest disclosure"
- *pagtatapat ng kasalanan* — "confession of sin"
- *tapat na pagtatapat* — "honest/sincere confession"
- *pagiging tapat* — "being honest, honesty as a state"
- *tapat ang kanyang loob* — "his/her heart is sincere"
- *tapat na loob* — "sincere inner self, honest disposition"
- *katapatan ng loob* — "sincerity of inner disposition"
- *tapat na salita* — "honest words, frank speech"
- *tapatang sagot* — "honest answer"
- *tapat na sagot* — "honest answer"
- *tapat na puso* — "an honest/sincere heart"; fixed formula in moral and religious discourse
- *tapat na ulat* — "honest/accurate report"
- *tapat na tao* — "an honest person"
- *taos-pusong katapatan* — "wholehearted sincerity"
- *hindi tapat* — "not honest, insincere"
- *walang katapatan* — "faithless, without loyalty/sincerity"
- *sa totoo lang* — "to tell the truth, honestly speaking" (overlaps with Group 1)
- *magsalita nang tapat* — "to speak honestly"
- *sinungaling* — "liar, lying, deceitful"; "You're a liar!" (*Sinungaling ka!*)
- *sinungaling ka* — "you are a liar"
- *kasinungalingan* — abstract noun: "lie, falsehood, untruth"
- *huwag magsabi ng kasinungalingan* — "do not tell a lie"
- *huwag kang magsinungaling* — "don't lie"
- *magsinungaling* — verbal: "to lie, to tell a lie"
- *nagsinungaling* — completed: "lied"
- *pagsisinungaling* — nominal: "the act of lying"
- *puno ng kasinungalingan* — "full of lies/falsehood"; fixed intensified accusation
- *Huwag kang maniwala sa kasinungalingan* — "Don't believe the lie"; fixed moral/religious formula
- *katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan* — "truth versus falsehood"
- *totoo sa sarili* — "true to oneself"; evaluates whether a person acts in accordance with their genuine values

**Notes:** *Sa totoo lang* appears in both Group 1 and here: it frames a statement as factually true (Group 1) and simultaneously signals that the speaker is being candid rather than evasive (this group). The two functions co-occur in the same expression. *Tapat* also extends into loyalty and fidelity (see Group 6), but the honesty-and-directness cluster is the most consistently attested usage pattern in interpersonal and moral discourse. The *loob* constructions (*tapat na loob*, *katapatan ng loob*) are specific to this group and reflect a culturally specific moral-psychological framework in Tagalog. *Pagtatapat* in religious contexts (confession of sin) involves an institutional dimension not present in the other items. *Kasinungalingan* and *sinungaling* are the primary contrasts to *tapat* in this group; they also appear in Group 11 (deliberate deception). *Tapat na pagmamahal* and *tapat na asawa* overlap with Group 6 (loyalty and fidelity) since *tapat* in those collocations foregrounds faithfulness rather than honesty of statement. *Katapatan* is the most cross-group item in the dataset, covering honesty of statement (this group), loyal commitment (Group 6), and credibility of witness (Group 7).

## Group 6: Expressions used in contexts of loyalty, fidelity, and sustained commitment to a person, institution, or cause

**Label:** Marking loyalty and faithful commitment

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons or relationships as characterized by sustained loyalty, fidelity, and dedication — to a spouse, nation, God, employer, or cause. They appear in religious, civic, and relational discourse. The contrast they mark is between genuine ongoing commitment and betrayal, abandonment, or mere nominal affiliation.

**Representative expressions:**

- *katapatan sa Diyos* — "faithfulness to God"
- *katapatan sa bansa* — "loyalty to the nation"
- *katapatan sa pag-ibig* — "faithfulness in love, fidelity in a relationship"
- *katapatan sa trabaho* — "dedication to work, commitment to one's job"
- *katapatan sa pangako* — "faithfulness to a promise"
- *tapat na asawa* — "a faithful spouse"
- *tapat na kaibigan* — "a loyal/faithful friend"
- *tapat na lingkod* — "faithful servant"
- *tapat na lingkod ng bayan* — "faithful servant of the nation"
- *tapat na serbisyo* — "faithful service"
- *tapat na paglilingkod* — "faithful service (over time)"
- *tapat na pag-ibig* — "faithful love"
- *tapat na kasamahan* — "loyal companion"
- *tapat na pangako* — "a sincere/genuine promise"
- *taos-pusong katapatan* — "wholehearted fidelity"
- *sinubok ang kanyang katapatan* — "his/her loyalty was tested"
- *ipinakita niya ang kanyang katapatan* — "he/she demonstrated his/her loyalty"
- *walang katapatan* — "faithless, disloyal"
- *tiwala* — "trust, confidence, reliance"; "I trust him/her" (*May tiwala ako sa kanya*)
- *may tiwala ako sa kanya* — "I trust him/her"
- *wala na akong tiwala sa kanya* — "I no longer trust him/her"
- *magtiwala* — verbal: "to trust, to rely on"
- *nagtiwala* — "trusted (and was let down)"; "He/she trusted the wrong person" (*Nagtiwala siya sa maling tao*)
- *nagtiwala ako sa kanya at niloko niya ako* — "I trusted him and he deceived me"
- *nagtitiwala* — "trusts (habitually)"
- *pagtitiwala* — "trust must be earned" (*Ang pagtitiwala ay kailangang kitain*)
- *tiwalahin* — verbal: "to trust, to rely on"
- *pagtitiwalaan* — "to be trusted; the act of placing trust"
- *walang tiwala* — "the citizens have no trust in the government"
- *kawalan ng tiwala* — "lack of trust, loss of confidence"
- *pinagkakatiwalaan* — "the one trusted by everyone" (*Siya ang pinagkakatiwalaan ng lahat*)
- *mapagkakatiwalaan* — "trustworthy, reliable, dependable"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* — "untrustworthy, unreliable"
- *tiwala sa sarili* — "self-confidence"
- *Magtiwala ka sa Diyos* — "Trust in God"; fixed religious formula
- *boto ng walang tiwala* — "vote of no confidence"; fixed political-institutional formula

**Notes:** This group shares the root *tapat* and the derived nominal *katapatan* with Group 5. The distinction between the two groups is not a distinction between two different words but between two different usage contexts for the same words: Group 5 covers honesty and frank disclosure in communication; this group covers sustained relational or institutional loyalty. Both senses are well attested and the P1 data explicitly notes the dual sense of *katapatan*. Many tokens of *katapatan* are unambiguously in one group or the other based on context (*katapatan sa asawa* is clearly loyalty; *tapat na salita* is clearly honesty in speech), but some tokens are genuinely ambiguous. *Tiwala* and its derivations are placed here because trust is the relational basis for the fidelity constructions, but *mapagkakatiwalaan* also participates in Group 7 (credibility assessment) through its application to information sources and witnesses. *Nagtiwala siya sa maling tao* ("he/she trusted the wrong person") is a particularly attested construction that belongs clearly to this group.

## Group 7: Expressions used to assess the credibility or reliability of persons and sources

**Label:** Credibility and reliability assessment

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a person, witness, source, or institution can be relied upon to provide accurate information or to perform their role faithfully. They appear prominently in legal, journalistic, and institutional discourse. The cluster involves *mapagkakatiwalaan* (trustworthy, reliable), *kredible* (credible), and related constructions.

**Representative expressions:**

- *mapagkakatiwalaan* — "trustworthy, reliable, dependable"
