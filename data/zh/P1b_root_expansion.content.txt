# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

Based on frequency and productivity across the P1 items, the three most central roots are:

### Root 1: 真 (zhēn)
- **Participates in**: 6 P1 items (真, 真实, 真相, 真理, 真正, 货真价实)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Authenticity, genuineness, correspondence to reality; contrasts with false/fake/artificial

### Root 2: 实 (shí)
- **Participates in**: 9 P1 items (实, 实际, 真实, 虚假, 事实, 诚实, 确实, 名副其实, 货真价实, 名不副实)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Solidity, actuality, substantiality; what is real/concrete rather than empty/theoretical

### Root 3: 正 (zhèng)
- **Participates in**: 4 P1 items (正确, 真正, 正当, 言行一致)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Correctness, uprightness, proper alignment with standards or norms

## STEP 2 — EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1: 真 (zhēn)

1. **真诚 (zhēnchéng)**
   - Literal: true + sincere
   - Translation: sincere, genuine
   - Context: Used to evaluate the sincerity of emotions, attitudes, or interpersonal conduct. Common in descriptions of relationships and personal character.
   - [CORE]

2. **真心 (zhēnxīn)**
   - Literal: true + heart
   - Translation: sincere, wholehearted, genuine intention
   - Context: Evaluates whether someone's intentions or feelings are genuine. Used in both everyday speech and formal contexts to assess authenticity of emotion or commitment.
   - [CORE]

3. **真挚 (zhēnzhì)**
   - Literal: true + sincere/earnest
   - Translation: sincere, genuine, heartfelt
   - Context: Literary and formal register term for deep sincerity, especially in emotions or relationships. More elevated than 真诚.
   - [CORE]

4. **真切 (zhēnqiè)**
   - Literal: true +切 (切: close,切实: practical)
   - Translation: vivid, distinct, genuine
   - Context: Describes experiences, feelings, or perceptions that are vivid and authentic. Often used for sensory or emotional experiences that feel real and immediate.
   - [CORE]

5. **真假 (zhēnjiǎ)**
   - Literal: true + false
   - Translation: true or false, genuine or fake, authenticity
   - Context: Used to discuss or question the authenticity of something. Common in contexts of verification, authentication, or distinguishing real from counterfeit.
   - [CORE]

6. **真伪 (zhēnwěi)**
   - Literal: true + false/counterfeit
   - Translation: authenticity, genuineness vs. falseness
   - Context: More formal than 真假, used in legal, academic, and technical contexts to discuss authentication and verification of documents, artifacts, or claims.
   - [CORE]

7. **真品 (zhēnpǐn)**
   - Literal: true + product/item
   - Translation: genuine article, authentic product
   - Context: Used in commerce and collecting to distinguish authentic items from counterfeits or reproductions. Common in art, antiques, and luxury goods contexts.
   - [CORE]

8. **真迹 (zhēnjì)**
   - Literal: true + trace/mark
   - Translation: authentic work (of calligraphy or painting), original
   - Context: Specifically used in art and calligraphy contexts to refer to original works by a master, as opposed to copies or forgeries.
   - [CORE]

9. **真传 (zhēnchuán)**
   - Literal: true + transmission
   - Translation: authentic teaching/tradition, true transmission
   - Context: Used in contexts of traditional arts, martial arts, or crafts to indicate authentic transmission of knowledge or technique from master to student.
   - [CORE]

10. **真知 (zhēnzhī)**
    - Literal: true + knowledge
    - Translation: true knowledge, genuine understanding
    - Context: Philosophical and educational contexts referring to authentic knowledge or understanding, as opposed to superficial or false knowledge.
    - [CORE]

11. **真情 (zhēnqíng)**
    - Literal: true + feeling/emotion
    - Translation: true feelings, genuine emotion
    - Context: Describes authentic emotions or the true state of affairs in a situation. Used in both emotional and factual contexts.
    - [CORE]

12. **真意 (zhēnyì)**
    - Literal: true + meaning/intention
    - Translation: true meaning, real intention
    - Context: Refers to the actual meaning or intention behind words or actions, especially when it may differ from surface appearance.
    - [CORE]

13. **真谛 (zhēndì)**
    - Literal: true + profound meaning
    - Translation: true meaning, essence, profound truth
    - Context: Elevated, often philosophical or religious term for deep or essential truth. Used in discussions of fundamental principles or meanings.
    - [CORE]

14. **真空 (zhēnkōng)**
    - Literal: true + empty/void
    - Translation: vacuum, void
    - Context: Scientific/technical term for vacuum. Also used in Buddhist philosophy for ultimate emptiness or void.
    - [CORE]

15. **真人 (zhēnrén)**
    - Literal: true + person
    - Translation: real person, genuine person; Daoist immortal/perfected person
    - Context: Can mean an actual person (vs. fictional) or in Daoist contexts, a perfected/enlightened being. Also used colloquially for authentic, unpretentious people.
    - [CORE]

16. **真实性 (zhēnshíxìng)**
    - Literal: true + real + nature/quality
    - Translation: authenticity, truthfulness, veracity
    - Context: Abstract noun used in academic, legal, and technical contexts to discuss the quality of being authentic or true.
    - [CORE]

17. **真材实料 (zhēn cái shí liào)**
    - Literal: true + material + solid + ingredient
    - Translation: genuine materials, real substance
    - Context: Fixed expression indicating something is made of genuine, quality materials. Used literally for products and metaphorically for substantial content.
    - [CORE]

18. **真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù)**
    - Literal: true + evidence + solid + proof
    - Translation: conclusive evidence, solid proof
    - Context: Legal and investigative contexts referring to reliable, concrete evidence. Emphasizes both authenticity and substantiality of proof.
    - [CORE]

19. **真刀真枪 (zhēn dāo zhēn qiāng)**
    - Literal: true + knife + true + spear
    - Translation: real swords and spears; genuine combat/effort
    - Context: Originally referring to actual weapons/combat, now used metaphorically for serious, genuine effort or real confrontation (not practice or pretense).
    - [CORE]

20. **真金不怕火炼 (zhēn jīn bù pà huǒ liàn)**
    - Literal: true + gold + not + fear + fire + refine
    - Translation: true gold fears no fire; genuine article stands testing
    - Context: Proverb meaning that what is genuine can withstand scrutiny or testing. Used to express confidence in authenticity or quality.
    - [CORE]

21. **真龙天子 (zhēn lóng tiān zǐ)**
    - Literal: true + dragon + heaven + son
    - Translation: true Son of Heaven, legitimate emperor
    - Context: Historical/literary term for the legitimate emperor. Used in historical narratives and sometimes metaphorically for rightful leader.

22. **真身 (zhēnshēn)**
    - Literal: true + body
    - Translation: real body, true form; actual person
    - Context: Can refer to someone's actual physical presence (vs. representation), or in Buddhist contexts, the true body of Buddha. Also used for relics.
    - [CORE]

23. **真丝 (zhēnsī)**
    - Literal: true + silk
    - Translation: real silk, genuine silk
    - Context: Commercial term distinguishing genuine silk from synthetic fabrics. Used in textile and fashion contexts.
    - [CORE]

24. **真皮 (zhēnpí)**
    - Literal: true + leather/skin
    - Translation: genuine leather
    - Context: Commercial term for real leather as opposed to synthetic or imitation leather. Common in product descriptions.
    - [CORE]

25. **真菌 (zhēnjūn)**
    - Literal: true + fungus
    - Translation: true fungi, eumycetes
    - Context: Scientific/biological term for true fungi as a taxonomic category.
    - [CORE]

26. **真核 (zhēnhé)**
    - Literal: true + nucleus
    - Translation: eukaryotic
    - Context: Biological term for organisms with true cell nuclei (eukaryotes).
    - [CORE]

27. **真分数 (zhēn fēnshù)**
    - Literal: true + fraction + number
    - Translation: proper fraction
    - Context: Mathematical term for fractions where numerator is less than denominator.
    - [CORE]

28. **真命题 (zhēn mìngtí)**
    - Literal: true + proposition
    - Translation: true proposition
    - Context: Logical/mathematical term for propositions that are true.
    - [CORE]

29. **认真 (rènzhēn)**
    - Literal: recognize/take + true
    - Translation: serious, earnest, conscientious
    - Context: Very common term describing serious, careful attitude toward tasks or responsibilities. Used in everyday speech and formal contexts.
   - [CORE]

30. **当真 (dàngzhēn)**
    - Literal: treat as + true
    - Translation: take seriously, take as true
    - Context: Used to question or affirm whether something should be taken seriously or literally. Common in conversational contexts.
    - [CORE]

**Note**: 真 is highly productive. Additional compounds exist in specialized domains (medicine: 真菌感染, law: 真凶, etc.). The above represents the most common and representative uses.

### ROOT 2: 实 (shí)

1. **实在 (shízài)**
   - Literal: solid/real + exist
   - Translation: real, true, honest; really, indeed
   - Context: Functions as both adjective (real, honest) and adverb (really, truly). Used in everyday speech to emphasize reality or sincerity.
   - [CORE]

2. **实情 (shíqíng)**
   - Literal: real + situation/feeling
   - Translation: actual situation, true facts, real circumstances
   - Context: Refers to the actual state of affairs, especially when it may differ from what is claimed or appears. Common in investigative and reporting contexts.
   - [CORE]

3. **实话 (shíhuà)**
   - Literal: real + speech
   - Translation: truth, honest words
   - Context: Everyday term for truthful speech. Common in phrases like 说实话 (speak the truth, to be honest).
   - [CORE]

4. **实证 (shízhèng)**
   - Literal: real + evidence/proof
   - Translation: empirical evidence, positive proof
   - Context: Used in academic and scientific contexts for empirical verification or evidence-based proof. Also refers to positivism in philosophy.
   - [CORE]

5. **实验 (shíyàn)**
   - Literal: real + test/examine
   - Translation: experiment, test
   - Context: Scientific and educational term for experiments or empirical testing. Very common in scientific and technical contexts.
   - [CORE]

6. **实践 (shíjiàn)**
   - Literal: real + practice/tread
   - Translation: practice, put into practice
   - Context: Refers to practical application or implementation, as opposed to theory. Important in Marxist philosophy and everyday usage contrasting theory with practice.
   - [CORE]

7. **实施 (shíshī)**
   - Literal: real + carry out
   - Translation: implement, carry out
   - Context: Formal term for implementing policies, plans, or measures. Common in administrative and legal contexts.

8. **实行 (shíxíng)**

9. **实现 (shíxiàn)**
   - Literal: real + appear/manifest
   - Translation: realize, achieve, bring about
   - Context: Very common term for achieving goals, realizing plans, or making something actual. Used in all registers.
   - [CORE]

10. **实力 (shílì)**
    - Literal: solid + strength
    - Translation: actual strength, real power, capability
    - Context: Refers to actual capability or strength, as opposed to nominal or claimed power. Used in military, economic, and competitive contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **实质 (shízhì)**
    - Literal: solid + quality/nature
    - Translation: essence, substance, essential nature
    - Context: Refers to the essential nature or substance of something, as opposed to superficial appearance. Used in analytical and philosophical contexts.
    - [CORE]

12. **实体 (shítǐ)**
    - Literal: solid + body/entity
    - Translation: entity, substance; physical (store/business)
    - Context: Philosophical term for substantial entity. Also used in business contexts to distinguish physical stores from online ones (实体店).
    - [CORE]

13. **实物 (shíwù)**
    - Literal: real + thing/object
    - Translation: material object, physical object, object in kind
    - Context: Refers to actual physical objects as opposed to representations, descriptions, or monetary equivalents. Used in commerce and documentation.
    - [CORE]

14. **实例 (shílì)**
    - Literal: real + example
    - Translation: actual example, concrete case
    - Context: Refers to real, concrete examples or instances, as opposed to hypothetical ones. Common in academic and explanatory contexts.
    - [CORE]

15. **实用 (shíyòng)**
    - Literal: real + use
    - Translation: practical, pragmatic, functional
    - Context: Evaluates something as practically useful or functional. Common in product descriptions and pragmatic assessments.
    - [CORE]

16. **实惠 (shíhuì)**

    - Context: Refers to tangible benefits or good value. Common in commercial contexts and discussions of practical advantages.

17. **实效 (shíxiào)**

18. **实况 (shíkuàng)**

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### Root 真 (zhēn) — Peripheral Extensions

**Scientific/Technical Domain**:
- 真空 (vacuum), 真菌 (true fungi), 真核 (eukaryotic), 真分数 (proper fraction): The root contributes a sense of "genuine/actual" category membership in taxonomic and technical classification, extending from truth-evaluation to categorical authenticity.
