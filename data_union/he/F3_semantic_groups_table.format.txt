| Group 1 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a statement or claim is correct or true | These expressions are used in everyday spoken Hebrew and in journalistic and analytical writing to confirm or deny that a statement, answer, or fact is correct. They function primarily as responses or evaluations in conversational exchange, and also appear in educational contexts (evaluating answers) and administrative contexts (temporally qualifying claims). They are the default register-neutral tools for asserting or denying factual accuracy in ordinary discourse. | Group 3 — Expressions used to evaluate precision and exactness | These expressions evaluate whether a statement, measurement, description, or representation achieves the required degree of exactness or specificity. They are particularly common in scientific, journalistic, legal, and technical registers, and they contrast with terms meaning "vague" or "approximate." The evaluative dimension concerns degree of fit between a representation and its object, not simply whether something is true or false. | *meduyaq* (מְדוּיָּק); *diyyuq* (דִּיּוּק); *bediyyuq* (בְּדִיּוּק); *ledayyeq* (לְדַיֵּק); *medayyeq* (מְדַיֵּק); *dayqan* (דַּיְקָן); *dayqanut* (דַּיְקָנוּת); *ʾi-diyyuq* (אִי-דִּיּוּק); *ḥoser diyyuq* (חֹסֶר דִּיּוּק); *lo meduyaq* (לֹא מְדוּיָּק); *meduyaq beyoter* (מְדוּיָּק בְּיוֹתֵר); *meduyaq yoter* (מְדוּיָּק יוֹתֵר); *lihyot meduyaq* (לִהְיוֹת מְדוּיָּק); *teʾur meduyaq* (תֵּאוּר מְדוּיָּק); *meydaʿ meduyaq* (מֵידָע מְדוּיָּק); *nekhonot* (נְכוֹנוּת) | The group overlaps with Group 1 (correctness evaluation) in that precision is a dimension of correctness | The precision cluster is distinguished by its emphasis on degree and exactness rather than binary true/false evaluation. *nekhonot* is placed here rather than in Group 1 because as an abstract noun it tends to appear in more formal evaluative contexts. |
| Group 4 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a person, source, or information can be trusted or relied upon | These expressions evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be depended upon to be honest and accurate over time. They are common in journalistic, academic, and interpersonal contexts. They differ from Group 5 (legal and quasi-legal evaluation) in that they are not restricted to formal legal proceedings and do not carry procedural implications about admissibility. The evaluative dimension concerns the reliability of the entity over time or across situations, not merely the truth of a single statement. | *ʾamin* (אָמִין); *ʾaminut* (אֲמִינוּת); *ʾaminut meyda* (אֲמִינוּת מֵידָע); *lo ʾamin* (לֹא אָמִין); *nirʾe ʾamin* (נִרְאֶה אָמִין); *paḥot ʾamin* (פָּחוֹת אָמִין); *ʾi-ʾaminut* (אִי-אֲמִינוּת); *maqor ʾamin* (מָקוֹר אָמִין); *maqor mehiman* (מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן); *musad mehiman* (מוּסָד מְהֵימָן); *mehiman* (מְהֵימָן); *mehimanut* (מְהֵימָנוּת); *lo mehiman* (לֹא מְהֵימָן); *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן); *neʾemanut* (נֶאֱמָנוּת); *lo neʾeman* (לֹא נֶאֱמָן); *neʾeman la-ʿuvdot* (נֶאֱמָן לָעוּבְדוֹת); *neʾeman la-teqst* (נֶאֱמָן לַטֶּקְסְט); *neʾeman la-ʾemet* (נֶאֱמָן לָאֱמֶת); *neʾeman li-dvarav* (נֶאֱמָן לִדְבָרָיו); *ʾemun* (אֱמוּן); *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* (אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר); *mashber ʾemun* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן); *mashber ʾaminut* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֲמִינוּת); *ʾemuna* (אֱמוּנָה); *lishmor ʾemunim* (לִשְׁמוֹר אֱמוּנִים); *binyan ʾamon* (בְּנִיַּת אָמוֹן); *ʾi-ʾamon* (אִי-אָמוֹן); *hatsbaʿat ʾi-ʾamon* (הַצְבָּעַת אִי-אָמוֹן) | *mehiman* and *ʾamin* are near-synonyms with register differences: *mehiman* is more formal and more specifically legal than *ʾamin*; *neʾeman* emphasizes ongoing faithfulness and loyalty (a relational quality), while *mehiman* emphasizes credibility and believability (an epistemic quality); *ʾaminut* appears in both this group and Group 5 (legal evaluation of testimony); *ʾemuna* in its religious sense belongs to Group 7; in its interpersonal sense (trust) it belongs here | The distinction between this group and Group 5 is contextual: Group 4 items are not restricted to formal legal proceedings and do not carry procedural implications about admissibility. |
| Group 5 — Expressions used in legal and quasi-legal evaluation of testimony, witnesses, and evidence | These expressions appear specifically in the context of legal proceedings, halakhic adjudication, or formal evaluation of testimony, where the question is whether a witness or piece of evidence meets the required standard of reliability and admissibility. They are used by courts, legal scholars, and in legal journalism.
