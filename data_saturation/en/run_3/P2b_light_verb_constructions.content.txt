# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 28 P1 base forms. The following categories are included where attested:

- **Fixed phrases and idioms**: multi-word expressions with non-compositional or semi-fixed meaning
- **Periphrastic constructions**: multi-word expressions that function as predicates, modifiers, or nominals
- **Collocations**: strongly preferred word combinations that are not fully fixed but are conventionally associated
- **Syntactic frames**: productive patterns in which the base form participates with specific syntactic partners

Constructions already documented in P1b (root expansion) are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. Derivational forms are excluded per protocol instructions. Where no common construction is identified for a base form or construction type, this is stated explicitly.

The protocol requires preservation of asymmetry. Some base forms participate in many constructions; others participate in few or none beyond their basic predicative and attributive uses.

### 1. Base Form: **true**

---

**Construction 1.1**
- Example: *come true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: (of a hope, dream, or prediction) to be realized or fulfilled
- Usage notes: Used with subjects that are wishes, dreams, predictions, or fears; *her dream came true*; *the worst fears came true*. A fixed verbal idiom; *true* is not substitutable here.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**Construction 1.2**
- Example: *ring true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to seem genuine or plausible; to have the quality of truth
- Usage notes: Used of statements, explanations, and accounts; *his explanation didn't quite ring true*. Derived from the practice of testing coins by their sound. Contrasts with *ring false* and *ring hollow*.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**Construction 1.3**
- Example: *ring false*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to seem implausible or insincere; to lack the quality of truth
- Usage notes: The negative counterpart of *ring true*; *her apology rang false*. Included here as it forms a minimal pair with *ring true* and is conventionally associated with the *true* base form.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**Construction 1.4**
- Example: *hold true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to remain valid or applicable; to continue to be the case
- Usage notes: Used of principles, rules, and generalizations; *this observation holds true across all cases*; *the same holds true for the second group*. A semi-fixed verbal phrase.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**Construction 1.5**
- Example: *prove true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to turn out to be true; to be confirmed as accurate
- Usage notes: Used of predictions, suspicions, and claims; *the prediction proved true*; *her fears proved true*. Compositional but conventionally used.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**Construction 1.6**
- Example: *true to one's word*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: keeping a promise; doing what one said one would do
- Usage notes: Used predicatively; *she was true to her word and returned the money*. A fixed prepositional phrase functioning as a predicate complement.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

**Construction 1.7**

### 8. Base Form: **fake**
