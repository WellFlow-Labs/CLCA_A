# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)
# Layer: P3_semantic_groups

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms appear in the same collocational environments, which are explicitly contrasted with each other, which share register restrictions, which participate in the same derivational patterns, and which are described as antonyms or near-synonyms in the source data. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

## GROUP 1: Terms used to evaluate whether a statement or claim corresponds to fact

**Label:** Evaluating statements as correct or incorrect

**Definition:** These terms are applied to propositions, claims, statements, and representations to judge whether they correspond to fact or to an accepted standard. They appear in contexts where the question is whether something said or asserted is accurate. They contrast with each other primarily along dimensions of register and degree of moral charge, not along a simple true/false binary.

**Representative expressions:**

- *doğru* — the broadest and most frequent term; applied to statements, beliefs, and actions across all registers; used to evaluate a statement, answer, or direction as correct or accurate
- *yanlış* — the primary antonym of *doğru*; applied to statements and beliefs judged to deviate from correctness
- *doğruluk* — the nominalized property; used when the quality of being true or accurate is itself under discussion; also names the property of being accurate or truthful in philosophical and everyday evaluation
- *doğruluk değeri* — technical use in logic for the truth value of a proposition
- *doğruluk koşulu* — technical use in formal semantics for truth conditions
- *doğruluk tablosu* — truth table; technical term in formal logic
- *yanlışlık* — the nominalized property of being wrong or incorrect; names the property or instance of being wrong
- *doğrulanabilir* / *doğrulanabilirlik* — used in scientific and philosophical discourse for claims that can in principle be verified
- *yanlışlanabilir* / *yanlışlanabilirlik* — the parallel falsificationist pair; used in philosophy of science
- *doğrulamak* / *doğrulama* — the act or process of verifying a claim
- *yanlışlamak* / *yanlışlama* — the act or process of demonstrating a claim to be false
- *doğru mu yanlış mı?* — the formulaic binary question in educational and evaluative contexts
- *doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek* — the periphrastic construction for discriminating true from false
- *doğru bilgi* — correct information; used in journalistic and educational contexts to distinguish accurate from inaccurate information
- *doğru haber* — accurate news; used in journalistic contexts
- *doğruyu söylemek* — to tell the truth; the canonical collocation for making a true statement
- *gerçeğe aykırı beyan* — statement contrary to fact; used in legal contexts
- *gerçeğe uygun beyan* — statement conforming to fact; used in legal contexts
- *gerçeği söylemek* — to tell the truth; collocation for stating what is factually the case
- *hakikati söylemek* — to speak the truth; more formal or literary variant
- *doğru yanlış sorusu* — a true/false question; educational usage
- *doğruluk payı var* — there is a grain of truth in it
- *doğruluk payı olmak* — there is a grain of truth in something
- *asılsız* — unfounded, baseless; used for claims lacking factual basis; appears in legal and journalistic contexts
- *asılsız iddia* — unfounded claim
- *asılsızlık* — groundlessness, lack of factual basis
- *aslında* — actually, in fact; used to correct a misapprehension about what is the case
- *doğru çıkmak* — to turn out to be correct
- *yalan çıkmak* — to turn out to be a lie
- *yalan yanlış* — thoroughly inaccurate, full of errors and falsehoods

**Notes:** *Doğru* and *yanlış* are the core pair. The technical terms (*doğruluk değeri*, *doğruluk koşulu*, *yanlışlanabilirlik*) are restricted to academic and philosophical registers but derive from the same base. *Gerçek* (see Group 2) overlaps with *doğru* in some uses but is distinguished in the data by its stronger ontological emphasis. *doğru* and *gerçek* overlap when used predicatively of statements (e.g., *bu doğru* and *bu gerçek* can both mean "this is true/correct"), but *gerçek* more typically asserts that something is real rather than that a statement is accurate. *hakikat* and *hakikaten* belong primarily here but carry a more elevated register; see also Group 5. *Yalan* overlaps with this group but carries an additional implication of intentionality (see Group 3).

## GROUP 2: Terms used to affirm or question whether something is real or genuine (as opposed to fake, illusory, or fictional)

**Label:** Affirming or questioning ontological genuineness

**Definition:** These terms are applied to objects, persons, identities, situations, and states of affairs to evaluate whether they are what they appear or claim to be — whether they have genuine existence or are counterfeit, simulated, or illusory. The central contrast in this group is between what is real and what is fake, simulated, or constructed. These terms appear frequently in legal contexts involving forgery and fraud, in philosophical contexts involving the nature of reality, and in everyday contexts involving authenticity of persons and feelings.

**Representative expressions:**

- *gerçek* — the primary term; applied to objects, persons, identities, and situations to affirm factual or ontological reality; used to assert that something is not illusory, fabricated, or simulated
- *gerçeklik* — the nominalized property of being real; used in philosophical and technical contexts (*sanal gerçeklik*, *artırılmış gerçeklik*); names the property of being real or the domain of reality; used in philosophical and psychological contexts
- *gerçekten* — the adverbial form; used to affirm or emphasize that something is truly the case
- *gerçek dışı* — used to characterize what does not correspond to reality; appears in legal (*gerçek dışı beyan*) and philosophical contexts; unreal, outside the bounds of reality
- *gerçek dışılık* — unreality
- *gerçeğe aykırı* — formal/legal expression for what is contrary to fact
- *gerçeğe uygun* — the positive counterpart; used in legal contexts for declarations that accurately reflect reality
- *gerçek şu ki* — discourse marker introducing a frank or corrective statement
- *sahte* — applied to objects, documents, and identities presented as genuine but not; the primary term for counterfeit
- *sahteci* / *sahtekâr* / *sahtecilik* / *sahtekârlık* — persons and practices of counterfeiting or fraud
- *sahte belge*, *sahte kimlik*, *sahte para* — fixed legal collocations for forged documents, false identity, counterfeit money
- *sahici* — used in everyday and literary contexts to affirm that something is genuinely what it appears to be, without pretense
- *gerçeği sahteden ayırt etmek* — the periphrastic construction for discriminating genuine from fake
- *sahici mi, sahte mi?* — the interrogative construction posing the authenticity question
- *gerçek kişi* — legal term for a natural person (as opposed to a legal entity)
- *gerçekleşmek* / *gerçekleştirmek* — used for events or plans that come into actual existence; their core meaning is actualization — bringing something from the domain of the possible or planned into the domain of the real
- *gerçekleşme* — realization, occurrence
- *gerçek mi bu?* — Is this real? Is this true?; colloquial expression of disbelief
- *gerçek hayat* — real life; contrasted with fiction or virtual environments
- *gerçek ile kurgu* — reality and fiction; the standard coordinate pair for this contrast
- *gerçek ile kurgu arasındaki sınır* — the boundary between reality and fiction
- *gerçek neden* — the real reason; used to distinguish actual causes from stated ones
- *gerçek yüzünü göstermek* — to show one's true face; reveal true character beneath appearance
- *hakikat* — used in philosophical and literary contexts to name what is essentially real; truth, reality; used in philosophical and literary contexts
- *hakikate ulaşmak* — to reach the truth/reality
- *hakikat arayışı* — the search for truth/reality
- *aslen* — originally, by origin; used to indicate what something fundamentally is
- *gerçek anlamda* — in the true/real sense; asserts that a predicate applies genuinely rather than nominally
- *gerçek manada* — in the true sense; more literary variant
- *gerçeği kabul etmek* — to accept the truth, to face reality
- *gerçekle yüzleşmek* — to confront the truth, to face reality
- *gerçeği yansıtmak* — to reflect reality
- *gerçeği çarpıtmak* — to distort reality
- *gerçeklikten kopuk* — detached from reality
- *gerçeklik algısı* — perception of reality
- *asıl* — the real, original, or fundamental version of something
- *özü* / *özünde* — at its core, in its essential nature

**Notes:** *Gerçek* and *sahte* are the central antonym pair in this group. *Sahici* overlaps with *gerçek* but is more affective and colloquial, particularly in contexts involving persons and emotions. *Gerçek dışı* and *gerçeğe aykırı* are more formal and legal in register. *Gerçekleşmek* and *gerçekleştirmek* are included because they derive from *gerçek* and involve the concept of actualization, but they are also flagged as peripheral: their primary meaning (to come true, to be realized, to carry out) is about actualization and occurrence rather than truth-evaluation or authenticity. *gerçek* and *doğru* overlap when used predicatively of statements, but *gerçek* more typically concerns ontological status (is this thing real?) while *doğru* concerns propositional accuracy (is this statement correct?). *hakikat* belongs here and in Group 5 (elevated register).

## GROUP 3: Terms used to evaluate the authenticity or originality of creative works, documents, and objects

**Label:** Evaluating originality and authenticity of works and objects

**Definition:** These terms are applied specifically to works, documents, and objects to evaluate whether they are the genuine original rather than a copy, imitation, or derivative. They appear in copyright, legal, commercial, and aesthetic contexts. The central contrast is between the original and the copy or imitation.

**Representative expressions:**

- *özgün* — used in aesthetic and intellectual contexts for works or ideas that are not derivative; carries a connotation of intrinsic creative originality
- *özgünlük* — the nominalized property; used in copyright and literary discourse
- *özgün eser* — the standard collocation in copyright law for an original work
- *özgün fikir* — original idea
- *özgün olmak* — to be original
- *özgün yaklaşım* — original approach
- *özgünlük iddiasında bulunmak* — to claim originality
- *özgünlükten yoksun* — lacking originality
- *orijinal* — used in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts to distinguish an authentic item from a copy; tends toward concrete objects
- *orijinallik* — the nominalized property; used in commercial and legal contexts
- *orijinal mi taklit mi?* — the interrogative construction in commercial contexts
- *orijinalinden ayırt edilememek* — the construction for a copy indistinguishable from the original
- *orijinal ürün* — genuine product
- *orijinal ambalaj* — original packaging
- *orijinal parça* — genuine spare part
- *orijinal baskı* — original edition
- *orijinal olmak* — to be genuine/original (of an object)
- *asıl* — used to identify the genuine, foundational, or original instance; *asıl nüsha* (original document)
- *asıl belge* — original document
- *asılsız* — used for claims that lack any evidentiary basis (see also Group 1)
- *sahih* — used in religious and historical scholarship for authentic texts and traditions; *sahih hadis*
- *hakiki* — genuine, real; used in commercial and formal contexts
- *hakiki deri* — genuine leather
- *sahte* — fake, counterfeit, forged; the primary term for fabricated objects or identities
- *sahtecilik* — forgery, counterfeiting, fraud
- *sahteci* — forger, counterfeiter
- *sahtekâr* — fraud, impostor
- *sahtekârlık* — fraud, imposture
- *sahte para* — counterfeit money
- *sahte kimlik* — false identity, fake ID
- *sahte belge* — forged document
- *sahte imza* — forged signature
- *sahte hesap* — fake account
- *sahte haber* — fake news
- *sahte ürün* — counterfeit product
- *sahte profil* — fake profile
- *sahte doktor* — fake doctor, medical impostor
- *sahte çıkmak* — to turn out to be fake
- *taklit* — imitation, counterfeit, copy; applied in aesthetic and commercial contexts
- *taklitçi* — imitator, counterfeiter
- *taklitçilik* — the practice of imitating or counterfeiting
- *taklit ürün* — counterfeit/imitation product
- *taklit etmek* — to imitate, to counterfeit
- *taklidi bırakmak* — to give up imitation
- *taklitçilik özgünlüğün karşıtıdır* — imitation is the opposite of originality (attested usage)

**Notes:** *Özgün* and *orijinal* overlap but are distinguished in the data: *özgün* emphasizes creative originality and intrinsic authenticity, while *orijinal* tends toward the concrete distinction between an authentic item and a copy. *Asıl* overlaps with both but also functions as an adverb (*aslında*, actually) and as a modifier meaning "fundamental" or "real" (see Group 2). *Sahih* is restricted to religious and historical registers and does not participate in the broader authenticity discourse of this group. *hakiki* overlaps with Group 5 (formal/philosophical register) when used in expressions like *hakiki dostluk*, but in commercial contexts (*hakiki deri*) it belongs here. *Taklit* overlaps with Group 4 (deceptive/fake) in commercial contexts where counterfeit goods are at issue, but in aesthetic contexts it describes imitation without necessarily implying intent to deceive.

## GROUP 4: Terms used to characterize claims as lacking evidentiary or factual basis

**Label:** Characterizing claims as unfounded or baseless

**Definition:** These terms are applied to allegations, accusations, and claims to indicate that they lack supporting evidence or factual grounding. They appear primarily in legal and journalistic contexts. The emphasis is not merely that the claim is wrong but that it has no foundation at all.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asılsız* — the primary term; applied to allegations and claims that lack any evidentiary basis; *asılsız iddia*
- *asılsızlık* — the nominalized property; used in legal discourse
- *asılsız olduğu kanıtlanmak* — the construction for formally demonstrating groundlessness
- *asılsız olduğunu öne sürmek* — the construction for alleging groundlessness

**Notes:** This group is small. *Asılsız* is the dominant term and its collocational range is narrow, centering on *asılsız iddia*. The group is distinguished from Group 1 (incorrect statements) by the specific emphasis on absence of foundation rather than mere incorrectness. *Asılsız* does not typically appear in philosophical or scientific discourse about truth values; it is primarily a legal and journalistic term.

## GROUP 5: Terms used in formal, philosophical, or religious registers to invoke deep or essential truth

**Label:** Elevated-register expressions for truth and reality

**Definition:** These expressions cover similar semantic ground to items in Groups 1 and 2 but are distinguished by their consistent appearance in formal, literary, philosophical, or religious registers rather than in everyday speech. They are typically of Arabic origin and carry a weight of seriousness or philosophical depth that their everyday counterparts lack. They are used in contexts where the speaker wishes to invoke a more serious or elevated tone.

**Representative expressions:**

- *hakikat* — truth, reality, the true nature of something; formal, philosophical, literary; deep or fundamental truth, the true nature of something; not merely factual accuracy but something closer to the true nature of things or a truth that demands acknowledgment
- *hakikaten* — truly, really, in truth; formal/literary adverb; more solemn than *gerçekten*
- *hakikati söylemek* — to speak the truth; formal/literary collocation; to tell the truth (in a formal or weighty sense)
- *hakikate ulaşmak* — to reach the truth; philosophical/literary
- *hakikat arayışı* — the search for truth; philosophical/literary
- *hakikat sonrası* — post-truth; contemporary journalistic/academic usage
- *hakiki* — genuine, real, true; formal or literary adjective
- *hakiki dostluk* — true friendship; literary/moral usage
- *hakikaten öyle* — it is truly so; formal affirmation
- *hakikat cesaret ister* — telling the truth requires courage; literary/moral register
- *hakikatperest* — one devoted to truth; literary
- *hakikat karşısında* — in the face of truth
- *hakikat ortaya çıkmak* — the truth comes out
- *acı hakikat* — bitter truth (fixed phrase)
- *hakikat acı olabilir* — the truth can be bitter
- *hakiki anlamda* — in the true sense; more elevated than *gerçek anlamda*
- *sahih* — sound, valid, authentic; used in Islamic and scholarly contexts for texts
- *sahihlik* — soundness, validity; used in textual and religious scholarship
- *sahih hadis* — sound hadith; domain-specific to Islamic studies

**Notes:** This group does not represent a distinct semantic domain but a distinct register distribution. The items here overlap substantially with Groups 1, 2, and 3 in meaning; what distinguishes them is their consistent restriction to elevated registers. *hakikat sonrası* is an exception in that it appears in contemporary journalistic usage, but it retains the formal weight of *hakikat* as its base. *Sahih* is the most domain-restricted item in this group, appearing almost exclusively in Islamic studies and textual scholarship. *Hakiki* overlaps with Group 3 (authenticity of objects and persons: *hakiki deri*) in commercial and interpersonal uses, but its formal register and participation in *hakikat*-cluster constructions places it primarily here.

## GROUP 6: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, decisions, or claims are justified or rightful

**Label:** Evaluating justification and rightfulness

**Definition:** These terms are applied to persons, actions, decisions, and claims to evaluate whether they are justified, rightful, or in accordance with what is owed. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. The central question is whether someone or something has the right on their side.

**Representative expressions:**

- *haklı* — the primary term; used to affirm that a person's position, action, or claim is justified; *haklısın*, *haklı çıkmak*; right, justified, in the right
- *haksız* — the antonym; applied to actions or persons that violate standards of justice or rightful entitlement; *haksız muamele*; unjust, wrong, unwarranted, in the wrong
- *haklılık* — the nominalized property of being justified or in the right; justification, rightness
- *haksızlık* — the nominalized property of being unjust; *haksızlığa uğramak*; injustice, wrong, unfairness
- *haklıca* — justifiably, rightly
- *haklı olmak* — to be right, to be justified
- *haklı çıkmak* — the construction for being vindicated by events; to be proven right, to be vindicated
- *haklı olduğunu kanıtlamak* — the construction for demonstrating one's justification
- *haksız yere* — the adverbial construction for acting without justification
- *haklı olarak* — the adverbial construction for acting with justification
- *haksız olduğunu kabul etmek* — the construction for acknowledging one was in the wrong
- *hak vermek* / *sana hak veriyorum* — the construction for conceding another's point
- *kim haklı, kim haksız?* — the formulaic interrogative in disputes
- *hak* — the base noun; used for rights, entitlements, and (in elevated register) truth
- *hak etmek* — to deserve or merit something
- *hakkını vermek* — to give someone their due; to do justice to something
- *hak ihlali* / *insan hakları* — legal collocations for rights violations
- *haksız kazanç*, *haksız rekabet*, *haksız fiil* — fixed legal collocations
- *haklı gerekçe* — justified reason, valid grounds
- *haklı eleştiri* — justified criticism
- *haklı kılmak* — to justify, to render justified
- *haklı şikâyet* — justified complaint, legitimate grievance
- *haksızca* — unjustly, wrongfully
- *haksız yere suçlamak* — to accuse unjustly
- *hak mı haksız mı?* — right or wrong? justified or unjust?
- *hakkını teslim etmek* — to give someone their due, to acknowledge their merit
- *hak teslimi* — acknowledgment of merit, giving credit where it is due
- *haklı şikâyet / haklı eleştiri / haklı itiraz* — a justified complaint / criticism / objection

**Notes:** *Haklı* and *haksız* are the central antonym pair. The data notes that *haklı* carries both epistemic (being correct) and moral (being justified) senses, making it semantically broad. *Haklı* and *doğru* both translate as "right" in English, but their usage patterns differ: *doğru* evaluates statements and facts, while *haklı* evaluates persons and positions as warranted. The distinction is consistent across the data. The *hak-* root also underlies *hakikat* (Group 5) and *hakkaniyet* (Group 7), but those terms are grouped separately because their primary usage contexts differ. *hak etmek* (to deserve) is related but belongs more naturally to Group 7 (entitlement and rights) because it concerns desert rather than the evaluation of a position as justified. *haklı çıkmak* bridges Groups 1 and 6: it involves both the vindication of a person and the confirmation that their claim was correct.

## GROUP 7: Terms used in contexts of rights, entitlements, and what is due to a person

**Label:** Legal entitlement and rights

**Definition:** These expressions are used in legal, moral, and political contexts to refer to what a person is entitled to, what they deserve, or what is owed to them. They appear in legal, political, and administrative registers. The focus is on entitlement and desert rather than on truth-evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**

- *hak* — right, entitlement, due; the base term
- *haklar* — rights, entitlements
- *hak etmek* — to deserve, to merit
- *hak kazanmak* — to earn a right, to acquire an entitlement
- *hakkını vermek* — to give something its due, to do justice to something
- *hakkını aramak* — to seek one's rights
- *hakkını yemek* — to deprive someone of their due
- *hak tanımamak* — to deny rights
- *hak gözetmek* — to uphold rights
- *hak ihlali* — rights violation
- *hak sahibi* — rights holder
- *hak sahibi olmak* — to be entitled
- *hak aramak* — to seek one's rights
- *hak arama özgürlüğü* — freedom to seek one's rights, access to justice
- *hak tanımak* — to recognize a right
- *hak kazandırmak* — to confer a right, to entitle
- *hak yemek* — to deprive someone of their rights
- *insan hakları* — human rights
- *insan hakları ihlali* — human rights violation
- *temel haklar* — fundamental rights
- *telif hakkı* — copyright
- *telif hakkı ihlali* — copyright infringement
- *fikri mülkiyet hakkı* — intellectual property right
- *oy hakkı* — right to vote
- *oy hakkını kullanmak* — to exercise the right to vote
- *hak ve adalet* — right and justice
- *hak-hukuk* — one's rights and legal entitlements
- *hakkaniyet* — the principle of equitable treatment; used in legal and administrative contexts; equity, fairness
- *hakkaniyetli* — the adjectival form; *hakkaniyetli karar*; equitable, fair
- *hakkaniyetsiz* — the antonym; inequitable, unfair
- *hakkaniyetli bir çözüm bulmak* — the construction for reaching an equitable resolution
- *meşru müdafaa* — self-defense (legitimate defense)

**Notes:** *Hak* participates in both Group 6 (justified/unjustified persons) and Group 7 (entitlements), and this overlap is genuine — the same word covers both domains in Turkish. *Hakkaniyet* is placed here rather than in Group 6 because its usage focuses on distributive fairness rather than on whether a specific person is in the right. *Hakkaniyetli* is more restricted to formal legal writing than *meşru*. *Meşru müdafaa* is placed here as a legal entitlement construction, though *meşru* belongs primarily to Group 8.

## GROUP 8: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, processes, or institutions conform to law or have legitimacy

**Label:** Evaluating legality, procedural validity, and legitimacy

**Definition:** These terms are applied to actions, regulations, rights, institutions, contracts, votes, arguments, and documents to evaluate whether they conform to written law, satisfy conditions required for legal or institutional recognition, or are sanctioned by norms and principles of fairness that may extend beyond written law. They appear in legal, political, and administrative contexts. The data explicitly distinguishes terms evaluating strict legality from those evaluating legitimacy beyond legality.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yasal* — the primary term for conformity with written law; *yasal düzenleme*, *yasal hak*
- *yasallık* — the nominalized property; *yasallık denetimi*
- *yasadışı* — the antonym; used for what is prohibited by law; *yasadışı faaliyet*
- *yasalaşmak* — the process by which a bill becomes law
- *yasalaştırmak* — the act of enacting something as law
- *yasal çerçeve içinde kalmak* — the construction for remaining within the legal framework
- *yasal açıdan değerlendirmek* — the construction for evaluating from a legal standpoint
- *yasal olmakla birlikte meşru olmamak* — the contrastive construction explicitly distinguishing legality from legitimacy
- *yasal ama meşru değil* — the cross-cutting construction distinguishing legality from legitimacy
- *meşru* — the primary term for what is sanctioned by law, norm, or right; *meşru müdafaa*; legitimate, lawful
- *meşruiyet* — the nominalized property; *meşruiyet krizi*; legitimacy
- *meşrulaştırmak* — the act of conferring legitimacy; to legitimize
- *meşrulaşmak* — the process of acquiring legitimacy; to become legitimate
- *meşruiyet zemini* — the basis of legitimacy
- *meşruluk* — legitimacy, lawfulness
- *gayri meşru* — the antonym; used for what lacks legitimacy; illegitimate, unlawful
- *meşru yollarla elde etmek* — the construction for obtaining through legitimate means
- *meşru zemine oturmak* — the construction for resting on legitimate grounds
- *meşruiyetini yitirmek* — to lose legitimacy
- *geçerli* — the primary term for what meets required conditions; used for contracts, arguments, votes, and documents; *geçerli sözleşme*, *geçerli argüman*; valid, in force, currently accepted
- *geçerlilik* — the nominalized property; *pasaportun geçerlilik süresi*; validity, the property of being in force
- *geçerliliğini yitirmek* — the construction for losing validity; for validity to expire
- *geçerli sayılmak* — the construction for being institutionally recognized as valid
- *geçersiz* — the antonym; used for what fails to meet required conditions; *geçersiz oy*, *geçersiz sözleşme*; invalid, void, null
- *geçersizlik* — the nominalized property; *geçersizlik davası*; invalidity, nullity
- *geçersiz kılmak* — the construction for rendering something void; to invalidate, to nullify
- *geçersiz sayılmak* — the construction for being declared invalid; to count as invalid
- *geçersiz ilan etmek* — the construction for formally declaring something void
- *hâlâ geçerli olmak* — the construction for affirming continuing validity
- *geçerli bir neden olmak* — the construction for evaluating whether a reason meets the required standard
- *geçerli neden / geçerli gerekçe* — a valid reason / justification
- *geçerliliği sona ermek* — for validity to expire
- *tasdik* — official certification, attestation
- *tasdik etmek* / *tasdik ettirmek* — to certify, to have certified
- *tasdikli kopya* — certified copy
- *teyit* — confirmation, corroboration
- *teyit etmek* — to confirm, to corroborate
- *teyit edilmek* — to be confirmed
- *teyitli haber* — confirmed news
- *teyit almak* — to receive confirmation
- *teyit beklemek* — to await confirmation
- *meşruiyet krizi* — legitimacy crisis
- *asılsız* — unfounded, baseless, groundless; evaluates claims as lacking the factual basis required for validity
- *asılsızlık* — groundlessness, baselessness
- *sahihlik* — soundness, validity (in scholarly/religious contexts)

**Notes:** The data explicitly contrasts *yasal* with *meşru*, noting that *yasal* refers to conformity with written law while *meşru* can extend to moral or political legitimacy beyond strict legality.
