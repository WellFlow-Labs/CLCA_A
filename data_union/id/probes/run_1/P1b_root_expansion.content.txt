# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **benar** (Latin script; no separate native script)

**Participation in P1 items:** 2 direct items (*benar* entry 1; *sebenarnya* mentioned in derivation); derivatives cited across multiple entries (*kebenaran*, *membenarkan*).

**Core meaning contribution:** The primary positive pole of factual and moral correctness in Indonesian. Serves as the base for the most productive derivational family in the truth-evaluation domain, generating nouns, verbs, adverbs, and fixed phrases covering truth, justification, confirmation, and rectification.

---

### Root 2: **sah** (Latin script; from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*)

**Participation in P1 items:** 3 items directly (*sah* entry 6; *absah* entry 7; *sahih* entry 17); *keabsahan* cited under both *sah* and *absah*; the Arabic root underlies all three.

**Core meaning contribution:** Validity and legitimacy — the evaluation of whether an act, document, status, or claim meets the conditions required for recognition. Operates across legal, religious, administrative, and procedural domains. The three P1 items represent three registers (everyday-formal, formal-legal, Islamic-scholarly) of the same underlying semantic contribution.

---

### Root 3: **bukti** (Latin script)

**Participation in P1 items:** 1 direct item (*terbukti* entry 23); *bukti* cited as base noun; *membuktikan* cited in derivation.

**Core meaning contribution:** Evidence and proof — the grounding of truth claims in demonstrable support. Highly productive in legal, journalistic, scientific, and procedural contexts. Selected over other candidates (e.g., *percaya*, *asli*) because its derivational family most directly concerns the epistemic process of establishing truth rather than merely attributing it.

### ROOT 1: **benar**

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *benar* (root — correct, true)
**Natural English translation:** true, correct, right
**Context of use:** Base adjective/predicate used to affirm factual accuracy or moral correctness in everyday and formal speech. The unmarked positive evaluator of truth in Indonesian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ke-* (nominalizer circumfix) + *benar* + *-an*
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, rightness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse to refer to the property of being true or correct, or to a specific true fact. *Mencari kebenaran* (to seek the truth) is a common collocation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *mem-* (active verb prefix) + *benar* + *-kan* (causative/applicative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to justify, to validate, to correct (something)
**Context of use:** Used in institutional, journalistic, and interpersonal contexts to mean either "to confirm that something is true" or "to justify/excuse an action." *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu* (The authorities confirmed the report).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A

**Context of use:** Discourse adverb used to introduce a correction, clarification, or revelation of what is actually the case, often contrasting with a prior assumption. Extremely common in spoken and written Indonesian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** N/A

**Context of use:** Base adjective/predicate used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to evaluate whether an act or status meets required conditions. *Pernikahan itu sah* (The marriage is valid/legal).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and political contexts to describe the act of officially approving or ratifying a document, law, or decision. *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru* (The parliament ratified the new law).

### ROOT 3: **bukti**

---

**1.**
**Form:** bukti
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *bukti* (root — evidence, proof)
**Natural English translation:** evidence, proof
**Context of use:** Base noun used in legal, journalistic, scientific, and everyday contexts to refer to material or informational support for a claim. *Ada bukti yang kuat* (There is strong evidence).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** membuktikan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *mem-* (active verb prefix) + *bukti* + *-kan* (causative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to prove, to demonstrate, to establish
**Context of use:** Used in legal, scientific, journalistic, and everyday contexts to describe the act of providing evidence sufficient to establish a claim. *Ia membuktikan bahwa ia tidak bersalah* (He proved that he was not guilty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** terbukti
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ter-* (passive/resultative prefix) + *bukti*
**Natural English translation:** proven, demonstrated, established
**Context of use:** Resultative passive used in legal verdicts, scientific conclusions, and journalistic reporting to state that a claim has been established through evidence. *Terbukti bersalah* (proven guilty) is a standard legal formula.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**

**Natural English translation:** proven guilty, found guilty
**Context of use:** Standard legal formula used in court decisions and legal reporting to state a verdict of guilt. The direct counterpart of *tidak terbukti bersalah* (not proven guilty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots in uses where the truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other semantic territory. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From *benar*:**

- **benar-benar** functions as a general intensifier (*benar-benar cantik* — truly beautiful; *benar-benar marah* — really angry) in contexts where no truth claim is being evaluated and the word appears to mark degree or emphasis rather than factual correctness.

- **membenarkan** in some contexts means "to permit" or "to allow" (*tidak dibenarkan masuk* — not permitted to enter) rather than "to confirm as true," shifting from epistemic to deontic meaning.

- **pembenaran** in theological contexts (particularly in Protestant Christian discourse) translates the doctrine of *justification* (*pembenaran oleh iman* — justification by faith), where the term carries a soteriological rather than epistemic meaning.

- **lurus dan benar** evaluates moral character (uprightness) rather than propositional truth, applying *benar* to a person's conduct and disposition.

- **jalan yang benar** in religious contexts refers to the righteous path of conduct rather than a factually correct statement.

---

**From *sah*:**

- **sah** in colloquial speech is used to mean "okay," "fine," or "agreed" (*sah, kita lakukan itu* — okay, let's do that), functioning as an informal marker of acceptance or agreement rather than a formal validity judgment.

- **mengesahkan** in political discourse can mean "to legitimize" in a critical sense, where the concern is not factual truth but the conferral of social or political recognition (*mengesahkan rezim otoriter* — to legitimize an authoritarian regime).

- **sahih** in the compound *Sahih Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim* functions as a proper name element (titles of canonical hadith collections), where the evaluative meaning is frozen into a title.

- **absah** in the phrase *mengabsahkan kekerasan* (to legitimize violence) applies validity-language to moral and political evaluation of conduct rather than to documents or legal acts.

---

**From *bukti*:**

- **membuktikan diri** applies the proof-concept to personal identity and capability demonstration in competitive or professional contexts, where what is "proven" is not a proposition but a person's worth or fitness.

- **bukti nyata keberhasilan** uses *bukti* in institutional and political rhetoric where the function appears to be persuasive display rather than neutral evidentiary assessment.

- **terbukti** in advertising and commercial language (*terbukti efektif* — proven effective) applies the legal/scientific proof concept to product claims, where the evidentiary standard implied may differ substantially from legal or scientific usage.

- **pembuktian terbalik** involves a procedural reversal that shifts the burden from accuser to accused, placing *bukti* in a context where the normal direction of epistemic responsibility is inverted.
