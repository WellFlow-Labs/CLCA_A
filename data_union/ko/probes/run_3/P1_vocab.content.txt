# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 Core Vocabulary Collection
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

**1.**
- **Form:** 진실
- **Transliteration:** jinsil
- **Literal meaning:** 真實 — "true/genuine" + "substance/reality"
- **Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, sincerity
- Used to describe statements, persons, or situations that correspond to reality or are free from deception. Appears in both everyday and formal registers. Participates in derivation: 진실하다 (to be truthful), 진실성 (truthfulness, as a quality).
- **[CORE]**

---

**2.**
- **Form:** 사실
- **Transliteration:** sasil
- **Literal meaning:** 事實 — "matter/affair" + "substance/reality"
- **Typical English translations:** fact, actuality, truth (in the sense of factual accuracy)
- Used to assert that something is the case in reality, often contrasted with opinion, rumor, or fiction. Frequently appears in legal and journalistic contexts. Participates in derivation: 사실적 (factual, realistic), 사실상 (in fact, effectively).
- **[CORE]**

---

**3.**
- **Form:** 진짜
- **Transliteration:** jinjja
- **Literal meaning:** Colloquial; 진 (真, genuine) + 짜 (suffix of uncertain origin, possibly from 것)
- **Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic, truly
- Used colloquially to distinguish genuine items, persons, or states of affairs from imitations or pretenses. Contrasts with 가짜 (fake). Functions as both adjective and adverb in informal speech.
- **[CORE]**

---

**4.**
- **Form:** 가짜
- **Transliteration:** gajja
- **Literal meaning:** 가 (假, false/temporary) + 짜 (suffix, parallel to 진짜)
- **Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, false
- Used to identify objects, persons, or claims that are not what they purport to be. Appears in consumer, legal, and everyday contexts. Directly contrasts with 진짜.
- **[CORE]**

---

**5.**
- **Form:** 정확
- **Transliteration:** jeonghwak
- **Literal meaning:** 正確 — "correct/upright" + "certain/confirmed"
- **Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness
- Used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, or representation corresponds precisely to what it describes. Participates in derivation: 정확하다 (to be accurate), 정확성 (accuracy as a property), 정확히 (accurately, exactly).
- **[CORE]**

---

**6.**
- **Form:** 올바르다
- **Transliteration:** olbareuda
- **Literal meaning:** 올 (upright, proper) + 바르다 (to be straight, correct)
- **Typical English translations:** to be correct, to be right, to be proper
- Used to evaluate conduct, reasoning, or statements as conforming to a correct or morally appropriate standard. Common in ethical and educational discourse. The adjectival form 올바른 modifies nouns directly.
- **[CORE]**

---

**7.**
- **Form:** 옳다
- **Transliteration:** olta
- **Literal meaning:** Native Korean root; "to be right, correct"
- **Typical English translations:** to be right, to be correct, to be just
- Used to assert that a claim, action, or judgment is correct or morally right. Appears in both logical and ethical contexts. Contrasts with 그르다 (to be wrong). Participates in the pair 옳고 그름 (right and wrong).
- **[CORE]**

---

**8.**
- **Form:** 그르다
- **Transliteration:** geureuda
- **Literal meaning:** Native Korean root; "to be wrong, incorrect"
- **Typical English translations:** to be wrong, to be mistaken, to be improper
- Used as the direct antonym of 옳다 in evaluations of correctness or moral rightness. Somewhat formal or literary in contemporary usage. Appears in the fixed expression 옳고 그름.
- **[CORE]**

---

**9.**
- **Form:** 진위
- **Transliteration:** jinwi
- **Literal meaning:** 眞僞 — "true" + "false"
- **Typical English translations:** truth or falsehood, authenticity, genuineness
- Used as a compound noun referring to the question of whether something is true or false, genuine or counterfeit. Common in legal, journalistic, and academic contexts. Appears in expressions such as 진위를 가리다 (to determine truth or falsehood).
- **[CORE]**

---

**10.**
- **Form:** 허위
- **Transliteration:** heowi
- **Literal meaning:** 虛僞 — "empty/false" + "false/deceptive"
- **Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, misrepresentation
- Used in formal and legal contexts to describe statements or representations that are deliberately false or misleading. Appears in legal terminology such as 허위 진술 (false statement) and 허위 광고 (false advertising).
- **[CORE]**

---

**11.**
- **Form:** 거짓
- **Transliteration:** geojit
- **Literal meaning:** Native Korean root; "lie, falsehood"
- **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, falseness
- Used broadly to describe statements or representations that are untrue. Contrasts with 진실. Participates in derivation: 거짓말 (a lie, literally "false word/speech"), 거짓말하다 (to lie).
- **[CORE]**

---

**12.**
- **Form:** 거짓말
- **Transliteration:** geojitmal
- **Literal meaning:** 거짓 (falsehood) + 말 (word, speech)
- **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood
- The standard everyday term for a spoken or communicated lie. Extremely common in all registers. Participates in further derivation: 거짓말쟁이 (liar), 거짓말하다 (to tell a lie).
- **[CORE]**

---

**13.**
- **Form:** 신뢰
- **Transliteration:** sinloe
- **Literal meaning:** 信賴 — "trust/faith" + "rely on"
- **Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, credibility
- Used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or source can be depended upon to be accurate or honest. Participates in derivation: 신뢰하다 (to trust), 신뢰성 (reliability, trustworthiness), 신뢰할 수 있다 (to be trustworthy).
- **[CORE]**

---

**14.**
- **Form:** 신빙성
- **Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong
- **Literal meaning:** 信憑性 — "trust" + "evidence/basis" + "nature/quality"
- **Typical English translations:** credibility, reliability, plausibility
- Used specifically to evaluate whether a claim, testimony, or source has sufficient basis to be believed. Common in legal and journalistic contexts. Often appears in 신빙성이 있다/없다 (to have/lack credibility).
- **[CORE]**

---

**15.**
- **Form:** 타당
- **Transliteration:** tadang
- **Literal meaning:** 妥當 — "appropriate/settled" + "proper/fitting"
- **Typical English translations:** validity, appropriateness, reasonableness
- Used to evaluate whether a claim, argument, or decision is well-founded or appropriate given the circumstances. Participates in derivation: 타당하다 (to be valid/reasonable), 타당성 (validity, reasonableness). Common in academic and legal discourse.
- **[CORE]**

---

**16.**
- **Form:** 유효
- **Transliteration:** yuhyo
- **Literal meaning:** 有效 — "to have" + "effect/validity"
- **Typical English translations:** validity, effectiveness, being in force
- Used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a document, contract, or action has legal force or is still operative. Participates in derivation: 유효하다 (to be valid), 유효성 (validity). Contrasts with 무효 (invalidity, nullity).
- **[CORE]**

---

**17.**
- **Form:** 무효
- **Transliteration:** muhyo
- **Literal meaning:** 無效 — "without" + "effect/validity"
- **Typical English translations:** invalidity, nullity, void
- Used in legal and formal contexts to declare that something lacks legal force or effect. Directly contrasts with 유효. Appears in 무효화하다 (to invalidate, to nullify).
- **[CORE]**

---

**18.**
- **Form:** 진정성
- **Transliteration:** jinjeonseong
- **Literal meaning:** 眞正性 — "true" + "correct/proper" + "nature/quality"
- **Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity
- Used to evaluate whether a person's actions, expressions, or an object's origin are genuine rather than performed or fabricated. Increasingly common in contemporary discourse about personal integrity and artistic authenticity.
- **[CORE]**

---

**19.**
- **Form:** 본질
- **Transliteration:** bonjil
- **Literal meaning:** 本質 — "root/origin" + "substance/nature"
- **Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, substance
- Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday contexts to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance or accidental properties. Participates in derivation: 본질적 (essential, fundamental), 본질적으로 (essentially).
- **[CORE]**

---

**20.**
- **Form:** 실체
- **Transliteration:** silche
- **Literal meaning:** 實體 — "real/actual" + "body/substance"
- **Typical English translations:** substance, entity, true nature, reality
- Used to refer to the actual underlying reality of something, often contrasted with appearance or representation. Appears in philosophical and legal discourse. Related to 실체적 (substantive, real).
- **[CORE]**

---

**21.**
- **Form:** 위조
- **Transliteration:** wijo
- **Literal meaning:** 僞造 — "false/counterfeit" + "to make/create"
- **Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeiting, fabrication
- Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of creating false documents, currency, or other items intended to deceive. Participates in derivation: 위조하다 (to forge, to counterfeit), 위조품 (forged item, counterfeit).
- **[CORE]**

---

**22.**
- **Form:** 모조
- **Transliteration:** mojo
- **Literal meaning:** 模造 — "to imitate/model" + "to make/create"
- **Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, copy
- Used to describe objects made in imitation of an original, not necessarily with intent to deceive but lacking the authenticity of the original. Appears in 모조품 (imitation product, replica). Distinct from 위조 in that deceptive intent is not necessarily implied.
- **[CORE]**

---

**23.**
- **Form:** 정당
- **Transliteration:** jeongdang
- **Literal meaning:** 正當 — "correct/upright" + "proper/fitting"
- **Typical English translations:** legitimacy, justness, rightfulness
- Used to evaluate whether an action, claim, or authority is rightful, justified, or proper. Participates in derivation: 정당하다 (to be legitimate/just), 정당성 (legitimacy, justification), 정당화하다 (to justify). Common in legal, political, and ethical discourse.
- **[CORE]**

---

**24.**
- **Form:** 합법
- **Transliteration:** hapbeop
- **Literal meaning:** 合法 — "to conform/fit" + "law"
- **Typical English translations:** legality, lawfulness
- Used specifically to evaluate whether an action or state of affairs conforms to law. Participates in derivation: 합법적 (legal, lawful), 합법성 (legality). Contrasts with 불법 (illegality).
- **[CORE]**

---

**25.**
- **Form:** 근거
- **Transliteration:** geungeo
- **Literal meaning:** 根據 — "root/basis" + "to rely on/basis"
- **Typical English translations:** basis, grounds, evidence, foundation
- Used to evaluate whether a claim or action has sufficient support or justification. Appears in 근거가 있다/없다 (to be well-founded/unfounded) and 근거 없는 주장 (unsubstantiated claim). Common across academic, legal, and everyday registers.
- **[CORE]**

---

**26.**
- **Form:** 왜곡
- **Transliteration:** waegok
- **Literal meaning:** 歪曲 — "crooked/distorted" + "bent/curved"
- **Typical English translations:** distortion, misrepresentation, twisting
- Used to describe the act of misrepresenting facts, history, or statements by bending or altering them from their true form. Common in journalistic, historical, and political discourse. Participates in derivation: 왜곡하다 (to distort), 왜곡된 (distorted).
- **[CORE]**

---

**27.**
- **Form:** 오류
- **Transliteration:** oryu
- **Literal meaning:** 誤謬 — "mistake/error" + "error/fallacy"
- **Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fallacy
- Used to identify mistakes in reasoning, data, or representation. Common in academic, logical, and technical contexts. Participates in derivation: 오류를 범하다 (to commit an error/fallacy). Overlaps with but is more formal than 실수 (mistake).
- **[CORE]**

---

**28.**
- **Form:** 유사
- **Transliteration:** yusa
- **Literal meaning:** 類似 — "category/kind" + "to resemble"
- **Typical English translations:** similarity, resemblance, likeness
- Used to evaluate the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant in contexts of comparison, imitation, and identification. Participates in derivation: 유사하다 (to be similar), 유사성 (similarity, resemblance). Appears in legal contexts such as 유사품 (similar/imitation product).
- **[CORE]**

---

**29.**
- **Form:** 표절
- **Transliteration:** pyojeol
- **Literal meaning:** 剽竊 — "to plagiarize/steal" + "to steal/pilfer"
- **Typical English translations:** plagiarism, literary theft
- Used to evaluate whether a work or statement has been copied from another source without proper attribution, thereby misrepresenting its origin. Common in academic and artistic contexts. Participates in derivation: 표절하다 (to plagiarize), 표절작 (plagiarized work).
- **[CORE]**

---

**30.**
- **Form:** 공정
- **Transliteration:** gongjeon
- **Literal meaning:** 公正 — "public/impartial" + "correct/just"
- **Typical English translations:** fairness, impartiality, justice
- Used to evaluate whether a process, judgment, or representation is unbiased and treats all parties appropriately. Participates in derivation: 공정하다 (to be fair/impartial), 공정성 (fairness, impartiality). Common in legal, journalistic, and ethical discourse.
- **[CORE]**
