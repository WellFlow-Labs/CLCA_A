# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** This step documents derivational morphology attested for P1 lexical items. Swahili derivation operates primarily through prefixation (noun class prefixes, infinitive *ku-*, adjectival *ki-*) and suffixation (verbal extensions, nominalizing suffixes). Only forms with clear attestation or strong corpus support are included. Paradigm gaps are preserved without comment unless the gap is itself informative. Forms already fully documented in P1 or P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-derived.

---

## BASE FORM 1: **kweli**

---

**Derived Form 1.1**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Derived Form:** ukweli
3. **Transliteration:** ukweli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix, class 14) + *kweli* (truth)
5. **Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
6. **Usage:** The standard abstract nominalization of *kweli*, used across formal, legal, philosophical, and everyday registers. Already documented in P1 as a core item; noted here as the primary derivational product of this root.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.2**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Derived Form:** kweli kweli
3. **Transliteration:** kweli kweli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *kweli* + *kweli* (full reduplication)
5. **Natural English translation:** really, truly, genuinely
6. **Usage:** Reduplication functions as an intensifier in Swahili; *kweli kweli* is the standard intensified form of *kweli*, used in spoken and written discourse to emphasize the genuine nature of a claim. Already documented in P1.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation from kweli:** No verbal derivation of *kweli* (e.g., a verb *\*kwelisha* or *\*kwelika*) is attested in standard usage. The root does not appear to take verbal extensions productively. The primary derivational output is *ukweli* (nominalization) and *kweli kweli* (reduplication).

## BASE FORM 3: **uongo**

---

**Derived Form 3.1**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Derived Form:** mwongo
3. **Transliteration:** mwongo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (noun class 1 prefix, human agent) + *-ongo* (falsehood/deception stem)
5. **Natural English translation:** liar, deceiver
6. **Usage:** *Mwongo* is the standard agentive noun for a person who lies or deceives, used in moral condemnation and character evaluation across everyday, literary, and religious registers (*yeye ni mwongo*, "he/she is a liar").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.2**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Derived Form:** waongo
3. **Transliteration:** waongo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (noun class 2 prefix, human plural) + *-ongo* (falsehood/deception stem)
5. **Natural English translation:** liars, deceivers
6. **Usage:** The plural of *mwongo*, used to refer to multiple persons characterized as liars. Appears in moral discourse and narrative (*waongo hawaamini*, "liars are not trusted").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on verbal derivation from uongo:** The verb *kuongea* (to speak, to talk) shares the *-ong-* stem but is not straightforwardly derived from *uongo* in the sense of "to lie"; its primary meaning is neutral speech. No verb meaning specifically "to lie" is derived directly from *uongo* in standard usage; the verb for that function is *kudanganya* (see base form 16 below).

6. **Usage:** A causative verbal form meaning to make something correct or to correct an error. Attested in educational and editorial contexts (*sahihisha makosa*, "correct the errors"), though less frequent than the adjectival base form. Appears in language teaching and proofreading contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form 5.3**

## BASE FORM 8: **haki**

---

**Derived Form 8.1**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** uhaki
3. **Transliteration:** uhaki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix, class 14) + *haki* (right/justice/truth)
5. **Natural English translation:** justice, righteousness, the state of being right
6. **Usage:** An abstract nominalization of *haki*, referring to the quality or condition of justice or rightness. Less frequent than *haki* itself; appears in formal moral and legal discourse. Note that *haki* already functions as an abstract noun, so *uhaki* represents a further degree of abstraction.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 8.2**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** haki haki
3. **Transliteration:** haki haki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *haki* + *haki* (full reduplication)
5. **Natural English translation:** truly, really, absolutely right, in all fairness
6. **Usage:** Reduplicated form of *haki* used as an intensifier, parallel to *kweli kweli*. Used in spoken Swahili to emphasize that something is genuinely right or true. Less frequent than *kweli kweli* but attested in spoken registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 8.3**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** haki za binadamu
3. **Transliteration:** haki za binadamu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *haki* (rights) + *za* (of, class 10 agreement) + *binadamu* (human beings)
5. **Natural English translation:** human rights
6. **Usage:** A fixed compound phrase that is the standard Swahili term for human rights. Documented here as a highly productive and institutionalized collocation rather than a morphological derivation; it is the basis for a large family of related expressions in legal and political discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The phrase *haki za binadamu* is included here because it represents the most institutionalized and productive extension of *haki* in formal discourse, though it is phrasal rather than morphological. It is flagged for completeness.

6. **Usage:** The stative/intransitive counterpart of *kudhihirisha*, meaning to become manifest or to be revealed without an explicit agent. Used in narrative and argumentative contexts (*ukweli ulidhihirika*, "the truth became evident/came to light").

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 14: **amini**

---

**Derived Form 14.1**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** uaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** uaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *amini* (to trust/believe) + *-fu* (adjectival/nominal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** faithfulness, trustworthiness, reliability, honesty
6. **Usage:** The standard abstract nominalization of *amini*, referring to the quality of being trustworthy or faithful. Already documented in P1 as a core item; noted here as the primary derivational product of this root.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.2**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** mwaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** mwaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (noun class 1 prefix, human agent) + *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** a faithful person, a trustworthy person, a loyal person
6. **Usage:** The agentive noun derived from *amini*, referring to a person who is faithful or trustworthy. Used in moral, religious, and professional contexts (*mwaminifu katika kazi*, "faithful in work"; in religious contexts, *mwaminifu* can also mean "a believer/faithful one").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.3**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** waaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** waaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (noun class 2 prefix, human plural) + *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** faithful people, trustworthy persons, the faithful (plural)
6. **Usage:** The plural of *mwaminifu*, used in moral, religious, and professional contexts to refer to multiple persons who are faithful or trustworthy. In religious discourse, *waaminifu* commonly refers to the body of believers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.4**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuamini
3. **Transliteration:** kuamini
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *amini* (to believe/trust)
5. **Natural English translation:** to believe, to trust, to have faith
6. **Usage:** The infinitive form of *amini*, used as a verbal noun and in infinitival constructions. Extremely frequent across religious, interpersonal, and everyday registers (*kuamini Mungu*, "to believe in God"; *vigumu kuamini*, "hard to believe").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.5**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminika
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminika
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive) + *amini* + *-ka* (stative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trustworthy, to be believable, to be credible
6. **Usage:** The stative form of *amini*, meaning to be in a state of being trustworthy or credible. Used in character evaluation and in assessing the credibility of claims (*habari hii haiaminiki*, "this news is not credible").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.6**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminiwa
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminiwa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive) + *amini* + *-wa* (passive suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trusted, to be believed, to be relied upon
6. **Usage:** The passive form of *amini*, meaning to be the object of trust or belief. Used in contexts of evaluating whether a person or claim is trusted by others (*yeye haaminiwa*, "he/she is not trusted").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.7**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminiwa na
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminiwa na
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-aminiwa* (passive infinitive) + *na* (by, agentive marker)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trusted by, to be believed by
6. **Usage:** The passive construction with agentive *na*, specifying who trusts or believes. Documented here as a productive syntactic pattern rather than a separate lexical item (*kuaminiwa na watu*, "to be trusted by people").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14.8**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** imani
3. **Transliteration:** imani
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *imani* (from Arabic *īmān*, "faith, belief"; cognate with *amini*)
5. **Natural English translation:** faith, belief, trust, conviction
6. **Usage:** *Imani* is the primary nominal form for faith or belief, particularly in religious contexts. It is cognate with *amini* through their shared Arabic root (*amana*/*īmān*) and functions as a near-synonym of *uaminifu* in the domain of belief, though with stronger religious connotations. Documented here as a cognate derivation rather than a direct morphological derivation within Swahili.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on imani:** The relationship between *amini* and *imani* in Swahili is one of shared Arabic etymology rather than synchronic Swahili derivation. Both entered Swahili from Arabic, but Swahili speakers treat them as related. *Imani* is not derived from *amini* by Swahili morphological rules but is documented here because of its semantic and etymological proximity and its high frequency in the truth-evaluation domain.

## Summary of Derivational Productivity

The following observations are directly supported by the data above:

- **Most morphologically productive roots:** *amini* (7 derivations documented), *danganya* (6 derivations), *dhahiri* (4 derivations), *fanana* (4 derivations), *tofauti* (3 derivations), *thibitisha* (5 derivations including base).

- **Primarily nominal in derivational behavior:** *kweli*, *halisi*, *sahihi*, *bora*, *asili* — these roots yield abstract nominalizations via *u-* prefix but show limited or uncertain verbal derivation.

- **Agentive noun pattern (m-/wa-):** Consistently attested for roots with a human-agent interpretation: *mwongo/waongo*, *mwaminifu/waaminifu*, *mdanganyifu/wadanganyifu*, *mlaghai/walaghai*, *mkosoaji*, *mkosaji*.

- **Causative -sha/-isha pattern:** Attested for *dhahiri* (*kudhihirisha*), *fanana* (*kufananisha*), *tofauti* (*kutofautisha*), *sahihi* (*kusahihisha*), *laghai* (*kulaghai*). Uncertain for *halisi* (*halisisha*).

- **Stative/potential -ka/-ika pattern:** Attested for *amini* (*kuaminika*), *danganya* (*kudanganyika*), *dhahiri* (*kudhihirika*).

- **Passive -wa pattern:** Attested for *amini* (*kuaminiwa*), *danganya* (*kudanganywa*), *thibitisha* (*kuthibitishwa*).

- **Roots with no identified verbal derivation:** *kweli*, *bora*, *bandia*, *haki ya kweli*, *uongo mtupu*.
