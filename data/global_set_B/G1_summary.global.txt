# G1 — CROSS-LANGUAGE SUMMARY OF OBSERVED SEMANTIC ORGANIZATION

## 1. Language Inventory

- **zh (Chinese)**: Extensive vocabulary with multiple roots (真 zhēn, 实 shí, 正 zhèng, 诚 chéng) showing specialized semantic distributions across authenticity, factuality, correctness, and sincerity domains.

- **he (Hebrew)**: Root-based system centered on א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n) with extensive derivational family; secondary roots צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q) for justice/rightness and ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r) for falsehood.

- **sa (Sanskrit)**: Classical language with philosophical vocabulary distinguishing ontological reality (सत्/असत्), epistemological validity (प्रमाण/भ्रम), and conventional versus ultimate truth (व्यवहार/परमार्थ).

- **el (Greek)**: Modern language with inherited classical vocabulary (αλήθεια, ψευδ-) plus extensive productive ψευδ- compounding for pseudo-X categories; ορθ- root spans correctness, orthodoxy, and technical domains.

- **en (English)**: Germanic-Romance hybrid with multiple lexical sources (true/real/genuine/authentic) showing partial semantic overlap; highly productive in phrasal constructions and discourse markers.

- **tl (Tagalog)**: Austronesian language with totoo/tunay distinction for truth/genuineness; extensive verbal derivation through affixation; rich particle system for discourse management.

- **sw (Swahili)**: Bantu language with Arabic loanwords (kweli, sahihi, haki, halisi); productive noun class system; extensive use of associative constructions for modification.

- **qu (Quechua)**: Agglutinative language with chiqan/llulla opposition; productive case system creating adverbial and directional constructions; obligatory evidential marking in main clauses.

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### Chinese (zh)

**Main lexical distinctions**: Multiple roots with specialized distributions: 真 (zhēn) for authenticity of objects and genuineness; 实 (shí) for substantiality and practical implementation; 正 (zhèng) for correctness and normativity; 诚 (chéng) for sincerity and honesty. These roots combine productively in compounds but maintain distinct semantic cores.

**Morphological patterns**: Extensive compounding (真实, 诚实, 正确); productive -性 suffix for abstract qualities; -者 agentive suffix exists but not productive in truth domain. Resultative verb compounds (改正, 落实, 证实) strongly preferred over periphrastic causatives.

**Constructional features**: Predicative adjective + 的 (de) nominalization extremely common; 把 (bǎ) disposal construction for corrections; discourse markers from truth vocabulary (说真的, 老实说) highly developed. No dedicated inchoative morphology; uses 变 (biàn) + adjective periphrastically.

**Oppositions**: Primary oppositions are 真/假, 对/错, 实/虚. Morphological negation (不) productive but many lexicalized antonym pairs exist. Fixed expressions often embed oppositions internally (名副其实/名不副实).

### Hebrew (he)

**Main lexical distinctions**: Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n) dominates with meanings spanning truth (אמת emet), reliability (אמין amin), faithfulness (נאמן ne'eman), and belief (אמונה emuna). Root צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q) covers justice/rightness. Root ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r) for falsehood.

**Morphological patterns**: Binyan system creates causatives (הצדיק lehatsדik "justify"), passives (מוצדק mutsдak "justified"), and intensives. Abstract nouns via -וּת (-ut) suffix highly productive (אמינות aminut, נאמנות ne'emanut). Agentive nouns via participial forms.

**Constructional features**: Zero copula in present tense standard; prepositional phrases (באמת be'emet, בצדק betsedek) function as adverbs; dual number productive for binary oppositions (סת्यासत्ययोः-like constructions). Discourse markers from truth vocabulary common (באמת? "really?").

**Oppositions**: Primary opposition אמת/שקר (emet/sheker). Morphological negation via לא (lo) productive; some prefixal negation (א- privative marginal). Many oppositions expressed through parallel constructions with contrasting roots rather than negation.

### Sanskrit (sa)

**Main lexical distinctions**: Ontological सत्य/असत्य (satya/asatya) versus epistemological प्रमा/भ्रम (pramā/bhrama) distinction fundamental. व्यवहार/परमार्थ (vyavahāra/paramārtha) opposition structures two-truths doctrine. Root √मा (mā "measure") generates extensive epistemological vocabulary.

**Morphological patterns**: Privative अ-/अन- (a-/an-) extremely productive for negation. Abstract nouns via -त्व/-ता (-tva/-tā) suffixes. Agentive nouns via -इन्/-वादिन् (-in/-vādin) patterns. Causative morphology (NIC suffix) theoretically available but not attested for truth-domain predicates.

**Constructional features**: Tatpuruṣa compounds overwhelmingly preferred over analytic modification. Dual number highly productive for binary oppositions. Instrumental case marks epistemic means (प्रमाणेन jñāyate). Ablative -तः (-taḥ) marks philosophical standpoint (परमार्थतः). Optative mood for moral obligation.

**Oppositions**: Systematic morphological negation (सत्य/असत्य, प्रमा/अप्रमा, विद्या/अविद्या). Lexical oppositions (सत्य/मिथ्या, व्यवहार/परमार्थ) equally important. Dual constructions embed oppositions (सत्यासत्ययोः).

### Greek (el)

**Main lexical distinctions**: αλήθεια (alítheia) for truth; ψευδ- (pseud-) root extremely productive for falsehood and pseudo-X categories; ορθ- (orth-) root spans correctness, orthodoxy, and technical domains (orthopedics, orthography). δίκαιος/άδικος (díkaios/ádikos) for justice domain.

**Morphological patterns**: Highly productive ψευδ- compounding creates critical/polemical terms (ψευδοεπιστήμη "pseudoscience", ψευδοδημοκρατία "pseudo-democracy"). Abstract nouns via -ότητα (-ótita) and -ία (-ía) suffixes. Agentive nouns via -της (-tis) suffix. Causative verbs via -ώνω (-óno) suffix.

**Constructional features**: Zero copula in present tense; prepositional phrases (στην αλήθεια, κατά λάθος) grammaticalized as discourse markers. Light verb constructions (λέω την αλήθεια, κάνω λάθος) extremely common. Metaphorical revelation constructions (βγαίνει η αλήθεια στη φόρα).

**Oppositions**: Primary oppositions αλήθεια/ψέμα, σωστός/λάθος, δίκαιος/άδικος. Morphological negation via α- privative (άδικος, άκυρος) and δεν/δεν είναι analytic negation both productive. Color metaphors (άσπρο/μαύρο ψέμα "white/black lie").

### English (en)

**Main lexical distinctions**: Multiple near-synonyms with partial overlap: true/real/genuine/authentic show different distributional preferences. True for propositions; real for existence; genuine/authentic for objects. Correct/right highly polysemous across epistemic, moral, and practical domains.

**Morphological patterns**: Mixed derivational patterns: -th suffix (truth), -ly adverbs (truly, falsely), -ness abstracts (truthfulness), -ify causatives (falsify, verify). Prefixal negation (un-, in-, dis-) productive but lexically restricted. Agentive -er suffix exists but gaps (*truther, but liar is suppletive).

**Constructional features**: Periphrastic causatives dominant (make X true, prove X false). Fixed phrasal verbs (come true, ring true/false). Extensive discourse marker development (to be honest, for real). Comparative/superlative analytic (more/most true).

**Oppositions**: Primary oppositions true/false, right/wrong, real/fake, genuine/counterfeit. Both morphological (incorrect, unreal) and lexical (true/false) negation strategies coexist. Many fixed contrastive phrases (true or false, right or wrong).

### Tagalog (tl)

**Main lexical distinctions**: totoo/tunay distinction: totoo for factual truth and reality; tunay for genuineness and authenticity. tama/mali for correctness/error. Extensive particle system (ba, nga, lang, naman, pa) modulates illocutionary force.

**Morphological patterns**: Productive affixation: mag- actor voice, -in patient voice, pag- nominalizer, ka-...-an abstract circumfix. Reduplication for emphasis (totoong-totoo, tunay na tunay). Causative pa- prefix productive (patunayan "to prove").

**Constructional features**: Extensive use of linker na/ng in modification. Existential may/walang constructions for presence/absence. Discourse markers highly developed (totoo lang, sa totoo lang). Semantic shift in tama na "enough, stop" from correctness domain.

**Oppositions**: Primary oppositions totoo/kasinungalingan, tama/mali, tunay/peke. Negation via hindi (emphatic) or di- prefix (colloquial). Particle-based oppositions (totoo ba?/totoo nga).

### Swahili (sw)

**Main lexical distinctions**: kweli/ukweli (truth) versus uongo (falsehood); sahihi (correct) from Arabic; halisi (genuine) versus bandia (fake). Extensive Arabic loanwords in formal/legal register (haki, hakika, dhahiri).

**Morphological patterns**: Noun class prefixes (u- abstract, m-/wa- person); associative -a construction highly productive for modification. Causative -isha/-esha suffix productive (sahihisha "verify", hakikisha "ensure"). Stative -ika suffix for resultative states.

**Constructional features**: Associative constructions (mtu wa kweli "truthful person") dominant modification strategy. Prepositional kwa + noun creates adverbials (kwa kweli "truly"). Existential constructions with copula ni/si. Evidential marking absent (unlike Quechua).

**Oppositions**: Primary oppositions kweli/uongo, halisi/bandia, sahihi/makosa. Negation via mana or negative copula si. Scalar modification with size adjectives (uongo mkubwa/mdogo "big/small lie").

### Quechua (qu)

**Main lexical distinctions**: chiqan (true, real, genuine) versus llulla (false, lie, deceptive); allin (good, well, correct) for broad positive evaluation. Extensive case system creates adverbial and directional constructions.

**Morphological patterns**: Agglutinative with productive suffixes: -cha-/-chi- causative, -ku- reflexive, -ya- inchoative/intensifier, -naku- reciprocal, -q agentive. Case suffixes (-ta accusative, -pi locative, -man directional, -manta ablative, -paq purposive) create rich constructional possibilities.

**Constructional features**: Obligatory evidential marking (-mi direct, -si reportative, -cha conjectural) in main clauses. Accusative + verb constructions (chiqanta rimay "speak truth"). Prohibitive ama...chu construction. Substantivizer kaq highly productive.

**Oppositions**: Primary opposition chiqan/llulla. Morphological negation via mana highly productive. Parallel constructions with contrasting quality terms (chiqan simi/llulla simi). Disjunctive questions with -chu (chiqanchu llullachu "true or false?").

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring Convergences (Patterns in Multiple Languages)

- **Morphological negation highly productive**: All languages show productive negation of truth-domain terms: Chinese 不 (bù), Hebrew לא (lo), Sanskrit अ-/अन- (a-/an-), Greek α- privative and δεν, English un-/in-/dis-, Tagalog hindi/di-, Swahili mana/si, Quechua mana. This creates systematic oppositions (true/untrue, correct/incorrect, etc.).

- **Abstract nominalization patterns**: All languages derive abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives: Chinese -性 (-xìng), Hebrew -וּת (-ut), Sanskrit -त्व/-ता (-tva/-tā), Greek -ότητα (-ótita)/-ία (-ía), English -ness/-ity/-hood, Tagalog ka-...-an, Swahili u-...-a/u-...-ifu, Quechua kay nominalization. These create philosophical/formal vocabulary (truthfulness, authenticity, etc.).

- **Causative derivations for verification**: Six languages show dedicated causative morphology for "making true/verifying": Chinese 证实 (zhèngshí), Hebrew אימת (imet), Sanskrit साधयति (sādhayati), Greek βεβαιώνω (vevaióno), Tagalog patunayan, Swahili thibitisha, Quechua chiqanchay. English lacks morphological causative, using periphrastic verify/prove.

- **Speech act constructions dominant**: All languages show highly conventionalized "speak/tell + truth/lie" constructions: Chinese 说实话/说谎, Hebrew לומר את האמת/לספר שקרים, Sanskrit सत्यं वद/असत्यं वदति, Greek λέω την αλήθεια/λέω ψέματα, English tell the truth/tell lies, Tagalog magsabi ng totoo/magsinungaling, Swahili sema ukweli/sema uongo, Quechua chiqanta rimay/llullata rimay.

- **Discourse marker development**: Seven languages show grammaticalization of truth vocabulary into discourse markers: Chinese 说真的, Hebrew באמת?, Greek αλήθεια?, English to be honest, Tagalog totoo lang, Swahili kwa kweli, Quechua chiqap. These mark speaker stance rather than evaluate truth directly.

- **Authenticity versus factual truth distinction**: Six languages distinguish material/personal authenticity from propositional truth through separate lexemes or constructions: Chinese 真 (objects) vs. 实 (facts), Hebrew אמיתי (genuine) vs. אמת (truth), Greek γνήσιος/αυθεντικός vs. αλήθεια, English genuine/authentic vs. true, Tagalog tunay vs. totoo, Swahili halisi vs. kweli. Sanskrit and Quechua show less clear lexical distinction.

- **Metaphorical revelation constructions**: Six languages use emergence/light metaphors for truth revelation: Chinese 真相大白 "truth becomes bright", Hebrew האמת תצא לאור "truth will come to light", Greek βγαίνει η αλήθεια στη φόρα "truth comes to surface", English the truth will out, Tagalog lumabas ang katotohanan, Swahili ukweli unadhihirika. Sanskrit uses passive revelation (प्रकाशते) less prominently.

### Major Divergences (Single-Language or Rare Features)

- **Two-truths doctrine vocabulary**: Sanskrit uniquely shows systematic lexicalization of conventional versus ultimate truth opposition (व्यवहार/परमार्थ) with dedicated adverbial forms (व्यवहारतः/परमार्थतः) and dual constructions. No parallel in other languages, though Greek shows some philosophical register distinctions.

- **Evidential systems**: Only Quechua shows obligatory grammatical evidential marking (-mi/-si/-cha) integrated with truth vocabulary. Swahili, despite geographic proximity to evidential languages, lacks this. Other languages use optional epistemic modals or adverbs.

- **Pseudo-X productivity**: Greek shows uniquely extensive ψευδ- (pseud-) compounding for creating critical terms (ψευδοεπιστήμη "pseudoscience", ψευδοδημοκρατία "pseudo-democracy", ψευδοδιανοούμενος "pseudo-intellectual"). English has pseudo- borrowing but less productive. Other languages lack equivalent pattern.

- **Root polysemy in Chinese**: Chinese 正 (zhèng) uniquely spans correctness, spatial precision (正中 "exact center"), temporal exactness (正午 "noon"), and mathematical terms (正数 "positive number"). No parallel polysemy pattern in other languages.

- **Agentive nominalization gaps**: English shows suppletive liar (not *falser/*untruer) and lacks productive truth-teller formation. Tagalog similarly lacks dedicated agentive for truth-tellers. Contrast with Hebrew, Sanskrit, Greek, Swahili, Quechua which have productive agentive patterns.

- **Inchoative restrictions**: English shows lexically restricted inchoative (come true only, not *come false/*become true). Chinese uses 变 (biàn) periphrastically with restrictions. Hebrew, Greek, Tagalog, Swahili, Quechua show more productive inchoative patterns through dedicated morphology.

- **Justice/truth conflation**: Hebrew צדק (tsedek) and Swahili haki uniquely conflate justice/righteousness with truth/correctness in single lexemes. Other languages maintain clearer separation (though Chinese 正 shows some overlap).

### Asymmetries, Gaps, and Irregularities

- **Causative asymmetries**: Multiple languages show causatives for "make true/verify" but lack or rarely use causatives for "make false": Chinese has 证实 but not *证假; Hebrew has אימת but falsification uses different roots; Sanskrit lacks attested causatives for truth-predicates; English uses verify but falsify (different root); Tagalog has patunayan but no *pamaliin. Only Greek shows symmetrical causatives (βεβαιώνω/διαψεύδω).

- **Agentive gaps**: Six languages lack productive agentive nominalization for "truth-teller": English (suppletive liar, compound truth-teller), Chinese (no 真者), Hebrew (uses participial circumlocution), Tagalog (no dedicated form), Swahili (uses mwenye ukweli possessive), Quechua (uses chiqan rimaq compound). Only Sanskrit (सत्यवादिन् satyavādin) and Greek (ψεύτης pséftis for liar, but no single word for truth-teller) show some productivity.

- **Inchoative restrictions across languages**: Most languages show restricted inchoative patterns with truth-domain terms. Common pattern: "become good/better" productive (Chinese 变好, Hebrew להשתפר, Greek γίνομαι καλύτερος, English improve, Tagalog gumanda, Swahili -zidi, Quechua allinyay) but "become true" restricted or uses different constructions (come true, be verified, be revealed).

- **Evidential integration**: Only Quechua shows obligatory evidential marking integrated with truth vocabulary (chiqapmi/chiqapsi/chiqapcha). This represents a typologically rare feature absent in all other sampled languages, including Swahili which lacks evidentials despite Bantu affiliation.

- **Dual number for oppositions**: Sanskrit and Hebrew productively use dual number for binary oppositions (Sanskrit सत्यासत्ययोः satyāsatyayoḥ "of truth and falsehood", Hebrew similar constructions). Other languages lack grammatical dual and use coordination (Chinese 真假, English true or false, etc.).

- **Resultative verb compounds**: Chinese uniquely shows extensive resultative verb compounding (改正 gǎizhèng "change-correct", 落实 luòshí "fall-solid") as preferred strategy over periphrastic causatives. Other languages use affixal causatives (Hebrew, Sanskrit, Greek, Tagalog, Swahili, Quechua) or periphrastic constructions (English).

- **Semantic shift patterns**: Tagalog tama shows unique semantic extension from "correct" to "enough, stop" (tama na). Chinese 认真 (rènzhēn) shifts from "recognize-true" to "serious, earnest". Greek safi extends from "clean" to colloquial "okay, fine". These represent language-specific grammaticalization paths not paralleled across languages.
