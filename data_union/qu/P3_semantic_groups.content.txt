# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, collocational behavior, and contextual distribution observed across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data support. Items that do not fit clearly into any group, or that fit into multiple groups, are noted explicitly. Epistemic statuses from prior steps are preserved where relevant.

---

## GROUP 1: Expressions used to assert or deny the truth of a claim

**Label:** Direct truth predication / Predicate assertions of truth and falsehood

**Definition:** These expressions are used as complete or near-complete utterances to assert that a statement, claim, or situation is true or false. They appear in contexts of testimony, dispute, and everyday affirmation, and they typically take the direct evidential enclitic *-m/-mi* or the negative polar *-chu*. Their primary function is to commit the speaker to a position on the truth-value of something. They appear primarily as predicates in simple declarative constructions, where a speaker asserts or denies that something is true, genuine, or false, and they are used in direct response to questions about whether something is the case.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqam* — 'it is true' (with direct evidential)
- *cheqami* — 'it is true' (with direct evidential, variant form)
- *cheqa* — "true, genuine" (used as predicate: *Cheqam kay.* "This is true.")
- *cheqaq* — "true, truthful" (used as predicate: *Cheqaqmi chay.*)
- *cheqam kay* — 'it is true / that is the truth'
- *cheqapuni* — "absolutely true, certainly real" (with emphatic *-puni*)
- *cheqaqpuni* — "absolutely true, certainly genuine" (emphatic predicate: *Cheqaqpunim chay.*)
- *cheqataq* — "and indeed true, but truly" (contrastive/confirmatory)
- *mana cheqachu* — 'it is not true / it is false'
- *manan cheqachu* — "it is not true, that is not correct"
- *mana cheqa* — "untrue, incorrect, false, not genuine"
- *mana cheqaq* — "not genuine, inauthentic, false"
- *llullam* — 'it is a lie / that is false'
- *llulla* — "false, a lie" (used as predicate: *Llullam chay.* "That is a lie.")
- *mana llullachu* — 'it is not a lie'
- *manan llullachu* — "it is not false, it is not a lie"
- *cheqachus?* — 'is it true?'
- *cheqachus, manachus?* — 'is it true or not?'
- *llullachu?* — 'is it a lie?'
- *pantasqam* — 'it is mistaken / that is wrong'
- *sut'im* — 'it is clear / that is plain'
- *sut'imi* — "it is clear, it is evident" (variant form)
- *mana sut'ichu* — 'it is not clear'
- *manan sut'ichu* — "it is not clear, it is not plain, that is ambiguous"
- *chiqapmi* — 'truly / indeed' (Ayacucho-Chanka) [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqap cheqam* — 'it is truly true' (emphatic, Ayacucho-Chanka)
- *cheqallam* — 'it is purely the truth'
- *kikinmi* — 'it is the very same / it is the genuine one'
- *kikinllam* — 'it is none other than (I know this directly)'
- *mana kikinchu* — 'it is not the same / it is not the genuine one'
- *hinam* — "it is so, that is how it is"
- *hinami* — "it is so, that is how it is" (variant form)
- *allinmi* — "it is good, it is correct"
- *chiqan* — "correct, just, right" (used as predicate: *Chiqanmi chay.*)
- *allin* — "good, right, proper" (used as predicate: *Allinmi chay ruway.*)
- *mana allin* — "not good, not correct, wrong"
- *mana chiqan* — "not correct, unjust" (negated predicate: *Mana chiqanmi chay ruway.*)
- *mana sut'i* — "unclear, ambiguous, not plain"
- *kikin* — "the very same, genuine, identical" (used as predicate: *Kikinmi pay.*)
- *qelqasqa* — "written, documented, on record" (predicate: *Qelqasqam chay.*)
- *cheqam, arím* — paired double affirmation
- *allinmi, cheqam* — paired correctness-and-truth affirmation
- *iñiymi, cheqam* — paired belief-and-truth affirmation
- *yachanim / yachaninim* — "I know (it), I know for certain"
- *manan pantanichu* — "I am not mistaken, I have not erred"
- *manan yachanichu* — "I do not know, I have no knowledge of it"
- *manan iñinichu* — "I do not believe it, I do not trust this"
- *manan riqsinichunim* — "I do not know/recognize (this person/thing)"
- *manan llullakuyta atinichu* — "I cannot bring myself to lie"
- *cheqanchasqam* — 'it has been verified / it is confirmed'
- *cheqanchasqa* — 'verified / officially confirmed / certified'

**Notes:** The direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi* is a near-obligatory feature of the assertoric members. The interrogative members (*-chu*) form a natural sub-cluster. *Pantasqam* and *sut'im* participate in this group but also appear in other groups (see below). The direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi* is not merely an intensifier but a grammatical marker of the speaker's epistemic commitment. The paired constructions (*cheqam, arím*; *allinmi, cheqam*; *iñiymi, cheqam*) represent an attested rhetorical pattern of reinforcing an assertion by combining two evidentially marked affirmatives. *Chiqapmi* is included with the uncertainty noted in P1. The interrogative clitic *-chu* is attested for *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *allin*, *chiqan*, *sut'i*, and *kikin* in this construction type, but not for *llulla* or *pantay* in available documentation — this asymmetry is preserved without explanation. *Qelqasqa* is included here because it appears in the same predicate construction type, though its primary meaning (being documented/written) is distinct from the others. Several items in this group function as the direct negated counterparts of items in the positive sub-cluster (e.g., *manan cheqachu* vs. *cheqam*). Some items (e.g., *manan yachanichu*, *manan riqsinichunim*) are conventionalized testimonial formulas for denying knowledge or recognition rather than denying the truth of a proposition per se; they are included here because they function in the same discourse slot as direct denials.

## GROUP 2: Expressions used to characterize a person's speech as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Evaluation of speech as truthful or deceptive / Enjoinment and description of truthful communication / Terms associated with the truthfulness or falsity of speech acts

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate a speech act or a speaker's words as honest, accurate, deceptive, or false. They appear in legal testimony, moral instruction, and everyday contexts where the reliability of what someone says is at issue. The target of evaluation is the speech act itself or the words produced, not a state of affairs in the world. They are also used in contexts where truthful speech is being required, described, or characterized as an action, and in imperative constructions requesting truthful speech. They are distinguished from Group 1 in that they are oriented toward the act of speaking rather than toward the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqata rimay* — 'tell the truth / speak truthfully'
- *cheqanta rimay* — "to tell the truth, to speak truthfully" (conventionalized collocation)
- *cheqata rimani* — 'I am telling the truth'
- *cheqa simi* — 'true word / truthful speech'
- *cheqa rimay* — 'truthful speaking / to speak truthfully'
- *llulla simi* — 'false word / lying speech'
- *llulla rimay* — 'to speak falsely / lying speech'
- *llulla nispa rimay* — 'to speak saying it is a lie / to speak falsely'
- *cheqa nispa rimay* — 'to assert truthfully'
- *mana cheqam nispa rimay* — 'to say that it is not true'
- *sut'ita rimay* — 'speak clearly / say it plainly'
- *sut'ita rimani* — 'I am speaking clearly'
- *allin rimay* — 'to speak well / proper speaking'
- *allin simi* — 'good word / proper speech'
- *kikin simiwan rimay* — 'to speak with one's own words / in one's own voice'
- *cheqam, Diospa sutimpi* — 'it is true, in God's name' (sworn testimony formula)
- *cheqaqta rimay* — "to speak the truth, to tell the truth" (imperative: *Cheqaqta rimay.*)
- *cheqaqta willaway* — "tell me the truth" (imperative with first-person object)
- *cheqaqtam rimasqanki* — "you spoke the truth" (assessment of prior speech)
- *chiqan rimay* — "to speak correctly, straight talk, honest speech"
- *chiqap rimay* — "to speak exactly, precise speech" [UNCERTAIN]
- *llulla willay* — "false report, to give a false account, to testify falsely" [UNCERTAIN]
- *willakuy* — "to tell, to narrate, to report" (in contexts where truthfulness of account is at issue)
- *cheqaqmi nin* — "he/she says it is true" (reportative construction)
- *llullam nin* — "he/she says it is a lie" (reportative construction)
- *llullam willakurqa* — "he/she reported falsely"
- *cheqa willay* — "to report truthfully, a truthful account"
- *sut'i simi* — "clear speech, plain language"
- *sut'i willay* — "clear report, to inform clearly"
- *allintam rimay* — "to speak well/correctly, to speak properly"
- *cheqanta rimayta munay* — "to want to tell the truth"
- *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan* — "you must tell the truth" (deontic)
- *sut'itatam rimanayki tiyan* — "you must speak clearly" (deontic)
- *cheqa kasqanta willay* — "to report what is truly the case"
- *cheqa kasqanman hina rimay* — "to speak in accordance with what is actually the case"
- *yachaq kaspa rimay* — "to speak knowing what one is talking about"
- *cheqanta rimay* vs. *mana yachaspa rimay* — the latter characterizes speech made without adequate knowledge, functioning as the negative counterpart in this context
- *llulla willakuy* — "false report, to give a false account, to testify falsely"

**Notes:** *Cheqata rimay* and *sut'ita rimay* are structurally parallel (adjective in accusative + *rimay*) and appear in the same instructional and testimonial contexts. *Cheqa simi* and *llulla simi* are direct contrasts within this group. *Allin rimay* and *allin simi* overlap with Group 5 (moral characterization of persons) but appear here specifically in speech-evaluation contexts. The deontic constructions (*cheqanta rimanayki tiyan*, *sut'itatam rimanayki tiyan*) are included because they appear in the same discourse contexts (legal testimony, moral instruction) as the descriptive expressions, and they specify the same action (truthful speech) as obligatory. *Cheqa willay* and *sut'i willay* extend the pattern from speech to reporting more broadly. The imperative constructions (*Cheqaqta rimay*, *Cheqaqta willaway*) are specifically attested in this group and not in others, suggesting that the enjoining of truthful speech is a distinct usage context. *Rimay* and *willakuy* are not themselves P1 items in the truth-evaluation domain but appear as the verbal heads in these constructions. *Simi* ("word, speech") collocations (*cheqa simi*, *llulla simi*) are included here because their primary attested context is the evaluation of speech acts.

## GROUP 3: Expressions used in the process of verifying or confirming something

**Label:** Verification and confirmation acts / Terms used in processes of checking, confirming, or rectifying

**Definition:** These expressions describe or name the act of checking a claim, document, or situation against reality and establishing its accuracy. They appear in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts where an agent actively investigates or certifies the truth of something. They are distinct from simple truth predication (Group 1) in that they describe a process rather than a result. They are distinguished from Group 1 (which involves asserting truth or falsehood) in that they describe processes or actions directed at establishing or restoring correctness, rather than simply stating that something is or is not true.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqanchay* — 'to verify / to confirm / to establish as true'
- *cheqanchakuy* — 'to verify for oneself / to confirm on one's own behalf'
- *cheqanchasqa* — 'verified / officially confirmed / certified'
- *cheqanchasqam* — 'it has been verified / it is confirmed' (used of documents)
- *cheqanchaq* — 'one who verifies / a verifier'
- *qelqata cheqanchay* — 'to verify a document'
- *cheqanchay atinichu* — 'I cannot verify it'
- *riqsispa cheqanchay* — 'to verify by recognizing / to confirm through identification' [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqachiy* — "to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct"
- *cheqachikuy* — "to verify for oneself, to confirm, to prove oneself genuine"
- *cheqachiq* — "verifier, one who confirms"
- *cheqachisqa* — "verified, confirmed, proven"
- *cheqachiy atiy* — "to be able to verify, to be capable of confirming"
- *manan cheqachiyta atinichu* — "I cannot verify it, I am unable to confirm it"
- *cheqaqchay* — "to verify, to confirm, to prove true, to authenticate"
- *cheqaqsiy* — "to be confirmed, to turn out to be true, to prove oneself genuine" [UNCERTAIN]
- *sut'inchay* — 'to clarify / to make explicit / to explain'
- *sut'inchakuy* — 'to clarify for oneself'
- *sut'inchasqa* — 'clarified / made explicit'
- *sut'inchaykuway* — 'please clarify it for me'
- *sut'inchay atinichu* — 'I cannot clarify it'
- *sut'ichay* — "to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify"
- *sut'ichakuy* — "to become clear, to clarify for oneself"
- *sut'ichasqa* — "clarified, made clear, specified"
- *sut'ichay atiy* — "to be able to clarify, to be capable of explaining"
- *sut'ichaway imachus cheqa kasqanta* — "clarify for me what is true" (imperative with complement)
- *allinchay* — 'to correct / to fix / to make right'
- *allinchakuy* — 'to correct oneself / to improve oneself'
- *allinchachiy* — 'to have something fixed / to cause someone to correct'
- *pantasqata allinchay* — 'to correct what is mistaken'
- *qelqata allinchay* — 'to correct a document'
- *allinchay atinichu* — 'I cannot fix it'
- *allinchaq* — "one who corrects, a fixer, a repairer"
- *allinchasqa* — "corrected, fixed, made right"
- *riqsichiy* — "to introduce, to make known, to identify (to someone)"
- *cheqa kasqanta yachay* — "to know what is truly the case"
- *llulla kasqanta riqsiy* — "to recognize that it is false, to identify something as a lie"
- *chiqanchay* — "to correct, to straighten, to rectify, to make right"
- *chiqanchasqa* — "corrected, rectified, set right"
- *llulla qelqa* — "false document, forged writing" (object of verification processes)
- *cheqaqchaykim kay qelqata* — "you must verify this document" [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqanchay tiyan kay pantasqata* — "this error needs to be corrected" [UNCERTAIN]

**Notes:** *Cheqanchay*, *sut'inchay*, and *allinchay* form a natural cluster here: all three are *-ncha-* causative derivatives of evaluative adjectives, all describe an agent acting on something to bring it into a correct or clear state, and all appear in administrative and pedagogical contexts. *Allinchay* also overlaps with Group 5 (moral correction of conduct). *Cheqanchasqa* and *sut'inchasqa* appear here as resultative forms describing the outcome of verification/clarification acts, but they also participate in Group 1 as predicative assertions. The expressions *cheqachiy* and *allinchay* are the most central; *sut'ichay* is included because in the attested contexts clarification functions as a form of accuracy-establishment. *Cheqachisqa* appears in both this group and Group 4 (positive reliability evaluations) because the past participle form describes the result of a verification process (Group 3) and simultaneously functions as a positive reliability evaluation (Group 4). *Qelqasqa* appears in both Group 1 and Group 3: it is used as a predicate asserting documentary status (Group 1) and as the object or result of verification processes (Group 3). *Chiqanchay* appears in both this group and Group 11 (correct procedure), as it describes both the process of correcting an error and the result of bringing conduct into alignment with what is right.

## GROUP 4: Expressions used to characterize a person or statement as honest, genuine, or reliable

**Label:** Positive character and reliability evaluations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as genuinely what it claims or appears to be — honest, authentic, trustworthy, or accurate. They appear in moral instruction, legal testimony, and character assessments. They are applied both to persons (evaluating their integrity) and to statements or objects (evaluating their authenticity or accuracy).

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa* — "true, real, correct, genuine, right" (base adjective)
- *cheqaq* — "true, real, genuine, authentic" (participial adjective)
- *cheqa runa* — "an upright person, a person of integrity"
- *cheqa simi* — "true speech, honest word"
- *cheqa sonqo* — "sincere, honest at heart, genuine in feeling"
- *cheqa sonqowan* — "sincerely, wholeheartedly, with genuine feeling"
- *iñinapaq* — "trustworthy, believable, credible, reliable"
- *mana llulla* — "not false, not deceptive, honest, genuine"
- *mana pantasqa* — "not mistaken, not in error, correct, accurate"
- *cheqachisqa* — "verified, confirmed, proven"
- *sut'ichasqa* — "clarified, made clear, specified"
- *allinchasqa* — "corrected, fixed, made right"
- *riqsisqa* — "known, recognized, identified"
- *allintan riqsini* — "I know him/her/it well, I recognize clearly"
- *iñinapaqmi kay* — "to be trustworthy, to be worthy of belief"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the evaluative direction (positive) and the target of evaluation (persons, statements, objects). The expressions *cheqa sonqo* and *cheqa sonqowan* are notable for locating the evaluation in the inner self (*sonqo*, heart), distinguishing inner sincerity from outward performance. *Riqsisqa* and *allintan riqsini* are included because in the attested contexts they function as reliability evaluations (confident, verified recognition) rather than merely descriptive identifications.

## GROUP 5: Expressions used to characterize a person or statement as dishonest, false, or unreliable

**Label:** Negative character and reliability evaluations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as dishonest, deceptive, inauthentic, or unreliable. They appear in moral condemnation, legal discourse, and character assessments. Like Group 4, they apply to both persons and statements, but with the opposite evaluative direction.

**Representative expressions:**

- *llulla* — "false, lying, deceptive, fake, untrue" (base adjective)
- *llullaq* — "liar, deceiver, false person" (agentive)
- *llullakuq* — "habitual liar, chronic deceiver"
- *llulla runa* — "a dishonest person, a liar"
- *llulla simi* — "false speech, lying words"
- *llulla sonqo* — "false-hearted, deceitful, insincere"
- *mana iñinapaq* — "untrustworthy, unbelievable, unreliable"
- *pantasqa* — "mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error"
- *pantaq* — "one who errs, one who makes mistakes"
- *llullasqa* — "deceived, having been lied to"
- *llullakusqa* — "having lied, having deceived"
- *mana iñinapaqmi kay* — "to be untrustworthy, to be unworthy of belief"
- *llullam, manan cheqachu* — paired assertion of falsehood and denial of truth

**Notes:** This group is the evaluative mirror of Group 4. The distinction between *llullaq* (one who lies, agentive) and *llullakuq* (habitual liar, reflexive-agentive) is attested within this group, with the latter emphasizing habitual or self-directed deception. *Pantasqa* (mistaken, in error) is included here because in the attested contexts it functions as a negative reliability evaluation, though it is distinct from *llulla* in that error does not necessarily imply intent to deceive — this distinction is noted under the overlapping items section below.

## GROUP 6: Expressions used to describe being misled or causing error in another

**Label:** Causing or suffering error through another's action

**Definition:** These expressions describe situations where one person's action leads another into false belief or mistaken conduct. They appear in legal and moral contexts where the source of an error is attributed to an external agent, and where responsibility for the error is distributed between the one who misled and the one who was misled. They are distinct from expressions of simple error (Group 7) in that they involve an agent who acts on another.

**Representative expressions:**

- *pantachiy* — 'to mislead / to cause to err / to lead astray'
- *pantachiq* — 'one who misleads / a deceiver'
- *pantachisqa* — 'misled / caused to err / led astray'
- *pantachiwanku* — 'they misled me'
- *mana pantachinaykichu* — 'you should not mislead anyone'
- *llullachiy* — 'to cause to lie / to induce falsehood'
- *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani* — 'I was mistaken because of his/her lying'
- *llullakuyta munay* — 'to want to lie / to intend to deceive'
- *llullachikuy* — "to deceive oneself, to let oneself be deceived"
- *pantachiy* — "to mislead, to cause to err, to lead astray"

**Notes:** *Pantachiy* and *llullachiy* are the core members. The construction *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani* is notable for linking deception by another (*llullakuy*) to one's own resulting error (*pantay*), making the causal relationship between Groups 6 and 7 explicit. *Llullakuyta munay* is placed here because it describes intentional deception directed at another, but it could also be grouped with expressions of deliberate lying (Group 7). *Pantachiy* (to mislead, to cause to err) describes the act of causing another to be in error, which functions alongside *llullachiy* (to deceive) in legal and moral discourse about harmful dishonesty.

## GROUP 7: Expressions used to describe the act of lying or being in error

**Label:** Acts of lying and erring / Terms describing mistakes, errors, and going astray / Characterization of deceptive communication and conduct

**Definition:** These expressions describe the act of lying, deceiving, or being mistaken, with the agent as the one who performs or undergoes the act. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts where the conduct of a person is evaluated as dishonest or erroneous. They are distinct from Group 6 (moral character evaluation) in that they focus on specific acts rather than stable dispositions, and from Group 6 in that no external agent causes the error. They are distinguished from the falsehood terms in Group 1 in that they do not necessarily imply deliberate deception — they describe deviation from correctness without implying intent.

**Representative expressions:**

- *llullakuy* — 'to lie / to deceive / to tell falsehoods'
- *llullakusqa* — 'having lied / the act of lying (completed)'
- *mana llullakuychu* — 'do not lie'
- *llullakuyta munay* — 'to want to lie / to intend to deceive'
- *pantay* — 'to err / to be mistaken / to make an error'
- *pantarqani* — 'I was wrong / I made a mistake'
- *mana pantaychu* — 'do not err'
- *pantakuy* — 'to err on one's own account / to go astray'
- *pantasqayta yachani* — 'I know that I was wrong'
- *pantasqa* — 'mistaken / erroneous / wrong'
- *pantasqam* — 'it is mistaken / that is wrong'
- *pantanim* — "I was wrong" (first-person acknowledgment of error)
- *mana pantanichu* — "I am not wrong" (denial of error)
- *pantasqaykiм chay* — "that is your mistake" (attribution of error)
- *pantasqam chay yuyay* — "that opinion is mistaken"
- *llulla rimay* — "to speak falsely, to lie in speech, false talk"
- *llulla simi* — "false speech, lying words"
- *llulla willay* — "false report, misinformation, to report falsely"
- *llullachiy* — "to deceive someone, to cause to lie, to mislead"
- *llullachikuy* — "to deceive oneself, to let oneself be deceived"
- *lulyayta yachay* — "to know how to lie, to be skilled at deceiving"
- *llullayta yachay* — "to know how to lie, to be skilled at deceiving"
- *llullayta saqiy* — "to stop lying, to abandon falsehood"
- *llulla nispa rimay* — "to speak calling it false, to speak saying it is a lie"
- *llulla tukuy* — "to pretend, to feign, to act falsely"
- *llulla hina rimay* — "to speak as if lying, to speak in the manner of a liar"
- *mana yachaspa rimay* — "to speak without knowing, to speak in ignorance"

**Notes:** *Pantay* and *llullakuy* are the core verbal members. *Pantasqa* and *pantasqam* overlap with Group 1 (direct truth predication) when used predicatively, but they are grouped here because their primary derivational and contextual home is the description of erring. *Mana llullakuychu* and *mana pantaychu* are negative imperatives prohibiting these acts, and they appear in the same moral instruction contexts as the positive forms. The contrast between *pantay* (error, mistake) and *llulla* (lie, deception) is supported by their distinct usage contexts: *pantay* appears in contexts of acknowledgment and attribution of error without the moral condemnation associated with *llulla*, and no prohibitive construction (*ama pantaychu*) is attested in available documentation, unlike *ama llullakuychu*. *Pantachiy* (to mislead, to cause to err) is included because in the attested contexts it describes the act of causing another to be in error, which functions alongside *llullachiy* (to deceive) in legal and moral discourse about harmful dishonesty. *Mana yachaspa rimay* (to speak without knowing) is included because it appears in the same discourse contexts as characterizations of unreliable or misleading testimony, though it does not necessarily imply intent to deceive. *Llulla tukuy* also appears in narrative contexts describing theatrical or ritual performance where the pretending is not morally condemned; in those contexts it does not function as a negative evaluation.

## GROUP 8: Expressions used to evaluate a person's character or inner disposition

**Label:** Moral characterization of persons / Terms applied to persons as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe a person as genuinely good, honest, bad, or deceptive in their character or inner disposition, rather than evaluating a specific act or statement. They appear in everyday moral discourse, narrative characterization, and religious contexts. Many involve the word *sonqo* ('heart, inner self') or the word *runa* ('person'), marking the evaluation as applying to the person as a whole rather than to a single action. They are distinguished from Group 7 in that they describe persons rather than propositions or situations.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa sonqo* — 'sincere / honest-hearted / genuine in spirit'
- *cheqa sonqoyoq* — 'one who has a true heart / a person of integrity'
- *cheqaq runa* — 'a truthful person / a genuine person'
- *cheqaq runam* — 'he/she is a truthful person'
- *cheqaqniyuq* — "one who possesses truthfulness" (moral characterization in ethical discourse)
- *allin sonqo* — 'good-hearted / kind / well-intentioned'
- *allin sonqoyoq* — 'one who has a good heart / a kind-hearted person'
- *
