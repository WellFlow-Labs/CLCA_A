# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Korean (ko)

---

## IDENTIFIED SEMANTIC GROUPS

### GROUP 1: Factual Correspondence Terms

**Definition:**
This group contains expressions used to mark statements, information, or representations as corresponding (or failing to correspond) to actual states of affairs. These terms are primarily used in contexts where the accuracy or factuality of claims is at issue, rather than moral evaluation or authenticity of objects. They frequently appear in investigative, journalistic, academic, and everyday discourse when verifying or asserting what is actually the case.

**Representative expressions:**
- 사실 (sasil) "fact" — used to mark factual information or introduce corrections
- 진실 (jinsil) "truth" — emphasizes correspondence to reality, often in contexts of revelation or concealment
- 실제 (silje) "reality, actuality" — contrasts actual circumstances with theory or appearance
- 실상 (silsang) "actual situation" — formal term for real state of affairs
- 실정 (siljeong) "actual conditions" — describes circumstances on the ground
- 실태 (siltae) "actual state" — used in surveys and investigations
- 정확하다 (jeonghwakhada) "to be accurate" — evaluates precision of correspondence
- 맞다 (matda) "to be correct" — indicates correspondence with facts or standards
- 틀리다 (teullida) "to be wrong" — marks deviation from correct facts
- 확실하다 (hwaksilhada) "to be certain" — indicates high degree of factual reliability
- 명백하다 (myeongbaekhada) "to be obvious" — marks facts as clearly apparent
- 사실을 확인하다 (sasireul hwaginhada) "to verify facts"
- 진실을 밝히다 (jinsireul balkhida) "to reveal the truth"
- 실제로는 (siljero-neun) "in reality, actually"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 진위 (jinwi) "truth or falsehood" — specifically used for determining factual status, bridges this group and authentication contexts
- 진상 (jinsang) "truth, real situation" — overlaps with investigative contexts but functions similarly to 사실
- 본질 (bonjil) "essence" — while about fundamental nature, often used in analytical contexts to determine what something actually is

### GROUP 2: Deliberate Deception Terms

**Definition:**
This group comprises expressions specifically marking intentional false representation or lying. These terms emphasize the deliberate nature of misrepresentation and are used primarily in moral, interpersonal, and legal contexts where the speaker's or actor's intent to deceive is relevant. They contrast with mere error or mistake and often carry moral disapproval.

**Representative expressions:**
- 거짓 (geojit) "falsehood, false" — marks deliberate falsity
- 거짓말 (geojinmal) "lie" — standard term for deliberate false statement
- 거짓말을 하다 (geojinmareul hada) "to tell a lie"
- 거짓말쟁이 (geojinmaljjaengi) "liar"
- 허위 (heowi) "falsehood" — formal/legal term for deliberate falsity
- 거짓증언 (geojitjeungeon) "false testimony"
- 거짓진술 (geojitjinsul) "false statement"
- 허위로 진술하다 (heowiro jinsulhada) "to testify falsely"
- 거짓말을 꾸미다 (geojinmareul kkumida) "to fabricate a lie"
- 진실을 숨기다 (jinsireul sumgida) "to hide the truth"
- 새빨간 거짓말 (saeppalgan geojinmal) "blatant lie"
- 하얀 거짓말 (hayan geojinmal) "white lie"
- 거짓없이 (geojiteobsi) "without lies, truthfully"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 거짓정보 (geojitjeongbo) "false information" — may or may not imply deliberate intent
- 거짓뉴스 (geojinnyuseu) "fake news" — contemporary term that may emphasize deliberateness or simply falsity

### GROUP 3: Authenticity and Genuineness Terms

**Definition:**
This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether objects, artifacts, products, or identities are genuine originals versus copies, counterfeits, or imitations. These terms are primarily used in commercial, artistic, and authentication contexts where the provenance or originality of physical items is at issue, rather than the truth of statements or moral character.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진짜 (jinjja) "real, genuine" — colloquial term for authenticity
- 가짜 (gajja) "fake, counterfeit" — marks inauthenticity
- 진본 (jinbon) "authentic original" — formal term for genuine artifacts
- 모조 (mojo) "imitation, counterfeit"
- 모조품 (mojopum) "imitation product, counterfeit goods"
- 진품 (jinpum) "genuine article"
- 진짜인지 가짜인지 (jinjjainji gajjainji) "whether real or fake"
- 진본과 모조품 (jinbongwa mojopum) "authentic original and imitation"
- 진짜배기 (jinjjabaegi) "the real deal, genuine article"
- 가짜를 구별하다 (gajjareul gubyeolhada) "to distinguish fakes"
- 모조품을 판별하다 (mojopumeul panbyeolhada) "to identify imitations"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 진정 (jinjeong) "genuine, sincere" — can apply to both objects and feelings/intentions
- 참 (cham) "true, real" — used both for authenticity and for truth of statements

---

### GROUP 4: Honest Character and Conduct Terms

**Definition:**
This group includes expressions that evaluate moral character or communicative behavior in terms of truthfulness, straightforwardness, and integrity. These terms focus on the disposition or habitual conduct of persons rather than the truth-value of specific statements. They appear primarily in character evaluation, moral instruction, and interpersonal contexts where trustworthiness and openness are valued.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정직 (jeongjik) "honesty, integrity" — moral quality of truthfulness
- 정직하다 (jeongjikada) "to be honest"
- 정직한 사람 (jeongjikhan saram) "honest person"
- 솔직 (soljik) "frankness, candor" — emphasizes direct communication
- 솔직하다 (soljikada) "to be frank"
- 진심 (jinsim) "sincerity, genuine heart" — authentic feelings
- 진정 (jinjeong) "genuineness, sincerity" — authentic intentions
- 정직하게 말하다 (jeongjikage malhada) "to speak honestly"
- 솔직히 말하면 (soljiki malhamyeon) "frankly speaking"
- 솔직하게 털어놓다 (soljikage teoreonota) "to confess frankly"
- 진심으로 (jinsimeuro) "sincerely"
- 진정으로 (jinjeongeuro) "genuinely"
- 부정직하다 (bujeongjikada) "to be dishonest"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 진실성 (jinsilseong) "truthfulness" — can refer to both character quality and statement quality
- 참되다 (chamdoeda) "to be true, genuine" — can apply to both character and statements

---

### GROUP 5: Correctness Evaluation Terms

**Definition:**
This group comprises expressions used to evaluate whether answers, judgments, procedures, or actions are correct versus incorrect according to established standards, rules, or norms. These terms are particularly common in educational, procedural, and evaluative contexts where conformity to standards is being assessed, and they often contrast with moral rightness or factual truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- 맞다 (matda) "to be correct, right" — indicates conformity to standards
- 틀리다 (teullida) "to be wrong, incorrect" — marks deviation from correct answer
- 옳다 (olda) "to be right, correct" — evaluates correctness in multiple senses
- 그르다 (geureuda) "to be wrong" — archaic term for incorrectness
- 옳고 그름 (olgo geureum) "right and wrong"
- 맞는 답 (manneun dap) "correct answer"
- 틀린 답 (teullin dap) "wrong answer"
- 맞는지 틀리는지 (manneunji teullineunji) "whether right or wrong"
- 옳은 일 (oleun il) "the right thing, correct action"
- 맞게 하다 (matge hada) "to do correctly"
- 맞춤 (matchum) "fitting, customization" — emphasizes proper fit

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 정확하다 (jeonghwakhada) "to be accurate" — overlaps with both factual correspondence and correctness evaluation
- 옳다 (olda) — spans both procedural correctness and moral rightness

---

### GROUP 6: Legitimacy and Justification Terms

**Definition:**
This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether actions, claims, rights, or arguments are properly grounded, justified, or legitimate according to legal, logical, or moral standards. These terms appear primarily in legal, political, philosophical, and argumentative discourse where the warrant or authorization for actions or claims is at issue, rather than simple factual truth or correctness.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정당하다 (jeongdanghada) "to be legitimate, justified"
- 타당하다 (tadanghada) "to be valid, reasonable"
- 정당성 (jeongdangseong) "legitimacy, justification"
- 타당성 (tadangseong) "validity, reasonableness"
- 정당한 이유 (jeongdanghan iyu) "legitimate reason"
- 정당한 권리 (jeongdanghan gwonri) "legitimate right"
- 타당한 근거 (tadanghan geungeo) "valid grounds"
- 정당성을 확보하다 (jeongdangseongeul hwakbohada) "to secure legitimacy"
- 타당성을 검토하다 (tadangseongeul geomtohada) "to examine validity"
- 부정당하다 (bujeongdanghada) "to be illegitimate"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 옳다 (olda) "to be right" — can indicate both correctness and moral/logical rightness, overlapping with this group

---

### GROUP 7: Credibility and Reliability Assessment Terms

**Definition:**
This group includes expressions used to evaluate whether sources, statements, evidence, or testimony can be trusted or believed. These terms focus on the trustworthiness or believability of information rather than its verified truth-value, and they are particularly common in journalistic, investigative, and evaluative contexts where the reliability of sources must be assessed before verification is possible.

**Representative expressions:**
- 신빙성 (sinbingseong) "credibility, reliability"
- 신빙성이 있다 (sinbingseongi itda) "to be credible"
- 신빙성이 없다 (sinbingseongi eopda) "to lack credibility"
- 신빙성이 떨어지다 (sinbingseongi tteoreojida) "to lose credibility"
- 그럴듯하다 (geureoldeuthada) "to be plausible, likely"
- 그럴듯한 이야기 (geureoldeuthan iyagi) "plausible story"
- 그럴듯하게 들리다 (geureoldeuthage deullida) "to sound plausible"
- 진실성 (jinsilseong) "truthfulness, veracity" — quality of being truthful
- 진실성이 의심되다 (jinsilseongi uissimdoeda) "truthfulness is doubted"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 확실하다 (hwaksilhada) "to be certain" — bridges certainty and reliability
- 진실성 (jinsilseong) — can refer to both character quality and statement reliability

---

### GROUP 8: Practical Reality and Implementation Terms

**Definition:**
This group comprises expressions that contrast actual practice, concrete implementation, or real-world application with theory, planning, or nominal status. These terms emphasize what actually happens or exists in practice rather than what is planned, claimed, or theoretically possible, and they are common in administrative, technical, and evaluative contexts where the gap between theory and practice is relevant.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실제 (silje) "reality, actuality, practice"
- 실제로 (siljero) "actually, in practice"
- 실질 (siljil) "substance, essence, reality"
- 실질적 (siljejeok) "substantial, actual"
- 실천 (silcheon) "practice, implementation"
- 실행 (silhaeng) "execution, implementation"
- 실현 (silhyeon) "realization, actualization"
- 실용 (silyong) "practical use, utility"
- 실무 (silmu) "practical work, actual duties"
- 실전 (siljeon) "actual combat, real situation"
- 실습 (silseup) "practical training"
- 실험 (silheom) "experiment"
- 실적 (siljeok) "actual results, performance"
- 실효 (silhyo) "actual effect, practical efficacy"
- 실력 (sillyeok) "actual ability, real skill"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 실제 (silje) — also appears in Group 1 (Factual Correspondence) when contrasting reality with appearance
- 실상 (silsang), 실정 (siljeong), 실태 (siltae) — overlap with Group 1 but emphasize practical circumstances

---

### GROUP 9: Investigative and Diagnostic Terms

**Definition:**
This group contains expressions used in contexts of examination, investigation, diagnosis, or determination of true states. These terms emphasize the process of discovering or establishing what is actually the case through systematic inquiry, and they appear primarily in medical, legal, investigative, and analytical contexts where expert examination is required.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진단 (jindan) "diagnosis, assessment"
- 진찰 (jinchal) "medical examination"
- 진료 (jillyo) "medical examination and treatment"
- 진맥 (jinmaek) "pulse diagnosis"
- 진술 (jinsul) "statement, testimony"
- 진상 (jinsang) "truth, real situation" — used in investigations
- 진위를 가리다 (jinwireul garida) "to determine truth from falsehood"
- 사실을 확인하다 (sasireul hwaginhada) "to verify facts"
- 진실을 밝히다 (jinsireul balkhida) "to reveal the truth"
- 사실관계 (sasilgwangye) "facts of the case"
- 진위 여부 (jinwi yeobu) "whether true or false"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- 진상 (jinsang) — overlaps with Group 1 (Factual Correspondence) but emphasizes investigative context
- 진술 (jinsul) — while meaning "statement," primarily used in legal/investigative contexts

---

### GROUP 10: Advancement and Progress Terms

**Definition:**
This group includes expressions using the morpheme 진 (jin) with meanings related to forward movement, advancement, or progress rather than truth or genuineness. These terms appear in contexts describing development, expansion, entry into new domains, or forward progression, and represent a distinct semantic domain from truth-evaluation despite sharing the same written form.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진보 (jinbo) "progress, advancement"
- 진화 (jinhwa) "evolution, development"
- 진행 (jinhaeng) "progress, proceeding"
- 진출 (jinchul) "advance, entry, expansion"
- 진입 (jinip) "entry, entrance"
- 진전 (jinjeon) "progress, advancement" [not in P1/P2 but common]
- 진행 중 (jinhaeng jung) "in progress"

**Note:** This group represents a homophonous root distinct from the truth-domain 진 (jin), though written identically. The semantic connection to truth/genuineness is minimal or absent.

## ITEMS WITH UNCLEAR OR MULTIPLE GROUP MEMBERSHIP

### Items spanning multiple groups:

1. **진실 (jinsil) "truth"**
   - Appears in Group 1 (Factual Correspondence) for its primary usage
   - Also relevant to Group 2 (Deliberate Deception) as what is concealed or revealed
   - Overlaps with Group 9 (Investigative) in contexts of truth-seeking

2. **실제 (silje) "reality, actuality"**
   - Primary membership in Group 1 (Factual Correspondence)
   - Also belongs to Group 8 (Practical Reality) when emphasizing practice vs. theory

3. **옳다 (olda) "to be right, correct"**
   - Spans Group 5 (Correctness Evaluation) and Group 6 (Legitimacy)
   - Can indicate both procedural correctness and moral rightness

4. **정확하다 (jeonghwakhada) "to be accurate"**
   - Overlaps Group 1 (Factual Correspondence) and Group 5 (Correctness Evaluation)
   - Used for both factual accuracy and conformity to standards

5. **진정 (jinjeong) "genuineness, sincerity"**
   - Appears in Group 3 (Authenticity) for objects
   - Also in Group 4 (Honest Character) for feelings and intentions

6. **참 (cham) "true, real, genuine"**
   - Spans Group 1 (Factual Correspondence) and Group 3 (Authenticity)
   - Native Korean term with broad application

### Items with specialized or limited usage:

1. **그르다 (geureuda) "to be wrong"**
   - Archaic term with limited contemporary usage
   - Primarily appears in fixed expression 옳고 그름 (right and wrong)

2. **진위 (jinwi) "truth or falsehood"**
   - Specialized term for authentication and verification
   - Functions primarily in formal expressions like 진위를 가리다

3. **진본 (jinbon) "authentic original"**
   - Highly specialized to art, document, and artifact authentication
   - Limited derivational productivity

4. **사실무근 (sasilmugeun) "groundless, baseless"**
   - Four-character compound with formal register
   - Specialized to official denials and formal statements

### Items not clearly fitting established groups:

1. **본질 (bonjil) "essence, nature"**
   - Philosophical and analytical term
   - While about fundamental nature/truth, functions differently from other truth-domain terms
   - Primarily used in abstract analytical discourse

2. **진가 (jinga) "true worth, real value"**
   - Evaluative term for merit or value
   - Not clearly about truth, authenticity, or correctness per se

3. **진의 (jinui) "true intention, real meaning"**
   - Specialized to interpretation of intentions
   - Bridges truth and intentionality in unique way

4. **실속 (silsok) "substance, practical benefit"**
   - Evaluative term for practical value
   - Distinct from both truth-evaluation and authenticity

5. **실없다 (sireopda) "frivolous, silly"**
   - Evaluates lack of substance or seriousness
   - Negative evaluation distinct from falsity or incorrectness

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Cross-cutting distinctions observed:

1. **Statement-focused vs. Object-focused**: Groups 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, and 9 primarily evaluate statements, claims, or information, while Group 3 focuses on physical objects and artifacts. Group 4 evaluates persons rather than their statements or products.

2. **Factual vs. Moral evaluation**: Groups 1, 3, 5, and 9 emphasize factual or procedural correctness, while Groups 2, 4, and 6 involve moral or normative evaluation.

3. **Verification vs. Character**: Groups 1, 7, and 9 involve processes of verification or assessment, while Group 4 evaluates stable character traits.

4. **Formal vs. Colloquial register**: Group 3 includes highly colloquial terms (진짜, 가짜), while Groups 6 and 9 cluster in formal registers.

### Asymmetries noted:

1. Terms for deliberate deception (Group 2) are less numerous than terms for truth and factuality (Group 1), but show rich derivational patterns.

2. Authenticity terms (Group 3) form a relatively small, specialized group compared to the extensive factual correspondence vocabulary.

3. The practical reality domain (Group 8) shows extensive vocabulary development, suggesting cultural emphasis on theory-practice distinction.

4. No clear group emerged for terms specifically about error or mistake (as opposed to deliberate deception or incorrectness), though 실수 (silsu) "mistake" exists.

### Homophony and semantic divergence:

The morpheme 진 (jin) shows remarkable polysemy, appearing in:
- Truth/genuineness domain (Groups 1, 3, 4)
- Advancement/progress domain (Group 10)
- Medical examination domain (Group 9)
- Calming/pacification contexts (진정하다 "to calm down")

This suggests either historical semantic extension or coincidental homophony of distinct roots.
