## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION and BECOME: Partial Overlap

The construction *lehitgalaʾh ke-* ("to turn out to be") is listed in P4a §4 under the BECOME/inchoative category, but its semantic content is closer to what CLCA labels REVELATION: it describes a state of affairs becoming known or apparent rather than a subject undergoing a change of state. The example *lehitgalaʾh ke-mezuyaf* ("to turn out to be a forgery") describes the epistemic exposure of a pre-existing condition, not a transition into a new state. In the P4a and P4b data, this construction appears only once, under the inchoative listing, and is not separately categorized. The data do not support a strong claim that REVELATION systematically replaces BECOME in Hebrew; rather, one construction (*lehitgalaʾh ke-*) straddles both operators. The two operators are otherwise served by distinct constructions: BECOME by *hitʿarer*, *gadal*, and *lehafokh le-*; REVELATION by *legallot*, *laḥsof*, and *laḥsof et ha-ʾemet*.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION: Shared Construction Base

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION in Hebrew share the same constructional base. The truth-predicate frame *nakhon she-* (P4a §8) serves VERIFICATION in interrogative contexts (*nakhon she-ʾamarta...?*), while the negated form *ze lo nakhon* (P4a §2, §8) serves DISCONFIRMATION in declarative contexts. The two operators are thus distinguished by illocutionary force (interrogative vs. declarative) and by the presence or absence of *lo*, not by separate constructions. This is explicitly noted in P4b: *nakhon she-* is "restricted to interrogative illocutionary force" and *ha-ʾemet hi she-* is "restricted to declarative illocutionary force." The data show no case where VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION require entirely different constructional resources.

### 2.3 CAUSATION (factual) and CAUSATION (character): Asymmetry

The data show a clear asymmetry between factual and character-level causation. Factual causation is served by the hifʿil binyan (*leʾasher*, *letsdiq*) and by periphrastic *lgaram she-* + clause. Character-level causation — causing a person to acquire or lose a truth-domain character trait (e.g., causing someone to become trustworthy or to become a liar) — has no attested construction in P4a or P4b. The only causation example that approaches this domain is *lgaram le-mishehu lehaʾamin* ("to cause someone to believe"), which is act-level (causing a belief state) rather than character-level (causing a person to become a believer by disposition). No example of causing someone to become *shaqran* ("a liar by character") or *meheymen* ("reliable by character") is attested. This asymmetry is not explicitly flagged in P4a or P4b but is derivable from the absence of any such example across the full dataset.

### 2.4 Morphological Causative Lexicalization

The hifʿil binyan, which is the standard morphological causative in Hebrew, has undergone lexicalization for several truth-domain roots such that the causative meaning is no longer transparently available. *Lehaʾamin* (hifʿil of א-מ-נ) means "to believe," not "to cause to believe." This means that for these roots, CAUSATION (factual) in the sense of causing a belief or credibility state must be expressed periphrastically. The morphological causative is available and productive for other roots in the domain (*letsdiq*, *leʾasher*), so this is a lexical gap for specific items rather than a morphosyntactic gap in the causative construction itself.

### 2.5 NEGATION: Exclusively Analytic

Hebrew expresses negation of truth-domain qualities exclusively through the analytic particle *lo* in predicative position. There is no productive morphological negative prefix for truth-domain adjectives (P4a §2 explicitly states: "There is no productive negative prefix, e.g., *\*lo-meheymen* as a compound attributive, in standard usage"). The nominal alternative *ḥoser* + abstract noun (*ḥoser kanut*, "lack of sincerity") provides a circumlocutionary nominal strategy but does not constitute a morphological negative. This is a morphosyntactic gap relative to languages that have productive negative attributive prefixes, but it is not a functional gap: the analytic construction is fully productive and rated [CORE].

### 2.6 Positive/Negative Asymmetry in Attributive Position

A directional asymmetry is observable between positive and negative attribution. Positive truth-domain attribution is available in both attributive position (*ʿed meheymen*, "a credible witness") and predicative position (*ha-meqor meheymen*, "the source is reliable"). Negative attribution is available only in predicative position (*ha-meqor lo meheymen*, "the source is not reliable"); there is no attributive negative form (*\*ʿed lo-meheymen* is not a standard construction). This means that to express "an unreliable source" attributively, Hebrew requires either a predicative relative clause (*meqor she-ʾeno meheymen*, "a source that is not reliable") or a nominal circumlocution. This asymmetry is derivable from the P4a §2 note but is not explicitly stated there.

### 2.7 Indefinite/Definite Contrast in Verbal Periphrasis

Within the VERBAL PERIPHRASIS construction (P4a §10), the data show a systematic contrast between indefinite and definite use of the truth-domain noun as object. *Ledaber ʾemet* ("to tell the truth," lit. "to speak truth") requires the indefinite form and is a fixed collocation. *Leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet* ("to distort the truth") requires the definite form with the accusative marker *et*. P4b notes that "the indefinite noun (*ʾemet* without *ha-*) is obligatory in this collocation" for *ledaber ʾemet*, while "the definite object (*ha-ʾemet* with *et*) is the expected form when referring to a specific truth" for distortion/concealment verbs. This contrast is internal to the construction and does not constitute a gap, but it is a systematic constraint on the distribution of definiteness within the verbal periphrasis pattern.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

No full structural gaps are identified. All ten CLCA operators listed in the mapping table have at least one attested construction in the P4a/P4b data, with the exception noted under CAUSATION (character) below.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **Morphological causative for *lehaʾamin***: The hifʿil form *lehaʾamin* has lexicalized as "to believe" and does not transparently express "to cause to believe." This is a lexical gap for this specific root: the causative meaning must be expressed periphrastically (*lgaram le-mishehu lehaʾamin*). The morphological causative construction itself is not absent.
- **Negative attributive adjective**: There is no single-word negative attributive form for truth-domain adjectives (e.g., no lexical item equivalent to "unreliable" functioning as a pre-nominal or post-nominal attributive modifier without a relative clause). The gap is at the level of individual lexical items rather than the construction type.

### 3.3 Morphosyntactic Gaps

- **No productive morphological negative prefix for truth-domain adjectives**: Negation in attributive position requires a relative clause strategy (*meqor she-ʾeno meheymen*) or predicative restatement. The analytic *lo* is restricted to predicative position. This is a morphosyntactic gap in the attributive negative construction specifically.
- **No dedicated inchoative morpheme**: BECOME is expressed through intransitive verbs, the hitpaʿel binyan, or periphrastic *lehafokh le-*. There is no single morphological marker dedicated to inchoative meaning in this domain.

### 3.4 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data. All operators — including REVELATION, VERIFICATION, DISCONFIRMATION, and NEGATION — are expressible in Hebrew, though some require periphrastic or analytic strategies.

### 3.5 Asymmetric Gaps

- **CAUSATION (character) vs. CAUSATION (factual)**: Factual causation is attested with multiple constructions. Character-level causation (causing a person to acquire or lose a truth-domain character trait) has **no attested construction** in the P4a/P4b data. This is an asymmetric gap: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION — circumlocution required** for CAUSATION (character).
- **Positive vs. negative attribution**: Positive truth-domain attribution is available in both attributive and predicative positions. Negative attribution is available only in predicative position; attributive negative modification requires a relative clause. This is an asymmetric morphosyntactic gap affecting the negative side of the ATTR operator.
- **Indefinite vs. definite in verbal periphrasis**: Truth-affirming verbal periphrasis (*ledaber ʾemet*) uses the indefinite noun; truth-distorting verbal periphrasis (*leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet*) uses the definite noun. This is an asymmetry internal to the VERBAL PERIPHRASIS construction, not a gap, but it is directionally consistent: positive/affirming collocations favor indefinite; negative/distorting collocations favor definite.
