# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Note on transliteration:** Tagalog uses the Latin-based Filipino alphabet. Native script (Baybayin) is not in active general use. Transliteration column is omitted where the written form is already Roman script; stress marking follows standard Philippine linguistic convention using acute accent where disambiguation is needed.

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Literal meaning:** Base form; cognate with Spanish *todo* disputed; morphologically simple root
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Usage:** Used to assert that a statement corresponds to fact or that something is genuine rather than simulated. Functions as predicate (*Totoo ba ito?* "Is this true/real?") and as discourse particle affirming sincerity. Participates in derivation: *katotohanan* (truth, reality), *totoong-totoo* (emphatic reduplication).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-an* (nominalizing circumfix); "the state/domain of being true"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the truth of the matter
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to truth as an abstract property or to a specific true state of affairs. Common in formal, moral, and philosophical registers (*Ang katotohanan ay mahalaga* "Truth is important"). Also used in legal and testimonial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tunay
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Usage:** Emphasizes authenticity or genuineness of an entity or relationship, often contrasting with imitation or pretense (*tunay na kaibigan* "a true/genuine friend"). Slightly more evaluative of intrinsic character than *totoo*, which more often concerns factual correspondence. Derives *katunayan* (proof, evidence, genuineness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** katunayan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "the state of being genuine/real"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, authenticity, the fact that
**Usage:** Used to refer to evidence or proof that establishes something as genuine or factually the case. Also functions as a conjunctive expression (*Sa katunayan* "In fact, as a matter of fact"). Common in both formal argumentation and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tama
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage:** Applied to statements, answers, actions, or judgments to indicate they are correct or appropriate (*Tama ang sagot mo* "Your answer is correct"). Also used in moral evaluation of conduct (*Tama ba ang ginawa niya?* "Was what he did right?"). Derives *katamaan* and participates in *tama-tama* (just right, exactly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** mali
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
**Usage:** Direct antonym of *tama*; applied to statements, beliefs, actions, or persons to indicate error or wrongness. Used in factual contexts (*Mali ang datos* "The data is wrong") and moral contexts (*Mali ang ginawa niya* "What he did was wrong"). Derives *pagkakamali* (mistake, error).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** pagkakamali
**Literal meaning:** *pagkaka-* (nominalization of accidental/completed action) + *mali*; "an instance of being wrong"
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault
**Usage:** Refers to a specific instance of error, whether factual, moral, or practical. Implies an action or judgment that deviated from what was correct. Commonly used in apology and accountability contexts (*Aminin ang pagkakamali* "Admit the mistake").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** huwad
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, forged, false
**Usage:** Refers to objects, documents, or representations that are deliberately made to appear genuine but are not (*huwad na pera* "counterfeit money"; *huwad na dokumento* "forged document"). Carries a connotation of intentional deception, distinguishing it from mere error. Common in legal and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** peke
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *pique* or via Philippine Spanish; root form in Tagalog
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, phony
**Usage:** Colloquial near-synonym of *huwad*, applied to objects, goods, or persons presenting a false identity (*pekeng produkto* "fake product"; *pekeng doktor* "fake doctor"). More informal in register than *huwad* and very widely used in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** lehitimo
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *legítimo*
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, genuine
**Usage:** Used in legal, institutional, and social contexts to evaluate whether a claim, authority, status, or person meets recognized standards of validity (*lehitimong pamahalaán* "legitimate government"; *lehitimong anak* "legitimate child"). Less common in purely factual contexts; primarily evaluative of standing or right.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** wastó
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** correct, proper, right, appropriate
**Usage:** Overlaps with *tama* in the sense of correctness but often carries a stronger sense of propriety or conformity to a standard (*wastong paraan* "the proper/correct way"). Used in prescriptive contexts about conduct, procedure, and language. Derives *kawastuhan* (correctness, propriety).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** kawastuhan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *wastó* + *-an*; "the state/quality of being correct or proper"
**Typical English translations:** correctness, propriety, rightness
**Usage:** Abstract nominal referring to the quality of being correct or proper, used in formal and evaluative discourse. Less frequent in casual speech than its root *wastó*. Appears in discussions of moral conduct, linguistic correctness, and procedural propriety.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** tapat
**Literal meaning:** Root form; also means "facing, directly opposite" (spatial); extended to moral/epistemic sense
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, straightforward, faithful
**Usage:** Evaluates persons or statements as honest and without concealment (*tapatang tao* "an honest person"; *magsalita nang tapat* "to speak honestly"). The spatial sense of directness appears to underlie the moral extension. Derives *katapatan* (honesty, sincerity, loyalty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** katapatan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tapat* + *-an*; "the state of being honest/faithful"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty
**Usage:** Abstract nominal covering both epistemic honesty (not deceiving) and relational faithfulness (loyalty). Used in moral evaluation of persons and relationships. The dual sense of sincerity and loyalty is characteristic and both are well attested.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** mapagkakatiwalaan
**Literal meaning:** *ma-* + *pagkakatiwalá* + *-an* (potential/ability form); "one/something that can be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** Predicative or attributive form evaluating persons, sources, or institutions as worthy of trust (*mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan* "a reliable source"). Morphologically complex but lexically conventionalized. Common in contexts of evaluating testimony, institutions, and persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** tiwala
**Literal meaning:** Root form; morphologically simple
**Typical English translations:** trust, confidence, reliance
**Usage:** Refers to the attitude of trust or confidence placed in a person, institution, or claim. Used both as a noun (*walang tiwala* "no trust") and in verbal constructions. Central to evaluations of reliability and credibility in interpersonal and institutional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** kredible
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *credible* (or Spanish *creíble*)
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable
**Usage:** Used in journalistic, legal, and formal evaluative contexts to assess whether a source, witness, or claim merits belief (*kredibleng saksi* "credible witness"). More formal and domain-specific than native equivalents; common in media and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *maka-* + *katotohanan* + *-an*; "in accordance with truth/reality"
**Typical English translations:** realistic, truthful, true-to-life
**Usage:** Applied to representations, narratives, or portrayals that accurately reflect reality (*makatotohanang larawan* "a realistic/truthful portrayal"). Common in aesthetic and critical evaluation of literature, film, and art. Distinct from *totoo* in that it evaluates resemblance to truth rather than asserting truth directly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** aktuwal
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *actual* / Spanish *actual*
**Typical English translations:** actual, real (as opposed to hypothetical or apparent)
**Usage:** Used to distinguish what is genuinely the case from what is supposed, hypothetical, or apparent (*aktuwal na nangyari* "what actually happened"). More common in formal and journalistic registers. Overlaps with *tunay* and *totoo* but emphasizes contrast with the hypothetical or supposed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** orihinal
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *original*
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic (not a copy)
**Usage:** Evaluates objects or works as being the genuine source rather than a copy or reproduction (*orihinal na kopya* is avoided as contradictory; *orihinal na obra* "original work"). Used in aesthetic, legal, and commercial contexts. Contrasts with *kopya* (copy) and *huwad/peke* (fake).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** awtentiko
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *auténtico* / English *authentic*
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** Used in formal, academic, and commercial contexts to evaluate whether something is genuinely what it claims to be (*awtentikong dokumento* "authentic document"). Somewhat more formal than *tunay* and more common in written registers. Overlaps substantially with *tunay* and *orihinal*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** paimbabaw
**Literal meaning:** *pa-* + *ibabaw* (surface, top); "on the surface only"
**Typical English translations:** superficial, insincere, hypocritical, surface-level
**Usage:** Evaluates conduct, expressions, or persons as lacking genuine depth or sincerity — performing an appearance without the corresponding reality (*paimbabaw na pagmamahal* "superficial/insincere love"). Carries a negative evaluative force regarding the gap between appearance and genuine character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** mapanlinlang
**Literal meaning:** *mapa-* + *linlang* (to deceive) + agentive/adjectival morphology; "prone to deceiving"
**Typical English translations:** deceptive, misleading, fraudulent
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, statements, or representations as designed or tending to create false impressions (*mapanlinlang na advertisement* "deceptive advertisement"). Used in moral, legal, and consumer-protection contexts. The root *linlang* (deception) also occurs independently.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** linlang
**Literal meaning:** Root form
**Typical English translations:** deception, deceit, to deceive
**Usage:** Refers to the act or practice of creating false impressions in another's mind. Used as a noun and verbal root (*nillinlang siya* "he/she was deceived"). Appears in moral condemnation of dishonest conduct and in legal descriptions of fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** pagsisinungaling
**Literal meaning:** *pag-* + *sinungaling* (liar) + nominalization; "the act of lying"
**Typical English translations:** lying, the act of telling a lie
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to the act of deliberately stating what one knows to be false. Derived from *sinungaling* (liar), which is itself a well-established root. Used in moral evaluation and in legal contexts involving false testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** sinungaling
**Literal meaning:** Root form (possibly from *sungaling*; etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** liar; lying (as adjective)
**Usage:** Refers to a person who lies habitually or to the quality of being a liar (*Sinungaling ka!* "You're a liar!"). One of the most common terms of moral reproach in Tagalog. Also used attributively (*sinungaling na salita* "lying/false words").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** pruweba
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from Spanish *prueba* (proof, test)
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, test
**Usage:** Refers to evidence or demonstration that establishes the truth or falsity of a claim (*Nasaan ang pruweba?* "Where is the proof?"). Common in legal, argumentative, and everyday contexts. Near-synonym of *katunayan* but more colloquial and direct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** ebidensya
**Literal meaning:** Borrowing from English *evidence* / Spanish *evidencia*
**Typical English translations:** evidence
**Usage:** Used primarily in legal and formal investigative contexts to refer to material or testimonial evidence supporting a claim (*walang ebidensya* "no evidence"). More domain-specific than *pruweba* and more formal in register. Common in legal proceedings and journalism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** huwag-huwaran
**Literal meaning:** *huwad* + reduplication/derivation pattern; "something made to imitate"
**Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, counterfeit (object)
**Usage:** Refers to an object made to resemble another, with connotations of inauthenticity. Less common than *huwad* alone and more specifically nominal. Usage is attested but less frequent; marks the object as a deliberate imitation rather than the act of faking.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**30.**
**Form:** di-tunay
**Literal meaning:** *di-* (negation prefix) + *tunay*; "not genuine"
**Typical English translations:** inauthentic, not real, false
**Usage:** Negated form of *tunay*, used to evaluate something as lacking genuine character or authenticity. Attested in written and formal registers but less lexicalized than *huwad* or *peke* for the same evaluative purpose. Functions as a predicate or attributive modifier.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
