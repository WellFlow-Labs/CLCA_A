1. **totta**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘true’; related to **tosi**  
- Typical English translations: true, correct, right

Usage: **Totta** is a very common predicate adjective and adverbial response in evaluating whether a statement matches reality. It is used in everyday speech, argumentation, and formal contexts, as in *Se on totta* ‘That is true’ or *Totta kai* ‘of course’.  
**[CORE]**

2. **tosi**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘true, real’  
- Typical English translations: true, real, genuine

Usage: **Tosi** is common in both colloquial and standard Finnish, though in colloquial use it also functions as an intensifier. In evaluative contexts it can refer to truth, authenticity, or realness, as in *tosi tarina* ‘a true story’ or *onko tämä tosi?* ‘is this true?’.  
**[CORE]**

3. **totuus**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘truth’; from **tosi/totta** + nominal suffix **-uus**  
- Typical English translations: truth

Usage: **Totuus** is the standard noun for ‘truth’ in ordinary, philosophical, moral, and legal discussion. It is used for both abstract truth and the truth of a matter, as in *puhua totuutta* ‘tell the truth’ and *asian totuus* ‘the truth of the matter’.  
**[CORE]**

4. **todellinen**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘real, actual’; from **tode-** / **tosi** family + adjectival suffix **-llinen**  
- Typical English translations: real, actual, genuine

Usage: **Todellinen** is used when distinguishing what is actual or genuine from what is apparent, fictional, or merely supposed. It appears in everyday, philosophical, and legal language, for example in *todellinen syy* ‘the real reason’ or *todellinen omistaja* ‘beneficial/actual owner’.  
**[CORE]**

5. **todellisuus**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘reality’; from **todellinen** + **-uus**  
- Typical English translations: reality

Usage: **Todellisuus** refers to reality as opposed to illusion, appearance, or representation. It is frequent in general language and in philosophical or critical discussion, especially when evaluating whether something corresponds to the real world.  
**[CORE]**

6. **aito**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘genuine, authentic’  
- Typical English translations: genuine, authentic, real

Usage: **Aito** is widely used for objects, feelings, style, and persons when evaluating genuineness rather than falseness or imitation. Typical uses include *aito nahka* ‘genuine leather’ and *aito ilo* ‘genuine joy’.  
**[CORE]**

7. **aidosti**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘genuinely, authentically’; **aito** + adverbial **-sti**  
- Typical English translations: genuinely, authentically, truly

Usage: **Aidosti** evaluates the manner or sincerity of an action, feeling, or representation. It is common in speech and writing, for example in *aidosti kiinnostunut* ‘genuinely interested’.  
**[CORE]**

8. **autenttinen**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: loanword ‘authentic’  
- Typical English translations: authentic

Usage: **Autenttinen** is used especially in cultural, aesthetic, academic, and commercial contexts for authenticity of experience, style, document, or object. Compared with **aito**, it is somewhat more formal or specialized in tone.  
**[CORE]**

9. **väärennös**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘forgery, fake’; from **väärentää** ‘to forge/falsify’ + noun suffix **-nös/-nnös**  
- Typical English translations: forgery, counterfeit, fake

Usage: **Väärennös** is used for forged documents, artworks, signatures, money, and similar items whose genuineness is denied. It is common in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts involving authenticity and deception.  
**[CORE]**

10. **väärä**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘wrong, false’  
- Typical English translations: wrong, false, incorrect

Usage: **Väärä** is a basic adjective for factual incorrectness, moral wrongness, or mismatch with what is proper or intended. It is highly frequent in expressions like *väärä tieto* ‘false information’, *väärä vastaus* ‘wrong answer’, and *väärä teko* ‘wrong act’.  
**[CORE]**

11. **virheellinen**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘erroneous’; from **virhe** ‘error’ + **-ellinen**  
- Typical English translations: erroneous, incorrect, faulty

Usage: **Virheellinen** is common in formal and semi-formal contexts for statements, data, reasoning, procedures, and representations that contain error. It is especially typical in administrative, technical, and academic language.  
**[CORE]**

12. **oikea**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘right, correct, proper’  
- Typical English translations: right, correct, proper, genuine

Usage: **Oikea** is a very broad and frequent adjective used for correctness, appropriateness, legitimacy, and sometimes genuineness. It appears in factual, moral, practical, and legal evaluations, as in *oikea vastaus* ‘correct answer’ and *oikea menettely* ‘proper procedure’.  
**[CORE]**

13. **oikein**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: adverb from **oikea**  
- Typical English translations: correctly, properly, right

Usage: **Oikein** is used to evaluate whether something is done or understood correctly. It is common in instruction, assessment, and moral or practical judgment, for example *tehdä oikein* ‘do the right thing / do correctly’.

14. **oikeus**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘rightness, justice, right’; from **oikea** + **-us**  
- Typical English translations: justice, right, legality, entitlement

Usage: **Oikeus** is a central noun in legal and moral contexts, covering justice, rights, and what is rightful. Depending on context it can refer to normative rightness, legal entitlement, or the judicial sphere.

- Typical English translations: just, fair

16. **laillinen**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘legal, lawful’; from **laki** ‘law’ + **-llinen**  
- Typical English translations: legal, lawful

Usage: **Laillinen** evaluates conduct, status, or procedure in relation to the law. It is standard in legal and administrative language and also common in public discussion, as in *laillinen oikeus* ‘legal right’ or *laillinen menettely* ‘lawful procedure’.  
**[CORE]**

17. **lainmukainen**
- Transliteration: —  
- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘in accordance with law’; **lain** ‘of the law’ + **mukainen** ‘according to’  
- Typical English translations: lawful, in accordance with the law, legal

Usage: **Lainmukainen** is a formal adjective used especially for actions, decisions, and procedures that conform to legal rules. It is common in official and juridical texts, often with a narrower compliance sense than everyday **laillinen**.

18. **epäaito**

Usage: **Epäaito** is used for things, expressions, or performances judged not to be genuine or sincere. It occurs in criticism, psychology, and everyday evaluation, though less frequently than **aito**.

19. **epätosi**

Usage: **Epätosi** is the standard opposite of **tosi** in logic, argumentation, and general statement evaluation. It is common in formal and semi-formal contexts, such as *väite on epätosi* ‘the claim is false’.

- Literal meaning / morphology: ‘to hold its place’  
- Typical English translations: be true, hold true, be correct

Usage: This fixed expression is a very common way to say that a statement or report is true or accurate. It is used in both speech and writing, often in forms like *Se pitää paikkansa* ‘That is true / That checks out’.
