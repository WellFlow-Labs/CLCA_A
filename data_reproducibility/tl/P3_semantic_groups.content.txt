# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## IDENTIFIED SEMANTIC GROUPS

### GROUP 1: Factual Correspondence Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to affirm or deny that statements, claims, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs in the world. These expressions are primarily used in evaluating whether what is said or claimed matches reality, often in contexts of verification, confirmation, or denial of factual claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- **totoo** (true, real) — used to affirm factual reality or actual existence
- **katotohanan** (truth, reality, fact) — abstract noun for truth as concept or body of facts
- **hindi totoo** / **di-totoo** (not true, false) — standard negation of factual truth
- **totoo ba** (is it true?) — question seeking factual confirmation
- **sa totoo lang** (to be honest, truthfully) — discourse marker introducing frank statements
- **walang-katotohanan** (without truth, baseless) — describes claims lacking factual basis
- **bulaán** (false, untrue, fabricated) — describes false or fabricated statements
- **kasinungalingan** (lie, falsehood) — false statements made with intent to deceive
- **kathâ** (fiction, invention, fabrication) — something invented or fabricated
- **kathang-isip** (imagination, figment of imagination) — dismisses imaginary claims

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tunay** (genuine, authentic) — overlaps with this group but emphasizes authenticity/genuineness rather than pure factual correspondence
- **makatotohanan** (realistic, true-to-life) — extends into aesthetic evaluation of verisimilitude

---

### GROUP 2: Authenticity and Genuineness Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish genuine, authentic, or real items and qualities from fake, counterfeit, or spurious ones. These expressions focus on whether something is what it purports to be, commonly used in evaluating the authenticity of objects, feelings, character, or nature rather than the truth of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tunay** (genuine, authentic) — emphasizes authenticity with emotional/qualitative weight
- **tunay na pagmamahal** (true love) — fixed expression for authentic love
- **tunay na kalikasan** (true nature, real character) — fundamental or true nature
- **peke** (fake, counterfeit, phony) — describes non-genuine objects or insincere behavior
- **huwad** (false, counterfeit, spurious) — formal term for counterfeit or fraudulent items
- **huwad na dokumento** (false document) — counterfeit documents
- **pagkapeke** (fakeness, state of being fake) — quality of being counterfeit
- **magpeke-peke** (to pretend, to act fake) — insincere behavior
- **katunayan** (proof, evidence, verification) — proof that verifies authenticity
- **patunayan** (to prove, to verify) — to demonstrate authenticity or truth

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **totoo** (true, real) — can be used for both factual truth and genuine existence
- **diwa** (essence, spirit, true meaning) — refers to essential nature but more abstract

---

### GROUP 3: Correctness Evaluation Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, answers, judgments, actions, or decisions are correct, right, or proper according to standards, rules, or appropriateness. These expressions are primarily used in instructional, evaluative, and decision-making contexts to assess accuracy or rightness.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tama** (correct, right) — indicates correctness of statements, actions, or judgments
- **tama ka** (you're right) — affirms someone's correctness
- **tama ba** (is it correct?) — seeks confirmation of correctness
- **tamang sagot** (correct answer) — standard phrase in educational contexts
- **mali** (wrong, incorrect) — denotes incorrectness or error
- **mali ka** (you're wrong) — indicates someone is incorrect
- **pagkakamali** (mistake, error) — refers to errors that have been made
- **wasto** (correct, proper, accurate) — formal term for correctness with emphasis on precision
- **wastong paraan** (correct method) — appropriate or correct methods
- **hustó** (exact, just right, fitting) — describes exact rightness or perfect fit
- **itama** (to correct, to fix) — causative verb for making corrections
- **pagtitama** (correction, act of correcting) — process of correcting

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tama na** (that's enough) — semantic shift from correctness to sufficiency
- **tamang panahon** (right time) — extends to temporal appropriateness
- **tumama** (to hit, to be correct) — dual meaning: physical hitting and correctness

---

### GROUP 4: Honesty and Truthfulness in Conduct Terms

**Definition:** Terms describing the moral quality of honesty, sincerity, and faithfulness in speech and behavior. These expressions evaluate character and conduct, focusing on whether persons are honest in what they say, faithful to commitments, and sincere in intentions, commonly used in moral discourse and character evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tapat** (honest, faithful, loyal) — describes honesty in speech and faithfulness in conduct
- **matapat** (honest, sincere, truthful) — adjectival form for honest persons
- **katapatan** (honesty, sincerity, loyalty) — abstract noun for quality of being honest
- **magtapat** (to confess, to be honest) — verb for revealing truth or confessing
- **tapat sa salita** (true to one's word) — keeps promises and commitments
- **tapatang loob** (honest heart, sincere intentions) — sincere or honest intentions
- **sinungaling** (liar) — person who habitually tells lies
- **magsinungaling** (to lie, to tell lies) — verb for telling lies
- **pagsisinungaling** (lying, act of lying) — practice of lying
- **walang-katapatan** (without honesty, dishonest) — lacks honesty or loyalty
- **tapat na sagot** (honest answer) — truthful response

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tapat sa tungkulin** (faithful to duty) — extends from honesty to duty fulfillment
- **kasinungalingan** (lie, falsehood) — overlaps with factual falsity group

---

### GROUP 5: Trust and Reliability Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether persons or sources can be trusted, relied upon, or have confidence placed in them. These expressions focus on the reliability and dependability of persons or information sources, commonly used in assessing character and evaluating the credibility of sources.

**Representative expressions:**
- **mapagkakatiwalaan** (trustworthy, reliable, dependable) — can be trusted or relied upon
- **magtiwala** (to trust, to have confidence in) — verb for trusting
- **pagtitiwala** (trust, confidence) — the act or state of trusting
- **ipagkatiwala** (to entrust) — to place confidence in someone
- **sigurado** (sure, certain) — indicates certainty or assurance
- **sigurado ka ba** (are you sure?) — seeks confirmation of certainty
- **magsigurado** (to make sure, to ensure) — to verify or ensure certainty
- **hindi mapagkakatiwalaan** (not trustworthy, unreliable) — cannot be trusted

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tapat** (honest, faithful) — honesty contributes to trustworthiness but is distinct
- **katapatan** (honesty, loyalty) — related to but not identical with trustworthiness

---

### GROUP 6: Resemblance and Similarity Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to indicate that one thing resembles, looks like, or is similar to another in appearance, character, or nature. These expressions are primarily used in making comparisons and describing likeness, with some specifically focused on physical appearance.

**Representative expressions:**
- **katulad** (similar, like, same as) — indicates similarity or sameness
- **katulad mo** (like you) — very common comparative construction
- **kamukha** (resembles in appearance, looks like) — specifically physical resemblance
- **kamukha mo** (looks like you) — common for comparing appearances
- **kawangis** (resemblance, likeness, similarity) — indicates resemblance
- **walang katulad** (without equal, incomparable) — indicates uniqueness
- **walang kamukha** (has no resemblance) — unique appearance

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **makatotohanan** (realistic, true-to-life) — describes resemblance to reality but in aesthetic contexts

---

### GROUP 7: Emphatic Confirmation and Reality Markers

**Definition:** Terms and particles used to emphasize the reality, truth, or certainty of statements, often functioning as discourse markers to strengthen affirmations or express surprise. These expressions are extremely common in conversational contexts for confirming, emphasizing, or questioning the reality of claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- **talaga** (really, truly, indeed) — emphasizes truth or reality of statements
- **talaga ba** (really? is that so?) — expresses surprise or seeks confirmation
- **totoo nga** (truly, indeed) — emphatic confirmation
- **talaga nga** (really indeed) — double emphasis
- **totoo naman** (it's true though) — concessive acknowledgment
- **totoo pa** (still true, moreover) — emphasizes continued truth
- **totoo rin** (also true) — acknowledges additional truth
- **napakatotoo** (very true, extremely real) — intensified truth
- **oo talaga** (yes really) — emphatic affirmation

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **sigurado** (sure, certain) — expresses certainty but less emphatic/conversational
- **totoo** (true, real) — can function as simple truth term or emphatic marker depending on context

---

### GROUP 8: Sufficiency and Cessation Markers

**Definition:** Terms that have shifted from correctness or exactness meanings to indicate sufficiency, adequacy, or to signal that something should stop or end. These expressions are used to mark boundaries, indicate when enough has been done or said, or call for cessation of actions.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tama na** (that's enough, stop now) — signals sufficiency or calls for cessation
- **tama na yan** (that's enough of that) — emphatic cessation command
- **tama na po** (that's enough, polite) — polite cessation request
- **hustó na** (that's enough, just right now) — indicates sufficiency
- **hustó lang** (just right, just enough) — exactly appropriate or sufficient

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tama** (correct) — base meaning is correctness, but "tama na" has shifted to sufficiency
- **hustó** (exact, just right) — can indicate both exactness and sufficiency

---

### GROUP 9: Essence and True Nature Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to refer to the fundamental nature, essential meaning, or underlying spirit of something, commonly employed in philosophical, interpretive, or analytical contexts when discussing what something fundamentally is or means.

**Representative expressions:**
- **diwa** (essence, spirit, true meaning) — essential nature or true meaning
- **diwa ng** (essence of, spirit of) — introduces what is being interpreted
- **tunay na kalikasan** (true nature, real character) — fundamental nature
- **ang tunay na kulay** (the true colors, real character) — reveals true character

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tunay** (genuine, authentic) — can refer to true nature but more commonly to authenticity
- **katotohanan** (truth) — abstract truth rather than essential nature

---

### GROUP 10: Physical Impact and Hitting Terms

**Definition:** Terms derived from correctness roots but used to describe physical hitting, striking, or being affected by something. These expressions show semantic extension from evaluative correctness to physical action and impact.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tamaan** (to be hit, to be struck, to be affected) — to be hit by something
- **tumama** (to hit, to strike) — to hit a target physically (also: to be correct)

**Note:** This is a small, specialized group showing polysemy of the **tama** root. The dual meaning (correctness + hitting) is maintained in some forms like **tumama**.

---

## ITEMS NOT CLEARLY GROUPED

The following items do not fit cleanly into the above groups or show characteristics that make grouping problematic:

1. **magkatotoo** (to come true, to be realized) — describes fulfillment of predictions/dreams rather than truth evaluation; semantic extension from factual truth to realization

2. **pagtotoo** (act of being truthful, truthfulness) — nominalized action form with limited usage; less common than **katotohanan**

3. **ikatotoo** (to cause to be true, to make real) — causative form with marginal usage; literary/formal contexts

4. **totoo lamang** (only true, merely true) — formal/literary usage with limited distribution

5. **paninindigan ng katotohanan** (standing for truth, upholding truth) — fixed expression about defending truth; more about moral stance than truth evaluation

6. **kawalang-katapatan** (lack of honesty, dishonesty) — formal expression with limited usage compared to simple negations

7. **kahuwaran** (model, example, pattern) — derived from **huwad** (false) but has undergone complete semantic shift to mean exemplar; no longer related to falsity

8. **pagkakamali-mali** (repeated mistakes) — emphasizes pattern of errors; marginal usage

9. **totoo kaya** (I wonder if it's true) — expresses speculation with particle **kaya**; functions more as epistemic hedge than truth evaluation

10. **kung totoo** (if true) — conditional construction; functions grammatically rather than semantically as distinct truth term

11. **parang hindi totoo** (seems unreal) — expression of disbelief using **parang** (seems); more about subjective impression than truth evaluation

12. **tama ang daan** (on the right path) — metaphorical extension to life direction; extends beyond correctness evaluation

13. **tama ang oras** (the time is right) — temporal appropriateness; extends beyond correctness to timing

14. **may tama ka** (you have a point) — acknowledges partial correctness; nuanced agreement rather than simple correctness evaluation

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Grouping Patterns:

1. **Clear Core Groups:** Groups 1-6 show relatively clear boundaries based on usage patterns: factual truth vs. authenticity vs. correctness vs. honesty vs. trust vs. resemblance.

2. **Functional Groups:** Groups 7-8 are defined more by discourse function (emphasis, cessation) than semantic content, showing how some terms have grammaticalized into discourse markers.

3. **Specialized Extensions:** Groups 9-10 represent semantic extensions or specialized uses of core terms in particular domains (philosophical essence, physical impact).

4. **Overlapping Boundaries:** Several terms could belong to multiple groups:
   - **tunay** spans factual truth and authenticity
   - **tapat** relates to both honesty and trustworthiness
   - **totoo** functions as both factual truth term and emphatic marker
   - **tama** covers correctness, sufficiency, and physical hitting

5. **Asymmetries:** 
   - Positive terms (truth, correctness, honesty) show more diverse groupings than negative terms
   - Native roots show richer semantic extensions than borrowed roots
   - Some groups (1-4) are large and productive; others (9-10) are small and specialized

6. **Ungrouped Items:** Many items resist grouping because they:
   - Represent grammatical constructions rather than semantic units
   - Show semantic shifts that distance them from core meanings
   - Have marginal or specialized usage
   - Function primarily as discourse markers or particles

7. **No Universal Categories:** The groupings reflect Tagalog-specific patterns of usage and do not map neatly onto assumed universal truth categories. For example, the distinction between **totoo** (factual correspondence) and **tunay** (authenticity/genuineness) is specific to Tagalog usage patterns.
