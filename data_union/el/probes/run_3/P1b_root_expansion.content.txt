# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** ἀλήθ- / αληθ-
**Transliteration:** alith-
**P1 items participating:** 5 (αληθής, αλήθεια, αληθινός, επαληθεύω, αναληθής)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes the concept of truth as unconcealment or correspondence with reality. It is the primary productive root for truth-related vocabulary in Greek, generating adjectives, nouns, verbs, and compounds across all registers.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ψευδ- / ψεύδ-
**Transliteration:** psevd-
**P1 items participating:** 4 (ψευδής, ψεύδος, διαψεύδω, and ψευδο- as a productive prefix in compounds such as ψευδοεπιστήμη)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes falsehood, deception, and fabrication. It is the primary productive root for the falsity pole of the truth-evaluation domain, generating adjectives, nouns, verbs, and a highly productive compounding prefix.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** εικ- / εικον-
**Transliteration:** eik- / eikon-
**P1 items participating:** 3 (εικονικός, εικαζόμενος, εικαστικός)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes the concept of likeness, image, and resemblance. It contributes to vocabulary concerning representation, simulation, and conjecture — covering the space between genuine reality and its copies or approximations.

---

*Note on selection rationale:* The ψευδ- root and αληθ- root are the two dominant poles of the truth-evaluation domain. The εικ-/εικον- root was selected over competing candidates (e.g., ακριβ-, ορθ-, αξι-) because it participates in three distinct P1 items across different semantic sub-areas (simulation, allegation, representation), making it the third most structurally prominent root in the collected data.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: αληθ- (alith-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εια (abstract noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
**Context of use:** The primary noun for truth in all registers. Already listed in P1; included here as the base form for the expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** true, truthful
**Context of use:** Base adjective for truth-value; already in P1. Included as anchor form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ινός (adjectival suffix indicating nature or material)
**Natural English translation:** real, genuine, true
**Context of use:** Already in P1. Included as anchor form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** αληθεύω
**Transliteration:** alithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to be true
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and formal written registers to mean "to tell the truth" or, of a proposition, "to be true." Less common in everyday speech than λέω την αλήθεια.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** επαληθεύω
**Transliteration:** epalithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** επί (upon, further) + αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to validate
**Context of use:** Already in P1. Included here as a derived verbal form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** επαλήθευση
**Transliteration:** epalíthefsi
**Morpheme breakdown:** επί + αληθ- + -ευση (verbal noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** verification, confirmation, validation
**Context of use:** The noun corresponding to επαληθεύω. Used in scientific, administrative, journalistic, and legal contexts to refer to the process or act of verifying a claim, result, or document.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** αναληθής
**Transliteration:** analithís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αν- (privative) + αληθ- (truth) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** untrue, false
**Context of use:** Already in P1 as [MARGINAL]. Formal/philosophical negation of αληθής.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
**Form:** αναλήθεια
**Transliteration:** analíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αν- (privative) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and formal written registers as the abstract noun corresponding to αναληθής. Less common than ψεύδος in everyday speech; appears in contexts of explicit philosophical discussion of truth-values.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**9.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια
**Transliteration:** alithofánia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -εια (abstract noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, appearance of truth, plausibility
**Context of use:** Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophy to describe the quality of appearing true or lifelike, without necessarily being true. Corresponds to the classical concept of verisimilitude.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** αληθοφανής
**Transliteration:** alithofanís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** verisimilar, plausible, lifelike, credible
**Context of use:** Applied to narratives, accounts, or representations that have the appearance of truth or reality. Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and everyday evaluation of stories or claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια αφήγησης
**Transliteration:** alithofánia afígisis
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude) + αφήγηση (narration)
**Natural English translation:** narrative verisimilitude
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in literary theory and criticism to describe the degree to which a narrative creates the impression of reality or truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** λέω την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** léo tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** λέω (I say) + την (the, acc.) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** I tell the truth
**Context of use:** The most common fixed expression for truth-telling in everyday Modern Greek. Used across all registers and age groups.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Transliteration:** i alíthia íne óti
**Morpheme breakdown:** η αλήθεια (the truth) + είναι (is) + ότι (that)
**Natural English translation:** the truth is that…
**Context of use:** A fixed discourse marker used to introduce a frank or candid statement, often one that qualifies or contradicts what has just been said. Extremely common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** κατά την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** katá tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** κατά (according to) + την αλήθεια (the truth)
**Natural English translation:** in truth, truthfully, to tell the truth
**Context of use:** A formal or literary adverbial phrase used to introduce a candid or corrective statement. More elevated in register than η αλήθεια είναι ότι.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** αλήθεια;
**Transliteration:** alíthia?
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) used as an interrogative particle
**Natural English translation:** Really? Truly? Is that so?
**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech as an exclamatory or interrogative particle expressing surprise or requesting confirmation. Equivalent to "Really?" in English.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** στ' αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** st' alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** εις (in, to) + τα (the, pl.) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth, in earnest
**Context of use:** An adverbial phrase used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm a statement, or to ask whether something is genuinely the case. Equivalent to "really" or "truly" in English.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** αλήθεια του Θεού
**Transliteration:** alíthia tu Theú
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + του Θεού (of God, gen.)
**Natural English translation:** God's truth, the absolute truth
**Context of use:** A colloquial intensifying expression used to assert that something is absolutely true, invoking divine authority as a guarantee. Used in everyday speech for strong affirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** αληθινά
**Transliteration:** alithiná
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (genuine, true) + -α (adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely
**Context of use:** The adverbial form of αληθινός. Used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm a statement, or to indicate that something is genuinely the case.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** αληθώς
**Transliteration:** alithós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truly, verily, in truth
**Context of use:** A formal or archaic adverb used in religious, liturgical, and elevated literary contexts. Appears in the Orthodox liturgical response Αληθώς ανέστη ("He is truly risen").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** Αληθώς ανέστη
**Transliteration:** Alithós anésti
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθώς (truly) + ανέστη (he rose, aorist of ανίσταμαι)
**Natural English translation:** He is truly risen
**Context of use:** The liturgical response in the Greek Orthodox Easter service, spoken in reply to Χριστός ανέστη ("Christ is risen"). A fixed religious formula.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** αλήθεια ή τόλμη
**Transliteration:** alíthia í tólmi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + ή (or) + τόλμη (dare, boldness)
**Natural English translation:** truth or dare
**Context of use:** The name of the well-known party game, used in everyday speech especially among younger speakers. A direct calque of the English "truth or dare."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** αλήθεια της στιγμής
**Transliteration:** alíthia tis stigmís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + της στιγμής (of the moment, gen.)
**Natural English translation:** moment of truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in everyday and journalistic registers to describe a decisive or revealing moment. Likely a calque of the English "moment of truth."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** αλήθεια είναι σκληρή
**Transliteration:** alíthia íne sklirí
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + είναι (is) + σκληρή (hard, harsh)
**Natural English translation:** the truth is hard/harsh
**Context of use:** A common proverbial or semi-fixed expression used in everyday speech to acknowledge that a true statement may be difficult to hear or accept.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** αλήθεια πικρή
**Transliteration:** alíthia pikrí
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + πικρή (bitter)
**Natural English translation:** bitter truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in everyday and literary registers to describe a truth that is unpleasant or painful to acknowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** μισή αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** misí alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** μισή (half) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** half-truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in everyday and journalistic registers to describe a statement that is partially true but misleading by omission or framing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** αλήθεια της ζωής
**Transliteration:** alíthia tis zoís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + της ζωής (of life, gen.)
**Natural English translation:** truth of life, life's truth, reality of life
**Context of use:** Used in literary, philosophical, and everyday registers to refer to fundamental or existential truths about human experience.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** αναζήτηση της αλήθειας
**Transliteration:** anazítisi tis alíthias
**Morpheme breakdown:** αναζήτηση (search, quest) + της αλήθειας (of truth, gen.)
**Natural English translation:** the search for truth, the quest for truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, journalistic, and everyday registers to describe the pursuit of truth as an intellectual or moral activity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** αλήθεια και δικαιοσύνη
**Transliteration:** alíthia ke dikeosyni
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + και (and) + δικαιοσύνη (justice)
**Natural English translation:** truth and justice
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in legal, political, and moral discourse to pair truth with justice as co-equal values. Appears in the names of political organizations and in formal rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** αλήθεια και συμφιλίωση
**Transliteration:** alíthia ke simfiliosi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + και (and) + συμφιλίωση (reconciliation)
**Natural English translation:** truth and reconciliation
**Context of use:** Used in political and transitional justice contexts, often in reference to truth and reconciliation commissions. A calque of the international term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** μετα-αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** meta-alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** μετα- (beyond, after) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** post-truth
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, political, and academic registers to describe a contemporary epistemic condition in which objective facts are less influential than emotional appeals. A calque of the English "post-truth."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The αληθ- root is highly productive. Additional forms exist, including αληθοφανώς (alithofanós, "plausibly"), and various philosophical collocations, but the 30 items above represent the most common and well-attested expressions.*

**Transliteration:** psévdos

**Transliteration:** psevdís

**7.**

### ROOT 3: εικ- / εικον- (eik- / eikon-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** εικόνα
**Transliteration:** ikóna
**Morpheme breakdown:** εικ- (likeness, image) + -όνα (noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** image, picture, icon
**Context of use:** The primary noun for "image" or "picture" in Modern Greek. Used across all registers for visual representations, photographs, mental images, and religious icons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions are flagged because the root's contribution appears to shift meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. No interpretation or categorization is offered.

---

**αληθ- root — peripheral extensions:**

- **αληθινά** (alithiná): Functions as an intensifier in everyday speech ("really," "truly") in contexts where truth-value is not the primary concern, e.g., *αληθινά όμορφος* ("really beautiful").
- **αλήθεια;** (alíthia?): Functions as a discourse particle expressing surprise or requesting confirmation, with no direct propositional truth-evaluation function.
- **αλήθεια ή τόλμη** (alíthia í tólmi): Appears in a game context where "truth" functions as a category of social performance rather than epistemic evaluation.
- **αληθοφάνεια** (alithofánia): The root contributes to a concept of *appearing* true rather than *being* true — a shift from truth to the aesthetics of plausibility.
- **αληθώς ανέστη** (alithós anésti): The root functions within a liturgical affirmation where truth-assertion is simultaneously a performative act of faith.
- **αλήθεια πικρή** / **μισή αλήθεια** (alíthia pikrí / misí alíthia): The root appears in collocations where truth is evaluated along affective or quantitative dimensions (bitterness, partiality) rather than binary truth-value.
- **αλήθεια και δικαιοσύνη** (alíthia ke dikeosyni): The root appears in a pairing where truth functions as a moral or political value alongside justice, not solely as an epistemic category.
- **μετα-αλήθεια** (meta-alíthia): The root appears in a compound where it designates a historical or cultural condition rather than a property of individual propositions.

---

**ψευδ- root — peripheral extensions:**

- **ψευδάργυρος** (psevdárgyros): The root contributes to a chemical/mineralogical term (zinc) where "false" describes resemblance to another substance, with no evaluative or epistemic function.
- **ψευδοπόδιο** (psevdopódio): The root contributes to a biological term where "false" describes morphological resemblance rather than deception or falsity of a claim.
- **ψευδαίσθηση** (psevdésthisi): The root appears in a psychological/perceptual term where "false" describes a mismatch between perception and reality, not a deliberate lie or propositional falsity.
- **ψευδοπροφήτης** / **ψευδοχριστός** (psevdoprofítis / psevdohristós): The root appears in religious terms where "false" designates illegitimate spiritual authority rather than propositional falsity.
- **ψευδοδιλήμμα** (psevdodilíma): The root appears in a logical/rhetorical term where "false" characterizes the structure of an argument rather than the truth-value of a proposition.
- **ψευδώνυμο** (psevdónymo): The root appears in a term for an assumed name where "false" describes identity concealment, not necessarily deception in a morally negative sense.

---

**εικ-/εικον- root — peripheral extensions:**

- **εικονική εταιρεία** / **εικονικό τιμολόγιο** (eikonikí etería / eikonikó timológio): The root contributes to legal/financial terms where "image-like" has shifted to mean "fictitious" or "sham," with strong negative evaluative force.
- **εικονοκλάστης** (ikonokláστis): The root appears in a term where the destruction of images functions as a metaphor for the rejection of established authority or belief, extending far beyond visual representation.
- **εικόνα μάρκας** (ikóna márkas): The root appears in a marketing collocation where "image" refers to public perception rather than a visual representation.
- **αυτοεικόνα** (aftoikóna): The root appears in a psychological term where "image" refers to a mental self-conception rather than a visual or pictorial representation.
- **εικόνα του κόσμου** (ikóna tu kósmu): The root appears in a philosophical collocation where "image" refers to a conceptual framework or worldview rather than a visual depiction.
- **εικασία** (ikasía): The root's contribution shifts from "likeness/image" to "conjecture" — the act of forming a mental image of something not directly perceived — marking a transition from visual representation to epistemic inference.
- **εικονοληψία** (ikonolipsía): The root appears in a technical broadcasting term where the primary concern is the mechanical capture of images, with no evaluative or truth-related dimension.
