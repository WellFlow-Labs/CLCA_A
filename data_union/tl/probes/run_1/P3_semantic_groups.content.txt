# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, collocational behavior, register distribution, and contextual deployment observed across P1 and P2 data. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Group boundaries are drawn where the data shows consistent clustering; where items straddle groups or resist grouping, this is noted explicitly.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that something is the case, as opposed to false or imagined

**Label:** Asserting factual correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used to predicate that a statement, situation, or entity corresponds to actual reality rather than being false, mistaken, or hypothetical. They appear in contexts where the primary question is whether something is the case or not — in confirmation-seeking, denial, correction, and disclosure. They are applied to propositions, events, and states of affairs.

**Representative expressions:**
- *totoo* — "Is this true?" (*Totoo ba ito?*); "The news is not true" (*Hindi totoo ang balita*)
- *katotohanan* — "Tell the whole truth" (*Sabihin ang buong katotohanan*); "The allegation has no truth to it" (*Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon*)
- *totoong-totoo* — "This is absolutely real, not a joke" (*Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro*)
- *totoo na* — "It's real now / It came true" (*Totoo na*)
- *totoo o hindi* — "True or not, we need to find out" (*Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman*)
- *ang totoo* — "The truth is, he has no money" (*Ang totoo, wala siyang pera*)
- *ang totoo nito* — "The truth of this matter is more complicated" (*Ang totoo nito ay mas kumplikado*)
- *sa totoo lang* — "To tell the truth, I don't know" (*Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam*)
- *walang katotohanan* — "The allegation has no truth to it" (*Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon*)
- *may katotohanan* — "There is some truth in what he said" (*May katotohanan sa sinabi niya*)
- *naniniwala akong totoo ito* — "I believe this is true"
- *kung totoo man o hindi* — "Whether true or not..."
- *aktuwal* — "What actually happened" (*aktuwal na nangyari*)

**Notes:** *Aktuwal* belongs here primarily through its use in contrasting what is genuinely the case with what is supposed or hypothetical, though it is more restricted in register than *totoo*. *Sa totoo lang* and *ang totoo* function as discourse-framing devices rather than simple predicates, but their function is to introduce a claim of factual correspondence.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to evaluate whether something is genuine rather than an imitation or pretense

**Label:** Evaluating genuineness of an entity or person

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a thing, person, or relationship is what it claims or appears to be — as opposed to being a copy, imitation, or performance. They are applied to objects, persons, relationships, and abstract entities (love, art, freedom), and the contrast they mark is between intrinsic authenticity and surface appearance or substitution. They appear frequently in attributive position modifying nouns.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tunay* — "a true/genuine friend" (*tunay na kaibigan*); "true love" (*tunay na pagmamahal*); "what truly happened" (*tunay na nangyari*)
- *tunay na tunay* — "absolutely genuine" (*Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal*)
- *tunay na mukha* — "true face" (*Ipakita ang tunay na mukha ng gobyerno*)
- *tunay na pagkatao* — "true character" (*Nakilala ko ang tunay na pagkatao niya*)
- *tunay na layunin* — "real intention" (*Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?*)
- *tunay na kahulugan* — "true meaning" (*Ang tunay na kahulugan ng kalayaan*)
- *tunay na Pilipino* — "a true Filipino"
- *tunay na pagbabago* — "genuine change"
- *ito ang tunay na suliranin* — "This is the real problem"
- *awtentiko* — "authentic document" (*awtentikong dokumento*)
- *orihinal* — "original work" (*orihinal na obra*)
- *di-tunay* — "inauthentic, not genuine"

**Notes:** *Tunay* in this group is applied to a very wide range of nouns — persons, emotions, political concepts, artistic works — wherever the question is whether something is the genuine instance of its category. *Awtentiko* and *orihinal* overlap with this group but are more restricted to formal registers and to objects/documents rather than persons or abstract qualities. *Aktuwal* (Group 1) and *tunay* overlap when the contrast is between what is real and what is supposed, but *tunay* more consistently evaluates intrinsic character while *aktuwal* contrasts with the hypothetical.

---

## Group 3: Expressions used in contexts of producing, detecting, or possessing counterfeit or forged items

**Label:** Marking deliberate inauthenticity of objects and documents

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in contexts involving objects, documents, currency, credentials, or goods that have been deliberately made to appear genuine but are not. They carry a connotation of intentional deception and appear in legal, commercial, and crime-reporting contexts. They are applied to physical items and to persons fraudulently presenting false credentials.

**Representative expressions:**
- *huwad* — "counterfeit money" (*huwad na pera*); "forged document" (*huwad na dokumento*)
- *huwad na huwad* — "completely forged" (*Huwad na huwad ang dokumento*)
- *paghuhuwad* — "the forgery of documents is a crime" (*Ang paghuhuwad ng dokumento ay isang krimen*)
- *maghuwad* — "to forge, to counterfeit"
- *naghuwad* — "forged (by an agent)"
- *natuklasang huwad* — "discovered to be fake/forged" (*Natuklasang huwad ang kanyang diploma*)
- *nahuli siyang may hawak na huwad na pera* — "caught holding counterfeit money"
- *peke* — "fake product" (*pekeng produkto*); "fake doctor" (*pekeng doktor*)
- *peke pala* — "it turns out it's fake"
- *mukhang peke* — "looks fake"
- *peke-peke* — "totally fake"
- *pekein* — "to fake (a specific item)"
- *huwad-huwaran* — "imitation, replica" [UNCERTAIN]

**Notes:** *Huwad* and *peke* overlap substantially in this group but differ in register: *huwad* is more formal and legal, *peke* is colloquial and very widely used. Both are applied to objects and persons, but *peke* extends more readily to emotional performances (see Group 4). *Huwad-huwaran* is included here with its [UNCERTAIN] status noted.

---

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate performances, displays, or representations as lacking genuine feeling or sincerity

**Label:** Marking insincerity in social and emotional performance

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person's expressed emotions, social displays, or interpersonal conduct as not reflecting genuine inner states — as performed rather than felt. They appear in interpersonal, psychological, and critical discourse and are applied to smiles, love, crying, intentions, and similar expressions of inner states.

**Representative expressions:**
- *peke ang kanyang ngiti* — "his/her smile is fake"
- *nagpeke ng sakit* — "faked an illness"
- *nagpepeke ng pagmamahal* — "faking love"
- *huwag magpeke ng emosyon* — "don't fake your emotions"
- *pagpepeke* — "the act of faking (emotions)"
- *paimbabaw* — "superficial/insincere love" (*paimbabaw na pagmamahal*); "surface-level, without genuine depth"
- *totoo ang nararamdaman* — "what I feel is real/genuine" (used to assert the opposite of this group's concern)
- *tapat ang kanyang intensyon* — "his/her intentions are sincere" (used to assert the opposite)

**Notes:** *Paimbabaw* belongs primarily here: it evaluates conduct or expression as lacking genuine depth, performing an appearance without the corresponding inner reality. *Peke* in this group overlaps with Group 3 (deliberate inauthenticity of objects) but the application to emotional performance is a distinct and well-attested usage pattern. *Totoo ang nararamdaman* and *tapat ang kanyang intensyon* are included as expressions that are used specifically to counter the concern this group addresses — they assert genuine feeling or sincere intention in contexts where inauthenticity is at issue.

---

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person's speech or conduct is honest and without concealment

**Label:** Evaluating honesty and directness in persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons, their speech, or their conduct as honest, frank, and without concealment — or as lacking these qualities. They appear in moral instruction, interpersonal requests for candor, and evaluations of character. The contrast they mark is between honest disclosure and concealment or deception, rather than between genuine and fake objects.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tapat* — "an honest person" (*tapatang tao*); "speak honestly" (*magsalita nang tapat*)
- *katapatan* — "honesty, sincerity" (*Ang katapatan ay mahalaga*)
- *tapatang-tapat* — "completely honest"
- *tapat na tapat* — "truly honest"
- *maging tapat sa akin* — "be honest with me"
- *tapat sa sarili* — "honest with oneself"
- *magtapat ka na* — "come clean already"
- *tapatan mo na siya* — "be honest with him/her already"
- *nagtapat* — "confessed, came clean"
- *nagtatapat* — "is confessing, is disclosing frankly"
- *pagtapat* — "the act of honest disclosure"
- *tapat ang kanyang loob* — "his/her heart is sincere"
- *katapatan ng loob* — "sincerity of heart"
- *tapat na salita* — "honest words"
- *tapat na puso* — "an honest heart"
- *tapat na ulat* — "honest report"
- *hindi tapat* — "not honest, insincere"
- *walang katapatan* — "faithless, without loyalty/sincerity"
- *sa totoo lang* — "to tell the truth, honestly speaking" (overlaps with Group 1)
- *magsalita nang tapat* — "to speak honestly"

**Notes:** *Sa totoo lang* appears in both Group 1 and here: it frames a statement as factually true (Group 1) and simultaneously signals that the speaker is being candid rather than evasive (this group). The two functions co-occur in the same expression. *Tapat* also extends into loyalty and fidelity (see Group 6), but the honesty-and-directness cluster is the most consistently attested usage pattern in interpersonal and moral discourse.

---

## Group 6: Expressions used in contexts of loyalty, fidelity, and sustained commitment to a person, institution, or cause

**Label:** Marking loyalty and faithful commitment

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons or relationships as characterized by sustained loyalty, fidelity, and dedication — to a spouse, nation, God, employer, or cause. They appear in religious, civic, and relational discourse. The contrast they mark is between genuine ongoing commitment and betrayal, abandonment, or mere nominal affiliation.

**Representative expressions:**
- *katapatan sa Diyos* — "faithfulness to God"
- *katapatan sa bansa* — "loyalty to the nation"
- *katapatan sa asawa* — "fidelity to one's spouse"
- *tapat na lingkod* — "faithful servant"
- *tapat na lingkod ng bayan* — "faithful servant of the nation"
- *tapat na serbisyo* — "faithful service"
- *tapat na paglilingkod* — "faithful service (over time)"
- *tapat na pag-ibig* — "faithful love"
- *tapat na kasamahan* — "loyal companion"
- *sinubok ang kanyang katapatan* — "his/her loyalty was tested"
- *ipinakita niya ang kanyang katapatan* — "he/she demonstrated his/her loyalty"
- *walang katapatan* — "faithless, disloyal"

**Notes:** This group shares the root *tapat* and the derived nominal *katapatan* with Group 5. The distinction between the two groups is not a distinction between two different words but between two different usage contexts for the same words: Group 5 covers honesty and frank disclosure in communication; this group covers sustained relational or institutional loyalty. Both senses are well attested and the P1 data explicitly notes the dual sense of *katapatan*. Many tokens of *katapatan* are unambiguously in one group or the other based on context (*katapatan sa asawa* is clearly loyalty; *tapat na salita* is clearly honesty in speech), but some tokens are genuinely ambiguous.

---

## Group 7: Expressions used to introduce, present, or challenge evidence for a claim

**Label:** Presenting and challenging evidence

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where a claim is being supported by, or challenged for lack of, concrete evidence. They appear in legal proceedings, journalistic investigation, formal argumentation, and everyday dispute. The central function is to mark something as serving an evidentiary role — as proof of a claim — or to assert that such proof is absent.

**Representative expressions:**
- *pruweba* — "Where is the proof?" (*Nasaan ang pruweba?*)
- *ebidensya* — "no evidence" (*walang ebidensya*)
- *katunayan* — "proof, evidence" (*Ang katunayan nito ay makikita sa datos*)
- *patunay* — "show proof of payment" (*Ipakita ang patunay ng pagbabayad*)
- *bilang katunayan* — "as proof, here is the document"
- *walang katunayan* — "the accusation has no proof"
- *may katunayan* — "his/her statement has proof to back it up"
- *ito ang nagsisilbing katunayan* — "this serves as proof"
- *ang katunayan ay nagsasalita para sa sarili nito* — "the evidence speaks for itself"
- *walang ebidensya* — "no evidence"
- *patunayan* — "prove what you are saying" (*Patunayan mo ang iyong sinasabi*)
- *napatunayan* — "has been proven"
- *mapatunayan* — "can be proven"
- *pinatunayan* — "proved (by an agent)"
- *pagpapatunay* — "the act of proving, verification"

**Notes:** *Pruweba* and *ebidensya* are both borrowings and are more domain-specific (legal, journalistic) than the native *katunayan* and *patunay*, which are used across a wider range of registers. *Katunayan* appears in both this group and Group 1 (as a nominal for truth/fact) and Group 2 (as proof of genuineness); the evidentiary function documented here is the most consistently attested usage in formal discourse.

---

## Group 8: Expressions used to evaluate whether a claim, authority, or status meets recognized institutional standards

**Label:** Evaluating institutional or legal validity

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person's claim, authority, status, or action is recognized as valid by relevant institutional, legal, or social standards. They appear in legal, political, and administrative contexts. The contrast they mark is between what is recognized as valid within a system and what is not — rather than between what is factually true or genuinely authentic.

**Representative expressions:**
- *lehitimo* — "legitimate government" (*lehitimong pamahalaán*); "legitimate claim" (*lehitimong paghahabol*)
- *lehitimidad* — "the legitimacy of the government is being questioned" (*Ang lehitimidad ng gobyerno ay kinukuwestiyon*)
- *di-lehitimo* — "illegitimate government" (*Di-lehitimong pamahalaán*)
- *iligitimo* — "illegitimate child" (*iligitimong anak*)
- *itinuturing na lehitimo* — "considered legitimate"
- *di-lehitimong pamamaraan* — "illegitimate method"
- *kredible* — "credible witness" (*kredibleng saksi*)
- *kredibilidad* — "the credibility of the witness is being questioned"
- *di-kredible* — "his/her statement is not credible"
- *mapagkakatiwalaan* — "a reliable source of information" (*mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon*)
- *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* — "a reliable witness"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* — "untrustworthy"

**Notes:** *Kredible* and *mapagkakatiwalaan* overlap with this group and with Group 9 (trust in persons). In legal and journalistic contexts, *kredible* and *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* are used specifically to evaluate whether a witness or source meets the institutional standard for credibility — this is the usage pattern that places them here. In interpersonal contexts, *mapagkakatiwalaan* shades into Group 9. *Lehitimo* is the clearest member of this group, as it is almost exclusively used in institutional and legal evaluation.

---

## Group 9: Expressions used in contexts of placing or withholding personal trust in a person

**Label:** Placing and withholding personal trust

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe the interpersonal attitude of trust or distrust — placing confidence in a person, having that confidence betrayed, or evaluating whether a person merits trust. They appear in personal relationship discourse, moral evaluation, and descriptions of interpersonal experience. The focus is on the subjective attitude of the person who trusts, rather than on institutional recognition.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tiwala* — "no trust" (*walang tiwala*); "trust him/her" (*Magtiwala ka sa kanya*)
- *magtiwala* — "to trust, to have confidence in"
- *nagtitiwala* — "trusts (habitually)"
- *nagtiwala* — "trusted (and was let down)" (*Nagtiwala siya sa maling tao*)
- *pagtitiwala* — "trust must be earned" (*Ang pagtitiwala ay kailangang kitain*)
- *walang tiwala* — "the citizens have no trust in the government"
- *kawalan ng tiwala* — "lack of trust, loss of confidence"
- *pinagkakatiwalaan* — "the one trusted by everyone" (*Siya ang pinagkakatiwalaan ng lahat*)
- *mapagkakatiwalaan* — "trustworthy, reliable"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* — "untrustworthy"
- *hindi ko matanggap na totoo ito* — "I can't accept that this is true" (overlaps: trust in a claim)

**Notes:** *Mapagkakatiwalaan* and *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* appear in both this group and Group 8. The distinction is contextual: when applied to witnesses and institutional sources, the usage pattern aligns with Group 8; when applied to persons in interpersonal relationships, it aligns here. *Nagtiwala siya sa maling tao* ("he/she trusted the wrong person") is a particularly attested construction that belongs clearly to this group.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used to evaluate correctness or accuracy of statements, answers, and procedures

**Label:** Evaluating correctness and accuracy

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, answer, action, or procedure is correct — conforming to fact, to a standard, or to what is required. They appear in educational, procedural, and evaluative contexts. The contrast they mark is between what is correct and what is in error, rather than between what is genuine and what is fake.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tama* — "your answer is correct" (*Tama ang sagot mo*); "you are right" (*Tama ka*)
- *tama na* — "that's enough / stop it"
- *tamang-tama* — "perfectly timed, just right"
- *tama-tama* — "just right, exactly correct"
- *katamaan* — "correctness, rightness"
- *itama* — "correct (something)"
- *itinama* — "corrected"
- *pagtama* — "the act of correcting"
- *iyan ang tama* — "that's the correct one"
- *tama ang desisyon mo* — "your decision is right"
- *kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi* — "if my understanding is correct"
- *tama ba ang aking naiintindihan* — "is my understanding correct?"
- *wastó* — "the proper/correct way" (*wastong paraan*); "proper use of words" (*wastong gamit ng salita*)
- *kawastuhan* — "correctness, propriety"
- *wastong-wasto* — "completely correct"
- *iwasto* — "correct, rectify"
- *pagwawasto* — "correction, rectification"
- *kawastuhan ng impormasyon* — "accuracy of information"

**Notes:** *Tama* and *wastó* overlap substantially in this group but differ in emphasis: *tama* is used more broadly for factual correctness and agreement, while *wastó* more consistently carries a sense of conformity to a standard or proper procedure. Both are used in educational and evaluative contexts. *Tama na* ("enough, stop") is a distinct semantic extension of *tama* toward sufficiency rather than correctness; it is included here because it derives from the same root and the P2 data documents it as a conventionalized construction, but it does not fit the correctness-evaluation function of the rest of the group.

---

## Group 11: Expressions used to describe or condemn acts of deliberate deception

**Label:** Describing deliberate deception and lying

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe or condemn acts in which a person intentionally creates false impressions in another's mind — through lying, misleading, or fraudulent conduct. They appear in moral condemnation, legal description, and evaluative discourse. The focus is on the intentional act of deceiving rather than on the object that is fake or the person who is insincere.

**Representative expressions:**
- *sinungaling* — "you're a liar!" (*Sinungaling ka!*)
- *pagsisinungaling* — "the act of lying"
- *linlang* — "deception, deceit; to deceive" (*nillinlang siya* "he/she was deceived")
- *mapanlinlang* — "deceptive advertisement" (*mapanlinlang na advertisement*)
- *itago ang katotohanan* — "hide the truth" (*Huwag itago ang katotohanan*)
- *paimbabaw* — "superficial, performing an appearance without the corresponding reality" (overlaps with Group 4)

**Notes:** *Paimbabaw* overlaps between this group and Group 4. In Group 4 it evaluates a performance as lacking genuine feeling; here it can describe conduct that is deliberately misleading about one's true character or intentions. The distinction is not always recoverable from the data. *Mapanlinlang* is the clearest member of this group that is not in Group 3 or 4: it evaluates persons, statements, or representations as designed to create false impressions, with a focus on the deceptive intent.

---

## Group 12: Expressions used in aesthetic evaluation of representations as realistic or true to life

**Label:** Evaluating representations as realistic or lifelike

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate narratives, portrayals, performances, or artworks as accurately reflecting reality or lived experience — or as failing to do so. They appear in literary, film, and dramatic criticism. The question they address is not whether something is true but whether it resembles truth closely enough to be convincing.

**Representative expressions:**
- *makatotohanan* — "a realistic/truthful portrayal" (*makatotohanang larawan*)
- *hindi makatotohanan* — "unrealistic, not true-to-life" (*Hindi makatotohanan ang kwento*)
- *makatotohanang-makatotohanan* — "extremely realistic" [MARGINAL]
- *parang totoo* — "as if it were real, seemingly true, lifelike" (*Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak*)
- *mukhang totoo* — "looks real, appears to be true" (*Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala*)
- *totoo sa buhay* — "true to life, realistic" (*Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela*)
- *tunay na sining* — "true art, genuine art" (*Ang tunay na sining ay nagmumula sa puso*)

**Notes:** *Parang totoo* and *mukhang totoo* are included here because their primary function in the data is to evaluate resemblance to reality rather than to assert reality. *Mukhang totoo* also appears in consumer contexts (evaluating whether a product looks genuine), which connects it to Group 3. *Tunay na sining* is included here because the P1b data documents it specifically in aesthetic criticism, but it also belongs to Group 2 (evaluating genuineness of an entity).

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items show usage patterns that place them in multiple groups without a clear primary affiliation, or that resist grouping:

---

**tapat / katapatan**
These items are distributed across Group 5 (honesty in speech and conduct) and Group 6 (loyalty and fidelity). The P1 data explicitly notes the dual sense. Many tokens are unambiguous in context, but the words themselves do not resolve to a single group.

---

**mapagkakatiwalaan / di-mapagkakatiwalaan**
Distributed across Group 8 (institutional credibility) and Group 9 (personal trust). The distinction is contextual rather than lexical.

---

**katunayan**
Appears in Group 1 (asserting factual correspondence, via *sa katunayan* "in fact"), Group 2 (proof of genuineness), and Group 7 (presenting evidence). The nominal form is used across all three contexts.

---

**sa totoo lang**
Appears in Group 1 (introducing a factual claim) and Group 5 (signaling candor and honest disclosure). Both functions are simultaneously present in most tokens.

---

**paimbabaw**
Appears in Group 4 (insincere emotional performance) and Group 11 (deliberate deception). The word evaluates a gap between appearance and reality, which can be framed as either a failure of genuine feeling or as a form of deception depending on context.

---

**mukhang totoo / parang totoo**
Appear in Group 12 (aesthetic realism) and Group 3 (detecting counterfeit objects). The same construction is used in both contexts; the group membership depends on whether the subject is a performance/narrative or a physical object.

---

**tama na**
Documented under Group 10 as a construction derived from *tama*, but its meaning ("enough, stop") does not belong to the correctness-evaluation function of that group. It is noted here as an item that does not fit cleanly into any group defined by the P1/P2 data.

---

**kredible / kredibilidad**
Primarily in Group 8 (institutional validity) but the P1 data notes it is also used to assess whether a claim merits belief, which connects it to Group 1. The institutional-evaluation usage is more consistently attested.

---

**Items that do not fit cleanly into any group:**

- *huwag-huwaran* [UNCERTAIN]: The P1 data marks this as uncertain in status and it is not attested in enough contexts to assign to a group with confidence.
- *di-tunay* [MARGINAL]: The P1 data marks this as marginal; it is a negation of *tunay* (Group 2) but is not attested as a conventionalized expression in its own right.
- *katotohanang-katotohanan* [MARGINAL]: Emphatic nominal form documented as marginal; attested in philosophical and religious registers but not in enough distinct contexts to characterize its grouping independently of *katotohanan*.
- *pagkatotoo*: Documented in P2a as attested in formal and philosophical registers but less frequent than *katotohanan*; its usage pattern is consistent with Group 1 but it is not attested in enough distinct constructions to characterize independently.
