# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the P1 lexical items. These are syntactically-driven, periphrastic, or idiomatic expressions that are not derivational forms (handled in P2a) and not simple collocations already documented as compounds in P1b. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are organized by P1 base form. Register variation is noted where relevant.

The following general observations are supported by the P1/P1b/P2a data and are stated here to avoid repetition in individual entries:

- Yoruba constructions frequently use the complementizer *pé* to introduce propositional complements.
- The verb *sọ* (to say, speak) is highly productive in constructions involving truth and falsehood.
- Negation is expressed by *kò* (verbal negation) or *àì-* (nominal/adverbial negation).
- The focus particle *ni* and the relativizer *tó* appear in cleft and relative constructions.
- Serial verb constructions are a major syntactic pattern in Yoruba and appear in several entries below.

## 1. Base Form: òtítọ́

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó sọ òtítọ́ fún mi
- **Transliteration:** Ó sọ òtítọ́ fún mi
- **English translation:** He/she told me the truth
- **Usage notes:** The most productive verbal construction with *òtítọ́*. The verb *sọ* (to say/speak) takes *òtítọ́* as its object and *fún* introduces the beneficiary/recipient. Appears across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ
- **Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ
- **English translation:** It is the truth that he/she spoke; What he/she spoke is the truth
- **Usage notes:** A cleft construction using the focus particle *ni* to topicalize *òtítọ́*. Used in legal and argumentative contexts to assert emphatically that what was said was true. The focus structure places strong emphasis on the truthfulness of the statement.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **English translation:** There is no truth in his/her words; His/her words contain no truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the existential negative *kò sí* (there is not) with a locative phrase *nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* (in his/her words). Used in legal and everyday contexts to reject the truthfulness of a statement entirely.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́
- **English translation:** Let us speak the truth; Let us tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** A hortative construction using *jẹ́ kí* (let, allow that) with first-person plural *a*. Used in moral exhortation, preaching, and deliberative contexts to call for truthful speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú
- **Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú
- **English translation:** Truth will not die; Truth is indestructible
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction using the prospective negative *kò ní* (will not) with the verb *kú* (to die). Widely attested as a proverb asserting the permanence of truth. Appears in moral discourse and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó jẹ́wọ́ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ó jẹ́wọ́ òtítọ́
- **English translation:** He/she confessed the truth; He/she admitted the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *jẹ́wọ́* (to confess, admit, acknowledge) with *òtítọ́* as object. Used in legal and religious contexts for the act of admitting or confessing what is true, particularly after prior concealment or denial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni
- **Transliteration:** Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni
- **English translation:** It became clear that it is the truth; It turned out to be true
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *farahàn* (to become clear, to appear, to be revealed) with the complementizer *pé* introducing the propositional content. Used in narrative and legal contexts when the truth of a matter is established after investigation or deliberation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ẹ bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí sọ òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ẹ bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí sọ òtítọ́
- **English translation:** Begin to speak the truth; Start telling the truth
- **Usage notes:** An imperative construction using *bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí* (to begin to) as an aspectual auxiliary. Used in interrogation and moral exhortation contexts to demand that a person start being truthful.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́
- **English translation:** He/she is concealing the truth; He/she is hiding the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *pa...mọ́* (to keep/conceal) with *òtítọ́* as the object. The progressive marker *ń* indicates ongoing action. Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe deliberate concealment of true information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn
- **Transliteration:** Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn
- **English translation:** He/she revealed the truth; He/she showed the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *fi...hàn* (to use/take...to show/reveal) with *òtítọ́* as the object of *fi*. Used in narrative and legal contexts for the act of bringing the truth to light.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

- **Base Form:** òtítọ́
- **Example:** Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́
- **English translation:** He/she accepted the truth; He/she received the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *gbà...gbọ́* (to accept/receive...to hear/believe) with *òtítọ́* as object. Used in religious and everyday contexts for the act of accepting a true statement or teaching.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: irọ́

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Ó sọ irọ́ fún mi
- **Transliteration:** Ó sọ irọ́ fún mi
- **English translation:** He/she told me a lie; He/she lied to me
- **Usage notes:** The primary verbal construction with *irọ́*, parallel to *sọ òtítọ́ fún*. Highly productive across all registers. Used in moral condemnation and legal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Irọ́ ni ó sọ
- **Transliteration:** Irọ́ ni ó sọ
- **English translation:** It is a lie that he/she spoke; What he/she said is a lie
- **Usage notes:** A cleft construction using the focus particle *ni* to topicalize *irọ́*, parallel to *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ*. Used in legal and argumentative contexts to assert emphatically that what was said was false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **English translation:** There is no lie in his/her words; His/her words contain no falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Parallel negative existential construction to *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*, used to assert the truthfulness of a statement by denying the presence of falsehood.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Ó ń pa irọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ó ń pa irọ́
- **English translation:** He/she is telling lies; He/she is lying
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *pa* (to kill, but idiomatically in this construction: to tell/spin) with *irọ́* as object. The progressive marker *ń* indicates ongoing action. This construction (*pa irọ́*) is a common alternative to *sọ irọ́* and appears frequently in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Irọ́ ò gbọdọ̀ jẹ́ ọ̀nà rẹ
- **Transliteration:** Irọ́ ò gbọdọ̀ jẹ́ ọ̀nà rẹ
- **English translation:** Lying must not be his/her way; Falsehood must not be his/her path
- **Usage notes:** A normative construction using the prohibitive *ò gbọdọ̀* (must not) with the copula *jẹ́* (to be). Used in moral instruction and preaching to prohibit habitual deception.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó
- **Transliteration:** Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó
- **English translation:** He/she used lies to obtain money; He/she obtained money through deception
- **Usage notes:** Uses the instrumental serial verb construction *fi...gbà* (to use...to obtain/receive) with *irọ́* as the instrument. Used in legal and moral contexts to describe fraud or deception for material gain.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Irọ́ ló sọ, kì í ṣe òtítọ́
- **Transliteration:** Irọ́ ló sọ, kì í ṣe òtítọ́
- **English translation:** It is a lie he/she spoke, it is not the truth
- **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction using the focus marker *ló* (emphatic form of *ni* + *ó*) and the negative copula *kì í ṣe* (it is not). Used in legal and argumentative contexts to explicitly contrast falsehood with truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** Ó ń gbìyànjú láti bo irọ́ rẹ̀ pamọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ó ń gbìyànjú láti bo irọ́ rẹ̀ pamọ́
- **English translation:** He/she is trying to conceal his/her lie; He/she is attempting to hide his/her falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Uses *gbìyànjú láti* (to try/attempt to) with the serial verb *bo...pamọ́* (to cover/conceal). Used in legal and narrative contexts to describe deliberate concealment of a deception.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

- **Base Form:** irọ́
- **Example:** A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni
- **Transliteration:** A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni
- **English translation:** We know that it is a lie; It is known to be a lie
- **Usage notes:** Uses *mọ̀ pé* (to know that) with the copular focus construction *irọ́ ni* (it is a lie). Used in legal and everyday contexts to assert established knowledge of a falsehood.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: òdodo

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** òdodo
- **Example:** Ó ṣe ohun tó tọ́ àti òdodo
- **Transliteration:** Ó ṣe ohun tó tọ́ àti òdodo
- **English translation:** He/she did what is right and just; He/she acted with correctness and righteousness
- **Usage notes:** A coordinative construction pairing *tọ́* (correct, right) and *òdodo* (justice, righteousness) through *àti* (and). Used in moral and legal evaluation to describe conduct that meets both factual and moral standards.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** òdodo
- **Example:** Kò sí òdodo nínú ìdájọ́ náà
- **Transliteration:** Kò sí òdodo nínú ìdájọ́ náà
- **English translation:** There is no justice in that judgment; That ruling lacks fairness
- **Usage notes:** Negative existential construction parallel to those documented for *òtítọ́* and *irọ́*. Used in legal and moral discourse to condemn a judgment as unjust.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** òdodo
- **Example:** Ẹ ṣe àdájọ́ pẹ̀lú òdodo
- **Transliteration:** Ẹ ṣe àdájọ́ pẹ̀lú òdodo
- **English translation:** Judge with justice; Render judgment with righteousness
- **Usage notes:** An imperative construction using *pẹ̀lú* (with) as a comitative/instrumental marker. Used in legal and religious exhortation to demand just adjudication.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** òdodo
- **Example:** Òdodo yóò ṣẹ
- **Transliteration:** Òdodo yóò ṣẹ
- **English translation:** Justice will prevail; Righteousness will be fulfilled
- **Usage notes:** Uses the prospective marker *yóò* (will) with the verb *ṣẹ* (to be fulfilled, to come to pass). Used in religious and moral discourse to assert the ultimate triumph of justice. Widely attested in preaching and proverbial speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

- **Base Form:** òdodo
- **Example:** Ó ń wá òdodo
- **Transliteration:** Ó ń wá òdodo
- **English translation:** He/she is seeking justice; He/she is looking for righteousness
- **Usage notes:** Uses the progressive construction *ń wá* (is seeking/looking for) with *òdodo* as object. Used in legal and religious contexts to describe the pursuit of justice or righteous conduct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 4. Base Form: ìmọ̀

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ó ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Ó ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀
- **English translation:** He/she has knowledge of it; He/she is aware of it
- **Usage notes:** Uses the possessive/existential verb *ní* (to have) with *ìmọ̀* as object and *nípa* (about, concerning) introducing the domain of knowledge. The standard construction for asserting that a person has relevant knowledge.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Kò ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Kò ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀
- **English translation:** He/she has no knowledge of it; He/she is unaware of it
- **Usage notes:** The negated form of Construction 1. Used in legal and everyday contexts to assert that a person lacks relevant knowledge, which may affect their credibility or culpability.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ nípa ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kò tó
- **Transliteration:** Ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ nípa ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kò tó
- **English translation:** His/her knowledge of this matter is insufficient; He/she does not know enough about this matter
- **Usage notes:** Uses the evaluative predicate *kò tó* (is not sufficient, does not reach) to assess the adequacy of a person's knowledge. Used in legal and academic contexts to evaluate whether a witness or expert has sufficient knowledge to testify reliably.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ó fi ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ hàn
- **Transliteration:** Ó fi ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ hàn
- **English translation:** He/she demonstrated his/her knowledge; He/she showed what he/she knows
- **Usage notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *fi...hàn* (to use/take...to show/demonstrate). Used in academic and legal contexts to describe the act of demonstrating one's knowledge or expertise.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ìmọ̀ ni agbára
- **Transliteration:** Ìmọ̀ ni agbára
- **English translation:** Knowledge is power
- **Usage notes:** A copular construction using the focus particle *ni*. Widely attested as a proverbial or aphoristic statement in Yoruba educational and motivational discourse. Appears in school contexts and public speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ó wà ní ìmọ̀ pé...
- **Transliteration:** Ó wà ní ìmọ̀ pé...
- **English translation:** He/she is aware that...; He/she has knowledge that...
- **Usage notes:** Uses the existential/locative construction *wà ní* (to be in/at) with *ìmọ̀* and the complementizer *pé* to introduce a propositional complement. Used in legal and formal contexts to assert that a person has specific propositional knowledge.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

- **Base Form:** ìmọ̀
- **Example:** Ó gbà ìmọ̀ lọ́wọ́ olùkọ́ rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Ó gbà ìmọ̀ lọ́wọ́ olùkọ́ rẹ̀
- **English translation:** He/she received knowledge from his/her teacher; He/she acquired knowledge from his/her teacher
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *gbà* (to receive, accept) with the source marker *lọ́wọ́* (from the hand of). Used in educational contexts to describe the transmission of knowledge from teacher to student.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: gbàgbọ́

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni
- **Transliteration:** Ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni
- **English translation:** He/she believes that it is the truth; He/she believes it to be true
- **Usage notes:** The standard propositional belief construction using *gbàgbọ́ pé* (to believe that) with a complement clause. Used across all registers to express propositional belief.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Kò gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Kò gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
- **English translation:** He/she does not believe his/her words; He/she disbelieves his/her account
- **Usage notes:** The negated form of *gbàgbọ́* with a nominal object. Used in legal and everyday contexts to express rejection of a claim or testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ẹ gbàgbọ́ mi
- **Transliteration:** Ẹ gbàgbọ́ mi
- **English translation:** Believe me; Trust me
- **Usage notes:** An imperative construction with a personal pronoun object. Used in everyday speech and in legal contexts when a speaker appeals for credibility. The plural/polite imperative *ẹ* is used here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ó ṣòro láti gbàgbọ́ pé...
- **Transliteration:** Ó ṣòro láti gbàgbọ́ pé...
- **English translation:** It is difficult to believe that...; It is hard to accept that...
- **Usage notes:** Uses the evaluative predicate *ṣòro* (to be difficult) with the infinitival *láti* and *gbàgbọ́ pé* introducing the propositional complement. Used in everyday and legal speech to express epistemic resistance to a claim.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Mo gbàgbọ́ nínú rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Mo gbàgbọ́ nínú rẹ̀
- **English translation:** I believe in him/her/it; I have faith in him/her/it
- **Usage notes:** Uses the locative *nínú* (in, inside) to express belief directed toward a person or entity. Standard in religious contexts for expressing faith in God or a deity, and in everyday speech for expressing trust in a person.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

- **Base Form:** gbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ó gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ láìbéèrè
- **Transliteration:** Ó gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ láìbéèrè
- **English translation:** He/she believed his/her words without questioning; He/she accepted his/her account uncritically
- **Usage notes:** Uses the adverbial *láìbéèrè* (without asking/questioning) to characterize the manner of belief as uncritical. Used in evaluative contexts, sometimes critically, to describe credulity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 6. Base Form: ìgbàgbọ́

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀
- **English translation:** His/her belief has no foundation; His/her faith lacks a basis
- **Usage notes:** Uses the negative possessive *kò ní* (does not have) with *ìpìlẹ̀* (foundation, basis). Used in philosophical and religious discourse to evaluate a belief as lacking adequate grounding.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára
- **Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára
- **English translation:** His/her faith is strong; His/her belief is firm
- **Usage notes:** Uses the predicate *lágbára* (to be strong, powerful) to evaluate the strength of a belief or faith. Used in religious and everyday contexts to commend strong conviction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ó dúró ṣinṣin nínú ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Ó dúró ṣinṣin nínú ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀
- **English translation:** He/she stood firm in his/her faith; He/she remained steadfast in his/her belief
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *dúró* (to stand, remain) with the adverb *ṣinṣin* (firmly, steadfastly) and the locative *nínú* (in). Used in religious and moral discourse to commend steadfastness in belief under pressure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
- **Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà
- **Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà
- **English translation:** His/her belief changed; His/her faith shifted
- **Usage notes:** Uses the serial verb *yí padà* (to turn around, to change) with *ìgbàgbọ́* as subject. Used in religious and philosophical discourse to describe a change of belief or conversion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 7. Base Form: ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé

---

**Construction 1**

- **Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
- **Example:** Ó tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Ó tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀
- **English translation:** It is right/proper to rely on him/her; He/she is trustworthy
- **Usage notes:** Uses the evaluative predicate *ó tọ́* (it is correct/proper) with the infinitival *láti* and the verbal form *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé*. Used in everyday and legal contexts to evaluate a person as deserving of trust.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

- **Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
- **Example:** Kò tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀
- **Transliteration:** Kò tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀
- **English translation:** It is not right/proper to rely on him/her; He/she is not trustworthy
- **Usage notes:** The negated form of Construction 1. Used to evaluate a person as untrustworthy or unreliable.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

- **Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
- **Example:** Ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ lórí rẹ̀ kò tọ́
- **Transliteration:** Ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ lórí rẹ̀ kò tọ́
- **English translation:** His/her reliance on him/her is not proper; Trusting him/her is not right
- **Usage notes:** A nominal construction using *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* as subject with the locative *lórí* (on, upon) and the evaluative predicate *kò tọ́*. Used in advisory and legal contexts to counsel against misplaced trust.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

- **Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
