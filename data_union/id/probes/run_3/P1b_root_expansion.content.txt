# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **benar**

**Root form:** benar (Latin script; no native script distinct from romanization)
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**P1 participation:** 2 items directly (*benar*, *membenarkan* mentioned in derivation); the root underlies *kebenaran*, *sebenarnya*, *membenarkan*, and is the primary positive pole of the truth-evaluation domain.
**Core meaning contribution:** Correspondence of a statement, fact, or action to reality or to a correct standard. The root anchors the affirmative side of the truth/correctness axis and is highly productive morphologically across formal, legal, moral, and everyday registers.

---

### Root 2: **salah**

**Root form:** salah (Latin script)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**P1 participation:** 2 items directly (*salah*, *kesalahan*, *menyalahkan* mentioned in derivation); also implicitly present in compounds such as *salah paham*, *salah kaprah*, etc.
**Core meaning contribution:** Deviation from correctness, fact, or proper conduct — whether through error, misjudgment, or moral fault. The root anchors the negative pole of the correctness axis and is highly productive in compounds expressing specific types of error or misattribution.

---

### Root 3: **asli / palsu** (treated as a paired root cluster)

> **Note on selection:** *asli* and *palsu* form a tightly coupled antonymic pair that together anchor the authenticity/inauthenticity axis in the P1 data. Each is individually productive. Because the protocol asks for 1–3 roots and these two function as a single conceptual axis with complementary derivational families, they are treated here as a paired cluster occupying the third root slot. Each is expanded separately below.

**Root form (a):** asli
**Root form (b):** palsu
**P1 participation:** 3 items directly (*asli*, *palsu*, *tiruan* contrasted with *asli*); derivational mentions include *memalsukan*, *pemalsuan*.
**Core meaning contribution:** The axis of genuine-vs.-counterfeit, original-vs.-fabricated, used across legal, commercial, cultural, and identity contexts.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: **benar**

---

**1.**
**Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *ke-* + *benar* + *-an* → nominalization of *benar*
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, rightness
**Context of use:** Used across philosophical, religious, journalistic, and everyday contexts to refer to the property or state of being true or correct. *Mencari kebenaran* (seeking the truth) is a common collocation in both legal and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *mem-* + *benar* + *-kan* → causative/confirmatory verb
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to justify, to validate, to correct (someone)
**Context of use:** Used to mean either (a) to confirm that something is true or correct, or (b) to justify or excuse an action. *Ia membenarkan laporan itu* (He confirmed the report). Also used in moral contexts: *tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu* (nothing can justify that action).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *se-* + *benar* + *-nya* → "in its trueness," adverbial
**Natural English translation:** actually, in truth, in fact, to be honest
**Context of use:** A high-frequency discourse marker used to introduce a correction, clarification, or admission of what is actually the case, often contrasting with a prior assumption or appearance. *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju* (Actually, I disagree).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**4.**
**Form:** benar-benar
**Transliteration:** N/A

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Context of use:** Used in legal, moral, and psychological contexts to refer to the act or process of justifying or confirming something. *Pembenaran diri* (self-justification) is a common collocation in psychological and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in uses where the core truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other semantic territory. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From ROOT 1 (benar):**

- **benar-benar** functions as a general intensifier (*benar-benar lelah*, truly exhausted) in contexts entirely unrelated to truth or correctness, where it marks degree or emphasis rather than epistemic evaluation.

- **membenarkan** in some contexts means "to correct someone's posture or position" (*membenarkan letak buku*, to straighten the position of the book), where the sense of physical adjustment rather than epistemic confirmation is primary.

- **sebenarnya** functions as a discourse softener or hedge in conversational Indonesian (*sebenarnya, saya mau tanya...*, actually, I wanted to ask...), where it marks pragmatic tentativeness rather than a correction of false belief.

- **kebenaran** appears in aesthetic and literary contexts (*kebenaran sebuah karya seni*, the truth of a work of art) where it evaluates artistic authenticity or expressive fidelity rather than factual correspondence.

- **pembenaran** in psychological discourse (*pembenaran diri*, self-justification) describes a cognitive or emotional process rather than an epistemic act of confirmation.

---

**From ROOT 2 (salah):**

- **salah satu** and **salah seorang** function as partitive selectors (one of, one among) with no connection to the error or wrongness meaning of *salah*. This is a grammaticalized use in which the original semantic content of the root is not recoverable from the compound's meaning.

- **salah langkah** in strategic and political contexts evaluates a decision's consequences rather than its factual correctness, shifting toward outcome-based judgment.

- **penyalahgunaan** and **menyalahgunakan** in contexts of drug use (*penyalahgunaan narkoba*, drug abuse) involve a moral and legal evaluation of improper use rather than a judgment about truth or factual error.

- **tidak ada salahnya** functions as a pragmatic permission-granting expression (*tidak ada salahnya mencoba*, there is no harm in trying) where *salah* marks moral permissibility rather than factual incorrectness.

- **kesalahan** in theological and moral discourse can approach the meaning of sin or moral transgression (*kesalahan manusia di hadapan Tuhan*, human wrongdoing before God), extending beyond epistemic error into moral-religious evaluation.

---

**From ROOT 3a (asli):**

- **asli** in colloquial Indonesian functions as an intensifier or authenticity marker for personal character (*dia asli baik*, he is genuinely good) where it evaluates sincerity of character rather than documentary or material authenticity.

- **orang asli** and **penduduk asli** in ethnographic and political contexts involve claims about identity, belonging, and territorial rights rather than about the truth or falsity of propositions.

- **asli daerah** in commercial contexts (*produk asli daerah*) participates in regional economic and cultural identity discourse, where authenticity is tied to geographic origin rather than to epistemic or documentary verification.

- **keaslian** in literary and artistic criticism (*keaslian sebuah karya*, the originality of a work) evaluates creative originality and non-derivativeness rather than the absence of forgery.

---

**From ROOT 3b (palsu):**

- **senyum palsu** and **janji palsu** apply *palsu* to emotional expressions and speech acts, where the falseness evaluated is insincerity of feeling or intention rather than documentary or material forgery.

- **kepalsuan** in moral and literary discourse (*kepalsuan hidup modern*, the falseness of modern life) extends the root into broad evaluations of inauthenticity in social existence, well beyond the legal or commercial domain of counterfeiting.

- **palsu** in colloquial speech can describe affected or pretentious behavior (*gayanya palsu*, his manner is fake/affected), where it evaluates social performance rather than material or documentary authenticity.
