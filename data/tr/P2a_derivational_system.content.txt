# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a DERIVATIONAL SYSTEM
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS IDENTIFIED

Based on the P1 vocabulary and P1b root expansion data, the following derivational patterns are attested in Turkish truth/epistemic vocabulary:

---

## PATTERN 1: Abstract Noun Formation with *-lık/-lik/-luk/-lük*

This suffix creates abstract nouns denoting quality, state, or condition. Highly productive across the truth vocabulary domain.

### 1.1 doğru → doğruluk
**Base:** doğru (true, correct)
**Derived Form:** doğruluk
**Transliteration:** doğruluk
**Literal Breakdown:** *doğru* + *-luk* (abstract noun suffix)
**English translation:** truth, truthfulness, correctness, honesty
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the quality of being true or honest. Used in both epistemic contexts (truth of statements) and moral contexts (honesty of character). Example: "doğruluk ve dürüstlük" (truth and honesty).
**Status:** [CORE]

### 1.2 gerçek → gerçeklik
**Base:** gerçek (real, true)
**Derived Form:** gerçeklik
**Transliteration:** gerçeklik
**Literal Breakdown:** *gerçek* + *-lik* (abstract noun suffix)
**English translation:** reality, realness, actuality
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the quality or state of being real. Common in philosophical discourse and everyday speech about perception of reality.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 1.3 haklı → haklılık
**Base:** haklı (right, justified)
**Derived Form:** haklılık
**Transliteration:** haklılık
**Literal Breakdown:** *haklı* + *-lık* (abstract noun suffix)
**English translation:** rightness, justification, being in the right
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the state of being right or justified. Less common than its opposite *haksızlık* but used in formal arguments and legal contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 1.4 haksız → haksızlık
**Base:** haksız (wrong, unjust)
**Derived Form:** haksızlık
**Transliteration:** haksızlık
**Literal Breakdown:** *haksız* + *-lık* (abstract noun suffix)
**English translation:** injustice, wrong, unfairness
**Usage:** Abstract noun for injustice or unfair treatment. Very common in moral and legal discourse. Example: "haksızlığa uğramak" (to suffer injustice).
**Status:** [CORE]

### 1.5 gerçekçi → gerçekçilik
**Base:** gerçekçi (realistic)
**Derived Form:** gerçekçilik
**Transliteration:** gerçekçilik
**Literal Breakdown:** *gerçekçi* + *-lik* (abstract noun suffix)
**English translation:** realism (artistic/philosophical movement), realistic approach
**Usage:** Term for the artistic/literary movement of realism, or for a realistic attitude or approach. Example: "edebiyatta gerçekçilik" (realism in literature).
**Status:** [CORE]

## PATTERN 2: Adjectival Derivation with *-lı/-li/-lu/-lü* (Possessive/Having)

This suffix creates adjectives indicating possession or characteristic quality. Very productive in Turkish.

### 2.1 hak → haklı
**Base:** hak (right, truth, justice)
**Derived Form:** haklı
**Transliteration:** haklı
**Literal Breakdown:** *hak* + *-lı* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** right, justified, in the right
**Usage:** Evaluates a person's position or claim as justified or correct. Extremely common in disputes and arguments. Example: "Sen haklısın" (You are right).
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.2 şüphe → şüpheli
**Base:** şüphe (doubt, suspicion, from Arabic *šubha*)
**Derived Form:** şüpheli
**Transliteration:** şüpheli
**Literal Breakdown:** *şüphe* + *-li* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** doubtful, suspicious, dubious, suspect
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, statements, or situations as warranting doubt or suspicion. Can function as noun meaning "suspect" in legal contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.3 kuşku → kuşkulu
**Base:** kuşku (doubt)
**Derived Form:** kuşkulu
**Transliteration:** kuşkulu
**Literal Breakdown:** *kuşku* + *-lu* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** doubtful, dubious, questionable
**Usage:** Similar to *şüpheli* but slightly less formal. Indicates uncertainty or dubiousness. More native Turkish than Arabic-derived *şüpheli*.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.4 isabet → isabetli
**Base:** isabet (hitting the mark, accuracy, from Arabic *iṣāba*)
**Derived Form:** isabetli
**Transliteration:** isabetli
**Literal Breakdown:** *isabet* + *-li* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** accurate, apt, appropriate, on-target
**Usage:** Describes statements, decisions, or actions that hit the mark or are appropriate. Often used for evaluating judgments or predictions.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.5 tutarlı
**Base:** *tut-* (to hold) + aorist *-ar*
**Derived Form:** tutarlı
**Transliteration:** tutarlı
**Literal Breakdown:** *tut-* + *-ar* + *-lı* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** consistent, coherent
**Usage:** Evaluates statements, behavior, or systems as internally consistent. Important in logical and moral evaluation.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.6 çelişki → çelişkili
**Base:** çelişki (contradiction)
**Derived Form:** çelişkili
**Transliteration:** çelişkili
**Literal Breakdown:** *çelişki* + *-li* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** contradictory, inconsistent
**Usage:** Evaluates statements or positions as containing internal contradictions. Used in logical and argumentative contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2.7 adalet → adaletli
**Base:** adalet (justice, from Arabic *ʿadāla*)
**Derived Form:** adaletli
**Transliteration:** adaletli
**Literal Breakdown:** *adalet* + *-li* (possessive suffix)
**English translation:** just, fair, characterized by justice
**Usage:** Evaluates systems, persons, or actions as embodying justice. Slightly more formal than *adil*.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 3.2 kusur → kusursuz

**Transliteration:** kusursuz
**Literal Breakdown:** *kusur* + *-suz* (privative suffix)
**English translation:** flawless, perfect, faultless
**Usage:** Evaluates objects, performances, or conduct as without defect or fault. Used for both technical perfection and moral blamelessness.

### 3.3 katık → katıksız
**Base:** katık (additive, mixture)
**Derived Form:** katıksız
**Transliteration:** katıksız
**Literal Breakdown:** *katık* + *-sız* (privative suffix)

**Usage:** Evaluates substances or abstract qualities as pure and unmixed. Used literally for substances and figuratively for truth or emotions.
**Status:** [CORE]

## PATTERN 5: Agentive/Causative Suffix *-ıcı/-ici/-ucu/-ücü*

This suffix creates adjectives or nouns indicating agent or causal property, often with sense of "causing" or "tending to."

### 5.1 yanılt- → yanıltıcı
**Base:** yanılt- (to mislead, causative of *yan-*)
**Derived Form:** yanıltıcı
**Transliteration:** yanıltıcı
**Literal Breakdown:** *yanılt-* + *-ıcı* (agentive suffix)
**English translation:** misleading, deceptive, delusive
**Usage:** Evaluates statements, appearances, or representations as tending to mislead or deceive. Common in critical analysis.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 5.2 aldat- → aldatıcı
**Base:** aldat- (to deceive)
**Derived Form:** aldatıcı
**Transliteration:** aldatıcı
**Literal Breakdown:** *aldat-* + *-ıcı* (agentive suffix)
**English translation:** deceptive, deceitful, misleading
**Usage:** Evaluates appearances, persons, or representations as deceptive. Stronger implication of intentional deception than *yanıltıcı*.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 5.3 inan- → inandırıcı
**Base:** inan- (to believe) + causative *-dır-*
**Derived Form:** inandırıcı
**Transliteration:** inandırıcı
**Literal Breakdown:** *inan-* + *-dır-* (causative) + *-ıcı* (agentive)
**English translation:** convincing, credible, believable, plausible
**Usage:** Describes arguments, evidence, or performances that are convincing or credible. Used for evaluating persuasiveness and believability.
**Status:** [CORE]

## PATTERN 6: Passive Potential Suffix *-ilir/-ılır/-ulur/-ülür*

This suffix creates adjectives indicating passive possibility or capability.

**Base:** güven (trust)
**Derived Form:** güvenilir
**Transliteration:** güvenilir
**Literal Breakdown:** *güven* + *-il-* (passive) + *-ir* (aorist/potential)
**English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable
**Usage:** Describes persons, sources, or information that can be trusted or relied upon. Commonly used for evaluating credibility of sources, witnesses, or data.
**Status:** [CORE]

### 6.2 geç- → geçerli
**Base:** geç- (to pass, be valid)
**Derived Form:** geçerli
**Transliteration:** geçerli
**Literal Breakdown:** *geç-* + *-er-* (aorist) + *-li* (possessive, but here functioning as potential)
**English translation:** valid, current, applicable, in effect
**Usage:** Indicates validity, currency, or applicability. Used for documents, arguments, rules, or tickets that are currently valid or applicable.
**Status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** gerçekleştirme
**Literal Breakdown:** *gerçekleştir-* + *-me* (verbal noun)
**English translation:** realization, implementation, accomplishment

**Status:** [CORE]

## PATTERN 9: Inchoative Verb Formation with *-leş/-laş*

This suffix creates verbs meaning "to become X" from nouns or adjectives.

### 9.1 gerçek → gerçekleşmek
**Base:** gerçek (real)
**Derived Form:** gerçekleşmek
**Transliteration:** gerçekleşmek
**Literal Breakdown:** *gerçek* + *-leş-* (inchoative) + *-mek* (infinitive)
**English translation:** to come true, to be realized, to materialize
**Usage:** Used for events, plans, or predictions that become reality. Example: "Hayalleri gerçekleşti" (His/her dreams came true).
**Status:** [CORE]

### 10.2 doğru → doğrultmak

**Derived Form:** doğrultmak

## NOTES ON PRODUCTIVITY AND GAPS

### High Productivity Patterns:
1. **Abstract noun formation with *-lık/-lik*** is extremely productive across the truth vocabulary domain
2. **Possessive adjective formation with *-lı/-li*** is highly productive for creating "having X" adjectives
3. **Privative formation with *-sız/-siz*** is productive for creating antonyms
4. **Light verb constructions** are extremely productive, particularly with *etmek*, *olmak*, *vermek*, *almak*, *çıkmak*

### Limited Productivity Patterns:
1. **Agentive *-çi/-ci*** appears limited in this domain (only *gerçekçi* attested)
2. **Inchoative *-leş/-laş*** appears primarily with *gerçek* → *gerçekleşmek*
3. **Directional noun *-ltu/-lti*** appears only with *doğru* → *doğrultu*

### Notable Gaps:
1. No attested derivations from *yanlış* (wrong) beyond the base form
2. No attested derivations from *sahte* (fake) beyond the base form
3. No attested derivations from *yalan* (lie) beyond the base form
4. Limited derivational morphology for Arabic loanwords beyond what is already present in the base forms

### Asymmetries:
1. *doğru* has extensive derivational family (doğruluk, doğrulamak, doğrulama, doğrudan, doğrultmak, doğrultu, doğruca, etc.)
2. *gerçek* has extensive derivational family focused on realization/actualization (gerçeklik, gerçekleşmek, gerçekleştirmek, gerçekçi, gerçekçilik, etc.)
3. *hak* has extensive light verb constructions but limited morphological derivations beyond *haklı/haksız*
4. Many truth-related terms show no productive derivational morphology, functioning primarily in fixed collocations
