# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Lexical Items

1. **אמת**
   - Transliteration: emet
   - Literal meaning: truth, firmness, stability (from root א-מ-נ)
   - Typical English translations: truth, true, truthfulness
   - Usage: The primary term for truth in Hebrew, used across all registers from biblical to modern contexts. Appears in moral, legal, philosophical, and everyday discourse to denote correspondence with reality or factual accuracy.
   - [CORE]

2. **שקר**
   - Transliteration: sheker
   - Literal meaning: falsehood, deception (from root ש-ק-ר)
   - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, deception, false
   - Usage: The standard antonym of emet, denoting falsehood or deception. Used in moral condemnation of dishonesty and in factual contexts to indicate something is not true.
   - [CORE]

3. **נכון**
   - Transliteration: nakhon
   - Literal meaning: correct, established, ready (from root כ-ו-נ)
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, true, accurate
   - Usage: Common in modern Hebrew for correctness or accuracy, particularly in factual or procedural contexts. Often used to confirm statements ("that's right") or indicate something is properly done.
   - [CORE]

4. **אמיתי**
   - Transliteration: amiti
   - Literal meaning: true, genuine (adjective derived from emet)
   - Typical English translations: true, real, genuine, authentic
   - Usage: Adjectival form emphasizing genuineness or authenticity of objects, persons, or statements. Distinguishes the real from the fake or superficial.
   - [CORE]

5. **אמין**
   - Transliteration: amin
   - Literal meaning: reliable, trustworthy (from root א-מ-נ)
   - Typical English translations: reliable, trustworthy, credible, faithful
   - Usage: Describes persons, sources, or information that can be trusted or relied upon. Emphasizes reliability and trustworthiness rather than factual truth alone.
   - [CORE]

6. **מדויק**
   - Transliteration: meduyak
   - Literal meaning: precise, exact (passive participle from root ד-י-ק)
   - Typical English translations: precise, accurate, exact
   - Usage: Indicates precision and exactness, particularly in technical, scientific, or detailed contexts. Emphasizes correspondence to fine detail rather than general truth.
   - [CORE]

7. **נאמן**
   - Transliteration: ne'eman
   - Literal meaning: faithful, loyal, reliable (from root א-מ-נ)
   - Typical English translations: faithful, loyal, reliable, trustworthy
   - Usage: Describes persons or things that are dependable and faithful. Used for both personal loyalty and reliability of information or testimony.
   - [CORE]

8. **כנה**
   - Transliteration: kene
   - Literal meaning: honest, sincere (from root כ-נ-ה)
   - Typical English translations: honest, sincere, candid
   - Usage: Describes honesty and sincerity in speech or conduct. Emphasizes the speaker's genuine intention and lack of deception.
   - [CORE]

9. **צודק**
   - Transliteration: tsodek
   - Literal meaning: right, just, correct (from root צ-ד-ק)
   - Typical English translations: right, correct, just
   - Usage: Indicates moral or factual rightness, often with connotations of justice. Used to affirm someone's position in an argument or dispute.
   - [CORE]

10. **יושר**
    - Transliteration: yosher
    - Literal meaning: straightness, uprightness, honesty (from root י-ש-ר)
    - Typical English translations: honesty, integrity, uprightness, fairness
    - Usage: Denotes moral uprightness and honesty in conduct. Often used in ethical and business contexts to describe fair dealing.
    - [CORE]

11. **מציאותי**
    - Transliteration: metsi'uti
    - Literal meaning: real, realistic (from metsi'ut "reality")
    - Typical English translations: real, realistic, actual
    - Usage: Describes what is real or corresponds to reality, as opposed to imaginary or theoretical. Also used for realistic assessments or expectations.
    - [CORE]

12. **אותנטי**
    - Transliteration: otenti
    - Literal meaning: authentic (loanword from European languages)
    - Typical English translations: authentic, genuine
    - Usage: Modern term for authenticity, particularly of objects, artworks, or cultural expressions. Emphasizes originality and genuineness.
    - [CORE]

13. **מהימן**
    - Transliteration: meheyman
    - Literal meaning: reliable, credible (from root א-מ-נ)
    - Typical English translations: reliable, credible, trustworthy
    - Usage: Formal or literary term for reliability and credibility, particularly of sources, testimony, or information. Somewhat more formal than amin.
    - [CORE]

14. **כזב**
    - Transliteration: kazav
    - Literal meaning: lie, falsehood (from root כ-ז-ב)
    - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, deception
    - Usage: Biblical and literary term for falsehood or lying. Less common in modern colloquial Hebrew than sheker but appears in formal or elevated registers.
    - [MARGINAL]

15. **תקף**
    - Transliteration: takef
    - Literal meaning: valid, in force (from root ת-ק-ף)
    - Typical English translations: valid, in effect, sound
    - Usage: Used primarily in legal and logical contexts to indicate validity of arguments, documents, or claims. Denotes that something is in force or holds.
    - [CORE]

16. **מוצדק**
    - Transliteration: mutsдak
    - Literal meaning: justified, warranted (passive participle from root צ-ד-ק)
    - Typical English translations: justified, warranted, legitimate
    - Usage: Indicates that something is justified or has legitimate grounds. Used in moral and logical argumentation to assess whether claims or actions are warranted.
    - [CORE]

17. **באמת**
    - Transliteration: be'emet
    - Literal meaning: in truth, truly (prepositional phrase)
    - Typical English translations: truly, really, indeed, in truth
    - Usage: Fixed expression used to emphasize truthfulness or reality of a statement. Functions as an adverb or discourse marker to strengthen assertions.
    - [CORE]

18. **דומה**
    - Transliteration: dome
    - Literal meaning: similar, resembling (from root ד-מ-ה)
    - Typical English translations: similar, like, resembling
    - Usage: Indicates resemblance or similarity between things. Used in comparisons and assessments of likeness or correspondence.
    - [CORE]

19. **מזויף**
    - Transliteration: mezuyaf
    - Literal meaning: forged, fake (passive participle from root ז-י-ף)
    - Typical English translations: fake, forged, counterfeit, false
    - Usage: Describes objects, documents, or representations that are deliberately falsified or counterfeit. Emphasizes intentional deception.
    - [CORE]

20. **אמת מידה**
    - Transliteration: amat mida
    - Literal meaning: measuring rod, standard (compound: truth + measure)
    - Typical English translations: criterion, standard, yardstick
    - Usage: Fixed expression denoting a standard or criterion by which something is evaluated. Used in contexts of assessment and judgment.
    - [CORE]

21. **ודאי**
    - Transliteration: vadai
    - Literal meaning: certain, sure (from root י-ד-ע)
    - Typical English translations: certain, sure, definite
    - Usage: Indicates certainty or definiteness of knowledge or facts. Used to express high confidence in the truth of statements.
    - [CORE]

22. **מוכח**
    - Transliteration: mukhaḥ
    - Literal meaning: proven, demonstrated (passive participle from root י-כ-ח)
    - Typical English translations: proven, demonstrated, established
    - Usage: Indicates that something has been proven or demonstrated through evidence or argument. Common in scientific and legal contexts.
    - [CORE]

23. **מעשי**
    - Transliteration: ma'asi
    - Literal meaning: practical, actual (from ma'ase "deed, act")
    - Typical English translations: practical, actual, real
    - Usage: Describes what is practical or actual as opposed to theoretical. Used to assess whether something works in reality.
    - [CORE]

24. **שווא**
    - Transliteration: shav
    - Literal meaning: vain, false, worthless (from root ש-ו-א)
    - Typical English translations: vain, false, worthless, empty
    - Usage: Biblical and literary term for falsehood or vanity, particularly in moral contexts. Appears in religious discourse and elevated registers.
    - [MARGINAL]

25. **ברור**
    - Transliteration: barur
    - Literal meaning: clear, obvious (from root ב-ר-ר)
    - Typical English translations: clear, obvious, evident
    - Usage: Indicates clarity or obviousness of truth or facts. Used when something is manifestly true or evident.
    - [CORE]
