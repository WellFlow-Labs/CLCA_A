# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

---

### Root 1

**Form:** tọ́ / tọ
**Transliteration:** tọ́ / tọ
**P1 participation:** 7 items — *òtítọ́* (1), *tọ̀nà* (12), *tọ́* (13), *ẹ̀tọ́* (19), *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* (25), *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* (25), *ìfọwọ́sí* (28, via *tọ́* in related forms); also embedded in *òdodo* (debated), and directly in *tọ̀nà*, *ẹ̀tọ́*, *òtítọ́*, *tọ́*, *ìtọ́sọ̀nà*
**Count (direct participation):** 5–6 items
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, propriety — the root that anchors factual accuracy, moral rightness, and conformity to a standard. It is the primary evaluative root in the truth domain.

---

### Root 2

**Form:** ẹ̀rí / rí
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí / rí
**P1 participation:** 4 items — *ẹ̀rí* (8), *ẹ̀rí ojú* (9), *jẹ́rìí* (10), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (11)
**Count:** 4 items
**Core meaning contribution:** Seeing, witnessing, evidencing — the root that anchors the evidentiary and testimonial dimension of truth evaluation: what can be seen, attested, and certified.

---

### Root 3

**Form:** gbọ́ / gbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** gbọ́ / gbàgbọ́
**P1 participation:** 3 items — *gbàgbọ́* (5), *ìgbàgbọ́* (6), *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (7, via *gbẹ́* "lean/rely," structurally parallel)
**Count:** 3 items (with *gbọ́* as the hearing/believing component appearing in 2 directly)
**Core meaning contribution:** Hearing, accepting, believing — the root that anchors the cognitive-receptive dimension: the act of taking something as true, trusting testimony, and committing to a proposition.

---

**Note on selection:** *tọ́* is the most productive root by a significant margin and is treated as the primary expansion target. *ẹ̀rí/rí* is the second most productive in the truth-evaluation domain. *gbọ́* is included as the third root given its role in the belief/trust cluster, though it is less morphologically productive than the first two.

## STEP 2 — EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1 EXPANSION: tọ́ / tọ

---

**1.**
**Form:** tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** Root verb/adjective; to be straight, correct, right, proper
**Natural English translation:** to be correct; to be right; to be proper
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate to evaluate factual accuracy (*ó tọ́* — "it is correct/right") and moral propriety. Appears in everyday speech, legal discourse, and moral evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ò-* (nominalizing prefix) + *tí* (relativizer/intensifier) + *tọ́* (correct, straight); "that which is truly straight/correct"
**Natural English translation:** truth, fact, correctness, accuracy
**Context of use:** The primary Yoruba noun for truth in propositional and moral senses. Used in everyday speech, legal testimony, and philosophical discourse. Already in P1 (item 1); included here to anchor the root expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀-* (nominalizing prefix) + *tọ́* (correct, right); "that which is right/due"
**Natural English translation:** right, entitlement, what is correct, what is due
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts for rights and entitlements. Already in P1 (item 19); included here to anchor the root expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizing prefix) + *tọ́* (correct, straight) + *sọ̀nà* (to direct/guide on a path); "directing correctly on a path"
**Natural English translation:** guidance, correct direction, proper instruction
**Context of use:** Used in religious, educational, and moral contexts for authoritative guidance. Already in P1 (item 25); included here to anchor the root expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** tọ̀nà

**Literal breakdown:** Noun; evidence, witness, testimony; related to *rí* (to see)
**Natural English translation:** evidence, proof, testimony, witness

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where the core truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other functional domains. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**tọ́ root — peripheral extensions:**

1. *ẹ̀tọ́* in the sense of "entitlement" or "right" (as in *ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn*, "human rights") — the root *tọ́* (correct, straight) here functions in a normative-legal domain of what is *due* to a person, not merely what is factually accurate.

2. *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* in religious contexts — the root *tọ́* here contributes to a sense of divinely sanctioned direction rather than factual correctness.

3. *tọ̀nà* — the root *tọ* combines with *nà* (path) to produce a spatial-moral metaphor; the "straightness" of the root is applied to a life-path or course of conduct rather than to a proposition.

4. *ó tọ́* used as a discourse marker of social appropriateness — in some contexts *ó tọ́* evaluates social propriety or procedural correctness rather than factual truth (e.g., "it is proper/fitting" in ceremonial contexts).

5. *ìtọ́* in aesthetic evaluation — the root appears in assessments of whether a craft object, performance, or composition is "correct" or "proper" in form, extending into aesthetic judgment.

6. *ọ̀nà tọ́* in technical/craft contexts — "correct method" applied to weaving, carving, or other crafts, where *tọ́* evaluates technical conformity to a standard rather than propositional truth.

---

**ẹ̀rí / rí root — peripheral extensions:**

1. *rí* (to see) as a root for perception more broadly — in compounds not listed above, *rí* contributes to expressions of visual experience, aesthetic perception, and recognition that are not directly evidentiary (e.g., *rírí* as "seeing/vision" in poetic contexts).

2. *ẹlẹ́rìí* in religious contexts — the term for "witness" is extended to refer to a martyr or one who bears witness to faith through suffering, shifting from legal testimony to religious self-attestation.

3. *ìjẹ́rìísí* in commercial contexts — certification of product quality or authenticity, where the root's contribution shifts from testimonial truth to quality assurance.

4. *jẹ́rìí* in Islamic *shahada* usage — the act of bearing witness to theological propositions (the oneness of God, the prophethood of Muhammad) extends the root into a domain of performative religious commitment rather than factual testimony about observed events.

---

**gbọ́ / gbàgbọ́ root — peripheral extensions:**

1. *gbọ́* in the sense of "to obey" — the root's contribution shifts from epistemic reception (hearing/understanding) to volitional compliance, as in *ìgbọ́ràn* (obedience), where the evaluative dimension is moral rather than epistemic.

2. *gbàgbọ́* in commercial and social trust contexts — "to trust" a vendor, institution, or product, where the root's contribution is practical reliability rather than propositional truth.

3. *gbọ́* in aesthetic contexts — *gbọ́* appears in expressions evaluating music or speech as "pleasing to hear" (*ó dára gbọ́*, "it is good to hear"), where the root contributes to aesthetic appreciation rather than epistemic assessment.

4. *ìgbàgbọ́* as "conviction" in non-religious contexts — used to describe strong personal commitment to a non-religious position or cause, extending from faith to general epistemic or motivational commitment.

5. *àìgbọ́ràn* in political discourse — "disobedience" applied to civil or political non-compliance, where the root's contribution shifts from epistemic non-reception to political resistance.

---

*Note: The root *tọ́* is by far the most productive of the three identified roots, with compounds and collocations extending across factual, moral, legal, procedural, spatial, and aesthetic domains. The root *ẹ̀rí/rí* is highly productive in legal and administrative contexts, with a secondary extension into religious testimonial discourse. The root *gbọ́* is productive in the belief/trust cluster but also extends significantly into obedience/compliance discourse, suggesting that the Yoruba conceptual domain linking hearing, believing, and obeying is tightly integrated.*
