# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register distribution, and contextual pairing observed across P1 and P1b/P2 documentation. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. Where items appear in multiple groups, this is noted explicitly. Items that do not fit cleanly into any group are listed at the end. Group sizes are uneven by design; this reflects the data.

---

## Group 1 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a statement or claim is correct or true

**Definition:** These expressions are used in everyday spoken Hebrew and in journalistic and analytical writing to confirm or deny that a statement, answer, or fact is correct. They function primarily as responses or evaluations in conversational exchange, and also appear in educational contexts (evaluating answers) and administrative contexts (temporally qualifying claims). They are the default register-neutral tools for asserting or denying factual accuracy in ordinary discourse. They are typically applied to statements and propositions rather than to persons or objects.

**Representative expressions:**
- *nakhon* (נָכוֹן) — the standard response confirming correctness; used predicatively to evaluate statements and answers, e.g., *ze nakhon* ("that is correct"), *nakhon le-ʿakhshav* ("accurate as of now")
- *ze nakhon* (זֶה נָכוֹן) — "that is correct"
- *ze lo nakhon* (זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן) — "that is not correct"
- *lo nakhon* (לֹא נָכוֹן) — "incorrect, wrong"
- *nakhon she-* (נָכוֹן שֶׁ-) — "it is true that / admittedly" (concessive construction)
- *nakhon lehagid* (נָכוֹן לְהַגִּיד) — "to be fair / it is right to say"
- *nakhon leʿakhshav* (נָכוֹן לְעַכְשָׁו) — "correct as of now"
- *nakhon letaʾarikh* (נָכוֹן לְתַאֲרִיךְ) — "correct as of [date]"; used in administrative and journalistic contexts to temporally qualify a claim
- *nakhon lomar she-* (נָכוֹן לוֹמַר שֶׁ-) — "it is correct to say that..."; used to introduce a carefully qualified assertion
- *lo bediyyuq nakhon* (לֹא בִּדְיוּק נָכוֹן) — "not exactly correct"
- *ze ʾemet* (זֶה אֱמֶת) — "that is true"
- *ze lo ʾemet* (זֶה לֹא אֱמֶת) — "that is not true"
- *ʾemet* (אֱמֶת) — "truth"; used as the primary noun for the property a statement has when it corresponds to reality; used predicatively: *ze ʾemet* ("that is true")
- *ʾamiti* (אֲמִתִּי) — "true, genuine, real"; used attributively, e.g., *sibbaʾh ʾamitit* ("the real reason"), *shmo ha-ʾamiti* ("his real name")
- *beʾemet* (בֶּאֱמֶת) — "really, truly" (as everyday intensifier/sincerity marker on statements)
- *ʾemet leʾamittah* (אֱמֶת לְאַמִּתָּהּ) — "the absolute truth / truth in its truest sense"; used as an intensifier in legal testimony and everyday speech
- *leʾamitto shel davar* (לֶאֱמִתּוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר) — "in actual fact"; used to signal that what follows is the genuine underlying reality
- *ha-ʾemet hi she-* (הָאֱמֶת הִיא שֶׁ-) — "the truth is that..."; a sentence frame used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement
- *lo kol ha-ʾemet* (לֹא כָּל הָאֱמֶת) — "not the whole truth"; used in legal and everyday contexts to characterize partial disclosure
- *kol ha-ʾemet ve-raq ha-ʾemet* (כָּל הָאֱמֶת וְרַק הָאֱמֶת) — "the whole truth and nothing but the truth"; used in legal testimony contexts
- *ḥatsi ʾemet* (חֲצִי אֱמֶת) — "half-truth"; used to characterize a statement that is technically accurate but misleading
- *meduyaq* (מְדוּיָּק) — "precise, exact, accurate"; used when the evaluation concerns closeness of correspondence to a standard or fact, e.g., *tiʾur meduyaq* ("an accurate description"), *nisuaḥ meduyaq* ("precise wording")
- *diyyuq* (דִּיּוּק) — "precision, exactness"; used as a noun for the quality of close correspondence, e.g., *be-diyyuq* ("exactly"), *ḥoser diyyuq* ("inaccuracy")
- *bediyyuq* (בְּדִיּוּק) — "exactly, precisely" (adverb; extremely common)
- *atta tsodeq / at tsoddeqet* (אַתָּה צוֹדֵק / אַתְּ צוֹדֶקֶת) — "you are right" (the most colloquial form; draws on root צ-ד-ק but in this usage has no moral or justice connotation)
- *hu tsadak* (הוּא צָדַק) — "he was right, he was correct" (verbal evaluation of correctness)
- *ʾim ʾani nakhon* (אִם אֲנִי נָכוֹן) — "if I am correct" (epistemic hedging)
- *mutʿe* (מוּטְעֶה) — "mistaken, erroneous" (applied to beliefs or persons who hold incorrect views; does not imply intentional deception)
- *taʿut* (טָעוּת) — "mistake, error"
- *ze sheqer* (זֶה שֶׁקֶר) — "that is a lie, that is false" (strong negation of a claim's truth)

**Notes:** *nakhon* and *meduyaq* are the most commonly contrasted within this group: *nakhon* evaluates correctness in a general sense, while *meduyaq* specifically evaluates precision of correspondence. *ʾemet* operates at a higher level of abstraction and appears in both this group and Groups 2 and 5. *ʾamiti* overlaps with Group 9 (genuineness of objects and persons) but appears here when applied to propositions or reasons. *beʾemet* in this group functions as a degree marker or sincerity signal rather than a propositional claim. *atta tsodeq* belongs primarily here in everyday colloquial use; in moral or legal argumentation it belongs to Group 8. *meduyaq* belongs more naturally to Group 3 (precision evaluation) but is included here because it participates in correctness evaluation. *mutʿe* is distinguished from *sheqer* in that it does not imply intentional deception.

## Group 2 — Expressions used in moral evaluation of persons as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a person's character or conduct is being evaluated as morally straightforward and free from deception, or as deceptive and dishonest. They are used in everyday moral discourse, interpersonal evaluation, character references, and ethical writing. They contrast with terms in Group 1 in that they evaluate persons and conduct rather than propositions.

**Representative expressions:**
- *kan* (כֵּן) — "sincere, honest"; used in moral evaluation of persons, e.g., *ʾadam kan* ("an honest person"), *lihyot kan ʿim ʿatsmo* ("to be honest with oneself")
- *kanut* (כַּנּוּת) — "sincerity, honesty"; the abstract noun for this quality, e.g., *ledabber be-kanut* ("to speak honestly"), *ḥoser kanut* ("lack of sincerity")
- *bekhannot* (בְּכַנּוּת) — "sincerely, honestly, candidly"; a fixed adverbial phrase used to frame a statement as genuine
- *be-kanut rabbah* (בְּכַנּוּת רַבָּה) — "in all sincerity, very honestly"
- *mitokh kanut* (מִתּוֹךְ כַּנּוּת) — "out of sincerity"
- *lemaʿan ha-kanut* (לְמַעַן הַכַּנּוּת) — "for the sake of honesty"
- *ḥoser kanut* (חֹסֶר כַּנּוּת) — "lack of sincerity, insincerity"
- *yashar* (יָשָׁר) — "upright, honest, straightforward"; used in moral evaluation of persons and conduct, e.g., *ʾadam yashar* ("an upright person"), *yashar ve-neʾeman* ("upright and faithful")
- *yosher* (יֹשֶׁר) — "integrity, uprightness"; the abstract noun for this quality, e.g., *ʾish yosher* ("a man of integrity"), *ḥoser yosher* ("lack of integrity")
- *yesharut* (יְשָׁרוּת) — "honesty, uprightness, straightforwardness"; near-synonym of *yosher* used in moral evaluation
- *be-yesharut* (בִּישָׁרוּת) — "straightforwardly, honestly"
- *yosher lev* (יֹשֶׁר לֵב) — "integrity of heart"; used in biblical and literary registers to describe genuine inner moral honesty
- *yeshirut* (יְשִׁירוּת) — "directness, straightforwardness"; used when the emphasis is on manner of communication rather than moral character, e.g., *medabber be-yeshirut* ("speaking directly")
- *ʾadam yashar* (אָדָם יָשָׁר) — "an upright person, an honest person"
- *ledabber yashar* (לְדַבֵּר יָשָׁר) — "to speak straight, to speak honestly"
- *yashar min ha-katef* (יָשָׁר מִן הַכָּתֵף) — "straight from the shoulder, bluntly"
- *dover ʾemet* (דּוֹבֵר אֱמֶת) — "truth-teller, one who speaks the truth"; used in moral evaluation of a person who consistently tells the truth
- *ledabber ʾemet* (לְדַבֵּר אֱמֶת) — "to speak the truth"
- *dover sheqer* (דּוֹבֵר שֶׁקֶר) — "one who speaks falsehood" (moral characterization)
- *shaqran* (שַׁקְרָן) — "liar"; used in moral condemnation of a person who habitually tells falsehoods
- *shaqranit* (שַׁקְרָנִית) — "liar" (feminine)
- *shaqranut* (שַׁקְרָנוּת) — "mendacity, habitual lying"; the abstract noun for the practice of lying
- *leshaqer* (לְשַׁקֵּר) — "to lie"; the standard verb for telling falsehoods
- *rammʾai* (רַמַּאי) — "deceiver, cheat"; used in moral condemnation of a person who deliberately misleads others
- *ramaʾut* (רַמָּאוּת) — "deceit, fraud, cheating"; the abstract noun for the practice of deliberate deception
- *leramot* (לְרַמּוֹת) — "to deceive, to cheat"; the standard verb for deliberate deception
- *mirmah* (מִרְמָה) — "deceit, fraud, guile"; used in legal and literary registers for deliberate deception
- *tsevʿiʿut* (צְבִיעוּת) — "hypocrisy, two-facedness"; used in moral evaluation of persons who present a false appearance of virtue; documented as the explicit antonym of *kanut*
- *sheqer lavan* (שֶׁקֶר לָבָן) — "white lie"; used in everyday discourse for a harmless or socially motivated falsehood
- *liḥyot be-sheqer* (לִחְיוֹת בְּשֶׁקֶר) — "to live a lie"; used in psychological and literary discourse
- *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן) — "faithful, loyal, trustworthy" (of persons); overlaps with Groups 4 and 5
- *neʾeman le-* (נֶאֱמָן לְ-) — "faithful to, loyal to"
- *neʾeman le-ʿatsmo* (נֶאֱמָן לְעַצְמוֹ) — "true to oneself"

**Notes:** *kan* and *yashar* are closely related and frequently co-occur (e.g., *yashar ve-neʾeman*), but *kan* emphasizes sincerity of intention while *yashar* emphasizes moral straightness of conduct. *yeshirut* overlaps with this group but is more specifically about communicative directness than moral character. *tsevʿiʿut* is the explicit antonym of *kanut*. *sheqer* and *ramaʾut* are both antonyms of honesty but differ: *sheqer* concerns false statements, while *ramaʾut* concerns deceptive conduct more broadly. *neʾeman* and *neʾemanut* appear in this group and also in Groups 4 and 5; context determines which dimension is primary. *yosher* appears in this group and also in Groups 6 and 8.

## Group 3 — Expressions used to evaluate precision and exactness

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a statement, measurement, description, or representation achieves the required degree of exactness or specificity. They are particularly common in scientific, journalistic, legal, and technical registers, and they contrast with terms meaning "vague" or "approximate." The evaluative dimension concerns degree of fit between a representation and its object, not simply whether something is true or false.

**Representative expressions:**
- *meduyaq* (מְדוּיָּק) — "precise, exact, accurate"
- *diyyuq* (דִּיּוּק) — "precision, exactness, accuracy"
- *bediyyuq* (בְּדִיּוּק) — "exactly, precisely" (extremely common adverbial)
- *ledayyeq* (לְדַיֵּק) — "to be precise, to specify exactly"
- *medayyeq* (מְדַיֵּק) — "precise person; being precise"
- *dayqan* (דַּיְקָן) — "a precise person, a stickler for accuracy"
- *dayqanut* (דַּיְקָנוּת) — "precision, meticulousness"
- *ʾi-diyyuq* (אִי-דִּיּוּק) — "imprecision, inaccuracy"
- *ḥoser diyyuq* (חֹסֶר דִּיּוּק) — "lack of precision"
- *lo meduyaq* (לֹא מְדוּיָּק) — "not precise, inaccurate"
- *meduyaq beyoter* (מְדוּיָּק בְּיוֹתֵר) — "most precise"
- *meduyaq yoter* (מְדוּיָּק יוֹתֵר) — "more precisely" (self-correction marker)
- *lihyot meduyaq* (לִהְיוֹת מְדוּיָּק) — "to be precise" (parenthetical)
- *teʾur meduyaq* (תֵּאוּר מְדוּיָּק) — "an accurate description"
- *meydaʿ meduyaq* (מֵידָע מְדוּיָּק) — "accurate information"
- *nekhonot* (נְכוֹנוּת) — "correctness, accuracy" (abstract noun; tends to appear in more formal evaluative contexts where the degree of accuracy is being assessed)

**Notes:** The root ד-י-ק generates the core cluster of this group. *bediyyuq* is among the most frequent items in everyday speech. The group overlaps with Group 1 (correctness evaluation) in that precision is a dimension of correctness, but the precision cluster is distinguished by its emphasis on degree and exactness rather than binary true/false evaluation. *nekhonot* is placed here rather than in Group 1 because as an abstract noun it tends to appear in more formal evaluative contexts.

## Group 4 — Expressions used to evaluate whether a person, source, or information can be trusted or relied upon

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be depended upon to be honest and accurate over time. They are common in journalistic, academic, and interpersonal contexts. They differ from Group 5 (legal and quasi-legal evaluation) in that they are not restricted to formal legal proceedings and do not carry procedural implications about admissibility. The evaluative dimension concerns the reliability of the entity over time or across situations, not merely the truth of a single statement.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾamin* (אָמִין) — "reliable, trustworthy, dependable" (somewhat more colloquial than *meheymen*)
- *ʾaminut* (אֲמִינוּת) — "reliability, credibility, trustworthiness"; used in journalistic and academic assessment of sources, e.g., *ʾaminut meqorot* ("source credibility")
- *ʾaminut meyda* (אֲמִינוּת מֵידָע) — "information reliability"
- *lo ʾamin* (לֹא אָמִין) — "unreliable"
- *nirʾe ʾamin* (נִרְאֶה אָמִין) — "seems reliable"
- *paḥot ʾamin* (פָּחוֹת אָמִין) — "less reliable"
- *ʾi-ʾaminut* (אִי-אֲמִינוּת) — "unreliability, lack of credibility"
- *maqor ʾamin* (מָקוֹר אָמִין) — "reliable source" (slightly less formal)
- *maqor mehiman* (מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן) — "reliable source" (more formal)
- *musad mehiman* (מוּסָד מְהֵימָן) — "reliable institution"
- *mehiman* (מְהֵימָן) — "reliable, credible, trustworthy" (somewhat formal; common in legal discourse)
- *mehimanut* (מְהֵימָנוּת) — "reliability, credibility, trustworthiness"
- *lo mehiman* (לֹא מְהֵימָן) — "not reliable, not credible"
- *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן) — "faithful, loyal, trustworthy"; also used for fidelity to a text or facts
- *neʾemanut* (נֶאֱמָנוּת) — "loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity"
- *lo neʾeman* (לֹא נֶאֱמָן) — "untrustworthy, disloyal"
- *neʾeman la-ʿuvdot* (נֶאֱמָן לָעוּבְדוֹת) — "faithful to the facts"
- *neʾeman la-teqst* (נֶאֱמָן לַטֶּקְסְט) — "faithful to the text"
- *neʾeman la-ʾemet* (נֶאֱמָן לָאֱמֶת) — "faithful to the truth"; used in journalistic ethics discourse
- *neʾeman li-dvarav* (נֶאֱמָן לִדְבָרָיו) — "true to his word"; used in moral evaluation of a person who keeps their promises
- *ʾemun* (אֱמוּן) — "trust, confidence" (institutional/relational)
- *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* (אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר) — "public trust"
- *mashber ʾemun* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן) — "crisis of trust"
- *mashber ʾaminut* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֲמִינוּת) — "credibility crisis"
- *ʾemuna* (אֱמוּנָה) — "faith, trust, faithfulness"; used in interpersonal and institutional contexts to describe the quality of being trustworthy, e.g., *be-tom lev u-ve-ʾemuna* ("in good faith and faithfully"), *mashber ʾemuna* ("a crisis of trust"); overlaps with Group 7
- *lishmor ʾemunim* (לִשְׁמוֹר אֱמוּנִים) — "to keep faith, to remain faithful"; used in moral and political discourse
- *binyan ʾamon* (בְּנִיַּת אָמוֹן) — "trust-building, confidence-building"
- *ʾi-ʾamon* (אִי-אָמוֹן) — "distrust, no-confidence"
- *hatsbaʿat ʾi-ʾamon* (הַצְבָּעַת אִי-אָמוֹן) — "vote of no confidence"

**Notes:** *mehiman* and *ʾamin* are near-synonyms with register differences: *mehiman* is more formal and more specifically legal than *ʾamin*. *neʾeman* emphasizes ongoing faithfulness and loyalty (a relational quality), while *mehiman* emphasizes credibility and believability (an epistemic quality). *ʾaminut* appears in both this group and Group 5 (legal evaluation of testimony); the distinction is contextual. *ʾemuna* in its religious sense belongs to Group 7; in its interpersonal sense (trust) it belongs here.

## Group 5 — Expressions used in legal and quasi-legal evaluation of testimony, witnesses, and evidence

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in the context of legal proceedings, halakhic adjudication, or formal evaluation of testimony, where the question is whether a witness or piece of evidence meets the required standard of reliability and admissibility. They are used by courts, legal scholars, and in legal journalism. They form a distinct cluster because they combine epistemic evaluation (is this credible?) with procedural evaluation (does this meet the legal standard?).

**Representative expressions:**
- *mehiman* (מְהֵימָן) — "trustworthy, credible" (formal/legal); used specifically of witnesses and sources in legal contexts, e.g., *ʿed mehiman* ("a credible witness"), *ʿedut mehimana* ("reliable testimony")
- *mehimanut* (מְהֵימָנוּת) — "credibility, trustworthiness" (legal abstract noun); used in legal assessment, e.g., *mehimanut ha-ʿed* ("the credibility of the witness")
- *nimtsa mehiman* (נִמְצָא מְהֵימָן) — "found to be credible" (judicial formula)
- *nimtsa lo mehiman* (נִמְצָא לֹא מְהֵימָן) — "found not to be credible" (judicial formula)
- *ʾaminut* (אֲמִינוּת) — "credibility, reliability" (legal/journalistic); used in legal evaluation of witnesses and sources, e.g., *ʾaminut ha-ʿed* ("witness credibility")
- *ʾaminut ʿed* (אֲמִינוּת עֵד) — "witness credibility"; a standard legal term in Israeli court proceedings
- *beḥinat ha-ʾaminut* (בְּחִינַת הָאֲמִינוּת) — "examination of credibility" (legal formula)
- *mivḥan ha-ʾaminut* (מִבְחַן הָאֲמִינוּת) — "the credibility test"
- *ḥoser ʾaminut* (חוֹסֶר אֲמִינוּת) — "lack of credibility"
- *pegiʿa ba-ʾaminut* (פְּגִיעָה בָּאֲמִינוּת) — "damage to credibility"
- *ʿed neʾeman* (עֵד נֶאֱמָן) — "reliable witness" (legal/religious)
- *ʿed ʾemet* (עֵד אֱמֶת) — "truthful witness" (legal/religious)
- *ʿed ʾamin* (עֵד אָמִין) — "reliable witness"
- *ʿed sheqer* (עֵד שֶׁקֶר) — "false witness, perjurer" (legal/religious); a fixed biblical and legal collocation
- *ʿedut* (עֵדוּת) — "testimony, evidence"; the standard term for testimony given in legal contexts, e.g., *ʿedut sheqer* ("false testimony"), *ʿedut neʾemenet* ("reliable testimony")
- *ʿedut sheqer* (עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר) — "false testimony, perjury"; a fixed legal collocation
- *neʾeman le-ʿedut* (נֶאֱמָן לְעֵדוּת) — "competent to testify, admissible as a witness"; a legal term indicating that a person meets the criteria to serve as a valid witness
- *kasher* (כָּשֵׁר) — "fit, valid, above suspicion"; used in legal and halakhic contexts to declare that a witness or document meets the required standard, e.g., *ʿed kasher* ("a valid witness")
- *kashrut* (כַּשְׁרוּת) — "fitness, validity" (in legal/general use)
- *pasul* (פָּסוּל) — "disqualified, inadmissible"; the antonym of *kasher* in legal and halakhic contexts
- *pesulut* (פְּסוּלוּת) — "invalidity, inadmissibility"
- *lifsol* (לִפְסֹל) — "to disqualify, to rule inadmissible"
- *pissul* (פִּסּוּל) — "disqualification, ruling of inadmissibility"
- *savir* (סָבִיר) — "reasonable, plausible" (legal standard)
- *sevirut* (סְבִירוּת) — "reasonableness" (legal standard)
- *ʾi-sevirut* (אִי-סְבִירוּת) — "unreasonableness" (administrative law)
- *bilti savir* (בִּלְתִּי סָבִיר) — "unreasonable"
- *sevara* (סְבָרָה) — "reasoned argument, logical inference" (Talmudic/legal)
- *ʾimun beyt mishpat* (אִמּוּן בֵּית מִשְׁפָּט) — "the court's trust, judicial confidence"; used in legal discourse to describe the degree to which a court places confidence in a witness or evidence
- *bar-ʾaman* (בַּר-אָמָן) — "trustworthy person in a specific domain"; used in rabbinic and halakhic literature to designate a person trusted in a particular legal matter
- *safek* (סָפֵק) — "doubt, uncertainty"; used in legal and halakhic contexts to indicate that the truth or validity of something is uncertain, triggering specific rules for resolution

**Notes:** *savir* and *sevirut* are particularly prominent in Israeli administrative law, where *ʾi-sevirut qitsunit* (extreme unreasonableness) is a key standard for judicial review. *kasher* and *pasul* form a direct antonym pair in this group and also in Group 6 (legal validity). *mehiman* is more formal and more specifically legal than *ʾamin*; both appear here but with different register weights. *ʾaminut* overlaps with Group 4 (source reliability). *ʿed sheqer* and *ʿedut sheqer* participate in Group 5 (truth and falsehood as values) as well.

## Group 6 — Expressions used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate the formal validity of documents, contracts, and procedures

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a document, contract, license, decision, or action has the formal legal status required for it to be operative or recognized. They appear in legal and administrative contexts and are concerned with formal validity rather than moral rightness or factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- *taqef* (תָּקֵף) — "valid, in force, legally binding"
- *toqef* (תֹּקֶף) — "validity, legal force"
- *lo taqef* (לֹא תָּקֵף) — "invalid, expired"
- *letaqqef* (לְתַקֵּף) — "to validate, to ratify"
- *ʾi-teqifut* (אִי-תְּקִיפוּת) — "invalidity, lack of legal force"
- *be-toqef* (בְּתֹקֶף) — "by virtue of, pursuant to" (fixed legal formula)
- *taqef ʿad* (תָּקֵף עַד) — "valid until" (expiry formula)
- *ḥoze taqef* (חוֹזֶה תָּקֵף) — "a valid contract"
- *darkon taqef* (דַּרְכּוֹן תָּקֵף) — "a valid passport"
