## Short narrative analysis

### Recurring patterns across unrelated language families
Several strategies recur across very different language families.

First, **abstract nominalization** is one of the strongest recurring patterns. Languages repeatedly build nouns for “truth,” “accuracy,” “validity,” “authenticity,” or “trustworthiness” from adjectival or evaluative bases: compare **zh 真实性 / 准确性**, **he אמינות / מהימנות**, **sa सत्यता / प्रामाण्य**, **el αυθεντικότητα / εγκυρότητα**, **en authenticity / validity / reliability**, **sw ukweli / usahihi / uaminifu**, and **tl katotohanan**.

Second, **negative derivation or negated predication** is widespread. Some languages use bound negatives productively (**sa असत्य, अयथार्थ; en untrue, invalid; zh 不实**), while others rely heavily on analytic negation (**he לא נכון, el δεν είναι σωστό, tl Hindi tama, sw si kweli, qu mana allin**).

Third, many languages use **compounding or tight noun-modifier constructions** to build truth-domain vocabulary around speech, persons, documents, and goods. This appears in **zh 真话, 假货**, **sa सत्यवचन, असत्यवादिन्**, **qu llulla simi, llulla runa**, **el πλαστή υπογραφή**, **en fake news, true story, forged document**, **tl pekeng dokumento**, and **sw habari za uongo, dawa bandia**.

Fourth, many languages develop **discourse markers from truth words**: “to tell the truth,” “in truth,” “actually,” “really.” This is strongly attested in **zh, he, el, en, tl, sw**, and also in more formal ways in **sa**.

### Striking asymmetries
A major asymmetry is that **negative person terms are often more lexicalized than positive ones**. The dataset repeatedly gives compact forms for “liar” or “forger”:
- **he** has no equally common everyday single-word counterpart to a positive truth-teller.
- **en** has **liar** as fully ordinary, while **truth-teller** is marked.
- **sw** has **mwongo** and **mkweli**, but the lying side is especially salient in accusation.
- **qu** has **llullaq / llulla runa**, while positive truth-person forms are less central.

Another asymmetry is that **truth-confirmation and authenticity-checking often have dedicated verbs or constructions, but “make true” is usually absent**. Languages more readily lexicalize:
- “verify/authenticate/validate” than “make true”
- “mislead/cause error” than “cause truth”

This is especially clear in **en authenticate / validate**, **he להצדיק**, **tl beripikahin**, **qu pantachiy**, and **sa प्रमाणीकृत / प्रमाणीकरणम्**.

### One-way or blocked derivational pathways
Several one-way patterns recur:

- **Adjective/base → abstract noun** is common, but the reverse is often not productive in the supplied data.
  - **zh**: 真实性, 准确性, 正当性 are common, but many of these nouns have no further common derivation listed.
  - **he**: מהימנות, אמינות, אמיתות are established, but often stop there.
  - **sa**: सत्य → सत्यता, यथार्थ → यथार्थता, but the abstract nouns themselves often show no further common derivation.
  - **el**: αυθεντικός → αυθεντικότητα, γνήσιος → γνησιότητα, but many abstract nouns are endpoints.
  - **tl**: katotohanan and saligan are central nouns, but not shown as bases for further productive families.

- **Truth base → “come true”** is not broadly available cross-linguistically.
  - Strongly supported in **tl** (**magkatotoo, nagkatotoo**)
  - Strongly supported in **en** (**come true, prove true**)
  - Only restricted or marginal elsewhere
  - Explicitly absent or not identified in **he, sw, qu**, and weak in **sa**

- **Authenticity base → “become authentic/genuine”** is often blocked.
  - The data repeatedly notes no common dedicated inchoative for this in **he, el, en, tl, sw**
  - Instead languages prefer **verification/result** expressions.

### Productive vs restricted vs lexicalized patterns

#### Productive
- **Abstract nominalization** is productive or at least broadly recurrent in **zh, he, sa, el, en, sw**
- **Negative derivation/negated predication** is productive in most languages
- **Attributive noun modification** for true/false/fake/correct is broadly productive in **zh, el, en, tl, sw**
- **Speech-object constructions** like “tell the truth / tell a lie” are highly productive in **he, sa, el, en, sw**

#### Restricted
- **Inchoative truth derivation** is restricted:
  - strong in **tl** and **en**
  - marginal in **sa**
  - absent/unclear in **he, sw, qu**
- **Causative truth derivation** is restricted:
  - usually limited to subdomains like validation, authentication, justification, or misleading
- **Participial/result-state derivation** is strong only in certain families, especially **he, el, qu**

#### Lexicalized / fossilized
- Many discourse markers and idioms are lexicalized:
  - **zh 名副其实, 货真价实**
  - **he רגע של אמת, למען האמת**
  - **el κατ’ ουσίαν, κατά γράμμα**
  - **en ring true, the real thing, truth be told**
  - **tl sa totoo lang, walang saligan**
  - **sw kwa kweli, ukweli mtupu**
  - **sa सत्यं वद, प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**
- Some formally related items have drifted semantically:
  - **he נכונות**
  - **sw uongozi**
  - **en realize**
  - these are best treated as related but not central truth-domain derivations

#### Unclear or weakly attested
- **qu** has several weakly supported or uncertain forms for adverbial truth and exactness (**chiqaqta, qampa**)
- **sa** has marginal inchoatives like **सत्यतां गच्छति**
- **el** has uncertain adverbial derivation from **έγκριτος**
- **tl** and **sw** show many stative evaluative forms but fewer clearly documented derivational expansions outside a few families

## Overall
The strongest recurring cross-language strategies are:
1. **abstract nominalization**
2. **negative derivation or analytic negation**
3. **compound / modifier constructions with truth, falsity, authenticity, and correctness terms**
4. **agentive derivation, especially on the falsehood side**
5. **lexicalized discourse markers from truth words**

By contrast, broad **inchoative** and **causative** derivation for truth states is much less general. Where dynamic morphology exists, it is usually **subdomain-specific**: “come true,” “mislead,” “authenticate,” “validate,” “justify,” rather than a single productive truth-domain pattern.
