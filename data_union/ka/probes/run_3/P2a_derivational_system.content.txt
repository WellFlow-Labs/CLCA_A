# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested derivational morphology for P1 lexical items. Forms already present in P1 and P1b are not re-listed as derivations of each other unless the derivational relationship requires explicit documentation. Gaps are preserved. ## P1 Item #1 — ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)

**Base Form:** č̣ešmariti

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტად
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritad
- **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmariti + adverbial suffix -ad; "in a true/genuine manner"
- **Natural English Translation:** truly, genuinely, in truth, verily
- **Usage:** Adverbial form used in formal, philosophical, and theological registers to assert that something is genuinely or truly the case. Appears in classical Georgian texts and contemporary formal prose; less common in everyday speech than martla.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
- **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
- **Natural English Translation:** truth, truthfulness, genuineness
- **Usage:** Already documented as P1 item #2. Noted here to record the adjectival-to-nominal derivational relationship explicitly.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Derived Form:** უჭეშმარიტო
- **Transliteration:** uč̣ešmarito
- **Literal Breakdown:** privative prefix u- + č̣ešmarit- + suffix -o; "lacking truth, not genuine"
- **Natural English Translation:** untrue, untruthful, not genuine
- **Usage:** Negated adjectival form used in formal and literary registers to characterize something as lacking genuine truth or authenticity. Less frequent than the base form; appears in theological and philosophical writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Summary of Derivational Productivity

The following observations are directly supported by the data documented above:

- **mart-** and **q̇alb-** roots show the broadest derivational productivity, generating adjectival, nominal, adverbial, verbal noun, resultative participial, agentive participial, and privative forms.
- **namdvili** and **zusti** show consistent productivity for the adverbial (-ad) and privative (u-...-o) patterns.
- **k'anonieri** shows productivity including the privative pattern (uk'anono), which is among the most frequent derived forms in legal discourse.
- **sarcmuno** shows derivational productivity including a nominal form (sarcmunoeba) that has developed an independent semantic domain (religious faith) beyond its base meaning.
- **arsi** shows a distinctive derivational pattern using -obriv- and -ebit- suffixes, both of which are productive in legal and philosophical registers.
- International borrowings (**p'ik't'iuri**, **avt'enturi**) show limited derivational productivity, primarily the -oba nominalization.
- Several nominalizations (**namdviloba**, **q̇alboba**, **k'anoniereba**, **avt'enturoba**) do not commonly serve as bases for further morphological derivation.
- Adverbial forms in **-ad** are attested for nearly all adjectival bases in this set, consistent with the general productivity of this suffix in Georgian.
- Privative forms in **u-...-o** are attested for several bases (**zusti**, **sc̣ori**, **sarcmuno**, **sando**, **k'anonieri**, **msgavsi**) but are not documented for all bases, and the pattern is not assumed where not attested.
