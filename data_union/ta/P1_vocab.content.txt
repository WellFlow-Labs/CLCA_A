# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

**1.**
**Form:** உண்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Derived from *uḷ* (to be/exist) + nominalizing suffix *-mai*; "that which is real/existent"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuineness
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality. Participates in productive derivation: *uṇmaiyāna* (true, genuine), *uṇmaiyāṉ* (truthful person), *uṇmaiyāka* (truly, honestly). Frequently appears in moral and religious discourse as a core virtue term. Contrasts with *poy* (falsehood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** பொய்
**Transliteration:** poy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Monomorphemic root; "falsehood" (etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, falsity, deception
**Usage:** The primary antonym of *uṇmai*; used to characterize statements, representations, or persons as false or deceptive. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Productive: *poyyan* (liar), *poyyana* (false, fake), *poy-sākṣi* (false witness/perjury), *poy-yāna* (false, attributive). Applied to statements, persons, and representations judged to be false or deceptive.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** நேர்மை
**Transliteration:** nērmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nēr* (straight, direct) + *-mai* (abstract nominalizer); "straightness"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness, straightforwardness, sincerity
**Usage:** Refers to a quality of character or conduct marked by transparency and absence of deception. Commonly used in moral evaluation of individuals in personal, professional, and civic contexts. Distinct from *uṇmai* in that it foregrounds conduct and disposition rather than propositional truth. Contrasts with *vaññam* (deceit), *kāpaṭyam* (hypocrisy), and *vañcaṉai* (deceit).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** உண்மையான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāna
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* + *-āna* (adjectival copular suffix); "that which is truth"
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** Attributive adjective modifying nouns to indicate genuineness or factual accuracy. Used of objects (genuine goods), persons (a true friend), and propositions (a true statement). Highly productive in contemporary spoken and written Tamil. Contrasts with *poyyana* (false) in parallel constructions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**5.**
**Form:** உண்மையாண
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* + adjectival copular suffix *-āṉa*; "that which is of/pertaining to *uṇmai*"
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** Adjectival form of *uṇmai*, used attributively to modify nouns denoting statements, persons, objects, or feelings. Widely used in both spoken and written Tamil. Contrasts with *poyyāṉa* (false) in parallel constructions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**6.**
**Form:** நம்பகமான
**Transliteration:** nampakamāna
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nampu* (to trust/believe) + *-akam* (nominalizing infix) + *-āna* (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, and information judged worthy of trust. Frequently used in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The base noun *nampakam* also occurs independently in formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**7.**
**Form:** நம்பகத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** nampakattaṉmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nampakam* (trustworthiness) + *taṉmai* (quality, nature)
**Typical English translations:** credibility, reliability, trustworthiness
**Usage:** A more abstract nominal form used in formal, journalistic, and academic contexts to refer to the property of being credible or reliable. Slightly more formal register than *nampakamāna*. Appears in discussions of evidence, testimony, and institutional conduct. Less frequent in spoken registers than *nampakamāna*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**8.**
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை
**Transliteration:** nampikkai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nampu* (to trust) + *-ikkai* (action/state nominalizer); "the act or state of trusting"
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence, credence
**Usage:** Refers to the epistemic or affective state of trusting a person, institution, or claim. Used in both religious contexts (faith) and secular evaluative contexts (confidence in reliability). Productive: *nampikkaiyāna* (trustworthy), *nampikkai iḻa* (to lose trust), *nampikkaiyiṉmai* (lack of trust).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**9.**
**Form:** சரி
**Transliteration:** cari
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly from Sanskrit *sari* (equal, correct); "correctness, rightness"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper
**Usage:** Extremely common in spoken Tamil to affirm that something is correct, appropriate, or in order. Used to evaluate statements, actions, calculations, and conduct. Also functions as a discourse particle confirming agreement. Productive: *cariyāna* (correct, proper), *caripār* (to check, verify), *caritāṉ* (it is indeed correct).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**10.**
**Form:** தவறு
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *tavar* (to err, deviate); "error, deviation"
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, wrong, fault
**Usage:** Applied to incorrect statements, mistaken beliefs, and morally wrong actions. Contrasts with *cari* (correct) in evaluative contexts. Used in both epistemic (factual error) and moral (wrongdoing) registers. Productive: *tavaṟāna* (wrong, erroneous), *tavaṟu ceytavar* (one who has done wrong), *tavaṟu ceytāl* (if one errs/does wrong).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**11.**
**Form:** உண்மைத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmait-taṉmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* (truth) + *taṉmai* (nature, quality); "the quality of being true"
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, genuineness
**Usage:** A more abstract nominal form used in philosophical, literary, and formal evaluative discourse to refer to the property of being true or authentic. Appears in literary criticism and philosophical writing when evaluating whether something genuinely possesses a quality. Less common in casual speech than *uṇmai* alone.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**12.**
**Form:** அசல்
**Transliteration:** acal
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *aṣl* (origin, root); "original, source"
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic (of objects, documents)
**Usage:** Primarily used to distinguish genuine articles from copies or counterfeits, especially in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts. Contrasts with *nakal* (copy, duplicate) and *pōli* (fake, counterfeit). Common in everyday speech when evaluating goods and documents: *acal āvaṇam* (original document).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**13.**
**Form:** நகல்
**Transliteration:** nakal
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic/Persian *naqal* (copy, transfer); "copy, duplicate"
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, imitation, counterfeit, transcript
**Usage:** Used to describe reproductions or imitations of an original, often in contexts where authenticity is being evaluated. Can carry neutral (certified copy of a document) or negative (fake) connotations depending on context. Appears in fixed expressions such as *nakal சரி* (certified copy). Evaluatively relevant to authenticity assessments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**14.**
**Form:** போலி
**Transliteration:** pōli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly from *pōl* (resemblance); "resemblance, imitation, counterfeit"
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, spurious, imitation, sham
**Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, or credentials that falsely claim to be genuine. Carries a strongly negative evaluative charge. Used in legal, commercial, and moral condemnation of fraud and misrepresentation. Productive in nominal compounds: *pōli ṭākṭar* (fake doctor), *pōli āvaṇam* (forged document), *pōli māṭṭu* (fake cattle/goods).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**15.**
**Form:** உண்மையில்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* + *-il* (locative case suffix); "in truth"
**Typical English translations:** in truth, actually, in reality, truly
**Usage:** Adverbial expression used to assert that what follows reflects the actual state of affairs, often correcting a misconception or contrasting appearance with reality. Functions as an epistemic marker in discourse. Common in both spoken and written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**16.**

**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyilēyē

**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in actual fact
**Usage:** An emphatic form of *uṇmaiyil*, used to stress the genuineness or reality of a state of affairs. Common in spoken Tamil and informal written registers to reinforce the truth of a claim or the sincerity of a statement. Marks stronger epistemic commitment than *uṇmaiyil*. Functions as an intensifier in evaluative assertions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**17.**
**Form:** நேர்
**Transliteration:** nēr
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Monomorphemic root; "straight, direct, exact"
**Typical English translations:** straight, direct, exact, correct, proper, honest
**Usage:** Used attributively and in compounds to indicate directness, exactness, or correctness. Base form from which *nērmai* and *nēṟtti* derive; used independently as an adjective or adverb to evaluate conduct, speech, or direction as direct and without deviation. Appears in *nēr-mai* (honesty), *nēr-mukaṃ* (face-to-face), and evaluative phrases about accuracy or proper conduct.

**18.**
**Form:** நியாயம்
**Transliteration:** niyāyam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Sanskrit *nyāya* (rule, logic, justice); "justice, reason, rightness"
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, reasonableness, justification, logic
**Usage:** Used in moral and legal contexts to evaluate whether an action, decision, or claim is just or well-founded. Also used in everyday speech to assert that something is fair or reasonable: *itu niyāyamā?* (Is this right/fair?). Used in philosophical contexts to mean logical reasoning or valid argument, reflecting its Sanskrit heritage. Productive: *niyāyamāna* (just, fair, reasonable), *niyāyam keḷ* (to ask for justice).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
