# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** This expansion follows the same scope and orthographic conventions as P1 (Southern Quechua, primarily Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties; normalized three-vowel orthography). Attestation is drawn from published grammars, dictionaries (principally Cusihuamán 1976, Soto Ruiz 1976, Itier 1997, and the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua 2005 dictionary), and documented literary and legal corpora. Where attestation is weaker or variety-specific, epistemic status is marked accordingly.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: cheqa**
- **Root form:** cheqa
- **P1 items participating:** 6 (cheqa, cheqam/cheqami, cheqaq, cheqachiy, cheqan, cheqanta rimay)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding truth, correctness, genuineness, and authenticity. It is the foundational morpheme of the truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua, participating in nominal, adjectival, verbal, and evidential constructions. It anchors the semantic field of accurate correspondence between representation and reality, and of moral integrity in persons.

---

**Root 2: llulla**
- **Root form:** llulla
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (llulla, llullakuy, llullaq)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding falsehood, deception, and dishonesty. It is the direct antonym of *cheqa* in the truth-evaluation domain and participates in derivations covering the act of lying, the character of a liar, and the quality of being false or counterfeit. It is the central morpheme for the negative pole of the truth domain.

---

**Root 3: sut'i**
- **Root form:** sut'i
- **P1 items participating:** 2 (sut'i, sut'ichay)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes clarity, plainness, explicitness, and distinctness. While not a direct synonym of *cheqa*, it contributes a related dimension of the truth-evaluation domain — the quality of being unambiguous, transparent, and evident. It participates in evaluations of communicative accuracy and representational precision, and appears in identity and naming contexts.

**Note on root count:** *Pantay* (to err) and *allin* (good/correct) each participate in 2–3 P1 items and are semantically relevant, but they function more as evaluative predicates than as domain-defining roots. *Cheqa*, *llulla*, and *sut'i* are the morphemes most specifically and productively associated with the truth-evaluation domain proper. *Pantay* and *allin* are noted in Step 3 where relevant.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: cheqa

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root; "true, real, correct, straight"
**Natural English translation:** true, real, correct, genuine, right
**Context of use:** Used as a bare adjective or adverb in predications of truth, correctness, and authenticity across all registers. The base form from which all derivatives below are built.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqam / cheqami
**Transliteration:** cheqam / cheqami
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + validating evidential clitic *-m/-mi*; "it is true (I assert this with direct knowledge)"
**Natural English translation:** it is true, indeed, certainly, that is correct
**Context of use:** Fixed assertive expression used in discourse to affirm a proposition with direct evidential commitment. Extremely common in both everyday conversation and formal testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + agentive/participial *-q*; "one who is true, that which is true"
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective characterizing persons of integrity and statements of factual accuracy. Attested in moral discourse and in descriptions of authentic objects or persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqachiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y*; "to cause to be true/correct"
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts for the act of establishing accuracy or correcting error. Appears in testimony and document-verification contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqan
**Transliteration:** cheqan
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + third-person possessive/nominalizer *-n*; "its truth, the correct one"
**Natural English translation:** the truth, the correct one, what is right
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the truth of a matter or the correct version of something. Appears in moral instruction and legal testimony contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** cheqanta rimay
**Transliteration:** cheqanta rimay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa-n-ta (truth-POSS-ACC) + rimay (to speak); "to speak the truth"
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
**Context of use:** Conventionalized verbal expression used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to enjoin or describe truthful speech. Appears in testimony and moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** cheqa runa
**Transliteration:** cheqa runa
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + runa (person); "a true/genuine person"
**Natural English translation:** an upright person, a person of integrity, a genuine person
**Context of use:** Used in moral evaluation of persons to characterize someone as honest, upright, and authentic. A standard collocation in moral discourse across Southern Quechua varieties.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** cheqa simi
**Transliteration:** cheqa simi
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + simi (mouth, language, word); "true word/speech"
**Natural English translation:** true speech, honest word, the truth (as spoken)
**Context of use:** Used to characterize speech or a statement as truthful and honest. *Simi* (literally "mouth") functions as the standard term for speech, language, and word in Quechua. The collocation appears in moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** cheqa rimay
**Transliteration:** cheqa rimay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + rimay (to speak); "to speak truly"
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to speak honestly, truthful speech
**Context of use:** Used as both a verbal expression (to speak truthfully) and a nominalized form (truthful speech). Appears in moral instruction and in descriptions of honest communication. Closely related to *cheqanta rimay* but with *cheqa* functioning adverbially rather than as a possessed nominal.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** cheqakayta
**Transliteration:** cheqakayta
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + stative/inchoative *-ka* + infinitive *-y* + accusative *-ta*; "the being true/real (as object of a verb)"
**Natural English translation:** being true, being real, the truth of (something)
**Context of use:** Nominalized verbal form used as the object of verbs of knowing, showing, or proving. Appears in philosophical and legal discourse when the truth-status of a proposition is itself under discussion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** cheqakaychiy
**Transliteration:** cheqakaychiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + stative *-ka* + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y*; "to cause to become true/real, to realize, to actualize"
**Natural English translation:** to realize, to actualize, to make real, to bring about
**Context of use:** Used in contexts where a plan, promise, or intention is brought into reality. Appears in administrative and political discourse as well as in religious contexts (fulfilling a vow or prophecy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** cheqa yachay
**Transliteration:** cheqa yachay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/correct) + yachay (knowledge); "true knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** true knowledge, correct understanding, genuine learning
**Context of use:** Used in educational and philosophical contexts to distinguish genuine understanding from superficial or mistaken knowledge. Appears in discussions of proper learning and in religious instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** cheqa iñiy
**Transliteration:** cheqa iñiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + iñiy (belief/faith); "true belief, true faith"
**Natural English translation:** true faith, genuine belief, sincere trust
**Context of use:** Used extensively in Christian missionary and catechetical Quechua to distinguish orthodox faith from false or superficial belief. Also appears in pre-Christian religious contexts in colonial documents.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** cheqa Dios
**Transliteration:** cheqa Dios
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/real) + Dios (God, loanword from Spanish); "the true God"
**Natural English translation:** the true God, the real God
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation in Christian Quechua religious discourse, used to distinguish the Christian deity from what missionaries characterized as false gods. Extensively attested in colonial and modern catechetical texts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] *(in Christian Quechua religious register)*

---

**15.**
**Form:** mana cheqa
**Transliteration:** mana cheqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** mana (negation) + cheqa (true/correct); "not true, not correct"
**Natural English translation:** untrue, incorrect, false, not genuine
**Context of use:** The standard negated form of *cheqa*, used to characterize statements, persons, or things as false, incorrect, or inauthentic. Functions as a fixed evaluative collocation across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** mana cheqaq
**Transliteration:** mana cheqaq
**Morpheme breakdown:** mana (negation) + cheqaq (true/genuine); "not genuine, not real"
**Natural English translation:** not genuine, inauthentic, false
**Context of use:** Used to characterize persons or things as lacking authenticity or integrity. Appears in moral condemnation and in evaluations of counterfeit objects or dishonest persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** cheqa sonqo
**Transliteration:** cheqa sonqo
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + sonqo (heart, inner self, mind); "true-hearted"
**Natural English translation:** sincere, honest at heart, genuine in feeling, wholehearted
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person as genuinely sincere, honest in their inner motivations, and not merely performing honesty outwardly. *Sonqo* (heart/inner self) is the standard locus of inner states in Quechua. Appears in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** cheqa sonqowan
**Transliteration:** cheqa sonqowan
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + sonqo (heart) + *-wan* (instrumental/comitative); "with a true heart, wholeheartedly"
**Natural English translation:** sincerely, wholeheartedly, with genuine feeling
**Context of use:** Used as an adverbial expression to characterize actions performed with genuine sincerity and inner commitment. Appears in religious, moral, and interpersonal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** cheqapuni
**Transliteration:** cheqapuni
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + emphatic *-puni* (definitely, absolutely, without exception); "absolutely true, certainly real"
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, certainly real, definitely correct
**Context of use:** Used to assert truth or genuineness with strong emphasis and without qualification. The suffix *-puni* adds a sense of incontrovertibility. Appears in emphatic assertions in everyday and formal speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** cheqataq
**Transliteration:** cheqataq
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + contrastive/additive *-taq*; "and indeed true, but truly"
**Natural English translation:** and indeed, but truly, and in fact
**Context of use:** Used in discourse to introduce a contrastive or confirmatory assertion — affirming that something is indeed the case, often in contrast to a prior doubt or denial. Appears in conversational and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** cheqa hina
**Transliteration:** cheqa hina
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/real) + hina (like, as, in the manner of); "as if true, like the real thing"
**Natural English translation:** as if real, like the genuine article, seemingly true
**Context of use:** Used to describe something that resembles or appears to be genuine without necessarily being so, or to describe something that is as good as the real thing. Appears in both evaluative and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** cheqamanta
**Transliteration:** cheqamanta
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa-n (truth-POSS) + *-manta* (ablative, "from, about, concerning"); "from/about the truth, truly, in truth"
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, from truth, about what is true
**Context of use:** Used as an adverbial expression meaning "truly" or "in truth," or as a postpositional phrase meaning "about the truth." Appears in formal assertions and in discourse about truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** cheqa kawsay
**Transliteration:** cheqa kawsay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + kawsay (life, living, existence); "true life, genuine existence"
**Natural English translation:** true life, genuine living, authentic existence
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, religious, and moral discourse to characterize a life lived in accordance with truth and proper values. Appears in Christian Quechua texts (eternal/true life) and in Andean philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** cheqa ñan
**Transliteration:** cheqa ñan
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/correct/straight) + ñan (road, path, way); "the true/straight path"
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the true way, the correct road
**Context of use:** Used metaphorically in moral and religious discourse to characterize the correct course of conduct or belief. Appears in Christian catechetical texts and in Andean moral instruction. The spatial meaning (a straight road) and the moral meaning coexist.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** cheqa tukuy
**Transliteration:** cheqa tukuy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/real) + tukuy (to become, to pretend to be, to finish); "to become real, to pretend to be true"
**Natural English translation:** to become real, to be realized; also: to pretend to be genuine
**Context of use:** Used in two related senses: (1) the actualization of something into reality, and (2) the pretense of being genuine when one is not. The second sense is ironic or critical. Appears in narrative and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** cheqa sutiy
**Transliteration:** cheqa sutiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/real) + suti (name) + *-y* (first-person possessive or infinitive); "true name, real name"
**Natural English translation:** true name, real name, proper name
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the genuine or proper name of a person or thing, as opposed to a nickname, alias, or mistaken identification. Appears in legal and administrative contexts and in discussions of identity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** cheqachikuy
**Transliteration:** cheqachikuy
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y*; "to verify for oneself, to confirm oneself, to prove oneself"
**Natural English translation:** to verify for oneself, to confirm, to prove oneself (to be genuine)
**Context of use:** Used in contexts where a person establishes their own credibility or verifies something personally. The reflexive *-ku* marks the action as self-directed. Appears in legal and interpersonal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** cheqa willay
**Transliteration:** cheqa willay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + willay (to inform, to report, to tell); "to report truly, truthful report"
**Natural English translation:** to report truthfully, a truthful account, honest information
**Context of use:** Used in contexts of reporting or informing where the accuracy and honesty of the report is emphasized. *Willay* (to inform/report) is the standard verb for conveying information to another. Appears in legal testimony and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** cheqa qillqay
**Transliteration:** cheqa qillqay
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/correct) + qillqay (to write, writing); "to write correctly, accurate writing"
**Natural English translation:** to write accurately, correct writing, truthful documentation
**Context of use:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to characterize written records as accurate and authentic. Appears in discussions of document verification and in educational contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** cheqa rimasqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa rimasqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + rimay (to speak) + past participle *-sqa*; "that which has been spoken truly"
**Natural English translation:** what has been truly said, a truthful statement, honest speech (as a completed act)
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a statement already made as having been truthful. Appears in legal and testimonial contexts when evaluating prior speech acts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The root *cheqa* is highly productive. Additional attested forms exist beyond those listed above, including further combinations with postpositions, additional nominal bases, and regional variants. The 30 items above represent the most commonly attested and cross-varietally documented expressions.

### ROOT 2: llulla

---

**1.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root; "false, lying, deceptive"
**Natural English translation:** false, lying, deceptive, fake, untrue
**Context of use:** The base adjective/noun used of persons (a liar), statements (a false claim), and objects (a counterfeit). The primary term for falsehood across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + reflexive/middle *-ku* + infinitive *-y*; "to lie, to deceive"
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods
**Context of use:** The standard verb for the act of lying or deceiving. Used in moral condemnation and in legal contexts. The reflexive *-ku* marks the action as internally motivated.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** llullaq
**Transliteration:** llullaq
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + agentive *-q*; "one who lies"
**Natural English translation:** liar, deceiver, false person
**Context of use:** Used as a noun or adjective to characterize a person as habitually dishonest. Carries strong moral condemnation. Attested widely across Southern Quechua varieties.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** llullakuq
**Transliteration:** llullakuq
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + reflexive *-ku* + agentive *-q*; "one who habitually lies/deceives"
**Natural English translation:** habitual liar, chronic deceiver
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person as one who regularly and habitually engages in deception. The addition of *-ku* to the agentive form emphasizes the habitual or self-directed nature of the deception. Appears in moral condemnation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** llullasqa
**Transliteration:** llullasqa
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + past participle *-sqa*; "having been deceived, deceived"
**Natural English translation:** deceived, having been lied to, taken in by a falsehood
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person who has been the victim of deception. Appears in narrative and legal contexts when describing the outcome of dishonest acts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** llulla simi
**Transliteration:** llulla simi
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false/lying) + simi (mouth, word, speech); "false speech, lying words"
**Natural English translation:** false speech, lying words, deceptive language
**Context of use:** Used to characterize speech or a statement as dishonest and deceptive. The direct antonym of *cheqa simi*. Appears in moral condemnation of dishonest communication and in legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** llulla rimay
**Transliteration:** llulla rimay
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + rimay (to speak); "to speak falsely, false speech"
**Natural English translation:** to speak falsely, to lie (in speech), false talk
**Context of use:** Used as both a verbal expression (to speak dishonestly) and a nominalized form (dishonest speech). The direct antonym of *cheqa rimay*. Appears in moral instruction and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** llulla willay
**Transliteration:** llulla willay
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + willay (to inform, to report); "false report, to report falsely"
**Natural English translation:** false report, misinformation, to report falsely
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to characterize a report or testimony as false or misleading. Appears in discussions of perjury and false accusation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** llulla runa
**Transliteration:** llulla runa
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false/lying) + runa (person); "a false/lying person"
**Natural English translation:** a dishonest person, a liar, a deceitful person
**Context of use:** Used in moral evaluation to characterize a person as fundamentally dishonest. The direct antonym of *cheqa runa*. Appears in moral instruction and in character assessments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** llulla sonqo
**Transliteration:** llulla sonqo
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false/deceptive) + sonqo (heart, inner self); "false-hearted, deceitful at heart"
**Natural English translation:** false-hearted, deceitful, insincere, two-faced
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person whose inner motivations are dishonest, who is deceptive at the level of their inner self. The direct antonym of *cheqa sonqo*. Appears in moral condemnation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** llulla iñiy
**Transliteration:** llulla iñiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + iñiy (belief/faith); "false belief, false faith"
**Natural English translation:** false belief, false faith, misplaced trust
**Context of use:** Used in religious discourse (particularly Christian catechetical texts) to characterize heterodox or idolatrous belief as false. Also used in everyday contexts for misplaced trust.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** llulla Dios
**Transliteration:** llulla Dios
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + Dios (God, loanword); "false god"
**Natural English translation:** false god, idol, counterfeit deity
**Context of use:** Used in Christian missionary and catechetical Quechua to characterize non-Christian deities as false. Extensively attested in colonial religious texts. The direct antonym of *cheqa Dios*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] *(in Christian Quechua religious register)*

**13.**

**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + kawsay (life, living); "false life, inauthentic existence"
**Natural English translation:** false life, inauthentic living, a life of deception
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse to characterize a life built on dishonesty or false values. Appears in Christian Quechua texts and in Andean moral instruction.

---

**14.**
**Form:** llulla ñan
**Transliteration:** llulla ñan
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false/deceptive) + ñan (road, path, way); "the false path, the wrong way"
**Natural English translation:** the wrong path, the false way, the path of deception
**Context of use:** Used metaphorically in moral and religious discourse to characterize a course of conduct or belief as dishonest or morally wrong. The direct antonym of *cheqa ñan*. Appears in moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** llulla tukuy
**Transliteration:** llulla tukuy
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + tukuy (to become, to pretend to be); "to pretend to be, to feign, to act falsely"
**Natural English translation:** to pretend, to feign, to act as if, to put on a false appearance
**Context of use:** Used to describe the act of pretending or feigning — presenting a false appearance of being something one is not. Appears in narrative, moral, and legal discourse. A common expression for dissimulation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** llullachiy
**Transliteration:** llullachiy
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y*; "to cause to lie, to make (someone) deceive, to deceive (someone)"
**Natural English translation:** to deceive (someone), to cause to lie, to mislead
**Context of use:** Used in contexts where one person causes another to be deceived or induces another to lie. The causative construction shifts agency to the person who engineers the deception. Appears in legal and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** llullachikuy
**Transliteration:** llullachikuy
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + causative *-chi* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y*; "to deceive oneself, to let oneself be deceived"
**Natural English translation:** to deceive oneself, to be self-deceived, to let oneself be misled
**Context of use:** Used to describe self-deception or the state of allowing oneself to be deceived. Appears in moral and psychological discourse about the dangers of wishful thinking or willful ignorance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** llullayta yachay
**Transliteration:** llullayta yachay
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla + *-y* (infinitive) + *-ta* (accusative) + yachay (to know); "to know how to lie, to be skilled at deceiving"
**Natural English translation:** to know how to lie, to be skilled at deception
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person as experienced or skilled in deception. Appears in narrative contexts and in moral condemnation of habitual liars.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** mana llulla
**Transliteration:** mana llulla
**Morpheme breakdown:** mana (negation) + llulla (false/lying); "not false, not lying"
**Natural English translation:** not false, not deceptive, honest, genuine
**Context of use:** Used to affirm that a person or statement is not deceptive. Functions as a positive characterization by negating the negative term. Appears in testimonial and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** llulla qillqay
**Transliteration:** llulla qillqay
**Morpheme breakdown:** llulla (false) + qillqay (to write, writing); "false writing, fraudulent document"
**Natural English translation:** false document, fraudulent writing, forged record
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to characterize a written record as falsified or fraudulent. Appears in discussions of document fraud and forgery.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The root *llulla* is moderately productive. The items above represent the principal attested compounds and collocations. The root does not appear to generate as many derivatives as *cheqa*, and most extensions follow the same structural patterns (llulla + nominal base, or llulla + verbal derivation). Fewer than 5 additional common forms beyond those listed are attested in the sources consulted.

### ROOT 3: sut'i

---

**1.**
**Form:** sut'i
**Transliteration:** sut'i
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root; "clear, plain, explicit, evident, distinct"
**Natural English translation:** clear, plain, explicit, evident, distinct, unambiguous
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate statements, explanations, and representations as clear and unambiguous. Also used of identity and naming. The base form from which all derivatives below are built.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** sut'ichay
**Transliteration:** sut'ichay
**Morpheme breakdown:** sut'i + causative *-cha* + infinitive *-y*; "to make clear, to clarify"
**Natural English translation:** to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify
**Context of use:** Used in contexts of clarification, specification, and disambiguation. Appears in both everyday and formal registers when a statement or situation requires greater precision.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** sut'im / sut'imi
**Transliteration:** sut'im / sut'imi
**Morpheme breakdown:** sut'i + validating evidential *-m/-mi*; "it is clear, it is evident (I assert this directly)"
**Natural English translation:** it is clear, it is evident, it is plain
**Context of use:** Fixed assertive expression used to affirm that something is evident or unambiguous, with direct evidential commitment from the speaker. Appears in discourse to confirm that a matter is beyond doubt.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** sut'iq
**Transliteration:** sut'iq

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are expressions where the roots *cheqa*, *llulla*, and *sut'i* appear in contexts where their contribution seems to shift meaning or function compared to their core truth-domain use. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**cheqa** appearing in non-truth contexts:

- **cheqa ñan** (cheqa + ñan, "road/path"): The spatial meaning of *cheqa* as "straight" (a straight road, as opposed to a winding one) coexists with the moral meaning (the right path). The geometric/spatial sense of straightness is present alongside the evaluative sense.

- **cheqa kawsay** (cheqa + kawsay, "life/living"): In some Andean philosophical discourse, *cheqa kawsay* appears in discussions of what constitutes authentic or proper human existence, extending beyond truth-of-statements into questions of ontological authenticity and proper mode of being.

- **cheqakaychiy** (cheqa + stative + causative): In administrative and political discourse, this form appears in contexts of "realizing" or "actualizing" plans and promises — a shift from truth-evaluation toward actualization and fulfillment of intentions.

- **cheqa** in degree/intensity contexts: In some attested usages, *cheqa* functions as an intensifier or degree marker (e.g., *cheqa hatun*, "truly big, very big"), where the contribution appears to be one of degree or emphasis rather than truth-evaluation per se.

- **cheqa runa** in identity contexts: In some Andean discourse, *cheqa runa* appears in discussions of what constitutes a proper or complete human being in a cultural or ontological sense, extending beyond moral integrity into questions of cultural identity and essential nature.

---

**llulla** appearing in non-truth contexts:

- **llulla tukuy** (llulla + tukuy, "to become/pretend"): This expression appears in narrative contexts describing theatrical or ritual performance — where the "pretending" is not morally condemned but is a recognized mode of representation (e.g., in festival drama). The boundary between condemned deception and recognized performance is not always lexically marked.

- **llulla** applied to objects: When *llulla* is applied to counterfeit or imitation objects (e.g., *llulla qullqi*, "false money/counterfeit coin"), the root's contribution shifts from the moral domain of lying to the domain of material authenticity and economic fraud.

- **llulla** in degree contexts: In some attested usages, *llulla* appears to function as a marker of something being merely apparent or superficial rather than genuinely false — a shift toward appearance/reality contrast rather than moral condemnation.

---

**sut'i** appearing in non-truth contexts:

- **sut'i** as a proper name: *Sut'i* is itself used as a personal name in Quechua-speaking communities, a use that is entirely outside the evaluative domain.

- **sut'i sutin** in taxonomic contexts: The expression appears in contexts of biological and botanical naming, where the concern is precise identification and correct nomenclature rather than truth-evaluation in the moral or epistemic sense.

- **sut'i** in aesthetic contexts: In some literary and poetic discourse, *sut'i* appears in evaluations of stylistic clarity and elegance — where clarity is valued as an aesthetic property of language rather than as an epistemic or moral virtue.

- **sut'ichay** in administrative contexts: The verb appears in contexts of formally specifying or designating something (e.g., specifying the terms of an agreement), where the action is one of institutional specification rather than epistemic clarification.
