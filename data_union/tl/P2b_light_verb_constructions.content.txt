# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)
# Layer: P2b_light_verb_constructions

---

**Note on scope:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions already documented in P1b (collocations, discourse particles, fixed phrases) are cross-referenced rather than re-documented in full; only constructions not yet captured are given full entries here. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven multi-word expressions that are conventionalized and attested. Compositionally transparent phrases that are not lexicalized are excluded.

"Multi-word construction" is understood here as a syntactically-driven, periphrastic, or idiomatic combination of two or more words that functions as a unit in discourse and is not reducible to a single derived form already documented in P2a. The constraint against generating constructions to fill expected semantic roles is strictly observed; where no common construction is identified for a given base form or pattern, this is explicitly stated.

Tagalog multi-word constructions frequently involve discourse particles (*na*, *nga*, *pala*, *naman*, *ba*, *pa*) that modulate epistemic stance, evidentiality, and pragmatic force. These particle combinations are documented where they form conventionalized units with P1 items rather than being freely compositional.

## Item 1 — Base Form: totoo

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo ba iyon?
- **Transliteration:** Totoo ba iyon?
- **English Translation:** Is that true? / Is that real? / Really?
- **Usage Notes:** The construction *totoo ba* + demonstrative/noun phrase is the standard interrogative formula for challenging or requesting confirmation of a claim. The particle *ba* marks the utterance as a yes/no question; *iyon* (distal demonstrative) is the most common variant, though *iyan* (proximal-to-addressee) also occurs. The construction as a whole functions as a fixed epistemic inquiry formula across all registers; it functions both as a genuine information-seeking question and as an expression of surprise or disbelief. Cross-referenced from P1b.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.
- **Transliteration:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.
- **English Translation:** To tell the truth, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
- **Usage Notes:** *Sa totoo lang* functions as a conventionalized discourse-initial marker of candor, introducing a statement the speaker presents as frank or unguarded. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the most frequent periphrastic honesty-framing construction in spoken Tagalog.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
- **Transliteration:** Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
- **English Translation:** The truth of the matter is, the situation is more complicated.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang totoo nito* is a fixed topic-marking construction used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement about a specific matter. *Ang totoo* uses *totoo* as a nominalized head followed by a genitive phrase (*nito* — "of this"), functioning as a discourse-initial formula pragmatically equivalent to "the fact of the matter is." Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard topic-fronted truth-framing construction in formal and journalistic registers, as well as in spoken Tagalog.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
- **English Translation:** True or not, we need to find out.
- **Usage Notes:** *Totoo o hindi* is a fixed binary-framing construction presenting truth and falsity as the exhaustive alternatives for a claim under investigation. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. The *man o hindi* variant (*totoo man o hindi*) adds a concessive force ("even if") and is equally attested in argumentative and deliberative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak.
- **Transliteration:** Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak.
- **English Translation:** Her crying seemed real. / His crying looked genuine.
- **Usage Notes:** *Parang totoo* is a conventionalized appearance-framing construction asserting resemblance to reality without asserting reality itself. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for evaluating verisimilitude in performance or representation. The *parang* + adjective pattern is productive, but *parang totoo* is particularly conventionalized in discourse about appearances versus reality.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala.
- **Transliteration:** Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala.
- **English Translation:** The product looked real but turned out to be fake.
- **Usage Notes:** *Mukhang totoo* is a conventionalized appearance-evaluation construction using *mukha* (face/appearance) as a grammaticalized evidential of visual impression. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. The contrast with *peke* in the same sentence is a highly attested pattern in consumer and fraud discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo ang nararamdaman ko.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo ang nararamdaman ko.
- **English Translation:** What I feel is real. / My feelings are genuine.
- **Usage Notes:** Construction applying *totoo* to the validity of emotional experience rather than to a propositional claim. Documented in P1b peripheral extensions and cross-referenced here as a conventionalized pattern in interpersonal and therapeutic discourse for validating subjective states.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela.
- **English Translation:** The characters in the novel are true to life.
- **Usage Notes:** *Totoo sa buhay* is a conventionalized aesthetic-evaluation construction applying *totoo* as a criterion of fidelity to lived experience rather than propositional truth. Documented in P1b peripheral extensions and cross-referenced here. Used to characterize depictions, stories, or portrayals as faithfully reflecting lived experience; functions as a semi-fixed evaluative expression in literary and media discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** totoo sa sarili
- **Transliteration:** totoo sa sarili
- **English Translation:** true to oneself, authentic to one's own nature
- **Usage Notes:** This construction extends *totoo* into the domain of personal authenticity and identity; it evaluates whether a person acts in accordance with their genuine values rather than performing for others. It appears in self-help, literary, and moral discourse and is not reducible to simple propositional truth-predication.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro.
- **Transliteration:** Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro.
- **English Translation:** This is absolutely real, not a joke.
- **Usage Notes:** The emphatic reduplication *totoong-totoo* combined with the contrastive denial *hindi biro* ("not a joke") is a highly conventionalized spoken construction for asserting unambiguous reality in the face of expressed or anticipated disbelief. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo na.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo na.
- **English Translation:** It's real now. / It's actually happening. / It came true. / It's become real.
- **Usage Notes:** The combination of *totoo* with the particle *na* (marking change of state or current relevance) is a fixed conversational formula used to mark that something previously doubted, anticipated, or hypothetical has been confirmed or has come to pass. The pragmatic force is distinct from simple predication with *totoo*. Extremely common in spoken Tagalog when something previously anticipated or disbelieved is confirmed (*Totoo na ang balita* "The news is real now / The news has been confirmed").
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Hindi ko matanggap na totoo ito.
- **Transliteration:** Hindi ko matanggap na totoo ito.
- **English Translation:** I can't accept that this is true. / I can't believe this is real.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction *hindi matanggap na totoo* ("cannot accept as true") is a conventionalized expression of psychological resistance to an unwelcome truth. Common in interpersonal and emotional discourse. The verb *tanggap* (to accept, to receive) combined with *totoo* as a complement is a stable collocation in this register.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Kung totoo man o hindi...
- **Transliteration:** Kung totoo man o hindi...
- **English Translation:** Whether true or not... / Regardless of whether it's true...
- **Usage Notes:** *Kung totoo man o hindi* is a conventionalized concessive-conditional construction framing a claim as irrelevant to the truth-value of what follows. Common in argumentative and deliberative discourse. The particle *man* adds a concessive force ("even if").
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Naniniwala akong totoo ito.
- **Transliteration:** Naniniwala akong totoo ito.
- **English Translation:** I believe this is true.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction *naniniwala + na + totoo* embeds *totoo* as the complement of the belief verb *naniniwala* (believes); this is the standard construction for expressing first-person epistemic commitment to the truth of a proposition in Tagalog. Extremely common across all registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Pinapatunayan na totoo ang kanyang sinabi.
- **Transliteration:** Pinapatunayan na totoo ang kanyang sinabi.
- **English Translation:** It is being proven that what he said is true.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction embedding *totoo* as a complement clause after a verification verb (*pinapatunayan*, *napatunayan*, *natuklasan*, etc.) is a productive and conventionalized pattern in legal and journalistic discourse for reporting the confirmation of a claim's truth.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo nga pala.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo nga pala.
- **English Translation:** So it really is true. / It turns out it's actually true.
- **Usage Notes:** The combination of *nga* (confirmatory/emphatic) and *pala* (realization, discovery) in a single utterance marks a speaker's arrival at a confirmed realization — something suspected or told to them has now been verified. This particle cluster with *totoo* is a conventionalized expression of epistemic update.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
- **Transliteration:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
- **English Translation:** The truth is, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang totoo* used as a sentence-initial discourse marker, followed by a comma pause, functions as a hedging or honesty-signaling formula before a potentially face-threatening admission. This use is pragmatically fixed and common across registers. Documented in P1b; included here as a multi-word construction pattern.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo naman ang sinabi ko.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo naman ang sinabi ko.
- **English Translation:** What I said is indeed true. / It really is true, what I said. / It's true, though. / Well, that's actually true.
- **Usage Notes:** The particle *naman* introduces a concessive or turn-managing function; this construction is used to concede a point in dialogue or to assert truth in response to an expressed or implied doubt. The combination is pragmatically fixed as a concessive acknowledgment or responsive affirmation formula in conversational Tagalog.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Hindi totoo iyan.
- **Transliteration:** Hindi totoo iyan.
- **English Translation:** That is not true. / That is false.
- **Usage Notes:** The negation *hindi* + *totoo* is the standard formula for denying the truth of a proposition. While compositionally transparent, it is the fixed and expected form for formal and informal denial of truth-claims and appears in legal denials, press statements, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo pa rin iyan.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo pa rin iyan.
- **English Translation:** That is still true. / That remains true nonetheless.
- **Usage Notes:** The combination *pa rin* (still, nonetheless, even so) with *totoo* forms a construction asserting the persistence of a truth despite contrary expectations or changed circumstances. Common in argumentative and conversational discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Hindi naman totoo iyon.
- **Transliteration:** Hindi naman totoo iyon.
- **English Translation:** That's not really true. / That isn't actually true.
- **Usage Notes:** The combination of *hindi* (negation) + *naman* (contrastive/softening particle) + *totoo* forms a conventionalized denial that is softer than bare *hindi totoo*, often used to correct a misconception without direct confrontation. Common in everyday conversational discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Absence note — totoo verbal constructions:** No common periphrastic verbal construction of the form *mag-* + *totoo* or *i-* + *totoo* in the truth-assertion sense is identified. The root *totoo* does not participate in productive periphrastic verbal constructions for asserting truth in the way that *tapat* participates in *magtapat*. This absence is consistent with the derivational gap noted in P2a.

## Item 2 — Base Form: katotohanan

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Sabihin mo ang buong katotohanan.
- **Transliteration:** Sabihin mo ang buong katotohanan.
- **English Translation:** Tell the whole truth.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang buong katotohanan* is a fixed nominal construction paralleling the legal formula "the whole truth." Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for demanding complete disclosure in legal and moral contexts. The construction *sabihin ang katotohanan* (without *buong*) is also a fixed imperative formula for demanding truth-telling, used in legal oaths, moral exhortations, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon.
- **Transliteration:** Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon.
- **English Translation:** The allegation has no truth to it. / The allegation is groundless.
- **Usage Notes:** *Walang katotohanan* is a fixed negative existential construction (*wala* + *-ng* + *katotohanan*) used in formal denials and press statements to dismiss a claim as entirely unfounded. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. Common in legal, political, and journalistic denial statements.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
- **Transliteration:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
- **English Translation:** There is some truth in what he said. / What he/she said has some truth to it.
- **Usage Notes:** *May katotohanan* is a fixed partial-affirmation construction acknowledging that a claim contains some truth without fully endorsing it. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction for nuanced epistemic evaluation. Functions as a conventionalized partial concession in argumentative and evaluative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 4**

- **Usage Notes:** *Katotohanan sa likod ng* is a fixed collocation introducing a revelation of what is actually the case beneath a surface account. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction in investigative journalism and narrative.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 6**

**Construction 7**

**Construction 8**

## Item 3 — Base Form: tunay

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal.
- **Transliteration:** Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal.
- **English Translation:** His/her love is absolutely genuine. / His/her love is truly real.
- **Usage Notes:** Emphatic reduplication construction documented in P1b and P2a; cross-referenced here. Used in everyday speech to emphasize emphatic genuineness, particularly in emotional and relational contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Ano ang tunay na nangyari?
- **Transliteration:** Ano ang tunay na nangyari?
- **English Translation:** What really happened? / What truly occurred?
- **Usage Notes:** *Tunay na nangyari* in an interrogative construction is a conventionalized formula for demanding the factual account of events as opposed to a reported or distorted version. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. The interrogative frame *ano ang tunay na...* is a conventionalized investigative question pattern used in testimonial and investigative contexts, often contrasted with false or distorted versions.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Ipakita ang tunay na mukha ng gobyerno.
- **Transliteration:** Ipakita ang tunay na mukha ng gobyerno.
- **English Translation:** Show the true face of the government.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tunay na mukha* is a conventionalized metaphorical construction using *mukha* (face) to refer to a person's or institution's real nature as opposed to its projected image. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction in political and critical discourse. The spatial/physical meaning of *mukha* (face) is extended to character evaluation; the construction functions as a fixed metaphorical formula in political commentary and literary discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?
- **Transliteration:** Ano ang tunay na layunin niya?
- **English Translation:** What is his/her true purpose? / What is his/her real intention?
- **Usage Notes:** *Tunay na layunin* in an interrogative construction is a conventionalized formula for questioning the actual motivation behind an action, implying that the stated purpose may be false. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. Used in investigative, political, and moral discourse to distinguish stated purposes from actual motivations.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Nakilala ko ang tunay na pagkatao niya.
- **Transliteration:** Nakilala ko ang tunay na pagkatao niya.
- **English Translation:** I came to know his/her true character.
- **Usage Notes:** *Nakilala ang tunay na pagkatao* is a conventionalized construction for describing the discovery of a person's authentic inner character, typically after extended acquaintance or a revealing situation. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Kailangan ng tunay na pagbabago.
- **Transliteration:** Kailangan ng tunay na pagbabago.
- **English Translation:** We need genuine change. / Real change is needed.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tunay na pagbabago* is a highly conventionalized political construction distinguishing substantive reform from cosmetic or nominal change. Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction in political rhetoric.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** tunay
- **Example:** Tunay na naniniwala ako.
- **Transliteration:** Tunay na naniniwala ako.
- **English Translation:** I truly believe. / I genuinely believe.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tunay na* functioning as an adverbial modifier before a verb of belief or attitude is a conventionalized construction for asserting the sincerity of one's
