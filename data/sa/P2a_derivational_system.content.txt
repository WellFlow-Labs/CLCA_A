# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Derivational Patterns: Attested Forms

### BASE: सत्य (satya) — "truth, true, real"

#### D1.1
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यता (satyatā)  
**Transliteration:** satyatā  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + -tā (abstract suffix)  
**Translation:** truthfulness, truth, reality  
**Usage description:** Abstract noun denoting the quality or state of being true. Used in philosophical discourse for the property of truth and in ethical contexts for the virtue of truthfulness.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.2
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यवादिन् (satyavādin)  
**Transliteration:** satyavādin  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + vādin (speaking, from √vad + -in agent suffix)  
**Translation:** truth-speaking, truthful person  
**Usage description:** Agent noun describing one who habitually speaks truth. Common in ethical and legal discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.3
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यवचन (satyavacana)  
**Transliteration:** satyavacana  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + vacana (speech, statement)  
**Translation:** true statement, truthful speech  
**Usage description:** Compound denoting a true utterance or truthful speech act. Used in contexts requiring verification of statements.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.4
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यव्रत (satyavrata)  
**Transliteration:** satyavrata  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + vrata (vow, observance)  
**Translation:** vow of truthfulness, committed to truth  
**Usage description:** Compound denoting commitment to truthfulness as religious or moral discipline. Also used as proper name.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.5
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यसन्ध (satyasandha)  
**Transliteration:** satyasandha  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + sandha (binding, keeping, from √dhā)  
**Translation:** keeping truth, faithful to promises  
**Usage description:** Compound describing one who keeps promises and commitments. Used in moral and legal contexts for reliability.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.6
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यापन (satyāpana)  
**Transliteration:** satyāpana  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + āpana (causing to reach, from ā + √āp + -ana)  
**Translation:** verification, confirmation  
**Usage description:** Modern administrative term for verification or authentication of facts. Used in legal and bureaucratic contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.7
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यनिष्ठ (satyaniṣṭha)  
**Transliteration:** satyaniṣṭha  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + niṣṭha (devoted to, established in)  
**Translation:** devoted to truth, firmly established in truth  
**Usage description:** Compound describing unwavering commitment to truthfulness. Used in moral and spiritual discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.8
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यार्थ (satyārtha)  
**Transliteration:** satyārtha  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + artha (meaning, object)  
**Translation:** true meaning, real purpose  
**Usage description:** Compound denoting genuine meaning or purpose as opposed to apparent or superficial sense. Used in interpretive contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.9
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यभाषिन् (satyabhāṣin)  
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣin  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + bhāṣin (speaking, from √bhāṣ + -in)  
**Translation:** truth-speaking, truthful speaker  
**Usage description:** Agent noun similar to satyavādin, describing one who speaks truth. Used in moral and legal contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.10
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यधर्म (satyadharma)  
**Transliteration:** satyadharma  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + dharma (duty, righteousness)  
**Translation:** duty of truthfulness, truth as religious duty  
**Usage description:** Compound denoting truthfulness as moral or religious obligation. Used in ethical discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### D1.11
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)  
**Derived Form:** सत्यक्रिया (satyakriyā)  
**Transliteration:** satyakriyā  
**Literal Breakdown:** satya + kriyā (action, rite)  
**Translation:** act of truth, truth-act  
**Usage description:** Ritual or solemn affirmation of truth, sometimes with supernatural efficacy. Used in narrative contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### D1.12

## Summary Notes

**Productivity patterns observed:**

1. **-tā/-tva abstract suffixes:** Highly productive across the domain, forming abstract nouns from adjectives and nouns (satyatā, mithyātva, pramāṇatva, yathārthatā, etc.)

2. **-in possessive/agent suffix:** Common for forming agent nouns and possessives (satyavādin, mithyāvādin, dharmin, svarūpin)

3. **-ika adjectival suffix:** Productive for forming adjectives from nouns (dhārmika, vyāvahārika, pāramārthika, śābdika)

4. **Compound formation:** Extremely productive, especially:
   - satya- compounds (satyavacana, satyavrata, satyasandha, etc.)
   - mithyā- compounds (mithyācāra, mithyājñāna, mithyādṛṣṭi, etc.)
   - Compounds with second members like -vādin, -jñāna, -dṛṣṭi

5. **Gerundive -eya:** Used for forming "to be X-ed" forms in epistemological contexts (prameya, anumeya)

6. **Agent suffix -tṛ:** Used for epistemological agents (pramātā)

**Gaps and asymmetries:**

- Some bases like विपर्यय (viparyaya) and भ्रान्ति (bhrānti) show no common derivations beyond their own formation
- Vedic terms (ṛta, anṛta) show limited derivational productivity compared to classical terms
- Modern administrative vocabulary (pramāṇīkaraṇa, pramāṇapatra, satyāpana) represents recent extensions

**Domain coherence:**

Derivational patterns strongly support the epistemological-moral nexus, with parallel formations across truth/falsity pairs and consistent patterns for forming agent nouns, abstract qualities, and compound elaborations.
