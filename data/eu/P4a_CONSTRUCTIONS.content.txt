# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X da** (X is) — predicative construction with copula "da" (is) for singular subjects, "dira" (are) for plural. The truth-domain noun appears as predicate nominal.

### Examples
- **Hori egia da**
  - hori egia da
  - "That is truth / That is true"

- **Gezurra da**
  - gezurra da
  - "It is a lie"

### Notes
Standard predicative construction. The article -a on the predicate nominal is obligatory in most contexts. Used for categorical assertions about truth-status.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X ez da** (X is not) — negative predicative construction with negator "ez" preceding the copula. Same structure as affirmative but with obligatory preverbal negation.

### Examples
- **Ez da egia**
  - ez da egia
  - "It is not true"

- **Ez da zuzena**
  - ez da zuzena
  - "It is not correct"

### Notes
Basque requires preverbal negation with "ez." Very common construction for denying truth-claims. The predicate nominal retains the article.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**ADJ + N** — truth-domain adjectives (egiazko, gezurrezko, zuzen, etc.) precede the noun they modify. Adjectives typically appear in relational form with -ko/-zko suffix when attributive.

### Examples
- **egiazko historia**
  - egiazko historia
  - "true story / real history"

- **gezurrezko dokumentua**
  - gezurrezko dokumentua
  - "false document / forged document"

### Notes
Attributive adjectives precede nouns in Basque. The relational suffix -ko/-zko is standard for derived adjectives in attributive position. Very productive pattern across all truth-domain terms.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**X bihurtu** (become X) — periphrastic inchoative construction using the verb "bihurtu" (to become, turn into). The truth-domain noun appears in absolutive case (unmarked) or with directional marking.

### Examples
- **egia bihurtu**
  - egia bihurtu
  - "to become truth / to turn into truth"

- **gezur bihurtu**
  - gezur bihurtu
  - "to become a lie / to turn into a lie"

### Notes
Basque lacks a simple "become" copula; inchoative meaning requires the verb "bihurtu." Less common with abstract truth-domain nouns than with concrete nouns. Alternative constructions use specific verbs like "okertu" (to go wrong) for "oker."

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
(no common expression identified)

### Examples
N/A

### Notes
Basque does not have a productive causative construction meaning "to make X true" or "to cause X to be true." Truth is established (egiazkotu), verified (ziurtatu), or revealed (atera), but not directly caused. The morphological causative suffix -arazi does not productively combine with truth-domain roots to create causative meanings. Verification and authentication verbs (egiazkotu, ziurtatu) serve related functions but are not true causatives.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -tasun + -a** — abstract quality nominals formed with suffix -tasun (quality, state) plus article -a. Derives abstract nouns denoting the quality of being X.

### Examples
- **egiazkotasuna**
  - egia-zko-tasun-a
  - "truthfulness / authenticity / veracity"

- **zintzotasuna**
  - zintzo-tasun-a
  - "honesty / sincerity / integrity"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern for creating abstract quality nouns. The suffix -tasun attaches to adjectival bases. Not all roots support this derivation (e.g., no *gezurtasuna in standard usage). Used in formal and philosophical discourse.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -ti/-tzaile + -a** — agentive nouns formed with suffix -ti (characteristic agent) or -tzaile (professional agent) plus article -a. Creates nouns denoting persons characterized by or professionally engaged in the activity.

### Examples
- **gezurtia**
  - gezur-ti-a
  - "liar / lying person"

- **egiaztatzailea**
  - egia-zta-tzaile-a
  - "verifier / authenticator"

### Notes
Two distinct agentive patterns: -ti for characteristic agents (often pejorative: gezurti "liar"), -tzaile for professional/functional agents (zuzentzailea "corrector"). Not all roots support both patterns. The -ti pattern is less productive than -tzaile.

---

## 8. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**NOUN + VERB** — truth-domain nouns combine with semantically light verbs (esan "say," egin "do/make," izan "have/be," eman "give") to form predicates. The noun remains in absolutive case (unmarked).

### Examples
- **egia esan**
  - egia esan
  - "to tell the truth"

- **gezurra esan**
  - gezurra esan
  - "to tell a lie"

### Notes
Extremely productive pattern. The verb "esan" (say) is the primary light verb for speech acts. Other common light verbs: "egin" (make/do), "izan" (have/be), "eman" (give), "aurkitu" (find). This is the standard way to express "to lie" (gezurra esan) since no simple verb *gezurtu exists.

---

## 9. CASE-MARKED ADVERBIAL PHRASES

### Pattern
**NOUN + CASE** — truth-domain nouns take case suffixes to form adverbial expressions. Common cases: instrumental (-z), locative (-tan/-ean), genitive + postposition.

### Examples
- **egiaz**
  - egia-z
  - "with truth / truthfully / truly"

- **benetan**
  - bene-ta-n
  - "really / truly / indeed"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern for creating manner adverbials. The instrumental case -z is common for manner (egiaz "truthfully"). The locative -tan/-ean creates emphatic truth-markers (egiatan, benetan). These forms function as both manner adverbials and discourse markers.

---

## 10. GENITIVE + POSTPOSITION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**NOUN-GEN + POSTPOSITION** — truth-domain nouns in genitive case (-ren/-aren) combine with postpositions to create complex adverbial or adjectival phrases.

### Examples
- **egiaren bila**
  - egia-ren bila
  - "in search of truth"

- **egiaren alde**
  - egia-ren alde
  - "on the side of truth / in favor of truth"

### Notes
Fixed phrases with specific postpositions: "bila" (in search of), "alde" (side/favor), "aurka" (against), "kontra" (against). These create evaluative and directional expressions. Very common in moral and political discourse.

---

## 11. VERIFICATION AND AUTHENTICATION VERBS

### Pattern
**ROOT + -tu/-atu** — verbal derivations from truth-domain roots using verbalizing suffixes -tu or -atu. Creates verbs of verification, authentication, or establishment.

### Examples
- **egiazkotu**
  - egia-zko-tu
  - "to verify / to authenticate"

- **ziurtatu**
  - ziur-ta-tu
  - "to make certain / to ensure / to verify"

### Notes
Productive pattern for creating verification verbs. Multiple derivational paths exist: egiazkotu, egiaztatu (both "verify"). The suffix -tu is the standard verbalizer. Not all roots support verbal derivation (no *gezurtu "to lie" — uses light verb construction instead).

---

## 12. RELATIONAL ADJECTIVE DERIVATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -ko/-zko/-ezko** — relational adjectives formed with suffix -ko (general relational), -zko (after vowels/specific consonants), or -ezko (after -r). Creates adjectives meaning "characterized by X" or "of X."

### Examples
- **egiazkoa**
  - egia-zko-a
  - "true / genuine / authentic"

- **gezurrezko**
  - gezur-ezko
  - "false / fake / lying"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern. The choice of allomorph (-ko/-zko/-ezko) is phonologically conditioned. These forms function both attributively (before nouns) and predicatively (after copula). The article -a is added for definite/substantive use.

---

## 13. COMPARATIVE AND CONTRASTIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X ala Y** — disjunctive construction with "ala" (or) creating binary contrasts between truth-domain opposites.

### Examples
- **egia ala gezurra**
  - egia ala gezurra
  - "truth or lie"

- **zuzen ala oker**
  - zuzen ala oker
  - "right or wrong / correct or incorrect"

### Notes
Standard pattern for presenting binary choices or contrasts. Very common in questions and evaluative contexts. The particle "ala" is the standard disjunctive coordinator. These constructions often appear in interrogatives or rhetorical questions.

---

## 14. EMPHATIC REDUPLICATION

### Pattern
**ROOT-ROOT** — emphatic reduplication of truth-domain roots, typically with hyphen, to intensify meaning.

### Examples
- **gezur-gezurra**
  - gezur-gezur-a
  - "complete lie / utter lie / total falsehood"

- **zuzen-zuzen**
  - zuzen-zuzen
  - "very straight / perfectly straight / exactly right"

### Notes
Limited productivity — only certain roots allow this pattern. Creates emphatic intensification. The reduplicated form may take the article (gezur-gezurra) or remain bare (zuzen-zuzen). Used for rhetorical emphasis.

---

## 15. INTENSIFIER + NOUN COLLOCATIONS

### Pattern
**INTENSIFIER + NOUN** — truth-domain nouns combine with intensifying adjectives (hutsa "pure/mere," garbia "clear," gordina "raw," sakona "deep") to create fixed emphatic expressions.

### Examples
- **egia hutsa**
  - egia huts-a
  - "pure truth / nothing but truth"

- **gezur garbia**
  - gezur garbi-a
  - "outright lie / blatant lie"

### Notes
Semi-fixed collocations with specific intensifiers. Common intensifiers: hutsa (pure/mere), garbia (clear/clean), gordina (raw/crude), sakona (deep), handia (big). These create emphatic or qualified truth-claims. Very common in rhetorical and legal discourse.

---

## 16. CONDITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X ba-da / X ba-litz** — conditional constructions with conditional particle "ba-" attached to copula. "Ba-da" for realis conditionals, "ba-litz" for irrealis/counterfactual.

### Examples
- **egia bada**
  - egia ba-da
  - "if it is true"

- **egia balitz**
  - egia ba-litz
  - "if it were true"

### Notes
Standard conditional marking in Basque. The particle "ba-" fuses with the copula. Realis "bada" for open conditions, irrealis "balitz" for counterfactuals. Very common in argumentative and hypothetical discourse.

---

## 17. INTERROGATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X da? / X al da?** — yes/no questions formed by intonation alone (X da?) or with interrogative particle "al" (X al da?). The particle "al" adds politeness or formality.

### Examples
- **Egia da?**
  - egia da?
  - "Is it true?"

- **Zuzen al da?**
  - zuzen al da?
  - "Is it correct?" (polite/formal)

### Notes
Two strategies for yes/no questions: intonation alone (informal) or particle "al" (formal/polite). The particle "al" appears between predicate and copula. Very common in conversational discourse for seeking confirmation.

---

## 18. RELATIVE CLAUSE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X den(a)** — relative clause construction with relativized copula "den" (that is) plus optional article -a for substantivization. Creates expressions meaning "that which is X."

### Examples
- **egia dena**
  - egia den-a
  - "that which is true / what is true"

- **egiazkoa dena**
  - egiazko-a den-a
  - "that which is true/genuine"

### Notes
Standard relativization pattern. The copula "da" becomes "den" in relative clauses. The article -a substantivizes the relative clause. Used to refer to truth as a category or to true things generally.

---

## 19. LOCATIVE PLURAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**NOUN-etan + VERB** — truth-domain nouns in locative plural case (-etan) combine with motion verbs to create idiomatic expressions about being engaged in or caught in states.

### Examples
- **gezurretan harrapatu**
  - gezur-etan harrapatu
  - "to catch in lies"

- **gezurretan ibili**
  - gezur-etan ibili
  - "to go about lying / to be engaged in lying"

### Notes
Semi-productive pattern with specific verbs: harrapatu (catch), ibili (go about). The locative plural -etan suggests being "in" or "among" lies. Used for habitual or ongoing states. Limited to certain roots (primarily gezur).

---

## 20. WORTHY-OF ADJECTIVE DERIVATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -garri + -a** — adjectives formed with suffix -garri (worthy of, able to be) plus article -a. Creates adjectives meaning "worthy of X" or "able to be X-ed."

### Examples
- **sinesgarria**
  - sines-garri-a
  - "believable / credible / plausible"

- **fidagarria**
  - fida-garri-a
  - "reliable / trustworthy / dependable"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern in truth-evaluation domain. The suffix -garri derives from "garri" (worthy). Creates evaluative adjectives assessing whether something merits belief, trust, or doubt. Very common in epistemic evaluation.

---

## 21. MANNER ADVERB DERIVATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -ki** — manner adverbs formed with suffix -ki. Creates adverbs meaning "in an X manner."

### Examples
- **egiazki**
  - egia-z-ki
  - "truly / really / genuinely"

- **zintzoki**
  - zintzo-ki
  - "honestly / sincerely"

### Notes
Productive pattern for manner adverbs. The suffix -ki attaches to adjectival bases. Not all roots support this derivation (no *gezurki in standard usage — uses "gezurrez" instead). Common in both formal and informal discourse.

---

## 22. ACTION NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**VERB-STEM + -tze/-keta/-pen** — action nominals formed with suffixes -tze (general action), -keta (process), or -pen (result/action). Creates nouns denoting the act or result of the verbal action.

### Examples
- **egiaztatze**
  - egia-zta-tze
  - "verification / the act of verifying"

- **zuzenketa**
  - zuzen-keta
  - "correction / the act of correcting"

### Notes
Multiple nominalization strategies: -tze for general action, -keta for process, -pen for result. The choice depends on the verb stem and semantic nuance. Very productive for creating abstract action nouns from verification and correction verbs.

---

## 23. NEGATION WITH PARTITIVE CASE

### Pattern
**X-rik gabe** — negative construction with partitive case (-rik) plus postposition "gabe" (without). Creates expressions meaning "without X."

### Examples
- **zalantzarik gabe**
  - zalantza-rik gabe
  - "without doubt / undoubtedly"

- **egirik gabe**
  - egi-rik gabe
  - "without truth / untruthfully" (less common)

### Notes
Fixed pattern for "without X" expressions. The partitive case -rik is obligatory with "gabe." Very common with "zalantza" (doubt) to create the adverbial "undoubtedly." Functions as both adverbial and adjectival modifier.

---

## 24. DISCOURSE MARKER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TRUTH-PHRASE** — truth-domain expressions functioning as discourse markers to frame or qualify statements. Often appear sentence-initially.

### Examples
- **Egia esan**
  - egia esan
  - "to tell the truth / truth be told" (discourse marker)

- **Benetan**
  - benetan
  - "really / truly" (confirmation-seeking or emphatic)

### Notes
Several truth-domain expressions have grammaticalized as discourse markers. "Egia esan" introduces candid admissions. "Benetan" functions as confirmation-seeker or emphatic marker. These maintain truth-domain semantics while serving discourse-structuring functions.

---

## 25. COMPOUND NOUN FORMATIONS

### Pattern
**ROOT + ROOT** — compound nouns formed by combining truth-domain roots with other nouns. Creates specialized terms, especially in legal and administrative domains.

### Examples
- **zuzenbide**
  - zuzen-bide
  - "law / jurisprudence / justice" (lit. "right-path")

- **egiaztagiri**
  - egia-zta-giri
  - "certificate / authentication document"

### Notes
Productive compounding pattern. The compound "zuzenbide" (law) has fully lexicalized. Other compounds remain more transparent (egiaztagiri = verification + document). Common in technical and legal terminology. The first element typically appears in bare form.
