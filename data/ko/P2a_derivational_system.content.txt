## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS IDENTIFIED

### Pattern 1: Nominalization via -성 (-seong) 性
**Function:** Creates abstract nouns denoting quality or nature of the base

#### 1.1
**Base form:** 진실 (jinsil) — truth
**Derived form:** 진실성
**Transliteration:** jinsilseong
**Literal breakdown:** 진실 (truth) + 성 (quality/nature)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality of being truthful. Common in formal evaluative contexts assessing character or statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.2
**Base form:** 진정 (jinjeong) — genuine
**Derived form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong

## NOTES ON PRODUCTIVITY

### High productivity patterns:
- **-성 (-seong) nominalization:** Highly productive with Sino-Korean adjectives and nouns in the truth domain. Creates abstract quality nouns systematically.
- **-적 (-jeok) adjectivalization:** Very productive with Sino-Korean nouns, creating adjectives meaning "pertaining to" or "having the nature of."
- **-하다 (-hada) verbalization:** Extremely productive with Sino-Korean nouns, creating verbs and adjectives. Nearly all Sino-Korean nouns in this domain can combine with -하다.
- **-로/-으로 adverbialization:** Productive with both Sino-Korean and native Korean nouns, creating manner adverbs.

### Limited productivity patterns:
- **-쟁이 (-jaengi) agentive:** Limited to specific nouns, typically those denoting actions or behaviors. Not freely productive.
- **-배기 (-baegi) intensification:** Limited to colloquial authenticity terms (진짜, 가짜). Not widely productive.
- **-투성이 (-tuseongi) saturation:** Limited productivity, used with nouns denoting undesirable qualities or substances.

### Compound formation:
- Compound formation with specific semantic heads (말 "speech," 증언 "testimony," 증거 "evidence," 정보 "information," 뉴스 "news") is productive within semantic constraints.
- These compounds follow regular compositional patterns but are limited to semantically appropriate combinations.

### Gaps and asymmetries:
- Native Korean roots (거짓, 참) show fewer derivational possibilities than Sino-Korean roots.
- Not all Sino-Korean nouns in the domain accept all derivational suffixes (e.g., 진위 does not commonly form *진위성, *진위적, *진위하다).
- Some bases appear only in derived forms (e.g., 신빙 appears primarily in 신빙성, rarely independently).
