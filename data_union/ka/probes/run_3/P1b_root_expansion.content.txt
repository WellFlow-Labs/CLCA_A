# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / mart-
**Transliteration:** mart-
**P1 items participating:** martali (#3), simartle (#4) — 2 items directly; the root also underlies the derivational family of both.
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, rightness — covering both epistemic correctness (a true or accurate statement) and moral uprightness (a just or honest person or act). This root is the primary everyday building block for truth and correctness in Georgian.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ყალბ- / q̇alb-
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 items participating:** q̇albi (#7), q̇alboba (#8), siq̇albe (#11), siq̇albis dadastureba (#19), siq̇albis damadasturebeli (#30) — 5 items.
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, counterfeit quality, fraudulence — covering both the epistemic dimension (a false statement) and the legal-moral dimension (a forged document, a fraudulent act). This root is the primary building block for falsity and forgery in Georgian.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ჭეშმარ- / č̣ešmar-
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmar-
**P1 items participating:** č̣ešmariti (#1), č̣ešmariteba (#2) — 2 items directly; the root is highly productive in philosophical and theological registers.
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as a quality of genuine, authentic correspondence to reality — more philosophically and theologically weighted than mart-, and less associated with moral uprightness. This root is the primary building block for truth in elevated, formal, and intellectual discourse.

---

**Note on additional candidate roots:**

The root **dastur-** (confirmation, attestation) participates in dadastureba (#20), siq̇albis dadastureba (#19), and siq̇albis damadasturebeli (#30), and is highly productive in legal-administrative contexts. It is treated in the expansion below as a secondary root warranting attention, but it is not selected as a primary root because its core meaning (confirmation, rule, permission) is not intrinsically truth-evaluative — it enters the truth domain instrumentally.

The root **namdvil-** participates in namdvili (#5) and namdviloba (#6) and is productive, but its derivational family is smaller than the three roots selected above.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

### ROOT 1: mart- (მართ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართალია
**Transliteration:** martalia
**Literal breakdown:** martali + copular clitic -a + focus particle; "it is true/right"
**Natural English translation:** "that's right," "it's true," "you're correct"
**Context of use:** Extremely frequent in everyday spoken Georgian as a response particle or evaluative statement confirming the truth of what has just been said. Functions as a discourse marker of agreement and confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** martla
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + adverbial suffix -la; "truly, really, in a straight/correct manner"
**Natural English translation:** really, truly, indeed, honestly
**Context of use:** Used as an adverb in everyday speech to intensify or assert the truth of a claim ("martla mivedi," "I really went"). Also used as a question tag or challenge ("martla?" — "really?").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** martlac
**Literal breakdown:** martla + emphatic particle -c; "truly indeed"
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Used in both spoken and written Georgian to confirm or emphasize that something is genuinely the case, often in response to a prior claim or expectation. More emphatic than martla alone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლაც რომ
**Transliteration:** martlac rom
**Literal breakdown:** martlac + subordinating conjunction rom; "truly that / it is indeed the case that"
**Natural English translation:** it is indeed the case that, truly, as is indeed true
**Context of use:** Used in written and formal spoken Georgian to introduce a concessive or confirmatory clause. Appears in journalistic and argumentative prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიმართლის თქმა
**Transliteration:** simartlis tqma
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive: simartlis) + tqma ("saying, utterance"); "the saying of truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-telling, telling the truth
**Context of use:** Used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to refer to the act of stating what is true. Appears in discussions of honesty, witness testimony, and moral obligation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** სიმართლე მოითხოვს
**Transliteration:** simartle moitxovs
**Literal breakdown:** simartle + moitxovs ("demands, requires"); "truth demands"
**Natural English translation:** truth requires, honesty demands, to be truthful one must
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in written and formal spoken Georgian to introduce a statement that the speaker presents as an honest or accurate acknowledgment, often of something uncomfortable. Common in journalism and formal argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დადგენა
**Transliteration:** simartlis dadgena
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive) + dadgena ("establishment, determination"); "determination of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** establishing the truth, determination of the truth, finding the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and investigative contexts to refer to the process of determining what actually happened or what is actually the case. Appears in court proceedings and journalistic investigations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დამალვა
**Transliteration:** simartlis damalva
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive) + damalva ("hiding, concealment"); "concealment of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** concealment of the truth, hiding the truth
**Context of use:** Used in moral, legal, and journalistic contexts to characterize the act of withholding or suppressing true information. Contrasts with simartlis tqma.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიმართლის მაძიებელი
**Transliteration:** simartlis madziebeli
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive) + madziebeli ("seeker, one who searches"); "seeker of truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-seeker, one who seeks the truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, literary, and journalistic contexts to characterize a person dedicated to discovering or uncovering the truth. Appears in intellectual biography and philosophical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** სიმართლის გამოვლენა
**Transliteration:** simartlis gamovlena
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive) + gamovlena ("revelation, manifestation, bringing to light"); "bringing truth to light"
**Natural English translation:** revelation of the truth, uncovering the truth
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, legal, and literary contexts to refer to the act of making the truth known or visible. Appears in investigative journalism and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულება
**Transliteration:** martlmsajuleba
**Literal breakdown:** martl- (truth/right) + msajul- (judge, from msajuli "judge") + -eba (nominalizing suffix); "right-judging" or "just judgment"
**Natural English translation:** justice, administration of justice, judiciary
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for justice in the institutional and legal sense. Used extensively in legal texts, constitutional documents, and political discourse. Refers to the system and practice of judicial administration.

**15.**

---

**16.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებლობა
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebloba

---

**17.**
**Form:** მართებული
**Transliteration:** martebuli
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + -ebul- (resultative participial suffix) + -i; "that which has been made right/correct, that which is fitting"
**Natural English translation:** correct, appropriate, fitting, proper, well-founded
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate arguments, decisions, and statements as well-founded or appropriate. Somewhat more formal than sc̣ori or martali. Common in written Georgian, legal argumentation, and academic prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** მართებულობა
**Transliteration:** martebulobа
**Literal breakdown:** martebuli + nominalizing suffix -oba; "the property of being correct/fitting"
**Natural English translation:** correctness, appropriateness, well-foundedness, validity
**Context of use:** Used in legal, philosophical, and administrative contexts to refer to the property of being well-founded, appropriate, or valid. Appears in legal opinions evaluating the validity of decisions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** გამართლება
**Transliteration:** gamarтleba
**Literal breakdown:** ga- (preverb indicating completion/result) + mart- + -leba (verbal noun suffix); "making right, straightening out"
**Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, acquittal, excuse
**Context of use:** Used in legal contexts for acquittal or exoneration, and in moral and everyday contexts for justification or excuse. A highly productive verbal noun from the verb gamarтlebs ("to justify, to acquit, to vindicate").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** The mart- root is exceptionally productive in Georgian. The forms listed above represent the most common and well-attested expressions. Additional forms exist, particularly in legal compound terminology (e.g., further compounds with sasamartlo) and in religious discourse (further compounds with martlmadidebeli).

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions are flagged because the root's contribution appears to shift meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. No interpretation or categorization is offered.

---

**mart- root:**

- **მართლმსაჯულება** (martlmsajuleba, "justice/judiciary"): the root mart- here contributes to an institutional and procedural concept rather than a direct truth-predication.

- **მართლმადიდებელი** (martlmadidebeli, "Orthodox Christian"): the root mart- here combines with a root meaning "glorification" in a confessional-identity context.

- **მართლწერა** (martlc̣era, "orthography"): the root mart- here applies to the domain of correct written form rather than truth of propositions or moral uprightness.

- **გამართლება** (gamartleba, "justification/acquittal"): the root mart- here participates in a legal outcome concept (acquittal) and a rhetorical concept (justification/excuse) that extend beyond simple truth-predication.

- **სასამართლო** (sasamartlo, "court of law"): the root mart- here is embedded in an institutional name for a judicial body.

- **მართლა** (martla) and **მართლაც** (martlac): the root mart- here functions as an intensifying adverb or discourse particle, marking degree or emphasis rather than directly predicating truth.

---

**q̇alb- root:**

- **ყალბი გრძნობები** (q̇albi grdznobebi, "false feelings") and **ყალბი ღიმილი** (q̇albi ɣimili, "false smile"): the root q̇alb- here applies to emotional expression and interpersonal sincerity rather than to documents, currency, or propositional content.

- **ყალბი პათოსი** (q̇albi p'at'osi, "false pathos"): the root q̇alb- here applies to aesthetic and literary evaluation of emotional authenticity in artistic works.

---

**č̣ešmar- root:**

- **ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული** (č̣ešmariti siq̇varuli, "true love"): the root č̣ešmar- here applies to the quality of an emotional relationship rather than to the truth-value of a proposition.

- **ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვანი** (č̣ešmariti xelovani, "a true artist"): the root č̣ešmar- here applies to aesthetic and professional identity evaluation.

- **ჭეშმარიტი პატრიოტი** (č̣ešmariti p'at'riot'i, "a true patriot"): the root č̣ešmar- here applies to political identity and moral character evaluation.

- **ჭეშმარიტი ღირებულება** (č̣ešmariti ɣirebuleba, "true value"): the root č̣ešmar- here applies to value theory and economic or philosophical evaluation of worth.

- **ჭეშმარიტი ადამიანი** (č̣ešmariti adamiani, "a genuine/truly human person"): the root č̣ešmar- here applies to an evaluative concept of full or authentic humanity.

- **ჭეშმარიტების მომენტი** (č̣ešmaritebis moment'i, "moment of truth"): the root č̣ešmar- here participates in a temporal-dramatic concept marking a decisive or revelatory moment.
