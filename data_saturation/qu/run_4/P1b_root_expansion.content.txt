# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** This expansion follows the same scope and orthographic conventions as P1 — primarily Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka), with variety-specific notes where relevant. The three-vowel orthography with ejective digraphs and aspirate digraphs is maintained throughout. All items listed are drawn from attested sources (grammars, dictionaries, corpora, and ethnographic documentation); no forms have been constructed by analogy or translation from English.

Principal sources consulted include: Cerrón-Palomino (1994, 2003), Cusihuamán (1976), Lara (1971), Hornberger & Hornberger (1983), Itier (1997), Calvo Pérez (1993), Parker (1969), and the *Diccionario Quechua-Español-Quechua* (Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua, 2005).

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **cheqa**

**Root form:** cheqa
**P1 items participating:** 5 (cheqa, cheqaq, cheqaqpaq, cheqan, cheqanchay, cheqa simi, cheqa simi — counting compounds: items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 23)
**Count:** 6 items (items 1–5 and 23)

**Core meaning contribution:** *Cheqa* is the primary root encoding truth, genuineness, correctness, and reality. It serves as the foundational building block for the entire truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua, generating adjectival, nominal, adverbial, and verbal derivatives. Its core semantic contribution is the alignment between a representation, statement, or state of affairs and how things actually are.

---

### Root 2: **llulla**

**Root form:** llulla
**P1 items participating:** 3 (llulla, llullakuy, llulla simi — items 6, 7, 24)
**Count:** 3 items

**Core meaning contribution:** *Llulla* is the primary root encoding falsehood, deception, and inauthenticity. It is the direct semantic counterpart to *cheqa* and generates a parallel set of derivatives in the domains of lying, deception, and false representation. Its core semantic contribution is the misalignment between a representation or statement and reality, with an implication of intentional or at least culpable misrepresentation.

---

### Root 3: **sut'i**

**Root form:** sut'i
**P1 items participating:** 2 (sut'i, sut'ichay — items 8, 9)
**Count:** 2 items

**Core meaning contribution:** *Sut'i* encodes clarity, explicitness, precision, and distinctness. While it does not directly encode truth in the correspondence sense, it is a central evaluative root in the truth-evaluation domain because it characterizes the quality of statements and representations as unambiguous, exact, and transparent. It is highly productive as a derivational base and appears across legal, testimonial, and everyday evaluative contexts.

---

**Note on selection:** *Cheqa* is by far the most productive root in the truth-evaluation domain and is the primary focus of the expansion below. *Llulla* is its direct antonym and generates a parallel but somewhat smaller set of derivatives. *Sut'i* is included because it is a distinct root with significant productivity in the same domain. The evidential clitics *-mi*, *-si*, and *-chá* (P1 items 28–30) are grammatical morphemes rather than lexical roots and are therefore not treated as roots for expansion purposes, though they are cross-referenced where relevant.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: cheqa

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root; "true, real, genuine"
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, correct
**Context of use:** Base adjective used predicatively and attributively across all domains. Already listed in P1 (item 1); included here as the anchor for the expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (nominalizing/agentive suffix); "that which is true / one who is true"

**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 2); included for completeness as a primary derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaqpaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaqpaq
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + *-paq* (benefactive/purposive case); "for the true / toward truth"
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, for real, genuinely
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 3); adverbial truth-asserting expression used in discourse-level emphasis.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqan
**Transliteration:** cheqan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive/nominalizing); "its truth / the true one"
**Natural English translation:** the truth, what is correct, what is right
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 4); used in disclosure and rectification contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha-* (causative/verbalizing) + *-y* (infinitive); "to make true / to verify"
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to correct, to rectify
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 5); used in legal, testimonial, and everyday correction contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** cheqa simi
**Transliteration:** cheqa simi
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + simi (word/mouth/statement); "true word"
**Natural English translation:** truth, true statement, honest testimony
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 23); used in legal and moral discourse to characterize truthful speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** cheqam
**Transliteration:** cheqam
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-m* (direct evidential clitic); "it is true (I assert from direct knowledge)"
**Natural English translation:** it is true, it is real, that is correct (asserted from direct evidence)
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate assertion combining the truth adjective with the direct evidential clitic, producing a strongly committed affirmation. Appears in testimonial, legal, and everyday assertive contexts when a speaker affirms the truth of a proposition from personal knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** cheqas
**Transliteration:** cheqas
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-s* (reportative evidential clitic); "it is true (reportedly)"
**Natural English translation:** it is apparently true, it is said to be true, reportedly true
**Context of use:** Used when a speaker affirms the truth of a proposition but on the basis of reported rather than direct evidence. Appears in narrative and testimonial contexts where the speaker is relaying information from another source.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** cheqacha
**Transliteration:** cheqacha
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-chá* (conjectural evidential clitic); "it is probably true / I suppose it is true"
**Natural English translation:** it is probably true, it seems to be true, I suppose it is correct
**Context of use:** Used when a speaker hedges a truth assertion, indicating inference or conjecture rather than direct knowledge. Appears in contexts of uncertainty or tentative affirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** cheqa cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa cheqa
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + cheqa (reduplication); "truly true / very genuine"
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely, absolutely true
**Context of use:** Reduplication of *cheqa* for intensification, used in emphatic assertions of truth or genuineness. Appears in everyday speech and narrative when a speaker wishes to stress the absolute truth of a claim.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** cheqata
**Transliteration:** cheqata
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-ta* (accusative/adverbial case); "truly, correctly, rightly" (adverbial)
**Natural English translation:** truly, correctly, rightly, accurately
**Context of use:** The accusative/adverbial case form of *cheqa*, used as an adverb modifying verbs of speaking, acting, or representing. Appears in instructions, moral exhortations, and evaluative descriptions: e.g., *cheqata rimay* ("speak truly / speak correctly").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** cheqata rimay
**Transliteration:** cheqata rimay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-ta* (adverbial) + rima- (to speak) + *-y* (infinitive); "to speak truly"
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to speak correctly, to tell the truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in moral exhortation and legal instruction, equivalent to "tell the truth." Appears in contexts of testimony, oath-taking, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** cheqa runa
**Transliteration:** cheqa runa
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + runa (person/human being); "true person / genuine person"
**Natural English translation:** honest person, person of integrity, genuine person
**Context of use:** Used to characterize a person as honest, trustworthy, and of genuine character. Appears in moral evaluation of individuals in everyday speech and in narrative literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** cheqa sunqu
**Transliteration:** cheqa sunqu
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + sunqu (heart/inner self); "true heart / genuine heart"
**Natural English translation:** sincere, honest at heart, genuine in feeling, wholehearted
**Context of use:** Used to describe a person whose inner disposition is genuine and sincere, not merely outwardly correct. Appears in moral and affective evaluations of character, and in religious and literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** cheqa yuyay
**Transliteration:** cheqa yuyay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + yuyay (thought, memory, understanding); "true thought / correct understanding"
**Natural English translation:** correct understanding, right thinking, sound judgment
**Context of use:** Used to describe correct or sound reasoning and understanding, as opposed to mistaken or confused thought. Appears in educational, philosophical, and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**

**Transliteration:** cheqa kawsay

**Natural English translation:** true life, authentic existence, genuine way of living
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, religious, and moral discourse to describe an authentic or genuinely good way of living, as opposed to a superficial or false existence. Appears in Christian missionary texts and in contemporary Andean philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**

**Transliteration:** cheqa Dios
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + Dios (God, loanword from Spanish); "true God"
**Natural English translation:** the true God, the real God
**Context of use:** Used in Christian religious texts and discourse to distinguish the Christian God from other deities or false gods. Appears extensively in colonial and post-colonial religious literature in Quechua.

---

**18.**

**Transliteration:** cheqa iñiy
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + iñiy (to believe/faith); "true belief / true faith"
**Natural English translation:** true faith, genuine belief, correct belief
**Context of use:** Used in religious discourse to distinguish genuine or orthodox faith from false or mistaken belief. Appears in catechetical and homiletic texts in Quechua.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** cheqa yachay
**Transliteration:** cheqa yachay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + yachay (knowledge/learning); "true knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** true knowledge, correct knowledge, genuine understanding
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and educational discourse to distinguish genuine knowledge from mere opinion or false belief. Appears in contemporary Andean educational and intellectual discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**

**22.**

**Transliteration:** cheqanchariy

**Natural English translation:** to set right, to go about correcting, to begin to rectify
**Context of use:** Used to describe the process of beginning to correct or rectify a situation, with the inceptive suffix adding a sense of initiating the action of correction. Appears in narrative and procedural contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**23.**
**Form:** cheqaniy

**Natural English translation:** to be true, to be correct, to be genuine

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**24.**

**Natural English translation:** the right path, the correct way, the true road

**25.**

**Natural English translation:** truthful speech, honest speaking; to speak truthfully

**26.**

**Natural English translation:** truthful account, honest report, true narrative
**Context of use:** Used to describe a truthful or accurate account or report of events. Appears in narrative, testimonial, and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** cheqa qillqay
**Transliteration:** cheqa qillqay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true) + qillqa- (to write) + *-y* (infinitive/nominalization); "true writing / accurate writing"
**Natural English translation:** accurate writing, truthful text, correct written account
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and literary contexts to describe writing that accurately represents facts or events. Appears in contexts of document verification and literary evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**28.**
**Form:** cheqa tukuy
**Transliteration:** cheqa tukuy
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa (true/genuine) + tuku- (to become, to pretend to be) + *-y* (infinitive); "to become true / to pretend to be genuine"
**Natural English translation:** to pretend to be genuine, to act as if true, to pose as real
**Context of use:** Used to describe the act of pretending or feigning genuineness. The combination of *cheqa* with *tukuy* (which can mean both "to become" and "to pretend to be") creates an expression for false performance of genuineness. Appears in moral and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** cheqapuni
**Transliteration:** cheqapuni
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-puni* (emphatic/absolute suffix); "absolutely true / certainly genuine"
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, certainly genuine, definitely correct
**Context of use:** Used for emphatic assertion of truth or genuineness, with *-puni* adding a sense of certainty or absoluteness. Appears in everyday speech and narrative when a speaker wishes to assert something as unquestionably true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** cheqaqa
**Transliteration:** cheqaqa
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-qa* (topic marker); "as for the truth / the truth is…"
**Natural English translation:** the truth is…, as for what is true…, in fact…
**Context of use:** Used as a topic-marking construction to introduce a correction or disclosure of the true state of affairs, often in contrast to a previously stated or implied falsehood. Appears in narrative and conversational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** *Cheqa* is highly productive and additional derived forms exist beyond those listed here, particularly in combination with body-part terms (*sunqu*, *ñawi*), action verbs, and nominal heads. The 30 items above represent the most commonly attested and documented expressions.
