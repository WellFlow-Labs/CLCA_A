# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The scope excludes derivational forms already documented in P2a. Constructions are included only where there is positive evidence of natural, attested use. Where a construction type does not exist or is not established for a given item, this is recorded explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

Constructions are organized by P1 base form. Within each base form, constructions are grouped by structural type where multiple types are present.

## 1. Base Form: **benar**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benar atau tidak, kabar itu sudah tersebar luas.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** True or not, the news has already spread widely.
**Usage Notes:** Concessive framing using the *benar atau tidak* binary to bracket a claim whose truth is set aside while a consequence is asserted. Common in journalistic and conversational registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kalau benar demikian, kita harus segera bertindak.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If that is indeed the case, we must act immediately.
**Usage Notes:** Conditional construction *kalau benar* (if true / if indeed) used to introduce a course of action contingent on the truth of a prior claim. Common in spoken and written deliberative discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Memang benar bahwa angka kemiskinan menurun.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is indeed true that poverty rates have declined.
**Usage Notes:** *Memang benar bahwa* is a fixed concessive-affirmative frame used to acknowledge a fact before introducing a qualification or contrast. *Memang* reinforces the truth claim. Common in formal argumentation and journalism.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benarkah ia yang melakukan itu?
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Is it really true that he did that? / Did he really do that?
**Usage Notes:** *Benar* + *-kah* (interrogative enclitic) forms a yes/no question challenging or seeking confirmation of a truth claim. Common in spoken and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Ia benar dalam hal ini.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He is right about this / correct on this point.
**Usage Notes:** *Benar dalam hal* (correct/right in the matter of) restricts the scope of a truth attribution to a specific domain or point. Common in debate, commentary, and academic writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Tidak ada yang benar-benar tahu apa yang terjadi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** No one truly knows what happened.
**Usage Notes:** *Tidak ada yang benar-benar* (no one truly / nothing really) combines negation with the intensifier *benar-benar* to assert the complete absence of genuine knowledge or certainty. Common in journalistic and narrative registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Sejauh yang saya tahu, hal itu benar adanya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** As far as I know, that is indeed the case.
**Usage Notes:** *Sejauh yang saya tahu* (as far as I know) is an epistemic hedge that qualifies the speaker's commitment to the truth claim introduced by *benar adanya*. Common in spoken and written registers where the speaker signals limited but genuine confidence.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Pada dasarnya benar, tetapi ada beberapa detail yang perlu dikoreksi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Essentially correct, but there are some details that need to be corrected.
**Usage Notes:** *Pada dasarnya benar* (essentially/fundamentally correct) is a partial concession construction used in academic, journalistic, and deliberative contexts to acknowledge the core truth of a claim while reserving the right to qualify it.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Saya rasa ada benarnya juga pendapat itu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** I think there is something to that view as well.
**Usage Notes:** *Ada benarnya* (there is some truth/correctness to it) is a partial concession expression used in conversational and deliberative contexts to acknowledge partial validity without full endorsement. The addition of *juga* (also) softens the concession further.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Itu benar, tapi tidak lengkap.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is true, but incomplete.
**Usage Notes:** *Benar, tapi* (true, but) is a standard concessive construction used to acknowledge a truth claim while introducing a qualification. Extremely common in argumentation, debate, and commentary across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Demi kebenaran dan keadilan, ia bersedia bersaksi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** For the sake of truth and justice, he was willing to testify.
**Usage Notes:** *Demi kebenaran dan keadilan* (for the sake of truth and justice) is a fixed collocation used in legal, moral, and political discourse to invoke truth and justice as paired values motivating an action. The pairing of *kebenaran* and *keadilan* is stable and frequent.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kebenarannya belum dapat dipastikan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Its truth has not yet been established / confirmed.
**Usage Notes:** *Kebenaran* + *-nya* (its truth) + *belum dapat dipastikan* (cannot yet be confirmed) is a standard journalistic and legal formula for suspending judgment on an unverified claim. Common in news reporting and official statements.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **salah**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Kalau saya tidak salah, pertemuan itu dijadwalkan hari Kamis.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If I am not mistaken, the meeting is scheduled for Thursday.
**Usage Notes:** *Kalau saya tidak salah* (if I am not mistaken) is a standard epistemic hedge used in spoken and written Indonesian to qualify a claim the speaker is not fully certain about. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Ia mengakui bahwa ia salah.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He admitted that he was wrong.
**Usage Notes:** *Mengakui bahwa ia salah* (to admit that one is wrong) is a standard construction for expressing acknowledgment of error or fault. *Mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake) is the nominalized variant. Both are common in interpersonal, legal, and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Bukan salah saya kalau informasinya tidak akurat.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is not my fault if the information is inaccurate.
**Usage Notes:** *Bukan salah* + possessive (not [someone's] fault) is a standard construction for deflecting blame. Common in interpersonal and institutional discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Siapa yang salah dalam kasus ini masih diperdebatkan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Who is at fault in this case is still being debated.
**Usage Notes:** *Siapa yang salah* (who is wrong/at fault) is a standard interrogative frame used in legal, journalistic, and interpersonal contexts to open the question of culpability. Common in news reporting and legal commentary.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah besar jika kita mengabaikan masalah ini.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It would be a serious mistake to ignore this problem.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah besar* (a big/serious mistake) is a fixed intensified construction used in moral, political, and practical discourse to characterize an error as grave or consequential. Common in editorial and advisory writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Tidak ada yang salah dengan pendekatan itu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** There is nothing wrong with that approach.
**Usage Notes:** *Tidak ada yang salah dengan* (there is nothing wrong with) is a standard construction for defending a practice or position against implied criticism. Common in argumentative and advisory discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Ia salah mengerti maksud saya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He misunderstood my meaning.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah* + verb (to wrongly/mistakenly do something) is a productive construction where *salah* functions as a pre-verbal modifier meaning "wrongly" or "mistakenly." *Salah mengerti* (to misunderstand), *salah membaca* (to misread), *salah mengira* (to mistakenly think) are all attested. Common across registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah langkah bisa berakibat fatal.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** A wrong step could have fatal consequences.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah langkah* (wrong step, misstep) is a fixed compound used in practical, political, and moral discourse to describe a consequential error in judgment or action. Common in advisory and journalistic writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Itu bukan salah satu alasan yang saya maksud.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is not one of the reasons I meant.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah satu* (one of) in its grammaticalized partitive use, combined with negation. Included here to document the negated form *bukan salah satu* (not one of), which is equally common. The partitive meaning of *salah* in this construction is fully grammaticalized.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: **asli**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Apakah dokumen ini asli atau palsu?
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Is this document genuine or fake?
**Usage Notes:** *Asli atau palsu* (genuine or fake) is the standard binary opposition used in legal, commercial, and administrative contexts to frame the question of authenticity. The pairing is stable and highly frequent.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Barang ini dijamin asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** This item is guaranteed to be genuine.
**Usage Notes:** *Dijamin asli* (guaranteed genuine) is a common commercial formula used in retail and online commerce to assert authenticity. The passive *dijamin* (guaranteed) combined with *asli* is a stable collocation in commercial language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Ia orang Jawa asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He is a genuine/native Javanese person.
**Usage Notes:** *[Ethnonym/origin] + asli* is a construction used to assert that a person is a native or authentic member of an ethnic, regional, or national group, as opposed to someone with partial or adopted affiliation. Common in identity and demographic discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Versi aslinya berbeda dari yang beredar sekarang.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** The original version differs from what is currently circulating.
**Usage Notes:** *Versi aslinya* (the original version of it) is a common construction in journalistic, legal, and academic contexts to distinguish an original document, text, or recording from later versions or copies.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Produk ini bukan produk asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** This product is not a genuine product.
**Usage Notes:** *Bukan [noun] asli* (not a genuine [noun]) is a standard negation construction used in commercial and legal contexts to identify counterfeit or inauthentic goods. Common in consumer protection and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Sertifikat asli harus diserahkan kepada panitia.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** The original certificate must be submitted to the committee.
**Usage Notes:** *[Document noun] + asli* (original [document]) is a standard administrative construction distinguishing the original document from copies (*salinan* or *fotokopi*). Extremely common in administrative and legal language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**No common construction identified** for *asli* in the following pattern:
- Periphrastic constructions expressing degrees of authenticity (e.g., "more authentic than"): *asli* participates in standard comparative constructions (*lebih asli dari*) but no fixed periphrastic construction specific to this meaning is established beyond the regular comparative morphosyntax.
