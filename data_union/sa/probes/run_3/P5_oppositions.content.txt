# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Preliminary Note

Oppositions are extracted from patterns explicitly documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. Each opposition is supported by direct attestation in the context. Where the context notes asymmetry, partial overlap, or domain restriction, this is preserved. No oppositions have been generated by inference beyond what the data states.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
**Term B:** असत्य / *asatya*
**Transliteration:** *satya* / *asatya*
**English Translation:** truth, the true / untruth, the false
**Description of Contrast:** *Asatya* is the standard morphological negation of *satya*, formed by prefixing *a-*; P1 describes it as "the standard negation of *satya*" used in ethical, legal, and philosophical contexts. The pair appears in witness evaluation, lists of vices, and discussions of testimony as a direct positive/negative opposition.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** ऋत / *ṛta*
**Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
**Transliteration:** *ṛta* / *anṛta*
**English Translation:** cosmic order, truth, the fitting / untruth, falsehood, disorder
**Description of Contrast:** P1 describes *anṛta* as "the standard antonym of *ṛta* in Vedic and later literature," formed by prefixing *an-*. The pair is used in Vedic, legal, and moral contexts to contrast proper cosmic and ritual ordering with its violation; P3 groups them together as the primary Vedic opposition in the domain of cosmic and moral order.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** सत् / *sat*
**Term B:** असत् / *asat*
**Transliteration:** *sat* / *asat*
**English Translation:** being, real, genuine / non-being, unreal, false
**Description of Contrast:** P1 describes *asat* as the "ontological and evaluative antonym of *sat*," formed by prefixing *a-*. The pair is foundational in Vedāntic and Upaniṣadic literature; the prayer *asato mā sad gamaya* ("lead me from the unreal to the real," P2b #33/34) treats the two as the poles of an ontological movement. P3 places both in Group 5 as the most fundamental terms in the domain of genuine reality versus appearance.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** धर्म / *dharma*
**Term B:** अधर्म / *adharma*
**Transliteration:** *dharma* / *adharma*
**English Translation:** right conduct, duty, moral order / unrighteousness, wrongdoing, moral disorder
**Description of Contrast:** P1 describes *adharma* as "the standard antonym of *dharma* in ethical and legal literature," formed by prefixing *a-*. The pair appears throughout *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature to evaluate conduct as conforming to or violating moral and legal norms.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*
**Term B:** अप्रमाण / *apramāṇa*
**Transliteration:** *pramāṇa* / *apramāṇa*
**English Translation:** valid means of knowledge / invalid means of knowledge
**Description of Contrast:** P1b and P2a document *apramāṇa* as the morphological negation of *pramāṇa*, designating a source or means of cognition that fails to meet the criteria for validity. The pair is operative in epistemological literature wherever the question of whether a source qualifies as a genuine *pramāṇa* is at issue.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** प्रमा / *pramā*
**Term B:** अप्रमा / *apramā*
**Transliteration:** *pramā* / *apramā*
**English Translation:** valid cognition, correct apprehension / erroneous cognition, invalid cognition
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *apramā* as the negation of *pramā*, serving in *Nyāya* literature as the general term for any cognition that fails to accurately represent its object. P1 distinguishes *pramā* (the result of a successful *pramāṇa*) from *apramā* (the result of a failed or absent *pramāṇa*), covering *bhrānti*, *saṃśaya*, and *tarka* as species of *apramā*.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** यथार्थ / *yathārtha*
**Term B:** अयथार्थ / *ayathārtha*
**Transliteration:** *yathārtha* / *ayathārtha*
**English Translation:** accurate, as the object is / inaccurate, not corresponding to the object
**Description of Contrast:** P1 describes *ayathārtha* as "the negation of *yathārtha*," formed by prefixing *a-*, used in epistemological contexts to characterize cognitions or statements that fail to represent their objects correctly. P1 notes that *ayathārtha* is less frequent than *yathārtha* but attested in commentarial literature.

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** साधु / *sādhu*
**Term B:** असाधु / *asādhu*
**Transliteration:** *sādhu* / *asādhu*
**English Translation:** correct, good, well-formed / incorrect, bad, ill-formed
**Description of Contrast:** P1 documents *asādhu* as the antonym of *sādhu* in both grammatical and ethical registers; in grammar, the pair marks a form as conforming to or violating Pāṇinian rules; in ethics, it marks a person or act as genuinely good or bad. P2b (#53, #54) documents the formulaic pair *sādhuḥ śabdaḥ* / *asādhuḥ śabdaḥ* as the standard grammatical evaluation formula.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** शुद्ध / *śuddha*
**Term B:** अशुद्ध / *aśuddha*
**Transliteration:** *śuddha* / *aśuddha*
**English Translation:** pure, correct, unadulterated / impure, incorrect, contaminated
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *aśuddha* as the antonym of *śuddha* in ritual, grammatical, and ethical contexts; the pair marks a form, substance, or person as free from or contaminated by error, impurity, or admixture. P2a also documents the parallel pair *śuddhi* / *aśuddhi* (purification / impurity, correctness / error) as the corresponding action-noun opposition.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** शुद्धि / *śuddhi*
**Term B:** अशुद्धि / *aśuddhi*
**Transliteration:** *śuddhi* / *aśuddhi*
**English Translation:** purification, correctness / impurity, incorrectness, a grammatical error
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents this pair as the action-noun opposition corresponding to *śuddha* / *aśuddha*; in grammar, *śuddhi* designates the correction of an error and *aśuddhi* designates the error itself. The pair is operative in both ritual and grammatical domains.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** प्रामाण्य / *prāmāṇya*
**Term B:** अप्रामाण्य (implied by the *svataḥ/parataḥ* debate)
**Transliteration:** *prāmāṇya* / (no separate entry for *aprāmāṇya* in the data)
**English Translation:** validity, the status of being a valid means of knowledge / (invalidity)
**Description of Contrast:** P2a and P1b document *prāmāṇya* as the abstract property that makes something a *pramāṇa*; the context does not separately attest *aprāmāṇya* as a lexical item, though *apramāṇa* (Opposition 5) covers the same semantic ground at the nominal level. This opposition is therefore only partially supported.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** ऋत / *ṛta*
**Term B:** सत्य / *satya*
**Transliteration:** *ṛta* / *satya*
**English Translation:** cosmic order, the fitting / truth, the real
**Description of Contrast:** P1 explicitly distinguishes *ṛta* and *satya* as "closely related but distinct," with *ṛta* carrying "stronger cosmological and ritual connotations" and *satya* being more broadly applicable to speech acts, persons, and metaphysical states. P3 places them in overlapping groups (Group 1 and Group 2) and notes the Vedic formulaic pairing *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca* treats them as complementary rather than synonymous. This is a lexical distinction within a shared domain, not a simple antonymy.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
**Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
**Transliteration:** *satya* / *mithyā*
**English Translation:** truth, the real / false, illusory, of indeterminate ontological status
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P3 document *mithyā* as used in contrast to *satya* in both ethical speech contexts (false speech versus true speech) and Advaita Vedāntic ontology (where *mithyā* designates what is neither absolutely *sat* nor absolutely *asat*). The contrast is lexical rather than morphological; *mithyā* is not derived from *satya*. P3 notes the dual usage of *mithyā* across Groups 4 and 5.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** सत् / *sat*
**Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
**Transliteration:** *sat* / *mithyā*
**English Translation:** being, the genuinely real / illusory, neither real nor unreal
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P3 document the Advaita Vedāntic usage in which *mithyā* designates a third ontological category — neither absolutely real (*sat*) nor absolutely unreal (*asat*) — making the *sat* / *mithyā* contrast asymmetric: *mithyā* does not simply negate *sat* but occupies an intermediate position. P2b (#67) documents the formula *mithyeti niścitam* as the standard way of assigning this status.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** वस्तु / *vastu*
**Term B:** प्रतिभास / *pratibhāsa*
**Transliteration:** *vastu* / *pratibhāsa*
**English Translation:** real thing, actual entity / mere appearance, semblance
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P3 document the contrast between *vastu* (what something genuinely is as a real entity) and *pratibhāsa* (how something appears, particularly when the appearance may not correspond to reality). P2b (#56) documents the construction *pratibhāse sati api vastu na tathā* ("even though there is an appearance, the real thing is not like that") as a formulaic expression of this contrast.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** वस्तु / *vastu*
**Term B:** आभास / *ābhāsa*
**Transliteration:** *vastu* / *ābhāsa*
**English Translation:** real thing, actual entity / semblance, pseudo-instance
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P2b (#63) document the formula *ābhāsamātram na vastu* ("a mere semblance is not a real thing") as a standard ontological denial in Vedāntic and epistemological literature, directly contrasting *ābhāsa* (something that resembles the genuine article without being it) with *vastu* (the genuine article itself).
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*
**Term B:** प्रमाणाभास / *pramāṇābhāsa*
**Transliteration:** *pramāṇa* / *pramāṇābhāsa*
**English Translation:** valid means of knowledge / pseudo-valid means of knowledge
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P2a document *pramāṇābhāsa* as designating something that resembles a valid means of knowledge but fails to meet the criteria for genuine validity; P2b (#62) documents the parallel formula *hetvābhāso na hetuḥ* ("a fallacious reason is not a reason") as the constructional expression of this contrast. The *-ābhāsa* suffix systematically marks the pseudo-instance as distinct from the genuine instance.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** हेतु / *hetu*
**Term B:** हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa*
**Transliteration:** *hetu* / *hetvābhāsa*
**English Translation:** (genuine) logical reason / fallacious reason, pseudo-reason
**Description of Contrast:** P2a and P2b (#62, #80) document *hetvābhāsa* as the primary technical application of the *ābhāsa* pattern in the logical domain, designating a reason that resembles a valid inferential mark without being one. The formula *hetvābhāso na hetuḥ* directly states the contrast.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥ-prāmāṇya*
**Term B:** परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥ-prāmāṇya*
**Transliteration:** *svataḥ-prāmāṇya* / *parataḥ-prāmāṇya*
**English Translation:** intrinsic validity (cognitions are self-validating) / extrinsic validity (cognitions require external confirmation)
**Description of Contrast:** P1b and P2a document this as a technical opposition between the *Mīmāṃsā* position (*svataḥ-prāmāṇya*: validity is intrinsic to the cognition) and the *Nyāya* position (*parataḥ-prāmāṇya*: validity must be confirmed from outside the cognition). The two terms are explicitly defined in contrast to each other in the epistemological literature.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** वस्तुतन्त्र / *vastu-tantra*
**Term B:** पुरुषतन्त्र / *puruṣa-tantra*
**Transliteration:** *vastu-tantra* / *puruṣa-tantra*
**English Translation:** object-dependent, governed by the real thing / subject-dependent, governed by the person

The formula *vastu puruṣatantraṃ na bhavati* ("a real thing is not subject-dependent") directly states the contrast.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** सत्कार्यवाद / *sat-kārya-vāda*
**Term B:** असत्कार्यवाद / *asat-kārya-vāda*
**Transliteration:** *sat-kārya-vāda* / *asat-kārya-vāda*
**English Translation:** the doctrine that effects pre-exist in the cause / the doctrine that effects are genuinely new
**Description of Contrast:** P1b and P2a document this as a technical opposition between the *Sāṃkhya* position (effects are already present in their causes in latent form: *sat-kārya-vāda*) and the *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* and Buddhist position (effects are genuinely new entities: *asat-kārya-vāda*). The two terms are defined explicitly in contrast to each other.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** सत् / *sat*
**Term B:** असत् / *asat* (in the Vedāntic prayer formula)
**Transliteration:** *asato mā sad gamaya*
**English Translation:** "Lead me from the unreal (*asat*) to the real (*sat*)"
**Description of Contrast:** P2b (#33/34) documents this as a fixed constructional opposition: the ablative of *asat* and the accusative of *sat* are paired in a directional formula expressing movement from one ontological pole to the other. This is the same morphological pair as Opposition 3 but here the contrast is realized at the constructional level as a directional movement rather than as simple predication.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** सत्यं वद / *satyaṃ vada* (imperative: speak truth)
**Term B:** अनृतं न वदेत् / *anṛtaṃ na vadet* (prohibitive optative: do not speak falsehood)
**Transliteration:** *satyaṃ vada* / *anṛtaṃ na vadet*
**English Translation:** "Speak the truth" / "One should not speak falsehood"
**Description of Contrast:** P2b (#1, #11) documents both as formulaic normative constructions in *Dharmaśāstra* and Upaniṣadic ethical instruction; the two constructions express the same normative content from opposite directions — positive prescription and negative prohibition — using different lexical items (*satya* vs. *anṛta*) and different verbal moods (imperative vs. negated optative). P4a (§9) identifies this as a sub-pattern within the normative injunction construction type.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** सत्यं ब्रूयात् / *satyaṃ brūyāt* (optative: normative expectation)
**Term B:** सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम् / *satyāt na pramaditavyam* (gerundive: stronger obligation)
**Transliteration:** *satyaṃ brūyāt* / *satyāt na pramaditavyam*
**English Translation:** "One should speak the truth" / "One must not deviate from truth"
**Description of Contrast:** P2b (#2, #6) and P4b document these as two constructions within the normative injunction pattern that differ in the strength of the obligation expressed: the optative (*brūyāt*) expresses normative expectation; the gerundive (*pramaditavyam*) with negation expresses stronger prohibition. Both are attested in the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* 1.11.1 and related literature. P4a (§9) identifies this as an intra-pattern contrast.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 35

**Term A:** वस्तुतः / *vastutaḥ*
**Term B:** प्रतिभासमात्रम् / *pratibhāsamātram*
**Transliteration:** *vastutaḥ* / *pratibhāsamātram*
**English Translation:** "in reality, as a matter of fact" / "mere appearance, nothing but semblance"
**Description of Contrast:** P2a and P2b document *vastutaḥ* as an adverbial formula asserting that a proposition corresponds to the actual state of affairs, and *pratibhāsamātram* as a formula denying genuine ontological status by characterizing something as mere appearance. The two function as opposing propositional operators in Vedāntic and epistemological prose: one asserts factual grounding, the other denies it.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 36

**Term A:** धर्मिन् / *dharmin*
**Term B:** धर्म / *dharma* (in the logical sense)
**Transliteration:** *dharmin* / *dharma*
**English Translation:** the property-bearer, the subject / the property
**Description of Contrast:** P2a (#36) documents the *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* technical opposition between *dharmin* (the subject or locus that bears a property) and *dharma* (the property itself). This is an internal opposition within the logical usage of *dharma*, distinct from its ethical usage; P2a notes that this technical logical usage is "distinct from the ethical sense."
**Contrast Type:** lexical

## Opposition 37

**Term A:** प्रतिभासिक / *pratibhāsika*
**Term B:** व्यावहारिक / *vyāvahārika* (implied by the Advaita three-level scheme)
**Transliteration:** *pratibhāsika* / *vyāvahārika*
**English Translation:** pertaining to mere appearance (the lowest level of reality) / pertaining to conventional reality (the intermediate level)
**Description of Contrast:** P2a (#72) documents *pratibhāsika* as designating the level of mere appearance or illusion in Advaita Vedānta's three-level ontological scheme (*pratibhāsika sattā*), as opposed to conventional reality (*vyāvahārika*) and ultimate reality (*pāramārthika*). *Vyāvahārika* and *pāramārthika* are named in P2a as the contrasting levels but are not themselves P1 items; the opposition is partially supported by the data.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 38

**Term A:** साक्षात् / *sākṣāt* (direct, unmediated)
**Term B:** अनुमान / *anumāna* (inferential, mediated)
**Transliteration:** *sākṣāt* / *anumāna*
**English Translation:** directly, face to face, with one's own eyes / inference, cognition proceeding from a mark
**Description of Contrast:** P1 and P3 document *sākṣāt* as asserting direct, unmediated perception or presence, and P1b documents *anumāna* as the *pramāṇa* that proceeds from a mark to a conclusion — a mediated form of cognition. P3 places *sākṣāt* in Group 8 (direct/immediate) and *anumāna* in Group 3 (epistemological classification), noting the contrast between immediacy and mediation as a structural feature of the epistemological domain. The two terms are not explicitly paired in a single formula in the data, making this opposition less directly attested than the others.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]
