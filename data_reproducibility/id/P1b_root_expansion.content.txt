# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **benar**
**Form:** benar  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Participates in:** 3 P1 items directly (benar, sebenarnya, membenarkan mentioned)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Correctness, truth, rightness — evaluates correspondence to fact or standard

### Root 2: **sungguh**
**Form:** sungguh  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items directly (sungguh, sesungguhnya)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, reality, seriousness — evaluates authenticity of state or sincerity

### Root 3: **sah**
**Form:** sah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items directly (sah, absah)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Validity, legitimacy, legal/procedural correctness — evaluates conformity to rules or law

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: **benar** Expansion

#### 1. kebenaran
**Form:** kebenaran  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + benar + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** truth, correctness, the truth  
**Context:** Abstract noun for truth as concept or property. Used in philosophical discourse ("mencari kebenaran" = "seeking truth"), religious contexts ("kebenaran Tuhan" = "God's truth"), and everyday speech ("katakan kebenaran" = "tell the truth").  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. membenarkan
**Form:** membenarkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + benar + -kan (causative verb)  
**Translation:** to confirm, to verify, to justify, to validate  
**Context:** Transitive verb meaning to confirm something as true or to justify an action. Used in statements of confirmation ("dia membenarkan laporan itu" = "he confirmed that report") and justification contexts ("membenarkan tindakan" = "to justify an action").  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. dibenarkan
**Form:** dibenarkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** di- + benar + -kan (passive voice)  
**Translation:** to be confirmed, to be permitted, to be justified  
**Context:** Passive form indicating something is confirmed or permitted. Used in permissions ("tidak dibenarkan" = "not permitted") and validation contexts ("telah dibenarkan" = "has been confirmed").  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. pembenaran
**Form:** pembenaran  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** peN- + benar + -an (nominalization of action)  
**Translation:** justification, validation, confirmation  
**Context:** Noun referring to act or process of justifying or validating. Common in formal discourse ("pembenaran atas tindakan" = "justification for action") and legal contexts ("pembenaran hukum" = "legal justification").  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. sebenar-benarnya
**Form:** sebenar-benarnya  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** se- + benar + reduplication + -nya (superlative/emphatic)  
**Translation:** most truly, in absolute truth, as truthfully as possible  
**Context:** Emphatic form used in oaths, legal testimony, and solemn declarations. Common in legal formulas ("dengan sebenar-benarnya" = "most truthfully") and sworn statements.  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. sebenar
**Form:** sebenar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** se- + benar (prefix indicating "as/truly")  
**Translation:** as true, truly  
**Context:** Less common variant, appears in some fixed expressions and older or formal texts. Sometimes used as shortened form in compounds.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 7. benar-benar
**Form:** benar-benar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** benar + reduplication (intensification)  
**Translation:** really, truly, genuinely, very  
**Context:** Adverbial intensifier used to emphasize reality or degree. Very common in everyday speech ("saya benar-benar lelah" = "I'm really tired") and to emphasize genuineness ("dia benar-benar jujur" = "he's truly honest").  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. membenar
**Form:** membenar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + benar (intransitive verb)  
**Translation:** to be correct, to turn out right  
**Context:** Less common intransitive form. Used when something proves correct or comes true ("ramalannya membenar" = "his prediction came true"). More common in formal or literary register.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 9. kebenar-benaran
**Form:** kebenar-benaran  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + benar + reduplication + -an  
**Translation:** excessive correctness, overly serious  
**Context:** Rare form suggesting excessive emphasis on correctness or taking things too seriously. Limited usage in colloquial contexts.  
**[UNCERTAIN]**

#### 10. memperbenarkan
**Form:** memperbenarkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + per- + benar + -kan (causative with per-)  
**Translation:** to justify, to vindicate, to prove right  
**Context:** More formal variant of membenarkan with emphasis on proving or demonstrating correctness. Used in formal discourse ("memperbenarkan argumen" = "to vindicate an argument").  
**[CORE]**

#### 11. terbenar
**Form:** terbenar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ter- + benar (accidental/superlative)  
**Translation:** most correct, truest  
**Context:** Superlative form indicating highest degree of correctness. Used in comparisons ("yang terbenar" = "the most correct one") and philosophical contexts.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 12. paling benar
**Form:** paling benar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** paling (most) + benar  
**Translation:** most correct, most true  
**Context:** Analytic superlative construction, more common than terbenar in everyday speech. Used in comparisons and evaluations ("cara paling benar" = "the most correct way").  
**[CORE]**

#### 13. kebenarannya
**Form:** kebenarannya  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + benar + -an + -nya (possessive/definite)  
**Translation:** its truth, the truth of it  
**Context:** Definite form referring to the truth of a specific matter. Common in discourse ("kebenarannya masih diragukan" = "its truth is still doubted").  
**[CORE]**

#### 14. yang benar
**Form:** yang benar  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** yang (relativizer) + benar  
**Translation:** the correct one, the true one, what is right  
**Context:** Relative clause construction identifying correct item or truth. Very common in questions ("yang mana yang benar?" = "which one is correct?") and statements.  
**[CORE]**

### ROOT 2: **sungguh** Expansion

#### 1. sungguh-sungguh
**Form:** sungguh-sungguh  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** sungguh + reduplication (intensification)  
**Translation:** seriously, earnestly, in earnest, with full effort  
**Context:** Intensified form emphasizing seriousness or earnestness of action or state. Very common in everyday speech ("belajar sungguh-sungguh" = "study seriously") and exhortations ("bekerja sungguh-sungguh" = "work earnestly").  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. kesungguhan
**Form:** kesungguhan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + sungguh + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** seriousness, earnestness, sincerity  
**Context:** Abstract noun for quality of being serious or earnest. Used in evaluations of character or effort ("kesungguhan hatinya" = "his sincerity of heart") and formal assessments.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. bersungguh-sungguh
**Form:** bersungguh-sungguh  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ber- + sungguh + reduplication (stative verb)  
**Translation:** to be serious, to be earnest, to act seriously  
**Context:** Verbal form indicating state or action of being serious. Used in descriptions of behavior ("dia bersungguh-sungguh dalam pekerjaannya" = "he is serious in his work").  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. menyungguhkan
**Form:** menyungguhkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + sungguh + -kan (causative verb)  
**Translation:** to make serious, to take seriously, to actualize  
**Context:** Less common causative form meaning to make something serious or real. Used in formal contexts ("menyungguhkan niat" = "to make intention real/serious").  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 5. sesungguh-sungguhnya
**Form:** sesungguh-sungguhnya  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** se- + sungguh + reduplication + -nya (superlative/emphatic)  
**Translation:** most truly, in absolute truth, most earnestly  
**Context:** Emphatic superlative form used in solemn declarations and formal statements. Similar usage to sebenar-benarnya in oaths and testimony.  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. sungguhpun
**Form:** sungguhpun  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** sungguh + pun (concessive particle)  
**Translation:** although, even though, despite  
**Context:** Concessive conjunction, more literary or formal register. Used to introduce contrasting clauses ("sungguhpun demikian" = "even so/nevertheless"). Less common in colloquial speech.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 7. kesungguh-sungguhan
**Form:** kesungguh-sungguhan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + sungguh + reduplication + -an  
**Translation:** excessive seriousness, overly earnest  
**Context:** Rare form suggesting excessive or exaggerated seriousness. Limited colloquial usage.  
**[UNCERTAIN]**

#### 8. tersungguh
**Form:** tersungguh  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ter- + sungguh (accidental/superlative)  
**Translation:** most genuine, most real  
**Context:** Rare superlative form. Occasionally appears in literary or formal texts but not common in everyday usage.  
**[MARGINAL]**

### ROOT 3: **sah** Expansion

#### 1. mengesahkan
**Form:** mengesahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + sah + -kan (causative verb)  
**Translation:** to validate, to legalize, to ratify, to authorize  
**Context:** Transitive verb meaning to make something legally valid or official. Very common in legal and administrative contexts ("mengesahkan dokumen" = "to validate documents," "mengesahkan undang-undang" = "to ratify a law").  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. pengesahan
**Form:** pengesahan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** peN- + sah + -an (nominalization of action)  
**Translation:** validation, legalization, ratification, authorization  
**Context:** Noun referring to act or process of validation. Common in legal and administrative discourse ("pengesahan kontrak" = "contract validation," "pengesahan pernikahan" = "marriage legalization").  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. disahkan
**Form:** disahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** di- + sah + -kan (passive voice)  
**Translation:** to be validated, to be legalized, to be ratified  
**Context:** Passive form indicating something is made valid or legal. Standard in official announcements ("telah disahkan" = "has been validated/ratified").  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. keabsahan
**Form:** keabsahan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + absah + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** validity, legitimacy, legality  
**Context:** Abstract noun for quality of being valid or legitimate. Used in legal and formal contexts ("keabsahan dokumen" = "validity of documents," "mempertanyakan keabsahan" = "to question the legitimacy").  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. kesahan
**Form:** kesahan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + sah + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** validity, legitimacy  
**Context:** Synonym of keabsahan, though keabsahan is more common. Used interchangeably in formal contexts ("kesahan hukum" = "legal validity").  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. sahih
**Form:** sahih  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** sahih (from Arabic ṣaḥīḥ, related root)  
**Translation:** valid, authentic, sound (especially of hadith)  
**Context:** Related term from Arabic, primarily used in Islamic religious contexts to evaluate authenticity of hadith ("hadis sahih" = "authentic hadith"). Also used more broadly for valid or sound arguments.  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. mensahkan
**Form:** mensahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + sah + -kan (alternative form)  
**Translation:** to validate, to legalize  
**Context:** Alternative form of mengesahkan, less common but still used. Same meaning and contexts as mengesahkan.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 8. tersah
**Form:** tersah  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ter- + sah (accidental passive)  
**Translation:** accidentally validated, happened to be valid  
**Context:** Rare form suggesting accidental or unintentional validation. Very limited usage.  
**[UNCERTAIN]**

#### 9. sahkan
**Form:** sahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** sah + -kan (imperative/bare causative)  
**Translation:** validate!, legalize! (command form)  
**Context:** Imperative or bare causative form. Used in commands or instructions ("sahkan dokumen ini" = "validate this document").  
**[CORE]**

#### 10. pengesah
**Form:** pengesah  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** peN- + sah (agent noun)  
**Translation:** validator, authorizer, one who validates  
**Context:** Agent noun referring to person or entity that validates. Used in administrative contexts ("pengesah dokumen" = "document validator").  
**[CORE]**

#### 11. sah-mengesahkan
**Form:** sah-mengesahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** sah + meN- + sah + -kan (compound)  
**Translation:** mutual validation, to validate each other  
**Context:** Reciprocal form indicating mutual validation. Used in contexts where parties validate each other's positions or documents.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 12. kesahihan
**Form:** kesahihan  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + sahih + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** validity, authenticity, soundness  
**Context:** Abstract noun from sahih, particularly common in Islamic scholarship contexts ("kesahihan hadis" = "authenticity of hadith") but also used more broadly.  
**[CORE]**

#### 13. yang sah
**Form:** yang sah  
**Transliteration:** N/A  
**Literal breakdown:** yang (relativizer) + sah  
**Translation:** the valid one, the legal one, the legitimate one  
**Context:** Relative clause construction identifying valid or legal item. Common in legal discourse ("dokumen yang sah" = "the valid document").  
**[CORE]**

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

### Extensions of **benar**:

1. **benar-benar** as pure intensifier: While core meaning relates to genuineness/truth, this form frequently functions as a general intensifier of degree or emphasis that may not involve truth evaluation at all (e.g., "benar-benar besar" = "really big" — evaluating size, not truth).

2. **membenarkan** in permission contexts: The causative form extends to granting permission or allowing, which shifts from truth-validation toward authorization/permission domain ("tidak dibenarkan masuk" = "not allowed to enter").

3. **kebenaran** in rights/permissions: The nominalized form can mean "permission" or "right" in some contexts, extending beyond truth into deontic/permission domain ("mendapat kebenaran" = "to receive permission").

### Extensions of **sungguh**:

1. **sungguh-sungguh** as effort/intensity marker: While rooted in genuineness, this form extensively marks degree of effort or intensity that may not involve authenticity evaluation ("hujan sungguh-sungguh" = "raining heavily" — intensity, not genuineness).

2. **sungguhpun** as concessive: The concessive conjunction use represents grammaticalization into discourse function, moving away from truth/reality evaluation toward logical relations between clauses.

### Extensions of **sah**:

1. **sah** in ritual/religious validity: Beyond legal validity, extends to ritual correctness and religious validity ("shalat sah" = "valid prayer" — evaluating ritual correctness according to religious law, not civil law).

2. **sahih** in argument evaluation: While primarily for hadith authentication, extends to evaluating logical soundness of arguments, shifting toward epistemic/logical domain beyond legal validity.

3. **pengesahan** in social recognition: Can extend to social validation or recognition beyond strictly legal contexts ("pengesahan sosial" = "social validation/acceptance").

---

**Note on productivity:** The root **benar** is highly productive with numerous additional compounds and collocations beyond those listed (estimated 40+ forms in regular use). The 14 forms listed represent the most common and central expressions. The roots **sungguh** and **sah** show moderate productivity with the forms listed representing the majority of common expressions.
