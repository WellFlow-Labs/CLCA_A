# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: totoo**

- **Root form:** totoo
- **P1 items participating:** 3 — *totoo* (item 1), *katotohanan* (item 2), *makatotohanan* (item 18)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Factual correspondence between a statement or representation and reality; the state of something being the case rather than false, simulated, or supposed.

---

**Root 2: tunay**

- **Root form:** tunay
- **P1 items participating:** 4 — *tunay* (item 3), *katunayan* (item 4), *awtentiko* is a near-synonym but not derived; *di-tunay* (item 30); *orihinal* and *awtentiko* are conceptually adjacent but not morphologically derived. Direct morphological participants: *tunay* (item 3), *katunayan* (item 4), *di-tunay* (item 30). Count: 3 direct morphological participants.
- **Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, authenticity, and intrinsic realness of an entity, person, or relationship — emphasizing that something is what it claims or appears to be, rather than an imitation or pretense.

---

**Root 3: tapat**

- **Root form:** tapat
- **P1 items participating:** 2 — *tapat* (item 13), *katapatan* (item 14)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Honesty, sincerity, and directness in communication and conduct; the absence of concealment or deception in a person's dealings with others. Also extends to relational faithfulness and loyalty.

**Note on selection:** *totoo* and *tunay* are the two most morphologically productive roots in the P1 data. *tapat* is selected as the third because it is the most productive native root in the honesty/sincerity subdomain and generates a distinct cluster of expressions not covered by *totoo* or *tunay*. *mali* is also moderately productive but its derivational family is smaller and more semantically uniform.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: totoo

The following lists all common compounds, collocations, derived expressions, and fixed phrases built from *totoo* that are in regular use in Tagalog, beyond those already documented in P1.

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoong-totoo
**Transliteration:** totoong-totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, really and truly real, genuinely genuine
**Context of use:** Used for emphatic assertion that something is unambiguously real or true, often in response to expressed doubt (*Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro* "This is absolutely real, not a joke"). Common in spoken Tagalog and informal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** totoo naman
**Transliteration:** totoo naman
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *naman* (discourse particle marking contrast, concession, or turn-taking)
**Natural English translation:** it's true though, that's actually true, well, it is real
**Context of use:** Used to concede or affirm a truth while managing conversational dynamics — often acknowledging a point made by another speaker (*Totoo naman na mahirap* "It's true though that it's difficult"). Extremely common in spoken Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** totoo ba?
**Transliteration:** totoo ba?
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *ba* (interrogative particle)
**Natural English translation:** Is it true? Really? Is that real?
**Context of use:** Standard interrogative formula for questioning the truth or reality of a claim or situation. One of the most frequent utterances in Tagalog conversation when expressing surprise or seeking confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** hindi totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *hindi* (negation) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** not true, untrue, not real, false
**Context of use:** Standard negation of *totoo*, used to deny the truth of a statement or the reality of something (*Hindi totoo ang balita* "The news is not true"). Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** parang totoo
**Transliteration:** parang totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *parang* (like, as if) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** as if it were real, seemingly true, lifelike
**Context of use:** Used to describe something that resembles reality closely enough to be mistaken for it, or that creates a convincing impression of truth (*Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak* "Her crying seemed real"). Common in aesthetic evaluation and in descriptions of deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** mukhang totoo
**Transliteration:** mukhang totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *mukha* (face, appearance) + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** looks real, appears to be true, seems genuine
**Context of use:** Evaluates the surface appearance of something as consistent with being real or true, without necessarily asserting that it is (*Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala* "The product looked real but turned out to be fake"). Common in consumer, journalistic, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** sa totoo lang
**Transliteration:** sa totoo lang
**Literal breakdown:** *sa* (locative/oblique marker) + *totoo* + *lang* (only, just — limiting particle)
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, honestly speaking, actually, in truth
**Context of use:** Discourse marker used to introduce a frank or candid statement, often one that contrasts with what was previously said or implied (*Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam* "To tell the truth, I don't know"). Very common in spoken Tagalog across all social registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ang totoo
**Transliteration:** ang totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *ang* (topic/nominative marker) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** the truth is, what is actually true, the real situation
**Context of use:** Used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement that reveals what is actually the case, often contrasting with a mistaken belief (*Ang totoo, wala siyang pera* "The truth is, he has no money"). Common in spoken and written Tagalog as a discourse-framing device.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ang totoo nito
**Transliteration:** ang totoo nito
**Literal breakdown:** *ang* + *totoo* + *nito* (of this, genitive of proximal demonstrative)
**Natural English translation:** the truth of this matter, what is really true about this
**Context of use:** Used to introduce a clarification or revelation about a specific matter under discussion (*Ang totoo nito ay mas kumplikado* "The truth of this matter is more complicated"). Common in formal speech, journalism, and argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** katotohanang-katotohanan
**Transliteration:** katotohanang-katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *katotohanan* + *-ng* (linker) + *katotohanan* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** absolute truth, the very truth itself, undeniable reality
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of *katotohanan*, used in philosophical, religious, or rhetorical contexts to assert the highest or most fundamental level of truth (*Ito ang katotohanang-katotohanan* "This is the absolute truth"). Less common than the base form; found in formal and literary registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**11.**
**Form:** sabihin ang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** sabihin ang katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *sabihin* (to say, infinitive) + *ang* + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, to state the truth
**Context of use:** Standard verbal collocation for the act of disclosing what is true (*Sabihin mo ang katotohanan* "Tell the truth"). Common in moral instruction, legal contexts, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ibunyag ang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** ibunyag ang katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *ibunyag* (to reveal, to expose — *i-* + *bunyag*) + *ang* + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** to reveal the truth, to expose the truth
**Context of use:** Used when a hidden or suppressed truth is brought to light, often in investigative journalism, legal proceedings, or dramatic narrative (*Ibunyag ang katotohanan sa likod ng kaso* "Reveal the truth behind the case").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** itago ang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** itago ang katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *itago* (to hide, to conceal — *i-* + *tago*) + *ang* + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
**Context of use:** Describes the act of suppressing or withholding true information (*Huwag itago ang katotohanan* "Don't hide the truth"). Common in moral, legal, and journalistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** harapin ang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** harapin ang katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *harapin* (to face, to confront — *harap* + *-in*) + *ang* + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** to face the truth, to confront reality
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, moral, and motivational contexts to describe accepting or confronting an uncomfortable truth (*Kailangan mong harapin ang katotohanan* "You need to face the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ang buong katotohanan
**Transliteration:** ang buong katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *ang* + *buong* (whole, entire — *buo* + *-ng*) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** the whole truth, the full truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal testimony and moral discourse to emphasize that nothing is being withheld (*Sabihin ang buong katotohanan* "Tell the whole truth"). Parallels the legal formula "the whole truth and nothing but the truth."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** walang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** walang katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *wala* (none, there is no) + *-ng* (linker) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** without truth, baseless, having no truth to it, groundless
**Context of use:** Used to dismiss a claim as entirely false or unfounded (*Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon* "The allegation has no truth to it"). Common in formal denials, press statements, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** may katotohanan
**Transliteration:** may katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *may* (there is, having) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** there is truth to it, it has some truth, it is partly true
**Context of use:** Used to partially affirm a claim, acknowledging that it contains some truth without fully endorsing it (*May katotohanan sa sinabi niya* "There is some truth in what he said"). Common in nuanced evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** makatotohanang-makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** makatotohanang-makatotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *makatotohanan* + *-ng* + *makatotohanan* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** extremely realistic, very true-to-life
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of *makatotohanan*, used in aesthetic criticism to describe a portrayal of exceptional realism (*Makatotohanang-makatotohanan ang pelikula* "The film is extremely true-to-life"). Found in literary and film criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**19.**
**Form:** hindi makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** hindi makatotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *hindi* + *makatotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** unrealistic, not true-to-life, implausible
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic and critical evaluation to describe a portrayal or narrative that fails to reflect reality convincingly (*Hindi makatotohanan ang kwento* "The story is unrealistic"). Common in literary and film criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** totoo sa buhay
**Transliteration:** totoo sa buhay
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *sa* (locative marker) + *buhay* (life)
**Natural English translation:** true to life, realistic, lifelike
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic evaluation to describe characters, situations, or portrayals that accurately reflect lived experience (*Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela* "The characters in the novel are true to life"). Common in literary and dramatic criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** totoo ang nararamdaman
**Transliteration:** totoo ang nararamdaman
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *ang* + *nararamdaman* (what is felt/experienced — *na-* + *ramdám* + *-an*)
**Natural English translation:** the feelings are real, what one feels is genuine
**Context of use:** Used to assert the authenticity of emotional experience, often in contexts of validating someone's feelings or in discussions of sincerity (*Totoo ang nararamdaman ko* "What I feel is real/genuine"). Common in interpersonal and therapeutic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** paghahanap ng katotohanan
**Transliteration:** paghahanap ng katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *pag-* + *hahanap* (reduplication of *hanap*, to search) + *ng* (genitive marker) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** the search for truth, seeking the truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, journalistic, and investigative contexts to describe the pursuit of truth as an activity or goal (*Ang paghahanap ng katotohanan ay mahirap* "The search for truth is difficult"). Common in academic and editorial discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan
**Transliteration:** tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *tagapagtanggol* (defender — *taga-* + *pagtanggol*) + *ng* + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** defender of truth, advocate for truth
**Context of use:** Used in moral, religious, and journalistic discourse to describe a person or institution that upholds and protects truth (*Ang mamamahayag ay tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan* "The journalist is a defender of truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** katotohanan sa likod ng
**Transliteration:** katotohanan sa likod ng
**Literal breakdown:** *katotohanan* + *sa likod ng* (behind, at the back of)
**Natural English translation:** the truth behind [something], the real story behind
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation used to introduce a revelation of what is actually the case beneath a surface appearance or official account (*Ang katotohanan sa likod ng insidente* "The truth behind the incident"). Common in journalism and investigative writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** totoo o hindi
**Transliteration:** totoo o hindi
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *o* (or) + *hindi* (no, not)
**Natural English translation:** true or not, real or not, whether it's true or false
**Context of use:** Used to frame a binary question about the truth-value of a claim (*Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman* "True or not, we need to find out"). Common in investigative, deliberative, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** totoo ang buhay
**Transliteration:** totoo ang buhay
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *ang* + *buhay* (life)
**Natural English translation:** life is real, reality of life
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, motivational, and literary contexts to assert the reality and seriousness of lived experience, often contrasting with fantasy or denial (*Totoo ang buhay at kailangan nating harapin ito* "Life is real and we must face it").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** *totoo* is highly productive in collocational and discourse-particle constructions. The items above represent the most common and conventionalized expressions. Additional nonce-like combinations exist but are not listed here as they are compositionally transparent and not lexicalized.

### ROOT 2: tunay

---

**1.**
**Form:** tunay na tunay
**Transliteration:** tunay na tunay
**Literal breakdown:** *tunay* + *na* (linker) + *tunay* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** absolutely genuine, truly authentic, really real
**Context of use:** Emphatic form asserting the highest degree of genuineness (*Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal* "His/her love is absolutely genuine"). Common in spoken Tagalog and in literary expression of strong emotion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** tunay na kaibigan
**Transliteration:** tunay na kaibigan
**Literal breakdown:** *tunay* + *na* (linker) + *kaibigan* (friend)
**Natural English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
**Context of use:** One of the most common collocations of *tunay*, used to distinguish a person of genuine loyalty and care from a superficial or fair-weather acquaintance. Extremely frequent in everyday speech and in proverbs.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tunay na pagmamahal
**Transliteration:** tunay na pagmamahal
**Literal breakdown:** *tunay* + *na* + *pagmamahal* (love — *pag-* + *mahal*)
**Natural English translation:** true love, genuine love, authentic love
**Context of use:** Used to distinguish sincere romantic or familial love from infatuation, pretense, or self-interest. Extremely common in popular culture, literature, and everyday discourse about relationships.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Context of use:** Used to identify the genuine source of opposition or threat, often in contrast to a perceived but false enemy (*Ang tunay na kaaway ay ang kahirapan* "The true enemy is poverty"). Common in political and rhetorical discourse.

**Context of use:** Used to assert the actual state of affairs as opposed to what is being reported, claimed, or perceived (*Alam mo ba ang tunay na nangyayari?* "Do you know what is really happening?"). Common in investigative and critical discourse.

---

**Note:** *tunay* is highly productive as an attributive modifier. The pattern *tunay na + [noun]* is essentially open and can combine with any noun to assert the genuine or authentic instance of that category. The items above represent the most conventionalized and frequently attested collocations. The verbal cluster around *patunayan* (items 15–19) is particularly productive and central to the domain of proof and verification.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the root's contribution shifting in meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**ROOT: totoo**

- *Totoo ang sakit* ("The pain is real") — *totoo* applied to physical sensation rather than propositional content.
- *Totoo ang buhay* — *totoo* applied to the ontological status of existence itself rather than to a statement or representation.
- *Totoo ang nararamdaman* — *totoo* applied to the validity or authenticity of emotional states.
- *Parang totoo* — *totoo* functioning as a standard of resemblance in aesthetic evaluation, where the question is not whether something is true but whether it resembles truth closely enough.
- *Makatotohanang-makatotohanan* — *totoo* embedded in an aesthetic evaluation of degree of realism, where the root contributes a standard of comparison rather than a direct truth-claim.
- *Totoo sa buhay* — *totoo* used as a criterion of aesthetic fidelity to lived experience.

---

**ROOT: tunay**

- *Tunay na halaga* — *tunay* applied to economic or intrinsic worth, where the contrast is between nominal/market value and some deeper or more essential value.
- *Tunay na Diyos* — *tunay* applied in theological discourse to distinguish a deity's ontological status from that of false gods; the contrast is metaphysical rather than factual or representational.
- *Tunay na sining* — *tunay* applied as an aesthetic criterion distinguishing authentic artistic expression from imitation or commercial production.
- *Tunay na kalayaan* — *tunay* applied to a political concept to distinguish substantive from formal instantiation.
- *Tunay na tagumpay* — *tunay* applied to evaluate the depth or quality of an achievement rather than its factual occurrence.
- *Tunay na buhay* — *tunay* applied to the quality or mode of existence rather than to a statement or object.
- *Tunay na pagkatao* — *tunay* applied to inner character or essential selfhood, where the contrast is between surface persona and deeper identity.
- *Tunay na kahulugan* — *tunay* applied in interpretive contexts to assert a privileged or authoritative reading of meaning.
- *Patunayan* (verbal) — the root *tunay* contributing to a causative-verificational verbal form where the action is one of establishing or demonstrating rather than simply asserting.

---

**ROOT: tapat**

- *Tapat* (spatial sense: "directly facing, opposite") — the root retains an active spatial meaning in non-epistemic contexts (*Tapat ang bahay nila sa amin* "Their house is directly across from ours"), where no truth or honesty evaluation is involved.
- *Itapat* (spatial use) — the directional verbal form used to physically align or orient an object, with no moral or epistemic content.
- *Tapat na puso* — *tapat* applied to an inner disposition or organ as a metaphor for sincerity, where the spatial sense of "directness" appears to underlie the moral extension.
- *Katapatan sa Diyos* / *katapatan sa bansa* — *katapatan* functioning in contexts of relational loyalty and devotion rather than epistemic honesty; the truth-domain contribution is absent or secondary.
- *Katapatan ng loob* — *tapat* combined with *loob* (inner self), where the expression evaluates the authenticity of inner disposition rather than the truth-value of a statement.
- *Tapat na paglilingkod* / *tapat na serbisyo* — *tapat* applied to the quality of institutional or civic service, where the primary evaluation is of dedication and integrity rather than of truthfulness in speech.
- *Tapat na pag-ibig* — *tapat* applied to romantic or religious love, where the evaluation concerns fidelity and constancy rather than honesty in communication.
