| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं वद | satyaṃ vada | Speak the truth. | A fixed injunctive phrase from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1), widely cited in ethical and educational contexts as a foundational moral injunction. The construction *satyaṃ vada* (with *satya* in the accusative as the object of *vad-*) is one of the most widely attested ethical imperatives in Sanskrit literature. Functions as a complete imperative sentence and is quoted as a unit across philosophical, didactic, and popular discourse. The phrase appears in the *śikṣāvallī*'s instructions to the graduating student: *satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara* ("Speak truth; walk in *dharma*"). | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं ब्रूहि | satyaṃ brūhi | Tell the truth; speak truly. | A near-synonym of *satyaṃ vada* using the root *brū-* (to speak, to say) rather than *vad-*; appears in legal and narrative contexts as an injunction to a witness or interlocutor to speak truthfully. The two constructions are functionally equivalent but *brūhi* is more common in direct address in legal and narrative contexts. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं ब्रूयात् | satyaṃ brūyāt | One should speak the truth. | A fixed prescriptive phrase from Manusmṛti and related Dharmaśāstra literature, stating the duty of truthful speech in the optative mood. Often cited as the first half of a longer injunction qualified by conditions under which truth-telling may be overridden. The full verse (*satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt, na brūyāt satyam apriyam*) is a well-known ethical maxim: "One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasant; one should not speak the truth if it is unpleasant." | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं ब्रूयात् प्रियं ब्रूयात् न ब्रूयात् सत्यमप्रियम् | satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt na brūyāt satyam apriyam | One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasing; one should not speak truth that is unpleasant. | A fixed three-part injunctive sequence from Manusmṛti (4.138), widely cited in ethical discourse on the conditions and limits of truthful speech. The full sequence is quoted as a unit in discussions of the relationship between truth and kindness in speech. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यमेव जयते | satyam eva jayate | Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails. | A fixed phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. The *eva* (emphatic particle, "alone, indeed") is integral to the construction and cannot be omitted without changing the meaning. Quoted as a complete sentence across philosophical, political, and rhetorical contexts; functions as a maxim asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः | satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ | Established in truth / grounded in truth. | A periphrastic construction using the locative of *satya* with the past passive participle *pratiṣṭhita* (established, grounded, from *prati-* + root *sthā-*), used in epic and Purāṇic literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness. Appears in Vedāntic and epic literature as a characterization of the sage or the virtuous person. The locative + *pratiṣṭhita* pattern is productive across ethical vocabulary. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यात् न प्रचलति | satyāt na pracalati | Does not deviate from truth / does not swerve from truth. | A periphrastic construction using the ablative of *satya* with the negative particle *na* and the verb *pracalati* (deviates, moves away from, from *pra-* + root *cal-*), used in epic literature to characterize a person of unwavering truthfulness. The ablative + *na pracalati* pattern is a standard way of expressing steadfast adherence to a value. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यात् न चलति / सत्यात् न विचलति | satyāt na calati / satyāt na vicalati | Does not deviate from truth; does not swerve from truth. | A construction using the ablative *satyāt* (from truth) with verbs of motion (*cal-*, *vical-*) and negation, expressing steadfast adherence to truth; common in epic literature as a characterization of virtuous heroes. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं परं धर्मः | satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ | Truth is the highest dharma. | A fixed maxim attested in the Mahābhārata (e.g., Ādiparvan) and widely cited in ethical discourse, asserting the primacy of truthfulness among all dharmic obligations. Functions as a complete nominal sentence and is quoted as a unit. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं हि परमो धर्मः | satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ | For truth is indeed the highest dharma. | A variant of the preceding maxim with the emphatic particle *hi* (indeed, for), widely attested in the Mahābhārata and in later ethical literature. The two forms (*satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ* and *satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ*) circulate as near-synonymous maxims and are sometimes cited interchangeably. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं नास्ति परो धर्मः | satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ | There is no higher *dharma* than truth. | A formulaic maxim attested in the Mahābhārata and widely cited in ethical literature. Functions as a fixed proverbial expression asserting the supremacy of truth among moral values. The construction *nāsti paro X* ("there is no higher X") is itself a formulaic pattern in Sanskrit ethical literature. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्येन उत्तभिता भूमिः | satyena uttabhitā bhūmiḥ | The earth is upheld by truth. | A fixed cosmological statement attested in Vedic and epic literature, asserting that truth is the foundational support of the earth and cosmos. Used in hymnic and didactic contexts to express the ontological and cosmological primacy of *satya*. The instrumental *satyena* (by means of truth) with a passive participle is the standard construction. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं न परित्यजेत् | satyaṃ na parityajet | One should not abandon truth. | A prescriptive construction using the accusative of *satya* with the negative optative of *parityaj-* (to abandon, from *pari-* + root *tyaj-*), used in Dharmaśāstra and epic literature to state the duty of maintaining truthfulness under all circumstances. The negative optative construction is a standard prescriptive form in Sanskrit ethical literature. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्येन शपामि / सत्येन शपे | satyena śapāmi / satyena śape | I swear by truth / I take an oath by truth. | An oath formula using the instrumental *satyena* (by truth) with the verb *śap-* (to swear, to take an oath); appears in legal and epic literature as a formula for oath-taking. The instrumental marks truth as the instrument or ground of the oath. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं किल / सत्यं खलु | satyaṃ kila / satyaṃ khalu | Truly indeed; it is indeed true (that...). | A discourse-marking construction in which *satyam* (neuter accusative used adverbially) combines with affirmative particles (*kila*, *khalu*) to assert the truth of a following proposition; common in narrative and philosophical prose as a marker of affirmation or concession. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं तु / सत्यं तथापि | satyaṃ tu / satyaṃ tathāpi | True, but... / True, yet even so... | A concessive construction in which *satyam* (adverbially used) concedes a point before introducing a qualification or counter-consideration; common in philosophical argumentation and commentary literature as a device for acknowledging an opponent's point before responding. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम् | satyāt na pramaditavyam | One must not deviate from truth. | A gerundive construction expressing prohibition, attested in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1) as part of the same passage as *satyaṃ vada*. The gerundive *pramaditavyam* (from root *mad-* with prefix *pra-*, "to be negligent, to deviate") with the ablative of *satya* and the negation *na* forms a fixed injunctive formula. | [CORE] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | सत्यं प्रतिजानीते | satyaṃ pratijānīte | One pledges truth; one vows truthfully. | A construction using *satya* in the accusative with the verb *pratijñā-* (to pledge, to vow); appears in legal and epic contexts to describe the act of making a truthful pledge or vow. | [MARGINAL] |
| सत्य (*satya*) | साधु इति मन्यते | sādhu iti manyate | Considers (it) to be correct / regards (it) as proper. | A periphrastic construction using *sādhu* within an *iti*-clause as the content of the verb *man-* (to think, to consider), used in philosophical and grammatical literature to describe the cognitive act of evaluating something as correct or proper. | [CORE] |
| ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतस्य पथा | ṛtasya pathā | By the path of cosmic order / along the way of truth. | A fixed phrase from Vedic hymns (Ṛgveda), using the genitive of *ṛta* with the instrumental of *path* (path, way), designating the way of cosmic order along which the gods and the righteous travel. Attested as a formulaic expression in ritual and cosmological contexts. Confined to Vedic poetic register. | [MARGINAL] |
| ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतस्य पन्थाः | ṛtasya panthāḥ | The path of *ṛta*; the way of cosmic order. | A genitive construction attested in the Ṛgveda to designate the path or way that accords with cosmic order, often in the context of ritual correctness or moral conduct. The phrase functions as a conventional expression for the right way of acting or proceeding. | [CORE] |
| ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतं च सत्यं च | ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca | Both cosmic order and truth / ṛta and satya. | A fixed dvandva-style pairing attested in Vedic texts (e.g., Ṛgveda 10.190.1: *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhyajāyata*; also Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.1.1), where *ṛta* and *satya* are enumerated together as the foundational principles arising from cosmic heat (*tapas*). The pairing functions as a formulaic expression of the two aspects of cosmic and moral truth. The coordination is not merely additive but marks a conventional pairing that appears repeatedly in Vedic literature; the two terms are treated as complementary aspects of a single domain. | [CORE] |
| ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतेन ऋतम् अपिहितम् | ṛtena ṛtam apihitam | Truth is covered by truth / cosmic order is veiled by cosmic order. | A fixed enigmatic phrase from the Ṛgveda (5.62.1), also attested in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (5.15.1) and Īśā Upaniṣad (15), using the instrumental of *ṛta* with the past passive participle *apihita* (covered, concealed, from *api-* + root *dhā-*), expressing the paradoxical concealment of cosmic order within itself. Cited in philosophical and exegetical literature as an example of Vedic enigmatic speech (*brahmodya*). It is a fixed liturgical and philosophical formula rather than a free compositional phrase. | [MARGINAL] |
| ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतस्य योनौ | ṛtasya yonau | In the womb/source of cosmic order. | A fixed locative phrase from Vedic hymns, using the genitive of *ṛta* with the locative of *yoni* (womb, source, origin), designating the primordial source from which cosmic order arises or in which it is grounded. | ऋत (*ṛta*) | ऋतस्य योनिः | ṛtasya yoniḥ | The womb/source of *ṛta*; the origin of cosmic order. | A genitive construction attested in Vedic literature to designate the source or origin of cosmic order, typically applied to a deity (especially Varuṇa or Mitra) or to the sacrificial fire.
