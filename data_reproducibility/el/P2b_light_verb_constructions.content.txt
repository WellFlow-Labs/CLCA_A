## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

### Base: αλήθεια (alítheia) "truth"

#### Construction 1: λέω την αλήθεια
**Native form:** λέω την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** léo tin alítheia
**Translation:** to tell the truth
**Usage:** Standard expression for truthful speaking. Extremely common in everyday discourse and formal contexts.
**[CORE]**

#### Construction 2: η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Native form:** η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Transliteration:** i alítheia íne óti
**Translation:** the truth is that
**Usage:** Fixed phrase introducing a frank or honest statement, often a correction or clarification. Very common in everyday discourse.
**[CORE]**

#### Construction 3: στην αλήθεια
**Native form:** στην αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** stin alítheia
**Translation:** in truth, actually, in fact
**Usage:** Prepositional phrase used to introduce truthful statements or corrections. Common in both everyday and formal speech.
**[CORE]**

#### Construction 4: αλήθεια; (interrogative)
**Native form:** αλήθεια;
**Transliteration:** alítheia?

**Usage:** Single-word question expressing surprise or seeking confirmation. Extremely common in everyday conversation.
**[CORE]**

#### Construction 5: αλήθεια μου
**Native form:** αλήθεια μου
**Transliteration:** alítheia mou

## CROSS-CUTTING PATTERNS

### Pattern 1: Truth-telling verbs + truth nouns

Multiple bases combine with λέω "to say/tell" to form standard expressions:
- λέω την αλήθεια (tell the truth)
- λέω ψέματα (tell lies)

This pattern is highly productive and represents the primary way of expressing truth-telling and lying in Greek.

### Pattern 2: Adjective + ιστορία "story"

Multiple truth-related adjectives combine with ιστορία:
- αληθινή ιστορία (true story)
- πραγματική ιστορία (real story)
- κατασκευασμένη ιστορία (fabricated story)

This pattern is productive for distinguishing factual from fictional narratives.

### Pattern 3: Adjective + πηγή "source"

Multiple reliability adjectives combine with πηγή:
- έγκυρη πηγή (valid source)
- αξιόπιστη πηγή (reliable source)

This pattern is common in academic and journalistic discourse for evaluating information sources.

### Pattern 4: Adjective + έγγραφο "document"

Multiple authenticity adjectives combine with έγγραφο:
- έγκυρο έγγραφο (valid document)
- άκυρο έγγραφο (invalid document)
- πλαστό έγγραφο (forged document)

This pattern is extremely common in legal and administrative contexts.

### Pattern 5: Color + ψέμα "lie"

Greek uses color adjectives to modify ψέμα:
- άσπρο ψέμα (white lie)
- μαύρο ψέμα (black lie)
- κόκκινο ψέμα (red lie/blatant lie)

This pattern creates a scale of lie severity through color metaphors.

### Pattern 6: Prepositional phrases with truth nouns

Several truth nouns form common prepositional phrases:
- στην αλήθεια (in truth, actually)
- στην πραγματικότητα (in reality, actually)
- από λάθος / κατά λάθος (by mistake)

These function as discourse markers and adverbial modifiers.

### Pattern 7: κάνω "to do/make" + error nouns

The verb κάνω combines with error terms:
- κάνω λάθος (to make a mistake)
- κάνω σωστά (to do correctly)

This pattern is extremely productive for expressing correctness and error in action.
