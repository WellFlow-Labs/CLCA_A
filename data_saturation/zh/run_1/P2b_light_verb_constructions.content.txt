# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve P1 base forms. The boundary between "construction" and "compound" is not always sharp in Chinese; the criterion applied here is that the construction must involve at least two syntactically separable elements where the P1 item functions as a component within a larger, conventionalized multi-word unit, rather than as a fused lexical item. Fixed four-character expressions (成语) already documented in P1 and P1b are not re-listed here unless they appear as components within a larger construction pattern. Derivational forms documented in P2a are excluded.

Where no common construction is identified for a P1 item or for a particular construction type, this is stated explicitly. Absence is preserved as data.

## Constructions by Base Form

---

### Base Form: 真 (zhēn)

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 这是真的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** This is true / This is real / This is genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The copular construction X + 是 + 真的 is the most basic predication of truth or genuineness in Chinese. The particle 的 nominalizes or substantivizes 真, producing a predicative structure. Ubiquitous across all registers and contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 是真是假
**Transliteration:** shì zhēn shì jiǎ
**English Translation:** Whether it is true or false; is it real or fake?
**Usage Notes:** The parallel structure 是X是Y presents two alternatives for evaluation. This construction frames the authenticity question as an open binary and is common in investigative, commercial, and everyday discourse. Closely related to 真假难辨.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 说真的
**Transliteration:** shuō zhēn de
**English Translation:** To be honest; speaking truthfully; honestly speaking.
**Usage Notes:** A discourse-initial or parenthetical expression used to signal that the speaker is about to make a frank or sincere statement. Parallel in function to 说实话 and 老实说. Common in conversational registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 来真的
**Transliteration:** lái zhēn de
**English Translation:** To do it for real; to get serious; to mean business.
**Usage Notes:** A colloquial construction using the verb 来 (to come/do) with 真的 to assert that something is being done genuinely rather than as a pretense or game. Contrasts with 来假的 (to go through the motions). Common in informal spoken registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 弄假成真
**Transliteration:** nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn
**English Translation:** What started as a pretense becomes real.
**Usage Notes:** Already documented in P1b (Root 3a, #11). Not re-analyzed here.
*(documented in P1b)*

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 假戏真做
**Transliteration:** jiǎ xì zhēn zuò
**English Translation:** To play a fake role as if it were real; to act out a fiction as though it were genuine.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed expression describing the situation in which someone performs a pretense with genuine commitment, so that the boundary between fiction and reality blurs. Used in dramatic, interpersonal, and political contexts. Distinct from 弄假成真 in that the performative frame is maintained even as the execution becomes genuine.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 不是真的
**Transliteration:** bù shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** It is not true / It is not real / It is not genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The negated copular construction, parallel to Construction 1. The most basic predication of non-truth or inauthenticity. Ubiquitous across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base Form: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 这是假的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì jiǎ de
**English Translation:** This is fake / This is false / This is not genuine.
**Usage Notes:** The copular construction X + 是 + 假的, parallel to Construction 1 for 真. The most basic predication of inauthenticity or falseness. Ubiquitous across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 装假
**Transliteration:** zhuāng jiǎ
**English Translation:** To put on a false front; to pretend; to fake it.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 装 (to pretend, to put on) combined with 假 produces a colloquial expression for performing inauthenticity. Common in everyday speech. Overlaps with 假装 but 装假 foregrounds the performance aspect more explicitly.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 打假
**Transliteration:** dǎ jiǎ
**English Translation:** To crack down on counterfeits; to combat fakes; anti-counterfeiting.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 打 (to strike, to combat) combined with 假 produces a widely used expression in commercial, regulatory, and legal discourse for efforts to suppress counterfeit goods or fraudulent practices. 打假行动 (anti-counterfeiting campaign) is a standard collocation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 造假
**Transliteration:** zào jiǎ
**English Translation:** To fabricate; to falsify; to manufacture fakes.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 造 (to make, to manufacture) combined with 假 produces a common expression for the act of creating false documents, data, or objects. Used in legal, academic, and journalistic contexts: 数据造假 (data fabrication), 学术造假 (academic fraud). Closely related to 伪造 but more colloquial and broader in application.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 掺假
**Transliteration:** chān jiǎ
**English Translation:** To adulterate; to mix in fake or inferior material.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 掺 (to mix in, to adulterate) combined with 假 describes the practice of mixing counterfeit or inferior material into genuine goods. Common in food safety, commercial, and regulatory discourse: 食品掺假 (food adulteration).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 作假
**Transliteration:** zuò jiǎ
**English Translation:** To falsify; to cheat; to engage in deception.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 作 (to do, to engage in) combined with 假 produces a common expression for deliberate falsification or cheating. Used in academic, commercial, and administrative contexts: 考试作假 (cheating on an exam), 账目作假 (falsifying accounts). Overlaps with 造假 but 作假 more often implies procedural or documentary falsification.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Example:** 纠正错误
**Transliteration:** jiūzhèng cuòwù
**English Translation:** To correct an error; to rectify a mistake.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 纠正 (to correct, to rectify) combined with 错误 is a standard collocation in educational, political, and everyday discourse. Appears frequently in political self-criticism and administrative language.

**Construction 27**
**Base Form:** 错误 (cuòwù)
**Example:** 承认错误
**Transliteration:** chéngrèn cuòwù
**English Translation:** To admit a mistake; to acknowledge an error.
**Usage Notes:** The verb 承认 (to admit, to acknowledge) combined with 错误 is a standard collocation in interpersonal, legal, and political discourse. Contrasts with 否认错误 (to deny a mistake).

---

**Construction 28**
**Base Form:** 错误 (cuòwù)
**Example:** 这是一个严重的错误

**English Translation:** This is a serious mistake.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Summary Observations

The following patterns are supported by the data above and are reported without interpretation beyond what the data warrant:

1. **Verb + P1 noun collocations** are the most productive construction type in this domain. The verbs 揭露, 掩盖, 查明, 还原, 歪曲, 尊重, 辨别, 鉴别 each combine productively with truth/authenticity nouns (真相, 事实, 真伪) to form standard collocations. These collocations are not fixed expressions but are highly conventionalized.

2. **P1 adjective + 的 + noun** (attributive construction) is productive for 客观, 可靠, 准确, 正确, 道地, and 名副其实, generating standard evaluative noun phrases.

3. **P1 adverb + verb** (adverbial modification) is productive for 如实, 准确, and 客观, generating standard collocations for truthful or accurate performance of communicative acts.

4. **Prohibitive constructions** (严禁/不得 + P1 item) are attested specifically for 弄虚作假 and 造假, reflecting their use in regulatory and administrative language. This pattern is not attested for other P1 items in this domain.

5. **Contrastive pairs** are productive: 揭露真相 / 掩盖真相; 表里如一 / 表里不一; 名副其实 / 名不副实; 客观 / 主观; 真的 / 假的. The existence of one pole of a contrastive pair reliably predicts the other.

6. **The construction 真相大白** (the truth comes to light) is the only attested resultative construction with a P1 item as subject in this domain. No parallel resultative construction was identified for 事实, 真实, or other P1 nouns.

7. **Degree modification** is attested for 错误 (严重的错误), 失实 (严重失实), and 主观 (过于主观), but not productively for 真相, 事实, or 真伪, which resist degree modification in standard usage.
