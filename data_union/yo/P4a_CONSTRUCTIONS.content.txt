# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note:** Yoruba is an SVO, isolating-analytic language with post-nominal modification. "Derivation" in the morphological sense is largely limited to prefixal nominalization. Most of the patterns below are syntactic rather than morphological. Tone marks follow standard Yoruba orthography throughout.

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + stative verb/adjective (*tọ́*, *gidi*, *dára*) OR Subject + copula *jẹ́* + nominal predicate (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*, *òdodo*)

Also: [NOUN] + [EVALUATIVE NOUN/ADJECTIVE] in attributive (post-nominal modifier) position; no copula required in attributive position.

### Examples
- **Ó tọ́** → *ó tọ́* → "It is correct / it is right"
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Your statement is the truth"
- Ó tọ́ → *ó tọ́* → It is correct / It is right / It is proper
- Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo → *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* → His/her certificate is genuine/valid
- **ènìyàn òtítọ́** → *ènìyàn òtítọ́* → "a truthful person / a person of truth"
- **ohun ojúlówó** → *ohun ojúlówó* → "a genuine thing / the real article"

### Notes
- The root *tọ́* functions directly as a stative predicate without a copula; *jẹ́* is used when the predicate is a full nominal. Both patterns are productive across the truth-domain vocabulary.
- The root *tọ́* functions directly as a stative predicate without a copula; for nominal predicates (e.g., *irọ́*, *òdodo*, *gidi*), the copula *jẹ́* is required. Both patterns are productive across factual, moral, and administrative domains.
- Yoruba attributive modification is strictly post-nominal. The modifier (*òtítọ́*, *ojúlówó*, *gidi*, *burúkú*, etc.) follows the head noun directly with no intervening morphology. This pattern is productive across the truth-domain vocabulary: *ọ̀rẹ́ gidi* ("a true friend"), *ìdájọ́ tọ́* ("a correct judgment").

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *kò* (negation) + stative verb/adjective OR Subject + *kò jẹ́* + nominal predicate

Also: *kì í ṣe* + nominal predicate (negative copula for nominal predicates)

Also: [NOUN] + **kò** + **jẹ́** + [EVALUATIVE NOUN/ADJECTIVE] OR [NOUN] + **kò** + [STATIVE PREDICATE]

### Examples
- **Kò tọ́** → *kò tọ́* → "It is not correct / it is wrong"
- **Kò jẹ́ gidi** → *kò jẹ́ gidi* → "It is not genuine / it is not authentic"
- Kò tọ́ → *kò tọ́* → It is not correct / It is wrong / It is improper
- Kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi → *kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* → It is not the real thing / It is not genuine
- **Kò jẹ́ ohun ojúlówó.** → *Kò jẹ́ ohun ojúlówó.* → "It is not a genuine thing. / It is not authentic."
- **Ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́.** → *Ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́.* → "The judgment is not correct / just."

### Notes
- *kò* is the standard negation marker for stative and copular predicates; it precedes the predicate directly. The pattern is fully parallel to the affirmative construction.
- *kò* negates stative and verbal predicates; *kì í ṣe* is the negative copula used with nominal predicates. The two forms are not interchangeable: *kò tọ́* (stative negation) vs. *kì í ṣe irọ́* (nominal negation).
- For nominal predicates (*ohun ojúlówó*, *ènìyàn gidi*), negation requires the copula *jẹ́* preceded by *kò*. For stative verbal predicates (*tọ́* "to be correct", *dára* "to be good"), *kò* precedes the verb directly with no copula. The two sub-patterns are structurally distinct.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Head noun + modifier (post-nominal position): N + *tọ́* / *rere* / *burúkú* / *gidi* / *ńlá*

Also: [HEAD NOUN] + [MODIFIER NOUN] by juxtaposition; the second noun restricts or specifies the first. No genitive particle is required for close compounds; the associative linker **ti** / **tí** appears in relative-clause modification.

### Examples
- **Ìdáhùn tọ́** → *ìdáhùn tọ́* → "Correct answer"
- **Ìwà rere** → *ìwà rere* → "Good character / virtue"
- ẹ̀rí ojú → *ẹ̀rí ojú* → eyewitness evidence (lit. eye-evidence)
- ìdáhùn tọ́ → *ìdáhùn tọ́* → correct answer (lit. answer [that is] correct)
- **ẹ̀rí ojú** → *ẹ̀rí ojú* → "eyewitness evidence" (lit. "evidence of the eye")
- **ìwádìí òtítọ́** → *ìwádìí òtítọ́* → "investigation of the truth / fact-finding"

### Notes
- Yoruba adjectives and evaluative modifiers follow the noun they modify. The modifier slot is productive: *tọ́*, *rere*, *burúkú*, *gidi*, *ńlá* (great), *jinlẹ̀* (deep) all occupy this position with truth-domain nouns.
- Yoruba is head-initial in nominal modification: the modifying noun or adjective follows the head noun directly, without an overt linker in most cases. Adjectival predicates (*tọ́*, *rere*, *búburú*) double as postnominal modifiers in this pattern.
- Yoruba adnominal modification by juxtaposition is the primary means of forming domain-specific compounds across the truth vocabulary: *ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn* ("human rights"), *ẹ̀rí àkọsílẹ̀* ("documentary evidence"), *ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí* ("birth certificate"). The pattern [HEAD + MODIFIER] is right-headed in the sense that the first noun names the category and the second specifies the domain.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*Di* (become) + nominal predicate OR directional motion construction (*padà sí*, *jáde kúrò nínú*) + nominal

Also: *ó farahàn pé* + propositional complement; *yí padà* (serial verb: to turn/change)

Also: **di** + [NOUN/ADJECTIVE] as the standard inchoative/change-of-state verb

### Examples
- **Padà sí ìgbàgbọ́** → *padà sí ìgbàgbọ́* → "Return to faith / come back to belief"
- **Jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀** → *jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀* → "Come out of ignorance / leave ignorance behind"
- Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni → *ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* → It became clear that it is the truth / It turned out to be true
- Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà → *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà* → His/her belief changed / His/her faith shifted
- **Ó di ènìyàn òtítọ́.** → *Ó di ènìyàn òtítọ́.* → "He/she became a truthful person."
- **Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ di lágbára.** → *Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ di lágbára.* → "His/her belief became strong."

### Notes
- Yoruba does not have a dedicated inchoative morpheme. Change-of-state meaning is expressed through motion verbs (*padà* "return", *jáde kúrò* "come out from") with directional complements, or through *di* (become) + nominal. The motion-based pattern is more frequent in the truth domain.
- Yoruba lacks a dedicated inchoative morpheme. Change-of-state is expressed lexically: *farahàn* (to become visible/clear) for epistemic revelation, and the serial verb *yí padà* (lit. turn-return) for change in a state or orientation.
- *di* is the standard inchoative/change-of-state verb in Yoruba and combines freely with nominal and adjectival predicates. It is not specific to the truth domain but applies across it. No dedicated inchoative morphology exists; the construction is fully periphrastic.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
*Jẹ́ kí* (let/cause) + NP subject + predicate OR instrumental *fi* + NP + verb

Also: Serial verb construction: *fi* X *hàn* (use X to show/reveal); *fi* X *gbà* Y (use X to obtain Y); *jẹ́ kí* + subjunctive

Also: **mú** + [NOUN/CLAUSE] + **wá** (serial verb: "bring about") OR **jẹ́ kí** + [CLAUSE] (permissive/causative) OR **fi** + [INSTRUMENT NOUN] + [VERB PHRASE] (instrumental causative)

### Examples
- **Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ** → *jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ* → "Let truth be your guide / cause truth to lead you"
- **Fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀** → *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀* → "Speak with truth / speak truthfully" (use truth as instrument of speech)
- Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀ → *ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* → He/she presented evidence before the court (caused evidence to be seen)
- Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó → *ó fi irọ́ gbà owó* → He/she obtained money through deception (used lies to cause receipt of money)
- **Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀.** → *Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀.* → "They used deception to take his/her money." (instrumental: deception as means)
- **Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn.** → *Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn.* → "It is testing that causes a person's character to be revealed." (focus + instrumental causative)

### Notes
- Yoruba lacks a productive causative morpheme in this domain. Causative meaning is expressed periphrastically via *jẹ́ kí* (permissive-causative) or via the instrumental serial verb *fi* (use/employ). The *fi* construction encodes manner rather than strict causation.
- Yoruba has no dedicated causative morpheme in this domain. Causation is expressed through serial verb constructions, most productively *fi* (to use/take) + object + result verb. The construction *jẹ́ kí* (let/allow that) expresses permissive causation in normative contexts (*Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́* — let us speak the truth).
- Yoruba has no morphological causative. The instrumental construction **fi** + [NOUN] + [VERB] is the most productive causative-adjacent pattern in the truth domain, expressing that one thing is the means by which another is brought about. The serial verb construction **mú...wá** ("bring") also functions causatively in some contexts.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ì-* prefix + verbal/adjectival root → abstract noun; also *à-* prefix + root; *o-/ọ-* prefix + root; *ẹ̀-* prefix + root

### Examples
- **Ìmọ̀** → *ìmọ̀* → "Knowledge" (from *mọ̀* "to know")
- **Àtúntọ́** → *àtúntọ́* → "Correction / rectification" (from *tún* "re-" + *tọ́* "correct")
- ìgbàgbọ́ → *ìgbàgbọ́* → belief, faith (from *gbàgbọ́*, to believe)
- òtítọ́ → *òtítọ́* → truth, correctness (from root *tọ́*, correct/straight)
- **ìmọ̀** → *ìmọ̀* → "knowledge" (from *mọ̀* "to know"; *ì-* + *mọ̀*)
- **òtítọ́** → *òtítọ́* → "truth, truthfulness" (from root *tọ* "to be straight/correct"; *ò-* + *tí* + *tọ́*)

### Notes
- The *ì-* prefix is the most productive nominalizer in the truth domain, yielding *ìmọ̀*, *ìgbàgbọ́*, *ìdánwò*, *ìjẹ́rìísí*, *ìtànjẹ́*, etc. The *à-* prefix appears in *àṣìṣe*, *àìmọ̀*, *àtúntọ́*. The *o-/ọ-* prefix appears in *òtítọ́*, *òdodo*. All three yield abstract nouns; the choice of prefix is lexically conditioned rather than semantically contrastive.
- Abstract nominalization is the most productive derivational process in this domain. The prefix *ì-* is the most frequent nominalizer of verbal roots; *ò-* and *ẹ̀-* produce distinct nominals from the same root with different semantic ranges (e.g., *òtítọ́* [truth] vs. *ẹ̀tọ́* [right/entitlement], both from *tọ́*). Negated abstract nominals are formed with *àì-* (*àìmọ̀* — ignorance, *àìgbàgbọ́* — unbelief).
- The *ì-* prefix is the most productive nominalizer across the truth-domain vocabulary: *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief), *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (trust), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (testimony), *ìtànjẹ́* (deception), *ìdánwò* (testing), *ìdájọ́* (judgment), *ìfọwọ́sí* (authentication), *ìfarahàn* (appearance/manifestation). The *ẹ̀-* prefix produces *ẹ̀rí* (evidence) and *ẹ̀tọ́* (right/correctness). The *à-* prefix produces *àṣìṣe* (error/wrongdoing) with a resultant-state or negative-outcome reading. Negation of nominalized forms is achieved via the prefix **àì-**: *àìmọ̀* (ignorance), *àìgbàgbọ́* (unbelief), *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (unreliability).

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*a-* prefix + verb root → agent noun (habitual/characterizing); *olù-* prefix + verb root → agent noun (role/function); *al-* / *ẹ-* prefix + noun → possessive agent

Also: *oní-* / *olú-* + nominal base → agentive from nominal base

Also: **ẹni** + [NOUN] → contracted form **ẹlẹ́-** + [NOUN]; OR **ènìyàn** + [EVALUATIVE NOUN] (periphrastic)

Also: *aláì-* + verbal root → negative agentive

### Examples
- **Aṣírọ́** → *aṣírọ́* → "Liar / one who tells lies" (from *ṣ-* "do" + *irọ́* "lie")
- **Adájọ́** → *adájọ́* → "Judge / one who adjudicates" (from *dájọ́* "pass judgment")
- olùgbàgbọ́ → *olùgbàgbọ́* → believer, one who believes (from *gbàgbọ́*)
- ẹlẹ́rìí → *ẹlẹ́rìí* → witness (contracted from *ẹni tó ní ẹ̀rí*, one who has/bears evidence)
- **ẹlẹ́rìí** → *ẹlẹ́rìí* → "witness" (lit. "person of evidence"; from *ẹni* + *ẹ̀rí*, contracted)
- **ènìyàn irọ́** → *ènìyàn irọ́* → "a lying person / a liar"

### Notes
- Three agentive prefixes are attested: *a-* (habitual agent, often with moral valence: *aṣírọ́*, *aṣìṣe*, *aṣètànjẹ́*), *olù-* (role agent: *olùdánwò* "examiner", *olùtọ́ni* "guide"), and *ẹ-/al-* (possessive agent: *ẹlẹ́rìí* "witness", *olòtítọ́* "truthful person"). The *a-* prefix tends to appear in morally evaluative terms; *olù-* in functional role terms.
- Two agentive strategies are attested: the prefix *olù-* attaches to verbal roots to produce agent nouns (*olùgbàgbọ́*, *olùmọ̀*, *olùjẹ́rìí*); the possessive relative construction *ẹni tó ní* + noun contracts to produce agent nouns from nominal bases (*ẹlẹ́rìí* from *ẹ̀rí*). The pattern *oní-* / *olú-* also produces agentives from nominal bases (*olóirọ́* — liar, *olòtítọ́* — truthful person). Negative agentives are formed with *aláì-* (*aláìgbàgbọ́* — unbeliever, *aláìmọ̀* — ignorant person).
- The contracted agentive form **ẹlẹ́-** + noun is attested specifically for *ẹ̀rí* → *ẹlẹ́rìí* ("witness") and extends to *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* ("eyewitness"). This contraction is lexicalized rather than fully productive. For other truth-domain agents (liar, truthful person, deceiver), the periphrastic pattern *ènìyàn* + [noun] is used: *ènìyàn òtítọ́* ("truthful person"), *ènìyàn irọ́* ("liar"), *ènìyàn ìtànjẹ́* ("a deceptive/fraudulent person"). No single productive agentive suffix exists across the domain.

## 8. FOCUS / EMPHATIC IDENTIFICATION

### Pattern
Predicate nominal or focused constituent + focus marker *ni* (copular-focus); or *ló* (= *ni* + *o*) on focused subject

Also: Focused constituent + *ni* (focus particle) + remainder of clause

Also: [FOCUSED ELEMENT] + **ló** (focus marker, from *ni* + *ó*) + [PREDICATE]

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ ni** → *òtítọ́ ni* → "It is the truth / that is true" (emphatic)
- **Ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là** → *ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là* → "It is knowledge that saves a person" (focus on *ìmọ̀*)
- Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ → *òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* → It is the truth that he/she spoke / What he/she spoke is the truth
- Ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn → *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* → It is character that makes a person
- **Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.** → *Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.* → "It is truth that saves one."
- **Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.** → *Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.* → "It is good character that saves one."

### Notes
- The focus construction is highly productive in truth-domain assertions and proverbs. *ni* follows the focused predicate in identificational sentences; *ló* (subject-focus) marks the focused NP as the unique or contrastive answer to an implicit question. This construction is not derivational but is a primary syntactic strategy for asserting truth-domain claims emphatically.
- The focus particle *ni* (and its fused form *ló* = *ni* + *ó* before a following subject pronoun) places contrastive or identificational focus on the fronted constituent. This construction is highly productive in truth-domain contexts for asserting or contesting the truth-value of a claim, and in proverbial statements asserting the primacy of a concept.
- The focus construction is pervasive in Yoruba proverbial and moral discourse and is not specific to the truth domain, but it is the standard form for asserting the primacy or causal role of truth (*òtítọ́*), knowledge (*ìmọ̀*), good character (*ìwà rere*), and testing (*ìdánwò*) in moral and epistemic contexts. It is structurally distinct from simple predication and warrants separate documentation.

## 9. PROPOSITIONAL COMPLEMENT CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Epistemic/evidential verb + *pé* (complementizer) + embedded clause

Also: **ní ìgbàgbọ́ pé** + [CLAUSE] ("have the belief/conviction that") OR **mọ̀ pé** + [CLAUSE] ("know that") OR **gbàgbọ́ pé** + [CLAUSE] ("believe that")

### Examples
- **Mọ̀ pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *mọ̀ pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Know that it is the truth"
- **Jẹ́rìí pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *jẹ́rìí pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Testify that it is true / bear witness that it is the truth"
- Ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni → *ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni* → He/she believes that it is the truth
- Ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀ → *ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀* → He/she testified that he/she was there
- **Ó ní ìgbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni.** → *Ó ní ìgbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni.* → "He/she has the conviction that it is the truth."
- **Mo mọ̀ dájúdájú pé ó ṣẹlẹ̀.** → *Mo mọ̀ dájúdájú pé ó ṣẹlẹ̀.* → "I know for certain that it happened."

### Notes
- *pé* is the standard complementizer introducing propositional content after epistemic verbs (*mọ̀* "know"), evidential verbs (*jẹ́rìí* "testify"), and evaluative predicates (*ó tọ́ pé* "it is right that"). This construction is the primary means of expressing propositional knowledge and testimony in Yoruba.
- The complementizer *pé* (that) is the standard introducer of propositional complements after epistemic verbs (*mọ̀*, *gbàgbọ́*), speech verbs (*sọ*, *jẹ́rìí*), and perception verbs. This construction is the primary syntactic frame for expressing propositional truth, belief, and testimony in Yoruba.
- The complementizer *pé* is obligatory in propositional belief and knowledge constructions. The epistemic intensifier *dájúdájú* ("certainly, definitely") inserts between the matrix verb and *pé*, as in *mo mọ̀ dájúdájú pé...*. Negation of propositional belief uses *kò gbàgbọ́ pé...* ("does not believe that...").

## 10. EXISTENTIAL ASSERTION / DENIAL OF EVIDENCE OR CERTAINTY

### Pattern
Noun phrase + *wà* (exist / there is) OR Noun phrase + *kò sí* (does not exist / there is no)

Also: **wà** + [LOCATIVE/CONTEXT] ("there is / it exists"); **kò sí** + [NOUN PHRASE] ("there is no / it does not exist")

### Examples
- **Ẹ̀rí kò sí** → *ẹ̀rí kò sí* → "There is no evidence / proof is absent"
- **Ìdánilójú wà** → *ìdánilójú wà* → "There is assurance / confirmation exists"
- Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ → *kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* → There is no truth in his/her words
- Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi → *kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi* → There is no sufficient evidence to convict him/her
- **Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó.** → *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó.* → "There is no sufficient evidence."
- **Ẹ̀rí wà tó fi hàn pé...** → *Ẹ̀rí wà tó fi hàn pé...* → "There is evidence that shows that..."

### Notes
- The existential pair *wà* / *kò sí* is a productive construction in legal and evaluative discourse for asserting the presence or absence of evidence, certainty, or verification. It is distinct from the attributive pattern (§1–2) in that it makes an existential rather than predicative claim.
- *kò sí* (there is not / there does not exist) is the standard negative existential construction. In truth-domain usage it denies the presence of truth, falsehood, justice, or evidence in a situation. The construction readily takes relative clause complements (*tó tó* — that is sufficient) and purpose clauses (*láti fi...* — in order to use...), making it productive in complex legal argument.
- This construction is the standard means of asserting or denying the existence of evidence (*ẹ̀rí*), truth (*òtítọ́*), resemblance (*ìjọra*), testimony (*ìjẹ́rìísí*), and entitlement (*ẹ̀tọ́*) in legal, moral, and everyday discourse. The emphatic particle *kankan* ("at all, whatsoever") reinforces total negation: *kò sí ìjọra kankan* ("there is no resemblance at all").
