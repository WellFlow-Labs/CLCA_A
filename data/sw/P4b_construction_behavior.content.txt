# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Swahili (sw)

| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | Habari hii ni kweli | habari hii ni kweli | "This news is true" | [CORE] | Standard predicative construction used across all registers. No restrictions observed. The copula ni/si shows invariant form in present tense across noun classes. |
| | Maneno yake ni sahihi | maneno yake ni sahihi | "His/her words are correct" | [CORE] | Same pattern as above; sahihi commonly used for correctness/accuracy in formal and informal contexts. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Hii si kweli | hii si kweli | "This is not true" | [CORE] | Most common negation strategy for truth-value denial. Used freely across all contexts. The invariant si form is standard for present tense negation. |
| | Bidhaa hizi si halisi | bidhaa hizi si halisi | "These products are not genuine" | [CORE] | Particularly common in commercial/consumer contexts for authenticity claims. Pattern identical to general negative copular construction. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | Mtu wa kweli | mtu wa kweli | "Genuine person / truthful person" | [CORE] | Highly productive associative construction. The wa variant shows class 1 agreement. Used for character attribution and moral evaluation. |
| | Ushahidi madhubuti | ushahidi madhubuti | "Reliable evidence" | [CORE] | Direct modification pattern common with adjectival truth-terms. Frequent in legal and formal contexts. Madhubuti shows class 11 agreement (u-). |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | Jambo limekamilika | jambo limekamilika | "The matter has become complete" | [CORE] | Stative extension -ik- productive for completion/finalization contexts. Common in formal and administrative language. |
| | Amekuwa mwongo | amekuwa mwongo | "He/she has become a liar" | [CORE] | Periphrastic inchoative with kuwa is the default strategy. Used for character change or revelation. More common than dedicated stative forms. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | Sahihisha maandishi | sahihisha maandishi | "Correct the writing / verify the documents" | [CORE] | Extremely productive causative pattern. Common in administrative, educational, and editorial contexts. The -ish- suffix is regular and predictable. |
| | Hakikisha ukweli | hakikisha ukweli | "Ensure the truth / verify the facts" | [CORE] | Very frequent in instructional and procedural language. Hakikisha is one of the most common causative forms in everyday speech. |

| | Uadilifu ni muhimu | uadilifu ni muhimu | "Justice is important" | [CORE] | The -fu suffix adds quality/character meaning. Common in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. Uadilifu specifically frequent in justice/fairness contexts. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Mwongo anasema uongo | mwongo anasema uongo | "The liar tells lies" | [CORE] | Simple m-/wa- prefix pattern for person nouns. Mwongo is common in moral evaluation and character description. |
| | Mdanganyifu hataaminiwa | mdanganyifu hataaminiwa | "The deceiver will not be trusted" | [CORE] | The -ifu suffix creates habitual agent nouns. Common in moral discourse and character assessment. Implies persistent or characteristic behavior. |

| | Shuhudia tukio | shuhudia tukio | "Witness the event / testify about the event" | [CORE] | Same applicative pattern. Shuhudia frequent in legal testimony and eyewitness contexts. Shows vowel harmony from shahidi root. |
| **9. EMPHATIC INTENSIFICATION** | Kweli kabisa | kweli kabisa | "Absolutely true / completely true" | [CORE] | Kabisa is the strongest intensifier. Common in emphatic assertions and confirmations across all registers. |
| | Ni sahihi sana | ni sahihi sana | "It is very correct" | [CORE] | Sana is the general intensifier. Used freely in all contexts. Less emphatic than kabisa. |
| **10. MANNER ADVERBIALS** | Fanya kwa usahihi | fanya kwa usahihi | "Do it correctly / with accuracy" | [CORE] | Highly productive kwa + abstract noun pattern. Common in instructional and procedural language. |
| | Sema kwa ukweli | sema kwa ukweli | "Speak truthfully / in truth" | [CORE] | Very frequent collocation. Used in moral exhortations and truth-telling contexts. Alternative kwa kweli (bare root) equally common. |
| **11. CONTRASTIVE COORDINATION** | Kweli au uongo | kweli au uongo | "True or false / truth or lies" | [CORE] | Fixed binary opposition. Common in evaluative, testing, and philosophical contexts. Order (positive first) is conventional. |

| | Uongo ule | uongo ule | "That lie / that falsehood" | [CORE] | Distal demonstrative for reference to previously mentioned or distant lies. Common in narrative and argumentative discourse. |
| **13. POSSESSIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Haki yake | haki yake | "His/her right / their entitlement" | [CORE] | Standard possessive construction with class agreement. Extremely common in legal, political, and moral discourse about rights and entitlements. |
| | Makosa yake | makosa yake | "His/her mistakes / their errors" | [CORE] | Common in evaluative contexts for error attribution. The plural makosa (class 6) takes ya- possessive prefix. |

| **15. EVIDENTIAL FRAMING** | Ni ukweli kwamba amefika | ni ukweli kwamba amefika | "It is true that he/she has arrived" | [CORE] | Standard complementizer construction for embedding propositions. Common in formal and evidential contexts for factual claims. |

| **16. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL** | Bila ushahidi | bila ushahidi | "Without evidence / without proof" | [CORE] | Bila + noun is standard privative construction. Very common in legal and argumentative contexts for absence of evidence. |
| | Bila ya hakika | bila ya hakika | "Without certainty / uncertainly" | [CORE] | Optional ya appears with some nouns. Common in epistemic contexts expressing uncertainty or lack of confirmation. |
| **17. VERB-OBJECT COLLOCATIONS** | Sema ukweli | sema ukweli | "Tell the truth / speak the truth" | [CORE] | Highly conventionalized collocation. One of the most frequent truth-domain expressions across all registers. |
| | Toa ushahidi | toa ushahidi | "Give evidence / provide testimony" | [CORE] | Fixed legal collocation. Standard expression in judicial and testimonial contexts. Toa (give) is the conventional verb for evidence provision. |
| **18. REDUPLICATION** | Kweli kweli | kweli kweli | "Really truly / very true / absolutely true" | [MARGINAL] | Full reduplication for emphasis. More common in informal/colloquial speech than formal registers. Not productive for all truth-terms. |

| **19. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC MODIFICATION** | Ukweli wa kisayansi | ukweli wa kisayansi | "Scientific truth / empirical truth" | [CORE] | Productive pattern with ki- prefix for domain specification. Common in academic and technical discourse for distinguishing truth types. |
| | Ukweli wa kisheria | ukweli wa kisheria | "Legal truth / juridical truth" | [CORE] | Same pattern for legal domain. Frequent in legal and philosophical contexts distinguishing legal from other truth types. |
| **20. SPATIAL/METAPHORICAL EXTENSION** | Ukweli wa ndani | ukweli wa ndani | "Inner truth / internal truth / subjective truth" | [MARGINAL] | Metaphorical spatial extension. More common in philosophical, psychological, or religious discourse than everyday speech. |

| **22. PASSIVE DERIVATION** | Ukweli umethibitishwa | ukweli umethibitishwa | "The truth has been confirmed / verified" | [CORE] | Regular passive formation from causative verbs. Very common in formal, legal, and administrative contexts for verification statements. |

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**CORE constructions (22/24):** The vast majority of identified constructions show high productivity and frequency across registers. Particularly robust are: copular attribution (1-2), causative derivation (5), abstract nominalization (6), agentive forms (7), manner adverbials (10), and verb-object collocations (17).

**MARGINAL constructions (2/24):** Only reduplication (18) and spatial/metaphorical extensions (20) show restricted usage. Reduplication is register-specific (informal), while spatial metaphors are domain-specific (philosophical/psychological).

**No UNCERTAIN ratings:** All constructions are well-attested with clear usage patterns.

**Alternate constructions noted:**
- Construction 4 (Become/Inchoative): Two strategies attested—stative -ik- extension vs. periphrastic kuwa + predicate
- Construction 10 (Manner Adverbials): Both kwa + abstract noun and kwa + bare root forms documented
- Construction 14 (Completive): Multiple markers (-ote, pekee, kamili) with different semantic nuances

**Register variation:** Formal/legal register favors constructions 8, 13, 15, 16, 17, 19, 22. Informal speech shows preference for construction 18. Most constructions are register-neutral.
