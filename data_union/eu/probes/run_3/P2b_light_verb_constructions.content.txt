# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Constructions already fully documented in P1b or P2a are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full, but are retained here for completeness of the construction inventory. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven multi-word patterns that are not derivational. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Asymmetry across items is preserved and reported.

A note on overlap with P1b: P1b documented collocations and fixed phrases as part of root expansion. Those forms are cross-referenced here with a note; new constructions not previously documented are described in full.

## 1. egia

**Base Form:** egia

---

**Construction 1.1**
- **Example:** Egia esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** The primary verbal collocation for the act of truth-telling. Used in imperative (*egia esazu*, "tell the truth"), declarative, and nominalized (*egia esatea*) forms across all registers. Already documented in P1b 1.1–1.3.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**
- **Example:** Egia da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** It is true; that is true
- **Usage notes:** The standard predicative construction for asserting propositional truth. Ubiquitous across all registers. Already documented in P1b 1.2.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**
- **Example:** Egia esan behar da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** One must tell the truth; the truth must be told
- **Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *egia esan* with the modal *behar* ("must, need"). Used in moral and legal contexts to assert an obligation of truthfulness. The impersonal *behar da* construction is standard Basque for expressing obligation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**
- **Example:** Egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The whole truth and nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed formula used in legal oath-taking contexts, parallel to the English legal formula. Already documented in P1b 1.13. Used in court proceedings and formal legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**
- **Example:** Egia erdiak esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell half-truths
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia erdiak* ("half-truths") with *esan* ("to say/tell"). Used in moral and political discourse to describe misleading partial disclosure. Already documented in P1b 1.14.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**
- **Example:** Egiaren bila ibili
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To be in search of the truth; to go about seeking the truth
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining the postpositional phrase *egiaren bila* ("in search of truth") with the motion verb *ibili* ("to go about, to be engaged in"). Used in philosophical, journalistic, and literary contexts. The *ibili* verb marks ongoing or habitual activity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**
- **Example:** Egia aitortu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To admit the truth; to confess the truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *aitortu* ("to admit, to confess, to acknowledge"). Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts where truth-telling involves an admission of something previously concealed or denied. Distinct from *egia esan* in its connotation of acknowledgment after resistance.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**
- **Example:** Egia gorde
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To hide the truth; to conceal the truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *gorde* ("to keep, to hide, to conceal"). Used in moral and legal contexts to describe the withholding of truth. Functions as a partial antonym of *egia esan* in contexts of concealment rather than active lying.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**
- **Example:** Egia argitara atera
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To bring the truth to light
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *egia* with *argitara atera* ("to bring to light," literally "to bring out into the light"). Used in journalistic, legal, and literary contexts to describe the revelation of hidden truth. The phrase *argitara atera* is a productive collocation in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.10**
- **Example:** Egia jakiteko eskubidea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The right to know the truth
- **Usage notes:** A nominal construction combining *egia jakiteko* ("to know the truth," genitive of purpose) with *eskubidea* ("the right"). Used in legal, political, and journalistic discourse, particularly in discussions of press freedom, transparency, and victims' rights.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**
- **Example:** Egia da ala ez?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Is it true or not?
- **Usage notes:** A fixed interrogative formula using the disjunctive particle *ala* ("or") to pose a binary truth question. Common in everyday speech and formal argumentation. The *ala ez* ("or not") construction is a standard Basque pattern for yes/no alternatives.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**
- **Example:** Hori egia bada
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** If that is true; if that be true
- **Usage notes:** A conditional construction using *bada* (conditional form of *da*, "is"). Used in argumentation and everyday speech to introduce a conditional premise. Standard Basque conditional syntax applied to truth-assertion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**
- **Example:** Egia esateko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell the truth; truth be told (discourse marker)
- **Usage notes:** A fixed adverbial phrase functioning as a discourse marker meaning "to tell the truth" or "truth be told," used to introduce a frank or candid statement. The *-teko* suffix marks purpose/infinitive. Common in both spoken and written Basque as a hedging or sincerity marker.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**
- **Example:** Egia esan nahi badut
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** If I want to tell the truth; if I'm being honest
- **Usage notes:** A longer periphrastic discourse marker combining *egia esan* with the conditional *nahi badut* ("if I want to"). Used in spoken registers to introduce a candid or frank statement, functioning similarly to English "if I'm being honest." More colloquial than *egia esateko*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.15**
- **Example:** Egia da, baina...
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** It is true, but...
- **Usage notes:** A concessive construction combining *egia da* with the adversative conjunction *baina* ("but"). Used in argumentation to concede a point before introducing a qualification or counterargument. Extremely common in spoken and written discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.16**
- **Example:** Egia dirudien arren
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Although it seems true; even though it appears to be true
- **Usage notes:** A concessive construction combining *egia* with *dirudien arren* ("although it seems," from *dirudi*, "it seems/appears"). Used to introduce a qualification of apparent truth. The *arren* concessive construction is standard in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.17**
- **Example:** Egia baino egiagoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Truer than true; more true than truth itself
- **Usage notes:** A comparative intensification construction using the comparative *baino...ago* ("more...than"). Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to emphasize the certainty or self-evidence of a claim. Functions as a hyperbolic intensifier.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 1.18**
- **Example:** Egia hutsa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The plain truth; the bare truth; pure truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *hutsa* ("empty, mere, pure, bare"). Used to emphasize that a statement is nothing but the truth, without embellishment or qualification. Common in everyday and rhetorical speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.19**
- **Example:** Egia mingarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The painful truth; the bitter truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *mingarria* ("painful, hurtful"). Used in everyday and literary contexts to describe truths that are difficult to hear or accept. Reflects the common conceptualization of truth as potentially painful.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.20**
- **Example:** Egia ukatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To deny the truth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egia* with *ukatu* ("to deny, to refuse"). Used in moral and legal contexts to describe the act of denying what is true. Distinct from *egia gorde* (concealing) in that *ukatu* implies active denial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. egiazkoa

**Base Form:** egiazkoa

---

**Construction 2.1**
- **Example:** Egiazko informazioa zabaldu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To spread/disseminate true information
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining the attributive *egiazko* with *informazioa* ("information") and *zabaldu* ("to spread, to disseminate"). Used in journalistic and media contexts, particularly in discussions of misinformation and fact-checking.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**
- **Example:** Egiazko adiskidea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** A true friend; a genuine friend
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egiazko* with *adiskidea* ("friend"). Used in everyday and literary contexts to distinguish genuine friendship from superficial or instrumental relationships. Common in moral and relational discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**
- **Example:** Egiazko arrazoia
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The real reason; the true reason
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egiazko* with *arrazoia* ("reason, cause"). Used to distinguish the actual underlying reason for something from stated or apparent reasons. Common in analytical and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.4**
- **Example:** Egiazko balioa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The true value; the genuine value; the real worth
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *egiazko* with *balioa* ("value, worth"). Used in economic, philosophical, and everyday evaluative contexts to distinguish genuine value from apparent or assigned value.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: egiazkoa participates in a large number of attributive collocations of the pattern egiazko + NOUN. The four above represent the most common patterns. No idiomatic or periphrastic constructions specific to egiazkoa beyond attributive modification are identified.*

## 3. benetakoa

**Base Form:** benetakoa

---

**Construction 3.1**
- **Example:** Benetako arazoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The real problem; the genuine problem
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining the attributive *benetako* with *arazoa* ("problem"). Used in analytical and everyday discourse to identify the underlying or actual problem as distinct from surface symptoms or stated concerns.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**
- **Example:** Benetako laguna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** A real friend; a true friend
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *benetako* with *laguna* ("friend, companion"). Used in everyday moral and relational discourse to distinguish genuine friendship from superficial acquaintance. Partially overlaps with *egiazko adiskidea* but *benetako* emphasizes authenticity of the relationship rather than propositional truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**
- **Example:** Benetako izena
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The real name; the true name
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *benetako* with *izena* ("name"). Used in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts to distinguish a person's actual name from a pseudonym, alias, or stage name.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.4**
- **Example:** Benetako helburua
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The real goal; the true objective
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *benetako* with *helburua* ("goal, objective"). Used in analytical and political discourse to distinguish actual intentions or goals from stated ones. Common in critical and investigative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.5**
- **Example:** Benetakoa al da?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Is it real? Is it genuine?
- **Usage notes:** A predicative interrogative construction using the question particle *al* (which marks yes/no questions in Basque). Used in everyday speech to question the authenticity of an object, claim, or person. The *al* particle is standard in Basque polar questions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.6**
- **Example:** Benetako maitasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** True love; genuine love; real love
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *benetako* with *maitasuna* ("love"). Used in literary, everyday, and philosophical contexts to distinguish genuine emotional attachment from superficial or performative affection. One of the most frequent collocations of *benetako* in literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. gezurra

**Base Form:** gezurra

---

**Construction 4.1**
- **Example:** Gezurra esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To tell a lie; to lie
- **Usage notes:** The primary verbal collocation for the act of lying. Already documented in P1b 1.21. Ubiquitous across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.2**
- **Example:** Gezurra da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** It is false; it is a lie
- **Usage notes:** The standard predicative construction for asserting falsity. Already documented in P1b 1.22. Direct parallel to *egia da*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.3**
- **Example:** Gezurretan ari da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He/she is lying; he/she is engaged in lying
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic progressive construction combining *gezurretan* (adverbial locative plural of *gezurra*) with *ari da* ("is engaged in, is doing"). This is the standard Basque progressive construction (*ari* + locative verbal noun). Used in everyday speech to describe ongoing deceptive behavior. Already documented in P2a 4.5.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.4**
- **Example:** Gezurra aitortu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To admit a lie; to confess to having lied
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra* with *aitortu* ("to admit, to confess"). Used in moral and legal contexts where a person acknowledges having lied. Parallel in structure to *egia aitortu* (Construction 1.7).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.5**
- **Example:** Gezurra harrapatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To catch someone in a lie; to catch a lie
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra* with *harrapatu* ("to catch, to trap"). Used in everyday speech to describe detecting or exposing a lie. Common in informal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.6**
- **Example:** Gezurra sinestu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To believe a lie
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra* with *sinestu* ("to believe"). Used in everyday and moral discourse to describe the state of having been deceived into accepting a falsehood as true.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.7**
- **Example:** Gezurra eta egia nahastu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To mix truth and lies; to confuse truth and falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra eta egia* ("lies and truth") with *nahastu* ("to mix, to confuse"). Used in critical discourse to describe the blending of true and false information in a way that makes them difficult to distinguish.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.8**
- **Example:** Gezurra txuria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** A white lie
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra* with *txuria* ("white"). Used in everyday moral discourse to describe a lie told with benign intent. The calque from the European "white lie" concept is attested in Basque usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.9**
- **Example:** Gezurra bizi da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The lie lives on; the lie persists
- **Usage notes:** A construction combining *gezurra* with *bizi da* ("lives, is alive"). Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to describe a falsehood that continues to circulate or be believed. Appears in political and moral commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 4.10**
- **Example:** Gezurra zabaldu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To spread a lie; to disseminate a falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *gezurra* with *zabaldu* ("to spread, to disseminate"). Used in journalistic and political contexts to describe the circulation of false information. Common in discussions of misinformation and propaganda.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 6. zuzena

**Base Form:** zuzena

---

**Construction 6.1**
- **Example:** Hori zuzena da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** That is correct; that is right
- **Usage notes:** The standard predicative construction for asserting correctness or rightness. Used across epistemic, moral, and procedural contexts. Ubiquitous in everyday speech and formal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.2**
- **Example:** Zuzen hitz egin
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To speak correctly; to speak directly; to speak frankly
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining the adverbial *zuzen* with *hitz egin* ("to speak"). Used in everyday speech to describe both linguistically correct speech and frank, direct communication. Already documented in P1b 3.20 as *zuzenean hitz egin*; this form uses the bare adverb *zuzen* rather than the locative *zuzenean*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.3**
- **Example:** Zuzen joatea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Going straight; going directly
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining adverbial *zuzen* with *joatea* ("going," verbal noun of *joan*). Used in both spatial senses (going straight ahead) and figurative senses (proceeding correctly or directly). Common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.4**
- **Example:** Zuzena izatea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Being correct; being right; being just
- **Usage notes:** A nominalized construction used in philosophical and ethical discourse. Already documented in P1b 3.19. Used to refer to the state or quality of being correct or just as an abstract concept.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.5**
- **Example:** Zuzena dela uste dut
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** I think it is correct; I believe it is right
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction embedding *zuzena dela* ("that it is correct," subordinate clause with *-la* complementizer) within *uste dut* ("I think/believe"). Standard Basque pattern for expressing a belief about correctness. The *uste dut* + *-la* complement construction is the primary Basque structure for propositional attitude reports.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.6**
- **Example:** Zuzen ala oker
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Right or wrong; correct or incorrect
- **Usage notes:** A fixed binary construction using the disjunctive *ala* ("or") to pair *zuzen* and *oker* as antonyms. Used in everyday and philosophical discourse to frame a binary evaluation. Common in moral and epistemic argumentation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.7**
- **Example:** Zuzena den ala ez den
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Whether it is correct or not
- **Usage notes:** A subordinate interrogative construction using *den ala ez den* ("whether it is or is not"). Used in argumentation and deliberative contexts to frame a question of correctness or rightness. Standard Basque indirect question syntax.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 7. okerra

**Base Form:** okerra

---

**Construction 7.1**
- **Example:** Oker nago
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** I am wrong; I am mistaken
- **Usage notes:** The primary first-person construction for admitting error, using *egon* (stative "to be") rather than *izan*. Already noted in P1 item 7. Ubiquitous in everyday epistemic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.2**
- **Example:** Oker zaude
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** You are wrong; you are mistaken
- **Usage notes:** Second-person form of *oker egon*. Used in everyday discourse to assert that another person is mistaken. The *zaude* form is second-person singular of *egon*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.3**
- **Example:** Oker ibili
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To be on the wrong track; to be going about it wrongly
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *oker* with *ibili* ("to walk, to go about, to be in a state"). Already documented in P1b 3.25. Used in everyday speech to describe proceeding incorrectly.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.4**
- **Example:** Okerreko bidea hartu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To take the wrong path; to go the wrong way
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *okerreko bidea* ("the wrong path") with *hartu* ("to take"). Used in both spatial and figurative senses (making a wrong decision, taking a wrong course of action). Common in everyday and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.5**
- **Example:** Oker egon daiteke
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He/she/it could be wrong; it may be mistaken
- **Usage notes:** A modal construction combining *oker egon* with *daiteke* (potential/possibility form of the auxiliary). Used to hedge a claim about another's error or to express epistemic uncertainty about one's own position. Standard Basque potential construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.6**
- **Example:** Oker ez banago
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** If I am not mistaken; unless I am wrong
- **Usage notes:** A conditional construction using *ez banago* ("if I am not," negative conditional of *egon*). Used as a hedging device in everyday speech and formal discourse to introduce a claim while acknowledging the possibility of error. Common in both spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 8. zehatza

**Base Form:** zehatza

---

**Construction 8.1**
- **Example:** Zehatz esateko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To be precise; precisely speaking; to put it precisely
- **Usage notes:** A fixed adverbial phrase functioning as a discourse marker, using *zehatz* (adverbial form) with *esateko* ("to say," purposive). Used to introduce a precise or exact statement, parallel to English "to be precise" or "precisely." Common in formal and analytical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8.2**
- **Example:** Zehatz-mehatz
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Exactly, precisely, thoroughly, in every detail
- **Usage notes:** A reduplicated compound adverb combining *zehatz* with *mehatz* (a near-synonym meaning "fine, detailed, thorough"). The reduplication intensifies the meaning. Used in everyday and formal speech to emphasize thoroughness or exactness. A fixed lexicalized compound.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8.3**
- **Example:** Zehatz-mehatz bete
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To fulfill exactly; to comply with to the letter
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *zehatz-mehatz* with *bete* ("to fulfill, to comply with, to fill"). Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe exact compliance with rules, orders, or obligations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8.4**
- **Example:** Zehaztasun handiz
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** With great precision; with great accuracy
- **Usage notes:** A prepositional phrase combining *zehaztasun* ("precision") with *handiz* ("with great," instrumental of *handia*, "big/great"). Used in scientific, journalistic, and formal contexts to characterize the degree of precision of a statement or measurement.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8.5**
- **Example:** Zehazki esanda
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Precisely stated; to be exact; strictly speaking
- **Usage notes:** A participial construction combining the adverb *zehazki* ("precisely") with *esanda* ("having said," participial form of *esan*). Used as a discourse marker to introduce a precise or exact formulation. Common in formal and analytical writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8.6**
- **Example:** Datu zehatzak eman
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** To provide precise data; to give exact figures
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *datu zehatzak* ("precise data/figures") with *eman* ("to give"). Used in journalistic, scientific, and administrative contexts when requesting or providing specific numerical or factual information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 9. fidagarria

**Base Form:** fidagarria

---

**Construction 9.1**
- **Example:** Iturri fidagarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** A reliable source; a trustworthy source
- **Usage notes:** A collocation combining *iturri* ("source") with *fidagarria*. The most frequent collocation of *fidagarria* in journalistic and academic discourse. Used to evaluate the reliability of information sources.
