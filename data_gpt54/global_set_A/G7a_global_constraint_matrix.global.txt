## Narrative analysis

### 1. Strongly recurrent constraints

The strongest cross-language regularities are the following.

#### A. Core evaluative states are widely allowed in simple attribution
Across the languages, the following dimensions are very broadly attested in simple predication:
- factual truth
- falsehood/non-truth
- genuineness/authenticity
- correctness
- error/incorrectness
- formal validity
- reliability
- credibility
- justification/legitimacy
- essence/underlying nature
- resemblance

This is one of the clearest global patterns. Languages differ in lexical partitioning, but they repeatedly allow “X is true / false / real / fake / correct / valid / reliable / justified / essential / similar.”

#### B. Negative attribution is broadly available
Analytic negation or equivalent negative predication is strongly recurrent across languages. This is especially robust for:
- truth/non-truth
- correctness/incorrectness
- validity/invalidity
- authenticity/non-authenticity
- reliability/unreliability

A repeated pattern in the data is that languages often prefer:
- a positive predicate plus negation,
- or a lexical negative counterpart,
- or both.

So “not true,” “not correct,” “not valid,” “not genuine,” “not reliable” are broadly allowed.

#### C. Adnominal modification is one of the most stable realization types
Many dimensions are strongly realized as noun modifiers:
- true story
- accurate information
- fake document
- reliable source
- valid contract
- genuine product
- false accusation
- proper attitude
- original work

This is one of the most cross-linguistically stable functions in the dataset.

#### D. Truth/fact as object of a verb is a major recurrent pattern
A very strong cross-language pattern is not just “X is true,” but:
- tell the truth
- reveal the truth
- hide the truth
- seek the truth
- know the truth
- check the facts
- distort the truth

This is strongly supported in Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean. It is one of the clearest shared constructional tendencies.

#### E. Verification/checking is broadly allowed
Another very strong recurrent function is procedural checking:
- establish truth
- verify facts
- check authenticity
- test validity
- confirm information
- compare with source
- verify accuracy

This is especially strong for:
- factual truth
- authenticity
- correctness
- precision
- conformity
- formal validity

This function is often more robust than direct change-of-state expressions.

#### F. Revelation/exposure is strongly favored for truth dynamics
A major cross-language regularity is that languages often encode truth dynamically as:
- being revealed,
- coming out,
- being brought to light,
- being exposed,
- turning out to be false,
rather than as simply “becoming true.”

This is strongly supported across Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, and partly Yoruba.

#### G. Abstract nominalization is broadly allowed
Most dimensions have strong support for abstract quality nouns:
- truth
- falsity
- authenticity
- correctness
- accuracy
- reliability
- validity
- legitimacy
- sincerity
- essence
- similarity

This is one of the most stable morphological/lexical patterns across the dataset.

### 2. Likely prohibitions or avoidance patterns

#### A. General “make X true” is broadly blocked
This is one of the clearest global constraints in the data.

Across multiple languages, there is no strong, broad, ordinary construction meaning:
- “make a proposition true”
- “cause something to become true”

Instead, languages repeatedly substitute:
- verify
- confirm
- reveal
- establish
- justify
- validate
- correct

This blocking is especially explicit in:
- Georgian
- Yoruba
- Basque
- Tamil
- Korean

and effectively also in:
- Indonesian
- Turkish
- Finnish

So the matrix marks **factual truth × causative** as **BLOCKED**.

#### B. “Become true” is not broadly general, only variable
A general inchoative “become true” is not uniformly supported. Some languages have strong or semi-strong patterns:
- Finnish
- Indonesian
- Turkish
- some Tamil contexts

But several others explicitly resist or replace it:
- Georgian
- Yoruba
- Basque
- Korean

So **factual truth × become/inchoative** is best treated as **VARIABLE**, not allowed globally.

#### C. “Become genuine/authentic” is broadly resisted
For authenticity, the data repeatedly shows that languages prefer:
- checking authenticity,
- confirming genuineness,
- determining whether something is genuine,
rather than saying it “became genuine.”

This is especially clear in:
- Georgian
- Yoruba
- Basque
- Tamil
- Indonesian
- Korean

So **genuineness/authenticity × become/inchoative** is best treated as **BLOCKED**.

#### D. “Make genuine/authentic” is also broadly resisted
The same pattern holds for causation:
languages often have strong verbs for:
- falsify,
- forge,
- counterfeit,
- invalidate,
but not equally strong ordinary verbs for:
- make genuine,
- make authentic.

So **genuineness/authenticity × causative** is also **BLOCKED**.

#### E. Reliability resists direct inchoative and causative patterns
The data repeatedly suggests that reliability is usually handled through:
- being trustworthy,
- gaining trust,
- being considered reliable,
- securing reliability,
rather than:
- becoming reliable in a simple lexicalized way,
- making something reliable via a direct truth-domain causative.

This is especially clear in Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, and partly Tamil and Yoruba.

So:
- **reliability × become/inchoative** = **BLOCKED**
- **reliability × causative** = **BLOCKED**

This is one of the clearer domain-specific restrictions.

#### F. Essence strongly resists direct causative treatment
Essence/underlying nature is widely expressed as:
- being essential,
- revealing the essence,
- understanding the essence,
- obscuring the essence,
but not as:
- making something essence,
- making something truly essential in a regular truth-domain causative pattern.

So **essence × causative** is best treated as **BLOCKED**.

---

### 3. Important divergences, substitutions, and asymmetries

#### A. Revelation often substitutes for inchoative truth
A major divergence across languages is that some languages allow “become true” more readily, but many prefer:
- truth came out,
- truth was established,
- facts were confirmed,
- falsity was revealed.

This substitution is especially strong in:
- Georgian
- Yoruba
- Basque
- Tamil
- Korean

and still important in:
- Indonesian
- Turkish
- Finnish

#### B. Institutional status change is easier than truth-state change
A striking asymmetry is that many languages more readily allow:
- become invalid
- be declared valid
- be recognized as valid
- invalidate
than:
- become true
- make true

This is especially strong in:
- Indonesian
- Turkish
- Korean
- Finnish

So formal validity behaves more naturally with inchoative and causative constructions than factual truth does.

#### C. Negative-side dynamics are often richer than positive-side dynamics
Many languages have strong lexical or constructional resources for:
- falsify
- forge
- invalidate
- expose as false
- prove fake

But weaker or absent parallel resources for:
- make true
- make genuine
- make reliable

This asymmetry is especially visible in:
- Indonesian
- Korean
- Tamil
- Finnish
- Georgian

#### D. Appearance-vs-reality is strong but unevenly lexicalized
This dimension is clearly present across several languages, but not always as a dedicated lexical opposition.
It is especially explicit in:
- Korean
- Indonesian
- Turkish
- Basque
- Tamil

Elsewhere it is more distributed across:
- authenticity vocabulary,
- revelation constructions,
- resemblance constructions.

So many cells involving appearance-vs-reality are **VARIABLE** rather than uniformly allowed.

#### E. Credibility is less stable cross-linguistically than reliability
Some languages clearly distinguish:
- reliable / trustworthy
from
- believable / convincing / plausible

This is strong in:
- Georgian
- Finnish
- Basque
- Turkish

But less sharply separated in:
- Tamil
- Indonesian
- Korean
- Yoruba

So credibility-related cells are often **VARIABLE** or weaker than reliability-related ones.

#### F. Some dimensions are split in some languages and merged in others
This matters for matrix interpretation.

- **Truth vs correctness**:
  - strongly merged in Yoruba
  - partly merged in Georgian, Turkish, Indonesian, Korean
  - more clearly split in Finnish and Indonesian formal vocabulary

- **Truth vs authenticity**:
  - partly merged in Basque, Tamil, Turkish, Yoruba
  - more clearly split in Finnish, Georgian, Korean formal vocabulary

- **Correctness vs moral rightness**:
  - often merged in Yoruba, Tamil, Turkish, Korean
  - more lexically differentiated in Finnish and Indonesian

- **Validity vs legitimacy/justification**:
  - clearly split in Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, Finnish
  - less sharply separated elsewhere

So some matrix cells reflect dimensions that are only partially recoverable as separate in some languages.

## Brief confidence assessment

### Strong evidence
Evidence is strongest for:
- simple attribution
- negative attribution
- adnominal modification
- truth/fact as object of verb
- verification/checking
- revelation/exposure
- abstract nominalization

And for the following dimensions:
- factual truth
- falsehood
- authenticity/genuineness
- fake/forged
- correctness
- formal validity
- reliability
- essence

These are repeatedly supported across many languages and multiple construction types.

### Moderate evidence
Evidence is moderate for:
- precision/exactness as distinct from correctness in all languages
- credibility/plausibility as distinct from reliability
- conformity/correspondence as a unified dimension rather than several neighboring ones
- justification/legitimacy/fairness as one cross-language dimension
- faithfulness to original/source as a separate dimension
- appearance-vs-reality as a stable constructional function across all languages

These are clearly present, but less evenly distributed or more language-specific.

### Weak or missing evidence
Evidence is weak or missing for:
- a broad general causative “make true”
- a broad general inchoative “become true”
- productive “become genuine/authentic”
- productive “make genuine/authentic”
- productive “make reliable”
- some appearance-vs-reality pairings outside a few languages
- some causative patterns for essence, resemblance, and sincerity domains

These are the areas where the matrix most often shows **BLOCKED**, **VARIABLE**, or **INSUFFICIENT DATA**.

---

## Final note
The clearest global pattern is not that languages freely map all truth-related meanings onto all construction types. Rather, the data strongly suggests a constrained system in which:
- **state attribution** is broadly available,
- **checking/verification** is broadly available,
- **revelation/exposure** is broadly available,
- but **direct truth-causation** and often **direct truth-inchoation** are broadly disfavored or replaced by other strategies.
