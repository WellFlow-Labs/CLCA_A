## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by BECOME

In English, as documented in P4a and P4b, there is no construction dedicated exclusively to REVELATION (the discovery or disclosure of a pre-existing truth-status). The constructions that express inchoative truth-status change — *turn out to be* + adjective and *prove* + adjective — simultaneously cover both the BECOME function (a state coming into being) and the REVELATION function (a state being discovered to have already obtained). The P4b notes describe *turn out to be* as "the general-purpose inchoative frame for discovered truth-status," which indicates that the discovered-truth reading (REVELATION) is expressed by the same form as the coming-to-be reading (BECOME). No separate REVELATION-only construction is attested in the data. REVELATION is therefore **SUBSTITUTED BY BECOME** in English.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are asymmetric but parallel

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are expressed by distinct dedicated verbs in English and do not share a single construction. VERIFICATION is expressed by *verify*, *validate*, *authenticate*, and *confirm*; DISCONFIRMATION is expressed by *falsify*, *disprove*, *refute*, and *invalidate*. The two sets are parallel in structure (dedicated derived verbs, mostly Latinate) but lexically non-overlapping. P4b notes that *falsify* is "strongly associated with scientific and philosophical discourse," while *verify* is "the most broadly distributed causative verb in this domain," indicating an asymmetry in register distribution: VERIFICATION has broader register coverage than DISCONFIRMATION.

### 2.3 NEGATION: analytic and morphological strategies coexist without consistent semantic difference

P4a and P4b document that English uses both analytic negation (*is not true*) and morphological negation (*untrue*, *inaccurate*, *invalid*) with "no consistent semantic difference between the two strategies." The choice is described as "largely lexical and register-driven," with morphological negation preferred in formal and written registers. This means NEGATION in English is not a single construction but a pair of constructions whose distribution is conditioned by register and by which lexical items have established morphologically negated forms. Not all truth-domain adjectives have attested morphological negatives (no *\*unvalid* alongside *invalid*; no *\*uncorrect* alongside *incorrect*), so the morphological strategy has lexical gaps that the analytic strategy fills.

### 2.4 CAUSATION (factual): lexical coverage is uneven

The CAUSATION (factual) operator is expressed in English by dedicated derived verbs rather than by a productive morphological causative. P4a explicitly notes the absence of a productive causative morpheme and lists adjectives for which no dedicated causative verb exists: *true* (no *\*truify*), *genuine* (no *\*genuinize*), *correct* (no *\*correctify*). For these adjectives, P4a states that "periphrastic constructions are used" or that a separate verb (*correct* as a verb; *realize* for *make real*) fills the gap. The CAUSATION (factual) operator is therefore available for a subset of truth-domain adjectives but requires circumlocution or a semantically adjacent verb for others.

### 2.5 CAUSATION (character): no construction attested

No construction in P4a or P4b addresses the causing or inducing of a truth-related character disposition (e.g., causing someone to become honest, or causing someone to become a truth-teller). The agentive nominalization section (P4a §7) describes agents of truth-domain practices but does not document any construction that causes such a character state to come about. This operator is absent from the data.

### 2.6 Positive/negative asymmetry in agentive nominalization

P4a §7 notes that "the agentive for the negative pole (*liar*, *deceiver*, *fraud*) is drawn from a separate lexical set not derived from the positive truth-domain adjectives," and that "no agentive is derived directly from *true*, *honest*, *sincere*, or *genuine*." This is an asymmetric lexical gap: the negative-pole agentives are well-attested but are not morphologically derived from the corresponding positive adjectives, while the positive-pole agentives either do not exist or are formed by compounding (*truth-teller*) rather than direct suffixation. The compound *truth-teller* is rated [MARGINAL] in P4b, indicating it is less frequent and more marked than the negative-pole agentives.

### 2.7 *Come true* is lexically restricted relative to the general BECOME frame

Within the BECOME operator, *come true* is restricted to subjects that are "wishes, predictions, dreams, or hopes" (P4b). It does not extend to other truth-domain adjectives (*\*came genuine*, *\*came valid*). The general BECOME frame (*turn out to be* + adjective) has no such subject restriction and covers the full range of truth-domain adjectives. This is an internal asymmetry within the BECOME operator: one form is lexically restricted, the other is general.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction in P4a or P4b expresses the causing of a truth-related character disposition. This is a **structural gap**: the operator is not expressed by any attested construction type.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **CAUSATION (factual), partial**: No dedicated causative verb exists for *true* (*\*truify*), *genuine* (*\*genuinize*), or *correct* (*\*correctify*). The gap is lexical, not morphosyntactic: the construction type (dedicated derived verb) exists, but specific lexical items are absent for certain adjectives.
- **AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION, positive pole**: No agentive noun is derived directly from *true*, *honest*, *sincere*, or *genuine* by suffixation. The compound *truth-teller* partially fills this gap but is rated [MARGINAL]. The gap is lexical.
- **MORPHOLOGICAL NEGATION, partial**: Not all truth-domain adjectives have attested morphologically negated forms (e.g., no *\*unvalid* is standard; *invalid* exists but is not transparently derived from *valid* for all speakers). The analytic negation strategy fills these gaps, so there is no structural gap, only a lexical one.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data. All operators that are present are expressed by constructions whose meanings are compositionally or idiomatically recoverable within the truth domain.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

- **Agentive nominalization**: Negative-pole agentives (*liar*, *deceiver*, *counterfeiter*) are well-attested and unmarked; positive-pole agentives derived directly from truth-domain adjectives are absent or marginal. The gap is **asymmetric: positive pole only**.
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Causative verbs exist for some truth-domain adjectives (*validate*, *authenticate*, *falsify*, *verify*) but not others (*true*, *genuine*, *correct*). The gap is **asymmetric: affects specific adjectives, not the operator as a whole**.
- **Register distribution of VERIFICATION vs. DISCONFIRMATION**: *Verify* has broader register coverage than *falsify*, which is restricted to scientific and philosophical discourse. This is an **asymmetric register gap: DISCONFIRMATION is register-restricted relative to VERIFICATION**.
- **BECOME internal asymmetry**: *Come true* is restricted to wishes/predictions/hopes; *turn out to be* is unrestricted. The gap is **asymmetric: restricted form exists only for positive truth-realization with specific subject types**.
