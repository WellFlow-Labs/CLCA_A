## BASE FORM 1: benar

### Derived Form 1.1: membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + benar + -kan (causative verb prefix + root + causative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to verify, to justify, to validate, to permit
**Usage description:** Causative verb indicating making something correct/true or confirming truth of a statement. Also extends to mean "permit" in regulatory contexts. Very common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.2: kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + benar + -an (nominalizing prefix + root + nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, the truth
**Usage description:** Abstract nominalization referring to truth as concept or specific truths. One of the most common abstract nouns in Indonesian, used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.3: sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** se- + benar + -nya (adverbial prefix + root + adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** actually, in fact, in truth, really
**Usage description:** Extremely common adverbial form introducing corrections, clarifications, or underlying reality. Functions as discourse marker across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.4: pembenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** peN- + benar + -an (action nominalizer prefix + root + nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, confirmation, authorization
**Usage description:** Nominalization referring to act or process of justifying or confirming. Common in formal, legal, and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.5: dibenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** di- + benar + -kan (passive prefix + root + causative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to be confirmed, to be validated, to be permitted
**Usage description:** Passive causative form indicating something is confirmed as true or permitted. Common in formal and regulatory contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.6: benar-benar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** benar + benar (full reduplication)
**Natural English translation:** really, truly, genuinely, completely
**Usage description:** Reduplicated intensifier emphasizing genuineness or completeness. Very common across all registers as both adverb and intensifying modifier.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.7: sebenar-benarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** se- + benar-benar + -nya (adverbial prefix + reduplication + adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** most truly, in absolute truth, as truthfully as possible
**Usage description:** Emphatic superlative form used in oaths, formal declarations, and legal testimony to emphasize complete truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.8: membenar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + benar (active verb prefix + root)
**Natural English translation:** to be correct, to turn out right
**Usage description:** Intransitive verb indicating something proves to be correct. Less common than membenarkan, used primarily in specific contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Derived Form 1.9: membenar-benarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** meN- + benar-benar + -kan (active prefix + reduplication + causative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to keep justifying, to always confirm
**Usage description:** Indicates repeated or habitual justification. Often carries slightly negative connotation of over-justifying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.10: terbenar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** ter- + benar (accidental/superlative prefix + root)
**Natural English translation:** most correct, accidentally proven right
**Usage description:** Can indicate superlative (most correct) or accidental correctness. Limited usage, primarily in formal or written contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Derived Form 1.11: kebenarannya
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** ke- + benar + -an + -nya (nominalizing prefix + root + nominalizing suffix + possessive/definite marker)
**Natural English translation:** its truth, the truth of it
**Usage description:** Possessed/definite form of kebenaran used when discussing truth-value of specific claims or statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form 1.12: pembenaran diri
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal breakdown:** peN- + benar + -an + diri (action nominalizer + root + nominalizing suffix + self)
**Natural English translation:** self-justification, self-vindication
**Usage description:** Fixed compound referring to act of justifying oneself. Common in psychological and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY NOTES

**Productivity patterns observed:**

1. **ke- ... -an nominalization** is highly productive across nearly all adjective/stative roots in the truth domain, creating abstract nouns for qualities.

2. **meN- ... -kan causative** is very productive, creating transitive verbs meaning "to make X" or "to cause to be X."

3. **di- ... -kan passive causative** regularly derives from meN- ... -kan forms.

4. **peN- ... -an action nominalization** productively derives from meN- ... -kan verbs, referring to the process or act.

5. **Full reduplication** is common for intensification, particularly with benar, betul, sungguh, sama, and nyata.

6. **se- ... -nya adverbial** is productive for creating adverbial forms meaning "in the manner of X" or "actually X."

7. **ber- intransitive** appears with some roots but is not fully productive across the domain.

8. **Applicative -i suffix** appears selectively (bohong → membohongi, percaya → mempercayai, adil → mengadili).

9. **Agentive peN-** (without -an) creates agent nouns (pembohong, pendusta, pemalsu, peradil).

**Gaps and asymmetries:**

- Not all roots take all affixes productively
- Some borrowed roots (valid, akurat, faktual) show hybrid patterns mixing Indonesian and borrowed morphology
- Some roots have limited derivational possibilities (tulen, sejati, hakiki show fewer derivatives)
- Certain semantic fields (legal: sah, absah, adil) show richer derivational families than others
