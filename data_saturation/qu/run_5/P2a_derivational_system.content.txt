# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested derivational morphology for the lexical items identified in P1 and expanded in P1b. The primary sources are Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka) descriptive grammars and lexicographic works (Cusihuamán, Cerrón-Palomino, Parker, Itier, and the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua). Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-derived. The constraint against paradigm completion is strictly observed: only forms with positive attestation evidence are included. Gaps are preserved and noted where they are informative.

---

## Derivational Forms by Base

---

### Base: **cheqa**

---

**D-cheqa-01**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaq
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (agentive/nominalizing suffix) → "one who is true / that which is true"
5. **Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine, real
6. **Usage:** Used predicatively or nominally to characterize a person as honest or a statement as truthful; appears in moral evaluation of character and in assessment of testimony. This is the most productive derived stem from cheqa, itself serving as a base for further derivation (see D-cheqaq series below).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqa-02**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqan
3. **Transliteration:** cheqan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive/nominalizing suffix) → "its truth / what is right"
5. **Natural English translation:** what is right, what is correct, the truth of the matter, justice
6. **Usage:** Used in moral and legal contexts to refer to what is proper or just in a given situation; appears in evaluations of whether a judgment or course of action is correct.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqa-03**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-qa* (topic marker) → "as for the truth / the truth is..."
5. **Natural English translation:** the truth is..., in truth, actually, in reality
6. **Usage:** Introduces a corrective or clarifying statement, signaling a shift from an apparent or reported state of affairs to what is actually the case; functions pragmatically to mark a more accurate account.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqa-04**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha* (causative/verbalizer) + *-y* (infinitive) → "to make true / to verify / to correct"
5. **Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to correct, to establish as true
6. **Usage:** Used in contexts of checking accuracy, confirming facts, or rectifying a false or mistaken state of affairs; appears in administrative, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqa-05**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqa cheqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa cheqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + cheqa (reduplication) → "truly true / very genuine"
5. **Natural English translation:** truly true, absolutely genuine, really real, the very truth
6. **Usage:** Reduplicated form used for emphatic affirmation that something is genuinely and completely true or real; appears in everyday speech.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on cheqa:** The root cheqa does not appear to directly generate a verbal stem without the *-ncha-* verbalizer (i.e., *cheqay* as a simple verb is not attested in available sources). The productive verbal derivation runs through *cheqanchay*. The *-q* agentive form *cheqaq* is itself a productive base (see below).

### Base: **cheqaq** (itself derived from cheqa)

---

**D-cheqaq-01**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqmi
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqmi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqaq + *-mi* (direct evidential suffix) → "it is indeed true (I know directly)"
5. **Natural English translation:** it is truly so, it is indeed true, certainly
6. **Usage:** Asserts truth on the basis of the speaker's direct knowledge or experience; used in everyday assertion and in formal testimony to mark first-hand epistemic grounding.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqaq-02**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqsi
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqsi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqaq + *-si* (reportative evidential suffix) → "reportedly true"
5. **Natural English translation:** reportedly true, said to be true, allegedly genuine
6. **Usage:** Marks a proposition as true according to hearsay or reported evidence; used in narration of events not directly witnessed and in evaluation of second-hand testimony.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqaq-03**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqchus
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqchus
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqaq + *-chus* (dubitative/interrogative suffix) → "is it true? / perhaps true"
5. **Natural English translation:** is it really true?, perhaps true, I wonder if it is genuine
6. **Usage:** Expresses doubt or uncertainty about whether something is true or genuine; used in everyday questioning and in contexts of evaluating uncertain claims.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**D-cheqaq-04**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqpuni
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqpuni
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqaq + *-puni* (emphatic/intensifying suffix, "definitely, always, without exception") → "definitely true / absolutely genuine"
5. **Natural English translation:** absolutely true, definitely genuine, truly and certainly so
6. **Usage:** An emphatic form used to assert truth or genuineness with strong force, ruling out doubt; appears in contexts of strong affirmation or in response to skepticism.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on cheqaq evidential paradigm:** The forms *cheqaqmi*, *cheqaqsi*, and *cheqaqchus* constitute a systematic evidential paradigm (direct, reportative, dubitative) applied to the truth predicate. This paradigm is regular and productive across Quechua predicates; its application to *cheqaq* specifically is well-attested. A fourth member, *cheqaqcha* (speculative), may exist by paradigm regularity but is not independently confirmed in available sources and is therefore not listed here.

**Note on llulla verbal morphology:** The relationship between *llullakuy* (with *-ku-*) and a hypothetical bare verbal stem *llullay* is not clearly established in available sources. A form *llullay* without *-ku-* is not confirmed in available documentation and is therefore not listed.

## Summary of Derivational Patterns Observed

The following observations are directly supported by the forms documented above:

**1. Evidential suffixation on truth predicates:** The evidential suffixes *-mi* (direct), *-si* (reportative), and *-chus* (dubitative) attach productively to truth-evaluating predicates (*cheqaq*, *hina*, *allin*). This is a general grammatical pattern in Quechua applied to the truth domain.

**2. Agentive *-q* derivation:** The agentive suffix *-q* attaches to verbal stems derived from truth-evaluation roots (*cheqaq*, *llullaq*, *pantaq*, *riqsiq*, *yachaq*) to produce nominal/adjectival forms characterizing persons or things. This is a general Quechua pattern with consistent application across the domain.

**3. Causative *-chi-* derivation:** The causative suffix *-chi-* attaches to verbal stems from truth-evaluation roots (*pantachiy*, *llullachiy*, *riqsichiy*, *yachachiy*) to produce forms describing the induction of a state in another agent. This pattern is attested for both error (*panta-*) and deception (*llulla-*) roots.

**4. Reflexive/middle *-ku-* derivation:** The reflexive/middle suffix *-ku-* attaches to verbal stems (*llullakuy*, *pantakuy*, *cheqanchakuy*, *riqsikuy*, *yachakuy*) to produce intransitive or self-directed forms. This is a general Quechua pattern consistently attested across the domain.

**5. Resultative/participial *-sqa* derivation:** The past participle/resultative suffix *-sqa* attaches to verbal stems (*pantasqa*, *llullasqa*, *cheqanchasqa*, *riqsisqa*, *yachasqa*) to produce adjectival forms characterizing a state resulting from the relevant process.

**6. Asymmetry in verbalizer use:** The *cheqa* root requires the *-ncha-* verbalizer to form a verbal stem (*cheqanchay*), while *panta-* and *llulla-* appear to have verbal stems directly. This asymmetry is preserved in the documentation above and is not normalized.

**7. Negation with *mana*:** Negation with *mana* applies to both adjectival (*mana cheqa*, *mana allin*, *mana pantasqa*) and agentive (*mana pantaq*, *mana yachaq*) forms, producing antonymic expressions. This is a general Quechua pattern.

**8. Roots with no productive derivation in this domain:** *tiqsi* and *rura* show no attested derivation in the truth-evaluation domain. This gap is preserved without normalization.
