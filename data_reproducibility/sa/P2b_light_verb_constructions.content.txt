# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Multi-Word Constructions Analysis

---

### BASE: सत्य / satya

#### Construction 1: सत्यं वद् / satyaṃ vad
- **Native script:** सत्यं वद
- **Transliteration:** satyaṃ vad
- **Translation:** speak truth, tell the truth
- **Usage:** Common imperative construction combining satya (truth) with √vad (to speak). Appears in ethical instruction and Upaniṣadic teachings (e.g., Taittirīya Upaniṣad: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara "speak truth, practice dharma").
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2: सत्यं ब्रू / satyaṃ brū
- **Native script:** सत्यं ब्रू
- **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brū
- **Translation:** speak truth, declare truth
- **Usage:** Alternative to satyaṃ vad using √brū (to speak, declare). Common in classical and Vedic texts.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 3: सत्यात् न चलितव्यम् / satyāt na chalitavyam
- **Native script:** सत्यात् न चलितव्यम्
- **Transliteration:** satyāt na chalitavyam
- **Translation:** one should not deviate from truth
- **Usage:** Gerundive construction expressing moral obligation not to abandon truth. Common in ethical discourse.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 4: सत्यमेव जयते / satyam eva jayate
- **Native script:** सत्यमेव जयते
- **Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
- **Translation:** truth alone triumphs/conquers
- **Usage:** Famous quotation from Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as national motto of India. Expresses ultimate victory of truth.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 5: सत्ये स्था / satye sthā
- **Native script:** सत्ये स्था
- **Transliteration:** satye sthā
- **Translation:** stand in/by truth, remain truthful
- **Usage:** Construction with √sthā (to stand) expressing steadfastness in truth. Common in ethical exhortation.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 6: सत्यं प्रतिज्ञा / satyaṃ pratijñā
- **Native script:** सत्यं प्रतिज्ञा
- **Transliteration:** satyaṃ pratijñā
- **Translation:** promise truthfully, make a true promise
- **Usage:** Construction combining satya with pratijñā (promise, assertion). Used for making truthful commitments.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 7: सत्येन विजयते / satyena vijayate
- **Native script:** सत्येन विजयते
- **Transliteration:** satyena vijayate
- **Translation:** conquers/triumphs by truth
- **Usage:** Instrumental construction expressing means of victory through truth. Related to satyam eva jayate.
- **Status:** [CORE]

### BASE: ऋत / ṛta

#### Construction 1: ऋतं सत्यं च / ṛtaṃ satyaṃ ca
- **Native script:** ऋतं सत्यं च
- **Transliteration:** ṛtaṃ satyaṃ ca
- **Translation:** cosmic order and truth
- **Usage:** Vedic collocation pairing ṛta and satya, emphasizing both cosmic and moral truth. Common in Vedic hymns.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2: ऋतस्य पथा / ṛtasya pathā
- **Native script:** ऋतस्य पथा
- **Transliteration:** ṛtasya pathā
- **Translation:** by the path of ṛta, according to cosmic order
- **Usage:** Vedic expression for following the proper cosmic/moral path. Instrumental construction.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 3: ऋतेन ऋतम् आप् / ṛtena ṛtam āp
- **Native script:** ऋतेन ऋतम् आप्
- **Transliteration:** ṛtena ṛtam āp
- **Translation:** attain ṛta through ṛta, reach truth through truth
- **Usage:** Vedic construction expressing means and end both as ṛta. Found in Ṛgveda.
- **Status:** [CORE]

**Note:** Most ṛta constructions are archaic Vedic expressions with limited classical usage.

## Summary Notes

### Highly Productive Construction Types:

1. **Verb + Object constructions**: Most core lexical items combine productively with semantically appropriate verbs (vad "speak", jñā "know", dṛś "see", kṛ "do", bhū "become", etc.)

2. **Instrumental constructions**: Extremely productive for expressing means, manner, or accordance (satyena, dharmeṇa, nyāyena, yuktyā, etc.)

3. **Ablative constructions**: Common for expressing source, cause, or standard of comparison (satyāt, dharmāt, bhramāt, etc.)

4. **Gerundive obligations**: Productive pattern for expressing moral/logical necessity (na vaktavyam, na chalitavyam, etc.)

5. **Contrastive pairs**: Natural pairing of opposites in dual or disjunctive constructions (satyāsatye, dharmādharmau, etc.)

### Limited or Absent Constructions:

1. **Archaic Vedic terms** (ṛta, anṛta): Show limited productivity in classical constructions, mostly confined to Vedic formulaic expressions

2. **Adverbial bases** (mṛṣā, mithyā, samyak): Combine with verbs but show limited nominal construction patterns

3. **Abstract derivatives**: Terms like prāmāṇya, sādṛśya show more limited construction patterns than their base forms

### Register and Context Variation:

- **Philosophical/technical contexts**: Favor constructions with pramāṇa, tattva, yukti, upapatti
- **Ethical/religious contexts**: Favor constructions with satya, dharma, sādhu
- **Legal contexts**: Favor constructions with nyāya, kūṭa, chala
- **Epistemological contexts**: Favor constructions with pramā, bhrama, viparyaya, yathārtha

### Asymmetries Noted:

- Positive terms (satya, dharma) show richer construction patterns than their negations
- Some expected parallel constructions are unattested or rare
- Vedic vs. classical usage shows significant divergence in construction productivity
- Technical philosophical terms show more restricted construction patterns than general vocabulary
