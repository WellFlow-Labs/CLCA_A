# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note:** Ratings and usage notes are based strictly on patterns documented in P4a. No additional constructions are introduced. Where P4a itself signals uncertainty or restricted attestation, that is reflected in the epistemic status below.

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-nominal attributive: [NOUN] + [EVALUATIVE NOUN/ADJ] | ènìyàn òtítọ́ | ènìyàn òtítọ́ | "a truthful person / a person of truth" | [CORE] | Productive across the truth-domain vocabulary in everyday, moral, and legal discourse. **Attested alternates:** *ọ̀rẹ́ gidi* ("a true friend"), *ìdájọ́ tọ́* ("a correct judgment") — same pattern, different modifiers.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal predicate negation: [NOUN] + *kò jẹ́* + [EVALUATIVE NOUN] | Kò jẹ́ ohun ojúlówó. | Kò jẹ́ ohun ojúlówó. | "It is not a genuine thing. / It is not authentic." | [CORE] | Used when the predicate is nominal; the copula *jẹ́* is obligatory in this sub-pattern. Omission of *jẹ́* with a nominal predicate is not attested in P4a. |
| Stative verbal predicate negation: [NOUN] + *kò* + [STATIVE VERB] | Ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́. | Ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́. | "The judgment is not correct / just." | [CORE] | Used when the predicate is a stative verb (*tọ́*, *dára*, etc.); *kò* precedes the verb directly with no copula. P4a explicitly marks these two sub-patterns as structurally distinct; they are not interchangeable. |

**Attested alternates:** None introduced beyond the two sub-patterns documented in P4a. The structural distinction between nominal and stative-verbal negation is treated as obligatory, not optional.

P4a notes that the associative linker *tí* appears in relative-clause modification, but this is documented as a distinct sub-type, not an alternate for the juxtaposition compound.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instrumental causative: *fi* + [INSTRUMENT NOUN] + [VERB PHRASE] | Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀. | Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀. | "They used deception to take his/her money." | [CORE] | The most productive causative-adjacent pattern in the truth domain per P4a; expresses the means by which an outcome is brought about. | Focus + instrumental causative | Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn. | Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn. | "It is testing that causes a person's character to be revealed." | [CORE] | Combines the focus construction (Pattern 10) with the instrumental *fi* construction; both layers are independently attested. **Attested alternates:** P4a documents *mú...wá* ("bring about") and *jẹ́ kí* + [CLAUSE] as additional causative-adjacent constructions, but provides no truth-domain examples for these two; they are listed in the pattern description only. They are therefore noted here as documented but not exemplified within the truth domain.

| *ò-* prefixal nominalization | òtítọ́ | òtítọ́ | "truth, truthfulness" | [CORE] | Attested for *òtítọ́*; P4a notes the internal structure (*ò-* + *tí* + *tọ́*) but does not claim this prefix is as productive as *ì-* across the domain. |
| *ẹ̀-* prefixal nominalization | ẹ̀rí | ẹ̀rí | "evidence" | [CORE] | Attested for *ẹ̀rí* ("evidence") and *ẹ̀tọ́* ("right/correctness"). P4a does not claim broad productivity for this prefix beyond these attested forms. |
| *à-* prefixal nominalization | àṣìṣe | àṣìṣe | "error/wrongdoing" | [MARGINAL] | Attested for *àṣìṣe* with a resultant-state or negative-outcome reading per P4a. | Negative nominalization: *àì-* + [VERBAL BASE] | àìmọ̀ | àìmọ̀ | "ignorance" | [CORE] | Productive negation of nominalized forms; attested for *àìmọ̀* ("ignorance"), *àìgbàgbọ́* ("unbelief"), *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* ("unreliability"). **Attested alternates:** No alternate nominalization strategies are documented in P4a; the prefixal patterns are presented as the primary derivational process.

## 9. EXISTENTIAL AFFIRMATION / DENIAL OF TRUTH-DOMAIN ENTITIES

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 10. FOCUS CONSTRUCTION FOR TRUTH-DOMAIN ASSERTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focus: [FOCUSED ELEMENT] + *ló* + [PREDICATE] | Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́. | Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́. | "It is truth that saves one." | [CORE] | Pervasive in proverbial and moral discourse per P4a; used to assert the primacy or causal role of truth, knowledge, good character, or testing. Not restricted to the truth domain but highly frequent within it. |
| Focus: [FOCUSED ELEMENT] + *ló* + [PREDICATE] | Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́. | Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́. | "It is good character that saves one." | [CORE] | Same construction with a different focused element (*ìwà rere*, "good character"); confirms the pattern is not lexically restricted to *òtítọ́* alone. P4a notes *ló* is a fusion of copula *ni* and subject pronoun *ó*. |

**Attested alternates:** P4a documents the focus construction as also applying to *ìmọ̀* ("knowledge") and *ìdánwò* ("testing") in moral and epistemic contexts, though no separate full examples are provided for those items beyond the pattern description. The combination of focus + instrumental causative (documented under Pattern 5) is the only attested structural combination involving this construction.
