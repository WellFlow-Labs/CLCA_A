## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### VERIFICATION → CAUSATION Substitution
Indonesian does not maintain a distinct VERIFICATION construction. The causative morphology (meN-...-kan) serves both factual causation and verification functions. Examples:
- **membenarkan** can mean both "make true" and "confirm/verify"
- **mengesahkan** means both "make valid" and "validate/ratify"

Context and argument structure determine interpretation. When the patient is a proposition or document (laporan "report", dokumen "document"), the reading tends toward verification. This represents functional substitution rather than structural gap.

### REVELATION vs. BECOME: Distinct Operators
Indonesian maintains clear distinction:
- **BECOME**: menjadi + [TRUTH-TERM] = change of state in the world
  - *dokumen menjadi sah* "the document became valid"
- **REVELATION**: ternyata + [CLAUSE] = epistemic discovery by speaker
  - *ternyata dia benar* "it turns out he/she was right"

"Ternyata" has grammaticalized from ter- (accidental/resultative prefix) + nyata (real/evident) into a pure evidential marker. It does not describe change in the world but rather change in speaker's knowledge state.

### Asymmetry: Negative vs. Positive Forms

**Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry:**
- Negative agents: pembohong "liar", pemalsu "forger", pendusta "deceiver" (productive)
- Positive agents: *penjujur, *penbenar (unattested/blocked)
- Alternative for positive: descriptive phrases (orang jujur "honest person")

This asymmetry reflects cultural/pragmatic focus on marking deviant agents rather than virtuous ones.

**Character Causation Asymmetry:**
- Negative character causation: membohongi "deceive/lie to" (attested)
- Positive character causation: *make someone honest* (no attested construction)

The causative morphology applies readily to deception but not to positive character transformation in the attested data.

### Hedging as Negation Alternative
"Kurang" + [TRUTH-TERM] functions as mitigated negation:
- Direct: tidak tepat "not correct"
- Hedged: kurang tepat "not quite correct"

This represents a pragmatic rather than semantic distinction, used for politeness and face-saving.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps

1. **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated construction for "prove false" or "show to be untrue". Requires circumlocution with negation + verification/causation (e.g., *membuktikan bahwa X tidak benar* "prove that X is not true").

2. **CAUSATION (character) - positive forms**: While causative morphology exists and applies to factual truth-domain terms, no attested examples of positive character causation (e.g., *make someone honest*). The morphological pattern exists but is not applied to this semantic domain.

### Lexical Gaps
None identified. All core truth-domain concepts have lexical expressions.

### Conceptual Gaps
None identified. All truth-domain concepts can be expressed, though some require circumlocution.

### Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Agentive Nominalization**: 
   - Present: pembohong "liar", pemalsu "forger" (negative agents)
   - Absent: *penjujur, *penbenar (positive agents)
   - Substitution: descriptive phrases (orang jujur "honest person")

2. **Character Causation**:
   - Present: membohongi "deceive" (negative)
   - Absent: *make honest* (positive)
   - Substitution: circumlocution required

3. **Evidential/Discovery (ter- prefix)**:
   - Fully grammaticalized: ternyata "it turns out"
   - Marginal: tersalah "mistakenly wrong"
   - Limited productivity beyond these forms

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available
- **ATTR**: [NP] + [TRUTH-TERM] (zero copula, core construction)
- **ADNOM**: [TRUTH-TERM] + [NOUN] (prenominal modification)
- **BECOME**: menjadi + [TRUTH-TERM] (inchoative)
- **REVELATION**: ternyata + [CLAUSE] (grammaticalized evidential)
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: meN-[ROOT]-kan (productive causative)
- **NEGATION**: tidak + [TRUTH-TERM] (analytic negation)
- **NOMINALIZATION (abstract)**: ke-[ROOT]-an (highly productive)

### Available with Substitution
- **VERIFICATION**: Expressed through CAUSATION morphology (meN-...-kan) with context-dependent interpretation. No distinct construction.

### Partially Available
- **NOMINALIZATION (agentive)**: Productive for negative agents (pembohong, pemalsu) but blocked for positive agents. Positive forms require descriptive alternatives.
- **CAUSATION (character)**: Attested for negative character (membohongi "deceive") but no positive forms attested. Morphological pattern exists but application is asymmetric.

### Missing Entirely
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated construction. Requires circumlocution (negation + verification/proof constructions).

---

## Summary Statement

Indonesian provides robust morphosyntactic support for most CLCA operators through:
1. Zero-copula attribution (ATTR)
2. Prenominal modification (ADNOM)
3. Inchoative verb "menjadi" (BECOME)
4. Grammaticalized evidential "ternyata" (REVELATION)
5. Productive causative morphology meN-...-kan (CAUSATION/VERIFICATION)
6. Analytic negation with "tidak" (NEGATION)
7. Productive abstract nominalization ke-...-an (NOMINALIZATION)

The language shows two notable patterns:
1. **Functional substitution**: VERIFICATION is expressed through CAUSATION morphology rather than a distinct construction
2. **Asymmetric gaps**: Agentive nominalization and character causation are productive for negative/deceptive terms but blocked or unattested for positive terms

The only complete structural gap is DISCONFIRMATION, which requires periphrastic expression. All other truth-domain operations are either directly available or accessible through systematic substitution patterns.
