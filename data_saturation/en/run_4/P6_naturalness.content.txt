# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents how English encodes dynamic truth-related meanings: inchoative (becoming), causative (causing to be), and stative-stability (remaining, holding) expressions involving the vocabulary established in P1–P5. All forms and constructions are drawn exclusively from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents a gap or constraint, it is preserved. Where no common expression exists, this is stated explicitly.

The analysis is organized by semantic type (inchoative, causative, stative-stability) and then by sub-domain (propositional truth, authenticity/genuineness, logical/epistemic, personal honesty, reliability/credibility, legitimacy). Within each section, items are treated separately where their behavior differs.

---

## Section 1: Inchoative Expressions ("X becomes [truth-related state]")

Inchoative expressions encode a transition into a truth-related state. As documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P4b, English lacks a dedicated inchoative morpheme for truth-domain adjectives. The inchoative is expressed periphrastically.

---

### 1.1 Propositional truth and falsity

---

**Expression 1.1.1**
- **Form:** *come true*
- **Example:** *Her prediction came true.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Her prediction was realized; what she anticipated actually happened.
- **Construction type:** Idiomatic inchoative; *come* + predicative *true*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 1.29), P4a (Construction 4), and P4b. The CONTEXT explicitly restricts this construction to subjects that are wishes, predictions, dreams, or hopes becoming actual. It is not extensible to other truth-domain adjectives: *\*came genuine*, *\*came valid*, *\*came correct* are not attested. The inchoative direction is from non-actual to actual, not from false to true in the propositional sense.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.1.2**
- **Form:** *turn out to be true / false / correct / incorrect*
- **Example:** *The report turned out to be false.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The report was subsequently discovered to be inaccurate.
- **Construction type:** General-purpose inchoative of discovered truth-status; *turn out to be* + truth-domain adjective
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P4b as "the general-purpose inchoative frame for discovered truth-status." The CONTEXT states it "applies across the full range of truth-domain adjectives": *turned out to be genuine / fake / correct / valid / authentic / bogus*. The inchoative here encodes epistemic discovery rather than ontological change: the truth-status was already fixed; what changes is the knower's awareness of it.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.1.3**
- **Form:** *prove true / prove false / prove correct*
- **Example:** *The hypothesis proved false.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The hypothesis was shown to be false; its falsity was established.
- **Construction type:** Formal inchoative of confirmed truth-status; *prove* + predicative adjective
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P4b as "a more formal variant" of *turn out to be*. Common in academic, scientific, and journalistic registers. Like *turn out to be*, this encodes epistemic discovery rather than ontological change. The CONTEXT attests *proved false* and *proved correct*; the pattern extends to *proved genuine*, *proved valid*, *proved bogus*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.1.4**
- **Form:** *hold true*
- **Example:** *This rule holds true in most cases.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** This rule remains valid or applicable in most cases.
- **Construction type:** Stative-stability construction (see Section 3); included here because the CONTEXT documents it as a verbal phrase in which *true* functions as a predicative complement. The inchoative counterpart (*cease to hold true*, *no longer holds true*) is not separately documented in the CONTEXT but is inferrable from the stative form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.1.5**
- **Form:** *ring true / ring false*
- **Example:** *Her explanation didn't ring true.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Her explanation did not seem genuine and believable.
- **Construction type:** Evaluative stative construction; *ring* + predicative *true* or *false*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (items 1.28), P2b (Construction 1.3, 9.10), and P5 (Opposition 38). The CONTEXT describes this as evaluating "perceived credibility based on intuitive impression rather than verified correspondence." The construction is stative in form but often appears in past tense or negative contexts that imply a transition from apparent credibility to perceived inauthenticity. No dedicated inchoative form is documented.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Gap note:** No common expression for *become true* in the ontological sense (a proposition changing its truth value) is documented in the CONTEXT. The CONTEXT does not attest *\*go true*, *\*grow true*, or *\*become true* as productive constructions. The closest attested form is *come true*, which is restricted to the actualization of wishes and predictions rather than propositional truth-value change.

---

### 1.2 Authenticity and genuineness

---

**Expression 1.2.1**
- **Form:** *turn out to be genuine / authentic / fake / counterfeit / bogus*
- **Example:** *The painting turned out to be a fake.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The painting was subsequently discovered to be not genuine.
- **Construction type:** General-purpose inchoative of discovered authenticity-status; *turn out to be* + authenticity adjective
- **Usage notes:** The CONTEXT documents *turn out to be* as applying "across the full range of truth-domain adjectives" (P4b), which includes the authenticity sub-domain. The inchoative encodes epistemic discovery: the object's status was fixed; what changes is the knower's awareness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.2.2**
- **Form:** *prove genuine / prove authentic*
- **Example:** *The document proved genuine after forensic analysis.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The document was shown to be genuine after forensic analysis.
- **Construction type:** Formal inchoative of confirmed authenticity; *prove* + predicative adjective
- **Usage notes:** Parallel to Expression 1.1.3. Common in legal, archival, and journalistic registers. The CONTEXT documents *prove* + predicative adjective as a formal variant of *turn out to be* (P4a, P4b).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Gap note:** No common expression for *become genuine* or *become authentic* in the sense of an object acquiring genuineness is documented in the CONTEXT. The CONTEXT documents *authenticate* as the causative verb for establishing authenticity (see Section 2), but no inchoative counterpart (*\*genuinize*, *\*become genuine* as a productive construction) is attested.

---

### 1.3 Logical and epistemic status

---

**Expression 1.3.1**
- **Form:** *turn out to be valid / invalid / sound / unsound*
- **Example:** *The argument turned out to be invalid.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The argument was subsequently found to lack logical validity.
- **Construction type:** General-purpose inchoative of discovered logical status; *turn out to be* + logical adjective
- **Usage notes:** The CONTEXT documents *turn out to be* as applying across the full range of truth-domain adjectives (P4b), which includes the logical sub-domain. In formal logic, validity and soundness are properties of argument forms rather than contingent states, so the inchoative here typically encodes epistemic discovery rather than genuine ontological change.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 1.3.2**
- **Form:** *prove falsifiable / prove verifiable*
- **Example:** *The hypothesis proved falsifiable under the new experimental design.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The hypothesis was shown to be testable under the new experimental design.
- **Construction type:** Formal inchoative of confirmed epistemic status; *prove* + predicative adjective
- **Usage notes:** Parallel to Expression 1.1.3. Common in philosophy of science and academic registers. The CONTEXT documents *falsifiable* (P2a-9.7) and *verifiable* (P2a-17.2) as technical terms in this domain.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Gap note:** No common expression for *become valid* or *become sound* in the sense of an argument acquiring logical validity is documented in the CONTEXT. The CONTEXT documents *validate* as a causative verb (see Section 2), but no inchoative counterpart is attested as a productive construction.

---

### 1.4 Personal honesty and sincerity

---

**Expression 1.4.1**
- **Form:** *turn out to be honest / dishonest / sincere / insincere*
- **Example:** *He turned out to be less honest than we had assumed.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He was subsequently found to be less honest than we had assumed.
- **Construction type:** General-purpose inchoative of discovered character assessment; *turn out to be* + honesty adjective
- **Usage notes:** The CONTEXT documents *turn out to be* as applying across the full range of truth-domain adjectives (P4b). Applied to persons, the inchoative encodes a revision of assessment rather than a change in the person's character.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Gap note:** No common expression for *become honest* or *become sincere* as a productive inchoative construction is documented in the CONTEXT. The CONTEXT documents the relevant adjectives and their negations but does not attest a dedicated inchoative construction for this sub-domain.

---

### 1.5 Legitimacy

---

**Expression 1.5.1**
- **Form:** *become legitimate / become illegitimate*
- **Example:** *The government became legitimate only after the election.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The government acquired legitimate status only after the election.
- **Construction type:** Copular inchoative; *become* + adjective
- **Usage notes:** The CONTEXT documents *legitimate* and *illegitimate* (P1-13, P2a-13.3) and the causative *legitimize* (P2a-13.6). The copular inchoative *become legitimate* is inferrable from the documented vocabulary and is consistent with the general English pattern for adjective inchoatives, though it is not separately attested as a fixed construction in the CONTEXT. The CONTEXT documents *legitimization* as "the process of making something legitimate" (P2a-13.7), which presupposes an inchoative transition.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Section 2: Causative Expressions ("Y causes X to be [truth-related state]")

Causative expressions encode an agent or force bringing about a truth-related state. As documented in P4a (Construction 5), English has no productive morphological causative; the causative function is expressed by dedicated verbs derived from or associated with truth-domain adjectives.

---

### 2.1 Causatives of truth and confirmation

---

**Expression 2.1.1**
- **Form:** *verify*
- **Example:** *The laboratory verified the results.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The laboratory confirmed that the results were accurate.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *verify* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1-17, P2a-17, P2b-17, P4a (Construction 5), and P4b. The CONTEXT describes *verify* as "the most broadly distributed causative verb in this domain," used across scientific, legal, administrative, and technical contexts. The causative direction is: agent establishes the truth or accuracy of a claim, identity, or document.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.2**
- **Form:** *verify against*
- **Example:** *Verify the data against the original records.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Check the accuracy of the data by comparing it with the original records.
- **Construction type:** Causative verb + prepositional complement; *verify* + *against* + reference standard
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 2.16) as "standard procedural phrasing in quality control and auditing." The *against* complement specifies the reference standard used in verification.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.3**
- **Form:** *validate*
- **Example:** *The committee validated the findings.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The committee confirmed the validity of the findings.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *validate* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-6.3 and P4a (Construction 5). The CONTEXT distinguishes *validate* from *verify*: *validate* "emphasizes procedural approval" (P4b), while *verify* emphasizes factual confirmation. Used in legal, scientific, psychological, and technical contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.4**
- **Form:** *authenticate*
- **Example:** *An expert authenticated the manuscript.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** An expert confirmed that the manuscript was genuine.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *authenticate* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-5.3, P1b (item 3b.2), P4a (Construction 5), and P4b. Used in legal, archival, art-historical, and digital security contexts. The causative direction is: an authorized agent establishes the authenticity of an object, document, or identity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.5**
- **Form:** *legitimize*
- **Example:** *The election legitimized the new government.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The election conferred legitimate status on the new government.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *legitimize* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-13.6. The CONTEXT notes that *legitimize* is "often used critically to suggest that something is being given an undeserved appearance of legitimacy." The causative direction is: an event or agent confers or appears to confer legitimate status.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.6**
- **Form:** *lend legitimacy to*
- **Example:** *His endorsement lent legitimacy to the claim.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** His endorsement made the claim appear more legitimate.
- **Construction type:** Periphrastic causative; *lend* + *legitimacy* + *to* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 13.3) and P5 (Opposition 46). The CONTEXT notes this is "used in political and journalistic discourse, often critically, to describe the effect of association or endorsement." The causative agent is typically an endorser or associated party rather than a formal authority.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.7**
- **Form:** *confer legitimacy on*
- **Example:** *The treaty conferred legitimacy on the new borders.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The treaty officially granted legitimate status to the new borders.
- **Construction type:** Periphrastic causative; *confer* + *legitimacy* + *on* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 13.4) and P5 (Opposition 46). The CONTEXT describes this as "more formal" than *lend legitimacy to* and used in "political science and legal discourse." The causative agent is typically a formal authority.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.1.8**
- **Form:** *lend credibility to*
- **Example:** *The expert testimony lent credibility to the prosecution's case.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The expert testimony made the prosecution's case more believable.
- **Construction type:** Periphrastic causative; *lend* + *credibility* + *to* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 24.3) and P5 (Opposition 47). Parallel in structure to *lend legitimacy to*. Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe the effect of association or endorsement on perceived credibility.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Causatives of falsity and invalidation

---

**Expression 2.2.1**
- **Form:** *falsify*
- **Example:** *New evidence falsified the hypothesis.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** New evidence showed the hypothesis to be false.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *falsify* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-9.5 and P4a (Construction 5). The CONTEXT distinguishes two senses: (a) to fraudulently alter a document or record to make it false, and (b) in philosophy of science, to show a hypothesis to be false through evidence. The CONTEXT notes that sense (b) is "strongly associated with scientific and philosophical discourse (Popperian falsificationism)."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.2.2**
- **Form:** *invalidate*
- **Example:** *The new evidence invalidated the earlier verdict.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The new evidence deprived the earlier verdict of its legal or logical force.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *invalidate* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-6.8, P4a (Construction 5), and P5 (Oppositions 31, 32). The CONTEXT notes that *invalidate* is used in legal contexts ("to *invalidate* a contract or election") and logical contexts ("to *invalidate* an argument by showing a false premise"). The causative direction is: an agent or event deprives something of its validity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.2.3**
- **Form:** *falsify* (fraudulent sense)
- **Example:** *He falsified the records to conceal the payments.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He fraudulently altered the records to conceal the payments.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *falsify* + object (fraudulent alteration sense)
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-9.5. This sense is distinct from the Popperian sense (Expression 2.2.1): here the agent deliberately makes a document or record false through fraudulent alteration, rather than demonstrating that a hypothesis is false through evidence. Common in legal and administrative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.2.4**
- **Form:** *strain credulity*
- **Example:** *The claim strained credulity.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The claim was so implausible as to be difficult to believe.
- **Construction type:** Periphrastic causative; *strain* + *credulity*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 24.4) and P5 (Opposition 47). The CONTEXT notes that the surface form is *credulity* rather than *credible*, but places the construction in the credibility domain. The causative direction is: a claim causes the listener's capacity for belief to be exceeded.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Causatives of correction

---

**Expression 2.3.1**
- **Form:** *correct* (verbal use)
- **Example:** *She corrected the error in the report.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** She removed the error from the report and replaced it with the accurate information.
- **Construction type:** Zero-derived causative verb; *correct* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-7 as a case of "zero derivation (conversion) from the adjective." The CONTEXT notes that "the verbal use generates *correcting*, *corrected*, and *correction* as standard forms." The causative direction is: an agent brings something into conformity with a standard or fact.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.3.2**
- **Form:** *put right*
- **Example:** *We need to put this right.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** We need to correct this; we need to remedy this error or injustice.
- **Construction type:** Periphrastic causative; *put* + object + *right*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 8.5). The CONTEXT describes this as "used to describe correcting an error, repairing a situation, or remedying an injustice." Common in everyday and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 2.3.3**
- **Form:** *realize* (in the sense of making actual)
- **Example:** *She realized her ambition.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** She made her ambition actual; she achieved what she had aimed for.
- **Construction type:** Dedicated causative verb; *realize* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-14.11 as one of the senses of *realize*: "making something actual." The CONTEXT notes that *realize* is "highly polysemous," covering also the cognitive sense (becoming aware) and the financial sense (converting to money). The causative-of-actualization sense is parallel to *come true* (Expression 1.1.1) but is agentive: an agent makes something real rather than something simply becoming real.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 Causatives with no dedicated verb

The CONTEXT explicitly documents the absence of dedicated causative verbs for several truth-domain adjectives:

- **No *\*truify*, *\*genuinize*, *\*correctify*:** The CONTEXT states in P4a (Construction 5) that "several adjectives lack [a dedicated causative verb] entirely (no *\*truify*, *\*genuinize*, *\*correctify*)." Where a dedicated causative verb is absent, "periphrastic constructions are used (*make correct* → *correct* as a verb; *make real* → *realize*)."
- **No dedicated causative for *honest* or *sincere*:** The CONTEXT notes in P2a-15 that "no verbal form derived directly from *honest* is in common use" and in P2a-16 that "no verbal form derived directly from *sincere* is in common use."
- **No dedicated causative for *genuine*:** The CONTEXT notes in P2a-4 that "no common verbal form derived from *genuine* is attested in English (no *\*genuinize* or *\*genuinate*)." The verbal function is supplied by *authenticate* from the parallel root.

## Section 3: Stative-Stability Expressions ("X remains [truth-related state]")

Stative-stability expressions encode the persistence of a truth-related state over time or across conditions. English expresses this periphrastically using *hold*, *remain*, *stay*, *stand*, and related verbs.

---

### 3.1 Propositional truth and falsity

---

**Expression 3.1.1**
- **Form:** *hold true*
- **Example:** *This rule holds true in most cases.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** This rule remains valid or applicable in most cases.
- **Construction type:** Semi-idiomatic stative-stability construction; *hold* + predicative *true*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 1.30), P2b (Construction 1.5), and P3 (Group 1). The CONTEXT describes this as used "in intellectual, scientific, and everyday discourse to assert that a principle or statement remains accurate under specified conditions." The verb *hold* here encodes persistence rather than physical holding.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.1.2**
- **Form:** *remain true*
- **Example:** *The principle remains true regardless of context.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The principle continues to correspond to the facts regardless of context.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + predicative *true*
- **Usage notes:** The CONTEXT documents *remain* as a stative-stability verb in the construction *remain faithful to* (P2b-20.2) and *remain true to* (P2b-1.8). The pattern *remain true* (without a complement) is inferrable from the documented vocabulary and consistent with the general English pattern, though it is not separately attested as a fixed construction in the CONTEXT.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.1.3**
- **Form:** *stand corrected*
- **Example:** *I stand corrected.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** I acknowledge that I was wrong and accept the correction.
- **Construction type:** Fixed stative construction; *stand* + past participle *corrected*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 7.5). The CONTEXT describes this as "used to formally acknowledge an error after being corrected." The verb *stand* here encodes a stable resulting state (being in the corrected position) rather than physical standing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Authenticity and genuineness

---

**Expression 3.2.1**
- **Form:** *remain faithful to*
- **Example:** *The translation remains faithful to the original.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The translation continues to correspond closely to the original.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + *faithful to* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 20.2). The CONTEXT describes this as used "to describe adherence to a commitment, principle, or original." Applied to reproductions and translations, it encodes the persistence of close correspondence to an original.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.2.2**
- **Form:** *stay true to*
- **Example:** *She stayed true to herself throughout the ordeal.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** She remained faithful to her own values and identity throughout the ordeal.
- **Construction type:** Stative-stability construction; *stay* + predicative *true* + *to* + object
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 1.8). The CONTEXT describes this as expressing "fidelity to personal identity or values." The pattern *stay/remain true to* + object is documented as productive: *true to his word*, *true to form*, *true to herself*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.2.3**
- **Form:** *remain authentic*
- **Example:** *The brand struggled to remain authentic as it grew.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The brand struggled to maintain its genuine character as it grew.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + predicative *authentic*
- **Usage notes:** Inferrable from the documented vocabulary. The CONTEXT documents *authentic* (P1-5), *authenticity* (P2a-5.2), and *living authentically* (P2b-5.2) as expressions of the authenticity sub-domain. The stative-stability construction *remain authentic* is consistent with the general English pattern and with the documented use of *remain faithful to* (Expression 3.2.1).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Logical and epistemic status

---

**Expression 3.3.1**
- **Form:** *remain valid*
- **Example:** *The ticket remains valid for thirty days.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The ticket continues to have legal force for thirty days.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + predicative *valid*
- **Usage notes:** Inferrable from the documented construction *the ticket is valid for thirty days* (P2b-6.2). The CONTEXT documents *valid* in its legal/administrative sense as encoding a state that persists for a specified duration. The stative-stability construction *remain valid* encodes the persistence of that state.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.3.2**
- **Form:** *hold true* (applied to logical principles)
- **Example:** *The law of non-contradiction holds true in classical logic.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The law of non-contradiction remains valid in classical logic.
- **Construction type:** Semi-idiomatic stative-stability construction; *hold* + predicative *true*
- **Usage notes:** The same construction documented in Expression 3.1.1, applied to logical principles rather than empirical generalizations. The CONTEXT documents *hold true* as used "in intellectual, scientific, and everyday discourse to assert that a principle or statement remains accurate under specified conditions" (P1b-1.30).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Personal honesty and sincerity

---

**Expression 3.4.1**
- **Form:** *remain honest*
- **Example:** *It is difficult to remain honest under pressure.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** It is difficult to continue telling the truth under pressure.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + predicative *honest*
- **Usage notes:** Inferrable from the documented vocabulary. The CONTEXT documents *honest* (P1-15) and its derivational family extensively. The stative-stability construction *remain honest* is consistent with the general English pattern, though it is not separately attested as a fixed construction in the CONTEXT.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 3.4.2**
- **Form:** *stay true to oneself*
- **Example:** *He managed to stay true to himself despite the pressure to conform.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He managed to remain faithful to his own values and identity despite the pressure to conform.
- **Construction type:** Stative-stability construction; *stay true to* + reflexive pronoun
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 1.8). The CONTEXT describes this as expressing "fidelity to personal identity or values," placing it at the intersection of the authenticity and personal honesty sub-domains.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Reliability and credibility

---

**Expression 3.5.1**
- **Form:** *remain credible*
- **Example:** *The witness remained credible throughout cross-examination.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The witness continued to be worthy of belief throughout cross-examination.
- **Construction type:** Copular stative-stability construction; *remain* + predicative *credible*
- **Usage notes:** Inferrable from the documented vocabulary. The CONTEXT documents *credible* (P1-24) and *a credible witness* (P2b-24.1) as central to the reliability sub-domain. The stative-stability construction *remain credible* is consistent with the general English pattern.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Section 4: Other Dynamic Expressions

This section documents dynamic truth-related expressions that do not fit cleanly into the inchoative, causative, or stative-stability categories above.

---

### 4.1 Expressions of gradual or partial change

---

**Expression 4.1.1**
- **Form:** *stretch the truth*
- **Example:** *He stretched the truth when describing his qualifications.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** He exaggerated or misrepresented his qualifications without outright lying.
- **Construction type:** Idiomatic dynamic expression; *stretch* + *the truth*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 2.11) and P3 (Group 8). The CONTEXT describes this as expressing "mild or moderate misrepresentation." The verb *stretch* encodes a gradual distortion of truth rather than a categorical transition from true to false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 4.1.2**
- **Form:** *bend the truth*
- **Example:** *She bent the truth to protect her colleague.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** She distorted or misrepresented the facts to protect her colleague.
- **Construction type:** Idiomatic dynamic expression; *bend* + *the truth*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 2.12) and P3 (Group 8). The CONTEXT describes this as parallel to *stretch the truth*, expressing "deliberate but partial misrepresentation." The verb *bend* encodes distortion rather than complete falsification.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 4.1.3**
- **Form:** *economical with the truth*
- **Example:** *The minister was economical with the truth in his statement to Parliament.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The minister deliberately omitted relevant facts in his statement to Parliament.
- **Construction type:** Fixed idiomatic expression; *economical with* + *the truth*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 2.10) and P3 (Group 8). The CONTEXT describes this as expressing "deliberate misleading through selective disclosure rather than outright falsehood." The dynamic encoded is one of withholding or rationing truth rather than falsifying it.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4.2 Expressions of revelation and disclosure

---

**Expression 4.2.1**
- **Form:** *the truth will out*
- **Example:** *Whatever they try to conceal, the truth will out.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The truth will eventually become known, whatever attempts are made to conceal it.
- **Construction type:** Fixed proverbial construction; *the truth* + *will* + *out* (intransitive)
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 2.7). The CONTEXT notes that "the verb *out* is used intransitively in an archaic sense meaning 'to come out' or 'to emerge.'" This encodes an inevitable inchoative: truth moving from concealed to revealed. The modal *will* encodes futurity and inevitability.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 4.2.2**
- **Form:** *speak truth to power*
- **Example:** *Journalists must be willing to speak truth to power.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** Journalists must be willing to confront those in authority with honest, unwelcome facts.
- **Construction type:** Fixed imperative/infinitival construction; *speak* + *truth* + *to* + *power*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 2.8) and P3 (Group 9). The CONTEXT describes this as "the act of challenging authority with honest speech." The dynamic encoded is one of directed disclosure: truth being communicated from a position of relative powerlessness to a position of authority.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 4.2.3**
- **Form:** *moment of truth*
- **Example:** *The moment of truth came when the results were announced.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The decisive moment at which the true nature of something was revealed came when the results were announced.
- **Construction type:** Fixed noun phrase used in dynamic contexts; *moment of truth*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 1.18) and P3 (ungrouped items). The CONTEXT describes this as "a critical point at which the true nature of something is revealed or a decisive test occurs." The dynamic encoded is one of revelation at a specific temporal point.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4.3 Expressions of verification as process

---

**Expression 4.3.1**
- **Form:** *independently verified*

- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 17.2). The CONTEXT describes this as used "in journalistic, scientific, and legal contexts to assert that a claim has been confirmed by an independent source." The dynamic encoded is the completion of a verification process by an independent agent.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Expression 4.3.2**
- **Form:** *unable to verify*

- **Transliteration:** N/A

- **Construction type:** Fixed construction encoding failed or incomplete verification; *unable to* + *verify*
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2b (Construction 17.3). The CONTEXT describes this as used "in journalistic and official contexts to indicate that a claim has not been confirmed." The dynamic encoded is the failure or incompletion of a verification process.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4.4 Expressions of realization and actualization

---

**Expression 4.4.1**
- **Form:** *realize* (cognitive sense)
- **Example:** *She realized that the account was false.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** She became aware that the account was false.
- **Construction type:** Cognitive inchoative; *realize* + *that*-clause
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-14.11. The CONTEXT notes that *realize* in this sense means "becoming aware of a fact." The dynamic encoded is a transition in the knower's epistemic state: from not knowing to knowing that something is true or false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Expression 4.4.2**
- **Form:** *realization*
- **Example:** *The realization that the document was forged came too late.*
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** The moment of becoming aware that the document was forged came too late.
- **Construction type:** Abstract nominalization of the cognitive inchoative; *realization* + *that*-clause
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a-14.12. The CONTEXT describes *realization* as covering "the act of realizing; the process of becoming aware." The nominalization allows the dynamic event to function as a subject or object of discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Section 5: Summary of Documented Patterns and Gaps

---

### 5.1 Patterns documented

The following patterns are attested across the CONTEXT for dynamic truth-related expressions in English:

1. **Inchoative of discovered truth-status:** *turn out to be* + truth-domain adjective (general-purpose, applies across all sub-domains)
2. **Inchoative of confirmed truth-status:** *prove* + predicative adjective (formal variant of *turn out to be*)
3. **Restricted inchoative of actualization:** *come true* (restricted to wishes, predictions, hopes)
4. **Stative-stability:** *hold true*, *remain* + adjective, *stay true to*, *remain faithful to* (general-purpose stative-stability frame)
5. **Dedicated causative verbs:** *verify*, *validate*, *authenticate*, *legitimize*, *falsify*, *invalidate*, *correct* (zero-derived), *realize* (actualization sense)
6. **Periphrastic causatives:** *lend legitimacy/credibility to*, *confer legitimacy on*, *put right*
7. **Gradual distortion:** *stretch the truth*, *bend the truth*, *economical with the truth*
8. **Revelation dynamics:** *the truth will out*, *speak truth to power*, *moment of truth*
9. **Verification process:** *independently verified*, *unable to verify*, *verify against*
10. **Cognitive inchoative:** *realize* + *that*-clause, *realization*

---

### 5.2 Gaps documented

The following gaps are explicitly documented in the CONTEXT or directly inferrable from documented absences:

1. **No productive inchoative morpheme** for truth-domain adjectives: the CONTEXT states in P4a that "English lacks a dedicated inchoative morpheme for truth-domain adjectives."
2. **No productive causative morpheme:** the CONTEXT states in P4a that "English has no productive morphological causative."
3. **No dedicated causative verbs** for *true*, *genuine*, *honest*, *sincere*, *real*, *credible*, *reliable*, *sound*, *plausible*, *faithful*: explicitly documented in P2a and P4a.
4. **No attested *\*come false*:** the CONTEXT documents *come true* but no parallel construction for the transition to falsity.
5. **No attested *\*become true* as a productive construction** in the propositional sense: the CONTEXT does not attest this form; the closest is *come true*, which is restricted to actualization of wishes and predictions.
6. **No attested *\*go true*, *\*grow true*:** not documented in the CONTEXT.
7. **The *come true* construction is not extensible** to other truth-domain adjectives: explicitly stated in P4b.
