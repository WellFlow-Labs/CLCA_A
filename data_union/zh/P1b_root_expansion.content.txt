# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 participation:** 真 (#1), 真实 (#3), 真相 (#5/#9), 真理 (#6), 真伪 (#11/#13), 失真 (#17/#18/#26), 名副其实 (#10/#14, as counterpart), 表里如一 (#22, implicitly), 货真价实 (#11/#24/#29), 真正 (#9), 逼真 (#25), 原真性 (#23/#29), 辨别真伪 (#14)
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts genuineness, authenticity, or correspondence to reality. Functions as the primary positive pole of the truth-evaluation domain, anchoring judgments about whether something is real, accurate, or unfeigned. Asserts that something corresponds to reality, is not counterfeit, and is what it claims to be.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 participation:** 真实 (#3), 事实 (#10), 名副其实 (#10/#14/#15), 名不副实 (#15/#16/#20), 如实 (#16/#17), 失实 (#15/#29/#30), 实质 (#14/#19/#21), 货真价实 (#11/#24/#29), 表里如一 (#22, via 一 but 实 appears in the set broadly), 核实 (#25), 诚实 (#19/#27), 属实 (#16)
**Core meaning contribution:** Denotes solidity, actuality, and factual grounding — what is concretely the case as opposed to what is nominal, empty, or merely claimed. Pairs with 真 to form the core truth-evaluation vocabulary and independently anchors the "fact" and "substance" clusters. Where 真 asserts genuineness, 实 asserts factual substance — what is actually the case, as opposed to what is nominal, empty, or merely formal.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**P1 participation:** 假 (#2), 虚假 (#4), 真伪 (#11/#13, via its antonym 伪), 冒充 (#13, semantically), 弄虚作假 (#23), 假冒 (#22), 伪装 (#27, via semantic field overlap), 假设 (mentioned in usage note for 假), 辨别真伪 (#14)
**Core meaning contribution:** Marks the negative pole of the authenticity axis — that which is fabricated, counterfeit, borrowed, or not what it presents itself as being. Functions as the primary negative pole contrasting with 真, covering both deliberate deception and mere non-genuineness. Also extends into grammatical and temporal uses (see Step 3).

**Note on 伪 (wěi):** 假 and 伪 function as near-synonymous negative-pole roots and are treated together in the expansion below, since they co-occur in compounds and are difficult to separate in the truth-evaluation domain. 伪 appears in 伪造, 真伪, and 伪装 and is a productive root in its own right. However, its independent productivity in everyday compounds is lower than that of 真, 实, or 假, and it overlaps substantially with 假 in the falsity domain. Each is expanded individually below.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Root 1: 真 (zhēn)

*This root is highly productive. The items below represent the most common compounds and expressions; additional forms exist in technical, religious, dialectal, and philosophical usage.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal breakdown:** true + upright/correct
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, real, authentic
**Context of use:** Used as an adverb or attributive adjective to intensify the claim that something is genuinely what it appears to be, not merely nominal or superficial. Extremely common across all registers: 真正的朋友 (a true friend), 真正理解 (to truly understand).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true + sincere/honest
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person's attitude, expression, or intention as genuinely felt rather than performed or strategic. Applied to persons, attitudes, or expressions to indicate that they are free from pretense or calculation. Slightly more formal than 真心. Common in interpersonal, diplomatic, and moral discourse: 真诚的道歉 (a sincere apology), 真诚待人 (to treat people sincerely).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真心
**Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
**Literal breakdown:** true + heart/mind
**Natural English translation:** wholehearted, sincere, genuinely; sincerity
**Context of use:** Asserts that an emotion, intention, or commitment is genuinely felt rather than feigned. Used to evaluate whether a person's feelings, intentions, or expressions are genuine rather than performed or strategic. Used in everyday speech and literary contexts: 真心话 (words from the heart), 真心喜欢 (to genuinely like).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** true + false
**Natural English translation:** genuine or fake, authenticity, true or false
**Context of use:** Names the binary question of whether something is authentic or counterfeit. Functions as a compound noun or binomial framing an evaluative question. Appears in collocations such as 辨别真假 (to distinguish the genuine from the fake), 真假难辨 (hard to tell real from fake), and 分不清真假 (unable to tell real from fake). Common in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal breakdown:** true + principle/reason
**Natural English translation:** truth (as a principle), Truth (philosophical)
**Context of use:** Refers to fundamental or universal truth, especially in philosophical, ideological, political, and scientific discourse. Used in Marxist-Leninist discourse ("真理标准" — the criterion of truth). Appears in expressions like 追求真理 (to pursue truth) and 真理越辩越明 (truth becomes clearer through debate). More abstract than 真相.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真实性
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ness (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, fidelity, factual accuracy
**Context of use:** The nominalized form of 真实, used to evaluate the degree to which a document, account, artwork, or claim corresponds to reality. Common in legal, journalistic, academic, and literary-critical discourse: 新闻的真实性 (the truthfulness of news reporting), 核实其真实性 (to verify its authenticity/accuracy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 真凭实据
**Transliteration:** zhēn píng shí jù
**Literal breakdown:** true + evidence/proof + solid/real + evidence/basis
**Natural English translation:** solid evidence, concrete proof, hard facts
**Context of use:** A fixed four-character expression asserting that a claim is backed by genuine and substantial evidence rather than speculation or hearsay. Used in legal argumentation and everyday disputes to demand or affirm evidentiary grounding. Often used in the phrase 拿出真凭实据 (to produce solid proof).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 真知灼见
**Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge + clear/penetrating + view/insight
**Natural English translation:** genuine insight, penetrating understanding, well-founded opinion
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to praise a judgment or analysis as both accurate and perceptive, as opposed to superficial or mistaken opinions. Common in academic, literary, and intellectual discourse as a compliment to a speaker or writer's views.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 真才实学
**Transliteration:** zhēn cái shí xué
**Literal breakdown:** true + talent + solid/real + learning
**Natural English translation:** genuine talent and solid learning, real ability
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person as possessing authentic skills and knowledge rather than merely a reputation or credentials. Used in educational and professional contexts to distinguish substantive competence from superficial qualification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 真金不怕火炼
**Transliteration:** zhēn jīn bù pà huǒ liàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + gold + not + fear + fire + smelt/test
**Natural English translation:** True gold fears no fire; genuine quality withstands scrutiny.
**Context of use:** A proverb asserting that what is genuinely good or authentic will prove itself under testing conditions or adversity. Used in moral and evaluative contexts to encourage confidence in genuine quality or integrity. Applied to persons, products, or principles.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 逼真
**Transliteration:** bīzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to press/approach + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** lifelike, realistic, true to life, vivid, convincingly real
**Context of use:** Evaluates a representation — painting, performance, imitation, description, special effects — as so close to the original that it seems real. Common in aesthetic and artistic criticism: 画得逼真 (painted so realistically), 模仿得逼真 (imitated convincingly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 认真
**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious, to take seriously
**Context of use:** Describes an attitude of genuine engagement and care in performing a task or holding a position. Extremely common in everyday, educational, and professional contexts: 认真工作 (to work conscientiously), 别太认真 (don't take it too seriously). Also used as a verb phrase meaning to take something seriously or literally.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 当真
**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to treat as + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to take seriously, to take as real, to mean it; really, actually (adverb)
**Context of use:** Used to ask or assert whether something is being treated as genuinely true or meant. Common in conversational contexts: 你当真吗？(Are you serious?), 别当真 (Don't take it literally/seriously), 你当真了？(Are you taking this seriously?). Also functions as an adverb meaning "really."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 纯真
**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** pure + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** pure, innocent, guileless, genuine, unspoiled
**Context of use:** Describes a person — especially a child — or an emotion as unaffected and without artifice. Common in literary, emotional, and moral evaluations: 纯真的童年 (an innocent childhood), 纯真的感情 (genuine, unaffected feelings).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 天真
**Transliteration:** tiānzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** heaven/natural + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** naive, innocent, artless, ingenuous; (of children) naturally genuine
**Context of use:** Describes a person or belief as naturally unaffected and without guile, sometimes with the implication of being unrealistically simple. Used both as a compliment (of children) and as mild criticism (of adults): 天真烂漫 (innocent and carefree), 太天真了 (too naive).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 真面目
**Transliteration:** zhēn miànmù
**Literal breakdown:** true + face + appearance
**Natural English translation:** true face, true nature, real identity
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual nature or identity of a person or thing, especially when it has been concealed. Appears in the fixed phrase 庐山真面目 (the true face of Mount Lu; one's true nature, from a Su Shi poem) and in everyday usage: 露出真面目 (to reveal one's true colors).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 真实感
**Transliteration:** zhēnshígǎn
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + sense/feeling
**Natural English translation:** sense of reality, feeling of authenticity, verisimilitude, sense of realism
**Context of use:** Used in literary, cinematic, and artistic criticism to evaluate whether a work produces a convincing impression of reality. Also used in psychological contexts to describe a person's sense of connection to reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 真伪莫辨
**Transliteration:** zhēn wěi mò biàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + false/spurious + cannot + distinguish
**Natural English translation:** impossible to tell genuine from fake, indistinguishable
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to describe a situation in which authentic and counterfeit items or claims are so similar that differentiation is impossible. Common in legal, archival, and investigative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 半真半假
**Transliteration:** bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** half + true + half + false
**Natural English translation:** half true, half false; partly genuine
**Context of use:** Describes a statement or situation that mixes truth and falsehood. Used in everyday and journalistic contexts to characterize claims that are not wholly reliable: 他说的话半真半假 (what he said was half true, half false). Also used to describe testimony, rumors, or narratives.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 真善美
**Transliteration:** zhēn shàn měi
**Literal breakdown:** true + good + beautiful
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, and beauty; the True, the Good, and the Beautiful
**Context of use:** A philosophical and aesthetic triad used in educational, literary, and moral discourse to name the three highest values, corresponding to classical Western formulations. Appears in discussions of art, ethics, and education: 追求真善美 (to pursue truth, goodness, and beauty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 写真
**Transliteration:** xiězhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to write/depict + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** portrait, realistic depiction, photo (in some contexts); photo shoot (especially glamour or idol photography)
**Context of use:** In classical and art-historical contexts, refers to realistic portraiture or life-drawing. In modern usage, especially in Taiwan and influenced by Japanese, refers to a photographic portrait or photo shoot, especially of celebrities or models. Used in both artistic and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 传真
**Transliteration:** chuánzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to transmit + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** fax, facsimile; also: to transmit a true likeness
**Context of use:** In modern usage, the standard term for facsimile (fax) transmission. The compound originally referred to transmitting a true likeness; the technical meaning is now dominant. Common in administrative and business contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 真空
**Transliteration:** zhēnkōng
**Literal breakdown:** true + empty/void
**Natural English translation:** vacuum (physical); also: a genuine void or absence
**Context of use:** The standard scientific and technical term for a vacuum (absence of matter). Also used in Buddhist philosophical discourse to refer to the concept of true emptiness (śūnyatā). Used metaphorically for a power vacuum or an absence of something expected: 权力真空 (power vacuum), 真空包装 (vacuum packaging). Common in both technical and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 真菌
**Transliteration:** zhēnjūn
**Literal breakdown:** true + fungus/bacterium
**Natural English translation:** true fungi, Fungi (taxonomic kingdom)
**Context of use:** The standard biological and taxonomic term for the kingdom Fungi, distinguishing true fungi from other fungus-like organisms. Used in scientific, medical, and educational contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 千真万确
**Transliteration:** qiān zhēn wàn què
**Literal breakdown:** thousand + true + ten-thousand + certain/precise
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, beyond any doubt, one hundred percent certain
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to assert with maximum emphasis that something is genuinely and certainly true. Common in everyday speech and informal writing when a speaker wishes to insist on the truth of a claim, often to counter skepticism: 这件事千真万确 (this matter is absolutely true).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 真相大白
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng dàbái
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance + greatly + clear/white
**Natural English translation:** the truth has come to light; the facts are fully revealed
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used to describe the moment when concealed facts are fully exposed or clarified. Common in journalistic, legal, and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 真迹
**Transliteration:** zhēnjì
**Literal breakdown:** true + trace/mark (especially calligraphic or painted work)
**Natural English translation:** authentic work; genuine original (of calligraphy or painting)
**Context of use:** Used specifically in art authentication to refer to a work that is genuinely by the attributed artist, as opposed to a copy or forgery. Central to Chinese art historical and connoisseurship discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 真品
**Transliteration:** zhēnpǐn
**Literal breakdown:** true + article/product
**Natural English translation:** genuine article; authentic product
**Context of use:** Used in commercial and authentication contexts to distinguish a genuine product from a counterfeit. Common in consumer protection and antiques evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 真本
**Transliteration:** zhēnběn
**Literal breakdown:** true + original/root copy
**Natural English translation:** authentic original; genuine edition
**Context of use:** Used in bibliographic and textual scholarship to refer to an authentic original text or edition, as opposed to a forgery or corrupt copy.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 真人
**Transliteration:** zhēnrén
**Literal breakdown:** true + person
**Natural English translation:** real person; (in Daoist usage) perfected person, realized being
**Context of use:** In everyday usage, refers to a real, actual person as opposed to a fictional or represented one: 真人秀 (reality show), 真人版 (live-action version). In Daoist philosophical and religious discourse, refers to a spiritually perfected individual.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** 真人秀
**Transliteration:** zhēnrénxiù
**Literal breakdown:** true + person + show (loanword)
**Natural English translation:** reality show; reality television
**Context of use:** The standard Chinese term for reality television programming. Extremely common in media and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Form:** 真实性原则
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng yuánzé
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ness + principle
**Natural English translation:** principle of truthfulness; principle of factual accuracy
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic ethics, legal procedure, and heritage conservation to name the normative requirement that representations or records conform to fact.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**33.**
**Form:** 真伪难辨
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi nán biàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + false + difficult + distinguish
**Natural English translation:** difficult to tell genuine from fake; hard to distinguish authentic from counterfeit
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used to describe situations where authenticity is genuinely uncertain or where deception is sophisticated. Common in authentication and investigative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** 真假难分
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ nán fēn
**Literal breakdown:** true + false + difficult + separate/distinguish
**Natural English translation:** hard to tell real from fake; indistinguishable from the genuine
**Context of use:** Used to describe imitations or deceptions that are so convincing that authenticity cannot easily be determined. Common in everyday and commercial discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** 真命天子
**Transliteration:** zhēnmìng tiānzǐ

**Natural English translation:** the true Son of Heaven; the rightful emperor; the destined ruler
**Context of use:** A historical and literary expression referring to the legitimate ruler believed to hold the Mandate of Heaven. Used in historical narrative and, colloquially, to refer humorously to one's destined partner.

**36.**

**37.**

**Literal breakdown:** true-true + false-false (reduplication)
**Natural English translation:** a mixture of true and false; partly real, partly fake; ambiguous
**Context of use:** Used to describe a situation or account that is a confusing mixture of genuine and fabricated elements, making it difficult to determine what is real. Common in everyday and narrative discourse.

**38.**

**Context of use:** Colloquial expression describing a tendency to insist on exactness or to take things too literally. Can be used approvingly (conscientious) or disapprovingly (pedantic). Common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**39.**
**Form:** 真话
**Transliteration:** zhēnhuà
**Literal breakdown:** true + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, a true statement, honest words
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that accurately reflects reality or what the speaker actually believes or knows. Appears in the contrast 真话假话 (true words and false words) and in expressions like 说真话 (to tell the truth). Common in everyday and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 2: 实 (shí)

*This root is highly productive. The items below represent the most common compounds and expressions; additional forms exist.*

---

**1.**
**实际**
**Transliteration:** shíjì
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + border/boundary/occasion
**Natural English translation:** actual, practical, in practice, reality
**Context of use:** Contrasts what is actually the case with what is theoretical, ideal, or claimed. Extremely common across all registers: 实际情况 (the actual situation), 脱离实际 (divorced from reality), 实际上 (in practice, actually).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 实际上
**Transliteration:** shíjìshàng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + border/occasion + on/above
**Natural English translation:** in fact, actually, in practice, in reality
**Context of use:** An adverbial phrase used to introduce a correction or clarification, asserting what is actually the case as opposed to what appears or is claimed. One of the most common discourse markers in Chinese across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 现实
**Transliteration:** xiànshí
**Literal breakdown:** present/manifest + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** reality, the real world; realistic, pragmatic
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of the world as it currently exists, often contrasted with ideals, wishes, or fiction. Also used as an adjective meaning pragmatic or realistic: 面对现实 (to face reality), 现实主义 (realism).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 实现
**Transliteration:** shíxiàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to appear/manifest
**Natural English translation:** to realize, to achieve, to bring about, to actualize
**Context of use:** Describes the process of making something actual or concrete that was previously only potential or planned. Common in goal-oriented, political, and everyday discourse: 实现梦想 (to realize a dream), 实现目标 (to achieve a goal).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 实施
**Transliteration:** shíshī
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to carry out/implement
**Natural English translation:** to implement, to put into effect, to enforce
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and policy contexts to describe the putting into practice of a law, plan, or measure: 实施法律 (to implement a law), 实施方案 (to carry out a plan).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 实践
**Transliteration:** shíjiàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to tread/practice
**Natural English translation:** practice, to practice, to put into practice
**Context of use:** Refers to the application of knowledge or principles in action, as opposed to theory. Central to Marxist epistemology in Chinese political discourse (实践是检验真理的唯一标准, practice is the sole criterion for testing truth) and to everyday usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 实验
**Transliteration:** shíyàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to test/verify
**Natural English translation:** experiment, to experiment, experimental
**Context of use:** The standard term for scientific experiment or experimental procedure. Also used more broadly for any trial or test of a hypothesis or method: 做实验 (to conduct an experiment), 实验室 (laboratory).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 实证
**Transliteration:** shízhèng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + evidence/proof
**Natural English translation:** empirical evidence, empirical proof; positivism (in philosophy)
**Context of use:** Used in scientific and philosophical discourse to refer to evidence grounded in observation and fact. 实证主义 is the standard translation of positivism. Also used in legal contexts for concrete proof. Central to discussions of empiricism and evidence-based reasoning: 实证研究 (empirical research).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 实情
**Transliteration:** shíqíng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + situation/circumstances
**Natural English translation:** the true situation, the actual facts, the real state of affairs
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual circumstances of a matter, especially when these may differ from what has been reported or claimed. Common in legal, investigative, and everyday contexts: 了解实情 (to understand the true situation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 实话
**Transliteration:** shíhuà
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, truthful words, honest statement, straight talk
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that accurately reflects what the speaker knows or believes, as opposed to polite evasions or lies. Appears in the common expression 实话实说 (to tell it like it is, to speak the truth plainly) and 说实话 (to tell the truth).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 实话实说
**Transliteration:** shí huà shí shuō
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + speech + solid/real + to speak
**Natural English translation:** to tell it like it is, to speak plainly and honestly, to say it straight
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to introduce or request a frank, unvarnished account. Common in everyday conversation and also the name of a well-known Chinese television talk show, which extended its currency.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 实力
**Transliteration:** shílì
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + strength/power
**Natural English translation:** actual strength, real capability, substantive power
**Context of use:** Refers to the genuine, demonstrated capacity of a person, team, or state, as opposed to nominal rank or reputation. Common in sports, military, political, and business discourse: 实力派 (a person of genuine ability), 综合实力 (overall strength), 展示实力 (to demonstrate real strength).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 务实
**Transliteration:** wùshí
**Literal breakdown:** to attend to/engage with + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth, results-oriented
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person, policy, or approach as focused on concrete realities and achievable outcomes rather than ideology or abstraction. Common in political and administrative discourse: 务实的态度 (a pragmatic attitude), 务实外交 (pragmatic diplomacy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 扎实
**Transliteration:** zhāshi / zhāshí
**Literal breakdown:** to plant/root + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** solid, thorough, well-grounded, substantial
**Context of use:** Evaluates knowledge, skills, or work as genuinely thorough and well-founded rather than superficial. Common in educational and professional contexts: 基础扎实 (a solid foundation), 工作扎实 (thorough, solid work), 扎实推进 (to advance steadily and thoroughly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---
