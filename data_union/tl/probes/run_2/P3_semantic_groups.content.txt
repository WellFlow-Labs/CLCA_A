# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of usage, contrast, collocation, and register distribution observed across P1 and P2. They are not imposed categories. Where items appear in multiple groups, this is noted explicitly. Where items do not fit cleanly into any group, they are listed separately at the end. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data rather than a predetermined structure.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement corresponds to what is actually the case

**Definition:** These expressions are used primarily in propositional contexts — to assert, deny, or query whether a claim, statement, or reported event is factually accurate. They appear in direct predication over propositions (*Totoo ba iyan?* — "Is that true?"), in formal denials (*Hindi totoo iyan*), and in discourse-initial corrections (*Ang totoo...*). The core contrast in this group is between asserting correspondence with reality and denying it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *totoo* — the primary predicate for asserting that a statement is true or a situation is real
- *katotohanan* — the abstract noun for truth; used in formal declarations and moral exhortations (*Sabihin ang katotohanan*)
- *hindi totoo* — the standard negation formula for denying a claim
- *Totoo ba iyan?* — the standard interrogative formula for querying truth
- *Ang totoo...* / *Ang totoo nito...* — discourse-initial formulas for introducing a corrective statement
- *totoo naman* — concessive acknowledgment in dialogue ("it's true, though")
- *totoo na* — marks that something previously doubted has been confirmed or has come to pass
- *Kung totoo man...* — conditional-concessive formula in argumentation
- *walang katotohanan* — formula for characterizing a claim as entirely without factual basis
- *may katotohanan* — formula for conceding partial truth in a claim
- *sa katotohanan* — grammaticalized discourse connector: "in truth / actually"
- *totoong kwento* — "true story"; characterizes a narrative as based on actual events
- *totoong pangyayari* — "real event"; used in journalistic and testimonial contexts
- *totoo ang lahat* — "it's all true"; used in confessional or testimonial contexts

**Notes:** *Katotohanan* also appears in Group 4 (abstract nominals used in moral and philosophical discourse). *Sa katotohanan* overlaps with Group 7 (discourse connectors). *Totoo na* and *totoo naman* are pragmatically specialized and could be considered a sub-pattern within this group rather than fully independent items.

## Group 2: Expressions used to evaluate whether something is genuine rather than imitation or false

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate objects, persons, documents, or relationships as authentically what they claim or appear to be, as opposed to being counterfeit, forged, or imitation. The central contrast is between *tunay* / *orihinal* / *awtentiko* on one side and *huwad* / *peke* on the other. This group is strongly associated with commercial, legal, and relational contexts rather than purely propositional ones.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tunay* — evaluates persons, objects, or relationships as genuine rather than false or imitation (*tunay na ginto* — "real gold")
- *orihinal* — evaluates something as the genuine source item rather than a copy (*orihinal na kopya*, *orihinal na obra*)
- *awtentiko* — formal/legal evaluation of a document or item as genuinely what it purports to be (*awtentikong dokumento*)
- *huwad* — characterizes something as falsely presenting itself as genuine; more formal/legal (*huwad na dokumento*, *huwad na pagkakakilanlan*)
- *peke* — colloquial term for counterfeit or inauthentic; applied to objects and persons (*pekeng produkto*, *Peke iyan*, *Peke ka*)
- *tunay na nangyari* — "what really happened"; asserts the authentic account of events
- *tunay na mukha* — metaphorical: a person's genuine character as opposed to a performed persona
- *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "document authentication"; the formal process of verifying a document's genuineness
- *awtentikong dokumento* — fixed legal formula for a genuine document

**Notes:** *Tunay* also appears in Group 3 (relational authenticity) and Group 5 (verification and proof). *Huwad* and *peke* are near-synonyms in meaning but differ sharply in register: *huwad* is formal/literary, *peke* is colloquial. *Awtentiko* and *orihinal* are both formal borrowings but *orihinal* foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction while *awtentiko* foregrounds the genuine-versus-fraudulent distinction.

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate the depth or authenticity of persons and relationships

**Definition:** These expressions apply *tunay* and related forms to evaluate whether a person, relationship, or personal quality is genuinely what it appears to be — a real friend, true love, genuine character. They appear in relational, moral, and literary discourse rather than in legal or commercial contexts. The contrast is between authentic relational bonds or personal qualities and superficial, performed, or false ones.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tunay na kaibigan* — "true/genuine friend"; the most common and stable collocation of *tunay* in relational discourse
- *tunay na pagmamahal* — "true/genuine love"; common in literature, song, and everyday discourse
- *tunay na pagkatao* — "true character / genuine humanity"
- *tunay na bayani* — "true hero"; evaluates whether a person genuinely merits a social designation
- *tunay na Pilipino* — "true/genuine Filipino"; used in identity and nationalist discourse
- *tunay na mukha* — "true face"; metaphorical reference to a person's hidden genuine nature
- *tapat* — evaluates persons or statements as honest and without concealment
- *katapatan* — abstract noun covering honesty in speech and faithfulness/loyalty in relationships
- *katapatan sa pangako* — "faithfulness to a promise"
- *katapatan sa bansa* — "loyalty to the nation"
- *tapat na puso* — "honest/sincere heart"; fixed formula in moral and religious discourse
- *totoo sa sarili* — "true to oneself"; evaluates whether a person acts in accordance with their genuine values
- *Peke ka* — colloquial accusation of personal inauthenticity or insincerity

**Notes:** *Tapat* and *katapatan* are distinctive within this group because they cover both the epistemic dimension (honesty in speech) and the relational-moral dimension (faithfulness, loyalty) simultaneously. *Tunay na Pilipino* is a specialized use in identity discourse. *Totoo sa sarili* extends the *totoo* root into this domain. *Peke ka* is the colloquial counterpart to the more formal evaluations of inauthenticity in this group.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate correctness of statements, answers, or actions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, answer, judgment, or action is correct — that is, whether it conforms to what is right, accurate, or proper. The central contrast is between *tama* / *wasto* (correct, right, proper) and *mali* (wrong, incorrect). This group is strongly associated with educational, evaluative, and procedural contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tama* — the primary predicate for correctness of answers, judgments, and actions (*Tama ang sagot* — "The answer is correct")
- *Tama ka* — "You are right/correct"; standard formula for affirming another's correctness
- *Tama ba?* — standard interrogative for seeking confirmation of correctness
- *Tama na* — pragmatically specialized: "That's enough / Stop" (marks sufficiency rather than correctness)
- *tama nga* — emphatic confirmation: "that's actually correct / exactly right"
- *nang tama* — adverbial: "correctly, in the right way"
- *wasto* — evaluates correctness with emphasis on propriety and conformity to standard (*wastong gamit*, *wastong pag-uugali*)
- *kawastuhan* — abstract noun: "correctness, propriety"
- *nang wasto* — adverbial: "correctly, properly"
- *wastong gamit* — "correct/proper usage"; fixed formula in language instruction
- *wastong pag-uugali* — "proper behavior"; fixed formula in moral and educational discourse
- *mali* — the primary predicate for incorrectness or wrongness (*Mali ang datos*, *Mali ka*)
- *Mali ka* — "You are wrong/mistaken"; standard formula for asserting another's incorrectness
- *kamalian* — abstract noun: "error, mistake, fault"
- *pagkakamali* — nominal: a specific instance of making a mistake
- *magkamali* / *nagkamali* — verb: to make a mistake, to err
- *maling akala* — "mistaken belief / misconception"; fixed formula
- *maling impormasyon* — "wrong information / misinformation"
- *maling paniniwala* — "false belief / mistaken conviction"
- *maling sabi* — "wrong statement / misstatement"
- *maling datos* — "wrong/incorrect data"
- *maling interpretasyon* — "misinterpretation"
- *maling pag-unawa* / *maling pagkaunawa* — "misunderstanding"
- *maling pagpapalagay* — "wrong assumption / false premise"
- *malinaw na mali* — "clearly wrong / manifestly erroneous"; intensified evaluative formula
- *hindi mali* — "not wrong"; used in hedged affirmations or defenses
- *Hindi mali ang...* — "There is nothing wrong with..."; formula for defending an action
- *Aminin ang pagkakamali* — "Admit the mistake"; fixed moral and legal formula
- *itama* — verb: to correct
- *iwasto* — verb: to correct, to rectify
- *pagwawasto* — nominal: the act of correcting
- *Iwasto ang mali* — "Correct the mistake"; fixed imperative formula

**Notes:** *Tama* and *wasto* overlap substantially but *wasto* carries a stronger sense of propriety and conformity to standard, while *tama* is more neutral in its correctness evaluation. *Tama na* is pragmatically specialized (Group note: it marks sufficiency rather than correctness and could be considered a separate item). The *maling [noun]* pattern is highly productive and generates collocations across virtually any domain; the examples listed above are the most stable and attested. *Maling landas* and *maling daan* (wrong path/road) are listed separately in Group 6 because their primary use is metaphorical rather than factual-correctness evaluation.

## Group 5: Expressions used in contexts of proving, verifying, or establishing something as true

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the truth or genuineness of a claim, document, or identity must be actively established through evidence or demonstration. They appear in legal, administrative, investigative, and argumentative discourse. The central activity is the production or evaluation of proof.

**Representative expressions:**

- *katunayan* — "proof, evidence, genuineness"; used both as abstract noun and as discourse connector
- *sa katunayan* — discourse connector: "in fact / as a matter of fact"
- *patunayan* — verb: "to prove, to demonstrate, to verify" (*Patunayan mo* — "Prove it")
- *mapatunayan* — "to be able to prove; to be provable" (*Hindi mapatunayan* — "cannot be proven")
- *napatunayan* — "has been proven / was verified"; completed-aspect form
- *pinatunayan* — "proved / demonstrated"; completed-aspect actor-focus form
- *pagpapatunay* — nominal: "the act of verifying / authenticating / proving"
- *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "document authentication"; fixed technical formula
- *pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan* — "identity verification"; fixed technical formula
- *katunayan ng pagkakakilanlan* — "proof of identity"
- *katunayan ng pagmamay-ari* — "proof of ownership"
- *katunayan sa hukuman* — "evidence in court"
- *walang katunayan* — "without proof / unproven"
- *may katunayan* — "with proof / supported by evidence"
- *pruweba* — "proof, evidence" (from Spanish *prueba*); used in legal and everyday contexts
- *Walang pruweba* — "There is no proof"; standard formula
- *May pruweba ka ba?* — "Do you have proof?"; standard demand for evidence
- *pruweba ng pagkakasala* — "proof of guilt"
- *ebidensya* — "evidence"; specifically legal-procedural in connotation
- *Walang ebidensya* — "There is no evidence"; standard legal formula
- *ebidensyang pisikal* — "physical evidence"; fixed legal term
- *ebidensyang sirkumstansyal* — "circumstantial evidence"; fixed legal term

**Notes:** *Katunayan* and *pruweba* overlap substantially in meaning but *katunayan* is morphologically native (from *tunay*) and also functions as a discourse connector (*sa katunayan*), while *pruweba* is a Spanish borrowing with no discourse-connector use. *Ebidensya* is more narrowly legal-procedural than *pruweba*. The verbal cluster around *patunayan* (*mapatunayan*, *napatunayan*, *pinatunayan*) is distinctive in this group as the only productive verbal paradigm for the act of proving. *Sa katunayan* also appears in Group 7 (discourse connectors).

## Group 6: Expressions used in moral evaluation of conduct and character

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons, actions, or conduct as morally right or wrong, honest or deceptive, faithful or disloyal. They appear in moral instruction, religious discourse, civic education, and interpersonal evaluation. The evaluations in this group concern character and conduct rather than the truth-value of propositions or the genuineness of objects.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tapat* — "honest, sincere, straightforward, loyal"; evaluates persons or statements as without concealment
- *katapatan* — abstract noun: "honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty"
- *Maging tapat ka sa akin* — "Be honest with me"; standard formula for requesting honesty
- *tapat na sagot* — "honest answer"
- *tapat na puso* — "honest/sincere heart"; fixed formula in moral and religious discourse
- *katapatan sa pangako* — "faithfulness to a promise"
- *katapatan sa bansa* — "loyalty to the nation"
- *sinungaling* — "liar / lying"; standard term for a person who lies (*Sinungaling ka* — "You are a liar")
- *kasinungalingan* — abstract noun: "lie, falsehood, untruth"
- *pagsisinungaling* — nominal: "the act of lying"
- *puno ng kasinungalingan* — "full of lies/falsehood"; fixed intensified accusation
- *Huwag kang maniwala sa kasinungalingan* — "Don't believe the lie"; fixed moral/religious formula
- *maling landas* / *maling daan* — "wrong path / wrong road"; fixed metaphorical formulas for moral misdirection
- *maling desisyon* — "wrong decision"; evaluates a decision as morally or practically harmful
- *maling paggamit* — "misuse / wrong use"; evaluates improper use of something
- *pagtatago ng katotohanan* — "concealment of the truth"; characterizes withholding of truth as a moral failing
- *pagsasabi ng katotohanan* — "telling the truth"; the act of truth-telling as a moral duty
- *totoo sa sarili* — "true to oneself"; evaluates personal authenticity as a moral value
- *Aminin ang pagkakamali* — "Admit the mistake"; moral and legal formula for acknowledging wrongdoing

**Notes:** *Tapat* and *katapatan* are central to this group because they cover both honesty in speech and faithfulness in relationships. *Sinungaling* and *kasinungalingan* are the primary terms for the deception/lying pole. *Maling landas* and *maling daan* are included here rather than in Group 4 because their primary attested use is metaphorical moral evaluation rather than factual incorrectness. *Pagtatago ng katotohanan* and *pagsasabi ng katotohanan* bridge Groups 1 and 6 because they involve both propositional truth and moral conduct.

## Group 7: Expressions functioning as discourse connectors or pragmatic markers

**Definition:** These expressions have grammaticalized or semi-grammaticalized uses as sentence-initial or clause-initial markers that introduce corrections, clarifications, concessions, or emphatic assertions. Their pragmatic function in discourse is distinct from — though related to — the base meaning of the forms from which they derive.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sa katotohanan* — "in truth / in reality / actually"; introduces a corrective or clarifying statement
- *sa katunayan* — "in fact / as a matter of fact"; introduces a factual clarification or emphasis
- *Ang totoo...* — "The truth is... / What is really true is..."; discourse-initial corrective formula
- *Ang totoo, hindi ko alam* — "The truth is, I don't know"; honesty-signaling formula before an admission
- *totoo naman* — "it's true, though / well, that's actually true"; concessive acknowledgment in dialogue
- *totoo na* — marks that something previously doubted has been confirmed or has come to pass
- *Tama na* — "That's enough / Stop"; marks sufficiency; pragmatically distinct from correctness-predication
- *Tumpak!* — standalone exclamation: "Exactly! / Precisely!"; discourse response marker
- *tama nga* — "that's actually correct / exactly right"; emphatic confirmation particle
- *Kung totoo man...* — "Even if it were true..."; conditional-concessive formula in argumentation

**Notes:** This group is not a semantic category in the same sense as the others; it is a functional category based on the grammaticalized or pragmatically specialized uses of forms that also appear in other groups. *Sa katotohanan* and *sa katunayan* are the most clearly grammaticalized items. *Tama na* is the most semantically divergent from its base form (*tama* = "correct"), as its pragmatic meaning (sufficiency/cessation) is not directly predictable from "correct." *Tumpak!* as a standalone exclamation is a response token rather than a predication.

## Group 8: Expressions used in legal and administrative contexts for establishing or challenging formal status

**Definition:** These expressions appear primarily in legal, administrative, and institutional discourse to evaluate whether a claim, authority, document, identity, or status is formally valid, rightfully held, or properly established. They are distinguished from other groups by their domain restriction and their association with formal procedures rather than everyday evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**

- *lehitimo* — "legitimate, lawful, rightful" (*lehitimong pamahalaan*, *lehitimong anak*, *lehitimong paghahabol*)
- *lehitimasyon* — "legitimation"; the formal process of establishing legitimacy
- *awtentiko* — "authentic"; formal evaluation of documents (*awtentikong dokumento*)
- *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "document authentication"
- *pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan* — "identity verification"
- *katunayan ng pagkakakilanlan* — "proof of identity"
- *katunayan ng pagmamay-ari* — "proof of ownership"
- *katunayan sa hukuman* — "evidence in court"
- *ebidensya* — "evidence"; specifically legal-procedural
- *ebidensyang pisikal* — "physical evidence"
- *ebidensyang sirkumstansyal* — "circumstantial evidence"
- *pruweba ng pagkakasala* — "proof of guilt"
- *huwad na dokumento* — "forged/counterfeit document"
- *huwad na pagkakakilanlan* — "false identity"
- *orihinal na kopya* — "original copy (of a document)"
- *kredible* — "credible"; used in legal and journalistic evaluation of witnesses and sources
- *kredibleng saksi* — "credible witness"
- *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* — "reliable/trustworthy witness"

**Notes:** Several items in this group also appear in other groups: *awtentiko* in Group 2, *katunayan* and *pruweba* and *ebidensya* in Group 5, *huwad* in Group 2. The distinguishing feature of this group is the institutional-procedural context rather than a distinct semantic content. *Lehitimo* and *lehitimasyon* are the items most exclusively confined to this group, as they concern formal rightfulness of authority and status rather than factual truth or material genuineness.

## Group 9: Expressions used to evaluate the reliability or trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or information source can be relied upon — whether their claims, testimony, or conduct can be trusted. They appear in contexts of evaluating witnesses, sources, and persons in positions of responsibility. The central concept is *tiwala* (trust, confidence).

**Representative expressions:**

- *tiwala* — "trust, confidence, reliance" (*May tiwala ako sa kanya* — "I trust him/her")
- *mapagkakatiwalaan* — "trustworthy, reliable, dependable"
- *tiwalahin* — verb: "to trust, to rely on"
- *pagtitiwalaan* — "to be trusted; the act of placing trust"
- *kawalan ng tiwala* — "distrust, lack of trust, loss of confidence"
- *Wala akong tiwala sa kanya* — "I don't trust him/her"; standard formula for distrust
- *boto ng walang tiwala* — "vote of no confidence"; fixed political-institutional formula
- *Magtiwala ka sa Diyos* — "Trust in God"; fixed religious formula
- *mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon* — "reliable source of information"
- *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* — "reliable/trustworthy witness"
- *kredible* — "credible"; used in journalistic and legal evaluation
- *kredibleng saksi* — "credible witness"
- *kredibleng pinagkukunan* — "credible source"
- *sigurado* — "sure, certain, reliable" (*Sigurado ka ba?* — "Are you sure?")
- *kasiguruhan* — abstract noun: "certainty, assurance, security"
- *siguraduhin* — verb: "to make sure, to ensure"
- *Sigurado na ako* — "I'm sure now / I've made sure of it"
- *Hindi ako sigurado* — "I'm not sure"; standard formula for epistemic uncertainty

**Notes:** *Kredible* and *mapagkakatiwalaan* overlap in meaning but differ in register: *kredible* is more formal and specifically epistemic (used in journalistic and legal evaluation of sources and witnesses), while *mapagkakatiwalaan* is more broadly applicable and morphologically native. *Sigurado* is somewhat different from the others in this group: it evaluates the speaker's own certainty rather than the reliability of an external person or source, but it appears in the same discourse contexts of epistemic evaluation and is closely associated with the trust/reliability cluster in practice.

## Group 10: Expressions used to evaluate accuracy of fit or correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something — a description, translation, measurement, or characterization — corresponds accurately to what it is meant to represent or fit. They appear in practical, aesthetic, and evaluative contexts where the question is whether something matches a standard or requirement. The contrast is between *akma* (fitting, matching) and *angkop* (appropriate, suitable), with *tumpak* (precise, exact) at the more emphatic end.

**Representative expressions:**

- *akma* — "fitting, appropriate, accurate (of a match or fit)" (*Akma ang paglalarawan* — "The description is accurate/fitting")
- *iakma* — verb: "to fit, to adjust, to make correspond"
- *Akma ba ito?* — "Does this fit? / Is this accurate?"
- *angkop* — "appropriate, suitable, fitting" (*angkop na pag-uugali* — "appropriate behavior")
- *iaangkop* — verb: "to adapt, to make appropriate"
- *kaangkupan* — abstract noun: "appropriateness, suitability"
- *angkop na pag-uugali* — "appropriate behavior"; fixed formula in moral and educational discourse
- *Angkop ba ito sa okasyon?* — "Is this appropriate for the occasion?"
- *tumpak* — "precise, exact, spot-on" (*Tumpak ang sinabi mo* — "What you said is exactly right")
- *katumpakan* — abstract noun: "precision, exactness, accuracy"
- *Tumpak!* — standalone exclamation: "Exactly! / Precisely!"
- *makatotohanan* — "realistic, true-to-life, verisimilar"; evaluates representations as faithfully corresponding to reality
- *makatotohanang pagkukuwento* — "realistic/truthful storytelling"
- *makatotohanang pagganap* — "realistic performance"
- *totoo sa buhay* — "true to life, realistic, lifelike"

**Notes:** *Akma* and *angkop* are near-synonyms but *akma* tends toward physical or descriptive fit (a size that fits, a description that matches) while *angkop* tends toward normative appropriateness (appropriate to a context or standard). *Tumpak* is stronger than both, implying exactness rather than mere fit or appropriateness. *Makatotohanan* and *totoo sa buhay* are included here because their primary use is evaluating the accuracy of representations (in literature, film, art) rather than asserting propositional truth; they belong to this group more than to Group 1. *Tumpak!* as a standalone exclamation also appears in Group 7 (discourse markers).

## Items That Appear in Multiple Groups

The following items have been placed in more than one group above, and the overlap is noted here for clarity:

- ***tunay*** — Group 2 (genuineness of objects/documents) and Group 3 (authenticity of persons and relationships). The distinction is between *tunay na ginto* (real gold — Group 2) and *tunay na kaibigan* (true friend — Group 3).

- ***tapat* / *katapatan*** — Group 3 (relational authenticity) and Group 6 (moral evaluation of conduct). The two dimensions — honesty in speech and faithfulness in relationships — are not separated in Tagalog usage; both are covered by the same root.

- ***sa katotohanan*** — Group 1 (truth-assertion) and Group 7 (discourse connectors). The grammaticalized discourse-connector use is distinct from the compositional meaning.

- ***sa katunayan*** — Group 5 (proof and verification) and Group 7 (discourse connectors). Same pattern as *sa katotohanan*.

- ***katunayan*, *pruweba*, *ebidensya*** — Group 5 (proof and verification) and Group 8 (legal and administrative contexts). The legal-administrative uses are a subset of the broader proof/verification cluster.

- ***awtentiko*** — Group 2 (genuineness) and Group 8 (legal and administrative). *Awtentiko* is more narrowly legal-administrative than *tunay*.

- ***huwad*** — Group 2 (genuineness/counterfeit) and Group 8 (legal and administrative). *Huwad* is used primarily in legal contexts.

- ***mapagkakatiwalaang saksi*, *kredibleng saksi*** — Group 8 (legal and administrative) and Group 9 (reliability and trustworthiness). These collocations appear in both legal proceedings and general credibility evaluation.

- ***sigurado*** — Group 9 (reliability and trustworthiness) and potentially Group 1 (truth-assertion). *Sigurado* evaluates the speaker's own certainty rather than the truth of a proposition, but it appears in the same epistemic discourse contexts.

- ***makatotohanan*, *totoo sa buhay*** — Group 1 (truth-assertion) and Group 10 (accuracy of fit/correspondence). These forms use the *totoo* root but their primary use is aesthetic/representational evaluation rather than propositional truth-assertion.

- ***Tumpak!*** — Group 10 (accuracy of fit) and Group 7 (discourse markers). As a standalone exclamation it functions as a response token.

- ***maling landas* / *maling daan*** — Group 4 (correctness evaluation) and Group 6 (moral evaluation). These are placed in Group 6 because their primary attested use is metaphorical moral evaluation.

- ***pagtatago ng katotohanan*, *pagsasabi ng katotohanan*** — Group 1 (truth-assertion) and Group 6 (moral evaluation of conduct). These constructions involve both propositional truth and moral conduct simultaneously.

## Items That Do Not Fit Cleanly into Any Group

***lehitimasyon*** — This term appears in Group 8 (legal and administrative) but its distribution is so narrow (legal documents and academic writing only) and its relationship to the other items in the field is so indirect that it sits at the margin of the grouping. It is a process-noun for a formal legal procedure rather than an evaluative term in the same sense as the others.

***Tama na*** — This construction is listed in Group 4 (correctness evaluation) and Group 7 (discourse markers), but its pragmatic meaning ("That's enough / Stop") is sufficiently divergent from correctness-evaluation that it arguably belongs to neither group cleanly. It is a pragmatic formula for marking sufficiency or cessation, and its connection to the *tama* (correct) root is etymological rather than synchronically transparent in this use.

***katotohanang pansarili*, *katotohanang panlipunan*, *katotohanang ganap*, *katotohanang relatibo*** — These compound forms (marked [MARGINAL] in P1b) appear in philosophical and academic discourse. They do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above because they are used in specialized theoretical discourse about the nature of truth rather than in the everyday, legal, moral, or aesthetic evaluation contexts that characterize the other groups. They are noted here as items that exist in the data but do not cluster with the other expressions in terms of usage context.

***tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan*** — This form (marked [MARGINAL] in P1b) designates a person committed to upholding truth. It appears in moral, religious, and journalistic discourse but is not a common evaluative formula in the way that the other items are. It does not fit cleanly into any group.

***katotohanang-katotohanan*** — This intensified form (marked [MARGINAL] in P1b) appears in rhetorical, religious, or philosophical registers. It is too infrequent and register-restricted to cluster with the other items.

***pagkakaakma*** — Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P2a. Its lexicalization as a stable item is uncertain, and it does not appear in fixed constructions in the P2b data. It is noted here as not fitting cleanly into any group due to insufficient attestation.

***pagka-orihinal*** — Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P2a. Same situation as *pagkakaakma*.

***magpruweba*** — Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P2a. Its status as a lexicalized verbal form is uncertain; it is not attested in fixed constructions in the P2b data.
