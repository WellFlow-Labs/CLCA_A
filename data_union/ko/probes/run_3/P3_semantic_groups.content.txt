# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 Natural Semantic Grouping
## Language: Korean (ko)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms appear in the same collocations, which are contrasted with each other, which share typical discourse contexts, and which participate in similar syntactic frames. No external theoretical framework has been applied. Where items resist grouping or fit multiple groups, this is noted explicitly.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that something is factually the case

**Label:** Factual assertion and denial

**Definition:** These terms are used in contexts where the central question is whether a claim, statement, or situation corresponds to what actually happened or exists. They appear in collocations such as 확인하다 (to verify), 사실이 아니다 (it is not a fact), 사실로 밝혀지다 (to be revealed as fact), and 엄연한 사실 (an undeniable fact). They are the primary vocabulary for asserting, denying, or verifying factual accuracy in journalistic, legal, and everyday discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- 사실 (sasil) — fact, actuality
- 사실이 아니다 (sasili anida) — it is not a fact / that is not true
- 사실을 확인하다 (sasilеul hwagin hada) — to verify the facts
- 사실로 밝혀지다 (sasillo balkhyeojida) — to be revealed as fact
- 엄연한 사실 (eomyeonhan sasil) — an undeniable fact
- 사실 여부를 따지다 (sasil yeobureul ttajida) — to examine whether something is factual
- 사실을 직시하다 (sasilеul jiksihada) — to face the facts squarely
- 사실관계를 파악하다 (sasil gwangyereul paakada) — to grasp the factual circumstances
- 사실상 (sasilsang) — in fact, effectively, de facto

**Notes:** 사실 is the primary term in this group. 진실 (see Group 2) can overlap with 사실 in some contexts, but 사실 is more strongly associated with verifiable factual content, while 진실 is more associated with the act of disclosure or the quality of honesty. 사실상 functions adverbially and is somewhat distinct from the others in this group in that it asserts effective rather than strictly factual truth.

## Group 2: Terms used in contexts of revealing, concealing, or investigating what is true

**Label:** Truth disclosure and investigation

**Definition:** These terms appear primarily in contexts where the central activity is uncovering, hiding, or formally establishing what is actually the case — particularly in journalistic, legal, and political discourse. They cluster around collocations with 밝히다 (to reveal), 규명하다 (to investigate and clarify), 숨기다 (to conceal), 드러나다 (to come to light), and 외면하다 (to turn away from). The emphasis is on the process of bringing truth into the open or preventing it from being known.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진실 (jinsil) — truth, truthfulness
- 진실을 밝히다 (jinsilеul balkhida) — to reveal the truth
- 진실을 숨기다 (jinsilеul sumgida) — to hide the truth
- 진실을 외면하다 (jinsilеul oemyeonhada) — to turn away from the truth
- 진실이 드러나다 (jinsili deureonada) — the truth comes to light
- 진실이 밝혀지다 (jinsili balkhyeojida) — the truth is uncovered
- 진실 규명 (jinsil gyumyeong) — truth investigation / truth-finding
- 진상 (jinsang) — the true facts / the real story
- 진상 규명 (jinsang gyumyeong) — investigation of the true facts
- 진실화해위원회 (jinsil hwahae wiwonhoe) — Truth and Reconciliation Commission
- 진실을 왜곡하다 (jinsilеul waegokada) — to distort the truth

**Notes:** 진실 is the central term in this group. 진상 is closely related and appears in nearly identical collocations (진상 규명, 진상을 파악하다), but 진상 tends to appear more specifically in investigative contexts about particular events, while 진실 can be used more broadly. 사실 (Group 1) overlaps here in some collocations (사실을 은폐하다, 사실을 왜곡하다), but the disclosure-investigation frame is more strongly associated with 진실 and 진상.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate whether a person or expression is genuine rather than performed

**Label:** Sincerity and genuineness of persons and expressions

**Definition:** These terms are used when evaluating whether a person's feelings, statements, or conduct are genuinely felt or meant, as opposed to being performed, insincere, or hypocritical. They appear in contexts of character evaluation, apology assessment, and interpersonal judgment. The contrast is not primarily between true and false propositions but between authentic and performed or hollow expression.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진실하다 (jinsil hada) — to be truthful / sincere
- 진실성 (jinsilseong) — truthfulness / sincerity as a quality
- 진심 (jinsim) — sincerity / genuine feeling
- 진심으로 (jinsimеuro) — sincerely / from the heart
- 진지하다 (jinji hada) — to be serious / earnest / sincere
- 진지하게 (jinji hage) — seriously / earnestly
- 진정성 (jinjeonseong) — authenticity / genuineness / sincerity
- 진정성이 있다 / 없다 (jinjeonseong i itda / eopda) — to be authentic / to lack authenticity
- 진정성을 의심하다 (jinjeonseong eul uisimhada) — to doubt the sincerity of
- 진정성을 보여주다 (jinjeonseong eul boyeojuda) — to show sincerity
- 진정성 있는 사과 (jinjeonseong inneun sagwa) — a sincere apology
- 위선 (wiseon) — hypocrisy / false virtue
- 위선자 (wiseonja) — hypocrite
- 위선적 (wiseonjeok) — hypocritical

**Notes:** 진정성 is the most prominent term in contemporary usage within this group, particularly in public and political discourse. 위선 and its derivatives function as the negative pole of this group — they describe the failure of genuineness in the specific form of false virtue. 진심 and 진지하다 are more interpersonal and emotional in register, while 진정성 is more evaluative and public-facing. 진실하다 overlaps with Group 2 but is included here because its primary usage is in character evaluation rather than propositional truth.

## Group 4: Terms used to distinguish genuine items from imitations or fakes

**Label:** Authenticity of objects and items

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether a physical object, document, artwork, or product is what it claims to be — the original or genuine article — as opposed to a copy, imitation, or forgery. They appear in commercial, legal, and art-authentication contexts. The contrast is between 진짜/진품 (genuine) and 가짜/위조품/모조품 (fake/forged/imitation).

**Representative expressions:**
- 진짜 (jinjja) — real / genuine / authentic
- 진짜배기 (jinjjabaegi) — the real deal / the genuine article
- 가짜 (gajja) — fake / counterfeit / imitation
- 가짜 뉴스 (gajja nyuseu) — fake news
- 진짜인지 가짜인지 모르다 (jinjjainji gajjainji moreuda) — not knowing whether it is real or fake
- 진짜와 가짜를 구별하다 (jinjja wa gajjareul gubyeolhada) — to distinguish the genuine from the fake
- 가짜로 판명되다 (gajjaro panmyeong doeda) — to be determined to be fake
- 진품 (jinpum) — genuine article / authentic item
- 진본 (jinbon) — authentic original (of a document or text)
- 위조 (wijo) — forgery / counterfeiting
- 위조하다 (wijo hada) — to forge / to counterfeit
- 위조품 (wijopum) — forged item / counterfeit
- 위조 문서 (wijo munseo) — forged document
- 위조 화폐 (wijo hwapye) — counterfeit currency
- 위조 여부를 감정하다 (wijo yeobureul gamjeong hada) — to appraise whether something is forged
- 위조로 판명되다 (wijoro panmyeong doeda) — to be determined to be a forgery
- 위작 (wijak) — forgery of an artwork / spurious work
- 모조 (mojo) — imitation / replica
- 모조품 (mojopum) — imitation product / replica
- 진품과 모조품을 구별하다 (jinpum gwa mojopumeul gubyeolhada) — to distinguish genuine articles from imitations
- 진위 (jinwi) — truth or falsehood / authenticity
- 진위를 가리다 (jinwireul garida) — to determine truth or falsehood / to establish authenticity
- 진위 여부 (jinwi yeobu) — the question of authenticity
- 진위 논란 (jinwi nollan) — controversy over authenticity

**Notes:** This group has a clear internal distinction: 위조 implies deliberate deceptive intent (forgery, counterfeiting), while 모조 does not necessarily imply intent to deceive (replica, imitation). 진짜/가짜 are the colloquial poles of this group and are used for both objects and claims. 진위 is the formal term that frames the question of authenticity without presupposing an answer. 가짜 뉴스 extends the fake/genuine contrast from objects to information content.

## Group 5: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts to assert or deny that something has force or effect

**Label:** Legal validity and invalidity

**Definition:** These terms appear primarily in legal, administrative, and contractual contexts where the question is whether a document, contract, action, or declaration has legal force or is operative. They are used in collocations with 계약 (contract), 선거 (election), 기간 (period), and verbs such as 선언하다 (to declare), 되다 (to become), and 처리되다 (to be processed). The central contrast is between 유효 (valid, in force) and 무효 (invalid, void).

**Representative expressions:**
- 유효 (yuhyo) — validity / effectiveness / being in force
- 유효하다 (yuhyo hada) — to be valid / to be in force
- 유효성 (yuhyoseong) — validity as a property
- 유효 기간 (yuhyo gigan) — validity period / expiration period
- 유효 기간이 지나다 (yuhyo gigani jinaeda) — for the validity period to expire
- 여전히 유효하다 (yeojeonhi yuhyohada) — to still be valid / to remain in force
- 계약이 유효하다 (gyeyagi yuhyohada) — the contract is valid
- 무효 (muhyo) — invalidity / nullity / void
- 무효화하다 (muhyohwa hada) — to invalidate / to nullify
- 무효를 선언하다 (muhyoreul seoneonhada) — to declare invalid
- 계약을 무효로 하다 (gyeyageul muhyoro hada) — to render a contract void
- 선거가 무효가 되다 (seongeoga muhyoga doeda) — an election becomes void
- 무효 처리되다 (muhyo cheoridoeda) — to be processed as invalid
- 무효 선언 (muhyo seoneon) — declaration of invalidity

**Notes:** 합법 / 불법 (legal / illegal) are related but distinct: they evaluate conformity to law rather than operative force. A contract can be 합법적 (lawful) but still 무효 (void) on procedural grounds, and vice versa. 타당 (valid/reasonable) overlaps with this group in academic and policy contexts but is not primarily a legal-force term. These distinctions are supported by the different collocational environments observed in P1 and P2.

## Group 6: Terms used to evaluate whether an action or claim is lawful

**Label:** Lawfulness and illegality

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether an action, practice, or state of affairs conforms to law. They appear in collocations with 취득하다 (to acquire), 절차 (procedure), 경계 (boundary), and in constructions such as 합법과 불법의 경계 (the boundary between legal and illegal). The central contrast is between 합법 (lawful) and 불법 (unlawful).

**Representative expressions:**
- 합법 (hapbeop) — legality / lawfulness
- 합법적 (hapbeopjeok) — legal / lawful
- 합법성 (hapbeopseong) — legality as a property
- 합법화하다 (hapbeophwa hada) — to legalize
- 합법화 (hapbeophwa) — legalization
- 합법적으로 취득하다 (hapbeopjeogеuro chwideukada) — to acquire legally
- 합법과 불법의 경계 (hapbeop gwa bulbeop ui gyeonggye) — the boundary between legal and illegal
- 합법을 가장하다 (hapbeobeul gajanghada) — to disguise as legal
- 합법성 여부를 따지다 (hapbeopseong yeobureul ttajida) — to examine the question of legality
- 불법 (bulbeop) — illegality / unlawfulness
- 실정법 (siljeongbeop) — positive law / enacted law

**Notes:** 합법 / 불법 evaluate conformity to existing law, while 정당 (Group 7) evaluates whether something is morally or procedurally justified — these can come apart (something can be 합법적 but 부당, or 불법 but 정당하다 in some ethical arguments). 실정법 is included here because it refers to law as it actually exists, which is the standard against which 합법 / 불법 are measured.

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate whether an action or claim is justified or rightful

**Label:** Justification and legitimacy

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether an action, authority, or claim is rightful, justified, or appropriate — not merely whether it is legal, but whether it has proper grounds. They appear in collocations with 이유 (reason), 절차 (procedure), 권한 (authority), and in constructions such as 정당성을 확보하다 (to secure legitimacy) and 정당한 절차를 밟다 (to follow due process). The central evaluative term is 정당, and its antonym 부당 marks the failure of justification.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정당 (jeongdang) — legitimacy / justness / rightfulness
- 정당하다 (jeongdang hada) — to be legitimate / just / rightful
- 정당성 (jeongdangseong) — legitimacy as a property
- 정당화하다 (jeongdanghwahada) — to justify
- 정당한 (jeongdanghan) — legitimate / just (as a modifier)
- 정당성을 확보하다 (jeongdangseong eul hwakbo hada) — to secure legitimacy
- 정당성을 잃다 (jeongdangseong eul ilta) — to lose legitimacy
- 정당한 절차를 밟다 (jeongdanghan jeolchareul balda) — to follow due process
- 폭력을 정당화하다 (poknyeogeul jeongdanghwahada) — to justify violence
- 정당 방위 (jeongdang bangwi) — self-defense / justifiable defense
- 정당 방위를 주장하다 (jeongdang bangwireul jujang hada) — to claim self-defense
- 부당 (budang) — impropriety / unjustness
- 부당하다 (budang hada) — to be unjust / improper
- 타당 (tadang) — validity / reasonableness / appropriateness
- 타당하다 (tadang hada) — to be valid / reasonable
- 타당성 (tadangseong) — validity / reasonableness as a property
- 타당한 근거를 제시하다 (tadanghan geungeoreul jesihada) — to present valid grounds
- 근거 (geungeo) — basis / grounds / evidence
- 근거가 있다 / 없다 (geungeoga itda / eopda) — to be well-founded / unfounded
- 근거를 제시하다 (geungeoreul jesihada) — to present grounds

**Notes:** 정당 and 타당 are related but distinguishable in usage: 정당 is more strongly associated with moral and political legitimacy (authority, rights, self-defense), while 타당 is more strongly associated with logical and procedural reasonableness (arguments, decisions, feasibility studies). Both contrast with 부당, which serves as the antonym for both in practice. 근거 is included here because it appears in the same evaluative frame (근거가 있다/없다 parallels 타당성이 있다/없다) and is frequently collocated with 타당한.

## Group 8: Terms used to describe the act of misrepresenting or distorting

**Label:** Misrepresentation and distortion

**Definition:** These terms are used when the central concern is that something true or accurate has been altered, bent, or presented in a misleading way. They appear in collocations with 사실 (fact), 진실 (truth), 역사 (history), and in constructions such as 왜곡하다 (to distort), 허위로 작성하다 (to fill out falsely), and 위장하다 (to disguise). The emphasis is on the act or result of making something appear other than it is.

**Representative expressions:**
- 왜곡 (waegok) — distortion / misrepresentation
- 왜곡하다 (waegokada) — to distort
- 왜곡된 (waegokdoen) — distorted (as a modifier)
- 사실을 왜곡하다 (sasilеul waegokada) — to distort the facts
- 진실을 왜곡하다 (jinsilеul waegokada) — to distort the truth
- 허위 (heowi) — falsehood / falsity / misrepresentation
- 허위 진술 (heowi jinsul) — false statement
- 허위 광고 (heowi gwanggo) — false advertising
- 허위 사실 (heowi sasil) — false facts / fabricated information
- 허위 사실 유포 (heowi sasil yupo) — spreading false information
- 허위 신고 (heowi singo) — false report
- 허위로 작성하다 (heowiro jakseong hada) — to fill out falsely
- 허위로 꾸미다 (heowiro kkumida) — to fabricate / to dress up falsely
- 허위임이 드러나다 (heowiimi deureonada) — it is revealed to be false
- 위장 (wijang) — disguise / camouflage / masquerade
- 위장하다 (wijang hada) — to disguise / to masquerade as
- 위장 취업 (wijang chwieop) — disguised employment
- 위장 결혼 (wijang gyeolhon) — sham marriage
- 표절 (pyojeol) — plagiarism / literary theft
- 표절하다 (pyojeolhada) — to plagiarize

**Notes:** 왜곡 implies that something originally true or accurate has been bent or altered — it presupposes a prior truth that has been distorted. 허위 is used for statements or representations that are simply false, without necessarily implying that a true original was altered. 위장 involves concealing one's true identity or nature rather than distorting a factual claim. 표절 is included here because it involves misrepresenting the origin of a work — presenting another's work as one's own — which is a form of misrepresentation, though its primary context (academic and artistic) is distinct from the others.

## Group 9: Terms used to evaluate the credibility or trustworthiness of a source or claim

**Label:** Credibility and trustworthiness assessment

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether a person, institution, source, or claim can be relied upon to be accurate or honest. They appear in scalar collocations (높다/낮다, high/low), in constructions with 의심하다 (to doubt), 확보하다 (to secure), 잃다 (to lose), and 회복하다 (to restore). The central evaluative question is not whether something is true but whether it is the kind of source or claim that warrants belief.

**Representative expressions:**
- 신뢰 (sinloe) — trust / reliability / credibility
- 신뢰하다 (sinloe hada) — to trust / to rely on
- 신뢰성 (sinloeseong) — reliability / trustworthiness
- 신뢰도 (sinloedo) — degree of trust / credibility rating
- 신뢰감 (sinloegam) — sense of trust / feeling of reliability
- 신뢰를 쌓다 (sinloereul ssada) — to build trust
- 신뢰를 잃다 (sinloereul ilta) — to lose trust
- 신뢰를 회복하다 (sinloereul hoebokada) — to restore trust
- 신뢰를 저버리다 (sinloereul jeobeorida) — to betray trust
- 신뢰가 무너지다 (sinloega muneoijida) — trust collapses
- 신뢰 관계를 형성하다 (sinloe gwangyereul hyeongseong hada) — to form a relationship of trust
- 신빙성 (sinbyeongseong) — credibility / reliability / plausibility
- 신빙성이 있다 / 없다 (sinbyeongseong i itda / eopda) — to be credible / to lack credibility
- 신빙성이 높다 / 낮다 (sinbyeongseong i nopda / natda) — to have high / low credibility
- 신빙성을 의심하다 (sinbyeongseong eul uisimhada) — to doubt the credibility of
- 신빙성을 확보하다 (sinbyeongseong eul hwakbo hada) — to secure credibility

**Notes:** 신뢰 and 신빙성 are distinguishable in usage: 신뢰 is used for ongoing relationships of trust (between persons, institutions, or a public and its leaders), while 신빙성 is used more specifically to evaluate whether a particular claim or testimony has sufficient basis to be believed. 신뢰 participates in relational collocations (쌓다, 잃다, 회복하다, 저버리다) that treat trust as something built and maintained over time; 신빙성 participates in evaluative collocations (있다/없다, 높다/낮다) that treat credibility as a property of a specific claim or source at a given moment.

## Group 10: Terms used in contexts of formal error, mistake, or incorrect reasoning

**Label:** Error and incorrectness

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether a claim, judgment, or action is wrong or mistaken — not in the sense of being deliberately false, but in the sense of failing to meet a standard of correctness. They appear in contexts of logical evaluation, academic assessment, and moral judgment. The central contrast is between what is correct (옳다, 올바르다, 정확하다) and what is wrong or mistaken (그르다, 오류, 틀리다).

**Representative expressions:**
- 옳다 (olta) — to be right / correct / just
- 옳은 (oreun) — right / correct (as a modifier)
- 옳고 그름 (olko geureum) — right and wrong
- 옳고 그름을 가리다 (olko geureumеul garida) — to distinguish right from wrong
- 옳은 일을 하다 (oreun ireul hada) — to do the right thing
- 그르다 (geureuda) — to be wrong / incorrect
- 그른 (geureun) — wrong / incorrect (as a modifier)
- 올바르다 (olbareuda) — to be correct / right / proper
- 올바른 (olbareun) — correct / right / proper (as a modifier)
- 올바르게 (olbareuge) — correctly / rightly / properly
- 올바른 판단을 내리다 (olbareun pandaneul naerida) — to make a correct judgment
- 오류 (oryu) — error / mistake / fallacy
- 오류를 범하다 (oryureul beomhada) — to commit an error / fallacy
- 정확 (jeonghwak) — accuracy / precision
- 정확하다 (jeonghwak hada) — to be accurate / precise
- 정확히 말하면 (jeonghwaki malhamyeon) — to be precise / strictly speaking
- 부정확 (bujeonghwak) — inaccuracy / imprecision
- 부정확하다 (bujeonghwak hada) — to be inaccurate

**Notes:** 옳다 and 올바르다 are closely related but distinguishable: 옳다 is used more in moral and logical judgments (this action is right, this claim is correct), while 올바르다 is used more in evaluations of conduct, procedure, and direction (the right path, the correct approach). 그르다 is the direct antonym of 옳다 but is notably more formal and literary in contemporary usage. 정확 and 오류 are more technical and are used in contexts of measurement, data, and reasoning rather than moral evaluation. 부정확 is the negation of 정확 and appears in the same technical contexts.

## Group 11: Terms used to refer to the actual or underlying state of affairs, as opposed to appearance or representation

**Label:** Actual state versus appearance

**Definition:** These terms are used when the contrast is between what something truly is or what is actually the case, and how it appears, is represented, or is claimed to be. They appear in collocations with 파악하다 (to grasp), 드러내다 (to reveal), 다르다 (to differ), and in constructions such as 실제와 다르다 (differs from reality) and 실체를 드러내다 (to reveal one's true nature). The emphasis is on the gap between surface and underlying reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실제 (silje) — reality / actuality
- 실제로 (siljero) — actually / in reality / in practice
- 실상 (silsang) — the actual state of affairs / the real situation
- 실태 (siltae) — actual state / real condition
- 실태 조사 (siltae josa) — survey of actual conditions
- 실질 (siljil) — substance / substantive reality / actual content
- 실질적 (siljiljeok) — substantive / real in effect
- 실체 (silche) — substance / entity / true nature
- 실체를 드러내다 (silchereul deureonaeda) — to reveal one's true nature
- 실체를 파악하다 (silchereul paakada) — to grasp the true nature of
- 실체가 없다 (silchega eopda) — to have no substance / to be insubstantial
- 실체적 진실 (silchejeok jinsil) — substantive truth / material truth
- 본질 (bonjil) — essence / essential nature
- 본질을 파악하다 (bonjileul paakada) — to grasp the essence
- 본질을 흐리다 (bonjileul heurida) — to obscure the essence
- 본질에서 벗어나다 (bonjile seo beoseonada) — to deviate from the essential point
- 문제의 본질 (munje ui bonjil) — the essence of the problem
- 진의 (jinui) — true intention / real meaning
- 진가 (jinga) — true value / real worth
- 진면목 (jinmyeonmok) — true nature / real character

**Notes:** 실제 / 실제로 are the most general terms in this group, used across all registers to contrast what is actually the case with what is claimed, imagined, or theoretical. 실상 and 실태 are more specific to investigative and journalistic contexts where the actual condition of something is being examined. 실체 is used when the underlying reality of something is being revealed or questioned, often with a critical tone. 본질 is used when the fundamental or essential nature of something is at issue, often in philosophical or analytical contexts. 진의, 진가, and 진면목 are more specific to persons and their intentions, worth, or character.

## Group 12: Terms used in contexts of empirical evidence and demonstrable proof

**Label:** Empirical verification and evidence

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether a claim is supported by concrete, observable evidence rather than speculation, theory, or assertion. They appear in academic, scientific, and legal contexts, in collocations with 연구 (research), 조사 (investigation), and in constructions such as 실증하다 (to demonstrate empirically) and 근거에 기반하다 (to be based on grounds). The emphasis is on the requirement that claims be grounded in demonstrable evidence.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실증 (siljeung) — empirical verification / positive proof
- 실증하다 (siljeunghada) — to demonstrate empirically
- 실증적 (siljeungjeok) — empirical / evidence-based
- 실증주의 (siljeungjuui) — positivism
- 근거 (geungeo) — basis / grounds / evidence
- 근거가 있다 / 없다 (geungeoga itda / eopda) — to be well-founded / unfounded
- 근거 없는 주장 (geungeo eomneun jujang) — unsubstantiated claim
- 근거를 제시하다 (geungeoreul jesihada) — to present grounds / evidence
- 신빙성 (sinbyeongseong) — credibility / plausibility (overlapping with Group 9)

**Notes:** This group is smaller and more specialized than others. 실증 and its derivatives are primarily academic and philosophical terms. 근거 is more broadly used across academic, legal, and everyday registers. 신빙성 overlaps with Group 9 (credibility assessment) but is included here because in legal and academic contexts it specifically concerns whether a claim has sufficient evidentiary basis — a concern shared with 실증 and 근거.

## Group 13: Terms used in contexts of legal proceedings involving false statements or false identity

**Label:** Legal falsification and false testimony

**Definition:** These terms appear specifically in legal contexts where the act of making false statements, creating false documents, or assuming false identities is at issue. They are associated with specific criminal offenses and legal proceedings. The shared context is the legal system's treatment of deliberate falsehood as a punishable act.

**Representative expressions:**
- 위증 (wijeung) — perjury / false testimony
- 위증하다 (wijeung hada) — to commit perjury
- 위증죄 (wijeungjoe) — the crime of perjury
- 위조죄 (wijojoe) — the crime of forgery
- 위조범 (wijobom) — forger / counterfeiter (as a person)
- 허위 진술 (heowi jinsul) — false statement
- 허위 사실 유포 (heowi sasil yupo) — spreading false information
- 허위 신고 (heowi singo) — false report
- 위명 (wimyeong) — false name / assumed name (in a deceptive context)
- 위장 취업 (wijang chwieop) — disguised employment
- 위장 결혼 (wijang gyeolhon) — sham marriage
- 허위 의식 (heowi uisik) — false consciousness (note: this is a theoretical term, not a legal one — see below)

**Notes:** 허위 의식 (false consciousness) is included in P2 but does not belong to this legal group; it is a term from Marxist critical theory and operates in a different discourse entirely. It is listed here only to flag that it shares the 허위 form with the legal terms but should not be grouped with them on the basis of usage. The legal terms in this group are distinguished from Group 8 (misrepresentation and distortion) by their specific association with criminal or civil legal proceedings and named offenses.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 present genuine difficulty in grouping and are noted here with the relevant ambiguity described.

---

**공정 (gongjeon) — fairness / impartiality / justice**

공정 appears in P1 but does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. Its usage is primarily in evaluating whether a process, judgment, or representation is unbiased — a concern that is related to but distinct from truth, accuracy, legitimacy, and credibility. It appears in journalistic and legal discourse (공정한 보도, fair reporting; 공정한 재판, fair trial) but the central evaluative question is impartiality rather than truth or validity. It could be placed adjacent to Group 7 (justification and legitimacy) or Group 9 (credibility), but its collocational environment is sufficiently distinct that forcing it into either group would misrepresent its usage. It is left ungrouped here.

---

**유사 (yusa) — similarity / resemblance**

유사 appears in P1 in contexts of comparison and imitation (유사품, similar/imitation product; 유사성, similarity). It is related to Group 4 (authenticity of objects) in that imitation involves resemblance, but 유사 itself does not evaluate genuineness — it describes the degree of resemblance between two things without asserting that one is fake. It is also used in legal contexts (유사품) and in general analytical comparison. It does not fit cleanly into any single group and is left ungrouped.

---

**오류 (oryu) — error / mistake / fallacy**

오류 is placed in Group 10 (error and incorrectness) but has a distinct sub-usage in formal logic (논리적 오류, logical fallacy) that is more closely related to Group 12 (empirical verification and evidence) in academic contexts. The overlap is noted but not resolved by splitting the entry.

---

**사실주의 (sasiljuui) — realism (as an artistic or philosophical movement)**

사실주의 is derived from 사실 (Group 1) but operates in a distinct discourse — literary and artistic criticism — where the question is not factual accuracy but aesthetic fidelity to reality. It does not fit cleanly into Group 1 or any other group and is left ungrouped.

---

**실존주의 (siljonjuui) — existentialism**
**실용주의 (siryongjuui) — pragmatism**
**본질주의 (bonjiljuui) — essentialism**

These three -주의 (doctrine/ism) forms are derived from roots that participate in the truth domain (실, 본질) but operate as names of philosophical movements. Their usage is in academic and intellectual discourse where the question is not truth evaluation but philosophical position. They are left ungrouped.

---

**실력 (sillyeok) — actual ability / real skill**
**실적 (siljeok) — actual results / performance record**
**실용 / 실용적 (siryong / siryongjeok) — practical use / utilitarian**

These 실 (實)-root forms are noted in P1b as peripheral extensions. Their usage is in competence evaluation, performance assessment, and practical value judgment rather than truth evaluation. They are left ungrouped.

---

**진심 (jinsim) / 진심으로 (jinsimеuro)**

These are placed in Group 3 (sincerity and genuineness of persons) but also participate in Group 11 (actual state versus appearance) in the sense that 진심 contrasts genuine feeling with performed or hollow expression. The overlap is noted; the primary placement in Group 3 reflects the dominant collocational environment (apologies, expressions of gratitude, interpersonal discourse).

---

**거짓말도 방편이다 (geojitmaldo bangpyeonida)**

This proverbial expression (even a lie can be a means to an end) does not fit into any of the descriptive groups above, which are organized around evaluation of truth, falsity, accuracy, and legitimacy. This expression is evaluative in a different way — it concerns the moral permissibility of lying under certain circumstances. It is left ungrouped.
