# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)**

**Native script:** א-מ-נ
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-n
**P1 items participating:** 3 — ʾemet (אֱמֶת, item 1), meheymen (מהימן, item 18), ʾamin (אמין, item 19)
**Note:** ʾemet is traditionally assigned to א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t) in some analyses, but the standard scholarly position treats it as a nominal derivative of א-מ-נ with final-nun weakening; both analyses are attested. The root is treated here as א-מ-נ following the majority lexicographic tradition.
**Core meaning contribution:** Firmness, reliability, trust, and faithfulness — the foundational semantic cluster from which Hebrew truth-vocabulary is built. This root generates both epistemic terms (truth, credibility) and relational/moral terms (faithfulness, trust).

---

**Root 2: כ-ו-נ (k-w-n)**

**Native script:** כ-ו-נ
**Transliteration:** k-w-n
**P1 items participating:** 1 — nakhon (נכון, item 3)
**Core meaning contribution:** Being established, set, fixed, correct, or prepared. Contributes the sense of correctness as a state of being properly set or aligned with reality. Highly productive in modern Hebrew across epistemic, procedural, and directional domains.

---

**Root 3: י-ש-ר (y-sh-r)**

**Native script:** י-ש-ר
**Transliteration:** y-sh-r
**P1 items participating:** 2 — yashar (ישר, item 10), yosher (יושר, item 11)
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, uprightness, directness. Generates a cluster of moral and epistemic terms centered on the metaphor of the straight line as the standard of correctness and integrity. Productive across moral, legal, and spatial domains.

---

*Note on selection rationale:* א-מ-נ is selected as the primary root on the basis of its direct participation in the most semantically central P1 items and its exceptional productivity in the truth-evaluation domain. כ-ו-נ is selected because nakhon is among the highest-frequency truth-evaluation terms in modern Hebrew and the root generates a large family of related expressions. י-ש-ר is selected because it participates in two P1 items and generates a substantial family of moral-epistemic expressions. Roots such as צ-ד-ק and ד-י-ק were considered but participate in fewer P1 items and are less central to the truth-evaluation domain specifically.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

---

**1.**
**Form:** אמת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root א-מ-נ (with final-nun weakening to tav in the standard analysis)
**Natural English translation:** truth, reality, genuineness
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 1; included here as the base form for the expansion. The most central Hebrew truth-term, used across all registers and periods.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** אמת אמת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + ʾemet (reduplication for emphasis)
**Natural English translation:** truly, in absolute truth, it is really true
**Context of use:** Used in rabbinic literature and traditional religious discourse for emphatic affirmation of truth. The doubled form intensifies the assertion beyond the simple ʾemet.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily in traditional/religious registers

---

**3.**
**Form:** באמת
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** be- (preposition "in/with") + ʾemet
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth, honestly
**Context of use:** Extremely common in everyday speech as an adverbial intensifier or sincerity marker. Used to affirm that a statement reflects genuine belief or reality ("באמת, לא ידעתי" — "I really didn't know").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** באמת ובתמים
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemet u-ve-tamim
**Morpheme breakdown:** be-ʾemet ("in truth") + u- ("and") + be-tamim ("in wholeness/integrity")
**Natural English translation:** in truth and integrity, honestly and wholeheartedly
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in formal, legal, and religious contexts to affirm that an action or statement is made with both truthfulness and complete sincerity. Appears in oaths and formal declarations.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — formal/religious register

---

**5.**
**Form:** אמת לאמיתה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet le-ʾamittah
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + le- ("to/of") + ʾamittah (feminine form of ʾamitti, "its truth")
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter, the real truth, truth in its genuine sense
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in rabbinic and philosophical discourse to distinguish genuine or essential truth from a superficial or approximate version. Also used in modern Hebrew to mean "the real truth" or "strictly speaking."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** חותמו של הקב"ה אמת
**Transliteration:** ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh hu ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** ḥotam ("seal") + shel ("of") + ha-qadosh barukh hu ("the Holy One, blessed be He") + ʾemet
**Natural English translation:** The seal of the Holy One, blessed be He, is truth
**Context of use:** A well-known rabbinic dictum (Talmud Bavli, Shabbat 55a) asserting that truth is the defining attribute of the divine. Widely cited in religious and ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — religious/traditional register, but widely known

---

**7.**
**Form:** אמת המידה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ha-middah
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + ha-middah ("the measure/standard")
**Natural English translation:** the standard, the benchmark, the true measure
**Context of use:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse to refer to the genuine standard or criterion by which something is judged. Common in journalistic and intellectual writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** אמת ושלום
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-shalom
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + ve- ("and") + shalom ("peace")
**Natural English translation:** truth and peace
**Context of use:** A biblical and rabbinic paired value (Zechariah 8:16, 8:19), used in religious and ethical discourse to describe the dual foundations of a just society. Also appears in the names of institutions and publications.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily religious/traditional register

---

**9.**
**Form:** אמת ויציב
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-yatsiv
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + ve- + yatsiv ("stable, established")
**Natural English translation:** true and established, true and certain
**Context of use:** The opening words of a liturgical passage in the morning prayer service (Shaḥarit), affirming the truth and stability of the preceding declaration of faith. Used in religious contexts; the phrase is also cited in discussions of the relationship between truth and certainty.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — liturgical/religious register

---

**10.**
**Form:** אמת בפיו
**Transliteration:** ʾemet be-fiv
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + be- ("in") + fiv ("his mouth")
**Natural English translation:** truth in his mouth, he speaks truthfully
**Context of use:** A biblical and literary expression (cf. 1 Kings 17:24) used to characterize a person as one whose words are true. Used in literary and religious contexts to evaluate a speaker's truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — literary/biblical register

---

**11.**
**Form:** אמיתי
**Transliteration:** ʾamitī
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + adjectival suffix -ī
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 2; included here as a key derivative. The standard modern Hebrew adjective for "true/genuine."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** אמיתיות
**Transliteration:** ʾamitiyut
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾamitī + abstract noun suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, realness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or real. Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something possesses true or authentic character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** אמן
**Transliteration:** ʾamen
**Morpheme breakdown:** Direct form of root א-מ-נ; affirmation particle
**Natural English translation:** amen, so be it, truly
**Context of use:** Used in religious liturgy and prayer as a response affirming the truth of a statement or blessing. Also used colloquially in modern Hebrew to express agreement or affirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** אמונה
**Transliteration:** ʾemunah
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root א-מ-נ with feminine suffix -ah
**Natural English translation:** faith, belief, trust, faithfulness
**Context of use:** One of the most important terms in Hebrew religious and moral vocabulary, denoting both religious faith and interpersonal trust or faithfulness. Used across biblical, rabbinic, and modern contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** אמונה תמימה
**Transliteration:** ʾemunah temimah
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemunah + temimah ("whole, complete, unblemished")
**Natural English translation:** wholehearted faith, complete trust, naive belief
**Context of use:** Used to describe unquestioning religious faith or, in secular contexts, naive or uncritical trust. The phrase can be used approvingly (sincere faith) or critically (blind credulity) depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** באמונה
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemunah
**Morpheme breakdown:** be- + ʾemunah
**Natural English translation:** faithfully, in good faith, reliably, trustworthily
**Context of use:** Used as an adverbial phrase to characterize action performed faithfully or in good faith. Common in legal and contractual contexts ("לפעול באמונה," "to act in good faith") and in everyday moral evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** אי-אמון
**Transliteration:** ʾi-ʾamon
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾi- (negation prefix) + ʾamon (trust, confidence)
**Natural English translation:** distrust, no-confidence, lack of trust
**Context of use:** Used in political and institutional contexts, most prominently in the phrase הצבעת אי-אמון (hatsbaʿat ʾi-ʾamon, "vote of no confidence"). Also used in interpersonal contexts to describe a breakdown of trust.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** הצבעת אי-אמון
**Transliteration:** hatsbaʿat ʾi-ʾamon
**Morpheme breakdown:** hatsbaʿah ("vote") + ʾi-ʾamon ("no-confidence")
**Natural English translation:** vote of no confidence
**Context of use:** A fixed political and parliamentary term denoting a formal vote to withdraw confidence from a government or official. Standard in Israeli political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** אמון
**Transliteration:** ʾamon
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** trust, confidence, reliance
**Context of use:** Used in interpersonal, institutional, and political contexts to denote trust or confidence placed in a person or institution. Distinct from ʾemunah in being more secular and relational in modern usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** בניית אמון
**Transliteration:** binyan ʾamon
**Morpheme breakdown:** binyan ("building") + ʾamon
**Natural English translation:** trust-building, confidence-building
**Context of use:** Used in diplomatic, organizational, and interpersonal contexts to describe processes aimed at establishing or restoring trust between parties. Common in political and management discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** אמנה
**Transliteration:** ʾamanah
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root א-מ-נ with feminine suffix
**Natural English translation:** covenant, agreement, charter, compact
**Context of use:** Used in formal and legal contexts to denote a binding agreement or charter. In modern Hebrew it appears in names of formal agreements and in biblical contexts where it denotes a covenant or solemn commitment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** אמן
**Transliteration:** ʾomman
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root א-מ-נ (via the sense of skilled/trained)
**Natural English translation:** craftsman, artisan, artist
**Context of use:** Used to denote a skilled craftsman or artist. The connection to the root א-מ-נ is through the sense of trained reliability and mastery. Used in everyday and artistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** אמנות
**Transliteration:** omanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾomman + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** art, craft, artistry
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew word for "art" in all its forms. Used across aesthetic, educational, and cultural contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** אמנותי
**Transliteration:** omanutī
**Morpheme breakdown:** omanut + adjectival suffix -ī
**Natural English translation:** artistic, of art
**Context of use:** Adjective used to describe things pertaining to art or having artistic quality. Common in cultural journalism, education, and criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** מהימן
**Transliteration:** meheymen
**Morpheme breakdown:** Hifʿil participial form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** reliable, credible, trustworthy
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 18; included here as a key derivative. Used to evaluate persons, sources, and information as trustworthy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** מהימנות
**Transliteration:** meheymanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** meheymen + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** reliability, credibility, trustworthiness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being reliable or credible. Standard in legal, journalistic, and scientific contexts when evaluating sources, witnesses, or data.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** אמין
**Transliteration:** ʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** Adjectival form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 19; included here as a key derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** אמינות
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾamin + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** reliability, credibility, trustworthiness
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew abstract noun for reliability or credibility. Used in journalism, law, science, and everyday evaluation of persons and information.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** אמן
**Transliteration:** ʾaman
**Morpheme breakdown:** Qal verb form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** to trust, to believe in, to have faith in
**Context of use:** The base verbal form, used in biblical and literary Hebrew to express trust or belief in a person or proposition. In modern Hebrew the Hifʿil form (heʾemin) is more common in this sense.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily biblical/literary register

---

**30.**
**Form:** האמין
**Transliteration:** heʾemin
**Morpheme breakdown:** Hifʿil verb form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** to believe, to trust, to have faith in
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew verb for "to believe" (a person, a claim, or a proposition). Used across all registers. "אני מאמין לך" ("I believe you") is a common everyday expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** מאמין
**Transliteration:** maʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** Hifʿil active participle of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** believer, one who believes/trusts
**Context of use:** Used as both a verbal participle ("I believe") and a noun ("a believer," especially a religious believer). Common in religious and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Form:** אי-אמינות
**Transliteration:** ʾi-ʾaminut
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾi- (negation) + ʾaminut
**Natural English translation:** unreliability, lack of credibility
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate persons, sources, or systems as unreliable or lacking credibility. Common in journalistic and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**
**Form:** נאמן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nifʿal participial form of root א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy, reliable
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate persons as faithful or loyal — to a person, institution, or principle. Also used in legal contexts (נאמן, neʾeman, "trustee"). Common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** נאמנות
**Transliteration:** neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity, trustworthiness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting loyalty or faithfulness. Used in interpersonal, political, and legal contexts. In legal contexts it can denote the fiduciary relationship of a trustee.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** נאמן לעצמו
**Transliteration:** neʾeman le-ʿatsmo
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman + le- ("to") + ʿatsmo ("himself/itself")
**Natural English translation:** true to oneself, faithful to oneself
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, philosophical, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether a person acts in accordance with their own values, character, or commitments. A common evaluative phrase in personal and artistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**36.**
**Form:** נאמן לעובדות
**Transliteration:** neʾeman la-ʿuvdot
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman + la- ("to the") + ʿuvdot ("facts")
**Natural English translation:** faithful to the facts, factually accurate
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, historical, and legal contexts to evaluate whether an account or representation accurately reflects the facts. A standard phrase in evaluating documentary or historical work.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**37.**
**Form:** נאמן לטקסט
**Transliteration:** neʾeman la-teqst
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman + la- + teqst ("text")
**Natural English translation:** faithful to the text, textually accurate
**Context of use:** Used in literary, translation, and interpretive contexts to evaluate whether a reading, translation, or adaptation accurately reflects the source text.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**38.**
**Form:** נאמן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nifʿal participial form of root א-מ-נ; used as a noun
**Natural English translation:** trustee, fiduciary
**Context of use:** In legal and financial contexts, neʾeman denotes a trustee or fiduciary — a person entrusted with managing assets or interests on behalf of another. Standard legal terminology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**39.**
**Form:** נאמנות
**Transliteration:** neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman + -ut
**Natural English translation:** trust (legal), fiduciary relationship, trusteeship
**Context of use:** In legal contexts, neʾemanut denotes a trust or fiduciary arrangement. Standard in Israeli property and financial law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**40.**
**Form:** אמת-ספר
**Transliteration:** ʾemet-sefer
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet + sefer ("book")
**Natural English translation:** book truth, literal/textual truth
**Context of use:** Used colloquially and in literary criticism to refer to truth as it appears in books or texts, sometimes contrasted with lived reality. Not a fixed technical term but a recognized collocation.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

*Note: The root א-מ-נ is exceptionally productive. The items above represent the most common and well-attested derivatives. Additional forms exist, including אמנם (ʾomnam, "indeed, truly"), אמנם כן (ʾomnam ken, "indeed so"), and various verbal conjugations of האמין and נאמן, which are excluded here as inflected forms.*

### Root כ-ו-נ (k-w-n)

---

**1.**
**Form:** נכון
**Transliteration:** nakhon
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nifʿal participial form of root כ-ו-נ
**Natural English translation:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 3; included here as the base form for the expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** נכונות
**Transliteration:** nekhonot
**Morpheme breakdown:** nakhon + abstract suffix -ut (with vowel shift)
**Natural English translation:** correctness, accuracy, rightness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being correct or accurate. Used in educational, scientific, and legal contexts to evaluate the degree to which a statement or procedure is correct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** לא נכון
**Transliteration:** lo nakhon
**Morpheme breakdown:** lo ("not") + nakhon
**Natural English translation:** incorrect, wrong, not right
**Context of use:** The standard everyday negation of nakhon, used to evaluate statements, answers, or actions as incorrect. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** נכון לציין
**Transliteration:** nakhon letsayen
**Morpheme breakdown:** nakhon + le- + tsayen ("to note, mark")
**Natural English translation:** it is correct/appropriate to note, it should be noted
**Context of use:** A fixed discourse marker used in written and spoken Hebrew to introduce a relevant qualification or additional point. Common in journalistic and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** נכון להיום
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-hayom
**Morpheme breakdown:** nakhon + le- + hayom ("today")
**Natural English translation:** as of today, current as of today, correct as of the present date
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and journalistic contexts to specify that information is accurate as of the current date. A standard qualifier in documents and reports.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** נכון לתאריך
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-taʾarikh
**Morpheme breakdown:** nakhon + le- + taʾarikh ("date")
**Natural English translation:** correct as of [date], valid as of [date]
**Context of use:** Used in legal, financial, and administrative documents to specify the date as of which information is accurate or valid. A standard documentary formula.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** כיוון
**Transliteration:** kivvun
**Morpheme breakdown:** Piʿel nominal form of root כ-ו-נ
**Natural English translation:** direction, orientation
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew word for "direction" (spatial or figurative). Used in navigation, organizational, and everyday contexts. The connection to the root is through the sense of being set or aimed toward a point.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** כיוון שגוי
**Transliteration:** kivvun shaguy
**Morpheme breakdown:** kivvun + shaguy ("erroneous, wrong")
**Natural English translation:** wrong direction, mistaken course
**Context of use:** Used figuratively to evaluate a course of action, argument, or policy as heading in the wrong direction. Common in political and organizational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** כיוון נכון
**Transliteration:** kivvun nakhon
**Morpheme breakdown:** kivvun + nakhon
**Natural English translation:** right direction, correct course
**Context of use:** Used figuratively to evaluate a course of action or argument as heading in the right direction. Common in political, organizational, and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** כוונה
**Transliteration:** kavvanah
**Morpheme breakdown:** Nominal form of root כ-ו-נ with feminine suffix
**Natural English translation:** intention, intent, purpose, kavvanah (in religious sense)
**Context of use:** Used in legal contexts to evaluate whether an act was intentional (כוונה פלילית, kavvanah plilit, "criminal intent") and in religious contexts to denote the directed intention or concentration required for prayer and ritual acts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** בכוונה
**Transliteration:** be-khavvanah
**Morpheme breakdown:** be- + kavvanah
**Natural English translation:** intentionally, deliberately, on purpose
**Context of use:** Standard adverbial phrase used to evaluate whether an action was performed deliberately. Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts. Contrasts with בשוגג (bi-shogeg, "inadvertently").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** בכוונה תחילה
**Transliteration:** be-khavvanah teḥillah
**Morpheme breakdown:** be-khavvanah + teḥillah ("from the beginning, premeditated")
**Natural English translation:** with premeditation, with prior intent, deliberately from the outset
**Context of use:** A legal term of art denoting premeditated intent, used in criminal law to distinguish premeditated acts from those committed in the heat of the moment. Standard in Israeli criminal law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ללא כוונה
**Transliteration:** lelo khavvanah
**Morpheme breakdown:** lelo ("without") + kavvanah
**Natural English translation:** without intent, unintentionally
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to evaluate whether an act lacked deliberate intent. Standard in legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** כוונת מכוון
**Transliteration:** kavvanat mekhuvvan
**Morpheme breakdown:** kavvanah + mekhuvvan (Puʿal participle of כ-ו-נ, "aimed, directed")
**Natural English translation:** deliberate intent, purposeful intention
**Context of use:** A reinforced expression used in legal and moral discourse to emphasize that an act was fully intentional and directed. Somewhat formal.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — formal/legal register

---

**15.**
**Form:** מכוון
**Transliteration:** mekhuvvan
**Morpheme breakdown:** Puʿal participial form of root כ-ו-נ
**Natural English translation:** aimed, directed, intentional, deliberate
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective to evaluate whether something is aimed at a specific target or done deliberately. Used in both literal (aimed weapon) and figurative (deliberate act) senses.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** מכוון לכך
**Transliteration:** mekhuvvan le-khakh
**Morpheme breakdown:** mekhuvvan + le- + khakh ("to that")
**Natural English translation:** aimed at that, directed toward that, intended for that purpose
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether an action, statement, or policy is specifically directed toward a particular goal or target. Common in legal and analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** כינון
**Transliteration:** kinnun
**Morpheme breakdown:** Piʿel verbal noun of root כ-ו-נ
**Natural English translation:** establishment, founding, setting up, constituting
**Context of use:** Used in formal and legal contexts to denote the act of establishing or constituting something (e.g., כינון ממשלה, kinnun memshalah, "formation of a government"; כינון חוקה, kinnun ḥuqqah, "establishment of a constitution"). Standard in political and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** הכנה
**Transliteration:** hakhanah

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots identified above in contexts where the contribution of the root appears to shift away from its core truth-evaluation function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**Root א-מ-נ:**

- **אמנות** (omanut, "art") and **אמן** (ʾomman, "craftsman/artist"): The root appears in the domain of aesthetic production and skilled craft, where the evaluative dimension concerns mastery and skill rather than truth or reliability.

- **אמן** (ʾamen): The root appears as a liturgical affirmation particle, where its function is performative rather than descriptive or evaluative.

- **אמנה** (ʾamanah, "covenant/charter"): The root appears in the domain of formal binding agreements, where the evaluative dimension concerns commitment and obligation rather than epistemic truth.

- **האמין** (heʾemin, "to believe"): The root appears in contexts of religious faith and personal trust, where the evaluative dimension concerns commitment and relationship rather than factual accuracy.

- **אמינות** (ʾaminut) and **מהימנות** (meheymanut): These terms appear in contexts of technical system reliability (e.g., software, infrastructure), where the evaluative dimension concerns functional dependability rather than truthfulness of statements.

- **נאמן** (neʾeman, "trustee"): The root appears in legal-financial contexts of fiduciary duty, where the evaluative dimension concerns legal obligation and role rather than epistemic reliability.

---

**Root כ-ו-נ:**

- **כיוון** (kivvun, "direction"): The root appears in spatial and organizational contexts of orientation and navigation, where the evaluative dimension concerns alignment with a target rather than correctness of a proposition.

- **הכנה** (hakhanah, "preparation") and **מוכן** (mukhan, "ready"): The root appears in contexts of readiness and preparation, where the evaluative dimension concerns a state of being set or prepared rather than correctness or truth.

- **תיקון עולם** (tiqqun ʿolam): The root (via the related ת-ק-נ) appears in contexts of cosmic or social repair, where the evaluative dimension concerns rectification of a broken state rather than epistemic correctness.

- **כוונה** (kavvanah) in religious contexts: The root appears in the domain of directed spiritual intention during prayer and ritual, where the evaluative dimension concerns the quality of inner orientation rather than the truth of a statement.

- **כן** (ken, "yes"): The root appears as a basic affirmation particle, where its function is pragmatic rather than evaluative.

---

**Root י-ש-ר:**

- **ישר כוח** (yishar koaḥ): The root appears in a conventional congratulatory expression, where the evaluative dimension concerns achievement and appreciation rather than moral uprightness or truth.

- **ליישר קו** (leyasher qav, "to align"): The root appears in organizational and military contexts of procedural alignment, where the evaluative dimension concerns coordination and consistency rather than moral integrity.

- **ישר מן הכתף** (yashar min ha-katef, "straight from the shoulder"): The root appears in the domain of communication style evaluation, where the dimension concerns directness and bluntness rather than moral uprightness.

- **ישרי לב** (yishrey lev) and related liturgical expressions: The root appears in contexts of inner spiritual character evaluation, where the dimension concerns the quality of the heart rather than the correctness of propositions.

- **ישר-אל** (yisraʾel): The root appears (via folk etymology) in a proper name, where any evaluative function is entirely absent from current usage.

- **ישרת דרך** and **ישר הולך**: The root appears in metaphors of moral conduct as physical movement along a path, where the spatial metaphor of straightness is applied to the domain of ethical behavior over time.
