# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + [benar / salah / sah / palsu / asli / jujur / keliru / bohong / tepat / akurat / adil / wajar / pantas / nyata / sesuai] (predicate adjective, no copula required)

### Examples
- *Kabar itu benar.* → N/A → "That news is true."
- *Pernikahan mereka sah.* → N/A → "Their marriage is valid."

### Notes
Indonesian has no obligatory copula; truth-domain adjectives function directly as predicates. Negation is pre-predicate *tidak*: *Kabar itu tidak benar.* The interrogative enclitic *-kah* attaches to the predicate: *Benarkah kabar itu?* ("Is that news really true?").

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *tidak* + [truth-domain adjective]

### Examples
- *Informasi itu tidak akurat.* → N/A → "That information is inaccurate."
- *Tindakan itu tidak sah menurut hukum yang berlaku.* → N/A → "That action is not valid under applicable law."

### Notes
*Tidak* is the standard negator for adjectives and stative predicates. The form *bukan* is used to negate noun predicates (*Ini bukan produk asli* — "This is not a genuine product"). The two negators are not interchangeable: *tidak benar* negates the property; *bukan kebenaran* negates the noun.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
[Noun] + *yang* + [truth-domain adjective] (postnominal relative clause); or [truth-domain adjective] + [Noun] (prenominal, restricted set)

### Examples
- *Data yang akurat sangat diperlukan untuk pengambilan keputusan.* → N/A → "Accurate data is essential for decision-making."
- *Alasan yang sah harus disertakan dalam permohonan.* → N/A → "A valid reason must be included in the application."

### Notes
The canonical position for adjectival modification in Indonesian is postnominal, linked by the relativizer *yang*. A small set of collocations permits prenominal order without *yang* (*berita palsu*, *uang palsu*, *hadis sahih*), but these are lexicalized collocations rather than a productive prenominal pattern. The *yang*-construction is fully productive across all truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*menjadi* + [truth-domain adjective/noun]; or *terbukti* / *dinyatakan* + [adjective] (resultative passives functioning inchoatively)

### Examples
- *Dusta yang diulang-ulang bisa menjadi kebenaran yang diterima.* → N/A → "A lie repeated many times can become an accepted truth."
- *Kontrak itu dinyatakan sah oleh pengadilan.* → N/A → "The contract was declared valid by the court."

### Notes
Indonesian has no dedicated inchoative morphology for this domain. Change-of-state is expressed periphrastically with *menjadi* ("to become") or through resultative passives (*dinyatakan* — "declared/pronounced"; *terbukti* — "proven/established"). The resultative passives are more frequent in legal and institutional registers than *menjadi* constructions.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
*meN-* + [base] + *-kan* (causative-applicative suffix); produces transitive verbs meaning "to cause/make [something] [adjective]" or "to confirm/validate/falsify [something]"

### Examples
- *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu.* → N/A → "The authorities confirmed that report."
- *Ia memalsukan tanda tangan pada dokumen itu.* → N/A → "He forged the signature on that document."

### Notes
The *meN-...-kan* construction is the primary causative pattern in Indonesian and is productive across truth-domain bases: *membenarkan* (to confirm/justify), *mengesahkan* (to validate/ratify), *memalsukan* (to falsify/forge), *mengelirukan* (to mislead), *menyatakan* (to declare/state). The corresponding passives (*di-...-kan*) are equally frequent in formal and legal registers: *dibenarkan*, *disahkan*, *dipalsukan*.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ke-* + [base] + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer); produces abstract nouns denoting the property or state

### Examples
- *Mencari kebenaran adalah tugas jurnalis.* → N/A → "Seeking the truth is the journalist's task."
- *Keabsahan kontrak itu masih dipertanyakan.* → N/A → "The validity of that contract is still being questioned."

### Notes
The *ke-...-an* circumfix is the primary abstract nominalization strategy for this domain and is productive across nearly all truth-domain bases: *kebenaran* (truth), *kesalahan* (error/fault), *keaslian* (authenticity), *keabsahan* (validity), *kejujuran* (honesty), *kebohongan* (falsehood), *kenyataan* (reality), *keadilan* (justice), *kesesuaian* (conformity), *kewajaran* (reasonableness), *kepantasan* (propriety), *keakuratan* (accuracy), *kekeliruan* (error). A second nominalization pattern, *peN-...-an*, produces process/result nouns: *pembenaran* (justification), *pemalsuan* (forgery/falsification), *pembuktian* (proving/proof), *pengesahan* (ratification), *pernyataan* (statement/declaration).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*peN-* + [base] (agentive nominalizer); produces nouns denoting a person who characteristically performs the action

### Examples
- *Ia dikenal sebagai pembohong ulung.* → N/A → "He is known as a skilled liar."
- *Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi.* → N/A → "The currency counterfeiter was finally arrested by police."

### Notes
Agentive nominalization is productive for the deception/falsification side of the domain: *pembohong* (liar, from *bohong*), *pendusta* (liar, more formal/literary, from *dusta*), *pemalsu* (forger/counterfeiter, from *palsu*), *peniru* (imitator, from *tiru*). The truth-telling side is less productive agentively: no established agentive *pembenar* (one who confirms) is in common use; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*orang yang jujur* — "an honest person"; *saksi yang terpercaya* — "a trustworthy witness"). *Pendusta* is restricted to formal, literary, and Islamic religious registers; *pembohong* is the everyday term.

---

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION (PROVING/ESTABLISHING)

### Pattern
*peN-* + [base] + *-an*; produces nouns denoting the act or process of establishing, validating, or demonstrating

### Examples
- *Beban pembuktian ada pada pihak penuntut.* → N/A → "The burden of proof lies with the prosecuting party."
- *Pengesahan undang-undang itu memakan waktu berbulan-bulan.* → N/A → "The ratification of that law took many months."

### Notes
This pattern is distinct from abstract nominalization (§6) in that it denotes a process or institutional act rather than an abstract property. Key forms: *pembuktian* (proving/proof, from *bukti*), *pengesahan* (ratification/validation, from *sah*), *pembenaran* (justification/confirmation, from *benar*), *pemalsuan* (falsification/forgery, from *palsu*), *peniruan* (imitation/copying, from *tiru*), *penyesuaian* (adjustment/adaptation, from *sesuai*). The legal formula *pembuktian terbalik* (reversal of the burden of proof) is a lexicalized compound within this pattern.

---

## 9. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Sentence-initial or clause-initial fixed phrase signaling the speaker's limited or qualified commitment to the following claim

### Examples
- *Kalau saya tidak salah, pertemuan itu dijadwalkan hari Kamis.* → N/A → "If I am not mistaken, the meeting is scheduled for Thursday."
- *Sejauh yang saya tahu, hal itu benar adanya.* → N/A → "As far as I know, that is indeed the case."

### Notes
Indonesian has a productive set of clause-initial epistemic hedges built from truth-domain vocabulary: *kalau saya tidak salah* / *jika saya tidak keliru* (if I am not mistaken — the latter more formal), *sejauh yang saya tahu* (as far as I know), *jujur saja* / *untuk jujur* (honestly/to be honest). These are not derivational forms but fixed multi-word constructions that manage the speaker's epistemic stance. They are extremely frequent in spoken Indonesian and in written registers approximating spoken discourse.

---

## 10. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
*memang benar bahwa* / *ada benarnya* / *benar, tapi* + contrasting clause; partial acknowledgment of a truth claim followed by qualification

### Examples
- *Memang benar bahwa angka kemiskinan menurun, tetapi ketimpangan masih tinggi.* → N/A → "It is indeed true that poverty rates have declined, but inequality remains high."
- *Ada benarnya pendapat itu, meski tidak sepenuhnya tepat.* → N/A → "There is something to that view, though it is not entirely accurate."

### Notes
Concessive truth constructions are a productive pattern in Indonesian argumentation and journalism. The scale runs from full concession (*memang benar bahwa* — "it is indeed true that") through partial concession (*ada benarnya* — "there is some truth to it"; *pada dasarnya benar* — "essentially correct") to minimal concession (*benar, tapi* — "true, but"). All involve *benar* or its derivatives as the conceded element.

---

## 11. DOMAIN-RESTRICTING VALIDITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
*sah* / *absah* / *valid* + *secara* + [domain noun] or *menurut* + [authority/standard noun]

### Examples
- *Pernikahan mereka sah secara hukum maupun agama.* → N/A → "Their marriage is valid both legally and religiously."
- *Keputusan itu sah secara prosedural tetapi dipertanyakan secara moral.* → N/A → "The decision is procedurally valid but morally questionable."

### Notes
Indonesian validity vocabulary regularly appears with domain-restricting adverbials (*secara hukum* — legally; *secara agama* — religiously; *secara prosedural* — procedurally; *menurut undang-undang* — under statute; *menurut agama* — according to religion). This pattern reflects the Indonesian legal context in which civil, religious, and procedural validity are treated as distinct and potentially separable dimensions. The construction is specific to the *sah/absah/valid* cluster and does not extend to *benar* or *tepat*.
