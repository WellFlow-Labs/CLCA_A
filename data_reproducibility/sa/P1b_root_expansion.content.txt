# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: √सत् / √sat (√as "to be")
**Native script:** सत्  
**Transliteration:** sat  
**P1 items:** 2 direct (satya, asatya)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Being, existence, reality. This root contributes the fundamental notion of "what is" or "what exists," forming the basis for truth as correspondence to being/reality.

### Root 2: √ऋ / √ṛ (√ṛ "to go, move, fit")
**Native script:** ऋत  
**Transliteration:** ṛta  
**P1 items:** 2 direct (ṛta, anṛta)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Proper order, fitting arrangement, cosmic/moral rightness. This root contributes the notion of correctness as proper arrangement or fitting into natural/moral order.

### Root 3: √मा / √mā ("to measure")
**Native script:** मा  
**Transliteration:** mā  
**P1 items:** 3 direct (pramāṇa, pramā, prāmāṇya)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Measurement, standard, valid cognition. This root contributes the epistemological dimension of truth as measurable, verifiable knowledge obtained through valid means.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: √सत् / sat (√as "to be")

1. **सत्त्व** / *sattva*
   - Literal: sat (being) + -tva (abstract suffix)
   - Translation: being, existence, essence; goodness, purity (in Sāṃkhya)
   - Context: Used philosophically for existence or essential nature; in Sāṃkhya philosophy denotes one of three guṇas (qualities) characterized by lightness, purity, and goodness. Common in both metaphysical and ethical discourse.
   - [CORE]

2. **सत्त्वत** / *sattvata*
   - Literal: sattva + -ta (abstract suffix)
   - Translation: existence, being, reality
   - Context: Abstract noun denoting the state of existence or reality. Used in philosophical texts to discuss ontological status.
   - [CORE]

3. **सत्कार** / *satkāra*
   - Literal: sat (good, true) + kāra (making, doing)
   - Translation: honor, respect, hospitality, good treatment
   - Context: Used for respectful treatment or hospitality shown to guests, teachers, or worthy persons. Common in social and religious contexts.
   - [CORE]

4. **सत्कृत** / *satkṛta*
   - Literal: sat (well, good) + kṛta (done, made)
   - Translation: honored, respected, well-treated
   - Context: Past participle describing someone who has been honored or treated well. Used in narrative and descriptive contexts.
   - [CORE]

5. **सत्पुरुष** / *satpuruṣa*
   - Literal: sat (good, virtuous) + puruṣa (man, person)
   - Translation: good person, virtuous man, gentleman
   - Context: Designates a morally upright or virtuous person. Common in ethical discourse and character evaluation.
   - [CORE]

6. **सज्जन** / *sajjana*
   - Literal: sat (good) + jana (person, people)
   - Translation: good people, virtuous persons, gentlemen
   - Context: Collective term for morally good or respectable people. Frequently used in contrast with durjana (wicked people).
   - [CORE]

7. **सद्गुण** / *sadguṇa*
   - Literal: sat (good) + guṇa (quality, virtue)
   - Translation: good quality, virtue, merit
   - Context: Denotes positive moral qualities or virtues. Used in ethical and character evaluation.
   - [CORE]

8. **सद्गति** / *sadgati*
   - Literal: sat (good) + gati (going, destination)
   - Translation: good destination, salvation, heaven
   - Context: Religious term for favorable rebirth or spiritual liberation. Common in soteriological discourse.
   - [CORE]

9. **सद्भाव** / *sadbhāva*
   - Literal: sat (being, good) + bhāva (state, existence)
   - Translation: existence, reality; goodwill, good disposition
   - Context: Used both for ontological existence and for positive mental disposition or goodwill toward others.
   - [CORE]

10. **सत्संग** / *satsaṅga*
    - Literal: sat (good, true) + saṅga (association, company)
    - Translation: association with the good, company of the virtuous
    - Context: Religious and ethical term for keeping company with spiritually advanced or morally good persons. Central concept in devotional traditions.
    - [CORE]

11. **सत्कर्म** / *satkarma*
    - Literal: sat (good) + karman (action, deed)
    - Translation: good deed, virtuous action
    - Context: Denotes morally meritorious actions. Used in ethical and religious discussions of karma.
    - [CORE]

12. **सत्पथ** / *satpatha*
    - Literal: sat (good, right) + patha (path, way)
    - Translation: right path, correct way
    - Context: Metaphorical expression for morally or spiritually correct course of action. Common in religious instruction.
    - [CORE]

13. **सद्वृत्त** / *sadvṛtta*
    - Literal: sat (good) + vṛtta (conduct, behavior)
    - Translation: good conduct, virtuous behavior
    - Context: Denotes morally proper behavior or character. Used in ethical evaluation and instruction.
    - [CORE]

14. **सद्वचन** / *sadvacana*
    - Literal: sat (good, true) + vacana (speech, word)
    - Translation: true speech, good advice, wise words
    - Context: Used for truthful or beneficial speech. Common in contexts of instruction and counsel.
    - [CORE]

15. **सत्क्रिया** / *satkriyā*
    - Literal: sat (good, proper) + kriyā (action, rite)
    - Translation: proper rite, funeral ceremony, good deed
    - Context: Often specifically refers to funeral rites or obsequies, but also used more broadly for proper ritual action.
    - [CORE]

16. **असत्कार** / *asatkāra*
    - Literal: a- (not) + sat (good) + kāra (making)
    - Translation: dishonor, disrespect, ill-treatment
    - Context: Opposite of satkāra; denotes disrespectful or improper treatment. Used in social and ethical contexts.
    - [CORE]

17. **असत्पुरुष** / *asatpuruṣa*
    - Literal: a- (not) + sat (good) + puruṣa (person)
    - Translation: bad person, wicked man
    - Context: Designates morally corrupt or unvirtuous person. Used in ethical evaluation.
    - [CORE]

18. **असद्भाव** / *asadbhāva*
    - Literal: a- (not) + sat (being/good) + bhāva (state)
    - Translation: non-existence; ill-will, bad disposition
    - Context: Used both for ontological non-existence and for negative mental states or ill-will.
    - [CORE]

19. **सत्त्वगुण** / *sattvaguṇa*
    - Literal: sattva (purity, goodness) + guṇa (quality)
    - Translation: quality of purity/goodness, sattva quality
    - Context: Technical term in Sāṃkhya philosophy for the quality of purity, one of three fundamental qualities of prakṛti.
    - [CORE]

20. **सत्त्वशुद्धि** / *sattvaśuddhi*
    - Literal: sattva (being, essence) + śuddhi (purification)
    - Translation: purification of being/essence, mental purification
    - Context: Used in spiritual contexts for purification of one's essential nature or mental state.
    - [CORE]

21. **सत्यवादिन्** / *satyavādin*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + vādin (speaking, one who speaks)
    - Translation: truth-speaker, truthful person, veracious
    - Context: Designates someone characterized by truthful speech. Used in character evaluation and ethical discourse.
    - [CORE]

22. **सत्यवचन** / *satyavacana*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + vacana (speech, word)
    - Translation: true speech, truthful statement
    - Context: Denotes speech that corresponds to fact or reality. Used in ethical and epistemological contexts.
    - [CORE]

23. **सत्यव्रत** / *satyavrata*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + vrata (vow, observance)
    - Translation: vow of truthfulness, one devoted to truth
    - Context: Religious/ethical term for commitment to truthfulness as spiritual discipline. Used as epithet for truthful persons.
    - [CORE]

24. **सत्यधर्म** / *satyadharma*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + dharma (duty, law)
    - Translation: law of truth, duty of truthfulness
    - Context: Denotes truthfulness as moral/religious duty. Common in dharmaśāstra and ethical texts.
    - [CORE]

25. **सत्यसन्ध** / *satyasandha*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + sandha (keeping, holding)
    - Translation: keeping truth, faithful to promises, truthful
    - Context: Describes someone who keeps their word or maintains truthfulness. Used in character evaluation.
    - [CORE]

26. **सत्यप्रतिज्ञ** / *satyapratijña*
    - Literal: satya (true) + pratijñā (promise, assertion)
    - Translation: true to one's promise, faithful to vows
    - Context: Describes someone who fulfills promises or maintains commitments. Common epithet in epic literature.
    - [CORE]

27. **सत्यार्थ** / *satyārtha*
    - Literal: satya (true) + artha (meaning, object)
    - Translation: true meaning, real purpose, genuine object
    - Context: Used for the actual or true meaning of something, as opposed to apparent or false meaning.
    - [CORE]

28. **सत्यभाषण** / *satyabhāṣaṇa*
    - Literal: satya (truth) + bhāṣaṇa (speaking)
    - Translation: truth-speaking, speaking the truth
    - Context: Denotes the act or practice of speaking truthfully. Used in ethical instruction.
    - [CORE]

29. **सत्यवाक्य** / *satyavākya*
    - Literal: satya (true) + vākya (sentence, statement)
    - Translation: true statement, truthful utterance
    - Context: Denotes a statement that corresponds to fact. Used in epistemological and ethical contexts.
    - [CORE]

30. **असत्यवादिन्** / *asatyavādin*
    - Literal: a- (not) + satya (truth) + vādin (speaking)
    - Translation: liar, untruthful speaker
    - Context: Designates someone characterized by false speech. Used in moral condemnation.
    - [CORE]

### ROOT 2: √ऋत / ṛta (√ṛ "to go, fit")

1. **ऋतज्ञ** / *ṛtajña*
   - Literal: ṛta (cosmic order) + jña (knowing)
   - Translation: knowing the cosmic order, wise
   - Context: Vedic term for one who understands cosmic/moral order. Used as epithet for gods and sages.
   - [CORE]

2. **ऋतावन्** / *ṛtāvan*
   - Literal: ṛta (order, truth) + -van (possessive suffix)
   - Translation: possessing ṛta, truthful, pious
   - Context: Vedic adjective describing gods or persons who embody cosmic order and truth.
   - [CORE]

3. **ऋतायु** / *ṛtāyu*
   - Literal: ṛta (truth, order) + āyu (life, going)
   - Translation: whose life is truth, living according to order
   - Context: Vedic epithet for those whose life follows cosmic/moral order.
   - [MARGINAL]

4. **ऋतचित्** / *ṛtacit*
   - Literal: ṛta (order) + cit (thinking, perceiving)
   - Translation: mindful of order, observing truth
   - Context: Vedic term for one who is conscious of or attentive to cosmic order.
   - [MARGINAL]

5. **ऋतधीति** / *ṛtadhīti*
   - Literal: ṛta (order) + dhīti (thought, devotion)
   - Translation: whose thought is truth, devoted to order
   - Context: Vedic compound describing proper mental orientation toward cosmic order.
   - [MARGINAL]

6. **ऋतव्रध्न** / *ṛtavradhna*
   - Literal: ṛta (order) + vradhna (growing, increasing)
   - Translation: increasing order, promoting truth
   - Context: Vedic epithet for deities who strengthen cosmic order.
   - [MARGINAL]

7. **ऋतसाप्** / *ṛtasāp*
   - Literal: ṛta (order) + sāp (following, serving)
   - Translation: following ṛta, servant of order
   - Context: Vedic term for one who follows or serves cosmic order.
   - [MARGINAL]

8. **ऋतु** / *ṛtu*
   - Literal: from √ṛ (to go, fit); related to ṛta
   - Translation: season, fixed time, proper time
   - Context: Denotes the seasons or any fixed/proper time. Represents temporal order and cyclical regularity in nature.
   - [CORE]

9. **ऋतुकाल** / *ṛtukāla*
   - Literal: ṛtu (season) + kāla (time)
   - Translation: seasonal time, proper season
   - Context: Used for the appropriate time according to seasons. Common in agricultural, ritual, and medical contexts.
   - [CORE]

10. **ऋतुमत्** / *ṛtumat*
    - Literal: ṛtu (season, menstrual period) + -mat (possessive)
    - Translation: in season; menstruating (of women)
    - Context: Used for women in their fertile period. Common in medical and dharmaśāstra texts.
    - [CORE]

11. **ऋतुसंहार** / *ṛtusaṃhāra*
    - Literal: ṛtu (season) + saṃhāra (collection, cycle)
    - Translation: cycle of seasons, collection of seasons
    - Context: Title of Kālidāsa's poem describing the six seasons. Used for seasonal cycles.
    - [CORE]

12. **अनृतवादिन्** / *anṛtavādin*
    - Literal: anṛta (untruth) + vādin (speaking)
    - Translation: speaking untruth, liar
    - Context: Designates one who speaks falsely. Used in ethical condemnation, particularly in Vedic and classical texts.
    - [CORE]

**Note:** The root ṛta/ṛ has relatively limited productivity in classical Sanskrit compared to its prominence in Vedic texts. Most compounds are archaic Vedic forms. The derivative ṛtu (season) has spawned more productive compounding in classical usage.

### ROOT 3: √मा / mā ("to measure")

1. **प्रमाता** / *pramātā*
   - Literal: pra- (forth) + mātṛ (measurer, knower; agent noun from √mā)
   - Translation: knower, cognizer, knowing subject
   - Context: Technical epistemological term for the subject of cognition, one who knows. Central in philosophical discussions of knowledge.
   - [CORE]

2. **प्रमेय** / *prameya*
   - Literal: pra- + √mā + -eya (gerundive suffix: "to be measured")
   - Translation: object of knowledge, knowable, measurable
   - Context: Technical term for the object of valid cognition. Used in epistemology for what can be known through pramāṇas.
   - [CORE]

3. **प्रमाणत्व** / *pramāṇatva*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + -tva (abstract suffix)
   - Translation: state of being a valid means of knowledge, validity
   - Context: Abstract noun denoting the property or status of being a pramāṇa. Used in epistemological analysis.
   - [CORE]

4. **प्रमाणाभास** / *pramāṇābhāsa*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + ābhāsa (appearance, semblance)
   - Translation: semblance of valid knowledge, pseudo-proof, fallacy
   - Context: Technical term in logic for reasoning that appears valid but is actually fallacious. Used in Nyāya texts.
   - [CORE]

5. **प्रमाणान्तर** / *pramāṇāntara*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + antara (other, different)
   - Translation: another means of knowledge, different proof
   - Context: Used when discussing alternative sources of validation or different epistemic instruments.
   - [CORE]

6. **प्रमाणबाध** / *pramāṇabādha*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + bādha (obstruction, contradiction)
   - Translation: contradiction by valid means, epistemic defeat
   - Context: Technical term for when one valid cognition is contradicted by another. Used in epistemological debates.
   - [CORE]

7. **प्रमाणसिद्ध** / *pramāṇasiddha*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + siddha (established, proved)
   - Translation: established by valid means, epistemically validated
   - Context: Describes what has been proven or established through valid epistemic instruments.
   - [CORE]

8. **प्रमाणविरुद्ध** / *pramāṇaviruddha*
   - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + viruddha (opposed, contradicted)
   - Translation: contradicted by valid means, epistemically invalid
   - Context: Describes claims that contradict validated knowledge. Used in philosophical refutation.
   - [CORE]

9. **प्रमाणीकरण** / *pramāṇīkaraṇa*
   - Literal: pramāṇa + -ī- + karaṇa (making, doing)
   - Translation: validation, authentication, verification
   - Context: The act or process of validating or authenticating something. Used in legal and epistemic contexts.
   - [CORE]

10. **प्रमाणीकृत** / *pramāṇīkṛta*
    - Literal: pramāṇa + -ī- + kṛta (made, done)
    - Translation: validated, authenticated, verified
    - Context: Past participle describing what has been validated or authenticated. Common in administrative and legal usage.
    - [CORE]

11. **अप्रमाण** / *apramāṇa*
    - Literal: a- (not) + pramāṇa (valid means)
    - Translation: invalid means, unreliable source, lack of proof
    - Context: Denotes epistemic sources that are not valid or reliable. Used in epistemological critique.
    - [CORE]

12. **अप्रमा** / *apramā*
    - Literal: a- (not) + pramā (valid cognition)
    - Translation: invalid cognition, false knowledge, error
    - Context: Technical term for cognition that is not epistemically valid. Includes doubt, error, and hypothetical cognition.
    - [CORE]

13. **प्रमाणफल** / *pramāṇaphala*
    - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + phala (fruit, result)
    - Translation: result of valid cognition, epistemic outcome
    - Context: Technical term for the cognitive result produced by a valid means of knowledge. Debated in epistemology.
    - [CORE]

14. **प्रमाणसमुच्चय** / *pramāṇasamuccaya*
    - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + samuccaya (collection, combination)
    - Translation: collection of valid means, combination of proofs
    - Context: Used when multiple epistemic sources converge or are combined. Discussed in epistemological texts.
    - [CORE]

15. **प्रमाणसमप्लव** / *pramāṇasamaplava*
    - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + samaplava (mutual destruction)
    - Translation: mutual cancellation of proofs, epistemic stalemate
    - Context: Technical term for when valid means of knowledge contradict each other, leading to suspension of judgment.
    - [MARGINAL]

16. **प्रमाणव्यवस्था** / *pramāṇavyavasthā*
    - Literal: pramāṇa (valid means) + vyavasthā (arrangement, system)

17. **प्रमाणता** / *pramāṇatā*
    - Literal: pramāṇa + -tā (abstract suffix)

    - Literal: pratyakṣa (perception) + pramāṇa (valid means)

19. **अनुमानप्रमाण** / *anumānapramāṇa*
    - Literal: anumāna (inference) + pramāṇa (valid means)

20. **शब्दप्रमाण** / *śabdapramāṇa*
    - Literal: śabda (word, testimony) + pramāṇa (valid means)

21. **उपमानप्रमाण** / *upamānapramāṇa*

    - [CORE]

22. **अर्थापत्तिप्रमाण** / *arthāpattipramāṇa*
    - Literal: arthāpatti (postulation, presumption) + pramāṇa (valid means)

23. **अनुपलब्धिप्रमाण** / *anupalabdhipramāṇa*
    - Literal: anupalabdhi (non-apprehension) + pramāṇa (valid means)
    - Translation: knowledge through non-perception, absence as valid means
    - Context: Technical term for knowledge of absence through non-perception. Discussed in Buddhist and some Hindu epistemologies.
    - [CORE]

24. **मान** / *māna*
    - Literal: from √mā (to measure)
    - Translation: measure, standard, respect, honor, pride

    - Literal: māna (measure) + daṇḍa (stick, rod)

    - [CORE]

26. **मानक** / *mānaka*
    - Literal: māna (measure) + -ka (suffix)

27. **उपमान** / *upamāna*

30. **अपरिमाण** / *aparimāṇa*
    - Literal: a- (not) + parimāṇa (measure)

31. **सम्यक्प्रमाण** / *samyakpramāṇa*
    - Literal: samyak (correct, right) + pramāṇa (valid means)
    - Translation: correct means of knowledge, truly valid source
    - Context: Emphasizes the correctness or genuine validity of an epistemic source. Used in epistemological precision.
    - [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

### ROOT 1: √सत् / sat

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

- **satkāra, satkṛta, satpuruṣa, sajjana, sadguṇa, sadgati, sadbhāva, satsaṅga, satkarma, satpatha, sadvṛtta, sadvacana, satkriyā**: The root sat- shifts from its core meaning of "being/existing/true" to function as an evaluative marker meaning "good, virtuous, proper." This extension appears systematically in moral and social evaluation, where sat- marks positive ethical value rather than ontological or epistemic status.

- **sattva**: In Sāṃkhya philosophy, this term shifts from general "being/existence" to denote a specific metaphysical quality (one of three guṇas) characterized by purity, lightness, and goodness. This represents a technical philosophical specialization.

- **satya-vrata, satya-sandha, satya-pratijña**: These compounds extend the truth domain into volitional/promissory contexts, where "truth" relates to keeping promises and maintaining commitments rather than factual accuracy.

- **satkriyā**: Specialized to funeral rites specifically, representing a domain-specific ritualistic usage.

### ROOT 2: √ऋत / ṛta

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

- **ṛtu, ṛtukāla, ṛtumat, ṛtusaṃhāra**: The derivative ṛtu (season) represents a shift from moral/cosmic order to temporal/natural cyclical order. While conceptually related (seasons as manifestation of natural order), the usage extends into purely temporal and biological domains (menstruation, agricultural timing) where moral/truth evaluation is not primary.

- **Vedic compounds (ṛtajña, ṛtāvan, ṛtāyu, ṛtacit, ṛtadhīti, ṛtavradhna, ṛtasāp)**: These archaic forms show ṛta functioning as a comprehensive cosmic-moral principle in Vedic religion, extending beyond truth-evaluation into ritual propriety, divine attributes, and cosmological order.

### ROOT 3: √मा / mā

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

- **māna (honor, pride, respect)**: The root shifts from epistemic measurement to social valuation, where "measure" becomes "esteem" or "honor." This represents extension into social hierarchy and self-regard.

- **parimāṇa, aparimāṇa**: These terms extend into purely quantitative measurement (size, extent, mathematical quantity) where epistemic validation is not the primary concern, though measurement remains the core semantic contribution.

- **pramāda**: Though phonetically similar, this term derives from √mad (intoxication, negligence) rather than √mā, representing a false cognate that nevertheless participates in the error/truth semantic field through its meaning of "careless mistake."

- **Technical pramāṇa compounds**: The extensive technical vocabulary (pratyakṣapramāṇa, anumānapramāṇa, śabdapramāṇa, etc.) represents systematic philosophical elaboration into epistemological taxonomy, where the root's contribution remains stable but the domain becomes highly specialized.
