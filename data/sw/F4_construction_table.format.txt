# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — F4 Construction Table
# Language: Swahili (sw)

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Usage Notes | Epistemic Status |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|------------------|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | Habari hii ni kweli | habari hii ni kweli | This news is true | Standard predicative construction used across all registers. No restrictions observed. The copula ni/si shows invariant form in present tense across noun classes. | [CORE] |
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | Maneno yake ni sahihi | maneno yake ni sahihi | His/her words are correct | Same pattern as above; sahihi commonly used for correctness/accuracy in formal and informal contexts. | [CORE] |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Hii si kweli | hii si kweli | This is not true | Most common negation strategy for truth-value denial. Used freely across all contexts. The invariant si form is standard for present tense negation. | [CORE] |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Bidhaa hizi si halisi | bidhaa hizi si halisi | These products are not genuine | Particularly common in commercial/consumer contexts for authenticity claims. Pattern identical to general negative copular construction. | [CORE] |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | Mtu wa kweli | mtu wa kweli | Genuine person / truthful person | Highly productive associative construction. The wa variant shows class 1 agreement. Used for character attribution and moral evaluation. | [CORE] |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | Ushahidi madhubuti | ushahidi madhubuti | Reliable evidence | Direct modification pattern common with adjectival truth-terms. Frequent in legal and formal contexts. Madhubuti shows class 11 agreement (u-). | [CORE] |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | Jambo limekamilika | jambo limekamilika | The matter has become complete | Stative extension -ik- productive for completion/finalization contexts. Common in formal and administrative language. | [CORE] |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | Amekuwa mwongo | amekuwa mwongo | He/she has become a liar | Periphrastic inchoative with kuwa is the default strategy. Used for character change or revelation. More common than dedicated stative forms. | [CORE] |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | Sahihisha maandishi | sahihisha maandishi | Correct the writing / verify the documents | Extremely productive causative pattern. Common in administrative, educational, and editorial contexts. The -ish- suffix is regular and predictable. | [CORE] |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | Hakikisha ukweli | hakikisha ukweli | Ensure the truth / verify the facts | Very frequent in instructional and procedural language. Hakikisha is one of the most common causative forms in everyday speech. | [CORE] |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | Ukweli wa mambo | ukweli wa mambo | The truth of the matter | Highly productive u- prefix pattern for abstract nouns. Ukweli is among the most frequent truth-domain terms across all registers. | [CORE] |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | Uadilifu ni muhimu | uadilifu ni muhimu | Justice is important | The -fu suffix adds quality/character meaning. Common in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. Uadilifu specifically frequent in justice/fairness contexts. | [CORE] |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Mwongo anasema uongo | mwongo anasema uongo | The liar tells lies | Simple m-/wa- prefix pattern for person nouns. Mwongo is common in moral evaluation and character description. | [CORE] |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Mdanganyifu hataaminiwa | mdanganyifu hataaminiwa | The deceiver will not be trusted | The -ifu suffix creates habitual agent nouns. Common in moral discourse and character assessment. Implies persistent or characteristic behavior. | [CORE] |
| **8. VERBAL DERIVATION FROM NOMINALS** | Hukumia mhalifu | hukumia mhalifu | Judge the offender / pass judgment on the offender | Applicative -ia creates transitive verbs from nouns. Hukumia very common in legal and judicial contexts. | [CORE] |
| **8. VERBAL DERIVATION FROM NOMINALS** | Shuhudia tukio | shuhudia tukio | Witness the event / testify about the event | Same applicative pattern. Shuhudia frequent in legal testimony and eyewitness contexts. Shows vowel harmony from shahidi root. | [CORE] |
| **9. EMPHATIC INTENSIFICATION** | Kweli kabisa | kweli kabisa | Absolutely true / completely true | Kabisa is the strongest intensifier. Common in emphatic assertions and confirmations across all registers. | [CORE] |
| **9. EMPHATIC INTENSIFICATION** | Ni sahihi sana | ni sahihi sana | It is very correct | Sana is the general intensifier. Used freely in all contexts. Less emphatic than kabisa. | [CORE] |
| **10. MANNER ADVERBIALS** | Fanya kwa usahihi | fanya kwa usahihi | Do it correctly / with accuracy | Highly productive kwa + abstract noun pattern. Common in instructional and procedural language. | [CORE] |
| **10. MANNER ADVERBIALS** | Sema kwa ukweli | sema kwa ukweli | Speak truthfully / in truth | Very frequent collocation. Used in moral exhortations and truth-telling contexts. Alternative kwa kweli (bare root) equally common. | [CORE] |
| **11. CONTRASTIVE COORDINATION** | Kweli au uongo | kweli au uongo | True or false / truth or lies | Fixed binary opposition. Common in evaluative, testing, and philosophical contexts. Order (positive first) is conventional. | [CORE] |
| **11. CONTRASTIVE COORDINATION** | Halisi au bandia | halisi au bandia | Genuine or fake / authentic or counterfeit | Very common in commercial contexts for authenticity assessment. Fixed pair used in product evaluation and quality control. | [CORE] |
| **12. DEMONSTRATIVE SPECIFICATION** | Ukweli huu | ukweli huu | This truth / this fact | Standard demonstrative pattern with class agreement. Used for discourse reference to specific propositions or claims. | [CORE] |
| **12. DEMONSTRATIVE SPECIFICATION** | Uongo ule | uongo ule | That lie / that falsehood | Distal demonstrative for reference to previously mentioned or distant lies. Common in narrative and argumentative discourse. | [CORE] |
| **13. POSSESSIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Haki yake | haki yake | His/her right / their entitlement | Standard possessive construction with class agreement. Extremely common in legal, political, and moral discourse about rights and entitlements. | [CORE] |
| **13. POSSESSIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Makosa yake | makosa yake | His/her mistakes / their errors | Common in evaluative contexts for error attribution. The plural makosa (class 6) takes ya- possessive prefix. | [CORE] |
| **14. COMPLETIVE/TOTALITY MARKING** | Ukweli wote | ukweli wote | The whole truth / all the truth | -Ote shows class agreement (w- for class 11). Very common in legal oaths and emphatic truth-telling contexts. | [CORE] |
| **14. COMPLETIVE/TOTALITY MARKING** | Ukweli pekee | ukweli pekee | Only the truth / nothing but the truth | Pekee is invariant. Common in exclusive truth claims and legal testimony. Emphasizes exclusivity rather than totality. | [CORE] |
| **15. EVIDENTIAL FRAMING** | Ni ukweli kwamba amefika | ni ukweli kwamba amefika | It is true that he/she has arrived | Standard complementizer construction for embedding propositions. Common in formal and evidential contexts for factual claims. | [CORE] |
| **15. EVIDENTIAL FRAMING** | Ni dhahiri kwamba wanajua | ni dhahiri kwamba wanajua | It is clear that they know | Same pattern with dhahiri (clear/evident). Frequent in argumentative and explanatory discourse. Functions as epistemic frame. | [CORE] |
| **16. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL** | Bila ushahidi | bila ushahidi | Without evidence / without proof | Bila + noun is standard privative construction. Very common in legal and argumentative contexts for absence of evidence. | [CORE] |
| **16. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL** | Bila ya hakika | bila ya hakika | Without certainty / uncertainly | Optional ya appears with some nouns. Common in epistemic contexts expressing uncertainty or lack of confirmation. | [CORE] |
| **17. VERB-OBJECT COLLOCATIONS** | Sema ukweli | sema ukweli | Tell the truth / speak the truth | Highly conventionalized collocation. One of the most frequent truth-domain expressions across all registers. | [CORE] |
| **17. VERB-OBJECT COLLOCATIONS** | Toa ushahidi | toa ushahidi | Give evidence / provide testimony | Fixed legal collocation. Standard expression in judicial and testimonial contexts. Toa (give) is the conventional verb for evidence provision. | [CORE] |
| **18. REDUPLICATION** | Kweli kweli | kweli kweli | Really truly / very true / absolutely true | Full reduplication for emphasis. More common in informal/colloquial speech than formal registers. Not productive for all truth-terms. | [MARGINAL] |
| **18. REDUPLICATION** | Sawa sawa | sawa sawa | Okay okay / alright alright / fine fine | Pragmatic function for enthusiastic agreement or reassurance. Informal register. Functions more as discourse marker than truth-value assertion. | [MARGINAL] |
| **19. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC MODIFICATION** | Ukweli wa kisayansi | ukweli wa kisayansi | Scientific truth / empirical truth | Productive pattern with ki- prefix for domain specification. Common in academic and technical discourse for distinguishing truth types. | [CORE] |
| **19. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC MODIFICATION** | Ukweli wa kisheria | ukweli wa kisheria | Legal truth / juridical truth | Same pattern for legal domain. Frequent in legal and philosophical contexts distinguishing legal from other truth types. | [CORE] |
| **20. SPATIAL/METAPHORICAL EXTENSION** | Ukweli wa ndani | ukweli wa ndani | Inner truth / internal truth / subjective truth | Metaphorical spatial extension. More common in philosophical, psychological, or religious discourse than everyday speech. | [MARGINAL] |
| **20. SPATIAL/METAPHORICAL EXTENSION** | Ukweli wa nje | ukweli wa nje | External truth / outward truth / objective truth | Contrasts with ndani construction. Less frequent than domain-specific patterns. Restricted to philosophical or introspective contexts. | [MARGINAL] |
| **21. SCALAR MODIFICATION** | Uongo mkubwa | uongo mkubwa | Big lie / great falsehood / serious lie | Standard size adjective modification with class agreement. Common for indicating severity or significance of falsehoods. | [CORE] |
| **21. SCALAR MODIFICATION** | Makosa madogo | makosa madogo | Small mistakes / minor errors | Same pattern with plural noun (class 6). Frequent in evaluative contexts for minimizing error significance. | [CORE] |
| **22. PASSIVE DERIVATION** | Ukweli umethibitishwa | ukweli umethibitishwa | The truth has been confirmed / verified | Regular passive formation from causative verbs. Very common in formal, legal, and administrative contexts for verification statements. | [CORE] |
| **22. PASSIVE DERIVATION** | Amehukumiwa kwa uongo | amehukumiwa kwa uongo | He/she has been judged/condemned for lying | Standard passive with kwa introducing reason/cause. Common in legal and moral judgment contexts. | [CORE] |
| **23. RECIPROCAL CONSTRUCTION** | Wanahojiana | wanahojiana | They are arguing with each other / debating | Regular reciprocal -ana suffix. Common for mutual argumentation or debate. Requires plural or conjoined subject. | [CORE] |
| **24. INSTRUMENTAL/MEANS EXPRESSION** | Danganya kwa maneno | danganya kwa maneno | Deceive with words / lie verbally | Kwa introduces instrument/means. Common pattern for specifying method of deception or truth-telling. | [CORE] |
| **24. INSTRUMENTAL/MEANS EXPRESSION** | Thibitisha kwa ushahidi | thibitisha kwa ushahidi | Confirm with evidence / verify through testimony | Same instrumental pattern. Frequent in legal and evidential contexts for specifying verification method. | [CORE] |
