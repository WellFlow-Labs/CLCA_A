# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + (copula-zero / *adalah* / *merupakan*) + truth-domain predicate adjective/noun

Indonesian has no obligatory copula. Predicate adjectives follow the subject directly, or *adalah* / *merupakan* may appear in formal registers.

### Examples
- *Informasi itu benar.* → Informasi itu benar. → "That information is true/correct."
- *Pernyataan ini adalah kebohongan.* → Pernyataan ini adalah kebohongan. → "This statement is a lie."

### Notes
The zero-copula pattern (*NP + Adj*) is the default in spoken and informal written registers. *Adalah* and *merupakan* appear in formal and written registers; *merupakan* is slightly more formal. Truth-domain adjectives (*benar*, *salah*, *palsu*, *sah*, *akurat*, *keliru*) all participate freely in this pattern.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *tidak* + predicate adjective (for adjectives); Subject + *bukan* + predicate noun/nominal

*Tidak* negates adjectives and verbs; *bukan* negates nouns and nominal predicates.

### Examples
- *Laporan itu tidak benar.* → Laporan itu tidak benar. → "That report is not true/incorrect."
- *Ini bukan dokumen asli.* → Ini bukan dokumen asli. → "This is not a genuine document."

### Notes
The *tidak* / *bukan* distinction is grammatically obligatory: *tidak benar*, *tidak sah*, *tidak akurat*, *tidak jujur* (adjective negation); *bukan kebohongan*, *bukan tiruan*, *bukan aslinya* (nominal negation). Mixing the two is ungrammatical in standard usage. *Tidak benar* is the standard formula in official denials and journalistic corrections.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Noun + (*yang*) + truth-domain adjective (post-nominal); truth-domain noun + noun (compound)

Indonesian adjectives follow the noun they modify. The relative particle *yang* is optional for single-adjective modification and obligatory when the modifier is a verb or longer phrase.

### Examples
- *sumber yang terpercaya* → sumber yang terpercaya → "a trusted/reliable source"
- *dokumen palsu* → dokumen palsu → "a forged/fake document"

### Notes
Single-adjective post-nominal modification without *yang* (*dokumen palsu*, *berita bohong*, *data akurat*) is the productive default for noun-modifier compounds in this domain. The *yang* form (*sumber yang terpercaya*, *informasi yang menyesatkan*) is preferred when the modifier is participial or when the construction is more explicitly predicative. Both patterns are fully productive across the truth-domain vocabulary.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*menjadi* + truth-domain noun/adjective; *terbukti* + truth-domain adjective (resultative)

*Menjadi* (to become) is the standard inchoative verb. *Terbukti* (proven, established as) functions as a resultative stative expressing that a truth-domain state has been reached through evidence.

### Examples
- *Hal itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.* → Hal itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan. → "That eventually became reality."
- *Klaim tersebut terbukti palsu.* → Klaim tersebut terbukti palsu. → "That claim was proven false."

### Notes
*Menjadi kenyataan* (to become reality) is a fixed collocation. *Terbukti* + truth-domain adjective (*terbukti benar*, *terbukti palsu*, *terbukti asli*) is a high-frequency resultative construction in legal and journalistic discourse, expressing that a truth-domain evaluation has been established through evidence or official process. *Terbukti* is a *ter-* stative passive of *bukti* (evidence/proof) and does not require an overt agent.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
*me-...-kan* causative verb derived from truth-domain adjective base

Indonesian forms causative verbs productively via the *me-...-kan* circumfix on adjective or noun bases. Several truth-domain bases participate.

### Examples
- *Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik.* → Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik. → "That statement misled/confused the public."
- *Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu.* → Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu. → "Nothing can justify/sanction that action."

### Notes
Attested causative forms in this domain: *membenarkan* (to confirm/justify, from *benar*), *mengelirukan* (to mislead/confuse, from *keliru*), *menyesatkan* (to lead astray, from *sesat*), *memalsukan* (to forge/falsify, from *palsu*), *mengesahkan* (to ratify/validate, from *sah*), *menyatakan* (to declare/state, from *nyata*). The *me-...-kan* pattern is productive but not all truth-domain adjectives form causatives: *akurat*, *sahih*, *sejati*, and *kredibel* lack attested causative forms in standard usage.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ke-...-an* circumfix on truth-domain adjective/noun base → abstract property noun

The *ke-...-an* circumfix is the primary Indonesian strategy for deriving abstract property nouns from adjective or noun bases.

### Examples
- *Kejujuran adalah nilai yang paling penting.* → Kejujuran adalah nilai yang paling penting. → "Honesty is the most important value."
- *Keabsahan dokumen itu dipertanyakan.* → Keabsahan dokumen itu dipertanyakan. → "The validity/legitimacy of that document was questioned."

### Notes
All major truth-domain adjectives in this inventory form *ke-...-an* nominalizations: *kebenaran* (truth), *kesalahan* (error/fault), *keaslian* (authenticity), *kepalsuan* (falseness), *kenyataan* (reality), *kejujuran* (honesty), *kebohongan* (falsehood), *kekeliruan* (error/misconception), *keabsahan* (validity/legitimacy), *kesahihan* (soundness/authenticity), *kesungguhan* (earnestness), *kesejatian* (trueness/genuineness), *keakuratan* (accuracy). The pattern is highly productive. A secondary nominalization strategy using *-itas* (from Dutch/Latin) applies to loanword bases: *validitas*, *kredibilitas*, *otentisitas*.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*pe(N)-* prefix on truth-domain verb/noun base → agent noun (person who performs the action)

The *pe(N)-* prefix derives agent nouns from verb or noun bases. Nasal assimilation applies according to the initial consonant of the base.

### Examples
- *Dia adalah pembohong yang tidak bisa dipercaya.* → Dia adalah pembohong yang tidak bisa dipercaya. → "He is a liar who cannot be trusted."
- *Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi.* → Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi. → "The currency counterfeiter was finally arrested by police."

### Notes
Attested agentive nominalizations in this domain: *pembohong* (liar, from *bohong*), *pendusta* (liar, formal register, from *dusta*), *pemalsu* (forger/counterfeiter, from *palsu*), *peniru* (imitator, from *tiru*), *penyesat* (one who leads astray, from *sesat*), *manipulator* (manipulator, loanword formation). A truth-teller agentive (*"one who tells the truth"*) is not lexicalized as a single derived form; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*orang yang jujur*, *orang yang berkata benar*). This asymmetry — productive agentive forms for liars and forgers, but not for truth-tellers — is consistent with the data.

## 8. PASSIVE / RESULTATIVE STATE

### Pattern
*di-...-kan* / *di-...-i* passive + truth-domain verb base; *ter-* stative passive

Indonesian has a productive passive system. The *di-* passive marks an action performed on a patient; *ter-* marks an achieved resultant state (often without specifying an agent).

### Examples
- *Dokumen itu diduga dipalsukan oleh tersangka.* → Dokumen itu diduga dipalsukan oleh tersangka. → "That document is suspected of having been forged by the suspect."
- *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan oleh DPR.* → Undang-undang itu telah disahkan oleh DPR. → "That law has been ratified by the parliament."

### Notes
The *di-* passive is the primary construction in legal and journalistic discourse for describing acts of forgery, validation, manipulation, and declaration: *dipalsukan* (forged), *disahkan* (ratified), *dibenarkan* (confirmed/sanctioned), *dinyatakan* (declared), *dimanipulasi* (manipulated), *dikelirukan* (misled), *disesatkan* (led astray), *dibohongi* (lied to), *divalidasi* (validated). The *ter-* stative (*terbukti*, *tersesat*, *terpercaya*) expresses a resultant state without necessarily implying a specific agent. This pattern is sufficiently distinct from the causative (§5) and inchoative (§4) to warrant separate documentation.

## 9. DISCOURSE-LEVEL FRAMING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Fixed adverbial phrase (sentence-initial or parenthetical) + proposition; used to frame the epistemic or ontological status of the following claim

### Examples
- *Pada kenyataannya, hal itu tidak pernah terjadi.* → Pada kenyataannya, hal itu tidak pernah terjadi. → "In reality, that never happened."
- *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju dengan pendapat itu.* → Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju dengan pendapat itu. → "Actually/In truth, I disagree with that opinion."

### Notes
This pattern is not derivational but emerges consistently from the data as a distinct construction type. Fixed adverbial frames built on truth-domain nominalizations include: *pada kenyataannya* (in reality), *sebenarnya* (actually, in truth), *pada hakikatnya* (in essence), *pada esensinya* (in essence), *jujur saja* (to be honest). Each introduces a claim with a specific epistemic or rhetorical stance — correction of false appearance (*sebenarnya*, *pada kenyataannya*), essential characterization (*pada hakikatnya*, *pada esensinya*), or candid admission (*jujur saja*). These constructions are highly frequent in spoken and written Indonesian across registers.
