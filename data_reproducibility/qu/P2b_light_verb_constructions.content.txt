# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

---

### BASE FORM 1: chiqaq (true, truthful)

#### Construction 1.1
**Example:** chiqaq simi
**Transliteration:** chiqaq simi
**Translation:** true word, truthful speech
**Usage:** Fixed expression for truthful or honest speech. Common in contexts emphasizing verbal honesty and accurate reporting.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2
**Example:** chiqaq runa
**Transliteration:** chiqaq runa
**Translation:** true person, genuine person, honest person
**Usage:** Expression for an authentic or honest person. Used to characterize someone as genuinely truthful or authentic in character.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.3
**Example:** chiqaqta rimay
**Transliteration:** chiqaqta rimay
**Translation:** to speak truthfully, to tell the truth
**Usage:** Common construction for the act of speaking truthfully. Very frequent in everyday discourse about honest communication.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.4
**Example:** chiqaqta willakuy
**Transliteration:** chiqaqta willakuy
**Translation:** to tell the truth, to report truthfully
**Usage:** Expression for truthful reporting or narration. Common in contexts of testimony and accurate reporting.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.5
**Example:** chiqaqmanta rimay
**Transliteration:** chiqaqmanta rimay
**Translation:** to speak from truth, to speak truly
**Usage:** Emphasizes speaking from a basis of truth. Used when stressing the truthful foundation of one's speech.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.6
**Example:** chiqaq yachay
**Transliteration:** chiqaq yachay
**Translation:** true knowledge, genuine knowledge
**Usage:** Expression distinguishing genuine knowledge from false belief or opinion. Used in epistemic contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 1.7
**Example:** chiqaq ñan
**Transliteration:** chiqaq ñan
**Translation:** true path, right way
**Usage:** Metaphorical expression for the correct or proper course of action. Common in moral and practical guidance contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OF CONSTRUCTION PATTERNS

### Highly Productive Construction Types:

1. **[ADJECTIVE] + runa**: Very productive pattern for character assessment (e.g., allin runa, llulla runa, k'uchi runa, qhapaq runa)

2. **[ADJECTIVE] + simi**: Productive pattern for speech characterization (e.g., chiqaq simi, llulla simi, allin simi, ch'usaq simi)

3. **[ADJECTIVE] + yuyay**: Productive pattern for mental/intentional states (e.g., allin yuyay, mana allin yuyay, pantasqa yuyay)

4. **[ADJECTIVE/ADVERB] + rimay**: Common pattern for manner of speaking (e.g., chiqaqta rimay, llullata rimay, sut'ita rimay)

5. **[ADJECTIVE/ADVERB] + ruway**: Productive pattern for manner of action (e.g., allinta ruway, mana allinta ruway, hunt'ata ruway)

6. **[ADJECTIVE/ADVERB] + yachay**: Pattern for epistemic qualification (e.g., allinta yachay, sut'ita yachay, rikuywan yachay)

7. **[ADJECTIVE] + kay**: Very productive stative construction (e.g., allin kay, chanin kay, sut'i kay, hunt'a kay)

8. **[SIZE/MAGNITUDE] + [FAULT/ERROR]**: Pattern for evaluating seriousness (e.g., huch'uy pantay, hatun pantay, huch'uy huch'a, hatun huch'a)

### Notable Patterns:

- **Epistemic source constructions**: rikuywan yachay (know by seeing), uyariywan yachay (know by hearing) - distinguish direct from indirect knowledge

- **Cultural concepts**: allin kawsay, sumaq kawsay - important philosophical concepts of proper/harmonious living

- **Institutional terms**: yachay wasi (school) - productive pattern for institutional naming

- **Universal quantification**: tukuy + NOUN patterns (tukuy ima, tukuy runa, tukuy pacha) - very productive

### Asymmetries Noted:

- Some bases (like ch'ulla, paqta) show limited multi-word construction productivity
- Moral/character assessment constructions are more productive than technical/measurement constructions
- Speech and thought characterizations are particularly productive domains
- Some expected constructions may exist but were not attested in available sources

### Register and Context Notes:

- Many constructions appear across registers (everyday, formal, religious)
- Some constructions (like sumaq kawsay, allin kawsay) carry significant cultural weight
- Legal/administrative contexts favor chanin-based constructions
- Moral discourse heavily uses allin/mana allin and related constructions
