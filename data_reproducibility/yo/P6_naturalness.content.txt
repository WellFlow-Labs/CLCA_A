# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 NATURALNESS: CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## SUBSECTION 1: BECOMING TRUE / CORRECT

### 1.1 Statements/Claims Becoming True

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + di + òtítọ́ (become truth)

**Attested expression:**
- Ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́
  - ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́
  - "That matter became the truth / turned out to be true"

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

**Usage notes:** This construction is grammatically possible but pragmatically marked. It does not express a change in truth-value (false → true) but rather **discovery or revelation** that something was true all along. The verb **di** (become, turn into) typically expresses transformation, but with **òtítọ́** it means "turned out to be" or "was revealed as" rather than "changed from false to true."

**Context of use:** Narrative contexts describing discovery, revelation, or vindication. Example: "What he said turned out to be true" (after verification).

**Alternative expressions:**
- **Ó hàn pé ó tọ́** (it appeared/was revealed that it is correct) — more natural for discovery
- **A rí i pé ó tọ́** (we saw that it is correct) — evidential construction, more common
- **Ó jẹ́ òtítọ́ lóòótọ́** (it is truth truly/indeed) — emphatic assertion rather than change

**Why marginal:** Yoruba conceptualizes truth as an inherent, static property of statements rather than something that can change. Statements don't "become true"—they are discovered to be true or revealed as true. The **di** construction is reinterpreted to fit this conceptualization.

---

### 1.2 Predictions/Prophecies Coming True

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + ṣẹ (come to pass, be fulfilled)

**Attested expression:**
- Àsọtẹ́lẹ̀ náà ṣẹ
  - àsọtẹ́lẹ̀ náà ṣẹ
  - "That prediction came to pass / was fulfilled"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **ṣẹ** (come to pass, be fulfilled, happen) is the natural way to express predictions or prophecies coming true. This is not about truth-value change but about **realization of predicted events**. The prediction was always true (in the sense of accurate), and now the predicted event has occurred.

**Context of use:** Common with predictions, prophecies, promises, and forecasts. Used when events confirm what was foretold.

**Related expressions:**
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ ṣẹ** (his/her word came to pass)
- **Ó ṣẹ gẹ́gẹ́ bí a ti sọ** (it happened just as we said)
- **Ó bá a mu pé ó ṣẹ** (it corresponds that it came to pass)

**Note:** This is distinct from statements "becoming true"—it's about events occurring as predicted, not about truth-value change.

---

### 1.3 Things Becoming Correct/Right

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + tọ́ (be correct) with inchoative markers

**Finding:** **NO COMMON INCHOATIVE CONSTRUCTION EXISTS**

**Explanation:** The stative verb **tọ́** does not naturally combine with inchoative markers or constructions. Things are evaluated as **tọ́** or **kò tọ́** (correct or incorrect) but don't "become correct" in the sense of changing state. 

**What is expressed instead:**
- **Correction/fixing:** **tún...ṣe** (redo, correct)
  - Mo tún ọ̀rọ̀ náà ṣe (I corrected that statement)
  - This expresses the act of correcting rather than the statement becoming correct

- **Discovery of correctness:** **rí i pé ó tọ́** (see that it is correct)
  - A rí i pé ó tọ́ (we saw/discovered that it is correct)
  - This expresses discovery rather than change

**Why no inchoative:** Correctness is treated as an inherent property that is discovered or established through correction, not as a state that things enter into spontaneously.

---

### 1.4 Resemblance Increasing/Developing

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí + jọ (begin to resemble)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ ọ́
  - ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ ọ́
  - "It began to resemble it / started to look like it"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **jọ** (resemble) can naturally combine with inchoative/inceptive markers like **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí** (begin to). This expresses gradual development of resemblance or similarity. Unlike truth/correctness, resemblance is conceptualized as something that can develop or increase over time.

**Context of use:** Common when describing gradual changes in appearance, behavior, or characteristics. Example: "The child is beginning to resemble his father."

**Related expressions:**
- **Ó ń jọ ọ́ síwájú sí i** (it is resembling it more and more)
- **Ó ti bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọra** (they have begun to resemble each other)

**Why natural:** Resemblance is conceptualized as gradable and developable, unlike truth which is binary and static.

## SUBSECTION 2: BECOMING FALSE / INCORRECT

### 2.1 Statements Becoming False

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + di + irọ́ (become lie/falsehood)

**Finding:** **NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS**

**Explanation:** Just as statements don't "become true," they don't "become false" in Yoruba conceptualization. Truth-value is not treated as something that changes over time. A statement is either **tọ́** or **kò tọ́**, **òtítọ́** or **irọ́**, from the outset.

**What is expressed instead:**
- **Discovery of falsehood:** **rí i pé kò tọ́** (see that it is not correct)
  - A rí i pé kò tọ́ (we saw/discovered that it is not correct)

- **Exposure as lie:** **fi hàn pé ó purọ́** (show that it is false)
  - Wọ́n fi hàn pé ó purọ́ (they showed that it is false)

- **Refutation:** **tako** (oppose, refute)
  - Wọ́n tako ọ̀rọ̀ náà (they refuted that statement)

**Why no inchoative:** The conceptual system treats truth-value as inherent and discoverable rather than changeable.

---

### 2.2 Things Becoming Incorrect/Wrong

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + di + àìtọ́ (become incorrectness)

**Finding:** **NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS**

**Explanation:** Same reasoning as above. Things don't "become incorrect"—they are discovered to be incorrect or are made incorrect through error.

**What is expressed instead:**
- **Making an error:** **ṣe àṣìṣe** (do error)
  - Mo ṣe àṣìṣe (I made an error)
  - This expresses the act of erring rather than something becoming incorrect

- **Going wrong:** **bàjẹ́** (spoil, go wrong)
  - Ọ̀rọ̀ náà ti bàjẹ́ (that matter has gone wrong)
  - This is about deterioration or failure rather than truth-value change

---

### 2.3 Loss of Correspondence/Agreement

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + padà + kò bá mu (return to not correspond)

**Finding:** **NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS**

**Explanation:** Correspondence (**bá mu**) is evaluated at a given time but not conceptualized as something that is lost or gained over time. Things either correspond or don't correspond when compared.

**What is expressed instead:**
- **Divergence:** **yàtọ̀** (differ, diverge)
  - Ó ti yàtọ̀ sí i (it has diverged from it / differs from it)
  - This expresses difference rather than loss of correspondence

- **Change making things not match:** **yí...padà** (change, turn around)
  - Ó ti yí i padà, kò bá a mu mọ́ (it has changed, it no longer corresponds to it)
  - This describes change followed by evaluation of non-correspondence

## SUBSECTION 3: CAUSING TRUTH / CORRECTNESS

### 3.1 Making Statements True

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Statement + tọ́ (make that statement be correct)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ náà tọ́
  - ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ náà tọ́
  - "He/she made that statement true / verified it"

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

**Usage notes:** This construction is grammatically possible but pragmatically reinterpreted. It cannot mean "cause a false statement to become true" (conceptually impossible). Instead, it is understood as:
- **Verification:** establishing or confirming that something is true
- **Fulfillment:** making a prediction come true by causing the predicted event
- **Vindication:** proving that a statement was true all along

**Context of use:** Rare in everyday speech. More likely in formal or philosophical discourse. The construction is marked because one cannot literally "make" truth—truth is discovered or established, not created.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **Jẹ́rìí sí i pé ó tọ́** (testify to it that it is correct) — providing evidence
- **Fi ẹ̀rí hàn pé ó tọ́** (show evidence that it is correct) — demonstration
- **Ṣe àfihàn pé ó tọ́** (demonstrate that it is correct) — proving

**Why marginal:** The causative construction conflicts with the conceptualization of truth as inherent and discoverable rather than causable.

---

### 3.2 Making Things Correct (Correction)

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + tún + Object + ṣe (redo/correct object)

**Attested expression:**
- Mo tún àṣìṣe náà ṣe
  - mo tún àṣìṣe náà ṣe
  - "I corrected that error / fixed that mistake"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **tún...ṣe** (redo, correct, revise) is the natural way to express correction or fixing of errors. This is not about causing truth but about **repairing incorrectness**. The construction focuses on the corrective action rather than the result of "becoming correct."

**Context of use:** Very common in everyday speech, educational contexts, editorial work, and any situation involving error correction.

**Related expressions:**
- **Ṣe àtúnṣe** (do correction/revision) — nominal construction
- **Tún...wò** (re-examine) — checking again
- **Ṣe àtúnṣe sí i** (make correction to it)

**Why natural:** This expresses an action (correcting) rather than causing a state change (making true), which fits Yoruba conceptualization.

---

### 3.3 Causing Correspondence/Matching

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + X + bá + Y + mu (make X correspond to Y)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ bá èyí mu
  - ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ bá èyí mu
  - "He/she made his/her statement correspond to this / aligned his statement with this"

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

**Usage notes:** This causative construction is possible but marked. It expresses **deliberate alignment or adjustment** to make things match. More natural than causing truth because correspondence is about relationship between things rather than inherent property.

**Context of use:** Formal or technical contexts where deliberate alignment is discussed. Less common in everyday speech.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **Mú...bá...dọ́gba** (make...equal to...) — equalization
- **Ṣe kó bá a mu** (do so-that it corresponds) — purposive construction
- **Ṣètò rẹ̀ kó bá a mu** (arrange it so-that it corresponds)

**Why marginal:** While more natural than "making true," it still involves deliberate causation of a relational property, which is marked.

---

### 3.4 Causing Belief

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Person + gbàgbọ́ (make person believe)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó mú kí n gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
  - ó mú kí n gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
  - "He/she made me believe that it is true / convinced me that it is true"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** This causative construction is natural and common. It expresses **persuasion or convincing**. Unlike causing truth (which is conceptually problematic), causing belief is straightforward—one can influence another person's mental state through evidence, argument, or persuasion.

**Context of use:** Very common in everyday speech when discussing persuasion, convincing, or influence on beliefs.

**Related expressions:**
- **Yí mi lọ́kàn pé...** (turn my mind that...) — change someone's mind
- **Fún mi ní ìdánilójú pé...** (give me assurance that...) — provide assurance
- **Jẹ́ kí n gbàgbọ́** (let me believe) — permissive rather than causative

**Why natural:** Belief is a mental state that can be influenced by external agents, unlike truth which is independent of agents.

---

### 3.5 Causing Certainty

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Person + dájú (make person be certain)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó mú kí n dájú
  - ó mú kí n dájú
  - "He/she made me certain / assured me"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** This causative construction is natural and common. It expresses **providing assurance or confirmation** that removes doubt. Like causing belief, causing certainty is conceptually straightforward.

**Context of use:** Common in contexts of reassurance, confirmation, and providing evidence or guarantees.

**Related expressions:**
- **Fún mi ní ìdájú** (give me certainty/assurance)
- **Ṣe ìdánilójú fún mi** (do assurance for me)
- **Jẹ́ kí n dájú** (let me be certain) — permissive

**Why natural:** Certainty is an epistemic state that can be influenced by providing evidence or assurance.

---

### 3.6 Causing Someone to Tell Truth

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Person + sọ òtítọ́ (make person tell truth)

**Attested expression:**
- Wọ́n mú kí ó sọ òtítọ́
  - wọ́n mú kí ó sọ òtítọ́
  - "They made him/her tell the truth / forced him/her to tell the truth"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** This causative construction is natural and common. It expresses **compelling or forcing someone to tell the truth**, often through pressure, interrogation, or authority. This is about causing an action (truth-telling) rather than causing a state (truth), which is conceptually unproblematic.

**Context of use:** Common in contexts of interrogation, confession, pressure, or authority. Can imply coercion or persuasion.

**Related expressions:**
- **Fi ipá mú kí ó sọ òtítọ́** (use force make him tell truth) — explicit coercion
- **Jẹ́ kí ó sọ òtítọ́** (let him tell truth) — permissive, less forceful
- **Rọ̀ ọ́ kó sọ òtítọ́** (beg him to tell truth) — request rather than force

**Why natural:** This causes an action (speech act) rather than a state (truth-value), which fits normal causative semantics.

---

### 3.7 Causing Someone to Lie

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Person + pa irọ́ (make person tell lie)

**Attested expression:**
- Wọ́n mú kí ó pa irọ́
  - wọ́n mú kí ó pa irọ́
  - "They made him/her tell a lie / forced him/her to lie"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** Parallel to causing truth-telling. This expresses **compelling or forcing someone to lie**, often through pressure, threats, or coercion. Common in contexts of false testimony, forced confession, or manipulation.

**Context of use:** Common in legal, political, or moral discourse about coercion and false testimony.

**Related expressions:**
- **Tì í lọ́wọ́ kó pa irọ́** (press him to tell lie)
- **Jẹ́ kí ó pa irọ́** (let him tell lie) — permissive
- **Rọ̀ ọ́ kó pa irọ́** (beg him to tell lie)

**Why natural:** Same reasoning as causing truth-telling—this causes an action rather than a state.

---

## SUBSECTION 4: PREVENTING TRUTH / CAUSING FALSEHOOD

### 4.1 Preventing Truth from Being Known

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + dènà + kí + òtítọ́ + má hàn (prevent that truth not appear)

**Attested expression:**
- Wọ́n dènà kí òtítọ́ má hàn
  - wọ́n dènà kí òtítọ́ má hàn
  - "They prevented the truth from being revealed / blocked the truth from appearing"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **dènà** (prevent, block, hinder) naturally combines with negative subjunctive (**kí...má**) to express prevention. This is about **preventing revelation or discovery** of truth, not about preventing truth itself (which is conceptually impossible).

**Context of use:** Common in contexts of cover-up, censorship, obstruction, or concealment. Used in political, legal, and moral discourse.

**Related expressions:**
- **Pamọ́ òtítọ́** (hide truth) — concealment without explicit prevention
- **Pa òtítọ́** (kill/destroy truth) — metaphorical suppression
- **Kò jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ hàn** (not let truth appear) — alternative prevention construction

**Why natural:** This prevents an event (revelation) rather than preventing a state (truth), which is conceptually coherent.

---

### 4.2 Distorting Truth

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + yí + òtítọ́ + padà (turn truth around/back)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó yí òtítọ́ padà
  - ó yí òtítọ́ padà
  - "He/she distorted the truth / twisted the truth"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **yí...padà** (turn around, reverse, change) is used metaphorically for distortion or twisting of truth. This doesn't change truth itself but **misrepresents or distorts the presentation** of truth.

**Context of use:** Common in contexts of deception, propaganda, manipulation, or biased reporting.

**Related expressions:**
- **Yí ọ̀rọ̀ padà** (twist the matter/word)
- **Pò òtítọ́ àti irọ́ pọ̀** (mix truth and lies together)
- **Ṣe àyípadà sí òtítọ́** (make change to truth)

**Why natural:** This is about manipulation of representation rather than changing truth-value itself.

---

### 4.3 Making Someone Believe Falsehood

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + mú + kí + Person + gbàgbọ́ + irọ́ (make person believe lie)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó mú kí wọn gbàgbọ́ irọ́
  - ó mú kí wọn gbàgbọ́ irọ́
  - "He/she made them believe a lie / deceived them"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** This causative construction is natural and common. It expresses **deception or misleading**. Like causing belief in truth, causing belief in falsehood is conceptually straightforward—it's about influencing mental states.

**Context of use:** Very common in contexts of deception, fraud, propaganda, or manipulation.

**Related expressions:**
- **Tan wọn jẹ** (deceive them, lit: spread-them-eat)
- **Ṣe ẹ̀tàn sí wọn** (do deception to them)
- **Jẹ́ kí wọn gbàgbọ́ irọ́** (let them believe lie) — permissive

**Why natural:** This causes a mental state (belief) rather than a truth-value, which is conceptually unproblematic.

---

## SUBSECTION 5: MAINTAINING TRUTH / STABILITY

### 5.1 Keeping/Maintaining Truth

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + pa + òtítọ́ + mọ́ (keep truth held/preserved)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó pa òtítọ́ mọ́
  - ó pa òtítọ́ mọ́
  - "He/she kept the truth / preserved the truth / held onto the truth"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **pa...mọ́** (keep, preserve, hold onto) is used for maintaining or preserving truth. This is about **protecting truth from being lost, forgotten, or suppressed**, not about maintaining truth-value (which doesn't change).

**Context of use:** Common in contexts of testimony, record-keeping, tradition, or resistance to suppression.

**Related expressions:**
- **Tọ́jú òtítọ́** (care-for truth, guard truth)
- **Dáàbò bo òtítọ́** (protect truth)
- **Kò jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ sọnù** (not let truth be lost)

**Why natural:** This is about preserving knowledge or testimony of truth, not about maintaining truth-value itself.

---

### 5.2 Standing by Truth / Remaining Truthful

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + dúró + lórí + òtítọ́ (stand on truth)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó dúró lórí òtítọ́
  - ó dúró lórí òtítọ́
  - "He/she stood by the truth / remained committed to truth"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **dúró lórí** (stand on/upon) is used metaphorically for **commitment to or adherence to truth**. This is about maintaining one's commitment rather than maintaining truth itself.

**Context of use:** Common in moral discourse, testimony, and character evaluation. Expresses steadfastness and integrity.

**Related expressions:**
- **Dúró ṣinṣin lórí òtítọ́** (stand firmly on truth)
- **Kò kúrò lórí òtítọ́** (not move from truth)
- **Gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀** (establish truth firmly)

**Why natural:** This is about personal commitment and moral stance rather than maintaining truth-value.

---

### 5.3 Truth Remaining/Enduring

**Pattern investigated:** òtítọ́ + wà + títí (truth exist continuously)

**Attested expression:**
- Òtítọ́ wà títí
  - òtítọ́ wà títí
  - "Truth endures / Truth remains forever"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The verb **wà** (exist, be located) with **títí** (continuously, forever) expresses **endurance or permanence** of truth. This is a philosophical or proverbial statement about the eternal nature of truth.

**Context of use:** Common in proverbial, religious, or philosophical discourse. Expresses the idea that truth is permanent and unchanging.

**Related expressions:**
- **Òtítọ́ kò lè parẹ́** (truth cannot perish)
- **Òtítọ́ á wà láéláé** (truth will exist forever)
- **Òtítọ́ kò lè bàjẹ́** (truth cannot spoil/deteriorate)

**Why natural:** This expresses permanence of truth as a concept or value, not maintenance of specific truth-values.

## SUBSECTION 6: ASPECTUAL BEHAVIOR WITH TRUTH PREDICATES

### 6.1 Progressive Aspect with Truth Predicates

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + ń + tọ́ (be currently correct)

**Finding:** **UNNATURAL / NOT ATTESTED**

**Explanation:** The stative verb **tọ́** does not naturally combine with progressive marker **ń**. Truth/correctness is not conceptualized as an ongoing process but as a state. One cannot be "in the process of being true."

**What is expressed instead:**
- **Ó tọ́** (it is correct) — simple stative, no aspect marking needed
- **Ó ń hàn pé ó tọ́** (it is appearing/becoming clear that it is correct) — progressive on revelation verb, not truth predicate

---

### 6.2 Perfective Aspect with Truth Predicates

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + ti + tọ́ (have become correct)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó ti tọ́
  - ó ti tọ́
  - "It has been correct / It is (now established as) correct"

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

**Usage notes:** The perfective marker **ti** can combine with **tọ́** but the interpretation is not "has become correct" but rather **"is now established/confirmed as correct"** or **"has been correct (all along)"**. The perfective marks the establishment or confirmation of correctness, not a change into correctness.

**Context of use:** Used when confirming or establishing that something is correct, often after verification or investigation.

**Alternative interpretation:**
- **A ti rí i pé ó tọ́** (we have seen that it is correct) — perfective on discovery verb, more natural

---

### 6.3 Habitual Aspect with Truth-Telling

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + máa ń + sọ òtítọ́ (habitually tell truth)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó máa ń sọ òtítọ́
  - ó máa ń sọ òtítọ́
  - "He/she habitually tells the truth / is a truth-teller"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** The habitual markers **máa ń** naturally combine with the action verb **sọ òtítọ́** (tell truth) to express **characteristic or habitual behavior**. This is common for describing someone's character as truthful.

**Context of use:** Very common in character evaluation and moral discourse.

**Related expressions:**
- **Ó máa ń pa irọ́** (he habitually tells lies) — parallel for lying
- **Ó máa ń ṣe òtítọ́** (he habitually does truth) — alternative construction

**Why natural:** Truth-telling is an action that can be habitual, unlike truth-value which is a state.

---

### 6.4 Inceptive Aspect with Resemblance

**Pattern investigated:** Subject + bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí + jọ (begin to resemble)

**Attested expression:**
- Ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ ọ́
  - ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ ọ́
  - "It began to resemble it / started to look like it"

**Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Usage notes:** Already discussed in Subsection 1.4. The verb **jọ** (resemble) naturally takes inceptive marking because resemblance can develop gradually.

**Why natural:** Resemblance is conceptualized as gradable and developable, unlike truth which is binary and static.

## SUBSECTION 7: SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

### 7.1 Patterns That Are Natural [CORE]

**Change/Becoming:**
- Predictions coming true: **ṣẹ** (come to pass, be fulfilled)
- Resemblance developing: **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ** (begin to resemble)

**Causation:**
- Causing belief: **mú kí...gbàgbọ́** (make believe)
- Causing certainty: **mú kí...dájú** (make certain)
- Causing truth-telling: **mú kí...sọ òtítọ́** (make tell truth)
- Causing lying: **mú kí...pa irọ́** (make tell lie)
- Correcting errors: **tún...ṣe** (redo, correct)

**Prevention/Distortion:**
- Preventing revelation: **dènà kí...má hàn** (prevent from appearing)
- Distorting truth: **yí...padà** (twist, distort)
- Causing false belief: **mú kí...gbàgbọ́ irọ́** (make believe lie)

**Maintenance/Stability:**
- Preserving truth: **pa...mọ́** (keep, preserve)
- Standing by truth: **dúró lórí** (stand on/by)
- Truth enduring: **wà títí** (exist continuously)

**Aspectual:**
- Habitual truth-telling: **máa ń sọ òtítọ́**
- Inceptive resemblance: **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí jọ**

---

### 7.2 Patterns That Are Marginal [MARGINAL]

**Change/Becoming:**
- Statements "becoming" true: **di òtítọ́** (reinterpreted as discovery/revelation)
- Causing correspondence: **mú kí...bá...mu** (make correspond)

**Causation:**
- Making statements true: **mú kí...tọ́** (reinterpreted as verification/vindication)

**Aspectual:**
- Perfective with truth predicates: **ti tọ́** (reinterpreted as establishment/confirmation)

---

### 7.3 Patterns That Do Not Exist

**Change/Becoming:**
- Statements becoming false: **NO EXPRESSION**
- Things becoming correct (inchoative): **NO EXPRESSION**
- Things becoming incorrect: **NO EXPRESSION**
- Loss of correspondence over time: **NO EXPRESSION**

**Aspectual:**
- Progressive with truth predicates: **UNNATURAL** (*ń tọ́)

---

### 7.4 Key Conceptual Patterns

1. **Truth is static, not dynamic:** Truth-value does not change over time. Statements don't "become true" or "become false"—they are discovered to be true or false.

2. **Discovery vs. change:** What appears to be change of truth-value is actually **discovery or revelation** of pre-existing truth. The verb **di** (become) is reinterpreted as "turn out to be" rather than "change into."

3. **Actions are causable, states are not:** One can cause **actions** related to truth (telling truth, revealing truth, correcting errors) but cannot cause **truth itself**. Causative constructions with truth predicates are reinterpreted as verification or vindication.

4. **Mental states are causable:** One can cause **belief** or **certainty** (mental states) but not **truth** (objective property). This asymmetry is consistent across the language.

5. **Resemblance is gradable, truth is binary:** **Jọ** (resemble) can take inceptive and degree modification because resemblance is conceptualized as gradable. **Tọ́** (be correct) resists these because truth is binary.

6. **Maintenance is about knowledge, not truth-value:** Expressions for "maintaining truth" (**pa òtítọ́ mọ́**, **dúró lórí òtítọ́**) are about preserving **knowledge of truth** or **commitment to truth**, not about maintaining truth-value itself.

7. **Aspectual restrictions:** Stative truth predicates resist progressive aspect (no ongoing process) but can take perfective aspect (reinterpreted as establishment/confirmation). Action verbs like **sọ òtítọ́** (tell truth) take all aspects naturally.

---

### 7.5 Cross-Linguistic Implications

The Yoruba patterns suggest a conceptual system where:
- Truth is **discovered** rather than **created**
- Truth is **inherent** rather than **emergent**
- Truth is **binary** rather than **scalar**
- Truth is **objective** rather than **agent-dependent**

This contrasts with languages that allow expressions like "make true" or "become true" more freely, suggesting different underlying conceptualizations of truth's ontological status.
