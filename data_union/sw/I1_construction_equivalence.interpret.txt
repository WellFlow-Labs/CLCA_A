## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by BECOME

The data in P4a and P4b do not attest a formally distinct REVELATION construction separate from the BECOME construction. Both the reading "the truth came to light" and the reading "the truth became evident" are expressed by the same *-ika* stative-intransitive construction: *Ukweli ulidhihirika* covers both. The *ni dhahiri kwamba* frame (§10) expresses a speaker's evidential stance toward a proposition but does not describe a prior event of revelation; it is a predication about current self-evidence, not a narrative of coming-to-light. No construction in the data is exclusively reserved for REVELATION as distinct from BECOME. The *-ika* construction therefore serves both operators without formal differentiation.

### 2.2 CAUSATION (character) substituted by ATTR + agentive nominalization

Swahili does not attest a dedicated construction for "X caused Y to become a truthful/deceptive person." The characterological reading — that a person habitually possesses a truth-domain property — is expressed by combining the ATTR construction (§1: *ni* + predicate) with a lexicalized agentive nominal (§7a: *muongo*, *mwaminifu*, *mdanganyifu*). The *-fu* suffix on these nominals encodes habitual or characterological quality directly in the nominal form. The construction *Yeye ni muongo* ("He/she is a liar") is the attested pattern; no causative or inchoative construction expressing "becoming a liar" or "being made into a liar" is attested in the data.

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION substituted by NEGATION (propositional level)

At the propositional level, DISCONFIRMATION is not expressed by a distinct construction. The same *si* + truth-domain predicate construction (§2) that expresses NEGATION also serves as the primary means of propositional disconfirmation. *Hii si kweli* ("This is not true") functions simultaneously as a negation of the attribution and as a disconfirmation of the claim. The contrastive two-clause pattern (*si halisi; ni bandia*) adds a positive counter-assertion but does not constitute a formally distinct DISCONFIRMATION construction; it is an extension of the NEGATION construction with an appended ATTR clause.

### 2.4 DISCONFIRMATION substituted by agentive nominalization (character level)

At the character level, disconfirmation of a person's honesty is expressed through agentive nominals in the ATTR frame (*Yeye ni muongo*, *Yeye ni mdanganyifu*) rather than through any negation of a positive character attribution. The data do not attest a pattern such as *si mwaminifu* ("not trustworthy") as a primary formula; the positive agentive nominals for negative character (*muongo*, *mdanganyifu*) carry the disconfirmation semantics lexically.

### 2.5 Asymmetry: positive vs. negative forms for lying vs. truth-telling

A consistent asymmetry runs through the data. The act of lying has a dedicated light-verb construction (*piga uongo*, §12) that is described as the most colloquial and frequent form in spoken Swahili. No parallel *piga kweli* construction is attested. The "tell the truth" directive uses a different light verb (*sema ukweli*), and no single-word lexicalized verb for "to truth-tell" is attested. Similarly, agentive nominals for deceivers and liars (*muongo*, *mdanganyifu*, *mlaghai*) are lexicalized and [CORE], while no agentive nominal for "truth-teller" is directly derived from *kweli* or *ukweli*; the concept requires periphrastic expression (*mtu anayesema ukweli*, *mtu wa kweli*).

### 2.6 Asymmetry: possessive epistemic construction restricted to certainty domain

The possessive epistemic construction (*nina/sina uhakika*, §12/§13) is attested only for the certainty/doubt sub-domain. No parallel *nina kweli* construction is attested for propositional truth. The two sub-domains — epistemic certainty and propositional truth — use formally distinct constructions: possessive *-na-* + nominal for certainty, and copular *ni/si* + predicate for propositional truth.

### 2.7 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction

VERIFICATION is expressed through the causative *-sha/-isha* construction and its passive counterpart (*kuthibitisha*, *kuthibitishwa*, *kuhakikisha*). DISCONFIRMATION is expressed through the *si* + predicate NEGATION construction. These are formally distinct constructions. They do not share the same morphosyntactic frame.

### 2.8 Negation is analytic, not morphological

The negative of the ATTR construction is formed by replacing the invariant copula *ni* with the suppletive invariant negative copula *si*. There is no morphological negative affix added to the predicate nominal or adjective. The predicate items (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *halisi*) appear in identical form in both positive (*ni kweli*) and negative (*si kweli*) constructions. Negation is therefore analytic in this construction type.

### 2.9 Light verbs as semantic carriers in periphrastic constructions

In the periphrastic verbal constructions (§11/§12), the truth-domain noun carries the primary semantic content and the light verb specifies the action type. *Piga* in *piga uongo* specifies the act of performing; *sema* in *sema ukweli* specifies the act of speaking; *kusanya* in *kusanya ushahidi* specifies the act of gathering. The light verbs are syntactically verbal (they carry tense-aspect-agreement morphology) but semantically underspecified relative to the truth-domain noun. This pattern compensates for the absence of lexicalized single-word verbs for several truth-domain actions.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

No structural gaps are identified. All ten CLCA operators are expressible in Swahili through attested constructions. No operator requires a construction type that is entirely absent from the morphosyntax of the language.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

The following lexical gaps are attested in the data:

- **No agentive nominal for "truth-teller"** directly derived from *kweli* or *ukweli*. The concept is expressed periphrastically: *mtu anayesema ukweli* ("a person who tells the truth"), *mtu wa kweli* ("a person of truth"). This is a lexical gap, not a morphosyntactic one; the periphrastic forms are grammatically regular.

- **No causative verb derived from *kweli* itself** (*kukwelisha* is not attested). The causative function for "making something true" is carried by *kuthibitisha* (to confirm/prove). This is a lexical gap in the derivational paradigm of *kweli*.

- **No *-ika* inchoative form directly from *kweli* or *halisi***. The inchoative construction is attested for *dhahiri* (*kudhihirika*) and *danganya* (*kudanganyika*) but not for *kweli* or *halisi* as bases. Cases requiring an inchoative reading for these bases require periphrastic expression.

- **No single-word lexicalized verb for "to truth-tell"**. The action is expressed periphrastically (*sema ukweli*). This is a lexical gap compensated by the periphrastic verbal construction.

- **No *piga kweli* construction** parallel to *piga uongo*. The light-verb collocation for lying (*piga uongo*) has no positive counterpart using the same light verb.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps are identified. All ten CLCA operators correspond to expressible meanings in Swahili. No operator is blocked by a conceptual restriction in the data.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (only negative or only positive forms missing)

The following asymmetries are attested:

- **Agentive nominalization: negative polarity only lexicalized.** Agentive nominals for deceivers and liars (*muongo*, *mdanganyifu*, *mlaghai*) are lexicalized; the positive counterpart ("truth-teller") is not lexicalized and requires periphrasis. The gap is on the positive side only.

- **Light-verb collocation: negative polarity only (*piga uongo*).** The *piga* + truth-domain noun collocation is attested only for *uongo*; no *piga kweli* is attested. The positive directive uses a different verb (*sema ukweli*). The gap is in the positive *piga*-collocation slot.

- **Possessive epistemic construction: certainty domain only.** *Nina/sina uhakika* is attested; *nina kweli* is not. The possessive epistemic construction covers the certainty/doubt sub-domain but not the propositional truth sub-domain. This is an asymmetry within the epistemic domain rather than a positive/negative asymmetry.

- **Inchoative *-ika* derivation: attested for deception and evidence bases, not for truth/genuineness bases.** *Kudanganyika* and *kudhihirika* are attested; no *-ika* form from *kweli* or *halisi* is attested. The gap is on the truth/genuineness side of the domain.
