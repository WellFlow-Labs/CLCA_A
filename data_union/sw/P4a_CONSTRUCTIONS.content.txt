# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Copula *ni* + truth-domain predicate adjective/noun. Subject (noun phrase, noun, or demonstrative) + *ni* + {*kweli* / *sahihi* / *halisi* / *haki* / *hakika* / *halali* / *uongo* / *bandia* / *ghushi* / *dhahiri*}.

### Examples
- *Hii ni kweli.* → Hii ni kweli. → "This is true."
- *Habari hii ni kweli.* → Habari hii ni kweli. → "This news is true."
- *Habari hii ni sahihi.* → Habari hii ni sahihi. → "This news is accurate."
- *Hati hii ni halisi.* → Hati hii ni halisi. → "This document is genuine."
- *Taarifa hii ni sahihi.* → Taarifa hii ni sahihi. → "This report is accurate."

### Notes
Swahili uses the invariant copula *ni* for equative and predicative attribution; there is no agreement morphology on *ni* itself. The predicate item (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *halisi*, etc.) follows directly; there is no agreement morphology on the predicate nominal/adjective in this construction. The negative counterpart uses *si* (see §2). Both *kweli* and *sahihi* function as predicate nominals/adjectives in this slot without inflection.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negative copula *si* + truth-domain predicate. Subject + *si* + {*kweli* / *sahihi* / *halisi* / *haki* / *halali* / *bandia* / *ghushi*}.

### Examples
- *Si kweli.* → Si kweli. → "It is not true. / That is false."
- *Si sahihi kabisa.* → Si sahihi kabisa. → "It is not correct at all. / This is completely inaccurate."
- *Hii si kweli.* → Hii si kweli. → "This is not true."
- *Bidhaa hizi si halisi; ni bandia.* → Bidhaa hizi si halisi; ni bandia. → "These goods are not genuine; they are fake."
- *Jibu lako si sahihi.* → Jibu lako si sahihi. → "Your answer is not correct."

### Notes
*Si* is the suppletive negative of *ni* and is likewise invariant. *Si* is the direct negative counterpart of *ni*; the pairs *ni kweli / si kweli* and *ni sahihi / si sahihi* function as the canonical binary truth-evaluation frames in Swahili. The intensifier *kabisa* ("completely, absolutely") can be added postposed to strengthen the negation. This construction is the primary means of propositional denial in Swahili. The contrastive pairing *halisi … bandia* is a characteristic pattern in commercial and legal discourse.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Head noun + postposed truth-domain adjective (no agreement morphology on the adjective for most borrowed forms). {*sahihi* / *halisi* / *bandia* / *kweli* / *halali*} follows the noun it modifies.

**3a. Bare adjective-type modifier** (no linker): Noun + {*kweli* / *sahihi* / *halisi* / *halali* / *bandia*}

**3b. Genitive modifier** (with linker): Noun + *wa/ya* + {*asili* / *uongo* / *haki* / *kweli*}

### Examples
- *jibu sahihi* → jibu sahihi → "correct answer" *(3a)*
- *bidhaa bandia* → bidhaa bandia → "fake goods / counterfeit products" *(3a)*
- *Dhahabu halisi* → Dhahabu halisi → "Genuine gold." *(3a)*
- *dhahabu halisi* → dhahabu halisi → "genuine gold" *(3a)*
- *Shahidi wa uongo* → Shahidi wa uongo → "A false witness." *(3b)*
- *ukweli wa jambo* → ukweli wa jambo → "the truth of the matter" *(3b)*

### Notes
Arabic-derived adjectives such as *sahihi*, *halisi*, and *bandia* are invariant in Swahili; they do not take noun-class agreement prefixes. This contrasts with Bantu-root adjectives, which do agree. The pattern Head + Adjective is the standard Swahili order.

Swahili truth-domain adjectives (*halisi*, *sahihi*, *bandia*, *halali*) follow the head noun without a linker. Noun-headed modifiers require the appropriate noun-class genitive linker (*wa*, *ya*, *la*, *za*, etc.) agreeing with the head noun's class.

The associative particle (*wa*, *ya*, *la*, etc.) agrees with the noun class of the head; *wa* is the most frequent in this domain. The pattern *X wa kweli* ("true/genuine X") is highly productive: *upendo wa kweli* ("true love"), *rafiki wa kweli* ("true friend"), *mtu wa kweli* ("truthful person").

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Stative-intransitive verbal suffix *-ika* / *-eka* (or *-ka*) attached to a verb stem, expressing the process of coming to be in a truth-relevant state without an expressed agent. Also: the verb *kuwa* ("to become/be") + truth-domain nominal.

### Examples
- *Ukweli ulidhihirika.* → Ukweli ulidhihirika. → "The truth became evident. / The truth came to light."
- *Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi.* → Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi. → "The truth became evident after the investigation."
- *Imani yake ilianza kuwa na uhakika.* → Imani yake ilianza kuwa na uhakika. → "His/her belief began to have certainty. / He/she became certain."
- *Alidanganyika kwa urahisi.* → Alidanganyika kwa urahisi. → "He/she was easily taken in / became deceived."
- *Alidanganyika.* → Alidanganyika. → "He/she was deceived / fell for it."

### Notes
The *-ika* suffix (e.g., *kudhihirika* from *kudhihirisha*) is the primary inchoative device for this domain; it derives an intransitive "become evident/apparent" reading from the causative base. A periphrastic *kuwa na* + nominal construction covers cases where no *-ika* form is lexicalized.

The suffix *-ika/-eka* attaches to the verb stem and produces an intransitive stative or inchoative reading. No external agent is expressed; the change of state is presented as self-occurring. This pattern is attested for *dhahiri* and *danganya* in the truth domain; it is not attested for *kweli* or *halisi* directly. No inchoative form is attested for *kweli* or *sahihi* as bases.

The stative *-ka*/*-ika* (this pattern) and the passive *-wa* (see §8/§10) overlap in expressing resultant states but differ in agentivity: *-wa* implies an external agent; *-ka*/*-ika* does not.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Causative suffix *-sha* / *-isha* attached to a truth-domain base, yielding a transitive verb meaning "to cause X to be true/correct/certain/evident/legitimate."

Attested forms: *kuthibitisha* (to confirm/verify), *kuhakikisha* (to make certain/verify), *kusahihisha* (to correct/rectify/validate), *kudhihirisha* (to make evident/demonstrate), *kuhalalisha* (to legitimize/make lawful), *kuharamisha* (to prohibit/criminalize).

### Examples
- *Tafadhali thibitisha taarifa hii.* → Tafadhali thibitisha taarifa hii. → "Please confirm / verify this report."
- *Hakikisha mlango umefungwa.* → Hakikisha mlango umefungwa. → "Make sure the door is closed. / Ensure the door is closed."
- *Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya wanafunzi.* → Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya wanafunzi. → "The teacher corrected the students' errors."
- *Serikali inahalalisha mkataba huo.* → Serikali inahalalisha mkataba huo. → "The government is legitimizing that contract."
- *Matendo yake yalidhihirisha tabia yake.* → Matendo yake yalidhihirisha tabia yake. → "His actions revealed / made evident his character."
- *Sahihisha makosa kabla ya kuwasilisha.* → Sahihisha makosa kabla ya kuwasilisha. → "Correct the errors before submitting."

### Notes
The causative is highly productive in this domain: *kuthibitisha* (to confirm/prove), *kuhakikisha* (to make certain/verify), *kusahihisha* (to correct/rectify), *kudhihirisha* (to make evident/demonstrate), *kuhalalisha* (to legitimize), *kuharamisha* (to prohibit/criminalize). All attach *-sha* to an Arabic-derived base. *kuhakikisha* extends into purely procedural contexts unrelated to truth evaluation.

No causative from *kweli* itself (*kukwelisha*) is attested; the causative function for truth-making is carried by *kuthibitisha*. The roots *kweli* and *halisi* do not show well-attested causative forms in common usage.

The causative suffix is productive across the truth domain for bases that admit an external agent bringing about a state. The suffix *-sha* / *-isha* is productive for roots that permit verbal derivation: *dhahiri* → *kudhihirisha* ("to make evident, to demonstrate"), *sahihi* → *kusahihisha* ("to correct, to make accurate"), *fanana* → *kufananisha* ("to compare, to liken"), *tofauti* → *kutofautisha* ("to distinguish").

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Class 11/14 prefix *u-* + truth-domain base, yielding an abstract noun denoting the property or quality. Alternatively, *-o* suffix on a causative stem yields a process/result nominal.

Attested: *ukweli* (truth/truthfulness), *uhalisi* (reality/genuineness/authenticity), *uhalali* (legitimacy/lawfulness), *uaminifu* (trustworthiness/faithfulness), *usahihi* (correctness/accuracy), *udanganyifu* (deceptiveness/deception), *udhahiri* (obviousness), *uasili* (originality/nativeness), *ubandia* (fakeness), *uhakika* (certainty), *ulaghai* (fraud), *ubora* (quality), *utofauti* (difference).

Process/result nominals with *-o*: *uthibitisho* (confirmation/verification/proof), *uhakikisho* (verification), *udhihirisho* (demonstration/manifestation), *usahihishaji* (correction process).

### Examples
- *ukweli* → ukweli → "truth, truthfulness" (*u-* + *kweli*)
- *uthibitisho* → uthibitisho → "confirmation, verification, proof" (*u-* + *thibitish-* + *-o*)
- *Ukweli wa jambo hili haujulikani.* → Ukweli wa jambo hili haujulikani. → "The truth of this matter is not known."
- *Uhalali wa uchaguzi unapingwa mahakamani.* → Uhalali wa uchaguzi unapingwa mahakamani. → "The legitimacy of the election is being contested in court."
- *Usahihi wa taarifa unahitajika.* → Usahihi wa taarifa unahitajika. → "The accuracy of the report is required."
- *Udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani.* → Udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani. → "His/her deception was exposed publicly."

### Notes
The *u-* prefix is the most consistent derivational pattern across this domain, attested on *kweli*, *halisi*, *sahihi*, *hakika*, *aminifu*, *halali*, *sawa*, and *haki*. The *u-* prefix is highly productive across this domain. The resulting nominals take class 11 subject-agreement prefixes. They appear as heads of genitive phrases specifying the domain of the property (*ukweli wa mambo*, *uhalali wa kisheria*).

The *-o* nominalizer on causative stems (*uthibitisho*, *uhakikisho*, *udhihirisho*, *usahihishaji*) yields result/process nominals and is a secondary but productive pattern.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**7a. Person prefix *m-* / *mw-* (singular) / Class 2 *wa-* (plural) + stem (optionally with *-aji* or *-fu* suffix):** produces a noun denoting a person characterized by the relevant property.

Attested: *muongo / waongo* (liar/s), *mwaminifu / waaminifu* (trustworthy/faithful person/people), *mwamini* (believer), *mdanganyifu / wadanganyifu* (deceiver/s), *mheshimiwa* (honored/respected person), *shahidi* (witness), *msahihishaji / wasahihishaji* (examiner-marker/s, corrector/s), *mlaghai / walaghai* (fraudster/s), *mkosoaji / wakosoaji* (critic/s), *mkosaji / wakosaji* (offender/s).

**7b. Relative clause / periphrastic construction** used where no lexicalized agentive nominal exists.

Attested: *mtu wa kuaminiwa* (a person to be trusted), *mtu anayesema kweli* (a person who tells the truth), *mtu wa kweli* (a person of truth).

### Examples
- *mdanganyifu / wadanganyifu* → mdanganyifu / wadanganyifu → "deceiver / deceivers, liar / liars"
- *msahihishaji / wasahihishaji* → msahihishaji / wasahihishaji → "examiner-marker / examiner-markers, corrector / correctors"
- *Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu.* → Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu. → "He/she is a consummate liar." *(7a)*
- *Tunahitaji mtu wa kuaminiwa.* → Tunahitaji mtu wa kuaminiwa. → "We need a trustworthy person / a person to be trusted." *(7b)*
- *Yeye ni mwongo — hasemi ukweli kamwe.* → Yeye ni mwongo — hasemi ukweli kamwe. → "He/she is a liar — never speaks the truth."
- *Yeye ni mdanganyifu wa hali ya juu.* → Yeye ni mdanganyifu wa hali ya juu. → "He/she is a thoroughgoing deceiver."

### Notes
Two sub-patterns are attested: (a) *m-/wa-* + adjectival stem (*mdanganyifu*, *mwaminifu*), and (b) *m-/wa-* + causative stem + *-aji* (*msahihishaji*, and by parallel *mghushi/waghushi* without *-aji*).

No agentive nominal for "truth-teller" built directly on *kweli* or *ukweli* is identified as a common fixed lexical item; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*mtu anayesema ukweli*, "a person who tells the truth"; *mtu wa kweli*, "a person of truth").

The *-fu* suffix in *mwaminifu* and *mdanganyifu* marks a habitual or characterological quality rather than a momentary act. Plural is formed by replacing *m-/mw-* with *wa-* (*waongo*, *waaminifu*, *wadanganyifu*).

*Shahidi* is from Arabic but functions as an agentive nominal in Swahili.

## 8. PASSIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Passive suffix *-wa* attached to the verb stem. Used extensively in the truth domain to express that a claim, document, or person has been verified, confirmed, corrected, or deceived by an external agent.

Attested: *kuthibitishwa* (to be confirmed/verified/authenticated), *kusahihishwa* (to be corrected/validated), *kuhalalishwa* (to be legitimized), *kudanganywa* (to be deceived), *kuaminiwa* (to be trusted), *kuheshimiwa* (to be respected), *kufunuliwa* (to be revealed).

### Examples
- *Habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi.* → Habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi. → "The news has been confirmed by multiple sources."
- *Nilidanganywa na maneno yake.* → Nilidanganywa na maneno yake. → "I was deceived by his/her words."
- *Hati ilithibitishwa na mamlaka.* → Hati ilithibitishwa na mamlaka. → "The document was authenticated by the authorities."

### Notes
The agent is introduced by *na* (by). The passive is highly productive across all causative and transitive truth-domain verbs. It is the standard construction in journalistic and legal discourse for reporting verification outcomes.

The stative *-ka*/*-ika* (§4) and the passive *-wa* overlap in expressing resultant states but differ in agentivity: *-wa* implies an external agent; *-ka*/*-ika* does not.

## 9. ADVERBIAL FRAMING

### Pattern
Prepositional particle *kwa* + truth-domain nominal, forming a sentence-level adverbial that frames the epistemic or sincerity status of the following assertion.

Attested: *kwa kweli* (in truth/honestly/truly), *kwa kweli kweli* (truly and sincerely — intensified by reduplication), *kwa haki* (justly/rightly/in truth), *kwa dhahiri* (evidently/clearly), *kwa uhakika* (with certainty/definitely), *kwa usahihi* (accurately), *kwa uaminifu* (faithfully/sincerely).

Parallel negative-framing pattern: *bila* + {*shaka*, *uhakika*} → *bila shaka* ("without doubt").

### Examples
- *Kwa kweli nakuambia.* → Kwa kweli nakuambia. → "I tell you truly. / Truly, I tell you."
- *Kwa uhakika, hii ilitokea.* → Kwa uhakika, hii ilitokea. → "With certainty, this happened. / This definitely happened."
- *Kwa kweli, sikujua chochote.* → Kwa kweli, sikujua chochote. → "Honestly, I knew nothing."
- *Kwa dhahiri, tatizo hili ni kubwa.* → Kwa dhahiri, tatizo hili ni kubwa. → "Evidently, this problem is serious."

### Notes
*Kwa* + {*kweli*, *kweli kweli*, *hakika*, *uhakika*, *usahihi*, *uaminifu*} is a productive adverbial pattern. These phrases function as sentence-initial or pre-verbal epistemic/sincerity frames and are not morphologically derived forms.

*Kwa kweli* and *kwa kweli kweli* function as sincerity markers; *kwa dhahiri* functions as an evidential marker of self-evidence. Reduplication intensifies: *kwa kweli* → *kwa kweli kweli*.

*Bila* + {*shaka*, *uhakika*} forms a parallel negative-framing pattern (*bila shaka*, "without doubt").

## 10. EVIDENTIAL / SELF-EVIDENCE PREDICATE

### Pattern
*Ni dhahiri kwamba* + complement clause: "It is evident/clear that…"

Also: *inaonekana kwamba* (it appears that…) as a weaker evidential.

### Examples
- *Ni dhahiri kwamba alikosea.* → Ni dhahiri kwamba alikosea. → "It is evident that he/she erred."
- *Ni dhahiri kabisa kwamba ushahidi unatosha.* → Ni dhahiri kabisa kwamba ushahidi unatosha. → "It is absolutely clear that the evidence is sufficient."

### Notes
*Ni dhahiri* functions as a raising predicate taking a *kwamba*-complement clause. The intensifier *kabisa* (absolutely/completely) may be inserted between *dhahiri* and *kwamba*. This construction is characteristic of argumentative and legal discourse where the speaker presents a conclusion as requiring no further support.

*Inaonekana kwamba* (it appears that…) falls outside the core truth-domain items.

## 11. PERIPHRASTIC VERBAL CONSTRUCTION WITH LIGHT VERB

### Pattern
**11a.** Light verb *piga* ("to strike/do") + truth-domain nominal object, expressing an act. Primarily attested for *uongo*.

**11b.** General-purpose verb (*sema*, *toa*, *tafuta*, *ficha*, *potosha*, *kukusanya*, *pinga*) + truth-domain noun as direct object. No single derived verb exists for many of these actions.

### Examples
- *Anapiga uongo.* → Anapiga uongo. → "He/she is lying. / He/she tells lies." *(11a)*
- *Usipige uongo.* → Usipige uongo. → "Don't lie. / Don't tell lies." *(11a)*
- *Kusema ukweli daima ni wajibu.* → Kusema ukweli daima ni wajibu. → "To always tell the truth is a duty." *(11b)*
- *Walikusanya ushahidi kwa miezi mingi.* → Walikusanya ushahidi kwa miezi mingi. → "They gathered evidence for many months." *(11b)*

### Notes
*Piga uongo* is the most colloquial and frequent construction for the act of lying in spoken Swahili, more common in informal registers than the morphologically derived *kudanganya*. No parallel *piga kweli* construction is attested; the "tell the truth" directive uses *sema ukweli* (imperative of *kusema*, "to speak/say") instead.

This pattern compensates for the absence of lexicalized single-word verbs for "to truth-tell," "to evidence," and "to seek-truth." The object noun (*ukweli*, *ushahidi*, *makosa*) carries the semantic content; the light verb specifies the action type (speaking, providing, seeking, hiding, distorting, gathering, contesting). The pattern is productive and not restricted to fixed collocations.

## 12. POSSESSIVE EPISTEMIC CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Subject prefix + *-na-* ("have") + truth-domain nominal, expressing the speaker's epistemic state as possession of certainty or doubt.

### Examples
- *Sina uhakika.* → Sina uhakika. → "I am not sure. / I have no certainty."
- *Nina uhakika.* → Nina uhakika. → "I am sure. / I have certainty."

### Notes
Swahili expresses epistemic states such as certainty and doubt through a "have/not-have" construction rather than through stative adjectives or modal verbs. *Nina/Sina uhakika* is the primary everyday formula for asserting or denying personal certainty. No parallel *nina kweli* construction is attested for propositional truth.

---

## 13. COMPLEMENTIZER FRAMING

### Pattern
Copular or evidential frame + *kwamba* / *kuwa* + embedded clause.

### Examples
- *Ukweli ni kwamba hakuna ushahidi.* → Ukweli ni kwamba hakuna ushahidi. → "The truth is that there is no evidence."
- *Ni dhahiri kwamba alikuwa anajua.* → Ni dhahiri kwamba alikuwa anajua. → "It is evident that he/she knew."

### Notes
The complementizers *kwamba* and *kuwa* ("that") introduce embedded propositional content after a truth-domain predicate. The frames *ukweli ni kwamba...* and *ni dhahiri kwamba...* are highly productive in journalistic, legal, and argumentative discourse for introducing asserted or evidentially grounded propositions.

---

## 14. PARENTHETICAL SINCERITY FRAME

### Pattern
Infinitive phrase (*kusema ukweli* / *kwa kweli*) + comma + main clause, functioning as a sentence-initial discourse marker signaling speaker sincerity.

### Examples
- *Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.* → Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo. → "To tell the truth, I didn't like that."
- *Kwa kweli, sijui.* → Kwa kweli, sijui. → "Honestly, I don't know."

### Notes
These sentence-initial phrases function as discourse markers signaling speaker sincerity rather than as predicates about a specific proposition. *Kusema ukweli* (infinitive: "to speak the truth") and *kwa kweli* (prepositional: "in truth") are the two most frequent forms; *kwa kweli kweli* is an intensified variant. These phrases are not morphologically integrated into the verb complex.
