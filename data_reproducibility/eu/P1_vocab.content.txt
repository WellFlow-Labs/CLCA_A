# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Basque (eu)

1. **egia**
   - Literal meaning: truth
   - Typical English translations: truth, true, reality
   - Usage: The fundamental term for truth in Basque, used both as a noun ("the truth") and in predicative constructions with izan ("to be"). Appears in everyday speech, philosophical discourse, and legal contexts to denote what is factually accurate or corresponds to reality.
   - [CORE]

2. **egiazkoa**
   - Literal meaning: egia (truth) + -zko (relational suffix) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: true, genuine, authentic, real
   - Usage: Adjectival form used to describe something as truthful, genuine, or authentic. Commonly applied to statements, objects, and persons to indicate they are what they claim or appear to be.
   - [CORE]

3. **gezurra**
   - Literal meaning: lie, falsehood
   - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
   - Usage: The standard term for a deliberate falsehood or lie. Used in everyday contexts to describe false statements and in moral discourse about dishonesty.
   - [CORE]

4. **gezurrezko**
   - Literal meaning: gezurra (lie) + -zko (relational suffix)
   - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit
   - Usage: Adjectival form meaning false or fake, used to describe statements, objects, or representations that are not genuine or do not correspond to reality.
   - [CORE]

5. **zuzena**
   - Literal meaning: zuzen (straight, correct) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, straight, just
   - Usage: Polysemous term used for both physical straightness and abstract correctness. Applied to answers, conduct, and moral judgments to indicate rightness or propriety.
   - [CORE]

6. **okerra**
   - Literal meaning: oker (wrong, crooked) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: wrong, incorrect, crooked, mistaken
   - Usage: Opposite of zuzena, used for both physical crookedness and abstract incorrectness. Describes errors, mistakes, and morally wrong actions.
   - [CORE]

7. **egiazkotasuna**
   - Literal meaning: egiazkoa (true) + -tasun (abstract noun suffix) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: truthfulness, authenticity, veracity
   - Usage: Abstract noun denoting the quality of being true or authentic. Used in formal contexts to discuss the truthfulness of statements or authenticity of objects.
   - [CORE]

8. **fidagarria**
   - Literal meaning: fida (trust, from Spanish "fiar") + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: reliable, trustworthy, dependable
   - Usage: Describes persons, sources, or things worthy of trust or confidence. Commonly used in evaluating the reliability of information or the dependability of people.
   - [CORE]

9. **sinesgarria**
   - Literal meaning: sines (belief) + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)
   - Typical English translations: believable, credible, plausible
   - Usage: Indicates that something is worthy of belief or credible. Applied to statements, accounts, and explanations to assess their plausibility.
   - [CORE]

10. **benetakoa**
    - Literal meaning: benetan (truly, really) + -ko (relational) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: real, genuine, actual, true
    - Usage: Emphasizes genuineness or reality, often contrasting with appearance or pretense. Used to affirm that something is truly what it purports to be.
    - [CORE]

11. **ziurra**
    - Literal meaning: certain, sure
    - Typical English translations: certain, sure, secure, reliable
    - Usage: Indicates certainty, security, or reliability. Used both epistemically (to express certainty about knowledge) and to describe dependable things or people.
    - [CORE]

12. **faltsua**
    - Literal meaning: false (from Latin falsus)
    - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit, deceitful
    - Usage: Borrowed term used to describe falseness, particularly in formal or technical contexts. Applied to counterfeit objects, false statements, and deceitful behavior.
    - [CORE]

13. **iruzurra**
    - Literal meaning: fraud, deception
    - Typical English translations: fraud, deception, swindle, deceit
    - Usage: Denotes deliberate deception or fraud, particularly in legal and commercial contexts. Stronger than gezurra, implying systematic or calculated dishonesty.
    - [CORE]

14. **antzekoa**
    - Literal meaning: antze (resemblance) + -ko (relational) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: similar, resembling, like
    - Usage: Indicates resemblance or similarity between things. Used in comparative contexts to note likeness without claiming identity.
    - [CORE]

15. **berdina**
    - Literal meaning: equal, same
    - Typical English translations: same, equal, identical
    - Usage: Denotes sameness, equality, or identity. Used in mathematical, legal, and everyday contexts to indicate that things are identical or equivalent.
    - [CORE]

16. **zuzentasuna**
    - Literal meaning: zuzen (correct) + -tasun (abstract noun suffix) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: correctness, rightness, justice, rectitude
    - Usage: Abstract noun denoting correctness or rightness, extending to moral and legal justice. Used in ethical and legal discourse about proper conduct.
    - [CORE]

17. **egokia**
    - Literal meaning: egoki (appropriate, suitable) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: appropriate, suitable, fitting, proper
    - Usage: Indicates appropriateness or suitability for a purpose or context. Used in evaluating whether something fits the circumstances or meets standards.
    - [CORE]

18. **zehatza**
    - Literal meaning: precise, exact
    - Typical English translations: precise, exact, accurate, specific
    - Usage: Denotes precision, exactness, or accuracy. Applied to measurements, descriptions, and statements to indicate they are detailed and accurate.
    - [CORE]

19. **zintzoa**
    - Literal meaning: honest, sincere
    - Typical English translations: honest, sincere, upright, fair
    - Usage: Describes honest or sincere persons and conduct. Central term in moral evaluation of character and behavior, emphasizing integrity.
    - [CORE]

20. **itxurazko**
    - Literal meaning: itxura (appearance) + -zko (relational suffix)
    - Typical English translations: apparent, seeming, ostensible
    - Usage: Indicates something is based on appearance rather than reality. Used to qualify statements or descriptions as being how things seem rather than how they are.
    - [CORE]

21. **benetan**
    - Literal meaning: bene (very) + -tan (locative/manner suffix)
    - Typical English translations: truly, really, indeed, actually
    - Usage: Adverbial expression emphasizing truth or reality. Used to affirm statements or contrast reality with appearance or expectation.
    - [CORE]

22. **funtsean**
    - Literal meaning: funtse (foundation, essence) + -an (locative suffix)
    - Typical English translations: fundamentally, essentially, basically, at bottom
    - Usage: Adverbial expression indicating what something is at its core or in essence. Used to identify fundamental nature beyond superficial characteristics.
    - [CORE]

23. **ageria**
    - Literal meaning: ager (visible, manifest) + -i (adjective suffix) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: evident, obvious, manifest, clear
    - Usage: Describes what is clearly visible or evident. Used to indicate that something is manifest or apparent to observation or reason.
    - [CORE]

24. **zalantzagarria**
    - Literal meaning: zalantza (doubt) + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: doubtful, dubious, questionable
    - Usage: Indicates something is worthy of doubt or questionable. Applied to claims, evidence, or conduct that raises suspicion or uncertainty.
    - [CORE]

25. **egiazkotua**
    - Literal meaning: egiazkotu (to verify) + -a (participle/article)
    - Typical English translations: verified, confirmed, authenticated
    - Usage: Participial form indicating something has been verified or confirmed as true. Used in formal contexts to describe validated information or authenticated objects.
    - [CORE]

26. **frogagarria**
    - Literal meaning: froga (proof) + -garri (worthy of/able to be) + -a (article)
    - Typical English translations: provable, demonstrable, verifiable
    - Usage: Indicates something can be proven or demonstrated. Used in logical, scientific, and legal contexts to describe claims that can be substantiated.
    - [CORE]

27. **hutsa**
    - Literal meaning: empty, mere, pure
    - Typical English translations: mere, pure, empty, sheer
    - Usage: Polysemous term that can indicate emptiness, purity, or mere appearance. In evaluative contexts, often used to dismiss something as "mere" or lacking substance.
    - [CORE]

28. **benetako**
    - Literal meaning: benetan (truly) + -ko (relational suffix)
    - Typical English translations: real, true, genuine, actual
    - Usage: Adjectival form emphasizing genuineness or reality. Used attributively to modify nouns, indicating they are authentic or real rather than apparent or false.
    - [CORE]
