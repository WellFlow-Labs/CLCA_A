# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgia (2002), as used in Georgian passports and official romanization, supplemented by standard academic convention for Georgian studies. Applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine quality) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and propositions, and also to characterize persons as truthful. Participates in productive derivation: č̣ešmariteba (truth, as abstract noun), č̣ešmaritad (truly, adverb). One of the most central terms for propositional and personal truth in Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in philosophical, theological, and everyday discourse. Appears in fixed expressions and proverbs. Widely used across registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominal prefix) + martal- (right, correct, just) + -e (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, justice, rightness
**Usage:** Frequently used interchangeably with č̣ešmariteba in everyday speech, though simartle often carries a stronger sense of factual correctness or fairness in a given situation. Also used in legal and moral contexts to mean what is right or just. The root martal- is independently productive.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** root martal- + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, true, just, honest
**Usage:** Applied to persons (an honest or just person), statements (a correct claim), and situations (a fair outcome). Closely related to simartle and participates in the same derivational family. Common in both colloquial and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -e
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, forgery, counterfeit quality
**Usage:** Denotes the quality of being false or counterfeit, applicable to documents, currency, statements, and persons. The root q̇albi is borrowed from Persian/Arabic via Turkish and is widely used. Contrasts directly with simartle and č̣ešmariteba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** root q̇alb- + adjectival suffix -i (loanword base)
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged
**Usage:** Applied to documents, banknotes, signatures, and statements to indicate inauthenticity or forgery. Common in legal and everyday contexts. Productive base for siq̇albe and the verb q̇alboba (to forge, to falsify).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** na- (participial/resultative prefix) + mdvil- (to place, establish) + -i; often glossed as "what has been established/settled"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** The primary adjective for authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, and states of affairs. Namdvili megobar- means a true/genuine friend; namdvili dokumenti means an authentic document. Widely used across all registers and highly productive.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** genuineness, authenticity, reality
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from namdvili, used in legal, philosophical, and administrative contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in discussions of document verification and ontological status.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (lie, false) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The standard noun for a lie or falsehood in everyday and moral discourse. Contrasts with simartle. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in proverbs about honesty. The root cru- also yields the verb tkma (to tell a lie) in combination.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მცდარი
**Transliteration:** mcdari
**Literal meaning:** m- (participial prefix) + cdar- (to err, to be mistaken) + -i
**Typical English translations:** erroneous, mistaken, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to beliefs, judgments, and statements that are factually or logically incorrect. Distinguished from q̇albi (which implies deliberate falsification) in that mcdari typically implies error rather than deception. Used in academic, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** შეცდომა
**Transliteration:** šecdoma
**Literal meaning:** še- (preverb) + cdom- (to err) + -a (verbal noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault
**Usage:** The standard noun for an error or mistake, applicable to factual, moral, and practical domains. Closely related to mcdari through the same root. Widely used in everyday speech, education, and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** სანდო
**Transliteration:** sando
**Literal meaning:** san- (prefix of quality/capacity) + do (from Persian/Turkish borrowing, trustworthy); alternatively analyzed as a loanword base
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources of information, institutions, and objects to indicate that they can be trusted or relied upon. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts. Participates in derivation: sandooba (reliability, trustworthiness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** სანდოობა
**Transliteration:** sandooba
**Literal meaning:** sando + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the property of being reliable or credible. Used in evaluations of witnesses, sources, institutions, and evidence. Appears in legal and journalistic registers with some frequency.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** უტყუარი
**Transliteration:** uṭq̇uari
**Literal meaning:** u- (privative prefix) + ṭq̇u- (lie, deception) + -ar- + -i
**Typical English translations:** infallible, indubitable, certain, irrefutable, unerring
**Usage:** Used to characterize evidence, proofs, or statements as beyond doubt or incapable of being false. Stronger than simply "true"; implies that no deception or error is possible. Found in legal, theological, and formal written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** გულწრფელი
**Transliteration:** gulc̣rpeli
**Literal meaning:** gul- (heart) + c̣rpel- (straight, sincere) + -i
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt, candid
**Usage:** Applied to persons, expressions, and intentions to indicate sincerity and absence of pretense. Evaluates the authenticity of emotional or communicative acts rather than propositional truth. Common in everyday and literary registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** გულწრფელობა
**Transliteration:** gulc̣rpeloba
**Literal meaning:** gulc̣rpeli + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** sincerity, candor, genuineness of feeling
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the quality of sincerity. Used in moral and interpersonal evaluations. Less common than the adjective but well attested in written Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** პირმოთნე
**Transliteration:** pirmoṭne
**Literal meaning:** pir- (face, mouth) + moṭne (one who flatters or dissembles); compound suggesting face-showing or dissembling
**Typical English translations:** hypocrite, dissembler, two-faced person
**Usage:** Characterizes a person who presents a false face — who says one thing and means another, or who behaves differently in public than in private. Used in moral condemnation. Related to the broader evaluation of sincerity and authenticity of persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** თვალთმაქცი
**Transliteration:** tvaltmaq̇ci
**Literal meaning:** tval- (eye) + t- + maq̇c- (deceiver, trickster) + -i; "one who tricks the eye"
**Typical English translations:** deceiver, illusionist, fraud, impostor
**Usage:** Refers to a person who creates false appearances or illusions, whether literally (a conjurer) or figuratively (a fraud or impostor). Used in moral and legal contexts to characterize deceptive conduct. Also used for stage magicians in a neutral sense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** მოჩვენებითი
**Transliteration:** močvenebiti
**Literal meaning:** mo- (preverb) + čven- (to show, appear) + -ebit- + -i; "of the nature of mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, illusory, ostensible, spurious
**Usage:** Applied to qualities, agreements, or states that appear to be something but are not genuinely so. Used in philosophical, legal, and rhetorical contexts to distinguish mere appearance from reality. Contrasts with namdvili.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avṭenturi
**Literal meaning:** loanword from Greek/international: authentikos
**Typical English translations:** authentic
**Usage:** A more recent borrowing used primarily in academic, archival, legal, and journalistic registers. Applied to documents, artworks, and texts. Coexists with namdvili but tends to appear in more formal or technical contexts involving verification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ავთენტურობა
**Transliteration:** avṭenturoba
**Literal meaning:** avṭenturi + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity
**Usage:** Abstract noun corresponding to avṭenturi. Used in archival science, art authentication, legal document verification, and philosophical discourse. More technical in register than namdviloba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarc̣muno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (prefix of capacity/suitability) + rc̣mun- (to believe, to trust) + -o
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable, trustworthy, reliable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, testimony, sources, and evidence to indicate that they merit belief. Used in legal contexts for credible witnesses and in everyday speech for reliable information. Closely related to sando but with a stronger emphasis on the capacity to be believed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** სარწმუნოება
**Transliteration:** sarc̣munoeba
**Literal meaning:** sarc̣muno + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** credibility, trustworthiness; also: religion, faith (in theological contexts)
**Usage:** In secular contexts, denotes credibility or trustworthiness. In theological and ecclesiastical contexts, the same form means religion or faith. This polysemy is well established and context-dependent. Both senses are widely attested.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** მიმსგავსება
**Transliteration:** mimsgavseba
**Literal meaning:** mi- (preverb) + m- + sgavs- (to resemble) + -eba (verbal noun)
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, likeness, similarity
**Usage:** Denotes the relation of resemblance between two things. Used in aesthetic, legal (e.g., resemblance to a person in identification), and everyday contexts. The root sgavs- is productive: msgavsi (similar, resembling), msgavseba (similarity).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** მსგავსი
**Transliteration:** msgavsi
**Literal meaning:** m- + sgavs- (resemble) + -i
**Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, like, akin
**Usage:** Standard adjective for similarity or resemblance. Used across all registers and domains. Participates in the same derivational family as mimsgavseba. Relevant in contexts of evaluating whether something resembles or corresponds to something else.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** არსი
**Transliteration:** arsi
**Literal meaning:** from ar- (to be) + nominal suffix; "that which is, being"
**Typical English translations:** essence, nature, substance, the fundamental nature of something
**Usage:** Used in philosophical, theological, and analytical discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is — its essential nature. Appears in discussions of the essence of a concept, person, or phenomenon. Also used in everyday speech in the sense of "the point" or "the gist" of something.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** არსებითი
**Transliteration:** arsebiti
**Literal meaning:** arseb- (existing, essential) + -it- + -i
**Typical English translations:** essential, substantial, fundamental, material (in the legal sense)
**Usage:** Applied to qualities, differences, and features that are fundamental or constitutive rather than accidental. Used in legal contexts (material fact), philosophical contexts (essential property), and everyday speech (essential point). Derived from the same root as arsi.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** იდენტური
**Transliteration:** identuri
**Literal meaning:** loanword from Latin/international: identicus
**Typical English translations:** identical
**Usage:** Used in legal, scientific, and administrative contexts to assert that two things are one and the same, or exactly alike. More technical than msgavsi (similar). Appears in identity verification, legal documents, and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** loanword (cf. Russian точный, but the Georgian form is well established); possibly from Persian
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate, correct
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, translations, reproductions, statements, and descriptions to indicate precision and accuracy. Widely used across all registers. Participates in derivation: zustoba (exactness, precision), zustad (exactly, precisely, adverb).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- + -e
**Typical English translations:** exactness, precision, accuracy
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the quality of being exact or accurate. Used in scientific, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate the degree to which a statement, measurement, or reproduction corresponds to what it represents. Closely paired with zusti.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
