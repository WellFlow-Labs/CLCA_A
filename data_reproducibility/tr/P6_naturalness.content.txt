# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## DYNAMIC EXPRESSIONS IN THE TRUTH DOMAIN

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE/CORRECT (Inchoative: Positive State)

### 1.1 Factual Correctness Domain

**Pattern:** [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **doğru çıkmak**
- Transliteration: doğru çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be true/correct"
- Usage: Indicates that something previously uncertain or disputed has been verified as true. Very common in narrative and evaluative contexts.
- Example: *Tahminleri doğru çıktı.* "His/her predictions turned out to be true."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **doğrulanmak** (passive of doğrulamak)
- Transliteration: doğrulanmak
- Translation: "to be verified, to be confirmed"
- Usage: Passive construction indicating that something undergoes verification and is confirmed as true. Common in scientific and administrative contexts.
- Example: *Bilgiler doğrulandı.* "The information was verified."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [ERROR/MISTAKE] + **düzeltilmek**
- Transliteration: düzeltilmek
- Translation: "to be corrected, to be fixed"
- Usage: Indicates correction of errors, moving from wrong to right state. Common across all registers.
- Example: *Hatalar düzeltildi.* "The errors were corrected."
- [CORE]

**Note:** Turkish does not have a common expression *"doğru olmak"* meaning "to become true" in the sense of a gradual process. The language prefers resultative *çıkmak* constructions or passive verification forms.

---

### 1.2 Reality/Authenticity Domain

**Pattern:** [PLAN/DREAM/PREDICTION] + **gerçekleşmek**
- Transliteration: gerçekleşmek
- Translation: "to come true, to be realized, to materialize"
- Usage: Extremely common inchoative indicating transition from potential/imagined to actual. Used for plans, dreams, predictions, and events becoming real.
- Example: *Hayalleri gerçekleşti.* "His/her dreams came true."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [SITUATION/FACT] + **gerçek olmak**
- Transliteration: gerçek olmak
- Translation: "to be real, to turn out to be real"
- Usage: Copular construction asserting reality. Can indicate discovery that something is real rather than fake/imagined.
- Example: *Bu hikâye gerçek olabilir mi?* "Could this story be real?"
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [IDENTITY/NATURE] + **ortaya çıkmak**
- Transliteration: ortaya çıkmak
- Translation: "to come to light, to be revealed, to emerge"
- Usage: Indicates revelation or discovery of true nature/identity. Common with *gerçek*, *hakikat*, *gerçek yüz*.
- Example: *Gerçek yüzü ortaya çıktı.* "His/her true face came to light."
- [CORE]

---

### 1.3 Certainty/Definiteness Domain

**Pattern:** [DECISION/PLAN] + **kesinleşmek**
- Transliteration: kesinleşmek
- Translation: "to become definite, to be finalized, to be confirmed"
- Usage: Common in administrative and planning contexts. Indicates transition from tentative to definite state.
- Example: *Tarih kesinleşti.* "The date has been finalized."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [DOUBT] + **giderilmek**
- Transliteration: giderilmek
- Translation: "to be removed, to be dispelled"
- Usage: Used with *şüphe*, *kuşku* to indicate removal of doubt, resulting in certainty.
- Example: *Şüpheler giderildi.* "The doubts were dispelled."
- [CORE]

---

### 1.4 Reliability/Trustworthiness Domain

**Pattern:** [SOURCE/PERSON] + **güvenilir hale gelmek**
- Transliteration: güvenilir hale gelmek
- Translation: "to become reliable/trustworthy"
- Usage: Explicit construction using *hale gelmek* "to become (into a state)." Less common than other inchoatives but attested.
- Example: *Kaynak zamanla güvenilir hale geldi.* "The source became reliable over time."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [ARGUMENT/STRUCTURE] + **sağlamlaşmak**
- Transliteration: sağlamlaşmak
- Translation: "to become solid/sound, to be strengthened, to solidify"
- Usage: Indicates strengthening or solidification of arguments, evidence, or structures. Common in both physical and metaphorical contexts.
- Example: *Kanıtlar sağlamlaştı.* "The evidence became stronger/more solid."
- [CORE]

---

### 1.5 Justice/Rights Domain

**Pattern:** [PERSON/POSITION] + **haklı çıkmak**
- Transliteration: haklı çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be right, to be proven right, to be vindicated"
- Usage: Very common expression for vindication in disputes or predictions. Indicates being proven correct or justified.
- Example: *Sonunda haklı çıktı.* "In the end, he/she turned out to be right."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [SITUATION] + **adil hale gelmek**
- Transliteration: adil hale gelmek
- Translation: "to become just/fair"
- Usage: Explicit construction for transition to just state. Less common than other patterns.
- Example: *Sistem daha adil hale geldi.* "The system became more just."
- [CORE]

---

### 1.6 Legitimacy Domain

**Pattern:** [ACTION/CLAIM/AUTHORITY] + **meşrulaşmak**
- Transliteration: meşrulaşmak
- Translation: "to become legitimate, to be legitimized"
- Usage: Common in political and legal discourse. Indicates gaining legitimacy or recognition as lawful.
- Example: *Hareket zamanla meşrulaştı.* "The movement became legitimate over time."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [DOCUMENT/TICKET] + **geçerli olmak**
- Transliteration: geçerli olmak
- Translation: "to be valid, to become valid"
- Usage: Copular construction for validity. Can indicate coming into force or being recognized as valid.
- Example: *Bilet yarından itibaren geçerli olacak.* "The ticket will be valid starting tomorrow."
- [CORE]

## 2. BECOMING FALSE/WRONG (Inchoative: Negative State)

### 2.1 Factual Incorrectness Domain

**Pattern:** [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **yanlış çıkmak**
- Transliteration: yanlış çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be wrong/incorrect"
- Usage: Common resultative construction indicating that something has been shown to be incorrect. Parallel to *doğru çıkmak*.
- Example: *Tahminleri yanlış çıktı.* "His/her predictions turned out to be wrong."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **yanlışlanmak** (passive of yanlışlamak)
- Transliteration: yanlışlanmak
- Translation: "to be falsified, to be proven wrong"
- Usage: Passive construction indicating that something is demonstrated to be false. Common in academic and argumentative contexts.
- Example: *Teori yanlışlandı.* "The theory was falsified."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [INFORMATION] + **yanlış olmak**
- Transliteration: yanlış olmak
- Translation: "to be wrong, to turn out to be wrong"
- Usage: Copular construction asserting incorrectness. Can indicate discovery of error.
- Example: *Bu bilgi yanlış olabilir.* "This information might be wrong."
- [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Inauthenticity Domain

**Pattern:** [OBJECT/DOCUMENT] + **sahte çıkmak**
- Transliteration: sahte çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be fake/counterfeit"
- Usage: Common resultative construction indicating discovery of inauthenticity.
- Example: *Para sahte çıktı.* "The money turned out to be counterfeit."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [CLAIM/APPEARANCE] + **yalan çıkmak**
- Transliteration: yalan çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be a lie, to be exposed as false"
- Usage: Very common expression for exposure of lies or false claims.
- Example: *Söyledikleri yalan çıktı.* "What he/she said turned out to be a lie."
- [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Loss of Reliability

**Pattern:** [SOURCE/PERSON] + **güvenilmez hale gelmek**
- Transliteration: güvenilmez hale gelmek
- Translation: "to become unreliable/untrustworthy"
- Usage: Explicit construction for loss of trustworthiness. Less common than positive counterpart.
- Example: *Kaynak güvenilmez hale geldi.* "The source became unreliable."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [TRUST] + **sarsılmak**
- Transliteration: sarsılmak
- Translation: "to be shaken, to be undermined"
- Usage: Common metaphorical expression for loss of trust or confidence.
- Example: *Güven sarsıldı.* "Trust was shaken."
- [CORE]

---

### 2.4 Loss of Justice/Rights

**Pattern:** [PERSON/POSITION] + **haksız çıkmak**
- Transliteration: haksız çıkmak
- Translation: "to turn out to be wrong, to be proven unjust"
- Usage: Common expression for being proven wrong or unjustified in disputes.
- Example: *Sonunda haksız çıktı.* "In the end, he/she turned out to be wrong."
- [CORE]

---

### 2.5 Loss of Validity/Legitimacy

**Pattern:** [DOCUMENT/TICKET] + **geçersiz olmak**
- Transliteration: geçersiz olmak
- Translation: "to be invalid, to become invalid"
- Usage: Copular construction for invalidity. Can indicate expiration or voiding.
- Example: *Pasaport geçersiz oldu.* "The passport became invalid."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [DOCUMENT/DECISION] + **iptal edilmek**
- Transliteration: iptal edilmek
- Translation: "to be cancelled, to be annulled, to be voided"
- Usage: Common passive construction for cancellation or annulment, resulting in invalidity.
- Example: *Karar iptal edildi.* "The decision was annulled."
- [CORE]

## 3. CAUSATIVE: MAKING SOMETHING TRUE/CORRECT

### 3.1 Verification and Confirmation

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **doğrulamak**
- Transliteration: doğrulamak
- Translation: "to verify, to confirm, to validate"
- Usage: Transitive verb meaning to establish or confirm the truth of something. Common in scientific, legal, and administrative contexts.
- Example: *Bilgileri doğruladık.* "We verified the information."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **doğru olduğunu kanıtlamak**
- Transliteration: doğru olduğunu kanıtlamak
- Translation: "to prove that [something] is true"
- Usage: Explicit causative construction using *kanıtlamak* "to prove." Common in argumentative and legal contexts.
- Example: *İddiasının doğru olduğunu kanıtladı.* "He/she proved that his/her claim was true."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [ERROR] + **düzeltmek**
- Transliteration: düzeltmek
- Translation: "to correct, to fix"
- Usage: Transitive verb meaning to correct errors, making something right. Very common across all registers.
- Example: *Hataları düzelttim.* "I corrected the errors."
- [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Realization and Actualization

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PLAN/GOAL] + **gerçekleştirmek**
- Transliteration: gerçekleştirmek
- Translation: "to realize, to accomplish, to carry out, to implement"
- Usage: Extremely common transitive causative meaning to make something real or actual. Used for implementing plans, achieving goals, carrying out actions.
- Example: *Planlarını gerçekleştirdi.* "He/she realized his/her plans."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH/REALITY] + **ortaya çıkarmak**
- Transliteration: ortaya çıkarmak
- Translation: "to reveal, to bring to light, to uncover"
- Usage: Causative construction meaning to cause truth/reality to emerge or be revealed. Common in investigative and journalistic contexts.
- Example: *Gerçeği ortaya çıkardılar.* "They brought the truth to light."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH/REALITY] + **göstermek**
- Transliteration: göstermek
- Translation: "to show, to demonstrate"
- Usage: Common verb for demonstrating or revealing truth. Used with *gerçek*, *hakikat*, *doğru*.
- Example: *Gerçeği gösterdi.* "He/she showed the truth."
- [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Establishing Certainty

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [DECISION/PLAN] + **kesinleştirmek**
- Transliteration: kesinleştirmek
- Translation: "to finalize, to confirm, to make definite"
- Usage: Transitive causative meaning to make something definite or final. Common in administrative and planning contexts.
- Example: *Tarihi kesinleştirdik.* "We finalized the date."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [DOUBT] + **gidermek**
- Transliteration: gidermek
- Translation: "to remove, to dispel, to eliminate"
- Usage: Transitive verb used with *şüphe*, *kuşku* to mean removing doubt and establishing certainty.
- Example: *Şüpheleri giderdik.* "We dispelled the doubts."
- [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Building Reliability

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [ARGUMENT/STRUCTURE] + **sağlamlaştırmak**
- Transliteration: sağlamlaştırmak
- Translation: "to solidify, to strengthen, to make sound"
- Usage: Transitive causative meaning to make something more solid or reliable. Used for strengthening arguments, evidence, or structures.
- Example: *Kanıtları sağlamlaştırdık.* "We strengthened the evidence."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUST/CONFIDENCE] + **güçlendirmek**
- Transliteration: güçlendirmek
- Translation: "to strengthen, to reinforce"
- Usage: General strengthening verb used with *güven* "trust" to mean building or reinforcing trust.
- Example: *Güveni güçlendirdik.* "We strengthened the trust."
- [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Establishing Justice

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **haklı çıkarmak**
- Transliteration: haklı çıkarmak
- Translation: "to prove [someone] right, to vindicate"
- Usage: Causative construction meaning to cause someone to be proven right. Less common than intransitive *haklı çıkmak*.
- Example: *Olaylar onu haklı çıkardı.* "Events proved him/her right."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **hak vermek**
- Transliteration: hak vermek
- Translation: "to agree with, to acknowledge [someone] is right"
- Usage: Very common idiomatic expression meaning to concede that someone is correct or justified. Not strictly causative but functions to establish rightness.
- Example: *Sana hak veriyorum.* "I agree with you / I acknowledge you're right."
- [CORE]

---

### 3.6 Legitimization

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [ACTION/CLAIM] + **meşrulaştırmak**
- Transliteration: meşrulaştırmak
- Translation: "to legitimize, to make legitimate"
- Usage: Transitive causative meaning to make something legitimate or lawful. Common in political and legal contexts.
- Example: *Eylemi meşrulaştırmaya çalıştılar.* "They tried to legitimize the action."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [DOCUMENT/CLAIM] + **geçerli kılmak**
- Transliteration: geçerli kılmak
- Translation: "to make valid, to validate"
- Usage: Causative construction using *kılmak* "to make" to mean establishing validity.
- Example: *Belgeyi geçerli kıldılar.* "They made the document valid."
- [CORE]

## 4. CAUSATIVE: MAKING SOMETHING FALSE/WRONG

### 4.1 Falsification and Refutation

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [STATEMENT/CLAIM] + **yanlışlamak**
- Transliteration: yanlışlamak
- Translation: "to falsify, to prove wrong, to refute"
- Usage: Transitive verb meaning to demonstrate that something is false or incorrect. Common in academic and argumentative contexts.
- Example: *Teoriyi yanlışladılar.* "They falsified the theory."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [CLAIM] + **yalanlamak**
- Transliteration: yalanlamak
- Translation: "to deny, to refute, to give the lie to"
- Usage: Transitive verb meaning to declare something false or to deny an accusation. Common in disputes and official denials.
- Example: *İddiaları yalanladı.* "He/she denied the allegations."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [STATEMENT] + **yanlış olduğunu kanıtlamak**
- Transliteration: yanlış olduğunu kanıtlamak
- Translation: "to prove that [something] is wrong"
- Usage: Explicit causative construction using *kanıtlamak* "to prove."
- Example: *İddiasının yanlış olduğunu kanıtladık.* "We proved that his/her claim was wrong."
- [CORE]

---

### 4.2 Deception and Misleading

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **yanıltmak**
- Transliteration: yanıltmak
- Translation: "to mislead, to deceive, to lead astray"
- Usage: Transitive causative verb meaning to cause someone to have false beliefs. Common in discussions of deception and misinformation.
- Example: *Halkı yanılttılar.* "They misled the public."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **yalan söylemek**
- Transliteration: yalan söylemek
- Translation: "to tell a lie to, to lie to"
- Usage: Very common light verb construction for lying. The causative effect is implicit—telling lies causes false beliefs.
- Example: *Bana yalan söyledi.* "He/she lied to me."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **aldatmak**
- Transliteration: aldatmak
- Translation: "to deceive, to trick, to cheat"
- Usage: General deception verb. While not specifically from truth-domain vocabulary, commonly used in contexts of causing false beliefs.
- Example: *Bizi aldattı.* "He/she deceived us."
- [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Undermining Reliability

**Pattern:** [AGENT/EVENT] + [TRUST] + **sarsmak**
- Transliteration: sarsmak
- Translation: "to shake, to undermine"
- Usage: Transitive verb meaning to shake or undermine trust, confidence, or reliability.
- Example: *Olay güveni sarstı.* "The incident shook the trust."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [SOURCE/PERSON] + **güvenilmez hale getirmek**
- Transliteration: güvenilmez hale getirmek
- Translation: "to make unreliable/untrustworthy"
- Usage: Explicit causative construction. Less common but attested.
- Example: *Davranışları onu güvenilmez hale getirdi.* "His/her behavior made him/her untrustworthy."
- [CORE]

---

### 4.4 Creating Injustice

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **haksız çıkarmak**
- Transliteration: haksız çıkarmak
- Translation: "to prove [someone] wrong, to show [someone] is unjust"
- Usage: Causative construction meaning to cause someone to be proven wrong. Less common than intransitive form.
- Example: *Kanıtlar onu haksız çıkardı.* "The evidence proved him/her wrong."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **haksızlık etmek**
- Transliteration: haksızlık etmek
- Translation: "to wrong [someone], to treat unjustly"
- Usage: Light verb construction meaning to commit injustice against someone. Very common in moral discourse.
- Example: *Ona haksızlık ettiler.* "They wronged him/her."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **hak yemek**
- Transliteration: hak yemek
- Translation: "to violate [someone's] rights, to wrong [someone]"
- Usage: Idiomatic expression (literally "to eat right") meaning to unjustly take what belongs to another. Strong moral condemnation.
- Example: *Hakkımı yedi.* "He/she violated my rights."
- [CORE]

---

### 4.5 Invalidation

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [DOCUMENT/DECISION] + **geçersiz kılmak**
- Transliteration: geçersiz kılmak
- Translation: "to invalidate, to void, to nullify"
- Usage: Causative construction meaning to make something invalid or void.
- Example: *Mahkeme kararı geçersiz kıldı.* "The court invalidated the decision."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [DOCUMENT/DECISION] + **iptal etmek**
- Transliteration: iptal etmek
- Translation: "to cancel, to annul, to void"
- Usage: Light verb construction for cancellation or annulment. Very common in administrative and legal contexts.
- Example: *Sözleşmeyi iptal ettik.* "We cancelled the contract."
- [CORE]

## 5. MAINTAINING TRUTH/CORRECTNESS (Stability)

### 5.1 Preservation of Truth

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH/CORRECTNESS] + **korumak**
- Transliteration: korumak
- Translation: "to protect, to preserve, to maintain"
- Usage: General preservation verb used with truth-domain nouns to indicate maintaining truth or correctness.
- Example: *Doğruluğunu koruduk.* "We preserved its correctness."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH] + **saklamak**
- Transliteration: saklamak
- Translation: "to keep, to preserve, to hide"
- Usage: Verb meaning to keep or preserve. Can mean both "to preserve truth" and "to hide truth" depending on context.
- Example: *Gerçeği sakladı.* "He/she hid the truth." (negative preservation)
- [CORE]

---

### 5.2 Maintaining Consistency

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [CONSISTENCY] + **sürdürmek**

- Translation: "to maintain, to continue, to sustain"
- Usage: General continuation verb used with *tutarlılık* "consistency" to indicate maintaining coherence.
- Example: *Tutarlılığını sürdürdü.* "He/she maintained his/her consistency."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [PERSON] + **tutarlı davranmak**
- Transliteration: tutarlı davranmak
- Translation: "to behave consistently, to act coherently"
- Usage: Expression for maintaining consistent behavior over time.
- Example: *Her zaman tutarlı davranır.* "He/she always behaves consistently."
- [CORE]

---

### 5.3 Maintaining Honesty

- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [PERSON] + **dürüst davranmak**

---

### 5.4 Maintaining Reliability

**Pattern:** [SOURCE/PERSON] + **güvenilir kalmak**

- Translation: "to remain reliable, to stay trustworthy"
- Usage: Construction using *kalmak* "to remain" to emphasize continuity of reliability despite potential challenges.
- Example: *Zor zamanlarda bile güvenilir kaldı.* "He/she remained reliable even in difficult times."
- [CORE]

---

### 5.5 Maintaining Justice

**Pattern:** [PERSON/SYSTEM] + **adil olmak**

- Translation: "to be just, to remain fair"
- Usage: Copular construction indicating ongoing state of justice or fairness.
- Example: *Sistem her zaman adil olmalı.* "The system should always be just."

**Pattern:** [PERSON] + **haklı olmak**
- Transliteration: haklı olmak
- Translation: "to be right, to be in the right"
- Usage: Copular construction indicating ongoing state of being right or justified.
- Example: *Bu konuda hep haklıydı.* "He/she was always right about this matter."
- [CORE]

---

### 5.6 Maintaining Validity

**Pattern:** [DOCUMENT/RULE] + **geçerli olmak**

- Translation: "to be valid, to remain in force"

**Pattern:** [DOCUMENT/RULE] + **geçerli kalmak**

## 6. PREVENTING FALSIFICATION (Negative Stability)

### 6.1 Preventing Errors

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [ERROR] + **önlemek**
- Transliteration: önlemek
- Translation: "to prevent, to avoid"
- Usage: General prevention verb used with *hata* "error," *yanlış* "wrong" to indicate preventing mistakes.
- Example: *Hataları önledik.* "We prevented the errors."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + **yanlış yapmamak**
- Transliteration: yanlış yapmamak
- Translation: "not to make mistakes, to avoid errors"
- Usage: Negative construction indicating avoidance of errors.
- Example: *Yanlış yapmamaya çalışıyorum.* "I'm trying not to make mistakes."
- [CORE]

---

### 6.2 Preventing Deception

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [PERSON] + **yanıltmamak**
- Transliteration: yanıltmamak
- Translation: "not to mislead, to avoid deceiving"
- Usage: Negative form of *yanıltmak* indicating deliberate avoidance of misleading.
- Example: *Kimseyi yanıltmadık.* "We didn't mislead anyone."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + **yalan söylememek**
- Transliteration: yalan söylememek
- Translation: "not to lie, to refrain from lying"
- Usage: Negative construction indicating avoidance of lying.
- Example: *Asla yalan söylemem.* "I never lie."
- [CORE]

---

### 6.3 Protecting Truth

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH] + **korumak**
- Transliteration: korumak
- Translation: "to protect, to guard"
- Usage: Protection verb used with *gerçek*, *hakikat*, *doğru* to indicate defending truth against falsification.
- Example: *Gerçeği korumalıyız.* "We must protect the truth."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [AGENT] + [TRUTH] + **sakınmak** (+ ablative: gerçekten sapmaktan sakınmak)
- Transliteration: sakınmak
- Translation: "to avoid, to refrain from"
- Usage: Used with ablative case to indicate avoiding deviation from truth.
- Example: *Gerçekten sapmaktan sakındı.* "He/she avoided deviating from the truth."
- [CORE]

## 7. GRADUAL CHANGE AND PROCESSES

### 7.1 Gradual Approach to Truth

**Pattern:** [STATEMENT/UNDERSTANDING] + **gerçeğe yaklaşmak**
- Transliteration: gerçeğe yaklaşmak
- Translation: "to approach the truth, to get closer to reality"
- Usage: Expression for gradual approximation of truth. Common in scientific and philosophical contexts.
- Example: *Araştırmalar gerçeğe yaklaşıyor.* "The research is approaching the truth."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [UNDERSTANDING] + **netleşmek**
- Transliteration: netleşmek
- Translation: "to become clear, to clarify"
- Usage: Inchoative verb meaning to become clear or distinct. Used for gradual clarification of truth or understanding.
- Example: *Durum netleşti.* "The situation became clear."
- [CORE]

---

### 7.2 Gradual Loss of Truth/Reliability

**Pattern:** [INFORMATION/MEMORY] + **bozulmak**
- Transliteration: bozulmak
- Translation: "to deteriorate, to become corrupted, to spoil"
- Usage: Inchoative verb indicating gradual deterioration or corruption. Used for information, memory, or reliability degrading over time.
- Example: *Bilgiler zamanla bozuldu.* "The information deteriorated over time."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [TRUST/RELIABILITY] + **azalmak**
- Transliteration: azalmak
- Translation: "to decrease, to diminish"
- Usage: General decrease verb used with *güven* "trust" to indicate gradual loss of trust or confidence.
- Example: *Güven azaldı.* "Trust decreased."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [CLARITY/CERTAINTY] + **bulanmak**
- Transliteration: bulanmak
- Translation: "to become cloudy/murky, to become confused"
- Usage: Metaphorical verb (literally "to become turbid") used for loss of clarity or certainty.
- Example: *Gerçek bulanıklaştı.* "The truth became murky."
- [CORE]

---

### 7.3 Processes of Verification

**Pattern:** [CLAIM] + **test edilmek**
- Transliteration: test edilmek
- Translation: "to be tested, to undergo testing"
- Usage: Passive construction indicating process of testing or verification.
- Example: *İddia test ediliyor.* "The claim is being tested."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [CLAIM] + **incelenmek**
- Transliteration: incelenmek
- Translation: "to be examined, to be investigated"
- Usage: Passive construction for examination or investigation process.
- Example: *Kanıtlar inceleniyor.* "The evidence is being examined."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [TRUTH] + **araştırılmak**
- Transliteration: araştırılmak
- Translation: "to be researched, to be investigated"
- Usage: Passive construction for research or investigation process.
- Example: *Gerçek araştırılıyor.* "The truth is being investigated."
- [CORE]

## 8. ASPECTUAL DISTINCTIONS

### 8.1 Perfective vs. Imperfective

Turkish aspectual system distinguishes completed actions (perfective) from ongoing or habitual actions (imperfective) through tense-aspect morphology:

**Perfective (completed change):**
- *Gerçekleşti.* "It was realized." (past perfective, -DI)
- *Doğru çıktı.* "It turned out to be true." (past perfective)
- *Kesinleşti.* "It was finalized." (past perfective)

**Imperfective (ongoing/habitual):**
- *Gerçekleşiyor.* "It is being realized." (present progressive, -Iyor)
- *Gerçekleşir.* "It gets realized / It is realized (habitually)." (aorist, -Ir/-Ar)
- *Gerçekleşmekte.* "It is in the process of being realized." (present continuous, -mAktA)

---

### 8.2 Inceptive (Beginning of State)

Turkish can mark the beginning of a state using auxiliary constructions:

**Pattern:** [INCHOATIVE VERB] + **başlamak**
- Transliteration: başlamak
- Translation: "to begin to [become X]"
- Usage: Auxiliary verb *başlamak* "to begin" combines with inchoatives to mark inception.
- Example: *Gerçekleşmeye başladı.* "It began to be realized."
- [CORE]

---

### 8.3 Continuative (Ongoing State)

**Pattern:** [STATE] + **devam etmek**
- Transliteration: devam etmek
- Translation: "to continue, to persist"
- Usage: Light verb construction indicating continuation of state.
- Example: *Doğruluğu devam ediyor.* "Its correctness continues."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [STATE] + **sürmek**
- Transliteration: sürmek
- Translation: "to continue, to last, to persist"
- Usage: Intransitive verb indicating duration or continuation.
- Example: *Geçerliliği sürüyor.* "Its validity continues."
- [CORE]

---

### 8.4 Terminative (End of State)

**Pattern:** [STATE] + **son bulmak**
- Transliteration: son bulmak
- Translation: "to come to an end, to terminate"
- Usage: Fixed expression indicating termination of state.
- Example: *Geçerliliği sona erdi.* "Its validity came to an end."
- [CORE]

**Pattern:** [STATE] + **bitmek**
- Transliteration: bitmek
- Translation: "to end, to finish, to expire"
- Usage: General termination verb.
- Example: *Güven bitti.* "Trust ended."
- [CORE]

## 9. SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### 9.1 Productivity Patterns

1. **Inchoative -leş/-laş**: Highly productive for creating "become X" verbs from quality adjectives (*gerçekleşmek*, *kesinleşmek*, *sağlamlaşmak*, *meşrulaşmak*, *doğallaşmak*).

2. **Causative -tır/-tir**: Productive for creating "make X" verbs from inchoatives (*gerçekleştirmek*, *kesinleştirmek*, *sağlamlaştırmak*, *meşrulaştırmak*).

3. **Resultative çıkmak**: Very productive construction for expressing outcomes (*doğru çıkmak*, *yanlış çıkmak*, *haklı çıkmak*, *haksız çıkmak*, *sahte çıkmak*, *yalan çıkmak*).

4. **Denominative -la/-le**: Productive for creating verbs from nouns/adjectives (*doğrulamak*, *yanlışlamak*, *yalanlamak*).

---

### 9.2 Asymmetries

1. **Positive bias in inchoatives**: More lexicalized inchoatives for positive states (*gerçekleşmek*, *kesinleşmek*) than negative states. Negative changes often expressed with general verbs (*bozulmak* "deteriorate," *azalmak* "decrease").

2. **Causative gaps**: Some causatives are less common or unattested (e.g., *?güvenilir kılmak* is possible but rare; speakers prefer *güven vermek* "to give trust").

3. **Light verb preferences**: Turkish strongly prefers light verb constructions (*yalan söylemek*, *haksızlık etmek*, *hak vermek*) over simple verbs in many contexts.

---

### 9.3 Semantic Distinctions

1. **Verification vs. realization**: *Doğrulamak* "to verify" (establishing existing truth) vs. *gerçekleştirmek* "to realize" (making something become real).

2. **Denial vs. falsification**: *Yalanlamak* "to deny" (speech act) vs. *yanlışlamak* "to falsify" (logical/empirical demonstration).

3. **Preservation vs. hiding**: *Korumak* can mean both "to protect truth" and "to preserve," while *saklamak* can mean both "to keep" and "to hide."

---

### 9.4 Constructions Not Attested

1. **No simple "become true" verb**: Turkish lacks a simple verb meaning "to become true" in the sense of gradual truth-acquisition. The language uses resultative *çıkmak* constructions or passive verification forms instead.

2. **Limited gradual change expressions**: While Turkish has rich aspectual marking, specific lexicalized expressions for gradual approach to truth are limited (*gerçeğe yaklaşmak* "approach truth" is attested but not highly productive).

3. **No dedicated "maintain truth" verb**: Maintenance of truth-states is expressed through general preservation verbs (*korumak*, *sürdürmek*) or stative constructions (*güvenilir olmak*, *geçerli kalmak*) rather than specialized truth-maintenance verbs.
