# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (privative) + λήθη (concealment, forgetting); "unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine
**Usage:** Used of statements, propositions, and persons to indicate correspondence with reality or fact. Appears in both everyday and philosophical registers. Participates in a productive derivational family (αλήθεια, αληθινός, αληθεύω).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Literal meaning:** nominal form of αληθής; "the state of being unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality
**Usage:** The primary noun for truth in Greek, used across everyday, legal, philosophical, and religious contexts. Can function as an exclamation ("αλήθεια;" = "Really?") and as a subject of predication ("η αλήθεια είναι ότι…").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Literal meaning:** adjectival extension of αλήθεια; "of the nature of truth"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Applied to persons, objects, feelings, and states of affairs to indicate that they are what they purport to be, as opposed to false or simulated. Slightly more colloquial than αληθής and more commonly used in modern spoken Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** γνήσιος
**Transliteration:** gníssios
**Literal meaning:** from γένος (birth, kind); "of legitimate birth," hence "genuine"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, legitimate, true
**Usage:** Used of objects (e.g., a genuine antique), persons (e.g., a true friend), and relationships to indicate authenticity or legitimacy of origin. Contrasts with νόθος (spurious, illegitimate).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** αυθεντικός
**Transliteration:** afthentikós
**Literal meaning:** from αυθέντης (one who acts on their own authority); "authoritative, original"
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine, original
**Usage:** Used in aesthetic, legal, and everyday contexts to indicate that something is original rather than a copy or forgery. Also applied to persons to mean that they behave in a manner consistent with their true character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ακριβής
**Transliteration:** akrivís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (intensive) + κρίβανος or related to ἄκρος (extreme, precise); "exact, precise"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, correct
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, descriptions, translations, and accounts to indicate that they correspond closely to what they represent. Commonly used in scientific, journalistic, and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ορθός
**Transliteration:** orthós
**Literal meaning:** "straight, upright, correct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, orthodox
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, procedures, and conduct as conforming to a standard or norm. Appears in compounds such as ορθογραφία (correct spelling) and ορθόδοξος (right-believing). In moral contexts it can indicate rightness of action.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** σωστός
**Transliteration:** sostós
**Literal meaning:** from σώζω (to save, preserve); "preserved intact," hence "correct, sound"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, accurate
**Usage:** The most common colloquial term for "correct" in modern Greek, used of answers, behavior, persons, and objects. Overlaps with ορθός but is more frequent in informal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** έγκυρος
**Transliteration:** éngiros
**Literal meaning:** from ἐν- (in) + κῦρος (authority, validity); "having authority"
**Typical English translations:** valid, authoritative, reliable, credible
**Usage:** Used in legal, administrative, and epistemic contexts to indicate that a document, argument, or source carries recognized authority or meets formal requirements. A passport or contract may be described as έγκυρος.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** αξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** aksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + πίστις (trust, faith); "worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, and testimonies to indicate that they merit confidence. Frequently used in journalistic and legal contexts when evaluating the credibility of witnesses or information sources.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** αξιόλογος
**Transliteration:** aksiólogos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + λόγος (word, account); "worthy of account"
**Typical English translations:** noteworthy, significant, considerable, creditable
**Usage:** Used to evaluate persons, achievements, or works as deserving recognition or serious consideration. Carries a positive evaluative force and appears in both formal and informal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** νόθος
**Transliteration:** nóthos
**Literal meaning:** "of illegitimate birth," hence "spurious, counterfeit"
**Typical English translations:** spurious, illegitimate, counterfeit, false
**Usage:** Historically used of illegitimate children; extended to texts, documents, and objects to indicate that they are not what they claim to be. Used in philological contexts (e.g., a νόθο κείμενο is a text of doubtful authenticity).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** πλαστός
**Transliteration:** plastós
**Literal meaning:** from πλάσσω (to mold, fabricate); "fabricated, molded"
**Typical English translations:** forged, counterfeit, fake, fabricated
**Usage:** Applied to documents, signatures, currency, and identities to indicate deliberate falsification. A πλαστή ταυτότητα is a forged identity document. Commonly used in legal and criminal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ψεύτικος
**Transliteration:** psévtikos
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύτης (liar); "of the nature of a lie, false"
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, lying
**Usage:** A common colloquial adjective applied to objects, persons, and statements to indicate that they are not genuine or truthful. Contrasts with αληθινός and γνήσιος in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ψευδής
**Transliteration:** psevdís
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύδος (falsehood); "characterized by falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** false, untrue, mendacious
**Usage:** More formal than ψεύτικος; used of statements, testimony, and representations in legal and philosophical contexts. A ψευδής κατάθεση is false testimony. Participates in compounds such as ψευδώνυμο (pseudonym).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ψεύδος
**Transliteration:** psévdos
**Literal meaning:** "falsehood, lie"
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth
**Usage:** The primary noun for a falsehood or lie, used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts. Appears in the compound ψευδώνυμο and in the fixed expression "λέω ψέματα" (I tell lies), where the plural ψέματα is the colloquial form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** αντίγραφο
**Transliteration:** andígrafo
**Literal meaning:** ἀντί (against, in place of) + γράφω (to write); "written in place of"
**Typical English translations:** copy, reproduction, transcript
**Usage:** Used of documents, artworks, and files to indicate that something is a reproduction rather than the original. The distinction between αντίγραφο and πρωτότυπο (original) is central to legal and archival evaluation of authenticity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** πρωτότυπο
**Transliteration:** protótipo
**Literal meaning:** πρῶτος (first) + τύπος (type, impression); "first impression, original"
**Typical English translations:** original, prototype
**Usage:** Used to designate the original version of a document, artwork, or text as opposed to copies or translations. In aesthetic and legal contexts, the πρωτότυπο carries special authority or value.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** αμφίβολος
**Transliteration:** amfívolos
**Literal meaning:** ἀμφί (on both sides) + βάλλω (to throw); "thrown both ways, doubtful"
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, dubious, questionable, ambiguous
**Usage:** Applied to persons, situations, and claims whose reliability or truth is in question. An αμφίβολη υπόθεση is a dubious case or affair. Used in both everyday and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** αμφισβητήσιμος
**Transliteration:** amfisvitiísimos
**Literal meaning:** from αμφισβητώ (to dispute, contest); "disputable, contestable"
**Typical English translations:** disputable, contestable, questionable, debatable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, titles, and facts that are open to challenge or have not been definitively established. Frequently used in legal and academic contexts to flag that a proposition or right is not settled.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** αμφισβητώ
**Transliteration:** amfisviтó
**Literal meaning:** ἀμφί (on both sides) + σβήνω or related to σβητός; conventionally "to dispute, contest"
**Typical English translations:** to dispute, to contest, to challenge, to question
**Usage:** A verb used to indicate that a claim, right, or fact is being actively challenged. Used in legal proceedings (αμφισβητώ μια κατάθεση) and in everyday argument. Productive derivational base for αμφισβήτηση, αμφισβητήσιμος.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** επαληθεύω
**Transliteration:** epalithévo
**Literal meaning:** ἐπί (upon, toward) + αληθεύω (to be true); "to verify, confirm as true"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to validate
**Usage:** Used in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts to indicate the process of checking whether a claim or datum is accurate. The noun επαλήθευση is used for the act of verification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** επαλήθευση
**Transliteration:** epalíthefsi
**Literal meaning:** nominal form of επαληθεύω; "the act of verifying"
**Typical English translations:** verification, confirmation, validation
**Usage:** Used in scientific, legal, and administrative contexts to refer to the process or result of checking accuracy or truth. Appears in formal documents and procedures.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** διαπίστωση
**Transliteration:** diapístoси
**Literal meaning:** from διαπιστώνω (to ascertain, establish); "the act of ascertaining"
**Typical English translations:** finding, ascertainment, determination, observation
**Usage:** Used to refer to the result of an investigation or observation that establishes a fact. Common in official reports, medical contexts, and everyday speech when someone states what they have found to be the case.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** τεκμήριο
**Transliteration:** tekmírio
**Literal meaning:** from τεκμαίρομαι (to infer, judge by signs); "sign, proof, evidence"
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, indication, token
**Usage:** Used in legal and argumentative contexts to refer to a piece of evidence or an indicator that supports a conclusion. A τεκμήριο is typically indirect evidence from which a fact is inferred, distinguishing it from direct proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** απόδειξη
**Transliteration:** apódhiksi
**Literal meaning:** from αποδεικνύω (to demonstrate, prove); "demonstration, proof"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, receipt, demonstration
**Usage:** Used in logical, legal, and everyday contexts to refer to something that proves or demonstrates a claim. In commercial contexts it also means "receipt." The dual usage reflects the general sense of something that establishes a fact.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** εικονικός
**Transliteration:** ikonikós
**Literal meaning:** from εικόνα (image); "of the nature of an image, simulated"
**Typical English translations:** simulated, virtual, fictitious, nominal
**Usage:** In modern usage, applied to simulated or nominal states of affairs (e.g., εικονική πραγματικότητα = virtual reality; εικονική σύμβαση = fictitious contract). In legal contexts, an εικονική πράξη is a sham transaction. The evaluative force is that something appears to be what it is not.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** ομοιότητα
**Transliteration:** omióтita
**Literal meaning:** from όμοιος (similar, like); "likeness, resemblance"
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used to describe the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant in aesthetic evaluation (resemblance of a portrait to its subject) and in legal contexts (resemblance of a trademark to another). Participates in evaluative judgments about representation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** παραποίηση
**Transliteration:** parapiísi
**Literal meaning:** from παραποιώ (to alter, falsify); "alteration, falsification"
**Typical English translations:** falsification, adulteration, distortion, tampering
**Usage:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to refer to the deliberate alteration of a document, substance, or account so as to misrepresent it. A παραποίηση εγγράφου is the falsification of a document, a criminal offense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** κίβδηλος
**Transliteration:** kívdhilos
**Literal meaning:** "adulterated, counterfeit" (of coins originally); "base, spurious"
**Typical English translations:** counterfeit, spurious, base, debased
**Usage:** Originally applied to debased or counterfeit coinage; extended metaphorically to persons, arguments, and values considered inauthentic or morally debased. More literary and formal in modern usage; less common in everyday speech than πλαστός or ψεύτικος.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
