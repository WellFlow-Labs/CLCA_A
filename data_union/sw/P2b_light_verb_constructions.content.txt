# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions are included only where there is positive evidence of attestation; absence is recorded explicitly. Phrasal constructions already documented in P1b root expansion are noted as prior-attested where they appear here, but are not duplicated in full. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven constructions not already covered.

A construction is included here if it meets the following criteria: (a) it involves a P1 item as a core component; (b) it is multi-word; (c) it is not a simple nominal modification phrase of the type *X wa Y* unless that phrase has idiomatic or fixed status beyond compositional meaning; (d) it is not a derivational form documented in P2a.

The following construction types are distinguished where the data supports the distinction:

- **Periphrastic verbal constructions** — multi-word expressions that perform a verbal function not carried by a single derived verb
- **Nominal collocations** — noun + modifier or noun + genitive phrases that function as fixed or semi-fixed nominal expressions
- **Adverbial phrases** — prepositional or other multi-word expressions functioning as adverbials
- **Predicate constructions** — copular or predicative multi-word expressions
- **Idiomatic or proverbial expressions** — fixed expressions where the meaning is not fully compositional or where the expression functions as a culturally recognized formula

Where a construction type is not attested for a given item, this is stated explicitly.

## 1. Base Form: **kweli**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli? / Kweli!
- **Transliteration:** Kweli? / Kweli!
- **English translation:** Really? / Really!
- **Usage notes:** Used as a one-word discourse particle functioning as a question tag or exclamation of surprise. Included here because it functions as a pragmatic construction in dialogue, not merely as a propositional assertion. Extremely common in spoken Swahili.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli
- **English translation:** truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial prepositional phrase used to frame a statement as sincere or factually grounded. Prior-attested in P1b. Included here as a confirmed periphrastic construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kwa kweli kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli kweli
- **English translation:** truly and sincerely; in all honesty; absolutely for real
- **Usage notes:** Further intensified adverbial phrase combining the prepositional particle *kwa* with the reduplicated form *kweli kweli*. Prior-attested in P1b. More colloquial than *kwa kweli*; used in emphatic spoken assertions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli kabisa
- **Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
- **English translation:** absolutely true; completely true; the absolute truth
- **Usage notes:** Intensified nominal/adverbial phrase using *kabisa* (completely, absolutely) as a postposed intensifier. Prior-attested in P1b. Common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli au uongo
- **Transliteration:** kweli au uongo
- **English translation:** true or false; truth or lie
- **Usage notes:** Paired contrastive construction used in educational true/false formats, everyday challenges to a statement's accuracy, and philosophical discussion. Prior-attested in P1b. Also attested in the extended form *Kweli au uongo — chagua moja* in examination and quiz formats.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** kweli ni kweli
- **English translation:** truth is truth; the truth is the truth
- **Usage notes:** Copular tautological construction used as a rhetorical affirmation that a fact cannot be denied or altered. Prior-attested in P1b. Functions as a proverb-like assertion in argument and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Nakuambia kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Nakuambia kweli.
- **English translation:** I am telling you the truth. / I tell you truly.
- **Usage notes:** Periphrastic assertion construction combining *kuambia* (to tell) with *kweli* as object. Used in everyday interpersonal discourse to assert sincerity. The verb *kuambia* takes a human object (*ku-* = you) and *kweli* as the content object.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Sema kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Sema kweli.
- **English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *sema* (speak/say) with *kweli* as direct object. Prior-attested in P1b as *sema ukweli*; this shorter form with bare *kweli* rather than *ukweli* is also attested in everyday speech, particularly in informal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Hii ni kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Hii ni kweli.
- **English translation:** This is true. / This is the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Basic copular predication using *ni* (copula) with *kweli* as predicate. Extremely common in everyday discourse as a simple assertion of truth. The demonstrative *hii* (this) can be replaced by other subjects.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Si kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Si kweli.
- **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not true. / That's false.
- **Usage notes:** Negative copular construction using *si* (negative copula) with *kweli* as predicate. Extremely common in everyday discourse as a denial or refutation. Functionally equivalent to "that's not true."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli kabla ya uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli kabla ya uongo.
- **English translation:** Truth before falsehood.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial or slogan-type construction asserting the priority of truth over falsehood. Attested in moral and religious discourse. The construction *X kabla ya Y* (X before Y) is syntactically regular but this specific pairing has semi-fixed status.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ninasema kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Ninasema kweli.
- **English translation:** I am telling the truth. / I am speaking truthfully.
- **Usage notes:** The standard periphrastic verbal construction for truth-telling, combining *sema* (to say/speak) with *kweli* as a direct object or adverbial complement. Used across all registers in moral, legal, and everyday contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
- **Transliteration:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
- **English translation:** Truly, I don't know. / In truth, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
- **Usage notes:** The prepositional adverbial phrase *kwa kweli* functions as a sentence-initial or sentence-medial truth-asserting adverbial and as a specific pragmatic construction — the honest-admission frame — which is highly frequent in spoken Swahili. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ni kweli kabisa.
- **Transliteration:** Ni kweli kabisa.
- **English translation:** It is absolutely true. / That is completely true.
- **Usage notes:** Copular predicate construction with *kabisa* (absolutely/completely) as an intensifying modifier. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here. Used as an emphatic affirmation across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Si kweli hiyo.
- **Transliteration:** Si kweli hiyo.
- **English translation:** That is not true. / That is false.
- **Usage notes:** Negative copular predicate construction using *si* (negative copula) + *kweli* + demonstrative *hiyo* (that). The standard construction for denying the truth of a specific preceding claim. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli au uongo?
- **Transliteration:** Kweli au uongo?
- **English translation:** True or false? / Truth or lie?
- **Usage notes:** A binary evaluative construction using *au* (or) to frame a truth-value question. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here. Used in educational, legal, and evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kwa kweli kweli, hii ni tatizo kubwa.
- **Transliteration:** Kwa kweli kweli, hii ni tatizo kubwa.
- **English translation:** Truly and sincerely, this is a serious problem. / In all seriousness, this is a big problem.
- **Usage notes:** The further intensified adverbial phrase *kwa kweli kweli* used as a sentence-initial emphatic marker. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli ya mambo ni hii.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli ya mambo ni hii.
- **English translation:** The truth of the matter is this. / The real state of affairs is this.
- **Usage notes:** Nominal predicate construction using *kweli ya mambo* as subject. Already documented in P1b; cross-referenced here. Used in analytical and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli haisemwi.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli haisemwi.
- **English translation:** The truth is not spoken. / Truth goes unsaid.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial expression commenting on social situations where truth is suppressed or avoided. Already documented in P1b as [MARGINAL]; cross-referenced here. The construction uses the negative passive of *sema*.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 20**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli inaumiza.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli inaumiza.
- **English translation:** The truth hurts. / Truth is painful.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial statement used in moral and interpersonal discourse to acknowledge that honest statements can cause pain. Functions as a standalone proverb and as a framing device before delivering unwelcome information. Distinct from *ukweli unauma* in using *kweli* rather than *ukweli* and the present tense *-umiza* (causative) rather than *-uma* (stative).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Sema kweli, hata kama inakudhuru.
- **Transliteration:** Sema kweli, hata kama inakudhuru.
- **English translation:** Tell the truth, even if it harms you.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial or moral imperative construction used in ethical instruction and religious discourse. The conditional clause *hata kama inakudhuru* ("even if it harms you") is a fixed extension of the truth-telling imperative and appears in this form in moral literature and sermons.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli haina budi kujulikana.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli haina budi kujulikana.
- **English translation:** The truth must inevitably become known. / Truth will out.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction using *haina budi* ("it cannot avoid," "it must inevitably") to assert the inescapability of truth becoming known. Appears in moral, legal, and narrative discourse as an expression of epistemic inevitability.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli ni mchungu.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli ni mchungu.
- **English translation:** Truth is bitter.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial copular construction characterizing truth by its unpleasant quality. *Mchungu* (bitter) is a common evaluative term in Swahili proverbial discourse. Used in moral instruction and as a framing device before delivering difficult information.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli haiozi.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli haiozi.
- **English translation:** Truth does not rot. / Truth does not decay.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the permanence or indestructibility of truth. *-oza* (to rot, to decay) is used metaphorically. Appears in moral and religious discourse to assert that truth endures regardless of attempts to suppress it.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli hushinda uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli hushinda uongo.
- **English translation:** Truth defeats falsehood. / Truth overcomes lies.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction using the habitual marker *hu-* to assert the general principle that truth prevails over falsehood. Appears in moral, religious, and political discourse. Related to *ukweli daima hushinda* (P1b) but with the explicit object *uongo* making the contrast explicit.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ni kweli kabisa kwamba...
- **Transliteration:** Ni kweli kabisa kwamba...
- **English translation:** It is absolutely true that...
- **Usage notes:** A framing construction used to introduce a strongly asserted proposition. The complementizer *kwamba* ("that") introduces the embedded clause. This pattern is highly productive and appears across formal and informal registers as a strong truth-assertion frame.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Je, ni kweli?
- **Transliteration:** Je, ni kweli?
- **English translation:** Is it true? / Really?
- **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction using the question particle *je* and the copular truth assertion *ni kweli*. Used in spoken Swahili to express surprise, seek confirmation, or challenge a claim. Extremely frequent in conversation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu.
- **English translation:** Truly, this is amazing. / It really is a wonder.
- **Usage notes:** *Kweli kweli* functioning as a sentence-initial intensifying discourse marker expressing genuine surprise or emphasis. Already documented in P1 and P1b; noted here as a specific pragmatic construction — the emphatic-surprise frame — distinct from its use as a simple adverbial intensifier.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ukweli ni kwamba...
- **Transliteration:** Ukweli ni kwamba...
- **English translation:** The truth is that... / The fact is that...
- **Usage notes:** A highly productive framing construction used to introduce a factual or corrective statement. The pattern *ukweli ni kwamba* + embedded clause is a standard discourse frame in journalism, formal speech, and everyday argumentation for presenting what the speaker asserts to be the actual facts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 30**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...
- **Transliteration:** Ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...
- **English translation:** The truth of the matter is that... / The reality of things is that...
- **Usage notes:** An extended version of the *ukweli ni kwamba* frame, with *wa mambo* ("of things/matters") adding specificity. Used in formal discourse, journalism, and deliberative contexts to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement about actual conditions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kwa mujibu wa ukweli...
- **Transliteration:** Kwa mujibu wa ukweli...
- **English translation:** According to the truth... / In accordance with the facts...
- **Usage notes:** A formal prepositional frame using *kwa mujibu wa* ("according to," "in accordance with") to introduce a statement grounded in factual accuracy. Appears in legal, journalistic, and formal administrative discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 32**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kwa upande wa ukweli...
- **Transliteration:** Kwa upande wa ukweli...
- **English translation:** On the side of truth... / From the standpoint of truth...
- **Usage notes:** A framing construction used in argumentative and moral discourse to position a statement as aligned with truth. *Kwa upande wa* ("on the side of") is a productive prepositional frame in Swahili argumentation.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 33**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.
- **Transliteration:** Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.
- **English translation:** To tell the truth, I didn't like that. / Honestly, I didn't like it.
- **Usage notes:** *Kusema ukweli* functioning as a sentence-initial parenthetical frame equivalent to English "to tell the truth" or "honestly." The infinitive phrase is used as a discourse marker signaling honest admission. Extremely frequent in spoken and written Swahili.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 34**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele.
- **Transliteration:** Ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele.
- **English translation:** A hidden truth cannot be hidden forever.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction combining the relative clause *uliofichwa* ("which was hidden," already documented in P1b) with the stative negative *haufichiki milele* ("cannot be hidden forever"). Used in moral and investigative discourse to assert the ultimate discoverability of suppressed truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 35**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Niambie ukweli, usiniambie uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Niambie ukweli, usiniambie uongo.
- **English translation:** Tell me the truth, don't tell me lies.
- **Usage notes:** A paired imperative construction contrasting *ukweli* and *uongo* in a direct address. The negative imperative *usiniambie uongo* reinforces the positive command. Used in interpersonal confrontation, moral instruction, and narrative dialogue.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 36**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli au uongo — chagua moja.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli au uongo — chagua moja.
- **English translation:** True or false — choose one.
- **Usage notes:** An educational and logical construction presenting a binary choice between truth and falsehood. Already documented in P1b as a binary evaluative frame; noted here as a specific instructional construction with the imperative *chagua moja* ("choose one"), appearing in examination and quiz formats.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 37**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Hii si nusu kweli — ni uongo mtupu.
- **Transliteration:** Hii si nusu kweli — ni uongo mtupu.
- **English translation:** This is not a half-truth — it is an outright lie.
- **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction opposing *nusu kweli* (half-truth, documented in P1b) with *uongo mtupu* (outright lie, documented in P1). Used in argumentative and journalistic discourse to escalate a charge from partial deception to complete falsehood.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 38**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo.
- **English translation:** A truthful person does not tell lies.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction combining *mtu wa kweli* (a truthful/genuine person, documented in P1b) with the habitual negative *hasemi uongo* ("does not tell lies"). Used in moral instruction and character evaluation to define the truthful person by their conduct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**No construction identified:**

- No common idiomatic construction using *kweli* as a verb complement in the sense of "to prove true" (a function carried by *thibitisha*) is attested.
- No common oath formula using *kweli* alone (without *ukweli* or *haki*) as the oath anchor is attested as a fixed expression.
