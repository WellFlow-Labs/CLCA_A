# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 base forms. Constructions already documented in P1b or P2a as fixed compounds or phrases are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full, but are included here for completeness where they qualify as multi-word constructions rather than derivational forms. Derivational forms (single-word forms documented in P2a) are excluded. The distinction between a fixed compound (P2a territory) and a multi-word construction (P2b territory) is applied as follows: forms that have been lexicalized into a single orthographic unit or that function as a single lexical item are treated as P2a; forms that remain syntactically separable multi-word units, even if conventionalized, are treated as P2b. Borderline cases are noted. Constructions are organized by P1 base item.

---

## Item 1 — אֱמֶת (ʾemet)

---

**Construction 1.1**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** בֶּאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemet
**English translation:** truly, really, in truth, honestly
**Usage notes:** Extremely common adverbial phrase functioning as a sincerity marker or intensifier across all registers. Documented in P1b and P2a; included here as a prepositional multi-word construction with independent discourse-marker status.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** אֱמֶת לַאֲמִתָּהּ
**Transliteration:** ʾemet le-ʾamittah
**English translation:** the real truth, the truth of the matter, strictly speaking
**Usage notes:** A fixed phrase used in rabbinic and philosophical discourse to distinguish genuine or essential truth from a superficial approximation; also used in modern Hebrew to mean "strictly speaking" or "in the fullest sense." Documented in P1b and P2a.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** אֱמֶת הַמִּדָּה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ha-middah
**English translation:** the standard, the benchmark, the true measure
**Usage notes:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse to refer to the genuine criterion by which something is judged. Common in journalistic and intellectual writing. Documented in P1b and P2a.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְדַבֵּר אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ledabber ʾemet
**English translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth
**Usage notes:** A basic verb-object construction used across all registers and periods to describe the act of telling the truth. The most neutral and common way to express truth-telling in Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לוֹמַר אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** lomar ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to say the truth, to tell the truth
**Usage notes:** A common alternative to ledabber ʾemet, using the verb lomar ("to say") with the definite article. Slightly more colloquial and more common in everyday speech than the ledabber construction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְגַלּוֹת אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** legallot ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to reveal the truth, to uncover the truth
**Usage notes:** Used when truth is understood as something hidden that must be brought to light. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts. Implies prior concealment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְהַסְתִּיר אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** lehastir ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
**Usage notes:** Used to evaluate conduct in which relevant true information is deliberately withheld. Common in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts. Contrasts with legallot ʾet ha-ʾemet.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְחַפֵּשׂ אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** leḥappes ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to search for the truth, to seek the truth
**Usage notes:** Used in philosophical, journalistic, and legal contexts to describe the pursuit of truth as an active process. Common in discourse about investigative journalism, judicial proceedings, and philosophical inquiry.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְהוֹדוֹת בָּאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** lehodot ba-ʾemet
**English translation:** to admit the truth, to acknowledge the truth
**Usage notes:** Used when a person concedes a true fact, often one that is unfavorable to themselves. Common in legal, interpersonal, and political contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.10**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** כָּל הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** kol ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** the whole truth, the entire truth
**Usage notes:** Used to emphasize that nothing true has been withheld. Appears in legal oaths and in everyday speech. The phrase כל האמת וכלום חוץ מהאמת (kol ha-ʾemet ve-klum ḥuts min ha-ʾemet, "the whole truth and nothing but the truth") is used in legal oath formulas.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** כָּל הָאֱמֶת וְכְלוּם חוּץ מֵהָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** kol ha-ʾemet ve-klum ḥuts min ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth
**Usage notes:** The standard Hebrew formulation of the legal oath formula. Used in court proceedings and formal legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** חֲצִי אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ḥatsi ʾemet
**English translation:** a half-truth
**Usage notes:** Used to evaluate a statement that is technically partially true but misleading because it omits relevant information. Common in journalistic and political discourse to criticize selective or incomplete disclosure.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** garʿin shel ʾemet
**English translation:** a kernel of truth, a grain of truth
**Usage notes:** Used to evaluate a claim that is mostly false or exaggerated but contains some element of truth. Common in everyday and journalistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** אֱמֶת מַרָּה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet marah
**English translation:** a bitter truth, an unpleasant truth
**Usage notes:** Used to describe a true fact that is painful or unwelcome to hear. Common in everyday and literary discourse. The adjective marah ("bitter") modifies ʾemet attributively.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.15**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** אֱמֶת וְשָׁלוֹם
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-shalom
**English translation:** truth and peace
**Usage notes:** A biblical and rabbinic paired value (Zechariah 8:16, 8:19), used in religious and ethical discourse to describe the dual foundations of a just society. Documented in P1b.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily religious/traditional register

---

**Construction 1.16**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemet u-ve-tamim
**English translation:** in truth and integrity, honestly and wholeheartedly
**Usage notes:** A fixed phrase used in formal, legal, and religious contexts to affirm that an action or statement is made with both truthfulness and complete sincerity. Documented in P1b.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — formal/religious register

---

**Construction 1.17**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** זוֹ הָאֱמֶת הַמָּרָה
**Transliteration:** zo ha-ʾemet ha-marah
**English translation:** this is the bitter truth, that is the hard truth
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction used to introduce or characterize an unwelcome true statement. Common in everyday and journalistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.18**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְהִתְעַמֵּת עִם הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** lehitʿammet ʿim ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to confront the truth, to face the truth
**Usage notes:** Used in psychological, political, and everyday contexts to describe the act of acknowledging and engaging with an unwelcome reality. Common in therapeutic and journalistic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.19**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְעַוֵּת אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to distort the truth, to twist the truth
**Usage notes:** Used to evaluate conduct in which true information is presented in a misleading or distorted way. Common in legal and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.20**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Example:** לְסַלֵּף אֶת הָאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** lesallef ʾet ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to distort the truth, to falsify the truth
**Usage notes:** Used similarly to leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet but with a stronger implication of deliberate falsification. Common in journalistic and legal contexts. The verb lesallef implies more active distortion than leʿavvet.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 2 — אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)

---

**Construction 2.1**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** חָבֵר אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** ḥaver ʾamitī
**English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
**Usage notes:** A very common attributive construction used to evaluate a friendship as genuine rather than superficial or instrumental. Used across all registers in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** אַהֲבָה אֲמִתִּית
**Transliteration:** ʾahavah ʾamitit
**English translation:** true love, genuine love
**Usage notes:** A common attributive construction evaluating love as genuine rather than superficial or pretended. Used in everyday, literary, and popular cultural contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** הַסִּיבָּה הָאֲמִתִּית
**Transliteration:** ha-sibba ha-ʾamitit
**English translation:** the real reason, the true reason
**Usage notes:** Used to distinguish the genuine underlying reason for something from a stated or apparent reason. Extremely common in everyday, journalistic, and analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.4**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** הַבְּעָיָה הָאֲמִתִּית
**Transliteration:** ha-beʿayah ha-ʾamitit
**English translation:** the real problem, the true problem
**Usage notes:** Used to distinguish the genuine underlying problem from a surface-level or apparent one. Common in analytical, journalistic, and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.5**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** הַמַּשְׁמָעוּת הָאֲמִתִּית
**Transliteration:** ha-mashmaʿut ha-ʾamitit
**English translation:** the true meaning, the real meaning
**Usage notes:** Used in interpretive, philosophical, and everyday contexts to distinguish the genuine meaning of a text, statement, or event from a superficial or mistaken reading.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.6**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** זֶה אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** ze ʾamitī
**English translation:** this is genuine, this is real, this is true
**Usage notes:** A simple predicative construction used to evaluate something as genuine or real. Common in everyday speech across all contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.7**
**Base Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Example:** לֹא אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** lo ʾamitī
**English translation:** not genuine, not real, not true
**Usage notes:** The standard negation of ʾamitī, used to evaluate something as lacking genuine character. Common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 3 — נָכוֹן (nakhon)

---

**Construction 3.1**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** זֶה נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** ze nakhon
**English translation:** that is correct, that is right
**Usage notes:** The most basic predicative construction with nakhon, used to affirm the correctness of a statement or action. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** לֹא נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** lo nakhon
**English translation:** incorrect, wrong, not right
**Usage notes:** The standard negation, used to evaluate a statement or action as incorrect. Documented in P2a (item 3.2). Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְהַיּוֹם
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-hayom
**English translation:** as of today, correct as of the present date
**Usage notes:** A fixed administrative and journalistic formula specifying that information is accurate as of the current date. Documented in P2a (item 3.3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.4**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְתַאֲרִיךְ
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-taʾarikh
**English translation:** correct as of [date], valid as of [date]
**Usage notes:** Used in legal, financial, and administrative documents to specify the date as of which information is accurate. Documented in P2a (item 3.4).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.5**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְצַיֵּן
**Transliteration:** nakhon letsayen
**English translation:** it is correct/appropriate to note, it should be noted
**Usage notes:** A fixed discourse marker used in written and spoken Hebrew to introduce a relevant qualification or additional point. Documented in P2a (item 3.4). Common in journalistic and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.6**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** הַתְּשׁוּבָה הַנְּכוֹנָה
**Transliteration:** ha-teshuva ha-nekhonah
**English translation:** the correct answer, the right answer
**Usage notes:** A very common attributive construction used in educational and evaluative contexts to identify the answer that meets the required standard of correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.7**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** הַדֶּרֶךְ הַנְּכוֹנָה
**Transliteration:** ha-derekh ha-nekhonah
**English translation:** the right way, the correct path
**Usage notes:** Used both literally (the correct route) and figuratively (the right course of action). Common in everyday, moral, and organizational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.8**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** הַהַחְלָטָה הַנְּכוֹנָה
**Transliteration:** ha-haḥlatah ha-nekhonah
**English translation:** the right decision, the correct decision
**Usage notes:** Used in evaluative contexts to assess whether a decision meets the relevant standard of correctness or appropriateness. Common in everyday and professional discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.9**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** אִם אֲנִי נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** ʾim ʾani nakhon
**English translation:** if I am correct, if I am right
**Usage notes:** A hedging construction used to introduce a claim with epistemic qualification, acknowledging the possibility of error. Common in everyday and academic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.10**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Example:** נָכוֹן שֶׁ...
**Transliteration:** nakhon she-...
**English translation:** it is true that..., it is correct that..., admittedly...
**Usage notes:** A concessive construction used to acknowledge a true or correct point before introducing a qualification or counterargument. Extremely common in spoken and written discourse ("נכון שהוא ניסה, אבל..." — "It is true that he tried, but...").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 4 — מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)

---

**Construction 4.1**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** לֹא מְדֻיָּק
**Transliteration:** lo meduyaq
**English translation:** not accurate, imprecise, inexact
**Usage notes:** The standard negation of meduyaq, used to evaluate a statement, measurement, or description as lacking the required degree of precision. Common across scientific, journalistic, and everyday registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.2**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** לְהִיּוֹת מְדֻיָּק
**Transliteration:** lihyot meduyaq
**English translation:** to be precise, to be accurate
**Usage notes:** An infinitival construction used to describe the state or requirement of being precise. Common in instructions, academic writing, and everyday speech ("צריך להיות מדויק בנושא הזה" — "One needs to be precise on this matter").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.3**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** לְהִיּוֹת יוֹתֵר מְדֻיָּק
**Transliteration:** lihyot yoter meduyaq
**English translation:** to be more precise, to be more accurate
**Usage notes:** A comparative construction used to introduce a more precise reformulation of a previous statement. Common in academic, journalistic, and everyday discourse as a self-correction or clarification marker.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.4**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** מְדֻיָּק יוֹתֵר
**Transliteration:** meduyaq yoter
**English translation:** more precisely, more accurately
**Usage notes:** Used as a discourse marker to introduce a more precise reformulation. Functions similarly to English "more precisely" or "to be more exact." Common in spoken and written discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.5**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** תֵּאוּר מְדֻיָּק
**Transliteration:** teʾur meduyaq
**English translation:** an accurate description, a precise description
**Usage notes:** A common attributive construction used to evaluate a description as meeting the standard of precision. Used in scientific, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.6**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Example:** מֵידָע מְדֻיָּק
**Transliteration:** meydaʿ meduyaq
**English translation:** accurate information, precise information
**Usage notes:** Used in journalistic, scientific, and administrative contexts to evaluate whether information meets the standard of precision required. Common in contexts of data quality evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 5 — שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)

---

**Construction 5.1**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** עֵד שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ʿed sheqer
**English translation:** false witness, perjurer
**Usage notes:** A fixed biblical and legal expression denoting a person who gives false testimony. Documented in P1 (item 28) and P2a (item 5.5). Functions as a conventionalized compound.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.2**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ʿedut sheqer
**English translation:** false testimony, perjury
**Usage notes:** A fixed legal and biblical expression denoting testimony that is false or fabricated. Listed as P1 item 28. Appears in biblical prohibitions and in modern legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.3**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** לְדַבֵּר שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ledabber sheqer
**English translation:** to speak falsehood, to lie
**Usage notes:** A verb-object construction used in biblical and literary Hebrew to describe the act of speaking falsely. More formal and literary than the everyday leshaqer; appears in biblical prohibitions and in formal moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily biblical/literary register; leshaqer is more common in everyday speech

---

**Construction 5.4**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** זֶה שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ze sheqer
**English translation:** that is a lie, that is false
**Usage notes:** A basic predicative construction used to evaluate a statement as false or a lie. Common across all registers in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.5**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** שֶׁקֶר מוּחְלָט
**Transliteration:** sheqer muḥlat
**English translation:** an outright lie, a complete falsehood, an absolute lie
**Usage notes:** An attributive construction used to evaluate a statement as entirely and unambiguously false. Common in everyday and journalistic discourse for emphatic denial.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.6**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** שֶׁקֶר וָכָזָב
**Transliteration:** sheqer va-khazav
**English translation:** lies and falsehoods, utter falsehood
**Usage notes:** A fixed paired expression using two near-synonyms for emphatic effect. Used in biblical and literary Hebrew and in elevated everyday speech to characterize something as thoroughly false. The pairing intensifies the evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — somewhat literary/elevated register

---

**Construction 5.7**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** מִדְבַּר שֶׁקֶר תִּרְחָק
**Transliteration:** midvar sheqer tirḥaq
**English translation:** distance yourself from falsehood, keep far from a false matter
**Usage notes:** A biblical injunction (Exodus 23:7) used in religious and ethical discourse as a foundational prohibition against falsehood. Cited in rabbinic literature and in discussions of legal ethics.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — primarily religious/traditional register

---

**Construction 5.8**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Example:** חוֹתֶמֶת שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ḥotemet sheqer
**English translation:** a stamp of falsehood, a mark of dishonesty
**Usage notes:** Used figuratively to describe something that bears the unmistakable character of falsehood. Less common than the other constructions; used in literary and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Item 6 — אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)

---

**Construction 6.1**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)

**Transliteration:** be-tsura ʾotentit
**English translation:** in an authentic manner, authentically
**Usage notes:** The standard adverbial construction for ʾotentī, used in cultural, artistic, and journalistic contexts to evaluate whether something is done in a genuinely authentic way. Serves the adverbial function that ʾotentī itself cannot fill directly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.2**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)
**Example:** חוֹמֶר אוֹתֶנְטִי
**Transliteration:** ḥomer ʾotentī
**English translation:** authentic material, genuine source material
**Usage notes:** Used in historical, journalistic, and academic contexts to evaluate whether source material is genuine and unaltered. Common in discussions of documentary evidence and historical research.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.3**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)
