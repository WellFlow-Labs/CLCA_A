# G6 — Cross-Language Opposition and Asymmetry Comparison

## 1. Per-Language Oppositional Structures

### Georgian (ka)

**Lexical oppositions** (6 core pairs from F5):
- სიმართლე/ტყუილი (simart'le/t'q'uili) "truth/lie"
- ჭეშმარიტი/ყალბი (ch'eshmarit'i/q'albi) "genuine/fake"
- ნამდვილი/ყალბი (namdvili/q'albi) "real/fake"
- სწორი/მცდარი (sts'ori/mts'dari) "correct/erroneous"
- მართალი/მცდარი (mart'ali/mts'dari) "right/mistaken"
- ბუნებრივი/ხელოვნური (bunebr'ivi/khelovnuri) "natural/artificial"

**Morphological oppositions** (3 pairs from F5):
- სამართლიანი/უსამართლო (samart'liani/usamart'lo) "just/unjust" (უ- privative prefix)
- უტყუარი/ტყუარა (ut'q'uari/t'q'uara) "infallible/liar" (უ- privative)
- მართებული/უმართებულო (mart'ebuli/umart'ebulo) "justified/unjustified" (უ- privative)

**Constructional oppositions** (4 pairs from F5):
- სიმართლის თქმა/ტყუილის თქმა "telling truth/telling lies"
- გამოვლინდა/დაიფარა "was revealed/was hidden"
- ეს არის სიმართლე/ეს არ არის სიმართლე "this is truth/this is not truth"

**F6 naturalness patterns**:
- Inchoatives with abstract truth [MARGINAL]; revelation verbs [CORE]
- Causative verification [CORE]
- Privative უ- oppositions all [CORE]

### Yoruba (yo)

**Lexical oppositions** (5 core pairs from F5):
- òtítọ́/irọ́ "truth/lie"
- tọ́/purọ́ "be true/to lie" (stative vs. active verb asymmetry)
- ìwà rere/ìwà búburú "good character/bad character"
- agbàgbọ́/aláìgbàgbọ́ "believer/unbeliever" (morphological: a- vs. aláì-)

**Constructional oppositions** (7 pairs from F5):
- sọ òtítọ́/sọ irọ́ "speak truth/tell lie"
- ó tọ́/kò tọ́ "it is true/it is not true"
- òtítọ́ ni/irọ́ ni "it is truth/it is a lie"
- gbàgbọ́/kò gbàgbọ́ "believe/not believe"

**F6 naturalness patterns**:
- Inchoatives with tọ́ [MARGINAL] (stative verb resists inchoative)
- Revelation constructions (purọ́ jáde) [CORE]
- Serial verb causatives [CORE]
- Ideophonic modification (déédé, gidigidi) [CORE]

**Notable**: Yoruba shows weaker morphological opposition system; relies more on lexical contrasts and constructional negation.

## 3. Naturalness Patterns from F6 Data

### Strongly Natural Combinations (Cross-Linguistically)

**1. REVELATION/DISCOVERY CONSTRUCTIONS**

All eight languages rate revelation/discovery constructions as [CORE] in F6:

- Georgian: გამოვლინდა "was revealed"
- Yoruba: purọ́ jáde "lie emerged"
- Finnish: paljastua "be revealed"
- Basque: egia atera "truth came out"
- Tamil: வெளிப்படு "comes out"
- Indonesian: terungkap "be revealed"
- Turkish: ortaya çıkmak "come to light"
- Korean: 드러나다 "come to light"

**Evidence**: All F6 tables rate these [CORE]. All F4 tables document as primary strategy for truth-discovery.

**Pattern**: Intransitive or passive revelation verbs are **universally natural** and preferred for expressing truth-discovery across all typological profiles.

**2. VERIFICATION/AUTHENTICATION CONSTRUCTIONS**

All eight languages rate verification constructions as [CORE] in F6:

- Georgian: დაადასტურა "confirmed"
- Yoruba: jẹ́rìí "testify"
- Finnish: todistaa "prove"
- Basque: egiazkotu "verify"
- Tamil: உண்மைப்படுத்து "verify"
- Indonesian: membenarkan "confirm"
- Turkish: doğrulamak "verify"
- Korean: 확인하다 "verify"

**Evidence**: All F6 tables rate verification constructions [CORE]. All F2a tables document productive derivational families.

**Pattern**: Verification/authentication is **universally expressed** through productive constructions across all languages.

**3. LIGHT VERB "TELL TRUTH/LIE" CONSTRUCTIONS**

All eight languages rate truth-telling/lying light verb constructions as [CORE] in F6:

- Georgian: სიმართლე თქმა/ტყუილის თქმა
- Yoruba: sọ òtítọ́/sọ irọ́
- Finnish: puhua totuutta/puhua valhetta
- Basque: egia esan/gezurra esan
- Tamil: உண்மை சொல்/பொய் சொல்
- Indonesian: mengatakan kebenaran/berbohong
- Turkish: doğru söylemek/yalan söylemek
- Korean: 진실을 말하다/거짓말을 하다

**Evidence**: All F6 tables rate these [CORE]. All F4 tables document as extremely frequent constructions.

**Pattern**: Truth-telling is **universally expressed** through light verb constructions rather than dedicated simple verbs.

### Recurrent Patterns of Restriction, Substitution, or Blocking

**PATTERN 1: Inchoative Blocking → Revelation Substitution**

**Restriction**: Simple inchoatives blocked with abstract truth terms in 6/8 languages (F6 evidence)

**Substitution**: Revelation/discovery verbs systematically substituted (F6 evidence):

- Tamil: வெளிப்படு [CORE] preferred over ஆகு

- Basque: atera [CORE] preferred over bihurtu [MARGINAL]

**Evidence strength**: **STRONG** — F6 tables explicitly rate both constructions across 6 languages with consistent pattern.

**Interpretation**: Languages avoid constructions implying truth "comes into being"; prefer constructions implying truth "comes into view." Reflects conceptualization of truth as pre-existing and discovered.

**PATTERN 2: Agentive Blocking → Periphrastic Substitution**

**Restriction**: Agentive nominalization blocked for "truth-teller" in all 8 languages (F5 and F2a evidence)

**Substitution**: Periphrastic constructions universally substituted (F4 evidence):

- F4 tables document actual periphrastic constructions used

**Evidence strength**: **STRONG** — F5 tables document gap for all 8 languages. F2a tables note periphrastic alternatives. F4 tables show actual usage.

**Interpretation**: Negative agent roles (marked behavior) require lexicalization; positive agent roles (unmarked behavior) expressed periphrastically. Pragmatic asymmetry in what needs naming.

**PATTERN 3: Causative Restriction → Verification Sense Only**

**Restriction**: Causative "make true" blocked in creation sense (F4 evidence from multiple languages)

**Allowed sense**: Verification/authentication causatives universally productive (F2a and F6 evidence)

**Evidence strength**: **MODERATE** — F4 tables note semantic split in 4 languages explicitly. F2a tables document productive verification verbs in all 8 languages.

**Interpretation**: Languages distinguish verification (establishing pre-existing truth) from creation (bringing truth into existence). Creation sense blocked because truth is discovered, not manufactured.

**PATTERN 4: Passive Preference in Revelation (Typological)**

**Active/intransitive preferred**:

**Evidence strength**: **MODERATE** — F4 tables explicitly document voice preferences for 6 languages.

**Interpretation**: Truth-domain follows general typological patterns for passive productivity. Not truth-domain-specific restriction.

## 4. Cross-Language Asymmetries

### Richly Lexicalized vs. Analytic Expression

**ASYMMETRY 1: Liar vs. Truth-Teller (UNIVERSAL)**

**Richly lexicalized** (all 8 languages):
- Dedicated agentive nouns for "liar" with productive derivational families
- Georgian: ტყუარა + ტყუარაობა "lying behavior" (F2a)
- Finnish: valehtelija + valehtelu "lying activity" (F2a)
- Indonesian: pembohong + kebohongan "falsehood" (F2a)
- Turkish: yalancı + yalancılık "lying behavior" (F2a)
- Korean: 거짓말쟁이 + 거짓말 "lie" (F2a)

**Analytically expressed** (7/8 languages):
- No dedicated agentive nouns for "truth-teller"
- Periphrastic constructions required (F4 documents):
  - Georgian: "person who tells truth"
  - Finnish: rehellinen ihminen "honest person"
  - Indonesian: orang jujur "honest person"
  - Turkish: dürüst insan "honest person"
  - Korean: 정직한 사람 "honest person"

**Exception**: Tamil உண்மைவாதி exists but F2a: "less common than liar terms"

**Evidence strength**: **STRONG** — All F5 tables document liar lexicalization. All F5 tables note truth-teller absence. All F2a tables confirm pattern.

**Interpretation**: **UNIVERSAL ASYMMETRY** — Negative agent roles require and receive lexicalization; positive agent roles expressed analytically. Reflects pragmatic principle: marked behaviors need naming, unmarked don't.

**ASYMMETRY 2: Revelation vs. Concealment Elaboration**

**More elaborated** (revelation):
- Korean: Multiple revelation verbs (드러나다, 밝혀지다, 탄로나다) documented in F1
- Georgian: Multiple revelation verbs (გამოვლინდა, გამოჩნდა) documented in F1
- Turkish: Multiple revelation constructions (ortaya çıkmak, açığa çıkmak, meydana çıkmak) documented in F1

**Less elaborated** (concealment):
- Typically one general "hide" verb per language (F1 tables)
- Fewer specialized concealment constructions

**Evidence strength**: **MODERATE** — F1 tables document revelation verb inventories. Pattern visible in 3 languages clearly; others show less elaboration.

**Interpretation**: Languages elaborate revelation vocabulary more than concealment vocabulary. Reflects cultural/pragmatic emphasis on truth-discovery over truth-hiding.

**ASYMMETRY 3: Error-Type Specification (Indonesian Unique)**

**Richly lexicalized** (Indonesian only):
- Exceptional productivity of salah compounds (F1 and F2b document 14+ compounds):
  - salah paham, salah guna, salah kaprah, salah tingkah, salah langkah, salah jalan, salah alamat, salah urus, salah tafsir, salah ucap, salah tulis, salah hitung, salah lihat, salah dengar

**Less elaborated** (other 7 languages):
- Use general error terms without systematic compounding
- Korean shows some similar patterns (F2b) but far less productive

**Evidence strength**: **STRONG** — Indonesian F1 and F2b tables extensively document. Other F2b tables lack parallel.

**Interpretation**: Indonesian shows unique elaboration of error typology through productive compounding. Represents **language-specific lexical elaboration** rather than universal pattern.

### Constructional Resistance in Specific Semantic Domains

**DOMAIN 1: Abstract Truth Inchoatives**

**Resistance**: Six of eight languages resist simple inchoative constructions with abstract truth nouns.

**Affected languages**: Korean, Georgian, Basque, Finnish (restricted), Tamil (restricted)

**Unaffected**: Indonesian, Turkish

**Substitution**: Revelation/discovery verbs (F6 documents all as [CORE])

**Evidence**: F6 naturalness tables explicitly rate constructions. F4 tables document substitution strategies.

**Interpretation**: Abstract truth domain resists inchoative semantics (coming into being). Languages substitute revelation semantics (coming into view). **Semantic domain restriction** rather than morphological blocking.

**DOMAIN 2: Positive Agent Roles**

**Resistance**: All eight languages resist or block agentive nominalization for positive truth-related agent roles.

**Affected**: Truth-teller, honest person (as agentive nouns)

**Unaffected**: Liar, deceiver (freely lexicalized)

**Substitution**: Periphrastic constructions (F4 documents)

**Evidence**: F5 tables document gap for all 8 languages. F4 tables document periphrastic alternatives.

**Interpretation**: Positive agent roles resist lexicalization. **Pragmatic domain restriction** — unmarked behaviors don't require dedicated agent nouns.

**DOMAIN 3: Truth Creation**

**Resistance**: Most languages resist causative constructions implying literal creation of truth.

**Affected**: "Make falsehood into truth," "create truth"

**Unaffected**: "Verify truth," "establish truth," "confirm truth"

**Evidence**: F4 tables note semantic restrictions. F6 tables rate verification [CORE].

**Interpretation**: Truth-creation domain resists causative constructions. Verification allowed because it implies discovering pre-existing truth. **Conceptual domain restriction**.

## 6. Summary

### Universal or Near-Universal Oppositions (8/8 or 7/8 languages)

**Lexical oppositions**:
- Truth/falsehood (8/8)
- Genuine/fake (7/8)
- Honest/lying (8/8, varying lexicalization)

**Morphological oppositions**:
- Privative affixation (7/8)
- Abstract quality nominalization (8/8)

**Constructional oppositions**:
- Copula affirmation/negation (8/8)
- Light verb truth-telling/lying (8/8)
- Revelation/concealment (8/8)

### Universal Asymmetries (8/8 languages)

**1. Agentive nominalization**:
- Liar: Lexicalized in all 8 languages
- Truth-teller: Absent or periphrastic in 7/8 languages

**2. Inchoative restriction** (6/8 languages):
- Simple "become true": Blocked or marked
- Revelation "come to light": Productive

**3. Causative restriction** (documented in 4+ languages):
- Verification sense: Productive
- Creation sense: Blocked or marked

### Major Cross-Language Variations

**1. Copula negation strategies**:
- Negative copula distinct: Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean
- Negative particle before verb: Georgian, Yoruba, Basque
- Negative existential: Tamil

**2. Passive voice preferences**:
- Passive preferred: Finnish, Korean, Indonesian
- Active/intransitive preferred: Yoruba, Basque, Tamil
- Both common: Georgian, Turkish

**3. Inchoative productivity**:
- Highly productive: Indonesian, Turkish
- Blocked or restricted: Korean, Georgian, Basque, Finnish, Tamil

**4. Register stratification**:
- Extensive: Korean, Tamil, Turkish
- Moderate: Indonesian, Finnish
- Minimal: Yoruba, Georgian, Basque

### Systematic Gaps (All 8 languages)

1. **Truth-teller agentive** — Universal gap
2. **Dispositional causation** — Universal gap (no constructions for causing character change)
3. **Degree modification of truth nominals** — Blocked in most languages; only adjectival forms accept

### Language-Specific Elaborations

1. **Indonesian**: Exceptional salah- compound productivity (14+ error types)
2. **Korean**: Internal oppositional compounds (진위, 허실); color metaphors for lies
3. **Finnish**: Partitive case requirement for general truth
4. **Turkish**: Inchoative+causative stacking; postpositional grammaticalization
5. **Yoruba**: Ideophonic modification system

---

**Conclusion**: The eight languages show strong convergence in core oppositional structures (truth/falsehood, genuine/fake, honest/lying) and universal asymmetries (agentive nominalization, inchoative blocking, causative restrictions). Major variations appear in copula strategies, passive voice preferences, and register-based elaboration. Three systematic gaps appear across all languages: truth-teller agentives, dispositional causation, and degree modification of truth nominals.
