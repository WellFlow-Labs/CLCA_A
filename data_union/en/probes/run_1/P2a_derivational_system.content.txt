## Item 1: *true*

---

**1.1**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *truly*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-ly* (adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, really
- **Usage:** Functions as an adverb of manner or degree, affirming genuineness or sincerity (*truly sorry*, *truly remarkable*). Also used in formal letter closings (*Yours truly*).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *truth*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + Old English *-th* (abstract noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** truth, the quality of being true
- **Usage:** The primary nominalization of *true*; refers to the property of being true or to a true statement. Already documented in P1 (item 2) and P1b (1.2).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *trueness*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-ness* (abstract noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being true, accuracy, fidelity
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being true or accurately aligned, particularly in technical and engineering contexts (*trueness of a measurement*). Less common than *truth* in philosophical or everyday use. Documented in P1b (1.6).
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**1.4**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *untrue*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* (negation) + *true*
- **Natural English translation:** untrue, false, unfaithful
- **Usage:** Applied to statements that do not correspond to fact, and to persons who are unfaithful or disloyal, particularly in literary or older usage. Documented in P1b (1.4).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *truer*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-er* (comparative suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** more true, more accurate, more genuine
- **Usage:** The regular comparative form of *true*, used in comparative constructions (*no truer statement has ever been made*). Fully productive but unremarkable morphologically.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.6**
- **Base Form:** *true*
- **Derived Form:** *truest*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-est* (superlative suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** most true, most accurate, most genuine
- **Usage:** The regular superlative form of *true*, used in superlative constructions (*the truest account available*). Fully productive but unremarkable morphologically.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note on *true*:** The comparative and superlative forms (*truer*, *truest*) are attested and grammatically regular but carry no special derivational interest beyond standard adjectival inflection. The most analytically significant derivations are *truth*, *truly*, *untrue*, and *trueness*. The form *true* also functions as a verb in technical contexts (*to true a wheel*, *to true up a surface*), generating *truing* and *trued*, but these are peripheral to the truth-evaluation domain.

## Item 2: *truth*

---

**2.1**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *truthful*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *truth* + *-ful* (having the quality of)
- **Natural English translation:** truthful, habitually telling the truth
- **Usage:** Applied to persons and statements to indicate a disposition toward or instance of telling the truth. Contrasts with *untruthful* and *dishonest*. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.2**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *truthfully*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *truth* + *-ful* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in a truthful manner, honestly
- **Usage:** Adverbial form of *truthful*, used to indicate that a statement is made honestly or that an action is performed with honesty (*I can truthfully say that...*).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.3**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *truthfulness*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *truth* + *-ful* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being truthful, honesty
- **Usage:** Refers to the habitual disposition to tell the truth, applied to persons and accounts. Used in moral, psychological, and journalistic assessments of character and reliability.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.4**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *untruthful*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *truth* + *-ful*
- **Natural English translation:** not truthful, dishonest, false
- **Usage:** Applied to persons and statements to indicate a failure or refusal to tell the truth. Softer in connotation than *lying* but stronger than *inaccurate*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.5**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *untruthfully*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *truth* + *-ful* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in an untruthful manner, dishonestly
- **Usage:** Adverbial form of *untruthful*, used to characterize statements or actions as dishonest. Less common than *falsely* or *dishonestly* but attested in formal and journalistic usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.6**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *untruthfulness*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *truth* + *-ful* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being untruthful, dishonesty
- **Usage:** Refers to the disposition or practice of not telling the truth. Used in moral and psychological assessments. Less common than *dishonesty* or *deceptiveness* in everyday usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.7**
- **Base Form:** *truth*
- **Derived Form:** *untruth*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *truth*
- **Natural English translation:** a false statement, a lie, the condition of being false
- **Usage:** Refers to a statement that is not true, often with a softer connotation than *lie*, suggesting that the falsity may be partial or that the speaker is being diplomatic. Documented in P1b (1.5).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note on *truth*:** The derivational family of *truth* is moderately productive. The *truthful / untruthful* branch is the most active. The form *truthy* (meaning having the superficial appearance of truth, or in informal usage, seeming true) is attested in contemporary informal and media discourse but remains marginal in formal registers; it is not listed here as a standard derivation. The compound forms (*truth-telling*, *truth-seeker*, *truth value*, *truth condition*, *truth table*, *truth claim*, *ground truth*, *home truth*, *half-truth*, *post-truth*) are documented in P1b and are not repeated here.

---

- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being accurate, precision, correctness
- **Usage:** Refers to the degree of correspondence between a measurement, description, or representation and the actual state of affairs. Used in scientific, technical, journalistic, and everyday contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.3**
- **Base Form:** *accurate*
- **Derived Form:** *inaccurate*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *in-* (negation) + *accurate*
- **Natural English translation:** not accurate, incorrect, imprecise
- **Usage:** Applied to measurements, descriptions, and representations to indicate that they do not closely correspond to fact or reality. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.4**
- **Base Form:** *accurate*
- **Derived Form:** *inaccurately*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *in-* + *accurate* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in an inaccurate manner, incorrectly, imprecisely
- **Usage:** Adverbial form of *inaccurate*, used to characterize statements or representations as failing to correspond to fact (*inaccurately reported*, *inaccurately described*).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.5**
- **Base Form:** *accurate*
- **Derived Form:** *inaccuracy*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *in-* + *accurate* + *-cy*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being inaccurate; a specific error or imprecision
- **Usage:** Used both as an abstract noun referring to the property of being inaccurate and as a count noun referring to a specific error or imprecision in a text, measurement, or account (*the report contained several inaccuracies*).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note on *accurate*:** The derivational family is compact and regular. No agent noun (*\*accurator*) or verbal form (*\*accurize* is attested only in highly specialized firearms contexts and is not considered a standard derivation here) is in common use. The family is essentially limited to the adjective, its adverb, its abstract noun, and their negated counterparts.

## Item 4: *genuine*

---

**4.1**
- **Base Form:** *genuine*
- **Derived Form:** *genuinely*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in a genuine manner, sincerely, really
- **Usage:** Adverbial form, used to affirm that something is real, sincere, or authentic (*genuinely surprised*, *genuinely concerned*). Common across all registers, including as an intensifier.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.2**
- **Base Form:** *genuine*
- **Derived Form:** *genuineness*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being genuine, authenticity, sincerity
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being real, authentic, or sincerely felt. Less common than *authenticity* or *sincerity* in formal contexts but fully attested.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.3**
- **Base Form:** *genuine*
- **Derived Form:** *disingenuine*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *dis-* (negation/reversal) + *in-* + *genuine*
- **Natural English translation:** not genuine, insincere
- **Usage:** Occasionally attested as a back-formation or blend from *disingenuous*, but not a standard or widely accepted derivation. Included here with caution; *disingenuous* (see below) is the standard form.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

> **Note on *genuine*:** The standard negation in this family is *disingenuous* (see item 4.4 below), which is not a direct derivation from *genuine* but from the related adjective *ingenuous*. There is no standard *\*ingenuine* or *\*ungenuine* in common use. The derivational family of *genuine* itself is therefore limited primarily to *genuinely* and *genuineness*.

---

**4.4**
- **Base Form:** *genuine* (via related form *ingenuous*)
- **Derived Form:** *disingenuous*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *dis-* (negation) + *in-* (into, within) + Latin *genuinus* (native, natural)
- **Natural English translation:** not candid or sincere, giving a false impression of frankness
- **Usage:** Applied to persons and statements to indicate a lack of candor or an appearance of openness that conceals a hidden agenda. Semantically adjacent to *genuine* but derived through *ingenuous* rather than directly from *genuine*. Common in formal, journalistic, and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.5**
- **Base Form:** *genuine* (via *disingenuous*)
- **Derived Form:** *disingenuously*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *disingenuous* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in a disingenuous manner, with false candor
- **Usage:** Adverbial form of *disingenuous*, used to characterize statements or behavior as lacking genuine candor (*disingenuously claimed*, *disingenuously presented*).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.6**
- **Base Form:** *genuine* (via *disingenuous*)
- **Derived Form:** *disingenuousness*
- **Literal Breakdown:** *disingenuous* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being disingenuous, lack of candor
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of appearing candid while concealing a hidden motive or agenda. Used in moral and political assessments.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
