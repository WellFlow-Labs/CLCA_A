# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

The groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which items appear in the same contexts, which are contrasted with each other, which share typical collocating verbs or objects, which are restricted to particular registers, and which participate in the same evaluative frames. No external conceptual framework has been imposed. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data as documented.

---

## Group 1: Items used to assert or deny that something is the real thing (as opposed to a copy, imitation, or counterfeit)

**Label:** Genuineness vs. counterfeit evaluation

**Definition:** These items are used in contexts where the central question is whether an object, product, document, or person is what it claims or appears to be, as opposed to being a fake, imitation, or forgery. They appear together in commercial, legal, and authentication contexts, and they collocate with verbs such as 辨别 (to distinguish), 鉴别 (to appraise), 识别 (to identify), and 打假 (to crack down on fakes). The dominant contrast is between the genuine article and a counterfeit or imitation.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真 (zhēn) — the basic positive pole; "genuine, real"
- 假 (jiǎ) — the basic negative pole; "fake, not genuine"
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — the binomial framing the question of genuineness
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — the binomial used specifically in authentication contexts (art, documents, antiques)
- 真品 (zhēnpǐn) — "genuine article, authentic product"
- 真迹 (zhēnjì) — "authentic original work" (calligraphy, painting)
- 假货 (jiǎhuò) — "fake goods, counterfeit merchandise"
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — "to pass off as genuine, to impersonate"
- 仿冒 (fǎngmào) — "to counterfeit, imitation fraud" (especially branded goods)
- 伪造 (wěizào) — "to forge, to fabricate" (documents, evidence)
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎmào wěiliè) — "fake and inferior goods" (fixed regulatory expression)
- 打假 (dǎjiǎ) — "to crack down on counterfeits"
- 造假 (zàojiǎ) — "to produce fakes, to fabricate"
- 作假 (zuòjiǎ) — "to falsify, to commit fraud"
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — "to pass off the fake as genuine"
- 真假难分 (zhēnjiǎ nán fēn) — "hard to tell real from fake"
- 真伪难辨 (zhēnwěi nán biàn) — "difficult to distinguish genuine from counterfeit"
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — "to distinguish genuine from fake"
- 鉴别真伪 (jiànbié zhēnwěi) — "to authenticate, to appraise"
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) — "genuine goods at fair prices; the real thing"
- 真金不怕火炼 (zhēnjīn bù pà huǒ liàn) — "true gold fears no fire; genuine quality withstands testing"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 真伪 is more restricted to formal authentication contexts (art, antiques, legal documents); 真假 is broader and more everyday.
- 伪造 implies deliberate fabrication and is primarily legal in register; 假货 and 造假 are more general.
- 仿冒 is more specifically associated with trademark and branded goods; 假冒 is broader.
- 真迹 and 真品 are used specifically for objects being authenticated, not for statements or persons.

## Group 2: Items used to evaluate whether a statement, report, or account corresponds to the facts

**Label:** Factual accuracy of statements and representations

**Definition:** These items are used when evaluating whether a verbal or written representation — a news report, testimony, claim, or description — accurately reflects what actually happened or what is actually the case. They appear in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts, and they collocate with verbs such as 核实 (to verify), 失实 (to be inaccurate), 如实 (truthfully), 属实 (to be confirmed as accurate), and 与事实相符 (consistent with the facts). The dominant concern is the match or mismatch between a representation and an external reality.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真实 (zhēnshí) — "real, truthful, factual" (of representations)
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — "truthfulness, factual accuracy" (as a property)
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — "false, fictitious, fraudulent" (of statements and claims)
- 失实 (shīshí) — "inaccurate, not in accordance with the facts"
- 失真 (shīzhēn) — "distorted, having lost fidelity to the original or to reality"
- 如实 (rúshí) — "truthfully, in accordance with the facts" (adverbial)
- 相符 (xiāngfú) — "consistent with, matching" (relational)
- 不相符 (bùxiāngfú) — "inconsistent with, not matching"
- 核实 (héshí) — "to verify, to confirm accuracy"
- 属实 (shǔshí) — "to be confirmed as accurate, verified"
- 不实 (bùshí) — "untrue, inaccurate" (formal)
- 据实 (jùshí) — "based on the facts, in accordance with the actual situation"
- 失实 (shīshí) — "inaccurate, contrary to fact"
- 严重失实 (yánzhòng shīshí) — "seriously inaccurate"
- 如实陈述 (rúshí chénshù) — "to state truthfully"
- 如实反映 (rúshí fǎnyìng) — "to accurately reflect"
- 如实汇报 (rúshí huìbào) — "to report truthfully"
- 如实记录 (rúshí jìlù) — "to record faithfully"
- 与事实相符 (yǔ shìshí xiāngfú) — "consistent with the facts"
- 与真实不符 (yǔ zhēnshí bù fú) — "inconsistent with reality"
- 虚假宣传 (xūjiǎ xuānchuán) — "false advertising"
- 发布虚假信息 (fābù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to disseminate false information"
- 散布虚假消息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xiāoxi) — "to spread false news"
- 还原真实 (huányuán zhēnshí) — "to reconstruct what actually happened"
- 反映真实情况 (fǎnyìng zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "to reflect the true situation"
- 基于真实事件 (jīyú zhēnshí shìjiàn) — "based on true events"
- 报道失实 (bàodào shīshí) — "the report is inaccurate"
- 实录 (shílù) — "faithful record, veritable records"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 失实 implies departure from factual accuracy without necessarily implying intent; 虚假 carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or fraud.
- 失真 is used both in technical contexts (audio/visual distortion) and in evaluations of historical or journalistic accuracy; it emphasizes departure from an original rather than from an external fact.
- 如实 is specifically adverbial and appears in constructions specifying how something should be done (stated, reported, recorded); it does not function as a predicate adjective.
- 核实 and 属实 are process-oriented (verification) and outcome-oriented (confirmed) respectively; both belong to this group but represent different stages of the same evaluative activity.
- 相符 is relational: it requires two terms being compared, unlike 真实 or 失实 which can be predicated of a single item.

## Group 3: Items used in evaluating correctness relative to a standard, rule, or norm

**Label:** Correctness and accuracy relative to a standard

**Definition:** These items are used when evaluating whether something — an answer, a method, a calculation, a translation, a procedure — conforms to an applicable standard, rule, or norm. They appear in educational, technical, scientific, and administrative contexts. The dominant contrast is between conforming and not conforming to a standard, rather than between genuine and fake, or between a representation and external reality.

**Representative expressions:**

- 正确 (zhèngquè) — "correct, right, accurate" (relative to a norm)
- 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) — "correctness" (as a property)
- 不正确 (bùzhèngquè) — "incorrect, wrong"
- 错误 (cuòwù) — "error, mistake, wrong"
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — "accurate, precise, exact"
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — "accuracy, precision" (as a property)
- 不准确 (bùzhǔnquè) — "inaccurate, imprecise"
- 准确无误 (zhǔnquè wúwù) — "accurate and without error"
- 犯错误 (fàn cuòwù) — "to make a mistake"
- 纠正错误 (jiūzhèng cuòwù) — "to correct an error"
- 承认错误 (chéngrèn cuòwù) — "to admit a mistake"
- 严重错误 (yánzhòng cuòwù) — "a serious error"
- 完全正确 (wánquán zhèngquè) — "completely correct"
- 基本正确 (jīběn zhèngquè) — "basically correct"
- 判断是否正确 (pànduàn shìfǒu zhèngquè) — "to judge whether something is correct"
- 表述准确 (biǎoshù zhǔnquè) — "the formulation is accurate"
- 准确把握 (zhǔnquè bǎwò) — "to accurately grasp"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 正确 evaluates conformity with a norm or rule (a correct answer, a correct method, a correct political line); 准确 evaluates degree of exactness or precision (an accurate measurement, a precise translation). Both appear in this group because both involve evaluation against a standard, but they are not interchangeable.
- 错误 is the standard antonym of 正确 and covers both factual mistakes and moral or political errors; it is broader in application than 不准确.
- This group overlaps with Group 2: 准确 can describe both the precision of a measurement (Group 3) and the accuracy of a report (Group 2). The distinction is that Group 3 items foreground conformity to a standard or norm, while Group 2 items foreground correspondence to external reality.

## Group 4: Items used in evaluating the reliability or trustworthiness of sources, persons, or information over time

**Label:** Reliability and trustworthiness of sources and persons

**Definition:** These items are used when evaluating whether a person, source, piece of equipment, or body of information can be depended upon consistently, not just in a single instance. They appear in contexts of evidence evaluation, witness assessment, source citation, and character evaluation. The dominant concern is consistency and dependability over time rather than a single judgment of truth or accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**

- 可靠 (kěkào) — "reliable, dependable, trustworthy"
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) — "reliability" (as a property)
- 不可靠 (bùkěkào) — "unreliable, untrustworthy"
- 可靠感 (kěkàogǎn) — "sense of reliability"
- 来源可靠 (láiyuán kěkào) — "the source is reliable"
- 据可靠消息 (jù kěkào xiāoxi) — "according to reliable information"
- 经过可靠验证 (jīngguò kěkào yànzhèng) — "reliably verified"
- 诚实 (chéngshí) — "honest, truthful, sincere" (of persons)
- 诚实守信 (chéngshí shǒuxìn) — "honest and trustworthy"
- 不诚实 (bùchéngshí) — "dishonest"
- 老实 (lǎoshí) — "honest, straightforward, simple" (of persons)
- 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō) — "to be honest, frankly speaking"
- 踏实 (tāshí) — "steady, grounded, reliable" (of persons and approaches)
- 扎实 (zhāshí) — "solid, thorough, well-grounded" (of skills and knowledge)
- 真才实学 (zhēncái shíxué) — "genuine talent and solid learning"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 可靠 is applied to sources, evidence, equipment, and persons; 诚实 is applied specifically to persons and their conduct. 可靠 focuses on consistency and dependability; 诚实 focuses on the absence of deception.
- 老实 and 踏实 are more colloquial and character-oriented than 可靠 and 诚实; they describe a general disposition rather than a specific evaluative judgment about a source or claim.
- 扎实 and 真才实学 evaluate the substantiality of skills and knowledge rather than the honesty of a person, but they belong here because the dominant concern is whether something is genuinely solid and dependable rather than superficial or merely claimed.
- This group overlaps with Group 1 (is the person or thing genuinely what it claims to be?) and Group 5 (is the person's conduct honest?). The distinction is that Group 4 items foreground consistency and dependability over time, while Group 1 items foreground the genuine/counterfeit contrast and Group 5 items foreground the moral dimension of honesty in conduct.

## Group 5: Items used in moral evaluation of persons' conduct as honest or deceptive

**Label:** Moral evaluation of honesty and deception in conduct

**Definition:** These items are used when making moral judgments about whether a person is behaving honestly or deceptively toward others. They appear in interpersonal, moral, and legal contexts, and they focus on the agent's intentions and conduct rather than on the accuracy of a representation or the genuineness of an object. The dominant contrast is between honest dealing and deliberate deception.

**Representative expressions:**

- 诚实 (chéngshí) — "honest, truthful, sincere" (moral character)
- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) — "to deceive, deception"
- 欺骗者 (qīpiànzhě) — "deceiver"
- 欺骗公众 (qīpiàn gōngzhòng) — "to deceive the public"
- 受到欺骗 (shòudào qīpiàn) — "to be deceived"
- 不得欺骗消费者 (bùdé qīpiàn xiāofèizhě) — "must not deceive consumers"
- 说真话 (shuō zhēnhuà) — "to tell the truth"
- 说假话 (shuō jiǎhuà) — "to tell a lie"
- 假话 (jiǎhuà) — "lie, false statement"
- 实话 (shíhuà) — "truthful words, the truth"
- 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō) — "to speak the plain truth"
- 说真的 (shuō zhēn de) — "to be honest, honestly speaking"
- 表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī) — "consistent inside and out, genuine"
- 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) — "inconsistent inside and out, hypocritical"
- 假仁假义 (jiǎrén jiǎyì) — "false benevolence and righteousness, hypocritical virtue"
- 虚情假意 (xūqíng jiǎyì) — "hollow expressions of feeling, insincerity"
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — "sincere, wholehearted"
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — "sincere, genuine, heartfelt"
- 诚实待人 (chéngshí dàirén) — "to deal honestly with others"
- 为人诚实 (wéirén chéngshí) — "to be an honest person"
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — "false pretense, feigned intention"
- 假装 (jiǎzhuāng) — "to pretend, to feign"
- 伪装 (wěizhuāng) — "to disguise, to masquerade as"
- 伪装者 (wěizhuāngzhě) — "one who disguises, impostor"
- 识破伪装 (shípò wěizhuāng) — "to see through a disguise"
- 无欺 (wúqī) — "without deception, honest dealing"
- 童叟无欺 (tóng sǒu wúqī) — "no cheating of anyone" (commercial motto)
- 假公济私 (jiǎgōng jìsī) — "to use public office for private gain"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 欺骗 implies an intentional act with a victim; 假装 and 伪装 describe the performance of a false identity or state, which may or may not have a specific victim.
- 表里如一 / 表里不一 evaluate the consistency between outward conduct and inner character; 假仁假义 and 虚情假意 evaluate the sincerity of expressed moral sentiments specifically.
- 真心 and 真诚 are positive evaluations of sincerity in feeling and expression; they contrast with 假意 and 虚情假意.
- 假公济私 belongs here because it describes a form of deceptive conduct (using public authority under false pretenses for private benefit), though it also has a strong legal dimension.
- This group overlaps with Group 4 (诚实 appears in both) and with Group 6 (伪装 also appears in contexts of concealing identity rather than moral deception).

## Group 6: Items used when the question is whether something's outward appearance matches its actual nature

**Label:** Correspondence between appearance/label and actual nature

**Definition:** These items are used when evaluating whether a surface appearance, label, title, or outward form accurately corresponds to what something actually is. They appear in evaluative discourse about persons, institutions, products, and concepts, and they focus on the relationship between how something presents itself and what it fundamentally is. The dominant concern is not factual accuracy of a statement but the match between form and substance.

**Representative expressions:**

- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — "living up to one's name, worthy of the name"
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — "in name only, failing to match the description"
- 名副其实的专家 (míng fù qí shí de zhuānjiā) — "a genuine expert"
- 名不副实的头衔 (míng bù fù shí de tóuxián) — "a title that doesn't live up to its name"
- 本质 (běnzhì) — "essence, fundamental nature"
- 本质上 (běnzhì shàng) — "essentially, in essence"
- 实质 (shízhì) — "substance, real content"
- 实质上 (shízhì shàng) — "in substance, in actual fact"
- 实质性 (shízhìxìng) — "substantive"
- 揭示本质 (jiēshì běnzhì) — "to reveal the essence"
- 认清本质 (rènqīng běnzhì) — "to clearly recognize the true nature"
- 抓住本质 (zhuāzhù běnzhì) — "to grasp the essence"
- 触及实质 (chùjí shízhì) — "to get to the substance"
- 真正 (zhēnzhèng) — "genuine, real, truly" (emphatic)
- 真实感 (zhēnshígǎn) — "sense of realism, feeling of authenticity"
- 逼真 (bīzhēn) — "lifelike, true to life"
- 弄假成真 (nòngjiǎ chéngzhēn) — "what started as pretense becomes real"
- 真真假假 (zhēnzhēn jiǎjiǎ) — "a mixture of true and false, ambiguous"
- 半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ) — "half true, half false"
- 虚实 (xūshí) — "the real and the illusory, actual vs. apparent"
- 假象 (jiǎxiàng) — "false appearance, misleading phenomenon"
- 伪装成 (wěizhuāng chéng) — "to disguise oneself as, to pose as"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 名副其实 / 名不副实 evaluate the match between a label or title and actual qualities; 本质 and 实质 are used to identify what something fundamentally is beneath its surface.
- 逼真 and 真实感 evaluate representations (paintings, films, performances) for how closely they approach reality; they are aesthetic evaluations rather than judgments about actual identity.
- 假象 describes a misleading surface appearance that conceals a different underlying reality; it is used analytically to warn against being misled.
- 虚实 is used in strategic and negotiation contexts to refer to the distinction between what is genuinely the case and what is feigned or performed.
- 弄假成真 and 真真假假 describe dynamic or ambiguous situations where the boundary between genuine and false is unstable or has shifted; they are distinct from the static evaluations in Group 1.

## Group 7: Items used in the process of investigating, verifying, or establishing what is actually the case

**Label:** Investigative and verification processes

**Definition:** These items are used in contexts where the truth or facts of a matter are being actively sought, confirmed, or established through inquiry, examination, or expert appraisal. They appear in journalistic, legal, forensic, and administrative contexts, and they describe processes rather than static properties. The dominant concern is the activity of finding out or confirming, not the property of being true.

**Representative expressions:**

- 核实 (héshí) — "to verify, to confirm accuracy"
- 核实信息 (héshí xìnxī) — "to verify information"
- 经过核实 (jīngguò héshí) — "after verification"
- 无法核实 (wúfǎ héshí) — "unable to verify"
- 独立核实 (dúlì héshí) — "independent verification"
- 鉴定 (jiàndìng) — "to authenticate, to appraise"
- 进行鉴定 (jìnxíng jiàndìng) — "to conduct an appraisal"
- 经专家鉴定 (jīng zhuānjiā jiàndìng) — "as authenticated by experts"
- 司法鉴定 (sīfǎ jiàndìng) — "forensic appraisal"
- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — "the true state of affairs, the facts" (as the object of investigation)
- 揭露真相 (jiēlù zhēnxiàng) — "to expose the truth"
- 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) — "to investigate and establish the truth"
- 掩盖真相 (yǎngài zhēnxiàng) — "to cover up the truth"
- 了解真相 (liǎojiě zhēnxiàng) — "to learn the truth"
- 还原真相 (huányuán zhēnxiàng) — "to reconstruct the truth"
- 真相不明 (zhēnxiàng bù míng) — "the truth is unclear"
- 真相大白 (zhēnxiàng dàbái) — "the truth has come to light"
- 坐实 (zuòshí) — "to confirm, to establish as fact"
- 求实 (qiúshí) — "to seek truth, to pursue factual accuracy"
- 实事求是 (shíshì qiúshì) — "to seek truth from facts"
- 真凭实据 (zhēnpíng shíjù) — "solid evidence, genuine proof"
- 实证 (shízhèng) — "empirical evidence, empirical proof"
- 无从判断真伪 (wúcóng pànduàn zhēnwěi) — "unable to determine authenticity"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 核实 is used for verifying information and claims; 鉴定 implies specialized expert appraisal of objects, substances, or documents. Both are process-oriented but differ in the type of object and the expertise required.
- 真相 functions in this group as the object of investigative activity (揭露真相, 查明真相, 掩盖真相); it is the thing being sought or concealed. This is distinct from its use as a simple noun meaning "the facts."
- 实事求是 is both a methodological principle and a political slogan; it belongs here because it describes an orientation toward grounding conclusions in verified facts.
- 真凭实据 and 实证 belong here because they describe the evidentiary basis for establishing what is true, which is the outcome of verification processes.

## Group 8: Items used in philosophical, political, or ideological discourse about truth as a principle or value

**Label:** Truth as an abstract principle or value

**Definition:** These items are used in contexts where truth is discussed not as a property of a particular statement or object but as an abstract principle, value, or goal. They appear in philosophical, political, educational, and moral discourse, and they are typically the objects of verbs such as 追求 (to pursue), 坚持 (to uphold), 掌握 (to possess), and 颠扑不破 (indestructible). The dominant concern is truth as something to be sought, upheld, or possessed rather than verified or authenticated.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真理 (zhēnlǐ) — "truth as a principle, universal truth"
- 追求真理 (zhuīqiú zhēnlǐ) — "to pursue truth"
- 坚持真理 (jiānchí zhēnlǐ) — "to uphold truth"
- 掌握真理 (zhǎngwò zhēnlǐ) — "to possess the truth"
- 颠扑不破的真理 (diānpū bùpò de zhēnlǐ) — "an indestructible truth"
- 真理越辩越明 (zhēnlǐ yuè biàn yuè míng) — "truth becomes clearer through debate"
- 真善美 (zhēn shàn měi) — "the true, the good, and the beautiful"
- 真知灼见 (zhēnzhī zhuójiàn) — "genuine insight, penetrating and well-founded views"
- 实事求是 (shíshì qiúshì) — "to seek truth from facts" (as a political-epistemological principle)
- 务实 (wùshí) — "pragmatic, focused on practical realities"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- 真理 is the central item in this group; the others either modify it (颠扑不破的真理), describe activities directed toward it (追求真理, 坚持真理), or belong to the same discourse register.
- 真善美 situates 真 within a philosophical triad; it is included here because its usage is specifically philosophical and aesthetic rather than practical or legal.
- 实事求是 appears in both Group 7 (as a methodological orientation toward verification) and here (as a political-ideological principle). This overlap is genuine and reflects the term's dual function in Chinese discourse.
- 务实 is included here because it appears in the same political and administrative discourse as 实事求是, but it is closer to Group 3 (practical correctness) than to philosophical truth-seeking. Its placement here is tentative.

## Group 9: Items used specifically in legal and regulatory contexts to characterize false or fraudulent conduct

**Label:** Legal and regulatory characterization of fraud and falsification

**Definition:** These items are used primarily in legal, regulatory, and administrative contexts to characterize conduct that involves deliberate falsification, fraud, or misrepresentation. They appear in statutes, legal proceedings, regulatory documents, and news reporting on legal cases. The dominant concern is the legal characterization of conduct as fraudulent or falsified, with implications for liability and punishment.

**Representative expressions:**

- 伪造 (wěizào) — "to forge, to fabricate" (documents, evidence)
- 伪造文件 (wěizào wénjiàn) — "to forge documents"
- 伪造证据 (wěizào zhèngjù) — "to fabricate evidence"
- 涉嫌伪造 (shèxián wěizào) — "suspected of forgery"
- 伪造者 (wěizàozhě) — "forger"
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — "to impersonate, to pass off as genuine"
- 假冒品牌 (jiǎmào pǐnpái) — "to counterfeit a brand"
- 假冒他人身份 (jiǎmào tārén shēnfèn) — "to impersonate another person"
- 假冒者 (jiǎmàozhě) — "impersonator"
- 仿冒商品 (fǎngmào shāngpǐn) — "counterfeit goods"
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎmào wěiliè) — "fake and inferior goods"
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — "false, fictitious, fraudulent" (in legal characterizations)
- 虚假宣传 (xūjiǎ xuānchuán) — "false advertising"
- 虚假性 (xūjiǎxìng) — "falseness, fraudulent nature" (as a legal property)
- 假证 (jiǎzhèng) — "forged document, fake certificate"
- 作假 (zuòjiǎ) — "to falsify, to commit fraud"
- 造假 (zàojiǎ) — "to produce fakes, to fabricate"
- 打假 (dǎjiǎ) — "to crack down on counterfeits"
- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) — "to deceive, deception" (as a legal wrong)
- 不得欺骗消费者 (bùdé qīpiàn xiāofèizhě) — "must not deceive consumers"
- 假公济私 (jiǎgōng jìsī) — "to use public office for private gain"
- 司法鉴定 (sīfǎ jiàndìng) — "forensic appraisal"
- 实名制 (shímíng zhì) — "real-name registration system"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- This group overlaps substantially with Group 1 (genuineness vs. counterfeit) and Group 5 (moral evaluation of deception). The distinction is that Group 9 items are specifically associated with legal characterization and regulatory language, where the concern is not just moral evaluation but legal liability.
- 伪造 is more specifically legal than 造假; 造假 is used in broader contexts including academic fraud and data falsification.
- 虚假 in legal contexts (虚假宣传, 虚假广告) is more formal and carries stronger implications of legal violation than 假 alone.
- 实名制 is included here because it belongs to the regulatory domain of identity verification, though it is not itself a term for fraud.

## Group 10: Items used to evaluate the match between a person's inner state and outward expression

**Label:** Sincerity and consistency between inner and outer

**Definition:** These items are used when evaluating whether a person's expressed feelings, attitudes, or conduct genuinely reflect their inner state, as opposed to being performed or feigned. They appear in interpersonal, moral, and literary contexts. The dominant concern is not factual accuracy or legal fraud but the authenticity of emotional and interpersonal expression.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — "sincere, wholehearted; sincerity"
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — "sincere, genuine, heartfelt"
- 诚实 (chéngshí) — "honest, truthful, sincere"
- 表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī) — "consistent inside and out"
- 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) — "inconsistent inside and out, hypocritical"
- 假仁假义 (jiǎrén jiǎyì) — "false benevolence and righteousness"
- 虚情假意 (xūqíng jiǎyì) — "hollow expressions of feeling, insincerity"
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — "false pretense, feigned intention"
- 假装 (jiǎzhuāng) — "to pretend, to feign"
- 纯真 (chúnzhēn) — "pure and genuine, innocent, unspoiled"
- 真心话 (zhēnxīnhuà) — "words spoken from the heart"

**Notes on internal distinctions within this group:**

- This group overlaps with Group 5 (moral evaluation of honesty and deception). The distinction is that Group 10 items focus specifically on the match between inner feeling and outward expression, while Group 5 items focus more broadly on honest vs. deceptive conduct toward others.
- 纯真 is primarily applied to children or to a quality of innocence; it is less about deliberate sincerity and more about an unspoiled natural state.
- 表里如一 / 表里不一 are the most explicitly structural items in this group, framing the evaluation as a comparison between two dimensions (inside and outside).
- 真诚 and 真心 are close in meaning but 真诚 is slightly more formal; both describe the quality of genuine feeling in expression.

## Items That Do Not Fit Cleanly into a Single Group

The following items from P1 and P2 are noted as ambiguous, overlapping across multiple groups, or not fitting clearly into any of the groups above.

---

**实事求是 (shíshì qiúshì)**
Appears in Group 7 (investigative processes) and Group 8 (truth as a principle). In practice, it functions simultaneously as a methodological orientation (seek evidence before drawing conclusions) and as a political-ideological slogan. Its placement in either group alone would be reductive.

---

**真正 (zhēnzhèng)**
Used as an intensifying modifier across many contexts: genuine friendship (Group 10), the real thing (Group 1), truly correct (Group 3). It does not belong primarily to any one group; it functions as a general intensifier of genuineness across the domain.

---

**真实感 (zhēnshígǎn) and 逼真 (bīzhēn)**
These items evaluate representations (paintings, films, performances) for their closeness to reality. They belong partly to Group 6 (appearance vs. actual nature) and partly to a distinct aesthetic domain not fully captured by any group above. Their primary context is aesthetic criticism rather than truth-evaluation in the epistemic or legal sense.

---

**认真 (rènzhēn)**
Documented in P1b as a peripheral extension of 真. Its primary meaning is "serious, conscientious, earnest" — an attitude toward a task — rather than a judgment about truth or genuineness. It does not fit clearly into any of the groups above. It is noted here as ungrouped.

---

**当真 (dàngzhēn) and 较真 (jiàozhēn)**
Both involve treating something as genuinely true or being particular about accuracy, but their primary usage is pragmatic and attitudinal rather than evaluative of an object or statement. 当真 means to take something seriously or literally; 较真 describes a disposition to insist on exactness. Neither fits cleanly into the groups above. They are noted here as ungrouped.

---

**真空 (zhēnkōng), 真菌 (zhēnjūn), 真核 (zhēnhé)**
These are scientific and technical terms where 真 functions as a taxonomic or technical classifier. They are documented in P1b as peripheral extensions and do not belong to any of the groups above, which are all concerned with truth-evaluation in some form. They are noted here as ungrouped.

---

**真命天子 (zhēnmìng tiānzǐ)**
This item belongs to a domain of political legitimacy and cosmological mandate that is distinct from the truth-evaluation domain. It is noted here as ungrouped.

---

**假期 (jiàqī) and 请假 (qǐngjià)**
These use the jià reading of 假 (leave, holiday) and are entirely unrelated to the truth-evaluation domain. They are noted here as ungrouped.

---

**假设 (jiǎshè) and 假说 (jiǎshuō)**
These use 假 in the sense of "provisional, hypothetical" rather than "false or deceptive." They belong to a domain of scientific and logical reasoning about provisional propositions. They do not fit clearly into any of the groups above, which are all concerned with evaluation of actual truth or falsity rather than provisional assumption. They are noted here as ungrouped.

---

**原真性 (yuánzhēnxìng)**
This term is restricted to heritage conservation and museology discourse. It overlaps with Group 1 (genuineness) and Group 6 (appearance vs. actual nature) but its register is so specialized that it does not fit naturally into either group as documented. It is noted here as domain-restricted and only partially fitting.

---

**写真 (xiězhēn) and 写实 (xiěshí)**
These are primarily aesthetic and artistic terms. 写真 refers to realistic portraiture or photo shoots; 写实 refers to a realistic style in art. They belong to an aesthetic domain that overlaps with Group 6 (appearance vs. actual nature) but is not fully captured by it. They are noted here as partially fitting Group 6 but primarily belonging to aesthetic discourse.

---

**实名 (shímíng) and 实名制 (shímíng zhì)**
These belong to the regulatory domain of identity verification. They fit partly in Group 9 (legal and regulatory) but their primary concern is administrative identity management rather than fraud characterization. They are noted here as partially fitting Group 9.

---

**真本 (zhēnběn)**
This is a marginal, domain-restricted term from bibliographic scholarship. It fits partly in Group 1 (genuine vs. counterfeit) but its usage is too restricted to place it confidently in any group. It is noted here as ungrouped.

---
