### BASE: chiqan (#1)

#### Construction 1: chiqan-ta rimay
**Example:** chiqan-ta rimay
**Transliteration:** chiqanta rimay
**Translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth
**Usage:** Common construction combining chiqan (accusative) with rimay "to speak." Used in everyday contexts when discussing truthful speech.
**Status:** [CORE]

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

### Pattern 1: Evidential Marking
**Description:** Truth-related terms frequently combine with evidential suffixes (-mi, -si, -cha) to indicate epistemic stance.
**Examples:** chiqapmi, chiqapsi, chiqapcha, allinmi, sutimi
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 2: Accusative + Verb Constructions
**Description:** Truth/falsity terms in accusative case combine with speech verbs (rimay, willakuy, niy) to form "speak/tell X" constructions.
**Examples:** chiqanta rimay, llullata willakuy, chiqanta niy
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 3: Locative Constructions
**Description:** Truth/falsity terms with locative -pi indicate being "in" a state of truth/falsity.
**Examples:** chiqanpi kay, llullapi tiyay, yuyaypi kay
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 4: Directional Constructions
**Description:** Truth/falsity terms with directional -man indicate movement toward or resemblance to truth/falsity.
**Examples:** chiqanman kutiy, chiqanman rikch'ay, llullaman rikch'ay
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 5: Purposive Constructions
**Description:** Truth/goodness terms with purposive -paq indicate purpose or benefit.
**Examples:** chiqanpaq rimay, allinpaq
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 6: Prohibitive Constructions
**Description:** Negative imperatives with ama...chu prohibit lying or error.
**Examples:** ama llullakychu, ama pantaychu
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 7: Substantivizing with kaq
**Description:** Quality terms combine with kaq to create "that which is X" constructions.
**Examples:** chiqan kaq, llulla kaq, allin kaq
**Status:** [CORE]

### Pattern 8: Negation with mana
**Description:** Quality terms negate with mana to create opposites or denials.
**Examples:** mana allin kay, mana chanin kay, mana hunt'a kay, mana yachay
**Status:** [CORE]

## Summary

The compositional constructions in Quechua truth/falsity domain show:

1. **High productivity** with case-marked constructions, particularly accusative + verb patterns
2. **Systematic evidential marking** on truth-asserting terms
3. **Spatial metaphors** through locative and directional constructions
4. **Productive negation** patterns with mana
5. **Limited idiomatic expressions** beyond basic compositional patterns
6. **Asymmetry** in construction types across different bases (some bases show rich constructional possibilities, others very few)

The data reveals that Quechua relies heavily on transparent compositional patterns rather than opaque idioms for expressing truth/falsity concepts. The case system and evidential system provide the primary structural resources for elaborating these meanings.
