# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
## Language: English (en)

Since the language is English, no transliteration is required (Latin script, native to the language).

---

**1. true**
- Literal/morphological: from Old English *trēowe* "faithful, trustworthy"; base for *truth*, *truly*, *truthful*.
- Translations: accurate, correct, genuine, factual.
- Usage: Applied to statements that correspond to fact ("that is true") and to things matching their nature ("a true friend"). Highly productive across factual, moral, and relational contexts.
- [CORE]

**2. false**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *falsus* "deceived, feigned"; base for *falsity*, *falsehood*, *falsify*.
- Translations: untrue, incorrect, fake, deceptive.
- Usage: Marks statements not corresponding to fact and objects that misrepresent ("false teeth," "a false claim"). Used in logical, moral, and material senses.
- [CORE]

**3. accurate**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *accuratus* "done with care"; base for *accuracy*, *accurately*.
- Translations: correct, precise, exact.
- Usage: Applied to representations, measurements, and accounts that match reality closely. Common in technical, journalistic, and everyday evaluation of correctness.
- [CORE]

**4. genuine**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *genuinus* "natural, innate"; base for *genuinely*, *genuineness*.
- Translations: authentic, real, sincere.
- Usage: Describes objects not counterfeit ("genuine leather") and sincere persons or feelings ("genuine concern"). Spans material authenticity and moral sincerity.
- [CORE]

**5. authentic**
- Literal/morphological: from Greek *authentikos* "original, genuine"; base for *authenticity*, *authenticate*.
- Translations: genuine, real, bona fide.
- Usage: Applied to objects of verified origin and to experiences or expressions true to themselves ("authentic cuisine," "authentic emotion"). Frequent in aesthetic, legal, and cultural contexts.
- [CORE]

**6. correct**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *correctus* "made straight"; base for *correctness*, *correctly*, *correction*.
- Translations: right, accurate, proper.
- Usage: Marks answers, conduct, and procedures conforming to a standard. Used in factual, normative, and etiquette senses ("the correct answer," "correct behavior").
- [CORE]

**7. real**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *realis* "actual, relating to things"; base for *reality*, *really*, *realism*.
- Translations: actual, genuine, existing.
- Usage: Contrasts existence or genuineness with imitation or appearance ("a real diamond," "real danger"). Extremely productive across philosophical and everyday registers.
- [CORE]

**8. fake**
- Literal/morphological: origin uncertain (18th c. cant); functions as noun, verb, adjective.
- Translations: counterfeit, false, sham.
- Usage: Describes objects passed off as genuine and behavior that is feigned ("fake designer bag," "fake smile"). Common in informal and commercial contexts.
- [CORE]

**9. counterfeit**
- Literal/morphological: from Old French *contrefait* "made in imitation"; base for *counterfeiting*.
- Translations: forged, fake, imitation.
- Usage: Chiefly applied to fraudulent copies of currency, goods, or documents. Carries legal and economic connotations of deliberate deception.
- [CORE]

**10. reliable**
- Literal/morphological: *rely* + *-able*; base for *reliability*, *reliably*.
- Translations: dependable, trustworthy, consistent.
- Usage: Applied to sources, persons, and instruments that consistently perform or report accurately ("a reliable witness," "reliable data"). Common in evidential and evaluative discourse.
- [CORE]

**11. valid**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *validus* "strong"; base for *validity*, *validate*, *invalid*.
- Translations: sound, well-grounded, legitimate.
- Usage: Applied to arguments, claims, and documents that meet standards of soundness or legal force ("a valid argument," "a valid passport"). Frequent in logical, legal, and administrative contexts.
- [CORE]

**12. legitimate**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *legitimus* "lawful"; base for *legitimacy*, *legitimately*.
- Translations: lawful, genuine, justified.
- Usage: Describes that which conforms to law, rule, or accepted standards ("a legitimate business," "a legitimate concern"). Spans legal, political, and evaluative usage.
- [CORE]

**13. sincere**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *sincerus* "pure, unmixed"; base for *sincerity*, *sincerely*.
- Translations: honest, heartfelt, candid.
- Usage: Applied to persons and expressions free from pretense ("a sincere apology"). Concentrated in moral and interpersonal evaluation.
- [CORE]

**14. honest**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *honestus* "honorable"; base for *honesty*, *honestly*.
- Translations: truthful, upright, candid.
- Usage: Describes persons who tell the truth and acts free of deceit ("an honest answer"). Core to moral evaluation of conduct and speech.
- [CORE]

**15. faithful**
- Literal/morphological: *faith* + *-ful*; base for *faithfully*, *faithfulness*.
- Translations: loyal, accurate, true.
- Usage: Applied to loyal persons and to accurate representations ("a faithful translation," "a faithful spouse"). Bridges moral loyalty and representational accuracy.
- [CORE]

**16. proper**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *proprius* "one's own"; base for *propriety*, *properly*.
- Translations: correct, appropriate, fitting.
- Usage: Marks conduct or usage conforming to accepted standards ("proper procedure," "proper behavior"). Common in normative and etiquette contexts.
- [CORE]

**17. right**
- Literal/morphological: from Old English *riht* "straight, just"; base for *rightly*, *rightness*, *righteous*.
- Translations: correct, just, morally good.
- Usage: Marks correctness of answers and moral acceptability of conduct ("the right answer," "the right thing to do"). One of the most productive evaluative terms across factual and moral domains.
- [CORE]

**18. accurate-to-life / lifelike**
- Literal/morphological: *life* + *like*; compositional resemblance term.
- Translations: realistic, true-to-life.
- Usage: Describes images or depictions closely resembling their subject ("a lifelike portrait"). Concentrated in aesthetic and representational contexts.
- [CORE]

**19. verisimilitude**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *verisimilis* "like the truth" (*verus* + *similis*).
- Translations: appearance of truth, plausibility, realism.
- Usage: Used in literary and philosophical discussion of how convincingly a representation resembles reality. Restricted largely to academic and critical registers.
- [MARGINAL]

**20. veracious**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *verax* "truthful"; related to *veracity*.
- Translations: truthful, accurate.
- Usage: Formal term for a person or account habitually telling the truth ("a veracious witness"). Rare in everyday speech; mostly literary or legal.
- [MARGINAL]

**21. bona fide**
- Literal/morphological: Latin "in good faith"; fixed Latin borrowing.
- Translations: genuine, in good faith, authentic.
- Usage: Describes something genuine or undertaken honestly ("a bona fide offer"). Common in legal and formal registers.
- [CORE]

**22. spurious**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *spurius* "illegitimate, false".
- Translations: false, fake, not genuine.
- Usage: Applied to claims, reasoning, or objects falsely presented as genuine ("a spurious argument," "spurious data"). Concentrated in scholarly and formal contexts.
- [CORE]

**23. forged**
- Literal/morphological: *forge* "to fabricate" + *-ed*; base for *forgery*.
- Translations: falsified, counterfeit.
- Usage: Applied chiefly to documents, signatures, and artworks fraudulently made ("a forged signature"). Strongly legal in connotation.
- [CORE]

**24. trustworthy**
- Literal/morphological: *trust* + *worthy*; compositional compound.
- Translations: reliable, dependable, credible.
- Usage: Describes persons or sources deserving of trust ("a trustworthy account"). Common in moral and evidential evaluation.
- [CORE]

**25. plausible**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *plausibilis* "deserving applause"; base for *plausibility*.
- Translations: credible, believable, reasonable.
- Usage: Marks claims that appear reasonable or worthy of belief, without asserting their truth ("a plausible explanation"). Frequent in evidential and argumentative discourse.
- [CORE]

**26. just**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *justus* "lawful, right"; base for *justice*, *justly*, *justify*.
- Translations: fair, right, equitable.
- Usage: Applied to acts, decisions, and persons conforming to fairness or law ("a just verdict"). Central to moral and legal evaluation.
- [CORE]

**27. truthful**
- Literal/morphological: *truth* + *-ful*; base for *truthfulness*.
- Translations: honest, veracious.
- Usage: Describes persons and statements that tell the truth ("a truthful account"). Common in moral evaluation of speech.
- [CORE]

**28. exact**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *exactus* "precise, completed"; base for *exactly*, *exactness*, *exactitude*.
- Translations: precise, accurate.
- Usage: Marks correspondence to a standard without deviation ("an exact copy," "the exact figure"). Common across technical and everyday measurement contexts.
- [CORE]

**29. mimetic**
- Literal/morphological: from Greek *mimētikos* "imitative"; related to *mimesis*.
- Translations: imitative, representational.
- Usage: Used in aesthetics and literary theory for art that imitates reality. Restricted to scholarly and philosophical registers.
- [MARGINAL]

**30. credible**
- Literal/morphological: from Latin *credibilis* "believable"; base for *credibility*, *credibly*.
- Translations: believable, trustworthy, convincing.
- Usage: Applied to sources, witnesses, and claims worthy of belief ("a credible threat," "a credible witness"). Frequent in evidential, journalistic, and legal contexts.
- [CORE]

**Note on item 18:** "lifelike" is the clearly attested single lexical compound; "accurate-to-life" is a free paraphrase and should be disregarded — the supported form is **lifelike** [CORE].
