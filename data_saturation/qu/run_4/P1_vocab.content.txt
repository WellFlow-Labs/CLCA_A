# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope:** Quechua is a dialect continuum with significant lexical variation across varieties (Cusco-Collao, Ayacucho, Ecuadorian, etc.). Items below are drawn primarily from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka), the most documented varieties. Variety-specific distribution is noted where relevant. Transliteration follows the standard three-vowel orthography used in academic Quechua linguistics (as in Cerrón-Palomino and related sources), with digraphs for ejectives (p', t', k', q', ch') and aspirates (ph, th, kh, qh, chh).

---

## Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; no further decomposable morphology identified
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, correct, right

**Usage description:** *Cheqa* is the primary adjective used to predicate truth or genuineness of a statement, person, or state of affairs. It appears in constructions such as *cheqa simicha* ("true word / truth") and as a predicate nominal. It is highly productive as a derivational base.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqa + nominalizing/agentive suffix *-q*; "that which is true / one who is true"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real

**Usage description:** *Cheqaq* functions as an adjective or nominal and is used interchangeably with *cheqa* in many contexts, though it can carry a slightly more nominal or substantivized character. It appears in moral and testimonial contexts to describe a truthful person or a genuine state of affairs.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaqpaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaqpaq
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqaq + *-paq* (benefactive/purposive case); "for the true / toward truth"
**Typical English translations:** truly, in truth, for real

**Usage description:** Used as an adverbial expression asserting that something is genuinely or truly the case, often in contexts of emphasis or contrast with deception. Functions as a discourse-level truth marker.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqan
**Transliteration:** cheqan
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive / nominalizing suffix); "its truth / the true one"
**Typical English translations:** the truth, what is right, what is correct

**Usage description:** *Cheqan* is used to refer to the truth of a matter or the correct state of affairs, often in contexts of disclosure or rectification. It appears in phrases meaning "the truth is that…" or "what is really the case is…"

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha-* (causative/verbalizing) + *-y* (infinitive); "to make true / to verify / to straighten"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to correct, to rectify

**Usage description:** Used as a verb meaning to establish or confirm the truth of something, or to correct an error. Appears in legal and testimonial contexts as well as everyday speech when someone is setting the record straight.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; no further decomposable morphology identified
**Typical English translations:** false, lying, deceptive, fake

**Usage description:** *Llulla* is the primary adjective for falsehood and deception, used of statements, persons, and objects. It is the direct semantic counterpart to *cheqa* and participates in the same derivational patterns. A *llulla runa* is a liar; *llulla simi* is a false statement.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** llulla + *-ku-* (reflexive/middle) + *-y* (infinitive); "to lie (for oneself) / to deceive"
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods

**Usage description:** The reflexive-middle verbal form of *llulla*, used to describe the act of lying or deceiving. It is the standard verb for lying in Southern Quechua and appears in moral and legal discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** sut'i
**Transliteration:** sut'i
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root (ejective final); no further decomposable morphology identified
**Typical English translations:** clear, plain, explicit, exact, precise

**Usage description:** *Sut'i* describes clarity, explicitness, and precision in statements or representations. It is used to evaluate whether something is stated or presented without ambiguity or distortion. It overlaps with *cheqa* in some contexts but emphasizes clarity and distinctness rather than truth per se.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** sut'ichay
**Transliteration:** sut'ichay
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** sut'i + *-cha-* (causative/verbalizing) + *-y* (infinitive); "to make clear / to clarify"
**Typical English translations:** to clarify, to make explicit, to specify, to explain

**Usage description:** Used as a verb meaning to make something clear or explicit, often in contexts of explanation, legal testimony, or correction of misunderstanding. It is a productive derivation from *sut'i* and is widely used.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**10.**
**Form:** riqsiy
**Transliteration:** riqsiy
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** riqsi- (root: to know/recognize) + *-y* (infinitive); "to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with"
**Typical English translations:** to know, to recognize, to identify

**Usage description:** *Riqsiy* refers to knowledge through acquaintance or recognition, as opposed to propositional knowledge. It is used in contexts of identifying whether something or someone is genuine or correctly identified. Relevant to evaluations of authenticity and identity.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** yachay
**Transliteration:** yachay
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** yacha- (root: to know/learn) + *-y* (infinitive); "to know, to learn"
**Typical English translations:** to know, to learn, knowledge

**Usage description:** *Yachay* is the primary verb for propositional and practical knowledge in Quechua. It is used in contexts of evaluating whether someone knows the truth of a matter or has correct information. As a nominalized form it also means "knowledge" or "learning."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** iñiy
**Transliteration:** iñiy
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** iñi- (root: to believe) + *-y* (infinitive); "to believe"
**Typical English translations:** to believe, to trust, to have faith in

**Usage description:** *Iñiy* is used for belief and trust, including religious faith and interpersonal trust. It is relevant to evaluations of reliability and credibility. It is distinguished from *yachay* (to know) and participates in epistemic contrasts in discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**13.**

**Transliteration:** iñisqa

**Typical English translations:** believed, trusted, accepted as true

**Usage description:** The participial form of *iñiy*, used to describe something that is held to be true or trusted. Appears in contexts evaluating the credibility or accepted status of a claim or person.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**14.**
**Form:** pantay
**Transliteration:** pantay
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** panta- (root: to err, to be mistaken) + *-y* (infinitive); "to err, to be wrong"
**Typical English translations:** to err, to be mistaken, to be wrong, to make an error

**Usage description:** *Pantay* is the standard verb for being in error or making a mistake, used in evaluations of correctness of statements, actions, and judgments. It is the primary term for error and is widely used in moral, practical, and epistemic contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**15.**

**Transliteration:** pantasqa
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** panta- + *-sqa* (past participle / resultative); "erred, mistaken, wrong"
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, in error, incorrect

**Usage description:** The participial/adjectival form of *pantay*, used to describe a person, statement, or action that is incorrect or mistaken. Commonly used in evaluative contexts to characterize errors of fact or judgment.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**16.**
**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; no further decomposable morphology identified
**Typical English translations:** good, right, well, proper, correct

**Usage description:** *Allin* is the primary adjective for goodness and rightness across moral, practical, and evaluative domains. It is used to evaluate conduct, objects, and states of affairs as correct or proper. It is highly productive and appears in numerous compounds and derivations.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Typical English translations:** bad, wrong, improper, not right

**Usage description:** The standard negated form of *allin*, used to evaluate conduct, statements, or objects as wrong or improper. Functions as a fixed evaluative expression in moral and practical discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**18.**
**Form:** allinchaykuy
**Transliteration:** allinchaykuy
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** allin + *-cha-* (causative) + *-yki* (second-person possessive, here incorporated) + *-y* (infinitive) — or more standardly: allin + *-cha-* + *-ku-* + *-y*; "to make right for oneself / to correct oneself / to fix"
**Typical English translations:** to correct, to fix, to make right, to improve

**Usage description:** Used as a verb meaning to correct or rectify something, including errors in conduct or statement. The reflexive-middle morphology (*-ku-*) can indicate action on one's own behalf. Relevant to contexts of self-correction and rectification.

**26.**
**Form:** hina
**Transliteration:** hina
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; "like, similar to, as, in the manner of"
**Typical English translations:** like, similar to, as if, resembling

**Usage description:** *Hina* is used in comparisons and resemblance statements, relevant to evaluations of whether something resembles or is like something else. It is a highly frequent particle in Quechua and appears in contexts of likeness and simulation.

**27.**
**Form:** hinam

**Transliteration:** hinam
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** hina + *-m* (direct evidential clitic); "it is so / that is how it is (I assert directly)"
**Typical English translations:** it is so, that is right, indeed, that is how it is

**Usage description:** *Hinam* combines the similarity/manner particle *hina* with the direct evidential clitic *-m(i)*, producing an assertive expression meaning "it is indeed so" or "that is correct." It is used to affirm the accuracy or correctness of a state of affairs based on direct knowledge.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** -mi / -m
**Transliteration:** -mi / -m
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Enclitic; direct evidential marker; "I assert this from direct evidence / I know this directly"
**Typical English translations:** (no direct translation); marks direct evidential commitment; glossed as "indeed," "certainly," or left unmarked in English

**Usage description:** The direct evidential clitic *-mi* is attached to the focused constituent of a clause and signals that the speaker has direct evidence (typically perception or personal experience) for the truth of the assertion. It is a grammaticalized marker of epistemic commitment to accuracy and is central to Quechua truth-evaluation grammar.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** -si / -s
**Transliteration:** -si / -s
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Enclitic; reportative/hearsay evidential marker; "I assert this on the basis of report / I heard this"
**Typical English translations:** (no direct translation); marks reportative or hearsay source; glossed as "reportedly," "they say," or left unmarked in English

**Usage description:** The reportative evidential clitic *-si* signals that the speaker's information comes from another source rather than direct experience, marking a lower degree of personal epistemic commitment. It is directly relevant to evaluations of reliability and the source of knowledge claims.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** -chá
**Transliteration:** -chá
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Enclitic; conjectural/inferential evidential marker; "I conjecture / I suppose / it seems"
**Typical English translations:** (no direct translation); marks conjecture or inference; glossed as "probably," "I suppose," "perhaps"

**Usage description:** The conjectural clitic *-chá* marks that the speaker is inferring or guessing rather than asserting from direct knowledge, signaling epistemic uncertainty about the truth of the proposition. It is part of the same grammaticalized evidential system as *-mi* and *-si* and is directly relevant to the calibration of truth claims.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: Items 28–30 are grammatical clitics rather than lexical items in the traditional sense, but they are included because they are conventionalized, non-compositional in their evidential function, and central to how Quechua speakers grammatically encode epistemic commitment to truth and accuracy. They are among the most thoroughly documented features of Quechua grammar.*
