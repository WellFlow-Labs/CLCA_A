# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1, P1b, P2a)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This document records attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving the P1 lexical items. The inventory draws on classical Sanskrit literature, philosophical treatises, grammatical texts, legal literature, epic and Purāṇic texts, and (where noted) modern Sanskrit-medium usage.

The boundary between derivational compounds (handled in P2a) and multi-word constructions is observed throughout. Compounds written as single orthographic units are excluded here even when semantically compositional; the focus is on constructions that remain syntactically separable or that function as fixed multi-word phrases, collocations, or periphrastic expressions. Where a construction is a fixed phrase attested as such in the literature, it is included even if its components could in principle be separated.

Absence is recorded explicitly for each P1 item where no common construction is identified. Asymmetry across items is preserved and reflects the data.

## P1 Item 1: सत्य / *satya*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं वद
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
4. **English translation:** Speak the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed injunctive phrase from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1), widely cited in ethical and educational contexts as a foundational moral injunction. Functions as a complete imperative sentence and is quoted as a unit across philosophical, didactic, and popular discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं ब्रूयात्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
4. **English translation:** One should speak the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed prescriptive phrase from Manusmṛti and related Dharmaśāstra literature, stating the duty of truthful speech in the optative mood. Often cited as the first half of a longer injunction qualified by conditions under which truth-telling may be overridden (*satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt*, "speak truth, speak what is pleasing").
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं ब्रूयात् प्रियं ब्रूयात् न ब्रूयात् सत्यमप्रियम्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt na brūyāt satyam apriyam
4. **English translation:** One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasing; one should not speak truth that is unpleasant.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed three-part injunctive sequence from Manusmṛti (4.138), widely cited in ethical discourse on the conditions and limits of truthful speech. The full sequence is quoted as a unit in discussions of the relationship between truth and kindness in speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यमेव जयते
3. **Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
4. **English translation:** Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. Quoted as a complete sentence across philosophical, political, and rhetorical contexts; functions as a maxim asserting the ultimate triumph of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः
3. **Transliteration:** satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ
4. **English translation:** Established in truth / grounded in truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the locative of *satya* with the past passive participle *pratiṣṭhita* (established, grounded, from *prati-* + root *sthā-*), used in epic and Purāṇic literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness. The locative + *pratiṣṭhita* pattern is productive across ethical vocabulary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यात् न प्रचलति
3. **Transliteration:** satyāt na pracalati
4. **English translation:** Does not deviate from truth / does not swerve from truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the ablative of *satya* with the negative particle *na* and the verb *pracalati* (deviates, moves away from, from *pra-* + root *cal-*), used in epic literature to characterize a person of unwavering truthfulness. The ablative + *na pracalati* pattern is a standard way of expressing steadfast adherence to a value.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं परं धर्मः
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ
4. **English translation:** Truth is the highest dharma.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed maxim attested in the Mahābhārata (e.g., Ādiparvan) and widely cited in ethical discourse, asserting the primacy of truthfulness among all dharmic obligations. Functions as a complete nominal sentence and is quoted as a unit.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं हि परमो धर्मः
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ
4. **English translation:** For truth is indeed the highest dharma.
5. **Usage notes:** A variant of the preceding maxim with the emphatic particle *hi* (indeed, for), widely attested in the Mahābhārata and in later ethical literature. The two forms (*satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ* and *satyaṃ hi paramo dharmaḥ*) circulate as near-synonymous maxims and are sometimes cited interchangeably.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्येन उत्तभिता भूमिः
3. **Transliteration:** satyena uttabhitā bhūmiḥ
4. **English translation:** The earth is upheld by truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed cosmological statement attested in Vedic and epic literature, asserting that truth is the foundational support of the earth and cosmos. Used in hymnic and didactic contexts to express the ontological and cosmological primacy of *satya*. The instrumental *satyena* (by means of truth) with a passive participle is the standard construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Example:** सत्यं न परित्यजेत्
3. **Transliteration:** satyaṃ na parityajet
4. **English translation:** One should not abandon truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A prescriptive construction using the accusative of *satya* with the negative optative of *parityaj-* (to abandon, from *pari-* + root *tyaj-*), used in Dharmaśāstra and epic literature to state the duty of maintaining truthfulness under all circumstances. The negative optative construction is a standard prescriptive form in Sanskrit ethical literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 2: ऋत / *ṛta*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य पथा
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya pathā
4. **English translation:** By the path of cosmic order / along the way of truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase from Vedic hymns (Ṛgveda), using the genitive of *ṛta* with the instrumental of *path* (path, way), designating the way of cosmic order along which the gods and the righteous travel. Attested as a formulaic expression in ritual and cosmological contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतं च सत्यं च
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca
4. **English translation:** Both cosmic order and truth / ṛta and satya.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed dvandva-style pairing attested in Vedic texts (e.g., Ṛgveda 10.190.1: *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhyajāyata*), where *ṛta* and *satya* are enumerated together as the foundational principles arising from cosmic heat (*tapas*). The pairing functions as a formulaic expression of the two aspects of cosmic and moral truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतेन ऋतम् अपिहितम्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtena ṛtam apihitam
4. **English translation:** Truth is covered by truth / cosmic order is veiled by cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed enigmatic phrase from the Ṛgveda (5.62.1), using the instrumental of *ṛta* with the past passive participle *apihita* (covered, concealed, from *api-* + root *dhā-*), expressing the paradoxical concealment of cosmic order within itself. Cited in philosophical and exegetical literature as an example of Vedic enigmatic speech (*brahmodya*).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Example:** ऋतस्य योनौ
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya yonau
4. **English translation:** In the womb/source of cosmic order.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed locative phrase from Vedic hymns, using the genitive of *ṛta* with the locative of *yoni* (womb, source, origin), designating the primordial source from which cosmic order arises or in which it is grounded. Attested in Ṛgvedic hymns in cosmological and ritual contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further constructions:** The derivational productivity of *ṛta* in multi-word constructions is largely confined to Vedic Sanskrit. Post-Vedic multi-word constructions built on *ṛta* as a free element are not commonly attested. The forms listed above represent the main attested expressions.

## P1 Item 3: यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं वदति
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ vadati
4. **English translation:** Speaks accurately / speaks as the matter is.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb (accusative of manner) with the verb *vadati* (speaks, from root *vad-*), used in philosophical and legal contexts to characterize speech that accurately represents its object. The adverbial use of *yathārtha* with verbs of speaking and knowing is widely attested.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं ज्ञानम्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ jñānam
4. **English translation:** Knowledge that is as the object is / accurate cognition.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominal phrase using *yathārtha* as an adjective modifying *jñāna* (knowledge), used in epistemological literature to characterize cognition that correctly corresponds to its object. Functionally equivalent to *pramā* but emphasizing the correspondence relation explicitly. Attested across Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā epistemological literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थं न भवति
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ na bhavati
4. **English translation:** Is not as the object is / does not correspond to the object.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb with the negative copula *na bhavati* (is not), used in epistemological and aesthetic contexts to characterize a representation, description, or cognition that fails to correspond to its object. The negative form is used to identify cases of inaccuracy or misrepresentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Example:** यथार्थदर्शिन्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthadarśin
4. **English translation:** One who sees things as they are / one who perceives accurately.
5. **Usage notes:** A compound adjective using *yathārtha* with *darśin* (one who sees, from root *dṛś-*), used in philosophical and epic literature to characterize a person who perceives or understands things as they actually are. While morphologically a compound, it functions as a predicate construction in context and is included here as a fixed characterizing expression.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** No idiomatic constructions specific to *yathārtha* that are not reducible to its adverbial or adjectival use are identified. The term functions primarily as an adjective and adverb, and its constructions are largely transparent compositional uses.

## P1 Item 4: प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणं किम्?
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇaṃ kim?
4. **English translation:** What is the means of valid knowledge? / What is the authority?
5. **Usage notes:** A standard interrogative formula in Sanskrit philosophical debate, used to challenge an opponent to specify the valid means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*) by which a claim is established. Attested as a formulaic opening move in philosophical disputation across Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Buddhist epistemological literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇena siddham
4. **English translation:** Established by a valid means of knowledge / proven by authority.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the instrumental of *pramāṇa* with the past passive participle *siddha* (established, from root *sidh-*), used in philosophical discourse to assert that a claim has been epistemically warranted. The instrumental + *siddha* pattern is a standard formula for asserting epistemic warrant across philosophical schools.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणाभावात्
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāvāt
4. **English translation:** Due to the absence of a valid means of knowledge / for lack of evidence.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed ablative phrase using the compound *pramāṇābhāva* (absence of a valid means of knowledge) in the ablative of cause, used in philosophical argument to explain why a claim cannot be established or why a position must be rejected. Widely attested as a standard dismissive formula in Sanskrit philosophical debate.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणं नास्ति
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇaṃ nāsti
4. **English translation:** There is no valid means of knowledge / there is no authority for this.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *pramāṇa* as subject with the negative existential *nāsti* (there is not, from *na* + *asti*), used in philosophical and legal discourse to deny that a claim has any epistemic warrant. A standard formula for rejecting an opponent's position.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रमाणतः सिद्धम्
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇataḥ siddham
4. **English translation:** Established from the standpoint of valid cognition / proven by the means of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the ablative/adverbial *pramāṇataḥ* (from the standpoint of valid means of knowledge) with *siddha*, used in philosophical argument to assert that a claim is warranted by the relevant epistemic standards. The *-taḥ* adverbial form is productive across epistemological vocabulary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** प्रत्यक्षं प्रमाणम्
3. **Transliteration:** pratyakṣaṃ pramāṇam
4. **English translation:** Perception is a valid means of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed nominal sentence attested in Nyāya and Buddhist epistemological literature, asserting the status of perception (*pratyakṣa*) as a *pramāṇa*. Functions as a foundational thesis in epistemological argument and is cited as a unit in discussions of the number and types of valid means of knowledge.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Example:** अनुमानं प्रमाणम्
3. **Transliteration:** anumānaṃ pramāṇam
4. **English translation:** Inference is a valid means of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed nominal sentence parallel to the preceding, asserting the status of inference (*anumāna*) as a *pramāṇa*. Attested in Nyāya and Buddhist epistemological literature as a foundational thesis. The pattern "X *pramāṇam*" is a standard formula for asserting the epistemic status of a cognitive source.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 5: प्रमा / *pramā*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. **Example:** प्रमा जायते
3. **Transliteration:** pramā jāyate
4. **English translation:** Valid cognition arises / true knowledge is produced.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *pramā* as subject with the verb *jāyate* (arises, is produced, from root *jan-*), used in epistemological literature to describe the production of valid cognition as the result of employing a *pramāṇa*. The construction marks *pramā* as the outcome of an epistemic process.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. **Example:** प्रमातृ-प्रमाण-प्रमेय-त्रयम्
3. **Transliteration:** pramātṛ-pramāṇa-prameya-trayam
4. **English translation:** The triad of knower, means of knowledge, and object of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed technical phrase enumerating the three elements of the epistemological triad in Sanskrit philosophy. While the three elements are compounds, the phrase as a whole — with *trayam* (triad) — functions as a fixed multi-word expression used to introduce or summarize the epistemological framework. Attested across Nyāya and related literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** *Pramā* as a technical noun does not generate a large set of independent multi-word constructions beyond its role in the epistemological triad and the basic predications noted above. Its constructions are largely absorbed into the *pramāṇa* cluster.

## P1 Item 6: अप्रमा / *apramā*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** अप्रमा (*apramā*)
2. **Example:** अप्रमा न प्रमाणजन्या
3. **Transliteration:** apramā na pramāṇajanyā
4. **English translation:** Erroneous cognition is not produced by a valid means of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used in epistemological literature to define *apramā* by negating its origin in a *pramāṇa*; the compound *pramāṇajanya* (produced by a valid means of knowledge) with the negative particle *na* is a standard way of characterizing invalid cognition. Attested in Nyāya epistemological texts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** *Apramā* is a technical term functioning primarily as the negation of *pramā*. Multi-word constructions built specifically on *apramā* as a free element are not commonly attested beyond the basic negation and definitional patterns. The term appears most often as a subject or predicate in technical philosophical sentences rather than in fixed multi-word expressions.

## P1 Item 7: सत् / *sat*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** सत् किम्?
3. **Transliteration:** sat kim?
4. **English translation:** What is real? / What truly exists?
5. **Usage notes:** A standard interrogative formula in Vedāntic philosophical inquiry, used to open investigation into the nature of genuine being or reality. Attested in Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic literature as a foundational question of ontological inquiry.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** सदेव सोम्येदमग्र आसीत्
3. **Transliteration:** sad eva somyedam agra āsīt
4. **English translation:** In the beginning, dear one, this was being alone / only *sat* existed at first.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed sentence from the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (6.2.1), one of the most cited passages in Vedāntic literature, asserting that pure being (*sat*) alone existed before creation. Quoted as a unit in philosophical discussions of cosmogony and the nature of ultimate reality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** सन्मात्रम्
3. **Transliteration:** sanmātram
4. **English translation:** Being alone / pure being / nothing but being.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed expression using *sat* with the limiting particle *mātra* (only, mere, nothing but), used in Vedāntic literature to characterize ultimate reality as pure undifferentiated being. While morphologically a compound, it functions as a fixed philosophical expression and is cited as a unit.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** सतः न असतः जायते
3. **Transliteration:** sataḥ na asataḥ jāyate
4. **English translation:** From being, not from non-being, is it produced.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed philosophical statement attested in Vedāntic literature (cf. Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.2.2), asserting the ontological principle that being arises only from being, not from non-being. Used in discussions of causation and the nature of reality; the ablative construction (*sataḥ*, *asataḥ*) with *jāyate* is the standard form.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** नासतो विद्यते भावः
3. **Transliteration:** nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ
4. **English translation:** Of non-being there is no existence / the unreal has no being.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed sentence from the Bhagavadgītā (2.16), asserting the ontological principle that the unreal (*asat*) has no genuine existence. Widely cited in Vedāntic and philosophical literature as a statement of the unreality of the phenomenal world. The genitive of *asat* with *na vidyate bhāvaḥ* is the standard construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
2. **Example:** नाभावो विद्यते सतः
3. **Transliteration:** nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ
4. **English translation:** Of being there is no non-existence / the real has no cessation.
5. **Usage notes:** The second half of the Bhagavadgītā verse (2.16) paired with the preceding construction, asserting the indestructibility of the real (*sat*). The two halves are regularly cited together as a unit in philosophical discussions of the nature of being and non-being.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## P1 Item 8: असत् / *asat*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)
2. **Example:** नासतो विद्यते भावः
3. **Transliteration:** nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ
4. **English translation:** Of non-being there is no existence.
5. **Usage notes:** See P1 Item 7, Construction 5. Listed here also as a construction involving *asat* as the primary term. The genitive *asataḥ* with *na vidyate bhāvaḥ* is the standard construction for asserting the unreality of *asat*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)
2. **Example:** असत् न प्रमाणगोचरम्
3. **Transliteration:** asat na pramāṇagocaram
4. **English translation:** The unreal is not within the range of valid means of knowledge.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used in Nyāya and Vedānta philosophical literature to assert that what is genuinely unreal (*asat*) cannot be an object of valid cognition. The compound *pramāṇagocara* (within the range of a valid means of knowledge) with the negative particle is a standard formula for asserting epistemic inaccessibility.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)
2. **Example:** असतः सत् न जायते
3. **Transliteration:** asataḥ sat na jāyate
4. **English translation:** From non-being, being is not produced.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed philosophical statement asserting the ontological principle that being cannot arise from non-being, attested in Vedāntic literature and in philosophical debate about causation. The ablative of *asat* with *na jāyate* is the standard construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 9: तथ्य / *tathya*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** तथ्य (*tathya*)
2. **Example:** तथ्यं वदति
3. **Transliteration:** tathyaṃ vadati
4. **English translation:** Speaks the facts / speaks what is factually true.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *tathya* in the accusative with the verb *vadati* (speaks), used in legal and narrative contexts to characterize testimony or report that accurately represents the facts. Attested in legal literature and in epic narrative contexts evaluating the reliability of speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** तथ्य (*tathya*)
2. **Example:** तथ्यं किम्?
3. **Transliteration:** tathyaṃ kim?
4. **English translation:** What is the fact? / What is actually the case?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction used in legal and narrative contexts to ask what the actual facts of a matter are, as opposed to what has been alleged or reported. Attested in legal literature and in epic contexts of inquiry and investigation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** *Tathya* does not generate a large set of multi-word constructions. Its use is more restricted than *satya*, and its constructions are largely transparent compositional uses of the adjective/noun in standard syntactic positions. No idiomatic constructions specific to *tathya* are identified.

## P1 Item 10: मिथ्या / *mithyā*

---

**Construction 1**
