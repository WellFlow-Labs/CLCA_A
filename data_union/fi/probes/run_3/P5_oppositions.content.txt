# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Preliminary Note

The following oppositions are extracted directly from patterns documented across P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4a/P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit attestation in the context. No oppositions have been inferred or constructed beyond what the data supports. Where asymmetry exists between the two terms (e.g., one is more productive, more frequent, or more restricted in register), this is noted in the description. The list is not organized by type in advance; type is assigned after description.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** tosi
**Term B:** epätosi
**Transliteration:** tosi / epätosi
**English translation:** true / untrue, false
**Description of contrast:** *Epätosi* is formed by applying the negating prefix *epä-* directly to *tosi*, producing its logical negation. The contrast is used in propositional truth attribution contexts (*se on tosi* — "that is true" vs. *väite on epätosi* — "the claim is false"); *epätosi* is noted as more characteristic of written and academic registers than *tosi*, which is common across all registers including colloquial speech.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** tosi
**Term B:** taru
**Transliteration:** tosi / taru
**English translation:** true, real / legend, myth, tale
**Description of contrast:** The fixed contrastive phrase *tosi vai tarua* ("fact or fiction / true or legend") pairs *tosi* with *taru* (legend, myth, tale) as a framing device in journalism and popular media. This is a lexical opposition between factual reality and legendary or fictional narrative, not a morphological negation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** totuus
**Term B:** epätotuus
**Transliteration:** totuus / epätotuus
**English translation:** truth / untruth, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** *Epätotuus* is formed by *epä-* prefixation on *totuus* and refers to a statement or claim that is not true. The context notes it is somewhat less formal than *valhe* (lie) and more explicitly the negation of *totuus*; it is used in everyday and journalistic contexts (*puhua epätotuuksia* — "to say untruths/falsehoods").
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** totuus
**Term B:** valhe
**Transliteration:** totuus / valhe
**English translation:** truth / lie
**Description of contrast:** The context notes *epätotuus* as "somewhat less formal than *valhe* (lie)" when describing falsehoods, implying *valhe* as the lexical counterpart to *totuus* in the domain of deliberate untruth. *Valhe* is not a P1 item but is referenced in the context as a contrasting term. The opposition is between truth as a property of statements and lying as a deliberate act.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** oikea
**Term B:** väärä
**Transliteration:** oikea / väärä
**English translation:** right, correct, genuine / wrong, false, fake
**Description of contrast:** These are the primary lexical antonyms for correctness and incorrectness across multiple domains documented in P1 and P3. The opposition operates in propositional contexts (*oikea vastaus* — "the correct answer" vs. *väärä väite* — "a false claim"), in personal epistemic attribution (*olla oikeassa* — "to be right" vs. *olla väärässä* — "to be wrong"), and in object authenticity contexts (*oikea nahka* — "real leather" vs. *väärä seteli* — "counterfeit banknote"). The context explicitly documents *oikein vai väärin* ("right or wrong") as a contrastive pair.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** olla oikeassa
**Term B:** olla väärässä
**Transliteration:** olla oikeassa / olla väärässä
**English translation:** to be right / to be wrong
**Description of contrast:** These are lexicalized inessive constructions using *olla* (to be) with *oikeassa* (inessive of *oikea*) and *väärässä* (inessive of *väärä*) respectively. They form the standard Finnish construction for attributing epistemic correctness or incorrectness to a person rather than to a proposition. The inessive case is lexicalized in both forms and does not carry its usual locative meaning. The context documents both as a natural contrastive pair (P2b, P4a Construction 10).
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** oikein
**Term B:** väärin
**Transliteration:** oikein / väärin
**English translation:** correctly, rightly / wrongly, incorrectly
**Description of contrast:** These are the adverbial forms of *oikea* and *väärä* respectively, documented as a contrastive pair (*oikein vai väärin* — "right or wrong") in P2b. Both combine productively with verbs: *tehdä oikein* ("to do correctly/rightly") contrasts with *tehdä väärin* ("to do wrong/incorrectly"). *Väärin* is additionally highly productive in forming misaction compounds (*ymmärtää väärin*, *tulkita väärin*, *käyttää väärin*), a productivity not paralleled by *oikein*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

## Opposition 9

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** vilpitön
**Term B:** vilpillinen
**Transliteration:** vilpitön / vilpillinen
**English translation:** sincere, in good faith, guileless / fraudulent, dishonest, deceitful, in bad faith
**Description of contrast:** Both are derived from the base noun *vilppi* (fraud, deceit): *vilpitön* with the privative suffix *-tön* (absence of vilppi) and *vilpillinen* with the adjectival suffix *-llinen* (characterized by vilppi). They are thus not in a simple *epä-* prefixation relationship but form a lexical opposition through different derivational paths from the same base. Both are P1 items and are explicitly contrasted in P1 (*vilpillinen* is "contrasted with *vilpitön*"). The opposition is central to both moral evaluation and legal good-faith doctrine.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
