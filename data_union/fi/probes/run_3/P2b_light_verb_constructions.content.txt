# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

**Preliminary note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Constructions already documented in P1b as fixed collocations (e.g., *tosiasioiden vääristely*, *väärä todistus*, *väärä vala*) are cross-referenced rather than re-documented in full. The boundary between a lexicalized compound (handled in P2a) and a compositional multi-word construction is not always sharp in Finnish; where a form has been treated as a compound in P1b or P2a, it is not re-listed here. Asymmetry across items is preserved throughout.

---

## 1. tosi

**Base Form:** tosi

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Example:** Se on tosi.
- **Transliteration:** Se on tosi.
- **English translation:** That is true. / That's real.
- **Usage notes:** Predicative use of *tosi* as a complement to *olla* (to be). The most basic propositional truth-attribution construction in Finnish. Used in both spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Example:** Onko se tosi?
- **Transliteration:** Onko se tosi?
- **English translation:** Is that true? / Is that real?
- **Usage notes:** Interrogative form of the predicative construction. Extremely common in everyday spoken Finnish when seeking confirmation of a surprising or unexpected claim.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Example:** tosi hyvä / tosi iso / tosi vaikea
- **Transliteration:** tosi hyvä / tosi iso / tosi vaikea
- **English translation:** really good / really big / really difficult
- **Usage notes:** *Tosi* used as a degree adverb modifying an adjective. This is a colloquial intensifier construction, extremely common in spoken Finnish, particularly among younger speakers. Functionally equivalent to *todella* + adjective but more informal.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Example:** tosi vai tarua
- **Transliteration:** tosi vai tarua
- **English translation:** fact or fiction / true or legend / real or myth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed contrastive phrase pairing *tosi* with *taru* (legend, myth, tale) in the partitive. Used as a heading or framing device in journalism, popular media, and everyday speech to introduce a question about whether something is real or legendary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Example:** Nyt on tosi kyseessä.
- **Transliteration:** Nyt on tosi kyseessä.
- **English translation:** Now it's for real. / This time it's serious.
- **Usage notes:** A colloquial construction indicating that a situation has become genuinely serious or real, as opposed to hypothetical or playful. *Kyseessä* is the inessive of *kysymys* (question, matter) in a fixed postpositional use.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Example:** Tosi on tosi.
- **Transliteration:** Tosi on tosi.
- **English translation:** True is true. / The truth is the truth. / A fact is a fact.
- **Usage notes:** A tautological affirmation used rhetorically to assert that something cannot be denied or argued away. Carries a resigned or emphatic tone in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. totuus

**Base Form:** totuus

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Example:** kertoa totuus
- **Transliteration:** kertoa totuus
- **English translation:** to tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** The basic verb-object construction for truth-telling. *Totuus* appears in the nominative as the direct object of *kertoa* (to tell, to narrate). Used across all registers. Cross-referenced from P1 item 2.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Example:** sanoa totuus
- **Transliteration:** sanoa totuus
- **English translation:** to say the truth, to tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** Variant of the truth-telling construction using *sanoa* (to say) rather than *kertoa*. Both are natural; *kertoa* implies a fuller account while *sanoa* is more direct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Example:** puhua totta
- **Transliteration:** puhua totta
- **English translation:** to tell the truth, to speak the truth
- **Usage notes:** A common construction using the partitive *totta* (of *tosi*) as the object of *puhua* (to speak). Note that this construction uses the partitive of *tosi* rather than *totuus*; it is included here because it expresses the same propositional content as *kertoa totuus* and is closely related semantically.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Example:** totuus on se, että...
- **Transliteration:** totuus on se, että...
- **English translation:** the truth is that..., the fact is that...
- **Usage notes:** A framing construction used to introduce a corrective or candid statement. Common in spoken and written Finnish across registers. Functionally similar to English "the truth is that."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Example:** totuus tulee ilmi
- **Transliteration:** totuus tulee ilmi
- **English translation:** the truth comes out, the truth comes to light
- **Usage notes:** A fixed verb-phrase construction using *tulla ilmi* (to come to light, to be revealed). Used in everyday, journalistic, and legal contexts to describe the revelation of previously hidden truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Example:** totuus paljastuu
- **Transliteration:** totuus paljastuu
- **English translation:** the truth is revealed, the truth comes out
- **Usage notes:** Uses the reflexive/passive verb *paljastua* (to be revealed, to be exposed). Semantically overlaps with *totuus tulee ilmi* but *paljastua* carries a stronger sense of exposure or unmasking.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Example:** etsiä totuutta
- **Transliteration:** etsiä totuutta
- **English translation:** to seek the truth, to search for the truth
- **Usage notes:** *Totuutta* is the partitive of *totuus*, used as the object of *etsiä* (to seek, to search for). Common in philosophical, journalistic, and everyday contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Example:** totuus on karvas
- **Transliteration:** totuus on karvas
- **English translation:** the truth is bitter, the truth hurts
- **Usage notes:** A conventional expression describing the unpleasant nature of truth. *Karvas* means bitter. Used in everyday speech and writing to acknowledge that a true statement is unwelcome.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
- **Example:** koko totuus
- **Transliteration:** koko totuus
- **English translation:** the whole truth, the full truth
- **Usage notes:** *Koko* (whole, entire) modifying *totuus* in a noun phrase. Used in legal contexts (cf. oath formulas) and everyday speech to emphasize completeness of disclosure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
- **Example:** koko totuus ja vain totuus
- **Transliteration:** koko totuus ja vain totuus
- **English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase used in oath-taking contexts, corresponding to the English legal formula. *Vain* means "only." Used in formal legal proceedings and also cited in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11:**
- **Example:** totuus on jotain muuta
- **Transliteration:** totuus on jotain muuta
- **English translation:** the truth is something else, the reality is different
- **Usage notes:** A corrective construction used to introduce a contradicting account. Common in journalistic and argumentative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12:**
- **Example:** totuuden nimissä
- **Transliteration:** totuuden nimissä
- **English translation:** in the name of truth, for the sake of truth
- **Usage notes:** A postpositional phrase using *nimissä* (in the name of, for the sake of). Used in rhetorical and moral contexts to invoke truth as a justification for a statement or action.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13:**
- **Example:** totuuden mukainen kertomus
- **Transliteration:** totuuden mukainen kertomus
- **English translation:** a truthful account, an account in accordance with the truth
- **Usage notes:** A noun phrase using the genitive *totuuden* + *mukainen* (conforming to) as a modifier of *kertomus* (account, narrative). Used in legal and journalistic contexts. Note: this is the compositional phrase underlying the lexicalized compound *totuudenmukainen* (P1 item 27); both forms are in use.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. oikea

**Base Form:** oikea

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Example:** oikea vastaus
- **Transliteration:** oikea vastaus
- **English translation:** the correct answer, the right answer
- **Usage notes:** The basic attributive construction with *oikea* modifying a noun. Cross-referenced from P1 item 3. Extremely common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Example:** oikea henkilö
- **Transliteration:** oikea henkilö
- **English translation:** the right person
- **Usage notes:** Attributive use of *oikea* to indicate the appropriate or correct person for a role or purpose. Cross-referenced from P1 item 3.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Example:** oikea nahka
- **Transliteration:** oikea nahka
- **English translation:** real leather, genuine leather
- **Usage notes:** Attributive use of *oikea* to indicate genuine material as opposed to imitation. Cross-referenced from P1 item 3.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Example:** olla oikeassa
- **Transliteration:** olla oikeassa
- **English translation:** to be right, to be correct
- **Usage notes:** A fixed predicative construction using *oikea* in the inessive case (*oikeassa*) with *olla* (to be). This is the standard Finnish construction for asserting that a person is correct in their view or claim. Extremely common in all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Example:** olla väärässä
- **Transliteration:** olla väärässä
- **English translation:** to be wrong, to be mistaken
- **Usage notes:** The parallel construction to *olla oikeassa*, using *väärä* in the inessive. Documented here under *oikea* as the two form a natural contrastive pair. See also under *väärä* below.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Example:** osoittautua oikeaksi
- **Transliteration:** osoittautua oikeaksi
- **English translation:** to prove to be correct, to turn out to be right
- **Usage notes:** Uses *osoittautua* (to prove to be, to turn out to be) with *oikeaksi* (translative of *oikea*). Used in everyday and journalistic contexts to describe a prediction or claim that is confirmed.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Example:** oikeaan aikaan
- **Transliteration:** oikeaan aikaan
- **English translation:** at the right time
- **Usage notes:** *Oikea* in the illative modifying *aika* (time). A common adverbial phrase used across all registers. Included here as an example of *oikea* in a temporal appropriateness context rather than an epistemic one.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Example:** oikealla tavalla
- **Transliteration:** oikealla tavalla
- **English translation:** in the right way, correctly
- **Usage notes:** *Oikea* in the adessive modifying *tapa* (way, manner). A common adverbial phrase used to describe correct procedure or method. Functionally equivalent to the adverb *oikein* in many contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
- **Example:** oikein vai väärin
- **Transliteration:** oikein vai väärin
- **English translation:** right or wrong, correct or incorrect
- **Usage notes:** A contrastive pair using the adverbial forms *oikein* (correctly) and *väärin* (incorrectly). Used in educational, evaluative, and everyday contexts to frame a binary correctness judgment.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
- **Example:** tehdä oikein
- **Transliteration:** tehdä oikein
- **English translation:** to do right, to do the right thing, to do correctly
- **Usage notes:** *Oikein* (adverb, correctly/rightly) as a complement to *tehdä* (to do). Used in both moral and procedural senses. Contrasts with *tehdä väärin* (to do wrong/incorrectly).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. oikeellisuus

**Base Form:** oikeellisuus

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Example:** kielellinen oikeellisuus
- **Transliteration:** kielellinen oikeellisuus
- **English translation:** linguistic correctness
- **Usage notes:** Cross-referenced from P1 item 4. The most common attributive construction with *oikeellisuus*, used in linguistic and editorial contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Example:** tietojen oikeellisuus
- **Transliteration:** tietojen oikeellisuus
- **English translation:** the correctness of the information, the accuracy of the data
- **Usage notes:** A genitive noun phrase with *tietojen* (genitive plural of *tieto*, information/data) modifying *oikeellisuus*. Common in administrative, legal, and data-processing contexts where the accuracy of recorded information is at issue.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Example:** varmistaa tietojen oikeellisuus
- **Transliteration:** varmistaa tietojen oikeellisuus
- **English translation:** to verify the accuracy of the information, to ensure the correctness of the data
- **Usage notes:** A verb-object construction with *varmistaa* (to ensure, to verify) taking *tietojen oikeellisuus* as its object. Standard in administrative and legal language concerning data accuracy obligations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Example:** vastata tietojen oikeellisuudesta
- **Transliteration:** vastata tietojen oikeellisuudesta
- **English translation:** to be responsible for the accuracy of the information
- **Usage notes:** Uses *vastata* (to be responsible for) with the elative *oikeellisuudesta*. A standard formulation in administrative and legal texts establishing responsibility for data accuracy.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

No further common multi-word constructions with *oikeellisuus* as the head or key element have been identified beyond these patterns.

- **Transliteration:** virheellinen menettely
- **English translation:** erroneous procedure, faulty conduct

**Construction 3:**

- **Transliteration:** virheellinen käsitys
- **English translation:** erroneous belief, mistaken understanding, false impression
- **Usage notes:** *Virheellinen* modifying *käsitys* (understanding, conception, impression). Used in everyday and academic contexts to describe a mistaken belief or false impression about something.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Example:** virheellinen päätös
- **Transliteration:** virheellinen päätös
- **English translation:** erroneous decision, flawed ruling
- **Usage notes:** *Virheellinen* modifying *päätös* (decision, ruling). Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe a decision that is procedurally or substantively flawed.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Example:** osoittautua virheelliseksi
- **Transliteration:** osoittautua virheelliseksi
- **English translation:** to prove to be erroneous, to turn out to be incorrect
- **Usage notes:** Uses *osoittautua* (to prove to be) with *virheelliseksi* (translative of *virheellinen*). Used in legal, scientific, and journalistic contexts when a claim or procedure is found to be in error.

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Example:** perustua virheelliseen tietoon
- **Transliteration:** perustua virheelliseen tietoon
- **English translation:** to be based on incorrect information
- **Usage notes:** Uses *perustua* (to be based on) with *virheelliseen tietoon* (illative of *virheellinen tieto*). A standard construction in legal and administrative contexts when challenging a decision made on the basis of erroneous data.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

- **Usage notes:** Postpositional phrase using *nimissä* (in the name of). Used rhetorically to invoke honesty as a justification for a candid statement.
