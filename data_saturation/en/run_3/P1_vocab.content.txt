# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
**Language: English (en)**

---

**1. true**
- Form: true
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *trēowe* "faithful, trustworthy"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used of statements, propositions, beliefs, and representations that correspond to fact or reality. Also applied to persons and objects to indicate genuineness or fidelity to a standard. Participates in productive derivation: *truly*, *truth*, *truthful*, *untrue*.

**[CORE]**

---

**2. truth**
- Form: truth
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *trēowth* "fidelity, quality of being true"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Refers to the property of being true, or to a true statement or fact. Used across philosophical, legal, moral, and everyday contexts. Participates in compounds: *truth-telling*, *truth-value*, *half-truth*, *untruth*.

**[CORE]**

---

**3. accurate**
- Form: accurate
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *accuratus* "done with care," from *ad-* + *cura* "care"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to statements, measurements, descriptions, and representations that correctly reflect the facts or a target. Implies precision and freedom from error. Derives: *accuracy*, *accurately*, *inaccurate*.

**[CORE]**

---

**4. genuine**
- Form: genuine
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *genuinus* "native, innate," possibly from *genu* "knee" (as in placing a child on the knee to acknowledge paternity)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to objects, persons, emotions, and documents to indicate that they are what they purport to be, not counterfeit or simulated. Contrasts with *fake*, *spurious*, *counterfeit*. Derives: *genuinely*, *genuineness*.

**[CORE]**

---

**5. authentic**
- Form: authentic
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Greek *authentikos* "original, primary," from *authentes* "one acting on one's own authority"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used of objects, documents, artworks, persons, and experiences to indicate that they are original, not copied or falsified, and consistent with what they claim to be. Widely used in legal, aesthetic, philosophical, and everyday contexts. Derives: *authenticity*, *authenticate*, *authentically*, *inauthenticity*.

**[CORE]**

---

**6. valid**
- Form: valid
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *validus* "strong, effective," from *valere* "to be strong"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to arguments, contracts, claims, and procedures to indicate that they meet the relevant formal or substantive requirements for correctness or legal force. In logic, specifically denotes that the conclusion follows necessarily from the premises. Derives: *validity*, *validate*, *invalid*, *invalidate*.

**[CORE]**

---

**7. correct**
- Form: correct
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *correctus*, past participle of *corrigere* "to set right," from *com-* + *regere* "to rule, guide"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to answers, statements, behavior, and procedures that conform to a standard, rule, or fact. Used in both descriptive and normative contexts. Derives: *correctly*, *correctness*, *incorrect*, *correction*.

**[CORE]**

---

**8. fake**
- Form: fake
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: origin uncertain; possibly from German *fegen* "to sweep, polish" or thieves' cant
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to objects, documents, persons, and claims that are not what they purport to be, produced or presented with intent to deceive. Used as adjective, noun, and verb. Derives: *faker*, *fakery*.

**[CORE]**

---

**9. counterfeit**
- Form: counterfeit
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old French *contrefait* "made in imitation," from *contre-* "against, in opposition" + *fait* "made"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Refers specifically to fraudulent imitation of currency, documents, goods, or other items of value. Carries strong legal connotations of deliberate deception. Used as adjective, noun, and verb.

**[CORE]**

---

**10. spurious**
- Form: spurious
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *spurius* "illegitimate, false"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to claims, arguments, documents, and attributions that are not what they appear to be or that lack genuine basis. Common in academic, legal, and philosophical discourse. Derives: *spuriously*, *spuriousness*.

**[CORE]**

**11. reliable**
- Form: reliable
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: *rely* (from Old French *relier* "to bind") + *-able*
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to persons, sources, instruments, and methods that can be consistently depended upon to perform correctly or provide accurate information. Derives: *reliability*, *reliably*, *unreliable*.

**[CORE]**

---

**12. credible**
- Form: credible
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *credibilis* "worthy of belief," from *credere* "to believe"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to persons, testimony, claims, and sources that are worthy of belief, typically because they are plausible and the source is trustworthy. Used in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts. Derives: *credibility*, *credibly*, *incredible*, *incredibility*.

**[CORE]**

**13. veracious**
- Form: veracious
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *verax* (stem *verac-*) "truthful," from *verus* "true"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to persons and statements to indicate habitual truthfulness or factual accuracy. More formal and less common than *truthful*. Derives: *veracity*, *veraciously*.

**14. veracity**
- Form: veracity
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *verax* + *-itas*; "quality of being truthful"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Refers to the quality of being truthful or accurate, applied to persons, accounts, and statements. Common in legal and formal contexts, particularly in evaluating witness testimony. Derives from the same root as *veracious*.

**[CORE]**

---

**15. faithful**
- Form: faithful
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: *faith* (Latin *fides* "trust") + *-ful*
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to translations, reproductions, accounts, and persons to indicate close correspondence to an original or to a commitment. A *faithful* translation or reproduction preserves the essential features of its source. Derives: *faithfully*, *faithfulness*, *unfaithful*.

**[CORE]**

**16. fraudulent**
- Form: fraudulent
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *fraudulentus*, from *fraus* (stem *fraud-*) "deceit, trickery"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to acts, representations, documents, and claims that involve deliberate deception for gain. Primarily a legal term but used broadly. Derives: *fraud*, *fraudulently*, *fraudulence*.

**[CORE]**

**17. legitimate**
- Form: legitimate
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *legitimatus*, from *lex* (stem *leg-*) "law" + *-atus*
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to claims, authority, procedures, and persons to indicate conformity with law, rules, or accepted standards. Also used more loosely to mean "genuine" or "reasonable." Derives: *legitimacy*, *legitimately*, *illegitimate*, *legitimize*.

**[CORE]**

**18. bona fide**
- Form: bona fide
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin "in good faith," from *bona* "good" + *fide* (ablative of *fides* "faith")
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used as an adjective or adverb to indicate that something is genuine, without fraud or deception, and undertaken in good faith. Common in legal and formal contexts. Also used informally to mean simply "genuine."

**[CORE]**

**19. apocryphal**
- Form: apocryphal
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Greek *apokryphos* "hidden, obscure," from *apo-* "away" + *kryptein* "to hide"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to stories, sayings, and texts whose authenticity or historical accuracy is doubtful, though they may be widely circulated. Originally referred to texts excluded from the biblical canon. Derives: *apocryphally*; the noun *Apocrypha* is used for specific textual corpora.

**[CORE]**

**20. plausible**
- Form: plausible
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *plausibilis* "deserving applause, acceptable," from *plaudere* "to applaud"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to claims, explanations, and accounts that appear reasonable and worthy of belief, though not necessarily verified. Implies a weaker epistemic status than *credible* or *true*. Derives: *plausibility*, *plausibly*, *implausible*.

**21. specious**
- Form: specious
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *speciosus* "good-looking, showy," from *species* "appearance"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to arguments, claims, and reasoning that appear sound or valid on the surface but are actually flawed or misleading. Carries a negative evaluative force. Derives: *speciously*, *speciousness*.

**[CORE]**

**22. verisimilitude**
- Form: verisimilitude
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *verisimilitudo*, from *verus* "true" + *similis* "like" + *-tudo* (abstract noun suffix)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Refers to the appearance or quality of being true or real, especially in literary and aesthetic contexts where a work seems lifelike or believable. Also used in philosophy of science for the degree to which a theory approximates truth. A formal and relatively specialized term.

**23. sound**
- Form: sound
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *sund* "healthy, whole"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to arguments, reasoning, judgments, and advice to indicate that they are both valid in form and based on true premises, or more generally that they are well-founded and reliable. Derives: *soundly*, *soundness*, *unsound*.

**24. bogus**
- Form: bogus
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: origin uncertain; possibly from a device for making counterfeit coins
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to objects, claims, credentials, and persons that are not genuine or are fraudulent. More informal in register than *counterfeit* or *spurious*. Derives: no productive derivation widely attested.

**[CORE]**

**25. truthful**
- Form: truthful
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: *truth* + *-ful*
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to persons and statements to indicate that they tell or reflect the truth. Distinguished from *true* in that it more commonly characterizes persons and their habitual disposition toward honesty. Derives: *truthfully*, *truthfulness*, *untruthful*.

**[CORE]**

**26. sincere**
- Form: sincere
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *sincerus* "whole, pure, genuine"; etymology debated (possibly *semel* "once" + *cera* "wax," or from *sine* "without" + *cera*)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to persons, expressions, and intentions to indicate that they are genuine and not feigned or hypocritical. Evaluates the alignment between inner state and outward expression. Derives: *sincerely*, *sincerity*, *insincere*, *insincerity*.

**[CORE]**

**27. fictitious**
- Form: fictitious
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *ficticius* "artificial, feigned," from *fingere* "to shape, feign"
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to names, identities, accounts, and entities that are invented or fabricated rather than real. Used in legal contexts (e.g., *fictitious name*) and in literary contexts. Derives: *fictitiously*, *fictitiousness*; related to *fiction*, *fictive*.

- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to measurements, reproductions, descriptions, and statements to indicate complete correspondence with a standard or original, without deviation. Derives: *exactly*, *exactness*, *exactitude*, *inexact*.
