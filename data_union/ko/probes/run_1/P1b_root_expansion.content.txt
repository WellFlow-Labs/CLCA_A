# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** 진 (眞)
**Transliteration:** jin
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 진짜 (jinjja), 진위 (jinwi), 진본 (jinbon), 진정성 (jinjeonseong), 참 (cham) [semantic parallel]
**Count:** 5 direct morphological participants (진실, 진짜, 진위, 진본, 진정성)
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "true," "genuine," "real" — marking that something corresponds to what it actually is, as opposed to being false, imitated, or fabricated. Functions as the primary positive pole of the truth-authenticity axis in the domain.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** 실 (實)
**Transliteration:** sil
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 사실 (sasil), 실제 (silje), 본질 (bonjil)
**Count:** 4 direct morphological participants
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "real," "actual," "solid," "substantial" — marking that something has genuine existence or substance, as opposed to being empty, theoretical, or merely apparent. Contributes the dimension of actuality and concreteness to truth-related expressions.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** 거짓
**Transliteration:** geojit
**P1 items participating:** 거짓 (geojit), 거짓말 (geojitmal)
**Count:** 2 direct morphological participants as root; also semantically paired with 허위, 위조, 위증 (all containing 僞)

**Note on Root 3 selection:** The native Korean root 거짓 is highly productive in everyday speech but generates a relatively small number of distinct lexical items compared to the Sino-Korean roots. The Sino-Korean morpheme 위 (僞, "false/fake") — appearing in 위조 (wijo), 위증 (wijeung), 허위 (heowi) — is an alternative candidate for the third root. Both are included in the expansion below. The 위 (僞) morpheme is treated as the third root given its broader morphological productivity across formal registers.

**Form:** 위 (僞)
**Transliteration:** wi
**P1 items participating:** 위조 (wijo), 위증 (wijeung), 허위 (heowi)
**Count:** 3 direct morphological participants
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "false," "fake," "counterfeit" — marking that something is not what it purports to be, produced or presented deceptively. Functions as the primary negative pole in formal and legal registers of the truth-authenticity domain.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Root 1: 진 (眞) — jin

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 實(real/solid)
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
**Context of use:** Used across legal, journalistic, moral, and everyday contexts to refer to what is actually the case or to the quality of being truthful. Already listed in P1.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 진실하다
**Transliteration:** jinsil-hada
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 하다(to do/be)
**Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be sincere, to be genuine
**Context of use:** Predicative form used to characterize persons or their conduct as honest and without pretense. Common in moral evaluation and interpersonal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 진실성
**Transliteration:** jinsilseong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 性(-ness, quality)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, sincerity as a quality
**Context of use:** Used in evaluative discourse — journalistic, legal, and academic — to assess whether a person, account, or work possesses the quality of being truthful or genuine.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 진실되다
**Transliteration:** jinsil-doeda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 되다(to become/be)
**Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be genuine (slightly more stative than 진실하다)
**Context of use:** Used to describe persons or expressions as genuinely truthful; appears in literary and formal registers slightly more than 진실하다, with a nuance of inherent rather than performed quality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 진실로
**Transliteration:** jinsilro
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 로(adverbial particle)
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, sincerely
**Context of use:** Adverbial expression used to emphasize the genuine or sincere character of a statement or action. Appears in formal speech, religious texts, and literary prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 진실게임
**Transliteration:** jinsil-geim
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + game (loanword)
**Natural English translation:** truth game, truth-or-dare (truth component)
**Context of use:** Used colloquially to refer to a social game in which participants must answer questions truthfully, or more broadly to any situation framed as a test of honesty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 진실의 순간
**Transliteration:** jinsil-ui sungan
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 의(genitive) + 瞬間(moment)
**Natural English translation:** moment of truth
**Context of use:** Used to describe a decisive moment when the true nature of something or someone is revealed. Appears in journalistic, literary, and everyday speech, often as a calque of the Spanish/English expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 진실화해위원회
**Transliteration:** jinsil-hwahae-wiwonhoe
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 和解(reconciliation) + 委員會(commission)
**Natural English translation:** Truth and Reconciliation Commission
**Context of use:** Institutional name used in South Korean political and historical discourse to refer to official bodies investigating past human rights abuses. Appears in legal, political, and historical registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 짜(suffix)
**Natural English translation:** real, genuine; really, truly
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 진짜배기
**Transliteration:** jinjja-baegi
**Literal breakdown:** 진짜(genuine) + 배기(suffix indicating the genuine article)
**Natural English translation:** the real thing, the genuine article, the real deal
**Context of use:** Emphatic colloquial form of 진짜, used to stress that something is unambiguously genuine rather than imitation. Common in everyday speech and advertising.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 僞(false)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, truth or falsity
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Used in formal and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 진위를 가리다
**Transliteration:** jinwi-reul garida
**Literal breakdown:** 眞僞(truth or falsity) + 를(object marker) + 가리다(to distinguish, to determine)
**Natural English translation:** to determine the truth or falsity of something, to establish authenticity
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation used in legal, journalistic, and investigative contexts when the task is to establish whether a claim, document, or item is genuine or false.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 진위 여부
**Transliteration:** jinwi yeobu
**Literal breakdown:** 眞僞(truth or falsity) + 與否(whether or not)
**Natural English translation:** whether something is true or false, the question of authenticity
**Context of use:** Fixed nominal phrase used in formal writing and legal discourse to frame the question of whether a claim or item is genuine. Frequently appears in news reporting and official documents.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 진본
**Transliteration:** jinbon
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(genuine) + 本(original/copy)
**Natural English translation:** authentic original, genuine document or artwork
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Used in archival, legal, and art-historical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 진본 확인
**Transliteration:** jinbon hwagin
**Literal breakdown:** 眞本(authentic original) + 確認(confirmation/verification)
**Natural English translation:** authentication, verification of authenticity
**Context of use:** Used in archival, museum, and legal contexts to describe the process of confirming that a document or artwork is the genuine original.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 正(correct/proper) + 性(-ness)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Prominent in contemporary discourse on art, politics, and personal relationships.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 진정성 있다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong itda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正性(authenticity) + 있다(to exist/have)
**Natural English translation:** to be authentic, to have genuineness
**Context of use:** Predicative collocation used to evaluate whether a person's expression, political stance, or artistic work reflects genuine inner conviction rather than performance or calculation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 진정성 없다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong eopda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正性(authenticity) + 없다(to not exist/have)
**Natural English translation:** to lack authenticity, to be inauthentic
**Context of use:** Predicative collocation used critically to characterize expressions, apologies, or political gestures as hollow or performative rather than genuine.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 진정
**Transliteration:** jinjeong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 正(correct/proper)
**Natural English translation:** genuine, true, real (adjectival/adverbial base); also: petition, appeal (homograph with different Hanja)
**Context of use:** Used as a modifier or adverb meaning "truly" or "genuinely" in formal and literary registers. Note: 진정 (眞正) meaning "genuine" is distinct from 진정 (陳情) meaning "petition/appeal," though they share the same phonological form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 진정한
**Transliteration:** jinjeongan
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正(genuine/true) + 한(adjectival ending)
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real (attributive adjective)
**Context of use:** Attributive modifier used to characterize persons, relationships, values, or states as genuinely what they are claimed to be. Extremely common in literary, political, and everyday discourse: 진정한 친구 (a true friend), 진정한 의미 (the true meaning).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 진정으로
**Transliteration:** jinjeongeuro
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正(genuine) + 으로(adverbial particle)
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, sincerely
**Context of use:** Adverbial expression used to emphasize that an action or statement reflects genuine inner conviction. Common in formal apologies, declarations, and literary prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 진상
**Transliteration:** jinsang
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 相(appearance/state)
**Natural English translation:** the true state of affairs, the real situation, the facts of the matter
**Context of use:** Used in investigative, journalistic, and legal contexts to refer to what actually happened, as opposed to what was reported or alleged. Appears in 진상 규명 (clarification of the true facts), 진상 조사 (investigation into the true facts).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 진상 규명
**Transliteration:** jinsang gyumyeong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞相(true state of affairs) + 糾明(clarification/investigation)
**Natural English translation:** clarification of the facts, establishing the truth of what happened
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation used in political, legal, and journalistic discourse when calling for or conducting an official investigation into the actual facts of an incident or event.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 진상 조사
**Transliteration:** jinsang josa
**Literal breakdown:** 眞相(true state of affairs) + 調査(investigation)
**Natural English translation:** fact-finding investigation, inquiry into the true facts
**Context of use:** Used in institutional and governmental contexts to describe a formal investigation aimed at establishing what actually occurred. Common in political and legal reporting.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 진의
**Transliteration:** jinui
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 意(intention/meaning)
**Natural English translation:** true intention, real meaning, genuine purpose
**Context of use:** Used to refer to what someone actually means or intends, as opposed to what they say on the surface. Appears in diplomatic, legal, and literary contexts: 진의를 파악하다 (to grasp the true intention).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 진가
**Transliteration:** jinga
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 價(value/worth)
**Natural English translation:** true value, real worth
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the genuine or intrinsic worth of a person or thing, often in contexts where this worth has been underestimated or is finally recognized. Appears in literary and evaluative discourse: 진가를 발휘하다 (to show one's true worth).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 진면목
**Transliteration:** jinmyeonmok
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 面目(face/appearance)
**Natural English translation:** true nature, real character, genuine face
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the authentic character or nature of a person or thing, often revealed under pressure or over time. Common in literary and journalistic registers: 진면목을 드러내다 (to reveal one's true nature).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 진수
**Transliteration:** jinsu
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 髓(marrow/essence)
**Natural English translation:** the essence, the quintessence, the very best
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the most essential or finest aspect of something — a tradition, art form, or body of knowledge. Appears in cultural, culinary, and aesthetic discourse: 한국 음식의 진수 (the essence of Korean cuisine).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 진품
**Transliteration:** jinpum
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(genuine) + 品(article/item/quality)
**Natural English translation:** genuine article, authentic item, the real thing
**Context of use:** Used in commercial, legal, and art-market contexts to designate an item as authentic rather than counterfeit or imitation. Contrasts with 가품 (fake item) and 위조품 (forged item).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 진품명품
**Transliteration:** jinpum-myeongpum
**Literal breakdown:** 眞品(genuine article) + 名品(famous/renowned item)
**Natural English translation:** genuine masterpiece, authentic and renowned work
**Context of use:** Known primarily as the title of a long-running Korean television program about antique authentication. Used more broadly to refer to items that are both genuine and of high quality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** 진단
**Transliteration:** jindan
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 斷(to judge/determine)
**Natural English translation:** diagnosis, assessment
**Context of use:** Used in medical contexts to mean diagnosis, and more broadly to mean an authoritative assessment of the true state of something. Participates in 진단하다 (to diagnose), 진단서 (medical certificate/diagnosis document).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The root 진 (眞) is highly productive. Additional forms exist beyond those listed above, including 진리 (眞理, truth/principle), 진술 (陳述, statement — though this uses a different 진), and various technical or specialized compounds. The 30 items above represent the most common and clearly attested forms.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots identified above but appear in contexts where the contribution of the root seems to shift from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**진 (眞) — peripheral extensions:**

- **진수** (眞髓, jinsu): The root 진 (true/genuine) appears in a context of aesthetic and culinary excellence — "the quintessence" or "the finest" — where the evaluative dimension concerns quality or refinement rather than correspondence to fact.

- **진가** (眞價, jinga): The root 진 appears in an expression of worth or value — "true value" — where the domain is evaluation of merit rather than truth of a proposition.

- **진단** (眞斷, jindan): The root 진 appears in a medical and diagnostic context where the primary activity is identification and classification of a condition, not assertion of a truth-value.

- **진면목** (眞面目, jinmyeonmok): The root 진 appears in a context of character revelation — "true face/nature" — where the domain concerns identity and self-presentation rather than propositional truth.

- **진정** (眞正, jinjeong): The same phonological form 진정 also exists as 陳情 (petition/appeal to authority), a completely unrelated meaning sharing only the sound.

---

**실 (實) — peripheral extensions:**

- **실력** (實力, sillok — note: not listed above as outside the truth-evaluation domain): The root 실 appears in expressions of actual ability or skill, where the domain is competence rather than truth.

- **실화** (實話, silhwa): The root 실 appears in a narrative/genre-classification context — "true story" — where the primary function is genre designation rather than propositional evaluation.

- **실명제** (實名制, silmyeongje): The root 실 appears in an administrative and identity-verification context, where the concern is identity authenticity rather than propositional truth.

- **사실주의** (事實主義, sasil-juui): The root 실 (via 사실) appears in an aesthetic and art-historical context — "realism" as an artistic movement — where the domain is representational fidelity and aesthetic doctrine rather than truth-evaluation of claims.

- **실현** (實現, silhyeon): The root 실 appears in a context of actualization or fulfillment — making something real — where the domain is achievement and process rather than truth-evaluation.

- **본질적** (本質的, bonjiljeok): The root 실 (via 질, 質) appears in a context of essential nature and ontological characterization, where the domain is metaphysical rather than epistemological.

---

**위 (僞) — peripheral extensions:**

- **위선** (僞善, wiseon): The root 위 (false) appears in a context of moral character evaluation — hypocrisy — where the domain is virtue and moral performance rather than propositional falsity or document fraud.

- **위장** (僞裝, wijang): The root 위 appears in military and tactical contexts — camouflage — where the domain is concealment and strategic deception rather than the falsity of a statement or document.

- **위작** (僞作, wijak): The root 위 appears in an aesthetic and art-historical context — forgery of artworks — where the domain is artistic attribution and authenticity of creative works rather than propositional truth.

- **위서** (僞書, wiseo): The root 위 appears in a scholarly and religious context — apocryphal or pseudepigraphical texts — where the domain is textual attribution and canon formation rather than everyday truth-evaluation.

---

**거짓 — peripheral extensions:**

- **거짓 평화** (geojit pyeonghwa): The root 거짓 appears in a political and theological context — "false peace" — where the domain is the evaluation of political or social conditions rather than the truth-value of a statement.

- **거짓 사랑** (geojit sarang): The root 거짓 appears in an emotional and relational context — "false love" — where the domain is the authenticity of feeling rather than the accuracy of a claim.

- **거짓 희망** (geojit huimang): The root 거짓 appears in a psychological and existential context — "false hope" — where the domain is the groundedness of an emotional state rather than the truth of a proposition.
