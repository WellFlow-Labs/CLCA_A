# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Adjective (truth-domain) + Noun (head), in standard pre-nominal attributive position. Agreement in gender and number is obligatory. Definite marking appears on both adjective and noun when the NP is definite.

### Examples
- חבר אמיתי / *ḥaver ʾamiti* / "a genuine friend"
- עד מהימן / *ʿed meheymen* / "a credible witness"

### Notes
Hebrew adjectives follow the noun they modify. Definiteness is marked by *ha-* on both noun and adjective: *ha-ʿed ha-meheymen* ("the credible witness"). Gender and number agreement is obligatory: *ʿedut meheymanah* (f.sg.), *meqorot meheymanim* (m.pl.).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negated predicate construction: Noun + *lo* + Adjective (predicative position), or attributive NP with negation at clause level. Hebrew has no negative attributive prefix productively applied to these adjectives; negation is syntactic rather than morphological.

### Examples
- המקור לא מהימן / *ha-meqor lo meheymen* / "the source is not reliable"
- זה לא נכון / *ze lo nakhon* / "that is not correct"

### Notes
Negation of attributive meaning is expressed predicatively with *lo* before the adjective. There is no productive negative prefix (e.g., *\*lo-meheymen* as a compound attributive) in standard usage. The construction *ḥoser* + abstract noun (*ḥoser kanut*, "lack of sincerity") provides an alternative nominal strategy for expressing the absence of a truth-domain quality.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Noun (truth-domain) in construct state (*smikhut*) or with *shel* ("of"), modifying a head noun. The truth-domain noun functions as a genitive modifier specifying a type or property of the head.

### Examples
- עדות שקר / *ʿedut sheker* / "false testimony" (lit. "testimony of falsehood")
- רגע האמת / *regaʿ ha-ʾemet* / "the moment of truth"

### Notes
The construct state (*smikhut*) is the primary structure: the modifying noun is bare and the head noun carries definiteness when the phrase is definite (*ʾaminut ha-ʿed*, "the credibility of the witness"). The *shel* periphrastic genitive is an alternative, more common in spoken registers. Both patterns are fully productive with truth-domain nouns.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Verb *lehafokh* ("to become") or *lehikhanes le-* ("to enter into a state of") + noun/adjective, or the verb *lehikhanes le-safek* ("to come into doubt"). More commonly, inchoative meaning is expressed periphrastically with *lehafokh le-* + noun.

### Examples
- האמון התערער / *ha-ʾimun hitʿarer* / "the trust was shaken / eroded" (inchoative of trust-loss)
- הספק גדל / *ha-safek gadal* / "the doubt grew"

### Notes
Hebrew lacks a dedicated inchoative morphological pattern for this semantic domain. Inchoative meaning is expressed through intransitive verbs (*hitʿarer*, "to be shaken"; *gadal*, "to grow") applied to truth-domain nouns, or through the verb *lehafokh le-* ("to become") with a complement. The hitpaʿel binyan frequently carries inchoative force: *lehitgalaʾh ke-mezuyaf* ("to turn out to be a forgery") is a common construction in this domain.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Hifʿil binyan verb derived from the relevant root, or periphrastic causative with *lgaram she-* ("to cause that") + clause. Direct morphological causatives exist for some roots.

### Examples
- להאמין / *lehaʾamin* / "to believe" (hifʿil of א-מ-נ; also functions as the standard belief verb)
- לאשר / *leʾasher* / "to confirm, to authorize, to cause something to be approved" (hifʿil/piʿel of י-ש-ר)

### Notes
The hifʿil binyan in Hebrew regularly expresses causative meaning, but for truth-domain roots the causative and non-causative senses have often lexicalized separately. *Lehaʾamin* means "to believe" rather than "to cause to believe"; the causative "to make someone believe" is expressed periphrastically (*lgaram le-mishehu lehaʾamin*). *Letsdiq* (hifʿil of צ-ד-ק, "to justify/vindicate") is a productive morphological causative in the legal domain.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Adjectival or verbal stem + suffix *-ut*, yielding an abstract noun denoting the quality or property. This is the primary productive pattern for truth-domain abstract nouns in Hebrew.

### Examples
- אמינות / *ʾaminut* / "credibility, trustworthiness" (from root א-מ-נ)
- מקוריות / *meqoriyut* / "originality, genuineness" (from *meqori*)

### Notes
The suffix *-ut* is fully productive across the truth-domain adjectives documented in P1–P3: *kanut* ("sincerity"), *meheymanut* ("reliability"), *ʾotentiyut* ("authenticity"), *yesharut* ("uprightness"), *nekhonot* ("correctness/readiness"), *shaqranut* ("mendacity"). Verbal nouns (piʿel pattern: *ziyyuf*, *diyuq*, *ḥiqquy*; hifʿil pattern: *honaʾaʾh*, *ʾishur*) constitute a parallel nominalization strategy for verbal roots.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Root + suffix *-an* (habitual/professional agent) or active participle (piʿel/hifʿil) for the agent of a specific act. Two distinct agentive patterns are attested: the *-an* suffix for habitual or characterological agents, and the active participle for agents defined by an ongoing or specific act.

### Examples
- שקרן / *shaqran* / "liar" (habitual agent; root ש-ק-ר + *-an*)
- מזייף / *mezayef* / "forger" (piʿel active participle; root ז-י-פ)

### Notes
The *-an* suffix marks a person characterized by habitual performance of the action: *shaqran* ("habitual liar"), *rammʾai* ("habitual deceiver/cheat"), *tsadqan* ("self-righteous person"). The active participle (*mezayef*, *meshaqer*, *merameh*) marks the agent of a specific or ongoing act and is used predicatively (*hu meshaqer*, "he is lying") as well as attributively. The two patterns are not interchangeable: *shaqran* implies character, *meshaqer* implies act.

---

## 8. TRUTH-PREDICATE FRAME

### Pattern
Fixed sentence frame *ha-ʾemet hi she-* ("the truth is that...") or *nakhon she-* ("it is correct that...") introducing a propositional complement clause. These frames function as epistemic stance markers signaling that the following clause represents the genuine state of affairs.

### Examples
- האמת היא שהמצב החמיר / *ha-ʾemet hi she-ha-matsav heḥmir* / "the truth is that the situation has worsened"
- נכון שאמרת שלא היית שם? / *nakhon she-ʾamarta she-lo hayita sham?* / "is it correct that you said you were not there?"

### Notes
These frames are syntactically distinct from simple predication: they introduce a full embedded clause (*she-* + clause) rather than a nominal or adjectival complement. *Ha-ʾemet hi she-* is used in declarative contexts to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement; *nakhon she-* is used in interrogative contexts, particularly in legal cross-examination, to confirm or challenge a prior assertion. Both are highly frequent across registers.

---

## 9. ADVERBIAL TRUTH-FRAMING

### Pattern
Prepositional phrase *be-* + truth-domain noun, functioning as a sentence adverbial modifying the illocutionary force or epistemic status of the utterance. These phrases are not argument-internal but frame the entire proposition.

### Examples
- בכנות, אני לא יודע / *be-kanut, ʾani lo yodeʿa* / "honestly, I don't know"
- בדיוק עשרה / *be-diyuq ʿasarah* / "exactly ten"

### Notes
The pattern *be-* + abstract noun is highly productive in this domain: *be-kanut* ("honestly"), *be-diyuq* ("exactly, precisely"), *be-ʾemet* ("truly, in truth"), *be-yosher* ("with integrity"), *be-mirmah* ("fraudulently, by deceit"). These phrases occupy sentence-initial or sentence-final adverbial position and modify the speaker's commitment to the truth or manner of the proposition. *Be-diyuq* has grammaticalized as a precision adverb and is among the most frequent items in this set.

---

## 10. VERBAL PERIPHRASIS WITH TRUTH-DOMAIN NOUN AS OBJECT

### Pattern
General-purpose verb (*ledaber*, *lomar*, *legallot*, *lehastir*, *leʿavvet*) + truth-domain noun as direct object, expressing an action performed on or with respect to truth. The truth-domain noun is typically definite (*ha-ʾemet*) when referring to a specific truth, and indefinite (*ʾemet*, *sheker*) when referring to the abstract property.

### Examples
- לדבר אמת / *ledaber ʾemet* / "to tell the truth" (lit. "to speak truth")
- לעוות את האמת / *leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet* / "to distort the truth"

### Notes
This is the primary verbal strategy for expressing truth-related actions in Hebrew, since the truth-domain roots themselves do not always yield productive verbal forms for all meanings. The range of verbs attested with *ha-ʾemet* as object — *legallot* ("to reveal"), *laḥsof* ("to expose"), *lehastir* ("to conceal"), *levarer* ("to ascertain"), *lesalef* ("to falsify"), *liḥyot ʿim* ("to live with") — reflects a systematic conceptual structure in which truth is treated as an object that can be revealed, concealed, distorted, or confronted.
