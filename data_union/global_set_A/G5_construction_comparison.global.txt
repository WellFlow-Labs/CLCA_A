# G5 — Cross-Language Construction Comparison

## 1. Main Recurring Constructional Functions

The F4 data across the eight languages reveals the following main constructional functions in the truth domain. These are identified inductively from the documented construction types; the labels are descriptive rather than theoretical.

**Function A: Attributing a truth-domain property to a subject (predicative attribution)**
Every language has a primary construction for asserting that something is true, false, genuine, accurate, valid, or honest. The subject receives a truth-domain predicate.

**Function B: Denying a truth-domain property (negative attribution)**
Every language has a construction for asserting that something is *not* true, not genuine, not valid, etc. This may be the same construction as Function A with a negation element, or it may use a distinct form.

**Function C: Modifying a noun with a truth-domain evaluative term (adnominal modification)**
Every language has a construction for placing a truth-domain evaluative term as a modifier of a noun (e.g., "accurate information," "forged document," "reliable source").

**Function D: Expressing that something transitions into a truth-domain state (inchoative/change-of-state)**
Every language has constructions for expressing that something comes to be true, turns out to be false, becomes valid, is revealed as genuine, etc. The subject undergoes a change.

**Function E: Expressing that an agent causes a truth-domain state (causative)**
Every language has constructions for expressing that an agent makes something true/false/valid/verified, forges a document, reveals the truth, conceals the truth, etc.

**Function F: Naming a truth-domain quality as an abstract entity (abstract nominalization)**
Every language has a strategy for producing a nominal that names a truth-domain property (truthfulness, accuracy, credibility, validity) as an abstract entity that can serve as subject, object, or genitive modifier.

**Function G: Naming the agent of a truth-domain action (agentive nominalization)**
Every language has constructions for naming persons who habitually lie, forge, or deceive. No language has a parallel lexicalized form for habitual truth-tellers.

**Function H: Framing a statement as frank, corrective, or truth-asserting (discourse framing)**
Every language has constructions that function as sentence-level markers signaling that what follows is the speaker's honest assessment, a correction of a prior assumption, or a frank disclosure.

**Function I: Evaluating whether a truth-domain quality is present, absent, or measurable (existential/scalar predication)**
Several languages have constructions that treat a truth-domain quality as something that can be possessed or lacked, or that can be measured on a scale, distinct from simple copular predication.

**Function J: Expressing that truth is revealed or concealed (truth-revelation/concealment)**
Every language has constructions in which truth is the grammatical subject of an emergence or concealment verb, treating truth as an entity that moves between hidden and visible states.

## 3. Narrative Analysis

### Recurring Cross-Language Constructional Patterns

**Predicative attribution (Function A) is the most universal construction type in the dataset.** All eight languages use a subject + copula/predicate construction to assert that something is true, false, genuine, accurate, valid, or honest. The main variation is in the form of the copula: Georgian uses an enclitic *-a* as the default (with the full copula *aris* required under negation), Yoruba uses a zero copula for stative predicates but requires *jẹ́* for nominal predicates, Finnish requires the partitive *totta* in the specific *olla totta* construction, Basque uses the 3sg copula *da* throughout, Tamil uses a zero copula with optional emphatic particles, Indonesian uses a zero copula with *adalah/merupakan* available in formal registers, Turkish uses a null copula as default with the *-dır* suffix restricted to formal/assertive contexts, and Korean uses *-ida* as the standard copular suffix. Despite this surface variation, the underlying constructional function is identical across all eight languages.

**The intransitive truth-revelation construction (Function J, intransitive) is documented in all eight languages and shows a consistent structural pattern.** In every language, truth can be the grammatical subject of an intransitive verb expressing emergence or disclosure. The spatial metaphor of truth "coming to light" or "coming to the surface" is consistent across all eight languages and all language families represented. This is one of the most robust cross-linguistic constructional patterns in the dataset. The specific verb varies (Georgian *gamovida*, Yoruba *farahàn*, Finnish *tulee ilmi*, Basque *ateratzen da azalera*, Tamil *veḷippaṭṭatu*, Indonesian *terungkap*, Turkish *ortaya çıktı*, Korean *드러나다/밝혀지다*), but the constructional pattern — truth as subject, intransitive emergence verb as predicate — is consistent.

**The agentive asymmetry (Function G) is consistent across all eight languages.** Every language has lexicalized single-word agentives for habitual liars and forgers, but none has a parallel lexicalized agentive for habitual truth-tellers. This is explicitly documented in the F4 construction tables for all eight languages and is not a gap in documentation. The concept of "truth-teller" is expressed periphrastically in all eight languages. This asymmetry is consistent across unrelated language families (South Caucasian, Niger-Congo, Finno-Ugric, isolate, Dravidian, Austronesian, Turkic, Koreanic) and appears to reflect a cross-linguistic pattern in how the truth domain is lexicalized.

**The discourse-framing construction (Function H) is present in all eight languages but shows the widest variation in degree of grammaticalization.** At one extreme, Indonesian *sebenarnya* and Turkish *aslında* are fully grammaticalized discourse markers whose compositional meanings are no longer active. At the other extreme, Yoruba uses a hortative construction (*jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́*, let us speak the truth) for the same discourse function rather than a grammaticalized adverbial. Between these extremes, Georgian *simartle rom vtq̇vat* is a subjunctive construction that retains compositional transparency, Finnish *totta puhuen* is a participial phrase, Basque *egia esan behar* is a purposive construction, Tamil *uṇmaiyil* is a locative case form, and Korean *사실을 말하자면* is a conditional verbal phrase. The functional outcome is the same across all eight languages — signaling that what follows is the speaker's honest assessment or a correction of a prior assumption — but the constructional form varies from fully grammaticalized to fully compositional.

**The abstract nominalization strategy (Function F) is universal and productive in all eight languages.** Every language produces abstract nouns naming truth-domain qualities through a suffix or circumfix on an adjectival or verbal base. The specific morphological mechanism varies — Turkish *-luk*, Finnish *-uus*, Basque *-tasun*, Tamil *-mai*, Indonesian *ke-...-an* circumfix, Korean *-seong*, Georgian *-eba/-oba*, Yoruba *ì-* prefix — but the functional outcome is the same. In all languages, the resulting nominal names the property as an abstract entity and participates in evaluative constructions. This pattern is productive in all eight languages and shows no signs of fossilization.

**The proverbial assertion that truth ultimately prevails (Function J, proverbial) is documented in all eight languages.** Georgian *simartle gamarjvebs* (truth prevails), Yoruba *òtítọ́ kò ní kú* (truth will not die), Finnish *totuus tulee ilmi* (used in this proverbial sense), Basque *egia beti ateratzen da azalera* (the truth always comes to the surface), Tamil *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail), Indonesian *kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya* (the truth will come out in the end), Turkish *gerçek er ya da geç ortaya çıkar* (the truth comes out sooner or later), Korean *진실은 하나다* (there is only one truth). The specific framing varies, but the proverbial register is present in all eight languages.

### Important Asymmetries, Substitutions, and Restrictions

**The most important asymmetry is the one-way blocked pathway for truth-teller agentives.** As documented above, all eight languages have lexicalized agentives for liars and forgers but none for truth-tellers. This is not a gap in documentation; it is explicitly noted in the F4 construction tables for all eight languages. The asymmetry is consistent across unrelated language families.

**The *있다/없다* existential frame in Korean substitutes for the copular predication strategy used in other languages for abstract quality nominals.** In Korean, *-seong* quality nominals (*진정성*, *신빙성*, *타당성*) cannot take *-hada* as a predicative suffix; they require the existential *있다/없다* frame instead. This is a morphological restriction documented in the F4 construction tables for Korean and has no parallel in other languages. In all other languages, abstract quality nominals participate in the standard copular predication construction (Function A). The Korean restriction creates a constructional substitution: where other languages say "it is credible" (copular predication), Korean says "credibility exists" (existential predication).

**The *osoittautua* + translative construction in Finnish substitutes for the periphrastic *menjadi/di* constructions used in other languages for inchoative truth-evaluation.** Finnish uses the verb *osoittautua* (to turn out to be) with a translative case complement (*väite osoittautui epätodeksi*, the claim turned out to be false) as the primary inchoative construction for truth-domain adjectives. The translative case is obligatory and encodes the resultant state. Other languages use periphrastic constructions with general-purpose change-of-state verbs (Indonesian *menjadi*, Basque *bihurtu*, Turkish *çıkmak*, Korean *밝혀지다/드러나다*) without a dedicated case form for the resultant state.

**The *fi* instrumental serial verb construction in Yoruba substitutes for the morphological causative constructions used in other languages.** Yoruba uses the serial verb *fi* (use/take) as the primary causative strategy: *ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* (he presented evidence before the court), *wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀* (they used deception to take his money). This construction encodes the instrument or means by which an outcome is brought about. Other languages use morphological causatives (Finnish *väärentää*, Turkish *doğrulamak*, Indonesian *memalsukan*) or transitive verbs (Georgian *gaq̇alba*, Korean *위조하다*) for the same function.

**The *değil* negation strategy in Turkish is more restricted than the negation strategies in other languages.** In Turkish, *değil* is the sole negation strategy for adjectival predicates; no alternate negation construction is attested in this domain. In other languages, multiple negation strategies are available: Georgian has both *ar aris* (syntactic) and *u-...-o* privative circumfix (morphological); Finnish has both *ei ole* (syntactic) and *epä-* prefix (morphological); Indonesian has both *tidak* (syntactic, for adjectival predicates) and *bukan* (syntactic, for nominal predicates); Korean has both *아니다* (for nominal predicates) and *-지 않다* (for adjectival predicates).

**The *bukan/tidak* distinction in Indonesian and the *아니다/-지 않다* distinction in Korean represent a cross-linguistically unusual split in negation strategy based on predicate type.** In both languages, nominal predicates and adjectival predicates require different negation forms: Indonesian *bukan* (nominal) vs. *tidak* (adjectival), Korean *아니다* (nominal) vs. *-지 않다* (adjectival). This split is not documented in the other six languages, where a single negation strategy applies across both predicate types.

**The post-nominal modifier position in Yoruba is the reverse of all other languages in the dataset.** In Yoruba, truth-domain evaluative modifiers follow the head noun (*ènìyàn òtítọ́*, a truthful person; *ẹ̀rí ojú*, eyewitness evidence), while in all other seven languages the modifier precedes the head noun. This is not a minor variation but a systematic structural difference in the direction of modification.

**The three-way inchoative verb distinction in Korean (*드러나다/밝혀지다/판명되다*) substitutes for the single inchoative verb used in most other languages.** Korean documents three distinct inchoative verbs for truth-revelation with slightly different nuances: *드러나다* (exposed/surfaced, stronger sense of unwanted revelation), *밝혀지다* (revealed through investigation, more neutral), *판명되다* (officially determined, most formal). Other languages typically use a single inchoative verb for this function. The Korean three-way distinction encodes nuances of agency, formality, and the nature of the revelation process that other languages express through context or additional modifiers.

### Cases Where a Function Is Weakly Expressed, Constructionally Indirect, or Absent

**The discourse-framing function (Function H) is most weakly expressed in Yoruba.** While all other languages have a grammaticalized or semi-grammaticalized adverbial form for introducing frank disclosures or corrections, Yoruba uses a hortative construction (*jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́*, let us speak the truth) for this function. This construction is more compositionally transparent and less grammaticalized than the equivalents in other languages. The F4 data for Yoruba does not document a grammaticalized discourse marker parallel to Indonesian *sebenarnya*, Turkish *aslında*, or Tamil *uṇmaiyil*.

**The existential/scalar predication function (Function I) is most weakly expressed in Georgian, Finnish, and Turkish.** In these languages, abstract quality nominals participate in the standard copular predication construction (Function A) rather than in a dedicated existential or scalar frame. The *있다/없다* existential frame documented in Korean, and the *badago/ez dago* frame documented in Basque, have no parallel in Georgian, Finnish, or Turkish. In these languages, the evaluative question "does this source have credibility?" is expressed through the same copular construction as "is this statement true?" — there is no constructional differentiation between the two evaluative targets.

**The morphological causative-inchoative chain (Function E, morphological chain) is absent or marginal in Yoruba, Tamil, and Korean.** While Turkish shows a productive *-laş-/-leş-tir-* chain and Basque shows a *-tu* causative on adjectival bases, Yoruba, Tamil, and Korean do not document this chain. In these languages, causative meaning is expressed through light-verb constructions (Yoruba *fi...hàn*, Tamil *col/pēcu*, Korean *하다*) rather than through morphological causative derivation.

**The morphological inchoative (Function D, morphological) is restricted or absent in most languages.** Only Georgian (*-deba* suffix, restricted to the *mart-* root), Turkish (*-laş/-leş* suffix, restricted to some bases), and Basque (*-tu* intransitive, lexically restricted) show morphological inchoatives in the truth domain. In Finnish, the morphological inchoative (*aitoutua*, to become authentic) is explicitly marked as marginal. In Yoruba, Tamil, Indonesian, and Korean, the periphrastic inchoative (Function D, periphrastic) is the only documented strategy. The morphological inchoative is thus a minority strategy in the dataset, with the periphrastic inchoative being the universal default.

**The parallel morphological opposition for verification/falsification (documented in Turkish as *doğrulamak/yanlışlamak*) is absent in most languages.** While Turkish shows a productive morphological pair encoding the verification/falsification distinction with parallel derivational stacking (*doğrulanabilirlik/yanlışlanabilirlik*), no other language in the dataset shows this as a productive morphological pair. Finnish has *todistaa* (to prove) and *osoittaa epätodeksi* (to show to be false), but these are not morphologically parallel. Basque has *frogatu* (to prove) and *gezurtatu* (to give the lie to), but these are not a parallel pair encoding the same philosophical distinction. This is a single-language observation for Turkish.

**The three-way verifiability distinction (*egiaztagarria/egiaztagaitza/egiaztaezina*) documented in Basque is absent in all other languages.** No other language in the dataset documents a three-way morphological distinction within the verifiability sub-domain. Other languages show only a binary verifiable/unverifiable opposition. The Basque three-way distinction — capable of being verified / difficult to verify / impossible to verify — is a single-language observation.

**The *-hada/-doeda* stative/agentive distinction documented in Korean is absent in all other languages.** Korean documents a systematic semantic distinction between *jinsil-hada* (to be truthful, active/performed quality) and *jinsil-doeda* (to be truthful, inherent/resultant quality). This morphological distinction between performed and inherent quality is not documented in any other language in the dataset. In all other languages, a single predicative form covers both the active/performed and the inherent/resultant readings of truth-domain predicates.

**The concessive construction (Function H, concessive) is not documented in Yoruba in the F4 data.** While all other seven languages show a documented construction for acknowledging a truth claim while introducing a qualification, the F4 data for Yoruba does not include this pattern. This may reflect a gap in documentation rather than a genuine absence, but it cannot be confirmed from the available data.
