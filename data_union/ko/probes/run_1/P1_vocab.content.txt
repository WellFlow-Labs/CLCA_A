# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 Core Vocabulary Collection
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Literal meaning:** 真實 — "true" + "real/solid"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, sincerity

Used to describe statements, accounts, or persons that accord with what is actually the case. Appears in legal, journalistic, and everyday moral contexts. Participates in derivation: 진실하다 (to be truthful/sincere), 진실성 (truthfulness, authenticity).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** 사실
**Transliteration:** sasil
**Literal meaning:** 事實 — "matter/affair" + "real/solid"
**Typical English translations:** fact, actuality, truth (in the sense of what actually happened)

Used to assert that something corresponds to actual events or states of affairs, as opposed to opinion or fabrication. Frequently used as a discourse marker meaning "in fact" or "actually." Participates in 사실이다 (to be a fact), 사실상 (in effect, de facto).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Literal meaning:** Colloquial; 진 (眞, true) + 짜 (suffix of uncertain origin, possibly from 것)
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic; (adverbially) really, truly

Used to distinguish genuine items, persons, or states from imitations, pretenses, or exaggerations. Contrasts with 가짜 (fake, counterfeit). Common in everyday speech across a wide range of contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** 가짜
**Transliteration:** gajja
**Literal meaning:** 가 (假, false/temporary) + 짜 (parallel to 진짜)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, phony

Used to characterize objects, documents, persons, or claims that are not what they purport to be. Directly contrasts with 진짜. Appears in legal and commercial contexts involving fraud or misrepresentation.

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** 정확
**Transliteration:** jeonghwak
**Literal meaning:** 正確 — "correct/upright" + "certain/firm"
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness

Used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, translation, or representation corresponds precisely to what it describes. Participates in 정확하다 (to be accurate), 정확성 (accuracy), 정확히 (accurately, exactly).

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** 올바르다
**Transliteration:** olbareuda
**Literal meaning:** 올 (right, proper) + 바르다 (to be straight, upright)
**Typical English translations:** to be correct, right, proper, upright

Applied to conduct, reasoning, values, and statements judged to conform to a standard of correctness or moral rightness. Used in ethical and educational contexts. Nominalized as 올바름 (rightness, correctness).

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** 맞다
**Transliteration:** matda
**Literal meaning:** to fit, to match, to be correct
**Typical English translations:** to be right, correct, to match, to fit

Extremely common verb used to affirm that a statement, answer, or identification is correct or that two things correspond. Used across factual, moral, and practical evaluative contexts. Contrasts with 틀리다 (to be wrong, mistaken).

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** 틀리다
**Transliteration:** teullida
**Literal meaning:** to be off, to deviate (from a standard or correct form)
**Typical English translations:** to be wrong, incorrect, mistaken

Used to evaluate statements, answers, or beliefs as failing to correspond to what is correct or true. Distinct from 다르다 (to be different), though the two are sometimes conflated in colloquial speech. Participates in 틀림없다 (to be without error, certain).

**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** 틀림없다
**Transliteration:** teullim-eopda
**Literal meaning:** 틀림 (error, deviation) + 없다 (to not exist/have)
**Typical English translations:** to be certain, undoubtedly correct, without error

Fixed expression used to assert strong confidence in the accuracy or truth of a claim. Functions epistemically to mark a statement as beyond reasonable doubt. Common in both spoken and written registers.

**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** 정직
**Transliteration:** jeongjik
**Literal meaning:** 正直 — "correct/upright" + "straight"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, straightforwardness

Used to evaluate persons or conduct with respect to truthfulness and the absence of deception. Appears prominently in moral, educational, and legal discourse. Participates in 정직하다 (to be honest), 정직성 (honesty as a quality).

**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** 거짓
**Transliteration:** geojit
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, lie (native Korean word; etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth, falsity

Used to characterize statements or representations that are not true. Appears in compounds such as 거짓말 (lie, literally "false word/speech") and 거짓말하다 (to lie). Contrasts with 진실 and 사실.

**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** 거짓말
**Transliteration:** geojitmal
**Literal meaning:** 거짓 (falsehood) + 말 (word, speech)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood

The standard lexical item for a deliberate false statement. Participates in 거짓말하다 (to tell a lie), 거짓말쟁이 (liar). Extremely common across all registers and contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** 신뢰
**Transliteration:** sinloe
**Literal meaning:** 信賴 — "trust/faith" + "rely on"
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, credibility

Used to evaluate persons, institutions, or sources with respect to whether they can be depended upon to be accurate or honest. Participates in 신뢰하다 (to trust), 신뢰성 (reliability, trustworthiness), 신뢰도 (degree of reliability).

**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** 신빙성
**Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong
**Literal meaning:** 信憑性 — "trust" + "evidence/basis" + "-ness"
**Typical English translations:** credibility, plausibility, reliability (of a claim or source)

Used specifically to evaluate whether a claim, testimony, or source has sufficient basis to be believed. Common in legal and journalistic contexts. More formal than 신뢰성.

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Literal meaning:** 眞僞 — "true" + "false"
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, truth or falsity, genuineness

Used to frame the question of whether something is true or false, genuine or counterfeit. Appears in formal and legal contexts: 진위를 가리다 (to determine the truth or falsity of something). Primarily nominal in use.

**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** 진본
**Transliteration:** jinbon
**Literal meaning:** 眞本 — "true/genuine" + "original/copy"
**Typical English translations:** authentic original, genuine copy, the real thing (of a document or artwork)

Used in archival, legal, and art-historical contexts to designate an item as the genuine original rather than a reproduction or forgery. Contrasts with 위본 (forged copy) and 복사본 (copy).

**[MARGINAL]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** 위조
**Transliteration:** wijo
**Literal meaning:** 僞造 — "false/fake" + "to make/manufacture"
**Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeiting, fabrication

Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe the act of producing false documents, currency, or signatures. Participates in 위조하다 (to forge, to counterfeit), 위조품 (forged item), 위조문서 (forged document).

**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** 허위
**Transliteration:** heowi
**Literal meaning:** 虛僞 — "empty/hollow" + "false"
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, misrepresentation

Used in formal, legal, and administrative registers to characterize statements or representations that are false. More formal than 거짓. Appears in 허위진술 (false statement), 허위신고 (false report), 허위광고 (false advertising).

**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong
**Literal meaning:** 眞正性 — "true" + "correct/proper" + "-ness"
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity

Used to evaluate whether a person's actions, expressions, or works reflect their actual inner state or values, rather than performance or pretense. Prominent in contemporary discourse on art, politics, and personal relationships. Participates in 진정성 있다/없다 (to have/lack authenticity).

**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** 본질
**Transliteration:** bonjil
**Literal meaning:** 本質 — "root/origin" + "substance/nature"
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, what something fundamentally is

Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday contexts to refer to the fundamental nature or defining character of something. Participates in 본질적 (essential, fundamental), 본질적으로 (essentially, fundamentally).

**[CORE]**

---

**21.**
**Form:** 실제
**Transliteration:** silje
**Literal meaning:** 實際 — "real/actual" + "boundary/occasion"
**Typical English translations:** reality, actuality; (adjectival/adverbial) actual, real, in practice

Used to contrast what is actually the case with what is apparent, claimed, or theoretical. Participates in 실제로 (actually, in reality), 실제적 (practical, actual). Common across registers.

**[CORE]**

---

**22.**
**Form:** 타당
**Transliteration:** tadang
**Literal meaning:** 妥當 — "fitting/appropriate" + "proper"
**Typical English translations:** validity, appropriateness, reasonableness, soundness

Used to evaluate arguments, decisions, or claims as well-founded or appropriate given relevant standards or evidence. Participates in 타당하다 (to be valid/sound), 타당성 (validity, soundness), 타당한 이유 (valid reason).

**[CORE]**

---

**23.**
**Form:** 근거
**Transliteration:** geungeo
**Literal meaning:** 根據 — "root" + "basis/foundation"
**Typical English translations:** basis, grounds, evidence, foundation

Used to refer to the evidential or rational basis for a claim or judgment. Appears in 근거가 있다/없다 (to be grounded/ungrounded), 근거 없는 주장 (unsubstantiated claim). Central to evaluative discourse about the reliability of claims.

**[CORE]**

---

**24.**
**Form:** 공정
**Transliteration:** gongjeon
**Literal meaning:** 公正 — "public/impartial" + "correct/just"
**Typical English translations:** fairness, impartiality, justice

Used to evaluate procedures, judgments, or persons with respect to whether they are free from bias and conform to standards of equal treatment. Participates in 공정하다 (to be fair), 공정성 (fairness), 공정한 평가 (fair evaluation).

**[CORE]**

---

**25.**
**Form:** 객관적
**Transliteration:** gaekgwanjeok
**Literal meaning:** 客觀的 — "guest/external" + "observe" + adjectival suffix
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial

Used to characterize evaluations, descriptions, or methods as based on external, verifiable criteria rather than personal perspective. Contrasts with 주관적 (subjective). Common in academic, journalistic, and everyday evaluative discourse.

**[CORE]**

---

**26.**
**Form:** 주관적
**Transliteration:** jugwanjeok
**Literal meaning:** 主觀的 — "subject/main" + "observe" + adjectival suffix
**Typical English translations:** subjective, personal

Used to characterize evaluations or perceptions as dependent on individual perspective rather than external standards. Contrasts with 객관적. Appears in discussions of aesthetic judgment, testimony, and the reliability of personal accounts.

**[CORE]**

---

**27.**
**Form:** 모순
**Transliteration:** mosun
**Literal meaning:** 矛盾 — "spear" + "shield" (classical Chinese allusion to logical contradiction)
**Typical English translations:** contradiction, inconsistency

Used to identify logical or factual inconsistency within a set of claims or between a claim and evidence. Participates in 모순되다 (to be contradictory), 모순이 있다 (to contain a contradiction). Common in argumentation and legal contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**28.**
**Form:** 유사
**Transliteration:** yusa
**Literal meaning:** 類似 — "category/kind" + "resemble"
**Typical English translations:** similarity, resemblance, analogy

Used to evaluate the degree to which two things resemble each other, relevant in contexts of identification, classification, and legal comparison (e.g., trademark similarity). Participates in 유사하다 (to be similar), 유사성 (similarity, resemblance).

**[CORE]**

---

**29.**
**Form:** 위증
**Transliteration:** wijeung
**Literal meaning:** 僞證 — "false" + "testimony/evidence"
**Typical English translations:** perjury, false testimony

Legal term for the act of giving false testimony under oath. Participates in 위증하다 (to commit perjury), 위증죄 (the crime of perjury). Domain-specific but clearly attested and productive.

**[MARGINAL]**

---

**30.**
**Form:** 참
**Transliteration:** cham
**Literal meaning:** Native Korean word; truth, genuineness, the real
**Typical English translations:** truth, the genuine/real; (adverbially) truly, really

Used as a noun meaning truth or reality (contrasting with 거짓), and as an adverb meaning truly or really. Appears in philosophical and literary registers as well as everyday speech. Participates in 참되다 (to be true, genuine), 참값 (true value, in mathematics and logic).

**[CORE]**
