# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Preliminary note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of usage, collocation, register distribution, and contrast documented in P1 and P1b–P2b. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data supports. Several items appear in more than one group; this is noted explicitly. Items that do not fit cleanly into any group are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or confirm that a statement is correct or accurate

**Label:** Asserting correctness of statements

**Definition:** These expressions are used in everyday and formal speech to affirm that a proposition, claim, or statement corresponds to the facts. They appear in contexts of confirmation, concession, and direct assertion of factual accuracy. They are frequently used predicatively (with the copula) or as discourse particles.

**Representative expressions:**

- სიმართლე / simartle — used in direct assertions ("simartle gitxra," "simartles vambob") and in predicative constructions affirming factual accuracy
- მართალი / martali — used predicatively ("martali xar," "martalia") to confirm that a person's statement or position is correct; also functions as a discourse particle in concessive constructions ("martalia, magram...")
- მართლა / martla — adverb used in everyday speech to affirm truth or to seek confirmation ("really?", "is that so?")
- მართლაც / martlac — emphatic adverb used to reinforce a statement as genuinely true
- ჭეშმარიტია, რომ... / č̣ešmaritia, rom... — formal construction used to introduce a proposition asserted as true
- ზუსტი / zusti — used to assert that a statement, measurement, or description is precise and accurate
- ზუსტად / zustad — adverb used in confirmation ("exactly!") and in technical specification
- უტყუარი / uṭq̇uari — used in formal and legal registers to assert that something is beyond doubt; stronger than the other items in this group

**Notes:** martali and simartle overlap heavily in this function. uṭq̇uari is distinguished by its stronger epistemic force — it implies not merely that something is correct but that it cannot be otherwise. zusti and sizuste are distinguished by their emphasis on precision and correspondence rather than on truth in a broader sense.

## Group 2: Expressions used in the detection or exposure of something false

**Label:** Detecting and exposing falsity

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where something previously accepted or presented as genuine is found to be false, forged, or deceptive. They cluster around constructions involving discovery verbs (gamodga, gamovlinda, q̇opila) and are common in legal, journalistic, and everyday registers.

**Representative expressions:**

- ყალბი გამოდგა / q̇albi gamodga — "it turned out to be fake"; the most common construction for the discovery of inauthenticity
- ყალბი ყოფილა / q̇albi q̇opila — evidential construction expressing belated discovery that something was false, often with surprise
- სიყალბე გამოვლინდა / siq̇albe gamovlinda — "the falsity was revealed"; used in legal and journalistic contexts
- სიცრუე გამოვლინდა / sicrue gamovlinda — "the lie was exposed"; parallel construction for the exposure of a lie
- მცდარი გამოდგა / mcdari gamodga — "it turned out to be wrong/erroneous"; used when a belief or judgment is found to be incorrect
- ნამდვილი გამოდგა / namdvili gamodga — the positive counterpart: "it turned out to be genuine"; included here because it participates in the same detection pattern
- პირმოთნე ყოფილა / pirmoṭne q̇opila — "he/she turned out to be a hypocrite"; evidential construction for the discovery of two-facedness
- თვალთმაქცი ყოფილა / tvaltmaq̇ci q̇opila — "he/she turned out to be a fraud"; same evidential pattern

**Notes:** The evidential copula q̇opila is a structural feature shared by several of these constructions and marks them as a usage cluster. namdvili gamodga belongs here as the positive pole of the same detection pattern, though it also belongs to Group 5.

## Group 3: Expressions used in legal and administrative contexts for document and identity verification

**Label:** Legal and administrative verification language

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal texts, administrative procedures, criminal law, and border control contexts. They are used to describe the formal processes of verifying, certifying, or challenging the authenticity of documents, identities, and evidence. Many are periphrastic constructions rather than single lexical items.

**Representative expressions:**

- ყალბი დოკუმენტი / q̇albi dokumenti — forged document; standard legal collocation
- ყალბი ხელმოწერა / q̇albi xelmoq̇era — forged signature
- ყალბი პასპორტი / q̇albi pasporṭi — forged passport
- ყალბი ვინაობა / q̇albi vinaoba — false identity
- გაყალბება დოკუმენტების / gaq̇albeba dokumentebis — falsification of documents; standard criminal law collocation
- ნამდვილობის დადასტურება / namdvilobis dadastureba — confirmation of authenticity; standard administrative construction
- ნამდვილობის შემოწმება / namdvilobis šemoc̣meba — authenticity check
- ავთენტური ასლი / avṭenturi asli — authenticated copy; notarial and administrative term
- ავთენტურობის დადასტურება / avṭenturobis dadastureba — certification of authenticity; more technical than namdviloba variant
- ნამდვილი ვინაობის დადგენა / namdvili vinaobis dadgena — establishing true identity
- ყალბი ვინაობით მოქმედება / q̇albi vinaobiт moq̇medeba — acting under a false identity; legal offense description
- ყალბი ჩვენება / q̇albi čveneba — false testimony; legal term
- ყალბი ბრალდება / q̇albi braldeba — false accusation
- სასამართლო / sasamartlo — court of law; derived from the marṭ- root, institutionally embedded
- მართლმსაჯულება / martlmsajuleba — justice, administration of justice; institutional term
- გამართლება / gamartleba — acquittal, vindication; legal term
- მართლსაწინააღმდეგო ქმედება / martlsac̣inaagmdego q̇medeba — unlawful act; legal term
- მართლზომიერება / martlzomiereba — lawfulness, proportionality; constitutional and administrative law term
- უტყუარი მტკიცებულება / uṭq̇uari mṭkicebuleba — irrefutable evidence; standard legal collocation

**Notes:** This group is defined by register and institutional context rather than by a single semantic feature. The items within it span several different roots (q̇alb-, namdvil-, avṭentur-, marṭ-, uṭq̇uar-) but converge in legal and administrative usage. avṭenturi and avṭenturoba appear here more than in other groups, reflecting their more technical register. uṭq̇uari also belongs to Group 1 but is particularly prominent in this legal context.

## Group 4: Expressions used to characterize persons as deceptive or insincere

**Label:** Characterizing persons as deceptive or two-faced

**Definition:** These expressions are applied to persons rather than to statements, documents, or objects. They evaluate a person's character or conduct as involving deception, pretense, or inconsistency between outward presentation and inner reality. They appear in moral condemnation and in character description across everyday, literary, and legal registers.

**Representative expressions:**

- პირმოთნე / pirmoṭne — hypocrite, dissembler; applied to a person who presents a false face
- პირმოთნეობა / pirmoṭneoba — hypocrisy, two-facedness; the abstract quality
- პირმოთნეობს / pirmoṭneobs — to behave hypocritically; the verbal form
- თვალთმაქცი / tvaltmaq̇ci — deceiver, fraud, impostor; one who creates false appearances
- თვალთმაქცობა / tvaltmaq̇coba — deception, imposture; the practice
- ყალბი მეგობარი / q̇albi megobari — false friend; applied to a person whose friendship is not genuine
- ყალბი ექიმი / q̇albi eq̇imi — fake doctor, quack; a person who falsely claims professional status
- ცრუ მოწმე / cru moq̇me — false witness, perjurer; a person who gives false testimony
- ცრუ წინასწარმეტყველი / cru c̣inasq̇armetq̇veli — false prophet; a person who falsely claims prophetic authority

**Notes:** pirmoṭne and tvaltmaq̇ci are the most dedicated items in this group — they are used almost exclusively to characterize persons. The q̇albi collocations (q̇albi megobari, q̇albi eq̇imi) extend the root's primary domain (documents, objects) to persons. cru moq̇me and cru c̣inasq̇armetq̇veli are more fixed, appearing primarily in legal and theological contexts respectively.

## Group 5: Expressions used to affirm that something is genuine rather than imitated or substituted

**Label:** Affirming genuineness of objects, persons, and states

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something is what it claims or appears to be — that it has not been substituted, imitated, or falsified. They appear across commercial, legal, aesthetic, and interpersonal contexts. The contrast with fake or counterfeit alternatives is often implicit or explicit.

**Representative expressions:**

- ნამდვილი / namdvili — genuine, real, authentic; the primary adjective for this function across all registers
- ნამდვილად / namdvilad — genuinely, really; adverb affirming that something is the case in a genuine way
- ნამდვილობა / namdviloba — genuineness, authenticity; the abstract property
- ნამდვილი ოქრო / namdvili oq̇ro — real gold; contrasts directly with q̇albi oq̇ro
- ნამდვილი დოკუმენტი / namdvili dokumenti — genuine document; contrasts with q̇albi dokumenti
- ნამდვილი ნაწარმოები / namdvili nac̣armobi — genuine work of art; used in authentication contexts
- ავთენტური / avṭenturi — authentic; used in more technical and formal registers for the same function
- ავთენტურობა / avṭenturoba — authenticity; more technical than namdviloba
- ნამდვილი გამოდგა / namdvili gamodga — it turned out to be genuine; detection construction (see also Group 2)
- ნამდვილი ყოფილა / namdvili q̇opila — it was genuine all along; evidential construction

**Notes:** namdvili is the dominant item in this group and is far more productive than avṭenturi. avṭenturi overlaps with this group but is more restricted to archival, legal, and academic registers (see also Group 3). The group overlaps with Group 2 through the detection constructions.

## Group 6: Expressions used in moral discourse about honesty and lying in interpersonal contexts

**Label:** Moral evaluation of honesty and lying in everyday life

**Definition:** These expressions appear in moral condemnation or praise of honest and dishonest conduct in interpersonal contexts. They are used in proverbs, in everyday moral instruction, and in the evaluation of personal character. They are not primarily legal or institutional in register.

**Representative expressions:**

- სიცრუე / sicrue — lie, falsehood; the standard noun for a lie in moral discourse
- ცრუობა / cruoba — the practice of lying; habitual dishonesty
- ცრუობს / cruobs — he/she lies; the verb
- სიცრუე ფეხებზე დგას / sicrue pexebze dgas — "a lie has short legs"; proverbial
- სიცრუე სიცრუეს ბადებს / sicrue sicrues badebs — "one lie breeds another"; proverbial
- სიცრუეში გაიჭირა / sicrueshi gaič̣ira — "he/she was caught in a lie"; everyday expression
- სიმართლე მწარეა / simartle mc̣area — "the truth is bitter"; proverbial acknowledgment
- სიმართლე ძვირი ღირს / simartle ʒviri ɣirs — "truth comes at a high price"; proverbial
- სიმართლე გამარჯვებს / simartle gamarjvebs — "truth prevails"; moral assertion
- სიმართლე ნახევარი სიცრუეა / simartle naxevari sicruea — "a half-truth is a lie"; proverbial
- გულწრფელი / gulc̣rpeli — sincere, candid; positive moral evaluation of a person's honesty
- გულწრფელობა / gulc̣rpeloba — sincerity; the abstract quality
- გულწრფელად გეუბნები / gulc̣rpelaد geubnebi — "I am telling you sincerely"; formula for candor
- ჭეშმარიტება მწარეა / č̣ešmariteba mc̣area — "truth is bitter"; more formal variant of the same proverb
- ჭეშმარიტება გამარჯვებს / č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs — "truth prevails"; more formal variant

**Notes:** This group is defined by its proverbial and moral-interpersonal register. simartle and č̣ešmariteba overlap here, with simartle being more common in everyday speech and č̣ešmariteba more common in formal and literary registers. gulc̣rpeli belongs here as the positive counterpart to sicrue and pirmoṭne. pirmoṭne also belongs to Group 4 but its moral condemnation function connects it to this group as well.

## Group 7: Expressions used in formal and institutional assertion of truth, particularly in legal and religious contexts

**Label:** Formal and institutional assertion of truth

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth is asserted with institutional weight — in legal oaths, religious formulas, and formal declarations. They tend to be more elevated in register than the everyday truth-assertion expressions in Group 1, and they often carry a performative or declaratory function.

**Representative expressions:**

- ჭეშმარიტება და მხოლოდ ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba da mxolod č̣ešmariteba — "the truth and nothing but the truth"; legal oath formula
- მთელი ჭეშმარიტება / mteli č̣ešmariteba — "the whole truth"; used in oath contexts
- ჭეშმარიტება გითხრა / č̣ešmariteba gitxra — "I will tell you the truth"; formal formula for candid disclosure
- ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი და ჭეშმარიტი კაცი / č̣ešmariti ɣmerti da č̣ešmariti kaci — "true God and true man"; Christological formula
- ჭეშმარიტი სარწმუნოება / č̣ešmariti sarc̣munoeba — "the true faith"; theological characterization of Orthodox Christianity
- მართლმადიდებელი / martlmadidebeli — Eastern Orthodox; the institutional name encoding "correct glorification"
- მართლი სარწმუნოება / martli sarc̣munoeba — "the true faith"; parallel theological formula
- მართლი სჯული / martli sjuli — "the true law"; ecclesiastical and historical legal usage
- სიმართლის თქმა / simartlis tq̇ma — "telling the truth"; used in oath-taking and witness testimony
- სიმართლის დადგენა / simartlis dadgena — "establishing the truth"; used in legal and investigative contexts
- უტყუარი / uṭq̇uari — beyond doubt, irrefutable; used in formal legal and theological registers

**Notes:** č̣ešmariteba and č̣ešmariti are the dominant items in this group, reflecting their higher register compared to simartle and martali. The theological collocations (č̣ešmariti ɣmerti, martlmadidebeli) represent a specific sub-cluster within this group. uṭq̇uari also belongs to Groups 1 and 3.

## Group 8: Expressions used to distinguish actual from apparent or surface qualities

**Label:** Contrasting actual and apparent qualities

**Definition:** These expressions are used to draw a distinction between what something appears to be and what it actually is. They appear in philosophical, analytical, political, and literary contexts. The contrast between appearance and reality is the defining usage pattern.

**Representative expressions:**

- მოჩვენებითი / močvenebiti — apparent, seeming, ostensible; the primary adjective for this function
- მოჩვენებითად / močvenebitad — apparently, ostensibly; the adverbial form
- მოჩვენებითი და ნამდვილი / močvenebiti da namdvili — "apparent and real"; the standard contrastive pairing
- მოჩვენებითი შეთანხმება / močvenebiti šetanxmeba — apparent agreement; political and diplomatic usage
- მოჩვენებითი წინააღმდეგობა / močvenebiti c̣inaagmdegoba — apparent contradiction; philosophical and legal usage
- ნამდვილი სახე / namdvili saxe — true face, real nature; used to describe actual character as opposed to presented appearance
- ნამდვილი ბუნება / namdvili buneba — true nature, real character
- ნამდვილი მიზეზი / namdvili mizezi — the real reason, the actual motive; contrasted with stated reasons
- ნამდვილი ვითარება / namdvili vitareba — the real situation, the actual state of affairs
- ნამდვილი სახის გამოვლენა / namdvili saxis gamovlena — revealing one's true face; unmasking

**Notes:** močvenebiti is the most dedicated item in this group — it is used almost exclusively in the appearance/reality contrast. namdvili participates in this group through its collocations with nouns denoting inner nature, motive, and situation, but it also belongs to Group 5. The appearance/reality contrast is less prominent for č̣ešmariti and simartle, which are more focused on propositional truth.

## Group 9: Expressions used to evaluate the reliability of sources and information

**Label:** Evaluating reliability of sources and information

**Definition:** These expressions are used in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts to assess whether a person, institution, or piece of information can be trusted or relied upon. They focus on the relationship between a source and its audience rather than on the truth of a specific proposition.

**Representative expressions:**

- სანდო / sando — reliable, trustworthy; the primary adjective for source reliability
- სანდოობა / sandooba — reliability, trustworthiness; the abstract property
- არასანდო / arasando — unreliable, untrustworthy; the negated form
- სანდო წყაროდან / sando c̣q̇arodan — "from a reliable source"; standard journalistic formula
- სანდო ინფორმაცია / sando inpormacia — reliable information
- სანდოობა კითხვის ნიშნის ქვეშ დგება / sandooba kitxvis nišnis qvesh dgeба — "credibility is called into question"
- სანდოობა დაიკარგა / sandooba daikarga — "credibility was lost"
- სარწმუნო / sarc̣muno — credible, believable; emphasis on capacity to be believed
- სარწმუნოება / sarc̣munoeba — credibility (in secular contexts); also religion/faith (in theological contexts)
- არასარწმუნო / arасarc̣muno — not credible, implausible
- ყალბი ინფორმაცია / q̇albi inpormacia — false information, disinformation
- ყალბი სიახლე / q̇albi siaxle — fake news

**Notes:** sando and sarc̣muno are closely related in this group but are distinguished in usage: sarc̣muno emphasizes the capacity to be believed (credibility), while sando emphasizes dependability and reliability more broadly. The q̇albi collocations with information nouns belong here as the negative pole of the same evaluative dimension. sarc̣munoeba's polysemy (credibility / religion) is noted but the religious sense falls outside this group.

## Group 10: Expressions used in the domain of correctness in language and formal norms

**Label:** Correctness in language, spelling, and formal norms

**Definition:** These expressions apply the concept of correctness to formal systems — particularly to language use, spelling, and pronunciation — rather than to propositional truth or moral conduct. They appear in educational, editorial, and linguistic contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- მართლწერა / martlc̣era — orthography, correct spelling; the standard term
- მართლწერის წესები / martlc̣eris c̣esebi — spelling rules, orthographic rules
- მართლმეტყველება / martlmetq̇veleba — correct speech, orthoepy, proper diction
- ზუსტი / zusti — exact, precise, accurate; applied to translations, measurements, and descriptions
- სიზუსტე / sizuste — exactness, precision, accuracy
- მართებული / martebuli — correct, appropriate, well-founded; applied to arguments and decisions
- მართებულობა / martebuloba — correctness, appropriateness, validity

**Notes:** This group is defined by the application of correctness concepts to formal and normative systems rather than to factual claims. martlc̣era and martlmetq̇veleba are the most specific items here. zusti and sizuste overlap with Group 1 (factual accuracy) but are included here because their usage in precision and measurement contexts is distinct. martebuli is a borderline item — it applies to arguments and decisions as well as to formal correctness, connecting it to Groups 1 and 3 as well.

## Ambiguous or overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into a single group:

**ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba and ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti**
These items appear in Groups 1, 6, and 7. Their usage spans everyday truth assertion, moral discourse, and formal/institutional contexts. The formal and theological register is more prominent for these items than for simartle and martali, but they are not exclusively formal. They could be placed in any of the three groups without distortion.

**სიმართლე / simartle and მართალი / martali**
These items appear in Groups 1, 6, and 7. They are more colloquial than č̣ešmariteba in most contexts. simartle is particularly prominent in proverbial and moral discourse (Group 6) and in everyday truth assertion (Group 1). martali is prominent in Group 1 through its predicative and discourse-particle uses.

**ნამდვილი / namdvili**
This item appears in Groups 2, 5, and 8. It is the most productive item in the dataset and participates in the widest range of usage contexts. Its core function (affirming genuineness) places it in Group 5, but its detection constructions connect it to Group 2 and its appearance/reality collocations connect it to Group 8.

**უტყუარი / uṭq̇uari**
This item appears in Groups 1, 3, and 7. It is distinguished from other Group 1 items by its stronger epistemic force and its restriction to formal registers. It is most at home in Group 3 (legal evidence) but its formal assertion function connects it to Groups 1 and 7.

**ყალბი / q̇albi and სიყალბე / siq̇albe**
These items appear in Groups 2, 3, 4, and 9. q̇albi is the most productive item on the falsity side of the dataset, combining freely with nouns denoting documents (Group 3), persons (Group 4), and information (Group 9). Its detection constructions place it in Group 2.

**სარწმუნოება / sarc̣munoeba**
This item appears in Group 9 in its secular sense (credibility) but its theological sense (religion, faith) connects it to Group 7. The two senses are context-dependent and the item does not fit cleanly into either group alone.

**მართებული / martebuli**
This item appears in Groups 1, 3, and 10. Its usage in legal reasoning and formal argument connects it to Group 3; its application to correctness of decisions and arguments connects it to Groups 1 and 10.

**გულწრფელი / gulc̣rpeli and გულწრფელობა / gulc̣rpeloba**
These items appear in Group 6 (moral discourse about honesty) but their usage in formal correspondence (gulc̣rpelaد, tkveni — "sincerely yours") and in evaluations of sincere intention (gulc̣rpeli neba) connects them to Group 7 as well. They are primarily interpersonal and moral rather than propositional, which distinguishes them from most items in Group 1.

## Items that do not fit cleanly into any group

**მიმსგავსება / mimsgavseba and მსგავსი / msgavsi**
These items denote resemblance and similarity. Their usage in P1 and P2 does not cluster with any of the groups above. They appear in aesthetic, legal (identification), and everyday contexts, but the concept of resemblance is not primarily organized around truth, falsity, authenticity, or reliability in the documented usage. They are noted here as ungrouped.

**არსი / arsi and არსებითი / arsebiti**
These items denote essence and what is fundamental or constitutive. Their usage in philosophical and legal contexts (material fact, essential property) is documented, but they do not cluster with the other items in any of the groups above. The concept of essence is related to but distinct from the concepts of truth, authenticity, and reliability that organize the other groups. They are noted here as ungrouped, though arsebiti's legal usage (material fact) creates a weak connection to Group 3.

**იდენტური / identuri and იდენტურობა / identuroba**
These items denote identity in the sense of being exactly the same. Their usage in legal and scientific contexts is documented, but they do not cluster with the other items in any of the groups above. The concept of identity (sameness) is distinct from the concepts of truth, authenticity, and reliability that organize the other groups. They are noted here as ungrouped, though their legal usage creates a weak connection to Group 3.
