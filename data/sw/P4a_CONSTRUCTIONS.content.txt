# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Copular construction: **NP + ni + TRUTH-TERM** (affirmative) or **NP + si + TRUTH-TERM** (negative). The copula **ni/si** agrees with subject class; truth-terms function as predicates.

### Examples
- **Habari hii ni kweli**
  - habari hii ni kweli
  - "This news is true"

- **Maneno yake ni sahihi**
  - maneno yake ni sahihi
  - "His/her words are correct"

### Notes
Standard predicative construction. Truth-terms (kweli, sahihi, ukweli, halisi, uongo, etc.) function as invariant predicates. Subject-verb agreement marked on copula only.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negative copular: **NP + si + TRUTH-TERM**. Uses negative copula **si** (invariant for all persons/classes in present tense).

### Examples
- **Hii si kweli**
  - hii si kweli
  - "This is not true"

- **Bidhaa hizi si halisi**
  - bidhaa hizi si halisi
  - "These products are not genuine"

### Notes
Negation marked solely on copula. Truth-terms remain unchanged. Past/future negation uses **si-** prefix on tensed copula forms (e.g., **sikuwa**, **sitakuwa**).

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Associative construction: **NOUN + -a + TRUTH-TERM** (with class agreement on associative marker **-a**). Alternative: direct adjective placement **NOUN + TRUTH-TERM** for some terms.

### Examples
- **Mtu wa kweli**
  - mtu wa kweli
  - "Genuine person / truthful person"

- **Ushahidi madhubuti**
  - ushahidi madhubuti
  - "Reliable evidence"

### Notes
Two patterns: (1) Associative **-a** links nouns to abstract truth-terms (wa/ya/za/la variants by class); (2) Direct modification for adjectival forms (sahihi, halisi, madhubuti). Some terms allow both.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Stative verb formation: **TRUTH-ROOT + -ik- + -a** (neuter/stative extension). Not productive for all roots. Alternative: **kuwa + TRUTH-TERM** (become + predicate).

### Examples
- **Jambo limekamilika**
  - jambo limekamilika
  - "The matter has become complete"

- **Amekuwa mwongo**
  - amekuwa mwongo
  - "He/she has become a liar"

### Notes
Stative **-ik-** attested for some roots (kamilika, danganyika). More commonly expressed periphrastically with **kuwa** (become) + predicate. Not all truth-terms have dedicated inchoative forms.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Causative suffix: **TRUTH-ROOT + -ish-/-sh- + -a**. Highly productive. Means "to make X" or "to cause to be X."

### Examples
- **Sahihisha maandishi**
  - sahihisha maandishi
  - "Correct the writing / verify the documents"

- **Hakikisha ukweli**
  - hakikisha ukweli
  - "Ensure the truth / verify the facts"

### Notes
Very productive pattern. Forms: sahihisha (make correct), hakikisha (make certain), thibitisha (confirm), kamilisha (complete), dhihirisha (make evident), safisha (purify/clarify). Regular passive **-ishwa** forms attested.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Abstract prefix: **u- + TRUTH-ROOT** (noun class 11/14). Creates mass/abstract nouns from adjectival or verbal bases.

### Examples
- **Ukweli wa mambo**
  - ukweli wa mambo
  - "The truth of the matter"

- **Uadilifu ni muhimu**
  - uadilifu ni muhimu
  - "Justice is important"

### Notes
Highly productive with **u-** prefix. Some forms add quality suffix **-fu** (uadilifu, umadhubuti). Creates abstract nouns denoting states or qualities. Can combine with further suffixes (**-a** in uhalisia).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Person prefix + root (+ optional agentive suffix): **m-/wa- + ROOT** or **m-/wa- + ROOT + -ifu**. Creates agent nouns (doers of action).

### Examples
- **Mwongo anasema uongo**
  - mwongo anasema uongo
  - "The liar tells lies"

- **Mdanganyifu hataaminiwa**
  - mdanganyifu hataaminiwa
  - "The deceiver will not be trusted"

### Notes
Two patterns: (1) Simple person prefix (mwongo/waongo from -ongo root); (2) Person prefix + agentive **-ifu** (mdanganyifu/wadanganyifu from -danganya). Plural uses **wa-** prefix (class 2). Patient nominals use **m-/wa-** without **-ifu** (mhukumu = judged person).

---

## 8. VERBAL DERIVATION FROM NOMINALS

### Pattern
Applicative/causative suffixes convert nouns to verbs: **NOUN-ROOT + -i-/-ia** (applicative) or **+ -ish-** (causative). Creates action verbs from nominal bases.

### Examples
- **Hukumia mhalifu**
  - hukumia mhalifu
  - "Judge the offender / pass judgment on the offender"

- **Shuhudia tukio**
  - shuhudia tukio
  - "Witness the event / testify about the event"

### Notes
Applicative **-ia** (hukumia from hukumu, shuhudia from shahidi) creates transitive verbs. Some forms show vowel harmony (shuhudia < shahidi). Productive for creating action verbs from abstract nouns.

---

## 9. EMPHATIC INTENSIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth-term + intensifier: **TRUTH-TERM + kabisa/sana/tu**. Intensifiers follow the modified term.

### Examples
- **Kweli kabisa**
  - kweli kabisa
  - "Absolutely true / completely true"

- **Ni sahihi sana**
  - ni sahihi sana
  - "It is very correct"

### Notes
Three main intensifiers: **kabisa** (completely, absolutely), **sana** (very, much), **tu** (only, just). Position: always post-posed. Can combine with copular constructions. **Tu** often minimizes rather than intensifies.

---

## 10. MANNER ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
Prepositional phrase: **kwa + ABSTRACT-NOUN**. Creates adverbial expressions of manner.

### Examples
- **Fanya kwa usahihi**
  - fanya kwa usahihi
  - "Do it correctly / with accuracy"

- **Sema kwa ukweli**
  - sema kwa ukweli
  - "Speak truthfully / in truth"

### Notes
Highly productive with preposition **kwa** (by/with/in) + abstract u-class noun. Functions adverbially to modify verbs. Alternative: **kwa + bare root** (kwa kweli, kwa hakika) also common.

---

## 11. CONTRASTIVE COORDINATION

### Pattern
Binary opposition: **TRUTH-TERM + au/na + OPPOSITE-TERM**. Uses conjunctions **au** (or) or **na** (and).

### Examples
- **Kweli au uongo**
  - kweli au uongo
  - "True or false / truth or lies"

- **Halisi au bandia**
  - halisi au bandia
  - "Genuine or fake / authentic or counterfeit"

### Notes
Fixed pairs common in evaluative contexts. **Au** (or) for alternatives; **na** (and) for co-occurrence or contrast (haki na batili = right and wrong). Order typically positive term first.

---

## 12. DEMONSTRATIVE SPECIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth-term + demonstrative: **TRUTH-TERM + DEMONSTRATIVE** (with class agreement on demonstrative).

### Examples
- **Ukweli huu**
  - ukweli huu
  - "This truth / this fact"

- **Uongo ule**
  - uongo ule
  - "That lie / that falsehood"

### Notes
Demonstratives follow noun. Agreement pattern: **huu/hii** (proximal), **ule/ile** (distal), **huyo/yule** (person class). Used for discourse reference to specific truth-claims.

---

## 13. POSSESSIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Truth-term + possessive: **TRUTH-TERM + POSSESSIVE-PRONOUN** (with class agreement).

### Examples
- **Haki yake**
  - haki yake
  - "His/her right / their entitlement"

- **Makosa yake**
  - makosa yake
  - "His/her mistakes / their errors"

### Notes
Possessive pronouns agree with noun class of possessed item. Common in legal/moral discourse for rights and responsibilities. Pattern: **-ake** (3sg), **-angu** (1sg), **-enu** (2pl), etc., with class prefix.

---

## 14. COMPLETIVE/TOTALITY MARKING

### Pattern
Truth-term + totality marker: **TRUTH-TERM + -ote/pekee/kamili**. Emphasizes completeness or exclusivity.

### Examples
- **Ukweli wote**
  - ukweli wote
  - "The whole truth / all the truth"

- **Ukweli pekee**
  - ukweli pekee
  - "Only the truth / nothing but the truth"

### Notes
Three patterns: **-ote** (all/whole, with class agreement), **pekee** (only/alone, invariant), **kamili** (complete, as modifier). Common in legal oaths and emphatic statements.

---

## 15. EVIDENTIAL FRAMING

### Pattern
Copular + truth-term + complementizer: **ni + TRUTH-TERM + kwamba + CLAUSE**. Introduces factual claims.

### Examples
- **Ni ukweli kwamba amefika**
  - ni ukweli kwamba amefika
  - "It is true that he/she has arrived"

- **Ni dhahiri kwamba wanajua**
  - ni dhahiri kwamba wanajua
  - "It is clear that they know"

### Notes
Frame construction for embedding propositions. Complementizer **kwamba** (that) introduces clause. Common with **ukweli, dhahiri, hakika**. Functions as evidential or epistemic frame.

---

## 16. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL

### Pattern
Negative preposition + truth-term: **bila (ya) + TRUTH-TERM**. Expresses absence or lack.

### Examples
- **Bila ushahidi**
  - bila ushahidi
  - "Without evidence / without proof"

- **Bila ya hakika**
  - bila ya hakika
  - "Without certainty / uncertainly"

### Notes
Preposition **bila** (without) takes optional associative **ya** before some nouns. Creates adverbial or adjectival phrases indicating absence. Common in legal and epistemic discourse.

---

## 17. VERB-OBJECT COLLOCATIONS

### Pattern
Action verb + truth-term object: **VERB + TRUTH-TERM**. Fixed or semi-fixed collocations.

### Examples
- **Sema ukweli**
  - sema ukweli
  - "Tell the truth / speak the truth"

- **Toa ushahidi**
  - toa ushahidi
  - "Give evidence / provide testimony"

### Notes
Common verbs: **sema** (speak), **toa** (give), **leta** (bring), **pata** (get), **fanya** (do/make). Some combinations highly conventionalized (toa ushahidi, sema ukweli). Object can take further modification.

---

## 18. REDUPLICATION

### Pattern
Full reduplication: **TRUTH-TERM + TRUTH-TERM**. Emphatic or intensifying function.

### Examples
- **Kweli kweli**
  - kweli kweli
  - "Really truly / very true / absolutely true"

- **Sawa sawa**
  - sawa sawa
  - "Okay okay / alright alright / fine fine"

### Notes
Full reduplication for emphasis or enthusiasm. More common in informal speech. Not productive for all truth-terms. Functions pragmatically for intensification or enthusiastic agreement.

---

## 19. DOMAIN-SPECIFIC MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth-term + domain marker: **TRUTH-TERM + wa + ki-DOMAIN**. Specifies epistemic domain or type.

### Examples
- **Ukweli wa kisayansi**
  - ukweli wa kisayansi
  - "Scientific truth / empirical truth"

- **Ukweli wa kisheria**
  - ukweli wa kisheria
  - "Legal truth / juridical truth"

### Notes
Pattern uses associative **wa** + **ki-** prefix (manner/type marker) + domain noun. Creates domain-specific truth concepts. Productive with **ukweli**: kisayansi (scientific), kisheria (legal), kimaandishi (documentary), kisiasa (political).

---

## 20. SPATIAL/METAPHORICAL EXTENSION

### Pattern
Truth-term + spatial term: **TRUTH-TERM + wa + SPATIAL-NOUN**. Metaphorical extension using spatial concepts.

### Examples
- **Ukweli wa ndani**
  - ukweli wa ndani
  - "Inner truth / internal truth / subjective truth"

- **Ukweli wa nje**
  - ukweli wa nje
  - "External truth / outward truth / objective truth"

### Notes
Uses spatial nouns **ndani** (inside) and **nje** (outside) to distinguish subjective from objective truth. Less productive than domain-specific pattern. Philosophical or psychological register.

---

## 21. SCALAR MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth-term + size/degree adjective: **TRUTH-TERM + SIZE-ADJECTIVE** (with class agreement on adjective).

### Examples
- **Uongo mkubwa**
  - uongo mkubwa
  - "Big lie / great falsehood / serious lie"

- **Makosa madogo**
  - makosa madogo
  - "Small mistakes / minor errors"

### Notes
Size adjectives (**mkubwa/kubwa** = big, **mdogo/dogo** = small) create scalar distinctions. Agreement pattern follows noun class. Implies significance or severity rather than physical size.

---

## 22. PASSIVE DERIVATION

### Pattern
Causative verb + passive suffix: **ROOT + -ish- + -wa**. Creates passive forms of causative truth-verbs.

### Examples
- **Ukweli umethibitishwa**
  - ukweli umethibitishwa
  - "The truth has been confirmed / verified"

- **Amehukumiwa kwa uongo**
  - amehukumiwa kwa uongo
  - "He/she has been judged/condemned for lying"

### Notes
Regular passive formation with **-wa** suffix. Applies to causative forms (thibitishwa, sahihishwa, hakikishwa). Agent optionally expressed with **na** (by). Very productive pattern.

---

## 23. RECIPROCAL CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Verb root + reciprocal suffix: **ROOT + -ana**. Indicates mutual action.

### Examples
- **Wanahojiana**
  - wanahojiana
  - "They are arguing with each other / debating"

### Notes
Reciprocal **-ana** suffix creates mutual/reciprocal meaning. Attested for **hoja** (argument) → **hojiana** (argue with each other). Not productive for all truth-domain roots. Subject must be plural or conjoined.

---

## 24. INSTRUMENTAL/MEANS EXPRESSION

### Pattern
Verb + **kwa** + truth-term: **VERB + kwa + TRUTH-TERM**. Specifies means or manner.

### Examples
- **Danganya kwa maneno**
  - danganya kwa maneno
  - "Deceive with words / lie verbally"

- **Thibitisha kwa ushahidi**
  - thibitisha kwa ushahidi
  - "Confirm with evidence / verify through testimony"

### Notes
Preposition **kwa** introduces instrument or means. Can specify method of truth-telling, deception, or verification. Productive with action verbs in truth domain.
