# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS ANALYSIS

---

### BASE: kweli (truth - adverbial/adjectival)

#### Construction 1: ni kweli
- **Example:** ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** ni kweli
- **Translation:** it is true, that's true
- **Usage:** Copular construction used to affirm the truth of a statement or proposition. Extremely common in conversational agreement and confirmation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2: si kweli
- **Example:** si kweli
- **Transliteration:** si kweli
- **Translation:** it is not true, that's not true, it's false
- **Usage:** Negative copular construction used to deny the truth of a statement. Very common in disagreement and refutation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 3: kweli au uongo
- **Example:** kweli au uongo
- **Transliteration:** kweli au uongo
- **Translation:** true or false, truth or lies
- **Usage:** Contrastive construction presenting a binary choice between truth and falsehood. Used in questioning, evaluation, and binary assessments.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 4: kweli kweli
- **Example:** kweli kweli
- **Transliteration:** kweli kweli
- **Translation:** really truly, very true, absolutely true
- **Usage:** Reduplicative construction for emphasis. Common in informal speech to strongly affirm truthfulness.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 5: hii ni kweli
- **Example:** hii ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** hii ni kweli
- **Translation:** this is true, this is the truth
- **Usage:** Demonstrative copular construction pointing to a specific truth claim. Common in explanatory and argumentative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productivity Patterns:

1. **Copular constructions (ni/si + adjective/noun):** Extremely productive across all truth-related terms for affirmation and negation.

2. **Prepositional phrases (kwa + abstract noun):** Highly productive for creating adverbial expressions of manner (kwa kweli, kwa ukweli, kwa usahihi, kwa uhalisia, kwa hakika, etc.).

3. **Associative constructions (noun + -a + quality noun):** Very productive for characterizing entities with truth-related qualities (mtu wa kweli, habari za uongo, etc.).

4. **Verb-object constructions:** Productive with action verbs and truth-related objects (sema ukweli, toa ushahidi, fanya makosa, etc.).

5. **Demonstrative constructions:** Regular and productive across all nominal forms (ukweli huu, hukumu yake, etc.).

6. **Emphatic constructions (base + kabisa/sana/tu):** Productive for intensification or mitigation across adjectival and adverbial forms.

### Asymmetries Noted:

- Some bases show richer phrasal possibilities (ukweli, ushahidi, haki) while others are more limited (madhubuti, uhalisia).
- Verbal bases naturally participate in more verb-complement constructions.
- Arabic-derived terms show full integration into Swahili phrasal patterns.
- Some expected contrastive pairs exist (kweli au uongo, halisi au bandia, haki na batili) while others are not conventionally paired.

### Absence Documentation:

- No common idiomatic expressions were found that significantly extend metaphorical meanings beyond those already documented.
- No specialized ritual or formulaic expressions were identified beyond legal and religious standard phrases.
- Comparative constructions (more true, less false) are not idiomatically fixed but use regular comparative syntax.
