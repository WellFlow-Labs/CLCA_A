# Root: **αληθ-** (*alith-*)


### 1. **αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** alítheia
- **Literal breakdown:** root noun
- **English:** truth
- **Context:** Basic everyday and formal noun for truth, both as truthfulness and as what is really the case. Used in conversation, journalism, religion, and philosophy.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **την αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** tin alítheia
- **Literal breakdown:** “the truth” (accusative phrase)
- **English:** the truth
- **Context:** Very common in expressions like *λέω την αλήθεια* and *η αλήθεια είναι*. Functions as the ordinary object phrase for “truth.”
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **η αλήθεια είναι (ότι)**
- **Transliteration:** i alítheia eínai (óti)
- **Literal breakdown:** “the truth is (that)”
- **English:** the truth is (that); actually
- **Context:** Extremely common discourse formula in speech and writing, often introducing a candid or corrective statement.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **στην αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** stin alítheia
- **Literal breakdown:** “in the truth”
- **English:** in truth; really
- **Context:** Less common than *στην πραγματικότητα*, but attested in elevated or rhetorical use to mean “truly” or “in reality.”
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 5. **αληθινός**
- **Transliteration:** alithinós
- **Literal breakdown:** αλήθεια + adjectival formation
- **English:** true, real, genuine
- **Context:** Common adjective for genuine objects, feelings, relations, and experiences. Often contrasts with fake, superficial, or merely apparent.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **αληθινά**
- **Transliteration:** alithiná
- **Literal breakdown:** adverbial/neuter plural use of *αληθινός*
- **English:** truly; really
- **Context:** Common adverb in everyday speech and writing for emphasis or sincerity, often close to “really” or “genuinely.”
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **αληθινή ιστορία**
- **Transliteration:** alithiní istoría
- **Literal breakdown:** “true story”
- **English:** true story
- **Context:** Common in media, conversation, and publishing to mark that an account is factual rather than fictional.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **αληθινός φίλος**
- **Transliteration:** alithinós fílos
- **Literal breakdown:** “true friend”
- **English:** true friend
- **Context:** Common interpersonal expression evaluating loyalty, authenticity, and depth of relationship rather than factual truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **αληθινή αγάπη**
- **Transliteration:** alithiní agápi
- **Literal breakdown:** “true love”
- **English:** true love
- **Context:** Frequent in everyday, literary, and popular-cultural use for genuine love, often with emotional or idealizing force.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **αληθής**
- **Transliteration:** alithís
- **Literal breakdown:** learned adjectival formation from the same root
- **English:** true, correct
- **Context:** Formal adjective used for propositions, statements, declarations, and logical values. Common in law, academia, and formal prose.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11. **αληθής δήλωση**
- **Transliteration:** alithís dílosi
- **Literal breakdown:** “true declaration”
- **English:** truthful / true statement
- **Context:** Used in legal, administrative, and formal evaluative settings for a declaration that corresponds to the facts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12. **αληθές / ψευδές**
- **Transliteration:** alithés / psevdés
- **Literal breakdown:** “true / false”
- **English:** true / false
- **Context:** Standard pair in logic, education, tests, and formal reasoning. Also used in computing and symbolic contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13. **αληθοφανής**
- **Transliteration:** alithofanís
- **Literal breakdown:** αληθ- + -οφανής “appearing”
- **English:** plausible; lifelike; truth-like
- **Context:** Used for something that appears true or convincing, such as an explanation, narrative, image, or performance. Common in literary criticism and everyday evaluation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14. **αληθοφάνεια**
- **Transliteration:** alithofáneia
- **Literal breakdown:** from *αληθοφανής*; “appearance of truth”
- **English:** plausibility; verisimilitude
- **Context:** Common in literary, theatrical, cinematic, and rhetorical discussion for the quality of seeming true or believable.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 15. **αληθεύω**
- **Transliteration:** alithevo
- **Literal breakdown:** verbal formation from αληθ-
- **English:** to tell the truth; to be true
- **Context:** Used both for persons speaking truthfully and for reports or claims turning out to be true. More formal/literary than *λέω την αλήθεια* in some uses.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 16. **αληθεύει**
- **Transliteration:** alithevi
- **Literal breakdown:** “it is true / it turns out true”
- **English:** it is true
- **Context:** Very common impersonal-style use in speech and writing to confirm a report, rumor, or proposition.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17. **αληθεύει ότι...**
- **Transliteration:** alithevi óti...
- **Literal breakdown:** “it is true that...”
- **English:** is it true that... / it is true that...
- **Context:** Common in questions, journalism, and conversation when checking whether a claim corresponds to reality.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18. **αληθινότητα**
- **Transliteration:** alithinótita
- **Literal breakdown:** from *αληθινός* + abstract suffix
- **English:** genuineness; truthfulness; realness
- **Context:** Attested but less common abstract noun, used in literary, philosophical, or evaluative contexts for the quality of being genuine or true.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 19. **αληθώς**
- **Transliteration:** alithós
- **Literal breakdown:** learned adverb from αληθ-
- **English:** truly; indeed
- **Context:** Elevated, literary, religious, or rhetorical adverb. Not common in ordinary colloquial speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 20. **εν αληθεία**
- **Transliteration:** en alitheía
- **Literal breakdown:** “in truth”
- **English:** in truth; truly
- **Context:** Learned or ecclesiastical phrase, especially in religious and elevated registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 21. **αληθινή τέχνη**
- **Transliteration:** alithiní téchni
- **Literal breakdown:** “true/genuine art”
- **English:** genuine art; true art
- **Context:** Used in aesthetic evaluation to distinguish art judged authentic or substantial from commercial or superficial production.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 22. **αληθινός εαυτός**
- **Transliteration:** alithinós eaftós
- **Literal breakdown:** “true self”
- **English:** true self
- **Context:** Common in psychological, self-help, and reflective discourse for one’s authentic identity.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**Note:** This root has additional forms in learned, literary, and religious usage beyond the items listed here.

# Root: **ουσι- / ουσία** (*ousi- / ousía*)


### 1. **ουσία**
- **Transliteration:** ousía
- **Literal breakdown:** root noun
- **English:** essence; substance
- **Context:** Used in philosophy, theology, formal discourse, and everyday speech for the essential point or core substance of something.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **ουσιώδης**
- **Transliteration:** ousiódis
- **Literal breakdown:** ουσία + adjectival suffix
- **English:** essential; substantial
- **Context:** Common in law, administration, philosophy, and formal writing for what is fundamental rather than incidental.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **ουσιωδώς**
- **Transliteration:** ousiodós
- **Literal breakdown:** adverb from *ουσιώδης*
- **English:** substantially; essentially
- **Context:** Common in legal, administrative, and formal argumentative prose.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **ουσιώδες**
- **Transliteration:** ousiódes
- **Literal breakdown:** neuter use of *ουσιώδης*
- **English:** the essential thing; what is essential
- **Context:** Common in formal and reflective discourse, often in contrasts between essentials and details.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **κατ’ ουσίαν**
- **Transliteration:** kat’ ousían
- **Literal breakdown:** “according to essence / in substance”
- **English:** essentially; in substance
- **Context:** Very common fixed phrase in law, politics, philosophy, and formal speech to indicate substantive rather than merely formal reality.

### 6. **στην ουσία**
- **Transliteration:** stin ousía
- **Literal breakdown:** “in the essence”

### 7. **επί της ουσίας**

- **Literal breakdown:** “upon the substance”
