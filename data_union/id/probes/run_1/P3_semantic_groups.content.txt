# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns documented in P1 and P2: collocational partners, register distributions, contrastive pairs, derivational productivity, and the contexts in which terms appear together or are distinguished from one another. No external framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns do not support a clean grouping, this is stated explicitly. Items may appear in more than one group where their usage genuinely spans multiple patterns.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to evaluate statements and claims as true or false

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a proposition, report, or claim corresponds to what is actually the case. They appear in contexts of verification, denial, correction, and acknowledgment — in journalism, legal proceedings, everyday conversation, and formal discourse. The central contrast in this group is between terms that affirm correspondence with reality and terms that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *benar* — the unmarked positive evaluator; used to affirm that a statement or claim is correct
- *tidak benar* — standard negation; used in official denials and corrections
- *salah* — primary antonym of *benar*; evaluates a statement as incorrect
- *keliru* — evaluates a belief or statement as based on error or misunderstanding, without necessarily implying deliberate deception; slightly more formal than *salah* in some registers
- *bohong* — evaluates a statement as deliberately untrue; functions as both noun and predicate
- *dusta* — near-synonym of *bohong*, more formal and literary in register; common in religious and moral discourse
- *kebenaran* — abstract noun for the property of being true; appears in *mencari kebenaran*, *menegakkan kebenaran*
- *kebohongan* — abstract noun for a lie or the quality of being false
- *kekeliruan* — abstract noun for error or mistaken belief
- *sebenarnya* — discourse adverb introducing a correction or revelation of what is actually the case
- *memang benar bahwa* — fixed concessive-affirmative frame acknowledging a fact before qualification
- *ada benarnya* — partial concession: "there is some truth to it"
- *benar, tapi* — standard concessive construction: "true, but"
- *tidak terbukti* — "not proven"; used to suspend or deny a truth claim for lack of evidence
- *informasi yang keliru* — "erroneous information"; used without implying deliberate deception
- *berita palsu* — "fake news"; used in contexts of deliberate misinformation

**Notes on internal distinctions:** Within this group, *bohong* and *dusta* specifically imply deliberate deception, while *keliru* and *salah* do not necessarily carry this implication. *Benar* and *tidak benar* are the most neutral and widely distributed; *dusta* is restricted to formal, literary, and religious registers where *bohong* would be used in everyday speech.

## Group 2: Terms used in legal and administrative validity judgments

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where an act, document, decision, or status is evaluated as meeting or failing to meet formally required conditions — in court decisions, contracts, official documents, administrative procedures, and legislative processes. The central question in this group is not whether something is factually true but whether it is recognized as binding, legitimate, or procedurally correct by an authoritative institution.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sah* — the primary term; used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to assert that something meets required conditions
- *tidak sah* — standard negation; used to declare an act, document, or status void or invalid
- *absah* — more formal variant of *sah*; used in legal writing and academic discourse
- *mengesahkan* — "to ratify, to validate, to legalize"; used for official approval of laws, documents, decisions
- *disahkan* — passive: "to be ratified"; standard in official announcements and legal reporting
- *pengesahan* — "ratification, validation"; used in *pengesahan konstitusi*, *pengesahan dokumen*
- *keabsahan* — abstract noun: "validity, legitimacy"; used in *keabsahan kontrak*, *keabsahan keputusan*
- *sah dan mengikat* — fixed legal formula: "valid and binding"
- *sah secara hukum* — "legally valid"; specifies the domain of validity as statutory law
- *sah menurut agama* — "valid according to religion"; used where religious and civil law intersect, particularly for marriage
- *dinyatakan sah* — "declared valid"; standard in court decisions and official rulings
- *alasan yang sah* — "valid/legitimate reason"; used in administrative law and official correspondence
- *tidak absah menurut hukum* — formal legal construction: "not legitimate under applicable law"
- *terbukti secara sah dan meyakinkan* — standard formula in Indonesian criminal verdicts, equivalent in function to "proven beyond reasonable doubt"
- *sah sebagai* — "recognized as, valid as"; used in legal recognition of status or capacity

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Sah* is the everyday-formal term; *absah* is more elevated and appears primarily in written legal and academic registers. *Sahih* (see Group 5) shares the same Arabic root but operates in a distinct domain (Islamic scholarly discourse) and is not interchangeable with *sah* in legal-administrative contexts. The formula *sah menurut agama* reflects the Indonesian legal context in which religious validity and civil validity are treated as distinct but related requirements.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate objects, documents, and persons as genuine or not genuine

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is whether an object, document, credential, or person is what it claims or appears to be — as opposed to being a copy, forgery, imitation, or assumed identity. They are used in commercial, legal, artistic, and identity contexts. The central contrast is between the original or genuine and the copy or fake.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asli* — the primary positive term: "genuine, original, native"; used for objects, documents, persons, and cultural items
- *palsu* — the primary negative term: "fake, counterfeit, forged"; implies deliberate deception
- *tiruan* — "imitation, copy"; less strongly negative than *palsu*; may be openly acknowledged as such
- *otentik* — "authentic"; more formal and restricted in distribution than *asli*; used in legal, academic, and journalistic registers
- *keaslian* — abstract noun: "authenticity, genuineness"; used in *mempertanyakan keaslian dokumen*
- *pemalsuan* — "falsification, forgery, counterfeiting"; used in legal contexts for the act or crime
- *pemalsu* — "forger, counterfeiter"; agentive noun used in legal and journalistic contexts
- *memalsukan* — "to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit"
- *asli atau palsu* — the standard binary opposition framing the question of authenticity
- *dijamin asli* — "guaranteed genuine"; common commercial formula
- *barang tiruan* — "imitation goods"; used in commercial and legal contexts
- *barang palsu* — "fake/counterfeit goods"; implies fraud
- *dokumen otentik* — "authentic document"; used in legal and notarial contexts
- *uang palsu* — "counterfeit money"; one of the most frequent collocations of *palsu*
- *identitas palsu* — "false identity"; used in crime reporting and legal proceedings
- *nama palsu* — "false name, alias"
- *ijazah palsu* — "forged diploma/certificate"
- *versi aslinya* — "the original version of it"; used to distinguish originals from later versions or copies
- *sertifikat asli* — "original certificate"; administrative construction distinguishing original from copies

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Palsu* implies deliberate deception and is used in legal contexts for fraud and forgery; *tiruan* describes a copy that may be openly acknowledged as such and does not necessarily imply fraud. *Asli* and *otentik* overlap in meaning but differ in register: *asli* is the everyday term, *otentik* is restricted to formal and written registers. *Sejati* (see Group 4) overlaps with *asli* in some uses but operates primarily in moral and relational contexts rather than object-authenticity contexts.

---

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate persons, qualities, and relationships as genuinely what they are claimed to be

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is not whether an object is a forgery but whether a person, quality, or relationship is deep, complete, and genuine rather than superficial, performed, or partial. They are used in moral, literary, philosophical, and interpersonal discourse. The evaluative concern is with depth and completeness of a quality rather than with the distinction between original and copy.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sejati* — "true, genuine, real"; used in *teman sejati* (true friend), *cinta sejati* (true love), *kebenaran sejati* (genuine truth); carries a sense of depth and completeness
- *jujur* — "honest, truthful, sincere"; evaluates a person's conduct or statements as free from deception or concealment
- *kejujuran* — abstract noun: "honesty, truthfulness, sincerity"
- *jujur dan amanah* — stable collocation: "honest and trustworthy"; used in character evaluation, particularly in political and religious contexts
- *jujur saja* — discourse marker: "honestly, to be honest"; introduces a candid statement
- *berkata jujur* — "to speak honestly, to tell the truth"
- *bersikap jujur* — "to conduct oneself honestly"; used in institutional and moral contexts
- *terpercaya* — "trustworthy, reliable, credible"; evaluates a person, source, or institution as worthy of trust
- *tidak terpercaya* — "untrustworthy"
- *senyum palsu* — "false smile"; extends *palsu* from object-authenticity into emotional expression, evaluating a display as performed rather than genuine
- *harapan palsu* — "false hope"; applies *palsu* to an abstract psychological state

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Sejati* and *asli* overlap in the sense of "genuine" but *sejati* is used for persons, qualities, and relationships where depth and completeness are at issue, while *asli* is used for objects, documents, and identity in the sense of "not a copy." *Jujur* specifically concerns freedom from deception in communication and conduct; it is not used for objects. *Senyum palsu* and *harapan palsu* represent extensions of *palsu* from Group 3 into this domain, where the concern is with performed or unfounded states rather than forged objects.

## Group 5: Terms used in Islamic scholarly evaluation of religious sources

**Definition:** These terms appear specifically in Islamic religious and scholarly discourse to evaluate hadith (prophetic traditions) and religious rulings as meeting or failing to meet criteria of authenticity, chain-of-transmission reliability, and doctrinal soundness. Their distribution is largely restricted to this domain in Indonesian usage.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahih* — "sound, authentic, correct"; the primary evaluative term for hadith that meet the highest criteria of authentication
- *hadis sahih* — "authentic hadith"; the most frequent collocation of *sahih* in Indonesian
- *kesahihan* — abstract noun: "soundness, authenticity"; used in *kesahihan hadis*
- *hadis sahih riwayat [narrator]* — standard citation formula in Islamic scholarly discourse
- *mendustakan ayat-ayat Allah* — "to deny/reject the signs of God"; collocation of *mendustakan* in religious discourse
- *para nabi didustakan oleh kaumnya* — "the prophets were rejected/called liars by their people"; standard formulation in Islamic religious texts
- *berdusta* — "to lie, to speak falsely"; more elevated in register than *berbohong*; common in Islamic moral discourse
- *pendusta* — "liar"; more elevated than *pembohong*; common in Islamic moral discourse
- *dilarang berdusta* — "lying is forbidden"; common moral formulation in Islamic discourse
- *bukti sahih* — "sound/authentic evidence"; bridges the *bukti* and *sahih* families in Islamic scholarly and formal legal contexts

**Notes:** *Sahih* shares its Arabic root (*ṣaḥīḥ*) with *sah* and *absah* but is not interchangeable with them in Indonesian usage. *Sah* and *absah* operate in legal-administrative contexts; *sahih* operates in Islamic scholarly contexts. The overlap is etymological rather than functional. *Dusta*, *berdusta*, and *pendusta* appear in both this group and Group 1, but their frequency and characteristic collocations in Islamic religious discourse are sufficiently distinct to note here.

## Group 6: Terms used in evidentiary and proof contexts

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is whether a claim has been established through evidence — in legal proceedings, scientific reporting, journalistic investigation, and administrative decision-making. The central concern is with the process and result of demonstrating that something is the case, as distinct from merely asserting it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *bukti* — "evidence, proof"; the base noun used across legal, journalistic, scientific, and everyday contexts
- *terbukti* — "proven, demonstrated, established"; resultative passive used in legal verdicts, scientific conclusions, and journalistic reporting
- *terbukti bersalah* — "proven guilty"; standard legal formula
- *tidak terbukti* — "not proven, unsubstantiated"; used to suspend or deny a claim for lack of evidence
- *membuktikan* — "to prove, to demonstrate, to establish"
- *pembuktian* — "the process of proving, demonstration"
- *beban pembuktian* — "burden of proof"; standard legal term
- *alat bukti* — "means of evidence, evidentiary instrument"; legal term for categories of admissible evidence
- *alat bukti yang sah* — "valid means of evidence"; combines Group 2 and Group 6 vocabulary
- *bukti nyata* — "concrete/tangible evidence"; emphasizes that evidence is material and observable
- *bukti kuat* — "strong evidence, compelling proof"
- *bukti fisik* — "physical evidence"
- *bukti tertulis* — "written/documentary evidence"
- *bukti ilmiah* — "scientific evidence"
- *bukti empiris* — "empirical evidence"
- *bukti tidak langsung* — "indirect/circumstantial evidence"
- *bukti langsung* — "direct evidence"
- *bukti permulaan* — "initial evidence, probable cause"; minimum evidentiary threshold for investigation
- *pembuktian terbalik* — "reversal of the burden of proof"; used in anti-corruption and money laundering law
- *terbukti secara sah dan meyakinkan* — standard formula in Indonesian criminal verdicts
- *mengumpulkan bukti* — "to gather evidence"
- *menyembunyikan bukti* — "to conceal evidence"
- *merusak bukti* — "to destroy/tamper with evidence"
- *terbukti* in advertising (*terbukti efektif*) — noted as an extension where the evidentiary standard implied differs from legal or scientific usage
- *diakui kebenarannya* — "its truth is acknowledged, recognized as true"; used in formal contexts where truth has been officially recognized

**Notes on internal distinctions:** This group is closely related to Group 1 (truth/falsity of claims) but is distinguished by its focus on the process and institutional recognition of establishing truth through evidence, rather than on the truth or falsity of a claim as such. *Terbukti secara sah dan meyakinkan* bridges Groups 2 and 6, combining legal validity vocabulary with evidentiary vocabulary.

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate correspondence with a standard, rule, or expectation

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where something is evaluated not as true or false in a propositional sense, but as conforming or failing to conform to an external standard, rule, requirement, or expectation. They are used in administrative, legal, technical, and everyday contexts. The central question is whether something matches or fits what is required or expected.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sesuai* — "in accordance with, conforming to, matching"; the primary term in this group
- *sesuai dengan* — "in accordance with"; the standard prepositional construction
- *tidak sesuai dengan* — "not in accordance with, not conforming to"; extremely common in official documents and legal writing
- *kesesuaian* — abstract noun: "conformity, correspondence, compatibility"
- *ketidaksesuaian* — "non-conformity, discrepancy, mismatch"
- *menyesuaikan* — "to adjust, to adapt, to conform"
- *penyesuaian* — "adjustment, adaptation, alignment"
- *sesuai standar* — "meeting standards, conforming to standards"
- *sesuai dengan fakta* / *tidak sesuai dengan fakta* — "corresponding/not corresponding to the facts"
- *tepat* — "precise, exact, fitting"; evaluates whether something corresponds exactly to a standard, target, or requirement
- *ketepatan* — abstract noun: "precision, accuracy, exactness"
- *tepat waktu* — "on time, punctual"
- *tepat sasaran* — "on target, well-targeted"
- *akurat* — "accurate"; used in technical, journalistic, and scientific contexts for data, reports, or measurements free from error
- *keakuratan* — abstract noun: "accuracy"
- *data yang akurat* — "accurate data"; one of the most frequent collocations of *akurat*
- *tidak akurat* — "inaccurate"
- *akurat dan terpercaya* — stable collocation pairing accuracy and reliability as information quality criteria
- *valid* — "valid"; used in academic, scientific, and administrative contexts for instruments, arguments, or procedures meeting required standards
- *validitas* — "validity"
- *data yang valid* — "valid data"; frequent in research methodology
- *dinyatakan valid* — "declared valid"; standard in research reporting

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Sesuai* is the most general term, used across all registers for conformity to any standard or expectation. *Tepat* emphasizes exactness and precision, including temporal precision (*tepat waktu*) and targeting precision (*tepat sasaran*). *Akurat* is more domain-specific, used primarily for data, measurements, and reports in technical and journalistic contexts. *Valid* is the most restricted, appearing primarily in academic and research methodology contexts. All four terms can appear in the same general domain (evaluation of information quality) but with different emphases and register distributions.

## Group 8: Terms used in moral and social evaluation of conduct and outcomes as appropriate or fitting

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where conduct, speech, appearance, or outcomes are evaluated as appropriate, fitting, deserved, or reasonable — or their opposites — according to social norms, moral expectations, or standards of propriety. They are used in interpersonal, social, and moral discourse rather than in legal or technical contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *wajar* — "reasonable, natural, proper"; evaluates conduct or outcomes as falling within normal, expected, or acceptable bounds
- *kewajaran* — abstract noun: "reasonableness, normality, propriety"
- *tidak wajar* — "unreasonable, improper, abnormal"
- *wajar saja jika* — "it is only natural/reasonable if"; used to normalize a reaction or outcome
- *dalam batas yang wajar* — "within reasonable limits"; used in legal and moral discourse
- *harga yang wajar* — "a fair/reasonable price"
- *pantas* — "fitting, deserved, appropriate"; evaluates whether something is suitable or merited; carries a stronger sense of social propriety or desert than *wajar*
- *kepantasan* — abstract noun: "propriety, fittingness, appropriateness"
- *tidak pantas* — "improper, inappropriate, unbecoming"
- *pantas mendapatkan* — "deserves to receive, worthy of"
- *sudah pantas* — "it is only fitting, it is deserved"; adds a sense of overdue desert
- *tidak pantas rasanya* — hedged evaluative construction: "it does not seem fitting"
- *adil* — "just, fair, equitable"; evaluates treatment, decisions, or distributions as conforming to principles of fairness
- *keadilan* — abstract noun: "justice, fairness, equity"; central in legal, moral, and political discourse
- *ketidakadilan* — "injustice, unfairness"
- *adil bagi semua pihak* — "fair to all parties"
- *perlakuan yang adil* — "fair treatment"
- *tidak adil jika* — "it is not fair if"
- *berlaku adil* — "to act fairly, to treat justly"
- *demi kebenaran dan keadilan* — "for the sake of truth and justice"; stable collocation pairing *kebenaran* and *keadilan* as paired values

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Wajar* evaluates something as falling within normal or expected bounds — the concern is with normality and reasonableness. *Pantas* evaluates something as fitting or deserved — the concern is with social propriety and merit. *Adil* evaluates treatment or distribution as conforming to principles of fairness — the concern is with equity and justice. All three can appear in moral discourse but with different emphases: *wajar* normalizes, *pantas* asserts desert or propriety, *adil* asserts equity. *Adil* is the most institutionally embedded of the three, generating the judicial institution terms *pengadilan* and *peradilan*.

## Group 9: Terms used as discourse markers to frame the speaker's epistemic stance

**Definition:** These terms appear at the level of discourse organization rather than as predicates of specific claims. They are used to signal the speaker's degree of confidence, to introduce corrections or candid assessments, or to hedge a claim. They are extremely common in spoken Indonesian and in written registers that approximate spoken discourse.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sebenarnya* — "actually, in truth, in fact"; introduces a correction or revelation of what is actually the case, often contrasting with a prior assumption
- *sebenar-benarnya* — emphatic form: "the absolute truth, truly and completely"; used in formal declarations and oaths
- *benar-benar* — intensifying adverb: "truly, really, genuinely, absolutely"; used across contexts including those where no truth claim is being evaluated
- *jujur saja* — "honestly, to be honest"; introduces a candid statement, often one anticipated to be unwelcome
- *untuk jujur* — "to be honest"; slightly more formal variant of *jujur saja*
- *kalau saya tidak salah* — "if I am not mistaken"; standard epistemic hedge qualifying a claim the speaker is not fully certain about
- *jika saya tidak keliru* — "if I am not mistaken"; slightly more formal variant of *kalau saya tidak salah*
- *sejauh yang saya tahu* — "as far as I know"; epistemic hedge signaling limited but genuine confidence
- *memang benar bahwa* — "it is indeed true that"; concessive-affirmative frame acknowledging a fact before introducing a qualification
- *pada dasarnya benar* — "essentially/fundamentally correct"; partial concession acknowledging core truth while reserving the right to qualify
- *ada benarnya* — "there is some truth to it"; partial concession without full endorsement
- *benar, tapi* — "true, but"; standard concessive construction
- *benar atau tidak* — concessive framing: "true or not"; brackets a claim whose truth is set aside while a consequence is asserted
- *kalau benar demikian* — "if that is indeed the case"; conditional construction introducing a course of action contingent on truth
- *benarkah* — interrogative: "is it really true that?"; challenges or seeks confirmation of a truth claim
- *nyata-nyata* — "clearly, evidently, manifestly"; emphasizes that something is clearly the case

**Notes:** This group is defined by functional role in discourse rather than by semantic content. Many of these expressions involve items from other groups (*benar*, *salah*, *keliru*, *jujur*, *nyata*) but their function here is to manage the speaker's epistemic commitment rather than to predicate truth or falsity of a specific claim. *Sebenarnya* is the most frequent and widely distributed of these; *sebenar-benarnya* is its formal and emphatic counterpart.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items from P1 and P2 show usage patterns that place them across multiple groups, or whose grouping is not clearly supported by a single dominant pattern.

---

**terpercaya / kredibel**

*Terpercaya* ("trustworthy, reliable, credible") and *kredibel* ("credible") appear in contexts of evaluating persons, sources, and institutions. They overlap with Group 4 (genuine qualities of persons) in that they evaluate a person's or institution's character, and with Group 7 (correspondence with standards) in that they evaluate whether a source meets standards of reliability. The collocation *akurat dan terpercaya* places *terpercaya* alongside an accuracy term (Group 7), while its use in character evaluation (*pemimpin yang jujur dan amanah*) places it alongside Group 4 terms. No single group captures both patterns. *Kredibel* is more restricted in distribution (formal, journalistic, academic registers) and appears primarily in source evaluation contexts.

---

**hakikat / esensi**

*Hakikat* ("essence, fundamental nature") and *esensi* ("essence") appear in philosophical, religious, and formal discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is. They are used in evaluative contexts where the question is whether something's true nature has been correctly identified (*pada hakikatnya* — "in essence, fundamentally"). This usage overlaps with Group 1 (truth of claims) in that it concerns what is really the case, and with Group 4 (genuine qualities) in that it concerns depth and authenticity of nature. However, neither group fully captures the philosophical register and the specific concern with fundamental nature rather than surface appearance. These terms do not fit cleanly into any single group.

---

**mirip / serupa**

*Mirip* ("resembling, similar to") and *serupa* ("similar, alike") evaluate the degree of resemblance between two entities. They appear in identification contexts (legal, forensic) and comparison contexts (aesthetic, everyday). Their connection to the truth-evaluation domain is indirect: resemblance is relevant to questions of authenticity (Group 3) and to identification of persons or objects, but *mirip* and *serupa* do not themselves assert truth or falsity, genuineness or fakeness. They are not clearly members of any group identified above. Their primary function is comparative rather than evaluative in the truth-domain sense.

---

**membenarkan** (in the sense of "to permit")

The use of *membenarkan* / *tidak dibenarkan* to mean "to permit" / "not permitted" in administrative and regulatory language represents a deontic extension of the *benar* family. In this use, the term operates in the domain of permission and prohibition rather than truth evaluation. This usage overlaps with Group 2 (legal-administrative validity) in its institutional and regulatory context, but it is not a validity judgment in the same sense as *sah*. It is noted here as a usage that does not fit cleanly into any single group.

---

**pembenaran** (in Protestant theological usage)

The use of *pembenaran* to translate the Christian theological doctrine of *justification* (*pembenaran oleh iman* — "justification by faith") represents a domain-specific extension where the term carries a soteriological meaning rather than an epistemic or legal one. This usage is noted as not fitting into any of the groups identified above.

---

**salah satu**

The grammaticalized partitive use of *salah* in *salah satu* ("one of") does not participate in any of the truth-evaluation groups. It is a high-frequency form but its meaning in this construction is entirely distinct from the evaluative meaning of *salah* documented in Groups 1 and 8.

---

**pengadilan / peradilan**

*Pengadilan* ("court, tribunal") and *peradilan* ("judiciary, judicial system") are derived from *adil* (Group 8) but function as institutional names rather than evaluative terms. Their connection to the truth-evaluation domain is indirect (courts are institutions where truth and validity are adjudicated), but they do not themselves evaluate truth, validity, or fairness. They are not members of any group in the sense used here.

---

## Items that do not fit clearly into any group

The following items from P1 are noted as not fitting clearly into any of the groups identified above, based on the usage patterns documented in P1 and P2:

- **sejati** partially fits Group 3 (genuine vs. not genuine) and Group 4 (genuine qualities of persons), but its characteristic collocations (*teman sejati*, *cinta sejati*, *kebenaran sejati*) suggest a usage pattern centered on depth and completeness of a quality in relational and philosophical contexts, which is not fully captured by either group. It is listed under Group 4 as the closest fit but the overlap is imperfect.

- **hakikat** and **esensi**: as noted above, these do not fit cleanly into any single group.

- **mirip** and **serupa**: as noted above, these are comparative rather than evaluative terms in the truth-domain sense and do not fit clearly into any group.
