# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational Morphology
## Language: English (en)

Since English uses Latin script natively, transliteration is identical to native form throughout.

The following documents **attested derivational forms** for each P1 base item. Forms already exhaustively covered under root expansion (true/truth, ver-, correct/right) are summarized with attested members; inflectional-only relationships are excluded.

---

### 1. true

- **truth** — *true* + *-th* (nominal) — "the quality of being true" — Central abstract noun across philosophy, law, ethics. [CORE]
- **truly** — *true* + *-ly* — "genuinely; in truth" — Everyday intensifier and affirmation. [CORE]
- **truthful** — *truth* + *-ful* — "habitually telling the truth" — Moral evaluation of persons/statements. [CORE]
- **truthfulness** — *truth* + *-ful* + *-ness* — "the quality of being truthful" — Ethical discourse. [CORE]
- **truthfully** — *truth* + *-ful* + *-ly* — "in a truthful manner" — Everyday and legal. [CORE]
- **untrue** — *un-* + *true* — "false; not faithful" — Statements and fidelity. [CORE]
- **untruth** — *un-* + *truth* — "a falsehood" — Formal/euphemistic. [CORE]
- **trueness** — *true* + *-ness* — "degree of conformity; straightness" — Technical (metrology, mechanical alignment). [MARGINAL]
- **truism** — *true* + *-ism* — "a self-evident or hackneyed truth" — Rhetorical/everyday. [CORE]

### 2. false

- **falsity** — *false* + *-ity* — "the state of being false" — Logical, philosophical evaluation. [CORE]
- **falsehood** — *false* + *-hood* — "a lie; an untrue statement" — Moral/everyday. [CORE]
- **falsify** — *false* + *-ify* — "to make false; to tamper with" — Scientific (falsifiable data), legal (falsify records). [CORE]
- **falsification** — *falsify* + *-ication* — "the act of falsifying" — Scientific methodology, legal. [CORE]
- **falsifiable** — *falsify* + *-able* — "able to be proven false" — Philosophy of science (Popperian). [CORE]
- **falsely** — *false* + *-ly* — "in a false manner" — Everyday ("falsely accused"). [CORE]
- **falseness** — *false* + *-ness* — "the quality of being false" — Everyday, less common than *falsity*. [MARGINAL]

### 3. accurate

- **inaccuracy** — *in-* + *accuracy* — "an error; lack of accuracy" — Everyday, journalistic. [CORE]

## Summary Notes on Asymmetry (data-supported)

- **Richest derivational fields:** *just* (justice/justify/justification/justifiable + negations), *real* (reality/realism/realist/realistic/realize), *valid*, *correct*, and *true* show full noun + adverb + verb + negation patterns.
- **Negation asymmetry:** the negating prefix varies lexically and is not predictable — *un-* (*untrue, unreal, unreliable, unjust, unfaithful*), *in-* (*inaccurate, invalid, incorrect, insincere, inexact*), *im-* (*improper, implausible*), *il-* (*illegitimate*), *dis-* (*dishonest*). No single productive default; gaps exist (e.g., no common *\*ungenuine*, *\*unauthentic* — instead *inauthentic*).
- **Minimal/absent derivation:** *lifelike*, *bona fide* yield no common further derivation; *trustworthy* and *truthful* (themselves derived/compound) extend only by *-ness*/*-ly*.
- **Conversion vs. affixation:** *fake* and *forge*/*forged* rely largely on conversion (noun/verb/adjective) rather than affixal derivation; their affixed forms (*faker*, *fakery*; *forger*, *forgery*) are agentive/abstract only.
- **Borrowings resist derivation:** Latin/Greek learned items (*bona fide*, *verisimilitude*, *mimetic*, *spurious*) produce few or only marginal English derivatives.
