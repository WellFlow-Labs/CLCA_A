## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Subject + di + òtítọ́ | Ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́ | ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́ | "That matter became the truth / turned out to be true" | [MARGINAL] | Used for discovery or revelation rather than change in truth-value. Not common for statements "becoming true" as truth is treated as static property. Context-dependent usage. |

**Usage note:** Yoruba conceptualizes truth as inherent rather than emergent. The **di** construction is pragmatically marked and typically implies that something was revealed to be true rather than changed from false to true. More common in narrative contexts describing discovery.

**Alternate constructions:**
- **Subject + hàn + bí òtítọ́** (appear as truth) — revelation/appearance construction
- **A rí i pé ó tọ́** (we saw that it is correct) — evidential construction, more common than inchoative

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Subject + mú + kí + Clause | Ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ náà tọ́ | ó mú kí ọ̀rọ̀ náà tọ́ | "He/she made that statement true / verified it" | [MARGINAL] | Periphrastic causative. Pragmatically marked; implies verification or making something correspond to truth rather than creating truth. Uncommon in everyday speech. |
| Subject + jẹ́ + kí + Clause | Ó jẹ́ kí n sọ òtítọ́ | ó jẹ́ kí n sọ òtítọ́ | "He/she allowed me to tell the truth" | [CORE] | Permissive causative. Common construction for allowing/enabling truth-telling. Used across registers. More natural than **mú kí** for truth domain. |

**Usage note:** Yoruba lacks morphological causative for truth predicates. The **mú kí** pattern is conceptually odd for truth (one cannot "make" truth), so it's reinterpreted as verification. The **jẹ́ kí** pattern is more natural as it expresses permission/enablement.

**Alternate constructions:**
- **Subject + ṣe + kí + Clause** (do/make that...) — alternative causative verb, similar restrictions
- **Subject + fún + Object + láyè + láti + Verb** (give X opportunity to...) — more explicit permissive

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| ò- + tí + Verb | òtítọ́ | òtítọ́ | "truth, truthfulness" | [CORE] | Lexicalized nominalization from **tọ́**. This specific form is the standard word for "truth." Not productive as a pattern—**òtítọ́** is a fixed lexical item. |
| ì- + Verb | ìgbàgbọ́ | ìgbàgbọ́ | "belief, faith" | [CORE] | Highly productive prefix for creating abstract nouns from verbs. Used extensively in truth domain: **ìmọ̀** (knowledge), **ìdájú** (certainty), **ìbámu** (correspondence). |
| àì- + Verb | àìtọ́, àìmọ̀ | — | "incorrectness, ignorance" | [CORE] | Productive negative nominalization prefix. Creates abstract nouns denoting absence or negation of the verbal property. Common in formal and philosophical discourse. |

**Alternate constructions:**
- **ìṣe + Noun** (doing of X) — periphrastic nominalization, less common
- **ọ̀nà + Verb** (way of Verb-ing) — manner nominalization, different semantic profile

## 8. COMPLEMENTATION (PROPOSITIONAL EMBEDDING)

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Verb + pé + Clause | Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́ | mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́ | "I believe that it is true" | [CORE] | Standard declarative complementation. Used with verbs of saying, thinking, believing. The complementizer **pé** is obligatory. Very common across all registers. |
| Verb + bí + Clause | Mo mọ̀ bí ó ṣe tọ́ | mo mọ̀ bí ó ṣe tọ́ | "I know how it is true / whether it is true" | [CORE] | Interrogative/manner complementation. Used for embedded questions or manner descriptions. Common with epistemic verbs like **mọ̀** (know). |

**Alternate constructions:**
- **Verb + wí + pé + Clause** (say that...) — quotative construction with **wí** (say)
- **Verb + Clause** (zero complementizer) — possible with some verbs but less common than **pé**

## 12. DEGREE MODIFICATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Predicate + púpọ̀ | Ó jọ ọ́ púpọ̀ | ó jọ ọ́ púpọ̀ | "It resembles it greatly / is very similar" | [CORE] | Standard intensification with **púpọ̀** (much, greatly). Common with gradable predicates. However, truth predicates like **tọ́** rarely take degree modification. |
| Predicate + jù + lọ | Ó tọ́ jù ú lọ | ó tọ́ jù ú lọ | "It is more correct than it" | [MARGINAL] | Comparative construction with **jù** (exceed). Grammatically possible but pragmatically odd for truth—truth is typically binary. More natural with **dára** (good) than **tọ́**. |
| Predicate + díẹ̀ | (implied in notes) | — | — | [MARGINAL] | Diminutive modification with **díẹ̀** (a little). Rarely used with truth predicates as truth doesn't scale down naturally. More common with other property concepts. |

**Usage note:** Truth predicates resist degree modification because truth is conceptualized as binary rather than scalar in Yoruba. Comparative constructions with **tọ́** are grammatical but pragmatically marked.

## 13. INTERROGATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Ṣé + Clause | Ṣé ó tọ́? | ṣé ó tọ́ | "Is it true? / Is it correct?" | [CORE] | Standard polar question marker. **Ṣé** precedes clause without changing word order. Very common in spoken and written Yoruba. No register restrictions. |
| Question-word + ni + Clause | Kí ni òtítọ́? | kí ni òtítọ́ | "What is truth?" | [CORE] | Wh-question with focus marker **ni**. Standard pattern for content questions. Used across all registers. The question word is typically fronted. |

**Alternate constructions:**
- **Clause + bí?** (Clause + question particle) — alternative polar question, less common
- **Njẹ́ + Clause** (question particle + clause) — alternative to **ṣé**, regional variation

## SUMMARY OF EPISTEMIC STATUS DISTRIBUTION

- **[CORE]**: 38 constructions — highly natural, frequent, unrestricted
- **[MARGINAL]**: 6 constructions — less common, restricted contexts, pragmatically marked
- **[UNCERTAIN]**: 0 constructions — all patterns have clear attestation status

**Key observations:**
1. Basic attribution, negation, and complementation patterns are all [CORE]
2. Causative and inchoative constructions are [MARGINAL] due to conceptual incompatibility with truth as static property
3. Degree modification is [MARGINAL] because truth is treated as binary rather than scalar
4. Serial verb and light verb constructions are highly productive and [CORE]
5. No constructions are [UNCERTAIN]—all have clear usage patterns in the data
