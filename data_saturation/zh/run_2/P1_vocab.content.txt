# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal meaning:** true; genuine
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Used across a wide range of contexts to assert that something is genuine, accurate, or real, as opposed to false or simulated. Participates productively in compounds such as 真实、真相、真理. Appears in both everyday speech and formal philosophical or legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** false; fake; borrowed
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
**Usage:** Contrasts directly with 真 and is used to describe objects, statements, or identities that are not genuine or are deceptive. Common in legal contexts (假冒) and everyday evaluation of goods or claims. Also participates in compounds such as 假冒、假设.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal meaning:** true + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** real, truthful, authentic, factual
**Usage:** Describes the quality of being in accordance with reality or fact, applied to statements, representations, or depictions. Frequently used in journalism, legal testimony, and aesthetic criticism (e.g., 真实感, a sense of realism). More explicitly evaluative than 真 alone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 虚假
**Transliteration:** xūjiǎ
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false
**Typical English translations:** false, fictitious, fraudulent, deceptive
**Usage:** Used to characterize statements, claims, or representations that are deliberately misleading or lack factual basis. Common in legal and regulatory language (e.g., 虚假广告, false advertising). Carries a stronger negative connotation than 假 alone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal meaning:** true + appearance/form
**Typical English translations:** the truth, the true state of affairs, the facts
**Usage:** Refers to the actual facts of a matter, especially when they have been concealed or are being sought. Commonly used in investigative, legal, and journalistic contexts (e.g., 揭露真相, to reveal the truth). Implies a contrast with a misleading surface appearance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal meaning:** true + principle/reason
**Typical English translations:** truth (as a principle), universal truth
**Usage:** Refers to truth understood as an objective principle or law, rather than a particular true statement. Prominent in philosophical, political, and moral discourse. Distinguished from 真相 by its more abstract and general character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 正确
**Transliteration:** zhèngquè
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Applied to statements, answers, methods, or conduct to indicate conformity with a standard, rule, or fact. Widely used in educational, moral, and technical contexts. Does not carry strong connotations of authenticity but focuses on correctness relative to a norm.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 错误
**Transliteration:** cuòwù
**Literal meaning:** wrong/mistaken + error
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Denotes a deviation from what is correct, accurate, or appropriate, applicable to statements, beliefs, actions, or judgments. Used across everyday, academic, legal, and moral registers. Contrasts with 正确 as its standard antonym.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 准确
**Transliteration:** zhǔnquè
**Literal meaning:** standard/precise + certain
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Emphasizes precision and exactness, often in technical, scientific, or measurement contexts. Applied to data, translations, descriptions, and predictions. Overlaps with 正确 but foregrounds degree of exactness rather than mere correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 可靠
**Transliteration:** kěkào
**Literal meaning:** can + lean on/depend on
**Typical English translations:** reliable, dependable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Describes persons, sources, methods, or information that can be trusted or depended upon. Used in evaluations of evidence, witnesses, equipment, and character. Focuses on consistency and trustworthiness over time rather than a single instance of accuracy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** true + false/spurious
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness vs. falsity; whether something is genuine or fake
**Usage:** A fixed binomial expression used to frame the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit. Common in authentication contexts such as art, documents, and antiques (e.g., 辨别真伪, to distinguish the genuine from the fake). Functions as a compound noun denoting the evaluative dimension itself.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 伪造
**Transliteration:** wěizào
**Literal meaning:** false/spurious + to make/fabricate
**Typical English translations:** to forge, to fabricate, to counterfeit; forgery
**Usage:** Refers to the deliberate fabrication or falsification of documents, signatures, evidence, or objects. Central to legal discourse on fraud and forgery. Implies intentional deception, distinguishing it from mere error.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 本质
**Transliteration:** běnzhì
**Literal meaning:** root/origin + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, fundamental character
**Usage:** Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance or accidental features. Appears in expressions such as 本质上 (essentially, in essence). Common in both academic and political rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 实质
**Transliteration:** shízhì
**Literal meaning:** solid/real + substance
**Typical English translations:** substance, substantive nature, real content
**Usage:** Refers to the actual or substantive content of something as opposed to its form or label, often in legal and analytical contexts (e.g., 实质上, in substance). Overlaps with 本质 but tends to be used in more practical or legal evaluations rather than philosophical ones.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 名副其实
**Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
**Literal meaning:** name + matches/corresponds to + its + reality
**Typical English translations:** living up to one's name, worthy of the name, as the name implies
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression (成语) used to evaluate whether a label, title, or description accurately corresponds to the actual nature or quality of the thing it names. Applied to persons, institutions, or objects. Its antonym 名不副实 is equally common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 名不副实
**Transliteration:** míng bù fù shí
**Literal meaning:** name + not + matches + reality
**Typical English translations:** not living up to one's name, in name only, failing to match the description
**Usage:** The negative counterpart of 名副其实, used to criticize a mismatch between a label or reputation and actual qualities. Common in evaluative discourse about institutions, products, and persons. Functions as a fixed idiomatic expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 如实
**Transliteration:** rúshí
**Literal meaning:** according to + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** truthfully, as things actually are, accurately
**Usage:** An adverbial expression meaning to report or represent something in accordance with the facts, without distortion. Frequently used in legal contexts (e.g., 如实陈述, to state truthfully) and in instructions to witnesses or reporters. Implies fidelity to reality in representation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 失真
**Transliteration:** shīzhēn
**Literal meaning:** lose + truth/genuineness
**Typical English translations:** distortion, loss of fidelity, inaccurate representation
**Usage:** Used to describe a representation, recording, or account that has deviated from the original or from reality. Common in technical contexts (audio/visual distortion) and in evaluations of historical or journalistic accuracy. Implies a departure from an original true state.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 诚实
**Transliteration:** chéngshí
**Literal meaning:** sincere/honest + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere
**Usage:** A moral evaluation of persons or conduct, indicating that one does not deceive or misrepresent. Applied to character, behavior, and speech. Distinguished from 真实 by its focus on the agent's disposition rather than the content of a statement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 欺骗
**Transliteration:** qīpiàn
**Literal meaning:** deceive + cheat
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to cheat, deception, fraud
**Usage:** Refers to the act of deliberately misleading another person through false statements or actions. Used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Implies intentionality and a victim, distinguishing it from mere error or inaccuracy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 仿冒
**Transliteration:** fǎngmào
**Literal meaning:** imitate + counterfeit/pass off as
**Typical English translations:** to counterfeit, to pass off an imitation as genuine, imitation fraud
**Usage:** Used specifically in commercial and legal contexts to describe the production or sale of imitation goods presented as genuine branded products. Overlaps with 假冒 but is more specifically associated with trademark and intellectual property violations. Common in consumer protection discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 假冒
**Transliteration:** jiǎmào
**Literal meaning:** false/fake + to pass off as
**Typical English translations:** to impersonate, to pass off as genuine, counterfeit
**Usage:** Describes the act of falsely presenting something or someone as genuine, whether a product, identity, or credential. Used in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts. Appears in fixed compounds such as 假冒伪劣 (fake and inferior goods).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 原真性
**Transliteration:** yuánzhēnxìng
**Literal meaning:** original + true + -ness (nominalization suffix)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity (especially of cultural heritage), originality
**Usage:** A more specialized term used in heritage conservation, museology, and cultural studies to refer to the authenticity and integrity of cultural artifacts or sites. Corresponds closely to the technical use of "authenticity" in international heritage discourse (e.g., UNESCO contexts). Less common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 相符
**Transliteration:** xiāngfú
**Literal meaning:** mutually + conform/match
**Typical English translations:** to correspond, to match, to be consistent with
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether two things — a statement and a fact, a copy and an original, a description and a reality — are in agreement or conformity. Common in legal, administrative, and documentary contexts (e.g., 与事实相符, consistent with the facts). Focuses on the relational match between two items.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 核实
**Transliteration:** héshí
**Literal meaning:** verify/check + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to check the accuracy of
**Usage:** Refers to the process of checking or confirming whether information, claims, or documents are accurate and genuine. Widely used in journalistic, legal, administrative, and investigative contexts. Implies an active process of verification rather than a static judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 鉴定
**Transliteration:** jiàndìng
**Literal meaning:** to appraise/distinguish + to determine/fix
**Typical English translations:** to authenticate, to appraise, to identify; authentication, appraisal
**Usage:** Refers to expert evaluation to determine the authenticity, nature, or quality of an object, substance, or document. Used in forensic, art authentication, legal, and scientific contexts. Implies specialized expertise and an authoritative judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 伪装
**Transliteration:** wěizhuāng
**Literal meaning:** false/spurious + dress/disguise
**Typical English translations:** to disguise, to camouflage, to masquerade as; disguise, pretense
**Usage:** Describes the act of concealing one's true nature or identity through a false appearance. Used in military, political, and everyday moral contexts. Implies deliberate concealment of what something or someone truly is.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 表里如一
**Transliteration:** biǎo lǐ rú yī
**Literal meaning:** outside + inside + like + one
**Typical English translations:** consistent inside and out, what one appears to be is what one is, genuine
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression used to evaluate persons or conduct as genuinely consistent between outward appearance and inner reality. Applied as a moral commendation indicating absence of hypocrisy or pretense. Its antonym 表里不一 describes inconsistency between appearance and reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 货真价实
**Transliteration:** huò zhēn jià shí
**Literal meaning:** goods + genuine + price + real/solid
**Typical English translations:** genuine goods at fair prices; the real thing; authentic
**Usage:** Originally a commercial expression affirming that goods are genuine and prices honest, now used more broadly to assert that something is the real thing and not a pretense or imitation. Can be applied to persons, skills, or qualifications as well as goods. Functions as a fixed idiomatic expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 失实
**Transliteration:** shīshí
**Literal meaning:** lose/depart from + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, contrary to fact, not in accordance with the facts
**Usage:** Used to characterize statements, reports, or accounts that deviate from the facts, whether through error or distortion. Common in legal and journalistic evaluations (e.g., 严重失实, seriously inaccurate). Implies a departure from factual accuracy without necessarily implying deliberate deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
