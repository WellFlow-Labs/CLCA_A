# G5 — Cross-Language Construction Comparison

---

## 1. Identification of Main Recurring Constructional Functions

Working directly from the F4 construction tables across all eight languages, the following constructional functions recur with sufficient frequency to warrant cross-language comparison. These are identified inductively from the data, not imposed from a predetermined list.

**Functions identified:**
1. Predicative attribution (asserting truth-value of a proposition or entity)
2. Predicative negation (denying truth-value)
3. Adnominal modification (truth-domain adjective modifying a noun)
4. Change-of-state / inchoative (something becoming true, real, or certain)
5. Causative (an agent making something true, verified, or real)
6. Abstract nominalization (quality or state of being true/honest/correct)
7. Agentive nominalization (person characterized by truth-related behavior)
8. Action/process nominalization (the act of verifying, lying, deceiving, etc.)
9. Light verb constructions for speech acts (telling truth, telling lies)
10. Light verb constructions for truth-seeking and discovery
11. Light verb constructions for verification and confirmation
12. Revelation and concealment constructions
13. Adverbial manner modification (doing something truthfully, honestly, correctly)
14. Emphatic affirmation and intensification
15. Interrogative constructions for truth-checking
16. Conditional constructions based on truth-value
17. Concessive constructions acknowledging truth
18. Discourse marker function (introducing frank or corrective statements)
19. Comparative and scalar constructions
20. Correspondence / matching constructions
21. Disjunctive / binary choice constructions (true or false)
22. Evidential and reportative constructions
23. Reflexive / self-directed constructions (self-deception)
24. Degree modification (very true, not quite correct, etc.)
25. Performative / oath constructions

## 2. Comparison Table

| Constructional Function | Common Realization Patterns | Languages | Notes on Variation, Restrictions, or Gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Predicative attribution** | Copula + truth-domain adjective or noun; zero copula in present tense | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: copular suffix -a on adjectives (ეს ჭეშმარიტია "this is true"); Yoruba: jẹ́ copula (Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí jẹ́ òtítọ́) or focus copula ni (Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀); Finnish: copula olla + partitive (se on totta); Basque: copula da (egia da); Tamil: zero copula in present (இது உண்மை "this is truth"); Indonesian: zero copula (cerita itu benar); Turkish: zero copula in present 3rd person (bu doğru) or overt -dır in formal register; Korean: copula -이다 (이것은 진실이다) or zero in informal speech. The zero copula in present tense is the unmarked pattern in Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean; Georgian, Finnish, and Basque use overt copulas. Yoruba uniquely has two copular strategies with different pragmatic force (jẹ́ neutral, ni emphatic/contrastive). |
| **2. Predicative negation** | Negation particle/auxiliary + copula or predicate | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: syntactic negation ar + copula (ეს არ არის სიმართლე); Yoruba: kì í ṣe (emphatic negative copula) or kọ́ (focus negative); Finnish: negative auxiliary ei + copula stem ole (se ei ole totta); Basque: ez + copula (ez da egia); Tamil: illai (existential negation: உண்மை இல்லை) or alla (copular negation); Indonesian: tidak + predicate (itu tidak benar); Turkish: değil (bu doğru değil); Korean: 아니다 for nominal predicates, -지 않다 for adjectival verbs. Yoruba shows the most elaborate negation system with distinct forms for emphatic (kì í ṣe) vs. focus (kọ́) negation. Tamil distinguishes existential negation (illai "there is no truth in it") from copular negation (alla "is not"). |
| **3. Adnominal modification** | Truth-domain adjective precedes noun; agreement varies | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: adjective + noun with case agreement (ჭეშმარიტი სიტყვა "true word"); Yoruba: noun + adjective in N+ADJ order (ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ "true statement"); Finnish: adjective precedes noun with case agreement (tosi väite "true claim"); Basque: relational suffix -zko/-ezko on noun before article (egiazko historia "true story"); Tamil: adjective + noun with -āna suffix (உண்மையான கதை "true story"); Indonesian: adjective precedes noun (benar adanya, doğru bilgi); Turkish: adjective precedes noun (doğru bilgi "correct information"); Korean: adjective + -한/-ㄴ + noun (진실한 마음 "sincere heart"). Yoruba is the only language with consistent N+ADJ order for truth-domain modification. Basque uses a relational suffix strategy rather than a simple adjective. |
| **4. Change-of-state / inchoative** | Light verb "become" + truth predicate; or dedicated inchoative suffix | All 8, but with major variation | **Widely attested constructionally; moderately attested morphologically.** Turkish and Finnish show productive morphological inchoatives on truth roots: Turkish gerçekleşmek "come true" (-leş-), Finnish toteutua "be realized" (-utua). Other languages use light verb constructions: Georgian: gamovlinda "was revealed" (preferred over "became true"); Yoruba: ó di òtítọ́ "it became truth" (di = become); Basque: egia bihurtu "become truth" (bihurtu = become), marked as marginal; Tamil: uṇmaiyāku "become true" (-āku); Indonesian: menjadi kenyataan "become reality" (menjadi = become); Korean: jinsiri doeda "truth becomes" (doeda = become). **Universal restriction:** no language treats "becoming true" as a natural state change for propositions; all prefer revelation/verification constructions (see Function 10). The inchoative is most natural for plans, dreams, and predictions "coming true," not for propositions changing truth-value. |
| **5. Causative** | Causative suffix or light verb construction; agent causes truth-state | All 8, with major gaps | **Moderately attested morphologically; widely attested constructionally.** Turkish: gerçekleştirmek "realize/implement" (-leştir-); Finnish: toteuttaa "implement" (-uttaa); Georgian: no dedicated morphological causative; uses light verbs (სიმართლე გამოაჩინა "revealed the truth"); Basque: morphological causative -arazi blocked on truth roots; uses light verbs; Tamil: uṇmaippaṭuttu "verify" (-paṭuttu factitive); Indonesian: membenarkan "confirm" (meN-...-kan causative); Korean: no morphological causative on truth roots; uses light verbs; Yoruba: mú "make/cause" (Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ "He made me believe"). **Universal gap:** no language shows a productive causative meaning "to cause a proposition to acquire truth value." All causatives mean "to verify," "to realize/implement," or "to confirm"—not "to make true." Basque explicitly blocks the morphological causative on truth roots. |
| **6. Abstract nominalization** | Suffix creates noun denoting quality of being true/honest/correct | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: -oba/-eba (სამართლიანობა "justice"); Yoruba: ì- prefix (ìgbàgbọ́ "belief"); Finnish: -uus/-yys (totuus "truth," rehellisyys "honesty"); Basque: -tasun (zintzotasuna "honesty," egiazkotasuna "truthfulness"); Tamil: -mai/-taṉmai (uṇmaittaṉmai "truthfulness"); Indonesian: ke-...-an (kejujuran "honesty," kebenaran "truth"); Turkish: -luk/-lık (doğruluk "truth/honesty"); Korean: -seong (jinsilseong "truthfulness," sinbingseong "credibility"). Suffixes are entirely language-specific but the functional pattern is universal. All languages produce abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives or verbs. |
| **7. Agentive nominalization: liar** | Suffix or compound creates noun for habitual liar | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: ma-+root+-ar-+-a (მატყუარა "liar"); Yoruba: a- prefix (apurọ́ "liar"); Finnish: no dedicated suffix; uses descriptions or compounds; Basque: -ti suffix (gezurtia "liar"); Tamil: -an masculine suffix (poyyan "liar"); Indonesian: peN- prefix (pembohong "liar"); Turkish: -cı/-ci suffix (yalancı "liar"); Korean: -jaengi suffix (geojinmaljaengi "liar"). All languages have a lexicalized form for habitual liar. Finnish is the weakest case. |
| **7b. Agentive nominalization: truth-teller** | Suffix or compound creates noun for habitual truth-teller | 4–5 languages, weakly | **Asymmetric across all 8 languages.** Yoruba: olótítọ́ "truthful person" (oní- prefix); Basque: egiazale "truth-lover" (-zale suffix, different from liar suffix); Tamil: uṇmaiyāḷar "truthful person" (honorific -āḷar suffix); Korean: no attested form; Georgian: no standard agentive; Finnish: no dedicated agentive; Indonesian: no standard agentive; Turkish: no standard agentive. Where positive agentives exist, they use different morphological strategies than the liar agentive, or are less lexicalized. This asymmetry is the most consistent cross-linguistic finding in the morphological data. |
| **8. Action/process nominalization** | Suffix creates noun for the act of verifying, lying, deceiving, etc. | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: -eba verbal noun (მოტყუება "deception," გამართლება "justification"); Yoruba: ì- prefix (ìpurọ́ "lying," ìgbàgbọ́ "belief"); Finnish: -minen suffix (todistaminen "proving," vääristäminen "distorting"); Basque: -tze/-pen (egiaztatze "verification," egiaztapen "verification result"); Tamil: -ttal/-al (uṇmaippaṭuttal "verification"); Indonesian: peN-...-an (pembenaran "justification," pemalsuan "forgery"); Turkish: -ma/-me (doğrulama "verification"); Korean: compound nominals (jinsilgyumyeong "truth clarification"). All languages produce action nominals from truth-domain verbs, distinct from abstract quality nouns. |
| **9. Light verb: truth-telling and lying** | Truth/falsehood noun + speech verb | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლე თქვა "tell truth" (simart'le + t'qva "say"); Yoruba: sọ òtítọ́ "speak truth" (sọ = say), pa irọ́ "tell lie" (pa = do/make); Finnish: puhua totuutta "speak truth" (puhua = speak), puhua valhetta "speak lie"; Basque: egia esan "tell truth" (esan = say), gezurra esan "tell lie"; Tamil: உண்மை சொல் "tell truth" (col = say), பொய் சொல் "tell lie"; Indonesian: mengatakan kebenaran "say truth," berbohong "lie" (also light verb); Turkish: doğruyu söylemek "tell truth" (söylemek = say), yalan söylemek "tell lie"; Korean: 진실을 말하다 "tell truth" (malhada = say), 거짓말을 하다 "tell lie" (hada = do). **Morphological asymmetry in Turkish:** doğruyu söylemek takes accusative case on the truth noun, while yalan söylemek does not—a lexicalized asymmetry. **Basque gap:** no simple verb *gezurtu "to lie"; the light verb construction is obligatory. |
| **10. Light verb: truth-seeking and discovery** | Truth noun + verb of searching, finding, or discovering | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლე გაარკვია "find out truth" (gaark'via = clarify/find out), სიმართლე იძიოს "search for truth" (iʒios = search); Yoruba: wá òtítọ́ "seek truth" (wá = seek), rí òtítọ́ "find truth" (rí = find/see); Finnish: etsiä totuutta "seek truth" (etsiä = seek), selvittää totuus "find out truth" (selvittää = clarify); Basque: egia bilatu "seek truth" (bilatu = search), egia aurkitu "find truth" (aurkitu = find); Tamil: உண்மையைத் தேடு "search for truth" (tēṭu = search), உண்மை கண்டறி "ascertain truth" (kaṇṭaṟi = discover); Indonesian: mencari kebenaran "seek truth" (mencari = seek); Turkish: gerçeğin peşinde olmak "be in pursuit of truth"; Korean: 진실을 밝히다 "reveal truth" (balkhida = illuminate/reveal). The verb choices differ but the pattern of combining a truth noun with a seeking/finding verb is consistent. |
| **11. Light verb: verification and confirmation** | Truth/fact noun + verb of confirming, verifying, or proving | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლე დაადასტურა "confirmed truth" (daadast'ura = confirmed), დაამოწმა "verified" (daamoc'ma); Yoruba: fẹsẹ̀ múlẹ̀ "confirm/verify" (lit. "place foot on ground"), fi òtítọ́ hàn "show truth" (fi...hàn = show); Finnish: todistaa "prove/testify," todentaa "verify/authenticate"; Basque: egiazkotu "verify," frogatu "prove/test"; Tamil: உண்மையை நிரூபி "prove truth" (nirūpi = prove), உண்மையை உறுதிப்படுத்து "confirm truth"; Indonesian: membenarkan "confirm/verify," memvalidasi "validate"; Turkish: doğrulamak "verify/confirm," kanıtlamak "prove"; Korean: 사실을 확인하다 "verify facts" (hwaginhada = confirm). All languages show this function; the specific verbs differ. |
| **12. Revelation constructions** | Truth noun + verb of revealing, exposing, or coming to light | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლე გამოვლინდა "truth was revealed" (intransitive), სიმართლე გამოაჩინა "revealed truth" (transitive); Yoruba: fi òtítọ́ hàn "show truth," irọ́ jáde "lie comes out" (jáde = come out); Finnish: totuus paljastui "truth was revealed" (paljastua = be revealed), paljastaa totuus "reveal truth"; Basque: egia atera "bring out truth" (atera = take out), egia agertu "truth appeared" (agertu = appear); Tamil: உண்மை வெளிப்படு "truth comes out" (veḷippaṭu = emerge), உண்மையை வெளிப்படுத்து "reveal truth" (causative); Indonesian: ternyata "it turns out" (grammaticalized), mengungkap kebenaran "reveal truth"; Turkish: gerçek ortaya çıkmak "truth comes out" (intransitive), gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak "bring truth to light" (transitive); Korean: 진실이 밝혀지다 "truth is revealed" (passive), 진실이 드러나다 "truth emerges" (intransitive). **All languages show both intransitive (truth reveals itself) and transitive (agent reveals truth) variants.** The intransitive is consistently preferred in narrative contexts. |
| **12b. Concealment constructions** | Truth noun + verb of hiding, covering, or suppressing | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლე დამალა "hid truth" (damala = hid), სიმართლე დაფარა "covered truth" (dap'ara = covered); Yoruba: ṣe àṣírí "keep secret"; Finnish: salata totuus "conceal truth" (salata = conceal); Basque: egia ezkutatu "hide truth" (ezkutatu = hide); Tamil: உண்மையை மறை "hide truth" (maṟai = hide), உண்மையை மூடி மறை "cover up truth" (intensified); Indonesian: menyembunyikan kebenaran "hide truth"; Turkish: gerçeği gizlemek "hide truth" (gizlemek = hide); Korean: 진실을 숨기다 "hide truth" (sumgida = hide). Consistently paired with revelation constructions as an opposition. |
| **13. Adverbial manner modification** | Truth-domain adjective/noun + adverbial suffix or case marker | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: -ad adverbial case (ნამდვილად "really," ზუსტად "precisely"); Yoruba: ní òtítọ́ "in truth" (prepositional phrase), gbàgbọ́ láì rí "believe without seeing" (láì = without); Finnish: -sti suffix (rehellisesti "honestly," todenmukaisesti "truthfully"); Basque: -ki suffix (zintzoki "honestly"), -z instrumental (egiaz "truthfully"); Tamil: -āka adverbializer (உண்மையாக "truly," பொய்யாக "falsely"); Indonesian: se-...-nya (sebenarnya "actually," sejujurnya "honestly"); Turkish: -ca/-ce or -dan ablative (doğruca "directly," gerçekten "really"); Korean: -게/-하게 (정직하게 "honestly," 솔직히 "frankly"). Yoruba is the only language that consistently uses prepositional phrases rather than suffixes for manner adverbials in this domain. |
| **14. Emphatic affirmation and intensification** | Intensifier + truth predicate; or reduplication; or emphatic particle | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: მართლაც რომ "really that" (emphatic), ნამდვილად? "really?" (question); Yoruba: gan-an intensifier (ó tọ́ gan-an "absolutely correct"), gidi gidi "very genuine"; Finnish: benar-benar "really" (id), tosi hyvä "really good" (colloquial intensifier); Basque: egia hutsa "pure truth," gezur-gezurra "complete lie" (reduplication); Tamil: உண்மைதான் "it is indeed true" (tāṉ emphatic particle), நிஜம்தான் "it's really true"; Indonesian: benar-benar "really," benar sekali "very true"; Turkish: kesinlikle "certainly," gerçekten de "really and truly"; Korean: 진짜 진짜 "really really" (reduplication), 틀림없이 "certainly." Finnish uniquely shows grammaticalization of tosi as a colloquial intensifier for non-truth adjectives (tosi hyvä "really good"), a function not found in other languages. |
| **15. Interrogative constructions for truth-checking** | Question particle or intonation + truth predicate | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სიმართლეა? "is it true?" (copular question), სწორია თუ არა "is it correct or not" (disjunctive); Yoruba: ṣé ó tọ́? "is it correct?" (ṣé = question marker), Kí ni òtítọ́? "what is truth?" (content question); Finnish: onko se totta? "is that true?" (onko = question copula); Basque: egia da? "is it true?" (intonation), egia al da? "is it true?" (al = polite question particle); Tamil: உண்மையா? "is it true?" (-ā question suffix), நிஜமா? "really?" (colloquial); Indonesian: apa benar? "is it true?" (apa = question marker), apakah...? (formal); Turkish: doğru mu? "is it correct?" (mu = question particle); Korean: 사실이에요? "is it a fact?" (-이에요? = question copula), 맞아요? "is it right?" All languages use question particles or intonation; the specific markers differ. |
| **16. Conditional constructions** | Conditional marker + truth predicate | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: მართალია რომ "it is true that" (concessive), ვითომ მართალია "supposedly it's true" (doubt marker); Yoruba: bí ó bá tọ́ "if it is correct" (bí...bá = conditional), tí o bá purọ́ "if you lie"; Finnish: jos se on totta "if it is true" (jos = if); Basque: egia bada "if it is true" (ba- = conditional), egia balitz "if it were true" (counterfactual); Tamil: உண்மையானால் "if it is true" (-āṉāl = conditional); Indonesian: kalau benar "if it's true" (kalau = if), jika terbukti "if proven"; Turkish: doğruysa "if it's true" (-sa/-se = conditional), gerçek olsaydı "if it were real" (counterfactual); Korean: 진실이라면 "if it is truth" (-라면 = conditional). Basque uniquely distinguishes realis (bada) from irrealis/counterfactual (balitz) conditional marking on truth predicates. |
| **17. Concessive constructions** | Concessive marker + truth acknowledgment | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: მართალია რომ "it is true that, admittedly" (discourse marker); Yoruba: bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé ó purọ́, mo gbàgbọ́ "even though he lied, I believe" (bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé = even though); Finnish: vaikka se on totta "even though it is true" (vaikka = although); Basque: egia bada ere "even if it is true" (-da ere = even if); Tamil: உண்மை என்றாலும் "even if it is true" (-ālum = even if); Indonesian: meskipun benar "even though correct" (meskipun = although); Turkish: doğru olsa bile "even if it's true" (bile = even); Korean: 진실이라도 "even if it is truth" (-라도 = even if). The concessive function is consistently expressed through general concessive markers applied to truth predicates, not through dedicated truth-domain constructions. |
| **18. Discourse marker function** | Grammaticalized truth form introduces frank or corrective statement | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: მართლაც "really" (emphatic), ნამდვილად "actually"; Yoruba: ní òtítọ́ "in truth," ní òtítọ́, kò ṣẹlẹ̀ bẹ́ẹ̀ "in truth, it didn't happen that way"; Finnish: totta puhuen "to tell the truth" (discourse marker), rehellisesti sanottuna "honestly speaking"; Basque: egia esan "truth be told" (fixed phrase), benetan "really" (confirmation-seeker); Tamil: உண்மையில் "in truth, actually" (very common), உண்மையில் சொல்லப்போனால் "to tell the truth"; Indonesian: sebenarnya "actually," sejujurnya "honestly speaking"; Turkish: doğrusu "to tell the truth," aslında "actually, in fact"; Korean: 사실은 "in fact, actually" (very common), 솔직히 말해서 "frankly speaking." All languages show this grammaticalization. The forms are derived from truth roots but function as discourse organizers rather than truth-evaluators. |
| **19. Comparative and scalar constructions** | Comparative marker + truth predicate; or scalar expressions | All 8, but restricted | **Widely attested for non-core terms; restricted for core truth terms.** Georgian: სიმართლესთან ახლოს "close to the truth" (spatial metaphor), სიმართლესთან შორს "far from the truth"; Yoruba: Òtítọ́ dára jù irọ́ lọ "truth is better than lies" (jù...lọ = comparative); Finnish: todenmukaisempi "more truthful" (-mpi comparative), luotettavampi "more reliable"; Basque: (limited; uses general comparative constructions); Tamil: (limited; uses general comparative constructions); Indonesian: lebih benar "more correct" (lebih = more), paling benar "most correct" (paling = most); Turkish: yalandan daha kötü "worse than a lie" (daha = more); Korean: (limited; truth terms rarely appear in degree comparisons). **Universal restriction:** core truth terms (true/false, real/fake) resist gradable comparison in all languages. Precision and reliability terms (accurate, trustworthy) show more gradability. |
| **20. Correspondence / matching constructions** | Truth noun + verb of corresponding, matching, or fitting | 5 of 8 clearly | **Moderately attested.** Georgian: სიმართლეს შეესაბამება "corresponds to truth" (sheesabameba = corresponds to, dative case obligatory), რეალობას შეესაბამება "corresponds to reality"; Yoruba: ìbámu "agreement, correspondence," bá mu "to correspond/match"; Finnish: pitää paikkansa "holds its place" (fixed idiom for "is true/accurate"); Basque: (expressed through general correspondence constructions); Tamil: (expressed through constructions like "differs from facts"); Indonesian: sesuai dengan "in accordance with" (sesuai = appropriate/conforming); Turkish: gerçeğe uygun "in accordance with reality," gerçeğe aykırı "contrary to reality"; Korean: (expressed through constructions). Georgian shows the most grammaticalized correspondence construction with obligatory dative case marking. Finnish pitää paikkansa is a unique fixed idiom. |
| **21. Disjunctive / binary choice constructions** | Truth term OR falsehood term; or truth term OR NOT | All 8 | **Widely attested.** Georgian: სწორია თუ არა "is it correct or not" (tu ara = or not), ნამდვილი ან ყალბი "real or fake"; Yoruba: Òtítọ́ tàbí irọ́ "truth or falsehood" (tàbí = or), ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ tàbí ọ̀rọ̀ irọ́ "true or false statement"; Finnish: tosi vai epätosi "true or false" (vai = or in questions); Basque: egia ala gezurra "truth or lie" (ala = or), zuzen ala oker "right or wrong"; Tamil: உண்மையா பொய்யா "true or false?" (-ā question suffix on both); Indonesian: benar atau salah "true or false" (atau = or), asli atau palsu "genuine or fake"; Turkish: doğru mu yanlış mı "true or false?" (double question particles), doğru yanlış "true/false" (fixed pair); Korean: 맞고 틀리고 "right and wrong," 진짜인지 가짜인지 "whether real or fake." All languages show binary opposition constructions; the coordinators differ. |

## 3. Narrative Analysis

### Recurring Cross-Language Constructional Patterns

**Predicative attribution through copula or zero copula is universal, but the copular strategy varies.** All eight languages express truth-value attribution through a copular construction, but the realization differs systematically. Languages with agglutinative morphology (Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Korean) tend to use overt copulas with case or agreement marking. Languages with more isolating tendencies (Yoruba, Indonesian, Turkish in informal registers) use zero copula in present tense. Yoruba is unique in maintaining two distinct copular strategies with different pragmatic force: jẹ́ for neutral attribution and ni for emphatic or contrastive attribution. This distinction between neutral and emphatic predication is not grammaticalized in the same way in other languages.

**Light verb constructions for truth-telling and lying are universal and obligatory in some languages.** The pattern of combining a truth or falsehood noun with a speech verb is attested in all eight languages and is the primary means of expressing the speech acts of truth-telling and lying. In Basque, this construction is obligatory: there is no simple verb *gezurtu "to lie," making the light verb construction gezurra esan the only grammatical option. In other languages (Finnish valehdella, Georgian moṭq'ueba, Indonesian berbohong), simple verbal forms exist alongside light verb constructions, but the light verb pattern remains productive and frequent. A morphological asymmetry is documented in Turkish: the truth-telling construction takes accusative case on the truth noun (doğruyu söylemek) while the lying construction does not (yalan söylemek), a lexicalized irregularity not found in other languages.

**Revelation and concealment constructions form a consistent paired opposition across all languages.** Every language shows productive constructions for truth being revealed or hidden, and in all languages both intransitive (truth reveals itself) and transitive (agent reveals truth) variants are attested. The intransitive revelation construction is consistently preferred in narrative contexts, suggesting that truth emergence is conceptualized as a natural process rather than an agentive act. The specific verbs differ substantially: Georgian uses preverb-based constructions (gamovlinda, gamoač'ina), Yoruba uses serial verb constructions (fi...hàn), Finnish uses paljastua/paljastaa, Basque uses atera/ezkutatu, Tamil uses veḷippaṭu/maṟai, Indonesian uses ternyata/mengungkap, Turkish uses ortaya çıkmak/gizlemek, and Korean uses balkhyeojida/sumgida. Despite this lexical diversity, the structural pattern of paired revelation/concealment constructions is consistent.

**Discourse markers grammaticalized from truth roots are universal.** All eight languages show at least one highly frequent discourse marker derived from a truth root that introduces corrections, frank statements, or factual information. The grammaticalization path is consistent: truth root → adverbial form → discourse marker. The specific forms are: Georgian mart'la/namdvilad, Yoruba ní òtítọ́, Finnish totta puhuen/rehellisesti sanottuna, Basque egia esan/benetan, Tamil uṇmaiyil, Indonesian sebenarnya/sejujurnya, Turkish doğrusu/aslında, Korean sasireun. This grammaticalization is one of the most robust cross-linguistic patterns in the data, attested across all language families represented.

**Disjunctive binary choice constructions are universal.** All eight languages show productive constructions presenting truth and falsehood as binary alternatives (true or false, real or fake, correct or not). The coordinators differ (Georgian tu, Yoruba tàbí, Finnish vai, Basque ala, Tamil -ā...-ā, Indonesian atau, Turkish veya/ya da, Korean -인지...-인지), but the structural pattern is consistent. These constructions are used in educational testing, authentication, and moral evaluation across all languages.

**Conditional constructions based on truth-value are universal.** All eight languages show conditional constructions of the form "if it is true, then..." applied to truth predicates. The conditional markers differ (Georgian -a + rom, Yoruba bí...bá, Finnish jos, Basque ba-, Tamil -āṉāl, Indonesian kalau/jika, Turkish -sa/-se, Korean -라면), but the function is consistent. Basque uniquely grammaticalizes a distinction between realis conditional (bada) and irrealis/counterfactual conditional (balitz) on truth predicates, a distinction that other languages express through different means (Turkish -sa vs. -saydı, Finnish jos vs. jos olisi).

### Important Asymmetries, Substitutions, and Restrictions

**The agentive nominalization asymmetry is the most consistent cross-linguistic finding.** In all eight languages, agentive forms for habitual liars are more lexicalized, more productive, or use more dedicated morphology than corresponding forms for habitual truth-tellers. Where positive agentives exist (Yoruba olótítọ́, Basque egiazale, Tamil uṇmaiyāḷar), they use different morphological strategies than the liar agentive, suggesting that the two are not simply mirror images of each other. This asymmetry appears to reflect a universal tendency to mark habitual deviation from expected truthfulness while leaving habitual conformity unmarked.

**The causative gap is universal.** No language shows a productive construction meaning "to cause a proposition to acquire truth value." All causative constructions in the truth domain mean "to verify," "to realize/implement," "to confirm," or "to authenticate"—not "to make true." This gap is explicitly noted in the F6 naturalness data for Georgian, Basque, Finnish, Tamil, and Indonesian, and is consistent with the patterns documented for the remaining languages. The gap appears to reflect a conceptual constraint: truth is treated as something discovered or established, not caused. In Basque, the morphological causative suffix -arazi is explicitly blocked on truth roots, making this restriction morphologically encoded rather than merely pragmatic.

**The inchoative restriction on core truth terms is near-universal.** While Turkish and Finnish show productive morphological inchoatives on truth roots (gerçekleşmek, toteutua), most languages treat truth as a categorical state that is revealed or established rather than gradually acquired. The F6 naturalness data for Finnish explicitly notes that expressions for propositions "becoming true" as state changes are unnatural; Finnish prefers revelation/verification constructions. Georgian, Basque, Tamil, and Korean show similar restrictions. The inchoative is most natural for plans, dreams, and predictions "coming true" (a different semantic domain), not for propositions changing truth-value.

**The degree modification restriction on core truth terms is universal.** All eight languages treat core truth terms as categorical rather than gradable. Expressions like "more true," "very true," or "half true" are either unattested, marked as marginal, or expressed through constructions rather than through productive morphological gradation. Precision terms (Georgian zusti, Finnish tarkka, Indonesian akurat) and reliability terms (Georgian sando, Finnish luotettava) show more gradability, but core truth terms (true/false, real/fake, honest/dishonest) resist degree modification across all languages. Indonesian is the only language that shows a productive hedged negation strategy (kurang benar "not quite correct") as a face-saving device, but even this is not a true gradable comparison.

**The saturation asymmetry parallels the agentive asymmetry.** Several languages show productive expressions for "full of lies" or "riddled with falsehood" (Korean geojittuseongi, Indonesian penuh bohong, Georgian ṭq'uilubralo) but no corresponding expressions for "full of truth." Similarly, privative constructions for "without lies" (Georgian uṭq'uari, Korean geojiseobsi) are more productive than constructions for "without truth." These asymmetries are consistent with the broader pattern: deceptive behavior is marked as an excess or absence of something expected, while truthful behavior is the unmarked default.

### Cases Where One Strategy Replaces Another

**Light verb constructions replace morphological verbing in some languages.** In Basque and Korean, light verb constructions are the primary or obligatory strategy for expressing truth-related speech acts, while morphological verbing from truth roots is restricted or absent. Basque lacks *gezurtu "to lie" and uses gezurra esan instead; Korean lacks a simple morphological verb for "to lie" and uses geojinmareul hada. In contrast, Finnish valehdella "to lie" and Georgian moṭq'ueba "deception" show productive morphological verbing. This substitution of light verb constructions for morphological verbing appears to be a language-specific feature rather than a universal pattern.

**Constructional revelation replaces morphological inchoative in most languages.** Where Turkish and Finnish use morphological inchoatives (gerçekleşmek, toteutua), most other languages use revelation constructions (truth comes to light, truth is revealed) to express the same conceptual content. Georgian gamovlinda "was revealed," Tamil uṇmai veḷippaṭu "truth comes out," and Korean jinsiri balkhyeojida "truth is revealed" all express the emergence of truth without using an inchoative construction on the truth term itself. This substitution is consistent with the broader restriction on inchoative derivation from core truth terms.

**Syntactic negation replaces morphological privation in several languages.** While Georgian (u-), Finnish (epä-), Turkish (-sız), and Yoruba (àì-) use productive morphological privatives on truth-domain adjectives, Indonesian, Tamil, and Korean rely primarily on syntactic negation (tidak, illai, 아니다) rather than morphological privation for truth-domain antonyms. This substitution does not affect the functional output—all languages can express the negation of truth-domain predicates—but the morphological strategy differs.

**Spatial metaphors replace scalar expressions for epistemic distance.** Georgian uniquely uses spatial postpositional constructions to express epistemic distance from truth (სიმართლესთან ახლოს "close to the truth," სიმართლესთან შორს "far from the truth"). This spatial metaphor strategy replaces the scalar degree modification that is restricted on core truth terms. Other languages express similar content through hedging constructions (Indonesian kurang benar "not quite correct"), evidential markers (Georgian vitom "supposedly"), or plausibility adjectives (Finnish uskottava "believable"), but the Georgian spatial metaphor is the most grammaticalized realization of this function.

### Cases Where a Function Is Weakly Expressed, Constructionally Indirect, or Absent

**Performative oath constructions are weakly attested in most languages.** While all languages can express oaths to tell the truth, only Tamil (sattiyam as a dedicated term for solemn truth/oath), Georgian (სიმართლეზე დაფიცება), and Korean (formal legal formula) show clearly grammaticalized oath constructions in the truth domain. Other languages express this function through general oath constructions applied to truth predicates, without dedicated truth-domain vocabulary.

**Correspondence constructions are moderately attested but not universal.** The function of expressing that a statement corresponds to or matches reality is clearly grammaticalized in Georgian (obligatory dative case in შეესაბამება constructions) and Finnish (fixed idiom pitää paikkansa "holds its place"). Other languages express this function through general correspondence vocabulary (Indonesian sesuai dengan, Turkish gerçeğe uygun) or through constructions that are not specifically grammaticalized for truth-domain correspondence. Yoruba ìbámu "agreement, correspondence" covers this function but is not specifically grammaticalized for truth-domain use.

**Reflexive self-deception constructions are weakly attested morphologically.** Georgian shows the most lexicalized morphological form (თვითმოტყუება "self-deception" with tviṭ- reflexive prefix). Other languages express self-deception through syntactic constructions with reflexive pronouns (Indonesian menipu diri sendiri, Turkish kendini aldatmak) rather than through dedicated morphological forms. This function is present in all languages but is only morphologically grammaticalized in Georgian.

**Consistency/coherence constructions are weakly attested in several languages.** Turkish (tutarlı/tutarsız) and Finnish (johdonmukainen) show the most dedicated vocabulary for logical consistency or coherence of arguments and behavior. This function is expressed less clearly in Yoruba (ìbámu covers correspondence but not specifically logical consistency), Basque, Tamil, and Korean. The function appears to be present in all languages but is not universally elaborated as a distinct constructional domain.

**The plausibility/seeming-true function is constructionally indirect in some languages.** Finnish (uskottava "believable"), Basque (sinesgarria "believable," itxuraz "apparently"), and Korean (그럴듯하다 "plausible") show the most dedicated vocabulary for the function of expressing surface plausibility. Yoruba and Tamil express this function primarily through constructions (Yoruba: ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó jẹ́ òtítọ́ "it is possible that it is true"; Tamil: உண்மை போல் "like truth") rather than through dedicated lexical items. This suggests that plausibility as a distinct epistemic category is present in all languages but is not universally lexicalized as a separate constructional domain.
