# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Methodological note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register distribution, and collocational behavior documented in P1, P1b, and P2. No external conceptual framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns support multiple group memberships, this is noted explicitly. Items for which no clear grouping is supported by the data are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that a claim or state of affairs corresponds to reality

**Label:** Asserting that something is the case

**Definition:** These expressions are used — in everyday speech, journalism, and formal writing — to assert that a proposition, account, or state of affairs is factually accurate. They appear in predicate constructions (*olla totta*, *pitää paikkansa*), as discourse markers (*tosiasia on, että*; *totuus on, että*), and as modifiers of claims (*todenperäinen*, *tosipohjainen*). They are regularly contrasted with expressions of falsity or appearance. The partitive construction *olla totta* and the idiomatic *pitää paikkansa* are near-synonyms in this function, with *pitää paikkansa* more common in evaluative assessment of specific claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- *olla totta* — to be true; to be the case
- *pitää paikkansa* — to hold; to be correct (idiomatic near-synonym of *olla totta*)
- *ei pidä paikkaansa* — is not true; does not hold
- *tosiasia on, että* — the fact is that (discourse marker)
- *totuus on, että* — the truth is that (discourse marker, slightly more rhetorical)
- *tosi* — true; real (base adjective/intensifier)
- *totuus* — truth (abstract noun)
- *todenperäinen* — factual; based on truth; true (of accounts)
- *todenperäisyys* — factual basis; truth of an account
- *tosiseikka* — fact; factual circumstance (legal and formal)
- *tosiasia* — fact (embedded in *tosiasia on, että*)
- *tosiasiallinen* — factual; actual; de facto
- *toden totta* — truly; really; in all truth (emphatic fixed phrase)
- *totta puhuen* — to tell the truth; frankly (adverbial phrase)
- *tosiaan* — really; indeed; actually (colloquial affirmative)
- *totta kai* — of course; certainly (colloquial affirmative)
- *puhua totta* — to tell the truth
- *sanoa totta* — to say something true
- *tosipohjainen* — fact-based; grounded in fact
- *todenmukainen* — true-to-life; realistic; accurate (of representations)
- *todenmukaisuus* — verisimilitude; true-to-lifeness

**Notes:** *Todenmukainen* and *todenmukaisuus* shade into Group 4 (representations evaluated for accuracy), since their primary context is aesthetic and journalistic evaluation of how faithfully something represents reality. *Tosiaan* and *totta kai* are primarily affirmative particles in spoken Finnish and are only loosely connected to the epistemic core of this group.

## Group 2: Expressions used in the production, evaluation, and weighing of evidence and proof

**Label:** Evidence, proof, and their procedural handling

**Definition:** These expressions are concentrated in legal, investigative, and formal procedural contexts. They name the acts of proving and testifying (*todistaa*, *todentaa*), the products of those acts (*todistus*, *todistusaineisto*, *väärennös*), the persons involved (*todistaja*, *tarkastaja*), and the procedural concepts governing how evidence is handled (*todistustaakka*, *todistusharkinta*, *todistusstandardi*, *vapaa todistusharkinta*). Many of these forms are compounds built on *todistus* as a first or second element. The cluster is internally differentiated: some items name physical objects or documents (*todistuskappale*, *syntymätodistus*), others name procedural principles (*todistustaakka*, *vapaa todistusharkinta*), and others name evaluative acts (*todistusharkinta*, *todistusvoima*).

**Representative expressions:**
- *todistaa* — to prove; to testify; to certify
- *todistaa oikeudessa* — to testify in court
- *todistaa valan nojalla* — to testify under oath
- *todistaa syyllisyys* — to prove guilt
- *todistaa syyttömyys* — to prove innocence
- *todistaa oikeaksi* — to certify as correct
- *todistaa vääräksi* — to prove false; to disprove
- *todistaa aidoksi* — to certify as genuine
- *todistaa henkilöllisyys* — to prove identity
- *todistus* — proof; testimony; certificate
- *todistaja* — witness
- *todistajanlausunto* — witness statement
- *todistusaineisto* — body of evidence
- *todistusvoima* — probative force; evidentiary weight
- *todistustaakka* — burden of proof
- *todistuskeino* — means of proof
- *todistuskappale* — exhibit; piece of evidence
- *todistusketju* — chain of evidence
- *todistusharkinta* — evaluation of evidence
- *todistusstandardi* — standard of proof
- *vapaa todistusharkinta* — free evaluation of evidence (procedural principle)
- *todistamaton* — unproved; unverified
- *todistettava* — provable; demonstrable
- *todistetusti* — demonstrably; as has been proved
- *todistettavasti* — demonstrably; verifiably (modal nuance)
- *todentaa* — to verify; to authenticate
- *todentaminen* — verification; authentication
- *syntymätodistus* — birth certificate
- *kuolemantodistus* — death certificate
- *lääkärintodistus* — medical certificate
- *koulutodistus* — school certificate
- *työtodistus* — employment certificate
- *aitoustodistus* — certificate of authenticity
- *todistus syyllisyydestä* — proof of guilt

**Notes:** The certificate compounds (*syntymätodistus*, *lääkärintodistus*, etc.) are administrative rather than strictly evidentiary, but they share the same base and the same function of certifying a fact. *Todentaa* and *todentaminen* overlap with Group 5 (verification and checking) in IT and administrative contexts. *Todistaa oikeaksi* and *todistaa aidoksi* also connect to Group 3 (authenticity evaluation).

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate whether something is genuine, original, or what it claims to be

**Label:** Evaluating genuineness and authenticity

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question at issue is whether an object, document, emotion, or person is what it presents itself as being — genuine rather than imitated, original rather than copied, authentic rather than performed. They appear in art authentication, consumer protection, legal proceedings, and everyday character evaluation. The central contrast is between *aito*/*alkuperäinen* (genuine/original) and *jäljitelmä*/*väärennös*/*epäaito* (imitation/forgery/inauthentic). The translative constructions *osoittautua aidoksi*, *osoittautua väärennökseksi*, and *todistaa aidoksi* are characteristic of this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *aito* — genuine; authentic; real
- *aitous* — authenticity; genuineness
- *aitoustodistus* — certificate of authenticity
- *aito ja alkuperäinen* — genuine and original (fixed binomial)
- *epäaito* — inauthentic; fake; spurious
- *epäaitous* — inauthenticity
- *alkuperäinen* — original; primary; authentic
- *alkuperäisyys* — originality; authenticity of provenance
- *alkuperäinen asiakirja* — the original document
- *alkuperäinen teos* — the original work
- *jäljitelmä* — imitation; replica; copy (with implication of lesser status)
- *halpa jäljitelmä* — cheap imitation
- *pelkkä jäljitelmä* — mere imitation
- *väärennös* — forgery; fake; counterfeit
- *paljastaa väärennös* — to expose a forgery
- *tunnistaa väärennös* — to identify a forgery
- *osoittautua väärennökseksi* — to turn out to be a forgery
- *osoittautua aidoksi* — to turn out to be genuine
- *todistaa aidoksi* — to certify as genuine
- *kyseenalaistaa aitous* — to call into question the authenticity
- *ei ole aito* — is not genuine; is fake
- *aito tunne / aito ilo* — genuine feeling / genuine joy (applied to emotions)

**Notes:** *Kopio* (neutral copy) sits at the edge of this group: it does not inherently imply inauthenticity or fraud, unlike *jäljitelmä* or *väärennös*, and is therefore listed separately below under ambiguous items. *Väärennös* also belongs to Group 6 (forgery and fraud) given its legal dimension. *Aito suomalainen* (a genuine Finn) extends the authenticity evaluation to persons and identity, which is a distinct usage pattern.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate whether a statement, account, or representation conforms to a standard or norm

**Label:** Conformity to a standard, norm, or rule

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether something — a statement, a procedure, a text, a decision — conforms to an applicable standard, norm, or rule. They are concentrated in administrative, legal, linguistic, and procedural registers. The key distinction from Group 1 is that these expressions do not primarily assert correspondence to reality but rather conformity to a rule or norm: *oikeellisuus* concerns conformity to a linguistic or procedural standard; *totuudenmukaisuus* concerns conformity to the truth as a standard for statements; *oikeakielisyys* concerns conformity to prescriptive language norms. The contrast between *oikeellisuus* (rule-conformity) and *totuus* (correspondence to fact) is explicitly noted in P1.

**Representative expressions:**
- *oikea* — right; correct; proper (covers both correctness and genuineness)
- *oikeellisuus* — correctness; accuracy; propriety (conformity to a rule)
- *oikein* — correctly; rightly (adverb)
- *tietojen oikeellisuus* — accuracy of information (administrative collocation)
- *tarkistaa oikeellisuus* — to check correctness
- *totuudenmukaisuus* — truthfulness; conformity to truth (of statements and accounts)
- *totuudenmukainen* — truthful; conforming to truth
- *totuudenvastaisuus* — untruthfulness; contrariness to truth
- *totuudenvastainen* — contrary to truth; false (of statements)
- *oikeakielisyys* — linguistic correctness; prescriptive correctness
- *oikeakielinen* — linguistically correct
- *oikeaoppinen* — orthodox; doctrinally correct
- *oikeaoppisuus* — orthodoxy; doctrinal correctness
- *vääräoppinen* — heretical; heterodox
- *vääräoppisuus* — heresy; heterodoxy
- *perusteeton* — unfounded; groundless; without basis
- *tarkka* — precise; exact; accurate; careful
- *tarkkuus* — accuracy; precision
- *tarkasti ottaen* — strictly speaking; to be precise

**Notes:** *Oikea* is highly polysemous and participates in Groups 1, 3, 4, and 5. *Totuudenmukaisuus* bridges Groups 1 and 4: it concerns both truth (Group 1) and conformity to truth as a standard (Group 4). *Oikeaoppinen* and *vääräoppinen* are specifically about doctrinal conformity and are somewhat peripheral to the core of this group, which is more administrative and procedural.

## Group 5: Expressions used for checking, verifying, and inspecting

**Label:** Checking and verification acts

**Definition:** These expressions describe acts of checking, verifying, inspecting, or monitoring — whether of documents, data, persons, or situations. They are distributed across administrative, technical, security, and scientific registers. The distinction between *tarkistaa* (to check/verify, everyday) and *tarkastaa* (to inspect/audit, formal/official) is register-based and semantically significant: *tarkastaa* implies an authoritative or institutional inspection, while *tarkistaa* is used for routine checking. *Todentaa* (to verify/authenticate) is more technical and is increasingly associated with IT security contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tarkistaa* — to check; to verify; to inspect; to review (everyday)
- *tarkistus* — check; verification; inspection (everyday)
- *tarkastaa* — to inspect; to audit; to examine (formal/official)
- *tarkastus* — inspection; audit; examination (formal/official)
- *tarkastaja* — inspector; auditor; examiner
- *tarkkailla* — to observe; to monitor; to watch carefully
- *tarkkailu* — observation; monitoring; surveillance
- *tarkkailija* — observer; monitor
- *todentaa* — to verify; to authenticate; to confirm as real
- *todentaminen* — verification; authentication
- *tarkistaa oikeellisuus* — to check correctness
- *tarkistaa tiedot* — to verify the information
- *tarkka silmä* — a sharp eye; an eye for detail
- *tarkkaavainen* — attentive; observant; alert
- *tarkkaavaisuus* — attentiveness; attention
- *mittaustarkkuus* — measurement accuracy

**Notes:** *Todentaa* and *todentaminen* also belong to Group 2 (evidence and proof) in legal and administrative contexts. *Tarkkailu* shades into surveillance contexts that are not strictly about truth or accuracy. *Tarkkaavainen* and *tarkkaavaisuus* are more about attentiveness as a personal quality than about verification acts.

## Group 6: Expressions used in contexts of deliberate falsification and fraud

**Label:** Deliberate falsification and fraudulent conduct

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the issue is not mere error or inaccuracy but deliberate deception — forging documents, counterfeiting currency, committing fraud, or acting in bad faith. They are concentrated in legal and criminal law registers. The key distinction from Group 7 (distortion and misrepresentation) is that these expressions typically imply intent and a specific fraudulent product or act: *väärentää* produces a *väärennös*; *vilppi* and *vilpillinen* name bad faith conduct. *Väärentäjä* names the agent. These expressions are standard in Finnish criminal law vocabulary.

**Representative expressions:**
- *väärentää* — to forge; to falsify; to counterfeit
- *väärentäminen* — forgery; falsification; counterfeiting
- *väärennös* — forgery; fake; counterfeit (the product)
- *väärentäjä* — forger; counterfeiter
- *asiakirjan väärentäminen* — document forgery (named criminal offense)
- *rahan väärentäminen* — counterfeiting of currency (named criminal offense)
- *väärentää asiakirja* — to forge a document
- *väärentää allekirjoitus* — to forge a signature
- *väärentää rahaa* — to counterfeit money
- *vilppi* — deceit; fraud; bad faith; cheating
- *vilpillinen* — dishonest; fraudulent; deceitful; in bad faith
- *vilpillinen menettely* — fraudulent conduct (fixed legal phrase)
- *akateeminen vilppi* — academic fraud/misconduct (fixed expression)
- *väärä nimi* — a false name; an alias
- *väärä seteli* — a counterfeit banknote
- *väärä todistus* — false testimony; perjury

**Notes:** *Väärennös* also belongs to Group 3 (authenticity evaluation). *Väärä todistus* connects to Group 2 (evidence and proof). *Vilppi* and *vilpillinen* are the most general terms for bad-faith conduct and are not limited to document forgery; they cover academic misconduct, commercial fraud, and general dishonesty.

## Group 7: Expressions used for distortion and misrepresentation (not necessarily involving forgery)

**Label:** Distortion and misrepresentation of facts or reality

**Definition:** These expressions describe the act of presenting something in a way that departs from reality or accuracy, but without the specific implication of producing a counterfeit object that characterizes Group 6. *Vääristellä* and *vääristely* are used for systematic or repeated distortion of facts, history, or data; *vääristää* and *vääristymä* are used for distortion of competition, images, or statistical data. These expressions are common in journalistic, political, and analytical discourse. The contrast with Group 6 is that intent is not always implied and no specific fraudulent product need result.

**Representative expressions:**
- *vääristellä* — to distort; to misrepresent; to twist (facts, history)
- *vääristely* — distortion; misrepresentation (of facts, history)
- *vääristää* — to distort; to warp; to skew (competition, images, data)
- *vääristymä* — distortion; skew; bias (in data, markets)
- *vääristellä tosiasioita* — to distort the facts
- *vääristellä historiaa* — to distort history
- *vääristää kilpailua* — to distort competition
- *tilastollinen vääristymä* — a statistical distortion/bias
- *väärentää historiaa* — to falsify history (overlaps with Group 6 when intent is implied)
- *näennäinen* — apparent; seeming; ostensible (flags gap between appearance and reality)
- *näennäisyys* — apparentness; superficiality
- *näennäisesti noudattaa* — to comply in appearance only
- *näennäinen demokratia* — sham democracy
- *näennäinen ratkaisu* — apparent solution; pseudo-solution

**Notes:** *Näennäinen* and its derivatives describe a gap between appearance and reality without necessarily implying active deception; they are used in analytical and critical discourse to flag that something is superficial or merely apparent. They are placed here rather than in Group 6 because they do not imply deliberate fraud. *Väärentää historiaa* can belong to either Group 6 or Group 7 depending on whether deliberate intent is foregrounded.

## Group 8: Expressions used to evaluate persons or conduct as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Evaluating honesty and dishonesty in persons and conduct

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate persons, statements, or conduct as characterized by honesty or its absence. They are used in moral, professional, and everyday evaluative contexts. The central contrast is between *rehellinen*/*rehellisyys* (honest/honesty) and *vilpillinen*/*vilppi* (dishonest/fraud). *Rehellinen* is applied to persons, answers, and conduct; *vilpillinen* is more formal and legal. These expressions are distinct from the truth-assertion group (Group 1) in that they evaluate the character or conduct of an agent rather than the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**
- *rehellinen* — honest; straightforward; upright; sincere
- *rehellisyys* — honesty; integrity; straightforwardness
- *rehellinen vastaus* — an honest answer
- *rehellinen ihminen* — an honest person
- *vilpillinen* — dishonest; fraudulent; deceitful; in bad faith
- *vilppi* — deceit; fraud; dishonesty; bad faith
- *vilpillinen menettely* — fraudulent conduct
- *totuudellinen* — truthful; veracious; truth-oriented (literary/religious register)
- *totuudellisuus* — truthfulness; veracity (literary/religious register)

**Notes:** *Vilpillinen* and *vilppi* also belong to Group 6 (deliberate falsification and fraud) given their legal dimension. *Totuudellinen* and *totuudellisuus* are marginal items that appear in moral and religious discourse; they evaluate a person's orientation toward truth rather than the truth-value of specific claims.

## Group 9: Expressions concentrated in legal rights, justice, and the institutional apparatus of law

**Label:** Legal rights, justice, and the institutional legal system

**Definition:** These expressions are concentrated in constitutional, procedural, and institutional legal language. They name legal rights (*oikeus*, *oikeusturva*), the institutions of justice (*oikeuslaitos*, *oikeusistuin*, *oikeudenkäynti*), principles of legal order (*oikeusvaltio*, *oikeusvarmuus*, *oikeusvaltion periaate*), and the academic study of law (*oikeustiede*, *oikeusfilosofia*, *oikeushistoria*). The shared base *oikeus* connects them morphologically, but their usage is institutional and procedural rather than epistemic. They are grouped together because they co-occur in legal and constitutional discourse and are built on the same derivational base.

**Representative expressions:**
- *oikeus* — right; justice; court; law
- *oikeuslaitos* — the judiciary; the court system
- *oikeusistuin* — court; tribunal
- *oikeudenkäynti* — trial; court proceedings
- *oikeudellinen* — legal; judicial; juridical
- *oikeudellisesti* — legally; in legal terms
- *oikeusturva* — legal protection; due process
- *oikeusvaltio* — rule-of-law state; Rechtsstaat
- *oikeusvaltion periaate* — the rule of law principle
- *oikeusvarmuus* — legal certainty; legal security
- *oikeusperiaate* — legal principle
- *oikeushenkilö* — legal person; legal entity
- *oikeustoimikelpoinen* — legally competent; having legal capacity
- *oikeusvoima* — legal force; res judicata
- *oikeussuoja* — legal protection; legal remedy
- *oikeusaste* — court instance; level of jurisdiction
- *oikeusministeriö* — Ministry of Justice
- *oikeusministeri* — Minister of Justice
- *oikeusapu* — legal aid
- *oikeusavustaja* — legal counsel; attorney
- *oikeuskansleri* — Chancellor of Justice
- *oikeusasiamies* — Parliamentary Ombudsman
- *oikeustiede* — jurisprudence; legal science
- *oikeusoppi* — legal doctrine; jurisprudence
- *oikeuslähteet* — sources of law
- *oikeusohje* — legal rule; legal norm
- *oikeussubjekti* — legal subject
- *oikeusperusta* — legal basis; legal foundation
- *oikeustila* — legal situation; state of the law
- *oikeusvertailu* — comparative law
- *oikeushistoria* — legal history
- *oikeusfilosofia* — philosophy of law
- *oikeussosiologia* — sociology of law
- *oikeuspsykologia* — forensic psychology; legal psychology
- *oikeustaloustiede* — law and economics
- *oikeudenkäymiskaari* — Code of Judicial Procedure
- *oikeudeton* — without rights; rightless
- *oikeuttaa* — to justify; to entitle; to authorize
- *oikeuttaminen* — justification; authorization

**Notes:** This group is the largest and most internally differentiated. It is held together by the shared base *oikeus* and by co-occurrence in legal and constitutional discourse, not by a single semantic function. *Oikeudenmukaisuus* and *oikeudenmukainen* (justice/fairness) are placed in Group 10 rather than here because their primary usage is moral and evaluative rather than institutional.

## Group 10: Expressions used to evaluate conduct, decisions, or outcomes as just or unjust

**Label:** Evaluating fairness and justice in conduct and outcomes

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate whether conduct, decisions, or outcomes conform to principles of fairness and right. They are used in moral, political, and legal discourse, but their primary function is evaluative rather than institutional. The contrast between *oikeudenmukainen* (just/fair) and *epäoikeudenmukainen* (unjust/unfair) is fundamental. *Oikeudenmukaisuus* is a central term in moral and political philosophy in Finnish. These expressions are distinct from Group 9 in that they evaluate substantive fairness rather than naming legal institutions or procedures.

**Representative expressions:**
- *oikeudenmukaisuus* — justice; fairness; equity
- *oikeudenmukainen* — just; fair; equitable
- *oikeudenmukaisesti* — justly; fairly; equitably
- *epäoikeudenmukainen* — unjust; unfair; inequitable
- *epäoikeudenmukaisuus* — injustice; unfairness; inequity
- *vääryys* — wrongness; injustice; wrong (a specific instance of injustice)
- *oikeamielinen* — right-minded; fair-minded; upright (marginal)
- *oikeamielisyys* — right-mindedness; fair-mindedness (marginal)

**Notes:** *Vääryys* connects this group to the *väärä* root cluster (Groups 6 and 7) but is used specifically in moral contexts to name an injustice or wrong done to someone, rather than a factual error or a forgery. *Oikeamielinen* and *oikeamielisyys* are marginal items noted as literary in P2a.

---

## Group 11: Expressions used in technical and philosophical discourse about truth as a formal or logical property

**Label:** Truth as a formal or logical property

**Definition:** These expressions are concentrated in academic philosophy, formal logic, and formal semantics. They treat truth not as a property of persons or conduct but as a formal property of propositions, sentences, or logical compounds. They are used in university-level instruction and academic writing and do not appear in everyday speech. The cluster is internally coherent: *totuusarvo*, *totuustaulu*, *totuusehdot*, *totuusehdollinen*, *totuusfunktionaalinen* form a tight technical vocabulary in logic and formal semantics.

**Representative expressions:**
- *totuusarvo* — truth value
- *totuustaulu* — truth table
- *totuusehdot* — truth conditions
- *totuusehdollinen* — truth-conditional
- *totuusfunktionaalinen* — truth-functional
- *totuusteoria* — theory of truth

**Notes:** This group is small and register-restricted. It does not overlap significantly with other groups. *Totuusteoria* is slightly broader, as theories of truth are discussed in both formal logic and general philosophy.

---

## Group 12: Expressions used to evaluate reliability and credibility of sources, persons, and claims

**Label:** Evaluating reliability and credibility

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a source, person, instrument, or claim can be trusted or believed. They appear in legal, journalistic, scientific, and everyday evaluative contexts. The distinction between *luotettava* (reliable/trustworthy — emphasizing dependability) and *uskottava* (credible/believable — emphasizing believability) is consistently maintained in usage: a *luotettava lähde* is one that can be depended upon to report accurately; an *uskottava selitys* is one that can reasonably be believed. Both have corresponding abstract nouns (*luotettavuus*, *uskottavuus*) and *epä-* negations (*epäluotettava*, *epäuskottava*).

**Representative expressions:**
- *luotettava* — reliable; trustworthy; dependable
- *luotettavuus* — reliability; trustworthiness
- *luotettavasti* — reliably; dependably
- *epäluotettava* — unreliable; untrustworthy
- *epäluotettavuus* — unreliability
- *luotettava lähde* — a reliable source
- *luotettava todistaja* — a reliable witness
- *luotettava kumppani* — a reliable partner
- *pitää luotettavana* — to consider reliable
- *osoittautua luotettavaksi* — to prove reliable
- *luotettavuuden arviointi* — assessment of reliability
- *heikentää luotettavuutta* — to undermine reliability
- *uskottava* — credible; believable; plausible; convincing
- *uskottavuus* — credibility; plausibility; believability
- *uskottavasti* — credibly; convincingly
- *epäuskottava* — incredible; implausible; unconvincing
- *epäuskottavuus* — implausibility; lack of credibility
- *uskottava selitys* — a credible explanation
- *uskottava uhka* — a credible threat
- *uskottava pelote* — credible deterrent
- *vaikuttaa uskottavalta* — to seem credible
- *pitää uskottavana* — to consider credible
- *horjuttaa uskottavuutta* — to undermine credibility
- *menettää uskottavuus* — to lose credibility
- *uskottavuuden arviointi* — assessment of credibility
- *nauttia uskottavuutta* — to command credibility

**Notes:** *Luotettavuus* and *uskottavuus* are explicitly distinguished in P1 (items 10 and 12). In legal contexts, both are evaluated for witnesses: *luotettavuus* concerns whether the witness is dependable and consistent; *uskottavuus* concerns whether the witness's account is believable. This distinction is maintained in Finnish legal proceedings.

## Group 13: Expressions used in contexts of misunderstanding and incorrect interpretation

**Label:** Misunderstanding and incorrect interpretation

**Definition:** These expressions describe situations where something has been understood, interpreted, or used incorrectly — without necessarily implying deliberate deception. They are built on the adverb *väärin* (wrongly) combined with verbs of understanding, interpretation, and use. They are common in everyday communication, legal interpretation, and administrative contexts. The absence of deliberate intent distinguishes them from Group 6 (deliberate falsification).

**Representative expressions:**
- *väärinkäsitys* — misunderstanding; misconception
- *väärinymmärrys* — misunderstanding; misapprehension
- *väärinkäyttö* — misuse; abuse; misappropriation
- *ymmärtää väärin* — to misunderstand
- *tulkita väärin* — to misinterpret; to interpret incorrectly
- *väärin* — wrongly; incorrectly (adverb)

**Notes:** *Väärinkäyttö* (misuse/abuse) can involve deliberate intent (e.g., *vallan väärinkäyttö* — abuse of power) and therefore has some overlap with Group 6. However, it is placed here because the primary semantic focus is on incorrect use rather than on producing a fraudulent object.

---

## Group 14: Expressions used to contrast real or actual situations with apparent, fictional, or theoretical ones

**Label:** Contrasting the real with the apparent, fictional, or theoretical

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something belongs to actual reality as opposed to fiction, theory, appearance, or idealized scenarios. They appear in everyday speech, journalism, and philosophical discourse. The cluster includes compounds of *tosi-* with nouns (*tosimaailma*, *tosielämä*, *tositapahtuma*, *tositarina*) and the contrast-marking uses of *oikea* (*oikea elämä*, *oikeasti*). The expression *perustuu tositapahtumiin* is a genre marker in film and literary contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tosimaailma* — the real world; the actual world
- *tosielämä* — real life; actual life
- *tositapahtuma* — real event; actual occurrence
- *tositarina* — true story; real story
- *perustuu tositapahtumiin* — based on true events (genre marker)
- *tosiTV* — reality TV (marginal)
- *oikea elämä* — real life; genuine life
- *oikeasti* — really; genuinely; actually (colloquial)
- *todenperäinen tarina* — a true story; a story based on real events
- *tosimaailmassa asiat ovat toisin* — in the real world, things are different (fixed phrase pattern)

**Notes:** *Tosiaan* (really; indeed) is a colloquial affirmative that can function in this contrast-marking role but is primarily an affirmative particle (see Group 1). *TosiTV* is marginal and primarily a media genre label.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items show clear membership in multiple groups and cannot be assigned to a single group without distortion:

- ***oikea*** — participates in Group 1 (asserting truth: *olla oikeassa*), Group 3 (genuineness: *se oikea*), Group 4 (correctness to a standard: *oikea vastaus*), Group 5 (verification: *todistaa oikeaksi*), and Group 14 (real vs. apparent: *oikea elämä*). This polysemy is a documented feature of the item.

- ***väärä*** — participates in Group 1 (falsity of propositions: *olla väärässä*, *todistaa vääräksi*), Group 4 (incorrectness to a standard: *väärä vastaus*), Group 6 (fraud: *väärä seteli*, *väärä nimi*), and Group 13 (misunderstanding: *ymmärtää väärin*). The adverb *väärin* is particularly productive across Groups 4, 6, and 13.

- ***väärennös*** — belongs to both Group 3 (authenticity evaluation: *osoittautua väärennökseksi*) and Group 6 (deliberate falsification: *asiakirjan väärentäminen*).

- ***todentaa* / *todentaminen*** — belongs to both Group 2 (evidence and proof, in legal and administrative contexts) and Group 5 (checking and verification, in IT and technical contexts).

- ***vilppi* / *vilpillinen*** — belongs to both Group 6 (deliberate falsification and fraud, in legal contexts) and Group 8 (evaluating honesty and dishonesty, in moral and professional contexts).

- ***totuudenmukaisuus* / *totuudenmukainen*** — belongs to both Group 1 (asserting correspondence to reality) and Group 4 (conformity to truth as a standard for statements and accounts).

- ***perusteeton*** — evaluated in P1 as belonging to the truth/falsity domain (unfounded claims), but its primary function is to evaluate whether a claim or accusation has adequate grounds. It sits between Group 1 (truth assertions) and Group 4 (conformity to a standard of justification). It is not cleanly assigned to either.

## Items for which no clear grouping is supported by the data

- ***kopio*** — A neutral term for a reproduction. It does not inherently imply inauthenticity (unlike *jäljitelmä* or *väärennös*) and does not primarily function in truth-assertion, verification, or fraud contexts. Its collocations (*virallinen kopio*, *ottaa kopio*, *varmuuskopio*) are administrative and practical. It contrasts with *alkuperäinen* but does not evaluate genuineness in the way *aito* does. No grouping from the above fits it cleanly.

- ***totuuskomissio*** — Used specifically in political and historical contexts for truth and reconciliation bodies. It participates in the *totuus* root cluster but its usage is too domain-specific (transitional justice) to fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

- ***totuuspuhe*** — Used in philosophical and political discourse, including in Foucauldian contexts. It is a specialized term that does not fit the everyday or legal patterns of the other groups.

- ***totuusseetti*** — Marginal item (P2a: [MARGINAL]). Used in popular and media contexts. No clear grouping.

- ***totuudellinen* / *totuudellisuus*** — Marginal items (P2a: [MARGINAL]). Used in moral and religious discourse. Placed tentatively in Group 8 but their register is distinct from the other items in that group.

- ***oikeamielinen* / *oikeamielisyys*** — Marginal items (P2a: [MARGINAL]). Literary register. Placed tentatively in Group 10 but peripheral.

- ***aidonkaltainen*** — Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P2a. Insufficient usage data to assign to a group.
