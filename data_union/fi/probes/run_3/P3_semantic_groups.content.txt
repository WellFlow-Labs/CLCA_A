# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed across P1 and P2 documentation. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries are determined by the data. Items that appear in multiple groups are listed under each relevant group and flagged in the overlapping items section. Items for which no clear grouping is supported are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to attribute truth or falsity to propositions and claims

**Label:** Propositional truth and falsity attribution

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize statements, claims, or propositions as true or false. They appear in contexts where the question at issue is whether something said or asserted corresponds to what is the case. They are used predicatively (*se on tosi*, *väite on epätosi*), attributively (*väärä väite*, *epätosi väite*), or in constructions that introduce or frame a truth claim (*totuus on se, että...*). The core contrast in this group is between expressions that affirm truth and those that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tosi* — predicative and attributive use: *se on tosi* (that is true)
- *totuus* — *kertoa totuus* (to tell the truth), *totuus on se, että...* (the truth is that...)
- *epätosi* — *väite on epätosi* (the claim is false), *epätosi väite* (a false claim)
- *väärä* (in propositional use) — *väärä väite* (a false claim)
- *todenmukainen* — *todenmukainen kuvaus* (an accurate/realistic description)
- *totuudenmukainen* — *totuudenmukainen kertomus* (a truthful account)
- *epätotuus* — *puhua epätotuuksia* (to say untruths/falsehoods)
- *totta puhuen* — discourse marker introducing a candid statement
- *totta tosiaan* — emphatic confirmation of a statement
- *toden totta* — emphatic assertion of truth
- *todenmukaisesti* — *kuvata tilanne todenmukaisesti* (to describe the situation accurately)
- *totuudenmukaisesti* — *kertoa totuudenmukaisesti* (to tell truthfully)
- *totuus tulee ilmi* / *totuus paljastuu* — the truth comes to light
- *osoittaa epätodeksi* — to show to be false
- *todistaa vääräksi* — to prove false
- *osoittautua vääräksi* — to turn out to be false
- *osoittautua oikeaksi* — to prove to be correct

**Notes:** *Väärä* belongs here only in its propositional use (false claim, false statement). Its uses in other contexts (counterfeit objects, moral wrongness) are covered in other groups. *Todenmukainen* and *totuudenmukainen* are closely related but the former is more commonly used in aesthetic and journalistic contexts (realistic, verisimilar), while the latter appears more frequently in legal contexts for witness accounts.

## Group 2: Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings concerning evidence, proof, and certification

**Label:** Evidence, proof, and certification in legal and administrative use

**Definition:** These expressions are used in formal legal and administrative contexts where the question is whether something has been established, certified, or demonstrated to a required standard. They appear in proceedings involving courts, notaries, administrative bodies, and official documents. The cluster includes both the acts of proving and certifying and the instruments or persons involved.

**Representative expressions:**

- *todistaa* — *todistaa syyllisyys* (to prove guilt), *todistaa asiakirja* (to certify a document), *todistaa oikeudessa* (to testify in court)
- *todiste* — *esittää todiste* (to present evidence), *riittävä todiste* (sufficient evidence), *kiistaton todiste* (incontrovertible evidence)
- *todistus* — *antaa todistus* (to issue a certificate), *todistus oikeaksi* (certified as correct)
- *todistaja* — *todistaja oikeudessa* (witness in court)
- *todistajanlausunto* — witness statement
- *todistusaineisto* — body of evidence
- *todistustaakka* — burden of proof
- *todistusvoima* — probative value
- *todistuskappale* — exhibit, piece of evidence
- *todistusharkinnan vapaus* — free evaluation of evidence
- *todistettava* — *todistettava väite* (a provable claim)
- *todistettu* — *todistettu syyllisyys* (proved guilt)
- *todistamaton* — *todistamaton väite* (an unproved claim)
- *todistaminen* — *syyllisyyden todistaminen* (proving guilt)
- *pätevä todiste* — valid evidence
- *pätemätön todiste* — inadmissible evidence
- *todentaa* — *todentaa henkilöllisyys* (to verify identity)
- *todentaminen* — identity verification
- *väärä todistus* — false testimony
- *väärä vala* — false oath
- *todistaa henkilöllisyys* — to prove identity

**Notes:** *Todistaa* is the central verb in this group, covering both proving and certifying. *Todiste* and *todistus* are distinct: *todiste* refers to an evidentiary item, while *todistus* refers to a certificate or act of testimony. *Todentaa* overlaps with *todistaa* in identity verification contexts but is more common in technical and digital administrative settings. *Väärä todistus* and *väärä vala* belong here as legal offenses but also connect to Group 4 (forgery and falsification).

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate the reliability or credibility of sources, persons, and information

**Label:** Reliability and credibility evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used when assessing whether a source, person, witness, or piece of information can be trusted or depended upon. They appear in journalistic, scientific, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The central contrast is between *luotettava* (reliable, trustworthy) and *epäluotettava* (unreliable, untrustworthy), and the group includes constructions that perform or express such evaluations.

**Representative expressions:**

- *luotettava* — *luotettava lähde* (a reliable source), *luotettava todistaja* (a reliable witness)
- *luotettavuus* — *lähteen luotettavuus* (the reliability of the source), *arvioida luotettavuutta* (to assess reliability)
- *epäluotettava* — *epäluotettava lähde* (an unreliable source)
- *epäluotettavuus* — *lähteen epäluotettavuus* (the unreliability of the source)
- *luotettavasti* — *toimia luotettavasti* (to act reliably)
- *pitää luotettavana* — to consider reliable
- *pitää epäluotettavana* — to consider unreliable
- *osoittautua luotettavaksi* — to prove to be reliable
- *osoittautua epäluotettavaksi* — to prove to be unreliable
- *kyseenalaistaa luotettavuus* — to call into question the reliability
- *luotettavuuden arviointi* — reliability assessment
- *ei kovin luotettava* — not very reliable (hedged evaluation)

**Notes:** This group is relatively self-contained. *Luotettava* derives from *luottaa* (to trust) and the group is anchored in the trust relationship between an evaluator and a source or person. It overlaps with Group 2 in legal contexts where witness credibility is at issue (*luotettava todistaja*).

## Group 4: Expressions used in contexts of forgery, falsification, and counterfeiting

**Label:** Forgery, falsification, and counterfeiting

**Definition:** These expressions are used when describing the deliberate production of false versions of documents, signatures, currency, or data, or the distortion of facts and representations. They appear predominantly in legal, criminal, and journalistic contexts. The group is anchored in the *väärä/vääre-* root and the verb *väärentää*, and includes both the acts and their products.

**Representative expressions:**

- *väärentää* — *väärentää asiakirja* (to forge a document), *väärentää allekirjoitus* (to forge a signature), *väärentää rahaa* (to counterfeit money), *väärentää tietoja* (to falsify data)
- *väärentäminen* — *asiakirjan väärentäminen* (document forgery), *rahan väärentäminen* (currency counterfeiting)
- *väärennös* — *asiakirjaväärennös* (document forgery [product])
- *väärentäjä* — forger, counterfeiter
- *vääristää* — *vääristää totuutta* (to distort the truth)
- *vääristely* — *tosiasioiden vääristely* (distortion of facts)
- *vääristymä* — distortion, aberration
- *vääristyä* — to become distorted
- *harhaanjohtava* — *harhaanjohtava mainos* (a misleading advertisement)
- *väärä todistus* — false testimony
- *väärä vala* — false oath
- *aito vai väärennös* — genuine or a forgery
- *syyttää väärentämisestä* — to charge with forgery
- *tuomita väärentämisestä* — to convict of forgery
- *väärennöstapaus* — forgery case

**Notes:** *Harhaanjohtava* belongs here because it describes deliberate misleading, though it does not require outright falsification — a statement can be *harhaanjohtava* without being technically false. *Vääristely* and *vääristää* cover distortion of facts rather than production of forged objects, placing them at the edge of this group and overlapping with Group 1 (propositional falsity). *Väärä todistus* and *väärä vala* overlap with Group 2.

## Group 5: Expressions used to distinguish genuine items or persons from imitations or fakes

**Label:** Genuineness and authenticity versus imitation

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether an object, document, person, or representation is the real thing as opposed to a copy, imitation, or fake. They appear in art authentication, legal, commercial, and philosophical contexts. The central contrast is between *aito* (genuine, authentic) and terms for imitation or falseness (*jäljitelmä*, *väärennös*, *väärä*).

**Representative expressions:**

- *aito* — *aito allekirjoitus* (genuine signature), *aito nahka* (real leather), *aito tunne* (genuine feeling)
- *aitous* — *todistaa aitous* (to prove authenticity), *epäillä aitoutta* (to doubt the authenticity)
- *aidosti* — *aidosti kiinnostunut* (genuinely interested)
- *epäaito* — *epäaito käytös* (inauthentic behavior)
- *epäaitous* — inauthenticity
- *jäljitelmä* — imitation, copy, replica
- *jäljitellä* — to imitate, to copy
- *jäljittely* — imitation as a practice
- *jäljittelemätön* — inimitable
- *todistaa aidoksi* — to certify as genuine
- *osoittautua aidoksi* — to prove to be genuine
- *aito vai väärennös* — genuine or a forgery
- *oikea* (in the sense of genuine) — *oikea nahka* (real leather), *oikea henkilö* (the right/genuine person)

**Notes:** *Oikea* in the sense of "genuine" (as opposed to imitation) overlaps with this group, though its primary uses are in Group 6 (correctness). *Jäljitelmä* does not imply fraudulent intent, distinguishing it from *väärennös* (which does). *Epäaito* and *epäaitous* are used more in philosophical and personal identity contexts (existential inauthenticity) than in the object-authentication contexts where *väärennös* and *jäljitelmä* appear.

---

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate correctness, accuracy, and conformity to a standard

**Label:** Correctness and conformity to standard

**Definition:** These expressions are used when assessing whether something — an answer, a procedure, a piece of data, a use of language — conforms to a correct or expected standard. They appear in educational, administrative, linguistic, and legal contexts. The central contrast is between *oikea* / *oikeellisuus* (correct, correctness) and *virheellinen* / *virhe* (erroneous, error).

**Representative expressions:**

- *oikea* — *oikea vastaus* (the correct answer), *olla oikeassa* (to be right)
- *oikeellisuus* — *kielellinen oikeellisuus* (linguistic correctness), *tietojen oikeellisuus* (the accuracy of the information)
- *oikein* — *tehdä oikein* (to do correctly), *oikein vai väärin* (right or wrong)
- *oikeakielisyys* — linguistic correctness, prescriptive correctness
- *oikeakielinen* — linguistically correct
- *virheellinen* — *virheellinen tieto* (incorrect information), *virheellinen käsitys* (erroneous belief)
- *virheellisyys* — *tiedon virheellisyys* (the incorrectness of the information)
- *virheellisesti* — *virheellisesti ilmoitettu* (incorrectly reported)
- *virhe* — error, mistake
- *virheetön* — *virheetön asiakirja* (an error-free document)
- *virheettömyys* — flawlessness, freedom from error
- *väärin* — *ymmärtää väärin* (to misunderstand), *tehdä väärin* (to do wrong/incorrectly)
- *väärinymmärrys* — misunderstanding
- *varmistaa tietojen oikeellisuus* — to verify the accuracy of the information
- *vastata tietojen oikeellisuudesta* — to be responsible for the accuracy of the information
- *osoittautua virheelliseksi* — to prove to be erroneous
- *perustua virheelliseen tietoon* — to be based on incorrect information
- *oikein vai väärin* — right or wrong

**Notes:** *Väärin* in this group covers procedural and epistemic incorrectness (*ymmärtää väärin*, *tulkita väärin*, *tehdä väärin*). Its moral sense (doing wrong) overlaps with Group 8. *Oikeakielisyys* is a specialized application of correctness to language use and sits at the edge of this group. *Virheellinen* is more formal and administrative than *väärä* in correctness contexts.

---

## Group 7: Expressions used in legal contexts concerning validity, legal force, and legal capacity

**Label:** Legal validity and legal capacity

**Definition:** These expressions are used in legal and administrative contexts to determine whether an instrument, argument, person, or action has legal force or meets the requirements for legal recognition. The central contrast is between *pätevä* (valid, competent) and *pätemätön* (invalid, incompetent). They appear in contract law, procedural law, and administrative law.

**Representative expressions:**

- *pätevä* — *pätevä todiste* (valid evidence), *pätevä peruste* (a valid ground), *pätevä syy* (a valid reason), *pätevä hakija* (a qualified applicant)
- *pätemätön* — *pätemätön sopimus* (an invalid contract), *pätemätön todiste* (inadmissible evidence), *pätemätön peruste* (an invalid ground)
- *pätevyys* — *argumentin pätevyys* (the validity of the argument)
- *pätemättömyys* — *sopimuksen pätemättömyys* (the invalidity of the contract)
- *päteä* — *laki pätee kaikille* (the law applies to everyone)
- *pätevästi* — validly, competently
- *julistaa pätemättömäksi* — to declare void, to annul
- *katsoa päteväksi* — to consider valid
- *todeta päteväksi* — to find valid
- *katsoa pätemättömäksi* — to deem invalid
- *oikeuskelpoinen* — having legal capacity
- *oikeustoimikelpoinen* — having capacity to act in law
- *oikeudellinen* — *oikeudellinen vastuu* (legal responsibility)
- *oikeudellisesti sitova* — legally binding
- *oikeutus* — justification, entitlement, warrant
- *oikeutettu* — *oikeutettu kritiikki* (justified criticism), *oikeutettu vaatimus* (a legitimate claim)
- *oikeuttaa* — *se ei oikeuta väkivaltaa* (that does not justify violence)

**Notes:** *Oikeuttaa*, *oikeutus*, and *oikeutettu* sit at the boundary between this group and Group 8 (moral and normative evaluation), since justification operates in both legal and moral registers. *Pätevä* in the sense of "qualified person" (*pätevä hakija*) is more about professional competence than legal validity, placing it at the edge of this group.

---

## Group 8: Expressions used in moral and normative evaluation of conduct and persons

**Label:** Moral and normative evaluation of honesty, dishonesty, and good or bad faith

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons and conduct in terms of honesty, sincerity, good faith, and their opposites. They appear in everyday moral discourse, legal contexts (particularly contract and property law), and professional ethics. The group includes both positive evaluations (*rehellinen*, *vilpitön*) and negative ones (*vilpillinen*, *epärehellinen*), as well as the legal concept of good faith (*vilpitön mieli*).

**Representative expressions:**

- *rehellinen* — *rehellinen vastaus* (an honest answer), *rehellinen ihminen* (an honest person)
- *rehellisyys* — *rehellisyys on paras politiikka* (honesty is the best policy)
- *rehellisesti* — *sanoa rehellisesti* (to say honestly)
- *rehellisesti sanottuna* — honestly speaking
- *ollaan rehellisiä* — let's be honest
- *olla rehellinen jollekin* — to be honest with someone
- *olla rehellinen itselleen* — to be honest with oneself
- *epärehellinen* — dishonest
- *epärehellisyys* — dishonesty
- *epärehellisesti* — dishonestly
- *vilpitön* — *vilpitön tarkoitus* (sincere intention), *vilpitön mieli* (good faith)
- *vilpittömyys* — *vilpittömyyden olettama* (the presumption of good faith)
- *vilpittömästi* — *vilpittömästi uskoa* (to sincerely believe)
- *toimia vilpittömässä mielessä* — to act in good faith
- *vilpitön ostaja* — bona fide purchaser
- *vilpitön kolmas osapuoli* — bona fide third party
- *vilpillinen* — *vilpillinen menettely* (fraudulent conduct)
- *vilpillisyys* — fraudulence
- *vilpillisesti* — *toimia vilpillisesti* (to act fraudulently/in bad faith)
- *toimia vilpillisessä mielessä* — to act in bad faith
- *vilpillinen kilpailu* — unfair competition
- *vilppi* — fraud, deceit
- *vääryys* — injustice, wrong, wrongdoing
- *tehdä vääryyttä* — to do wrong/injustice

**Notes:** *Vilpitön* and *vilpillinen* operate in both moral and legal registers; the legal use (*vilpitön mieli*, *vilpitön ostaja*) is a specialized application of the moral concept of good faith. *Vääryys* (injustice, wrongdoing) belongs here in its moral sense but is derived from *väärä*, which primarily anchors Groups 1, 4, and 6. *Rehellinen* is more squarely in the everyday moral register, while *vilpitön/vilpillinen* are more prominent in legal discourse.

## Group 9: Expressions used to refer to facts, reality, and what is actually the case

**Label:** Factual reality and what is actually the case

**Definition:** These expressions are used to refer to established facts, to what exists in reality as opposed to appearance or fiction, and to contrast actual states of affairs with how things seem or are claimed to be. They appear in journalistic, legal, scientific, and everyday contexts, often in corrective or contrastive functions.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tosiasia* — *tosiasiat puhuvat puolestaan* (the facts speak for themselves)
- *tosiasiat* — *pitäytyä tosiasioissa* (to stick to the facts)
- *tosiasiassa* — *tosiasiassa tilanne on eri* (in fact, the situation is different)
- *tosiasiallinen* — *tosiasiallinen tilanne* (the actual/de facto situation)
- *tosiasiallisesti* — factually, de facto
- *todellisuus* — *paeta todellisuutta* (to escape reality)
- *todellisuudessa* — *todellisuudessa asia on toisin* (in reality, the matter is otherwise)
- *todellinen* — *todellinen ongelma* (a real problem), *todellinen syy* (the real reason)
- *todella* — *todella tapahtui* (it really happened)
- *todellisuuspako* — escapism
- *todellisuudentaju* — sense of reality
- *todentua* — *ennuste todentui* (the prediction came true)
- *tosi vai tarua* — fact or fiction
- *tosi on tosi* — a fact is a fact
- *Nyt on tosi kyseessä* — now it's for real
- *epätodellinen* — *tilanne tuntui epätodelliselta* (the situation felt unreal)
- *tosiasioiden vääristely* — distortion of facts

**Notes:** This group is anchored in the *tosi/tode-* root but focuses on factual reality and what actually exists or occurs, rather than on propositional truth attribution (Group 1). The distinction is one of emphasis: Group 1 expressions are used to evaluate statements, while Group 9 expressions are used to refer to states of affairs. There is significant overlap, and many items from the *tosi/tode-* family could appear in either group depending on context. *Todellisuuspako* and *todellisuudentaju* are at the periphery of this group, involving psychological relationships to reality rather than factual claims.

## Group 10: Expressions used in contexts of misuse, misinterpretation, and incorrect application

**Label:** Misuse, misinterpretation, and incorrect application

**Definition:** These expressions are used when something — a word, a rule, a resource, a power — is used or applied in a way that deviates from correct or intended use. They are formed productively from *väärin* (wrongly, incorrectly) combined with verbs of use, understanding, and interpretation. They appear in legal, administrative, communicative, and everyday contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *väärinkäyttö* — *vallan väärinkäyttö* (abuse of power), *lääkkeiden väärinkäyttö* (drug misuse)
- *väärinkäyttää* — to misuse, to abuse
- *käyttää väärin* — to misuse, to abuse
- *ymmärtää väärin* — to misunderstand
- *väärinymmärrys* — misunderstanding
- *tulkita väärin* — to misinterpret
- *väärinkuuleminen* — mishearing
- *väärinlukeminen* — misreading
- *väärinkohtelu* — mistreatment

**Notes:** This group is defined by the productive pattern of *väärin* + verb of cognitive or practical action. It is distinct from Group 6 (correctness) in that the focus is on the act of misapplication rather than on the property of being incorrect. It is also distinct from Group 4 (forgery) in that no deliberate deception is necessarily implied — misunderstanding can be innocent. *Väärinkäyttö* in the sense of abuse of power has a stronger normative dimension and overlaps with Group 8.

## Overlapping and Ambiguous Items

The following items appear in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into a single group:

---

**väärä**
Appears in Group 1 (false claim), Group 4 (counterfeit object), Group 5 (fake versus genuine), Group 6 (incorrect procedure), and Group 8 (wrong action). The word covers a wide range of uses that Finnish speakers do not appear to treat as sharply distinct; the same form is used for propositional falsity, physical counterfeiting, and moral wrongness. No single group captures all its uses.

---

**väärin**
Appears in Group 6 (doing something incorrectly) and Group 10 (misuse, misinterpretation). Also has a moral dimension (*tehdä väärin* — to do wrong) that connects to Group 8. The adverbial form is highly productive and its uses span multiple domains.

---

**todistaa**
Appears in Group 2 (legal proof and certification) and Group 5 (certifying genuineness: *todistaa aidoksi*). Also relevant to Group 1 in the sense of demonstrating the truth of a claim (*todistaa vääräksi* — to prove false).

---

**oikea**
Appears in Group 5 (genuine: *oikea nahka*), Group 6 (correct: *oikea vastaus*, *olla oikeassa*), and Group 7 (legal: *todistaa oikeaksi*). The spatial sense (right direction) is not represented in any group, as it does not appear in the documented usage patterns relevant to this domain.

---

**oikeuttaa / oikeutus / oikeutettu**
Appear in Group 7 (legal validity and entitlement) and Group 8 (moral justification). The distinction between legal authorization and moral justification is not consistently marked in usage.

---

**vilpitön / vilpillinen**
Appear in Group 8 (moral evaluation) and Group 2 (legal proceedings, particularly good-faith purchaser doctrine). The legal uses are specialized applications of the moral concepts.

---

**vääryys**
Appears in Group 8 (moral wrongdoing) but derives from the *väärä* root that anchors Groups 1, 4, and 6. Its primary usage is moral-normative rather than epistemic.

---

**harhaanjohtava**
Appears in Group 4 (misleading as a form of deception) and Group 1 (producing false impressions). It does not require outright falsity, which distinguishes it from most Group 1 items, but it is used in contexts where the concern is with false beliefs being induced in others.

---

**totuudenhetki**
Appears in Group 9 (factual reality) in the sense of a decisive moment of revelation, but its usage is often figurative and does not necessarily involve propositional truth claims. It is a calque and its connection to the *totuus* root is etymological rather than fully semantic in many uses.

---

**totuuskomissio / totuusserum / totuusarvo / totuusteoria**
These compounds use *totuus* as a first element but function as specialized technical or institutional terms. *Totuusarvo* (truth value) and *totuusteoria* (theory of truth) belong to logical and philosophical discourse and are not clearly placed in any of the usage-based groups above, which are oriented toward everyday, legal, and journalistic registers. They are noted here as items that do not fit cleanly.

---

**oikeaoppinen / oikeaoppisuus / vääräoppinen / vääräoppisuus**
These form a natural contrastive pair in religious and doctrinal contexts (orthodox versus heretical). They use the *oikea/väärä* roots in a domain — doctrinal conformity — that is distinct from the other groups documented here. They could form a small separate group, but the evidence base from P1 and P2 is limited to a few entries, and no broader cluster of expressions in this domain was documented. They are flagged as items that do not fit cleanly into the groups above.

---

## Items Without a Clear Group

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups above based on the documented usage patterns:

- **oikeudenmukaisuus / oikeudenmukainen / epäoikeudenmukainen / epäoikeudenmukaisuus** — These expressions concern fairness and justice in the distributive and procedural sense. Their usage is in moral, political, and legal discourse about fair treatment and just outcomes. This is a distinct domain from the other groups, which focus on truth, falsity, correctness, evidence, and honesty. A group could be formed around these items, but the P1 and P2 documentation does not provide sufficient additional expressions in this domain to characterize the group's boundaries with confidence.

- **todellisuuspako** — Documented in Group 9 as peripheral. Its primary usage is in psychological and cultural commentary about avoidance of reality, which is a distinct register from the other Group 9 items.

- **virhearvio** — A technical statistical term (error estimate, margin of error) that appears in scientific and technical contexts. No cluster of similar expressions was documented in P1 or P2.

- **totuuspuhe** — Marked [MARGINAL] in P1b. Its usage in Finnish appears limited and specialized (philosophical discourse on *parrhesia*). It does not cluster with other items in the documented data.

- **totuusjoukkue** — Marked [MARGINAL] in P1b. A political journalism term with limited attestation. No cluster.
