# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / REAL (inchoative with "totoo")

### Pattern
**Mag-ka- + totoo** — Inchoative prefix combination marks transition into state of being true or becoming reality.

### Examples
- Magkatotoo ang pangarap ko.
  - magkatotoo ang pangarap ko
  - "My dream came true / was realized / became reality."

- Nagkatotoo ang hula niya.
  - nagkatotoo ang hula niya
  - "His/her prediction came true / was realized."

- Magkatotoo sana ang sinabi mo.
  - magkatotoo sana ang sinabi mo
  - "I hope what you said comes true / becomes reality."

### Notes
The **mag-ka-** inchoative specifically marks achievement or realization of something previously predicted, hoped for, or imagined. This is NOT used for statements becoming factually true, but rather for predictions, dreams, wishes, or prophecies being fulfilled or realized. The pattern is highly productive and common in everyday speech. Aspect marking is regular: **nag-ka-** (completed/past), **mag-ka-** (future/contemplated), **nagka-ka-** (progressive).

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and natural for expressing fulfillment of predictions or dreams.

---

## 2. BECOMING CORRECT (inchoative with "tama")

### Pattern
**Tuma- + ma** (actor-focus verb) — The verb **tumama** has dual meaning: (1) to hit a target physically, (2) to guess or answer correctly.

### Examples
- Tumama ang sagot ko.
  - tumama ang sagot ko
  - "My answer was correct / I guessed correctly."

- Tumama ka sa hula mo.
  - tumama ka sa hula mo
  - "You were correct in your guess / Your guess was right."

### Notes
The inchoative sense of "becoming correct" is expressed through the **-um-** actor-focus verb **tumama**, which emphasizes the achievement of correctness, particularly in guessing or prediction contexts. This is distinct from simple attribution of correctness (**tama ang sagot**). The physical hitting sense and correctness sense coexist in this form.

**Note:** There is NO common **mag-ka-tama** form meaning "to become correct" parallel to **magkatotoo**. The language does not typically frame correctness as a state one "becomes" in the same way truth "becomes realized."

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Common in contexts of guessing, prediction, or hitting targets (both literal and figurative).

---

## 3. BECOMING WRONG / MAKING A MISTAKE (inchoative with "mali")

### Pattern
**Mag-ka- + mali** — Inchoative marks transition into state of error or commission of mistake.

### Examples
- Nagkamali ako.
  - nagkamali ako
  - "I made a mistake / I was wrong / I erred."

- Nagkamali siya sa pagkakaintindi.
  - nagkamali siya sa pagkakaintindi
  - "He/she made a mistake in understanding / misunderstood."

- Huwag kang magkamali.
  - huwag kang magkamali
  - "Don't make a mistake / Don't be wrong."

### Notes
The **mag-ka-mali** pattern is the standard way to express making a mistake or becoming wrong. This is highly productive and extremely common in everyday speech. The inchoative reading emphasizes the transition into error rather than simply being in a state of error. Aspect marking is regular: **nag-ka-** (completed), **mag-ka-** (future/contemplated), **nagka-ka-** (progressive).

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and natural for expressing errors or mistakes.

---

## 4. BECOMING GENUINE / AUTHENTIC (inchoative with "tunay")

### Pattern
**NO COMMON INCHOATIVE FORM EXISTS**

### Notes
There is no attested common expression using **mag-ka-tunay** or similar inchoative pattern meaning "to become genuine/authentic." The language does not typically frame authenticity as a state one transitions into. Authenticity is treated as an inherent quality rather than an achieved state.

If one needs to express that something "turned out to be genuine," this would be expressed through discovery or verification constructions rather than inchoative marking:
- **Napatunayan na tunay ito** (It was verified/proven to be genuine)
- **Nalaman kong tunay ito** (I found out this is genuine)

**Naturalness:** [NO COMMON EXPRESSION]

---

## 5. BECOMING HONEST (inchoative with "tapat")

### Pattern
**NO COMMON INCHOATIVE FORM EXISTS**

### Notes
There is no attested common expression using **mag-ka-tapat** meaning "to become honest." Honesty is treated as a character trait or behavioral practice rather than a state one transitions into. 

The verb **magtapat** exists but means "to confess, to be honest, to tell the truth" (an action) rather than "to become honest" (a state change). If one needs to express character development toward honesty, this would be expressed periphrastically:
- **Naging matapat na siya** (He/she became honest now — using **naging** + adjective)
- **Natuto siyang maging matapat** (He/she learned to be honest)

**Naturalness:** [NO COMMON EXPRESSION for inchoative state change]

## 6. CAUSING TO BE TRUE / MAKING TRUE (causative with "totoo")

### Pattern
**Ika- + totoo** — Causative prefix marks causing something to be true or making something real.

### Examples
- Ikatotoo mo ang pangako mo.
  - ikatotoo mo ang pangako mo
  - "Make your promise true / Fulfill your promise / Make it real."

### Notes
The **ika-** causative form **ikatotoo** is MARGINAL and literary/formal. It is not commonly used in everyday speech. The meaning is "to cause to be true" or "to make real," but this is typically expressed through other constructions in natural speech:
- **Tuparin mo ang pangako mo** (Fulfill your promise — using dedicated verb **tuparin**)
- **Gawing totoo** (Make it true — using general causative **gawin**)

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL] — Formal/literary; not common in everyday speech.

---

## 7. CAUSING TO BE CORRECT / CORRECTING (causative with "tama")

### Pattern
**I- + tama** — Causative prefix marks causing something to be correct or making corrections.

### Examples
- Itama mo ang pagkakamali.
  - itama mo ang pagkakamali
  - "Correct the mistake / Make it right / Fix the error."

- Itinatama ko ang mali kong sagot.
  - itinatama ko ang mali kong sagot
  - "I am correcting my wrong answer."

- Itama mo ang iyong gawi.
  - itama mo ang iyong gawi
  - "Correct your behavior / Make your conduct right."

### Notes
The **i-** causative **itama** is HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE and extremely common. This is the standard way to express correction or making something right. The pattern is unrestricted and used across all registers. Progressive aspect uses reduplication: **itinatama** (is correcting). Completed aspect: **itinama** (corrected). This is one of the most natural and frequent causative constructions in the truth domain.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and natural across all contexts.

---

## 8. CAUSING TO BE CORRECT / PROPER (causative with "wasto")

### Pattern
**I- + wasto** — Causative prefix marks causing something to be correct or proper, with emphasis on precision.

### Examples
- Iwasto mo ang pagkakasulat.
  - iwasto mo ang pagkakasulat
  - "Correct the writing / Make the writing proper."

- Iwasto ang mga mali.
  - iwasto ang mga mali
  - "Correct the errors / Rectify the mistakes."

### Notes
The **i-** causative **iwasto** is PRODUCTIVE and common in formal or technical contexts. It emphasizes precision, propriety, or accuracy more than simple **itama**. The pattern is fully productive with regular aspect marking. This is more formal than **itama** but still widely used, especially in educational, administrative, and technical contexts.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Common in formal/technical contexts; natural but more formal than **itama**.

## 9. CAUSING TO BE EXACT / ADJUSTING (causative with "hustó")

### Pattern
**I- + hustó** — Causative prefix marks causing something to be exactly right or making precise adjustments.

### Examples
- Ihustó mo ang sukat.
  - ihustó mo ang sukat
  - "Adjust the measurement exactly / Make the measurement just right."

- Ihustó ang timpla.
  - ihustó ang timpla
  - "Adjust the mixture / Make the blend just right."

### Notes
The **i-** causative **ihustó** is PRODUCTIVE but more specialized than **itama** or **iwasto**. It emphasizes exact adjustment or making something precisely right, particularly for measurements, amounts, or proportions. Common in contexts requiring precision or fine-tuning.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural in contexts requiring precision or exact adjustment.

## 10. CAUSING TO BE PROVEN / PROVING (causative with "tunay")

### Pattern
**Pa- + tunay + -an** — Causative circumfix marks causing something to be proven or verified.

### Examples
- Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo.
  - patunayan mo ang sinabi mo
  - "Prove what you said / Verify your statement / Demonstrate it."

- Pinatunayan niya ang katotohanan.
  - pinatunayan niya ang katotohanan
  - "He/she proved the truth / verified the truth."

- Papatunayan ko na totoo ito.
  - papatunayan ko na totoo ito
  - "I will prove that this is true."

### Notes
The **pa-...-an** causative **patunayan** is HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE and extremely common. This is the standard way to express proving, verifying, or demonstrating truth or authenticity. The pattern is unrestricted and used across all registers. Aspect marking is regular: **pinatunayan** (completed), **papatunayan** (future), **pinapatunayan** (progressive). This is one of the most frequent causative constructions in the truth domain.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and natural across all contexts.

## 11. CAUSING TO BE HONEST / MAKING CONFESS (causative with "tapat")

### Pattern
**NO PRODUCTIVE CAUSATIVE FORM EXISTS**

### Notes
There is no common causative form meaning "to cause someone to be honest" or "to make someone honest." The verb **magtapat** means "to confess, to be honest" but this is an action the subject performs voluntarily, not something caused by an external agent.

If one needs to express causing someone to confess or reveal truth, this would be expressed through other verbs:
- **Paaminin** (to make someone admit — from **amin** = admit)
- **Pagsabihan na magtapat** (to tell someone to be honest)
- **Pilitin na magsabi ng totoo** (to force to tell the truth)

**Naturalness:** [NO COMMON CAUSATIVE FORM]

---

## 12. CAUSING TO BECOME FALSE / FALSIFYING (causative with negative terms)

### Pattern
**NO COMMON DEDICATED CAUSATIVE FORMS EXIST**

### Notes
There are no attested common causative forms meaning "to falsify" or "to make false" using the negative truth-domain vocabulary (mali, bulaán, huwad, peke, kasinungalingan).

To express falsification or making something false, Tagalog uses:
- **Gawing peke** (make it fake — using general causative **gawin**)
- **Humarad** (to counterfeit, to falsify — from **huwad**)
- **Magsinungaling** (to lie — but this is the act of lying, not causing something to be false)
- **Siraing katotohanan** (destroy the truth — using **sirain** = destroy)

The language does not have productive causative morphology specifically for making things false, in contrast to the productive causatives for making things true/correct (**itama**, **iwasto**, **patunayan**).

**Naturalness:** [NO COMMON DEDICATED CAUSATIVE FORMS]

## 13. REMAINING TRUE / STAYING TRUE (continuative/durative)

### Pattern
**Nananatiling + truth-domain term** — Continuative verb **manatili** (to remain, to stay) + linker + predicate.

### Examples
- Nananatiling totoo ang sinabi ko.
  - nananatiling totoo ang sinabi ko
  - "What I said remains true / stays true."

- Nananatili pa rin ang katotohanan.
  - nananatili pa rin ang katotohanan
  - "The truth still remains / endures."

### Notes
The continuative/durative sense of "remaining true" or "staying true" is expressed through the verb **manatili** (to remain, to stay) + linker + truth-domain predicate. This is a productive pattern but not specific to truth-domain vocabulary — it's the general way to express continuation of any state. The pattern is natural and common when emphasizing persistence or endurance of truth over time.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural and common for emphasizing persistence of truth.

### Pattern

## 15. CEASING TO BE TRUE / BECOMING FALSE (terminative)

### Pattern
**Hindi na totoo** — Negator + **na** (already/now) + truth predicate marks cessation of truth.

### Examples
- Hindi na totoo ang sinabi niya.
  - hindi na totoo ang sinabi niya
  - "What he/she said is no longer true / is not true anymore."

- Hindi na totoo iyon ngayon.
  - hindi na totoo iyon ngayon
  - "That is no longer true now / is not true anymore now."

### Notes
Tagalog does not have dedicated terminative morphology for truth-domain terms. The cessation of truth is expressed through negation + **na** (already/now), which marks change of state. The particle **na** is crucial for indicating the change from a previous state of being true to a current state of not being true. This is the standard and natural way to express that something has ceased to be true.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Standard and natural way to express cessation of truth.

---

## 16. CEASING TO BE CORRECT / BECOMING WRONG (terminative)

### Pattern
**Hindi na tama** or **mali na** — Negation + **na** or positive wrong term + **na**.

### Examples
- Hindi na tama ang dating sagot.
  - hindi na tama ang dating sagot
  - "The previous answer is no longer correct / is not correct anymore."

- Mali na ang lumang paraan.
  - mali na ang lumang paraan
  - "The old method is wrong now / has become wrong."

### Notes
Same pattern as with **totoo** — cessation is marked by negation or opposite term + **na** particle. The **na** particle is essential for marking the change of state. Both patterns (**hindi na tama** and **mali na**) are natural and common.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Standard and natural for expressing cessation of correctness.

## 17. DISCOVERING TRUTH / FINDING OUT (achievement)

### Pattern
**Nalaman/natuklasan + na + truth clause** — Discovery verbs mark achievement of knowledge about truth.

### Examples
- Nalaman kong totoo ang balita.
  - nalaman kong totoo ang balita
  - "I found out that the news is true / I learned the news is true."

- Natuklasan niya ang katotohanan.
  - natuklasan niya ang katotohanan
  - "He/she discovered the truth."

- Nalaman na nila na mali sila.
  - nalaman na nila na mali sila
  - "They found out that they were wrong / They learned they were wrong."

### Notes
Discovery or realization of truth is expressed through achievement verbs like **malaman** (to find out, to learn) and **matuklasan** (to discover). These verbs take completed aspect (**na-**) to mark the achievement. The pattern is highly productive and natural. The complementizer **na** introduces the truth clause. This is distinct from inchoative constructions — it's not that truth "becomes," but that knowledge of truth is achieved.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and natural for expressing discovery of truth.

## 18. VERIFYING / CONFIRMING TRUTH (verification process)

### Pattern
**Mag-verify constructions or checking verbs + truth-domain terms**

### Examples
- Sinusuri kung totoo ang balita.
  - sinusuri kung totoo ang balita
  - "Checking whether the news is true / Verifying if the news is true."

- Tinitingnan kung tama ang sagot.
  - tinitingnan kung tama ang sagot
  - "Checking if the answer is correct / Verifying the answer's correctness."

- Sinisigurado kong totoo ito.
  - sinisigurado kong totoo ito
  - "I am making sure this is true / I am verifying this is true."

### Notes
Verification processes are expressed through various checking/examining verbs (**suri** = examine, **tingnan** = look at/check, **sigurado** = make sure) in progressive aspect, followed by conditional clauses with **kung** (if/whether) + truth-domain predicate. The verb **magsigurado** (to make sure, to verify) from **sigurado** is particularly common. These constructions emphasize the ongoing process of verification rather than achievement of knowledge.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural and common for expressing verification processes.

- Ipagtanggol ang katotohanan.

- Panindigan ang katotohanan.
  - panindigan ang katotohanan

### Notes
Maintaining or upholding truth is expressed through various verbs of maintenance (**panatilihin** = maintain), defense (**ipagtanggol** = defend), or standing firm (**panindigan** = stand firm for). These take truth nominalizations (particularly **katotohanan**) as objects. The expression **paninindigan ng katotohanan** (standing for truth) was noted in P1b as a fixed expression. These constructions emphasize active maintenance or defense of truth against challenges.

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural in moral and political discourse about defending truth.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productive Dynamic Patterns:

1. **Inchoative (becoming):**
   - **Mag-ka-totoo** (to come true, be realized) — HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Mag-ka-mali** (to make a mistake, become wrong) — HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Tumama** (to be correct, guess right) — PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - NO common inchoatives for **tunay** (genuine) or **tapat** (honest)

2. **Causative (making/causing):**
   - **Itama** (to correct) — HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Iwasto** (to rectify) — PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Ihustó** (to adjust exactly) — PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Patunayan** (to prove) — HIGHLY PRODUCTIVE [CORE]
   - **Ikatotoo** (to make true) — MARGINAL [literary/formal]
   - NO productive causatives for negative terms (falsify, make false)

3. **Continuative (remaining/staying):**
   - Uses general continuative verb **manatili** + truth-domain predicates
   - Productive across all truth-domain terms [CORE]

4. **Terminative (ceasing to be):**
   - Uses negation + **na** particle: **hindi na totoo**, **hindi na tama**
   - Productive across all truth-domain terms [CORE]

5. **Achievement (discovering/finding out):**
   - Uses discovery verbs **malaman**, **matuklasan** + truth clauses
   - Highly productive [CORE]

6. **Verification (checking/confirming):**
   - Uses checking verbs **suri**, **tingnan**, **sigurado** + conditional clauses
   - Productive [CORE]

7. **Maintenance/Defense:**
   - Uses maintenance/defense verbs + truth nominalizations
   - Productive [CORE]

8. **Disclosure/Concealment:**
   - Uses disclosure verbs (**ilantad**, **ihayag**) or concealment verbs (**itago**, **ikubli**)
   - Productive [CORE]

9. **Distortion:**
   - Uses distortion verbs (**baluktot**, **sira**) + truth nominalizations
   - Productive [CORE]

10. **Acceptance/Denial:**
    - Uses acceptance verbs (**tanggapin**, **kilalanin**) or denial verbs (**tanggihan**, **itanggi**)
    - Productive [CORE]

11. **Epistemic Stance:**
    - Uses doubt verbs (**mag-alinlangan**, **magduda**) + oblique or conditional complements
    - Productive [CORE]

### Key Asymmetries:

1. **Positive causatives are productive; negative causatives are not:**
   - Rich causative morphology for making things true/correct (**itama**, **iwasto**, **patunayan**)
   - No dedicated causative morphology for making things false/incorrect
   - Falsification expressed through general verbs or periphrastic constructions

2. **Realization vs. state change:**
   - **Magkatotoo** expresses realization/fulfillment of predictions, not simple truth acquisition
   - No parallel inchoative for statements "becoming true" in factual sense
   - Truth is discovered (**nalaman**) rather than "become"

3. **Character traits resist inchoative marking:**
   - No common inchoatives for **tunay** (genuine) or **tapat** (honest)
   - These qualities are treated as inherent or developed gradually, not achieved suddenly

4. **Verification is processual:**
   - Rich vocabulary for ongoing verification processes (**sinusuri**, **tinitingnan**, **sinisigurado**)
   - Distinct from achievement of knowledge (**nalaman**, **natuklasan**)

5. **Truth maintenance requires active verbs:**
   - Truth doesn't simply "remain" passively
   - Active maintenance (**panatilihin**), defense (**ipagtanggol**), or standing firm (**panindigan**) is emphasized

6. **Disclosure/concealment are symmetric:**
   - Productive patterns for both revealing (**ilantad**, **ihayag**) and hiding (**itago**, **ikubli**) truth
   - Both directions are equally grammaticalized

### Naturalness Hierarchy:

**Most Natural/Productive:**
- Causatives for correction: **itama**, **iwasto**, **patunayan**
- Inchoatives for realization: **magkatotoo**, **magkamali**
- Discovery/verification: **malaman**, **matuklasan**, **magsigurado**
- Disclosure/concealment: **ilantad**, **itago**
- Acceptance/denial: **tanggapin**, **tanggihan**

**Moderately Natural:**
- Continuative constructions with **manatili**
- Distortion constructions with **baluktot**, **sira**
- Epistemic stance with **mag-alinlangan**, **magduda**

**Marginal/Formal:**
- **Ikatotoo** (causative "to make true")
- Specialized maintenance expressions like **paninindigan ng katotohanan**

**No Common Expression:**
- Inchoatives for **tunay** (becoming genuine) or **tapat** (becoming honest)
- Dedicated causatives for falsification or making incorrect
- Causatives for making someone honest
