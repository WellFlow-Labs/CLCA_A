## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented across P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit textual evidence from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents an asymmetry, imbalance, or partial contrast, this is preserved rather than normalized. Oppositions are listed in the order in which the relevant evidence appears most prominently in the CONTEXT.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** benar
**Term B:** salah
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**English Translation:** true/correct vs. wrong/incorrect
**Description of Contrast:** *Benar* affirms that a statement, fact, or action corresponds to reality or a correct standard; *salah* indicates deviation from correctness, fact, or proper conduct. The pair is explicitly documented as a binary evaluative frame (*benar atau salah*, true or false; *benar-salah*, right and wrong) used in educational, logical, and moral contexts, and the two roots are treated in P1b as anchoring opposite poles of the correctness axis.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** asli
**Term B:** palsu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** genuine/original vs. fake/counterfeit
**Description of Contrast:** *Asli* indicates that an object, document, person, or cultural item is not a copy, forgery, or imitation; *palsu* indicates that it is fraudulently presented as genuine. The pair is explicitly documented in P1b as a tightly coupled antonymic pair anchoring the authenticity/inauthenticity axis, and the binary frame *asli atau palsu* (genuine or fake) is attested as a fixed evaluative construction in commercial and forensic contexts.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** asli
**Term B:** tiruan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** genuine/original vs. imitation/copy
**Description of Contrast:** *Asli* indicates that something is the original or genuine article; *tiruan* indicates that it is made to resemble something else, without necessarily implying fraudulent intent. The CONTEXT explicitly contrasts the two (*ini asli, bukan tiruan*; *hanya tiruan, bukan yang asli*) and notes that *tiruan* occupies an intermediate position between *asli* and *palsu* in that it describes resemblance without necessarily implying fraud.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** benar
**Term B:** tidak benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** true/correct vs. not true/incorrect
**Description of Contrast:** *Tidak benar* is the standard morphological negation of *benar*, formed by prefixing the general negator *tidak* to the adjective. The CONTEXT documents *tidak benar* as a high-frequency formula used in official denials and journalistic corrections (*informasi itu tidak benar*), and P4b identifies the *tidak* + predicate adjective pattern as the obligatory negation strategy for adjectives in this domain.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** sah
**Term B:** tidak sah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** valid/lawful vs. invalid/unlawful
**Description of Contrast:** *Sah* indicates that an act, document, or status meets the required conditions for official recognition; *tidak sah* is its negation, indicating that those conditions are not met. The CONTEXT documents *tidak sah demi hukum* (null and void under the law) as a fixed legal formula and notes that *pernikahan itu dinyatakan tidak sah* (the marriage was declared invalid) is a standard construction in Indonesian legal discourse.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** bohong
**Term B:** dusta
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** lie (everyday register) vs. lie (formal/literary register)
**Description of Contrast:** *Bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms both denoting deliberate untruth, but they are distinguished by register: *bohong* is the everyday term used across spoken and informal written contexts, while *dusta* is more formal and literary, appearing primarily in religious, elevated, and written discourse. The CONTEXT explicitly documents this register distinction in P1, P2a, and P3, and notes that most constructions involving lying in everyday discourse use *bohong* while *dusta* appears in formal, religious, and literary contexts.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** salah (error/wrongness meaning)
**Term B:** salah satu / salah seorang (partitive meaning)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** wrong/mistaken vs. one of / one among
**Description of Contrast:** The form *salah* carries two distinct and non-overlapping meanings: in its primary use it denotes error, wrongness, or moral fault; in the grammaticalized compounds *salah satu* and *salah seorang* it functions as a partitive selector meaning "one of" or "one among," with no connection to the error meaning. The CONTEXT explicitly documents this as a grammaticalized use in which the original semantic content of the root is not recoverable from the compound's meaning, and flags it as a peripheral extension in P1b and as ungrouped in P3.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** kebenaran formal
**Term B:** kebenaran materiil
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** formal truth / procedural correctness vs. material truth / substantive truth
**Description of Contrast:** *Kebenaran formal* refers to truth established through proper legal procedure, while *kebenaran materiil* refers to the actual facts of a case as they occurred. The CONTEXT documents this as a distinction in Indonesian criminal procedure, where the two terms are explicitly contrasted: *kebenaran formal* may be established without *kebenaran materiil* and vice versa.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** sah
**Term B:** absah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** valid/lawful vs. valid/legitimate (more formal)
**Description of Contrast:** *Sah* and *absah* are near-synonyms both derived from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*, both denoting legal or formal validity, but *absah* is documented as slightly more formal in register in some contexts. The CONTEXT also notes that most constructions involving legal validity use *sah* rather than *absah*.
**Contrast Type:** lexical

## Opposition 12

**Term B:** membenarkan (to justify/rationalize)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** to confirm that something is true vs. to justify or excuse an action
**Description of Contrast:** The single form *membenarkan* carries two distinct readings documented in P1b and P2a: (a) to confirm that something is true or correct (*ia membenarkan laporan itu*, he confirmed the report), and (b) to justify or excuse an action (*tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu*, nothing can justify that action). The CONTEXT notes that disambiguation is context-dependent and that both readings are attested in standard usage.

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** pemalsuan (act of forging)
**Term B:** kepalsuan (quality of being false)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** falsification/forgery (as a criminal act or process) vs. falseness/inauthenticity (as an abstract quality)
**Description of Contrast:** Both are nominalizations of *palsu* but they denote different aspects: *pemalsuan* refers to the act or crime of falsifying documents, currency, or identities, while *kepalsuan* refers to the abstract quality or state of being false or inauthentic, used in moral, literary, and philosophical contexts. The CONTEXT explicitly distinguishes the two in P1b, noting that *kepalsuan* is "more abstract than *pemalsuan*, which refers to the act of forging."

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** pe(N)- agentive for deception/forgery (pembohong, pendusta, pemalsu, penyesat)
**Term B:** (no lexicalized agentive for truth-teller)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** liar / forger / one who leads astray vs. (no single derived form)
**Description of Contrast:** The *pe(N)-* agentive nominalization is productive for bases denoting deception and forgery (*pembohong*, *pendusta*, *pemalsu*, *peniru*, *penyesat*), but no single derived agentive form for a truth-teller is attested in the data. The CONTEXT documents this asymmetry explicitly in P4a and P4b, noting that the concept of a truth-teller is expressed periphrastically (*orang yang jujur*, *orang yang berkata benar*) and that the asymmetry is consistent across the data.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** tidak + predicate adjective (e.g., tidak benar, tidak sah, tidak akurat)
**Term B:** bukan + predicate noun/nominal (e.g., bukan dokumen asli, bukan kebohongan)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** negation of adjective predicate vs. negation of nominal predicate
**Description of Contrast:** Indonesian obligatorily uses *tidak* to negate adjective predicates and *bukan* to negate nominal predicates in the truth domain; the two negators are not interchangeable and mixing them is ungrammatical in standard usage. The CONTEXT documents this grammatical constraint in P4a and P4b as a structural feature of the negative attribution construction, with the distribution determined by predicate category rather than by semantic content.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** actually/in truth (correcting a prior assumption) vs. in essence/fundamentally (characterizing essential nature)

The CONTEXT documents this functional distinction in P2b and P4b, where the two are listed as non-interchangeable members of the discourse-level framing construction category.

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** otentik
**Term B:** asli
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** authentic (loanword, formal/legal/academic) vs. genuine/original (native form, broader distribution)
**Description of Contrast:** *Otentik* and *asli* both denote genuineness or authenticity but differ in register and distribution: *otentik* is a loanword from Dutch used in formal, legal, and academic contexts, while *asli* is the native form used across a much wider range of registers including everyday speech. The CONTEXT notes in P2a that *keaslian* (built on *asli*) is "the more common nominal form" for the concept of authenticity associated with *otentik*, and P3 Group 4 documents *otentik* as "more restricted to formal and legal registers" than *asli*.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** sungguh-sungguh (earnest, sincere in effort or feeling)
**Term B:** bohong-bohongan (pretend, make-believe)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** genuinely/earnestly (in all seriousness) vs. in pretense/make-believe (not for real)
**Description of Contrast:** *Sungguh-sungguh* indicates that an action is performed with full sincerity and genuine commitment; *bohong-bohongan* (the reduplicated *-an* form of *bohong*) describes something done in play or pretense rather than in earnest. The CONTEXT documents both forms in P2a, where *bohong-bohongan* is described as used for "something done in play or pretense rather than in earnest" and *sungguh-sungguh* as indicating "full sincerity and effort." The two reduplicated forms thus occupy opposite ends of a sincerity-versus-pretense axis.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** salah kaprah (widespread misconception accepted as correct)
**Term B:** benar (correct)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** commonly accepted error vs. correct
**Description of Contrast:** *Salah kaprah* describes an error or misconception that has become so widespread that it is accepted as correct by most people, creating a situation where the socially normalized form is factually wrong. The CONTEXT documents this in P1b as distinctive within the error-characterization group: unlike other *salah* compounds, *salah kaprah* describes error that has achieved the social status of correctness, placing it in tension with *benar* in a way that is not merely individual but systemic.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** kenyataan (reality, as it actually is)
**Term B:** jauh dari kenyataan (far from reality)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** reality vs. divorced from reality
**Description of Contrast:** *Kenyataan* refers to the state of things as they actually are; the fixed construction *jauh dari kenyataan* (far from reality) characterizes a claim, plan, or expectation as unrealistic or disconnected from actual conditions. The CONTEXT documents both in P2b, where *jauh dari kenyataan* is described as a fixed evaluative construction used in political commentary, journalism, and everyday evaluative discourse to mark a gap between a representation and actual reality.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
