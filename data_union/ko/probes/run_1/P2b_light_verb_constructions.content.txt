# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 lexical items. "Multi-word constructions" are understood here as syntactically separable expressions — periphrastic predicates, fixed collocations, idiomatic phrases, and syntactically-driven patterns — that are not reducible to single-word derivations (documented in P2a) or to simple compositional noun phrases. The boundary between a "construction" and a "collocation" is not always sharp; where a multi-word sequence shows fixedness, non-compositionality, or domain-specific conventionalization, it is included.

Constructions already documented in P1b (root expansion) are cross-referenced but not repeated in full. The focus here is on constructions not yet captured in prior steps.

Absence is recorded explicitly where no common construction is identified for a given base item.

## Constructions by P1 Base Item

### 1. Base Form: 진실 (jinsil)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 말하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul malhada
4. **English Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** The standard collocation for the act of truthful speech. Appears across all registers — legal testimony, interpersonal contexts, moral instruction. The object-verb structure is fully compositional but the collocation is highly conventionalized.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 밝히다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul balkhida
4. **English Translation:** to reveal the truth, to bring the truth to light
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in investigative, journalistic, and legal contexts to describe the act of uncovering or disclosing what is actually the case. 밝히다 (to illuminate, to clarify) is the preferred verb in this collocation; contrasts with 진실을 말하다 in that it implies prior concealment.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 숨기다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul sumgida
4. **English Translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe deliberate concealment of what is actually the case. Common in journalistic, legal, and narrative contexts. Contrasts with 진실을 밝히다 as the negative counterpart.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 외면하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul oemyeonhada
4. **English Translation:** to turn away from the truth, to ignore the truth, to avert one's eyes from the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to characterize willful avoidance of an uncomfortable or inconvenient truth. 외면하다 (to turn one's face away) carries a moral dimension of culpable avoidance. Common in political and ethical discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 왜곡하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul waegokada
4. **English Translation:** to distort the truth, to twist the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe the misrepresentation of facts. 왜곡하다 (to distort, to warp) implies deliberate or systematic misrepresentation rather than simple error.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실이 밝혀지다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-i balkhyeojida
4. **English Translation:** the truth comes to light, the truth is revealed
5. **Usage Notes:** Passive/resultative construction describing the emergence of truth, typically after a period of concealment or uncertainty. Common in narrative, journalistic, and legal contexts. The subject is 진실 rather than an agent.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실이 드러나다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-i deureonada
4. **English Translation:** the truth emerges, the truth is exposed
5. **Usage Notes:** Similar to 진실이 밝혀지다 but 드러나다 (to be exposed, to surface) carries a slightly stronger implication of something previously hidden becoming visible. Common in investigative and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실과 거짓을 가리다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-gwa geojit-eul garida
4. **English Translation:** to distinguish truth from falsehood, to separate truth from lies
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed collocation framing the epistemic task of discrimination between true and false. 가리다 (to distinguish, to determine) is the standard verb in this construction. Appears in legal, journalistic, and philosophical contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실의 순간
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-ui sungan
4. **English Translation:** moment of truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe a decisive moment when the true nature of something or someone is revealed. Documented in P1b; cross-referenced here as a fixed nominal construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실만이 살 길이다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-mani sal gil-ida
4. **English Translation:** truth is the only way to survive, only the truth will save us
5. **Usage Notes:** Proverbial expression used in moral and political discourse to assert that honesty is the only viable course of action in a difficult situation. The construction is formulaic and appears in public speech and media commentary.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 2. Base Form: 사실 (sasil)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 확인하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul hwagin-hada
4. **English Translation:** to verify the facts, to confirm the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard collocation for the act of checking whether reported information is accurate. Common in journalistic, administrative, and everyday contexts. The basis for the compound 사실확인 (fact-checking) documented in P1b.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실에 근거하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-e geungeo-hada
4. **English Translation:** to be based on facts, to be grounded in fact
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to characterize a claim, argument, or representation as having factual foundation. Common in academic, journalistic, and legal discourse. The prepositional phrase 사실에 (on/in fact) combines with the verb 근거하다 (to be grounded in).
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 왜곡하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul waegokada
4. **English Translation:** to distort the facts, to misrepresent the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe deliberate misrepresentation of factual information. Parallel in structure to 진실을 왜곡하다 but focuses on specific facts rather than truth as an abstract value.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 직시하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul jiksihada
4. **English Translation:** to face the facts squarely, to look the facts in the face
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the act of confronting reality without avoidance or self-deception. 직시하다 (to look directly at) implies a deliberate, unflinching engagement with what is actually the case. Common in motivational, political, and analytical discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실이 아니다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-i anida
4. **English Translation:** it is not a fact, that is not true, that is not the case
5. **Usage Notes:** The standard negation construction for factual claims. Extremely common across all registers as a denial or correction. Fully compositional but highly conventionalized as a denial formula.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실로 드러나다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-ro deureonada
4. **English Translation:** to turn out to be true, to be revealed as fact
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the confirmation of a claim that was previously uncertain or disputed. The construction marks a transition from alleged or suspected to established. Common in journalistic and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실 여부를 따지다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil yeobu-reul ttajida
4. **English Translation:** to examine whether something is factual, to question the factual basis of something
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in evaluative and investigative contexts to describe the act of scrutinizing whether a claim is actually true. 따지다 (to examine closely, to question) implies critical scrutiny. Common in journalistic and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 말하자면
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul malhajaMyeon
4. **English Translation:** to tell the truth, if I'm being honest, speaking factually
5. **Usage Notes:** Discourse-framing construction used to introduce a frank or corrective statement. Functions as a hedging or truth-marking device at the beginning of an utterance. Common in spoken and written registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 3. Base Form: 진짜 (jinjja)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜인지 가짜인지 모르다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja-inji gajja-inji moreuda
4. **English Translation:** not knowing whether it is real or fake, unable to tell the genuine from the counterfeit
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed interrogative-embedded construction used to express uncertainty about the authenticity of an item, person, or claim. The paired 진짜/가짜 structure is formulaic. Common in everyday speech and consumer contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜 같다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja gatda
4. **English Translation:** it seems real, it looks genuine, it appears to be the real thing
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to express the appearance of genuineness without full commitment to its being genuine. 같다 (to seem, to appear like) marks epistemic tentativeness. Common in everyday evaluative speech about objects, performances, and claims.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜로 받아들이다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja-ro badadeulida
4. **English Translation:** to take as genuine, to accept as real
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the cognitive act of treating something as authentic or true. Common in contexts of credulity, trust, or deliberate acceptance. The construction 진짜로 (as genuine/real) functions adverbially.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜 실력을 발휘하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja sillyeok-eul balhwihada
4. **English Translation:** to show one's true ability, to demonstrate one's real skill
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the display of genuine competence as opposed to performance or pretense. 진짜 functions as an attributive modifier emphasizing authenticity of the quality being displayed. Common in sports, professional, and educational contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on 진짜:** Beyond the constructions above, 진짜 functions very productively as an intensifying adverb (진짜 좋다, "really good"; 진짜 힘들다, "really difficult") across all registers. This adverbial use is fully productive and not a fixed construction; it is noted here but not enumerated as individual constructions.

### 4. Base Form: 가짜 (gajja)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜로 판명되다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-ro panmyeongdoeda
4. **English Translation:** to be determined to be fake, to be found to be counterfeit
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal, journalistic, and commercial contexts to describe the official or investigative determination that an item or claim is not genuine. 판명되다 (to be determined, to be found to be) marks an authoritative conclusion.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜를 진짜로 속이다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-reul jinjja-ro sogida
4. **English Translation:** to pass off a fake as genuine, to deceive by presenting a counterfeit as real
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of fraudulent misrepresentation of an inauthentic item as genuine. The construction 가짜를 진짜로 (fake as real) is a fixed contrastive frame. Common in consumer fraud and art forgery contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜 뉴스를 퍼뜨리다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja nyuseu-reul peotteurida
4. **English Translation:** to spread fake news, to disseminate false information
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in contemporary media and political discourse to describe the deliberate circulation of fabricated or misleading news content. 퍼뜨리다 (to spread, to disseminate) is the standard verb in this construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜인 줄 알다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-in jul alda
4. **English Translation:** to know that it is fake, to be aware that it is not genuine
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe epistemic awareness of inauthenticity. The construction -인 줄 알다 (to know that something is ~) is a standard epistemic frame. Common in legal contexts where knowledge of falsity is relevant to culpability.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 5. Base Form: 정확 (jeonghwak)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확히 말하면
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwaki malhamyeon
4. **English Translation:** to be precise, strictly speaking, to put it exactly
5. **Usage Notes:** Discourse-framing construction used to introduce a more precise or corrective formulation of a claim. Functions as a precision marker at the beginning of a clause. Common in academic, journalistic, and everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확한 정보를 제공하다
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwakan jeongbo-reul jegonghada
4. **English Translation:** to provide accurate information
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard collocation in journalistic, administrative, and public communication contexts. 정확한 (accurate, attributive form of 정확하다) modifies 정보 (information); 제공하다 (to provide) is the standard verb.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확한 사실을 파악하다
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwakan sasil-eul paakada
4. **English Translation:** to grasp the accurate facts, to ascertain the precise facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in investigative and analytical contexts to describe the act of establishing what is precisely the case. 파악하다 (to grasp, to ascertain) implies active cognitive effort. Common in journalistic and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확도가 높다 / 정확도가 낮다
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwakdo-ga nopda / jeonghwakdo-ga natda
4. **English Translation:** to have high accuracy / to have low accuracy
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard evaluative constructions for assessing the degree of accuracy of a measurement, test, or system. 정확도 (accuracy rate) combines with the evaluative predicates 높다 (high) and 낮다 (low). Common in scientific, technical, and testing contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 6. Base Form: 올바르다 (olbareuda)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 올바르다 (olbareuda)
2. **Example:** 올바른 길을 가다
3. **Transliteration:** olbareun gil-eul gada
4. **English Translation:** to go the right path, to follow the correct course
5. **Usage Notes:** Idiomatic construction used in moral and educational discourse to describe living or acting in accordance with correct values. 길 (path, way) is used metaphorically. Common in religious, educational, and motivational contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 올바르다 (olbareuda)
2. **Example:** 올바른 판단을 내리다
3. **Transliteration:** olbareun pandan-eul naerida
4. **English Translation:** to make a correct judgment, to reach the right decision
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in evaluative and deliberative contexts to describe the act of arriving at a sound or correct judgment. 판단을 내리다 (to render a judgment) is a fixed collocation; 올바른 modifies the quality of that judgment.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 올바르다 (olbareuda)
2. **Example:** 올바른 자세를 갖추다
3. **Transliteration:** olbareun jase-reul gatchuda
4. **English Translation:** to have the right attitude, to adopt the correct posture/stance
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in educational and moral discourse to describe the adoption of a correct orientation — both physical (posture) and attitudinal (stance toward a task or value). 자세 (posture, attitude) is used in both literal and figurative senses.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 올바르다 (olbareuda)
2. **Example:** 올바르게 이해하다
3. **Transliteration:** olbareuge ihaehada
4. **English Translation:** to understand correctly, to have the right understanding
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in instructional and analytical contexts to describe correct comprehension of a concept, text, or situation. Common in educational and academic discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 7. Base Form: 맞다 (matda)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 맞다 (matda)
2. **Example:** 맞는 말이다
3. **Transliteration:** manneun mal-ida
4. **English Translation:** that is a correct statement, that is right, what you say is true
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed construction used to affirm the correctness of a statement just made. 맞는 (correct, attributive form) modifies 말 (words, speech); 이다 is the copula. Extremely common in conversation as an affirmation response.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 맞다 (matda)
2. **Example:** 맞는지 틀린지 모르다
3. **Transliteration:** manneun-ji teullin-ji moreuda
4. **English Translation:** not knowing whether it is right or wrong, unable to tell if it is correct
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed paired construction expressing uncertainty about the correctness of a claim or answer. The 맞다/틀리다 pairing is formulaic. Common in everyday speech and educational contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 맞다 (matda)
2. **Example:** 맞고 틀리고를 떠나서
3. **Transliteration:** matgo teulligo-reul tteonaseo
4. **English Translation:** setting aside the question of right or wrong, beyond the issue of correctness
5. **Usage Notes:** Discourse construction used to bracket the question of factual correctness in order to address a different dimension of an issue. Common in deliberative and interpersonal discourse when shifting from factual to relational or evaluative considerations.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 맞다 (matda)
2. **Example:** 딱 맞다
3. **Transliteration:** ttak matda
4. **English Translation:** to be exactly right, to fit perfectly, to be a perfect match
5. **Usage Notes:** The intensifier 딱 (exactly, precisely) combines with 맞다 to assert perfect correspondence or fit. Used across factual, physical, and evaluative contexts. Common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 맞다 (matda)
2. **Example:** 맞아떨어지다
3. **Transliteration:** majatteoreojida
4. **English Translation:** to fit together perfectly, to add up, to check out
5. **Usage Notes:** Compound verb formed from 맞다 (to fit/match) + 떨어지다 (to fall/drop), used to describe a situation where all elements align or a prediction proves exactly correct. Common in analytical and investigative contexts: 계산이 맞아떨어지다 (the calculation checks out).
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 8. Base Form: 틀리다 (teullida)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 틀리다 (teullida)
2. **Example:** 틀린 정보를 퍼뜨리다
3. **Transliteration:** teullin jeongbo-reul peotteurida
4. **English Translation:** to spread incorrect information, to disseminate wrong information
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and media literacy contexts to describe the circulation of inaccurate information. 틀린 (incorrect, attributive form) modifies 정보 (information). Distinct from 거짓 정보 in that 틀리다 does not necessarily imply deliberate deception.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 틀리다 (teullida)
2. **Example:** 틀려도 괜찮다
3. **Transliteration:** teullyeodo gwaenchanda
4. **English Translation:** it is okay to be wrong, being wrong is acceptable
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in educational and interpersonal contexts to encourage risk-taking and reduce fear of error. The concessive construction -아도/어도 (even if) combined with 괜찮다 (to be okay) is a standard reassurance formula. Common in pedagogical discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 틀리다 (teullida)
2. **Example:** 뭔가 틀렸다
3. **Transliteration:** mwonga teullyeotda
4. **English Translation:** something is wrong, something is off
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to express a sense that something is incorrect or amiss, often without specifying what. 뭔가 (something) combined with 틀렸다 (past/resultative form of 틀리다) is a common colloquial construction expressing vague unease or suspicion.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 틀리다 (teullida)
2. **Example:** 다르다고 틀린 게 아니다
3. **Transliteration:** dareudago teullin ge anida
4. **English Translation:** being different does not mean being wrong, difference is not the same as incorrectness
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed construction used to distinguish difference from error, addressing the common conflation of 다르다 (to be different) and 틀리다 (to be wrong) in colloquial Korean. Appears in educational, social, and media discourse on tolerance and diversity.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 9. Base Form: 틀림없다 (teullim-eopda)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 틀림없다 (teullim-eopda)
2. **Example:** 틀림없이 그럴 것이다
3. **Transliteration:** teullim-eopsi geureol geosida
4. **English Translation:** it will certainly be so, that is undoubtedly what will happen
5. **Usage Notes:** The adverbial form 틀림없이 (documented in P2a) combines with the future/inferential construction -ㄹ 것이다 to assert strong confidence about a future or inferred state. Common in everyday speech and formal writing.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 틀림없다 (teullim-eopda)
2. **Example:** 그게 틀림없다
3. **Transliteration:** geuge teullim-eopda
4. **English Translation:** that is certainly the case, there is no doubt about that
5. **Usage Notes:** Direct predicative use of 틀림없다 to assert certainty about a specific claim. The subject is a demonstrative or noun phrase; the predicate asserts the absence of any possibility of error. Common in spoken and written assertion.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on 틀림없다:** The primary constructions involving this item are adverbial (틀림없이 + predicate) and predicative (X가/이 틀림없다). No idiomatic or non-compositional constructions beyond these are identified.

### 10. Base Form: 정직 (jeongjik)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 정직 (jeongjik)
2. **Example:** 정직하게 말하다
3. **Transliteration:** jeongjik-age malhada
4. **English Translation:** to speak honestly, to tell the truth straightforwardly
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard collocation for honest speech. 정직하게 (honestly, adverbial form) modifies 말하다 (to speak). Common across all registers as a description of or instruction for honest communication.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 정직 (jeongjik)
2. **Example:** 정직이 최선의 방책이다
3. **Transliteration:** jeongjik-i choesonui bangchaek-ida
4. **English Translation:** honesty is the best policy
5. **Usage Notes:** Proverbial construction, a calque of the English proverb. Used in moral and educational discourse to assert the practical and ethical value of honesty. Appears in written and spoken registers, particularly in educational contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 정직 (jeongjik)
2. **Example:** 정직한 사람으로 알려지다
3. **Transliteration:** jeongjikan saram-euro allyeojida
4. **English Translation:** to be known as an honest person, to have a reputation for honesty
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe a person's social reputation for honesty. The construction -으로 알려지다 (to be known as) is a standard reputational frame. Common in character evaluation and biographical discourse.

1. **Base Form:** 근거 (geungeo)
2. **Example:** 충분한 근거가 있다
3. **Transliteration:** chungbunhan geungeo-ga itda
4. **English Translation:** there is sufficient evidence, there are adequate grounds
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal and academic contexts to assert that a claim is adequately supported by evidence. 충분한 (sufficient) modifies 근거; 있다 asserts its existence. The standard positive evidential construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

3. **Transliteration:** ~e geungeuhan pandan
4. **English Translation:** a judgment based on ~, a decision grounded in ~
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed attributive construction using the participial form 근거한 (based on, grounded in) to characterize a judgment or decision as having a specified evidential or rational foundation. Common in legal, academic, and policy discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
