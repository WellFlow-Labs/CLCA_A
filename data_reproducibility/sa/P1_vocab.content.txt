# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Lexical Items

1. **सत्य** / *satya*
   - Literal meaning: being, existing; from √as "to be"
   - Typical English translations: truth, true, real, genuine
   - Used to denote both factual accuracy and moral/ethical truthfulness in speech and conduct. Appears extensively in philosophical, legal, and religious contexts as both noun and adjective.
   - [CORE]

2. **असत्य** / *asatya*
   - Literal meaning: non-being, not existing; a- (negative) + satya
   - Typical English translations: untruth, false, untrue
   - Negation of satya, used for factually incorrect statements or morally wrong speech. Common in ethical and philosophical discourse.
   - [CORE]

3. **ऋत** / *ṛta*
   - Literal meaning: fitted, proper, right; from √ṛ "to go, move"
   - Typical English translations: cosmic order, truth, right, proper
   - Denotes cosmic and moral order, correctness, and truth in Vedic contexts. Represents both natural law and ethical rightness.
   - [CORE]

4. **अनृत** / *anṛta*
   - Literal meaning: not-ṛta; a- (negative) + ṛta
   - Typical English translations: untruth, falsehood, wrong
   - Opposite of ṛta, indicating disorder, falsehood, or moral wrongness. Primarily found in Vedic and classical texts.
   - [CORE]

5. **तथ्य** / *tathya*
   - Literal meaning: being so, corresponding to fact; from tathā "thus, so"
   - Typical English translations: true, real, actual, factual
   - Emphasizes correspondence to actual state of affairs or facts. Used in philosophical and ordinary discourse for factual accuracy.
   - [CORE]

6. **मृषा** / *mṛṣā*
   - Literal meaning: falsely, vainly; adverbial form
   - Typical English translations: falsely, in vain, untruly
   - Functions primarily as adverb meaning "falsely" or "in vain." Commonly appears in compounds and expressions denoting false speech or action.
   - [CORE]

7. **प्रमाण** / *pramāṇa*
   - Literal meaning: measure, standard; pra- (forth) + √mā "to measure"
   - Typical English translations: valid means of knowledge, proof, authority, standard
   - Technical term in epistemology for valid sources of knowledge (perception, inference, testimony, etc.). Central to philosophical discussions of truth and verification.
   - [CORE]

8. **प्रमा** / *pramā*
   - Literal meaning: correct knowledge; from pra- + √mā
   - Typical English translations: valid cognition, true knowledge, correct understanding
   - Denotes epistemically valid or true knowledge as opposed to error. Technical term in Indian epistemology and logic.
   - [CORE]

9. **भ्रम** / *bhrama*
   - Literal meaning: wandering, confusion; from √bhram "to wander"
   - Typical English translations: error, illusion, false notion, mistake
   - Indicates cognitive error or mistaken perception. Used in epistemological contexts for false cognition or illusion.
   - [CORE]

10. **यथार्थ** / *yathārtha*
    - Literal meaning: according to the object/meaning; yathā "as" + artha "object, meaning"
    - Typical English translations: true to fact, accurate, corresponding to reality
    - Denotes correspondence between cognition and object, or statement and fact. Common in philosophical discussions of truth.
    - [CORE]

11. **धर्म** / *dharma*
    - Literal meaning: that which holds/sustains; from √dhṛ "to hold, support"
    - Typical English translations: duty, righteousness, law, proper conduct, religion
    - Multivalent term indicating right conduct, moral law, duty, and cosmic order. Central to ethical, legal, and religious evaluation.
    - [CORE]

12. **अधर्म** / *adharma*
    - Literal meaning: non-dharma; a- (negative) + dharma
    - Typical English translations: unrighteousness, wrong conduct, injustice
    - Negation of dharma, indicating morally wrong or improper conduct. Used in ethical and legal contexts.
    - [CORE]

13. **साधु** / *sādhu*
    - Literal meaning: straight, right; from √sādh "to go straight to, accomplish"
    - Typical English translations: good, proper, correct, virtuous, well done
    - Indicates correctness, propriety, or moral goodness. Used as exclamation of approval and as adjective for proper conduct or correct statements.
    - [CORE]

14. **न्याय** / *nyāya*
    - Literal meaning: going into, method; from ni- + √i "to go"
    - Typical English translations: justice, logic, proper method, reasoning
    - Denotes both logical reasoning/correct inference and justice/fairness. Name of major philosophical school focused on logic and epistemology.
    - [CORE]

15. **युक्त** / *yukta*
    - Literal meaning: joined, yoked, proper; past participle of √yuj "to join"
    - Typical English translations: proper, fitting, appropriate, right, reasonable
    - Indicates what is fitting, appropriate, or logically sound. Used for both practical propriety and logical correctness.
    - [CORE]

16. **अयुक्त** / *ayukta*
    - Literal meaning: not joined, improper; a- (negative) + yukta
    - Typical English translations: improper, unfitting, inappropriate, unreasonable
    - Negation of yukta, indicating what is unfitting or logically unsound.
    - [CORE]

17. **सम्यक्** / *samyak*
    - Literal meaning: completely together; sam- (together) + √añc "to bend"
    - Typical English translations: correct, right, proper, complete, true
    - Adverbial and adjectival form indicating completeness and correctness. Common in Buddhist contexts (e.g., samyag-dṛṣṭi "right view").
    - [CORE]

18. **मिथ्या** / *mithyā*
    - Literal meaning: falsely, wrongly; adverbial form
    - Typical English translations: falsely, wrongly, erroneously, illusory
    - Functions as adverb and in compounds to indicate falsehood or error. Central term in Vedānta for illusory appearance.
    - [CORE]

19. **प्रामाण्य** / *prāmāṇya*
    - Literal meaning: state of being a pramāṇa; abstract noun from pramāṇa
    - Typical English translations: validity, authoritativeness, reliability
    - Abstract noun denoting the property of being a valid source of knowledge. Technical term in epistemological discussions.
    - [CORE]

20. **विपर्यय** / *viparyaya*
    - Literal meaning: reversal, inversion; vi- (apart) + pari- (around) + √i "to go"
    - Typical English translations: error, perversion, mistaken cognition, reversal
    - Denotes cognitive error or mistaken understanding. One of five types of mental modifications in Yoga philosophy.
    - [CORE]

21. **अभास** / *ābhāsa*
    - Literal meaning: appearance, semblance; ā- (toward) + √bhās "to shine"
    - Typical English translations: appearance, semblance, false appearance, fallacy
    - Indicates mere appearance as opposed to reality, or fallacious reasoning. Used in logic for pseudo-inference.
    - [CORE]

22. **सदृश** / *sadṛśa*
    - Literal meaning: similar, like; sa- (with) + √dṛś "to see"
    - Typical English translations: similar, like, resembling, analogous
    - Denotes similarity or resemblance between objects or concepts. Used in comparative and analogical reasoning.
    - [CORE]

23. **रूप** / *rūpa*
    - Literal meaning: form, appearance; from √rūp "to form"
    - Typical English translations: form, appearance, shape, nature, character
    - Indicates visible form or essential nature of something. Used in philosophical contexts for both appearance and reality.
    - [CORE]

24. **स्वरूप** / *svarūpa*
    - Literal meaning: own-form; sva "own" + rūpa "form"
    - Typical English translations: own nature, essential form, true character
    - Denotes the essential or true nature of something as opposed to accidental properties. Common in philosophical discourse.
    - [CORE]

25. **तत्त्व** / *tattva*
    - Literal meaning: thatness, true principle; tat "that" + -tva (abstract suffix)
    - Typical English translations: reality, truth, principle, essence, category
    - Indicates fundamental reality or true principle. Used for metaphysical categories and essential truths.
    - [CORE]

26. **वस्तु** / *vastu*
    - Literal meaning: thing, object, reality; from √vas "to dwell"
    - Typical English translations: thing, object, reality, substance, fact
    - Denotes real object or actual state of affairs. Used to distinguish reality from appearance or conception.
    - [CORE]

27. **अलीक** / *alīka*
    - Literal meaning: untrue, false; etymology uncertain
    - Typical English translations: false, untrue, unreal, counterfeit
    - Indicates falsity or unreality, often of statements or appearances. Less common than satya/asatya but well-attested.
    - [MARGINAL]

28. **कूट** / *kūṭa*
    - Literal meaning: false, fraudulent; also "peak, heap"
    - Typical English translations: false, counterfeit, fraudulent, deceptive
    - Used for deliberate deception or counterfeit objects. Common in legal and ethical contexts for fraud.
    - [CORE]

29. **छल** / *chala*
    - Literal meaning: fraud, deception; from √chal "to move"
    - Typical English translations: fraud, deception, quibbling, fallacy
    - Denotes deceptive argumentation or fraudulent behavior. Technical term in Nyāya logic for sophistical refutation.
    - [CORE]

30. **उपपत्ति** / *upapatti*
    - Literal meaning: occurrence, arising; upa- (near) + √pad "to fall, occur"
    - Typical English translations: logical possibility, coherence, reasonableness, proof
    - Indicates logical coherence or rational justification. Used in philosophical contexts for what is rationally demonstrable.
    - [CORE]
