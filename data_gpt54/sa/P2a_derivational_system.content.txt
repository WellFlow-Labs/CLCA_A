## 1. Base Form: **सत्य**  
### Derived Form: **सत्यम्**
**Transliteration:** satyam  
**Literal Breakdown:** neuter form of *satya*  
**Natural English translation:** truth; true; it is true  
**Usage description:** Commonly used as a noun meaning “truth,” and also in predicative use (“it is true”). It is widespread in literary, religious, and philosophical Sanskrit.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यता**
**Transliteration:** satyatā  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + abstract suffix *-tā*  
**Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; correctness  
**Usage description:** This abstract noun denotes the quality of being true. It is especially common in reflective, scholastic, and evaluative discussion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यवचन**
**Transliteration:** satyavacana  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vacana* “speech, utterance”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful speech; true statement  
**Usage description:** Used for speech that accords with fact or truth. It appears especially in ethical and normative contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यवादिन्**
**Transliteration:** satyavādin  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* “speaker”  
**Natural English translation:** truth-speaker; truthful person  
**Usage description:** A common descriptive and ethical term for one who speaks the truth. It occurs in narrative, didactic, and religious contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यवादी**
**Transliteration:** satyavādī  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vādī* “speaker, one who says”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful; one who tells the truth  
**Usage description:** Widely used for a truthful person, especially in later and Sanskritized usage. It overlaps in function with *satyavādin*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यभाषण**
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣaṇa  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *bhāṣaṇa* “speaking”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful speaking  
**Usage description:** Used in moral and didactic contexts where truthfulness in speech is treated as a virtue. It emphasizes the act or practice of speaking truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यभाषिन्**
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣin  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *bhāṣin* “speaker”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful speaker  
**Usage description:** Refers to a person who habitually speaks truth. It is used in praise and character evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यसंध**
**Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* “firm, faithful to”  
**Natural English translation:** true to one’s word; faithful to truth  
**Usage description:** Common in epic and kāvya for a person who keeps promises and remains faithful to truth. It often has a strong ethical and heroic coloring.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यपरायण**
**Transliteration:** satyaparāyaṇa  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *parāyaṇa* “devoted to”  
**Natural English translation:** devoted to truth  
**Usage description:** Used for a person deeply committed to truthfulness. It is common in ethical, religious, and laudatory discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यनिष्ठ**
**Transliteration:** satyaniṣṭha  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *niṣṭha* “steadfast, grounded in”  
**Natural English translation:** steadfast in truth; truthful  
**Usage description:** Describes stable commitment to truth rather than a single true utterance. It is common in moral and character evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यशील**
**Transliteration:** satyaśīla  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *śīla* “conduct, character”  
**Natural English translation:** of truthful character  
**Usage description:** Used for a person whose disposition or conduct is truthful. It appears in ethical and literary description.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यपालन**
**Transliteration:** satyapālana  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *pālana* “keeping, maintaining”  
**Natural English translation:** observance of truth; keeping truth  
**Usage description:** Refers to maintaining truthfulness or keeping one’s truthful commitment. It is used in ethical and religious contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **सत्यप्रतिज्ञ**
**Transliteration:** satyapratijña  
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijña* “vow, pledge”  
**Natural English translation:** true to one’s vow; whose pledge is true  
**Usage description:** Common in epic and literary usage for one whose promise is fulfilled. It often shades into reliability and vow-keeping.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **असत्य**
**Transliteration:** asatya  
**Literal Breakdown:** privative *a-* + *satya*  
**Natural English translation:** false; untrue  
**Usage description:** The standard antonym of *satya*, used for falsehood in speech, character, or representation. It is common across ethical and descriptive contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **असत्यवचन**
**Transliteration:** asatyavacana  
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* + *satya* + *vacana*  
**Natural English translation:** false statement; untruthful speech  
**Usage description:** Used for false utterance or lying, especially in ethical and juridical contexts. It directly contrasts with *satyavacana*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Derived Form: **असत्यवादिन्**
**Transliteration:** asatyavādin  
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* + *satya* + *vādin*  
**Natural English translation:** speaker of falsehood; liar  
**Usage description:** Used for one who speaks falsely. It appears in moral, narrative, and polemical contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
