# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: English (en)

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis documents only **attested multi-word constructions** that are conventionalized or semi-fixed in English usage. English shows significant variation in how truth/falsity concepts are expressed through multi-word patterns, with some lexical items participating in many fixed expressions and others in few or none.

The constructions are organized by base form from P1, with explicit notation where no common constructions exist.

---

## BASE: true

### Construction 1: come true
- **Base Form**: true
- **Example**: Her dreams came true.
- **Transliteration**: /kʌm truː/
- **Translation**: be realized, be fulfilled
- **Usage**: Fixed verbal expression denoting realization of hopes, predictions, or desires. Very common in narrative and aspirational contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Construction 2: hold true
- **Base Form**: true
- **Example**: This principle holds true across cultures.
- **Transliteration**: /həʊld truː/
- **Translation**: remain valid, continue to apply
- **Usage**: Fixed verbal expression denoting continued validity or applicability. Common in academic and analytical discourse.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Construction 3: ring true
- **Base Form**: true
- **Example**: His explanation rings true.
- **Transliteration**: /rɪŋ truː/
- **Translation**: seem genuine, sound convincing
- **Usage**: Fixed verbal expression denoting impression of authenticity or plausibility. Common in evaluative contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Construction 4: true to form

- **Transliteration**: /truː tə fɔːm/
- **Translation**: as expected, characteristically
- **Usage**: Fixed adverbial phrase marking behavior consistent with established pattern. Common in narrative and character description.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### Construction 5: true to life
- **Base Form**: true

- **Status**: [CORE]

### Construction 6: true to one's word

### Construction 7: too good to be true
