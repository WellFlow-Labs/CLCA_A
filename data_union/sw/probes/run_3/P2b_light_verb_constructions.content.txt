# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — built on P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions already fully documented in P1b (root expansion) are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The focus is on constructions not yet captured in prior steps. Absence is recorded explicitly where no common construction is identified for a given base form or construction type.

---

## BASE FORM: **kweli**

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli inaumiza.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli inaumiza.
4. **English translation:** The truth hurts. / Truth is painful.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial statement used in moral and interpersonal discourse to acknowledge that honest statements can cause pain. Functions as a standalone proverb and as a framing device before delivering unwelcome information. Distinct from *ukweli unauma* (P1b) in using *kweli* rather than *ukweli* and the present tense *-umiza* (causative) rather than *-uma* (stative).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Sema kweli, hata kama inakudhuru.
3. **Transliteration:** Sema kweli, hata kama inakudhuru.
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth, even if it harms you.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial or moral imperative construction used in ethical instruction and religious discourse. The conditional clause *hata kama inakudhuru* ("even if it harms you") is a fixed extension of the truth-telling imperative and appears in this form in moral literature and sermons.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli haina budi kujulikana.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli haina budi kujulikana.
4. **English translation:** The truth must inevitably become known. / Truth will out.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction using *haina budi* ("it cannot avoid," "it must inevitably") to assert the inescapability of truth becoming known. Appears in moral, legal, and narrative discourse as an expression of epistemic inevitability.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli ni mchungu.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli ni mchungu.
4. **English translation:** Truth is bitter.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial copular construction characterizing truth by its unpleasant quality. *Mchungu* (bitter) is a common evaluative term in Swahili proverbial discourse. Used in moral instruction and as a framing device before delivering difficult information.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli haiozi.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli haiozi.
4. **English translation:** Truth does not rot. / Truth does not decay.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the permanence or indestructibility of truth. *-oza* (to rot, to decay) is used metaphorically. Appears in moral and religious discourse to assert that truth endures regardless of attempts to suppress it.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli hushinda uongo.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli hushinda uongo.
4. **English translation:** Truth defeats falsehood. / Truth overcomes lies.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction using the habitual marker *hu-* to assert the general principle that truth prevails over falsehood. Appears in moral, religious, and political discourse. Related to *ukweli daima hushinda* (P1b) but with the explicit object *uongo* making the contrast explicit.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
3. **Transliteration:** Kwa kweli, sijui.
4. **English translation:** Honestly, I don't know. / Truly, I don't know.
5. **Usage notes:** *Kwa kweli* functioning as a sentence-initial discourse marker expressing sincerity or honest admission. Already documented in P1b as an adverbial phrase; noted here as a specific pragmatic construction — the honest-admission frame — which is highly frequent in spoken Swahili.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ni kweli kabisa kwamba...
3. **Transliteration:** Ni kweli kabisa kwamba...
4. **English translation:** It is absolutely true that...
5. **Usage notes:** A framing construction used to introduce a strongly asserted proposition. The complementizer *kwamba* ("that") introduces the embedded clause. This pattern is highly productive and appears across formal and informal registers as a strong truth-assertion frame.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Je, ni kweli?
3. **Transliteration:** Je, ni kweli?
4. **English translation:** Is it true? / Really?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction using the question particle *je* and the copular truth assertion *ni kweli*. Used in spoken Swahili to express surprise, seek confirmation, or challenge a claim. Extremely frequent in conversation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu.
4. **English translation:** Truly, this is amazing. / It really is a wonder.
5. **Usage notes:** *Kweli kweli* functioning as a sentence-initial intensifying discourse marker expressing genuine surprise or emphasis. Already documented in P1 and P1b; noted here as a specific pragmatic construction — the emphatic-surprise frame — distinct from its use as a simple adverbial intensifier.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ukweli ni kwamba...
3. **Transliteration:** Ukweli ni kwamba...
4. **English translation:** The truth is that... / The fact is that...
5. **Usage notes:** A highly productive framing construction used to introduce a factual or corrective statement. The pattern *ukweli ni kwamba* + embedded clause is a standard discourse frame in journalism, formal speech, and everyday argumentation for presenting what the speaker asserts to be the actual facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...
3. **Transliteration:** Ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...
4. **English translation:** The truth of the matter is that... / The reality of things is that...
5. **Usage notes:** An extended version of the *ukweli ni kwamba* frame, with *wa mambo* ("of things/matters") adding specificity. Used in formal discourse, journalism, and deliberative contexts to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement about actual conditions.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kwa mujibu wa ukweli...
3. **Transliteration:** Kwa mujibu wa ukweli...
4. **English translation:** According to the truth... / In accordance with the facts...
5. **Usage notes:** A formal prepositional frame using *kwa mujibu wa* ("according to," "in accordance with") to introduce a statement grounded in factual accuracy. Appears in legal, journalistic, and formal administrative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kwa upande wa ukweli...
3. **Transliteration:** Kwa upande wa ukweli...
4. **English translation:** On the side of truth... / From the standpoint of truth...
5. **Usage notes:** A framing construction used in argumentative and moral discourse to position a statement as aligned with truth. *Kwa upande wa* ("on the side of") is a productive prepositional frame in Swahili argumentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 15**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.
3. **Transliteration:** Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.
4. **English translation:** To tell the truth, I didn't like that. / Honestly, I didn't like it.
5. **Usage notes:** *Kusema ukweli* functioning as a sentence-initial parenthetical frame equivalent to English "to tell the truth" or "honestly." The infinitive phrase is used as a discourse marker signaling honest admission. Extremely frequent in spoken and written Swahili.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele.
3. **Transliteration:** Ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele.
4. **English translation:** A hidden truth cannot be hidden forever.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction combining the relative clause *uliofichwa* ("which was hidden," already documented in P1b) with the stative negative *haufichiki milele* ("cannot be hidden forever"). Used in moral and investigative discourse to assert the ultimate discoverability of suppressed truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Niambie ukweli, usiniambie uongo.
3. **Transliteration:** Niambie ukweli, usiniambie uongo.
4. **English translation:** Tell me the truth, don't tell me lies.
5. **Usage notes:** A paired imperative construction contrasting *ukweli* and *uongo* in a direct address. The negative imperative *usiniambie uongo* reinforces the positive command. Used in interpersonal confrontation, moral instruction, and narrative dialogue.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Kweli au uongo — chagua moja.
3. **Transliteration:** Kweli au uongo — chagua moja.
4. **English translation:** True or false — choose one.
5. **Usage notes:** An educational and logical construction presenting a binary choice between truth and falsehood. Already documented in P1b as a binary evaluative frame; noted here as a specific instructional construction with the imperative *chagua moja* ("choose one"), appearing in examination and quiz formats.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Hii si nusu kweli — ni uongo mtupu.
3. **Transliteration:** Hii si nusu kweli — ni uongo mtupu.
4. **English translation:** This is not a half-truth — it is an outright lie.
5. **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction opposing *nusu kweli* (half-truth, documented in P1b) with *uongo mtupu* (outright lie, documented in P1). Used in argumentative and journalistic discourse to escalate a charge from partial deception to complete falsehood.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Example:** Mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo.
3. **Transliteration:** Mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo.
4. **English translation:** A truthful person does not tell lies.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction combining *mtu wa kweli* (a truthful/genuine person, documented in P1b) with the habitual negative *hasemi uongo* ("does not tell lies"). Used in moral instruction and character evaluation to define the truthful person by their conduct.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: **uongo**

---

**Construction 26**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Kusema uongo ni dhambi.
3. **Transliteration:** Kusema uongo ni dhambi.
4. **English translation:** Telling lies is a sin. / Lying is a sin.
5. **Usage notes:** A copular construction characterizing the act of lying as a moral/religious transgression. *Dhambi* (sin, from Arabic *dhanb*) is the standard term for sin in Swahili religious discourse. Used in religious instruction and moral condemnation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
3. **Transliteration:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
4. **English translation:** A lie has short legs. / Lies don't travel far.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting that lies are eventually exposed because they cannot sustain themselves. *Miguu mifupi* ("short legs") is a fixed metaphor in this proverb. Appears in moral instruction and everyday discourse as a warning against deception.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Uongo ni adui wa amani.
3. **Transliteration:** Uongo ni adui wa amani.
4. **English translation:** Falsehood is an enemy of peace.
5. **Usage notes:** A copular construction characterizing deception as destructive to social harmony. Used in moral, political, and religious discourse. The pattern *X ni adui wa Y* ("X is an enemy of Y") is productive in Swahili moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Amejaa uongo.
3. **Transliteration:** Amejaa uongo.
4. **English translation:** He/she is full of lies. / He/she is completely dishonest.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using the stative perfect *amejaa* ("he/she is full of") to characterize a person as thoroughly dishonest. *-jaa* (to be full of) is a productive evaluative verb in Swahili character assessment. Used in moral condemnation across everyday and literary registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 30**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Example:** Uongo wake ulidhihirika.
3. **Transliteration:** Uongo wake ulidhihirika.
4. **English translation:** His/her lie was exposed. / His/her deception became evident.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *uongo* with *kudhihirika* (to become evident, documented in P2a under *dhahiri*) to describe the exposure of a deception. Used in narrative and journalistic contexts to describe the unmasking of falsehood.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**
1. **Base Form:** uongo

**Construction 32**

4. **English translation:** To spread lies, to disseminate falsehood
5. **Usage notes:** A verbal construction using *kueneza* ("to spread, to disseminate") with *uongo* as object. Used in journalistic, legal, and moral discourse to describe the deliberate propagation of false information. Appears in media criticism and legal proceedings concerning defamation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 33**

6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 36**
