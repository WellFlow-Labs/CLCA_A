# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Quechua (Southern: Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka)

---

**Scope note:** Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka). Examples drawn from attested forms documented in P1–P3. Orthography follows the normalized three-vowel system.

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Predicate-nominal or predicate-adjectival construction: evaluative adjective (cheqa / cheqaq / llulla / chiqan / allin / sut'i / kikin) + direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi*, with copula *kay* absent or implicit in non-verbal predication. The evidential clitic attaches to the predicate and marks the speaker's direct epistemic commitment. No copula is required in the present tense. The construction is also attested as evaluative adjective (cheqa / llulla / allin / pantasqa) + noun, in prenominal position, where no copula is required and the evidential enclitic attaches to the predicate or focused element, not to the modifier.

### Examples
- **Cheqam** → cheqa-m → "It is true / That is correct"
- **Llullam** → llulla-m → "It is a lie / That is false"
- **Cheqam kay.** → cheqa-m kay → "This is true."
- **Llullam chay.** → llulla-m chay → "That is a lie / That is false."
- **cheqa qelqa** → *cheqa qelqa* → 'authentic document / genuine text'
- **llulla simi** → *llulla simi* → 'false word / lying speech'

### Notes
Quechua non-verbal predication requires no overt copula in the present tense; the evidential clitic *-m/-mi* is the primary marker of assertive force. The construction *cheqam* is a complete predication. For third-person past or reported contexts, the copula *kasqa* appears: *cheqa kasqam* ("it turned out to be true").

The direct evidential clitic *-mi/-m* is obligatory in assertive contexts; its attachment to the predicate signals that the speaker has direct knowledge of the truth of the assertion. The construction is attested for *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *llulla*, *chiqan*, *allin*, *sut'i*, and *kikin*.

Quechua adjectives precede the noun they modify. The attributive construction is structurally identical to a simple juxtaposition; no linking morphology is required. *Cheqaq* (with agentive *-q*) is preferred over bare *cheqa* in some attributive positions (see §7).

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
*Mana* (negation) + predicate adjective + negative suffix *-chu*. The direct evidential *-n* optionally attaches to *mana*. Both poles of the truth/falsehood opposition are negatable by this construction. In attributive position (prenominal), *-chu* does not appear; the negation is expressed by *mana* alone preceding the adjective-noun sequence.

### Examples
- **Manan cheqachu** → mana-n cheqa-chu → "It is not true / That is not correct"
- **Manan llullachu** → mana-n llulla-chu → "It is not false / That is not a lie"
- **Mana cheqachu.** → mana cheqa-chu → "It is not true. / That is not the case."
- **Mana chiqanmi chay ruway.** → mana chiqan-mi chay ruway → "That action is not right/just."
- **mana cheqa qelqa** → *mana cheqa qelqa* → 'inauthentic document / not a genuine text'
- **mana allinchu** → *mana allinchu* → 'it is not good / it is wrong / it is bad'

### Notes
The *mana...chu* frame is the standard Quechua negation pattern for predicates. The evidential *-n* on *mana* marks the speaker's direct assertion of the denial. Paired constructions combining positive assertion with negative denial of the opposite pole are attested as a rhetorical pattern: *cheqam, manan llullachu* ("it is true, it is not a lie").

*Mana* is the general negator; *-chu* is required on the predicate in negative declarative clauses. The *mana … -chu* frame is the standard negation pattern in Southern Quechua. Negative attribution of truth-domain predicates follows the same structural pattern as general predicate negation; no special construction is required.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Modifier-head order (head-final): evaluative adjective (cheqa / llulla / sut'i / chiqan) precedes the head noun directly, with no agreement morphology. The modifier is invariant in form regardless of the number or case of the head noun. The evidential clitic attaches to the predicate of the larger clause, not to the modifier within the NP. Possessive nominalization (*X + kay + possessive suffix*) can also function adnominally to express essential nature.

### Examples
- **Cheqa runa** → cheqa runa → "An upright person / A person of integrity"
- **Llulla simi** → llulla simi → "False speech / Lying words"
- **Cheqaq runám pay.** → cheqaq runa-m pay → "He/she is an honest person." (lit. "truthful person-EVID he/she")
- **Llulla qelqam chay.** → llulla qelqa-m chay → "That is a false document / forged writing."
- **cheqanchasqa qelqa** → *cheqanchasqa qelqa* → 'a verified/certified document'
- **cheqa kaynin** → *cheqa kaynin* → 'its true nature / what it genuinely is'

### Notes
Quechua adjectives precede their head nouns and do not inflect for agreement. The same evaluative root (*cheqa*, *llulla*) functions as both a predicate (§1–2 above) and a prenominal modifier without morphological change. Collocations such as *cheqa sonqo* ("true-hearted, sincere") and *llulla sonqo* ("false-hearted, deceitful") extend this pattern to body-part nominals encoding inner states.

Quechua adjectival modifiers are prenominal and uninflected for agreement. The same forms (*cheqaq*, *llulla*, *chiqan*) function both as standalone predicates (§1) and as prenominal modifiers; context and clause structure distinguish the two uses.

Participial modifiers (*-sqa*, *-q*) follow the same prenominal order as simple adjectives. The possessive nominalization *cheqa kaynin* ('its being-true') is a more abstract adnominal construction expressing essential nature rather than a simple property; it appears in philosophical and religious registers.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Two morphological strategies are attested:

**(a) Root + stative/inchoative suffix *-ya* + infinitive *-y*** (for adjective bases): derives intransitive verbs meaning "to become [adjective]."

**(b) Root + stative *-ka-* (intransitive verbalizer / inceptive) + *-y* (infinitive):** for the *cheqa* root specifically; yields an intransitive verb meaning 'to become X' or 'to come to be X'.

**(c) [truth-domain adjective] + *tukuy* ("to become, to turn into"):** the adjective precedes the verb *tukuy*, which carries tense and person inflection. [UNCERTAIN]

### Examples
- **Allinyay** → allin-ya-y → "To become correct / To improve / To get right"
- **Cheqakayta** → cheqa-ka-y-ta → "The being true / The becoming real" (nominalized, as object of a verb)
- **cheqakay** → *cheqakay* → 'to become true / to be realized / to come true'
- **allinkay** → *allinkay* → 'to become good / to get well / to improve / to recover'
- **Chiqan tukun.** → chiqan tuku-n → "He/she/it becomes correct / becomes just." [UNCERTAIN]
- **Cheqaq tukun.** → cheqaq tuku-n → "He/she becomes truthful / turns out to be genuine." [UNCERTAIN]

### Notes
The inchoative *-ya* is the productive suffix on adjectival bases (*allin* → *allinyay*). The *cheqa* root uses *-ka* (stative) rather than *-ya*, yielding *cheqakay* ("to be/become true/real"). The nominalized accusative form *cheqakayta* appears as the object of epistemic verbs (e.g., "to know that something is true"). No inchoative form of *llulla* is attested as a common expression in the sources consulted.

The *-ka-* pathway is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sumaq*. It contrasts systematically with the transitive *-ncha-* causative (§5): *cheqakay* is intransitive ('it becomes true'), while *cheqanchay* is transitive ('to make something true / to verify'). No *-ka-* inchoative is documented for *llulla*.

*Tukuy* is the general inchoative/transformative verb in Quechua. The *tukuy* examples are marked [UNCERTAIN], reflecting limited specific attestation; the construction is structurally regular and follows the general *[adjective] + tukuy* pattern attested across the language.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Two distinct causative pathways are attested:

**(a) Evaluative adjective + *-ncha-* (causative verbalizer) + *-y* (infinitive):** yields a transitive verb meaning 'to make X' or 'to bring X about'. The *-ncha-* verbalizer applies to adjectival roots (*cheqa*, *allin*, *sut'i*, *sumaq*) and yields transitive verbs of making or establishing.

**(b) Root + causative suffix *-chi-* / *-chay* / *-chiy* (on verbal or adjectival bases) + infinitive *-y*:** yields 'to cause someone to do/be X'. The *-chi-* causative applies to verbal roots (*panta-*, *llulla-*, *yacha-*) and yields verbs of causing an action in another. Applied directly to adjectival/nominal roots (*cheqaq*, *chiqan*, *llulla*), it produces a transitive verb.

**(c) Periphrastic causative:** [verb] + *-chiy* applied to a verbal stem (standard Quechua causative morphology); not separately documented for truth-domain items beyond *llullachiy*.

### Examples
- **cheqanchay** → *cheqanchay* → 'to verify / to confirm / to make true / to correct'
- **pantachiy** → *pantachiy* → 'to mislead / to cause someone to err'
- **Cheqachiy** → cheqa-chi-y → "To verify / To confirm / To prove / To correct"
- **Llullachiy** → llulla-chi-y → "To deceive (someone) / To cause to lie / To mislead"
- **Kay pantasqata chiqanchay tiyan.** → kay pantasqa-ta chiqancha-y tiyan → "This mistake needs to be corrected." (lit. "this erroneous-ACC correct-INF exists/is-necessary")
- **Llullachirqan payta.** → llullachiqa-n pay-ta → "He/she deceived him/her / caused him/her to believe a lie."

### Notes
Two distinct causative pathways are attested. The *-ncha-* verbalizer applies to adjectival roots (*cheqa*, *allin*, *sut'i*, *sumaq*) and yields transitive verbs of making or establishing. The *-chi-* causative applies to verbal roots (*panta-*, *llulla-*, *yacha-*) and yields verbs of causing an action in another. The root *llulla* uses *-chi-* directly (*llullachiy* 'to cause to lie') rather than *-ncha-*, an asymmetry preserved from the data.

The causative is highly productive across all roots in this domain: *cheqachiy* (to verify), *llullachiy* (to deceive), *sut'ichay* (to clarify), *allinchay* (to correct), *pantachiy* (to mislead). The reflexive *-ku* combines with the causative to yield self-directed or internally motivated variants: *cheqachikuy* ("to verify for oneself"), *llullachikuy* ("to deceive oneself"). The causative-reflexive combination is attested on all four major roots.

The *-chay/-chiy* causative suffix is the primary morphological strategy. It applies directly to the adjectival/nominal root (*cheqaq*, *chiqan*, *llulla*) to produce a transitive verb. This is a productive morphological pattern in Southern Quechua, not a periphrastic construction.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Three strategies are attested:

**(a) Evaluative adjective + *kay* (copular root / nominalizer):** yields an abstract nominal meaning 'the state/quality of being X' or 'truth / goodness / reality'.

**(b) Root + third-person possessive/nominalizer *-n*:** yielding a possessed nominal "its truth / the truth"; the possessed form *cheqan* is the primary abstract nominal for "truth."

**(c) Root used directly as a nominal:** *cheqa*, *llulla*, *chiqan* function as nouns without additional derivation ("truth," "a lie," "correctness"). Also: root + stative *-ka* + infinitive *-y* + accusative *-ta* (yielding a verbal nominalization "the being true").

**(d) Nominalized clause with *-sqa* + possessive + case:** embedded proposition nominalized via the past-participle nominalizer *-sqa* plus a possessive suffix, then case-marked.

### Examples
- **cheqa kay** → *cheqa kay* → 'truth / reality / the state of being true / genuineness'
- **allin kay** → *allin kay* → 'goodness / well-being / the state of being good or right'
- **Cheqan** → cheqa-n → "The truth / What is right / The correct one" (as in *cheqanta rimay*, "to speak the truth")
- **Cheqakayta** → cheqa-ka-y-ta → "The being true / The truth of (something)" (as object of epistemic verbs)
- **Cheqaqta rimay allinmi.** → cheqaqta rimay allin-mi → "Speaking the truth is good." (bare infinitive nominalization)
- **Llullakusqanmantam p'enqakun.** → llullakusqa-n-manta-m p'enqa-ku-n → "He/she is ashamed of having lied." (lit. "his/her-having-lied-ABL-EVID ashamed-REFL-3")

### Notes
The copular root *kay* ('to be / being') nominalizes the evaluative adjective to yield an abstract concept. This construction is productive across evaluative roots. The possessive extension *cheqa kaynin* ('its true nature / its genuine being') adds a third-person possessive suffix to specify whose or what's truth/nature is at issue.

Quechua does not use a dedicated abstract nominalizer suffix equivalent to English *-ness* or *-ity* for this domain. The possessed form *cheqan* (lit. "its truth") functions as the primary abstract nominal and takes case suffixes: accusative *cheqanta* (in *cheqanta rimay*), ablative *cheqanmanta* ("about the truth / truly"). No parallel abstract nominal is attested for *llulla* in the sources consulted; the concept of "falsehood" as an abstract noun is expressed through the bare adjective or through verbal nominalizations.

Quechua has no single abstract-nominalization suffix parallel to English *-ness* or *-ity*. Abstract meanings ("truth," "falsehood") are expressed either by the bare root used nominally or by nominalized clauses. The *-sqa* + possessive + case construction is the productive strategy for nominalizing events or states for use as clause arguments.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Verbal base (or adjectival root) + *-q* (agentive nominalizer). Yields a nominal meaning 'one who does X' or 'one who is X (characteristically).' Applied to truth-domain roots, this yields characterizations of persons by their habitual relation to truth or falsehood. Two bases are attested: the adjectival root directly (*llulla + -q*) and the verbal stem (*llullakuy + -q*). The possessive-agentive pattern *cheqaqniyuq* (cheqaq + *-niyuq*, "possessing") is also attested.

### Examples
- **llullaq** → *llullaq* → 'liar / one who lies / deceiver'
- **cheqanchaq** → *cheqanchaq* → 'one who verifies / a verifier / one who confirms'
- **Llullaq** → llulla-q → "Liar / Deceiver / One who lies"
- **Llullakuq** → llulla-ku-q → "Habitual liar / Chronic deceiver / One who habitually deceives"
- **Llullaqtam ama iñiychu.** → llullaq-ta-m ama iñiy-chu → "Do not believe a liar." (lit. "liar-ACC-EVID PROH believe-PROH")
- **Cheqaqniyuqmi pay.** → cheqaqniyuq-mi pay → "He/she is a person of truthfulness / one who possesses honesty."

### Notes
The *-q* nominalizer applies to verbal bases (*llullaku-q*, *cheqancha-q*, *pantachi-q*) and, in the case of *cheqaq*, directly to the adjectival root. *Cheqaq* ('truthful / genuine') is the standard attributive form of *cheqa* in prenominal position and also nominalizes to mean 'a truthful person'. The double-base form *llullakuq* (from *llullaku-* + *-q*) adds a habitual nuance compared to bare *llullaq*.

The agentive *-q* is productive across all roots in this domain: *cheqaq* ("one who is true / genuine"), *llullaq* ("liar"), *yachaq* ("one who knows"), *pantaq* ("one who errs"), *riqsiq* ("one who recognizes"). The combination reflexive *-ku* + agentive *-q* (*llullakuq*) adds a habitual or self-directed nuance absent from the bare agentive. *Cheqaq* functions primarily as an adjective ("genuine, authentic") rather than as a person-characterization, in contrast to *llullaq* which is primarily a person-characterization; this asymmetry is present in the attested data.

*Llullaq* (llulla + *-q*) and *llullakuq* (llullakuy + *-q*) are near-synonymous agentive nominals for "liar"; the former derives from the adjectival root, the latter from the verbal stem. *Cheqaqniyuq* (cheqaq + *-niyuq*, "possessing") is the attested form for "truthful person" in the possessive-agentive pattern, rather than a bare *cheqaq + -q* form.

## 8. EVIDENTIAL MARKING OF TRUTH CLAIMS

### Pattern
Predicate (including truth-evaluation predicates) + evidential enclitic: **-m/-mi** (direct / first-hand), **-s/-si** (reportative / heard from another), **-chá** (conjectural / inferred). The enclitic attaches to the predicate or the focused constituent.

### Examples
- **cheqam** / **cheqas** / **cheqachá** → *cheqam* / *cheqas* / *cheqachá* → 'it is true (I know directly)' / 'it is true (reportedly)' / 'it is probably true'
- **llullachá** → *llullachá* → 'it is probably a lie / I suspect it is false'

### Notes
Evidential enclitics constitute a grammatically obligatory system in Southern Quechua: speakers mark the source and certainty of their claims as a matter of grammar, not optional emphasis. The three-way contrast (*-m/-mi* vs. *-s/-si* vs. *-chá*) applies to all truth-evaluation predicates documented in this inventory and represents a structurally distinct layer of meaning that cross-cuts all other construction types.

## 9. REFLEXIVE / SELF-DIRECTED VERIFICATION

### Pattern
Evaluative adjective + **-ncha-** + **-ku-** (reflexive / middle / self-directed) + **-y**. Yields a verb meaning 'to verify / correct / clarify for oneself or on one's own initiative'. The *-ku-* suffix added to the *-ncha-* causative base shifts the action from externally directed to self-directed or internally motivated.

### Examples
- **cheqanchakuy** → *cheqanchakuy* → 'to verify for oneself / to assure oneself of the truth'
- **allinchakuy** → *allinchakuy* → 'to correct oneself / to improve oneself / to set oneself right'

### Notes
This three-morpheme sequence (*-ncha-ku-y*) is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sut'i* and represents a productive sub-pattern within the causative system.

The reflexive *-ku* combines with the causative to yield self-directed or internally motivated variants: *cheqachikuy* ("to verify for oneself"), *llullachikuy* ("to deceive oneself"). The causative-reflexive combination is attested on all four major roots.

## 10. PROPOSITIONAL KNOWLEDGE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Nominalized subordinate clause: **[predicate + kasqanta]** (accusative nominalization of 'its being X') as object of **yachay** ('to know') or **riqsiy** ('to recognize'). Expresses propositional knowledge of a truth-evaluation state.

### Examples
- **cheqa kasqanta yachani** → *cheqa kasqanta yachani* → 'I know that it is true / I know it to be genuine'
- **pantasqa kasqanta mana yachanichu** → *pantasqa kasqanta mana yachanichu* → 'I did not know that it was wrong / I was unaware of the error'

### Notes
The construction *[X kasqanta] yachay* is the standard Quechua pattern for expressing propositional knowledge ('knowing that'). The nominalized predicate takes the accusative *-ta*, marking it as the object of *yachay*. Negation of the knowledge verb (*mana yachanichu*) expresses ignorance and is relevant in legal contexts of disclaiming responsibility for an error.

## 11. DEONTIC PERIPHRASIS

### Pattern
Verb infinitive + obligative nominalizer *-na* + possessive suffix (person-marked) + purposive/benefactive *-paq* (optional) + existential/deontic verb *tiyan* ("it is necessary / it exists"). This construction expresses obligation to perform the action named by the verb.

### Examples
- **Cheqanta rimanayki tiyan** → cheqanta rima-nayki tiyan → "You must tell the truth / You are obliged to speak truthfully"
- **Sut'itatam rimanayki tiyan** → sut'i-ta-m rima-nayki tiyan → "You must speak clearly / You are obliged to express yourself plainly"

### Notes
This is the standard Quechua construction for expressing obligation and is not specific to the truth domain, but it is attested in legal and testimonial contexts specifically with truth-evaluation verbs. The possessive suffix on *-na* encodes the person bearing the obligation (*-yki* = second person singular). The direct evidential *-m* on the adverb in the second example marks the speaker's direct assertion of the obligation.

## 12. ABILITY PERIPHRASIS

### Pattern
Verb infinitive + accusative *-ta* + modal verb *atiy* ("to be able to, can"), negated with *mana...chu*. This construction expresses ability or inability to perform the action named by the verb.

### Examples
- **Cheqachiy atiy** → cheqachiy atiy → "To be able to verify / To be capable of confirming"
- **Manan cheqachiyta atinichu** → mana-n cheqachiy-ta atini-chu → "I cannot verify it / I am unable to confirm it"

### Notes
The modal verb *atiy* combines freely with infinitives of truth-evaluation verbs (*cheqachiy*, *sut'ichay*, *pantachiy*). The accusative *-ta* on the infinitive is the standard object-marking of infinitives in Quechua modal constructions. This pattern is attested in legal contexts where the capacity to verify or clarify a claim is at issue.

## 13. CONVERB CONSTRUCTIONS (circumstantial and quotative)

### Pattern
Two sub-patterns are attested:

**(a)** Verb stem + converb suffix *-spa* (circumstantial: "while/having [verb]ed"), creating an adverbial frame for a main verb.

**(b)** Predicate + quotative converb *nispa* ("saying"), embedding an assertion within a speech-act frame.

### Examples
- **Mana yachaspa rimay** → mana yachay-spa rimay → "To speak without knowing / To speak in ignorance"
- **Cheqa nispa rimay** → cheqa nispa rimay → "To speak saying it is true / To affirm as true while speaking"

### Notes
The *-spa* converb creates circumstantial adverbial clauses modifying the main verb; *mana yachaspa rimay* characterizes speech made without adequate epistemic grounds. The quotative *nispa* (converb of *niy*, "to say") embeds a direct assertion within a speech-act description and is the standard construction for reported speech in Quechua; it appears in testimonial contexts when characterizing how a statement was made or framed.

## 14. ADVERBIAL TRUTH ASSERTION

### Pattern
*Cheqaqta* (cheqaq + accusative/adverbial *-ta*) functions as a pre-verbal adverb modifying a verb of speaking, knowing, or reporting: *cheqaqta* + [verb]. The evidential clitic *-mi* may attach to *cheqaqta* to yield *cheqaqtam*.

### Examples
- **Cheqaqta rimay.** → cheqaqta rimay → "Speak the truth." (imperative)
- **Cheqaqtam yachani.** → cheqaqta-m yacha-ni → "I truly know. / I know for certain." (with direct evidential)

### Notes
This construction is specific to Quechua's evidential-adverbial system: the adverbial case *-ta* on *cheqaq* produces a manner adverb ("truly, in truth"), and the direct evidential clitic *-mi* can attach to it to add the speaker's epistemic commitment. The construction is widely attested in testimonial and legal discourse and has no direct parallel in the *llulla* paradigm.

## 15. PROHIBITIVE MORAL INJUNCTION

### Pattern
*Ama* (prohibitive particle) + [nominal or verbal form] + *-chu* (prohibitive/negative clitic): `Ama [llulla/llullakuy]-chu`. The nominal form (*ama llulla*) omits *-chu*; the verbal form (*ama llullakuychu*) requires it.

### Examples
- **Ama llulla, ama suwa, ama qella.** → ama llulla, ama suwa, ama qella → "Do not lie, do not steal, do not be lazy." (the traditional Andean moral triad)
- **Ama llullakuychu.** → ama llullakuy-chu → "Do not lie." (fuller verbal prohibitive)

### Notes
This construction is attested exclusively with *llulla* and *llullakuy* among truth-domain items; no parallel prohibitive is documented for *cheqa*, *chiqan*, or *allin* in available sources. The triadic formula (*ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qella*) is a fixed formulaic expression widely cited across Andean cultural and educational contexts.
