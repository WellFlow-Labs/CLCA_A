# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:**
This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. The constructions below are drawn from patterns attested in the P1 and P1b data and from well-documented Yoruba usage. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are organized by P1 base form.

A note on Yoruba syntax: Yoruba is an SVO language with post-nominal modification. Many "constructions" in Yoruba are syntactically transparent (verb + object, noun + modifier) but are nonetheless fixed or semi-fixed in usage. These are included where the combination is conventionalized beyond its compositional parts. Purely compositional and non-fixed combinations are excluded.

## 1. Base Form: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**Construction 1:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo.
**Transliteration:** Sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo.
**English Translation:** Always speak the truth. / Tell the truth at all times.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed moral injunction used in parenting, religious instruction, and moral discourse. The construction *sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo* ("speak truth at all times") is a conventionalized imperative formula appearing in proverb-adjacent speech and moral teaching.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú.
**English Translation:** Truth will not die. / Truth is imperishable.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial assertion about the enduring nature of truth; widely attested in Yoruba oral tradition and moral discourse. The construction uses the negative future *kò ní* + verb, a fixed pattern for asserting that something will not occur.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ bí ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀, ó ń tọ́ ojú ọ̀nà.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ bí ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀, ó ń tọ́ ojú ọ̀nà.
**English Translation:** Truth is like the divination chain — it points the way correctly.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial comparison (*bí* = "like") attested in Yoruba oral literature, comparing truth to the *ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀* (Ifá divination chain) as a reliable guide. Documents the intersection of truth and divination in Yoruba epistemology.
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Construction 4:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́.
**English Translation:** Let us speak the truth. / Let us tell the truth.
**Usage Notes:** A hortative construction using *jẹ́ kí* ("let") + first-person plural, used in deliberative, legal, and religious contexts to call for honest speech. Widely attested in formal and semi-formal registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**English Translation:** It is truth that saves one. / Truth is what rescues a person.
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* = focus marker on *ni*) asserting the salvific or protective power of truth. Attested in moral and religious discourse; appears in proverb-like formulations and sermon language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ìwádìí òtítọ́ ń lọ síwájú.
**Transliteration:** Ìwádìí òtítọ́ ń lọ síwájú.
**English Translation:** The investigation of the truth is proceeding. / The truth inquiry is moving forward.
**Usage Notes:** A predicative construction used in journalistic and legal discourse to describe an ongoing truth-finding process. The progressive aspect marker *ń* + motion verb *lọ síwájú* ("go forward") is a conventionalized expression for ongoing progress.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀.
**Transliteration:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀.
**English Translation:** There is no truth in his/her words. / His/her speech contains no truth.
**Usage Notes:** A negated existential construction (*kò sí* = "there is no") used to deny the truthfulness of a statement or person's speech. Widely attested in legal testimony, social criticism, and everyday dispute contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**English Translation:** He/she stood by the truth. / He/she adhered to the truth.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using *dúró mọ́* ("to stand by, to adhere to") expressing steadfast commitment to truth. Attested in moral, legal, and religious discourse as a description of integrity under pressure.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

**Transliteration:** Irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúkúrú.
**English Translation:** A lie has short legs. / Falsehood has short legs.
**Usage Notes:** A widely attested Yoruba proverb asserting that lies cannot travel far or endure; used in moral instruction and in contexts of exposing deception. The construction is fixed and formulaic.

**Construction 2:**
**Base Form:** irọ́

**Transliteration:** Sọ irọ́ fún ènìyàn

**Usage Notes:** The standard ditransitive construction for lying to a specific person, using *fún* ("for/to") to introduce the recipient. Attested across all registers in moral instruction and legal testimony.

---

**Construction 3:**
**Base Form:** irọ́

**Transliteration:** Irọ́ kò ní gbádùn.
**English Translation:** A lie will not prosper. / Falsehood will not be enjoyed/succeed.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial assertion using the negative future construction *kò ní* + verb, asserting that deception ultimately fails. Attested in moral and religious discourse.

---

**Construction 4:**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó mú irọ́ wá.
**Transliteration:** Ó mú irọ́ wá.
**English Translation:** He/she brought a lie. / He/she came with a falsehood.
**Usage Notes:** A serial verb construction (*mú* = to take/bring + *wá* = to come) used to describe someone who presents a false account or fabricated story. Attested in everyday and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
**Base Form:** irọ́

**Transliteration:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀.
**English Translation:** There is no lie in it. / It contains no falsehood.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ àti òtítọ́ kò lè papọ̀ gbé.
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ àti òtítọ́ kò lè papọ̀ gbé.
**English Translation:** Lie and truth cannot coexist. / Falsehood and truth cannot live together.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the incompatibility of truth and falsehood, using *àti* ("and") to conjoin the two terms and *kò lè papọ̀ gbé* ("cannot live together") as a fixed predicate. Attested in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## 5. Base Form: **ẹ̀tọ́** (right, entitlement, correctness)

---

**Construction 1:**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Example:** Ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni láti sọ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ.
**Transliteration:** Ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni láti sọ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ.
**English Translation:** It is your right to speak your mind. / You have the right to express yourself.
**Usage Notes:** A rights-assertion construction using *ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni* ("it is your right") + infinitival *láti* + verb phrase. Attested in legal, civic, and everyday discourse asserting entitlement.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Example:** Wọ́n kọ ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ̀.
**Transliteration:** Wọ́n kọ ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ̀.
**English Translation:** They denied his/her right. / They refused his/her entitlement.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using *kọ* ("to refuse, to deny") + *ẹ̀tọ́* + possessive, used in legal and advocacy discourse to describe the denial of rights. Attested in human rights and legal contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Example:** Gbà ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ.
**Transliteration:** Gbà ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ.
**English Translation:** Claim your right. / Take what is due to you.
**Usage Notes:** An imperative construction using *gbà* ("to receive, to take, to claim") + *ẹ̀tọ́* + possessive, used in advocacy and legal contexts to encourage assertion of one's rights. Attested in civic education and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Example:** Ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni, má jẹ́ kí wọ́n gba lọ́wọ́ rẹ.
**Transliteration:** Ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni, má jẹ́ kí wọ́n gba lọ́wọ́ rẹ.
**English Translation:** It is your right; do not let them take it from you.
**Usage Notes:** A compound construction combining a rights assertion (*ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni*) with a negative imperative (*má jẹ́ kí*) and a serial verb phrase (*gba lọ́wọ́ rẹ* = "take from your hand"). Attested in advocacy and legal education discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Example:** Kò sí ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ̀ nínú rẹ̀.
**Transliteration:** Kò sí ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ̀ nínú rẹ̀.
**English Translation:** He/she has no right to it. / There is no entitlement of his/hers in it.
**Usage Notes:** A negated existential construction used in legal and social contexts to deny someone's claim to a right or entitlement. Attested in court language and dispute resolution contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 3:**

**Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó.
**English Translation:** His/her faith/belief has collapsed. / His/her conviction has crumbled.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using *ti wó* ("has collapsed/fallen") to describe the loss or failure of belief or faith. Attested in religious and moral discourse about doubt and apostasy.

**Construction 4:**

**Construction 5:**

**English Translation:** He/she does not believe that it is true. / He/she disbelieves it.
**Usage Notes:** A negated propositional belief construction (*kò gbàgbọ́ pé* = "does not believe that") + embedded clause. The standard construction for expressing disbelief in a proposition. Widely attested.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
