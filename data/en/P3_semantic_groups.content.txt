# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: English (en)

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis identifies natural groupings based solely on observed usage patterns, contrasts, and distributional evidence from P1 and P2. Groups reflect how speakers actually use and distinguish these terms in practice, not imposed theoretical categories. Group boundaries are often fuzzy, with significant overlap and asymmetries preserved.

---

## GROUP 1: CORRESPONDENCE TO FACTS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate whether statements, beliefs, or representations correspond to actual facts or reality. These expressions are primarily applied to propositions, claims, and assertions, marking whether they accurately describe the world. Commonly used in contexts of verification, fact-checking, and epistemic evaluation.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **true** /truː/ - marks statements as corresponding to facts
- **false** /fɔːls/ - marks statements as not corresponding to facts
- **correct** /kəˈrɛkt/ - marks conformity to facts or standards
- **incorrect** /ˌɪnkəˈrɛkt/ - marks deviation from facts or standards
- **right** /raɪt/ - marks correctness of answers or statements
- **wrong** /rɒŋ/ - marks incorrectness of answers or statements
- **accurate** /ˈækjərət/ - marks precision in representation
- **inaccurate** /ɪnˈækjərət/ - marks lack of precision
- **erroneous** /ɪˈrəʊniəs/ - marks mistaken beliefs or statements

**Related constructions:**
- **the truth of the matter** - introduces factual clarification
- **get it right/wrong** - achieve/fail correct understanding
- **prove false** - demonstrate lack of correspondence
- **hold true** - maintain validity

### Typical Contrasts
These terms are regularly contrasted with each other in verification contexts: "Is that true or false?" "That's not quite right/correct/accurate." They focus on propositional content rather than objects or persons.

## GROUP 2: AUTHENTICITY OF OBJECTS AND CREDENTIALS

### Definition
Terms used to distinguish genuine articles, documents, or credentials from counterfeits, imitations, or fraudulent versions. These expressions evaluate whether physical objects, artworks, documents, or credentials are what they purport to be, focusing on origin, authorship, and legitimacy rather than abstract truth. Common in commercial, legal, and art contexts.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **genuine** /ˈdʒɛnjuɪn/ - marks authentic rather than counterfeit objects
- **authentic** /ɔːˈθɛntɪk/ - marks undisputed origin or authorship
- **fake** /feɪk/ - marks counterfeit objects or credentials
- **counterfeit** /ˈkaʊntərfɪt/ - marks fraudulent imitations (especially currency, documents)
- **bogus** /ˈbəʊɡəs/ - marks spurious credentials or objects
- **spurious** /ˈspjʊəriəs/ - marks superficially plausible but false items

**Related constructions:**
- **the genuine article** - emphasizes authenticity
- **the real McCoy** - emphasizes authenticity
- **the real deal** - emphasizes authenticity
- **fake it till you make it** - pretend competence
- **spot a fake** - identify counterfeit
- **under false pretenses** - through fraudulent means

**Related derivations:**
- **authenticate** /ɔːˈθɛntɪkeɪt/ - verify authenticity
- **authentication** /ɔːˌθɛntɪˈkeɪʃən/ - verification process
- **counterfeit** (verb) - produce fraudulent copies
- **fakery** /ˈfeɪkəri/ - practice of faking

### Typical Contrasts
"Is this painting genuine or fake?" "That's an authentic Rolex, not a counterfeit." These terms apply to tangible objects and documents rather than abstract propositions.

## GROUP 3: ONTOLOGICAL REALITY VS. APPEARANCE

### Definition
Terms that distinguish actual existence or substance from appearance, simulation, imagination, or virtuality. These expressions mark whether something exists in the physical/actual world versus being imaginary, simulated, or merely apparent. Frequently used to contrast lived experience with fiction, theory, or digital simulation.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **real** /rɪəl/ - marks actual existence vs. simulation/imagination
- **unreal** /ʌnˈrɪəl/ - marks lack of reality or dreamlike quality
- **reality** /riˈæləti/ - actual existence or conditions
- **unreality** /ˌʌnrɪˈæləti/ - dreamlike or non-existent quality

**Related constructions:**
- **in real life** - in actual experience vs. representation
- **in the real world** - in practical conditions vs. theory
- **for real** - seriously, genuinely (not joking)
- **get real** - be realistic, stop fantasizing
- **reality check** - confrontation with actual conditions
- **virtual reality** - computer-simulated environment
- **augmented reality** - digital overlay on physical world
- **escape from reality** - avoid actual conditions

**Related derivations:**
- **realize** /ˈrɪəlaɪz/ - become aware of reality / make actual
- **realization** /ˌrɪəlaɪˈzeɪʃən/ - moment of awareness / actualization
- **realism** /ˈrɪəlɪzəm/ - emphasis on reality in art/philosophy
- **realistic** /ˌrɪəˈlɪstɪk/ - accurately representing reality / practically feasible
- **unrealistic** /ˌʌnrɪəˈlɪstɪk/ - not feasible or accurate

### Typical Contrasts
"Is this real or just a dream?" "In real life, things don't work that way." "Virtual reality vs. actual reality." These terms focus on existence and actuality rather than truth of statements or authenticity of objects.

## GROUP 4: LOGICAL AND ARGUMENTATIVE SOUNDNESS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate the logical structure and inferential validity of arguments, reasoning, and claims. These expressions assess whether conclusions follow from premises, whether reasoning is well-founded, or whether arguments contain logical flaws. Primarily used in academic, philosophical, and analytical discourse.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **valid** /ˈvælɪd/ - marks logically sound arguments
- **invalid** /ɪnˈvælɪd/ - marks logically flawed arguments
- **sound** /saʊnd/ - marks well-founded reasoning
- **unsound** /ʌnˈsaʊnd/ - marks flawed reasoning
- **fallacious** /fəˈleɪʃəs/ - marks logically deceptive reasoning
- **fallacy** /ˈfæləsi/ - logical error in reasoning

**Related constructions:**
- **spurious correlation** - false statistical relationship
- **truth value** - whether proposition is true or false
- **truth condition** - circumstances making statement true

**Related derivations:**
- **validity** /vəˈlɪdəti/ - quality of being logically valid
- **validate** /ˈvælɪdeɪt/ - confirm logical soundness
- **validation** /ˌvælɪˈdeɪʃən/ - process of confirming validity
- **invalidate** /ɪnˈvælɪdeɪt/ - nullify logical force
- **soundness** /ˈsaʊndnəs/ - quality of being well-founded
- **fallibility** /ˌfæləˈbɪləti/ - susceptibility to error
- **infallible** /ɪnˈfæləbəl/ - incapable of error

### Typical Contrasts
"That's a valid argument but unsound premises." "The reasoning is fallacious." These terms evaluate logical structure rather than factual content, though the distinction often blurs in practice.

## GROUP 5: RELIABILITY AND DEPENDABILITY

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate whether sources, persons, instruments, or systems can be consistently depended upon to function properly or provide accurate information. These expressions assess trustworthiness based on track record and consistency rather than single instances. Common in contexts of testimony, mechanical function, and institutional assessment.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **reliable** /rɪˈlaɪəbəl/ - marks consistent dependability
- **unreliable** /ˌʌnrɪˈlaɪəbəl/ - marks inconsistent or undependable
- **trustworthy** /ˈtrʌstwɜːrði/ - marks deserving of confidence
- **untrustworthy** /ʌnˈtrʌstwɜːrði/ - marks undeserving of confidence

**Related constructions:**
- **safe and sound** - unharmed and reliable
- **tried and reliable** - proven through experience

**Related derivations:**
- **reliability** /rɪˌlaɪəˈbɪləti/ - quality of being dependable
- **reliably** /rɪˈlaɪəbli/ - in a dependable manner
- **trustworthiness** /ˈtrʌstwɜːrðinəs/ - quality deserving confidence
- **trust** /trʌst/ - confidence in reliability
- **distrust** /dɪsˈtrʌst/ - lack of confidence

### Typical Contrasts
"Is this source reliable?" "He's proven trustworthy over the years." These terms emphasize consistency and track record rather than single-instance accuracy.

## GROUP 6: MORAL HONESTY AND TRUTHFULNESS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate whether persons habitually tell the truth, act without deception, or represent themselves sincerely. These expressions assess character and moral disposition toward truthfulness rather than accuracy of specific statements. Common in character evaluation and interpersonal trust contexts.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **honest** /ˈɒnɪst/ - marks freedom from deceit in character
- **dishonest** /dɪsˈɒnɪst/ - marks deceptive character
- **truthful** /ˈtruːθfəl/ - marks habitual truth-telling
- **untruthful** /ʌnˈtruːθfəl/ - marks habitual lying
- **faithful** /ˈfeɪθfəl/ - marks loyalty and accurate representation

**Related constructions:**
- **to be honest** - discourse marker introducing frank statement
- **honest to God** - emphatic affirmation of truthfulness
- **honest broker** - impartial mediator
- **honest mistake** - unintentional error
- **bear false witness** - give false testimony
- **faithful to the original** - accurately representing source

**Related derivations:**
- **honesty** /ˈɒnɪsti/ - quality of being honest
- **honestly** /ˈɒnɪstli/ - in truthful manner / discourse marker
- **dishonesty** /dɪsˈɒnɪsti/ - quality of being deceptive
- **truthfulness** /ˈtruːθfəlnəs/ - quality of truth-telling
- **faithfulness** /ˈfeɪθfəlnəs/ - quality of loyalty/accuracy
- **unfaithful** /ʌnˈfeɪθfəl/ - disloyal or inaccurate

### Typical Contrasts
"She's an honest person." "He's been dishonest with us." These terms evaluate character and habitual behavior rather than single statements or objects.

## GROUP 7: APPROPRIATENESS AND FITTINGNESS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate whether something is suitable, proper, or well-matched to its context, purpose, or circumstances. These expressions assess contextual fit and propriety rather than factual accuracy or authenticity. Common in evaluations of descriptions, responses, behavior, and social conduct.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **apt** /æpt/ - marks particularly fitting quality
- **fitting** /ˈfɪtɪŋ/ - marks appropriate to circumstances
- **proper** /ˈprɒpər/ - marks conformity to standards or appropriateness
- **appropriate** (implied through usage patterns)

**Related constructions:**
- **apt to (do something)** - likely to, inclined to
- **it is fitting that** - it is appropriate that
- **prim and proper** - excessively formal

**Related derivations:**
- **aptly** /ˈæptli/ - in fitting manner
- **aptness** /ˈæptnəs/ - quality of being fitting
- **aptitude** /ˈæptɪtjuːd/ - natural ability or fitness
- **fittingly** /ˈfɪtɪŋli/ - in appropriate manner
- **inept** /ɪˈnɛpt/ - unsuitable, lacking skill
- **ineptitude** /ɪˈnɛptɪtjuːd/ - lack of appropriateness or skill

### Typical Contrasts
"That's an apt description." "It's fitting that we honor her." "That's not proper behavior." These terms evaluate suitability and contextual appropriateness rather than truth or authenticity.

## GROUP 8: LEGITIMACY AND AUTHORIZATION

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate whether claims, authority, status, or actions conform to law, rules, or accepted standards of legitimacy. These expressions assess legal or institutional validity and proper authorization rather than factual accuracy. Common in legal, political, and institutional contexts.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **legitimate** /lɪˈdʒɪtɪmət/ - marks conformity to law or accepted standards
- **valid** /ˈvælɪd/ (in legal sense) - marks legal force
- **bona fide** /ˌbəʊnə ˈfaɪdi/ - made in good faith, genuine

**Related constructions:**
- **legitimate concern/question** - valid issue deserving attention
- **no longer valid** - expired, invalidated
- **bona fide offer** - genuine offer made in good faith

**Related derivations:**
- **legitimacy** (implied) - quality of being lawful
- **validity** /vəˈlɪdəti/ (legal sense) - legal force
- **validate** /ˈvælɪdeɪt/ (legal sense) - confirm legal status
- **invalidate** /ɪnˈvælɪdeɪt/ - nullify legal force

### Typical Contrasts
"Is this a legitimate business?" "The contract is no longer valid." These terms focus on legal/institutional status rather than factual truth or logical soundness.

## GROUP 9: PRECISION AND EXACTNESS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate the degree of precision, exactness, or detailed correctness in measurements, representations, or descriptions. These expressions emphasize fine-grained accuracy and attention to detail rather than general correspondence to facts. Common in technical, scientific, and careful descriptive contexts.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **accurate** /ˈækjərət/ - marks precision in detail
- **inaccurate** /ɪnˈækjərət/ - marks lack of precision
- **exact** (implied through usage patterns)
- **precise** (implied through usage patterns)

**Related constructions:**
- **accurate to within (X)** - precise within specified margin
- **strictly accurate** - precisely correct in all details
- **right on the money** - exactly correct

**Related derivations:**
- **accuracy** /ˈækjərəsi/ - degree of precision
- **accurately** /ˈækjərətli/ - in precise manner
- **inaccuracy** /ɪnˈækjərəsi/ - lack of precision or error

### Typical Contrasts
"The measurement is accurate to within 0.1mm." "That's not strictly accurate." These terms emphasize degree of precision rather than simple truth/falsity.

## GROUP 10: RIGHTNESS IN MORAL AND PRACTICAL DOMAINS

### Definition
Terms used to evaluate moral propriety of actions, practical correctness of procedures, or appropriate functioning of situations. These expressions span moral judgment, practical know-how, and situational assessment, often blurring boundaries between ethical and pragmatic evaluation. Extremely common in everyday discourse across multiple domains.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **right** /raɪt/ - marks moral propriety or practical correctness
- **wrong** /rɒŋ/ - marks moral impropriety or practical incorrectness
- **correct** /kəˈrɛkt/ (in procedural sense) - marks proper procedure

**Related constructions:**
- **do right by (someone)** - treat fairly
- **in the wrong** - at fault
- **serve someone right** - be deserved consequence
- **go wrong** - fail, malfunction
- **can't go wrong (with X)** - will certainly succeed
- **two wrongs don't make a right** - revenge doesn't justify wrongdoing
- **set things right** - correct problems
- **make it right** - provide correction or restitution

**Related derivations:**
- **rightness** /ˈraɪtnəs/ - quality of being right
- **rightful** /ˈraɪtfəl/ - legitimate, proper
- **righteous** /ˈraɪtʃəs/ - morally correct, virtuous
- **righteousness** /ˈraɪtʃəsnəs/ - moral correctness
- **wrongdoing** /ˈrɒŋduːɪŋ/ - immoral action
- **wrongful** /ˈrɒŋfəl/ - unjust, illegal

### Typical Contrasts
"That's the right thing to do." "You're in the wrong." "Everything went wrong." These terms operate across moral, practical, and functional domains with fluid boundaries.

## GROUP 11: TECHNICAL VERIFICATION AND TESTING

### Definition
Terms used in technical, scientific, and diagnostic contexts to mark results of testing, verification, or validation procedures. These expressions often appear in specialized collocations related to testing methodology, statistical analysis, and quality control. Common in scientific, medical, and technical discourse.

### Representative Expressions

**Core members:**
- **valid** /ˈvælɪd/ (in testing sense) - marks acceptable test result
- **invalid** /ɪnˈvælɪd/ (in testing sense) - marks unacceptable result

**Related constructions:**
- **false positive** - erroneous positive test result
- **false negative** - erroneous negative test result
- **false alarm** - unfounded warning
- **spurious correlation** - false statistical relationship

**Related derivations:**
- **validate** /ˈvælɪdeɪt/ (technical sense) - verify through testing
- **validation** /ˌvælɪˈdeɪʃən/ (technical sense) - testing process
- **falsifiable** /ˌfɔːlsɪˈfaɪəbəl/ - capable of being proven false
- **falsification** /ˌfɔːlsɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/ (scientific sense) - disproof

### Typical Contrasts
"The test gave a false positive." "This hypothesis is falsifiable." These terms operate in specialized technical contexts with specific methodological meanings.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

### Items Spanning Multiple Groups

**true/truth**
- Belongs to GROUP 1 (correspondence to facts): "That's true"
- Overlaps with GROUP 10 (moral/practical): "true friend," "true to one's word"
- Overlaps with GROUP 3 (reality): "come true," "too good to be true"
- Shows semantic range across factual, moral, and ontological domains

**false/falsity**
- Belongs to GROUP 1 (correspondence to facts): "That's false"
- Overlaps with GROUP 2 (authenticity): "false bottom," "false teeth"
- Overlaps with GROUP 11 (technical): "false positive," "false alarm"
- Shows semantic range across propositional, material, and technical domains

**valid/validity**
- Belongs to GROUP 4 (logical soundness): "valid argument"
- Overlaps with GROUP 8 (legitimacy): "valid passport"
- Overlaps with GROUP 11 (technical verification): "valid test result"
- Shows distinct senses in logical, legal, and technical contexts

**correct/incorrect**
- Belongs to GROUP 1 (correspondence to facts): "correct answer"
- Overlaps with GROUP 10 (moral/practical): "correct behavior," "politically correct"
- Overlaps with GROUP 9 (precision): "strictly correct"
- Shows range across epistemic, moral, and procedural domains

**real/reality**
- Belongs to GROUP 3 (ontological reality): "real vs. imaginary"
- Overlaps with GROUP 2 (authenticity): "the real deal," "the real McCoy"
- Shows tension between ontological and authenticity senses

**sound**
- Belongs to GROUP 4 (logical soundness): "sound argument"
- Overlaps with GROUP 5 (reliability): "of sound mind"
- Note: Distinct from homophonous "sound" (auditory)

**faithful**
- Belongs to GROUP 6 (moral honesty): "faithful person"
- Overlaps with GROUP 9 (precision): "faithful to the original"
- Shows range from moral loyalty to representational accuracy

**right/wrong**
- Belongs to GROUP 1 (correspondence to facts): "right/wrong answer"
- Strongly overlaps with GROUP 10 (moral/practical): "right/wrong action"
- Shows extensive polysemy across epistemic, moral, and practical domains

### Items with Unclear or Marginal Group Membership

**veridical** /vɛˈrɪdɪkəl/
- Technical philosophical/psychological term
- Could belong to GROUP 1 (correspondence) but highly specialized
- Limited to academic discourse; minimal integration with everyday usage patterns

**veritable** /ˈvɛrɪtəbəl/
- Functions primarily as emphatic modifier ("a veritable paradise")
- Does not clearly belong to any identified group
- Operates more as intensifier than truth-evaluator

**so-called** /ˈsəʊkɔːld/
- Functions as skeptical modifier questioning appropriateness of designation
- Could relate to GROUP 2 (authenticity) but operates differently
- Primarily metalinguistic rather than directly evaluative

**bogus** /ˈbəʊɡəs/
- Belongs to GROUP 2 (authenticity) but shows limited derivational/constructional integration
- Primarily informal register with restricted distribution

## ITEMS NOT CLEARLY GROUPED

The following items from P1/P2 do not fit cleanly into the identified groups or show insufficient distinctive patterning:

**truism** /ˈtruːɪzəm/
- Denotes obvious truth rather than evaluating truth
- Metalevel comment on banality rather than truth-evaluation
- Does not pattern with other truth-related terms

**truthiness** /ˈtruːθinəs/
- Neologism denoting feeling of truth without factual basis
- Critiques rather than evaluates truth
- Limited to specific political/media criticism context

**post-truth** /pəʊstˈtruːθ/
- Describes cultural/political condition rather than evaluating truth
- Metalevel sociological term
- Does not function as truth-evaluator

**half-truth** /hɑːfˈtruːθ/
- Denotes partially true statement
- Could relate to GROUP 1 but operates as noun describing type of statement
- Does not pattern as evaluative adjective

**realpolitik** /reɪˈɑːlpɒlɪtiːk/

- Uses "real" but in specialized political theory sense
- Does not integrate with other reality-related terms

**reality TV** /riˈæləti tiːˈviː/
- Genre designation using "reality" ironically
- Does not evaluate reality but names media format
- Metalevel usage distinct from reality-evaluation

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Observed Asymmetries

1. **Group size variation**: Groups range from 3-4 core members (GROUP 7, 8, 11) to 9+ members (GROUP 1, 10). This reflects actual usage patterns, not analytical artifact.

2. **Derivational productivity**: Some groups show extensive derivational families (GROUP 4: valid/validity/validate/validation/invalidate), while others show minimal derivation (GROUP 2: bogus has no standard derivatives).

3. **Constructional density**: GROUP 10 (right/wrong) shows extremely high density of multi-word constructions, while GROUP 8 (legitimacy) shows minimal constructional elaboration.

4. **Register stratification**: Some groups operate primarily in specialized registers (GROUP 4: academic/philosophical; GROUP 11: technical/scientific), while others span all registers (GROUP 1, 10).

### Fuzzy Boundaries

Many items show gradient membership across groups rather than discrete categorization. The groups represent usage clusters rather than mutually exclusive categories. Terms like "right/wrong," "true/false," and "valid" show particularly extensive cross-group participation.

### Unforced Gaps

No attempt has been made to create symmetrical oppositions or complete coverage. Some semantic spaces (e.g., sincerity, pretense, deception as acts rather than character traits) are not well-represented in the P1/P2 inventory, and no artificial group has been created to accommodate scattered items.
