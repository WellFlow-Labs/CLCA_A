# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The scope and orthographic conventions follow those established in P1 and P1b (primarily Southern Quechua, Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka; three-vowel orthography with ejective and aspirate digraphs). Sources consulted are those cited in P1b, supplemented by Cusihuamán (1976), Itier (1997), and available colonial and contemporary Quechua text corpora.

**Distinction from P2a:** Derivational forms (suffixed forms, participials, causatives, etc.) have been documented in P2a and are not repeated here. This step covers multi-word constructions: collocations, periphrastic expressions, predicative constructions, and syntactically-driven patterns that are not reducible to single-word derivation. Forms already listed as compounds in P1b (e.g., *cheqa simi*, *cheqa runa*) are cross-referenced where relevant but are not re-documented at length unless they participate in a larger syntactic construction.

**A note on Quechua syntax and multi-word constructions:** Quechua is a head-final, agglutinative language with extensive morphological encoding of information that in other languages appears as multi-word syntax. Many meanings that would be expressed periphrastically in English are expressed by single suffixed forms in Quechua. This means that the inventory of genuinely multi-word constructions in the truth-evaluation domain is smaller than might be expected from a typological perspective, and absence of constructions for particular semantic roles is a predictable and significant feature of the data.

## P1 Item 1: cheqa

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam kay.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m kay
4. **English translation:** This is true. / This is real. / This is the genuine one.
5. **Usage notes:** A minimal predicative construction in which *cheqa* functions as a predicate nominal or adjective with the copular demonstrative *kay* ("this") as subject. The direct evidential clitic *-m* is attached to *cheqa* as the focused predicate. Used in testimonial and everyday assertive contexts to affirm the truth or genuineness of something present or under discussion.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam hina.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m hina
4. **English translation:** It is indeed so. / That is truly how it is. / It is as true as that.
5. **Usage notes:** A predicative construction combining *cheqa* with the manner/similarity particle *hina* and the direct evidential clitic. Used to affirm that a state of affairs is genuinely as described, often in response to a report or claim. Appears in conversational and narrative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nispa rimay.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** Speak saying it is true. / Say it truly. / Affirm it as true when you speak.
5. **Usage notes:** A converbal construction using *nispa* (the quotative/manner converb of *niy*, "to say") to embed *cheqa* as the content of speech. Used in moral instruction and legal contexts to direct someone to speak truthfully or to affirm something as true in their testimony. The construction *[X] nispa rimay* is a productive pattern in Quechua for specifying the manner or content of speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Mana cheqachu.
3. **Transliteration:** mana cheqa-chu
4. **English translation:** It is not true. / It is not real. / That is not correct.
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negated predicative construction with *mana* (negator) and the negative polar suffix *-chu* on the predicate. Used to deny the truth or genuineness of a proposition or object. This is the regular negation pattern in Quechua and is highly frequent in testimonial and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta yachani.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-ta yacha-ni
4. **English translation:** I know that it is true. / I know of its being true.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominalized complement construction in which the embedded clause *cheqa kasqan* ("its being true," nominalized with *-sqa* + third-person possessive *-n* + accusative *-ta*) serves as the object of *yachay* ("to know"). This is the standard Quechua pattern for propositional complement clauses and is used to express knowledge of the truth of a state of affairs.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanmanta willaway.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-manta willa-wa-y
4. **English translation:** Tell me about its being true. / Inform me of the truth of it.
5. **Usage notes:** A nominalized complement construction with the ablative/source case *-manta* ("about, from") and the verb *willaway* ("tell me," with first-person object *-wa-*). Used to request information about the truth of a matter. Appears in testimonial and investigative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 2: cheqaq

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Cheqaqmi kay runa.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaq-mi kay runa
4. **English translation:** This person is truly genuine. / This is a truthful person (I assert from direct knowledge).
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *cheqaq* as predicate nominal, *kay runa* ("this person") as subject, and the direct evidential clitic *-mi* marking the predicate. Used in moral evaluation of a person's character as genuinely honest or trustworthy.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaq
2. **Example:** Mana cheqaqchu.
3. **Transliteration:** mana cheqaq-chu
4. **English translation:** It is not genuine. / It is not truthful. / That is not real.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard negated predicative construction with *mana* and *-chu*. Used to deny the genuineness or truthfulness of a person, statement, or object. Functions as the negated counterpart to affirmative *cheqaqmi*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 3: cheqaqpaq

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqaqpaq

3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqpaq-mi nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** Speak saying it is truly so. / Say it as genuinely true.

The construction is an extension of the *[X] nispa rimay* pattern documented under *cheqa*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqpaq-mi chay
4. **English translation:** That is truly so. / That is genuinely the case (I assert directly).
5. **Usage notes:** A minimal predicative construction using the demonstrative *chay* ("that") as subject and *cheqaqpaqmi* as predicate. Used as a discourse-level affirmation that a previously mentioned state of affairs is genuinely true. Appears in conversational and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 4: cheqan

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqan
2. **Example:** Cheqanmi kay.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-n-mi kay
4. **English translation:** This is the truth. / This is what is correct.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *cheqan* ("its truth / the true one") as predicate and *kay* ("this") as subject, with direct evidential *-mi*. Used in disclosure contexts to identify something as the truth of a matter, often in contrast to a previously stated falsehood or misunderstanding.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** cheqan
2. **Example:** Cheqanta rimay.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-n-ta rimay
4. **English translation:** Speak the truth. / Say what is correct.
5. **Usage notes:** A verbal construction with *cheqanta* (accusative of *cheqan*: "the truth, what is correct") as the object of *rimay* ("to speak"). Used in moral exhortation and legal instruction as a directive to tell the truth. Functionally parallel to *cheqata rimay* (documented in P1b) but with the nominalized form *cheqan* rather than the adverbial *cheqata*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 5: cheqanchay

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchay munani.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchay muna-ni
4. **English translation:** I want to verify it. / I want to confirm it.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction with the infinitive *cheqanchay* as the complement of *munay* ("to want"), expressing the desire or intention to verify or confirm something. This is the standard Quechua pattern for expressing volitional modality with an infinitival complement.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchayta atinichu.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqancha-y-ta ati-ni-chu
4. **English translation:** I cannot verify it. / I am unable to confirm it.
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction with the nominalized infinitive *cheqanchayta* (accusative of the nominalized *cheqanchay*) as the complement of *atiy* ("to be able to"), negated with *-chu*. Used to express inability to verify or confirm something. Appears in testimonial and investigative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 12: iñiy

---

**Construction 1**
1. **Base Form:** iñiy
2. **Example:** Iñinichu chay simita.
3. **Transliteration:** iñi-ni-chu chay simi-ta
4. **English translation:** I do not believe that statement. / I do not trust that word.
5. **Usage notes:** Negated transitive construction with *chay simita* ("that statement/word," accusative) as the object of *iñiy*. Used to express disbelief or distrust of a statement or testimony. Appears in testimonial and evaluative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
1. **Base Form:** iñiy
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta iñini.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-ta iñi-ni
4. **English translation:** I believe it to be true. / I trust that it is genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** Nominalized complement construction with *cheqa kasqanta* as the object of *iñiy*. Used to express belief in the truth of something, in contrast to *cheqa kasqanta yachani* (which asserts knowledge rather than belief). The contrast between *iñiy* and *yachay* constructions is epistemically significant in Quechua discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
1. **Base Form:** iñiy
2. **Example:** Iñinim, mana yachanichu.
3. **Transliteration:** iñi-ni-m, mana yacha-ni-chu
4. **English translation:** I believe it (I assert), but I do not know it.
5. **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction juxtaposing *iñiy* (belief) with negated *yachay* (knowledge), marking the epistemic distinction between believing and knowing. Used in testimonial and philosophical contexts to calibrate the speaker's epistemic commitment. The direct evidential *-m* on *iñini* is notable: the speaker asserts from direct knowledge that they believe, while disclaiming direct knowledge of the proposition itself.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Items 28–30: -mi/-m, -si/-s, -chá

---

**Note on evidential clitics as construction elements:** The evidential clitics *-mi*, *-si*, and *-chá* are grammatical morphemes that attach to host words rather than forming multi-word constructions in their own right. Their role in multi-word constructions has been documented throughout this step wherever they appear on predicates or focused constituents within constructions (e.g., *cheqam*, *llullam*, *hinam*, etc.). No additional multi-word constructions are identified for the clitics themselves as independent bases, consistent with the treatment in P2a.

The following cross-construction pattern is noted as a documented feature of Quechua evidential grammar rather than as a new construction:

---

**Cross-construction pattern: Evidential contrast in truth-evaluation discourse**
1. **Base Forms:** -mi/-m, -si/-s, -chá
2. **Example:** Cheqam nispa rimawanku; noqataqmi mana cheqachu nispa yuyani.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m nispa rima-wa-n-ku; noqa-taq-mi mana cheqa-chu nispa yuya-ni
4. **English translation:** They speak to me saying it is true (direct evidential); but I for my part think (direct evidential) saying it is not true.
5. **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction in which the direct evidential *-mi* appears on both clauses, marking that the speaker has direct knowledge of both the others' claim and their own contrary assessment. This pattern of evidential contrast is documented in Quechua narrative and testimonial discourse as a means of marking disagreement about the truth of a proposition while maintaining epistemic commitment to one's own assessment.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
