# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:**
This step analyzes how Yoruba encodes dynamic states — becoming, causing, and maintaining or losing truth-related conditions — using only vocabulary documented in P1 through P5. The analysis draws on constructions already attested in P2b, P4a, and P4b, supplemented where necessary by auxiliary verbs, aspect markers, and function words that are grammatically required but do not introduce new lexical items. Where no common expression is attested in the context for a given dynamic type applied to a given item, this is stated explicitly. Items are treated separately where their behavior differs.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### Pattern
**di** + [NOMINAL/ADJECTIVAL PREDICATE]
The inchoative verb *di* ("to become") is the primary means of encoding change of state in Yoruba (documented in P4a, Pattern 4). It combines with nominal and adjectival predicates freely.

---

### 1.1 Becoming truthful / becoming a person of truth

**Form:** di ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó di ènìyàn òtítọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ènìyàn òtítọ́.
**English translation:** He/she became a truthful person. / He/she became a person of truth.
**Source:** Directly attested in P4a (Pattern 4, first example).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 Becoming genuine / authentic

**Form:** di ojúlówó
**Example:** Ó di ohun ojúlówó.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ohun ojúlówó.
**English translation:** It became a genuine thing. / It became authentic.
**Source:** The *di* + nominal predicate pattern is attested in P4a; *ojúlówó* is a post-nominal modifier documented in P1 and P3 (Group 3). The combination follows the attested pattern directly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.3 Becoming a person of good character

**Form:** di ọmọlúwàbí
**Example:** Ó di ọmọlúwàbí.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ọmọlúwàbí.
**English translation:** He/she became a person of good character. / He/she became a virtuous, well-bred person.
**Source:** *di* + nominal predicate pattern (P4a); *ọmọlúwàbí* documented in P1b (Root 2, item 6) and P2a (Base 16, Derived Form 4) as a core evaluative nominal.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.4 Becoming strong in belief / faith

**Form:** di lágbára (applied to *ìgbàgbọ́*)
**Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ di lágbára.
**Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ di lágbára.
**English translation:** His/her belief became strong. / His/her faith grew strong.
**Source:** Directly attested in P4a (Pattern 4, second example).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.5 Becoming reliable / trustworthy

**Form:** di ènìyàn tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Example:** Ó di ènìyàn tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ènìyàn tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé.
**English translation:** He/she became a person one can trust. / He/she became reliable.
**Source:** *di* + nominal predicate pattern (P4a); the relative-clause nominal *ènìyàn tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* is attested in P2a (Base 8, Derived Form 1) and P2b (Base 8, Construction 4) as the standard expression for a trustworthy person.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.6 Becoming correct / right (of a judgment or statement)

**Form:** di tọ́ (stative predicate)
**Example:** Ìdájọ́ náà di tọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ìdájọ́ náà di tọ́.
**English translation:** The judgment became correct. / The verdict became right.
**Source:** *di* + stative verbal predicate pattern (P4a); *tọ́* as a stative evaluative predicate is attested in P1b (Root 1, item 3) and P2b (Base 18, Construction 2).
**Notes:** P4a notes that *di* "combines freely with nominal and adjectival predicates." The stative verb *tọ́* functions adjectivally in predicative position; the combination is structurally consistent with the attested pattern. However, no specific example of *di tọ́* is attested in the context; the form is derived by pattern extension.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.7 No common expression attested for the following becoming-states:

- **Becoming evidence (*ẹ̀rí*):** No construction of the form *di ẹ̀rí* is attested in the context. *ẹ̀rí* is used as a noun denoting evidence that exists or does not exist (existential constructions, P4a Pattern 9), but no inchoative construction with *di* is documented for it.
- **Becoming a lie (*irọ́*):** No construction of the form *di irọ́* is attested. The context documents *irọ́* in verbal collocations (*sọ irọ́*) and existential constructions (*kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀*) but not in inchoative constructions.
- **Becoming knowledge (*ìmọ̀*):** No construction of the form *di ìmọ̀* is attested. *ìmọ̀* appears in possessive constructions (*ó ní ìmọ̀*) and existential constructions but not in inchoative constructions in the context.

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH / OPPOSED STATE

### Pattern
**di** + [OPPOSED NOMINAL/ADJECTIVAL PREDICATE]
or
Collapse/destruction constructions: **ti fọ́** ("has broken/shattered"), **ti wó** ("has collapsed/fallen")
The collapse constructions are attested specifically for *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* and *ìgbàgbọ́* (P2b, P5 Opposition 14) and encode irreversible loss of a truth-related state rather than gradual change.

---

### 2.1 Becoming a liar / a person of falsehood

**Form:** di ènìyàn irọ́
**Example:** Ó di ènìyàn irọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ènìyàn irọ́.
**English translation:** He/she became a liar. / He/she became a person of falsehood.
**Source:** *di* + nominal predicate pattern (P4a); *ènìyàn irọ́* ("a lying person / a liar") is attested in P2a (Base 2, Derived Form 2) and P4a (Pattern 7) as the periphrastic agentive for liar.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Becoming a person of bad character

**Form:** di ènìyàn ìwà burúkú
**Example:** Ó di ènìyàn ìwà burúkú.
**Transliteration:** Ó di ènìyàn ìwà burúkú.
**English translation:** He/she became a person of bad character. / He/she became wicked.
**Source:** *di* + nominal predicate pattern (P4a); *ìwà burúkú* is a core evaluative expression documented throughout P1–P5.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Trust breaking down (irreversible collapse)

**Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́
**Example:** Ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́.
**English translation:** Trust in him/her has broken. / His/her reliability has been shattered.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 8, Construction 2) and P5 (Opposition 14).
**Notes:** The perfective aspect marker *ti* + *fọ́* ("to break/shatter") encodes a completed, irreversible change of state. This construction is specific to *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* in the attested data; it is not documented for other truth-domain nominals.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 Belief / faith collapsing

**Form:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó
**Example:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó.
**Transliteration:** Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó.
**English translation:** His/her faith/belief has collapsed. / His/her conviction has crumbled.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 7, Construction 3) and P5 (Opposition 14).
**Notes:** The perfective *ti* + *wó* ("to collapse/fall") encodes irreversible loss of the belief state. P5 notes that *fọ́* is used for *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* and *wó* for *ìgbàgbọ́*, and that these are "non-identical but parallel constructions." The two verbs of collapse are not interchangeable across the two nominals in the attested data.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.5 Conduct becoming incorrect / improper

**Form:** ìwà rẹ̀ di àìtọ́
**Example:** Ìwà rẹ̀ di àìtọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ìwà rẹ̀ di àìtọ́.
**English translation:** His/her conduct became improper. / His/her character became incorrect.
**Source:** *di* + adjectival predicate pattern (P4a); *àìtọ́* (negated *tọ́*, via the *àì-* prefix) is documented in P2a (Base 16, Derived Form 7) as the negative element in *ìwà àìtọ́* ("incorrect conduct, improper character").
**Notes:** No specific example of *di àìtọ́* is attested in the context; the form is derived by pattern extension from the attested *di* + predicate pattern and the attested *àìtọ́* element.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.6 Becoming hypocritical / sham in conduct

**Form:** ìwà rẹ̀ di àgàbàgebè
**Example:** Ìwà rẹ̀ di àgàbàgebè.
**Transliteration:** Ìwà rẹ̀ di àgàbàgebè.
**English translation:** His/her conduct became pretense. / His/her character became sham.
**Source:** *di* + nominal predicate pattern (P4a); *àgàbàgebè* is documented in P1 and P2b (Base 13) as a core nominal for pretense and hypocrisy.
**Notes:** The copular construction *ìwà rẹ̀ jẹ́ àgàbàgebè* ("his/her conduct is pretense") is directly attested in P2b; the inchoative *di* version follows the same structural pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.7 No common expression attested for the following opposed-state transitions:

- **Becoming ignorant (*àìmọ̀*):** No construction of the form *di àìmọ̀* is attested in the context. *àìmọ̀* is documented as a nominalized state (P2a) but not in inchoative constructions.
- **Becoming a counterfeit (*àdàwọ̀*):** *àdàwọ̀* appears in the context only in the contrastive construction *kì í ṣe àdàwọ̀* (P2b); no inchoative construction with it is attested.

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### Patterns
Three causative-adjacent patterns are documented in P4a (Pattern 5) and P4b:
- **fi** + [INSTRUMENT NOUN] + [VERB PHRASE] — instrumental causative
- **mú** + [OBJECT] + **wá** — serial verb "bring about" (documented in P4a but not exemplified within the truth domain)
- Focus construction **ló fi** + [INSTRUMENT] + [VERB] — focus + instrumental causative

An additional causative-adjacent pattern is documented in P2b:
- **ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́** — focus construction asserting that X is what saves/rescues one (a conventionalized causal assertion)

---

### 3.1 Testing causes character to be revealed

**Form:** ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn
**Example:** Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn.
**Transliteration:** Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn.
**English translation:** It is testing that causes a person's character to be revealed. / Trial is what shows one's true nature.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 17, Construction 4) and P4a (Pattern 5, second example).
**Notes:** This construction combines the focus marker *ló* with the instrumental *fi* to assert that *ìdánwò* (testing/trial) is the causal agent that brings *ìwà* (character) into visibility. P4a describes it as "a compound of two productive patterns rather than a distinct third type."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Deception causes material loss (instrumental causative)

**Form:** fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà [OBJECT] lọ́wọ́ [PERSON]
**Example:** Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀.
**Transliteration:** Wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀.
**English translation:** They used deception to take his/her money. / They obtained his/her money through fraud.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 14, Construction 2) and P4a (Pattern 5, first example).
**Notes:** *ìtànjẹ́* occupies the instrument slot in the *fi* construction; the verb phrase *gbà owó rẹ̀* ("take his/her money") specifies the outcome. P4a notes this is "the most productive causative-adjacent pattern in the truth domain."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Truth causes salvation / rescue (focus causative)

**Form:** òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**English translation:** It is truth that saves one. / Truth is what rescues a person.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 1, Construction 5) and P4a (Pattern 10).
**Notes:** The focus construction *ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* ("is what saves one") is a conventionalized causal assertion in Yoruba moral discourse. The progressive aspect marker *ń* indicates ongoing or habitual causation rather than a single event.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Good character causes salvation / rescue (focus causative)

**Form:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Example:** Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**English translation:** It is good character that saves one. / Good character is what rescues a person.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 16, Construction 1), P4a (Pattern 10), and P5 (Opposition 25).
**Notes:** Structurally parallel to 3.3 above; the same focus + habitual causative construction applies to *ìwà rere* as to *òtítọ́*. P4a documents both as instances of the same pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Knowledge causes salvation / rescue (focus causative)

**Form:** ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Example:** Ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**English translation:** It is knowledge that saves one. / Knowledge is what rescues a person.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 6, Construction 1).
**Notes:** Same focus + habitual causative construction as 3.3 and 3.4; P2b notes it "parallels the equivalent construction with *òtítọ́*."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.6 Bad character causes destruction (focus causative)

**Form:** ìwà burúkú ló pa á
**Example:** Ìwà burúkú ló pa á.
**Transliteration:** Ìwà burúkú ló pa á.
**English translation:** It is bad character that destroyed him/her. / Bad conduct was his/her undoing.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 16, Construction 5) and P5 (Opposition 25).
**Notes:** The focus construction here uses *pa* ("to kill, to destroy") rather than *gbani lọ́wọ́* ("to save"), encoding a destructive rather than protective causal outcome. P5 documents this as the negative pole of the *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* / *ìwà burúkú ló pa á* opposition.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.7 Causing truth to be acknowledged / admitted

**Form:** jẹ́ kí ìjẹ́ òtítọ́ wà
**Example:** Jẹ́ kí ìjẹ́ òtítọ́ wà.
**Transliteration:** Jẹ́ kí ìjẹ́ òtítọ́ wà.
**English translation:** Let there be acknowledgment of the truth. / Cause the truth to be admitted.
**Source:** The permissive/causative *jẹ́ kí* + clause construction is documented in P4a (Pattern 5) as a causative-adjacent pattern; *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* ("acknowledgment of the truth") is attested in P2a (Base 1, Derived Form 4) and P1b (Root 1, item 15).
**Notes:** *jẹ́ kí* is documented in P4a as a causative pattern but is not exemplified within the truth domain in P4a itself. The combination with *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* is derived by pattern extension; it is not directly attested as a fixed expression in the context.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

### 3.8 Causing authentication / endorsement to occur

**Form:** fọwọ́ sí [DOCUMENT/AGREEMENT]
**Example:** Wọ́n fọwọ́ sí àdéhùn náà.
**Transliteration:** Wọ́n fọwọ́ sí àdéhùn náà.
**English translation:** They endorsed/ratified the agreement. / They caused the agreement to be authenticated.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 20, Construction 1).
**Notes:** *fọwọ́ sí* is the verbal base of *ìfọwọ́sí* ("endorsement, authentication"); the act of putting one's hand to a document is the causal event that brings about the state of authentication. This is a direct causative in the sense that the agent's action produces the authenticated state.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.9 No common expression attested for the following causative types:

- **Causing someone to believe (*ìgbàgbọ́*):** No construction of the form "X caused Y to believe" is attested in the context. *ìgbàgbọ́* appears in possessive (*ó ní ìgbàgbọ́ pé...*) and inchoative (*di lágbára*) constructions but not in a causative construction where an external agent brings about belief.
- **Causing evidence (*ẹ̀rí*) to exist:** The imperative *mú ẹ̀rí wá* ("bring evidence") is attested in P2b and encodes a directive to produce evidence, but this is a directive rather than a causative statement about what brings evidence into existence.
- **Causing someone to know (*ìmọ̀*):** No construction of the form "X caused Y to know" is attested in the context. *ìmọ̀* appears in possessive and existential constructions but not in a causative construction in the attested data.

## 4. MAINTAINING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE (STABILITY)

### Pattern
**dúró mọ́** + [NOUN] — "to stand by, to adhere to" (documented in P2b)
**dúró dè** + [NOUN] — "to stand waiting for, to await" (documented in P2b)
**ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin** — "steadfast character, integrity, firmness of conduct" (documented in P1b and P2a)

---

### 4.1 Standing by / adhering to truth

**Form:** dúró mọ́ òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**English translation:** He/she stood by the truth. / He/she adhered to the truth.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 1, Construction 8).
**Notes:** *dúró mọ́* ("to stand by, to adhere to") encodes the maintenance of a commitment to truth under conditions where deviation is possible. P2b describes it as "a description of integrity under pressure," indicating that the stability construction is specifically relevant in contexts of challenge or temptation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.2 Steadfast character as a stable state

**Form:** ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin
**Example:** Ó ní ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin.
**Transliteration:** Ó ní ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin.
**English translation:** He/she has steadfast character. / He/she possesses integrity and firmness of conduct.
**Source:** *ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin* is attested in P1b (Root 2, item 20) and P2a (Base 16, Derived Form 9); the possessive construction *ó ní* + nominal is the standard means of asserting that someone possesses a character quality (attested throughout P2b).
**Notes:** *ìdúróṣinṣin* is itself derived from *dúró* ("to stand") + *ṣinṣin* ("firmly, steadily"), encoding stability as the defining feature of this character quality. P2a describes it as used "to describe a person whose character is consistent, firm, and reliable over time."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Truth not dying / enduring

**Form:** òtítọ́ kò ní kú
**Example:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú.
**English translation:** Truth will not die. / Truth is imperishable.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 1, Construction 2).
**Notes:** The negative future construction *kò ní* + verb encodes the permanent stability of truth — its resistance to destruction or cessation. P2b describes this as "a proverbial assertion about the enduring nature of truth." This is a stability assertion rather than a causative or inchoative one: it asserts that truth does not undergo the change of dying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.4 Justice must not die / must be maintained

**Form:** òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú
**Example:** Òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú.
**Transliteration:** Òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú.
**English translation:** Justice must not die. / Righteousness must not perish.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 3, Construction 2).
**Notes:** The deontic *kò gbọdọ̀* ("must not") encodes a normative stability requirement — justice is something that ought to be maintained. This differs from the descriptive stability of *òtítọ́ kò ní kú* (which asserts that truth simply does not die) by encoding an obligation to maintain the state rather than a natural property of permanence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.5 Awaiting judgment (stability of a pending state)

**Form:** dúró dè ìdájọ́
**Example:** Ó dúró dè ìdájọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó dúró dè ìdájọ́.
**English translation:** He/she is awaiting judgment. / He/she is standing in wait for the verdict.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 18, Construction 4).
**Notes:** *dúró dè* ("to stand waiting for") encodes a state of maintained suspension — the subject remains in a stable condition of anticipation while the judgment has not yet been rendered. This is a stability construction in the sense that it describes a state that persists over time, though the state is one of waiting rather than of possessing a truth-related quality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.6 Knowledge having no end (stability of an epistemic resource)

**Form:** ìmọ̀ kò ní òpin
**Example:** Ìmọ̀ kò ní òpin.
**Transliteration:** Ìmọ̀ kò ní òpin.
**English translation:** Knowledge has no end. / Learning is limitless.
**Source:** Directly attested in P2b (Base 6, Construction 3).
**Notes:** The construction *kò ní òpin* ("has no end/limit") encodes the inexhaustibility of knowledge as a stable, unbounded resource. P2b describes it as "a proverbial assertion" used in educational and philosophical discourse. Like *òtítọ́ kò ní kú*, this is a descriptive stability assertion rather than a causative or inchoative one.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Observations on distributional patterns:**

**1. The collapse constructions (*ti fọ́*, *ti wó*) are specific to relational/internal states.** They are attested only for *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (trust) and *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief/faith) in the context. They are not documented for *òtítọ́*, *ìwà*, *ìmọ̀*, or other truth-domain nominals. The use of different verbs (*fọ́* for trust, *wó* for faith) is not interchangeable in the attested data (P5, Opposition 14).

**2. The focus causative (*ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* / *ló pa á*) is attested for abstract moral and epistemic agents.** It applies to *òtítọ́*, *ìwà rere*, *ìwà burúkú*, and *ìmọ̀* — all abstract nominals that function as causal forces in Yoruba moral discourse. It is not attested for concrete procedural items like *ìdánwò* or *ìfọwọ́sí* in this specific construction.

**3. The instrumental causative (*fi* + instrument + verb) is attested specifically for *ìtànjẹ́* and *ìdánwò** in the truth domain.** *ìtànjẹ́* occupies the instrument slot to describe deception as a means of material harm; *ìdánwò* occupies the causal agent slot in the focus + instrumental construction to describe testing as the means by which character is revealed.

**4. Stability constructions divide into two types:** (a) descriptive permanence (*kò ní kú*, *kò ní òpin*) — asserting that a truth-related entity does not undergo change or cessation; and (b) normative stability (*kò gbọdọ̀ kú*) — asserting that a truth-related state ought to be maintained. Only *òtítọ́* and *ìmọ̀* are attested with descriptive permanence constructions; only *òdodo* is attested with the deontic stability construction in the context.

**5. The *di* inchoative is the most broadly applicable dynamic construction** across the truth domain, combining with nominal and adjectival predicates for *òtítọ́*, *irọ́*, *ìwà*, *ìgbàgbọ́*, *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*, *ojúlówó*, and *ìdájọ́*. However, it is not attested for *ẹ̀rí*, *ìmọ̀*, or *irọ́* as a nominal predicate in the context; those items appear in other construction types (existential, possessive) rather than inchoative ones.
