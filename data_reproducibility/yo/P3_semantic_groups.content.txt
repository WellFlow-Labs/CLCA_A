# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## IDENTIFIED SEMANTIC GROUPINGS

### GROUP 1: Factual Correctness and Accuracy

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to evaluate whether statements, claims, or representations correspond to facts or reality. These terms are used when assessing the accuracy of information, the correctness of descriptions, or the truthfulness of assertions. They appear in contexts where verification, confirmation, or factual evaluation is at stake.

**Representative expressions:**
- òtítọ́ (truth, truthfulness)
- tọ́ (to be correct, to be true)
- irọ́ (lie, falsehood)
- purọ́ (to lie, to be false)
- ṣe déédé (to be exact, to be accurate)
- pé (to be complete, to be exact)
- ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ (true word, correct statement)
- ìró tọ́ (true report, accurate news)
- ìwé tọ́ (correct writing, accurate document)
- kò tọ́ (to be incorrect, to be wrong)
- àṣìṣe (error, mistake)
- ṣìṣe (to err, to make a mistake)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- nitootọ́ / ní tòótọ́ (truly, truthfully) — overlaps with emphasis/assertion group
- ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀ (to be so, to be the case) — overlaps with confirmation group
- dájú (to be certain) — overlaps with epistemic certainty group

---

### GROUP 2: Correspondence and Matching

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether things align, match, or correspond to each other. These terms assess the degree of fit between different entities, statements, or expectations. They are commonly used in comparative contexts, verification processes, and when checking whether something meets a standard or matches a description.

**Representative expressions:**
- bá mu (to correspond, to match)
- ìbámu (correspondence, agreement, conformity)
- jọ (to resemble, to be similar)
- ìjọ (resemblance, similarity)
- jọra (to resemble each other)
- ìjọra (mutual resemblance)
- bá mu pọ̀ (to correspond greatly)
- kò bá mu (to not correspond, to not match)
- àìbámu (non-correspondence, disagreement)
- ìbámu pẹ̀lú (correspondence with)
- bá mu gẹ́gẹ́ bí (to correspond according to)
- ìbámu pípé (complete correspondence)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀ (to be exactly so) — overlaps with factual correctness group
- pé (to be complete, exact) — overlaps with factual correctness group

---

### GROUP 3: Genuineness and Authenticity

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that distinguish the real from the fake, the genuine from the imitation, or the authentic from the counterfeit. These terms are used when evaluating whether objects, persons, or situations are what they claim or appear to be, rather than being false representations or imitations.

**Representative expressions:**
- gidi (genuine, real, authentic)
- ṣe gidi (to be genuine, to be real)
- wúrà gidi (genuine gold)
- ọmọ gidi (genuine person)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- òtítọ́ (truth) — can be used for authenticity but primarily factual
- tọ́ (to be correct) — can evaluate authenticity but broader usage

---

### GROUP 4: Moral Character and Conduct

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate the moral quality of persons, their behavior, and their character. These terms assess whether conduct is virtuous or vicious, whether character is good or bad, and whether actions are just or unjust. They appear in moral discourse, character evaluation, and ethical instruction.

**Representative expressions:**
- ìwà rere (good character, good conduct)
- ìwà búburú (bad character, bad conduct)
- òdodo (justice, righteousness, fairness)
- ṣe òdodo (to do justice, to act righteously)
- ọmọ òtítọ́ (child of truth, truthful person)
- ẹni òtítọ́ (person of truth)
- tọ́ ọkàn / ọkàn tọ́ (straight heart, sincere)
- ọkàn méjì (two hearts, duplicitous)
- ọkàn kan (one heart, sincere)
- tọ́ lọ́nà (to be on the right path)
- ọ̀nà tọ́ (straight path, right way)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- òtítọ́ (truth) — used in moral contexts but also factual
- purọ́ (to lie) — moral evaluation but also factual incorrectness
- apurọ́ (liar) — character designation but based on factual behavior

---

### GROUP 5: Epistemic Certainty and Belief

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to indicate the degree of confidence, certainty, or belief that a speaker has regarding claims or propositions. These terms assess whether something is known with certainty, believed with confidence, or held with doubt. They appear in contexts of knowledge claims, belief statements, and epistemic evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- ìdájú (certainty, assurance, confidence)
- dájú (to be certain, to be sure)
- dájúdájú (very certain, absolutely sure)
- ìdánilójú (assurance, confirmation)
- ìgbàgbọ́ (belief, faith, trust)
- gbàgbọ́ (to believe, to trust)
- agbàgbọ́ (believer)
- gbígbàgbọ́ (believing, the act of believing)
- ìmọ̀ (knowledge)
- mọ̀ (to know, to be clear)
- àìmọ̀ (ignorance, lack of knowledge)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- dájú pé ó tọ́ (certain that it is true) — overlaps with factual correctness
- gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́ (believe that it is true) — overlaps with factual correctness
- ṣe é mọ̀ (to be clear, to be known) — overlaps with clarity/understanding

---

### GROUP 6: Appropriateness and Fittingness

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether actions, statements, or situations are fitting, proper, or appropriate in their context. These terms assess contextual suitability rather than factual accuracy, focusing on what is proper, seemly, or right for particular circumstances or persons. They appear in normative discourse about proper conduct and situational appropriateness.

**Representative expressions:**
- yẹ (to be fitting, to be appropriate, to be proper)
- ìyẹ (fittingness, appropriateness)
- àìyẹ (unfittingness, inappropriateness)
- tọ́ sí (to be right for, to be appropriate for)
- ó yẹ kó ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀ (it is fitting that it be so)
- ó yẹ ọ́ (it befits you)
- kò yẹ (it is not fitting)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- tọ́ (to be correct) — can indicate appropriateness but broader
- ṣe pàtàkì (to be important) — relevance overlaps with appropriateness

---

### GROUP 7: Possibility and Feasibility

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to evaluate whether something can occur, be done, or be accomplished. These terms assess the possibility or impossibility of states of affairs, actions, or events. They appear in contexts of practical reasoning, planning, and modal evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- ṣe é ṣe (to be possible, can be done)
- kò ṣe é ṣe (to be impossible, cannot be done)
- ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó tọ́ (it is possible that it is true)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified — this group is relatively distinct

---

### GROUP 8: Importance and Significance

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to evaluate the weight, relevance, or significance of matters, statements, or situations. These terms assess whether something matters, is important, or deserves attention. They appear in contexts of prioritization, relevance evaluation, and significance assessment.

**Representative expressions:**
- ṣe pàtàkì (to be important, to be significant)
- ó ṣe pàtàkì púpọ̀ (it is very important)
- kò ṣe pàtàkì (it is not important, it doesn't matter)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ṣe ìdí (to be the reason) — causally important but different sense

---

### GROUP 9: Speech Acts and Assertion

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used when making statements, telling, speaking, or asserting things. These terms focus on the act of communication itself and the manner in which information is conveyed. They appear in contexts of testimony, declaration, and communicative acts.

**Representative expressions:**
- sọ òtítọ́ (to tell the truth)
- pa irọ́ (to tell a lie)
- sọ irọ́ (to speak a lie)
- òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ (true word, truthful statement)
- ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ (true word, correct statement)
- ọ̀rọ̀ òfo (empty word, vain statement)
- nitootọ́ (truly, truthfully, in truth)
- sísọ (speaking, saying)
- ṣe ìjẹ́rìí (to bear witness, to testify)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- òtítọ́ (truth) — the content rather than the act
- irọ́ (lie) — the content rather than the act

---

### GROUP 10: Investigation and Verification Processes

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used to describe activities of examining, investigating, researching, or verifying information. These terms refer to systematic processes of discovering or confirming truth rather than the truth itself. They appear in investigative, analytical, and verificational contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- wádìí (to investigate)
- ṣe ìwádìí (to do research, to investigate)
- yẹ̀wò (to examine)
- ṣe àyẹ̀wò (to examine, to scrutinize)
- ṣe àkíyèsí (to observe, to notice)
- ṣe àtúnyẹ̀wò (to re-examine, to review)
- fi òtítọ́ hàn (to reveal the truth)
- pamọ́ òtítọ́ (to hide the truth)
- pa òtítọ́ (to destroy/suppress truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- dájú (to be certain) — result of verification rather than process
- gbàgbọ́ (to believe) — epistemic state rather than investigative process

---

### GROUP 11: Explanation and Clarification

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used when explaining, clarifying, describing, or making things understandable. These terms focus on making information clear or comprehensible rather than establishing its truth. They appear in educational, communicative, and interpretive contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ṣe àlàyé (to explain, to clarify)
- ṣe àpèjúwe (to describe, to depict)
- ṣe àpẹẹrẹ (to give an example)
- àtọ́kùn (explanation, clarification)
- ṣe àtọ́kùn (to clarify, to explain)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ṣe àfihàn (to demonstrate) — overlaps with evidence/proof
- ṣe é mọ̀ (to be clear) — state rather than process

---

### GROUP 12: Record and Documentation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions used for recording, documenting, correcting, or preserving information. These terms refer to activities of creating and maintaining accurate records rather than evaluating truth directly. They appear in administrative, archival, and editorial contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ṣe àkọsílẹ̀ (to record, to document)
- ṣe àtúnṣe (to correct, to revise)
- àṣìṣe (error, mistake)
- ṣe àṣìṣe (to make an error)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ìwé tọ́ (correct document) — evaluates accuracy of records
- ṣìṣe (to err) — making errors rather than correcting them

---

### GROUP 13: Evidence and Demonstration

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used when providing evidence, showing proof, or demonstrating claims. These terms focus on the presentation of support for assertions rather than the assertions themselves. They appear in argumentative, legal, and evidential contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ṣe àfihàn (to demonstrate, to show, to prove)
- ṣe àmì (to be a sign, to serve as evidence)
- ẹ̀rí (evidence, witness)
- jẹ́rìí (testimony, witness)
- ṣe ìjẹ́rìí (to bear witness, to testify)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- fi òtítọ́ hàn (to reveal the truth) — overlaps with investigation
- ṣe àpẹẹrẹ (to give example) — overlaps with explanation

---

### GROUP 14: Proverbial and Idiomatic Expressions

**Definition:** This group contains conventionalized sayings, proverbs, and idiomatic expressions related to truth and falsehood. These are fixed or semi-fixed expressions that convey cultural wisdom or common observations about truth, lies, and related concepts. They appear in moral instruction, encouragement, and cultural discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- òtítọ́ kọjá irọ́ (truth surpasses lies, truth prevails)
- òtítọ́ korò (truth is bitter, truth is hard)
- ẹnu ń sọ, ọkàn ò mọ̀ (mouth speaks but heart doesn't know)
- ọmọ òtítọ́ (child of truth)
- ẹni òtítọ́ (person of truth)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These expressions often draw on items from other groups but function as conventionalized units

---

## ITEMS WITH UNCLEAR OR MULTIPLE GROUP MEMBERSHIP

### Items that span multiple groups:

1. **òtítọ́ (truth)** — Appears in factual correctness, moral character, speech acts, and proverbial groups. This is the most central and versatile term in the domain.

2. **tọ́ (to be correct)** — Spans factual correctness, appropriateness, and moral character groups. The root meaning of "straightness" extends across multiple domains.

3. **dájú (to be certain)** — Belongs to epistemic certainty but also relates to verification processes and factual correctness.

4. **ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀ (to be so)** — Functions in factual correctness and confirmation contexts.

5. **pé (to be complete, exact)** — Overlaps between factual correctness and correspondence groups.

### Items that do not fit cleanly into groups:

1. **ṣe ìdí (to be the reason)** — Relates to causation and explanation but doesn't fit neatly with other explanatory terms or with importance/significance.

2. **ṣe (to do, to be)** — This highly productive auxiliary/copular verb appears in numerous constructions across all groups but is not itself a truth-related term.

3. **bẹ́ẹ̀ (so, like that)** — A demonstrative element that appears in many constructions but is not itself evaluative.

4. **títọ́ (being correct, gerund)** — Marginal form with limited usage, unclear group membership.

5. **atọ́ (straightness, correctness)** — Marginal form, less common than òtítọ́, unclear distinct usage pattern.

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Cross-cutting patterns observed:

1. **Factual vs. Moral domains**: Many expressions show parallel usage in both factual evaluation (is it true?) and moral evaluation (is it right?). The root **tọ́** particularly bridges these domains through its core meaning of "straightness."

2. **State vs. Process**: Several groups distinguish between states (being true, being certain) and processes (investigating, verifying, explaining). However, Yoruba often uses the same root with different constructions to express both.

3. **Content vs. Act**: Some expressions focus on content (truth, lies) while others focus on communicative acts (telling truth, lying). The distinction is maintained through different verb choices (sọ vs. pa with irọ́).

4. **Internal vs. External**: Character/sincerity expressions (ọkàn tọ́) evaluate internal states while factual correctness expressions evaluate external correspondence.

### Asymmetries noted:

1. **Truth elaboration vs. Falsehood**: The domain of truth shows much richer elaboration than falsehood. There are many more expressions for investigating, verifying, and demonstrating truth than for deception or falsification.

2. **Certainty without doubt**: The epistemic certainty group includes expressions for certainty and belief but no conventional expressions specifically for doubt or uncertainty (these are expressed through negation: kò dájú).

3. **Moral character emphasis**: The moral character group is well-developed, suggesting that truth-related concepts in Yoruba are deeply integrated with ethical evaluation.

4. **Limited scalar expressions**: Most groups show binary or categorical evaluation (true/false, correct/incorrect) rather than scalar or gradual distinctions. Degree is typically expressed through intensifiers (púpọ̀, gan-an) rather than inherent gradability.

---

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTES

This grouping is based solely on observed usage patterns in the P1 and P2 data. Groups were identified by:
- Contexts of typical use
- Patterns of co-occurrence
- Contrasts and oppositions in usage
- Functional similarities in discourse

No theoretical framework was imposed. The groups reflect natural clustering in Yoruba usage rather than universal semantic categories. Overlaps and ambiguities are preserved as they reflect actual patterns of use.
