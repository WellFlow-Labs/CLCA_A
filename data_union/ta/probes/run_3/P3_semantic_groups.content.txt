# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms appear in the same contexts, which are contrasted with each other, which share collocational partners, and which are restricted to particular registers or discourse types. No external framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns do not support a clean grouping, this is stated.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether something is true or false as a matter of fact

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker is asserting, questioning, or denying that a proposition or representation matches what is actually the case. They are the primary resources used in Tamil for basic truth-checking interactions — *atu uṇmaiyā?* (is that true?), *atu uṇmaitāṉ* (that is indeed true), *atu poyyillai* (that is not a lie) — and in contexts where the factual accuracy of a claim is at issue. They contrast directly with each other in evaluative constructions and appear as predicates, objects of *col* and *pēcu*, and subjects of motion verbs like *veḷippaṭu* and *teriya va*.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை / *uṇmai* — the primary positive term; used as predicate (*atu uṇmai*), as object (*uṇmai col*, *uṇmai pēcu*), and as subject (*uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu*)
- பொய் / *poy* — the primary negative term; appears in direct contrast with *uṇmai* and in parallel constructions (*poy collāte*, *poy pēcukiṟāṉ*)
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāṉa* — adjectival form; contrasts with *poyyāṉa* in attributive position
- பொய்யான / *poyyāṉa* — adjectival form; the direct antonym of *uṇmaiyāṉa*
- உண்மையாக / *uṇmaiyāka* — adverbial/predicative form; *itu uṇmaiyāka irukku* (this is genuinely the case)
- பொய்யாக / *poyyāka* — adverbial/predicative form; *poyyāka colluvāṉ* (he will speak falsely)
- உண்மையில் / *uṇmaiyil* — sentence-frame adverbial introducing corrections or clarifications of what is actually the case
- உண்மையிலேயே / *uṇmaiyilēyē* — emphatic adverbial reinforcing the sincerity or factual accuracy of a claim
- பொய்மை / *poymai* — abstract nominal; appears in literary and philosophical contexts contrasting *uṇmai* and *poymai* as opposing qualities
- அது உண்மையா? / *atu uṇmaiyā?* — basic truth-checking interrogative
- அது உண்மைதான் / *atu uṇmaitāṉ* — emphatic affirmative
- அது பொய்யில்லை / *atu poyyillai* — negative denial of falsehood
- உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை / *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai* — epistemic construction expressing uncertainty about the actual state of affairs

## Group 2: Terms used to describe the disclosure or concealment of what is actually the case

**Label:** Expressions used when truth is hidden, revealed, or sought

**Definition:** These expressions appear in narrative, journalistic, and legal contexts where the focus is on the movement of information between concealment and disclosure — truth coming to light, being suppressed, being sought, or being admitted. They cluster around verbs of motion (*veḷippaṭu*, *teriya va*), concealment (*maṟai*, *mūṭu*), and admission (*ottukkō*), and they presuppose a gap between what is actually the case and what is publicly known or acknowledged. They are distinct from Group 1 in that they are not primarily about evaluating whether something is true, but about the social and epistemic process of truth becoming known or being hidden.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது / *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* — the truth came to light
- உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது / *uṇmai teriya vantatu* — the truth became known (slightly more colloquial)
- உண்மை மறைக்கப்பட்டது / *uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu* — the truth was concealed (passive)
- உண்மையை மூடி மறைத்தார்கள் / *uṇmaiyai mūṭi maṟaittārkaḷ* — they covered up the truth (compound verb, emphasizing thoroughness)
- உண்மை மறைத்தல் / *uṇmai maṟaittal* — concealment of the truth (verbal noun; legal and journalistic)
- உண்மை தேடுதல் / *uṇmai tēṭutal* — seeking the truth (philosophical, journalistic, legal)
- உண்மையை அறிய வேண்டும் / *uṇmaiyai aṟiya vēṇṭum* — one must know the truth (deontic)
- உண்மையை எதிர்கொள்ள வேண்டும் / *uṇmaiyai etiṟkoḷḷa vēṇṭum* — one must face the truth
- உண்மையை ஒத்துக்கொண்டான் / *uṇmaiyai ottukkōṇṭāṉ* — he admitted the truth (implies prior concealment or denial)
- உண்மை நிலை என்ன? / *uṇmai nilai eṉṉa?* — what is the actual state of affairs? (presupposes gap between appearance and reality)
- உண்மை விளக்கம் / *uṇmai viḷakkam* — a true/accurate explanation (journalistic and administrative)
- நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam* — proof, demonstration (the act of establishing something conclusively; legal and formal)
- நிரூபிக்கப்படாத / *nirūpikkap-paṭāta* — unproven (passive negative; legal)
- ஆதாரம் இல்லாமல் குற்றம் சாட்டினான் / *ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭiṉāṉ* — he accused without evidence

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate whether a source, person, or piece of information can be relied upon

**Label:** Expressions used to assess trustworthiness or credibility

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is not simply whether something is true or false, but whether a person, source, institution, or piece of information is worthy of being believed or relied upon. They are used in legal evaluation of witnesses, journalistic assessment of sources, and everyday judgments of persons. They cluster around the root *nampu* (to trust, believe) and appear in constructions like *nampakamāṉa māṟṟu* (reliable source) and *cāṭciyiṉ nampakat-taṉmai kēḷvikkuriyatu* (the credibility of this witness is questionable). They are distinct from Group 1 in that they evaluate the *source* of a claim rather than the claim itself.

**Representative expressions:**

- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāṉa* — reliable, trustworthy, credible (attributive; journalistic and legal)
- நம்பகத்தன்மை / *nampakat-taṉmai* — reliability, credibility (abstract nominal; formal written Tamil)
- நம்பகமற்ற / *nampakam aṟṟa* — unreliable, not credible (privative)
- நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* — trust, belief, confidence (broader; moral, religious, and everyday)
- நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiyāṉa* — trustworthy, reliable (adjectival)
- நம்பிக்கையற்ற / *nampikkaiyaṟṟa* — untrustworthy, faithless
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worthy of trust, credible (formal; legal and journalistic)
- நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta* — unworthy of trust, not credible (formal; legal)
- நம்பமுடியாத / *nampamūṭiyāta* — unbelievable, incredible, not to be trusted (everyday)
- நம்பகமான ஆதாரத்திலிருந்து கிடைத்த தகவல் / *nampakamāṉa ātārattiliruntu kiṭaitta takaval* — information obtained from a reliable source
- இந்த சாட்சியின் நம்பகத்தன்மை கேள்விக்குரியது / *inta cāṭciyiṉ nampakat-taṉmai kēḷvikkuriyatu* — the credibility of this witness is questionable
- நம்பகத்தன்மை இல்லாத தகவல் / *nampakat-taṉmai illāta takaval* — information lacking credibility
- சரிபார்க்கப்படாத / *caripārkkap-paṭāta* — unverified (administrative and journalistic)
- சரிபார்க்கப்பட்ட / *caripārkkap-paṭṭa* — verified (administrative and journalistic)

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate the honesty or integrity of a person's conduct or character

**Label:** Expressions used to characterize persons or conduct as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the evaluation is of a person's character or habitual conduct rather than of a specific proposition or claim. They are used in moral instruction, professional evaluation, and civic discourse to describe whether a person acts transparently and without deception. They cluster around the root *nēr* (straight, direct) and the nominal *nērmai*, and they appear in constructions like *nērmayāṉa āḷ* (an honest person), *nērmayāka pēcu* (speak honestly), and *nērmai illai* (there is no honesty in him). They are distinct from Group 1 in that they evaluate persons and conduct rather than propositions, and from Group 3 in that they are about character rather than the reliability of a specific source or claim.

**Representative expressions:**

- நேர்மை / *nērmai* — honesty, integrity, uprightness (the primary term for this quality)
- நேர்மையான / *nērmayāṉa* — honest, upright, principled (adjectival; extremely common)
- நேர்மையாக / *nērmayāka* — honestly, uprightly (adverbial)
- நேர்மையற்ற / *nērmaiyaṟṟa* — dishonest, lacking integrity (privative)
- நேர்மையின்மை / *nērmayiṉmai* — dishonesty, absence of honesty (abstract nominal; formal)
- நேர்மையாளர் / *nērmayāḷar* — person(s) of integrity (agentive; formal and political)
- நேர்தன்மை / *nēr-taṉmai* — straightforwardness, directness (more literary/philosophical; overlaps with *nērmai*)
- நேர்மையாக பேசு / *nērmayāka pēcu* — speak honestly
- நேர்மை காட்டினான் / *nērmai kāṭṭiṉāṉ* — he showed honesty (in a specific situation)
- நேர்மை கடைப்பிடிக்கிறார் / *nērmai kaṭaippiṭikkiṟār* — he/she adheres to honesty (sustained practice)
- நேர்மையுடன் பணி செய்கிறார் / *nērmayuṭaṉ paṇi ceykiṟār* — he/she works with integrity
- அவரிடம் நேர்மை இல்லை / *avaṟiṭam nērmai illai* — there is no honesty in him/her
- நேர்மையான மனிதன் / *nērmayāṉa maṉitaṉ* — an honest person (very common collocation)
- நேர்மையான விடை / *nērmayāṉa viṭai* — an honest/straightforward answer

## Group 5: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate documents, testimony, and evidence

**Label:** Expressions used in formal proceedings to assess the status of documents, testimony, and proof

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts where the authenticity of documents, the validity of testimony, and the sufficiency of evidence are at issue. They include terms for original versus forged documents, for the act of bearing witness, for the body of evidence, and for the process of verification and proof. They are distinct from the other groups in that they are register-restricted — they appear in legal proceedings, official documentation, and journalism about legal matters — and they involve institutional rather than purely interpersonal evaluations.

**Representative expressions:**

- அசல் / *acal* — original, genuine (of documents; contrasts directly with *pōli* and *nakal*)
- போலி / *pōli* — fake, counterfeit (of documents, credentials, currency)
- போலி ஆவணம் / *pōli āvaṇam* — forged document (fixed compound; extremely common in legal Tamil)
- போலி ஆவணம் தயாரித்தான் / *pōli āvaṇam tayārittāṉ* — he prepared a forged document
- போலி நாணயம் புழக்கத்தில் விட்டான் / *pōli nāṇayam puḻakkattil viṭṭāṉ* — he circulated counterfeit currency
- அது போலியா, அசலா? / *atu pōliyā, acalā?* — is that fake or genuine? (standard disjunctive contrast)
- சாட்சி / *cāṭci* — witness, testimony (legal and religious)
- சாட்சியம் / *cāṭciyam* — the act or body of testimony
- பொய்ச் சாட்சி / *poy-c-cāṭci* — false testimony, perjury
- சாட்சி சொல்கிறான் / *cāṭci colkiṟāṉ* — he is giving testimony
- சாட்சி கொடுத்தான் / *cāṭci koṭuttāṉ* — he gave testimony/evidence
- சாட்சி விசாரணை நடந்தது / *cāṭci vicāraṇai naṭantatu* — the examination of witnesses took place
- சாட்சி இல்லாமல் நிரூபிக்க முடியாது / *cāṭci illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu* — it cannot be proven without a witness
- ஆதாரம் / *ātāram* — evidence, proof, basis (legal, academic, everyday)
- ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāram aṟṟa* — unfounded, without basis
- ஆதாரமில்லாத / *ātāramillāta* — without evidence, baseless
- நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam* — proof, demonstration (formal and legal)
- நிரூபிக்கு / *nirūpikku* — to prove, demonstrate (verb; legal and formal)
- சரிபார்த்தல் / *caripārtttal* — verification, checking (administrative and technical)
- உண்மைப்படுத்துதல் / *uṇmaippaṭuttutal* — authentication, substantiation (formal and administrative)
- உண்மைப்படுத்தப்பட்ட / *uṇmaippaṭuttap-paṭṭa* — authenticated (passive past; administrative)
- உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāṉa* — contrary to truth (formal legal expression)

## Group 6: Terms used to characterize deliberate deception or betrayal of trust

**Label:** Expressions used to describe intentional deception, especially in literary and formal registers

**Definition:** These expressions appear in literary, classical, and formal written Tamil to characterize deception that is deliberate and involves a betrayal of trust or expectation, going beyond simple falsehood. They tend to appear in moral condemnation and in narrative characterization of villains or morally compromised figures. The binomial *kaḷḷam kapaṭam* and the term *vañcakam* are particularly associated with this cluster. They are distinct from *poy* (Group 1) in that they foreground intentionality and betrayal rather than simple falsity, and they are more register-restricted, appearing primarily in literary, classical, and formal contexts rather than everyday speech.

**Representative expressions:**

- கள்ளம் / *kaḷḷam* — deceit, fraud, concealment (classical and literary; moral failing)
- வஞ்சகம் / *vañcakam* — treachery, deliberate betrayal (literary and formal; stronger connotation of intentional betrayal than *kaḷḷam*)
- வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai* — deception, treachery (abstract nominal; literary and formal; overlaps with *vañcakam*)
- வஞ்சகன் / *vañcakaṉ* — traitor, deceiver (agentive; literary and formal)
- கள்ளம் கபடம் இல்லாத மனிதன் / *kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta maṉitaṉ* — a person without deceit or guile (fixed binomial; the pairing *kaḷḷam kapaṭam* is conventionalized)
- கள்ளம் கபடம் அறியாத குழந்தை / *kaḷḷam kapaṭam aṟiyāta kuḻantai* — an innocent child who does not know deceit or guile
- மனதில் கள்ளம் வைத்திருக்கிறான் / *maṉatil kaḷḷam vaittirukkirāṉ* — he harbors deceit in his heart
- வஞ்சகம் செய்தான் / *vañcakam ceytāṉ* — he committed treachery
- வஞ்சகம் நிறைந்த உலகம் / *vañcakam niṟainta ulakam* — a world full of treachery
- நம்பிக்கை துரோகம் / *nampikkai turōkam* — betrayal of trust, breach of faith (moral and legal; involves the trust-evaluation cluster)
- கள்ளமான / *kaḷḷamāṉa* — deceitful, fraudulent (adjectival; literary and formal)
- வஞ்சகமான / *vañcakamāṉa* — treacherous, deceitful (adjectival; literary and formal)
- கள்ளமற்ற / *kaḷḷam aṟṟa* — without deceit, guileless (privative; literary and devotional)

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate whether actions, decisions, or treatment are fair or just

**Label:** Expressions used to assess fairness, justice, or legitimacy of conduct and decisions

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the evaluation is of whether an action, decision, price, or situation is fair, legitimate, or morally right — not whether a proposition is true or a person is honest, but whether a course of action or outcome is just. They cluster around *niyāyam* and *anyāyam* and appear in moral protest (*itu niyāyamā?*, *itu anyāyam*), demands for fair treatment (*niyāyam kēṭkiṟēṉ*), and assertions about the ultimate triumph of justice (*niyāyam vellum*). They are distinct from Group 4 (honesty of persons) in that they evaluate outcomes and procedures rather than character.

**Representative expressions:**

- நியாயம் / *niyāyam* — justice, fairness, rightness, reason (the primary positive term)
- நியாயமான / *niyāyamāṉa* — just, fair, reasonable, legitimate (adjectival; extremely common)
- நியாயமாக / *niyāyamāka* — justly, fairly, reasonably (adverbial)
- நியாயமற்ற / *niyāyam aṟṟa* — unjust, unfair (privative)
- நியாயவாதி / *niyāyavāti* — advocate of justice, one who argues for what is right
- அன்யாயம் / *anyāyam* — injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness (the primary negative term; direct antonym of *niyāyam*)
- அன்யாயமான / *anyāyamāṉa* — unjust, unfair, wrongful (adjectival)
- அன்யாயமாக / *anyāyamāka* — unjustly, unfairly (adverbial)
- இது நியாயமா? / *itu niyāyamā?* — is this right/fair? (common moral protest or request for justification)
- இது அன்யாயம் / *itu anyāyam* — this is unjust (direct moral protest)
- நியாயம் கேட்கிறேன் / *niyāyam kēṭkiṟēṉ* — I am asking for justice
- நியாயம் கிடைக்கவில்லை / *niyāyam kiṭaikkavil̲l̲ai* — justice was not obtained
- நியாயம் வெல்லும் / *niyāyam vellum* — justice will prevail
- அன்யாயம் பொறுக்கமாட்டேன் / *anyāyam poṟukkamaṭṭēṉ* — I will not tolerate injustice
- அன்யாயத்திற்கு எதிராக குரல் கொடுத்தான் / *anyāyattirku etirāka kural koṭuttāṉ* — he raised his voice against injustice
- நியாயமான விலையில் கொடு / *niyāyamāṉa vilaiyil koṭu* — give it at a fair price (commercial)
- நியாயமான கோரிக்கை / *niyāyamāṉa kōrikkai* — a reasonable/just demand (political and legal)
- நேர்மையான போட்டி / *nērmayāṉa pōṭṭi* — fair competition (commercial and electoral; involves *nērmai* root in a fairness-of-procedure frame)
- நேர்மையான வியாபாரம் / *nērmayāṉa viyāpāram* — honest business, fair trade

---

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate correctness or accuracy in practical and technical contexts

**Label:** Expressions used to assess whether something is correct, accurate, or properly done

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the evaluation is of whether a calculation, answer, action, or piece of work is correct or accurate — not in the moral or propositional sense of *uṇmai*, but in the practical sense of conforming to a standard or expectation. They cluster around *cari* (correct, right) and *tavaṟu* (mistake, error) and appear in everyday checking interactions (*caripārtttal*, *cariyāka purinthukōṇṭāyā?*), admissions of error (*tavaṟu ceytuviṭṭēṉ*), and corrections (*tavaṟai tiruttikkōṇṭāṉ*). They are distinct from Group 1 in that the standard of evaluation is practical correctness rather than correspondence to reality, and from Group 4 in that they evaluate actions and outputs rather than character.

**Representative expressions:**

- சரி / *cari* — correct, right, okay (also used as response particle and interjection)
- சரியான / *cariyāṉa* — correct, proper, accurate (adjectival)
- சரியாக / *cariyāka* — correctly, properly, accurately (adverbial)
- சரியில்லாத / *cariyillāta* — not right, incorrect, not functioning properly
- தவறு / *tavaṟu* — mistake, error, wrong, fault (both epistemic and moral)
- தவறான / *tavaṟāṉa* — wrong, erroneous, incorrect (adjectival)
- தவறாக / *tavaṟāka* — wrongly, erroneously, by mistake (adverbial)
- தவறில்லாத / *tavaṟillāta* — faultless, without error, flawless
- சரிபார்த்தல் / *caripārtttal* — verification, checking (act of checking correctness)
- சரி பார்த்துவிட்டேன் / *cari pārttuviṭṭēṉ* — I have checked/verified it
- சரியாக புரிந்துகொண்டாயா? / *cariyāka purinthukōṇṭāyā?* — did you understand correctly?
- அது சரிதானா? / *atu caritāṉā?* — is that really correct? (mild doubt or request for confirmation)
- சரி செய்துவிட்டார்கள் / *cari ceytuviṭṭārkaḷ* — they have fixed/corrected it
- தவறு செய்துவிட்டேன் / *tavaṟu ceytuviṭṭēṉ* — I have made a mistake
- தவறை ஒத்துக்கொண்டான் / *tavaṟai ottukkōṇṭāṉ* — he admitted the mistake
- தவறை திருத்திக்கொண்டான் / *tavaṟai tiruttikkōṇṭāṉ* — he corrected his mistake
- அது என் தவறு இல்லை / *atu eṉ tavaṟu illai* — that is not my mistake
- தவறிலிருந்து படிக்கணும் / *tavaṟiliruntu paṭikkaṇum* — one must learn from mistakes
- தரம் / *taram* — quality, standard, grade (evaluation against a standard of adequacy)
- தரமான / *taramāṉa* — of good quality, up to standard
- தரமற்ற / *taram aṟṟa* — substandard, of poor quality

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items appear in the data but do not fit cleanly into a single group, or participate in multiple groups in ways that reflect genuine usage overlap rather than analytical imprecision.

---

**நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* (trust, belief, faith, confidence)**

This term participates in Group 3 (trustworthiness of sources and persons) but also appears in religious and devotional contexts where the relevant state is faith in a deity or doctrine rather than an epistemic assessment of a claim, and in political contexts (*nampikkai vākku* — vote of confidence) where the trust relation is institutionalized. The term's range across interpersonal trust, epistemic credence, religious faith, and political confidence means it does not belong exclusively to any one group. It is noted here as a term whose usage spans multiple of the identified groups and extends beyond all of them.

---

**தவறு / *tavaṟu* (mistake, error, wrong, fault)**

This term appears in Group 8 (practical correctness) in contexts of factual or technical error, but also in moral contexts (*tavaṟu ceytāl* — if one does wrong) where it overlaps with Group 7 (fairness and justice) and with the moral evaluation of conduct in Group 4. The P1 entry notes that it appears in both epistemic contexts (factual error) and moral contexts (wrongdoing, fault). The term's dual use means it participates in both Group 8 and, in moral contexts, in the broader evaluative territory shared by Groups 4 and 7.

---

**உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāṉa* used with non-propositional nouns**

When *uṇmaiyāṉa* modifies nouns like *aṉpu* (love), *naṇpaṉ* (friend), or *vaḻi* (path), it is not evaluating a proposition or a document but characterizing a relationship or mode of life as genuine rather than feigned. These uses — *uṇmai aṉpu* (true love), *uṇmai naṇpaṉ* (true friend), *uṇmai vaḻi* (the path of truth) — do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above, which are organized around propositional truth, disclosure, trustworthiness, honesty of conduct, legal status, deliberate deception, fairness, or practical correctness. They represent a use of the *uṇmai* root as a general authenticity or genuineness marker that extends beyond the truth-evaluation domain as identified in P1b's peripheral extensions. These items are noted but not assigned to a group.

---

**நியாயம் / *niyāyam* in philosophical and logical senses**

The P1 entry notes that *niyāyam* also carries a sense of logical reasoning or valid argument, reflecting its Sanskrit heritage (*nyāya*). This use — where *niyāyam* means something closer to "valid reasoning" or "logical grounds" — does not fit cleanly into Group 7 (fairness and justice) and is not well-represented in the P2 constructions documented. It is noted here as a usage that exists in the data but is not sufficiently represented to form a group of its own.

---

**உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmai-t-taṉmai* and நேர்தன்மை / *nēr-taṉmai***

Both of these abstract nominals are marked [MARGINAL] in P1 and appear primarily in philosophical and literary written Tamil. They do not anchor multi-word constructions in P2b and do not appear in the collocational clusters that define the groups above. They are noted as items that participate in the domain but whose usage is too restricted and too compositionally transparent to generate grouping patterns of their own.

---

**உண்மையான அர்த்தத்தில் / *uṇmaiyāṉa arttattil* and உண்மைத் தன்மையுள்ள / *uṇmai-t-taṉmaiyuḷḷa***

These are semi-fixed phrases used in formal and philosophical written Tamil to specify that a term is being used in its strict or proper sense. They do not fit into any of the groups above, which are organized around evaluative and social uses of truth-related vocabulary. They represent a metalinguistic or definitional use of the *uṇmai* root that is distinct from all the identified groups. They are noted but not assigned.

## Items That Do Not Fit Any Identified Group

**நிரூபணம் / *nirūpaṇam*** participates in both Group 2 (disclosure and establishment of truth) and Group 5 (legal and administrative evaluation of evidence). It is listed in both groups above, reflecting genuine overlap in its usage contexts.

**சரிபார்த்தல் / *caripārtttal*** and its passive forms (*caripārkkap-paṭāta*, *caripārkkap-paṭṭa*) participate in both Group 5 (legal and administrative verification) and Group 8 (practical correctness checking). Both assignments are supported by usage.

**நேர்மையான போட்டி / *nērmayāṉa pōṭṭi*** and **நேர்மையான வியாபாரம் / *nērmayāṉa viyāpāram*** are listed in Group 7 because their primary evaluative concern is procedural fairness rather than personal honesty, even though they are formed from the *nērmai* root. This reflects the observation in P1b's peripheral extensions that the *nēr* root in these contexts operates in a fairness-of-procedure frame.
