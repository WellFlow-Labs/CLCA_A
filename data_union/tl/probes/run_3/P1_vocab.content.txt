# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Note on transliteration:** Tagalog uses the Latin-based Filipino alphabet. Native script (Baybayin) is not in active general use. Transliteration column is omitted where the form is already in Latin script; stress marking follows standard Philippine linguistic convention using acute accent where disambiguation is needed.

---

## Lexical Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Literal meaning:** root form; no transparent morphological decomposition in modern usage
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Usage:** Used to affirm the truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs, as in *totoo ba iyan?* ("is that true/real?"). Also used to assert that something is not fabricated or pretended. Participates in derivation: *katotohanan* (truth, reality), *totoong-totoo* (intensified: very real/true).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-an* (nominalizing circumfix); "the state/quality of being totoo"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the truth of the matter
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to truth as an abstract quality or to a specific true state of affairs. Commonly used in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse, e.g., *sabihin ang katotohanan* ("tell the truth"). Frequently contrasted with *kasinungalingan* (falsehood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tunay
**Literal meaning:** root form; no transparent decomposition
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Usage:** Used to assert that something is what it claims or appears to be, often with emphasis on authenticity rather than mere factual accuracy, e.g., *tunay na ginto* ("genuine gold"), *tunay na pagmamahal* ("real/genuine love"). Participates in derivation: *katunayan* (proof, evidence; the fact/reality of something).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** katunayan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "the state/quality of being tunay"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, the fact that, in fact, verification
**Usage:** Used both as a noun meaning "proof" or "evidence" and as a discourse marker meaning "in fact" or "as a matter of fact." In legal and formal contexts it refers to documentary or testimonial evidence establishing that something is genuine or true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tama
**Literal meaning:** root form
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage:** Applied to answers, judgments, actions, or measurements to indicate they are correct or accurate, e.g., *tama ang sagot* ("the answer is correct"). Also used in moral evaluation to mean "right" or "proper." Contrasted with *mali* (wrong, incorrect).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** mali
**Literal meaning:** root form
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong, e.g., *mali ang ginawa mo* ("what you did was wrong"). Participates in derivation: *pagkakamali* (mistake, error), *magkamali* (to make a mistake).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** wasto
**Literal meaning:** root form; possibly from Spanish *vasto* or independent development
**Typical English translations:** correct, proper, accurate, right
**Usage:** Often used in more formal or technical registers than *tama* to indicate correctness or propriety, e.g., *wastong paraan* ("correct/proper method"), *wastong pagbigkas* ("correct pronunciation"). Also carries a sense of moral or procedural propriety.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** tamà o malî
**Literal meaning:** *tama* (correct/right) + *o* (or) + *mali* (wrong/incorrect)
**Typical English translations:** right or wrong, correct or incorrect
**Usage:** A fixed paired expression used in evaluative contexts, particularly in moral reasoning and test/examination discourse, e.g., *tamà o malî ang ginawa niya?* ("Was what he did right or wrong?"). Functions as a conventionalized binary framing for evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** huwad
**Literal meaning:** root form
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, spurious
**Usage:** Used to describe objects, documents, or representations that are not genuine but are made to appear so, e.g., *huwad na pera* ("counterfeit money"), *huwad na dokumento* ("fake document"). Commonly used in legal and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** peke
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish *fake* via English, or directly from English *fake*
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, phony
**Usage:** Colloquial and widely used alternative to *huwad*, applied to objects, persons, or claims that are not genuine, e.g., *pekeng produkto* ("fake product"). More informal in register than *huwad* but extremely common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** kasinungalingan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *sinungaling* + *-an*; *sinungaling* = liar; circumfix nominalizes to "the quality/act of lying"
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** Refers to a deliberate false statement or to the quality of being false/deceptive, e.g., *huwag magsabi ng kasinungalingan* ("do not tell a lie"). Commonly used in moral and religious discourse. Related form: *sinungaling* (liar, lying person).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** sinungaling
**Literal meaning:** root or derived form; agent/quality noun referring to one who lies
**Typical English translations:** liar, lying, deceitful
**Usage:** Used as a noun ("a liar") or predicate adjective ("he is lying/a liar"), e.g., *sinungaling ka* ("you are a liar"). Evaluates a person's character or a specific act of speech as dishonest.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** mapagkakatiwalaan
**Literal meaning:** *ma-* + *pagkakatiwalaan*; from *tiwala* (trust, confidence) + multiple affixes; "one/something that can be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** Used to evaluate persons, sources, or institutions as worthy of trust, e.g., *mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon* ("reliable source of information"). Common in contexts of assessing credibility and reliability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** tiwala
**Literal meaning:** root form
**Typical English translations:** trust, confidence, reliance
**Usage:** Refers to the state of trusting or having confidence in a person or thing, e.g., *may tiwala ako sa kanya* ("I trust him/her"). Evaluatively relevant as the basis for assessing reliability and credibility of persons or sources.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** kredible
**Literal meaning:** from English/Spanish *credible*
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable
**Usage:** Used in formal, journalistic, and legal registers to evaluate whether a witness, source, or claim is believable and worthy of acceptance, e.g., *kredible na saksi* ("credible witness"). Borrowed term that has entered standard formal Tagalog/Filipino usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** lehitimo
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish *legítimo*
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, genuine
**Usage:** Used in legal and formal contexts to evaluate whether something is lawful, officially recognized, or genuine, e.g., *lehitimong negosyo* ("legitimate business"), *lehitimong anak* ("legitimate child"). Carries both legal and authenticity-related senses.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** orihinal
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish/English *original*
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, not a copy
**Usage:** Used to distinguish an authentic item from a copy or imitation, e.g., *orihinal na kopya* ("original copy"), *orihinal na produkto* ("genuine/original product"). Common in commercial and aesthetic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** aktuwal
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish/English *actual*
**Typical English translations:** actual, real (as opposed to hypothetical or apparent)
**Usage:** Used to assert that something is the real or factual case rather than supposed or hypothetical, e.g., *aktuwal na nangyari* ("what actually happened"). More formal in register; used in journalistic and academic discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Literal meaning:** *maka-* + *totoo* + *-an*; "in accordance with truth/reality"
**Typical English translations:** realistic, truthful, true-to-life, verisimilar
**Usage:** Used in aesthetic and narrative contexts to evaluate whether a representation, portrayal, or depiction corresponds faithfully to reality, e.g., *makatotohanang pelikula* ("realistic/true-to-life film"). Also used in moral contexts to mean "truthful."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** tapat
**Literal meaning:** root form; also means "facing, directly opposite"
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, straightforward, frank
**Usage:** Evaluates a person or statement as honest and without concealment, e.g., *maging tapat* ("be honest"), *tapatang sagot* ("honest/straightforward answer"). The spatial sense ("facing directly") and the honesty sense coexist and are likely related metaphorically.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** katapatan
**Literal meaning:** *ka-* + *tapat* + *-an*; "the quality/state of being tapat"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, fidelity, loyalty
**Usage:** Nominal form used to refer to honesty, sincerity, or fidelity as a quality, e.g., *katapatan sa trabaho* ("fidelity/dedication to work"), *katapatan ng salita* ("honesty of word"). Covers both epistemic honesty and moral loyalty/fidelity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** dalisay
**Literal meaning:** root form
**Typical English translations:** pure, unadulterated, genuine, uncontaminated
**Usage:** Used to evaluate substances, emotions, or intentions as pure and unmixed, e.g., *dalisay na ginto* ("pure gold"), *dalisay na puso* ("pure heart"). In philosophical and religious discourse it can carry a sense of moral or ontological purity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** perpekto
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish *perfecto*
**Typical English translations:** perfect, flawless, exact
**Usage:** Used to evaluate objects, performances, or reproductions as without flaw or as exactly matching a standard, e.g., *perpektong kopya* ("perfect copy"), *perpektong sagot* ("perfect answer"). Common in everyday and aesthetic evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** eksaktong-eksakto
**Literal meaning:** reduplication of *eksakto* (from Spanish *exacto*); intensified "exact"
**Typical English translations:** exactly right, precisely accurate, spot-on
**Usage:** Intensified form used to assert that something matches a standard, measurement, or description with complete precision, e.g., *eksaktong-eksakto ang sukat* ("the measurement is exactly right"). The reduplication pattern is productive in Tagalog for intensification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** eksakto
**Literal meaning:** from Spanish *exacto*
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** Used to evaluate measurements, statements, or reproductions as precisely accurate, e.g., *eksakto ang oras* ("the time is exact/precise"). Common in everyday and technical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** mapanlinlang
**Literal meaning:** *mapa-* + *linlang* (to deceive, to mislead) + agentive/adjectival affix; "tending to deceive"
**Typical English translations:** deceptive, misleading, fraudulent
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, representations, or persons as deliberately misleading or deceptive, e.g., *mapanlinlang na advertisement* ("deceptive advertisement"). Common in consumer, legal, and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** huwad na
**Literal meaning:** *huwad* (fake/counterfeit) + *na* (already; now; marker of changed state)
**Typical English translations:** it turns out to be fake, proven fake, now known to be counterfeit
**Usage:** Fixed predicate expression used to assert that something has been determined or revealed to be fake or counterfeit, e.g., *huwad na ang dokumento* ("the document has been found to be fake"). The particle *na* marks the evaluation as a conclusion reached.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** parang totoo
**Literal meaning:** *parang* (like, as if, seemingly) + *totoo* (true/real); "as if true/real"
**Typical English translations:** seems real, looks genuine, as if true, realistic-seeming
**Usage:** Used to evaluate something as having the appearance of being real or true without asserting that it actually is, e.g., *parang totoo ang kanyang kwento* ("his story seems true/real"). Common in everyday evaluative discourse about appearances versus reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** pagpapatunay
**Literal meaning:** *pag-* + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-ay* (verbal nominalization); "the act of making/proving tunay"
**Typical English translations:** verification, authentication, confirmation, proof
**Usage:** Refers to the act or process of verifying, authenticating, or confirming that something is genuine or true, e.g., *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* ("document authentication/verification"). Used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** di-mapagkakatiwalaan
**Literal meaning:** *di-* (negation) + *mapagkakatiwalaan* (trustworthy); "not trustworthy"
**Typical English translations:** untrustworthy, unreliable, not to be trusted
**Usage:** Negative counterpart of *mapagkakatiwalaan*, used to evaluate persons, sources, or institutions as lacking in reliability or credibility, e.g., *di-mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* ("unreliable witness"). The *di-* negation prefix is productive and standard.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
