# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## IDENTIFIED GROUPS

---

### GROUP 1: Factual Correctness Evaluation

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, beliefs, or information align with facts or reality. These expressions focus on the accuracy of propositional content rather than moral rightness or authenticity. Commonly used in contexts of verification, correction, error identification, and knowledge assessment. Distinguished from moral evaluation and from assessments of genuineness or authenticity.

**Representative expressions:**
- doğru (true, correct)
- yanlış (wrong, false, incorrect)
- doğruluk (truth, correctness)
- yanlışlık (wrongness, incorrectness)
- doğrulamak (to verify, to confirm)
- yanlışlamak (to falsify, to prove wrong)
- doğrulama (verification, confirmation)
- yanlışlama (falsification, refutation)
- hatalı (erroneous, faulty)
- hata (error, mistake)
- hatasız (flawless, error-free)
- yanılmak (to be mistaken, to err)
- yanılma (error, mistake, being mistaken)
- yanılgı (error, fallacy, misconception)
- isabetli (accurate, apt, on target)
- isabet (hit, accuracy, aptness)
- isabetsiz (inaccurate, inapt, off-target)
- doğru olmak (to be true, to be correct)
- yanlış olmak (to be wrong, to be incorrect)
- doğru çıkmak (to turn out to be true/correct)
- yanlış çıkmak (to turn out to be wrong)
- hata yapmak (to make a mistake)
- yanlış anlamak (to misunderstand)
- yanlış bilmek (to know incorrectly)
- doğru kabul etmek (to accept as true)
- doğru bulmak (to find correct, to consider right)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- doğru: Also used for spatial directness and moral rightness
- hak: Can indicate factual truth but more commonly used for rights and justice
- gerçek: Overlaps but emphasizes reality/authenticity over mere correctness

### GROUP 2: Reality and Authenticity Assessment

**Definition:** Terms distinguishing what is real, genuine, or authentic from what is fake, artificial, or merely apparent. These expressions evaluate ontological status and authenticity rather than propositional accuracy. Used in contexts of authentication, distinguishing originals from copies, identifying counterfeits, and evaluating whether something is what it appears or claims to be. Often applied to objects, identities, and experiences.

**Representative expressions:**
- gerçek (real, true, genuine)
- sahte (fake, counterfeit, false)
- gerçeklik (reality, realness, actuality)
- sahtelik (fakeness, counterfeitness, fraudulence)
- gerçekten (really, truly, actually)
- asıl (original, genuine, essential)
- özgün (original, authentic, genuine)
- özgünlük (originality, authenticity)
- taklit (imitation, copy, fake)
- taklitçi (imitator, copyist, mimic)
- taklit etmek (to imitate, to copy)
- hakiki (real, genuine, true, authentic)
- doğal (natural, genuine, authentic)
- yapay (artificial, synthetic, man-made)
- doğallık (naturalness, genuineness)
- yapaylık (artificiality, artificialness)
- sahtekâr (fraud, swindler, counterfeiter)
- sahtekârlık (fraud, swindling, counterfeiting)
- gerçek olmak (to be real, to be true)
- sahte olmak (to be fake, to be counterfeit)
- sahte çıkmak (to turn out to be fake)
- gerçek hayat (real life, actual life)
- gerçek kimlik (true identity, real identity)
- gerçek yüz (true face, real nature)
- asıl kimlik (true identity, original identity)
- sahte para (counterfeit money)
- sahte belge (forged document)
- taklit ürün (imitation product)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- gerçek: Also used for factual truth, not just ontological reality
- doğru: Can indicate genuineness in some contexts (doğru dürüst)
- hakikat: Overlaps but more elevated/philosophical

### GROUP 3: Trustworthiness and Reliability Evaluation

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether persons, sources, information, or objects can be depended upon or trusted. These expressions assess reliability, dependability, and consistency rather than truth content per se. Used in contexts of source evaluation, character assessment, product quality, and determining what or whom one can rely upon. Distinguished from evaluations of honesty (which focus on truthful intent) and from factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- güvenilir (reliable, trustworthy, dependable)
- güvenilirlik (reliability, trustworthiness, dependability)
- güvenilmez (unreliable, untrustworthy)
- güvenilmezlik (unreliability, untrustworthiness)
- sağlam (solid, sound, reliable, robust)
- sağlamlık (solidity, soundness, reliability)
- sağlamlaşmak (to become solid/sound, to be strengthened)
- sağlamlaştırmak (to solidify, to strengthen)
- inandırıcı (convincing, credible, believable)
- inandırıcılık (convincingness, credibility, persuasiveness)
- tutarlı (consistent, coherent, logical)
- tutarlılık (consistency, coherence)
- tutarsız (inconsistent, incoherent, contradictory)
- tutarsızlık (inconsistency, incoherence)
- güvenilir olmak (to be reliable, to be trustworthy)
- güvenilir kaynak (reliable source)
- güvenilir bilgi (reliable information)
- sağlam olmak (to be solid, to be sound)
- sağlam kanıt (solid evidence)
- sağlam delil (solid evidence)
- sağlam kaynak (reliable source)
- inandırıcı olmak (to be convincing, to be credible)
- inandırıcı gelmek (to seem convincing)
- tutarlı olmak (to be consistent)
- tutarlı davranmak (to behave consistently)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- sağlam: Can refer to physical solidity as well as epistemic reliability
- inandırıcı: Focuses on persuasive power, which may not correlate with actual truth

### GROUP 4: Honesty and Moral Character

**Definition:** Terms evaluating the moral character of persons, particularly regarding truthfulness, integrity, and sincerity in speech and action. These expressions assess whether someone habitually tells the truth and acts with integrity, rather than evaluating specific statements or claims. Used in character descriptions, moral judgments, and evaluations of personal conduct. Distinguished from factual accuracy (which concerns statements) and from justice/fairness (which concerns treatment of others).

**Representative expressions:**
- dürüst (honest, truthful, sincere)
- dürüstlük (honesty, truthfulness, integrity)
- dürüstçe (honestly, truthfully)
- yalancı (liar, lying, false)
- yalancılık (lying as practice, mendacity)
- sahtekâr (fraud, swindler, counterfeiter)
- sahtekârlık (fraud, swindling, counterfeiting)
- doğrucu (truthful person, honest person)
- doğrulukçu (advocate of truth, principled person)
- dürüst olmak (to be honest, to be truthful)
- dürüst davranmak (to behave honestly)
- dürüst insan (honest person)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- doğruluk: Can refer to both factual truth and moral honesty
- sahte/sahtekâr: Overlap with authenticity assessment but also imply moral failing

---

### GROUP 5: Deception and Misleading

**Definition:** Terms describing statements, appearances, or actions that cause or are likely to cause false beliefs, whether intentionally or not. These expressions focus on the misleading effect rather than simply being false. Used in contexts of warning about misinformation, identifying propaganda, exposing deception, and analyzing how appearances can mislead. Distinguished from simple falsity by emphasis on the causal relationship to false belief.

**Representative expressions:**
- yanıltıcı (misleading, deceptive, deceiving)
- yanıltmak (to mislead, to deceive, to lead astray)
- yanıltma (deception, misleading)
- yalan (lie, falsehood, untruth)
- yalanlamak (to deny, to refute, to give the lie to)
- yalanlama (denial, refutation)
- yanıltıcı olmak (to be misleading)
- yanıltıcı bilgi (misleading information)
- yalan söylemek (to tell a lie, to lie)
- yalan olmak (to be a lie)
- yalan çıkmak (to turn out to be a lie)
- yalan yanlış (false and incorrect)
- yalan dolan (lies and deceit)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- yanlış: Simple incorrectness vs. misleading effect
- sahte: Counterfeit objects may mislead but emphasis is on inauthenticity

### GROUP 6: Doubt and Suspicion

**Definition:** Terms expressing or describing states of uncertainty, doubt, or suspicion regarding truth, reliability, or trustworthiness. These expressions indicate epistemic caution or lack of confidence rather than asserting falsity. Used in contexts of expressing reservations, questioning claims, indicating uncertainty, and describing suspicious circumstances. Can apply to both epistemic doubt (uncertainty about truth) and moral suspicion (distrust of persons).

**Representative expressions:**
- şüpheli (doubtful, suspicious, dubious, suspect)
- şüphe (doubt, suspicion)
- şüphecilik (skepticism, doubtfulness)
- şüpheci (skeptic, skeptical, doubter)
- şüphelenmek (to suspect, to doubt, to be suspicious)
- şüphelenme (suspicion, doubting)
- şüphesiz (doubtless, undoubtedly, without doubt)
- kuşkulu (doubtful, questionable, dubious)
- kuşku (doubt, suspicion)
- kuşkusuz (doubtless, undoubtedly)
- kuşkulanmak (to doubt, to suspect, to be suspicious)
- şüpheli olmak (to be suspicious, to be doubtful)
- şüpheli görünmek (to look suspicious)
- şüpheli kişi (suspicious person, suspect)
- kuşkulu olmak (to be doubtful, to be questionable)
- kuşkulu görünmek (to look doubtful)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- şüpheli/kuşkulu: Can indicate both epistemic doubt and moral suspicion

### GROUP 7: Certainty and Definiteness

**Definition:** Terms expressing or describing high degrees of confidence, certainty, or definiteness regarding truth or decisions. These expressions indicate absence of doubt, finality of determination, or precision of specification. Used in contexts of emphatic assertion, final decisions, precise measurements, and confident claims. Contrasts with expressions of doubt and uncertainty.

**Representative expressions:**
- kesin (certain, definite, absolute, precise)
- kesinlik (certainty, definiteness)
- kesinlikle (certainly, definitely, absolutely)
- kesinleşmek (to become definite, to be finalized)
- kesinleştirmek (to finalize, to confirm, to make definite)
- şüphesiz (doubtless, undoubtedly, without doubt)
- kuşkusuz (doubtless, undoubtedly)
- kesin olmak (to be certain, to be definite)
- kesin bilgi (definite information)
- kesin cevap (definite answer)
- kesin karar (final decision)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- şüphesiz/kuşkusuz: Formed from doubt terms but express opposite meaning

### GROUP 8: Rights, Justice, and Fairness

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, situations, or treatments conform to principles of justice, fairness, and rightful entitlement. These expressions assess moral and legal rightness in terms of what is due, fair, or just, rather than factual accuracy or authenticity. Used in legal contexts, moral disputes, political discourse, and everyday judgments about fair treatment. Distinguished from factual truth evaluation and from honesty assessment.

**Representative expressions:**
- hak (right, truth, justice)
- haklı (right, justified, in the right)
- haksız (unjust, unfair, wrong, unrightful)
- haklılık (rightness, justification)
- haksızlık (injustice, unfairness, wrong)
- hakketmek (to deserve, to merit, to earn)
- hakkaniyet (fairness, equity, justice)
- adil (just, fair, equitable)
- adalet (justice, fairness)
- adaletli (just, fair, equitable)
- adaletsiz (unjust, unfair)
- adaletsizlik (injustice, unfairness)
- hak vermek (to agree with, to side with)
- hak yemek (to violate someone's rights)
- hakkı olmak (to have the right, to be entitled)
- haksızlık etmek (to wrong someone)
- hak iddia etmek (to claim a right)
- hak aramak (to seek justice)
- hak ve adalet (right and justice)
- hakkını almak (to get one's due)
- hakkını helal etmek (to forgive someone's debt/wrong)
- hakkını vermek (to give credit, to do justice to)
- hak ettiği değer (deserved value)
- hak sahibi (rightful owner, rights holder)
- hak gözetmek (to respect rights)
- haklı olmak (to be right, to be justified)
- haklı çıkmak (to turn out to be right)
- haksız çıkmak (to turn out to be wrong)
- haksız yere (unjustly, wrongly)
- adil olmak (to be just, to be fair)
- adil davranmak (to act justly)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- hak: Also used for truth in some contexts, but primarily justice-oriented
- hakikat: Related etymologically but focuses on truth/reality rather than justice
- doğru: Can indicate moral rightness but more commonly factual correctness

### GROUP 9: Validity and Legitimacy

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether something meets required standards, conditions, or norms to be accepted or recognized as proper, lawful, or applicable. These expressions assess conformity to established criteria, legal requirements, or accepted procedures rather than factual truth or moral rightness. Used in legal, administrative, logical, and temporal contexts to determine whether something "counts" or is "in force." Distinguished from truth evaluation and from moral justice.

**Representative expressions:**
- geçerli (valid, current, applicable)
- geçerlilik (validity, applicability)
- geçersiz (invalid, inapplicable, void)
- geçersizlik (invalidity, voidness)
- meşru (legitimate, lawful, rightful)
- meşruiyet (legitimacy, lawfulness)
- meşrulaştırmak (to legitimize, to make legitimate)
- meşrulaşmak (to become legitimate)
- geçerli olmak (to be valid, to be current)
- geçerli sayılmak (to be considered valid)
- meşru olmak (to be legitimate, to be lawful)
- meşru savunma (legitimate defense, self-defense)
- meşru hak (legitimate right, lawful right)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified; this group is relatively distinct

### GROUP 10: Fundamental Truth and Reality (Elevated Register)

**Definition:** Terms used in philosophical, religious, or literary contexts to refer to deep, ultimate, or fundamental truth and reality. These expressions carry more weight and formality than everyday truth terms, often implying metaphysical or spiritual dimensions. Used in elevated discourse, religious texts, philosophical writing, and literary language. Distinguished from everyday factual accuracy by register and depth of meaning.

**Representative expressions:**
- hakikat (truth, reality, the real truth)
- hakikaten (really, truly, indeed, in reality)
- hakiki (real, genuine, true, authentic)
- hakikatçi (seeker of truth, realist)
- hakikat ortaya çıkmak (the truth comes to light)
- hakikat payı (grain of truth)
- hakikate ulaşmak (to reach the truth)
- Allah hakkı için (for God's sake, in the name of God)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- gerçek: Can be used in elevated contexts but more neutral in register
- hak: Related etymologically and semantically but more justice-focused

---

### GROUP 11: Directness and Straightforwardness

**Definition:** Terms describing direct, unmediated, or straightforward manner of action, communication, or spatial movement. These expressions emphasize lack of intermediaries, deviation, or indirectness. Used in contexts of communication style, spatial navigation, and describing approaches that are frank or without circumlocution. This group shows how spatial/physical directness extends metaphorically to communicative and behavioral directness.

**Representative expressions:**
- doğrudan (directly, straightforwardly)
- doğrultmak (to straighten, to direct, to aim)
- doğrultu (direction, line, course)
- doğruca (directly, straight away)
- doğru yol (right path, correct way, straight path)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- doğru: Core meaning includes straightness but primarily used for correctness
- These expressions show extension from spatial to communicative domains

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with Multiple Group Memberships

1. **doğru**: Participates in Groups 1 (factual correctness), 8 (moral rightness in some uses), and 11 (directness). The term's polysemy makes it central to multiple domains.

2. **gerçek**: Primarily in Group 2 (reality/authenticity) but overlaps significantly with Group 1 (factual correctness) and Group 10 (elevated truth discourse).

3. **hak**: Primarily in Group 8 (rights/justice) but etymologically and semantically connected to Group 10 (fundamental truth) and occasionally used for factual truth.

4. **sahte/sahtekâr**: Primarily in Group 2 (authenticity) but also relevant to Group 4 (moral character) and Group 5 (deception).

5. **şüphesiz/kuşkusuz**: Formed from doubt terms (Group 6) but express opposite meaning, functioning more like Group 7 (certainty).

### Items Difficult to Classify

1. **doğru dürüst / doğru düzgün**: These reduplicative/alliterative compounds express general adequacy or propriety rather than specifically truth-related meanings. They represent semantic extension beyond the core truth domain into general quality evaluation.

2. **hakkında**: This postposition has grammaticalized from "hak" to mean simply "about/concerning" with no remaining truth or justice semantics. It represents complete semantic bleaching.

3. **aslen**: Adverbial form meaning "originally, by origin" used primarily for geographic/ethnic origin rather than authenticity evaluation.

4. **doğa**: While derived from the same root as "doğal," this noun simply means "nature" and doesn't directly participate in truth evaluation.

### Observations on Group Structure

1. **Overlapping boundaries**: Groups 1 (factual correctness) and 2 (reality/authenticity) show significant overlap, as do Groups 4 (honesty) and 5 (deception). This reflects genuine ambiguity in usage rather than analytical failure.

2. **Register stratification**: Group 10 (elevated truth discourse) is distinguished primarily by register and context rather than core semantics, showing how the same conceptual domain can be expressed at different levels of formality.

3. **Semantic extension patterns**: Several items show extension from concrete/spatial domains (Group 11) to abstract truth evaluation (Group 1), or from truth evaluation to general quality assessment (doğru dürüst).

4. **Morphological families split across groups**: Derivatives of the same root may belong to different groups (e.g., hak in Group 8 vs. hakikat in Group 10), showing how derivational morphology can shift semantic domain.

5. **Cultural-specific expressions**: Some constructions (hakkını helal etmek, Allah hakkı için) are culturally and religiously specific, showing how truth-related vocabulary intersects with cultural practices and religious frameworks.
