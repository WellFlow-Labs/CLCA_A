Below is a **P2b compositional-construction inventory** for the Indonesian P1 items. I include only **multi-word constructions** and avoid listing single-word derivations already handled in P2a.

---

## 1. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **benar bahwa ...**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “it is true that ...”  
**Usage notes:** A common formal and semi-formal construction used to affirm the truth of a proposition. It often appears in reporting, argumentation, and clarification.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **benar juga**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “that’s true too”; “you’re right, actually”  
**Usage notes:** Common in conversation when a speaker concedes that another person’s point is correct. It has a discourse-pragmatic flavor rather than a purely propositional one.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **memang benar**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “it is indeed true”; “indeed”  
**Usage notes:** Very common in speech and writing to strongly confirm a statement. It often introduces agreement while leaving room for further qualification.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **tidak benar bahwa ...**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “it is not true that ...”  
**Usage notes:** A standard way to deny the truth of a proposition, especially in formal rebuttals, journalism, and public statements. It is more explicit than simply saying *salah*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **benar atau tidak**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “whether true or not”  
**Usage notes:** Common in discussion of rumors, claims, and uncertain information. It frames a proposition as pending verification.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 6. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **yang benar saja!**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “come on!”; “seriously?!”  
**Usage notes:** An idiomatic exclamation expressing disbelief, annoyance, or protest. Its meaning is no longer a straightforward truth judgment.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 7. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **benar-benar tidak ...**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “really not ...”; “absolutely not ...”  
**Usage notes:** This uses the reduplicated form in a larger construction as an intensifier. It is extremely common but semantically shifted away from literal truth evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## 8. Base Form: **benar**
**Example:** **sebenarnya ...**  
**Transliteration:** —  
**English translation:** “actually ...”; “in fact ...”  
**Usage notes:** Very common as a discourse-organizing construction introducing what is really the case. It often contrasts appearance, assumption, or prior wording with reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
