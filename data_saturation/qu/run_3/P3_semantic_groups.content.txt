# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, collocational behavior, and contextual distribution observed across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data support. Items that do not fit clearly into any group, or that fit into multiple groups, are noted explicitly. Epistemic statuses from prior steps are preserved where relevant.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that something is the case

**Label:** Predicate assertions of truth and falsehood

**Definition:** These expressions appear primarily as predicates in simple declarative constructions, where a speaker asserts or denies that something is true, genuine, or false. They are the forms most consistently attested in the predicate-plus-evidential-clitic construction (e.g., *cheqaqmi*, *llullam*, *chiqanmi*), and they are used in direct response to questions about whether something is the case. They contrast with each other as positive and negative assertions within the same construction type.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqa* — "true, genuine" (used as predicate: *Cheqam kay.* "This is true.")
- *cheqaq* — "true, truthful" (used as predicate: *Cheqaqmi chay.*)
- *llulla* — "false, a lie" (used as predicate: *Llullam chay.* "That is a lie.")
- *chiqan* — "correct, just, right" (used as predicate: *Chiqanmi chay.*)
- *allin* — "good, right, proper" (used as predicate: *Allinmi chay ruway.*)
- *sut'i* — "clear, evident, plain" (used as predicate: *Sut'im chay.*)
- *kikin* — "the very same, genuine, identical" (used as predicate: *Kikinmi pay.*)
- *mana cheqa* — "not true, not genuine" (negated predicate)
- *mana allin* — "not right, wrong" (negated predicate: *Mana allinmi chay.*)
- *mana chiqan* — "not correct, unjust" (negated predicate: *Mana chiqanmi chay ruway.*)
- *cheqaqpuni* — "absolutely true, certainly genuine" (emphatic predicate: *Cheqaqpunim chay.*)
- *qelqasqa* — "written, documented, on record" (predicate: *Qelqasqam chay.*)

**Notes:** The direct evidential clitic *-mi/-m* is consistently attested with all of these forms in predicate position. The interrogative clitic *-chu* is attested for *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *allin*, *chiqan*, *sut'i*, and *kikin* in this construction type, but not for *llulla* or *pantay* in available documentation — this asymmetry is preserved without explanation. *Qelqasqa* is included here because it appears in the same predicate construction type, though its primary meaning (being documented/written) is distinct from the others.

## Group 2: Expressions used in moral characterization of persons

**Label:** Terms applied to persons as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to characterize a person's moral disposition or habitual conduct as honest, truthful, deceptive, or dishonest. They appear in nominal predicate constructions of the type *[term] runam pay* ("he/she is a [term] person") and in moral evaluation discourse, proverbs, and ethical instruction. They are distinguished from Group 1 in that they describe persons rather than propositions or situations.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaq* — "truthful, honest" (as person-characterization: *Cheqaq runám pay.* "He/she is an honest person.")
- *cheqaqniyuq* — "one who possesses truthfulness" (moral characterization in ethical discourse)
- *cheqa sonqo* — "genuine of heart, sincere" (inner disposition: honest at heart)
- *llulla* — "liar, deceptive" (as person-characterization: *Llulla runám pay.* "He/she is a liar.")
- *llullaq* — "liar, habitual deceiver" (agentive: one who lies)
- *llullakuq* — "one who lies" (agentive from verbal stem)
- *llulla sonqo* — "false-hearted, insincere" (inner disposition: deceitful at heart)
- *cheqaq runa* — "honest person, truthful person" (nominal phrase)
- *llulla runa* — "liar, dishonest person" (nominal phrase)

**Notes:** The *sonqo* ("heart, inner self") collocations (*cheqa sonqo*, *llulla sonqo*) are specifically attested in contexts that locate honesty or deception in a person's inner disposition rather than in their outward conduct or statements. This distinguishes them from other person-characterization terms that describe habitual behavior. *Cheqaq* appears in both Group 1 and Group 2, as it is used both as a predicate about propositions and as a modifier of persons.

## Group 3: Expressions used in speech and testimony contexts

**Label:** Terms associated with the truthfulness or falsity of speech acts

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in contexts where the truthfulness or falsity of a speech act — testimony, reporting, narrating, or stating — is being evaluated or enjoined. They are attested in imperative constructions requesting truthful speech, in legal and testimonial discourse, and in collocations with verbs of speaking (*rimay*, *willakuy*, *niy*). They are distinguished from Group 1 in that they are oriented toward the act of speaking rather than toward the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqa rimay* — "to speak the truth, truthful speech"
- *cheqaqta rimay* — "to speak the truth, to tell the truth" (imperative: *Cheqaqta rimay.*)
- *cheqaqta willaway* — "tell me the truth" (imperative with first-person object)
- *cheqaqtam rimasqanki* — "you spoke the truth" (assessment of prior speech)
- *chiqan rimay* — "to speak correctly, straight talk, honest speech"
- *chiqap rimay* — "to speak exactly, precise speech" [UNCERTAIN]
- *llulla rimay* — "to speak falsely, false talk"
- *llulla simi* — "false speech, lying words, deceptive talk"
- *cheqa simi* — "true word, honest speech, truthful statement"
- *llulla willakuy* — "false report, to give a false account, to testify falsely"
- *willakuy* — "to tell, to narrate, to report" (in contexts where truthfulness of account is at issue)
- *cheqaqmi nin* — "he/she says it is true" (reportative construction)
- *llullam nin* — "he/she says it is a lie" (reportative construction)
- *llullam willakurqa* — "he/she reported falsely"

**Notes:** The imperative constructions (*Cheqaqta rimay*, *Cheqaqta willaway*) are specifically attested in this group and not in others, suggesting that the enjoining of truthful speech is a distinct usage context. *Rimay* and *willakuy* are not themselves P1 items in the truth-evaluation domain but appear as the verbal heads in these constructions. *Simi* ("word, speech") collocations (*cheqa simi*, *llulla simi*) are included here because their primary attested context is the evaluation of speech acts.

## Group 4: Expressions used in the traditional moral triad and prohibitive ethical discourse

**Label:** Terms appearing in prohibitive moral injunctions

**Definition:** These expressions appear in the traditional Andean prohibitive moral formula (*ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qella* — "do not steal, do not lie, do not be lazy") and in related prohibitive constructions. Their usage in this context is formulaic and widely cited across cultural, educational, and political discourse. They are distinguished from other uses of the same roots by their fixed, formulaic character and their appearance in the prohibitive construction with *ama*.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ama llulla* — "do not lie, be not false" (part of the moral triad)
- *ama llullakuychu* — "do not lie" (fuller verbal prohibitive form)
- *llullakuy ama* — "do not lie" (prohibitive construction)
- *ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qella* — the full triadic formula (fixed expression)

**Notes:** This group is small and defined primarily by the formulaic construction type rather than by semantic content alone. *Llulla* and *llullakuy* appear in other groups as well; their inclusion here reflects the distinct usage context of the prohibitive formula. No parallel prohibitive construction is attested for *cheqa*, *chiqan*, or other truth-positive terms in available documentation.

## Group 5: Expressions used in verification and correction of claims or documents

**Label:** Terms used in processes of checking, confirming, or rectifying

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a claim, document, or action is being actively checked for accuracy, confirmed as genuine, or corrected when found to be wrong. They are distinguished from Group 1 (which involves asserting truth or falsehood) in that they describe processes or actions directed at establishing or restoring correctness, rather than simply stating that something is or is not true.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaqchay* — "to verify, to confirm, to prove true, to authenticate"
- *cheqaqsiy* — "to be confirmed, to turn out to be true, to prove oneself genuine" [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqanchay* — "to correct, to straighten, to rectify, to make right"
- *chiqanchasqa* — "corrected, rectified, set right"
- *sut'ichay* — "to clarify, to explain, to make explicit"
- *sut'ichaway imachus cheqa kasqanta* — "clarify for me what is true" (imperative with complement)
- *llulla qelqa* — "false document, forged writing" (object of verification processes)
- *qelqasqa* — "written, documented, recorded" (status of a verified record)
- *cheqaqchaykim kay qelqata* — "you must verify this document" [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqanchay tiyan kay pantasqata* — "this error needs to be corrected" [UNCERTAIN]

**Notes:** *Qelqasqa* appears in both Group 1 and Group 5: it is used as a predicate asserting documentary status (Group 1) and as the object or result of verification processes (Group 5). *Sut'ichay* is included here because its attested use is specifically in resolving ambiguity or obscurity — a process directed at establishing clarity — rather than simply asserting that something is clear.

## Group 6: Expressions used in epistemic contexts of knowing and believing

**Label:** Terms associated with states of knowing, believing, or being uncertain

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker's epistemic state — whether they know, believe, or are uncertain about something — is being described or evaluated. They are distinguished from Group 1 in that they describe the speaker's or another person's relationship to a proposition, rather than the truth-value of the proposition itself.

**Representative expressions:**
- *yachay* — "to know, to learn, knowledge" (propositional and practical knowledge)
- *cheqaqta yachay* — "to know truly, to have correct knowledge" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqaqtam yachani* — "I truly know" (first-person assertion of certain knowledge)
- *yachanichu imachus cheqa kasqanta* — "I do not know what is true" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqaqta yachanichu* — "I do not truly know" (disclaimer of certain knowledge)
- *riqsiy* — "to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with" (acquaintance-knowledge)
- *cheqa riqsiy* — "to truly know, to genuinely recognize" [UNCERTAIN]
- *riqsinichu payta* — "I do not recognize him/her" (disclaimer of recognition)
- *cheqaqtam riqsini payta* — "I truly know him/her" [UNCERTAIN]
- *iñiy* — "to believe, to trust, to have faith in"
- *iñisqa* — "believed, trusted, credible" [UNCERTAIN]
- *iñinichu chay simita* — "I do not believe that statement"
- *iñinim cheqaq kasqanta* — "I believe it is true" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqaqchus chay?* — "Is that really true? Can it be genuine?" (dubitative)

**Notes:** *Yachay* and *riqsiy* are distinguished within this group by their attested usage: *yachay* appears in propositional contexts ("to know that..."), while *riqsiy* appears in acquaintance and recognition contexts ("to know a person, to recognize"). *Iñiy* is distinguished from both in that it appears in contexts of trust and credibility as well as belief. The dubitative construction *cheqaqchus* is included here because it expresses epistemic uncertainty about whether something is genuinely the case, rather than asserting or denying it.

## Group 7: Expressions used in contexts of error and deviation from correctness

**Label:** Terms describing mistakes, errors, and going astray

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a person, statement, or action has deviated from what is correct, true, or proper. They are used in acknowledgment of error, attribution of error to others, and evaluation of claims or conduct as mistaken. They are distinguished from the falsehood terms in Group 1 in that they do not necessarily imply deliberate deception — they describe deviation from correctness without implying intent.

**Representative expressions:**
- *pantay* — "to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong, to go astray"
- *pantasqa* — "mistaken, erroneous, wrong, in error"
- *pantanim* — "I was wrong" (first-person acknowledgment of error)
- *mana pantanichu* — "I am not wrong" (denial of error)
- *pantasqaykiм chay* — "that is your mistake" (attribution of error)
- *pantasqam chay yuyay* — "that opinion is mistaken"

**Notes:** The contrast between *pantay* (error, mistake) and *llulla* (lie, deception) is supported by their distinct usage contexts: *pantay* appears in contexts of acknowledgment and attribution of error without the moral condemnation associated with *llulla*, and no prohibitive construction (*ama pantaychu*) is attested in available documentation, unlike *ama llullakuychu*. This distinction is preserved without interpretation.

## Group 8: Expressions used in contexts of resemblance and appearance

**Label:** Terms used when something appears to be true or false without confirmation

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where something resembles truth or falsehood, or appears to be genuine or deceptive, without the speaker committing to a definitive assertion. They are associated with the comparative particle *hina* ("like, as, similar to") and with verbs of appearing (*rikuriy*). They are distinguished from Group 1 in that they describe resemblance or appearance rather than assertion.

**Representative expressions:**
- *hina* — "like, similar to, as if, resembling" (primary term for resemblance)
- *cheqa hina* — "as if true, like the real thing, resembling the genuine" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqa hina rimasqa* — "spoken as if true" [UNCERTAIN]
- *llulla hina* — "like a lie, resembling falsehood" [UNCERTAIN]
- *llulla hina rikurin* — "it appears to be a lie" [UNCERTAIN]

**Notes:** All items in this group carry [UNCERTAIN] status from prior steps, reflecting limited attestation. The group is identified because the *hina* comparative construction appears consistently in contexts where truth or falsehood is being described as an appearance rather than asserted as a fact. This is a small group and may not be robustly established in available documentation.

## Group 9: Expressions used in contexts of ways of living and modes of existence

**Label:** Terms applied to ways of life rather than to propositions or persons

**Definition:** These expressions appear in philosophical, religious, and ethical discourse where *cheqa* or *llulla* is applied not to a proposition or a person's conduct but to a mode of existence or way of living. They are distinguished from other uses of the same roots by their collocational partner *kawsay* ("life, living, existence") and by their appearance in Andean philosophical and religious texts.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqa kawsay* — "true life, genuine way of living, authentic existence"
- *llulla kawsay* — "false way of life, deceptive existence, inauthentic living" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqa ñan* — "the right path, the correct way, the true road"
- *chiqan ñan* — "the straight path, the correct way, the just road"

**Notes:** *Cheqa ñan* and *chiqan ñan* are included here because they appear in the same discourse contexts as *cheqa kawsay* — moral and religious texts describing the correct course of life — and because the *ñan* ("path, road") metaphor is used in both spatial and moral senses without clear separation in available documentation. *Llulla kawsay* carries [UNCERTAIN] status. This group is defined by a specific collocational and discourse context rather than by a shared morphological pattern.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used in contexts of correct procedure and just conduct

**Label:** Terms associated with correctness of action and procedural justice

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the correctness or justice of an action, procedure, or course of conduct is being evaluated, rather than the truth of a proposition or the honesty of a person's speech. They are associated primarily with the root *chiqan* and its collocates *ruway* ("to do, action") and *kay* ("to be, being"), and they appear in legal, administrative, and moral evaluation of conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- *chiqan* — "straight, correct, just, right" (in conduct and procedural contexts)
- *chiqan ruway* — "to act correctly, to do the right thing, correct conduct"
- *chiqan kay* — "justice, correctness, the state of being right" [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqan tukuy* — "to become correct, to act justly" [UNCERTAIN]
- *chiqanchay* — "to correct, to straighten, to rectify, to make right"
- *mana chiqan* — "incorrect, unjust, crooked, not right"
- *mana chiqanmi chay ruway* — "that action is not right/just"
- *allin* — "good, right, proper, correct" (in conduct evaluation)
- *mana allin* — "not right, wrong, improper"
- *chiqanchasqa* — "corrected, rectified, set right"

**Notes:** *Chiqan* and *allin* overlap in this group in that both are used in moral evaluation of conduct as correct or proper. However, *allin* is more general (it is the primary term for goodness and rightness across domains), while *chiqan* is more specifically associated with straightness, directness, and justice in legal and procedural contexts. *Chiqanchay* appears in both Group 5 (verification and correction) and Group 10 (correct procedure), as it describes both the process of correcting an error and the result of bringing conduct into alignment with what is right.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items appear in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single group:

**cheqaq** — Appears in Group 1 (predicate assertions), Group 2 (person characterization), Group 3 (speech and testimony), and Group 6 (epistemic states). It is the most widely distributed item across groups, reflecting its high morphological productivity and broad contextual range.

**llulla** — Appears in Group 1 (predicate assertions), Group 2 (person characterization), Group 3 (speech and testimony), Group 4 (prohibitive formula), and Group 8 (resemblance). Its distribution is broad but somewhat narrower than *cheqaq*.

**chiqan** — Appears in Group 1 (predicate assertions), Group 9 (ways of living), and Group 10 (correct procedure). The spatial meaning of *chiqan* ("straight") is not clearly separable from its moral and procedural meanings in all attested contexts.

**cheqa** — Appears in Group 1 (predicate assertions), Group 9 (ways of living), and as the base for items in Groups 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, and 9. As the primary root, it participates in virtually all groups through its derivatives and collocations.

**qelqasqa** — Appears in Group 1 (predicate assertions) and Group 5 (verification and correction). Its primary meaning (being documented/written) gives it a distinct character from other Group 1 items.

**chiqanchay** — Appears in Group 5 (verification and correction) and Group 10 (correct procedure). The distinction between correcting an error and rectifying unjust conduct is not clearly maintained in all attested uses.

**cheqa ñan / chiqan ñan** — These appear in Group 9 (ways of living) but also have spatial uses (a straight road) that are not within the truth-evaluation domain. The boundary between spatial and moral uses is not clearly established in available documentation.

**cheqa sonqo / llulla sonqo** — These appear in Group 2 (person characterization) but are distinguished within that group by their specific reference to inner disposition (*sonqo*, "heart") rather than outward conduct or habitual behavior. They could be treated as a sub-group within Group 2 or as a separate group, but available documentation does not clearly support a separate grouping.

## Items That Do Not Fit Clearly Into Any Group

**pura** — P1 marks this item as [UNCERTAIN] in its evaluative use. Its primary meaning is relational ("among, between") and its use in contexts of genuineness or purity is not well-attested in available documentation. No grouping is assigned.

**paylla** — P1 marks this item as [UNCERTAIN]. Its use in identity verification contexts is limited in available documentation. It is closest to Group 1 (predicate assertions of genuineness) but is not sufficiently attested to assign confidently.

**recto / riqtu** — P1 marks this item as [MARGINAL] as a Spanish loanword with uneven distribution. No grouping is assigned.

**cheqniy** — P1 marks this item as [MARGINAL] with indirect relevance to the target domain. Its primary meaning (to hate, to abhor) is affective rather than truth-evaluative. No grouping is assigned.

**cheqa hatun** — P1b marks this item as [UNCERTAIN]. Its use of *cheqa* as an intensifier of degree or magnitude rather than as an evaluator of truth or genuineness places it outside the patterns observed in other groups. No grouping is assigned.

**cheqaqta yachay / cheqa riqsiy** — Both are marked [UNCERTAIN] in P1b and P2a. They are closest to Group 6 (epistemic states) but their attestation is limited. They are noted as candidates for Group 6 without confident assignment.

**cheqa yuyay / llulla yuyay** — *Cheqa yuyay* ("correct understanding, true opinion") is attested in philosophical and educational contexts; *llulla yuyay* ("false belief, mistaken opinion") is marked [UNCERTAIN]. These could form a sub-group within Group 6 (epistemic states) or Group 7 (error), but available documentation does not clearly establish their grouping.
