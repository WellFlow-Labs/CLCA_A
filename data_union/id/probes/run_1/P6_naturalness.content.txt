# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 DYNAMICS OF TRUTH-RELATED MEANINGS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents how Indonesian encodes change, causation, and stability in the truth-evaluation domain, drawing exclusively on vocabulary and constructions attested in the CONTEXT. Each subsection treats a distinct dynamic pattern. Where a pattern is not attested for a given term or cluster, this is stated explicitly. Where the CONTEXT documents a relevant form in passing (e.g., in Peripheral Extensions or construction notes), it is included here with a cross-reference.

Terms are treated separately where their behavior differs. Groupings within subsections reflect shared morphosyntactic patterns, not imposed semantic categories.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1a. Periphrastic *menjadi* constructions

The CONTEXT documents *menjadi* ("to become") as the primary periphrastic strategy for encoding change-of-state in this domain (P4a §4). The pattern is grammatically available for truth-domain nouns and adjectives but is described as less frequent than resultative passives and more typical of rhetorical, journalistic, or philosophical prose.

**Attested and attested-adjacent examples from CONTEXT:**

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Dusta yang diulang-ulang bisa menjadi kebenaran yang diterima.* | "A lie repeated many times can become an accepted truth." | P4a §4; P2b Construction 3 under *dusta* |
| *Hal itu bisa menjadi bukti yang kuat.* | "That could become strong evidence." | Compositional; *bukti kuat* attested P1b |

**Notes:** The CONTEXT attests *menjadi* + *kebenaran* as the primary lexicalized example of this pattern. The construction presupposes a prior state of non-truth and encodes a transition. The modal *bisa* ("can, could") frequently co-occurs, framing the change as possible rather than actual — a pattern consistent with the rhetorical and philosophical register noted in P4a.

No *menjadi* + *sah*, *menjadi* + *terbukti*, or *menjadi* + *asli* constructions are attested in the CONTEXT. For those terms, resultative passives (§1b below) are the documented strategy.

---

### 1b. Resultative passive constructions (*dinyatakan*, *terbukti*, *disahkan*, *dipalsukan*)

The CONTEXT documents resultative passives as the dominant inchoative strategy in legal and institutional registers (P4a §4). These encode a change-of-state as the result of an institutional act or evidentiary process.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Kontrak itu dinyatakan sah oleh pengadilan.* | "The contract was declared valid by the court." | P4a §4; P2b under *sah* |
| *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan.* | "The law has been ratified." | P2a under *sah* Derived Form 3 |
| *Ia terbukti bersalah.* | "He was proven guilty." | P1b Root 3 item 4; P2a under *terbukti* |
| *Dokumen itu dipalsukan.* | "The document was forged / has been falsified." | P2a under *palsu* Derived Form 2 |
| *Karya seni itu dinyatakan otentik oleh para ahli.* | "The artwork was declared authentic by experts." | P2b under *otentik* |
| *Instrumen penelitian ini dinyatakan valid.* | "This research instrument has been declared valid." | P2b under *valid* |

**Notes:** These constructions encode a transition from an unresolved or prior state to a recognized state. The agent of the change is typically an authoritative institution (*pengadilan* — court; *para ahli* — experts; *DPR* — parliament) or an evidentiary process. The CONTEXT describes *dinyatakan sah* as "a near-formulaic legal expression" (P4b), indicating high lexicalization. *Terbukti* is itself a resultative passive (*ter-* prefix) and encodes the endpoint of a proving process rather than an ongoing one.

---

### 1c. No common expression identified

The following change-of-state patterns are not attested in the CONTEXT:

- *menjadi benar* ("to become true"): not attested. The CONTEXT does not document this construction. Truth in the *benar* sense is not typically encoded as a state that something transitions into; rather, *benar* is attributed or denied.
- *menjadi jujur* ("to become honest"): not attested in the CONTEXT as a fixed or frequent construction.
- *menjadi asli* ("to become genuine"): not attested. Genuineness in the *asli* sense is not encoded as a state that something transitions into; objects are evaluated as *asli* or *palsu* but do not become one or the other through a described process in the attested data.

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH OR OPPOSED STATE

### 2a. Negation + resultative passive

The CONTEXT documents negated resultative passives as the primary construction for encoding failure to achieve a truth-related state.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Tuduhan itu tidak terbukti.* | "The accusation was not proven." | P1b Root 3 item 5 |
| *Pernikahan itu tidak sah.* | "That marriage is invalid." | P2a under *sah* Derived Form 6; P2b under *sah* |
| *Tindakan itu tidak absah menurut hukum yang berlaku.* | "That action is not legitimate under applicable law." | P2b under *absah* |

**Notes:** These constructions encode a state of non-attainment rather than a transition from a prior positive state. The CONTEXT does not document a dedicated construction for "ceasing to be valid" or "losing validity"; the negated forms describe a static evaluation rather than a dynamic change.

---

### 2b. *menjadi* + negative truth-domain noun

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Dusta yang diulang-ulang bisa menjadi kebenaran yang diterima.* | "A lie repeated many times can become an accepted truth." | P4a §4; P2b under *dusta* |

**Notes:** This construction, already documented in §1a, encodes the inverse dynamic: a falsehood (*dusta*) transitions into an accepted truth (*kebenaran*). The CONTEXT presents this as a rhetorical observation about the mechanism of propaganda and misinformation (P2b under *dusta* Construction 3). No parallel construction encoding the reverse transition (truth becoming falsehood) is attested in the CONTEXT.

---

### 2c. *memalsukan* and related causative-resultative constructions

The CONTEXT documents *memalsukan* ("to falsify, to forge") and its passive *dipalsukan* as encoding a transition from genuine to false for objects and documents (P2a under *palsu*).

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Dokumen itu dipalsukan.* | "The document was forged / falsified." | P2a under *palsu* Derived Form 2 |
| *Tanda tangan itu dipalsukan.* | "The signature was forged." | Compositional; *memalsukan tanda tangan* attested P2a |

**Notes:** *Dipalsukan* encodes a change from *asli* to *palsu* as the result of a deliberate act. The CONTEXT does not document a reverse construction (restoring a falsified document to genuine status); the *asli/palsu* opposition is treated as a one-directional transition in the attested data.

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 3a. *meN-...-kan* causative constructions

The CONTEXT documents the *meN-...-kan* causative as the primary morphosyntactic strategy for encoding causation of truth-related states (P4a §5). These constructions encode an agent causing a truth-domain state to obtain for an object.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu.* | "The authorities confirmed that report." | P4a §5; P2a under *benar* Derived Form 2 |
| *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru.* | "The parliament ratified the new law." | P2a under *sah* Derived Form 2; P1b Root 2 item 2 |
| *Informasi itu mengelirukan masyarakat.* | "That information misled the public." | P2a under *keliru* Derived Form 2 |
| *Ia memalsukan tanda tangan pada dokumen itu.* | "He forged the signature on that document." | P4a §5; P2a under *palsu* Derived Form 1 |
| *Mengabsahkan tindakan kekerasan.* | "To legitimize acts of violence." | P2a under *absah* Derived Form 1; P1b Root 2 item 11 |
| *Ia menyatakan bahwa tuduhan itu tidak benar.* | "He stated that the accusation was untrue." | P2a under *nyata* Derived Form 2 |

**Notes:** The *meN-...-kan* causatives in this domain divide into two functional subtypes based on the CONTEXT:

1. **Institutional validation causatives** (*mengesahkan*, *membenarkan* in the sense of confirming, *mengabsahkan*, *memvalidasi*): an authorized agent causes a truth-related state to be officially recognized. The CONTEXT consistently associates these with institutional agents (parliament, court, authorities).

2. **Deception/falsification causatives** (*memalsukan*, *mengelirukan*, *membohongi*): an agent causes a false or erroneous state to obtain, either in an object (forgery) or in a person's belief (misleading). The CONTEXT notes *mengelirukan* means "to cause someone to be mistaken or confused" (P2a).

---

### 3b. *meN-...-i* causative-applicative constructions (directed at a person)

The CONTEXT documents *membohongi* and *mendustakan* as constructions where the causative act is directed at a specific person or entity as the object.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Ia membohongi saya.* | "He lied to me / deceived me." | P2a under *bohong* Derived Form 2 |
| *Mendustakan ayat-ayat Allah.* | "To deny / reject the signs of God." | P2a under *dusta* Derived Form 2 |
| *Para nabi didustakan oleh kaumnya.* | "The prophets were rejected / called liars by their people." | P2a under *dusta* Derived Form 3 |

**Notes:** The *-i* suffix marks the person deceived or rejected as the direct object, distinguishing these from the *-kan* constructions in §3a where the object is the thing being falsified or confirmed. The CONTEXT notes this distinction explicitly for *membohongi* vs. *berbohong* (P2a under *bohong*): *membohongi* requires a specific object (the person deceived), while *berbohong* is intransitive.

---

### 3c. Causing a state of proof: *membuktikan*

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Ia membuktikan bahwa ia tidak bersalah.* | "He proved that he was not guilty." | P1b Root 3 item 2 |
| *Ia harus membuktikan dirinya.* | "He must prove himself." | P1b Root 3 item 27 |

**Notes:** *Membuktikan* encodes an agent causing a claim to achieve the status of *terbukti* (proven). The CONTEXT documents this as used "in legal, scientific, journalistic, and everyday contexts" (P2a). The reflexive collocation *membuktikan diri* ("to prove oneself") is documented as a distinct use where what is proven is not a proposition but a person's worth or capability (P1b Peripheral Extensions).

---

### 3d. Causing a state of justification: *membenarkan* (deontic extension)

The CONTEXT documents a deontic extension of *membenarkan* where the causative act is one of permission rather than epistemic confirmation (P1b Peripheral Extensions; P2a Derived Form 2 notes).

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Tidak dibenarkan masuk.* | "Not permitted to enter." | P1b Peripheral Extensions; P2a under *benar* Derived Form 4 |
| *Hal itu tidak dibenarkan.* | "That is not permitted / not justified." | P2a under *benar* Derived Form 4 |

**Notes:** In this use, *dibenarkan* encodes institutional authorization rather than epistemic confirmation. The CONTEXT describes this as a shift "from epistemic to deontic meaning" (P1b). The construction is passive and typically negated (*tidak dibenarkan*) in its most frequent attested form, encoding prohibition rather than permission.

## 4. MAINTAINING OR UPHOLDING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 4a. *menegakkan* + truth/justice noun

The CONTEXT documents *menegakkan* ("to uphold, to enforce" — from *tegak*, upright) as the primary verb for encoding the active maintenance or defense of a truth-related state.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Menegakkan kebenaran.* | "To uphold the truth / to stand up for what is right." | P1b Root 1 item 23; P2b under *benar* Construction 11 |
| *Menegakkan keadilan.* | "To uphold justice." | P2a under *adil* Derived Form 1 (collocation noted) |

**Notes:** *Menegakkan* encodes an active, effortful maintenance of a state against implied opposition or erosion. The CONTEXT describes it as used "in legal, moral, and political discourse to describe the act of defending or enforcing truth and justice" (P1b). The pairing *demi kebenaran dan keadilan* ("for the sake of truth and justice") is documented as a stable collocation in P2b, suggesting that *kebenaran* and *keadilan* are treated as parallel values requiring active defense.

---

### 4b. *menepati* + commitment noun

The CONTEXT documents *menepati* ("to fulfill, to keep to, to honor") as encoding the maintenance of a correspondence between a prior commitment and subsequent action.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Menepati janji.* | "To keep a promise." | P2a under *tepat* Derived Form 2 |
| *Janji yang tidak ditepati.* | "A promise that was not kept." | P2a under *tepat* Derived Form 3 |

**Notes:** *Menepati* encodes the maintenance of *tepat* (exactness, fittingness) between a commitment and its fulfillment over time. The CONTEXT describes it as used "in interpersonal and contractual contexts" (P2a). The negated passive *tidak ditepati* encodes the failure to maintain this correspondence.

---

## 5. UNDERMINING OR DESTROYING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 5a. Evidence destruction and concealment

The CONTEXT documents a cluster of constructions encoding deliberate acts against the evidentiary basis of truth claims.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Menyembunyikan bukti.* | "To conceal evidence." | P1b Root 3 item 19 |
| *Merusak bukti.* | "To destroy / tamper with evidence." | P1b Root 3 item 20 |
| *Mengumpulkan bukti.* | "To gather evidence." | P1b Root 3 item 18 |

**Notes:** The CONTEXT presents *menyembunyikan bukti* and *merusak bukti* as legal collocations used "in discussions of obstruction of justice and evidence tampering" (P1b). These encode acts that prevent a truth-related state (*terbukti*) from being achieved or maintained. *Mengumpulkan bukti* is included here as the constructive counterpart: the act of assembling the evidentiary basis for a truth claim.

---

### 5b. Spreading falsehood

The CONTEXT documents constructions encoding the active propagation of false states.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Menyebarkan dusta kepada publik.* | "To spread falsehoods to the public." | P2b under *dusta* Construction 2 |
| *Berita palsu menyebar dengan cepat di media sosial.* | "False news spreads quickly on social media." | P2b under *palsu* Construction 3 |
| *Dusta yang diulang-ulang bisa dianggap kebenaran.* | "A lie repeated many times can come to be regarded as truth." | P2b under *dusta* Construction 3 |

**Notes:** The CONTEXT presents *menyebarkan dusta* as a formal collocation and *berita palsu menyebar* as a construction that has become highly frequent in the context of social media misinformation (P2b). The third example encodes a process by which repeated falsehood erodes the distinction between *dusta* and *kebenaran* — the only attested construction in the CONTEXT where a false state actively displaces a true one through repetition rather than through institutional act.

---

### 5c. Reversal of the burden of proof

The CONTEXT documents *pembuktian terbalik* as a legal construction encoding a structural inversion of the normal direction of epistemic responsibility.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Pembuktian terbalik.* | "Reversal of the burden of proof / reverse onus." | P1b Root 3 item 22; P2a §8 notes |

**Notes:** The CONTEXT describes this as used in Indonesian anti-corruption and money laundering law "to refer to provisions requiring the accused to prove the legitimate origin of assets, reversing the normal burden of proof" (P1b). This is the only attested construction in the CONTEXT where the normal causal direction of proof (accuser proves guilt) is formally inverted. *Terbalik* ("reversed" — *ter-* + *balik*, reverse) encodes the inversion morphologically.

## 6. SELF-DIRECTED DYNAMIC CONSTRUCTIONS

The CONTEXT documents a set of constructions where the agent and the object of a truth-related dynamic act are the same entity.

| Indonesian | Translation | Source in CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| *Membenarkan diri.* | "To justify oneself / to vindicate oneself." | P1b Root 1 item 13 |
| *Pembenaran diri.* | "Self-justification / rationalization." | P1b Root 1 item 9; P2a under *benar* Derived Form 4 |
| *Membuktikan diri.* | "To prove oneself." | P1b Root 3 item 27 |
| *Menyesuaikan diri.* | "To adapt oneself." | P2a under *sesuai* Derived Form 2; P2b under *sesuai* |
| *Mengesahkan diri.* | "To legitimize oneself / to establish one's own legitimacy." | P2a under *sah* Derived Form 7 |

**Notes:** The CONTEXT notes that *membenarkan diri* "carries a slightly negative connotation of self-serving rationalization" (P1b), while *membuktikan diri* is used in "interpersonal, professional, and competitive contexts to describe the act of demonstrating one's abilities or character through performance" (P1b) — a neutral or positive framing. *Menyesuaikan diri* ("to adapt oneself") encodes self-directed conformity to an external standard and is described as "a very common collocation" (P2a). *Mengesahkan diri* is described as "less common" and appearing "in political commentary and academic writing" (P2a).

## 7. SUMMARY OF ATTESTED DYNAMIC PATTERNS

| Pattern | Primary Construction | Key Terms | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| Becoming true (rhetorical/philosophical) | *menjadi* + truth noun | *kebenaran* | Rhetorical, journalistic, philosophical |
| Becoming valid/proven (institutional) | Resultative passive (*dinyatakan*, *disahkan*, *terbukti*) | *sah*, *valid*, *otentik*, *terbukti* | Legal, administrative, academic |
| Becoming false/falsified | *dipalsukan*; *menjadi* + false noun | *palsu*, *dusta* | Legal, journalistic, rhetorical |
| Causing truth/validity (institutional) | *meN-...-kan* causative | *mengesahkan*, *membenarkan*, *memvalidasi* | Legal, administrative, formal |
| Causing error/deception (directed at person) | *meN-...-i* causative | *membohongi*, *mendustakan* | Everyday, religious, formal |
| Causing error/deception (directed at object) | *meN-...-kan* causative | *memalsukan*, *mengelirukan* | Legal, journalistic |
| Causing proof | *membuktikan* | *bukti*, *terbukti* | Legal, scientific, journalistic |
| Upholding truth/justice | *menegakkan* + noun | *kebenaran*, *keadilan* | Legal, moral, political |
| Maintaining correspondence | *menepati* | *tepat* | Interpersonal, contractual |
| Undermining evidence | *menyembunyikan*, *merusak* + *bukti* | *bukti* | Legal |
| Spreading falsehood | *menyebarkan* + *dusta*; *menyebar* | *dusta*, *berita palsu* | Journalistic, formal |
| Inverting proof direction | *pembuktian terbalik* | *pembuktian* | Legal |
| Self-directed justification/proof | *meN-...-kan/i diri* | *benar*, *bukti*, *sah*, *sesuai* | Moral, professional, political |

## 8. PATTERNS NOT ATTESTED IN THE CONTEXT

The following dynamic patterns are not attested in the CONTEXT and are reported as absent rather than assumed:

- **"X becomes true" with *benar* as the endpoint**: no *menjadi benar* construction is attested. *Benar* is attributed or denied but not encoded as a state that something transitions into in the attested data.
- **"X becomes honest" with *jujur* as the endpoint**: no *menjadi jujur* construction is attested.
- **"X becomes genuine" with *asli* as the endpoint**: not attested. The *asli/palsu* opposition is treated as a one-directional transition (genuine → falsified) in the attested data; no reverse transition is documented.
- **"X causes Y to be true" with *benar* as the caused state**: the causative *membenarkan* means "to confirm as true" or "to justify," not "to make true." No construction encoding the causing of propositional truth (as opposed to its confirmation or validation) is attested.
- **Gradual or incremental change constructions**: no constructions encoding gradual change toward or away from a truth-related state (e.g., "becoming more accurate," "gradually losing validity") are attested as fixed or frequent expressions in the CONTEXT beyond the regular comparative morphosyntax noted in P2b.
