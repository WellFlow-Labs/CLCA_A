## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by BECOME

The CLCA category REVELATION — expressing that a truth-value is discovered or comes to light — is not realized by a dedicated construction in Finnish as documented in P4a/P4b. The verb *osoittautua* + translative, classified under BECOME, carries both the change-of-state sense ("turned out to be") and the epistemic discovery sense ("proved to be," "was revealed to be"). The P4a notes describe *osoittautua* as "to turn out to be, to prove to be," and the P4b examples include both a forecast (*Ennuste osoittautui oikeaksi*) and an art-authentication case (*Maalaus osoittautui väärennökseksi*), both of which involve discovery of a pre-existing state rather than a genuine change of state. Finnish thus uses a single construction where some languages maintain a distinction between BECOME (genuine change) and REVELATION (discovery of existing state). No separate REVELATION operator is attested.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction

The *todistaa* + NP-accusative + translative construction (Pattern 10) covers both positive verification (*todistaa oikeaksi* — certify as correct) and disconfirmation (*todistaa vääräksi* — prove false) through alternation of the translative complement. The construction is not split into two formally distinct patterns; the semantic direction is determined entirely by the lexical content of the translative complement (*oikeaksi* vs. *vääräksi*, *aidoksi* vs. *väärennökseksi*). This means VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are formally unified in Finnish under a single transitive certification construction.

Additionally, the idiomatic *ei pidä paikkansa* functions exclusively as a DISCONFIRMATION/NEGATION form with no positive counterpart. There is no attested idiomatic *\*pitää paikkansa* used as a standalone positive verification formula in the P4a/P4b data; the positive direction relies on the copular attribution construction (*se on totta*, *väite on oikea*) rather than on a parallel idiomatic form.

### 2.3 Asymmetry in CAUSATION (factual): falsity vs. truth

The causative domain shows a clear directional asymmetry. Causation toward falsity is lexically well-attested: *väärentää* (to forge, to cause to be counterfeit) and *vääristää* (to distort, to cause to be skewed) are both documented as [CORE] items with distinct semantic and legal profiles. Causation toward truth — making something true, genuine, or accurate — has no parallel productive causative verb. P4a explicitly notes: "No productive causative from *tosi*, *aito*, or *luotettava* is attested; those concepts are expressed periphrastically (*todistaa aidoksi*, *tehdä luotettavaksi*)." The periphrastic forms are noted but not exemplified in P4b, and neither is rated [CORE]. This is a one-directional lexical gap: the language has dedicated morphological causatives for falsification but not for authentication or truth-making.

### 2.4 CAUSATION (character) is absent

No construction for inducing a truth-related character property — making someone honest, making a source reliable — is attested in either P4a or P4b. The P4a note for Pattern 5 mentions *tehdä luotettavaksi* as a periphrastic possibility but does not exemplify it, does not rate it, and does not treat it as a productive pattern. This gap is consistent with the broader absence of character-directed causatives in the documented data.

### 2.5 Negation is uniformly analytic

Across all attested negative constructions, negation is expressed analytically through the negative auxiliary *ei* combined with the main verb in its connegative form (*ei ole*, *ei pidä*). No morphological negation of adjectives or nouns is attested in this domain. The partitive case in *ei ole totta* is retained under negation (as noted in P4a Pattern 2), which is a morphosyntactic feature of the construction but does not constitute morphological negation of the adjective itself. The idiomatic *ei pidä paikkansa* is a fixed negative form with no productive internal structure in use.

### 2.6 Nominalization is structurally differentiated

Finnish maintains four formally distinct nominalization subtypes in this domain, each with a different suffix and a different semantic function: *-uus/-yys* for abstract quality nouns, *-ja/-jä* for agent nouns, *-us/-ys* for result nouns, and *-minen* for action/process nouns. P4a and P4b treat all four as [CORE] and fully productive. No single nominalization construction covers all functions; the language distributes nominalization across dedicated morphological patterns.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction — morphological, periphrastic, or idiomatic — for inducing a truth-related character property is attested or exemplified in P4a/P4b. The periphrastic *tehdä luotettavaksi* is mentioned in a note but is not documented as a common expression. **MISSING (structural gap).**

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **CAUSATION (factual) — truth direction**: No productive causative verb meaning "to make true," "to make genuine," or "to make accurate" is attested. The gap is one-directional: causatives toward falsity (*väärentää*, *vääristää*) are fully attested; causatives toward truth are absent as lexical items. Periphrasis (*todistaa aidoksi*) is noted but not exemplified as a common expression. **Lexical gap on the truth-directed side of CAUSATION (factual).**

- **REVELATION as a distinct lexical item**: No verb or construction dedicated to the revelation-of-truth sense (distinct from change-of-state) is attested. The function is absorbed by *osoittautua*. **Lexical gap for a dedicated REVELATION operator.**

- **Positive idiomatic counterpart to *ei pidä paikkansa***: No idiomatic fixed form parallel to *ei pidä paikkansa* for positive confirmation is attested. **Asymmetric lexical gap.**

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps — cases where the semantic operation is blocked or inexpressible — are indicated by the P4a/P4b data. All major truth-domain operations (attribution, modification, becoming, verification, disconfirmation, negation, nominalization) are expressible in Finnish, though some require periphrasis or share constructions.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

| Domain | Positive (truth) | Negative (falsity) | Asymmetry |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAUSATION (factual) | No productive causative verb attested | *väärentää*, *vääristää* fully attested [CORE] | Falsity-directed causatives present; truth-directed causatives absent |
| Idiomatic fixed form | No positive counterpart to *ei pidä paikkansa* attested | *ei pidä paikkansa* [CORE] | Negative idiomatic form present; positive idiomatic form absent |
| REVELATION | *osoittautua* covers both directions | *osoittautua* covers both directions | No asymmetry within BECOME/REVELATION; asymmetry is between BECOME and a dedicated REVELATION operator |
