# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4. Each opposition is supported by explicit textual evidence from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents an opposition explicitly (e.g., "the direct semantic opposite of"), this is noted. Where an opposition is inferred from co-occurrence in contrastive constructions or from documented antonym relationships, the source is identified. No opposition has been constructed by inference from English semantic categories or from general linguistic knowledge not grounded in the CONTEXT.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *egia*
**Term B:** *gezur* / *gezurra*
**Transliteration:** egia / gezurra
**English translation:** truth / lie; falsehood
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly describes *gezur* as "the direct semantic opposite of *egia*," and the two terms appear as the primary binary poles of propositional evaluation in everyday Basque speech. The construction *egia da ala gezurra?* ("is it true or false?") and the paired collocation *egia eta gezurra nahastu* ("to mix truth and lies") both document this as a lexically established binary opposition. The pair also appears in *gezurra eta egia nahastu* and *gezurra eta egia bereiztea*. Both terms have parallel derivational families (*egiazkoa*/*gezurrezkoa*; *egiatasuna*/*gezurtasuna*; *egia esan*/*gezurra esan*; *egia da*/*gezurra da*) that mirror each other systematically across P1b, P2a, and P2b. The pair constitutes the baseline binary for propositional evaluation documented in P3 Group 1.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *egiazko*
**Term B:** *gezurrezko*
**Transliteration:** egiazko / gezurrezko
**English translation:** true, genuine, authentic (attributive) / false, lying, counterfeit, fake (attributive)
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *egiazko* "contrasts with *faltsuko* or *gezurrezko* in evaluative contexts." P2a explicitly states that *gezurrezkoa* contrasts directly with *egiazkoa*. Both are adjectival forms derived from their respective nominal roots via the *-zko*/*-ezko* suffix and appear in the same attributive and predicative positions. The two relational adjectives are documented as adnominal modifiers in parallel legal collocations: *egiazko lekukotasuna* ("true testimony") vs. *gezurrezko lekukotasuna* ("false testimony"), and *egiazko kopiak* ("certified true copies") vs. *gezurrezko agiriak* ("false documents"). Both are formed with the same relational suffix pattern (*-zko* / *-rezko*) on opposing base nouns. They also appear in parallel attributive collocations (*egiazko informazioa zabaldu* / *gezurrezko informazioa zabaldu*).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** *egiazkoa*
**Term B:** *gezurrezkoa*
**Transliteration:** egiazkoa / gezurrezkoa
**English translation:** true, truthful, genuine (definite/predicative) / false, lying, based on lies (definite/predicative)
**Description of contrast:** These are the definite predicative forms of the *egiazko*/*gezurrezko* pair (Opposition 2). The morphological alternation between the indefinite attributive form (*egiazko*) and the definite predicative form (*egiazkoa*) is documented in P1b, P2a, and P4a as a systematic positional contrast.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** *zuzen* / *zuzena*
**Term B:** *oker* / *okerra*
**Transliteration:** zuzena / okerra
**English translation:** correct, right, straight / wrong, mistaken, crooked
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly describes *oker* as "the primary antonym of *zuzen*." Both carry a spatial metaphor (straight vs. crooked) that underlies both physical and evaluative senses. The two terms appear as a documented antonym pair across epistemic (propositional correctness), moral (rightness of conduct), and spatial (straight vs. crooked) domains. Parallel constructions are attested: *zuzen egon* ("to be right") vs. *oker egon* ("to be wrong"), *zuzen ibili* ("to behave correctly") vs. *oker ibili* ("to go wrong"), *zuzen esan* ("to say correctly") vs. *oker esan* ("to say incorrectly"), *zuzen ulertu* ("to understand correctly") vs. *oker ulertu* ("to misunderstand"). The pair also appears in the fixed binary construction *zuzen ala oker* ("right or wrong") and the subordinate interrogative *zuzena den ala ez den*. Their derivational families mirror each other (*zuzentasuna*/*okertasuna*; *zuzendu*/*okertu*; *zuzenketa*/*okerkeria*). The collocation *okerra zuzendu* (to correct the error) directly combines both terms.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** *zuzena*
**Term B:** *zuzengabea*
**Transliteration:** zuzena / zuzengabea
**English translation:** correct, just / unjust, unfair, incorrect, wrongful
**Description of contrast:** P2a documents *zuzengabea* as a privative form of *zuzena* via the *-gabe* suffix. P2a notes it is less common than *okerra* as the antonym of *zuzena* and is attested primarily in formal registers. This is a morphological opposition on the same root, distinct from the lexical opposition in Opposition 6.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** *oker nago* / *oker zaude*
**Term B:** *zuzena da* / *zuzen dago*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** I am wrong / you are wrong — vs. — it is correct / he/she is right
**Description of contrast:** The *oker* + *egon* construction uses the stative copula *egon* to mark a contingent, reversible state of being mistaken, while the positive correctness claim uses *zuzena da* with the equative copula *da* (*izan*). This is an asymmetry documented in P4a: *egon* marks contingent states, *izan* marks inherent properties. The two constructions are not morphologically parallel despite being semantically contrasting. No single fixed positive counterpart construction to *oker nago* is attested; the positive is expressed predicatively as *zuzena da* or *zuzen dago*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** *egiaztagarri* / *egiaztagarria*
**Term B:** *egiaztaezin* / *egiaztaezina*
**Transliteration:** egiaztagarria / egiaztaezina
**English translation:** verifiable; capable of verification / unverifiable; impossible to confirm

**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** *egiaztatu*
**Term B:** *egiaztatu gabeko* / *egiaztatu gabe utzi*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to verify; verified / uncertified; unverified; to leave unverified
**Description of contrast:** P1b and P2b document *egiaztatu gabeko* ("lacking verification") and *egiaztatu gabe utzi* ("to leave without verification") as participial constructions expressing the absence of the action named by *egiaztatu*. The contrast is morphological in that *gabe* (without, lacking) negates the participial form, and constructional in that the negation is postposed rather than prefixed. P2b documents *egiaztatu gabe utzi* as a periphrastic privative construction describing the failure to verify, contrasting the completed verification act with its deliberate or negligent omission.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** *egiaztagarria* / *egiaztaezina* / *egiaztatu gabe*
**Term B:** (three-way contrast; see description)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** verifiable (can be verified) / unverifiable (cannot be verified) / not yet verified or left unverified
**Description of contrast:** These three forms from the *egiazta-* stem represent distinct contrasts that are not fully reducible to a single binary. *Egiaztagarria* vs. *egiaztaezina* is a contrast of possibility (can be verified / cannot be verified). *Egiaztatu gabe* expresses that verification has not occurred, which is distinct from impossibility. P4a notes that *-gabe* expresses absence while *ezin* expresses impossibility, and these are not interchangeable. The three-way structure is documented rather than collapsed.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** *baliozko* / *baliozkoa*
**Term B:** *baliogabe* / *baliogabea*
**Transliteration:** baliozkoa / baliogabea
**English translation:** valid; legitimate / invalid; null; void; worthless
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly identifies *baliogabea* as the direct antonym of *baliozkoa*. P2a documents *baliogabe* as the negative counterpart to *baliozko* within the validity paradigm. The opposition is morphological in that *gabe* (without, lacking) negates the base *balio* (value, validity), producing the antonym of the *-zko* relational adjective. *Baliozkoa* is formed with the adjectival suffix *-zko*; *baliogabea* is formed with the privative suffix *-gabe*, both on the root *balio*. The pair appears in legal and administrative contexts.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** *baliozkotu*
**Term B:** *baliogabetu*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to validate; to ratify / to invalidate; to annul; to void; to nullify

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** *fidagarri* / *fidagarria*
**Term B:** *fidagaitz* / *fidagaitza*
**Transliteration:** fidagarria / fidagaitza
**English translation:** trustworthy; reliable / untrustworthy; hard to trust
