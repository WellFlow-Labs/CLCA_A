Below is a P2b-style inventory of **attested multi-word constructions** built around the **P1 base forms**. I avoid listing simple derivational outputs already handled in P2a unless the item functions as a broader **multi-word construction**. Where I do not identify a common construction centered on the P1 base, I state that explicitly.

## Base Form: **אמת**

### 1. Example: **לומר את האמת**
**Transliteration:** *lomar et ha-ʾemet*  
**English translation:** to tell the truth  
**Usage notes:** Very common everyday construction for speaking truthfully or admitting what is really the case. It is neutral in register and widely used in conversation, media, and narrative contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. Example: **לדעת את האמת**
**Transliteration:** *ladaʿat et ha-ʾemet*  
**English translation:** to know the truth  
**Usage notes:** Common construction for possessing the real facts or actual state of affairs. It is used in personal, legal, journalistic, and political contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. Example: **לגלות את האמת**
**Transliteration:** *legalot et ha-ʾemet*  
**English translation:** to discover / reveal the truth  
**Usage notes:** Common in investigative, dramatic, and journalistic contexts. Depending on context, it can mean either finding out the truth or bringing it to light.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. Example: **להוציא את האמת לאור**
**Transliteration:** *lehotsi et ha-ʾemet la-ʾor*  
**English translation:** to bring the truth to light  
**Usage notes:** Established idiomatic construction for exposing what really happened. It is especially common in journalism, legal discourse, and public controversy.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. Example: **גרעין של אמת**
**Transliteration:** *garʿin shel ʾemet*  
**English translation:** a grain/kernel of truth  
**Usage notes:** Common idiomatic expression used when a claim is partly true but exaggerated or distorted. It often appears in argument, commentary, and media language.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. Example: **אמת מרה**
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet marah*  
**English translation:** a bitter truth  
**Usage notes:** Common literary and everyday collocation for an unpleasant but real fact. It is often used when acknowledging something emotionally difficult.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. Example: **אמת פשוטה**
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet peshutah*  
**English translation:** a simple truth  
**Usage notes:** Common rhetorical collocation for a supposedly obvious or basic truth. It appears in essays, speeches, and argumentative prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. Example: **חיפוש האמת**
**Transliteration:** *ḥipus ha-ʾemet*  
**English translation:** the search for truth  
**Usage notes:** Common in philosophical, journalistic, legal, and educational discourse. It frames truth as something pursued or investigated.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. Example: **איש האמת**
**Transliteration:** *ʾish ha-ʾemet*  
**English translation:** man of truth / truth-teller  
**Usage notes:** This is attested, but much less neutral and more literary or rhetorical than ordinary everyday description. It tends to appear in elevated, moralizing, or political praise.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Base Form: **נכון**

### 1. Example: **זה נכון ש־**
**Transliteration:** *ze nakhon she-*  
**English translation:** it is true/correct that—  
**Usage notes:** Very common clause-introducing construction used to confirm a proposition. It appears in both speech and writing.

### 2. Example: **נכון לעכשיו**
**Transliteration:** *nakhon le-ʿakhshav*  
**English translation:** as of now / currently  
**Usage notes:** Very common discourse construction in journalism, administration, and everyday speech. Here נכון has developed a temporal-adverbial use rather than a simple truth-evaluative one.

### 3. Example: **נכון מאוד**
**Transliteration:** *nakhon meʾod*  
**English translation:** very true / exactly right  
**Usage notes:** Common response formula confirming that someone’s statement is correct. It is frequent in conversation, teaching, and interviews.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. Example: **נכון או לא נכון**
**Transliteration:** *nakhon o lo nakhon*  
**English translation:** true or false / correct or incorrect  
**Usage notes:** Common evaluative pairing in educational, argumentative, and quiz-like contexts. It frames a binary judgment of correctness.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. Example: **אם זה נכון**
**Transliteration:** *im ze nakhon*  
**English translation:** if that is true/correct  
**Usage notes:** Very common conditional construction in speech and writing. It is fully natural but syntactically ordinary rather than idiomatic.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
