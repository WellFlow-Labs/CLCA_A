# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## GROUP 1: Statements and Their Accuracy

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to evaluate whether spoken or written statements correspond to actual states of affairs. These terms appear in contexts where the accuracy of claims, reports, testimony, or accounts is being assessed. They are commonly used when verifying information, correcting errors, or distinguishing accurate from inaccurate representations.

**Representative expressions:**
- **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness) — used for statements that match reality
- **irọ́** (lie, falsehood) — used for statements that do not match reality
- **tọ́** (to be correct, true) — affirms accuracy of statements
- **purọ́** (to lie) — describes making false statements
- **ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́** (true statement) — characterizes specific utterances as accurate
- **ọ̀rọ̀ irọ́** (false statement) — characterizes specific utterances as false
- **sọ òtítọ́** (tell the truth) — command or description of truthful speech
- **pa irọ́** (tell a lie) — description of false speech
- **àìtọ́** (incorrectness, untruth) — characterizes statements as inaccurate
- **ṣòótọ́** (to be truthful, speak truth) — describes habitual truthful speech

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **jẹ́** (to be, affirm) — can affirm truth but also has broader copular functions
- **ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀** (to be so, be the case) — confirms states of affairs but not limited to statements
- **bẹ́ẹ̀ ni** (it is so) — affirms accuracy but also general agreement

---

## GROUP 2: Personal Character and Habitual Conduct

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that characterize persons based on their typical patterns of behavior, particularly regarding honesty, deception, and moral conduct. These terms are used in moral evaluation of individuals, describing whether someone is trustworthy, deceitful, righteous, or corrupt. They appear frequently in contexts of reputation, character assessment, and moral judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- **olótítọ́** (truthful person) — characterizes someone as habitually honest
- **apurọ́** (liar) — characterizes someone as habitually dishonest
- **ẹlẹ́rọ́** (liar) — alternative term for habitual liar
- **oníròrọ́** (habitual liar) — emphasizes chronic lying
- **olódodo** (righteous person) — characterizes moral uprightness
- **ìwà rere** (good character) — describes virtuous conduct patterns
- **ìwà búburú** (bad character) — describes immoral conduct patterns
- **ìwà ọmọlúàbí** (exemplary character) — describes ideal moral character
- **aláìlótítọ́** (untruthful person) — characterizes habitual dishonesty
- **ẹlẹ́tàn** (deceiver, trickster) — characterizes someone as deceptive
- **abàjẹ́** (corrupt person) — characterizes moral corruption
- **aṣòótọ́** (truthful person) — characterizes habitual truthfulness

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **ìwà** (character, behavior) — general term that requires qualification
- **òdodo** (righteousness) — can describe both persons and actions
- **ìbàjẹ́** (corruption) — can describe both persons and situations

---

## GROUP 3: Epistemic Stance and Confidence

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that indicate the speaker's level of confidence, certainty, or belief regarding claims or situations. These terms are used when expressing degrees of conviction, from complete certainty to doubt, and when describing whether someone accepts or rejects information as reliable. They commonly appear in contexts of verification, investigation, and epistemic evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- **gbàgbọ́** (to believe, trust) — expresses acceptance as true
- **ìgbàgbọ́** (belief, faith) — state of believing or trusting
- **dájú** (to be certain, sure) — expresses high confidence
- **ìdájú** (certainty, assurance) — state of being certain
- **ìdánilójú** (assurance, guarantee) — providing certainty
- **àrídájú** (certainty, proof) — grounds for certainty
- **kò gbàgbọ́** (does not believe) — expresses disbelief
- **aláìgbàgbọ́** (unbeliever, skeptic) — characterizes lack of belief
- **gbàgbọ́ láì rí** (believe without seeing) — faith without evidence
- **gbàgbọ́ jù** (believe too much) — excessive credulity
- **kò dájú** (not certain) — expresses uncertainty
- **ṣé o gbàgbọ́?** (do you believe?) — questions epistemic stance

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **mọ òtítọ́** (know the truth) — overlaps with both knowledge and belief
- **rí òtítọ́** (see the truth) — involves perception and recognition
- **agbàgbọ́** (believer) — overlaps with character description

---

## GROUP 4: Verification and Evidence

**Definition:** This group contains expressions related to establishing, proving, or confirming the accuracy of claims through evidence, testimony, or investigation. These terms appear in legal, investigative, and formal contexts where facts need to be established or verified. They involve processes of checking, demonstrating, or providing grounds for accepting something as true.

**Representative expressions:**
- **ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀** (confirmation, verification) — establishing as true
- **fẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀** (confirm, verify) — act of establishing truth
- **ìjẹ́rìí** (testimony, witness, evidence) — evidence supporting claims
- **jẹ́rìí sí** (testify to) — give evidence about
- **ẹlẹ́rìí** (witness) — person who provides testimony
- **ṣe ìjẹ́rìí** (give testimony) — act of testifying
- **ṣe àyẹ̀wò** (examine, investigate) — conduct investigation
- **ṣe ìwádìí** (investigate, research) — systematic inquiry
- **ṣe àfihàn** (show, demonstrate, prove) — provide proof
- **gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀** (establish the truth) — prove truth
- **mú irọ́** (catch in a lie) — expose falsehood
- **fi òtítọ́ hàn** (reveal the truth) — make truth visible
- **tún àṣìṣe ṣe** (correct the error) — fix mistakes

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **rí àṣìṣe** (find an error) — overlaps with detection and correction
- **ìtọ́ka** (indication, reference) — can be evidence but also general pointing
- **àrídájú** (certainty, proof) — overlaps with epistemic confidence

---

## GROUP 5: Moral and Legal Rightness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that evaluate actions, judgments, and conduct according to moral and legal standards of rightness, justice, and propriety. These terms appear in contexts of ethical evaluation, legal judgment, and social appropriateness. They assess whether behavior conforms to proper norms rather than whether statements correspond to facts.

**Representative expressions:**
- **òdodo** (righteousness, justice, uprightness) — moral/legal correctness
- **ṣe òdodo** (do righteousness, act justly) — perform right actions
- **ìwà òdodo** (righteous character) — morally correct conduct
- **ọ̀nà òdodo** (path of righteousness) — proper moral conduct
- **ìdájọ́ òdodo** (righteous judgment) — fair legal judgment
- **òfin òdodo** (righteous law) — just laws
- **yẹ** (to be fitting, appropriate) — social/moral appropriateness
- **ó yẹ kí** (it is fitting that, should) — moral obligation
- **kò yẹ** (not fitting, inappropriate) — moral prohibition
- **ìṣe òdodo** (righteous deed) — morally correct action
- **òdodo àti òtítọ́** (righteousness and truth) — moral and factual correctness
- **aláìṣe òdodo** (unrighteous person) — morally wrong conduct

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tọ́** (to be correct) — can mean both factually correct and morally right
- **ìwà rere** (good character) — overlaps with character description
- **ṣe déédé** (do correctly) — can mean both accurate and proper

---

## GROUP 6: Deception and Manipulation

**Definition:** This group contains expressions specifically related to intentional deception, trickery, and fraudulent behavior. These terms emphasize the deliberate and often cunning nature of misleading others, going beyond simple false statements to include schemes, fraud, and manipulation. They appear in contexts of exposing fraud, warning against tricksters, or describing elaborate deceptions.

**Representative expressions:**
- **ẹ̀tàn** (deception, fraud, trickery) — deliberate misleading
- **ṣe ẹ̀tàn** (deceive, commit fraud) — act of deception
- **tan ni** (deceive someone, trick) — deceive a person
- **ẹlẹ́tàn** (deceiver, trickster, fraudster) — habitual deceiver
- **irọ́ ẹ̀tàn** (deceitful lie, fraudulent falsehood) — lie as part of scheme
- **títàn** (deceiving, act of deceiving) — process of deception
- **gbé irọ́ ró** (fabricate a lie, concoct falsehood) — construct deception
- **dá irọ́ rò** (fabricate a lie) — plan deception

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **purọ́** (to lie) — simple lying vs. elaborate deception
- **irọ́** (lie) — can be simple falsehood or part of deception
- **ìbàjẹ́** (corruption) — moral decay that may involve deception

---

## GROUP 7: Accuracy and Precision

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that evaluate the exactness, precision, or correctness of execution, measurement, or representation. These terms focus on how closely something matches a standard, model, or proper method. They appear in contexts of instruction, evaluation of performance, and assessment of whether something has been done according to specifications.

**Representative expressions:**
- **ṣe déédé** (do correctly, do properly) — execute accurately
- **déédé** (correctly, exactly, precisely) — with precision
- **ṣe é déédé** (do it correctly) — command for accurate execution
- **kò ṣe é déédé** (did not do it correctly) — inaccurate execution
- **àṣìṣe** (error, mistake) — deviation from correctness
- **ṣe àṣìṣe** (make an error) — commit mistake
- **àìṣedéédé** (incorrectness, impropriety) — not done correctly
- **tọ́ ọwọ́** (be skillful, dexterous) — manual accuracy
- **ètò tọ́** (correct system, proper arrangement) — organizational accuracy
- **bí ó ṣe déédé** (as it should be done correctly) — proper method

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tọ́** (to be correct) — overlaps with truth and accuracy
- **ìtọ́** (correctness, straightness) — can be factual or procedural
- **àtọ́** (correction) — overlaps with fixing errors

---

## GROUP 8: Correspondence and Agreement

**Definition:** This group contains expressions that evaluate whether things match, correspond, or agree with each other. These terms assess relationships of similarity, harmony, or alignment between different entities, statements, or standards. They appear in contexts of comparison, checking consistency, and evaluating whether representations match their referents.

**Representative expressions:**
- **ìbámu** (agreement, correspondence, harmony) — state of matching
- **bá mu** (correspond, match, agree with) — relationship of correspondence
- **bá mu pẹ̀lú** (correspond with) — match with something
- **kò bá mu** (does not correspond) — lack of agreement
- **jọra** (to resemble, be similar) — similarity relationship
- **ìjọra** (resemblance, similarity) — quality of resemblance
- **jọra pẹ̀lú** (resemble, be similar to) — resemble something
- **àfarawé** (comparison, resemblance) — act of comparing
- **ṣe àfarawé** (make comparison) — compare things
- **fara jọ** (to resemble) — look like
- **tọ́ sílẹ̀** (be well-founded, grounded in truth) — proper foundation

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tọ́ sí** (be directed toward correctly) — can involve correspondence
- **ìtọ́ka** (indication, reference) — pointing relationship
- **bí ó tọ́** (as it should be) — conformity to standard

---

## GROUP 9: Authenticity and Genuineness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that distinguish real, genuine, or authentic things from fake, counterfeit, or false representations. These terms evaluate whether something is what it purports to be, focusing on the distinction between genuine instances and imitations. They appear in contexts of authentication, identifying fakes, and affirming the real nature of things.

**Representative expressions:**
- **gidi** (genuine, real, authentic) — truly what it claims to be
- **gidi gidi** (very genuine, completely real) — emphatic authenticity
- **jẹ́ gidi** (be genuine, be authentic) — affirm genuineness
- **kì í ṣe gidi** (not genuine, not real) — identify as fake
- **pàtàkì** (important, genuine, authentic) — real/essential
- **jẹ́jẹ́** (truly, really, genuinely) — emphatic genuineness
- **òtítọ́-inú** (sincerity, inner truth) — genuine inner state
- **tọ́ ọkàn** (be sincere, have straight heart) — genuine intention
- **òdodo ọkàn** (sincerity, uprightness of heart) — genuine character
- **irọ́ inú** (inner falsehood, insincerity) — lack of genuineness

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **òtítọ́** (truth) — can mean both factual truth and genuineness
- **ìwà ọmọlúàbí** (exemplary character) — genuine vs. pretended virtue
- **pàtàkì** (important) — importance vs. genuineness meanings

---

## GROUP 10: Directness and Straightness

**Definition:** This group contains expressions involving spatial or metaphorical straightness, directness, or lack of deviation. These terms can describe physical paths, direct approaches, or straightforward conduct. They appear in contexts of giving directions, describing routes, and metaphorically in discussions of honest or direct behavior.

**Representative expressions:**
- **tọ́ lọ** (go straight, proceed directly) — direct movement
- **tọ́ wá** (come straight, come directly) — direct approach
- **tọ́ bọ̀** (return straight, come back directly) — direct return
- **ọ̀nà tọ́** (straight path, correct way) — direct route
- **tọ́tọ́** (very straight, completely direct) — emphatic straightness
- **títọ́** (being straight, being correct) — state of straightness
- **ìtọ́** (straightness, correctness) — quality of straightness
- **tọ́ sí** (be directed toward) — orientation toward

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **tọ́** (to be straight/correct) — physical vs. abstract straightness
- **ọ̀nà òdodo** (path of righteousness) — metaphorical path
- **ìtọ́sọ́nà** (guidance, direction) — showing the way

---

## GROUP 11: Disclosure and Concealment

**Definition:** This group includes expressions related to revealing, hiding, or managing the visibility of information. These terms describe making things known or keeping them secret, and appear in contexts involving transparency, secrecy, exposure of hidden information, and decisions about what to disclose.

**Representative expressions:**
- **fi òtítọ́ hàn** (reveal the truth, show the truth) — make truth visible
- **ṣe ìfihàn** (reveal, disclose, make known) — disclosure
- **fi irọ́ hàn** (expose a lie, reveal falsehood) — expose deception
- **ṣe àṣírí** (keep secret, conceal) — hide information
- **irọ́ jáde** (lie came out, lie was exposed) — revelation of falsehood
- **purọ́ jáde** (lie was exposed) — exposure of deception
- **irọ́ tú** (lie was exposed, lie fell apart) — lie revealed
- **mú irọ́** (catch in a lie, expose falsehood) — detect deception

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- **rí òtítọ́** (see the truth) — perception vs. revelation
- **gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀** (establish the truth) — making known vs. proving
- **ṣe àfihàn** (show, demonstrate) — overlaps with proving

## ITEMS NOT CLEARLY GROUPED

The following items do not fit cleanly into the groups above or show significant ambiguity:

- **jẹ́** (to be, affirm) — too general; copular function extends beyond truth domain
- **ṣe** (to do, make, be the case) — extremely broad; appears in many constructions
- **ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀** (to be so, be the case) — general affirmation, not specific to any group
- **bẹ́ẹ̀ ni** (it is so, that's right) — general agreement marker
- **òtítọ́ ni** (it is truth, truly) — fixed emphatic expression
- **ìwà** (character, behavior) — too general without qualification
- **rere** (good) — general positive evaluation
- **burúkú** (bad) — general negative evaluation
- **kọ́ òtítọ́** (teach truth) — pedagogical context, not clearly grouped
- **wá òtítọ́** (seek truth) — search activity, not clearly grouped
- **gbọ́ òtítọ́** (hear the truth) — reception of information
- **tọ́jú** (care for, look after) — extends to caregiving, peripheral to truth
- **tọ́ka sí** (point to, indicate) — reference function, not clearly grouped
- **ìtọ́kasí** (reference, indication) — technical reference term
- **ìrántí** (remembrance, memory) — memory domain, peripheral
- **àkíyèsí** (notice, observation) — attention/perception, preliminary to evaluation

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

1. **Overlapping boundaries:** Many expressions appear in multiple contexts. For example, **tọ́** participates in statements about factual accuracy (Group 1), moral rightness (Group 5), and procedural correctness (Group 7).

2. **Gradient membership:** Some items are central to a group while others are peripheral. For example, **òtítọ́** and **irọ́** are central to Group 1, while **jẹ́** is peripheral.

3. **Context-dependent interpretation:** Several expressions shift meaning based on context. **ìwà** (character) requires qualification to belong to Group 2, while **pàtàkì** can mean either "important" or "genuine" depending on usage.

4. **Asymmetric oppositions:** Not all groups have clear opposites. Group 6 (Deception) is more specific than simple negation of Group 1 (Accurate Statements).

5. **Compound expressions:** Many multi-word constructions combine elements from different groups, such as **òdodo àti òtítọ́** (righteousness and truth) bridging Groups 1 and 5.

6. **Degree and emphasis:** Reduplication (e.g., **gidi gidi**, **tọ́tọ́**) and intensifiers (e.g., **gan-an**, **púpọ̀**) create emphatic versions without changing group membership.
