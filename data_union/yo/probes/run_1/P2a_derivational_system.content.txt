# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note on Yoruba derivational morphology:** Yoruba derivational morphology operates primarily through prefixation (nominalization via *i-*, *ì-*, *à-*, *o-*, *ọ-*, *ẹ-*), compounding, and reduplication. Suffixation is rare as a productive derivational process. The boundary between derivation and compounding is not always sharp. The forms documented below are restricted to those with clear attestation in the P1 and P1b data or in well-documented Yoruba lexicographic and linguistic sources. Paradigm completion is not attempted.

---

## Derived Forms by Base Item

---

### Base: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**1.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** olòtítọ́
**Transliteration:** olòtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *olò-* (person-prefix, variant of *ẹni tó ni* "one who has/possesses") + *títọ́* (truthfulness); "one who possesses truth / a truthful person"
**Natural English Translation:** truthful person, honest person, one who tells the truth
**Usage:** Used to describe a person whose habitual conduct is characterized by truthfulness. Appears in moral assessments and in character evaluations in everyday and formal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** àìtọ́
**Transliteration:** àìtọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *àì-* (negation nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correctness/straightness); "not-correctness, incorrectness"
**Natural English Translation:** incorrectness, wrongness, inaccuracy
**Usage:** Used as the negated counterpart of *òtọ́/òtítọ́* to evaluate a statement, action, or situation as incorrect or not right. Appears in legal and moral assessments and in the compound *ẹ̀rí àìtọ́* (documented in P1b).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìtọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straight/correct); bare nominalization of the root
**Natural English Translation:** straightness, correctness, rightness
**Usage:** The bare nominalization of the root *tọ́*; used in philosophical and moral discourse to refer abstractly to the quality of being correct. Less frequent in everyday speech than *òtítọ́* but attested in formal and literary registers. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 3).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** tọ́tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́tọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** Reduplication of *tọ́*; intensified form
**Natural English Translation:** very straight, very correct, truly right
**Usage:** The reduplicated form of the root, used as an intensifier in adjectival and adverbial function. Appears in *ọ̀nà tọ́tọ́* ("the very straight/correct path") documented in P1b. Reduplication here signals intensification rather than a new lexical category.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** àtúntọ́
**Transliteration:** àtúntọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (nominalizer) + *tún* (again, re-) + *tọ́* (correct/straighten); "re-correcting"
**Natural English Translation:** correction, rectification, reform, amendment
**Usage:** Used in administrative, legal, and moral contexts to describe the act of correcting something previously wrong. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 9). The *tún* element marks iterative or restorative action.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### Base: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

---

**6.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** aláìrọ́
**Transliteration:** aláìrọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix variant) + *àì-* (negation) + *rọ́* (lie); "one who does not lie"
**Natural English Translation:** truthful person, one who does not lie, honest person
**Usage:** Used to describe a person who is characterized by not telling lies. Appears in moral praise and in character assessments. Functions as a positive character descriptor.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** aṣírọ́
**Transliteration:** aṣírọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (person-nominalizer) + *ṣ-* (do/make) + *irọ́* (lie); "one who makes/tells lies"
**Natural English Translation:** liar, one who tells lies, fabricator
**Usage:** Used to describe a habitual liar. Appears in moral condemnation and in legal contexts where a witness's credibility is being challenged. A strong term of moral criticism.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** irọ́ pátápátá
**Transliteration:** irọ́ pátápátá
**Literal Breakdown:** *irọ́* (lie) + *pátápátá* (completely, entirely, totally); "complete/total lie"
**Natural English Translation:** complete lie, total falsehood, outright fabrication
**Usage:** Used to intensify the evaluation of a statement as entirely false, with no element of truth. The adverbial *pátápátá* is a productive intensifier in Yoruba. Appears in everyday and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *irọ́*:** The root does not appear to generate a large paradigm of prefixed nominalizations in the way that *tọ́* or *mọ̀* do. The primary derivational products are agentive forms (the liar) and intensified forms. No causative or other derivational forms are identified with confidence.

### Base: **òdodo** (justice, righteousness, fairness)

---

**9.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** alòdodo
**Transliteration:** alòdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *òdodo* (justice/righteousness); "one who possesses/practices justice"
**Natural English Translation:** just person, righteous person, one who acts with fairness
**Usage:** Used to describe a person characterized by just and righteous conduct. Appears in moral praise and in assessments of judges, leaders, and community figures.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** àìdodo
**Transliteration:** àìdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *àì-* (negation nominalizer) + *dodo* (root of *òdodo*); "not-righteous, unjust"
**Natural English Translation:** injustice, unrighteousness, unfairness
**Usage:** Used to evaluate conduct, decisions, or persons as unjust or unrighteous. Appears in moral and legal criticism. Functions as the negated counterpart of *òdodo*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *òdodo*:** The reduplication in *òdodo* itself is already a morphological feature of the base form. Further derivational productivity beyond agentive and negated forms is not well-attested in available data.

### Base: **ìmọ̀** (knowledge, understanding, awareness)

---

**11.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** amọ̀na
**Transliteration:** amọ̀na
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (person-nominalizer) + *mọ̀* (know) + *na* (the way/path, contracted); "one who knows the way"
**Natural English Translation:** guide, one who knows the way, knowledgeable leader
**Usage:** Used to describe a person who has the knowledge to guide others. Appears in contexts of leadership, navigation (literal and metaphorical), and expertise. The agentive prefix *a-* combines with the verbal root.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *ẹ̀rọ* (machine/mechanism/device); "knowledge of machines"
**Natural English Translation:** technology, technical knowledge, engineering
**Usage:** The standard Yoruba term for technology. A lexicalized compound in which the *ìmọ̀* element has been incorporated into a domain-specific term. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 19). Highly productive as a base for further compounds (e.g., *ìmọ̀ ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** aláìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** aláìmọ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *àì-* (negation) + *mọ̀* (know); "one who does not know"
**Natural English Translation:** ignorant person, one who lacks knowledge, uninformed person
**Usage:** Used to describe a person characterized by ignorance or lack of knowledge. Appears in moral and educational discourse, sometimes as a mitigating descriptor and sometimes as a criticism.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** olùkọ́
**Transliteration:** olùkọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *olù-* (person-prefix for agent of action) + *kọ́* (teach/instruct); "one who teaches"

**Note:** This form derives from *kọ́* (to teach) rather than directly from *ìmọ̀*, but is included here because it is the standard agentive counterpart in the knowledge-transmission domain. It is the person who transmits *ìmọ̀*. The derivation is from *kọ́*, not *mọ̀*.

**Natural English Translation:** teacher, instructor, educator
**Usage:** The standard Yoruba term for a teacher or instructor. Used in educational and social contexts to describe the person responsible for transmitting knowledge.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on item 14:** This item is included with explicit acknowledgment that it derives from *kọ́* (teach), not from *mọ̀* (know). It is documented here because it is the primary agentive form in the knowledge-transmission domain and is semantically adjacent to *ìmọ̀*, but the derivational base is distinct.

---

### Base: **àìmọ̀** (ignorance, lack of knowledge)

---

**15.**
**Base Form:** àìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** aláìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** aláìmọ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *àìmọ̀* (ignorance/not-knowing); "one characterized by not-knowing"
**Natural English Translation:** ignorant person, one who does not know
**Usage:** Documented above under *ìmọ̀* (item 13). The agentive form applies equally to the negated base. Included here for completeness of the *àìmọ̀* entry.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *àìmọ̀*:** As a negated nominalization, *àìmọ̀* is itself a derivational product of *mọ̀*. Further derivation from *àìmọ̀* as a base is limited. The agentive form *aláìmọ̀* is the primary attested derivative.

### Base: **ìgbàgbọ́** (belief, faith, trust, conviction)

---

**16.**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Derived Form:** aláìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** aláìgbàgbọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *àì-* (negation) + *gbàgbọ́* (believe); "one who does not believe"
**Natural English Translation:** unbeliever, one who lacks faith, skeptic
**Usage:** Used in religious contexts to describe a person who does not hold the relevant faith or belief. Common in Christian and Islamic Yoruba discourse. A significant evaluative category in religious communities.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Derived Form:** olùgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** olùgbàgbọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *olù-* (person-prefix for agent) + *gbàgbọ́* (believe); "one who believes"
**Natural English Translation:** believer, one who has faith
**Usage:** Used in religious contexts to describe a person who holds the relevant faith. Appears in Christian and Islamic Yoruba discourse as a positive identity category. Contrasts with *aláìgbàgbọ́*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Base Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Derived Form:** ìgbàgbọ́ jinlẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ jinlẹ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief/faith) + *jinlẹ̀* (deep); "deep belief/faith"
**Natural English Translation:** deep faith, profound belief, strong conviction
**Usage:** Used to evaluate the depth and sincerity of a person's faith or belief. Appears in religious and moral discourse to distinguish superficial from genuine conviction. The modifier *jinlẹ̀* is productive with knowledge and belief nouns.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé** (trust, reliance, dependability)

---

**19.**
**Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Derived Form:** aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Transliteration:** aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *àì-* (negation) + *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (rely on/trust); "one who cannot be relied upon"
**Natural English Translation:** untrustworthy person, unreliable person, one who cannot be depended on
**Usage:** Used to describe a person who is not dependable or trustworthy. Appears in interpersonal and commercial evaluative contexts as a strong negative character assessment.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Base Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Derived Form:** olùgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Transliteration:** olùgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Literal Breakdown:** *olù-* (person-prefix for agent) + *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (rely on/trust); "one who relies on / one who is relied upon"
**Natural English Translation:** one who trusts, one who relies; also: one who is trusted
**Usage:** Used to describe either the person who places trust or the person in whom trust is placed, depending on context. The semantic direction (agent vs. recipient of trust) is resolved by context. Appears in interpersonal and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*:** The compound structure of the base (*gbé* + *kẹ̀lé*) limits further morphological derivation. The primary attested derivatives are the negated agentive form and the *olù-* agentive. No other derivational forms are identified with confidence.

### Base: **ẹ̀rí** (evidence, proof, testimony, witness)

---

**21.**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Derived Form:** ẹlẹ́rìí
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí
**Literal Breakdown:** *ẹ-* (person-nominalizer) + *lẹ́rìí* (possessing/having *ẹ̀rí*); "one who has/bears witness"
**Natural English Translation:** witness, one who bears testimony, a person with evidence
**Usage:** The standard term for a witness in legal proceedings. Documented in P1b (Root 3, item 14). Highly productive as a base for further compounds (*ẹlẹ́rìí ojú*, *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Derived Form:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú
**Literal Breakdown:** *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness) + *ojú* (eye); "eye-witness"
**Natural English Translation:** eyewitness, direct witness
**Usage:** Documented in P1b (Root 3, item 15). The most credible category of witness in Yoruba legal discourse. Formed by compounding the agentive *ẹlẹ́rìí* with the sensory modifier *ojú*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Base Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Derived Form:** ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness) + *irọ́* (lie/falsehood); "false witness"
**Natural English Translation:** false witness, perjurer
**Usage:** Documented in P1b (Root 3, item 16). Used in legal and moral contexts to describe a person who gives false testimony. Strongly condemned in Yoruba moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### Base: **jẹ́rìí** (to testify, to bear witness)

---

**26.**
**Base Form:** jẹ́rìí
**Derived Form:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *jẹ́rìí* (bear witness) + *-sí* (directional/completive); nominalization of the verbal act of testifying
**Natural English Translation:** testimony, attestation, certification, witness
**Usage:** The primary nominalization of the verb *jẹ́rìí*. Already documented as a P1 base item (item 10). Included here to document the derivational relationship between the verbal base and its nominalized form explicitly.

**27.**
**Base Form:** jẹ́rìí
**Derived Form:** olùjẹ́rìí
**Transliteration:** olùjẹ́rìí
**Literal Breakdown:** *olù-* (person-prefix for agent of action) + *jẹ́rìí* (bear witness/testify); "one who testifies"
**Natural English Translation:** witness, testifier, one who bears testimony
**Usage:** An agentive nominalization of the verb *jẹ́rìí*. Used in legal and religious contexts to describe the person who performs the act of testifying. Partially synonymous with *ẹlẹ́rìí* but derived from the verbal rather than nominal base.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: **gidi** (genuine, real, authentic)

---

**28.**
**Base Form:** gidi
**Derived Form:** gidi gidi
**Transliteration:** gidi gidi
**Literal Breakdown:** Reduplication of *gidi*; intensified form
**Natural English Translation:** very genuine, truly real, absolutely authentic
**Usage:** The reduplicated form used as an intensifier to emphasize authenticity or genuineness. Appears in colloquial Yoruba to strengthen the evaluation of something as real rather than imitation. Reduplication here functions as degree intensification.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *gidi*:** No prefixed nominalization or agentive derivation of *gidi* is identified with confidence. The primary derivational product is the reduplicated intensifier. The item functions primarily as an adjective/adverb and does not appear to generate a productive nominalization paradigm.

### Base: **òtọ́** (correct, right, accurate, proper)

---

**29.**
**Base Form:** òtọ́
**Derived Form:** àìtọ́
**Transliteration:** àìtọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *àì-* (negation nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "not-correct, incorrectness"

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *òtọ́*:** As a predicative adjective/noun closely related to *òtítọ́*, *òtọ́* shares the derivational products of the *tọ́* root documented above. No additional derivations specific to *òtọ́* as distinct from the root family are identified.

### Base: **ojúlówó** (genuine, authentic, real, legitimate)

### Base: **ìtànjẹ́** (deception, fraud, deceit, trickery)

---

**40.**
**Base Form:** ìtànjẹ́
**Derived Form:** aṣètànjẹ́
**Transliteration:** aṣètànjẹ́
