## OPPOSITION 1

**Term A:** chiqaq  
**Term B:** llulla  
**Transliteration:** chiqaq / llulla  
**Translation:** true, truthful / lie, false  
**Description:** Core lexical opposition between truth and falsehood. These terms contrast in evaluating correspondence to reality versus deviation from it, appearing in parallel constructions for speech evaluation (*chiqaq simi* vs *llulla simi*), character assessment (*chiqaq runa* vs *llulla runa*), and manner adverbials (*chiqaqta rimay* vs *llullata rimay*).  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Key observations:**

1. **Morphological negation highly productive:** The negator *mana* creates systematic oppositions with positive evaluative terms (*allin/mana allin*, *yachay/mana yachay*, *chanin/mana chanin*), but these are compositional rather than lexicalized antonyms.

2. **Asymmetric lexicalization:** Several positive terms lack dedicated lexical opposites (e.g., *sut'i* "clear" has no attested opposite for "obscure"; *allinyay* "improve" lacks opposite for "worsen").

3. **Causative pairs productive:** Multiple causative oppositions exist with parallel morphological structure (*hunt'achiy/ch'usaqchay*, *hatunchay/huch'uyachiy*, *sumaqyachiy/millaychay*).

4. **Evidential system:** The three-way evidential opposition (*-mi/-si/-cha*) is grammatically obligatory and central to epistemic evaluation.

5. **Constructional patterns:** Many oppositions are realized through parallel constructions combining evaluative terms with the same noun (*X simi*, *X runa*, *X yuyay*), creating systematic contrasts.

6. **Cultural concepts:** Some culturally important terms (*allin kawsay*, *sumaq kawsay*) lack symmetrically lexicalized negative counterparts.

7. **Perception-based epistemology:** Opposition between visual and auditory evidence (*rikuywan yachay/uyariywan yachay*, *rikusqa/uyarisqa*) is central to epistemic evaluation.
