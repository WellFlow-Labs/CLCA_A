# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X + copula (present: zero, past/future: hayah/yihyeh) + TRUTH-TERM**

The basic attributive construction uses the copula (zero in present tense, overt in past/future) to predicate truth-domain terms of subjects. Word order is typically Subject-Predicate, though Predicate-Subject is common for emphasis or with demonstratives.

### Examples
- **הסיפור אמת** / *ha-sipur emet* / "the story is truth/true"
- **התשובה נכונה** / *ha-tshuvah nkhonah* / "the answer is correct"
- **המסמך מזויף** / *ha-mismakh mezuyaf* / "the document is forged"
- **זה שקר** / *ze sheker* / "that/this is a lie"

### Notes
Present tense attribution uses zero copula with direct juxtaposition. Adjectives show gender/number agreement with subject (nkhonah feminine, mezuyaf masculine). Demonstrative subjects (ze, zot) are extremely common in truth-evaluation contexts.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X + lo/eino + TRUTH-TERM**

Negation uses the particle *lo* before the predicate, or the negative copula *eino* (inflected for gender/number) in more formal registers. The negative particle precedes the truth-term directly.

### Examples
- **זה לא נכון** / *ze lo nakhon* / "that's not correct"
- **הדבר אינו אמת** / *ha-davar eino emet* / "the thing is not true" (formal)
- **המידע לא אמין** / *ha-meyda lo amin* / "the information is not reliable"
- **אני לא בטוח** / *ani lo batuakh* / "I'm not sure"

### Notes
*Lo* is the standard colloquial negator; *ein/eino* (inflected negative copula) is more formal. Some terms have lexicalized negative forms (*bilti amin* "unreliable") using the prefix *bilti-* rather than *lo*.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**NOUN + TRUTH-TERM-ADJ** or **TRUTH-TERM-ADJ + NOUN** (less common)

Truth-domain adjectives typically follow the noun they modify and show gender/number agreement. Definite article appears on both noun and adjective when definite. Some terms appear in construct state (N1 + N2) for fixed expressions.

### Examples
- **סיפור אמיתי** / *sipur amiti* / "true story"
- **מקור אמין** / *makor amin* / "reliable source"
- **תשובה נכונה** / *tshuvah nkhonah* / "correct answer"
- **עד שקר** / *ed sheker* / "false witness" (construct state)
- **אמת מידה** / *amat midah* / "criterion" (lit. "measure of truth," construct)

### Notes
Standard adjective-noun agreement operates for most terms. Construct state formations (*ed sheker*, *amat midah*) are lexicalized and function as fixed compounds. Word order is predominantly N-Adj, though Adj-N occurs in poetic or emphatic contexts.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**X + nihyah/ne'esah + TRUTH-TERM** (formal)
**X + hafsikh lihyot + TRUTH-TERM** (colloquial: "stopped being")

Hebrew lacks a dedicated "become" verb for truth-domain terms. Inchoative meaning is expressed through the verb *nihyah* "became" (formal) or through aspectual constructions. More commonly, change of state is expressed through causative constructions or simply through past/future tense of the copula.

### Examples
- **הדבר נעשה ברור** / *ha-davar ne'esah barur* / "the thing became clear"
- **האמת התגלתה** / *ha-emet hitgaltah* / "the truth was revealed/came to light"

### Notes
True inchoative constructions are rare with truth-domain terms. Change of state is more idiomatically expressed through verbs of revelation (*hitgalah* "was revealed"), discovery (*nimtsa* "was found"), or clarification (*hitbarar* "became clear"). The construction "X became true/false" is not natural in Hebrew; instead, one says "it turned out that X" (*hitbarar she-X*).

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**X + hif'il-VERB (causative stem) + TRUTH-TERM-related**

Causative meaning is expressed through the hif'il verbal stem for roots that have truth-domain meanings. Not all truth-terms have productive causative forms; causation is often expressed periphrastically.

### Examples
- **הוא הוכיח שזה אמת** / *hu hokhiakh she-ze emet* / "he proved that it's true"
- **היא הטעתה אותי** / *hi hit'atah oti* / "she misled me" (lit. "caused me to err")
- **הם זייפו את המסמך** / *hem ziyfu et ha-mismakh* / "they forged the document"
- **הוא הצדיק את עצמו** / *hu hitsdik et atsmo* / "he justified himself"

### Notes
Causative constructions use hif'il stem (*hit'ah* "cause to err," *hitsdik* "justify/cause to be right") or pi'el intensive stem (*ziyef* "forge/falsify"). Many truth-domain terms lack dedicated causative forms; causation is expressed through verbs like *hokhiakh* "prove," *shiknea* "convince," or *gilah* "reveal."

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT/ADJ + -ut suffix** (primary pattern)
**ROOT + noun patterns** (various)

Abstract nominalizations are formed primarily through the *-ut* suffix attached to adjectives, creating abstract nouns denoting qualities or states. Some roots use other nominal patterns.

### Examples
- **אמיתות** / *amitiyut* / "authenticity" (from *amiti* "true/genuine")
- **נכונות** / *nekhonut* / "correctness; readiness" (from *nakhon* "correct")
- **אמינות** / *aminut* / "reliability" (from *amin* "reliable")
- **כנות** / *khenut* / "honesty" (from *khen* "honest")
- **ודאות** / *vada'ut* / "certainty" (from *vadai* "certain")
- **אמת** / *emet* / "truth" (root noun, not derived)

### Notes
The *-ut* suffix is highly productive for creating abstract nouns from adjectives. Some abstract nouns are root forms themselves (*emet*, *sheker*, *tsedek*) rather than derivations. The suffix *-ut* can attach to both native Hebrew forms and loanwords (*otentiyut* "authenticity" from *otenti*).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -an suffix** (habitual agent)
**Participle forms** (active agent)

Agentive nouns denoting persons characterized by truth-domain behaviors use the *-an* suffix for habitual agents or participial forms for active agents.

### Examples
- **שקרן** / *shakran* / "liar" (habitual, from *sheker* "falsehood")
- **ספקן** / *safkan* / "skeptic" (habitual, from *safek* "doubt")
- **משקר** / *meshaker* / "liar" (active participle, from *shiker* "lied")
- **מזייף** / *mezayef* / "forger" (active participle, from *ziyef* "forged")
- **מאמין** / *ma'amin* / "believer" (from *he'emin* "believed")

### Notes
The *-an* suffix creates nouns for habitual or characteristic behavior (*shakran* "habitual liar," *safkan* "habitual doubter"). Participles function as agentive nouns for active agents (*meshaker* "one who lies," *mezayef* "one who forges"). No common agentive noun exists for "truth-teller" — this is expressed periphrastically (*mi she-omer emet* "one who tells truth").

---

## 8. PREPOSITIONAL ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
**be- + TRUTH-TERM** (primary pattern)
**le- + TRUTH-TERM** (less common)

Prepositional phrases with *be-* "in/with" are highly productive for creating adverbial expressions from truth-domain nouns, indicating manner or accordance.

### Examples
- **באמת** / *be-emet* / "truly, really, in truth"
- **בצדק** / *be-tsedek* / "justly, rightly"
- **ביושר** / *be-yosher* / "honestly, uprightly"
- **בכנות** / *be-khenut* / "honestly, sincerely"
- **בוודאי** / *be-vadai* / "certainly, surely"
- **בדיוק** / *be-diyuk* / "exactly, precisely"
- **בטעות** / *be-ta'ut* / "by mistake, mistakenly"

### Notes
The preposition *be-* is extremely productive for creating manner adverbials from truth-domain nouns. These expressions are among the most frequent in conversational Hebrew. The pattern is fully productive and can apply to any appropriate noun in the domain.

---

## 9. COMPLEMENTIZER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TRUTH-TERM + she- + CLAUSE**

Many truth-domain terms take clausal complements introduced by the complementizer *she-* "that," allowing evaluation of propositions rather than just entities.

### Examples
- **נכון ש...** / *nakhon she-...* / "it's correct that..."
- **ודאי ש...** / *vadai she-...* / "it's certain that..."
- **בטוח ש...** / *batuakh she-...* / "sure that..."
- **דומה ש...** / *domeh she-...* / "it seems that..."
- **האמת היא ש...** / *ha-emet hi she-...* / "the truth is that..."
- **עובדה היא ש...** / *uvdah hi she-...* / "the fact is that..."

### Notes
The complementizer *she-* introduces finite clauses expressing propositions. This construction is extremely common for epistemic evaluation. Some expressions use copula + *she-* (*ha-emet hi she-*), while others use the truth-term directly with *she-* (*nakhon she-*).

---

## 10. VERBAL COLLOCATIONS

### Pattern
**VERB + (et) + TRUTH-TERM-NOUN**

Truth-domain nouns commonly collocate with specific verbs to express truth-related actions. The accusative marker *et* appears with definite objects.

### Examples
- **לומר את האמת** / *lomar et ha-emet* / "to tell the truth"
- **לדבר אמת** / *ledaber emet* / "to speak truth"
- **לעשות טעות** / *la'asot ta'ut* / "to make a mistake"
- **לגלות את האמת** / *legalot et ha-emet* / "to reveal the truth"
- **להטיל ספק** / *lehatil safek* / "to cast doubt"
- **לתקן טעות** / *letaken ta'ut* / "to correct a mistake"

### Notes
Specific verbs collocate with truth-domain nouns: *lomar/ledaber* "say/speak" with *emet/sheker*; *la'asot* "make/do" with *ta'ut* "mistake"; *lehatil* "cast" with *safek* "doubt." These are semi-fixed collocations with some flexibility. The accusative marker *et* is required with definite direct objects.

## 11. INTENSIFICATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-TERM + me'od** (very)
**TRUTH-TERM + le-gamrei/le-khlutin** (completely)
**TRUTH-TERM + be-me'ah akhuz** (100%)

Intensification uses degree adverbs following the truth-term to emphasize strength of evaluation.

### Examples
- **נכון מאוד** / *nakhon me'od* / "very correct"
- **בטוח לגמרי** / *batuakh le-gamrei* / "completely sure"
- **צודק לחלוטין** / *tsodek le-khlutin* / "absolutely right"
- **אמיתי לגמרי** / *amiti le-gamrei* / "completely true/genuine"

### Notes
Standard intensifiers are *me'od* "very," *le-gamrei* "completely," and *le-khlutin* "absolutely." Percentage expressions (*be-me'ah akhuz* "100%") are also used for emphatic intensification. Intensifiers follow the truth-term directly.

---

## 12. COMPARISON

### Pattern
**TRUTH-TERM + yoter** (more)
**ha-khi + TRUTH-TERM** (most)
**TRUTH-TERM + mi-** (than)

Comparative and superlative constructions use the adverb *yoter* "more" for comparatives and the definite article + *khi* for superlatives. The preposition *mi-* "from/than" introduces the standard of comparison.

### Examples
- **נכון יותר** / *nakhon yoter* / "more correct"
- **מדויק יותר** / *meduyak yoter* / "more precise"
- **הכי נכון** / *ha-khi nakhon* / "most correct"
- **יותר אמין מ...** / *yoter amin mi-...* / "more reliable than..."

### Notes
Hebrew uses analytic comparison: *yoter* + adjective for comparative, *ha-khi* + adjective for superlative. The standard of comparison is introduced by *mi-* "from/than." This pattern applies productively to all truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 13. EXISTENTIAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**yesh/ein + TRUTH-TERM**

Existential constructions use *yesh* "there is" or *ein* "there is not" with truth-domain nouns to assert or deny existence or presence.

### Examples
- **יש ספק** / *yesh safek* / "there is doubt"
- **אין ספק** / *ein safek* / "there is no doubt"
- **יש אמת בדבריו** / *yesh emet be-dvarav* / "there is truth in his words"

### Notes
Existential *yesh/ein* constructions are particularly common with *safek* "doubt" to express certainty (*ein safek* "no doubt") or uncertainty (*yesh safek* "there is doubt"). The pattern can apply to any truth-domain noun but is most frequent with abstract terms.

---

## 14. PERSONAL EPISTEMIC CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**PRONOUN + TRUTH-TERM-ADJ**

Personal pronouns combine with truth-domain adjectives (especially epistemic terms) to express the subject's epistemic state or evaluation.

### Examples
- **אני בטוח** / *ani batuakh* / "I'm sure"
- **אתה צודק** / *atah tsodek* / "you're right"
- **אתה מוטעה** / *atah mut'eh* / "you're mistaken"
- **הוא צודק** / *hu tsodek* / "he's right"

### Notes
These constructions are extremely frequent in conversational Hebrew for expressing epistemic states (*ani batuakh* "I'm sure") and evaluating others' positions (*atah tsodek* "you're right," *atah mut'eh* "you're mistaken"). Present tense uses zero copula; past/future require overt copula forms.

---

## 15. DATIVE EXPERIENCER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**TRUTH-TERM + li/lekha/lo... + she-CLAUSE**

Some truth-domain terms (especially *domeh* "seems") take dative pronouns indicating the experiencer of the epistemic state, followed by a clausal complement.

### Examples
- **דומה לי ש...** / *domeh li she-...* / "it seems to me that..."
- **נראה לי ש...** / *nir'eh li she-...* / "it appears to me that..."

### Notes
The dative experiencer construction is particularly common with verbs/adjectives of appearance and seeming (*domeh*, *nir'eh*). The dative pronoun (*li* "to me," *lekha* "to you," etc.) indicates whose perspective is being expressed. This is a key hedging strategy in Hebrew discourse.
