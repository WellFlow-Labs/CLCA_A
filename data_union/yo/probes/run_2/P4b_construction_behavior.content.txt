# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Construction Usage Table

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness Rating | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** (stative predicate) | *ó tọ́* | ó tọ́ | It is correct / It is right / It is proper | [CORE] | Used across factual, moral, and administrative contexts; *tọ́* predicates directly without copula. Nominal predicates require *jẹ́* (*ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo*); the two sub-patterns are not interchangeable. |
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** (nominal predicate) | *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* | ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo | His/her certificate is genuine/valid | [CORE] | Standard copular construction with *jẹ́* for nominal predicates; productive in administrative and legal registers. Restricted to nominal complements; stative adjectives do not take *jẹ́* in this pattern. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** (stative) | *kò tọ́* | kò tọ́ | It is not correct / It is wrong / It is improper | [CORE] | *kò* is the standard negator for stative and verbal predicates; widely used across registers. Not interchangeable with *kì í ṣe*, which is restricted to nominal predicate negation. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** (nominal) | *kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* | kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi | It is not the real thing / It is not genuine | [CORE] | *kì í ṣe* is the negative copula for nominal predicates; used where *jẹ́* would appear in the affirmative. Restricted to nominal complements; cannot negate stative predicates. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | *ẹ̀rí ojú* | ẹ̀rí ojú | eyewitness evidence (lit. eye-evidence) | [CORE] | Head-initial order is the standard pattern in Yoruba nominal modification; no overt linker required in most cases. Adjectival predicates (*tọ́*, *rere*, *búburú*) function as postnominal modifiers in the same pattern (*ìdáhùn tọ́*). |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | *ìdáhùn tọ́* | ìdáhùn tọ́ | correct answer (lit. answer [that is] correct) | [CORE] | Productive with adjectival predicates in postnominal position; no overt linker attested in these examples. Modifier follows head directly. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** (epistemic) | *ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* | ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni | It became clear that it is the truth / It turned out to be true | [CORE] | Used for epistemic revelation or the emergence of clarity about a proposition; takes *pé* + complement clause. No dedicated inchoative morpheme; change-of-state expressed lexically. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** (change of state) | *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà* | ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà | His/her belief changed / His/her faith shifted | [CORE] | Serial verb *yí padà* (lit. turn-return) expresses change in state or orientation; productive with abstract nominal subjects. Restricted to change-of-state contexts; not used for epistemic revelation. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** (serial verb *fi...hàn*) | *ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* | ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀ | He/she presented evidence before the court | [CORE] | Serial verb construction with *fi* (use/take) + object + result verb; productive in legal and evidential contexts. No dedicated causative morpheme; causation expressed through serial verb structure. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** (serial verb *fi...gbà*) | *ó fi irọ́ gbà owó* | ó fi irọ́ gbà owó | He/she obtained money through deception | [CORE] | *fi* + instrument + *gbà* (obtain) expresses instrumental causation; attested in contexts of deception and fraud. Instrument slot takes nominal; result verb varies by outcome. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *ìgbàgbọ́* | ìgbàgbọ́ | belief, faith | [CORE] | *ì-* prefixation is the most productive nominalizer of verbal roots in this domain; forms are widely used across registers. Negated forms with *àì-* (*àìgbàgbọ́*, *àìmọ̀*) are also attested and productive. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *òtítọ́* | òtítọ́ | truth, correctness | [CORE] | *ò-* prefix produces abstract nominals with distinct semantic range from *ẹ̀-* forms derived from the same root (cf. *ẹ̀tọ́* — right/entitlement, both from *tọ́*). The two prefixes are not interchangeable for a given root. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** (*olù-*) | *olùgbàgbọ́* | olùgbàgbọ́ | believer, one who believes | [CORE] | *olù-* attaches to verbal roots to produce agent nouns; productive across the domain. Negative agentives formed with *aláì-* (*aláìgbàgbọ́*, *aláìmọ̀*) are also attested. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** (possessive relative contraction) | *ẹlẹ́rìí* | ẹlẹ́rìí | witness | [CORE] | Contracted from *ẹni tó ní ẹ̀rí* (one who has/bears evidence); produces agent nouns from nominal bases. This contraction strategy is distinct from *olù-* prefixation and applies to nominal rather than verbal bases. |
| **8. PROPOSITIONAL COMPLEMENT** | *ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni* | ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni | He/she believes that it is the truth | [CORE] | *pé* is the standard complementizer after epistemic, speech, and perception verbs; primary syntactic frame for propositional truth and belief. Productive across registers; no attested restriction on the class of propositional complement verbs in this domain. |
| **8. PROPOSITIONAL COMPLEMENT** | *ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀* | ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀ | He/she testified that he/she was there | [CORE] | Same *pé*-complement frame with speech/testimony verb; used in legal and evidential contexts. No variation in complementizer form attested across the examples provided. |
| **9. CLEFT / FOCUS** (*ni*) | *òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* | òtítọ́ ni ó sọ | It is the truth that he/she spoke | [CORE] | Focus particle *ni* places contrastive or identificational focus on the fronted constituent; highly productive in truth-domain assertion and contestation. The fused form *ló* (*ni* + *ó*) appears before a following subject pronoun, as in the second attested example. |
| **9. CLEFT / FOCUS** (*ló*) | *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* | ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn | It is character that makes a person | [CORE] | *ló* is the fused form of *ni* + *ó*; attested in proverbial statements asserting the primacy of a concept. Restricted to contexts where the focused constituent is followed by a third-person singular subject pronoun. |
| **10. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL** | *kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* | kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ | There is no truth in his/her words | [CORE] | *kò sí* is the standard negative existential; used to deny the presence of truth, evidence, or justice in a situation. Readily takes relative clause complements (*tó tó* — that is sufficient) and purpose clauses (*láti fi...*), as attested in the second example. |
| **10. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL** (with relative and purpose clause) | *kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi* | kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi | There is no sufficient evidence to convict him/her | [CORE] | Extended form of the negative existential with embedded relative (*tó tó*) and purpose clause (*láti fi dá a lẹ́bi*); attested in legal argumentation. Demonstrates the construction's productivity in complex syntactic environments. |

## Alternate Constructions

| Construction Type | Alternate Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | *jẹ́* + nominal (e.g., *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo*) | Attested as the nominal sub-pattern within Construction 1; not an alternate to the stative pattern but a distinct sub-type. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | *kì í ṣe* + nominal | Attested as the nominal negation sub-pattern; not interchangeable with *kò* + stative. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | *jẹ́ kí* + subjunctive | Attested in P4a for permissive causation (*Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́*); noted as distinct from the *fi*-serial verb causative in function (permissive vs. instrumental). |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | *àì-* + verbal root (e.g., *àìmọ̀*, *àìgbàgbọ́*) | Attested as the negated abstract nominal form; productive alongside the affirmative nominalization patterns. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *oní-* / *olú-* + nominal base (e.g., *olóirọ́*, *olòtítọ́*) | Attested in P4a as a third agentive strategy from nominal bases; distinct from both *olù-* and the possessive relative contraction. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | *aláì-* + root (e.g., *aláìgbàgbọ́*, *aláìmọ̀*) | Attested as the negative agentive form; productive alongside affirmative agentive patterns. |
| **9. CLEFT / FOCUS** | *ló* (fused *ni* + *ó*) | Attested as the phonologically fused form of the focus particle before third-person singular subject; not a separate construction but a conditioned variant of *ni*. |
