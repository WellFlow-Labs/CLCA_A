# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents how Indonesian encodes dynamic truth-related meanings — change into or out of truth-related states, causation of such states, and expressions of stability or persistence. All vocabulary is drawn exclusively from the CONTEXT (P1 through P5). Auxiliary verbs, aspect markers (*sudah*, *telah*, *sedang*, *belum*, *masih*), modal verbs (*bisa*, *dapat*, *harus*, *perlu*), and function words are added as needed to form natural expressions. Where a particular dynamic meaning has no common expression for a given term, this is stated explicitly. Terms are treated separately where their behavior differs.

The analysis is organized by dynamic meaning type, with subsections for each. Within each subsection, terms are treated individually where their behavior differs.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE ("X becomes [truth-related state]")

### 1.1 Becoming true / becoming correct

**Primary construction:** *menjadi* + *benar* / *kenyataan*

The inchoative verb *menjadi* (to become) is the standard strategy for expressing entry into a truth-related state. The most frequent collocation documented in the CONTEXT is *menjadi kenyataan* (to become reality), which is described in P4a as a fixed collocation.

> *Prediksi itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.*
> Prediksi itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.
> "That prediction eventually became reality."

> *Hal yang semula diragukan ternyata menjadi benar.*
> Hal yang semula diragukan ternyata menjadi benar.
> "What was initially doubted turned out to become true."

**Resultative construction:** *terbukti benar*

The *ter-* stative passive *terbukti* (proven, established through evidence) combines with *benar* to express that a truth-state has been reached through an evidential process. This construction is documented in P4a and P4b as a high-frequency resultative in legal and journalistic discourse.

> *Dugaan itu sudah terbukti benar.*
> Dugaan itu sudah terbukti benar.
> "That suspicion has already been proven true."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 Becoming valid / becoming legally recognized

**Primary construction:** *menjadi sah* / *disahkan*

Entry into a state of legal validity is expressed either through *menjadi sah* (to become valid) or, more commonly in formal and legal registers, through the passive *disahkan* (to be ratified/validated), which expresses the resultant state of having been officially made valid. The CONTEXT documents *disahkan* as the standard formula in legislative and administrative discourse.

> *Rancangan undang-undang itu akhirnya menjadi sah setelah disetujui.*
> Rancangan undang-undang itu akhirnya menjadi sah setelah disetujui.
> "That draft law finally became valid after being approved."

> *Pernikahan mereka baru menjadi sah setelah dicatatkan di kantor sipil.*
> Pernikahan mereka baru menjadi sah setelah dicatatkan di kantor sipil.
> "Their marriage only became valid after being registered at the civil registry office."

**Note:** The passive *disahkan* (ratified, made valid) is more frequent than *menjadi sah* in formal legal discourse. The CONTEXT documents *undang-undang itu telah disahkan oleh DPR* as the standard formula; *menjadi sah* is more common in informal and explanatory registers.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.3 Becoming authentic / becoming recognized as genuine

**Primary construction:** *terbukti asli* / *diakui keasliannya*

Entry into a recognized state of authenticity is expressed through the resultative *terbukti asli* (proven genuine) or the passive *diakui keasliannya* (whose authenticity is acknowledged). The CONTEXT documents both constructions in P2b.

> *Lukisan itu terbukti asli setelah melalui uji forensik.*
> Lukisan itu terbukti asli setelah melalui uji forensik.
> "That painting was proven genuine after undergoing forensic testing."

> *Keaslian dokumen itu akhirnya diakui oleh pihak berwenang.*
> Keaslian dokumen itu akhirnya diakui oleh pihak berwenang.
> "The authenticity of that document was finally acknowledged by the authorities."

**Note:** *Menjadi asli* (to become genuine) is not a natural expression in Indonesian, because authenticity is understood as a property that is either present or absent from the outset rather than something that comes into being. The CONTEXT does not document *menjadi asli* as an attested construction. The relevant dynamic expressions are resultative (*terbukti asli*) or recognition-of-existing-state (*diakui keasliannya*) rather than inchoative.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE] for *terbukti asli* and *diakui keasliannya*; no common expression for *menjadi asli*.

---

### 1.4 Becoming reality / becoming evident

**Primary construction:** *menjadi kenyataan* / *menjadi nyata*

*Menjadi kenyataan* is documented in P4a as a fixed collocation. *Menjadi nyata* (to become evident/real) is a productive variant.

> *Mimpi itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.*
> Mimpi itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.
> "That dream finally became reality."

> *Ancaman yang tadinya abstrak kini menjadi nyata.*
> Ancaman yang tadinya abstrak kini menjadi nyata.
> "The threat that was previously abstract has now become real/concrete."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.5 Becoming honest / becoming trustworthy

**Primary construction:** *menjadi jujur* / *menjadi terpercaya*

These inchoative constructions are grammatically well-formed but are less frequently attested as fixed collocations than the constructions above. They appear in moral and institutional discourse.

> *Ia mulai menjadi lebih jujur setelah kejadian itu.*
> Ia mulai menjadi lebih jujur setelah kejadian itu.
> "He began to become more honest after that incident."

> *Lembaga itu berusaha menjadi terpercaya kembali di mata publik.*
> Lembaga itu berusaha menjadi terpercaya kembali di mata publik.
> "That institution is trying to become trusted again in the eyes of the public."

**Note:** The CONTEXT documents *tidak lagi terpercaya di mata publik* (no longer trusted in the eyes of the public) in P2b, which implies a prior state of trustworthiness that has been lost. The inchoative *menjadi terpercaya kembali* (to become trusted again) is the natural expression for re-entry into that state.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH / OPPOSED STATE ("X becomes [non-truth / opposed state]")

### 2.1 Becoming false / becoming untrue

**Primary construction:** *terbukti palsu* / *terbukti tidak benar*

The CONTEXT documents *terbukti palsu* as a high-frequency resultative construction in legal and journalistic discourse (P2b, P4a). *Terbukti tidak benar* is the parallel construction for propositional falsity.

> *Klaim yang selama ini diyakini akhirnya terbukti palsu.*
> Klaim yang selama ini diyakini akhirnya terbukti palsu.
> "The claim that had long been believed was finally proven false."

> *Informasi yang beredar terbukti tidak benar.*
> Informasi yang beredar terbukti tidak benar.
> "The information that was circulating was proven not true."

**Note:** *Menjadi palsu* (to become fake) is not a natural expression for the same reason as *menjadi asli*: fakeness, like genuineness, is understood as a property of origin rather than something that comes into being through a process. The relevant dynamic expression is resultative (*terbukti palsu*) or revelation (*ternyata palsu*, as it turned out to be fake). The CONTEXT documents *ternyata itu semua palsu belaka* in P2b, where *ternyata* (it turns out) marks the revelation of a pre-existing state of falsity.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE] for *terbukti palsu*; no common expression for *menjadi palsu*.

---

### 2.2 Becoming invalid / losing legal validity

**Primary construction:** *dinyatakan tidak sah* / *menjadi tidak sah*

The CONTEXT documents *dinyatakan tidak sah* (declared invalid) as a standard legal formula in P2b and P5. *Menjadi tidak sah* expresses the inchoative entry into invalidity.

> *Kontrak itu menjadi tidak sah setelah salah satu pihak menarik diri.*
> Kontrak itu menjadi tidak sah setelah salah satu pihak menarik diri.
> "That contract became invalid after one of the parties withdrew."

> *Pernikahan itu dinyatakan tidak sah oleh pengadilan.*
> Pernikahan itu dinyatakan tidak sah oleh pengadilan.
> "That marriage was declared invalid by the court."

**Note:** The CONTEXT documents the fixed legal formula *tidak sah demi hukum* (null and void under the law) in P2b, which expresses a state of invalidity established by operation of law rather than through a process of becoming. This is a stative rather than inchoative expression.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Becoming mistaken / going astray

**Primary construction:** *tersesat* / *menjadi keliru*

The *ter-* stative *tersesat* (to be lost, to have gone astray) is documented in P2a as expressing the state of having been led astray, whether literally or figuratively. *Menjadi keliru* expresses entry into a state of error.

> *Ia tersesat dalam pemikiran yang salah.*
> Ia tersesat dalam pemikiran yang salah.
> "He went astray in wrong thinking."

> *Penilaian awal kami menjadi keliru setelah fakta baru terungkap.*
> Penilaian awal kami menjadi keliru setelah fakta baru terungkap.
> "Our initial assessment became mistaken after new facts came to light."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE] for *tersesat*; [CORE] for *menjadi keliru*.

---

### 2.4 Losing credibility / becoming untrustworthy

**Primary construction:** *tidak lagi terpercaya* / *kehilangan kredibilitas*

The CONTEXT documents *tidak lagi terpercaya di mata publik* (no longer trusted in the eyes of the public) in P2b as a trust-loss construction. *Kehilangan kredibilitas* (losing credibility) is the parallel construction built on *kredibilitas*.

> *Lembaga itu tidak lagi terpercaya setelah skandal terungkap.*
> Lembaga itu tidak lagi terpercaya setelah skandal terungkap.
> "That institution is no longer trusted after the scandal came to light."

> *Ia kehilangan kredibilitasnya di hadapan publik.*
> Ia kehilangan kredibilitasnya di hadapan publik.
> "He lost his credibility before the public."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.5 Becoming dishonest / becoming a liar

**Primary construction:** *menjadi pembohong* / *mulai berbohong*

These constructions express entry into a state of dishonesty or the initiation of lying behavior.

> *Ia yang dulu jujur kini menjadi pembohong.*
> Ia yang dulu jujur kini menjadi pembohong.
> "He who was once honest has now become a liar."

> *Tekanan itu membuatnya mulai berbohong.*
> Tekanan itu membuatnya mulai berbohong.
> "That pressure caused him to start lying."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## 3. CAUSATION OF TRUTH-RELATED STATES ("Y causes X to be [truth-related state]")

### 3.1 Causing something to be confirmed / validated

**Primary construction:** *membenarkan* / *mengesahkan* / *memvalidasi*

The CONTEXT documents *membenarkan* (to confirm, to validate, from *benar*), *mengesahkan* (to ratify, to validate, from *sah*), and *memvalidasi* (to validate, from *valid*) as causative verbs in P2a and P4a. Each operates in a distinct domain.

> *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan tersebut.*
> Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan tersebut.
> "The authorities confirmed that report."

> *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru itu.*
> DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru itu.
> "The parliament ratified that new law."

> *Tim peneliti memvalidasi data sebelum dipublikasikan.*
> Tim peneliti memvalidasi data sebelum dipublikasikan.
> "The research team validated the data before it was published."

**Note on domain distribution:** *Membenarkan* operates in the domain of propositional confirmation and moral justification; *mengesahkan* operates in the domain of legal and administrative ratification; *memvalidasi* operates in academic and technical domains. The CONTEXT documents these as distinct causative forms with non-overlapping primary domains, though *membenarkan* and *mengesahkan* have some overlap in formal contexts.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Causing something to be falsified / forged

**Primary construction:** *memalsukan*

The CONTEXT documents *memalsukan* (to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit, from *palsu*) as the primary causative verb for the creation of fraudulent imitations. It is described in P2a as a standard term in Indonesian criminal law.

> *Tersangka diduga memalsukan dokumen resmi.*
> Tersangka diduga memalsukan dokumen resmi.
> "The suspect is alleged to have falsified official documents."

> *Ia memalsukan tanda tangan direktur untuk mencairkan dana.*
> Ia memalsukan tanda tangan direktur untuk mencairkan dana.
> "He forged the director's signature to withdraw funds."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Causing someone to be misled / led astray

**Primary construction:** *menyesatkan* / *mengelirukan* / *membohongi* / *disesatkan*

The CONTEXT documents multiple causative constructions for causing someone to hold a false belief or to go astray. These differ in the type of misleading involved.

> *Informasi yang salah itu menyesatkan publik.*
> Informasi yang salah itu menyesatkan publik.
> "That incorrect information misled the public."

> *Pernyataan ambigu itu mengelirukan para pembaca.*
> Pernyataan ambigu itu mengelirukan para pembaca.
> "That ambiguous statement confused/misled the readers."

> *Ia membohongi rekan-rekannya selama bertahun-tahun.*
> Ia membohongi rekan-rekannya selama bertahun-tahun.
> "He lied to his colleagues for years."

> *Mereka disesatkan oleh propaganda yang sistematis.*
> Mereka disesatkan oleh propaganda yang sistematis.
> "They were led astray by systematic propaganda."

**Note on distinctions:** The CONTEXT documents a distinction between *menyesatkan* (which does not require intent) and *sengaja menyesatkan* (which marks intentional misleading). *Mengelirukan* focuses on causing confusion or error in understanding. *Membohongi* is specifically directed at a person (the *-i* suffix marks the deceived party as object). *Disesatkan* is the passive, expressing the state of having been led astray.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Causing something to be manipulated / distorted

**Primary construction:** *memanipulasi* / *mendistorsi*

The CONTEXT documents *memanipulasi* (to manipulate, from *manipulasi*) and *mendistorsi* (to distort, from *distorsi*) as causative verbs for the deliberate distortion of information or data.

> *Pihak tertentu diduga memanipulasi data pemilu.*
> Pihak tertentu diduga memanipulasi data pemilu.
> "Certain parties are alleged to have manipulated election data."

> *Laporan itu mendistorsi fakta yang sebenarnya terjadi.*
> Laporan itu mendistorsi fakta yang sebenarnya terjadi.
> "That report distorted the facts of what actually happened."

**Note:** The CONTEXT distinguishes *manipulasi* and *distorsi* in P1 and P3: *manipulasi* focuses on deliberate distortion of facts or situations to produce a false impression, while *distorsi* focuses more specifically on the warping of an original form or content. Both are documented as causative in P2a.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Causing someone to be blamed / held at fault

**Primary construction:** *menyalahkan*

The CONTEXT documents *menyalahkan* (to blame, to fault, to hold responsible, from *salah*) as a causative verb in P2a and P2b.

> *Ia selalu menyalahkan orang lain atas kegagalannya.*
> Ia selalu menyalahkan orang lain atas kegagalannya.
> "He always blames others for his failures."

> *Jangan menyalahkan diri sendiri atas hal yang bukan kesalahanmu.*
> Jangan menyalahkan diri sendiri atas hal yang bukan kesalahanmu.
> "Don't blame yourself for something that is not your fault."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.6 Causing something to be declared / stated

**Primary construction:** *menyatakan*

The CONTEXT documents *menyatakan* (to state, to declare, to announce, from *nyata*) as a causative verb in P2a. In legal contexts, *menyatakan* + [status] is the standard formula for official declarations of truth-related states.

> *Hakim menyatakan terdakwa bersalah.*
> Hakim menyatakan terdakwa bersalah.
> "The judge declared the defendant guilty."

> *Pemerintah menyatakan informasi itu tidak benar.*
> Pemerintah menyatakan informasi itu tidak benar.
> "The government declared that information to be untrue."

> *Komisi pemilu menyatakan hasil pemungutan suara sah.*
> Komisi pemilu menyatakan hasil pemungutan suara sah.
> "The election commission declared the vote results valid."

**Note:** *Menyatakan* is the primary verb for official declarations of truth-related states in legal and administrative discourse. The CONTEXT documents the passive *dinyatakan* + [status] (*dinyatakan bersalah*, *dinyatakan sah*, *dinyatakan tidak sah*) as fixed legal formulas in P2b and P4b.

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.7 Causing misuse / abuse

**Primary construction:** *menyalahgunakan*

The CONTEXT documents *menyalahgunakan* (to misuse, to abuse, to misappropriate, from *salah* + *guna*) in P2a as a causative verb for the improper use of something.

> *Ia terbukti menyalahgunakan wewenangnya sebagai pejabat.*
> Ia terbukti menyalahgunakan wewenangnya sebagai pejabat.
> "He was proven to have abused his authority as an official."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## 4. STABILITY AND PERSISTENCE OF TRUTH-RELATED STATES

### 4.1 Remaining true / persisting as correct

**Primary construction:** *masih benar* / *tetap benar*

The aspect marker *masih* (still) and the continuative *tetap* (remain, continue to be) express the persistence of a truth-related state.

> *Prinsip itu masih benar hingga hari ini.*
> Prinsip itu masih benar hingga hari ini.
> "That principle is still true to this day."

> *Fakta itu tetap benar meskipun banyak yang meragukannya.*
> Fakta itu tetap benar meskipun banyak yang meragukannya.
> "That fact remains true even though many doubt it."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.2 Remaining valid / continuing to be legally recognized

**Primary construction:** *masih sah* / *tetap sah*

> *Perjanjian itu masih sah dan mengikat kedua belah pihak.*
> Perjanjian itu masih sah dan mengikat kedua belah pihak.
> "That agreement is still valid and binding on both parties."

> *Izin usahanya tetap sah selama belum dicabut.*
> Izin usahanya tetap sah selama belum dicabut.
> "His business license remains valid as long as it has not been revoked."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Remaining trustworthy / continuing to be credible

**Primary construction:** *masih terpercaya* / *masih kredibel*

The CONTEXT documents *meragukan apakah lembaga itu masih kredibel* (doubting whether that institution is still credible) in P2b, which presupposes a prior state of credibility and questions its persistence.

> *Lembaga itu masih terpercaya di mata masyarakat.*
> Lembaga itu masih terpercaya di mata masyarakat.
> "That institution is still trusted in the eyes of the public."

> *Apakah sumber itu masih kredibel setelah kontroversi ini?*
> Apakah sumber itu masih kredibel setelah kontroversi ini?
> "Is that source still credible after this controversy?"

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.4 Persisting in error / remaining mistaken

**Primary construction:** *tetap keliru* / *masih salah*

> *Pandangan itu tetap keliru meskipun sudah sering dikoreksi.*
> Pandangan itu tetap keliru meskipun sudah sering dikoreksi.
> "That view remains mistaken even though it has been corrected many times."

> *Data yang digunakan masih salah dan belum diperbaiki.*
> Data yang digunakan masih salah dan belum diperbaiki.
> "The data being used is still wrong and has not yet been corrected."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.5 Persisting in dishonesty

**Primary construction:** *terus berbohong* / *masih tidak jujur*

> *Ia terus berbohong meskipun bukti sudah menumpuk.*
> Ia terus berbohong meskipun bukti sudah menumpuk.
> "He keeps lying even though the evidence has piled up."

> *Laporan keuangan itu masih tidak jujur menurut auditor.*
> Laporan keuangan itu masih tidak jujur menurut auditor.
> "That financial report is still dishonest according to the auditors."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## 5. REVERSAL AND CORRECTION OF TRUTH-RELATED STATES

### 5.1 Correcting an error / restoring correctness

**Primary construction:** *membenarkan* (in the sense of correcting) / *mengoreksi kesalahan*

The CONTEXT documents *membenarkan* as carrying both a confirmation reading and a correction reading (P1b, P2a). In the correction reading, it means to set something right that was previously wrong.

> *Ia membenarkan pernyataannya yang keliru sebelumnya.*
> Ia membenarkan pernyataannya yang keliru sebelumnya.
> "He corrected his previously mistaken statement."

> *Redaksi mengoreksi kesalahan yang muncul dalam edisi sebelumnya.*
> Redaksi mengoreksi kesalahan yang muncul dalam edisi sebelumnya.
> "The editorial team corrected the errors that appeared in the previous edition."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.2 Exposing a lie / revealing falsity

**Primary construction:** *mengungkap kebohongan* / *membongkar kepalsuan*

The CONTEXT documents *mengungkap kebohongan* (to expose a lie) in P2a as a common collocation. *Membongkar kepalsuan* (to uncover/expose falseness) is a parallel construction built on *kepalsuan*.

> *Investigasi itu berhasil mengungkap kebohongan yang telah berlangsung lama.*
> Investigasi itu berhasil mengungkap kebohongan yang telah berlangsung lama.
> "That investigation succeeded in exposing the lie that had been going on for a long time."

> *Jurnalis itu membongkar kepalsuan di balik klaim perusahaan tersebut.*
> Jurnalis itu membongkar kepalsuan di balik klaim perusahaan tersebut.
> "That journalist exposed the falseness behind the company's claims."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.3 Revoking validity / rendering invalid

**Primary construction:** *mencabut* + [valid document/status] / *membatalkan* + [decision/act]

The CONTEXT documents *tidak sah demi hukum* (null and void under the law) and *belum dicabut* (not yet revoked) as expressions presupposing the possibility of revocation. *Mencabut* (to revoke, to withdraw) and *membatalkan* (to cancel, to annul) are the standard verbs for rendering a previously valid state invalid.

> *Izin itu dicabut karena terbukti diperoleh dengan cara yang tidak sah.*
> Izin itu dicabut karena terbukti diperoleh dengan cara yang tidak sah.
> "That license was revoked because it was proven to have been obtained by unlawful means."

> *Pengadilan membatalkan keputusan yang dianggap tidak sah.*
> Pengadilan membatalkan keputusan yang dianggap tidak sah.
> "The court annulled the decision that was considered invalid."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.4 Rehabilitating credibility / restoring trust

**Primary construction:** *membangun kembali kredibilitas* / *memulihkan kepercayaan*

The CONTEXT documents *membangun kredibilitas* (to build credibility) in P2b. The addition of *kembali* (again, back) expresses restoration of a previously held state.

> *Lembaga itu berupaya membangun kembali kredibilitasnya.*
> Lembaga itu berupaya membangun kembali kredibilitasnya.
> "That institution is working to rebuild its credibility."

> *Kepercayaan publik yang hilang sulit untuk dipulihkan.*
> Kepercayaan publik yang hilang sulit untuk dipulihkan.
> "Public trust that has been lost is difficult to restore."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.5 Acknowledging error / admitting fault

**Primary construction:** *mengakui kesalahan* / *mengakui kekeliruan*

The CONTEXT documents *mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake/fault) in P1b and P2b as a common collocation across interpersonal, legal, and moral contexts.

> *Ia akhirnya mengakui kesalahannya di depan publik.*
> Ia akhirnya mengakui kesalahannya di depan publik.
> "He finally acknowledged his mistake before the public."

> *Mengakui kekeliruan adalah tanda kedewasaan.*
> Mengakui kekeliruan adalah tanda kedewasaan.
> "Acknowledging one's error is a sign of maturity."

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 6. SUMMARY OF CONSTRUCTION BEHAVIOR BY TERM

The following table summarizes which dynamic construction types are attested for each major truth-domain term in the CONTEXT. Gaps are preserved.

| Term | Becoming (inchoative) | Causing (causative) | Stability (persistence) | Reversal/correction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **benar** | *menjadi benar*, *terbukti benar* | *membenarkan* | *masih benar*, *tetap benar* | *membenarkan* (correction reading) |
| **salah** | *menjadi salah* | *menyalahkan* | *masih salah* | *mengakui kesalahan* |
| **asli** | *terbukti asli*, *diakui keasliannya* (not *menjadi asli*) | *mengotentikasi* | *masih asli* | no common reversal expression |
| **palsu** | *terbukti palsu*, *ternyata palsu* (not *menjadi palsu*) | *memalsukan* | *masih palsu* | *mengungkap kepalsuan* |
| **nyata** | *menjadi nyata*, *menjadi kenyataan* | *menyatakan* | *masih nyata* | no common reversal expression |
| **sah** | *menjadi sah*, *disahkan* | *mengesahkan* | *masih sah*, *tetap sah* | *mencabut*, *membatalkan* |
| **absah** | *menjadi absah*, *mengabsahkan* | *mengabsahkan* | *masih absah* | no common reversal expression distinct from *sah* |

## 7. STRUCTURAL OBSERVATIONS

The following observations are drawn from the patterns documented above. They are reported without interpretation beyond what the data supports.

**7.1 Asymmetry between *asli*/*palsu* and inchoative constructions**

The CONTEXT does not document *menjadi asli* or *menjadi palsu* as natural expressions. Authenticity and fakeness are treated in the data as properties of origin rather than as states that come into being through a process. The relevant dynamic expressions for these terms are resultative (*terbukti asli*, *terbukti palsu*) or revelation-of-pre-existing-state (*ternyata palsu*) rather than inchoative. This contrasts with *benar*, *sah*, *nyata*, and *jujur*, which all participate naturally in *menjadi* + adjective constructions.

**7.2 The *terbukti* construction as a distinct resultative type**

The CONTEXT documents *terbukti* + truth-domain adjective (*terbukti benar*, *terbukti palsu*, *terbukti asli*) as a distinct construction type that expresses the reaching of a truth-domain state through an evidential process. This construction is documented in P4a and P4b as a high-frequency pattern in legal and journalistic discourse. It differs from *menjadi* + adjective in that it implies an evidential basis for the state rather than a simple change of state.

**7.3 Causative gaps**

Several truth-domain terms lack attested causative forms in the CONTEXT: *jujur*, *terpercaya*, *kredibel*, *tepat*, *akurat*, *sahih*. The concept of causing someone to be honest, trustworthy, or accurate is expressed periphrastically in the data (e.g., *mendorong kejujuran*, encouraging honesty) rather than through a single causative verb. This contrasts with the productive causative forms documented for *benar* (*membenarkan*), *sah* (*mengesahkan*), *palsu* (*memalsukan*), *keliru* (*mengelirukan*), *sesat* (*menyesatkan*), and *manipulasi* (*memanipulasi*).

**7.4 The *menyatakan* construction as a declaration type**

The CONTEXT documents *menyatakan* + [truth-domain state] as the primary verb for official declarations that bring a truth-related state into being through a performative speech act (*menyatakan bersalah*, *menyatakan sah*, *menyatakan tidak benar*). This construction is distinct from the causative constructions in §3 in that the declaration itself constitutes the state rather than causing it through a separate process. The passive *dinyatakan* + [state] is the standard formula in legal and administrative discourse.

**7.5 Reversal constructions are concentrated in the error and deception domains**

The CONTEXT documents the richest inventory of reversal constructions for terms in the error and deception domains: *mengakui kesalahan* (admitting error), *mengakui kekeliruan* (acknowledging misconception), *mengungkap kebohongan* (exposing a lie), *membongkar kepalsuan* (uncovering falseness), *membenarkan* in its correction reading (correcting a mistake). Reversal constructions for the validity domain (*mencabut*, *membatalkan*) are also attested. Reversal constructions for the authenticity domain (*asli*) are not documented in the CONTEXT.
