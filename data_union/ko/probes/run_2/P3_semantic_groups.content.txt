# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Korean (ko)

---

**Transliteration standard:** Revised Romanization of Korean (국립국어원 표준 로마자 표기법), applied consistently throughout.

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms co-occur, which are contrasted with each other, which appear in the same institutional or discourse contexts, and which share characteristic verb pairings or collocational partners. No predefined semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as found.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that a statement corresponds to what actually occurred

**Label:** Factual-status assertion terms

**Definition:** These terms are used primarily to evaluate whether a specific claim, report, or statement matches what actually happened or is the case. They appear characteristically in constructions that affirm or deny factual correspondence — 사실이 아니다, 사실과 다르다, 사실로 밝혀지다, 허위로 밝혀지다 — and in legal and journalistic contexts where the primary question is whether a claim is factually grounded. They are contrasted with each other (사실 vs. 허위사실) and with opinion or interpretation.

**Representative expressions:**
- 사실 (sasil) — "fact / in fact"
- 사실이 아니다 (sasiri anida) — "it is not a fact / that is not true"
- 사실과 다르다 (sasil-gwa dareuda) — "to differ from the facts"
- 사실로 밝혀지다 (sasillo balkyeojida) — "to turn out to be true / to be revealed as fact"
- 사실 여부를 따지다 (sasil yeobureul ttajida) — "to examine whether something is factual"
- 엄연한 사실 (eomyeonhan sasil) — "an undeniable fact"
- 허위 (heowi) — used in formal and legal registers to characterize statements as false
- 허위사실 (heowi sasil) — "false fact / fabricated claim presented as fact"
- 허위로 밝혀지다 (heowiro balkyeojida) — "to be revealed as false"
- 허위사실 유포 (heowi sasil yupo) — "spreading false information"

**Notes:** 사실 and 허위 are the primary poles of this group. 사실 also functions as a sentence-level adverb ("in fact / actually"), which is a pragmatic extension rather than a separate grouping. 허위 appears almost exclusively in formal, legal, and journalistic registers; 사실 appears across all registers. 거짓 (geojit) and 거짓말 (geojitmal) overlap with this group but are treated separately below because their characteristic usage patterns differ (see Group 2).

---

## Group 2: Terms used in moral evaluation of deliberate deception in speech

**Label:** Lying and deception evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate speech acts as involving deliberate deception — the intentional production of false statements to mislead. They appear characteristically in everyday moral discourse, in interpersonal contexts, and in constructions that name the act of lying (거짓말하다), the person who lies (거짓말쟁이), and the exposure of deception (거짓이 드러나다, 거짓말이 들키다). The moral charge is more prominent here than in Group 1; these terms evaluate conduct, not just factual status.

**Representative expressions:**
- 거짓 (geojit) — "falsehood / untruth"
- 거짓말 (geojitmal) — "lie / false speech"
- 거짓말하다 (geojitmal-hada) — "to lie"
- 거짓말쟁이 (geojitmaljaengi) — "liar / habitual liar"
- 새빨간 거짓말 (saepalgan geojitmal) — "outright lie / barefaced lie"
- 거짓말이 들키다 (geojitmari deulkida) — "to be caught in a lie"
- 거짓이 드러나다 (geojisi deureonada) — "the falsehood is exposed"
- 거짓말로 일관하다 (geojitmallo ilgwan-hada) — "to maintain a consistent lie throughout"
- 거짓말인 줄 알면서도 (geojitmalin jul almyeonseodo) — "even knowing it is a lie"
- 거짓말도 방편이다 (geojitmalto bangpyeonida) — proverbial: "even a lie can be a means"
- 거짓 없이 (geojit eopsi) — "without falsehood / honestly"

**Notes:** 거짓말 is the most frequent and everyday term in this group; 거짓 is slightly more formal and also appears in philosophical and analytical contexts (거짓과 진실을 구별하다). The proverbial expression 거짓말도 방편이다 is notable as the only item in the P1/P2 data that frames deception as potentially justified, which distinguishes it from the otherwise uniformly negative moral evaluation in this group. 허위 (Group 1) overlaps here in legal contexts (허위진술), but its primary register is formal-legal rather than everyday moral.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate whether something is the genuine article as opposed to a copy or fake

**Label:** Authenticity-of-objects terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, credential, or item is the original genuine article or a copy, imitation, or counterfeit. They appear characteristically in consumer, legal, art-authentication, and customs contexts. The primary contrast is between 진품 (genuine article) and 가품/모조품/위조품 (fake/imitation/forged goods), and the characteristic verbal constructions involve determining, exposing, or passing off authenticity (진위를 가리다, 가짜를 진짜로 속이다, 진품과 모조품을 구별하다).

**Representative expressions:**
- 진짜 (jinjja) — "real / genuine"
- 가짜 (gajja) — "fake / counterfeit"
- 진품 (jinpum) — "genuine article / authentic item"
- 가품 (gapum) — "fake goods"
- 모조 (mojo) — "imitation / replica"
- 모조품 (mojopum) — "imitation goods"
- 위조 (wijo) — "forgery / counterfeiting"
- 위조품 (wijopum) — "counterfeit goods"
- 위조지폐 (wijo jipye) — "counterfeit banknote"
- 위조문서 (wijo munseo) — "forged document"
- 진위 (jinwi) — "authenticity vs. falsity"
- 진위를 가리다 (jinwireul garida) — "to determine authenticity"
- 진위 여부 (jinwi yeobu) — "the question of whether genuine or false"
- 진짜배기 (jinjjabaegi) — "the real thing / the genuine article"
- 가짜로 판명되다 (gajjaro panmyeongdoeda) — "to be determined to be fake"
- 가짜를 진짜로 속이다 (gajjareul jinjjaro sogida) — "to pass off a fake as genuine"
- 진품과 모조품을 구별하다 (jinpum-gwa mojopumeul gubyeol-hada) — "to distinguish genuine articles from imitations"
- 위조 여부를 감정하다 (wijo yeobureul gamjeong-hada) — "to conduct forensic examination for forgery"

**Notes:** 위조 (forgery) and 모조 (imitation) are distinguished in usage: 위조 carries a stronger implication of criminal intent and appears almost exclusively in legal and criminal contexts, while 모조 can describe authorized replicas or imitations without necessarily implying fraud. 진짜/가짜 are the most colloquial pair; 진품/가품 are more formal and domain-specific to commerce and art. 진위 is the most abstract term in this group, framing the question of authenticity without specifying the outcome.

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate whether a person's expressed feelings or commitments are genuine

**Label:** Sincerity and inner-genuineness evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a person's expressed feelings, intentions, apologies, or commitments reflect their actual inner state, as opposed to being performed, strategic, or hollow. They appear characteristically in interpersonal, public, and critical discourse, and their characteristic constructions involve sensing, doubting, or demonstrating genuineness (진정성이 느껴지다, 진정성을 의심하다, 진정성을 보여주다, 진심으로). The primary contrast is between genuine inner feeling and outward performance.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진정 (jinjeong) — "genuine / sincere / truly"
- 진정성 (jinjeongseong) — "authenticity / genuineness / sincerity"
- 진정한 (jinjeonghan) — "true / genuine" (attributive)
- 진정으로 (jinjeong-euro) — "truly / genuinely / sincerely"
- 진정으로 사과하다 (jinjeong-euro sagwa-hada) — "to apologize sincerely"
- 진정한 의미에서 (jinjeonghan uimieseo) — "in the true sense of the word"
- 진정성이 있다 / 없다 (jinjeongseong-i itda / eopda) — "to have / lack sincerity"
- 진정성이 느껴지다 (jinjeongseong-i neukkkyeojida) — "sincerity is felt"
- 진정성을 의심하다 (jinjeongseong-eul uisim-hada) — "to doubt the sincerity of"
- 진정성에 의문을 제기하다 (jinjeongseong-e uimun-eul jegi-hada) — "to raise doubts about sincerity"
- 진정성을 보여주다 (jinjeongseong-eul boyeojuda) — "to demonstrate sincerity"
- 진심 (jinsim) — "sincere feeling / genuine heart"
- 진심으로 (jinsimeuro) — "sincerely / from the heart"

**Notes:** 진정성 is the most prominent term in contemporary public discourse for this evaluative function; it is frequently applied to public figures, apologies, and artistic works. 진심 and 진심으로 are more interpersonal and emotional in register, appearing in expressions of gratitude, apology, and affection. 진정하다 and 진정한 are the adjectival/predicative forms. This group is distinct from Group 3 (authenticity of objects) in that the subject of evaluation is a person's inner state rather than the physical genuineness of an item, though both groups share the root 진 (眞).

## Group 5: Terms used in investigations and formal inquiries into what actually happened

**Label:** Truth-investigation and fact-finding terms

**Definition:** These terms appear characteristically in contexts of formal or institutional investigation into the actual facts of a past event — legal proceedings, government commissions, journalistic inquiries, and historical investigations. Their characteristic constructions involve revealing, clarifying, or investigating what actually occurred (진실을 밝히다, 진상을 밝히다, 진상조사, 진실규명). The primary concern is uncovering what was hidden or disputed, rather than evaluating ongoing conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진실 (jinsil) — "truth / truthfulness"
- 진실을 밝히다 (jinshireul balkida) — "to reveal the truth"
- 진실이 밝혀지다 (jinsili balkyeojida) — "the truth comes to light"
- 진실을 숨기다 (jinshireul sumgida) — "to hide the truth"
- 진실을 외면하다 (jinshireul oemyeonhada) — "to turn away from the truth"
- 진실규명 (jinsil gyumyeong) — "truth investigation / clarification of the truth"
- 진실화해위원회 (jinsil hwahae wiwonhoe) — "Truth and Reconciliation Commission"
- 진상 (jinsang) — "the true state of affairs / the real facts"
- 진상조사 (jinsang josa) — "fact-finding investigation"
- 실체를 밝히다 (silchereul balkida) — "to reveal the true nature of something"
- 실체를 드러내다 (silchereul deureoneda) — "to expose what something really is"
- 실태 (siltae) — "actual state / real condition"
- 실태조사 (siltae josa) — "survey of actual conditions"
- 사실확인 (sasil hwagin) — "fact-checking / verification of facts"
- 사실을 확인하다 (sasireul hwagin-hada) — "to verify the facts"

**Notes:** 진실 and 진상 are closely related in this group but differ in typical scale and register: 진실 appears in both everyday and institutional contexts, while 진상 appears almost exclusively in formal investigative contexts (진상조사, 진상을 밝히다). 실체 and 실태 overlap here but extend into other contexts (실체 into philosophical discourse, 실태 into policy and administrative surveys). The verb 밝히다 (to illuminate/reveal) is the characteristic verb across this group, appearing with 진실, 진상, and 실체.

## Group 6: Terms used to evaluate the accuracy or precision of statements, measurements, and information

**Label:** Accuracy and precision evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, figure, or piece of information corresponds correctly and precisely to its referent. They appear characteristically in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve asserting, measuring, or providing accurate information (정확히 말하자면, 정확한 정보를 제공하다, 정확도가 높다/낮다). The primary concern is correctness of correspondence, not moral evaluation of the speaker.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정확 (jeonghwak) — "accuracy / precision / exactness"
- 정확하다 (jeonghwak-hada) — "to be accurate / precise / exact"
- 정확한 (jeonghwakan) — "accurate / precise / exact" (attributive)
- 정확히 (jeonghwaki) — "accurately / exactly / precisely"
- 정확성 (jeonghwakseong) — "accuracy / precision"
- 정확도 (jeonghwakdo) — "degree of accuracy / accuracy rate"
- 정확도가 높다 / 낮다 (jeonghwakdoga nopda / natda) — "accuracy is high / low"
- 부정확 (bujeonghwak) — "inaccuracy / imprecision"
- 부정확하다 (bujeonghwak-hada) — "to be inaccurate"
- 정확히 말하자면 (jeonghwaki malhajamyeon) — "to be precise / strictly speaking"
- 정확한 정보를 제공하다 (jeonghwakan jeongboreul jegong-hada) — "to provide accurate information"
- 정확하게 짚어내다 (jeonghwakage jjipeoneda) — "to pinpoint accurately"

**Notes:** 정확 is the primary term in this group and is notably more technical and measurement-oriented than 진실 (Group 5) or 정직 (Group 7). The suffix 도 (度, degree) in 정확도 is characteristic of this group's quantitative orientation. 사실확인 (Group 5) overlaps here in journalistic fact-checking contexts.

---

## Group 7: Terms used in moral evaluation of a person's honesty as a character trait

**Label:** Personal honesty evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate a person's character or conduct as honest or dishonest — as a stable trait rather than a single act. They appear characteristically in moral, educational, and professional contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve describing how a person speaks or lives (정직하게 말하다, 정직하게 살다) and evaluating honesty as a quality (정직성, 정직이 최선의 방책이다). The primary concern is the person's disposition toward truthfulness in their dealings with others.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정직 (jeongjik) — "honesty / integrity"
- 정직하다 (jeongjik-hada) — "to be honest"
- 정직한 (jeongjikan) — "honest / straightforward" (attributive)
- 정직하게 (jeongjik-hage) — "honestly / straightforwardly"
- 정직성 (jeongjikseong) — "honesty as a quality / integrity"
- 부정직 (bujeongjik) — "dishonesty"
- 부정직하다 (bujeongjik-hada) — "to be dishonest"
- 정직하게 말하다 (jeongjik-hage malhada) — "to speak honestly"
- 정직하게 살다 (jeongjik-hage salda) — "to live honestly"
- 정직이 최선의 방책이다 (jeongjigi choesonui bangchaegida) — "honesty is the best policy"
- 정직함을 잃다 (jeongjikameul ilta) — "to lose one's honesty"
- 올바르다 (olbareuda) — "to be right / correct / proper / upright"
- 올바른 (olbareun) — "right / correct / proper" (attributive)
- 올바르게 (olbareuge) — "rightly / correctly / properly"

**Notes:** 정직 is the primary term for honesty as a personal virtue. 올바르다 overlaps here but extends more broadly into moral and civic conduct evaluation (올바른 길을 가다, 올바른 판단을 내리다) and is not limited to honesty specifically. The proverbial 정직이 최선의 방책이다 is the only item in this group that frames honesty in explicitly pragmatic terms.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate whether a process, judgment, or treatment is free from bias

**Label:** Impartiality and fairness evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a process, judgment, competition, or treatment is conducted without bias or unfair advantage. They appear characteristically in legal, institutional, and media contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve guaranteeing, doubting, or questioning impartiality (공정하게 처리하다, 공정성을 의심하다, 공정성 시비가 일다). The primary concern is procedural evenhandedness rather than factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- 공정 (gongjeon) — "fairness / impartiality"
- 공정하다 (gongjeon-hada) — "to be fair / impartial"
- 공정한 (gongjeonhan) — "fair / impartial" (attributive)
- 공정하게 (gongjeon-hage) — "fairly / impartially"
- 공정성 (gongjeonseong) — "fairness / impartiality"
- 불공정 (bulgongjeon) — "unfairness"
- 공정하게 처리하다 (gongjeon-hage cheorichada) — "to handle fairly"
- 공정성을 의심하다 (gongjeonseong-eul uisim-hada) — "to doubt the fairness of"
- 공정한 경쟁을 보장하다 (gongjeonhan gyeongjaengeul bojang-hada) — "to guarantee fair competition"
- 공정성 시비가 일다 (gongjeonseong sibi-ga ilda) — "controversy over fairness arises"
- 공정거래 (gongjeon georae) — "fair trade / fair dealing"
- 공정거래위원회 (gongjeon georae wiwonhoe) — "Fair Trade Commission"
- 객관적 (gaekgwanjeok) — "objective / impartial / unbiased"
- 객관성을 유지하다 (gaekgwanseong-eul yujihada) — "to maintain objectivity"

**Notes:** 공정 and 객관적 overlap in this group in contexts where impartiality and objectivity are treated as related requirements (e.g., journalistic standards). However, 객관적 also appears in purely epistemic contexts (see Group 9) where the concern is with the basis of a judgment rather than the fairness of a process. 공정 does not appear in epistemic contexts in the P1/P2 data.

## Group 9: Terms used to contrast personal-perspective judgments with fact-based or perspective-independent judgments

**Label:** Objective/subjective contrast terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to mark whether a judgment, description, or evaluation is based on personal perspective or on facts independent of the evaluator. They appear characteristically in academic, critical, and analytical discourse, and they are used as a contrasting pair (객관적 vs. 주관적). The primary function is methodological or epistemic self-positioning rather than moral evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- 객관적 (gaekgwanjeok) — "objective / impartial / unbiased"
- 객관적으로 보다 (gaekgwanjeogeuro boda) — "to look at objectively"
- 객관적인 근거를 제시하다 (gaekgwanjeogin geungeoreul jesihada) — "to present objective evidence"
- 객관적 사실에 기반하다 (gaekgwanjeok sasire gibanhada) — "to be based on objective facts"
- 객관성을 유지하다 (gaekgwanseong-eul yujihada) — "to maintain objectivity"
- 주관적 (jugwanjeok) — "subjective / personal"
- 주관적으로 판단하다 (jugwanjeogeuro pandanhada) — "to judge subjectively"
- 지극히 주관적인 의견 (jigeuki jugwanjeok-in uigyeon) — "a highly subjective opinion"
- 주관적 해석을 배제하다 (jugwanjeok haeseogeul baeje-hada) — "to exclude subjective interpretation"

**Notes:** 객관적 and 주관적 function as a matched pair in Korean usage and are rarely discussed independently of each other in the P1/P2 data. 주관적 is used both neutrally (in aesthetic contexts, where personal response is expected) and critically (in epistemic contexts, where personal bias is a problem). This dual use of 주관적 is preserved as found and not collapsed.

## Group 10: Terms used to evaluate whether an argument or claim is logically or normatively sound

**Label:** Validity and soundness evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether an argument, claim, policy, or procedure has adequate logical or normative grounding. They appear characteristically in academic, legal, and deliberative contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve examining, securing, or questioning soundness (타당성을 검토하다, 타당한 근거를 제시하다, 타당성이 부족하다). The primary concern is whether a position is well-supported, not whether it is factually accurate or morally honest.

**Representative expressions:**
- 타당 (tadang) — "validity / appropriateness / reasonableness"
- 타당하다 (tadang-hada) — "to be valid / reasonable / sound"
- 타당한 (tadanghan) — "valid / reasonable / sound" (attributive)
- 타당하게 (tadang-hage) — "validly / reasonably"
- 타당성 (tadangseong) — "validity / soundness / reasonableness"
- 타당성을 검토하다 (tadangseong-eul geomto-hada) — "to examine the validity of"
- 타당성을 확보하다 (tadangseong-eul hwakbo-hada) — "to secure validity"
- 타당성이 부족하다 (tadangseong-i bujok-hada) — "to be lacking in validity"
- 타당한 근거를 제시하다 (tadanghan geungeoreul jesihada) — "to present valid grounds"
- 타당하다고 볼 수 있다 (tadanghadago bol su itda) — "it can be seen as valid"
- 부당 (budang) — "unjust / unreasonable / improper"

**Notes:** 타당 is notably more restricted to deliberative and academic registers than most other terms in the P1/P2 data. 부당 (the antonym, formed from 당 當 alone) extends into legal and labor contexts (부당 해고, wrongful dismissal) where the concern is normative legitimacy rather than logical validity. This extension is preserved as found.

## Group 11: Terms used to evaluate whether something is correct or incorrect as a factual or logical matter

**Label:** Correctness/incorrectness evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a statement, answer, calculation, or judgment is factually or logically correct or incorrect. They appear characteristically in educational, everyday, and deliberative contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve affirming or denying correctness (네 말이 옳다, 틀린 답, 틀린 것을 인정하다). The primary concern is whether something is right or wrong as a matter of fact or reasoning, not moral character.

**Representative expressions:**
- 옳다 (olta) — "to be right / correct / proper"
- 옳은 (oreun) — "right / correct" (attributive)
- 옳고 그름 (olko geureum) — "right and wrong"
- 옳고 그름을 따지다 (olko geureumreul ttajida) — "to examine right and wrong"
- 옳은 일을 하다 (oreun ireul hada) — "to do the right thing"
- 네 말이 옳다 (ne mari olta) — "what you say is right"
- 그르다 (geureuda) — "to be wrong / incorrect / improper"
- 그른 (geureun) — "wrong / incorrect" (attributive)
- 틀리다 (teullida) — "to be wrong / incorrect / mistaken"
- 틀린 (teullin) — "wrong / incorrect" (attributive)
- 틀린 답 (teullin dap) — "wrong answer"
- 틀린 것을 인정하다 (teullin geoseul injeong-hada) — "to admit being wrong"
- 틀려도 괜찮다 (teullyeodo gwaenchanda) — "it's okay to be wrong"
- 다른 것과 틀린 것 (dareun geot-gwa teullin geot) — "what is different vs. what is wrong"

**Notes:** 옳다 and 그르다 are a matched antonym pair, but 그르다 is notably less frequent in contemporary everyday Korean than 틀리다, which has largely displaced it in factual-incorrectness contexts. The metalinguistic construction 다른 것과 틀린 것 (what is different vs. what is wrong) is unique in the P1/P2 data as an item that explicitly addresses a usage distinction within Korean itself. 옳다 appears in both epistemic contexts (correct answer) and moral contexts (the right thing to do), and this dual use is preserved as found rather than split.

## Group 12: Terms used to evaluate whether something can be relied upon as a source of accurate information

**Label:** Source reliability and credibility evaluation terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, testimony, or source of information can be depended upon to be accurate and honest. They appear characteristically in legal, journalistic, and institutional contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve building, losing, verifying, or questioning reliability (신뢰를 쌓다, 신뢰를 잃다, 신뢰성을 검증하다, 신빙성이 있다). The primary concern is whether a source warrants reliance.

**Representative expressions:**
- 신뢰 (sinloe) — "trust / reliability / credibility"
- 신뢰하다 (sinloe-hada) — "to trust / to rely on"
- 신뢰성 (sinloeseong) — "reliability / trustworthiness / credibility"
- 신뢰도 (sinloedo) — "degree of trust / reliability rating"
- 신뢰할 수 있다 (sinloehal su itda) — "trustworthy / can be trusted"
- 신뢰를 쌓다 (sinloereul ssada) — "to build trust"
- 신뢰를 잃다 (sinloereul ilta) — "to lose trust"
- 신뢰를 회복하다 (sinloereul hoebokada) — "to restore trust"
- 신뢰를 저버리다 (sinloereul jeobeorida) — "to betray trust"
- 신뢰가 무너지다 (sinloega muneoijida) — "trust collapses"
- 신뢰성이 높다 / 낮다 (sinloeseong-i nopda / natda) — "credibility is high / low"
- 신뢰성을 검증하다 (sinloeseong-eul geomjeung-hada) — "to verify credibility"
- 신뢰성에 의문이 제기되다 (sinloeseong-e uimun-i jegidoeda) — "doubts are raised about credibility"
- 불신 (bulsin) — "distrust / mistrust"
- 신빙성 (sinbyeongseong) — "credibility / plausibility of testimony or evidence"
- 신빙성이 있다 / 없다 (sinbyeongseong-i itda / eopda) — "to be credible / to lack credibility"
- 신빙성을 인정하다 (sinbyeongseong-eul injeong-hada) — "to acknowledge credibility"

**Notes:** 신뢰 and 신빙성 are distinguished in usage: 신뢰 is broader and applies to persons, institutions, and relationships, while 신빙성 is more domain-specific to legal and evidentiary contexts (testimony, evidence, accounts). The verb constructions associated with 신뢰 (쌓다, 잃다, 회복하다, 저버리다, 무너지다) form a notably rich set describing the lifecycle of trust — building, losing, restoring, betraying, collapsing — which is not paralleled for 신빙성.

## Group 13: Terms used to probe what something fundamentally is beneath its surface appearance

**Label:** Underlying-reality probing terms

**Definition:** These terms are used in contexts where the primary concern is uncovering or characterizing what something actually is at a deeper level, as opposed to what it appears to be or is claimed to be. They appear characteristically in investigative, journalistic, philosophical, and analytical contexts, and their characteristic constructions involve revealing, grasping, or obscuring underlying reality (실체를 밝히다, 본질을 파악하다, 실체가 없다, 본질을 흐리다). The primary contrast is between surface appearance and underlying substance.

**Representative expressions:**
- 본질 (bonjil) — "essence / essential nature"
- 본질적 (bonjiljeok) — "essential / fundamental / intrinsic"
- 본질적으로 (bonjiljeogeuro) — "essentially / fundamentally"
- 본질을 파악하다 (bonjireul paak-hada) — "to grasp the essence"
- 본질을 흐리다 (bonjireul heurida) — "to obscure the essence"
- 본질에서 벗어나다 (bonjileso beoseonada) — "to deviate from the fundamental issue"
- 문제의 본질 (munje-ui bonjil) — "the essence of the problem"
- 실체 (silche) — "substance / true nature / real entity"
- 실체적 (silchejeok) — "substantive / pertaining to true nature"
- 실체를 밝히다 (silchereul balkida) — "to reveal the true nature"
- 실체를 드러내다 (silchereul deureoneda) — "to expose what something really is"
- 실체를 파악하다 (silchereul paak-hada) — "to grasp the true nature"
- 실체가 없다 (silchega eopda) — "to have no substance / to be insubstantial"
- 실체적 진실 (silchejeok jinsil) — "substantive truth" (legal term)

**Notes:** 본질 and 실체 are related but distinguishable in usage: 본질 appears more in philosophical and analytical contexts where the concern is with what something essentially is by nature, while 실체 appears more in investigative and legal contexts where the concern is with what something actually is as opposed to what it presents itself as. The construction 실체가 없다 (to have no substance) has no parallel with 본질, which suggests that 실체 carries a stronger implication of concrete existence. Both terms share the verb 파악하다 (to grasp/comprehend) as a characteristic pairing.

## Group 14: Terms used in contexts of distortion, misrepresentation, and correction

**Label:** Distortion and correction terms

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a representation, account, or record has been altered away from accurate correspondence to reality, and to describe the act of correcting such distortions. They appear characteristically in journalistic, historical, legal, and administrative contexts. The characteristic constructions involve distorting (왜곡하다, 진실을 왜곡하다, 사실을 왜곡하다), correcting (정정하다, 바로잡다), and publishing corrections (정정 보도).

**Representative expressions:**
- 왜곡 (waegok) — "distortion / misrepresentation / falsification"
- 왜곡하다 (waegok-hada) — "to distort / misrepresent"
- 진실을 왜곡하다 (jinshireul waegok-hada) — "to distort the truth"
- 사실을 왜곡하다 (sasireul waegok-hada) — "to distort the facts"
- 역사 왜곡 (yeoksa waegok) — "historical distortion"
- 정정 (jeongjeon) — "correction / rectification"
- 정정 보도 (jeongjeon bodo) — "correction report / retraction in journalism"
- 정오표 (jeongopyo) — "errata / list of corrections"
- 바로잡다 (barojapda) — "to correct / to set right / to rectify"
- 역사를 바로잡다 (yeoksareul barojapda) — "to correct history"
- 잘못을 바로잡다 (jalmoseul barojapda) — "to correct a mistake"
- 표절 (pyojeol) — "plagiarism / copying without attribution"

**Notes:** 왜곡 and 표절 are both in this group but differ in what is distorted: 왜곡 involves altering the content of a representation away from accuracy, while 표절 involves misrepresenting the origin of a work. 표절 could also be placed in Group 3 (authenticity) given its concern with the genuine vs. copied status of a work, but its characteristic context (academic, literary, intellectual property) and its primary evaluative concern (attribution rather than physical authenticity) distinguish it from the object-authentication terms in Group 3. 바로잡다 spans both epistemic correction (correcting a false account) and moral-political rectification (correcting an injustice), which is preserved as found.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 present notable ambiguity or overlap across multiple groups and are documented here rather than assigned to a single group.

---

**진실 (jinsil) / 진실을 말하다 / 진실은 하나다**

진실 participates in Group 5 (truth investigation) as the primary object of investigative and institutional inquiry, and also in Group 2 (deception evaluation) as the positive pole contrasted with 거짓말. The construction 진실은 하나다 ("there is only one truth") functions as a rhetorical claim in popular culture and legal discourse that does not map cleanly onto any single group. 진실 is the most broadly distributed term in the P1/P2 data and resists assignment to a single group.

---

**옳다 (olta) / 옳은 일을 하다**

옳다 appears in Group 11 (correctness/incorrectness) in epistemic contexts (네 말이 옳다, "what you say is right") and also in moral contexts (옳은 일을 하다, "to do the right thing") that overlap with Group 7 (personal honesty) and Group 8 (fairness). The coordinated pair 옳고 그름 (right and wrong) functions as a general moral binary that is not specific to any single evaluative domain.

---

**객관적 (gaekgwanjeok)**

객관적 appears in Group 9 (objective/subjective contrast) as a methodological term and also in Group 8 (impartiality and fairness) in journalistic contexts where objectivity is treated as a component of fair reporting. The two uses are distinguishable in context but the term itself does not mark the distinction.

---

**실제 (silje) / 실제로 (siljero)**

실제 and 실제로 appear frequently in P1b as part of the 실 (實) root expansion but were not listed as P1 base items. They appear in constructions that overlap with Group 1 (factual-status assertion: 실제로 일어난 일, "what actually happened") and Group 13 (underlying-reality probing: 실제 상황, "the actual situation"). They are noted here as items that would likely participate in multiple groups if included.

---

**진의 (jinui) / 진면목 (jinmyeonmok) / 진가 (jinga)**

These terms from P1b share the 진 (眞) root and appear in contexts adjacent to Group 4 (sincerity) and Group 13 (underlying reality) but are not clearly assignable to either. 진의 (true intention) is used in legal and diplomatic contexts to probe what someone actually meant; 진면목 (true character) is used to describe the revelation of authentic nature; 진가 (true value) is used in contexts of recognizing previously unacknowledged worth. These three terms share a concern with what is genuinely the case about a person or thing but do not form a coherent usage-based group with each other or with the groups above.

---

**진리 (jilli)**

진리 appears in philosophical, religious, and academic contexts to refer to fundamental or universal truth as an object of pursuit. Its usage pattern (진리를 추구하다, "to pursue truth"; 영원한 진리, "eternal truth") is distinct from all groups above, which are concerned with evaluating specific claims, persons, or objects. 진리 does not fit cleanly into any of the groups identified and is noted as ungrouped.

---

**표절 (pyojeol)**

표절 is placed in Group 14 (distortion and correction) on the basis of its concern with misrepresentation of origin, but it could also be placed in Group 3 (authenticity of objects) given its concern with whether a work is genuinely original. Its characteristic context (academic, literary, intellectual property) and the specific nature of the misrepresentation (attribution rather than physical authenticity) are the basis for its placement in Group 14, but this placement is noted as uncertain.

## Items Without Clear Group Assignment

The following P1 items do not fit clearly into any of the groups identified above based on the usage patterns in P1 and P2.

**진리 (jilli):** As noted above, its usage pattern as an object of philosophical or religious pursuit is distinct from all evaluative groups identified.

**본질주의 (bonjil juui) / 사실주의 (sasil juui) / 실증주의 (siljeung juui) / 실존주의 (siljon juui) / 실용주의 (silyong juui):** These -주의 (ism) compounds from P1b appear in academic and intellectual discourse as labels for philosophical or aesthetic movements. Their usage is primarily classificatory and definitional rather than evaluative in the sense characteristic of the groups above. They are noted as ungrouped.

**진실화해위원회 (jinsil hwahae wiwonhoe):** This is a fixed institutional name rather than a productive evaluative term. It participates in Group 5 by reference but does not itself function as an evaluative expression.
