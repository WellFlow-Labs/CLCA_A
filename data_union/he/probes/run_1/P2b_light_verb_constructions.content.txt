# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Constructions are listed per P1 base form. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where a construction was already documented in P1b as a fixed phrase, it is cross-referenced rather than re-described in full. Absence of common constructions is recorded explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant. No constructions have been generated to fill expected semantic roles; only patterns supported by attested usage are included.

---

## P1 Item 1 — אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)

---

**1.1**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Example:** אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna*
**English translation:** truth and faithfulness; in truth and in faith
**Usage notes:** A fixed liturgical and legal phrase affirming both factual truth and sincere intent. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-נ, item 14); cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Example:** אֱמֶת וָשָׁלוֹם
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet va-shalom*
**English translation:** truth and peace
**Usage notes:** A biblical and liturgical phrase (Zechariah 8:19) describing the tension and complementarity between strict truth-telling and social harmony. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 17); cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Example:** אֱמֶת וּמִשְׁפָּט
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet u-mishpat*
**English translation:** truth and justice
**Usage notes:** A biblical and legal phrase describing the dual requirements of truthfulness and just judgment. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 18); cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)
**Example:** בֶּאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** *beʾemet*
**English translation:** truly, really, honestly, in truth
**Usage notes:** An extremely common adverbial phrase functioning as an intensifier or sincerity marker in everyday speech. Already documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 13); cross-referenced here.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (*ʾemet*)

## P1 Item 2 — אמין (*ʾamin*)

---

**2.1**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Example:** מָקוֹר אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *maqor ʾamin*
**English translation:** reliable source, credible source
**Usage notes:** A common collocation in journalistic and intelligence discourse describing a source of information considered credible. Slightly less formal than *maqor mehiman* (documented in P1b); both are in active use.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.2**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Example:** עֵד אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *ʿed ʾamin*
**English translation:** reliable witness, credible witness
**Usage notes:** Used in legal discourse to describe a witness whose testimony is considered credible. Slightly less formal than *ʿed neʾeman* (documented in P1b); both collocations are attested in legal Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.3**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Example:** לֹא אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *lo ʾamin*
**English translation:** unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
**Usage notes:** The standard negated form used to describe a person, source, or system that cannot be trusted. Already documented in P2a (item 2.2); cross-referenced here as a productive construction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.4**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Example:** נִרְאֶה אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *nirʾe ʾamin*
**English translation:** seems reliable, appears credible
**Usage notes:** A common predicative construction using *nirʾe* (seems, appears) with *ʾamin* as complement. Used in everyday and journalistic discourse to evaluate the apparent credibility of a source or claim without full confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.5**
**Base Form:** אמין (*ʾamin*)
**Example:** פָּחוֹת אָמִין
**Transliteration:** *paḥot ʾamin*
**English translation:** less reliable, less credible
**Usage notes:** A comparative construction using *paḥot* (less) with *ʾamin*. Used in evaluative discourse to describe a source or claim as having reduced credibility relative to another. Common in journalistic and legal evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 3 — נכון (*nakhon*)

---

**3.1**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** זֶה נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** *ze nakhon*
**English translation:** that is correct, that is right
**Usage notes:** The standard everyday construction for affirming that something is correct. Extremely common in spoken Hebrew as a response to a statement or answer. Functions as the most frequent affirmation of correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.2**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** *ze lo nakhon*
**English translation:** that is not correct, that is wrong
**Usage notes:** The standard everyday construction for denying the correctness of a statement. Extremely common in spoken Hebrew. Slightly softer in register than *ze sheqer* (that is a lie).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.3**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְהַגִּיד
**Transliteration:** *nakhon lehagid*
**English translation:** it is right to say, it is correct to say, to be fair
**Usage notes:** A common construction used to introduce a concession or qualification in discourse — acknowledging that something is correct before adding a counterpoint. Common in journalistic and analytical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.4**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** נָכוֹן שֶׁ-
**Transliteration:** *nakhon she-*
**English translation:** it is true that, it is correct that, admittedly
**Usage notes:** A common construction introducing a concessive clause. Used in everyday and formal discourse to acknowledge a point before qualifying or countering it. Functionally equivalent to "it is true that" or "admittedly" in English.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.5**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** לֹא בִּדְיוּק נָכוֹן
**Transliteration:** *lo bediyyuq nakhon*
**English translation:** not exactly correct, not quite right
**Usage notes:** A common hedging construction combining *lo* (not), *bediyyuq* (exactly), and *nakhon* (correct). Used in everyday discourse to indicate partial inaccuracy without full denial.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.6**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְעַכְשָׁו
**Transliteration:** *nakhon leʿakhshav*
**English translation:** correct as of now, currently accurate, valid at this time
**Usage notes:** A common construction used in administrative, journalistic, and everyday contexts to indicate that information is accurate at the present moment but may change. Common in disclaimers and data presentations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.7**
**Base Form:** נכון (*nakhon*)
**Example:** נָכוֹן לְתַאֲרִיךְ
**Transliteration:** *nakhon letaʾarikh*
**English translation:** correct as of [date], accurate as of [date]
**Usage notes:** A standard administrative and journalistic construction used to indicate the date as of which information is accurate. Common in official documents, reports, and data publications.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 6 — מקורי (*meqori*)

---

**6.1**
**Base Form:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Example:** עֹתֶק מְקוֹרִי
**Transliteration:** *ʿoteq meqori*
**English translation:** original copy, original version
**Usage notes:** A common collocation in legal and administrative contexts describing the original document as opposed to a photocopy or reproduction. Used in legal proceedings and official transactions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.2**
**Base Form:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Example:** יְצִירָה מְקוֹרִית
**Transliteration:** *yetsira meqorit*
**English translation:** original work, original creation
**Usage notes:** A standard collocation in intellectual property, aesthetic, and copyright discourse describing a work that is genuinely creative and not derivative. Central in Israeli copyright law discussions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.3**
**Base Form:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Example:** רַעְיוֹן מְקוֹרִי
**Transliteration:** *raʿyon meqori*
**English translation:** original idea, original concept
**Usage notes:** A common collocation in everyday and intellectual discourse describing an idea that is genuinely new and not borrowed. Used in academic, creative, and business contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.4**
**Base Form:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Example:** לֹא מְקוֹרִי
**Transliteration:** *lo meqori*
**English translation:** not original, unoriginal, derivative
**Usage notes:** The standard negated form used to describe something that lacks originality or is a copy or imitation. Common in aesthetic and intellectual evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.5**
**Base Form:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Example:** הַמָּקוֹר הַמְּקוֹרִי
**Transliteration:** *ha-maqor ha-meqori*

## P1 Item 16 — ישר (*yashar*)

---

**16.1**
**Base Form:** ישר (*yashar*)
**Example:** הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינָיו
**Transliteration:** *ha-yashar beʿeynav*
**English translation:** what is right in his eyes, what seems right to him
**Usage notes:** A biblical phrase (appearing repeatedly in Judges and elsewhere) describing conduct guided by individual judgment rather than divine command. Used in modern Hebrew to describe subjective moral judgment, often with a critical connotation implying moral relativism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** *ʾadam yashar*

**Usage notes:** A common collocation in everyday and literary Hebrew describing a person of moral integrity and honesty. Used in character evaluation across registers.
