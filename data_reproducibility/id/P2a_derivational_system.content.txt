### BASE: salah

#### 1. kesalahan
**Derived Form:** kesalahan  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** ke- + salah + -an (nominalization)  
**Translation:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing  
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to an error, mistake, or wrongdoing. Very common in everyday speech ("kesalahan saya" = "my mistake") and formal contexts ("kesalahan sistem" = "system error").  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. menyalahkan
**Derived Form:** menyalahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** meN- + salah + -kan (causative verb)  
**Translation:** to blame, to fault, to accuse  
**Usage:** Transitive verb meaning to blame someone or declare something wrong. Common in interpersonal contexts ("jangan menyalahkan orang lain" = "don't blame others") and evaluative discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. disalahkan
**Derived Form:** disalahkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** di- + salah + -kan (passive voice)  
**Translation:** to be blamed, to be faulted  
**Usage:** Passive form indicating someone/something is blamed. Standard in discourse about responsibility ("dia selalu disalahkan" = "he is always blamed").  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. menyalahi
**Derived Form:** menyalahi  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** meN- + salah + -i (locative/applicative verb)  
**Translation:** to violate, to break (rules), to go against  
**Usage:** Transitive verb meaning to violate or break rules/norms. Common in legal and normative contexts ("menyalahi aturan" = "to violate rules").  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. salah-salah
**Derived Form:** salah-salah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** salah + reduplication  
**Translation:** if by mistake, lest, in case of error  
**Usage:** Reduplicated form expressing caution or hypothetical error. Used in warnings ("salah-salah kamu terlambat" = "you might end up being late").  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. tersalah
**Derived Form:** tersalah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal Breakdown:** ter- + salah (accidental passive)  
**Translation:** mistaken, wrongly done (accidentally)  
**Usage:** Indicates accidental error or mistake. Used when something is done wrong unintentionally ("tersalah ambil" = "took the wrong one by mistake").  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. penyalah

**Translation:** abuser, misuser  
**Usage:** Agent noun referring to one who misuses something. Common in compounds ("penyalah narkoba" = "drug abuser," "penyalahguna" = "abuser/misuser").  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. penyalahgunaan

#### 9. menyalahgunakan

## SUMMARY NOTES

### Productivity Patterns Observed:

1. **High productivity roots:** benar, salah, sama, nyata, tepat, cocok, betul
   - These roots show 6+ productive derivations each
   - Support full range of affixation patterns (ke-an, meN-kan, peN-an, etc.)

2. **Moderate productivity roots:** palsu, bohong, jujur, sah, absah, batal, mirip, wajar, pantas
   - These roots show 3-5 productive derivations
   - Support common affixation patterns but with some gaps

3. **Low productivity roots:** asli, sejati, keliru, akurat, hakikat, khilaf, tulen, faktual
   - These roots show 1-3 derivations
   - Limited affixation patterns, often only ke-an nominalization

4. **Borrowed roots (valid, faktual, akurat):** Show adapted Indonesian affixation but also retain borrowed derivational patterns (-itas, -asi)

### Common Derivational Patterns:

- **ke-...-an nominalization:** Nearly universal for quality/state abstraction
- **meN-...-kan causative:** Very productive for action verbs
- **di-...-kan passive:** Standard passive formation
- **peN-...-an action nominalization:** Common for process/result nouns
- **peN- agent nominalization:** Common for actor/instrument nouns
- **Reduplication:** Productive for intensification, iteration, or semantic modification
- **ber- stative/intransitive:** Moderately productive
- **ter- accidental/resultative:** Moderately productive

### Gaps and Asymmetries:

- Not all roots support all derivational patterns
- Some roots (especially borrowed terms) show limited Indonesian affixation
- Literary/formal roots (dusta, sejati, khilaf) show restricted productivity
- Semantic domains influence productivity (legal/administrative terms like sah show specific derivational patterns)
