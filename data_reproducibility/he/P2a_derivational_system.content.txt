# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Derivational Analysis

### BASE FORM 1: אמת (emet) "truth"

#### Derived Form 1.1
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** אמיתי (amiti)
**Transliteration:** amiti
**Literal breakdown:** emet + -i (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, authentic
**Usage description:** Adjectival derivation from the noun emet, used to characterize entities as true or genuine. Highly productive in forming attributive descriptions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.2
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** אמיתות (amitiyut)
**Transliteration:** amitiyut
**Literal breakdown:** amiti + -ut (abstract noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, genuineness
**Usage description:** Abstract noun derived from the adjective amiti, denoting the quality of being genuine or authentic. Common in philosophical and evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.3
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** באמת (be-emet)
**Transliteration:** be-emet
**Literal breakdown:** be- (preposition "in/with") + emet
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth
**Usage description:** Prepositional phrase functioning as adverb, used to emphasize truth or reality of statements. Highly frequent in spoken Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.4
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** לאמיתו של דבר (la-amito shel davar)
**Transliteration:** la-amito shel davar
**Literal breakdown:** le- "to" + amito (emet + possessive suffix -o "its") + shel "of" + davar "thing/matter"
**Natural English translation:** in truth, actually, to tell the truth
**Usage description:** Fixed idiomatic expression using possessive form of emet, employed to introduce what speaker considers the real truth. Common in argumentative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.5
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** אמת מידה (amat midah)
**Transliteration:** amat midah
**Literal breakdown:** emet (construct state) + midah "measure"
**Natural English translation:** criterion, standard, yardstick
**Usage description:** Compound using construct state of emet with "measure," creating a fixed expression for evaluative standards. The construct state form amat is morphologically derived.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.6
**Base form:** אמת (emet)
**Derived form:** אמת ויציב (emet ve-yatsiv)
**Transliteration:** emet ve-yatsiv
**Literal breakdown:** emet + ve- "and" + yatsiv "firm/stable"
**Natural English translation:** true and firm, absolutely true
**Usage description:** Fixed liturgical compound emphasizing absolute truth through coordination with "firm." Functions as intensified truth expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### BASE FORM 2: שקר (sheker) "falsehood"

**Transliteration:** shikri
**Literal breakdown:** sheker + -i (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** false, lying, deceptive
**Usage description:** Adjectival derivation from sheker, used to characterize statements or representations as false. Parallel formation to amiti from emet.

#### Derived Form 2.2
**Base form:** שקר (sheker)
**Derived form:** שקרן (shakran)
**Transliteration:** shakran
**Literal breakdown:** sh-k-r + -an (agentive suffix)
**Natural English translation:** liar, habitual liar
**Usage description:** Agentive noun denoting a person characterized by lying. The -an suffix indicates habitual or characteristic behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2.3
**Base form:** שקר (sheker)
**Derived form:** שקרנות (shakranut)
**Transliteration:** shakranut
**Literal breakdown:** shakran + -ut (abstract noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** lying, mendacity, habitual dishonesty
**Usage description:** Abstract noun derived from shakran, denoting the quality or practice of habitual lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2.4
**Base form:** שקר (sheker)
**Derived form:** שיקר (shiker)
**Transliteration:** shiker
**Literal breakdown:** shi- (pi'el pattern) + k-r (verb)
**Natural English translation:** lied, told lies
**Usage description:** Verbal derivation in pi'el pattern, meaning to lie or tell falsehoods. Standard verb form for the act of lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2.5
**Base form:** שקר (sheker)
**Derived form:** משקר (meshaker)
**Transliteration:** meshaker
**Literal breakdown:** me- (pi'el participle) + sh-k-r
**Natural English translation:** liar, one who lies
**Usage description:** Participial form functioning as noun or adjective, describing someone who lies. Less marked than shakran for habitual behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2.6
**Base form:** שקר (sheker)
**Derived form:** דבר שקר (davar sheker)
**Transliteration:** davar sheker
**Literal breakdown:** davar "thing/statement" + sheker (in construct)
**Natural English translation:** false statement, lie
**Usage description:** Fixed compound expression for false statement, common in legal and moral discourse. Uses sheker attributively.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary Notes

### Derivational Patterns Observed:

1. **Adjectival suffix -i:** Highly productive for creating adjectives from nouns (emet → amiti, metsi'ut → metsi'uti, uvdah → uvdati, mahut → mahuti)

2. **Abstract noun suffix -ut:** Highly productive for creating abstract nouns from adjectives (amiti → amitiyut, nakhon → nekhonut, amin → aminut, meheyман → meheymanut, vadai → vada'ut)

3. **Agentive suffix -an:** Used to create agent nouns indicating habitual behavior (sheker → shakran, safek → safkan)

4. **Prepositional phrases with be-:** Very productive for creating adverbial expressions (emet → be-emet, tsedek → be-tsedek, yosher → be-yosher, khenut → be-khenut, vadai → be-vadai, bitakhon → be-bitakhon, safek → be-safek)

5. **Negation with lo/bilti/ein:** Standard negation patterns (nakhon → lo nakhon, amin → bilti amin, safek → ein safek)

6. **Verbal derivations:** Hebrew root system allows multiple verbal patterns (pi'el, hif'il, hitpa'el, etc.) from the same root, creating related verbs with causative, intensive, or reflexive meanings

7. **Construct state compounds:** Common for creating fixed expressions (emet + midah → amat midah, tsedek + mishpat → mishpat tsedek)

### Asymmetries and Gaps:

- Not all base forms show equal derivational productivity
- Some forms (like ken, ba-emet, lo nakhon) show minimal or no further derivation
- Loanwords (otenti) follow Hebrew derivational patterns once borrowed
- Some derivational relationships are bidirectional (unclear which is historically base)
