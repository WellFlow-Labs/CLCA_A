# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** सत् / *sat* (from root *as-*, to be)
**P1 items participating:** सत् (*sat*), असत् (*asat*), सत्य (*satya*), असत्य (*asatya*), तत्त्व (*tattva*, via the copular element *tat* + *-tva*), वास्तव (*vāstava*, via *vastu* from *vas-*, cognate domain of being/dwelling), वस्तु (*vastu*)

Note: *sat* directly participates in the *satya/asat/asatya* cluster. Items *vastu* and *vāstava* derive from a distinct but semantically adjacent root (*vas-*); they are counted separately below. The core count for *sat/as-* is 4 direct items (*sat*, *asat*, *satya*, *asatya*), with *tattva* tangentially related through the copular structure.

**Core meaning contribution:** Being, existence, reality, genuineness. The root supplies the ontological grounding for truth-evaluation: what is *sat* is real and therefore true; what is *asat* is unreal and therefore false. The root fuses ontological and evaluative senses throughout the domain. *Sat* contributes the equation of truth with being or existence — to be true is to be real, to exist. This root generates the largest and most philosophically loaded cluster in the Sanskrit truth-evaluation domain, spanning ontology, ethics, and epistemology.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** प्र + मा / *pra-mā-* (prefix *pra-* + root *mā-*, to measure); also root *mā-* alone
**P1 items participating:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*), प्रमा (*pramā*), अप्रमा (*apramā*)

**Core meaning contribution:** Correct measuring, valid apprehension. The root *mā-* (to measure) combined with the prefix *pra-* (forth, correctly) generates the central epistemological vocabulary of Sanskrit philosophy: the means of knowing, the act of knowing correctly, and its negation. This cluster is the technical core of the domain in philosophical discourse. *Mā-* contributes the idea of truth as correct measurement or calibration — a cognition or standard is valid insofar as it accurately measures or delimits its object.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** धृ / *dhṛ-* (to hold, to sustain)
**P1 items participating:** धर्म (*dharma*), अधर्म (*adharma*)

Note: *dhṛ-* yields fewer P1 items than *sat* or *mā-*, but the semantic range of *dharma* is exceptionally broad within the truth-evaluation domain (right conduct, property, sustaining order), and the root is highly productive derivationally. It is included as a central root on the basis of the breadth of its contribution rather than raw item count.

**Core meaning contribution:** Holding, sustaining, and the right order that is thereby maintained. The root supplies the framework for moral and legal evaluation: what *dharma* holds in place is right; what violates it is wrong.

---

**Root 4**

**Form:** ऋत / *ṛta* (from root *ṛ-*, to go, to fit, to be ordered)
**P1 items participating:** ऋत (*ṛta*), अनृत (*anṛta*)

Note: The root *ṛ-* (to go rightly, to fit) underlies *ṛta* and *anṛta*. It is less derivationally productive in post-Vedic Sanskrit than the three roots above, but generates a significant cluster of terms in the Vedic, cosmological, and moral-order domains, and is the etymological ancestor of the *ṛta/ṛtu/ṛju* family. It is the oldest and cosmologically most fundamental stratum of Sanskrit truth-vocabulary.

**Core meaning contribution:** Proper ordering, fittingness, and the regularity that underlies both natural law and moral rightness. Distinct from *sat* in emphasizing order and fit rather than sheer being.

---

**Supplementary note on** ***artha*:**
The stem *artha* (object, referent, purpose, meaning) is not a verbal root but is the pivot of the correspondence-and-levels-of-truth cluster, participating in items such as यथार्थ (*yathārtha*) and परमार्थ (*paramārtha*). It contributes the referential or objective pole of truth — truth is evaluated as the relation between a word, cognition, or claim and its object or intended meaning. Compounds from this stem are listed under a supplementary expansion below.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: सत् / *sat* (root *as-*, to be; present participle *sat*; also stems *satya*, *vastu*, *vāstava*)

The following lists all common compounds, collocations, derived expressions, and fixed phrases built from *sat* / *satya* / *vastu* / *vāstava* and their extensions attested across the probes.

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्यता
**Transliteration:** satyatā
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being true"
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, truth (as an abstract property)
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and ethical discourse to name the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from particular true statements. Appears in discussions of the nature of truth and in characterizations of persons of truthful character. Also used in epistemological and ethical literature to denote the abstract property of being true or truthful, as predicated of statements, persons, or cognitions; appears in *Nyāya* commentaries and *Dharmaśāstra*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** सत्यत्व
**Transliteration:** satyatva
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *-tva* (abstract suffix); "truth-ness"
**Natural English translation:** the property of being true, truthfulness
**Context of use:** A more technical philosophical variant of *satyatā*, used in logical and epistemological discussions to refer to the property of truth as a formal category. Appears in Navya-Nyāya literature.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**3.**
**Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*); "one who speaks truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully
**Context of use:** Used in epic, Purāṇic, and legal literature to characterize a person whose speech is reliably truthful. Appears as a term of praise in *Dharmaśāstra* witness evaluation and in epic characterization of virtuous figures. A standard epithet of virtuous characters in narrative literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** सत्यवचन
**Transliteration:** satyavacana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vacana* (speech, utterance, from root *vac-*); "truthful utterance"
**Natural English translation:** truthful speech, speaking the truth
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal contexts to designate the act or practice of speaking truthfully. Appears in Dharmaśāstra enumerations of virtuous conduct and in vow formulas.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** सत्यवाक्य
**Transliteration:** satya-vākya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* (truth) + *vākya* (sentence, utterance, from root *vac-*)
**Natural English translation:** a true statement, a truthful utterance
**Context of use:** Used in logical, legal, and ethical contexts to designate an utterance that corresponds to the facts. Appears in discussions of testimony (*śabda-pramāṇa*) and in legal evaluation of witness statements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** सत्यव्रत
**Transliteration:** satyavrata
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *vrata* (vow, observance); "vow of truth"
**Natural English translation:** vow of truthfulness, one who has taken a vow of truth
**Context of use:** Used in epic and Purāṇic literature both as a compound noun naming the vow of always speaking truth and as an epithet of figures who have taken such a vow. Also a proper name.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** सत्यसंध
**Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* (bound, pledged, from root *dhā-* with *sam-*); "bound to truth, true to one's word"
**Natural English translation:** true to one's word, faithful to one's pledge
**Context of use:** Used in epic literature (Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa) as an epithet of heroes and kings who keep their promises. Evaluates reliability and fidelity to commitments as a form of truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** सत्यनिष्ठ
**Transliteration:** satyanistha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *nistha* (devoted to, grounded in, from root *sthā-*); "grounded in truth"
**Natural English translation:** devoted to truth, steadfast in truthfulness
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness. Appears in enumerations of virtuous qualities.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *āpana* (causing to reach/obtain, from causative of root *āp-*); "causing truth to be established"
**Natural English translation:** verification, attestation, confirmation
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for the act of verifying or attesting to the truth of a statement or document. Appears in modern Sanskrit-medium legal and official usage as the standard term for verification. Also used in traditional legal literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** सत्यापित
**Transliteration:** satyāpita
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *āpita* (past passive participle of causative of *āp-*); "caused to be verified, attested"
**Natural English translation:** verified, attested, certified
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative documents to indicate that a statement or copy has been officially verified. Standard in modern Sanskrit-influenced official language. Appears on official documents to indicate that a statement has been verified.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** सत्यग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyagraha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *graha* (grasping, holding, from root *grah-*); "holding to truth"
**Natural English translation:** holding to truth, truth-force (Gandhian political concept)
**Context of use:** Coined or crystallized by Gandhi as a term for nonviolent resistance grounded in truth. Widely used in political, historical, and ethical discourse in modern Indian languages and internationally. The compound is formed on classical Sanskrit morphological patterns.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** सत्याग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyāgraha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence, from root *grah-*); "holding firmly to truth"
**Natural English translation:** insistence on truth, truth-force
**Context of use:** Coined or systematized by Mohandas Gandhi to denote nonviolent resistance grounded in the force of truth. Used in political, ethical, and historical discourse in modern Sanskrit and in discussions of Gandhi's philosophy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** सत्यलोक
**Transliteration:** satyaloka
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *loka* (world, realm); "world of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the realm of truth, the highest world
**Context of use:** Used in Purāṇic cosmology for the highest of the cosmic realms, identified with the abode of Brahmā and associated with ultimate reality. Appears in enumerations of the cosmic worlds (lists of the seven *lokas*) and in descriptions of the afterlife destinations of virtuous souls. Used in cosmological and devotional literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** सत्ययुग
**Transliteration:** satyayuga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *yuga* (age, era); "the age of truth"
**Natural English translation:** the age of truth, the golden age
**Context of use:** Used in Purāṇic and epic literature for the first and most virtuous of the four cosmic ages (*yugas*), characterized by universal adherence to truth and righteousness. Widely attested in cosmological, ethical, and nostalgic literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** सत्यप्रतिज्ञ
**Transliteration:** satyapratijna
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijñā* (pledge, promise, from *prati-* + root *jñā-*); "whose pledge is truth"
**Natural English translation:** one whose promises are true, one who keeps his word
**Context of use:** Used in epic and Purāṇic literature as an epithet of figures renowned for keeping their pledges. Appears in the *Mahābhārata* and *Rāmāyaṇa*. Evaluates the reliability of a person's commitments as a form of truthfulness.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**16.**
**Form:** सत्कार
**Transliteration:** satkāra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *kāra* (doing, making, from root *kṛ-*); "doing what is good/real"
**Natural English translation:** honor, respectful treatment, hospitality
**Context of use:** Used widely in social and religious contexts for the act of honoring or showing respect to a person, deity, or guest. The evaluative sense of *sat* (genuine, good) underlies the notion of treating someone as they deserve. Common in narrative and prescriptive literature describing social norms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** सत्पुरुष
**Transliteration:** satpuruṣa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *puruṣa* (man, person); "a genuine/good man"
**Natural English translation:** a good man, a man of genuine virtue, a worthy person
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and social discourse to designate a person of genuine virtue and good character. The *sat* component carries both the ontological sense (genuinely what a person should be) and the evaluative sense (morally good). Appears in *Dharmaśāstra*, epic, and didactic literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** सत्संग
**Transliteration:** satsaṅga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *saṅga* (company, association, from root *sañj-*); "company of the good/real"
**Natural English translation:** company of the good, association with virtuous or spiritually realized persons
**Context of use:** Used in devotional and religious contexts for the practice of associating with spiritually advanced or virtuous persons, considered beneficial for one's own development. Widely attested in Bhakti literature and in modern religious usage. Also the name of many religious gatherings.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** सत्कर्मन्
**Transliteration:** sat-karman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *karman* (action, deed); "right/good action"
**Natural English translation:** good deed, right action, virtuous conduct
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to denote actions that are morally correct and in accordance with *dharma*. Contrasted with *asat-karman* (wrong or evil action). Appears in ethical and ritual literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** सद्गुण
**Transliteration:** sadguṇa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi form *sad-*) + *guṇa* (quality, virtue); "genuine/good quality"
**Natural English translation:** good quality, genuine virtue, positive attribute
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and aesthetic contexts to designate a genuinely good quality or virtue in a person or thing. Appears in enumerations of virtuous characteristics and in literary criticism to evaluate the positive qualities of a work.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** सद्वृत्त
**Transliteration:** sadvṛtta
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *sad-*) + *vṛtta* (conduct, behavior, from root *vṛt-*); "good conduct"
**Natural English translation:** good conduct, virtuous behavior, right behavior
**Context of use:** Used in Dharmaśāstra and ethical literature to designate conduct that conforms to what is genuinely right and good. Appears in prescriptive enumerations of proper behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** असत्कार
**Transliteration:** asatkāra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *kāra* (doing); "doing what is not good, disrespect"
**Natural English translation:** disrespect, dishonor, discourteous treatment
**Context of use:** Used in social and ethical contexts as the negation of *satkāra*, designating disrespectful or dishonoring treatment of a person. Appears in discussions of proper social conduct and its violations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** असत्प्रतिग्रह
**Transliteration:** asatpratigṛha
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *pratigṛha* (acceptance, receiving, from *prati-* + root *grah-*); "acceptance of what is not genuine/proper"
**Natural English translation:** improper acceptance (of gifts), acceptance from unworthy sources
**Context of use:** Used in Dharmaśāstra for the improper acceptance of gifts or donations from sources that are not legitimate or from persons whose gifts should not be received. A category of wrongful conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** सत्यमेव जयते
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, nominative) + *eva* (indeed, only) + *jayate* (conquers, from root *ji-*); "truth alone conquers"
**Natural English translation:** Truth alone conquers / Truth alone prevails
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase from the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as the national motto of India. Used in philosophical, political, and rhetorical contexts to assert the ultimate triumph of truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** सत्यं वद
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, accusative) + *vada* (speak, imperative of root *vad-*); "speak truth"
**Natural English translation:** Speak the truth
**Context of use:** A fixed injunctive phrase from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (1.11.1), widely cited in ethical and educational contexts as a foundational moral injunction. Used in discussions of the duty of truthful speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** सत्यं ब्रूयात्
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, accusative) + *brūyāt* (should speak, optative of root *brū-*); "one should speak truth"
**Natural English translation:** One should speak the truth
**Context of use:** A fixed prescriptive phrase from Manusmṛti and related Dharmaśāstra literature, used in ethical discourse to state the duty of truthful speech. Often followed by qualifications about when truth-telling may be overridden.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** सत्यम् शिवम् सुन्दरम्
**Transliteration:** satyam śivam sundaram
**Morpheme breakdown:** *satyam* (truth, neuter accusative) + *śivam* (auspiciousness, goodness) + *sundaram* (beauty); "truth, goodness, beauty"
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, beauty (a triad of ultimate values)
**Context of use:** A well-known philosophical and aesthetic formula identifying the three highest values. Used in philosophical, devotional, and aesthetic discourse. Corresponds loosely to the Western *verum, bonum, pulchrum*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** सत्ताशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** sattāśāstra
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sattā* (being, existence, from *sat* + *-tā*) + *śāstra* (treatise, discipline); "treatise on being"
**Natural English translation:** ontology (as a discipline)
**Context of use:** Used in modern Sanskrit philosophical writing as a rendering of "ontology." Less common than *sattāmīmāṃsā* but attested in academic Sanskrit discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** सत्ता
**Transliteration:** sattā
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *-tā* (abstract suffix); "being-ness, existence"
**Natural English translation:** being, existence, ontological status
**Context of use:** Used in Vaiśeṣika and Vedānta as a technical term for the property of existence or being, and in general philosophical discourse for the ontological status of an entity. Appears in debates about whether existence is a universal property or a specific relation. Distinguished from *sattva* in being a more abstract ontological term.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** सत्त्व
**Transliteration:** sattva
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *-tva* (abstract suffix); "the state of being, being-ness"
**Natural English translation:** being, existence, essence; also: the quality of goodness/clarity (in Sāṃkhya); courage, vitality
**Context of use:** In Sāṃkhya philosophy, one of the three *guṇas* (fundamental qualities of *prakṛti*), associated with clarity, purity, lightness, and goodness. In general philosophical usage, also denotes the quality of genuine existence or substantiality. In general usage, also means courage, strength of character, or vitality. Appears across philosophical, epic, and medical literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** असत्त्व
**Transliteration:** asattva
**Morpheme breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *sattva* (being, the quality of goodness)
**Natural English translation:** non-existence; absence of the quality of goodness
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical contexts as the negation of *sattva* in its ontological sense, and in ethical contexts as the absence of the *sattva* quality. Less frequent than *sattva* but attested in commentarial literature.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**32.**
**Form:** सत्कार्यवाद
**Transliteration:** satkāryavāda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* + *kārya* (effect, from root *kṛ-*) + *vāda* (doctrine, position); "doctrine that the effect is real (pre-exists in the cause)"
**Natural English translation:** the doctrine of the pre-existence of the effect, the theory that the effect is real in the cause
**Context of use:** A fixed technical term in Sāṃkhya and Vedānta philosophy for the position that the effect genuinely exists in the cause prior to its production, as opposed to *asatkāryavāda* (the effect is new, not pre-existent). Central to debates about causation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**
**Form:** असत्कार्यवाद
**Transliteration:** asatkāryavāda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *asat* + *kārya* + *vāda*; "doctrine that the effect is not real (prior to production)"
**Natural English translation:** the doctrine that the effect is not pre-existent in the cause
**Context of use:** The opposing position to *satkāryavāda*, associated with Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, holding that the effect is a genuinely new entity not pre-existing in the cause. Used in philosophical debate about causation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** सच्चिदानन्द
**Transliteration:** saccidānanda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (being) + *cit* (consciousness) + *ānanda* (bliss); "being-consciousness-bliss"
**Natural English translation:** being-consciousness-bliss (the triple characterization of Brahman)
**Context of use:** A fixed compound in Advaita Vedānta and related traditions designating the ultimate reality (Brahman) as constituted by pure being, pure consciousness, and pure bliss. Widely used in devotional, philosophical, and liturgical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** सन्मार्ग

**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *san-*) + *mārga* (path, way); "the good/right path"
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the path of virtue
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious literature to designate the path of virtuous conduct and right living. Contrasted with *kumārga* (wrong path). Appears in prescriptive contexts contrasting the right way of life with its opposite.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**36.**
**Form:** सद्धर्म

**Morpheme breakdown:** *sat* (sandhi *sad-*) + *dharma*; "genuine dharma, true teaching"
**Natural English translation:** the true teaching, the genuine dharma, the good law
**Context of use:** Used in Buddhist Sanskrit literature (and in Hindu contexts) for the authentic or genuine teaching, as opposed to corrupt or false versions. Appears in the title *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* (Lotus Sūtra) and in discussions of authentic religious doctrine.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**37.**

**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* + *śodhana* (purification, investigation, from root *śudh-*); "investigation/purification of truth"
**Natural English translation:** investigation of truth, truth-seeking, verification
**Context of use:** Used in modern Sanskrit and Sanskrit-influenced discourse (including Gandhi's autobiography, *Satya-śodhana* / *My Experiments with Truth*) for the process of investigating or establishing the truth of a matter. Appears in academic and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**38.**
**Form:** सत्यनारायण

**Morpheme breakdown:** *satya* (true, real) + *Nārāyaṇa* (a name of Viṣṇu); "the true/real Nārāyaṇa"
**Natural English translation:** Satyanārāyaṇa (a form of Viṣṇu worshipped as the embodiment of truth)
**Context of use:** A devotional and ritual name used in the *Satyanārāyaṇa vrata* (a widely performed Hindu votive rite). The name characterizes the deity as the ultimate reality or truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**39.**
**Form:** असत्य
**Transliteration:** asatya
**Morpheme breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *satya*; "not true"
**Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood, lie
**Context of use:** Used across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as the direct negation of *satya*. Denotes a false statement or the quality of being untrue. Common in moral prescriptions against lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**40.**

**Natural English translation:** liar, one who speaks falsely
**Context of use:** Used in legal and ethical literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks falsehoods. Appears in legal texts as a category of unreliable witness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Context of use:** Used in ethical and legal literature to designate the act of uttering falsehoods. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* lists of prohibited conduct and in discussions of witness credibility.
