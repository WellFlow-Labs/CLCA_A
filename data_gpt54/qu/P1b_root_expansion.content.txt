## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **chiqa- / chiqaq**
- **Root form:** **chiqaq** — transliteration: **chiqaq**
- **Participates in P1 items:** **3**
  - *chiqaq*
  - *chiqap*
  - *chiqaq kay*
- **Core meaning contribution:** truth, reality, genuineness, actuality

### 2. **llulla-**
- **Root form:** **llulla** — transliteration: **llulla**
- **Participates in P1 items:** **3**
  - *llulla*
  - *llullakuy*
  - *llullasqa*
- **Core meaning contribution:** falsehood, lying, deception

### 3. **panta- / pantay**
- **Root form:** **pantay** — transliteration: **pantay**
- **Participates in P1 items:** **2**
  - *pantay*
  - *pantasqa*
- **Core meaning contribution:** error, mistake, being wrong

These are the clearest recurring lexical bases in the P1 set. Other important items in the domain (*allin, hucha, iñiy, riqsiy*) are central semantically, but they do not recur across multiple P1 derivatives as strongly as these roots do.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

### Root: **chiqaq**  
If restricted to well-attested common expressions, this root appears to have **relatively few common fixed expansions**. **Fewer than 5** broadly usable expressions are securely identifiable from the P1 base and common usage.

#### 1. **chiqaq**
- **Transliteration:** chiqaq
- **Literal breakdown:** simplex root/adjective
- **English translation:** true; real; genuine; correct
- **Context of use:** Used for statements, reports, claims, and also for things judged genuine rather than fake or merely apparent. It also appears in emphatic speech for sincerity or actuality.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. **chiqap**
- **Transliteration:** chiqap
- **Literal breakdown:** derived adverbial form from *chiqaq*
- **English translation:** truly; really; in truth
- **Context of use:** Used adverbially to insist that something is actually so. Common in discourse where a speaker reinforces seriousness, sincerity, or factual truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. **chiqaq kay**
- **Transliteration:** chiqaq kay
- **Literal breakdown:** *chiqaq* ‘true, real’ + *kay* ‘being, existence’
- **English translation:** truth; reality; true nature
- **Context of use:** Used in reflective, philosophical, or elevated discourse for truth or reality as such. It can also refer to the real nature of a thing as opposed to appearance.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 4. **chiqaqta**
- **Transliteration:** chiqaqta
- **Literal breakdown:** *chiqaq* + accusative/adverbial *-ta*
- **English translation:** truly; really; in a true way
- **Context of use:** Used adverbially in some varieties and textual usage to mark that something is said or done truly or genuinely. It overlaps functionally with *chiqap* in emphatic assertion.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

#### 5. **chiqaq simi**
- **Transliteration:** chiqaq simi
- **Literal breakdown:** *chiqaq* ‘true’ + *simi* ‘word, speech’
- **English translation:** true word; truthful speech
- **Context of use:** Used in moral, rhetorical, or religious discourse for truthful utterance or speech that is not deceptive. It is more elevated and less basic than plain *chiqaq*.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### Root: **llulla**

This root is more productive in ordinary moral and interpersonal evaluation, but the set of **securely common fixed expressions still appears limited**. **Fewer than 5** highly common non-inflectional expansions are clearly available from the present evidence base.

#### 1. **llulla**
- **Transliteration:** llulla
- **Literal breakdown:** simplex root
- **English translation:** lie; falsehood; false; liar; deceitful
- **Context of use:** A central term for falsehood and deception, used both for untrue statements and for persons characterized by lying. It is strongly moral in force.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. **llullakuy**
- **Transliteration:** llullakuy
- **Literal breakdown:** *llulla* + verbal/reflexive derivation
- **English translation:** to lie; to tell lies; to deceive
- **Context of use:** Used for the act of lying or engaging in deception. Common in everyday accusation, moral instruction, and judgments of honesty.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. **llullasqa**
- **Transliteration:** llullasqa
- **Literal breakdown:** *llulla* + participial *-sqa*
- **English translation:** false; deceptive; lied-about
- **Context of use:** Used for a report, claim, representation, or account judged false or deceitful. It can also characterize a person or narrative as marked by deception.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 4. **llulla runa**
- **Transliteration:** llulla runa
- **Literal breakdown:** *llulla* ‘false, lying’ + *runa* ‘person’
- **English translation:** liar; deceitful person
- **Context of use:** A common characterological expression for a person known to lie or deceive. It is used in everyday moral judgment and social criticism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 5. **llulla simi**
- **Transliteration:** llulla simi
- **Literal breakdown:** *llulla* ‘false, lying’ + *simi* ‘word, speech’
- **English translation:** lie; false speech; deceitful word
- **Context of use:** Used for lying speech or false statements, especially in moralizing, rhetorical, or religious contexts. It directly contrasts with truthful or upright speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 6. **llullaq**
- **Transliteration:** llullaq
- **Literal breakdown:** *llulla* + agentive/characterizing *-q*
- **English translation:** liar; one who lies
- **Context of use:** Used for a habitual liar or one characterized by deception. It overlaps with *llulla runa* but is more compact and lexicalized.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### Root: **pantay**

This root is productive around error and mistaken judgment. **Fewer than 5** common fixed expansions are securely identifiable.

#### 1. **pantay**
- **Transliteration:** pantay
- **Literal breakdown:** simplex verbal/nominal base
- **English translation:** error; mistake; to err; to be mistaken
- **Context of use:** A basic term for factual, practical, or cognitive error. It applies to wrong statements, wrong judgments, and mistaken actions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. **pantasqa**
- **Transliteration:** pantasqa
- **Literal breakdown:** *pantay* + participial *-sqa*
- **English translation:** mistaken; wrong; incorrect
- **Context of use:** Used of a statement, answer, belief, or action judged incorrect. It is a common descriptive form for identifying error.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. **pantachiy**
- **Transliteration:** pantachiy
- **Literal breakdown:** *panta-* + causative *-chi(y)*
- **English translation:** to mislead; to cause to err; to confuse
- **Context of use:** Used when one person causes another to make a mistake or to hold a false understanding. It is relevant in interpersonal, pedagogical, and moral contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 4. **pantachisqa**
- **Transliteration:** pantachisqa
- **Literal breakdown:** *pantachiy* + participial *-sqa*
- **English translation:** misled; mistaken through deception or confusion
- **Context of use:** Used of a person or judgment that has been led into error. It appears where responsibility for confusion or misinformation is at issue.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 5. **pantayniyuq**
- **Transliteration:** pantayniyuq
- **Literal breakdown:** *pantay* ‘error’ + possessive/linking + *-yuq* ‘having’
- **English translation:** erroneous; having mistakes; faulty
- **Context of use:** Used for something containing errors, such as an account, text, calculation, or judgment. It is especially relevant in evaluative or corrective contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

#### 6. **pantayman churay**
- **Transliteration:** pantayman churay
- **Literal breakdown:** *pantay-man* ‘into error’ + *churay* ‘put’
- **English translation:** to lead into error; to misdirect
- **Context of use:** Used in discourse about causing someone to go wrong in judgment or action. It overlaps semantically with *pantachiy* but is more phrasal.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Step 3 — Peripheral extensions

The following expressions show root use beyond narrow truth/falsity evaluation and should be flagged as peripheral extensions:

- **chiqaq kay** — extends toward ontology, essence, and reality rather than simple truth-value.
- **chiqaq simi** — shifts toward moral/rhetorical evaluation of speech.
- **llulla runa** — shifts from false statement to stable character judgment.
- **llulla simi** — shifts from propositional falsity to moralized speech behavior.
- **pantachiy** — shifts from being wrong to causing another’s error.
- **pantachisqa** — shifts toward social causation of error rather than simple incorrectness.
- **pantayniyuq** — shifts toward defectiveness/faultiness in objects, texts, or procedures.
