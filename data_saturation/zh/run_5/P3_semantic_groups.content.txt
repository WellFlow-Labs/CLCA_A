# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b: which items collocate with each other, which items are explicitly contrasted in usage, which items share syntactic frames, which items appear in the same discourse contexts, and which items are described in the source data as near-synonyms or antonyms. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that something is the genuine article

**Label:** Genuine-vs.-fake evaluation of objects, goods, and documents

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, credential, or product is authentic or counterfeit. They appear predominantly in commercial, legal, forensic, and art-authentication contexts. The central contrast is between something that is what it claims to be and something that is not, with no necessary implication about the beliefs or intentions of any person.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真 (zhēn) — "genuine, real" (as applied to goods, materials, documents)
- 假 (jiǎ) — "fake, counterfeit"
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — "real vs. fake"
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — "genuine vs. spurious"
- 真伪难辨 (zhēnwěi nán biàn) — "hard to tell genuine from fake"
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — "to distinguish genuine from fake"
- 鉴别真伪 (jiànbié zhēnwěi) — "to authenticate through expert assessment"
- 核查真伪 (héchá zhēnwěi) — "to check and verify authenticity"
- 伪造 (wěizào) — "to forge, to counterfeit"
- 伪造文件 (wěizào wénjiàn) — "to forge documents"
- 伪造签名 (wěizào qiānmíng) — "to forge a signature"
- 伪造货币 (wěizào huòbì) — "to counterfeit currency"
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — "counterfeit, to pass off as genuine"
- 仿冒 (fǎngmào) — "to imitate and pass off as genuine"
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎmào wěiliè) — "fake and inferior goods"
- 假冒伪劣产品 (jiǎmào wěiliè chǎnpǐn) — "counterfeit and substandard products"
- 仿冒品牌 (fǎngmào pǐnpái) — "counterfeit brand"
- 仿冒商品 (fǎngmào shāngpǐn) — "counterfeit goods"
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — "to use fakes to confuse the genuine"
- 打假 (dǎ jiǎ) — "to crack down on fakes"
- 造假 (zào jiǎ) — "to fabricate, to produce fakes"
- 掺假 (chān jiǎ) — "to adulterate"
- 鉴定 (jiàndìng) — "to authenticate, expert appraisal"
- 经专家鉴定 (jīng zhuānjiā jiàndìng) — "upon expert authentication"
- 送去鉴定 (sòng qù jiàndìng) — "to send for authentication"
- 真迹 (zhēnjì) — "authentic original work (of calligraphy or painting)"
- 真本 (zhēnběn) — "authentic original edition"
- 辨伪 (biànwěi) — "to detect forgeries" [MARGINAL]
- 辨伪存真 (biànwěi cún zhēn) — "to distinguish forgeries and preserve authentic works" [MARGINAL]
- 反伪造 (fǎn wěizào) — "anti-counterfeiting"
- 反假冒 (fǎn jiǎmào) — "anti-counterfeiting"
- 逼真 (bīzhēn) — "lifelike, so realistic it could be mistaken for the real thing"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 逼真 sits at the edge of this group: it describes a representation that closely resembles the genuine article but is not itself a forgery. It appears in aesthetic contexts (painting, acting, sound effects) as well as in contexts where the resemblance is so close as to enable deception. 真本 and 辨伪 are primarily scholarly and bibliographic terms and are less central to the commercial and legal core of this group.

## Group 2: Expressions used in the verification and confirmation of reported facts

**Label:** Verification and confirmation of factual claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used in administrative, legal, and journalistic contexts to describe the process of checking whether a reported claim, account, or piece of information corresponds to what actually occurred. They appear in official documents, investigative reports, and news contexts, and they typically involve an agent (an investigator, official, or journalist) who examines evidence and reaches a conclusion about factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**

- 属实 (shǔshí) — "confirmed as factual"
- 经查属实 (jīng chá shǔshí) — "verified upon investigation to be true"
- 情况属实 (qíngkuàng shǔshí) — "the situation is confirmed as factual"
- 核实 (héshí) — "to verify, to confirm"
- 查实 (cháshí) — "to confirm upon investigation"
- 坐实 (zuòshí) — "to nail down as fact, to definitively confirm"
- 如实 (rúshí) — "truthfully, as it actually is"
- 如实反映 (rúshí fǎnyìng) — "to reflect accurately"
- 如实陈述 (rúshí chénshù) — "to state truthfully"
- 如实汇报 (rúshí huìbào) — "to report truthfully"
- 失实 (shīshí) — "not in accordance with the facts, inaccurate"
- 报道失实 (bàodào shīshí) — "the report is inaccurate"
- 严重失实 (yánzhòng shīshí) — "seriously inaccurate"
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — "authenticity, veracity (as a property)"
- 核实真实性 (héshí zhēnshíxìng) — "to verify authenticity"
- 事实性错误 (shìshíxìng cuòwù) — "factual error"
- 实证 (shízhèng) — "empirical evidence; to verify empirically"
- 真凭实据 (zhēnpíng shíjù) — "solid and genuine evidence"
- 实据 (shíjù) — "solid evidence"
- 经过可靠渠道证实 (jīngguò kěkào qúdào zhèngshí) — "confirmed through reliable channels"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 如实 overlaps with Group 1 (it evaluates correspondence to fact) and with Group 5 (it describes honest conduct in reporting). 坐实 is more colloquial and journalistic than the others and sometimes appears in contexts of confirming rumors rather than official verification. 实证 has a more specifically scientific and methodological use that places it partly in a different register from the administrative verification terms.

## Group 3: Expressions used to describe the act of revealing or concealing what is actually the case

**Label:** Disclosure and concealment of the true state of affairs

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe actions directed at the actual facts of a situation — either bringing them to light or suppressing them. They appear in journalistic, political, legal, and narrative contexts and typically presuppose that there is a gap between what is publicly known or claimed and what actually occurred. The central contrast is between exposure and concealment.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — "the truth, the true facts behind appearances"
- 揭露真相 (jiēlù zhēnxiàng) — "to expose the truth"
- 掩盖真相 (yǎngài zhēnxiàng) — "to cover up the truth"
- 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) — "to investigate and establish the truth"
- 了解真相 (liǎojiě zhēnxiàng) — "to know the truth"
- 隐瞒真相 (yǐnmán zhēnxiàng) — "to hide the truth"
- 还原真相 (huányuán zhēnxiàng) — "to reconstruct the truth"
- 真相大白 (zhēnxiàng dàbái) — "the truth comes to light"
- 真相不明 (zhēnxiàng bù míng) — "the truth is unclear"
- 还原事实真相 (huányuán shìshí zhēnxiàng) — "to reconstruct the true facts"
- 谎言终究掩盖不了真相 (huǎngyán zhōngjiū yǎngài bù liǎo zhēnxiàng) — "lies cannot ultimately cover up the truth"
- 原形毕露 (yuánxíng bì lù) — "to show one's true colors, to be exposed"
- 终于原形毕露 (zhōngyú yuánxíng bì lù) — "finally exposed for what they really are"
- 让…原形毕露 (ràng … yuánxíng bì lù) — "to cause someone to be exposed"
- 识破假象 (shípò jiǎxiàng) — "to see through a false appearance"
- 假象 (jiǎxiàng) — "false appearance, misleading phenomenon"
- 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn) — "to eliminate the false and retain the genuine"
- 戳穿谎言 (chuōchuān huǎngyán) — "to expose a lie"
- 揭示本质 (jiēshì běnzhì) — "to reveal the essential nature"
- 认清本质 (rènqīng běnzhì) — "to clearly recognize the true nature"
- 还原真实情况 (huányuán zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "to reconstruct the true state of affairs"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 原形毕露 and 识破假象 involve a moral or evaluative dimension (the thing exposed is typically negative) that places them partly in Group 6. 去伪存真 has a methodological dimension that places it partly in Group 2. 揭示本质 and 认清本质 overlap with Group 7 (essence and surface).

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate the accuracy or precision of statements, measurements, and representations

**Label:** Accuracy and precision of statements and representations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, description, measurement, translation, or account corresponds to what is actually the case, with particular attention to degree of fit rather than a simple true/false judgment. They appear in scientific, technical, journalistic, editorial, and legal contexts, and they frequently appear in assessments of data quality, wording, and reporting.

**Representative expressions:**

- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — "accurate, precise, exact"
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — "accuracy, precision (as a property)"
- 不准确 (bù zhǔnquè) — "inaccurate, imprecise"
- 措辞准确 (cuòcí zhǔnquè) — "the wording is precise"
- 把握准确 (bǎwò zhǔnquè) — "to grasp accurately"
- 表达准确 (biǎodá zhǔnquè) — "to express accurately"
- 正确 (zhèngquè) — "correct, right"
- 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) — "correctness (as a property)"
- 不正确 (bù zhèngquè) — "incorrect"
- 完全正确 (wánquán zhèngquè) — "completely correct"
- 错误 (cuòwù) — "error, mistake, incorrect"
- 犯错误 (fàn cuòwù) — "to make a mistake"
- 纠正错误 (jiūzhèng cuòwù) — "to correct a mistake"
- 承认错误 (chéngrèn cuòwù) — "to admit a mistake"
- 错误百出 (cuòwù bǎi chū) — "riddled with errors"
- 真实 (zhēnshí) — "truthful, factual, authentic"
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — "veracity, authenticity (as a property)"
- 真实感 (zhēnshígǎn) — "sense of reality, feeling of authenticity"
- 不真实 (bù zhēnshí) — "unreal, untrue"
- 反映真实情况 (fǎnyìng zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "to reflect the true state of affairs"
- 失实 (shīshí) — "not in accordance with the facts"
- 言过其实 (yán guò qí shí) — "to exaggerate, to overstate"
- 歪曲事实 (wāiqū shìshí) — "to distort the facts"
- 捏造事实 (niēzào shìshí) — "to fabricate facts"
- 事实性错误 (shìshíxìng cuòwù) — "factual error"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 正确 and 准确 are explicitly distinguished in the source data: 正确 evaluates binary conformity to a standard or rule, while 准确 foregrounds degree of precision. Both are included here because they share the same evaluative contexts (assessments of statements, descriptions, and data). 失实 and 言过其实 overlap with Group 3 (concealment/distortion) and Group 6 (deliberate deception). 真实 overlaps with Group 1 (genuine vs. fake) and Group 7 (reality vs. appearance).

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate persons as honest or dishonest in their conduct

**Label:** Evaluation of persons as honest or dishonest

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize a person's habitual conduct or character as truthful and non-deceptive, or as deceptive and insincere. They appear primarily in moral assessments, character evaluations, educational contexts, and interpersonal discourse. The focus is on the person rather than on a specific statement or object.

**Representative expressions:**

- 诚实 (chéngshí) — "honest, truthful"
- 不诚实 (bù chéngshí) — "dishonest"
- 诚实守信 (chéngshí shǒuxìn) — "honest and trustworthy"
- 为人诚实 (wéirén chéngshí) — "honest in one's conduct toward others"
- 对…诚实 (duì … chéngshí) — "to be honest with [someone]"
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — "sincere, genuine"
- 真诚性 (zhēnchéngxìng) — "sincerity (as a property)" [MARGINAL]
- 真诚相待 (zhēnchéng xiāngdài) — "to treat each other with sincerity"
- 出于真诚 (chūyú zhēnchéng) — "out of genuine sincerity"
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — "sincere, wholehearted"
- 真心话 (zhēnxīnhuà) — "words from the heart, what one truly thinks"
- 真话 (zhēnhuà) — "truthful words, what one really thinks"
- 说真话 (shuō zhēnhuà) — "to tell the truth, to speak honestly"
- 假话 (jiǎhuà) — "false words, what one does not genuinely mean"
- 说假话 (shuō jiǎhuà) — "to say things one does not mean"
- 实话 (shíhuà) — "honest words, what one really thinks"
- 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) — "to tell the truth, to be honest"
- 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō) — "to tell it like it is"
- 虚伪 (xūwěi) — "hypocritical, insincere"
- 虚伪性 (xūwěixìng) — "hypocrisy (as a property)" [MARGINAL]
- 虚伪的面具 (xūwěi de miànjù) — "a mask of hypocrisy"
- 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) — "inconsistent between appearance and conduct"
- 虚情假意 (xūqíng jiǎyì) — "false sentiments and feigned intentions"
- 伪善 (wěishàn) — "false virtue, hypocrisy"
- 伪善者 (wěishànzhě) — "hypocrite"
- 伪君子 (wěi jūnzǐ) — "false gentleman, hypocrite"
- 可靠 (kěkào) — "reliable, dependable, trustworthy"
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) — "reliability (as a property)"
- 不可靠 (bù kěkào) — "unreliable, untrustworthy"
- 信实 (xìnshí) — "trustworthy, faithful to fact" [MARGINAL]

**Notes on overlapping items:** 可靠 and 信实 sit at the edge of this group: they evaluate persons but also evaluate sources, information, and equipment (see Group 2). 真诚 is distinguished in the source data from 诚实 in that it emphasizes inner genuineness of feeling rather than factual truthfulness; it overlaps with Group 6 (sincerity vs. performance). 虚伪, 伪善, and 伪君子 carry strong moral disapproval and overlap with Group 6 (deliberate deception).

## Group 6: Expressions used to describe deliberate deception or the performance of a false identity or state

**Label:** Deliberate deception and false performance

**Definition:** These expressions describe acts in which a person intentionally causes another to hold a false belief, or presents a false identity, state, or appearance. They appear in moral, legal, interpersonal, and political discourse and typically imply intent on the part of the deceiver. The focus is on the act of deceiving rather than on the object being evaluated.

**Representative expressions:**

- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) — "to deceive, deception"
- 欺骗性 (qīpiànxìng) — "deceptiveness (as a property)"
- 欺骗者 (qīpiànzhě) — "deceiver"
- 欺骗消费者 (qīpiàn xiāofèizhě) — "to deceive consumers"
- 自欺欺人 (zì qī qī rén) — "to deceive oneself and others"
- 受到欺骗 (shòudào qīpiàn) — "to be deceived"
- 反欺骗 (fǎn qīpiàn) — "anti-deception"
- 谎言 (huǎngyán) — "lie, falsehood"
- 撒谎 (sā huǎng) — "to tell a lie"
- 编造谎言 (biānzào huǎngyán) — "to fabricate a lie"
- 弥天大谎 (mítiān dà huǎng) — "a monstrous lie"
- 谎言重复一千遍就成了真理 — "a lie repeated a thousand times becomes the truth"
- 假装 (jiǎzhuāng) — "to pretend, to feign"
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — "false pretense, feigned intention"
- 伪装 (wěizhuāng) — "to disguise, to masquerade as"
- 伪装者 (wěizhuāngzhě) — "impostor, masquerader"
- 虚假信息 (xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "false information, misinformation"
- 散布虚假信息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to spread false information"
- 虚假广告 (xūjiǎ guǎnggào) — "false advertising"
- 虚假陈述 (xūjiǎ chénshù) — "false statement, misrepresentation"
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — "false, fraudulent, deceptive"
- 虚假性 (xūjiǎxìng) — "falseness (as a property)" [MARGINAL]
- 弄虚作假 (nòng xū zuò jiǎ) — "to engage in fraud and falsification"
- 伪证 (wěizhèng) — "false testimony, perjury"
- 伪证罪 (wěizhèng zuì) — "the crime of perjury"
- 伪造罪 (wěizào zuì) — "the crime of forgery"
- 假冒他人身份 (jiǎmào tārén shēnfèn) — "to impersonate another person"
- 半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ) — "half true, half false"
- 真真假假 (zhēnzhēn jiǎjiǎ) — "a mixture of truth and falsehood"
- 真真假假，难以分辨 — "a mixture of truth and falsehood, hard to tell apart"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 虚假 overlaps with Group 1 (fake goods) and Group 4 (inaccurate statements). 伪造 and 假冒 overlap with Group 1 (counterfeit objects). 谎言 is distinguished in the source data from 错误 (unintentional error) by the implication of intent; this distinction is what places it in this group rather than Group 4. 半真半假 and 真真假假 describe mixed situations rather than pure deception and sit at the boundary of this group and Group 4.

## Group 7: Expressions used to contrast surface appearance with underlying reality or essential nature

**Label:** Surface appearance vs. underlying or essential nature

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that the true or fundamental character of something differs from its outward appearance, nominal designation, or surface presentation. They appear in philosophical, political, analytical, and critical discourse and typically involve a claim that proper understanding requires going beneath the surface. The contrast is between what something appears to be and what it actually is at a deeper level.

**Representative expressions:**

- 本质 (běnzhì) — "essence, essential nature"
- 本质性 (běnzhìxìng) — "essential (as an attributive)" [MARGINAL]
- 本质上是… (běnzhì shàng shì …) — "essentially is…, fundamentally is…"
- 揭示本质 (jiēshì běnzhì) — "to reveal the essential nature"
- 认清本质 (rènqīng běnzhì) — "to clearly recognize the true nature"
- 实质 (shízhì) — "substance, real nature"
- 实质性 (shízhìxìng) — "substantive (as an attributive)"
- 实质上是… (shízhì shàng shì …) — "in substance is…, amounts to…"
- 抓住实质 (zhuāzhù shízhì) — "to grasp the substance"
- 虚实 (xūshí) — "the real and the illusory; actual vs. apparent"
- 名实 (míngshí) — "name and reality, nominal vs. actual"
- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — "living up to one's name"
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — "not living up to one's name"
- 名副其实的专家 — "a genuine expert who truly deserves the title"
- 徒有虚名 (tú yǒu xū míng) — "to have only an empty reputation"
- 言过其实 (yán guò qí shí) — "to overstate, to say more than the facts warrant"
- 假象 (jiǎxiàng) — "false appearance, misleading phenomenon"
- 识破假象 (shípò jiǎxiàng) — "to see through a false appearance"
- 原形毕露 (yuánxíng bì lù) — "to show one's true colors"
- 真正 (zhēnzhèng) — "genuine, real, truly (as opposed to nominal)"
- 真实情况 (zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "the actual situation"
- 实情 (shíqíng) — "the actual situation, the true state of affairs"
- 实际 (shíjì) — "actual, in reality, in practice"
- 实际上 (shíjì shàng) — "in reality, actually, as a matter of fact"

**Notes on overlapping items:** 实际 and 实际上 are extremely common and appear across many registers; they are included here because their core function is to contrast what is actually the case with what is nominal, theoretical, or claimed. 原形毕露 and 识破假象 overlap with Group 3 (disclosure/concealment) and Group 6 (deception). 名实 is primarily a classical philosophical term and is less central to contemporary usage than the other items. 虚实 has a distinct use in military strategy and in aesthetics (documented in P1b) where the truth-domain meaning is absent or transformed; in those uses it does not belong to this group.

## Group 8: Expressions used in political and ideological discourse about truth, facts, and correct understanding

**Label:** Truth, facts, and correct understanding in political and ideological discourse

**Definition:** These expressions appear predominantly in Chinese political, administrative, and ideological discourse, where they are used to characterize an approach to knowledge, policy, or conduct as grounded in actual facts rather than dogma, wishful thinking, or formalism. They are associated with specific political rhetorical traditions and carry evaluative weight beyond their literal meanings.

**Representative expressions:**

- 实事求是 (shíshì qiúshì) — "to seek truth from facts"
- 求真务实 (qiúzhēn wùshí) — "to seek truth and be pragmatic"
- 求真 (qiúzhēn) — "to seek truth, truth-seeking"
- 务实 (wùshí) — "pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth"
- 真理 (zhēnlǐ) — "truth as a universal principle"
- 追求真理 (zhuīqiú zhēnlǐ) — "to pursue truth"
- 坚持真理 (jiānchí zhēnlǐ) — "to uphold truth"
- 掌握真理 (zhǎngwò zhēnlǐ) — "to possess the truth"
- 真理越辩越明 (zhēnlǐ yuè biàn yuè míng) — "truth becomes clearer through debate"
- 正确 (zhèngquè) — "correct, right" (in political evaluation of direction, line, policy)
- 方向正确 (fāngxiàng zhèngquè) — "the direction is correct"
- 政治上正确 (zhèngzhì shàng zhèngquè) — "politically correct"
- 错误 (cuòwù) — "error" (in political evaluation of conduct or ideology)
- 犯错误 (fàn cuòwù) — "to make a mistake" (including ideological error)
- 弄虚作假 (nòng xū zuò jiǎ) — "to engage in falsification and fraud" (as administrative misconduct)
- 落实 (luòshí) — "to implement, to follow through on" (ensuring policies are actually carried out)
- 谎言重复一千遍就成了真理 — "a lie repeated a thousand times becomes the truth" (critical discourse about propaganda)
- 伪史 (wěishǐ) — "fabricated history"
- 伪政权 (wěi zhèngquán) — "puppet regime, illegitimate government"
- 真善美 (zhēn shàn měi) — "truth, goodness, and beauty" (in ideological and educational rhetoric)

**Notes on overlapping items:** Many items in this group also appear in other groups (正确 in Group 4, 错误 in Group 4, 弄虚作假 in Group 6, 真理 in philosophical discourse). They are placed here because their usage in political and administrative discourse has specific connotations and collocational patterns that distinguish them from their uses in other registers. 落实 is included because its core function — ensuring that what is nominally committed to is actually carried out — is closely related to the name-reality contrast that runs through this group, though it is primarily an administrative rather than an epistemic term. 伪史 and 伪政权 are included because they appear in politically charged historical and political discourse where the designation of something as 伪 (spurious, illegitimate) is itself a political act.

## Group 9: Expressions describing a quality of genuine, unspoiled, or natural authenticity in persons or states

**Label:** Natural or unspoiled genuineness in persons and states

**Definition:** These expressions describe a quality of being genuinely what one is, without artifice, performance, or corruption — typically applied to children, emotions, relationships, or early states of things. They appear in literary, interpersonal, and evaluative contexts and carry a positive evaluative tone. The contrast is not primarily with forgery or deception but with artificiality, performance, or loss of an original state.

**Representative expressions:**

- 纯真 (chúnzhēn) — "pure, innocent, guileless, unspoiled"
- 天真 (tiānzhēn) — "naturally innocent, naive" (positive in children; mixed in adults)
