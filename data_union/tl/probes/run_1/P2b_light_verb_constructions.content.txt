# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Note on scope:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions already documented in P1b (collocations, discourse particles, fixed phrases) are cross-referenced rather than re-documented in full; only constructions not yet captured are given full entries here. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven multi-word expressions that are conventionalized and attested. Compositionally transparent phrases that are not lexicalized are excluded.

---

## Item 1 — Base Form: totoo

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo ba iyon?
- **Transliteration:** Totoo ba iyon?
- **English Translation:** Is that true? / Is that real? / Really?
- **Usage Notes:** Standard interrogative construction for questioning the truth or reality of a reported claim or situation; extremely frequent in spoken Tagalog as an expression of surprise or request for confirmation. Cross-referenced from P1b (*totoo ba?*); the distal demonstrative *iyon* is the most common variant.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.
- **Transliteration:** Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.
- **English Translation:** To tell the truth, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
- **Usage Notes:** *Sa totoo lang* functions as a conventionalized discourse-initial marker of candor, introducing a statement the speaker presents as frank or unguarded; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the most frequent periphrastic honesty-framing construction in spoken Tagalog.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
- **Transliteration:** Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.
- **English Translation:** The truth of the matter is, the situation is more complicated.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang totoo nito* is a fixed topic-marking construction used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement about a specific matter; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard topic-fronted truth-framing construction in formal and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo o hindi, kailangan nating malaman.
- **English Translation:** True or not, we need to find out.
- **Usage Notes:** *Totoo o hindi* is a fixed binary-framing construction presenting truth and falsity as the exhaustive alternatives for a claim under investigation; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak.
- **Transliteration:** Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak.
- **English Translation:** Her crying seemed real. / His crying looked genuine.
- **Usage Notes:** *Parang totoo* is a conventionalized appearance-framing construction asserting resemblance to reality without asserting reality itself; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for evaluating verisimilitude in performance or representation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala.
- **Transliteration:** Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala.
- **English Translation:** The product looked real but turned out to be fake.
- **Usage Notes:** *Mukhang totoo* is a conventionalized appearance-evaluation construction using *mukha* (face/appearance) as a grammaticalized evidential of visual impression; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. The contrast with *peke* in the same sentence is a highly attested pattern in consumer and fraud discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo ang nararamdaman ko.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo ang nararamdaman ko.
- **English Translation:** What I feel is real. / My feelings are genuine.
- **Usage Notes:** Construction applying *totoo* to the validity of emotional experience rather than to a propositional claim; documented in P1b peripheral extensions and cross-referenced here as a conventionalized pattern in interpersonal and therapeutic discourse for validating subjective states.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Sabihin mo ang buong katotohanan.
- **Transliteration:** Sabihin mo ang buong katotohanan.
- **English Translation:** Tell the whole truth.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang buong katotohanan* is a fixed nominal construction paralleling the legal formula "the whole truth"; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for demanding complete disclosure in legal and moral contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon.
- **Transliteration:** Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon.
- **English Translation:** The allegation has no truth to it. / The allegation is groundless.
- **Usage Notes:** *Walang katotohanan* is a fixed negative existential construction used in formal denials and press statements to dismiss a claim as entirely unfounded; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
- **Transliteration:** May katotohanan sa sinabi niya.
- **English Translation:** There is some truth in what he said.
- **Usage Notes:** *May katotohanan* is a fixed partial-affirmation construction acknowledging that a claim contains some truth without fully endorsing it; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction for nuanced epistemic evaluation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Ibunyag ang katotohanan sa likod ng kaso.
- **Transliteration:** Ibunyag ang katotohanan sa likod ng kaso.
- **English Translation:** Reveal the truth behind the case.
- **Usage Notes:** *Katotohanan sa likod ng* is a fixed collocation introducing a revelation of what is actually the case beneath a surface account; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction in investigative journalism and narrative.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Ang paghahanap ng katotohanan ay mahirap.
- **Transliteration:** Ang paghahanap ng katotohanan ay mahirap.
- **English Translation:** The search for truth is difficult.
- **Usage Notes:** *Paghahanap ng katotohanan* is a conventionalized nominal construction framing truth as the object of an active search; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a standard construction in philosophical, journalistic, and investigative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo sa buhay ang mga karakter sa nobela.
- **English Translation:** The characters in the novel are true to life.
- **Usage Notes:** *Totoo sa buhay* is a conventionalized aesthetic-evaluation construction applying *totoo* as a criterion of fidelity to lived experience; documented in P1b peripheral extensions and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro.
- **Transliteration:** Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro.
- **English Translation:** This is absolutely real, not a joke.
- **Usage Notes:** The emphatic reduplication *totoong-totoo* combined with the contrastive denial *hindi biro* ("not a joke") is a highly conventionalized spoken construction for asserting unambiguous reality in the face of expressed or anticipated disbelief; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**New constructions not previously documented:**

---

**Construction 15**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Totoo na.
- **Transliteration:** Totoo na.
- **English Translation:** It's real now. / It's actually happening. / It came true.
- **Usage Notes:** *Totoo na* with the aspect/change-of-state particle *na* ("now, already") is a conventionalized construction marking the transition from a hypothetical, planned, or doubted state to confirmed reality; extremely common in spoken Tagalog when something previously anticipated or disbelieved is confirmed (*Totoo na ang balita* "The news is real now / The news has been confirmed").
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Hindi ko matanggap na totoo ito.
- **Transliteration:** Hindi ko matanggap na totoo ito.
- **English Translation:** I can't accept that this is true. / I can't believe this is real.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction *hindi matanggap na totoo* ("cannot accept as true") is a conventionalized expression of psychological resistance to an unwelcome truth; common in interpersonal and emotional discourse. The verb *tanggap* (to accept, to receive) combined with *totoo* as a complement is a stable collocation in this register.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Kung totoo man o hindi...
- **Transliteration:** Kung totoo man o hindi...
- **English Translation:** Whether true or not... / Regardless of whether it's true...
- **Usage Notes:** *Kung totoo man o hindi* is a conventionalized concessive-conditional construction framing a claim as irrelevant to the truth-value of what follows; common in argumentative and deliberative discourse. The particle *man* adds a concessive force ("even if").
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Naniniwala akong totoo ito.
- **Transliteration:** Naniniwala akong totoo ito.
- **English Translation:** I believe this is true.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction *naniniwala + na + totoo* embeds *totoo* as the complement of the belief verb *naniniwala* (believes); this is the standard construction for expressing first-person epistemic commitment to the truth of a proposition in Tagalog. Extremely common across all registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Example:** Pinapatunayan na totoo ang kanyang sinabi.
- **Transliteration:** Pinapatunayan na totoo ang kanyang sinabi.
- **English Translation:** It is being proven that what he said is true.
- **Usage Notes:** The construction embedding *totoo* as a complement clause after a verification verb (*pinapatunayan*, *napatunayan*, *natuklasan*, etc.) is a productive and conventionalized pattern in legal and journalistic discourse for reporting the confirmation of a claim's truth.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Item 2 — Base Form: katotohanan

*See constructions 8–12 above, documented under Item 1 as the constructions involve the derived nominal. Additional constructions follow.*

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Ang katotohanan ay masakit.
- **Transliteration:** Ang katotohanan ay masakit.
- **English Translation:** The truth hurts. / Truth is painful.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang katotohanan ay masakit* is a conventionalized proverbial construction expressing the culturally recognized idea that truthful disclosure is often painful to receive; functions as a fixed saying in moral and interpersonal discourse. Attested in both spoken and written Tagalog.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Harapin ang katotohanan.
- **Transliteration:** Harapin ang katotohanan.
- **English Translation:** Face the truth. / Confront reality.
- **Usage Notes:** *Harapin ang katotohanan* is a conventionalized imperative construction in motivational, psychological, and moral discourse; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for urging acceptance of an unwelcome truth.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Itago ang katotohanan.
- **Transliteration:** Itago ang katotohanan.
- **English Translation:** Hide the truth. / Conceal the truth.
- **Usage Notes:** *Itago ang katotohanan* is a conventionalized construction describing the suppression of true information; documented in P1b and cross-referenced here. The negative imperative *Huwag itago ang katotohanan* ("Don't hide the truth") is equally attested.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Ang katotohanan ay lalabas din.
- **Transliteration:** Ang katotohanan ay lalabas din.
- **English Translation:** The truth will come out eventually. / The truth will surface in the end.
- **Usage Notes:** *Ang katotohanan ay lalabas din* is a conventionalized proverbial construction expressing the culturally held belief that truth cannot be permanently suppressed; *lalabas* (will come out/emerge) combined with *din* (also, eventually) is a stable collocation in this context. Common in moral and narrative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** katotohanan
- **Example:** Tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan.
- **Transliteration:** Tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan.
- **English Translation:** Defender of truth. / Advocate for truth.
- **Usage Notes:** Documented in P1b and cross-referenced here as a conventionalized nominal construction in journalistic and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Item 5 — Base Form: tama

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tama ka.
- **Transliteration:** Tama ka.
- **English Translation:** You are right. / You are correct.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tama ka* is the standard minimal construction for affirming a person's correctness; one of the most frequent utterances in Tagalog conversation for agreement or confirmation. The subject pronoun varies (*Tama siya* "He/she is right"; *Tama kami* "We are right").
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tama na.
- **Transliteration:** Tama na.
- **English Translation:** That's enough. / Stop it. / That will do.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tama na* with the change-of-state particle *na* is a highly conventionalized construction meaning "enough already" or "stop"; this is a distinct semantic extension of *tama* from its correctness sense toward sufficiency or cessation. Extremely common in spoken Tagalog across all registers and age groups.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tama na, sobra na.
- **Transliteration:** Tama na, sobra na.
- **English Translation:** Enough already, it's too much.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tama na, sobra na* is a fixed paired construction expressing that a limit has been exceeded; functions as a formulaic protest or complaint. The pairing of *tama na* (enough) with *sobra na* (too much already) is highly conventionalized in spoken Tagalog and in political slogans.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tamang-tama ang pagdating niya.
- **Transliteration:** Tamang-tama ang pagdating niya.
- **English Translation:** His/her arrival was perfectly timed. / He/she arrived just at the right moment.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tamang-tama* in constructions of temporal or situational precision is documented in P2a; cross-referenced here as a conventionalized construction for describing exact appropriateness of timing or fit.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi...
- **Transliteration:** Kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi...
- **English Translation:** If my understanding is correct... / If I understand correctly...
- **Usage Notes:** *Kung tama ang aking pagkakaintindi* is a conventionalized hedging construction used to introduce an interpretation or inference while acknowledging the possibility of error; common in formal discussion, academic writing, and careful speech.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tama ba ang aking naiintindihan?
- **Transliteration:** Tama ba ang aking naiintindihan?
- **English Translation:** Is my understanding correct? / Am I understanding this right?
- **Usage Notes:** *Tama ba ang aking naiintindihan* is a conventionalized comprehension-checking construction used in instructional, professional, and interpersonal contexts to verify that one has correctly understood something.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Iyan ang tama.
- **Transliteration:** Iyan ang tama.
- **English Translation:** That's the correct one. / That's the right answer.
- **Usage Notes:** *Iyan ang tama* is a standard identificational construction used in educational and evaluative contexts to designate the correct option or answer; extremely common in classroom and testing discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Natamaan siya ng kanyang mga salita.
- **Transliteration:** Natamaan siya ng kanyang mga salita.
- **English Translation:** He/she was struck by his/her words. / His/her words hit home.
- **Usage Notes:** *Natamaan ng salita* is a conventionalized construction using the completive patient-focus form *natamaan* (was struck/hit) to describe being emotionally affected by words; documented in P2a and cross-referenced here as a stable idiomatic construction in interpersonal discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** tama
- **Example:** Tama ang desisyon mo.
- **Transliteration:** Tama ang desisyon mo.
- **English Translation:** Your decision is right. / Your decision is correct.
- **Usage Notes:** *Tama ang [noun phrase]* is the standard predicate construction for evaluating a decision, action, or judgment as correct; extremely common across all registers. The subject can be any noun phrase referring to a decision, action, statement, or judgment.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Item 6 — Base Form: mali

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** mali
- **Example:** Mali ka.
- **Transliteration:** Mali ka.
- **English Translation:** You are wrong. / You are mistaken.
- **Usage Notes:** *Mali ka* is the standard minimal construction for asserting a person's incorrectness; direct antonym of *Tama ka*. Extremely common in spoken Tagalog for disagreement or correction.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** mali
- **Example:** Mali ang iyong pagkakaintindi.
- **Transliteration:** Mali ang iyong pagkakaintindi.
- **English Translation:** Your understanding is wrong. / You have misunderstood.
- **Usage Notes:** *Mali ang pagkakaintindi* is a conventionalized construction for identifying a misunderstanding; common in clarificatory and corrective discourse across all registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** mali
- **Example:** Kung mali man ako...
- **Transliteration:** Kung mali man ako...
- **English Translation:** If I am wrong... / Even if I am mistaken...
- **Usage Notes:** *Kung mali man ako* is a conventionalized concessive-conditional hedging construction used to introduce a claim while acknowledging the possibility of error; common in careful argumentation and polite disagreement.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** mali
- **Example:** Maling-mali ang iyong pag-iisip.
- **Transliteration:** Maling-mali ang iyong pag-iisip.
- **English Translation:** Your thinking is completely wrong.
- **Usage Notes:** *Maling-mali* in a predicate construction is documented in P2a; cross-referenced here as a conventionalized emphatic correction construction in spoken Tagalog.

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** mali
- **Example:** Huwag kang magkamali.
- **Transliteration:** Huwag kang magkamali.
- **English Translation:** Don't make a mistake. / Don't be wrong.
- **Usage Notes:** *Huwag magkamali* is a conventionalized negative imperative construction warning against error; documented in P2a and cross-referenced here as the standard construction for this function in instructional and cautionary discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

---

**Construction 7**
