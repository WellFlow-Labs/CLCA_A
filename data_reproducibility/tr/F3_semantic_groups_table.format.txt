| Honesty and Moral Character | Terms evaluating the moral character of persons, particularly regarding truthfulness, integrity, and sincerity in speech and action. | Deception and Misleading | Terms describing statements, appearances, or actions that cause or are likely to cause false beliefs, whether intentionally or not. | Fundamental Truth and Reality (Elevated Register) | Terms used in philosophical, religious, or literary contexts to refer to deep, ultimate, or fundamental truth and reality. | Directness and Straightforwardness | Terms describing direct, unmediated, or straightforward manner of action, communication, or spatial movement. Used in contexts of communication style, spatial navigation, and describing approaches that are frank or without circumlocution. This group shows how spatial/physical directness extends metaphorically to communicative and behavioral directness. | doğrudan (directly, straightforwardly), doğrultmak (to straighten, to direct, to aim), doğrultu (direction, line, course), doğruca (directly, straight away), doğru yol (right path, correct way, straight path) | doğru: Core meaning includes straightness but primarily used for correctness; These expressions show extension from spatial to communicative domains | Shows how spatial/physical directness extends metaphorically to communicative and behavioral directness |
| (Ungrouped Items) | Items with multiple group memberships or difficult to classify | **Multiple group memberships:** doğru (Groups 1, 8, 11), gerçek (Groups 2, 1, 10), hak (Groups 8, 10, occasionally 1), sahte/sahtekâr (Groups 2, 4, 5), şüphesiz/kuşkusuz (formed from Group 6 but function like Group 7). **Difficult to classify:** doğru dürüst / doğru düzgün (general adequacy/propriety rather than truth-related), hakkında (grammaticalized postposition with no remaining truth/justice semantics), aslen (geographic/ethnic origin rather than authenticity), doğa (simply means "nature," doesn't participate in truth evaluation) | All items listed represent overlapping or ambiguous cases | Groups 1 and 2 show significant overlap, as do Groups 4 and 5; morphological families may split across groups; some items show semantic extension beyond core truth domain; some items have undergone complete semantic bleaching |
