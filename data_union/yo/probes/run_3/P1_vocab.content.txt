# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Note on script and transliteration:** Yoruba is written in a Latin-based orthography with diacritics marking tone (acute = high, grave = low, unmarked or macron = mid) and vowel quality (subdot for open vowels). The standard orthography used here follows the Yoruba Studies Association / Akin Oyètádé convention as reflected in major Yoruba dictionaries (e.g., Abraham 1958; Bamgboṣe 1966). Tones are marked on the native orthographic forms. No separate transliteration column is provided, as the script is already Latin-based and the orthography is the standard academic form.

## Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** òtítọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalized form; related to *tọ* (to be straight, correct, proper); the *ò-* prefix nominalizes; *-tí-* may be an intensifying or aspectual element (analysis debated)
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, accuracy, correctness
**Usage:** The most widely used Yoruba term for truth in everyday, moral, legal, and religious discourse. It appears in proverbs, court contexts, and Christian/Islamic religious language. It participates in productive constructions such as *sọ òtítọ́* ("to speak the truth") and *ènìyàn òtítọ́* ("a truthful person").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** irọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; morphological decomposition uncertain; sometimes analyzed as related to *rọ* (to be soft, to be easy, to flow), but the semantic connection is not transparent
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, deception
**Usage:** The standard antonym of *òtítọ́*; used across all registers to denote a false statement or act of deception. Appears in fixed expressions such as *sọ irọ́* ("to tell a lie") and in proverbs contrasting truth and falsehood.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** òdodo
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Reduplication of *dodo* or related to *dọ* (to be right, fitting); the reduplication may intensify; also homophonous with *òdodo* meaning "flower/hibiscus" — context disambiguates
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, fairness, rightness
**Usage:** Used in moral, legal, and religious contexts to describe conduct or outcomes that conform to what is right or just. Appears in political discourse and in Yoruba translations of religious texts. Contrasts with *àìdọ́gba* (inequality) and *ẹ̀tọ́* in some contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rọ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Uncertain; sometimes glossed in relation to *rọ* (to think, to be calm); morphological status debated
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness (in some dialects and registers); also "thought," "device"
**Usage:** This form is polysemous and context-dependent; its use specifically in the sense of genuineness or authenticity is attested but not universal across dialects. Caution is warranted in assigning a stable "authenticity" reading.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**5.**
**Form:** gidi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Adjective/adverb; morphological origin unclear; possibly a loanword or early borrowing, though this is debated
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, actual, true (intensifying)
**Usage:** Functions as an intensifier or qualifier meaning "real" or "genuine," as in *ènìyàn gidi* ("a real/genuine person") or *ọ̀rẹ́ gidi* ("a true friend"). Widely used in colloquial and informal registers and increasingly in urban Yoruba. Also used in Yoruba Pidgin-influenced speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** tọ̀nà
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly *tọ* (straight, correct) + *nà* (path/way); compound analysis uncertain
**Typical English translations:** correct path, right way; by extension, correctness of conduct
**Usage:** Used in moral and advisory contexts to describe adherence to the correct or proper course of action. Attested in proverb literature and elder speech. Less common in younger urban registers.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalized form of *tọ* (to be straight, right, correct); *ẹ̀-* is a nominalizing prefix
**Typical English translations:** right, entitlement, correctness, what is due
**Usage:** Used in legal, moral, and social contexts to refer to a right or entitlement, and also to what is correct or proper. Appears in expressions such as *ẹ̀tọ́ rẹ ni* ("it is your right/it is correct for you"). Participates in productive nominal constructions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Nominalized form of *mọ̀* (to know); *ì-* is a nominalizing prefix
**Typical English translations:** knowledge, understanding, awareness
**Usage:** Used broadly for knowledge or understanding, including knowledge of what is true or accurate. Relevant in epistemic contexts where the basis for evaluating truth claims is at issue. Appears in compounds such as *ìmọ̀ ènìyàn* ("human knowledge/understanding").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *gbà* (to receive, accept) + *gbọ́* (to hear, believe); compound nominalization
**Typical English translations:** belief, faith, trust, conviction
**Usage:** Refers to the act or state of believing or trusting, including belief in the truth of a statement or the reliability of a person. Widely used in religious contexts (Christian and Islamic) and in everyday discourse about trust and credence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (to rely on, to trust); the verbal base may decompose further but analysis is not fully settled
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, dependability, confidence in
**Usage:** Used to describe the quality of being reliable or trustworthy, applied to persons, institutions, or statements. Appears in evaluative contexts such as *ènìyàn tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* ("a person one can rely on / a reliable person").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *jẹ́rìísí*; the verbal base relates to witnessing or attesting; possibly influenced by Arabic *shahāda* via Islamic contact, though the Yoruba form is nativized
**Typical English translations:** testimony, attestation, witness, evidence, proof
**Usage:** Used in legal and religious contexts for testimony or attestation that something is true or genuine. Appears in court language and in Islamic religious discourse. Also used more broadly for any form of corroborating evidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; related to *rí* (to see); *ẹ̀-* nominalizes; thus roughly "that which is seen / evidence"
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, witness, sign
**Usage:** Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts to refer to evidence or proof supporting the truth of a claim. Appears in *ẹ̀rí ojú* ("eyewitness evidence," literally "evidence of the eye") and similar compounds. Closely related in usage to *ìjẹ́rìísí* but more general.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** àṣìṣe
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *à-* (nominalizer, often for negative or resultant states) + *ṣìṣe* (doing, action from *ṣe*, to do); thus roughly "wrong-doing" or "mis-doing"
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing, inaccuracy
**Usage:** Used to describe an action, statement, or judgment that is incorrect, mistaken, or morally wrong. Relevant in both epistemic contexts (inaccuracy) and moral contexts (fault, transgression). Appears in expressions such as *ṣe àṣìṣe* ("to make a mistake / to err").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ni
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (to be correct, straight) + *ni* (copula/linker, or part of verbal complex); analysis of the full form is not fully settled
**Typical English translations:** correction, guidance, direction toward what is right
**Usage:** Used in educational, moral, and advisory contexts to refer to the act of correcting someone or directing them toward what is accurate or right. Less frequent than *àṣìṣe* in general discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ojúlówó
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ojú* (face, surface, eye) + *lówó* (in the hand of / possessing); compound; roughly "having a face" or "face-possessing," by extension "genuine, having real substance"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, legitimate
**Usage:** Used to describe something or someone as genuinely what they appear or claim to be, as opposed to counterfeit or pretended. Appears in commercial, social, and moral contexts: *ènìyàn ojúlówó* ("a genuine/authentic person"), *ohun ojúlówó* ("a genuine/real thing").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** àgàbàgebè
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Morphological decomposition uncertain; the form may be reduplicated or compounded; origin debated
**Typical English translations:** pretense, sham, fake behavior, hypocrisy
**Usage:** Used to describe conduct or presentation that is false, pretended, or hypocritical — the opposite of genuine behavior. Appears in social criticism and proverb-like expressions. Attested in literary and colloquial Yoruba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ìtànjẹ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tànjẹ́* (to deceive, to defraud); verbal base *tàn* (to deceive) + *jẹ* (to eat/consume, here in a serial verb construction implying harm or gain)
**Typical English translations:** deception, fraud, trickery, misrepresentation
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception or misrepresentation, often with an implication of material or moral harm to the deceived party. Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts. Closely related to *irọ́* but carries a stronger connotation of intentional manipulation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ìfarahàn
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *farahan* (to appear, to manifest, to reveal oneself); *fara* (body) + *hàn* (to show/appear)
**Typical English translations:** appearance, manifestation, revelation; what something shows itself to be
**Usage:** Used to describe how something presents or reveals itself, relevant in contexts where appearance is evaluated against underlying reality. Appears in philosophical and religious discourse about the nature of things. The gap between *ìfarahàn* and underlying reality is a recurrent theme in Yoruba proverbial thought.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ìwà
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *wà* (to exist, to be); thus roughly "being, existence, character"
**Typical English translations:** character, nature, being, essence, conduct
**Usage:** A central term in Yoruba moral and philosophical discourse referring to a person's fundamental character or nature, which is evaluated as good (*ìwà rere*) or bad (*ìwà burúkú*). Relevant to questions of what a person fundamentally is, as distinct from surface appearance. Widely attested across registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ìwà rere
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ìwà* (character, nature) + *rere* (good, fine); fixed attributive expression
**Typical English translations:** good character, virtue, moral goodness
**Usage:** A fixed and highly frequent expression in Yoruba moral discourse. *Ìwà rere* is treated in Yoruba ethical thought as the foundational virtue, the basis of all other moral evaluations. Appears in proverbs, religious texts, and everyday moral instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ìwà burúkú
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ìwà* (character) + *burúkú* (bad, evil); fixed attributive expression
**Typical English translations:** bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
**Usage:** The standard antonym of *ìwà rere*; used to evaluate persons whose conduct or fundamental nature is judged to be morally deficient or harmful. Widely attested across all registers and genres.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** ìdánwò
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *dán* (to test, to try) + *wò* (to look, examine); compound nominalization meaning roughly "testing and examining"
**Typical English translations:** test, trial, examination, verification
**Usage:** Used for the act of testing or verifying whether something or someone meets a standard of accuracy, quality, or reliability. Appears in educational, legal, and everyday contexts. Relevant to processes of evaluating genuineness or correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** ìdájọ́
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *dá* (to create, establish, judge) + *jọ́* (to resemble, to be fitting, or verbal extension); the compound means roughly "the establishing of judgment"
**Typical English translations:** judgment, verdict, ruling, adjudication
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts for the act of rendering a judgment or verdict on the truth, guilt, or rightness of a matter. Appears in court language and in religious discourse about divine judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** dájúdájú
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Reduplication of *dájú* (to be certain, clear, definite); *dá* (to establish) + *jú* (to surpass, exceed, or aspectual marker); reduplication intensifies
**Typical English translations:** certainly, definitely, surely, without doubt
**Usage:** Used as an adverb or sentence modifier to assert that something is definitely true or accurate, without doubt. Appears across registers as an epistemic intensifier. The reduplicated form is more emphatic than the simple *dájú*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ìfọwọ́sí
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *fọwọ́* (with the hand, to use the hand) + *sí* (to, toward; or verbal extension); roughly "putting the hand to it," i.e., endorsing or confirming
**Typical English translations:** endorsement, confirmation, ratification, authentication
**Usage:** Used in legal, administrative, and social contexts for the act of formally confirming or authenticating something — a document, a claim, a decision. Relevant to the evaluation of whether something is officially genuine or approved.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ìjọra
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *jọra* (to resemble one another); *jọ* (to resemble) + *ra* (reflexive/reciprocal element)
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used to describe the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant in aesthetic and evaluative contexts where resemblance to an original or standard is at issue. Appears in discussions of portraiture, imitation, and comparison.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** àṣà
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Noun; morphological decomposition uncertain; sometimes analyzed as related to *ṣe* (to do) with a nominalizing prefix, but this is not settled
**Typical English translations:** custom, tradition, established practice, norm
**Usage:** Refers to established custom or tradition, which in Yoruba evaluative discourse often serves as a standard against which conduct, objects, or representations are measured for correctness or propriety. Relevant in aesthetic and moral evaluation contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** ìpìlẹ̀
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *pilẹ̀* (to found, establish at the base); *pí* + *ilẹ̀* (ground/earth); roughly "the founding/grounding"
**Typical English translations:** foundation, basis, origin, fundamental nature
**Usage:** Used to refer to the foundational or essential nature of something — what something fundamentally is at its base. Appears in philosophical, religious, and analytical discourse. Relevant to questions about the true or essential nature of things.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
