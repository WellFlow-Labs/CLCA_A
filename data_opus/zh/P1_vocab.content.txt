# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 Core Vocabulary Collection
# Language: Chinese (zh)
# Transliteration Standard: Hanyu Pinyin (with tone marks)

---

**1. 真 (zhēn)**
- Literal: true, real, genuine
- English: true, real, genuine, authentic
- Usage: A foundational term applied to statements, objects, and feelings to assert correspondence with reality or genuineness. Highly productive in compounds (真理, 真实, 真相). Contrasts directly with 假.
- [CORE]

**2. 假 (jiǎ)**
- Literal: false, fake, borrowed
- English: false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
- Usage: Marks something as not genuine, counterfeit, or feigned, applied to objects, claims, and conduct. Productive in compounds (假货, 假装, 虚假). Direct antonym of 真.
- [CORE]

**3. 真实 (zhēnshí)**
- Literal: true + solid/real
- English: real, authentic, truthful
- Usage: Describes accounts, emotions, and situations as corresponding to fact and not fabricated. Common in everyday and journalistic registers when asserting accuracy. Often modifies nouns (真实情况, 真实感受).
- [CORE]

**4. 真理 (zhēnlǐ)**
- Literal: true + principle
- English: truth (abstract/universal)
- Usage: Refers to objective or universal truth, frequent in philosophical, political, and scientific discourse. Treated as something to be sought or possessed (追求真理). More abstract than 真相.
- [CORE]

**5. 真相 (zhēnxiàng)**
- Literal: true + appearance/phase
- English: the truth, the real facts
- Usage: Denotes the actual facts of a situation, especially when concealed or sought after. Common in news, investigation, and legal contexts (揭露真相). Concrete rather than abstract.
- [CORE]

**6. 事实 (shìshí)**
- Literal: matter + solid/real
- English: fact, reality
- Usage: Refers to what is actually the case, contrasted with opinion or fabrication. Pervasive in legal, journalistic, and argumentative contexts (事实上, 摆事实). Highly productive.
- [CORE]

**7. 正确 (zhèngquè)**
- Literal: upright/correct + accurate
- English: correct, right, accurate
- Usage: Evaluates answers, judgments, and decisions as accurate or in accordance with standards. Extremely common in educational, technical, and political registers (政治正确, 正确答案). Antonym of 错误.
- [CORE]

**8. 错误 (cuòwù)**
- Literal: error + mistake
- English: wrong, mistake, error
- Usage: Marks statements, actions, or beliefs as incorrect or mistaken. Used both as noun and adjective across all registers (犯错误, 错误观念). Direct antonym of 正确.
- [CORE]

**9. 对 (duì)**
- Literal: correct, facing
- English: right, correct
- Usage: Very common colloquial term affirming correctness of a statement or judgment. Functions as predicate and interjection (你说得对). Antonym of 错.
- [CORE]

**10. 错 (cuò)**
- Literal: wrong, mistaken
- English: wrong, mistaken
- Usage: Colloquial term marking something as incorrect or erroneous, applied to statements and actions. Functions as predicate and in compounds (说错, 没错). Antonym of 对.
- [CORE]

**11. 准确 (zhǔnquè)**
- Literal: accurate/standard + accurate
- English: accurate, precise
- Usage: Evaluates measurements, descriptions, and predictions for precision and correspondence to actual values. Common in technical, scientific, and journalistic contexts (准确无误). Emphasizes exactness.
- [CORE]

**12. 可靠 (kěkào)**
- Literal: able + to lean on/depend
- English: reliable, dependable, trustworthy
- Usage: Describes sources, people, equipment, and information as trustworthy and dependable. Common across everyday and professional registers (可靠消息, 可靠的人). Productive with 不 (不可靠).
- [CORE]

**13. 可信 (kěxìn)**
- Literal: able + to believe/trust
- English: credible, believable, trustworthy
- Usage: Marks testimony, claims, or persons as worthy of belief. Frequent in evaluative and argumentative contexts (可信度). Slightly more formal than 可靠.
- [CORE]

**14. 真诚 (zhēnchéng)**
- Literal: true + sincere
- English: sincere, genuine
- Usage: Describes persons, attitudes, and expressions as honest and heartfelt, applied chiefly to conduct and feeling. Common in moral and interpersonal contexts (真诚的态度). Carries positive moral valuation.
- [CORE]

**15. 诚实 (chéngshí)**
- Literal: sincere + solid/real
- English: honest, truthful
- Usage: Evaluates persons and statements with respect to honesty and lack of deceit. Central moral term in everyday and educational discourse (诚实守信). Antonym of 撒谎/虚伪.
- [CORE]

**16. 虚伪 (xūwěi)**
- Literal: empty/false + false
- English: hypocritical, false, insincere
- Usage: Describes persons and conduct as hypocritical or feigned, carrying strong negative moral judgment. Common in interpersonal and social criticism (虚伪的人). Contrasts with 真诚.
- [CORE]

**17. 真伪 (zhēnwěi)**
- Literal: true + false
- English: authenticity (genuineness vs. falsity)
- Usage: A fixed binomial referring to the question of whether something is genuine or fake, especially documents, artifacts, and claims. Common in legal, scholarly, and authentication contexts (辨别真伪). Somewhat formal.
- [CORE]

**18. 逼真 (bīzhēn)**
- Literal: to press/approach + true
- English: lifelike, true to life, realistic
- Usage: Describes representations, imitations, and performances as closely resembling reality. Common in aesthetic and descriptive contexts (画得逼真). Emphasizes resemblance rather than genuineness.
- [CORE]

**19. 像 (xiàng)**
- Literal: resemble, image
- English: resemble, be like, similar
- Usage: Very common term asserting resemblance between things or persons. Functions as verb and in comparisons (很像, 像样). Foundational to judgments of likeness.
- [CORE]

**20. 相似 (xiāngsì)**
- Literal: mutually + resemble
- English: similar, alike
- Usage: Describes two or more things as resembling each other in qualities or appearance. Common in descriptive, scientific, and everyday contexts (相似之处). More formal than 像.
- [CORE]

**21. 本质 (běnzhì)**
- Literal: root + nature/substance
- English: essence, intrinsic nature
- Usage: Refers to the fundamental nature of a thing as opposed to appearance, frequent in philosophical and analytical discourse (本质上, 透过现象看本质). Contrasts with 现象/表象.
- [CORE]

**22. 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí)**
- Literal: name + corresponds + its + reality
- English: worthy of the name, true to its name
- Usage: A fixed idiom asserting that something genuinely matches its name or reputation. Used as positive evaluation in formal and journalistic registers. Negative form is 名不副实.
- [CORE]

**23. 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí)**
- Literal: goods + true + price + real
- English: genuine goods at a fair price; genuine, the real thing
- Usage: A fixed idiom asserting authenticity and genuineness, originally commercial but extended to persons and qualities. Common in colloquial and promotional contexts. Carries strong affirmation of genuineness.
- [CORE]

**24. 正宗 (zhèngzōng)**
- Literal: orthodox + lineage/school
- English: authentic, orthodox, genuine
- Usage: Describes food, traditions, and practices as authentic and faithful to an original lineage. Common in culinary and cultural contexts (正宗川菜). Emphasizes orthodox authenticity.
- [CORE]

**25. 地道 (dìdao)**
- Literal: ground + way
- English: authentic, genuine, pure (idiomatic)
- Usage: Colloquial term describing something as authentic, genuine, or up to standard, especially food, language, and craftsmanship (地道的北京话). Conveys faithfulness to a true type. Common in spoken register.
- [CORE]

**26. 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí)**
- Literal: name + not + correspond + reality
- English: not living up to its name, undeserved reputation
- Usage: A fixed idiom asserting a mismatch between name/reputation and actual reality. Used as negative evaluation in formal and critical registers. Negation of 名副其实.
- [CORE]

**27. 真切 (zhēnqiè)**
- Literal: true + close/earnest
- English: vivid, distinct, real and clear
- Usage: Describes perceptions and feelings as vivid, clear, and genuinely felt (真切地感受到). Somewhat literary in flavor, used in descriptive prose. Less common than 真实.
- [MARGINAL]

**28. 确凿 (quèzáo)**
- Literal: certain + chisel/solid
- English: conclusive, irrefutable, well-founded
- Usage: Describes evidence and facts as solid and beyond doubt, chiefly in legal and argumentative contexts (证据确凿). Formal register. Collocates narrowly.
- [MARGINAL]

**29. 真挚 (zhēnzhì)**
- Literal: true + earnest/sincere
- English: sincere, heartfelt, earnest
- Usage: Describes emotions and friendships as deeply sincere, chiefly in literary and formal registers (真挚的感情). Narrower and more elevated than 真诚. Applied to feelings rather than persons directly.
- [MARGINAL]

**30. 如实 (rúshí)**
- Literal: according to + reality
- English: truthfully, faithfully, as it really is
- Usage: An adverbial expression meaning to report or represent something faithfully without distortion (如实汇报). Common in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts. Modifies verbs of reporting.
- [CORE]
