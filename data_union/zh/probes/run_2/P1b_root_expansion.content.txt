# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**
**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 items participating:** 真 (1), 真实 (3), 真相 (5), 真理 (6), 真伪 (11), 失真 (18), 原真性 (23), 表里如一 [implicitly contrasted], 货真价实 (29)
**Count:** 9 items
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts genuineness, authenticity, or correspondence to reality. Functions as the primary positive pole of the truth-evaluation domain, anchoring judgments about whether something is real, accurate, or unfeigned.

---

**Root 2**
**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 items participating:** 真实 (3), 实质 (14), 名副其实 (15), 名不副实 (16), 如实 (17), 失实 (30), 核实 (25), 诚实 (19), 货真价实 (29)
**Count:** 9 items
**Core meaning contribution:** Denotes solidity, factual reality, or substantive content as opposed to mere appearance, name, or form. Contributes the sense of "what is actually the case" or "what has real substance," and frequently marks the factual pole in correspondence-based evaluations.

---

**Root 3**
**Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**P1 items participating:** 假 (2), 虚假 (4), 假冒 (22), 伪装 (27 — via semantic field, not morphologically), 假设 (mentioned in usage note for 假)
**Count:** 4 direct morphological participations (plus semantic field overlap)
**Core meaning contribution:** Marks falsity, inauthenticity, or the counterfeit. Functions as the primary negative pole contrasting with 真, covering both deliberate deception and mere non-genuineness.

**Note on selection:** 假 participates in fewer P1 items morphologically than 真 or 实, but it is the dominant negative root in the domain and highly productive in compounds. It is retained as a third root for expansion. The character 伪 (wěi) is also productive (appearing in 真伪, 伪造, 伪装) but functions more narrowly as a marker of deliberate falsification; it is noted here but not treated as a separate root for full expansion, as its compounds are fewer and largely covered under the 假 expansion.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: 真 (zhēn)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真心
**Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
**Literal breakdown:** true + heart/mind
**Natural English translation:** sincere, wholehearted; sincerity
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether a person's feelings, intentions, or expressions are genuine rather than performed or strategic. Common in interpersonal and moral discourse (e.g., 真心话, words spoken from the heart).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true + sincere/honest
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Applied to persons, attitudes, or expressions to indicate that they are free from pretense or calculation. Widely used in moral evaluation and interpersonal contexts; slightly more formal than 真心.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** true + false
**Natural English translation:** genuine or fake; authenticity; whether something is real or not
**Context of use:** Used to frame the question of authenticity in everyday and commercial contexts (e.g., 分不清真假, unable to tell real from fake). Functions as a compound noun or binomial framing an evaluative question.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal breakdown:** true + upright/correct
**Natural English translation:** genuine, real, true (emphatic); truly, really
**Context of use:** Used as an intensifying modifier to assert that something is genuinely what it claims or appears to be, as opposed to nominal or superficial. Extremely common across all registers (e.g., 真正的朋友, a true friend).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真实性
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ness (nominalization suffix)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, truthfulness, factual accuracy
**Context of use:** The nominalized form of 真实, used in academic, legal, and journalistic discourse to refer to the property of being in accordance with reality (e.g., 新闻的真实性, the truthfulness of news reporting).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真相大白
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng dàbái
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance + greatly + clear/white
**Natural English translation:** the truth has come to light; the facts are fully revealed
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used to describe the moment when concealed facts are fully exposed or clarified. Common in journalistic, legal, and narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**

**Natural English translation:** genuine insight; penetrating and well-founded views

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**

**Literal breakdown:** true + talent + solid/real + learning/knowledge
**Natural English translation:** genuine talent and solid learning; real ability and knowledge
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to evaluate whether a person's skills and knowledge are authentic and substantive, as opposed to superficial or merely credentialed. Common in educational and professional evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**10.**

**14.**

**21.**

**Natural English translation:** the true Son of Heaven; the rightful emperor; the destined ruler

---

**22.**
**Form:** 真空
**Transliteration:** zhēnkōng
**Literal breakdown:** true + empty/void
**Natural English translation:** vacuum; true vacuum
**Context of use:** The standard scientific and technical term for a vacuum (absence of matter). Also used in Buddhist philosophical discourse to refer to the concept of true emptiness (śūnyatā). Common in both scientific and philosophical registers.

**23.**

**Transliteration:** zhēnjūn
**Literal breakdown:** true + fungus/bacterium
**Natural English translation:** true fungi; Fungi (taxonomic kingdom)

**24.**

**Transliteration:** zhēnhé

---

**25.**

**Transliteration:** xiězhēn
**Literal breakdown:** write/depict + true/real
**Natural English translation:** portrait; realistic depiction; photo shoot (especially glamour or idol photography)

**26.**
**Form:** 逼真
**Transliteration:** bīzhēn

**Natural English translation:** lifelike, realistic, true to life, convincingly real

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 认真
**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious; to take seriously

**28.**

**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn

**29.**

**30.**

**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn

**Note:** Additional compounds with 真 exist (e.g., 真气, 真言, 真如 in religious/philosophical contexts; 真数 in mathematics). The 20–30 most common and analytically significant have been listed above.

### ROOT 2: 实 (shí)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 实际
**Transliteration:** shíjì
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + boundary/limit/occasion
**Natural English translation:** actual, practical, realistic; in practice; reality
**Context of use:** One of the most common words in the domain, used to contrast what is actually the case with what is theoretical, nominal, or ideal. Appears across all registers (e.g., 实际上, in actuality; 脱离实际, divorced from reality).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 实际上
**Transliteration:** shíjì shàng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + boundary/occasion + on/above
**Natural English translation:** in actuality, in practice, actually, in fact
**Context of use:** An extremely common adverbial phrase used to introduce a correction or clarification of what is actually the case as opposed to what appears or is claimed. Used across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 事实
**Transliteration:** shìshí
**Literal breakdown:** matter/affair + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** fact; the facts; factual
**Context of use:** The standard term for "fact" in Chinese. Used across legal, journalistic, scientific, and everyday contexts. Appears in compounds such as 事实上 (in fact), 事实证明 (the facts prove), and 基本事实 (basic facts).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 事实上
**Transliteration:** shìshí shàng
**Literal breakdown:** matter/affair + solid/real + on/above
**Natural English translation:** in fact, as a matter of fact, actually
**Context of use:** An adverbial phrase introducing a statement of what is actually the case, often correcting a prior assumption. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 现实
**Transliteration:** xiànshí
**Literal breakdown:** present/manifest + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** reality; realistic; practical
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of the world as it currently exists, as opposed to ideals, fantasies, or abstractions. Used in philosophical, political, and everyday discourse. Also functions as an adjective meaning "realistic" or "pragmatic."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**6.**
**Form:** 现实主义
**Transliteration:** xiànshí zhǔyì

**Context of use:** The standard term for realism as an aesthetic movement or political philosophy. Used in literary criticism, art history, and political theory.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 实情
**Transliteration:** shíqíng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + situation/circumstances
**Natural English translation:** the actual situation; the true state of affairs; the facts of the matter
**Context of use:** Used to refer to the genuine circumstances of a situation, often in contexts where these have been concealed or misrepresented. Common in legal, administrative, and investigative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 实话
**Transliteration:** shíhuà

**Natural English translation:** the truth; truthful words; what one really thinks
**Context of use:** Refers to a truthful statement as opposed to a polite evasion or lie. Appears in common expressions such as 说实话 (to tell the truth) and 实话实说 (to speak plainly and truthfully).

**9.**

**Natural English translation:** to speak the plain truth; to say it as it is; straight talk
**Context of use:** A fixed expression meaning to speak honestly and directly without embellishment or evasion. Used in everyday moral and communicative contexts; also the name of a well-known television talk show.

### ROOT 3: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 假话
**Transliteration:** jiǎhuà

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are cases where the roots 真, 实, and 假 appear in contexts where their contribution to the truth-evaluation domain is shifted, attenuated, or redirected into other semantic or functional domains. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**真 (zhēn) — peripheral extensions:**

- **认真** (rènzhēn): The root 真 here participates in an expression primarily denoting an attitude of seriousness or conscientiousness toward a task, rather than a judgment about factual accuracy or genuineness.

- **真空** (zhēnkōng): 真 contributes to a scientific and technical term (vacuum) and to a Buddhist philosophical concept (true emptiness/śūnyatā), where the "truth" dimension intersects with metaphysical and ontological frameworks distinct from everyday truth-evaluation.

- **真菌** (zhēnjūn) and **真核** (zhēnhé): 真 functions as a taxonomic classifier distinguishing "true" members of a biological category from superficially similar organisms, a usage that operates within scientific classification rather than truth-evaluation per se.

- **真善美** (zhēn shàn měi): 真 appears as one term in a philosophical triad alongside goodness and beauty, situating it within aesthetic and moral philosophy rather than purely epistemic discourse.

- **真命天子** (zhēnmìng tiānzǐ): 真 contributes to a concept of political legitimacy and cosmological mandate, where "true" refers to rightful authority rather than factual accuracy.

- **真人秀** (zhēnrénxiù): 真 participates in a media genre label where "real" functions as a genre marker (reality television) rather than a direct truth-claim about content.

- **真气** (zhēnqì, not listed above): In traditional Chinese medicine and Daoist practice, 真 modifies 气 (vital energy) to denote an authentic or primordial form of vital force, operating within a cosmological rather than epistemic framework.

- **逼真** (bīzhēn): 真 functions as the target of aesthetic approximation — the standard against which a representation is measured — rather than as a direct predicate of truth or falsity.

- **纯真** (chúnzhēn): 真 participates in an expression primarily denoting innocence and unspoiled character, where the evaluative dimension concerns moral purity rather than factual accuracy.

---

**实 (shí) — peripheral extensions:**

- **充实** (chōngshí): 实 contributes to an expression denoting fullness, richness, or substantiality in a general sense (e.g., a full life, enriched content), where the connection to factual accuracy is indirect.

- **踏实** (tāshí): 实 participates in an expression denoting psychological settledness or groundedness, as well as a character trait of steadiness, where the truth-evaluation dimension is attenuated.

- **落实** (luòshí): 实 functions in an expression primarily denoting implementation and concretization of plans or policies, where "real" marks the transition from abstract intention to concrete action rather than a judgment of factual accuracy.

- **扎实** (zhāshí): 实 contributes to an expression evaluating the solidity or thoroughness of skills and knowledge, where the primary dimension is quality and depth rather than truth or falsity.

- **实力** (shílì, not listed above): 实 combines with 力 (power/strength) to denote actual capability or power, where the evaluative dimension concerns capacity rather than truth.

- **实验** (shíyàn, not listed above): 实 participates in the standard term for "experiment," where it marks the empirical or practical dimension of a procedure rather than a truth-judgment about its results.

- **实用** (shíyòng, not listed above): 实 combines with 用 (use) to denote practicality or utility, where the evaluative dimension concerns usefulness rather than truth.

- **写实** (xiěshí): 实 functions as the target of aesthetic representation — the standard of reality against which a depiction is measured — operating within aesthetic evaluation rather than direct truth-assertion.

- **实录** (shílù): 实 participates in a historiographical genre term where it marks fidelity to historical events, situating truth-evaluation within the specific domain of historical record-keeping.

---

**假 (jiǎ/jià) — peripheral extensions:**

- **假期** (jiàqī) and **请假** (qǐngjià): The jià reading of 假 (leave, holiday) is entirely distinct in meaning from the jiǎ reading (false, fake), representing a homographic character with a separate semantic field unrelated to truth-evaluation.

- **假借** (jiǎjiè) in linguistics: 假 participates in a technical term from Chinese philology denoting a category of character formation based on phonetic borrowing, where "false" or "borrowed" marks a relationship between a character's form and its meaning that is distinct from everyday truth-evaluation.

- **假设** (jiǎshè) and **假说** (jiǎshuō): 假 functions in scientific and logical terminology to mark a proposition as provisional or hypothetical rather than as false or deceptive, representing a shift from the negative (false) to the neutral (provisional/conditional).

- **假名** (jiǎmíng) in Buddhist philosophy: 假 participates in a technical philosophical term (conventional designation, prajñapti) where it marks the conventional or provisional nature of linguistic labels, operating within a metaphysical framework concerning the relationship between language and reality.

- **弄假成真** (nòngjiǎ chéngzhēn): 假 appears in an expression describing a process of transformation from feigned to genuine, where the evaluative focus is on the dynamic between the two poles rather than a static judgment of falsity.
