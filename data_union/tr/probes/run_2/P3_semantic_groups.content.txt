# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Preliminary Note

The groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register distribution, and domain restriction observed across P1 and P2. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data supports. Several items appear in more than one group; this is noted explicitly. Items for which no clear grouping is supported are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that a statement or claim corresponds to what is the case

**Label:** Asserting propositional correctness or factual correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate or assert that a statement, claim, or piece of information accurately corresponds to facts or to what actually happened. They appear in contexts where the question at issue is whether something said or reported is accurate. They contrast with expressions in Group 2 (which concern the realness of objects or situations rather than the accuracy of statements) and Group 5 (which concern the absence of fabrication in objects).

**Representative expressions:**

- *doğru* — the primary term for asserting that a statement is correct; used across everyday, educational, and moral contexts
- *doğruluk* — names the property of being correct or true; used in philosophical and everyday evaluation
- *doğru bilgi* — correct information; used in journalistic and educational contexts to distinguish accurate from inaccurate information
- *doğru haber* — accurate news; used in journalistic contexts
- *doğruyu söylemek* / *doğruyu söylemek* — to tell the truth; the canonical collocation for making a true statement
- *doğruluk değeri* — truth value; technical term in formal logic
- *doğruluk koşulu* — truth condition; technical term in formal semantics
- *doğruluk tablosu* — truth table; technical term in formal logic
- *yanlış* — the primary term for asserting that a statement or judgment is incorrect
- *yanlış bilgi* — incorrect information, misinformation
- *gerçeğe aykırı beyan* — statement contrary to fact; used in legal contexts
- *gerçeğe uygun beyan* — statement conforming to fact; used in legal contexts
- *gerçeği söylemek* — to tell the truth; collocation for stating what is factually the case
- *hakikati söylemek* — to speak the truth; more formal or literary variant
- *doğru yanlış sorusu* — a true/false question; educational usage
- *doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek* — to distinguish the true from the false
- *doğruluk payı var* — there is a grain of truth in it
- *asılsız* — unfounded, baseless; used for claims lacking factual basis
- *asılsız iddia* — unfounded claim
- *asılsızlık* — groundlessness, lack of factual basis
- *aslında* — actually, in fact; used to correct a misapprehension about what is the case

**Notes:** *doğru* and *gerçek* overlap here when used predicatively of statements (e.g., *bu doğru* and *bu gerçek* can both mean "this is true/correct"), but *gerçek* more typically asserts that something is real rather than that a statement is accurate; see Group 2. *hakikat* and *hakikaten* belong primarily here but carry a more elevated register; see also Group 7.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to assert that something is real, actual, or not illusory

**Label:** Asserting ontological realness or actuality

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something exists or is the case in reality, as opposed to being fictional, imaginary, virtual, or merely apparent. They appear in contexts where the contrast is between what is real and what is invented, simulated, or illusory, rather than between a correct and an incorrect statement. They are used in philosophical, journalistic, and everyday contexts to ground claims in actual existence or occurrence.

**Representative expressions:**

- *gerçek* (as adjective and noun) — the primary term for what is real or actually the case; contrasted with fiction, appearance, or simulation
- *gerçeklik* — reality, the condition of being real; used in philosophical and psychological contexts
- *gerçekten* — really, truly; used to assert that something is genuinely the case
- *gerçek mi bu?* — Is this real? Is this true?; colloquial expression of disbelief
- *gerçek hayat* — real life; contrasted with fiction or virtual environments
- *gerçek ile kurgu* — reality and fiction; the standard coordinate pair for this contrast
- *gerçek ile kurgu arasındaki sınır* — the boundary between reality and fiction
- *gerçek dışı* — unreal, surreal; outside of ordinary reality
- *gerçek dışılık* — unreality
- *gerçek neden* — the real reason; used to distinguish actual causes from stated ones
- *gerçek yüzünü göstermek* — to show one's true face; reveal true character beneath appearance
- *gerçekleşmek* — to come true, to materialize; describes events or plans that are actualized
- *gerçekleştirmek* — to realize, to carry out; bringing something into actual existence
- *gerçekleşme* — realization, occurrence
- *hakikat* — truth, reality; used in philosophical and literary contexts to name what is essentially real
- *hakikate ulaşmak* — to reach the truth/reality
- *hakikat arayışı* — the search for truth/reality
- *aslında* — actually, in fact; asserts what is actually the case (overlaps with Group 1)
- *aslen* — originally, by origin; used to indicate what something fundamentally is
- *gerçek anlamda* — in the true/real sense; asserts that a predicate applies genuinely rather than nominally
- *gerçek manada* — in the true sense; more literary variant

**Notes:** *gerçek* and *doğru* overlap when used predicatively of statements, but *gerçek* more typically concerns ontological status (is this thing real?) while *doğru* concerns propositional accuracy (is this statement correct?). *hakikat* belongs here and in Group 7 (elevated register). *gerçekleşmek* and *gerçekleştirmek* are included because their core meaning is actualization — bringing something from the domain of the possible or planned into the domain of the real.

## Group 3: Expressions used in contexts of verifying, confirming, or establishing something as true or proven

**Label:** Verification, confirmation, and proof

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it has been checked, confirmed, or formally established. They appear in administrative, legal, journalistic, and scientific registers and typically involve a process or procedure of checking. They contrast with expressions in Group 1 (which assert correctness without necessarily implying a checking procedure) and Group 4 (which concern the absence of fabrication in objects).

**Representative expressions:**

- *doğrulamak* — to verify, to confirm, to validate; the primary verb for the act of checking and confirming
- *doğrulama* — verification, confirmation; the nominalized form
- *doğrulanmak* — to be verified, to be confirmed; passive form
- *doğrulanabilir* — verifiable; technical term in philosophy of science
- *doğrulanabilirlik* — verifiability; technical term in philosophy of science
- *doğrulayıcı* — verifying, confirmatory; describes a process or mechanism that confirms
- *doğrulama kodu* — verification code; digital security term
- *kimlik doğrulama* — identity verification, authentication
- *kimliğini doğrulamak* — to verify one's identity
- *iddiayı doğrulamak* — to confirm the claim
- *haberi doğrulamak* — to confirm the news
- *doğrulama kodu girmek* — to enter a verification code
- *doğruluk testi* — truth test, accuracy test, fact-check
- *doğruluk testi uygulamak* — to apply a truth/accuracy test
- *doğruluk beyanı* — declaration of accuracy
- *kanıtlamak* — to prove, to demonstrate; used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts
- *kanıt* — proof, evidence; the base noun
- *kanıtlama* — proving, demonstration
- *kanıtlanmak* — to be proven
- *kanıtlanmış* — proven, established
- *kanıtlayıcı* — probative, demonstrative
- *kanıtsız* — without evidence, unsubstantiated
- *kanıt sunmak* — to present evidence
- *kanıta dayalı* — evidence-based
- *kanıta dayalı tıp* — evidence-based medicine
- *kanıtlanmış gerçek* — proven fact
- *kanıt yok* — there is no evidence
- *ispat* — proof, demonstration; more formal, primarily legal
- *ispatlamak* — to prove; formal/legal variant
- *ispat yükü* — burden of proof
- *ispat yükünü taşımak* — to bear the burden of proof
- *ispat yükünü tersine çevirmek* — to reverse the burden of proof
- *suçsuzluğunu ispat etmek* — to prove one's innocence
- *ispat edilemez* — cannot be proven
- *ispat vasıtası* — means of proof
- *ispatsız* — without proof
- *yanlışlamak* — to falsify, to refute; used in logical and scientific contexts
- *yanlışlama* — falsification, refutation
- *yanlışlanabilir* — falsifiable
- *yanlışlanabilirlik* — falsifiability
- *yanlışlama ilkesi* — the principle of falsification
- *yanlışlama ölçütü* — the criterion of falsification
- *hipotezi yanlışlamak* — to falsify the hypothesis

**Notes:** *doğrulamak* and *kanıtlamak* overlap in this group but differ in emphasis: *doğrulamak* tends toward checking and confirming (often administrative or journalistic), while *kanıtlamak* tends toward demonstrating through evidence (often legal or scientific). *ispat* is more restricted to formal legal registers than *kanıt*. *yanlışlamak* belongs here because it is a procedure for establishing the epistemic status of a claim, even though it establishes falsity rather than truth.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate whether objects, documents, or identities are genuine rather than fabricated

**Label:** Genuineness versus fabrication of objects and identities

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, product, or identity is what it purports to be, as opposed to being a forgery, counterfeit, or imitation. They appear primarily in legal, commercial, and administrative contexts. They differ from Group 1 (which concerns the accuracy of statements) and Group 2 (which concerns ontological realness) in that the question here is specifically about fabrication or imitation of a concrete thing.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahte* — fake, counterfeit, forged; the primary term for fabricated objects or identities
- *sahtecilik* — forgery, counterfeiting, fraud
- *sahteci* — forger, counterfeiter
- *sahtekâr* — fraud, impostor
- *sahtekârlık* — fraud, imposture
- *sahte para* — counterfeit money
- *sahte kimlik* — false identity, fake ID
- *sahte belge* — forged document
- *sahte imza* — forged signature
- *sahte hesap* — fake account
- *sahte haber* — fake news
- *sahte ürün* — counterfeit product
- *sahte profil* — fake profile
- *sahte doktor* — fake doctor, medical impostor
- *sahte çıkmak* — to turn out to be fake
- *taklit* — imitation, counterfeit, copy
- *taklitçi* — imitator, counterfeiter
- *taklitçilik* — the practice of imitating or counterfeiting
- *taklit ürün* — counterfeit/imitation product
- *taklit etmek* — to imitate, to counterfeit
- *orijinal* — original, genuine (of an object)
- *orijinal ürün* — genuine product
- *orijinal ambalaj* — original packaging
- *orijinal parça* — genuine spare part
- *orijinal baskı* — original edition
- *orijinal olmak* — to be genuine/original (of an object)
- *orijinallik* — originality, authenticity (of an object)
- *asıl* — original, genuine; used for the original version as opposed to a copy
- *asıl belge* — original document
- *hakiki* — genuine, real; used in commercial and formal contexts
- *hakiki deri* — genuine leather
- *sahih* — authentic, sound; used in Islamic scholarly contexts for texts
- *sahihlik* — authenticity, soundness (of texts)

**Notes:** *orijinal* and *asıl* overlap here but differ in register and typical context: *orijinal* is more common in commercial contexts (genuine product vs. copy), while *asıl* is used more broadly for the original or fundamental version of something. *hakiki* overlaps with Group 6 (personal authenticity) when used in expressions like *hakiki dostluk*, but in commercial contexts (*hakiki deri*) it belongs here. *sahih* is restricted to Islamic scholarly usage and is marginal in general contemporary Turkish.

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person's position or claim is justified or warranted

**Label:** Evaluating whether a person is in the right or justified

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person's claim, position, or action is warranted — whether they have grounds for what they assert or do. They appear in interpersonal, legal, and moral contexts and typically involve a judgment about a person rather than about a proposition or object in isolation. The central contrast is between being justified (*haklı*) and being unjustified (*haksız*).

**Representative expressions:**

- *haklı* — right, justified, in the right
- *haklılık* — justification, rightness
- *haklıca* — justifiably, rightly
- *haklı olmak* — to be right, to be justified
- *haklı çıkmak* — to be proven right, to be vindicated
- *haklı gerekçe* — justified reason, valid grounds
- *haklı eleştiri* — justified criticism
- *haklı kılmak* — to justify, to render justified
- *haklı şikâyet* — justified complaint, legitimate grievance
- *haksız* — unjust, wrong, unwarranted, in the wrong
- *haksızlık* — injustice, wrong, unfairness
- *haksızca* — unjustly, wrongfully
- *haksız yere suçlamak* — to accuse unjustly
- *hak vermek* — to concede that someone is right
- *hakkını teslim etmek* — to give someone their due, to acknowledge their merit
- *hak teslimi* — acknowledgment of merit, giving credit where it is due
- *hak mı haksız mı?* — right or wrong? justified or unjust?
- *hakkını vermek* — to give someone what they are due

**Notes:** This group is distinct from Group 1 (propositional correctness) in that the evaluation here is of a person's position or standing rather than of a statement's accuracy. *hak etmek* (to deserve) is related but belongs more naturally to Group 8 (entitlement and rights) because it concerns desert rather than the evaluation of a position as justified. *haklı çıkmak* bridges Groups 1 and 5: it involves both the vindication of a person and the confirmation that their claim was correct.

---

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate whether a person is honest, consistent, or not deceptive in their conduct

**Label:** Evaluating personal honesty, integrity, and non-deception

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons — rather than statements or objects — as honest, consistent between their inner convictions and outward conduct, or as deceptive and misleading. They appear in moral, interpersonal, and character-evaluation contexts. The contrast is between persons who are genuine in their conduct and those who deceive or perform.

**Representative expressions:**

- *dürüst* — honest, upright, straightforward
- *dürüstlük* — honesty, integrity
- *dürüstlük ilkesi* — the principle of honesty/integrity
- *özü sözü bir* — sincere, a person of integrity; whose inner self and word are one
- *doğruluk* — integrity (in the sense of consistency between word and deed); overlaps with Group 1
- *doğru dürüst* — properly, decently; also used of a person as honest and upright
- *yanıltıcı* — misleading, deceptive; applied to statements or representations that lead others into error
- *aldatıcı* — deceptive, fraudulent; implies intentional deception; stronger than *yanıltıcı*
- *aldatıcı reklam* — deceptive advertising
- *aldatıcı davranış* — deceptive conduct
- *sahtekâr* — fraud, impostor; evaluates a person's character as deceptive (overlaps with Group 4)
- *sahtekârlık* — fraud, imposture (overlaps with Group 4)
- *hakiki dostluk* — true friendship; genuine as opposed to superficial or self-interested
- *sahici* — genuine, authentic (colloquial); used to describe persons or feelings that are not performed
- *sahici duygu* — genuine feeling
- *sahici arkadaş* — a genuine friend
- *sahicilik* — genuineness, authenticity (colloquial)

**Notes:** *yanıltıcı* and *aldatıcı* differ in that *yanıltıcı* does not necessarily imply intent (something can be misleading without the speaker intending to deceive), while *aldatıcı* implies intentional deception. *sahtekâr* belongs here when used to evaluate a person's character, and in Group 4 when used to describe the act of producing forgeries. *özü sözü bir* is the most specific expression in this group, being restricted to the evaluation of consistency between inner conviction and outward speech.

---

## Group 7: Expressions that carry a more formal, literary, or elevated register in the truth/reality domain

**Label:** Elevated-register expressions for truth and reality

**Definition:** These expressions cover similar semantic ground to items in Groups 1 and 2 but are distinguished by their consistent appearance in formal, literary, philosophical, or religious registers rather than in everyday speech. They are typically of Arabic origin and carry a weight of seriousness or philosophical depth that their everyday counterparts lack. They are used in contexts where the speaker wishes to invoke a more serious or elevated tone.

**Representative expressions:**

- *hakikat* — truth, reality, the true nature of something; formal, philosophical, literary
- *hakikaten* — truly, really, in truth; formal/literary adverb
- *hakikati söylemek* — to speak the truth; formal/literary collocation
- *hakikate ulaşmak* — to reach the truth; philosophical/literary
- *hakikat arayışı* — the search for truth; philosophical/literary
- *hakikat sonrası* — post-truth; contemporary journalistic/academic usage
- *hakiki* — genuine, real, true; formal or literary adjective
- *hakiki dostluk* — true friendship; literary/moral usage
- *hakikaten öyle* — it is truly so; formal affirmation
- *hakikat cesaret ister* — telling the truth requires courage; literary/moral register

**Notes:** This group does not represent a distinct semantic domain but a distinct register distribution. The items here overlap substantially with Groups 1, 2, and 4 in meaning; what distinguishes them is their consistent restriction to elevated registers. *hakikat sonrası* is an exception in that it appears in contemporary journalistic usage, but it retains the formal weight of *hakikat* as its base.

---

## Group 8: Expressions used in contexts of legal entitlement, rights, and legitimate claims

**Label:** Legal entitlement and rights

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a person holds a recognized entitlement or whether an action, authority, or claim is sanctioned by law or recognized principle. They appear in legal, political, and administrative registers. They are related to Group 5 (being justified) but differ in that the focus here is on formal or institutional entitlement rather than on the interpersonal evaluation of whether someone is in the right.

**Representative expressions:**

- *hak* — right, entitlement; the base term
- *haklar* — rights, entitlements
- *hak etmek* — to deserve, to merit
- *hak kazanmak* — to earn a right, to acquire an entitlement
- *hak ihlali* — rights violation
- *insan hakları* — human rights
- *insan hakları ihlali* — human rights violation
- *hak sahibi* — rights holder
- *hak sahibi olmak* — to be entitled
- *hak aramak* — to seek one's rights
- *hak arama özgürlüğü* — freedom to seek one's rights, access to justice
- *hakkını aramak* — to seek one's own rights
- *hak tanımak* — to recognize a right
- *hak kazandırmak* — to confer a right, to entitle
- *hak yemek* — to deprive someone of their rights
- *telif hakkı* — copyright
- *telif hakkı ihlali* — copyright infringement
- *oy hakkı* — right to vote
- *oy hakkını kullanmak* — to exercise the right to vote
- *hak ve adalet* — right and justice
- *meşru* — legitimate, lawful
- *meşruiyet* — legitimacy
- *meşrulaştırmak* — to legitimize
- *meşrulaşmak* — to become legitimate
- *meşruluk* — legitimacy, lawfulness
- *meşru müdafaa* — self-defense, legitimate defense
- *gayri meşru* — illegitimate, unlawful
- *geçerli* — valid, in force, accepted
- *geçerlilik* — validity
- *geçersiz* — invalid, void, null
- *geçersizlik* — invalidity, nullity
- *geçerliliğini yitirmek* — to lose validity, to expire
- *geçersiz kılmak* — to invalidate, to nullify

**Notes:** *hak* is polysemous and appears in Groups 5 and 7 as well; in this group it functions specifically as a legal or institutional entitlement. *meşru* and *geçerli* overlap in this group: both concern whether something is recognized or sanctioned, but *meşru* tends toward political and moral legitimacy while *geçerli* tends toward procedural or administrative validity (a document, an argument, a rule). *geçerli* also appears in logical contexts (a valid argument), which connects it to Group 3.

---

## Group 9: Expressions used in aesthetic, intellectual, or creative contexts to evaluate originality

**Label:** Originality and non-derivativeness in creative or intellectual work

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate works, ideas, or approaches as original — not copied or derived from something else. They appear primarily in literary criticism, academic writing, and aesthetic evaluation. They differ from Group 4 (genuineness of objects) in that the question here is not whether something is a forgery of a specific original but whether it represents a genuinely new contribution.

**Representative expressions:**

- *özgün* — original, authentic, unique; used in aesthetic and intellectual contexts
- *özgünlük* — originality, authenticity
- *özgün eser* — original work
- *özgün fikir* — original idea
- *özgün olmak* — to be original
- *özgün yaklaşım* — original approach
- *orijinallik* — originality, authenticity (of an object or work); overlaps with Group 4
- *gerçekçilik* — realism; the aesthetic and intellectual movement
- *edebi gerçekçilik* — literary realism
- *siyasi gerçekçilik* — political realism
- *gerçekçi yaklaşım* — realistic approach
- *gerçekçice* — realistically

**Notes:** *özgün* and *orijinal* overlap in this group but differ in typical context: *özgün* is used for creative and intellectual originality (an original idea, an original style), while *orijinal* is more commonly used for the material authenticity of an object (the original product, the original edition). *gerçekçilik* and related forms belong here because in aesthetic and intellectual contexts they evaluate whether a work or approach engages with reality rather than idealization, which is a distinct evaluative dimension from the truth/falsity of statements.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used in contexts of reliability and credibility of sources or witnesses

**Label:** Reliability and credibility of sources, witnesses, and information

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or piece of information can be depended upon or believed. They appear in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts and typically involve an assessment of a source rather than of a specific claim. They differ from Group 1 (propositional correctness) in that the evaluation here is of the source's general trustworthiness rather than of the accuracy of a specific statement.

**Representative expressions:**

- *güvenilir* — reliable, trustworthy, credible
- *güvenilirlik* — reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
- *güvenilmez* — unreliable, untrustworthy
- *güvenilmezlik* — unreliability, untrustworthiness
- *güven* — trust, confidence
- *güvenmek* — to trust, to rely on
- *güvensiz* — insecure, distrustful
- *güvensizlik* — distrust, insecurity
- *inanılır* — believable, credible, plausible
- *inanılırlık* — believability, credibility
- *inanılmaz* — unbelievable, incredible
- *inanılmazlık* — incredibility
- *inandırıcı* — convincing, persuasive, credible
- *inandırıcılık* — convincingness, persuasiveness
- *inandırmak* — to convince, to persuade
- *inanmak* — to believe, to have faith in
- *inanç* — belief, faith, conviction
- *kaynak güvenilirliği* — source reliability/credibility

**Notes:** *güvenilir* and *inanılır* differ in emphasis: *güvenilir* evaluates a source or person as generally dependable, while *inanılır* evaluates whether a specific claim or account is believable. *inandırıcı* is related but focuses on the capacity to produce belief in an audience, which connects it to rhetorical contexts. *inanç* (belief, faith) is included here because it names the state that credibility assessments are concerned with producing or withholding, but it also extends into religious and philosophical domains beyond this group.

## Ambiguous or Overlapping Items

The following items show patterns of use that place them in more than one group, or that do not fit cleanly into any single group. These overlaps are noted without resolution.

---

**doğruluk**
Belongs to Group 1 (propositional correctness: *doğruluk değeri*, truth value) and Group 6 (personal integrity: *doğruluğa bağlılık*, commitment to integrity). The same form covers both the logical property of propositions and the moral property of persons.

---

**hakikat**
Belongs to Group 2 (ontological realness: *hakikate ulaşmak*, to reach reality/truth) and Group 7 (elevated register). Its use in *hakikat sonrası* (post-truth) connects it to Group 1 (propositional truth in public discourse).

---

**hak**
Belongs to Group 5 (being justified: *hak vermek*, to concede someone is right), Group 7 (elevated register, including theological usage), and Group 8 (legal entitlement: *insan hakları*, human rights). The polysemy of *hak* is not resolvable into a single group.

---

**hakiki**
Belongs to Group 4 (genuineness of objects: *hakiki deri*, genuine leather) and Group 7 (elevated register: *hakiki dostluk*, true friendship). The same form is used in commercial authentication and in literary moral evaluation.

---

**aslında**
Belongs to Group 1 (correcting a false statement: *aslında öyle değil*, it's not actually like that) and Group 2 (asserting what something fundamentally is). The adverb functions in both propositional correction and ontological assertion.

---

**asıl**
Belongs to Group 4 (original document: *asıl belge*) and Group 2 (the real/fundamental issue: *asıl mesele*). The same form covers both the material original and the fundamental or essential aspect of something.

---

**sahtekâr**
Belongs to Group 4 (producing forgeries) and Group 6 (evaluating a person's character as deceptive). The form is used both for the specific act of forgery and for the general character trait of fraudulence.

---

**geçerli**
Belongs to Group 8 (administrative and legal validity: *geçerli pasaport*) and Group 3 (logical validity: *geçerli argüman*). The same form covers both procedural acceptance and logical soundness.

---

**gerçekçi**
Belongs to Group 9 (aesthetic realism: *edebi gerçekçilik*) and to a usage pattern not fully captured by any group: the everyday pragmatic use (*gerçekçi olalım*, let's be realistic) which is an evaluative stance toward planning and expectation rather than an aesthetic or epistemic judgment.

---

**inanç**
Belongs to Group 10 (the state that credibility assessments concern) but also extends into religious and philosophical domains (*dini inanç*, religious faith; *inanç özgürlüğü*, freedom of belief) that are not represented in the P1/P2 data as a distinct group.

---

## Items for Which No Clear Grouping Is Supported

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups above, based on the usage patterns documented. This is noted without forcing them into existing groups.

---

**özgürlük** (freedom, liberty)
Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P1. Its derivational family (*özgür*, *özgürce*, *özgürleşmek*, *özgürleştirmek*) operates primarily in the domain of freedom from constraint rather than in the truth/authenticity domain. No consistent pattern of co-occurrence or contrast with the other items in P1/P2 has been identified that would place it in a natural grouping with them.

---

**doğrusal** (linear)
Documented in P1b and P2a as a peripheral extension of the *doğru* root into mathematics and engineering. Its usage is entirely within technical geometric and mathematical contexts and shows no pattern of contrast or co-occurrence with the truth/authenticity vocabulary.

---

**doğrultu** / **doğrultusunda** (direction, in line with)
The spatial and administrative uses of this form (*bu karar doğrultusunda*, in accordance with this decision) operate in a domain of alignment and procedural conformity that is distinct from the truth/authenticity domain. No grouping with other P1/P2 items is supported by the usage data.

---

**doğru açı** (right angle)
A technical geometric term. No grouping with other P1/P2 items is supported.

---

**güvenlik** (security, safety)
Documented as a derivation of *güven* in P2a. Its primary uses (national security, security cameras, institutional safety) are in a domain of physical and institutional security that is distinct from the credibility/trustworthiness domain of Group 10, even though it shares the same root. No grouping with other P1/P2 items beyond its root connection is supported.

---

**sahih** / **sahihlik**
Marked [MARGINAL] in P1. Usage is restricted to Islamic scholarly contexts for evaluating the authenticity of hadith. The domain specificity is too narrow to support grouping with the general commercial and legal authenticity vocabulary of Group 4, and the register is too specialized to group with Group 7.

---

**hakikat sonrası** (post-truth)
A contemporary calque of English *post-truth*. While it uses *hakikat* as its base, its usage pattern in contemporary journalism is distinct from the philosophical and literary uses of *hakikat* documented in Group 7. It is noted here as an item whose grouping is uncertain pending more extensive usage data.
