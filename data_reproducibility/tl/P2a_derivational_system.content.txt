## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### ROOT 1: **totoo** (true, real)

#### Derived Form 1
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Derived Form:** katotohanan
- **Transliteration:** katotohanan
- **Literal Breakdown:** ka- + totoo + -an (circumfix nominalizer)
- **Natural English Translation:** truth, reality, fact
- **Usage Description:** Abstract noun referring to truth as a concept or body of true statements. Used in formal and philosophical contexts when discussing truth itself rather than specific true statements.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Derived Form:** makatotohanan
- **Transliteration:** makatotohanan
- **Literal Breakdown:** maka- + totoo + -an (adjective-forming affix combination)
- **Natural English Translation:** realistic, true-to-life, believable
- **Usage Description:** Describes narratives, depictions, or representations that accurately reflect reality or have verisimilitude. Common in literary and artistic criticism.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 3
- **Base Form:** totoo
- **Derived Form:** pagkatotoo
- **Transliteration:** pagkatotoo
- **Literal Breakdown:** pagka- + totoo (state/quality nominalizer)
- **Natural English Translation:** truthfulness, reality, state of being true
- **Usage Description:** Refers to the quality or state of being true or real. Used in abstract discussions about the nature of truth or reality.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 4
- **Base Form:** totoo

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productivity Patterns:

1. **Highly Productive Roots:**
   - **totoo**: Shows extensive derivation with multiple nominalizers (ka-...-an, pagka-), verbalizers (mag-, -um-), and intensifiers (napaka-, paka-)
   - **tapat**: Shows full verbal paradigm (mag-, reciprocal -an, causative paka-) plus nominalizations
   - **tama**: Shows extensive derivation including causative (i-), progressive forms, and both correctness and physical impact senses
   - **tunay**: Shows full paradigm including causative (pa-...-an) and nominalized causative forms

2. **Moderately Productive Roots:**
   - **mali**: Shows verbal derivation (mag-ka-) and nominalization (pagkaka-) but more limited than positive terms
   - **sinungaling**: Shows standard verbal and nominal derivations despite being itself derived
   - **wasto/hustó**: Show standard derivations but more limited distribution

3. **Limited Productivity:**
   - **peke**: Borrowed root shows some native affixation but limited compared to native roots
   - **sigurado**: Borrowed root shows limited integration with native morphology
   - **talaga**: Functions primarily as particle with minimal derivation

4. **Asymmetries:**
   - Positive truth terms (totoo, tunay, tapat, tama) show significantly more derivational productivity than negative terms (mali, bulaán, huwad)
   - Native roots show greater productivity than borrowed roots (peke, sigurado, hustó)
   - Some forms in P1 are themselves highly derived (mapagkakatiwalaan, kawangis, katulad, kamukha) with limited further derivation

5. **Semantic Extensions:**
   - **tama** shows notable polysemy: correctness → physical hitting/striking
   - **huwad** derivatives show semantic shift: falseness → exemplarity (kahuwaran)
   - Several roots extend from epistemic truth to moral honesty/faithfulness (tapat, matapat)
