# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Indonesian (id)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Derivations are documented only where the derived form is independently attested in use. Paradigm gaps are preserved. Where a morphological slot appears to exist but the specific form is not attested or is marginal, this is noted explicitly. Forms already documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full, but are included in the inventory for completeness. Where a P1 item is itself a derived form (e.g., *kebenaran*, *sebenarnya*), its base is identified and its derivational relationship is described, but it is not re-derived unless further derivatives are independently attested.

Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation: [CORE] = well-attested in standard usage across multiple registers; [MARGINAL] = attested but restricted in register, frequency, or distribution; [UNCERTAIN] = reported or inferrable but attestation is not fully secure.

## 1. Base Form: **benar**

---

**Derived Form 1**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* (circumfix nominalizer, open) + *benar* + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer, close)
**Natural English Translation:** truth, correctness, rightness
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the property of being true or correct, or to a specific true fact. Used across philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday registers; *mencari kebenaran* (to seek the truth) is a stable collocation. One of the most frequent nominalizations in the truth domain.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *mem-* (active transitive prefix, nasal assimilation of *meN-*) + *benar* + *-kan* (causative/applicative suffix)
**Natural English Translation:** to confirm, to justify, to validate; also to correct (someone or something); also to permit
**Usage:** Active transitive verb used in journalistic, institutional, and interpersonal contexts to mean either "to confirm that something is true" or "to justify/excuse an action." In administrative and regulatory language, *tidak dibenarkan* (not permitted) represents a deontic extension of the base meaning. Both the epistemic sense (to confirm that something is true) and the moral/legal sense (to justify or excuse an action) are well established across journalistic, legal, and everyday registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** dibenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *benar* + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to be confirmed; to be permitted, to be justified
**Usage:** Passive counterpart of *membenarkan*. The formula *tidak dibenarkan* is standard in regulations and official documents to mean "not permitted" or "not justified." Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** pembenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *pem-* (process/result nominalizer, nasal assimilation of *peN-*) + *benar* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** justification, confirmation, validation, rationalization
**Usage:** Noun referring to the act or result of justifying or confirming. Used in moral, psychological, legal, and theological discourse; *pembenaran diri* (self-justification) and *pembenaran oleh iman* (justification by faith, in Protestant theological usage) are established collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *se-* (equative/specifying prefix) + *benar* + *-nya* (specifying/nominalizing enclitic)
**Natural English Translation:** actually, in truth, in fact, really
**Usage:** Discourse adverb used to introduce a correction, clarification, or revelation of what is actually the case, frequently contrasting with a prior assumption or appearance. Extremely common in both spoken and written Indonesian across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** sebenar-benarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *se-* + *benar* + full reduplication of *benar* + *-nya*
**Natural English Translation:** the absolute truth, truly and completely, in all honesty
**Usage:** Emphatic form of *sebenarnya*, used in formal declarations, oaths, and solemn statements to assert that what follows is the complete and unqualified truth. Common in legal testimony and formal written declarations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** benar-benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *benar* + full reduplication
**Natural English Translation:** truly, really, genuinely, absolutely
**Usage:** Intensifying adverb used to emphasize the degree or genuineness of a quality or action. Functions as a general intensifier across contexts, including those where no truth claim is being evaluated (*benar-benar cantik* — truly beautiful). Very common in everyday speech and writing across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** membenarkan diri
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *membenarkan* (to justify) + *diri* (self/oneself)
**Natural English Translation:** to justify oneself, to rationalize one's own actions
**Usage:** A stable collocation used in moral and psychological discourse to describe the act of excusing or rationalizing one's own behavior. Closely related to *pembenaran diri* but in verbal rather than nominal form.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** kebenaran hakiki
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *kebenaran* (truth) + *hakiki* (fundamental, essential — from Arabic *ḥaqīqī*, adjectival form of *ḥaqīqa*)
**Natural English Translation:** fundamental truth, ultimate truth, essential truth
**Usage:** Collocation used in philosophical, theological, and literary discourse to refer to truth at its deepest or most essential level, as opposed to surface or partial truth.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** kebenaran mutlak
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *kebenaran* (truth) + *mutlak* (absolute — from Arabic *muṭlaq*)
**Natural English Translation:** absolute truth
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and religious discourse to refer to truth that is unconditional and not relative to perspective or context. Contrasts with *kebenaran relatif*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 11**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** kebenaran relatif
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *kebenaran* (truth) + *relatif* (relative — from Dutch *relatief*)
**Natural English Translation:** relative truth
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and academic discourse to refer to truth conditioned by perspective, context, or framework. Appears in epistemology, social science, and popular intellectual writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** kebenaran ilmiah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *kebenaran* (truth) + *ilmiah* (scientific — adjectival form from Arabic *ʿilm*, knowledge)
**Natural English Translation:** scientific truth
**Usage:** Used in academic and scientific discourse to refer to truth established through scientific method and empirical evidence. Appears in research methodology discussions and science education.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 13**
**Base Form:** benar
**Derived Form:** menegakkan kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *menegakkan* (to uphold, to enforce — *meN-* + *tegak*, upright + *-kan*) + *kebenaran* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** to uphold the truth, to stand up for what is right
**Usage:** Collocation used in legal, moral, and political discourse to describe the act of defending or enforcing truth and justice. Common in formal speeches, journalism, and advocacy writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *benar* productivity:** The *ke-...-an* nominalization (*kebenaran*) and the *meN-...-kan* verb (*membenarkan*) with its passive (*dibenarkan*) and process nominalization (*pembenaran*) are all well-attested and frequent. The *se-...-nya* adverb (*sebenarnya*) and its emphatic reduplicated form are stable lexical items. Full reduplication (*benar-benar*) functions as a general intensifier. The form *pembenar* (one who confirms/justifies; agent noun) is occasionally encountered in formal writing but is substantially less frequent than *pembenaran* and the verbal forms; it is not documented as a separate entry here due to limited attestation relative to the other forms.

## 2. Base Form: **salah**

---

**Derived Form 1**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** kesalahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* + *salah* + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer)
**Natural English Translation:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing
**Usage:** Abstract noun used across everyday, legal, and moral registers to refer to an error, mistake, or act of wrongdoing. *Mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake/fault) is a common collocation. *Kesalahan fatal* (fatal error) and *kesalahan prosedur* (procedural error) are stable collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** menyalahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* (active transitive prefix, nasal assimilation) + *salah* + *-kan* (causative suffix)
**Natural English Translation:** to blame, to fault, to hold responsible
**Usage:** Active transitive verb used in interpersonal, legal, and moral contexts to describe the act of attributing fault or blame to someone. *Jangan menyalahkan orang lain* (Don't blame others) is a common moral instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** disalahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *salah* + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to be blamed, to be held at fault
**Usage:** Passive counterpart of *menyalahkan*, used in interpersonal and legal contexts to describe the state of being blamed or held responsible for an error or wrongdoing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** penyalahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *peN-* (process nominalizer) + *salah* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** blaming, attribution of fault
**Usage:** Noun referring to the act of blaming. Less common than *kesalahan* and *menyalahkan*; appears in formal and academic writing on moral psychology and conflict.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 5**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** menyalahi
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* (active transitive prefix) + *salah* + *-i* (applicative/directional suffix)
**Natural English Translation:** to violate, to contravene, to go against (a rule, norm, or agreement)
**Usage:** Used in legal and regulatory contexts to describe the act of contravening a rule, norm, or agreement. *Menyalahi aturan* ("to violate the rules") and *menyalahi perjanjian* ("to breach an agreement") are stable collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** salah-salahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *salah-salahan* — partial or full reduplication of *salah* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** mutual blaming, blaming each other
**Usage:** Used in informal contexts to describe a situation in which parties blame each other reciprocally. The *-an* suffix with reduplication here carries a reciprocal or iterative sense. Attested in conversational and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 7**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** bersalah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ber-* (stative/possessive prefix) + *salah*
**Natural English Translation:** guilty, at fault, in the wrong
**Usage:** Stative verb/predicate adjective used in legal and moral contexts to describe a state of guilt or culpability. *Terbukti bersalah* (proven guilty) is a standard legal formula in Indonesian court verdicts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** tidak bersalah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *tidak* (negation) + *bersalah* (guilty)
**Natural English Translation:** not guilty, innocent
**Usage:** Standard legal and moral formula used to assert innocence or absence of culpability. The direct counterpart of *bersalah* in legal proceedings and moral evaluation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** salah paham
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *salah* (wrong, mistaken) + *paham* (understanding, comprehension)
**Natural English Translation:** misunderstanding, misconception
**Usage:** Compound noun/predicate used in everyday and formal contexts to describe a failure of mutual understanding. *Terjadi salah paham* (A misunderstanding occurred) is a common collocation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** salah kaprah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *salah* (wrong) + *kaprah* (widespread, common — referring to common practice)
**Natural English Translation:** widespread misconception, commonly accepted error
**Usage:** Fixed expression used to describe an error or misconception that has become so widespread it is accepted as correct. Common in language commentary, journalism, and educational writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 11**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** salah satu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *salah* (one of, some — in this construction) + *satu* (one)
**Natural English Translation:** one of (the), one among
**Usage:** Fixed expression meaning "one of" — used to single out one item from a set. Note: in this construction *salah* does not carry its primary meaning of "wrong/mistaken" but functions as a partitive quantifier. This is a semantic extension documented here for completeness; it does not contribute to the truth-evaluation domain.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** menyalahgunakan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* + *salah* + *guna* (use, function) + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to misuse, to abuse, to misappropriate
**Usage:** Used in legal, administrative, and moral contexts to describe the improper or unauthorized use of something, particularly authority, power, or resources. *Menyalahgunakan wewenang* (to abuse authority) is a standard legal and journalistic collocation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 13**
**Base Form:** salah
**Derived Form:** penyalahgunaan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *peN-* + *salah* + *guna* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** misuse, abuse, misappropriation
**Usage:** Noun form of *menyalahgunakan*, used in legal, administrative, and policy contexts. *Penyalahgunaan narkoba* (drug abuse) and *penyalahgunaan wewenang* (abuse of authority) are high-frequency collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *salah* compounds:** An extensive series of *salah* + [verb/noun] compounds (*salah paham*, *salah tafsir*, *salah sangka*, *salah langkah*, etc.) represents productive compounding patterns rather than affixal derivations in the strict sense. The partitive use of *salah* in *salah satu* and *salah seorang* represents a grammaticalized function. No *pe(m)-* agentive form (*penyalah*, "one who blames/wrongs") is sufficiently attested to include; this gap is preserved.

## 3. Base Form: **asli**

---

**Derived Form 1**
**Base Form:** asli
**Derived Form:** keaslian
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* + *asli* + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer)
**Natural English Translation:** authenticity, genuineness, originality
**Usage:** Abstract noun used in legal, commercial, artistic, cultural, and identity contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or original. *Mempertanyakan keaslian dokumen* (to question the authenticity of a document) and *keaslian karya seni* (authenticity of an artwork) are common collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** asli
**Derived Form:** mengasli
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* + *asli*
**Natural English Translation:** to authenticate, to verify as genuine; to become original again, to revert to the original state
**Usage:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to describe the act of verifying or certifying that something is genuine; less common than *mengesahkan*. Also encountered in contexts of restoration or return to an original condition. Less frequent than other derivatives in this family.
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** asli
**Derived Form:** aslinya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *asli* + *-nya* (specifying enclitic)
**Natural English Translation:** originally, in its original form, the original (of it)
**Usage:** Used in everyday and formal speech to refer to the original version or state of something, or to introduce what something originally was. *Aslinya dokumen ini berbahasa Belanda* (Originally, this document was in Dutch).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *asli* productivity:** The *ke-...-an* nominalization (*keaslian*) is well-attested and is the primary productive derivation. Verbal derivation from *asli* is not productive; the *meN-* form (*mengasli*) is attested but marginal to uncertain. The enclitic *-nya* form (*aslinya*) functions as a discourse adverb. No *meN-...-kan* causative (*mengaslikan*, "to make genuine/original") is sufficiently attested; this gap is preserved. The root participates extensively in compounding and collocation rather than in morphological derivation.

## 4. Base Form: **palsu**

---

A standard term in Indonesian criminal law.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** palsu
**Derived Form:** dipalsukan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *palsu* + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to be falsified, to be forged, to be counterfeited
**Usage:** Passive counterpart of *memalsukan*, used in legal and journalistic contexts to describe objects or documents that have been fraudulently altered or fabricated.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** palsu
**Derived Form:** pemalsuan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *pem-* (process nominalizer) + *palsu* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** falsification, forgery, counterfeiting, fraud
**Usage:** Noun used in legal contexts to refer to the act or crime of falsification or forgery. *Pemalsuan dokumen* (document forgery) and *pemalsuan uang* (counterfeiting currency) are standard legal collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
**Base Form:** palsu
**Derived Form:** kepalsuan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* + *palsu* + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer)
**Natural English Translation:** falseness, inauthenticity, fraudulence, the quality of being fake
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality or state of being false or inauthentic. Less common than *pemalsuan* in legal contexts; appears more in literary, moral, and philosophical discourse to describe the quality of falseness in a person or situation.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 5**
**Base Form:** palsu
**Derived Form:** pemalsu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *pe-* (agentive nominalizer) + *malsu* (phonological reduction of *palsu* after prefix)
**Natural English Translation:** forger, counterfeiter, falsifier
**Usage:** Agentive noun referring to a person who falsifies or forges. Used in legal and journalistic contexts. *Pemalsu uang* (currency counterfeiter) and *pemalsu dokumen* (document forger) are attested collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *palsu* productivity:** The root *palsu* (from Portuguese *falso*) supports a full set of active, passive, agentive, and process derivations via standard Indonesian morphology.

## 5. Base Form: **nyata**

---

**Derived Form 1**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** kenyataan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* + *nyata* + *-an* (circumfix nominalizer)
**Natural English Translation:** reality, fact, actuality
**Usage:** Abstract noun used across philosophical, journalistic, legal, and everyday registers to refer to what is actually the case. *Menghadapi kenyataan* (to face reality) is a common collocation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** menyatakan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* (active transitive prefix) + *nyata* + *-kan* (causative suffix), with phonological reduction
**Natural English Translation:** to state, to declare, to express, to announce
**Usage:** Active transitive verb used in formal, journalistic, official, and everyday contexts to describe the act of making something known or declaring something. *Ia menyatakan bahwa tuduhan itu tidak benar* (He stated that the accusation was untrue). *Menyatakan sikap* ("to state one's position") and *menyatakan perang* ("to declare war") are stable collocations. The semantic connection to the root meaning of *nyata* (manifest, evident) is present in the sense of making something explicit or public; the meaning has shifted toward "to declare/state."
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** dinyatakan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *nyata* + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to be stated, to be declared, to be pronounced
**Usage:** Passive counterpart of *menyatakan*, used in official, legal, and journalistic contexts. *Dinyatakan bersalah* (declared/found guilty) and *dinyatakan sah* ("declared valid") are standard legal formulas.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** pernyataan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *per-* (nominalizer) + *nyata* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** statement, declaration, announcement
**Usage:** Noun referring to a formal or public statement or declaration. *Pernyataan resmi* (official statement), *pernyataan pers* (press statement), and *pernyataan sikap* (statement of position) are common collocations in journalistic and administrative language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** nyata-nyata
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *nyata* + full reduplication
**Natural English Translation:** clearly, evidently, manifestly, obviously
**Usage:** Intensifying adverb used to emphasize that something is clearly or manifestly the case. *Ia nyata-nyata berbohong* (He was clearly/manifestly lying) and *nyata-nyata melanggar hukum* ("clearly violating the law") are typical usage patterns. Common in spoken, journalistic, and legal registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** senyatanya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *se-* + *nyata* + *-nya*
**Natural English Translation:** as it actually is, in reality, in actual fact
**Usage:** Discourse adverb used in formal and literary registers to assert that what follows reflects actual reality. Less common than *sebenarnya* but attested in formal writing and journalism.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 7**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** menyatai
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meN-* + *nyata* + *-i* → verb with *-i* suffix
**Natural English Translation:** (no standard English equivalent; approximately: to make manifest to, to reveal to)
**Usage:** This form is encountered in some formal and literary texts but is substantially less common than *menyatakan*. Its usage is not fully stable across registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 8**
**Base Form:** nyata
**Derived Form:** menampakkan kenyataan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *menampakkan* (to reveal, to show — *meN-* + *tampak*, visible + *-kan*) + *kenyataan* (reality)
**Natural English Translation:** to reveal reality, to show the truth of the matter
**Usage:** Collocation used in literary, journalistic, and philosophical discourse to describe the act of making reality visible or apparent. Not a lexicalized compound but a stable collocation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *nyata* productivity:** The root *nyata* is highly productive. The *ke-...-an* nominalization (*kenyataan*) and the *meN-...-kan* / *di-...-kan* / *per-...-an* set are all well attested. The form *penyataan* (parallel to *pernyataan*) is occasionally encountered but *pernyataan* is the dominant nominalization in standard usage; *penyataan* is not documented as a separate entry here.

## 6. Base Form: **sah**

---

**Derived Form 1**
**Base Form:** sah
**Derived Form:** mengesahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *meng-* (active transitive prefix) + *e* (linking vowel) + *sah* + *-kan* (causative suffix)
**Natural English Translation:** to validate, to ratify, to legalize, to authenticate, to endorse, to officiate
**Usage:** Active transitive verb used in legal, administrative, and political contexts to describe the act of officially approving or ratifying a document, law, or decision. *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru* (The parliament ratified the new law). *Mengesahkan pernikahan* (to officiate/validate a marriage) is also attested. Standard term in legislative and notarial language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
**Base Form:** sah
**Derived Form:** disahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *sah* + *-kan*
**Natural English Translation:** to be ratified, to be validated, to be legalized
**Usage:** Passive counterpart of *mengesahkan*, used in official announcements, legal documents, and news reporting. *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan* (The law has been ratified). *Undang-undang disahkan* ("the law was ratified") is a standard formula in news reporting on legislation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
**Base Form:** sah
**Derived Form:** pengesahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *peng-* (process nominalizer) + *e* + *sah* + *-an*
**Natural English Translation:** ratification, validation, legalization, authentication
**Usage:** Noun referring to the act or process of official validation or ratification. *Pengesahan konstitusi* (ratification of the constitution), *pengesahan dokumen* (document authentication), and *pengesahan undang-undang* (ratification of a law) are common collocations.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
**Base Form:** sah
**Derived Form:** keabsahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal Breakdown:** *ke-* + *absah* (formal variant of *
