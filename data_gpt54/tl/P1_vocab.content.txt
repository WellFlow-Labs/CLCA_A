1. **totoo**  
   **Transliteration:** totoo  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** reduplicated form historically related to *toto* / emphatic native form  
   **Typical English translations:** true; real; genuine; correct

   **Usage:** *Totoo* is one of the most common Tagalog words for saying that a statement is true or that something is real rather than fake. It is used for factual claims, sincerity, and authenticity, as in *Totoo ba iyan?* or *totoong kaibigan*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **katotohanan**  
   **Transliteration:** katotohanan  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-han*; “truth; reality; what is true”  
   **Typical English translations:** truth; reality; fact

   **Usage:** *Katotohanan* is a standard abstract noun used in formal and everyday contexts for “truth” or “the truth of the matter.” It appears in legal, philosophical, journalistic, and moral discourse, as in *ang katotohanan ng pangyayari*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **tama**  
   **Transliteration:** tama  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
   **Typical English translations:** correct; right; accurate

   **Usage:** *Tama* is widely used to evaluate answers, judgments, actions, and statements as correct or right. It can refer to factual correctness, procedural correctness, or moral rightness depending on context, as in *Tama ang sagot mo* or *Hindi tama ang ginawa niya*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **wasto**  
   **Transliteration:** wasto  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
   **Typical English translations:** proper; correct; accurate; appropriate

   **Usage:** *Wasto* is common in somewhat more formal usage for correctness, propriety, or proper form. It is often used for grammar, procedure, reasoning, and official conduct, as in *wastong gamit* or *wastong proseso*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **wastong**  
   **Transliteration:** wastong  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** ligatured form of *wasto* before a following modifier  
   **Typical English translations:** proper; correct; appropriate

   **Usage:** *Wastong* is the attributive form commonly seen before nouns, especially in educational, bureaucratic, and public-information language. Typical phrases include *wastong baybay*, *wastong asal*, and *wastong impormasyon*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **tumpak**  
   **Transliteration:** tumpak  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
   **Typical English translations:** exact; accurate; precise; correct

   **Usage:** *Tumpak* is used when emphasis falls on precision or exact match, especially in measurements, descriptions, and statements. It is common in expressions like *tumpak na sagot* or *tumpak ang sinabi mo*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **eksakto**  
   **Transliteration:** eksakto  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** borrowing from Spanish/English “exact”  
   **Typical English translations:** exact; exactly; precise

   **Usage:** *Eksakto* is widely used in colloquial and semi-formal Tagalog for exactness or precise correspondence. It can evaluate numbers, timing, wording, or resemblance, as in *eksakto ang sukat* or *Eksakto!* in agreement.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **tiyak**  
   **Transliteration:** tiyak  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
   **Typical English translations:** certain; definite; sure

   **Usage:** *Tiyak* is used for certainty and definiteness, often in relation to whether something is established or reliable. It appears in statements like *Hindi pa tiyak ang resulta* or *tiyak na impormasyon*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **sigurado**  
   **Transliteration:** sigurado  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** borrowing from Spanish *seguro*  
   **Typical English translations:** sure; certain; guaranteed

   **Usage:** *Sigurado* is very common in everyday speech for certainty, assurance, and reliability. It can refer to confidence in a claim, a dependable outcome, or a secure condition, as in *Sigurado ka ba?* or *siguradong panalo*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **mapagkakatiwalaan**  
    **Transliteration:** mapagkakatiwalaan  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** *ma-pagka-tiwala-an*; “able to be trusted / worthy of trust”  
    **Typical English translations:** trustworthy; reliable; dependable

    **Usage:** This word is used for persons, institutions, sources, and testimony judged to be reliable or deserving of trust. It is common in discussions of character, media credibility, and social relations, as in *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* or *mapagkakatiwalaang kaibigan*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **maaasahan**  
    **Transliteration:** maaasahan  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *asa* with adjectival/abilitative morphology; “can be relied on”  
    **Typical English translations:** reliable; dependable; trustworthy

    **Usage:** *Maaasahan* is a common adjective for someone or something that can be counted on. It is used for people, services, information sources, and performance, as in *maaasahang empleyado* or *maaasahang datos*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

    **Usage:** *Tapat* evaluates a person’s character or speech as honest, loyal, or morally straight. It is used in interpersonal, moral, and political contexts, as in *tapat siyang tao* or *maging tapat sa pagsasalita*.

13. **matapat**

    **Typical English translations:** honest; faithful; sincere

    **Usage:** *Matapat* is a very common descriptive form for a person, statement, or intention judged to be honest or sincere. It often appears in moral and relational contexts, such as *matapat na lingkod-bayan* or *matapat na pahayag*.

14. **tunay**

    **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
    **Typical English translations:** real; genuine; true; authentic

    **Usage:** *Tunay* is used for genuineness, authenticity, and real identity, often with nouns. It is common in phrases like *tunay na ginto*, *tunay na kaibigan*, and *tunay na dahilan*.

15. **totohanan**  
    **Transliteration:** totohanan  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *totoo* with nominal morphology; “realness / seriousness”  
    **Typical English translations:** seriousness; for real; in earnest

    **Usage:** *Totohanan* occurs in expressions like *sa totohanan* or in contrast with joking or pretending. It is less central than *katotohanan* and often concerns whether something is meant seriously or is actually real.  
    **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

16. **pekeng**

    **Typical English translations:** fake; counterfeit; bogus

    **Usage:** *Pekeng* is the common attributive form used before nouns to mark something as not genuine. It is frequent in everyday and legal-commercial contexts, as in *pekeng dokumento* or *pekeng produkto*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **peke**  
    **Transliteration:** peke  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
    **Typical English translations:** fake; counterfeit; false

    **Usage:** *Peke* is widely used for objects, documents, identities, and even emotions judged to be not genuine. It can also be used predicatively, as in *Peke iyan* or *peke ang pirma*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **huwad**

20. **akma**

    **Typical English translations:** fitting; appropriate; suitable; apt

    **Usage:** *Akma* is used when something matches, suits, or corresponds properly to a situation, standard, or model. It can evaluate wording, behavior, artistic form, or legal response, as in *akmang parusa* or *akmang paglalarawan*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **angkop**

    **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
    **Typical English translations:** appropriate; suitable; fitting

    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **salig**  
    **Transliteration:** salig  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic lexical form  
    **Typical English translations:** trust; reliance; confidence; basis

    **Usage:** In Tagalog, *salig* is mostly encountered in formal compounds and expressions involving reliance or grounding, such as *mapananaligan* and *walang saligan*. It relates to whether a claim, judgment, or confidence has a dependable basis.  
    **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

23. **saligan**  
    **Transliteration:** saligan  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** from *salig* + *-an*; “basis; foundation”  
    **Typical English translations:** basis; foundation; grounds

    **Usage:** *Saligan* is used in formal and academic language for the basis or grounding of a claim, argument, or judgment. It is common in phrases like *walang saligan ang paratang* and *saligang prinsipyo*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **walang saligan**  
    **Transliteration:** walang saligan  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “without basis/foundation”  
    **Typical English translations:** baseless; unfounded; groundless

    **Usage:** This fixed expression is common in legal, journalistic, and argumentative contexts for accusations, rumors, or conclusions lacking evidence or justification. It directly evaluates a representation or claim as unsupported, as in *walang saligang akusasyon*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **beripikado**  
    **Transliteration:** beripikado  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** borrowing from English “verified”  
    **Typical English translations:** verified; authenticated; confirmed

    **Usage:** *Beripikado* is used in media, bureaucracy, and digital contexts for information, accounts, or documents that have been checked and confirmed. It is especially common in contemporary institutional language, though less native in form than older Tagalog vocabulary.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **beripikahin**  
    **Transliteration:** beripikahin  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** borrowed stem + *-hin*; “to verify”  
    **Typical English translations:** verify; check; authenticate

    **Usage:** This verb is used for checking the truth, accuracy, or authenticity of information, identity, or records. It is common in administrative, journalistic, and online-safety contexts, as in *beripikahin ang impormasyon*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

27. **wasto ba**  
    **Transliteration:** wasto ba  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “correct/proper + question particle”  
    **Typical English translations:** is it correct?; is it proper?

    **Usage:** This fixed interrogative expression is used when asking whether a statement, form, action, or procedure is correct or proper. It is especially natural in instructional and evaluative settings, though it remains compositionally transparent.  
    **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

28. **sa totoo lang**  
    **Transliteration:** sa totoo lang  
    **Literal meaning / morphology:** “in the true only / to tell the truth”  
    **Typical English translations:** honestly; to tell the truth; in truth

    **Usage:** This is a very common discourse expression used to frame a statement as candid or truthful. It often introduces personal evaluation, admission, or correction, as in *Sa totoo lang, hindi ako kumbinsido*.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
