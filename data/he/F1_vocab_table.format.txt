# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — F1 Vocabulary Table

| Form | Transliteration | Literal Meaning | Common Translations | Usage Description | Epistemic Status |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| אמת | emet | truth, firmness, stability (from root א-מ-נ) | truth, true, truthfulness | The primary term for truth in Hebrew, used across all registers from biblical to modern contexts. Appears in moral, legal, philosophical, and everyday discourse to denote correspondence with reality or factual accuracy. | [CORE] |
| שקר | sheker | falsehood, deception (from root ש-ק-ר) | lie, falsehood, deception, false | The standard antonym of emet, denoting falsehood or deception. Used in moral condemnation of dishonesty and in factual contexts to indicate something is not true. | [CORE] |
| נכון | nakhon | correct, established, ready (from root כ-ו-נ) | correct, right, true, accurate | Common in modern Hebrew for correctness or accuracy, particularly in factual or procedural contexts. | אמין | amin | reliable, trustworthy (from root א-מ-נ) | reliable, trustworthy, credible, faithful | Describes persons, sources, or information that can be trusted or relied upon. Emphasizes reliability and trustworthiness rather than factual truth alone. | [CORE] |
| מדויק | meduyak | precise, exact (passive participle from root ד-י-ק) | precise, accurate, exact | Indicates precision and exactness, particularly in technical, scientific, or detailed contexts. Emphasizes correspondence to fine detail rather than general truth. | [CORE] |

| תקף | takef | valid, in force (from root ת-ק-ף) | valid, in effect, sound | Used primarily in legal and logical contexts to indicate validity of arguments, documents, or claims. Denotes that something is in force or holds. | [CORE] |
| מוצדק | mutsдak | justified, warranted (passive participle from root צ-ד-ק) | justified, warranted, legitimate | Indicates that something is justified or has legitimate grounds. Used in moral and logical argumentation to assess whether claims or actions are warranted. | [CORE] |
| באמת | be'emet | in truth, truly (prepositional phrase) | truly, really, indeed, in truth | Fixed expression used to emphasize truthfulness or reality of a statement. Functions as an adverb or discourse marker to strengthen assertions. | [CORE] |

| מזויף | mezuyaf | forged, fake (passive participle from root ז-י-ף) | fake, forged, counterfeit, false | Describes objects, documents, or representations that are deliberately falsified or counterfeit. Emphasizes intentional deception. | [CORE] |
| אמת מידה | amat mida | measuring rod, standard (compound: truth + measure) | criterion, standard, yardstick | Fixed expression denoting a standard or criterion by which something is evaluated. Used in contexts of assessment and judgment. | [CORE] |
| ודאי | vadai | certain, sure (from root י-ד-ע) | certain, sure, definite | Indicates certainty or definiteness of knowledge or facts. Used to express high confidence in the truth of statements. | [CORE] |
| מוכח | mukhaḥ | proven, demonstrated (passive participle from root י-כ-ח) | proven, demonstrated, established | Indicates that something has been proven or demonstrated through evidence or argument. Common in scientific and legal contexts. | [CORE] |
| מעשי | ma'asi | practical, actual (from ma'ase "deed, act") | practical, actual, real | Describes what is practical or actual as opposed to theoretical. Used to assess whether something works in reality. | [CORE] |
| שווא | shav | vain, false, worthless (from root ש-ו-א) | vain, false, worthless, empty | Biblical and literary term for falsehood or vanity, particularly in moral contexts. Appears in religious discourse and elevated registers. | [MARGINAL] |
| ברור | barur | clear, obvious (from root ב-ר-ר) | clear, obvious, evident | Indicates clarity or obviousness of truth or facts. Used when something is manifestly true or evident. | [CORE] |
| אמונה | emuna | ʾ-m-n + feminine noun pattern | faith, belief, trust | Used in religious contexts for faith in God, and in secular contexts for trust or confidence in persons, institutions, or ideas. Central to theological discourse but also common in everyday expressions of trust. | [CORE] |
| להאמין | leha'amin | causative verb form of ʾ-m-n | to believe, to trust, to have faith | The standard verb for believing something to be true or trusting someone. Used across all registers from casual conversation to formal discourse. | [CORE] |

| אמן | amen | ʾ-m-n (fixed liturgical form) | amen, so be it, truly | Liturgical response affirming the truth or desirability of what has been said. Used in religious services and adopted into secular contexts for emphatic agreement. | [CORE] |
| נאמנות | ne'emanut | ne'eman + abstract noun suffix -ut | faithfulness, loyalty, fidelity | Describes the quality of being faithful or loyal, used in personal relationships, professional ethics, and discussions of textual or historical accuracy. | [CORE] |

| באמונה | be'emuna | in/with + faith/trust | faithfully, in good faith, trustingly | Adverbial expression indicating that something is done with trust, sincerity, or good faith. Used in legal contexts and descriptions of sincere conduct. | [CORE] |
| מהימנות | meheymanut | meheyman + abstract noun suffix -ut | reliability, credibility, authenticity | Formal term for the quality of being reliable or credible, particularly of sources, documents, or testimony. Common in academic and legal writing. | [CORE] |
