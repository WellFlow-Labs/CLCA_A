### Notes on Mapping:

**[1] BECOME vs. REVELATION:**
Finnish shows a systematic preference for REVELATION-type inchoatives in the truth domain. While the general inchoative pattern (*-u-/-y-*) exists, the most common examples involve verbs with inherent "reveal/verify" semantics:
- *paljastua* "be revealed" (< *paljastaa* "reveal")
- *todentua* "be verified" (< *todentaa* "verify")
- *toteutua* "be realized" (< *toteuttaa* "realize")

The pattern *"X becomes true"* is not expressed as **\*tosi + tulla* "become" but rather through these revelation-type inchoatives. Simple state change to truth/falsity without revelation semantics has **NO COMMON EXPRESSION**.

**[2] CAUSATION (character) gap:**
Finnish lacks productive constructions for "make someone truthful/honest/a liar." The causative pattern applies to factual states (*todistaa* "prove," *vääristää* "distort") but not to character transformation. Circumlocution required:
- *tehdä jostakuin rehellinen* "make someone honest" (analytic causative, marked as non-idiomatic)
- *muuttaa joku valehtelijaksi* "change someone into a liar" (translative case, not a standard pattern)

## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION Substitutes for BECOME

Finnish systematically uses REVELATION-type inchoatives where other languages might use simple BECOME constructions. The truth domain strongly prefers verbs encoding discovery/manifestation:

- **Attested**: *Totuus paljastuu* "The truth is revealed" [CORE]
- **Attested**: *Totuus todentuu* "The truth is verified" [implied in P4a]
- **Not attested**: *\*Totuus tulee todeksi* "The truth becomes true" [not idiomatic]

This pattern extends to realization of plans/predictions (*toteutua* "be realized") where the inchoative encodes both state change AND manifestation of a prior potential state.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION Share Construction

Finnish does not distinguish VERIFICATION from DISCONFIRMATION at the constructional level. Both use:
- **Pattern**: Causative verb + translative case marking result state
- **Verification**: *todistettiin oikeaksi* "proven correct"
- **Disconfirmation**: *todistettiin vääräksi* "proven false"

The construction is symmetric; only the result adjective differs. No specialized "disconfirm" construction exists.

### 2.3 Negation: Analytic with Obligatory Case Marking

Finnish negation is analytic (particle *ei*) but triggers obligatory partitive case on predicates:
- **Positive**: *Se on totta* (partitive already required with abstract noun)
- **Negative**: *Se ei ole totta* (partitive obligatory in negation)

The fixed expression *ei pidä paikkaansa* "does not hold" functions as a formal alternative to simple negation, particularly for claims/statements. This is a lexicalized negation pattern specific to truth evaluation.

### 2.4 Asymmetry: Factual vs. Character Causation

A clear asymmetry exists between:
- **Factual causation**: Fully productive (*todistaa* "prove," *vääristää* "distort," *todellistaa* "realize")
- **Character causation**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION**

Finnish readily expresses "make X true/false" but lacks idiomatic means to express "make someone truthful/honest." This represents a conceptual restriction on causative application to character traits in the truth domain.

### 2.5 Multiple Nominalization Strategies

Finnish employs multiple nominalization patterns without clear functional distinction:
- **Abstract quality**: *-uus/-yys* (*totuus* "truth," *rehellisyys* "honesty")
- **Action/process**: *-minen* (*todentaminen* "verifying")
- **Result/product**: *-us/-e/-ma/-os* (*todiste* "proof," *todistus* "certificate," *toteamus* "statement")
- **Agent**: *-ja* (*todistaja* "witness")

Choice of suffix is often lexically determined rather than semantically predictable. The system shows high morphological richness but limited semantic transparency in suffix selection.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

**CAUSATION (character)**: No productive construction for "make someone truthful/honest/a liar"
- **Evidence**: P4a Construction #22 notes causative absence for character terms
- **Status**: Requires circumlocution with analytic causatives (*tehdä jostakuin X*)
- **Type**: Morphosyntactic gap — the causative derivation pattern exists but does not apply to character transformation in truth domain

**Simple BECOME without REVELATION**: No idiomatic expression for "X becomes true" without revelation/verification semantics
- **Evidence**: All P4a inchoative examples involve revelation verbs (*paljastua*, *todentua*, *toteutua*)
- **Status**: Conceptual restriction — state change in truth domain requires manifestation/discovery component
- **Type**: Lexical gap within productive morphological pattern

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

**Agent noun from "speak truth"**: No simple agent noun for "truth-teller"
- **Evidence**: P4a Construction #7 notes absence of simple agent from *puhua totuutta*
- **Attested alternatives**: Compound *totuudenpuhuja* or descriptive *rehellinen ihminen* "honest person"
- **Type**: Lexical gap — pattern exists (*-ja* productive) but not applied to this concept

**Causative from *aito* "genuine"**: No causative *\*aitouttaa* "make genuine"
- **Evidence**: P4a Construction #22 notes absence
- **Type**: Lexical gap — causative pattern productive but not extended to this adjective

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

**Character transformation**: Systematic absence of causatives for character traits
- **Scope**: Affects *rehellinen* "honest," *vilpitön* "sincere," and related character terms
- **Interpretation**: Finnish conceptualizes character traits as non-causable in truth domain
- **Contrast**: Factual causation fully productive (*todistaa*, *vääristää*)

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Frequentative derivation**: Productive only for *valhe* → *valehdella* "to lie"
- **Evidence**: P4a Construction #19 notes *-lla/-llä* pattern not productive beyond this example
- **Missing**: *\*totuudella*, *\*todistella* not in standard usage
- **Type**: Lexical restriction on otherwise productive pattern

**Negative prefix productivity**: *Epä-* less established in truth domain than elsewhere
- **Evidence**: P4b rates *epätosi* as [MARGINAL]; *väärä* preferred
- **Pattern**: *Epä-* productive generally but competes with established antonyms in truth domain
- **Type**: Preference pattern rather than absolute gap

## 5. Summary of Key Findings

1. **REVELATION dominates BECOME**: Finnish truth domain strongly prefers inchoatives with inherent revelation/verification semantics over simple state change.

2. **Symmetric VERIFICATION/DISCONFIRMATION**: No constructional distinction; both use causative + translative pattern with different result adjectives.

3. **Factual vs. Character asymmetry**: Causative derivation productive for factual states but blocked for character transformation.

4. **Rich nominalization system**: Multiple productive patterns with lexically determined suffix selection.

5. **Case-based grammar**: Partitive (negation, abstract predicates), translative (result states), inessive (fixed expressions) central to truth-domain constructions.

6. **Limited structural gaps**: Most gaps are lexical (specific items not formed) rather than constructional (patterns unavailable).
