# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** correct, right
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate

Used to affirm that a statement, fact, or claim corresponds to reality. Also used in moral contexts to mean "right" or "proper." Participates in derivation: *kebenaran* (truth, correctness), *membenarkan* (to justify, to confirm), *sebenarnya* (actually, in truth).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** salah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** wrong, mistaken
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, false, mistaken

Used to indicate that a statement, action, or judgment is incorrect or improper. Appears in moral and legal contexts as well as factual ones. Derivations include *kesalahan* (error, fault, wrongdoing) and *menyalahkan* (to blame, to fault).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** asli
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** original, native
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, original, real

Applied to objects, documents, persons, and cultural products to indicate they are not copies, forgeries, or imitations. Contrasts with *palsu* and *tiruan*. Commonly used in legal, commercial, and aesthetic contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** palsu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** false (from Portuguese *falso*)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged

Used of documents, currency, goods, and statements that are deceptive or not genuine. Carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception than *salah*. Derivations include *memalsukan* (to falsify, to forge) and *pemalsuan* (falsification, forgery).

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** nyata
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** manifest, evident
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, evident, concrete

Indicates that something is perceptible, factual, or not merely theoretical or imagined. Used in both everyday and formal registers. Related forms include *kenyataan* (reality, fact) and *menyatakan* (to state, to declare).

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** sungguh
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** truly, earnestly
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, seriously

Used as an intensifier to assert sincerity or reality of a claim or state. Appears in the fixed expression *sungguh-sungguh* (in earnest, genuinely). Also used in *bersungguh-sungguh* (to be earnest, to be serious about something).

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** tepat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** precise, fitting
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, appropriate

Used to evaluate whether something fits, corresponds, or is correctly timed or placed. Applied to measurements, descriptions, decisions, and actions. Derivations include *ketepatan* (accuracy, precision) and *menepati* (to fulfill, to keep to).

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** akurat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** accurate (from Dutch *accuraat*)
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise

Used primarily in formal, journalistic, and technical registers to describe data, information, or reporting that is free from error. Slightly more restricted in register than *tepat*. Related form: *keakuratan* (accuracy).

**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** sahih
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*)
**Literal meaning:** sound, valid (Arabic: healthy, correct)
**Typical English translations:** valid, authentic, sound, correct

Used in religious (especially Islamic), legal, and academic contexts to indicate that something meets the required conditions for validity or correctness. Commonly applied to hadith, legal documents, and arguments. Related form: *mengesahkan* (to validate, to ratify).

**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** sah
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Arabic *ṣaḥḥa*)
**Literal meaning:** valid, lawful
**Typical English translations:** valid, legal, legitimate, official

Used in legal, administrative, and religious contexts to indicate that an act, document, or status is formally recognized and binding. Contrasts with *tidak sah* (invalid, illegitimate). Derivations include *mengesahkan* (to legalize, to ratify) and *pengesahan* (ratification, validation).

**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** absah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid, legitimate
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, lawful

Closely related to *sah* and used in similar legal and formal contexts, though somewhat more formal or literary in register. The relationship between *absah* and *sah* is not fully transparent to all speakers. Marked as slightly more restricted in distribution.

**[MARGINAL]**

**12.**
**Form:** jujur
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** honest, straight
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere, frank

Used to evaluate persons, statements, and conduct as free from deception or concealment. A central term in moral evaluation. Derivations include *kejujuran* (honesty) and *berjujur* (to be honest, to confess).

**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** bohong
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, untruthful

Used of statements or persons that deliberately misrepresent reality. Contrasts with *jujur*. Derivations include *berbohong* (to lie), *pembohong* (liar), and *kebohongan* (lie, falsehood).

**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** dusta
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

A near-synonym of *bohong*, but somewhat more formal or literary in register. Used in religious and moral discourse. Derivations include *berdusta* (to lie) and *pendusta* (liar).

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** tulus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** sincere, pure
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, wholehearted

Used to evaluate the inner motivation or intention behind an action or statement as free from hidden agenda or pretense. Often applied to feelings, intentions, and expressions. Related form: *ketulusan* (sincerity).

**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** ikhlas
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Arabic *ikhlāṣ*)
**Literal meaning:** sincerity, purity of intention (Arabic: purification)
**Typical English translations:** sincere, wholehearted, selfless, accepting

Used to describe actions or attitudes performed without expectation of reward or recognition, particularly in religious and moral contexts. Carries a stronger connotation of selfless acceptance than *tulus*. Related form: *keikhlasan* (sincerity, selflessness).

**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** murni
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** pure, unadulterated
**Typical English translations:** pure, genuine, unadulterated, sheer

Applied to substances, motives, and abstract qualities to indicate freedom from contamination, mixture, or corruption. Used in both physical and figurative senses. Related form: *kemurnian* (purity).

**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** tiruan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** imitation (from *tiru*, to imitate)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, copy, replica, fake

Used of objects or performances that replicate an original without being the original. Does not necessarily imply deceptive intent, unlike *palsu*. Participates in the derivational family of *meniru* (to imitate) and *peniru* (imitator).

**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** otentik
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Dutch *authentiek*)
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine

Used in formal, academic, and legal registers to describe documents, works, or statements that are genuine and of verified origin. More restricted in register than *asli*. Related form: *keaslian* is preferred in informal contexts where *otentik* might otherwise appear.

**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** valid
**Transliteration:** N/A (borrowing from English/Dutch)
**Typical English translations:** valid

Used in academic, scientific, and formal administrative contexts to describe arguments, data, or procedures that meet required standards. More restricted to technical registers than *sah* or *sahih*. Related form: *validitas* (validity).

**[MARGINAL]**

**21.**
**Form:** keliru
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, erroneous
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, erroneous, wrong

Used to indicate that a belief, judgment, or action is based on error or misunderstanding, without necessarily implying moral fault. Contrasts with *salah* in that it more strongly foregrounds inadvertent error. Related form: *kekeliruan* (error, mistake).

**[CORE]**

**22.**
**Form:** hakiki
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Arabic *ḥaqīqī*)
**Literal meaning:** real, essential (Arabic: relating to truth/reality)
**Typical English translations:** true, real, essential, fundamental

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal literary contexts to indicate the essential or fundamental nature of something, as opposed to its surface appearance. Related to *hakikat* (essence, true nature). More restricted to formal registers.

**[MARGINAL]**

---

**23.**
**Form:** hakikat
**Transliteration:** N/A (from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*)
**Literal meaning:** truth, essence, reality (Arabic: true nature of a thing)
**Typical English translations:** essence, true nature, reality, fundamental truth

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal discourse to refer to the essential or underlying nature of something, as opposed to its outward form. Appears in expressions such as *hakikat manusia* (the true nature of humanity). Related to *hakiki*.

**[MARGINAL]**

**24.**
**Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** *ke-benar-an* (nominalization of *benar*)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, validity

The nominalized form of *benar*, used to refer to truth as an abstract quality or to the correctness of a specific claim or state of affairs. Widely used in moral, legal, philosophical, and everyday contexts. A highly productive and central form.

**[CORE]**

---

**25.**
**Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** *se-benar-nya* (in its truth/correctness)
**Typical English translations:** actually, in truth, in fact, really

A fixed adverbial form derived from *benar*, used to introduce a correction, clarification, or disclosure of what is actually the case. Extremely common in spoken and written Indonesian. Functions as a discourse marker signaling contrast with prior assumption.

Used to describe conduct, statements, or interpretations that depart from a correct, standard, or expected norm. Applied in moral, legal, doctrinal, and factual contexts. Related forms: *penyimpangan* (deviation, distortion, aberration).
