# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE/CORRECT (benar)

### Pattern: menjadi benar
**Form:** menjadi benar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become true/correct"
**Usage:** Standard inchoative construction indicating transition to truth or correctness. Common in contexts where statements, predictions, or situations are verified or proven correct.

**Example:**
- **ramalan itu menjadi benar**
  "that prediction became true"
  
**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Very natural and commonly used.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **terbukti benar** (ter-+bukti+benar) — "proven true" (emphasizes evidential basis)
- **ternyata benar** (ternyata+benar) — "turns out to be true" (evidential discovery)

**Notes:** The inchoative "menjadi" is the default strategy. "Terbukti benar" adds evidential emphasis. "Ternyata benar" is extremely common in narrative contexts marking epistemic discovery.

## 2. BECOMING FALSE/WRONG (salah)

### Pattern: menjadi salah
**Form:** menjadi salah
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become wrong/incorrect"
**Usage:** Less common than "menjadi benar" but grammatically well-formed. Used when something previously correct becomes incorrect, or when error is discovered.

**Example:**
- **jawaban yang tadinya benar menjadi salah**
  "the answer that was previously correct became wrong"

**Naturalness:** [MARGINAL] — Grammatically acceptable but less frequently attested than discovery constructions.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **ternyata salah** (ternyata+salah) — "turns out to be wrong" (much more common)
- **terbukti salah** (ter-+bukti+salah) — "proven wrong"

**Notes:** The evidential/discovery constructions with "ternyata" and "terbukti" are far more natural than simple "menjadi salah." Indonesian speakers prefer to frame error discovery rather than error becoming.

## 3. BECOMING GENUINE/AUTHENTIC (asli)

### Pattern: menjadi asli
**Form:** menjadi asli
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become genuine/authentic"
**Usage:** Rare and pragmatically odd. Objects are inherently genuine or fake; they don't typically "become" authentic.

**Example:**
- **?barang itu menjadi asli** 
  "?that item became genuine"

**Naturalness:** [UNCERTAIN/MARGINAL] — Grammatically possible but pragmatically strange.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **terbukti asli** (ter-+bukti+asli) — "proven genuine" (much more natural)
- **ternyata asli** (ternyata+asli) — "turns out to be genuine"

**Notes:** Authenticity is typically discovered rather than achieved. The evidential constructions are strongly preferred. "Menjadi asli" might be used metaphorically (e.g., someone becoming their authentic self) but not for physical objects.

## 4. BECOMING FAKE/COUNTERFEIT (palsu)

### Pattern: menjadi palsu
**Form:** menjadi palsu
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become fake/counterfeit"
**Usage:** Pragmatically odd for the same reasons as "menjadi asli." Objects don't become fake; they are manufactured as fakes or discovered to be fake.

**Example:**
- **?dokumen itu menjadi palsu**
  "?that document became fake"

**Naturalness:** [UNCERTAIN/MARGINAL] — Grammatically possible but pragmatically strange.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **ternyata palsu** (ternyata+palsu) — "turns out to be fake" (natural)
- **terbukti palsu** (ter-+bukti+palsu) — "proven fake"

**Notes:** Like "asli," the evidential discovery frame is strongly preferred. Counterfeiting is an action (memalsu) rather than a state change.

## 5. BECOMING HONEST (jujur)

### Pattern: menjadi jujur
**Form:** menjadi jujur
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become honest"
**Usage:** Natural construction for character development or behavioral change. Used when someone transitions from dishonesty to honesty.

**Example:**
- **dia menjadi jujur setelah insiden itu**
  "he/she became honest after that incident"

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural and commonly used for character change.

**Alternative expressions:**

- **berubah menjadi jujur** (ber-+ubah+menjadi+jujur) — "change to become honest" (emphatic)

**Notes:** Character traits readily accept inchoative constructions. "Menjadi jujur" is natural for describing moral development or reform.

## 6. BECOMING DISHONEST / LYING (bohong)

### Pattern: menjadi pembohong
**Form:** menjadi pembohong
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become a liar"
**Usage:** Natural construction using the agentive nominalization. Describes character deterioration or development of dishonest behavior.

**Example:**
- **dia menjadi pembohong setelah bergaul dengan mereka**
  "he/she became a liar after associating with them"

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Natural with agentive noun form.

- **mulai suka bohong** (mulai+suka+bohong) — "start to like lying"

**Notes:** The agentive form "pembohong" is preferred for character description. The verbal form "berbohong" is used for behavioral description.

## 7. BECOMING VALID/LEGAL (sah)

### Pattern: menjadi sah
**Form:** menjadi sah
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become valid/legal"
**Usage:** Very common in legal and administrative contexts. Describes transition from invalid/unofficial to valid/official status.

**Example:**
- **pernikahan itu menjadi sah setelah dicatat**
  "that marriage became legal after being registered"

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely natural in legal/administrative contexts.

**Alternative expressions:**
- **disahkan** (di-+sah+-kan) — "validated, legalized" (passive causative, more common)
- **menjadi sah secara hukum** — "become legally valid" (with specification)

**Notes:** While "menjadi sah" is natural, the passive causative "disahkan" (validated/legalized) is more frequent because it foregrounds the official action that creates validity.

**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "become real/actual"

## 14. CAUSATIVE: MAKING TRUE/CORRECT (membenarkan)

### Pattern: membenarkan
**Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "to confirm, to verify, to validate, to justify"
**Usage:** Highly productive causative meaning to make something true/correct or to confirm something as true. Multiple semantic extensions.

**Examples:**
- **dia membenarkan laporan itu**
  "he/she confirmed that report"
  
- **bukti membenarkan tuduhan**
  "evidence confirms the accusation"

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common and productive.

**Semantic range:**
1. Confirm/verify as true
2. Validate/authenticate
3. Justify/vindicate
4. Grant permission (extended meaning)

**Notes:** This is one of the most productive causatives in the truth domain with rich semantic extensions.

**Form:** menyalahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "to blame, to fault, to declare wrong"
**Usage:** Highly productive causative with primary meaning of blaming or attributing fault. Can also mean to declare something wrong.

2. Declare wrong/incorrect
3. Find fault with

**Notes:** The blame/fault attribution meaning is dominant. The semantic shift from "make wrong" to "blame" is well-established.

## 19. CAUSATIVE: MAKING SOMEONE LIE (membohongi)

### Pattern: membohongi
**Form:** membohongi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Translation:** "to lie to, to deceive"
**Usage:** Highly productive applicative/causative meaning to lie to someone. The -i suffix marks the affected party.

**Example:**
- **jangan membohongi saya**
  "don't lie to me"

**Naturalness:** [CORE] — Extremely common.

**Semantic note:** This is technically an applicative (meN-+-i) rather than pure causative (meN-+-kan), marking the person lied to rather than causing someone to lie.

**Alternative for true causative:**
- **membuat bohong** (membuat+bohong) — "make lie" (rare, analytic)
- **menyuruh bohong** (meN-+suruh+bohong) — "order to lie"

**Notes:** The applicative "membohongi" (lie to) is productive; true causative (make someone lie) uses analytic constructions.

**Notes:** "Tetap sama" is one of the most frequent stability expressions in Indonesian.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Highly Productive Patterns

**Inchoative (menjadi):**
- Productive with: benar, salah, jujur, sah, batal, nyata, mirip, sama, cocok, tepat, wajar, pantas, akurat
- Less natural with: asli, palsu (authenticity discovered, not achieved)
- Very common for character traits and status changes

**Causative (meN-...-kan):**
- Highly productive: membenarkan, menyalahkan, mengesahkan, membatalkan, menyatakan, menyamakan, mencocokkan, membetulkan, mengelirukan, mewajarkan
- Moderately productive: memalsu/memalsukan, membohongi (applicative)
- Not productive: mengaslikan, mengakuratkan, memantaskan
- Asymmetry: negative/deceptive actions more readily causativized than positive character traits

**Stability (tetap, masih):**
- Fully productive across all truth-domain terms
- "Tetap" (remain) and "masih" (still) are default stability markers
- Very common in legal and evaluative contexts

**Gradual Change (semakin/makin):**
- Productive with gradable terms: mirip, akurat, tepat, cocok, jelas
- Not used with binary terms: asli/palsu, sah/batal
- Extremely common in descriptive discourse

**Evidential Discovery (ternyata, terbukti):**
- Fully productive across all truth-domain terms
- "Ternyata" is one of the most frequent constructions in Indonesian
- "Terbukti" emphasizes evidential verification
- Preferred over simple inchoatives for authenticity (ternyata asli/palsu >> menjadi asli/palsu)

### Asymmetries and Gaps

**Causative Asymmetries:**
1. Negative/deceptive actions readily causativized (memalsu, membohongi, mengelirukan)
2. Positive character traits lack productive causatives (*menjujurkan not attested)
3. Authenticity lacks productive causative (*mengaslikan marginal)
4. Accuracy/precision use alternative strategies (memperbaiki, memverifikasi)

**Semantic Shifts in Causatives:**
- **membenarkan**: "make true" → "confirm, validate, justify, permit"
- **menyalahkan**: "make wrong" → "blame, fault"
- **menyatakan**: "make manifest" → "state, declare" (speech act)
- **menetapkan**: "make precise" → "determine, establish, set"

**Preferred Constructions:**
- Authenticity: evidential discovery (ternyata asli/palsu) >> inchoative (menjadi asli/palsu)
- Legal status: passive causative (disahkan, dibatalkan) >> inchoative (menjadi sah/batal)
- Character change: inchoative natural (menjadi jujur) but causative rare (*menjujurkan)

### Register and Domain Variation

**Legal/Administrative:**
- High frequency of: mengesahkan, membatalkan, tetap sah, terbukti
- Specialized constructions: batal demi hukum (void by law)

**Epistemic/Evidential:**
- Dominant constructions: ternyata, terbukti, sebenarnya
- Very high frequency in narrative and explanatory discourse

**Gradual Change:**
- Common with: semakin mirip, makin akurat, semakin jelas
- Typical in descriptive and comparative contexts

**Character/Moral:**
- Natural inchoatives: menjadi jujur, tetap jujur
- Causatives rare or use analytic constructions

This analysis reveals that Indonesian has rich resources for encoding change, causation, and stability in the truth domain, with clear productivity patterns and systematic asymmetries based on semantic class and pragmatic function.
