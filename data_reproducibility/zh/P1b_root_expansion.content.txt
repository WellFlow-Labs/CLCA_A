# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

### Root 1: 真 (zhēn)
- **Participates in**: 6 P1 items (真, 真实, 真相, 真理, 真正, and appears in 名副其实/名不副实 via 实)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Genuineness, authenticity, correspondence to reality; marks what is real rather than false or artificial

### Root 2: 实 (shí)
- **Participates in**: 8 P1 items (实, 事实, 真实, 诚实, 确实, and in 名副其实/名不副实)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Actuality, substantiality, concrete reality; marks what is solid, actual, or materially present rather than empty or theoretical

### Root 3: 假 (jiǎ)
- **Participates in**: 2 P1 items (假, 虚假)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Falseness, artificiality, counterfeit nature; marks what is not genuine or does not correspond to reality

## STEP 2 — EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1: 真 (zhēn) — EXPANSION

1. **真诚 (zhēnchéng)**
   - Literal: true + sincere = sincere, genuine
   - Translation: sincere, genuine, heartfelt
   - Context: Describes sincere emotions, intentions, or attitudes where inner feelings match outward expression. Common in evaluating interpersonal relationships and moral character.
   - [CORE]

2. **真心 (zhēnxīn)**
   - Literal: true + heart = sincere heart, genuine intention
   - Translation: sincere, wholehearted, genuine
   - Context: Emphasizes genuine feelings or intentions from the heart, often used adverbially (真心地) or as a noun describing sincere attitude. Common in everyday speech about emotions and intentions.
   - [CORE]

3. **真挚 (zhēnzhì)**
   - Literal: true + sincere/earnest = sincere, genuine
   - Translation: sincere, genuine, earnest
   - Context: Describes deep sincerity in feelings or relationships, often in more formal or literary contexts. Used for evaluating the authenticity of emotions, friendships, or expressions.
   - [CORE]

4. **认真 (rènzhēn)**
   - Literal: recognize/serious + true = serious, earnest, conscientious
   - Translation: serious, earnest, conscientious, take seriously
   - Context: Describes serious, careful, or conscientious attitude toward tasks or responsibilities. Extremely common in everyday speech about work ethic, study habits, and attention to detail.
   - [CORE]

5. **当真 (dàngzhēn)**
   - Literal: regard as + true = take seriously, take as real
   - Translation: take seriously, take as real, really mean it
   - Context: Questions or affirms whether something should be taken seriously or literally. Common in conversational contexts to verify sincerity or seriousness of statements.
   - [CORE]

6. **真假 (zhēnjiǎ)**
   - Literal: true + false = true or false, authenticity
   - Translation: true or false, genuine or fake, authenticity
   - Context: Refers to the distinction between genuine and fake, or the question of authenticity. Used when evaluating whether something is real or counterfeit, or discussing truth versus falsehood.
   - [CORE]

7. **真伪 (zhēnwěi)**
   - Literal: true + false/counterfeit = authenticity, genuineness
   - Translation: authenticity, genuineness, true or false
   - Context: More formal term for distinguishing authentic from counterfeit, often used in contexts of document verification, art authentication, or formal investigation of claims.
   - [CORE]

8. **真切 (zhēnqiè)**
   - Literal: true +切 (切: cut, close, earnest) = vivid, distinct, genuine
   - Translation: vivid, distinct, genuine, real
   - Context: Describes experiences, feelings, or perceptions that are vivid and genuine, felt or perceived clearly and authentically. Used for evaluating the reality and intensity of experiences.
   - [CORE]

9. **真情 (zhēnqíng)**
   - Literal: true + feeling/emotion = true feelings, genuine emotion
   - Translation: true feelings, genuine emotion, real sentiment
   - Context: Refers to authentic emotions or feelings, often contrasted with pretense or superficiality. Common in discussions of relationships, artistic expression, and emotional authenticity.
   - [CORE]

10. **真知 (zhēnzhī)**
    - Literal: true + knowledge = true knowledge, genuine understanding
    - Translation: true knowledge, genuine understanding, real insight
    - Context: Refers to genuine knowledge or understanding as opposed to superficial or false knowledge. Often appears in the phrase 真知灼见 (true knowledge and penetrating insight). Used in intellectual and educational contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **真传 (zhēnchuán)**
    - Literal: true + transmit/tradition = authentic teaching, true transmission
    - Translation: authentic teaching, true transmission, genuine tradition
    - Context: Refers to authentic transmission of knowledge, skills, or traditions, especially in martial arts, crafts, or traditional learning. Marks genuine lineage versus superficial or corrupted versions.
    - [CORE]

12. **真品 (zhēnpǐn)**
    - Literal: true + product/item = genuine article, authentic item
    - Translation: genuine article, authentic item, real thing
    - Context: Refers to authentic products or items as opposed to counterfeits or imitations. Common in commercial contexts, art markets, and discussions of product authenticity.
    - [CORE]

13. **真迹 (zhēnjì)**
    - Literal: true + trace/mark = authentic work, original
    - Translation: authentic work, original (of calligraphy or painting)
    - Context: Specifically refers to original works of calligraphy, painting, or writing by the actual artist/author, as opposed to copies or forgeries. Technical term in art authentication.
    - [CORE]

14. **真人 (zhēnrén)**
    - Literal: true + person = real person, Daoist immortal
    - Translation: real person, actual person; Daoist immortal/perfected person
    - Context: Two distinct uses: (1) actual living person versus fictional character or representation; (2) in Daoist philosophy, an enlightened or perfected person who has realized their true nature.
    - [CORE]

15. **真空 (zhēnkōng)**
    - Literal: true + empty/void = vacuum, true emptiness
    - Translation: vacuum, true void
    - Context: Scientific term for vacuum (空间真空); also Buddhist/philosophical term for ultimate emptiness or void. Technical usage in physics and philosophical usage in Buddhism.
    - [CORE]

16. **真丝 (zhēnsī)**
    - Literal: true + silk = real silk, pure silk
    - Translation: real silk, pure silk, genuine silk
    - Context: Refers to genuine silk fabric as opposed to synthetic imitations. Common in textile and fashion contexts to mark authentic material composition.
    - [CORE]

17. **真金 (zhēnjīn)**
    - Literal: true + gold = real gold, pure gold
    - Translation: real gold, pure gold, genuine gold
    - Context: Refers to genuine gold as opposed to fake or plated gold. Often appears in the saying 真金不怕火炼 (real gold fears no fire / genuine quality withstands testing).
    - [CORE]

18. **真本 (zhēnběn)**
    - Literal: true + book/edition = authentic edition, original text
    - Translation: authentic edition, original text, genuine copy
    - Context: Refers to authentic or original editions of texts, as opposed to corrupted or altered versions. Used in textual scholarship and bibliography.
    - [CORE]

19. **真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù)**
    - Literal: true + evidence + actual + proof = solid evidence, conclusive proof
    - Translation: solid evidence, conclusive proof, hard facts
    - Context: Fixed expression emphasizing concrete, reliable evidence. Common in legal, investigative, and argumentative contexts to stress the solidity of proof.
    - [CORE]

20. **货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí)**
    - Literal: goods + true + price + actual = genuine goods at fair prices
    - Translation: genuine goods at fair prices, authentic and reasonably priced
    - Context: Commercial expression guaranteeing both authenticity of products and fairness of pricing. Used in advertising and business contexts to assure quality and value.
    - [CORE]

21. **真才实学 (zhēn cái shí xué)**
    - Literal: true + talent + actual + learning = real talent and solid learning
    - Translation: real talent and genuine learning, true ability
    - Context: Describes genuine competence and substantial knowledge, as opposed to superficial credentials or pretense. Used in evaluating qualifications and expertise.
    - [CORE]

22. **真刀真枪 (zhēn dāo zhēn qiāng)**
    - Literal: real + knife + real + spear = real swords and spears, for real
    - Translation: for real, in earnest, with real weapons/tools
    - Context: Idiomatic expression meaning to do something seriously or for real, not as practice or pretense. Can be literal (actual weapons) or figurative (genuine effort).
    - [CORE]

23. **真情实感 (zhēn qíng shí gǎn)**
    - Literal: true + feeling + actual + emotion = genuine feelings and emotions
    - Translation: genuine feelings and emotions, true sentiments
    - Context: Emphasizes authentic emotional expression, often in artistic or literary contexts. Used to evaluate sincerity of emotional content in works or personal expression.
    - [CORE]

24. **真心实意 (zhēn xīn shí yì)**
    - Literal: true + heart + actual + intention = wholehearted sincerity
    - Translation: wholehearted, sincere, genuine
    - Context: Emphasizes complete sincerity of intentions and feelings. Common in everyday speech about genuine commitment or sincere attitudes toward others.
    - [CORE]

25. **真龙天子 (zhēn lóng tiān zǐ)**
    - Literal: true + dragon + heaven + son = true Son of Heaven, legitimate emperor
    - Translation: true/legitimate emperor, rightful ruler
    - Context: Historical/literary term for the legitimate emperor, emphasizing rightful claim to throne. Used in historical narratives and traditional discourse about imperial legitimacy.
    - [MARGINAL]

26. **真身 (zhēnshēn)**
    - Literal: true + body = true body, actual person
    - Translation: true body, actual person, real self
    - Context: Refers to one's actual physical presence (versus representation), or in Buddhist contexts, the true dharma body. Used when distinguishing actual presence from images or proxies.
    - [CORE]

27. **真言 (zhēnyán)**
    - Literal: true + words = true words, mantra
    - Translation: true words, truth, mantra
    - Context: Can mean truthful words or, in Buddhist contexts, sacred mantras (Sanskrit: mantra). Used in religious contexts for sacred formulas and in general contexts for truthful speech.
    - [CORE]

28. **真谛 (zhēndì)**
    - Literal: true + principle/truth = true meaning, real truth
    - Translation: true meaning, real truth, essence
    - Context: Refers to the true or essential meaning of something, often in philosophical or profound contexts. Used when discussing deep truths or fundamental principles.
    - [CORE]

29. **真率 (zhēnshuài)**
    - Literal: true + straightforward = frank, candid, natural
    - Translation: frank, candid, natural, unaffected
    - Context: Describes frank, natural, unaffected manner or character. Often used in literary contexts to praise genuine, unpretentious expression or behavior.
    - [CORE]

30. **纯真 (chúnzhēn)**
    - Literal: pure + true = pure, innocent, naive
    - Translation: pure, innocent, naive, unspoiled
    - Context: Describes innocent, unspoiled character or emotions, especially in children or uncorrupted states. Common in describing personality traits and emotional states.
    - [CORE]

31. **失真 (shīzhēn)**
    - Literal: lose + true = distorted, lose fidelity
    - Translation: distorted, lose fidelity, inaccurate
    - Context: Technical term describing loss of accuracy or fidelity in reproduction, transmission, or representation. Common in audio/visual technology and information transmission contexts.
    - [CORE]

32. **逼真 (bīzhēn)**
    - Literal: press/force + true = lifelike, realistic, true to life
    - Translation: lifelike, realistic, vivid, true to life
    - Context: Describes representations, imitations, or performances that closely resemble reality. Common in evaluating artistic representations, simulations, and performances.
    - [CORE]

33. **写真 (xiězhēn)**
    - Literal: write/depict + true = portrait, photograph
    - Translation: portrait, photograph, realistic depiction
    - Context: Originally meant realistic portrait painting; now commonly means photograph, especially portrait photography. Used in photography and art contexts.
    - [CORE]

### ROOT 2: 实 (shí) — EXPANSION

1. **实际 (shíjì)**
   - Literal: actual + boundary/limit = actual, practical, reality
   - Translation: actual, practical, realistic, in practice
   - Context: Refers to actual circumstances or practical reality as opposed to theory or ideals. Extremely common in distinguishing what actually happens from what is planned or imagined.
   - [CORE]

2. **实在 (shízài)**
   - Literal: actual + exist/be = real, true, honest, really
   - Translation: real, true, honest, really, indeed
   - Context: Multiple uses: (1) describes genuine, honest character; (2) emphasizes reality or actuality; (3) used adverbially to mean "really" or "truly." Very common in everyday speech.
   - [CORE]

3. **实践 (shíjiàn)**
   - Literal: actual + practice/tread = practice, put into practice
   - Translation: practice, put into practice, praxis
   - Context: Refers to practical action or implementation as opposed to theory. Central term in Marxist philosophy and common in educational and professional contexts about applying knowledge.
   - [CORE]

4. **实现 (shíxiàn)**
   - Literal: actual + appear/manifest = realize, achieve, bring about
   - Translation: realize, achieve, bring about, implement
   - Context: Describes bringing plans, goals, or potentials into actual reality. Common in contexts of goal achievement, policy implementation, and making abstract ideas concrete.
   - [CORE]

5. **实施 (shíshī)**
   - Literal: actual + carry out = implement, put into effect
   - Translation: implement, put into effect, carry out
   - Context: Formal term for implementing policies, plans, or measures. Common in administrative, legal, and organizational contexts about executing decisions.
   - [CORE]

6. **实行 (shíxíng)**
   - Literal: actual + carry out = implement, practice, put into effect
   - Translation: implement, practice, put into effect, carry out
   - Context: Similar to 实施 but slightly less formal; refers to putting policies or practices into actual operation. Common in discussing implementation of systems or methods.
   - [CORE]

7. **实验 (shíyàn)**
   - Literal: actual + test/examine = experiment, test
   - Translation: experiment, test, trial
   - Context: Scientific or practical experimentation to test hypotheses or methods. Standard term in scientific, educational, and technical contexts for empirical testing.
   - [CORE]

8. **实习 (shíxí)**
   - Literal: actual + practice/study = internship, field work, practicum
   - Translation: internship, field work, practicum, practical training
   - Context: Refers to practical training or internship where students or trainees gain hands-on experience. Common in educational and professional training contexts.
   - [CORE]

9. **实用 (shíyòng)**
   - Literal: actual + use = practical, pragmatic, functional
   - Translation: practical, pragmatic, functional, utilitarian
   - Context: Describes practical utility or functionality of things. Common in evaluating whether something serves actual practical purposes versus being merely theoretical or decorative.
   - [CORE]

10. **实力 (shílì)**
    - Literal: actual + strength/power = actual strength, real capability
    - Translation: actual strength, real capability, substantial power
    - Context: Refers to real, substantial power or capability as opposed to nominal or apparent strength. Common in military, economic, and competitive contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **实质 (shízhì)**
    - Literal: actual + quality/substance = substance, essence, substantive
    - Translation: substance, essence, substantive, essential nature
    - Context: Refers to the substantial or essential nature of something as opposed to superficial form. Common in analytical contexts distinguishing substance from appearance.
    - [CORE]

12. **实体 (shítǐ)**
    - Literal: actual + body/entity = entity, substance, physical object
    - Translation: entity, substance, physical object, concrete thing
    - Context: Philosophical and general term for concrete entities or substances. Used in philosophy, business (实体店 = physical store vs. online), and general discourse about material reality.
    - [CORE]

13. **实物 (shíwù)**
    - Literal: actual + thing/object = physical object, material object, real thing
    - Translation: physical object, material object, real thing, goods in kind
    - Context: Refers to actual physical objects as opposed to representations, descriptions, or monetary equivalents. Common in commercial and administrative contexts.
    - [CORE]

14. **实况 (shíkuàng)**
    - Literal: actual + situation = actual situation, live (broadcast)
    - Translation: actual situation, live broadcast, on-the-spot report
    - Context: Refers to actual conditions or live reporting of events as they happen. Common in media contexts for live broadcasts and in general contexts for actual circumstances.
    - [CORE]

15. **实惠 (shíhuì)**
    - Literal: actual + benefit = tangible benefit, substantial, economical
    - Translation: tangible benefit, substantial, economical, good value
    - Context: Describes practical benefits or good value, especially in commercial contexts. Common in everyday speech about getting real benefits or good deals.
    - [CORE]

16. **实证 (shízhèng)**
    - Literal: actual + evidence/proof = empirical evidence, positive proof
    - Translation: empirical evidence, positive proof, empirical verification
    - Context: Refers to empirical evidence or verification through actual observation. Technical term in scientific and legal contexts; also refers to positivism (实证主义).
    - [CORE]

17. **实录 (shílù)**
    - Literal: actual + record = faithful record, veritable record
    - Translation: faithful record, veritable record, documentary
    - Context: Refers to accurate, factual records of events. Used in historical documentation and modern documentary contexts to emphasize factual accuracy.
    - [CORE]

18. **实情 (shíqíng)**
    - Literal: actual + situation/feelings = actual situation, true state of affairs
    - Translation: actual situation, true state of affairs, real circumstances
    - Context: Refers to the true or actual state of affairs, often what is hidden or not immediately apparent. Common in investigative and explanatory contexts.
    - [CORE]

19. **实例 (shílì)**
    - Literal: actual + example = concrete example, actual case
    - Translation: concrete example, actual case, real instance
    - Context: Refers to concrete, real examples as opposed to hypothetical or abstract ones. Common in educational and argumentative contexts to illustrate points with real cases.
    - [CORE]

20. **实数 (shíshù)**
    - Literal: actual + number = real number, actual number
    - Translation: real number (mathematics), actual number/count
    - Context: Mathematical term for real numbers; also refers to actual counts or quantities as opposed to estimates. Technical usage in mathematics and general usage for accurate counts.
    - [CORE]

21. **实心 (shíxīn)**
    - Literal: actual + heart/center = solid (not hollow), sincere
    - Translation: solid (not hollow), sincere, wholehearted
    - Context: Two uses: (1) physically solid rather than hollow; (2) sincere or wholehearted in attitude. Used in both material descriptions and character evaluation.
    - [CORE]

22. **实地 (shídì)**
    - Literal: actual + ground/place = on the spot, on-site
    - Translation: on the spot, on-site, in the field
    - Context: Refers to being at the actual location or conducting activities at the real site. Common in investigative, research, and inspection contexts.
    - [CORE]

23. **实足 (shízú)**
    - Literal: actual + sufficient/foot = full, complete, solid
    - Translation: full, complete, solid, genuine
    - Context: Emphasizes completeness or full measure of something, often in commercial contexts about full weight or measure. Also describes solid, genuine quality.
    - [CORE]

24. **实打实 (shí dǎ shí)**
    - Literal: actual + hit/strike + actual = absolutely real, genuine, solid
    - Translation: absolutely real, genuine, solid, down-to-earth
    - Context: Colloquial expression emphasizing genuine, solid, no-nonsense quality. Common in everyday speech about honest dealings or substantial quality.
    - [CORE]

25. **扎实 (zhāshi)**
    - Literal: pierce/firm + actual = solid, sturdy, sound
    - Translation: solid, sturdy, sound, well-grounded
    - Context: Describes solid foundation, thorough work, or sound knowledge. Common in evaluating quality of work, learning, or construction.
    - [CORE]

26. **踏实 (tāshi)**
    - Literal: step/tread + actual = steady, dependable, at ease
    - Translation: steady, dependable, at ease, down-to-earth
    - Context: Describes steady, reliable character or feeling secure and at ease. Common in personality descriptions and emotional states.
    - [CORE]

27. **老实 (lǎoshi)**
    - Literal: old + actual = honest, frank, well-behaved
    - Translation: honest, frank, well-behaved, simple
    - Context: Describes honest, straightforward character, sometimes with connotation of being simple or naive. Very common in everyday character evaluation.
    - [CORE]

28. **朴实 (pǔshí)**
    - Literal: simple/plain + actual = simple, plain, down-to-earth
    - Translation: simple, plain, down-to-earth, unpretentious
    - Context: Describes simple, unpretentious character or style. Common in evaluating personal character, artistic style, or lifestyle choices.
    - [CORE]

29. **充实 (chōngshí)**
    - Literal: fill + actual = substantial, rich, fulfilling
    - Translation: substantial, rich, fulfilling, enrich
    - Context: Describes substantial content or fulfilling experience. Common in describing rich content, fulfilling life, or the act of enriching something.
    - [CORE]

30. **殷实 (yīnshí)**
    - Literal: abundant + actual = well-off, substantial, prosperous
    - Translation: well-off, substantial, prosperous, wealthy
    - Context: Describes substantial wealth or prosperous circumstances, often of families or households. Used in describing economic well-being.
    - [CORE]

31. **翔实 (xiángshí)**
    - Literal: detailed/soar + actual = detailed and accurate, exhaustive
    - Translation: detailed and accurate, exhaustive, comprehensive
    - Context: Describes detailed and accurate information or documentation. Common in academic and documentary contexts praising thoroughness and accuracy.
    - [CORE]

32. **平实 (píngshí)**
    - Literal: level/plain + actual = plain, simple, down-to-earth
    - Translation: plain, simple, down-to-earth, straightforward
    - Context: Describes plain, straightforward style without embellishment. Common in evaluating writing style, speech, or artistic expression.

33. **厚实 (hòushí)**
    - Literal: thick + actual = thick, solid, substantial
    - Translation: thick, solid, substantial, sturdy
    - Context: Describes physical thickness or solidity, and by extension, substantial quality or reliable character. Used for both material and personal evaluation.

34. **结实 (jiēshi)**
    - Literal: tie/knot + actual = sturdy, durable, strong

    - Context: Describes physical sturdiness or durability of objects, or robust health of people. Common in everyday descriptions of build quality and physical condition.

35. **坚实 (jiānshí)**

    - Translation: solid, firm, substantial, strong
    - Context: Describes solid foundation or firm basis, whether physical or metaphorical. Common in describing foundations, bases, or groundwork.

### ROOT 3: 假 (jiǎ) — EXPANSION

1. **假如 (jiǎrú)**
   - Literal: suppose + if = if, supposing, in case
   - Translation: if, supposing, in case
   - Context: Conditional conjunction introducing hypothetical situations. Common in everyday speech and writing for conditional statements.
   - [CORE]

2. **假设 (jiǎshè)**
   - Literal: suppose + establish = suppose, assume, hypothesis
   - Translation: suppose, assume, hypothesis, hypothetical
   - Context: Introduces suppositions or hypotheses, both in everyday reasoning and scientific contexts. Standard term for hypotheses in scientific method.
   - [CORE]

3. **假定 (jiǎdìng)**
   - Literal: suppose + fix/determine = assume, suppose, given that
   - Translation: assume, suppose, given that, presume
   - Context: Similar to 假设 but often more formal; establishes assumptions or presuppositions. Common in logical, legal, and analytical contexts.
   - [CORE]

4. **假想 (jiǎxiǎng)**
   - Literal: suppose + think = imagine, suppose, hypothetical
   - Translation: imagine, suppose, hypothetical, imaginary
   - Context: Refers to imagined or hypothetical scenarios, often in planning or strategic contexts (假想敌 = hypothetical enemy). Used for imagined situations.
   - [CORE]

5. **假装 (jiǎzhuāng)**
   - Literal: false +装 (pretend/dress up) = pretend, feign, fake
   - Translation: pretend, feign, fake, make believe
   - Context: Describes deliberate pretense or feigning of states, emotions, or identities. Common in everyday speech about deceptive behavior or pretending.
   - [CORE]

6. **假冒 (jiǎmào)**
   - Literal: false + pretend/冒 (risk, falsely claim) = counterfeit, pass off as
   - Translation: counterfeit, pass off as, falsely claim to be
   - Context: Describes counterfeiting or falsely claiming identity or origin. Common in commercial contexts about fake products (假冒伪劣 = counterfeit and shoddy).
   - [CORE]

7. **假造 (jiǎzào)**
   - Literal: false + make = fabricate, forge, counterfeit
   - Translation: fabricate, forge, counterfeit, fake
   - Context: Describes fabrication or forgery of documents, evidence, or objects. Common in legal and investigative contexts about fraudulent creation.
   - [CORE]

8. **假借 (jiǎjiè)**
   - Literal: false + borrow = use as pretext, borrow falsely
   - Translation: use as pretext, borrow falsely, phonetic loan (linguistics)
   - Context: Two uses: (1) using something as false pretext; (2) linguistic term for phonetic borrowing. Used in both deceptive contexts and linguistic analysis.
   - [CORE]

9. **假托 (jiǎtuō)**
   - Literal: false + entrust/托 (rely on, use as excuse) = use as pretext, falsely claim
   - Translation: use as pretext, falsely claim, attribute falsely
   - Context: Describes falsely attributing something to someone or using false pretexts. Common in literary and historical contexts about false attribution.
   - [CORE]

10. **假意 (jiǎyì)**
    - Literal: false + intention = pretense, feigned intention
    - Translation: pretense, feigned intention, insincerely
    - Context: Describes insincere or pretended intentions. Used when someone's stated intentions don't match their real motives.
    - [CORE]

11. **假象 (jiǎxiàng)**
    - Literal: false + appearance = false appearance, illusion
    - Translation: false appearance, illusion, semblance
    - Context: Refers to misleading appearances that don't reflect reality. Common in analytical contexts about distinguishing appearance from reality.
    - [CORE]

12. **假话 (jiǎhuà)**
    - Literal: false + speech = lie, falsehood, untruth
    - Translation: lie, falsehood, untruth
    - Context: Refers to false statements or lies. Common everyday term for dishonest speech, often contrasted with 真话 (truth).
    - [CORE]

13. **假证 (jiǎzhèng)**
    - Literal: false + certificate/evidence = false certificate, forged document
    - Translation: false certificate, forged document, fake ID
    - Context: Refers to forged documents or false credentials. Common in legal and administrative contexts about fraudulent documentation.
    - [CORE]

14. **假牙 (jiǎyá)**
    - Literal: false + tooth = false tooth, dentures
    - Translation: false tooth, dentures, dental prosthesis
    - Context: Medical/dental term for artificial teeth or dentures. Standard term in dental and medical contexts.
    - [CORE]

15. **假发 (jiǎfà)**
    - Literal: false + hair = wig, hairpiece, toupee
    - Translation: wig, hairpiece, toupee
    - Context: Refers to artificial hair or wigs. Common term in fashion and theatrical contexts.
    - [CORE]

16. **假肢 (jiǎzhī)**
    - Literal: false + limb = artificial limb, prosthesis
    - Translation: artificial limb, prosthesis, prosthetic
    - Context: Medical term for artificial limbs or prosthetics. Standard term in medical and rehabilitation contexts.
    - [CORE]

17. **假释 (jiǎshì)**
    - Literal: temporary + release = parole, release on parole
    - Translation: parole, release on parole
    - Context: Legal term for parole or conditional release from prison. Standard term in criminal justice contexts.
    - [CORE]

18. **假期 (jiàqī)**
    - Literal: vacation + period = vacation, holiday, leave
    - Translation: vacation, holiday, leave
    - Context: Refers to vacation time or holidays. Very common term for time off from work or school. Note: different tone (jiàqī) from other 假 compounds.
    - [CORE]

19. **假日 (jiàrì)**
    - Literal: vacation + day = holiday, day off
    - Translation: holiday, day off
    - Context: Refers to holidays or days off. Common in scheduling and calendar contexts. Note: different tone (jiàrì).
    - [CORE]

20. **假条 (jiàtiáo)**
    - Literal: leave + slip = leave permit, excuse note
    - Translation: leave permit, excuse note, sick note
    - Context: Written permission for absence from work or school. Common in administrative and educational contexts. Note: different tone (jiàtiáo).
    - [CORE]

21. **假公济私 (jiǎ gōng jì sī)**
    - Literal: false/borrow + public + help + private = use public office for private gain
    - Translation: use public office for private gain, abuse public position
    - Context: Fixed expression criticizing abuse of public position for personal benefit. Common in political and ethical discourse about corruption.
    - [CORE]

22. **弄虚作假 (nòng xū zuò jiǎ)**
    - Literal: make + empty/false + do + false = practice fraud, engage in deception
    - Translation: practice fraud, engage in deception, falsify
    - Context: Fixed expression describing fraudulent or deceptive practices. Common in criticizing dishonest behavior in various contexts.
    - [CORE]

23. **虚情假意 (xū qíng jiǎ yì)**
    - Literal: empty + feeling + false + intention = hypocritical, insincere
    - Translation: hypocritical, insincere, false sentiments
    - Context: Fixed expression describing insincere emotions or hypocritical behavior. Common in evaluating authenticity of expressed feelings.
    - [CORE]

24. **假戏真做 (jiǎ xì zhēn zuò)**
    - Literal: false + play/drama + true + do = pretense becomes reality
    - Translation: pretense becomes reality, fake becomes real
    - Context: Idiomatic expression describing situations where what starts as pretense becomes real, often in romantic contexts. Common in narrative and relationship contexts.
    - [CORE]

25. **真真假假 (zhēn zhēn jiǎ jiǎ)**
    - Literal: true true false false = mix of truth and falsehood, hard to distinguish
    - Translation: mix of truth and falsehood, hard to distinguish real from fake
    - Context: Describes situations where truth and falsehood are mixed or hard to distinguish. Common in describing complex or ambiguous situations.
    - [CORE]

26. **假以时日 (jiǎ yǐ shí rì)**
    - Literal: give/lend + with + time + days = given time, in due course
    - Translation: given time, in due course, with time
    - Context: Literary expression meaning "given sufficient time." Used in formal contexts to indicate that something will happen with time.
    - [CORE]

27. **不假思索 (bù jiǎ sī suǒ)**
    - Literal: not + borrow + think + search = without thinking, offhand
    - Translation: without thinking, offhand, spontaneously
    - Context: Describes acting or speaking without deliberation. Common in describing spontaneous or unreflective responses.

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### Root 真 (zhēn) — Peripheral Extensions

**Intensity/Degree marking:**
- 认真 (rènzhēn): While related to truth domain through "taking seriously," functions primarily as an evaluative term for diligence and carefulness in work/study, extending beyond truth-evaluation to general quality assessment.

**Religious/Metaphysical:**
- 真人 (zhēnrén): In Daoist contexts, shifts from factual authenticity to spiritual perfection/enlightenment.
- 真言 (zhēnyán): In Buddhist contexts, refers to sacred mantras rather than truthful speech.
- 真空 (zhēnkōng): Buddhist philosophical term for ultimate emptiness, extending beyond empirical truth to metaphysical reality.

**Material/Physical properties:**
- 真丝 (zhēnsī), 真金 (zhēnjīn): Apply authenticity concept to material composition rather than propositional truth.

**Artistic/Aesthetic evaluation:**
- 逼真 (bīzhēn), 写真 (xiězhēn): Shift from truth-correspondence to aesthetic realism and representational accuracy.

**Political/Imperial legitimacy:**
- 真龙天子 (zhēn lóng tiān zǐ): Extends authenticity to political legitimacy and divine mandate.

### Root 实 (shí) — Peripheral Extensions

**Physical solidity:**
- 实心 (shíxīn), 结实 (jiēshi), 厚实 (hòushí), 坚实 (jiānshí): Apply to physical density and structural integrity rather than truth/actuality.

**Economic/Material prosperity:**
- 殷实 (yīnshí), 实惠 (shíhuì): Extend from actuality to material wealth and practical benefit.

**Character/Personality traits:**
- 老实 (lǎoshi), 朴实 (pǔshí), 踏实 (tāshi): Apply actuality concept to personality evaluation, marking simplicity, honesty, or reliability as character traits.

**Emotional/Psychological states:**
- 充实 (chōngshí): Extends from substantial content to subjective feeling of fulfillment.
- 踏实 (tāshi): Includes feeling of security or being at ease, psychological rather than epistemic.

**Mathematical/Technical:**
- 实数 (shíshù): Technical mathematical term where "real" has specialized meaning distinct from everyday truth-evaluation.

### Root 假 (jiǎ) — Peripheral Extensions

**Temporal/Administrative (different tone: jiàqī, jiàrì, jiàtiáo):**
- 假期, 假日, 假条: Completely different semantic domain (vacation/leave) with different pronunciation, though written with same character. Not related to falsity.

**Conditional/Hypothetical reasoning:**
- 假如 (jiǎrú), 假设 (jiǎshè), 假定 (jiǎdìng), 假想 (jiǎxiǎng): Shift from falsity to hypothetical/suppositional reasoning, marking epistemic stance rather than truth-value.

**Medical/Prosthetic:**
- 假牙 (jiǎyá), 假发 (jiǎfà), 假肢 (jiǎzhī): Apply falsity concept to artificial body parts, extending to functional substitutes rather than deceptive imitations.

**Legal/Administrative:**
- 假释 (jiǎshì): Legal term for parole, where "temporary" rather than "false" is the salient meaning.

**Linguistic/Technical:**
- 假借 (jiǎjiè): In linguistic contexts, technical term for phonetic borrowing, distinct from deceptive usage.
