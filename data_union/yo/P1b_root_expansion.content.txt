# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1

**Form:** *tọ́* (also appearing as *tọ*, *-tọ-* in compounds)
**Transliteration:** tọ́ / tọ
**P1 participation:** Items directly — *òtítọ́* (1), *òtọ́* (13), *ìtọ́ni* (14), *ẹ̀tọ́* (19), *tọ̀nà* (12), *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* (25); morphologically embedded in *òtítọ́* as the core stem
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, rightness, accuracy, propriety. This root provides the evaluative criterion of correspondence to a standard — whether a path, statement, measurement, or action is straight/correct rather than deviant/wrong. It is the primary morphological carrier of correctness and rightness in the Yoruba lexicon, and it underlies the most central truth-term (*òtítọ́*, "truth") as well as the term for rights/entitlements (*ẹ̀tọ́*).

---

### Root 2

**Form:** *mọ̀*
**Transliteration:** mọ̀
**P1 participation:** 3 items — *ìmọ̀* (4), *àìmọ̀* (5), and as a component in evaluative constructions throughout the domain
**Core meaning contribution:** Knowing, recognizing, being acquainted with. This root anchors the epistemic grounding dimension of truth-evaluation — whether a person has the cognitive basis for a claim.

---

### Root 3

**Form:** *ẹ̀rí* / *jẹ́rìí* / *rí*
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí / jẹ́rìí / rí
**P1 participation:** Items — *ẹ̀rí* (8), *ẹ̀rí ojú* (9), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (10/11), *jẹ́rìí* (10/11)
**Core meaning contribution:** Seeing, witnessing, attesting, evidencing. This root cluster covers the evidentiary and testimonial dimension of truth — the process by which truth claims are confirmed through witness or proof. The verbal root *rí* ("to see") is extremely productive in Yoruba generally, but its participation in the truth-evaluation domain is anchored primarily through the nominal *ẹ̀rí* ("evidence, proof") and the compound *ìjẹ́rìísí* ("testimony/certification").

---

### Root 4

**Form:** *ìwà*
**Transliteration:** ìwà
**P1 participation:** 3 items — *ìwà* (19), *ìwà rere* (20), *ìwà burúkú* (21)
**Core meaning contribution:** Character, nature, being, essential existence. The root/nominal *ìwà* (from *wà*, "to exist, to be") denotes character, nature, being, and essential existence. It is the primary vehicle in Yoruba moral and philosophical discourse for evaluating what something or someone fundamentally *is*, as opposed to how they appear. It anchors the domain of essential nature and moral character evaluation.

---

### Root 5

**Form:** *gbọ́* / *gbàgbọ́*
**Transliteration:** gbọ́ / gbàgbọ́
**P1 participation:** 3 items — *gbàgbọ́* (5), *ìgbàgbọ́* (6), *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (7, via *gbẹ́* "lean/rely," structurally parallel)
**Core meaning contribution:** Hearing, accepting, believing. The root that anchors the cognitive-receptive dimension: the act of taking something as true, trusting testimony, and committing to a proposition.

---

**Note on selection rationale:** The root *tọ́* was prioritized as the primary expansion target because it is the morphological core of the most semantically central item in the domain (*òtítọ́*, "truth") and generates the widest range of truth-evaluative derivatives. *Mọ̀* was selected because it anchors the epistemic grounding dimension. The *ẹ̀rí/jẹ́rìí/rí* cluster was selected because it covers the evidentiary and testimonial dimension, which is highly productive in Yoruba legal and religious discourse. *Ìwà* is the most semantically central root for the character/essential-nature subdomain. *Gbọ́* is included given its role in the belief/trust cluster, though it is less morphologically productive than the first two. The root *dá* (appearing in *ìdánilójú*, *ìdánwò*, *ìdájọ́*) is noted as a strong candidate but is not expanded here.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: *tọ́* — Straightness, Correctness, Accuracy, Propriety

---

**1.**
**Form:** tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** Root verb/adjective; to be straight, correct, right, proper
**Natural English translation:** to be correct; to be right; to be proper; to be straight
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate to evaluate factual accuracy (*ó tọ́* — "it is correct/right") and moral propriety. Appears in everyday speech, legal discourse, and moral evaluation. The most basic predicative use of the root; used in everyday speech to affirm that a statement, action, or situation is correct or appropriate. Functions as a general affirmative evaluative judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ó tọ́
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ó* (3sg subject pronoun) + *tọ́* (be correct/straight)
**Natural English translation:** It is correct; it is right; that is proper
**Context of use:** A highly frequent evaluative sentence used in everyday speech to affirm that a statement, action, or situation is correct or appropriate. Functions as a discourse marker of affirmation. In some contexts evaluates social propriety or procedural correctness rather than factual truth (e.g., "it is proper/fitting" in ceremonial contexts).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** kò tọ́
**Transliteration:** kò tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *kò* (negation) + *tọ́* (correct, right)
**Natural English translation:** it is not correct; it is wrong; it is improper
**Context of use:** The negated form of *ó tọ́*, used to reject a claim, action, or situation as incorrect or improper. Appears in everyday speech, moral condemnation, and legal argument.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** tọ́ sí
**Transliteration:** tọ́ sí
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/straight) + *sí* (toward/to)
**Natural English translation:** to be directed correctly toward; to be appropriate to; to be fitting for; to correct (something/someone), to set right, to direct properly
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether an action, response, or statement is appropriately directed or suited to a situation. Common in moral and practical assessments. Also used as a verbal phrase meaning to correct an error or to direct someone properly. Appears in everyday speech and educational contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** tọ́ ni
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/right) + *ni* (it is / copula-focus marker)
**Natural English translation:** it is indeed correct; that is right (emphatic)
**Context of use:** An emphatic affirmation used in conversation and debate to confirm that a statement is accurate or a judgment is sound. The focus marker *ni* adds emphasis.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** tọ́ pátápátá
**Transliteration:** tọ́ pátápátá
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct, right) + *pátápátá* (completely, entirely, wholly)
**Natural English translation:** completely correct, entirely right, wholly proper
**Context of use:** Used for emphatic affirmation that something is entirely correct or proper. Appears in everyday speech and in legal or moral argument where completeness of correctness is asserted.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** tọ́ ara rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** tọ́ ara rẹ̀
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/straighten) + *ara* (body/self) + *rẹ̀* (his/her/its, possessive)
**Natural English translation:** to correct oneself; to straighten oneself out; to reform
**Context of use:** Used in moral and behavioral contexts to describe self-correction or self-reform. Appears in moral instruction and in contexts of personal accountability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** tọ́ sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** tọ́ sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct, straight) + *sọ̀nà* (to direct on a path)
**Natural English translation:** to guide correctly, to direct on the right path
**Context of use:** Verbal expression used in moral and practical advisory contexts. A teacher, elder, or religious leader *tọ́ sọ̀nà* those in their care.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** tọ́ọ̀tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́ọ̀tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** Reduplicated/intensified form of *tọ́*; "truly correct, genuinely right"
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, really, indeed (adverbial intensifier)
**Context of use:** Used as an adverbial intensifier to affirm that something is genuinely or truly the case. Appears in everyday speech and in formal discourse to add emphasis to a truth claim.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ìtọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straightness/correctness)
**Natural English translation:** straightness, correctness, rightness, appropriateness
**Context of use:** The bare nominalization of the root; used in philosophical and moral discourse to refer abstractly to the quality of being correct or straight. Less common than *òtítọ́* in everyday speech but appears in formal and literary registers. Also used in evaluative contexts to refer abstractly to the quality of being correct or proper in procedural assessments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sí
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sí
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct/straight) + *sí* (toward); "directing correctly toward"
**Natural English translation:** direction toward, orientation, alignment; correction, rectification, amendment
**Context of use:** Used in contexts describing the correct orientation or alignment of something toward a goal or standard. Appears in religious and philosophical discourse about moral orientation. Also used in educational and administrative contexts to refer to the act of correcting an error or bringing something into conformity with the correct standard. Appears in school and editorial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straight/correct) + *sọ̀nà* (on the path/way; *sọ* = to say/direct + *nà* = path/way); "directing correctly on the path"
**Natural English translation:** guidance, direction, leading aright, correct direction, proper instruction
**Context of use:** Used in educational, religious, and moral contexts to describe the act of guiding someone along the correct path. Common in pastoral and pedagogical discourse. Appears in school and church/mosque discourse: *ìtọ́sọ̀nà tó dára* ("good guidance"). In religious contexts the root *tọ́* contributes to a sense of divinely sanctioned direction rather than factual correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

**Natural English translation:** correct path, right direction, proper guidance
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse as a nominalized form referring to the state or quality of being on the right path. Appears in Islamic and Christian Yoruba discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse for the righteous or correct course of conduct or belief. Used in moral and advisory contexts, particularly in proverb literature and elder speech, to describe adherence to the proper course of action. The root *tọ* combines with *nà* (path) to produce a spatial-moral metaphor; the "straightness" of the root is applied to a life-path or course of conduct rather than to a proposition.

**Context of use:** Used to describe the careful and correct maintenance of a person, object, or institution. Common in medical, agricultural, and interpersonal contexts.
