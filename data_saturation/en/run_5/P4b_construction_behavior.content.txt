**Language: English (en)**

## Notes on Format Adaptation

The standard P4b table format includes a Transliteration column. English requires no transliteration; that column is retained but marked N/A throughout for protocol consistency.

## Cross-Construction Observations

**Register stratification is the primary axis of variation.** No construction is unattested or doubtful; variation is distributional rather than grammatical. Formal registers favor synthetic negation, lexical causatives, process nominalizations, and the predicate complement with epistemic verb. Conversational registers favor analytic negation, periphrastic causatives, and discourse-marker constructions.

**Lexical restriction is the primary constraint.** The most significant restrictions documented are lexical rather than structural: prefix availability in negative attribution, the narrow distribution of *come true*, the non-productivity of agentive nominalization across the P1 inventory, and the restriction of compounding to *truth* and *false* specifically. These restrictions are item-specific and not derivable from general rules.

**No construction is rated [UNCERTAIN].** All ten construction types identified in P4a are attested with sufficient evidence to assign a definite naturalness rating. The two [MARGINAL] ratings reflect restricted distribution, not doubtful grammaticality.
