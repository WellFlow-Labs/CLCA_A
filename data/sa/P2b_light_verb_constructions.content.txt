# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Multi-word Constructions: Attested Forms

---

### BASE: सत्य (satya) — "truth, true, real"

#### C1.1
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यं वद (satyaṃ vada)
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
**English translation:** "speak truth" / "tell the truth"
**Usage notes:** Common imperative construction from Upaniṣadic injunction. Used as moral exhortation to truthfulness, appears in educational and ethical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.2
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यं ब्रूयात् (satyaṃ brūyāt)
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
**English translation:** "one should speak truth"
**Usage notes:** Optative construction expressing moral obligation. Common in dharmaśāstra literature as prescription for truthful speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.3
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यमेव जयते (satyam eva jayate)
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**English translation:** "truth alone conquers/prevails"
**Usage notes:** Famous quotation from Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (3.1.6), adopted as national motto of India. Used to express ultimate triumph of truth over falsehood.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.4
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्ये स्थितः (satye sthitaḥ)
**Transliteration:** satye sthitaḥ
**English translation:** "established in truth" / "standing firm in truth"
**Usage notes:** Locative construction describing one who is firmly committed to truth. Used in moral and spiritual characterization.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.5
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यात् न चलेत् (satyāt na calet)
**Transliteration:** satyāt na calet
**English translation:** "one should not deviate from truth"
**Usage notes:** Ablative construction expressing prohibition against departing from truth. Used in moral instruction and dharmaśāstra.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.6
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यस्य वचनम् (satyasya vacanam)
**Transliteration:** satyasya vacanam
**English translation:** "utterance of truth" / "truthful statement"
**Usage notes:** Genitive tatpuruṣa construction denoting the act of speaking truth. Used in formal and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.7
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यं प्रतिज्ञाय (satyaṃ pratijñāya)
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ pratijñāya
**English translation:** "having promised truth" / "having vowed truthfulness"
**Usage notes:** Absolutive construction describing one who has made a vow of truthfulness. Used in narrative and ritual contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.8
**Base Form:** सत्य (satya)
**Construction:** सत्यं न हन्ति (satyaṃ na hanti)
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ na hanti
**English translation:** "does not destroy/violate truth"
**Usage notes:** Negative construction describing one who does not violate truth. Used in moral characterization and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary Observations

### Productive Construction Types:

1. **Instrumental constructions with -ena:** Highly productive for expressing means or manner (pramāṇena, anumānena, pratyakṣeṇa, dharmeṇa, avidyayā, svarūpeṇa)

2. **Ablative constructions with -taḥ suffix:** Productive for expressing perspective or standpoint (pramāṇataḥ, vyavahārataḥ, paramārthataḥ)

3. **Locative constructions:** Common for expressing level or domain (vyavahāre, paramārthe, satye sthitaḥ)

4. **Copular constructions with bhavati:** Productive for predication and characterization (mithyā bhavati, pramāṇaṃ bhavati, sadṛśaṃ bhavati)

5. **Passive constructions with jñāyate:** Standard for epistemological contexts describing knowledge acquisition (pramāṇena jñāyate, pratyakṣeṇa jñāyate)

6. **Imperative constructions:** Common for moral exhortation (satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara)

7. **Dual genitive constructions:** Productive for contrasting pairs (satyāsatyayoḥ, pramābhramayoḥ, vidyāvidyayoḥ)

### Domain-Specific Patterns:

**Epistemological domain:** Strong preference for instrumental constructions with pramāṇa terms and passive jñāyate constructions

**Moral domain:** Frequent imperative and prohibitive constructions with satya and dharma

**Two-truths framework:** Specialized locative and ablative constructions with vyavahāra and paramārtha

**Soteriological domain:** Instrumental constructions with avidyā/vidyā expressing causal relationships

### Notable Absences:

- Limited multi-word constructions for asādhu, anṛta (beyond simple predications)
- Vedic terms (ṛta, anṛta) show fewer productive constructions than classical terms
- Some technical terms (viparyaya, bhrānti) have limited phraseological elaboration beyond basic predications

### Register Variation:

- Vedic/archaic constructions: ṛtasya pathā, ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca
- Classical philosophical: vyavahārataḥ, paramārthataḥ, pramāṇena jñāyate
- Proverbial/gnomic: satyam eva jayate, dharmaḥ rakṣati rakṣitaḥ, vidyā dadāti vinayam
- Exclamatory/evaluative: sādhu sādhu, sādhu uktam
