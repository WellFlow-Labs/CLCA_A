# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: English (en)

## OPPOSITION 1: true vs. false

**Term A:** true  
**Term B:** false  
**Transliteration:** /truː/ vs. /fɔːls/  
**Translation:** "corresponding to facts" vs. "not corresponding to facts"  
**Description:** Primary lexical opposition for evaluating propositional content. These terms mark whether statements, beliefs, or claims correspond to actual facts or reality.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## OPPOSITION 2: correct vs. incorrect

**Term A:** correct  
**Term B:** incorrect  
**Transliteration:** /kəˈrɛkt/ vs. /ˌɪnkəˈrɛkt/  
**Translation:** "conforming to standards/facts" vs. "not conforming to standards/facts"  
**Description:** Opposition marking conformity to established norms, rules, or facts. Spans epistemic (correct answer) and procedural (correct behavior) domains.  
**Contrast type:** morphological (in- prefix negation)  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 45: CONSTRUCTIONAL — Analytic vs. Synthetic Negation

**Construction A:** Subject + BE + NOT + truth-adj  
**Construction B:** Subject + BE + negated-adj (un-/in-/dis-)  
**Example A:** His claim is not true.  
**Example B:** The signature is inauthentic.  
**Transliteration:** /hɪz kleɪm ɪz nɒt truː/ vs. /ðə ˈsɪɡnətʃər ɪz ˌɪnɔːˈθɛntɪk/  
**Translation:** "His claim does not correspond to facts" vs. "The signature is not genuine"  
**Description:** Opposition between two negation strategies: analytic (not + adjective) is neutral, while synthetic (prefixed form) often carries stronger implications. Some bases prefer one strategy ("dishonest" standard, "*not honest" marked).  
**Contrast type:** constructional  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 46: CONSTRUCTIONAL — "Prove false" vs. "Come true"

**Construction A:** Subject + PROVE + false  
**Construction B:** Subject + COME + true  
**Example A:** The rumor proved false.  
**Example B:** His predictions came true.  
**Transliteration:** /ðə ˈruːmər pruːvd fɔːls/ vs. /hɪz prɪˈdɪkʃənz keɪm truː/  
**Translation:** "The rumor was revealed to be untrue" vs. "His predictions were realized"  
**Description:** Opposition between revelation of falsity (prove false) and realization of truth/prediction (come true). Different verbs mark different aspectual meanings; "come true" is fixed expression not productive with other adjectives.  
**Contrast type:** constructional  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Key patterns observed:**

1. **Morphological negation dominates:** The majority of oppositions (33/70) use prefix negation (un-, in-, dis-, mis-), reflecting English's productive use of negative prefixes in this domain.

2. **Multiple negation strategies:** Some bases show both analytic ("not true") and synthetic ("untrue," "false") negation, with subtle semantic/pragmatic differences.

3. **Asymmetric lexical pairs:** Several lexical oppositions show asymmetry: "liar" (suppletive) vs. "truth-teller" (compound); "honest/dishonest" (synthetic negation standard) vs. "*not honest" (marked).

4. **Constructional oppositions cluster around specific verbs:** Many constructional oppositions involve fixed expressions with specific verbs (ring, prove, get, do, make, go).

5. **Domain-specific patterns:** Technical domains (GROUP 11) show specialized oppositions (false positive/negative); moral domains show proverbs and fixed expressions.

6. **Overlapping oppositions:** Some terms participate in multiple oppositions (e.g., "true" opposes "false," "untrue," "bogus"; "real" opposes "unreal," "fake," "surreal").

7. **Gaps and asymmetries:** Not all terms have clear opposites (e.g., "self-righteous" lacks direct morphological opposite; "veridical" shows minimal oppositional patterning).
