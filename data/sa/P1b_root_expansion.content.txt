# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: √सत् (sat)
**Transliteration:** √sat  
**P1 participation:** 3 items (satya #1, asatya #2, satyatā #19)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Being, existence, reality; what genuinely is or exists. Forms the primary ontological axis of truth-evaluation in Sanskrit, linking truth to existence and reality.

### Root 2: √मा (mā) 
**Transliteration:** √mā  
**P1 participation:** 3 items (pramāṇa #7, pramā #8, anumāna #28)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Measuring, ascertaining, knowing correctly. Forms the primary epistemological axis, denoting valid cognition and means of knowledge acquisition.

### Root 3: मिथ्या (mithyā)
**Transliteration:** mithyā  
**P1 participation:** 2 items (mithyā #6, mithyātva #18)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Falsity, illusoriness, unreality. While not a verbal root in the traditional sense, this stem is the primary lexical marker of falsity and illusion, functioning as the direct antonym to satya in philosophical discourse.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: √सत् (sat) EXPANSION

#### 1.1
**Form:** सत्
**Transliteration:** sat
**Literal breakdown:** √as (to be) present participle stem
**Translation:** being, existing, real, good
**Context of use:** Used as adjective meaning "real, genuine, good" and as noun meaning "being, reality, the existent." Appears in philosophical compounds and as honorific prefix. Common in Upaniṣadic formula "sat-cit-ānanda" (being-consciousness-bliss).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.2
**Form:** सत्त्व
**Transliteration:** sattva
**Literal breakdown:** sat + -tva (abstract suffix)
**Translation:** being, existence, essence, goodness, purity
**Context of use:** Denotes existence or essential nature in philosophical contexts. In Sāṃkhya, one of three guṇas (qualities) representing purity, lightness, illumination. Used for character quality meaning goodness or courage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.3
**Form:** सत्ता
**Transliteration:** sattā
**Literal breakdown:** sat + -tā (abstract suffix)
**Translation:** existence, being, reality, entity
**Context of use:** Abstract noun for existence or being. Used in ontological discussions to denote the fact or state of existing. Common in philosophical discourse about what has being versus non-being.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.4
**Form:** सत्कार
**Transliteration:** satkāra
**Literal breakdown:** sat + kāra (making, doing)
**Translation:** honor, respect, hospitality, good treatment
**Context of use:** Denotes respectful treatment, hospitality, or honoring someone. Used in social and ritual contexts for proper reception of guests or showing respect to worthy persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.5
**Form:** सत्पुरुष
**Transliteration:** satpuruṣa
**Literal breakdown:** sat + puruṣa (man, person)
**Translation:** good person, virtuous man, gentleman
**Context of use:** Denotes person of good character or virtue. Also technical grammatical term (satpuruṣa-samāsa) for determinative compound. Used in moral evaluation of character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.6
**Form:** सत्कृत
**Transliteration:** satkṛta
**Literal breakdown:** sat + kṛta (made, done)
**Translation:** honored, respected, well-treated, esteemed
**Context of use:** Past participle describing someone who has been honored or treated well. Used in social contexts for proper reception and in descriptions of respectful treatment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.7
**Form:** सत्सङ्ग
**Transliteration:** satsaṅga
**Literal breakdown:** sat + saṅga (association, company)
**Translation:** association with the good, company of the virtuous
**Context of use:** Religious and moral term for keeping company with virtuous or spiritually advanced persons. Considered important spiritual practice in devotional traditions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.8
**Form:** असत्
**Transliteration:** asat
**Literal breakdown:** a- (negative) + sat
**Translation:** non-being, unreal, bad, evil
**Context of use:** Denotes non-existence, unreality, or what is bad/evil. Used in ontological discussions (sat vs. asat) and moral contexts. Appears in cosmogonic discussions of being emerging from non-being.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.9
**Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Literal breakdown:** satya + vādin (speaking, from √vad)
**Translation:** truth-speaking, truthful, veracious
**Context of use:** Describes person who speaks truth habitually. Used as moral virtue in ethical discourse and legal contexts where truthful testimony is required.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.10
**Form:** सत्यवचन
**Transliteration:** satyavacana
**Literal breakdown:** satya + vacana (speech, statement)
**Translation:** true statement, truthful speech
**Context of use:** Denotes a true or truthful utterance. Used in contexts requiring verification of statements or in moral discourse about truthful speech as virtue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.11
**Form:** सत्यव्रत
**Transliteration:** satyavrata
**Literal breakdown:** satya + vrata (vow, observance)
**Translation:** vow of truthfulness, committed to truth
**Context of use:** Denotes vow or commitment to truthfulness. Used for persons dedicated to truth-speaking as religious or moral discipline. Also appears as proper name.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.12
**Form:** सत्यसन्ध
**Transliteration:** satyasandha
**Literal breakdown:** satya + sandha (binding, keeping)
**Translation:** keeping truth, faithful to promises, honest
**Context of use:** Describes person who keeps promises and commitments. Used in moral and legal contexts for reliability and faithfulness to one's word.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.13
**Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Literal breakdown:** satya + āpana (causing to reach, from ā + √āp)
**Translation:** verification, confirmation, validation
**Context of use:** Modern administrative and legal term for verification or confirmation of facts. Used in contexts requiring official validation or authentication of claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.14
**Form:** सत्यनिष्ठ
**Transliteration:** satyaniṣṭha
**Literal breakdown:** satya + niṣṭha (devoted to, established in)
**Translation:** devoted to truth, firmly established in truth
**Context of use:** Describes person firmly committed to truthfulness. Used in moral and spiritual discourse for unwavering dedication to truth as principle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.15
**Form:** सत्यम्
**Transliteration:** satyam
**Literal breakdown:** satya (neuter nominative/accusative singular)
**Translation:** truly, indeed, verily (as adverb); truth (as noun)
**Context of use:** Used as emphatic particle meaning "truly, indeed" in affirmations. Also functions as noun for truth. Common in ritual and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.16
**Form:** सत्यार्थ
**Transliteration:** satyārtha
**Literal breakdown:** satya + artha (meaning, object, purpose)
**Translation:** true meaning, real purpose, genuine object
**Context of use:** Denotes the true or real meaning of something, as opposed to apparent or superficial meaning. Used in interpretive contexts and discussions of genuine versus ostensible purposes.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.17
**Form:** सत्यभाषिन्
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣin
**Literal breakdown:** satya + bhāṣin (speaking, from √bhāṣ)
**Translation:** truth-speaking, truthful speaker
**Context of use:** Similar to satyavādin, describes one who speaks truth. Used in moral characterization and legal contexts requiring truthful testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.18
**Form:** सत्यधर्म
**Transliteration:** satyadharma
**Literal breakdown:** satya + dharma (duty, righteousness)
**Translation:** duty of truthfulness, truth as religious duty
**Context of use:** Denotes truthfulness as moral or religious obligation. Used in ethical discourse where truth-speaking is prescribed as dharmic duty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 1.19
**Form:** सत्यक्रिया
**Transliteration:** satyakriyā
**Literal breakdown:** satya + kriyā (action, rite)
**Translation:** act of truth, truth-act (ritual affirmation)
**Context of use:** Ritual or solemn affirmation of truth, sometimes with magical efficacy. Used in narrative contexts where truthful declaration produces supernatural effects.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 1.20
**Form:** सत्यलोक
**Transliteration:** satyaloka
**Literal breakdown:** satya + loka (world, realm)
**Translation:** world of truth, realm of Brahman
**Context of use:** In cosmology, the highest heaven or realm of ultimate reality, abode of Brahman. Used in religious and mythological discourse about celestial realms.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### ROOT 2: √मा (mā) EXPANSION

#### 2.1
**Form:** माप
**Transliteration:** māpa
**Literal breakdown:** √mā + -a (action noun)
**Translation:** measuring, measurement
**Context of use:** General term for act of measuring or measurement. Used in practical contexts of quantification and in technical discussions of standards and metrics.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.2
**Form:** मान
**Transliteration:** māna
**Literal breakdown:** √mā + -ana (instrument/action noun)
**Translation:** measure, standard, respect, honor, pride
**Context of use:** Polysemous term meaning measurement/standard in quantitative contexts, and respect/honor in social contexts. Also denotes pride or self-regard (sometimes negative connotation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.3
**Form:** मात्रा
**Transliteration:** mātrā
**Literal breakdown:** √mā + -trā (instrument suffix)
**Translation:** measure, quantity, degree, vowel-length mark
**Context of use:** Denotes measure or quantity in general sense. Technical term in prosody for syllabic quantity (long/short). Used for degree or extent of qualities.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.4
**Form:** प्रमाता
**Transliteration:** pramātā
**Literal breakdown:** pra + √mā + -tṛ (agent suffix)
**Translation:** knower, cognizer, subject of knowledge
**Context of use:** Technical epistemological term for the knowing subject, one who cognizes. Used in philosophical analysis of knowledge situation (knower-knowing-known).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.5
**Form:** प्रमेय
**Transliteration:** prameya
**Literal breakdown:** pra + √mā + -eya (gerundive suffix)
**Translation:** knowable, object of knowledge
**Context of use:** Technical term for object of valid cognition, what can be known. Used in epistemological discussions of the knowable versus unknowable.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.6
**Form:** प्रमाणत्व
**Transliteration:** pramāṇatva
**Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -tva (abstract suffix)
**Translation:** validity, status of being a valid means of knowledge
**Context of use:** Abstract term for the quality or status of being a valid epistemic instrument. Used in philosophical debates about what constitutes valid knowledge sources.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.7
**Form:** प्रमाणाभास
**Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāsa
**Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + ābhāsa (appearance, semblance)
**Translation:** semblance of valid knowledge, pseudo-proof
**Context of use:** Technical term for what appears to be valid knowledge but is actually fallacious. Used in logic and epistemology for spurious arguments or invalid reasoning.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.8
**Form:** अप्रमा
**Transliteration:** apramā
**Literal breakdown:** a- (negative) + pramā
**Translation:** invalid cognition, erroneous knowledge
**Context of use:** Negation of pramā, denoting cognition that fails to correspond to reality. Used in epistemological discussions contrasting valid and invalid knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.9
**Form:** उपमान
**Transliteration:** upamāna
**Literal breakdown:** upa- (near, like) + √mā + -ana
**Translation:** comparison, analogy, simile (as means of knowledge)
**Context of use:** As pramāṇa, knowledge through comparison or analogy (e.g., knowing gavaya is like cow). Also used generally for comparison or simile in rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.10
**Form:** उपमा
**Transliteration:** upamā
**Literal breakdown:** upa- + √mā + -ā
**Translation:** comparison, simile, resemblance
**Context of use:** Rhetorical term for simile or comparison. One of the primary figures of speech (alaṅkāra) in poetics. Used for any comparative statement or analogy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.11
**Form:** प्रमाणीकरण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa
**Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ī- + karaṇa (making)
**Translation:** validation, authentication, certification
**Context of use:** Modern administrative term for process of validating or certifying something as authentic. Used in legal and bureaucratic contexts for official authentication.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.12
**Form:** प्रमाणित
**Transliteration:** pramāṇita
**Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ita (past participle suffix)
**Translation:** validated, authenticated, certified, proven
**Context of use:** Describes something that has been validated or proven. Used in legal, administrative, and scholarly contexts for authenticated or certified items.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.13
**Form:** प्रमाणपत्र
**Transliteration:** pramāṇapatra
**Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + patra (document, letter)
**Translation:** certificate, credential, testimonial
**Context of use:** Modern term for official certificate or credential document. Used in educational, professional, and administrative contexts for certification documents.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.14
**Form:** अनुमिति
**Transliteration:** anumiti
**Literal breakdown:** anu- (after) + √mā + -ti (action noun)
**Translation:** inference, inferential knowledge
**Context of use:** Technical term for the act or result of inference. Used interchangeably with anumāna in epistemological discourse about inferential reasoning.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.15
**Form:** अनुमेय
**Transliteration:** anumeya
**Literal breakdown:** anu- + √mā + -eya (gerundive)
**Translation:** inferable, to be inferred
**Context of use:** Technical term for what can be or is to be inferred. Used in logical analysis of inference structure (inferable property from perceived evidence).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.16
**Form:** अनुमापक
**Transliteration:** anumāpaka
**Literal breakdown:** anu- + √mā + -aka (agent suffix)
**Translation:** inferential sign, probans, reason
**Context of use:** Technical term in logic for the inferential sign or reason (hetu) that enables inference. Used in analysis of inference structure.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.17
**Form:** परिमाण
**Transliteration:** parimāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** pari- (around, complete) + √mā + -ana
**Translation:** measure, size, quantity, extent
**Context of use:** General term for measurement, size, or quantity of something. Used in mathematical, physical, and general descriptive contexts for dimensions or extent.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.18
**Form:** निर्माण
**Transliteration:** nirmāṇa
**Literal breakdown:** nir- (out, forth) + √mā + -ana
**Translation:** construction, creation, making, building
**Context of use:** General term for construction or creation of something. Used for physical building, artistic creation, and abstract formation or establishment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.19
**Form:** सम्मान
**Transliteration:** sammāna
**Literal breakdown:** sam- (together, complete) + √mā + -ana
**Translation:** honor, respect, esteem
**Context of use:** Term for showing honor or respect to someone. Used in social contexts for respectful treatment and formal honoring of persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2.20
**Form:** अपमान
**Transliteration:** apamāna
**Literal breakdown:** apa- (away, negative) + √mā + -ana
**Translation:** dishonor, disrespect, insult
**Context of use:** Opposite of sammāna, denotes disrespect or insult. Used in social and legal contexts for dishonorable treatment or defamation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

### Extensions of √सत् (sat):

**सत्कार (satkāra)**, **सत्पुरुष (satpuruṣa)**, **सत्कृत (satkṛta)**, **सत्सङ्ग (satsaṅga)**: The root sat- here shifts from ontological "being/reality" to evaluative "good/virtuous." These compounds use sat- as marker of positive moral quality rather than truth or existence per se.

**सत्त्व (sattva)**: In Sāṃkhya usage as one of three guṇas, the term extends beyond simple "existence" to denote a specific quality (purity, lightness, illumination) in material nature—a shift from ontological to qualitative-physical domain.

**सत्यलोक (satyaloka)**: Cosmological usage where satya denotes highest realm/heaven represents extension into mythological-spatial domain, though connection to "ultimate reality" maintains some link to truth-domain.

**सत्यक्रिया (satyakriyā)**: Extension into ritual-magical domain where truth-statement has performative/supernatural efficacy, beyond simple correspondence to facts.

### Extensions of √मा (mā):

**मान (māna)** as "respect, honor, pride": Significant shift from epistemological "measuring/knowing" to social-psychological domain of esteem and self-regard. The connection through "evaluation" is present but represents domain extension.

**सम्मान (sammāna)** and **अपमान (apamāna)**: Similar extension into social domain of honor/dishonor, moving away from cognitive measurement toward social evaluation.

**निर्माण (nirmāṇa)**: Extension from "measuring" to "making/constructing"—shift from cognitive-epistemic to productive-creative domain.

**उपमा (upamā)** and **उपमान (upamāna)**: While upamāna as pramāṇa remains epistemic, upamā as rhetorical simile extends into aesthetic-literary domain of figurative language.

**मात्रा (mātrā)**: In prosodic usage as vowel-length marker, represents technical-grammatical extension beyond general quantification.

### Extensions of मिथ्या (mithyā):

**मिथ्योपचार (mithyopacāra)**: Extension into grammatical-rhetorical domain where mithyā marks figurative/metaphorical usage rather than cognitive error—shift from epistemological to linguistic-conventional domain.

**मिथ्यारोप (mithyāropa)**: While remaining in philosophical domain, this technical Vedāntic term represents specialized metaphysical usage beyond simple falsity, denoting specific mechanism of illusion-production.
