# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 NATURALNESS: CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE/CORRECT

### 1.1 menjadi benar
**Transliteration:** menjadi benar
**Translation:** to become true, to become correct
**Usage notes:** Standard inchoative construction with *menjadi* "to become." Used when something that was false or incorrect becomes true or correct. Common in contexts of verification or correction.
**Example context:** "Prediksi itu akhirnya menjadi benar" (That prediction finally became true)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 1.2 menjadi kenyataan
**Transliteration:** menjadi kenyataan
**Translation:** to become reality, to come true
**Fixedness:** Fixed collocation, extremely common
**Usage notes:** Preferred over *menjadi nyata* for abstract realization of hopes, dreams, or predictions. Very frequent in narrative and aspirational contexts.
**Example context:** "Impiannya menjadi kenyataan" (His/her dream came true)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 1.3 menjadi nyata
**Transliteration:** menjadi nyata
**Translation:** to become real, to be realized
**Usage notes:** Used for concrete realization or manifestation of something previously abstract or potential. Less common than *menjadi kenyataan* for abstract concepts.
**Example context:** "Rencana itu menjadi nyata" (That plan became real/was realized)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 1.4 ternyata benar
**Transliteration:** ternyata benar
**Translation:** it turns out to be true, it proved to be true
**Usage notes:** Evidential construction marking discovery that something is true, often contrary to prior belief or expectation. Very common discourse pattern.
**Example context:** "Kabar itu ternyata benar" (That news turned out to be true)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 1.5 terbukti benar
**Transliteration:** terbukti benar
**Translation:** proven true, proven correct
**Usage notes:** Marks demonstrated truth through evidence or proof. Common in investigative and legal contexts.
**Example context:** "Teorinya terbukti benar" (His/her theory proved true)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 1.6 berubah menjadi benar
**Transliteration:** berubah menjadi benar
**Translation:** to change to become true, to turn out to be true
**Usage notes:** Emphasizes transformation from false to true state. *Berubah* "to change" adds emphasis on the transformation process.
**Example context:** "Informasi yang salah itu berubah menjadi benar setelah diverifikasi" (That incorrect information changed to become true after verification)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. BECOMING FALSE/WRONG

### 2.1 menjadi salah
**Transliteration:** menjadi salah
**Translation:** to become wrong, to become incorrect
**Usage notes:** Standard inchoative construction. Used when something correct becomes wrong, or when an action results in error.
**Example context:** "Jawabannya menjadi salah karena kesalahan hitung" (The answer became wrong due to calculation error)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.2 ternyata salah
**Transliteration:** ternyata salah
**Translation:** it turns out to be wrong, it proved to be wrong
**Usage notes:** Evidential construction marking discovery of error, often contrary to prior belief. Very common discourse pattern.
**Example context:** "Dugaan saya ternyata salah" (My guess turned out to be wrong)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.3 terbukti salah
**Transliteration:** terbukti salah
**Translation:** proven wrong, proven incorrect
**Usage notes:** Marks demonstrated incorrectness through evidence. Common in investigative and argumentative contexts.
**Example context:** "Tuduhan itu terbukti salah" (That accusation proved wrong)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.4 terbukti bohong
**Transliteration:** terbukti bohong
**Translation:** proven false, proven to be a lie
**Usage notes:** Specifically marks demonstrated falsehood or deception. Stronger moral judgment than *terbukti salah*.
**Example context:** "Kesaksiannya terbukti bohong" (His/her testimony proved to be a lie)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.5 ternyata bohong
**Transliteration:** ternyata bohong
**Translation:** it turns out to be a lie, it turned out to be false
**Usage notes:** Evidential construction marking discovery of deception. Common in contexts of betrayal or revelation.
**Example context:** "Janjinya ternyata bohong" (His/her promise turned out to be a lie)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2.6 berubah menjadi salah
**Transliteration:** berubah menjadi salah
**Translation:** to change to become wrong
**Usage notes:** Emphasizes transformation from correct to incorrect state. Less common than simple *menjadi salah*.
**Example context:** "Data yang benar berubah menjadi salah karena kesalahan input" (The correct data changed to become wrong due to input error)
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 42. EXPRESSIONS WITH NO COMMON EQUIVALENT

### 42.1 "X becomes more genuine/authentic"
**Status:** NO COMMON EXPRESSION
**Notes:** Authenticity is typically treated as binary (genuine or fake) rather than gradual in Indonesian. While *menjadi lebih asli* is grammatically possible, it is not a natural or common expression. Authenticity is verified (*terbukti asli*) rather than acquired gradually.

### 42.2 "X becomes less fake"
**Status:** NO COMMON EXPRESSION
**Notes:** Similar to above, fakeness is typically binary. *Menjadi kurang palsu* is grammatically possible but not natural. Items are either fake or genuine, not gradually less fake.

### 42.3 "X becomes more real"
**Status:** NO COMMON EXPRESSION
**Notes:** While *menjadi lebih nyata* is grammatically possible and occasionally used, it is not a common or natural expression. Reality is typically treated as achieved (*menjadi nyata*, *terwujud*) rather than gradual.

### 42.4 "X causes Y to become fake"
**Status:** NO COMMON EXPRESSION
**Notes:** Fakeness is typically created intentionally (*memalsukan* "to forge") rather than caused to develop. There is no common expression for causing something genuine to become fake.

### 42.5 "X maintains fakeness"
**Status:** NO COMMON EXPRESSION
**Notes:** While *tetap palsu* "remains fake" is possible, there is no common expression for actively maintaining fakeness. Fakes are discovered (*ternyata palsu*) rather than maintained.

### 42.6 "X becomes gradually more honest"
**Status:** LIMITED EXPRESSION
**Notes:** While *menjadi lebih jujur* "to become more honest" exists (see 5.2), there is no specific expression for gradual progressive honesty like *semakin jujur*. Honesty changes are typically described as discrete states rather than gradual processes.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productive Patterns:

1. **Inchoative with *menjadi***: Highly productive across all truth-domain adjectives for state changes
2. **Evidential with *ternyata***: Very productive for discoveries of truth-states
3. **Resultative with *terbukti***: Productive for demonstrated truth-states through evidence
4. **Causative with *meN-...-kan***: Highly productive for causing truth-states
5. **Stative with *tetap***: Productive for maintaining truth-states
6. **Aspectual with *masih***: Productive for continued truth-states
7. **Progressive with *semakin/makin***: Productive for gradual changes in truth-states
8. **Light verb constructions**: Very productive with truth-nominalizations (*kebenaran*, *kejujuran*, *kesalahan*, etc.)

### Restricted Patterns:

1. **Authenticity changes**: Authenticity (*asli/palsu*) resists gradual change expressions; typically binary
2. **Reality changes**: Reality (*nyata/fiktif*) shows limited gradual change expressions
3. **Character transformations**: Honesty/dishonesty changes are more discrete than gradual

### Asymmetries:

1. **Truth vs. Falsehood**: More expressions for discovering/proving truth than for discovering/proving falsehood
2. **Becoming vs. Causing**: More causative expressions than simple inchoative expressions for some domains
3. **Positive vs. Negative**: More expressions for maintaining truth than for maintaining falsehood
4. **Gradual vs. Sudden**: More expressions for gradual approach to truth than for sudden changes
