## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for non-volitional BECOME

The BECOME operator is only partially covered in Georgian. The adessive + motion verb construction (*siq̇albeze c̣asvla*, "resorting to falsehood") expresses volitional, agentive transition into a state of falsity or deception, and is rated [MARGINAL] in P4b. For non-volitional becoming — the coming-to-light of a hidden truth or fraud without an expressed agent — Georgian uses the REVELATION construction (*siq̇albe gamoašk'aravda*, "the fraud was exposed / came to light"), which is rated [CORE]. The two constructions are not interchangeable: the adessive + motion verb requires a volitional agent, while the resultative verb construction takes the truth-domain nominal itself as subject with no agent expressed. The REVELATION construction therefore systematically covers the non-volitional portion of the BECOME semantic space.

### 2.2 AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION + ATTR substitutes for CAUSATION (character)

No dedicated predicate construction for CAUSATION (character) — that is, a construction that predicates of a person that they habitually or characteristically cause a truth-domain state — is attested. Instead, Georgian uses agentive nominals (*mt'q̇uari*, "liar"; *gamaq̇albebeli*, "forger") in the ATTR construction (Construction 1) to assign a character-level truth-domain property to a person. The agentive nominal encodes the agent of the relevant action, and the copular predication assigns that characterization to the subject. This is a two-step substitution: AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION (Construction 7) + ATTR (Construction 1) together cover the CAUSATION (character) slot.

### 2.3 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share constructions; differentiation is lexical

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are not distinguished constructionally in Georgian. Both are expressed through the same two constructions: resultative participial attribution (Construction 9) and genitive modification (Construction 8). The distinction between confirming/verifying and falsifying/disconfirming is carried entirely by the choice of lexical root (verification root vs. falsification root), not by any difference in construction type, morphological pattern, or syntactic frame. This is a lexical differentiation within a shared constructional template.

### 2.4 Asymmetry in agentive nominalization: truth-teller vs. liar

A lexical asymmetry is noted within the AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION construction. A single lexicalized agentive nominal for "liar" (*mt'q̇uari*) is attested, but no parallel single-word agentive nominal for "truth-teller" is attested. The concept of a habitual truth-teller is expressed periphrastically (*simartlis mtqveli*, "one who tells the truth"), which is a genitive modification + participial construction rather than a root-derived agentive nominal. This asymmetry is lexical, not morphosyntactic: the **-ari** pattern is productive and could in principle generate such a form, but no such form is attested in the data.

### 2.5 Register asymmetry in NOMINALIZATION

Within the NOMINALIZATION construction, the **si-...-e/le** and **-eba/-oba** patterns are not in free variation. The **si-...-e/le** pattern (*simartle*, *siq̇albe*, *sizuste*) is associated with colloquial and everyday registers, while the **-eba/-oba** pattern (*č̣ešmariteba*, *k'anoniereba*) is associated with formal, philosophical, and legal registers. Both patterns are [CORE] and syntactically equivalent (both serve as subjects, objects, and genitive bases), but they are not interchangeable across registers. This is a register-internal differentiation within a single CLCA operator slot, not a gap.

### 2.6 NEGATION: both analytic and morphological strategies are available without restriction

Georgian supports both analytic negation (*sicrue ar aris*, "it is not a lie") and morphological privative negation (*uzusto*, "inaccurate") within the NEGATION operator. The analytic strategy applies to all nominal predicates and is not domain-restricted. The morphological privative (**u-...-o**) applies to adjectival bases and is productive across the truth domain. The two strategies are not in simple complementary distribution: both are available for adjectival bases, but only the analytic strategy is available for nominal predicates. No asymmetry between positive and negative polarity is noted at the constructional level.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps (missing constructions)

- **CAUSATION (factual):** No synchronically productive morphological or syntactic causative construction applied to truth-domain adjectives as a class is attested. The lexical verbal formations (*gaq̇albeba*, *gasc̣oreba*) exist but are individual lexical items, not instances of a productive causative pattern. This is a **morphosyntactic gap**: the construction type is absent, though individual lexical items cover some of the semantic ground.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps (missing words)

- **Agentive nominal for "truth-teller":** No single lexicalized agentive nominal parallel to *mt'q̇uari* ("liar") is attested for the positive-polarity agent. The concept requires periphrastic expression (*simartlis mtqveli*). This is a **lexical gap** within an otherwise productive morphological pattern.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps (blocked operations)

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data. All major truth-domain semantic operations (attribution, modification, becoming, revelation, verification, disconfirmation, negation, nominalization, agent characterization) are expressible in Georgian, though some require substitution or circumlocution.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (only one polarity attested)

- **BECOME (volitional):** The adessive + motion verb construction (*siq̇albeze c̣asvla*) is attested only for the falsity/deception pole (transition into falsehood). No parallel construction for volitional transition into truth or truthfulness is attested in the data. This is an **asymmetric gap** within the BECOME construction: the falsity-directed form is marginal but attested; the truth-directed form is unattested.
- **Agentive nominalization polarity:** As noted in 3.2, the **-ari** agentive nominal is attested for the falsity/lying pole (*mt'q̇uari*) but not for the truth/truth-telling pole. This is an **asymmetric lexical gap**.
