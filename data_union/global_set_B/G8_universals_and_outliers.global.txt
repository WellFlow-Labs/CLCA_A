# G8 — Candidate Universals, Outliers, and Open Questions

---

## 1. Candidate Universals / Strong Tendencies

---

**CU-01. Copular attribution of truth-domain properties is available in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus uses a copular or zero-copula construction to predicate truth-domain properties of subjects. This construction is attested for all eleven semantic dimensions wherever the dimension is lexicalized, and no blocking of this function is documented for any language.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages (zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu) across D1–D11. The copula is overt in zh (*是*), el (*είναι*), en (*be*), tl (*ay*), sw (*ni*); absent by default in he, sa, and qu. In qu, the obligatory evidential enclitic (*-m/-mi*) substitutes for the copula as the primary marker of assertive force. F-A is classified ALLOWED for all 11 dimensions in G7a with no documented exceptions.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The form of the copula varies considerably. In qu, the evidential enclitic performs functions that the copula performs in other languages, making the structural parallel less direct. In sa, the copula *asti* is optionally retained for emphasis but is not the default. These are formal variations, not functional exceptions.

**Confidence rating.** High.

---

**CU-02. Negation of truth-domain attributions is available in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has at least one strategy for negating truth-domain attributions, and this strategy applies across all semantic dimensions where the dimension is lexicalized.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. Strategies include prefixal negation (sa *a-/an-*; el *αν-/α-*; en *in-/un-/dis-*; he *ʾi-* on nominalizations; tl *di-*), syntactic negation (zh *不/不是*; tl *hindi*; qu *mana…-chu*), and suppletive copular negation (sw *si* replacing *ni*). F-B is classified ALLOWED for all 11 dimensions in G7a with no documented exceptions.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The morphological form of negation varies substantially. The he *ʾi-* prefix applies to *-ut* nominalizations but not to the corresponding adjectives, creating an asymmetry between adjectival and nominalized negation not paralleled in other languages. The qu *mana…-chu* pattern requires the negative polar marker *-chu* on the predicate, making the negation morphologically integrated into the predicate in a way not paralleled elsewhere. These are language-specific formal properties, not exceptions to the availability of negation.

**Confidence rating.** High.

---

**CU-03. Abstract nominalization of truth-domain evaluative bases is productive in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus derives abstract property nouns from truth-domain evaluative bases, and these nominals are used in formal, legal, and philosophical registers.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The nominalizing element varies: suffix in zh (*-性*), he (*-ut*), sa (*-tā/-tva*), el (*-ότητα*), en (*-ity/-ness*); prefix in sw (*u-*); circumfix in tl (*ka-…-an*); copular nominalization in qu (*-kay*). The resulting nominals are used in legal, journalistic, and philosophical registers across all languages. F-F is classified ALLOWED for D1–D7 in all 8 languages in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In zh, the *-性* suffix is restricted to disyllabic adjectival compounds; monosyllabic roots (真, 假) do not take *-性* productively. In qu, the copular nominalization strategy is less morphologically compact than the suffix strategies in other languages. For D8–D11, abstract nominalization is more variable: D9 (verisimilitude) is nominalized in 5 of 8 languages; D10 and D11 primarily in zh and sa.

**Confidence rating.** High for D1–D7; medium for D8–D11.

---

**CU-04. Negative-pole agents in the truth domain are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents across all attested languages.**

**Statement.** In every language in the corpus, the concept of "one who lies" or "one who deceives" is expressed by a single lexicalized word, while the concept of "one who tells the truth" requires either a compound form or a periphrastic construction.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages across unrelated language families. Single-word negative-pole agentives: zh *骗子* (swindler), *伪造者* (forger); he *shaqran* (liar); sa *asatyavādin* (false-speaker); el *ψεύτης* (liar); en *liar*, *faker*, *counterfeiter*; tl *sinungaling* (liar); sw *mwongo* (liar), *mdanganyifu* (deceiver); qu *llullaq* (liar). Positive-pole agentives requiring compound or periphrasis: zh *说真话的人*; he *dover ʾemet* (participial construction); sa *satyavādin* (compound); el *αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια*; en *truth-teller* (compound); tl *taong tapat*; sw *mtu wa kweli*; qu *cheqaq runa* (compound). This asymmetry is documented in G4 and G7a and is not explained by any single morphological factor.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In sa, *satyavādin* (truth-speaker) is a compound but is a single lexical item in the grammatical sense; it is not a periphrastic construction. In he, *dover ʾemet* (one who speaks truth) is a participial construction that is conventionalized but not a single morphological word. The asymmetry holds across all 8 languages but the degree of lexicalization of the positive-pole agentive varies. In some languages (sa, he), the positive-pole agentive is a conventionalized compound rather than a fully periphrastic construction.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-05. Discourse-framing constructions signaling frank or corrective speech are attested in all languages and show a cross-linguistic tendency toward grammaticalization.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has at least one fixed or semi-fixed expression used at the beginning or margin of an utterance to signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment of the actual state of affairs, often in contrast to a prior claim or expectation. These constructions show a cross-linguistic tendency to grammaticalize from compositional phrases into discourse particles or adverbials.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages: zh *实际上*, *事实上*, *本质上*; he *ha-ʾemet hi she-* (the truth is that); sa *vastutaḥ*, *tattvataḥ* (*-taḥ* adverbials); el *η αλήθεια είναι ότι*; en *to tell the truth*, *truth be told*; tl *sa totoo lang*, *ang totoo*; sw *kwa kweli*, *ukweli ni kwamba*; qu *cheqaqta rimay*. The degree of grammaticalization varies from fully grammaticalized adverbials (zh *实际上*; sa *vastutaḥ*) to conventionalized fixed phrases (en *to tell the truth*; tl *sa totoo lang*; sw *kwa kweli*). F-H is classified ALLOWED for D1 and D5 in all 8 languages in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The degree of grammaticalization varies considerably across languages. In qu, the framing function is expressed through an imperative construction (*cheqaqta rimay*: speak the truth) rather than through a nominalized or adverbial form. In sa, the *-taḥ* adverbials are conventionalized in commentarial prose but may not be as broadly distributed across registers as the corresponding expressions in other languages. The function is consistent; the form varies substantially.

**Confidence rating.** High for the existence of the function; medium for the grammaticalization tendency.

**CU-06. Causative constructions for verification, authentication, and correction are productive in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has productive causative constructions for the act of verifying, confirming, authenticating, or correcting a truth-domain state. These constructions are particularly associated with legal, administrative, and journalistic registers.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The morphological form varies: zh uses dedicated lexical verbs (*核实*, *鉴定*, *纠正*); he uses the Pi'el binyan (*leʾammet*, *letaqqef*); sa uses the *-aya-* causative suffix (*satyāpayati*); el uses dedicated derived verbs (*επαληθεύω*, *αποδεικνύω*); en uses dedicated derived verbs (*verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*); tl uses the *pa-…-an* paradigm (*patunayan*); sw uses the *-sha/-isha* suffix (*kuthibitisha*, *kuhakikisha*); qu uses *-ncha-* or *-chi-* (*cheqanchay*, *cheqachiy*). F-E is classified ALLOWED for D1 and D2 in all 8 languages in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** Zh is the only language in the corpus where the causative function in this domain is expressed exclusively through dedicated lexical verbs rather than through a productive causative morpheme. In qu, the causative pathway is asymmetric: the *-ncha-* verbalizer applies to positive-pole adjectival roots but not to the negative-pole root *llulla*, which uses *-chi-* directly. These are language-specific formal properties, not exceptions to the availability of the causative function.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-07. Propositional complement embedding of truth-domain evaluations is available in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus can embed a truth-domain predication as the complement of a verb of knowing, believing, proving, or saying.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The complementizer strategy varies: nominalized clause in qu (*cheqa kasqanta yachani*: I know that it is true) and sa; complementizer particle in he (*she-*), el (*ότι*), en (*that*), tl (*na*), sw (*kwamba*); embedded clause in zh. F-I is classified ALLOWED for all dimensions in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The structural form of the embedded clause varies considerably. In qu and sa, the embedded clause is nominalized (the predication is treated as a nominal argument of the matrix verb). In other languages, a complementizer particle introduces the embedded clause without nominalization. These are structural differences, not exceptions to the availability of the function.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-08. Deontic constructions enjoining truthful speech or prohibiting lying are attested in all languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has constructions that express an obligation to tell the truth or a prohibition against lying. These constructions are among the most culturally salient in the truth domain.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages: zh *严禁弄虚作假*, *不得造假*; he *tsarikh ledaber ʾemet*; sa *satyaṃ brūyāt*, *satyaṃ vada*, *satyāt na pramaditavyam*; el *πρέπει να λέει την αλήθεια*; en *must tell the truth*, *it is prohibited to deceive*; tl *kailangan mong sabihin ang katotohanan*; sw *kusema ukweli ni wajibu*; qu *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan*, *ama llullakuychu*. F-J is classified ALLOWED for D1, D5, and D6 in all 8 languages in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In zh, prohibitive constructions (*严禁*, *不得*) are restricted to the deliberate-falsification sub-domain and do not extend freely to truth-evaluation adjectives or authenticity nouns. In sa, the optative (*brūyāt*) and gerundive (*pramaditavyam*) are the standard forms for ethical injunctions; these are morphologically distinct from the modal constructions used in other languages. The function is consistent; the morphological form varies.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-09. Degree modification of truth-domain predications is available in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has constructions for asserting that something is absolutely, completely, or only partially true, genuine, or correct.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The strategy varies: degree adverbs in most languages (zh *完全正确*, *千真万确*; he *bediyyuq*; el *αληθώς*; en *absolutely true*); reduplication in tl (*totoong-totoo*), sw (*kweli kweli*), and zh (partial); emphatic suffix *-puni* and limitative suffix *-lla* in qu; intensifier *kabisa* in sw. F-K is classified ALLOWED for D1–D6 in all 8 languages in G7a.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The morphological form of degree modification varies considerably. In qu, degree modification is morphologically integrated into the predicate through suffixes (*-puni*, *-lla*), while in other languages it is expressed through separate adverbs or through reduplication. Reduplication as an intensification strategy is documented in tl, sw, and zh but not in he, sa, el, or en for truth-domain items specifically.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-10. The primary truth/falsehood binary is lexicalized in all attested languages through a foundational antonym pair.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus has a primary lexical pair that names the positive and negative poles of the truth-evaluation domain, and both poles participate in the same construction types (predication, modification, nominalization, agentive nominalization).

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages: zh 真/假; he *ʾemet/sheqer*; sa *satya/asatya*; el *αληθής/ψευδής*; en *true/false*; tl *totoo/kasinungalingan*; sw *kweli/uongo*; qu *cheqa/llulla*. The pair is the foundational evaluative axis of the domain in every language. Both poles participate in the same construction types in all languages. This is documented in G1, G2, G3, G5, and G6.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In tl, the negative pole (*kasinungalingan*) is a derived nominal from *sinungaling* (liar), while the positive pole (*totoo*) is a simple root. This creates a morphological asymmetry between the two poles not present in most other languages. In sw, *kweli* and *uongo* are both simple roots but differ in their derivational productivity: *kweli* generates *ukweli* (abstract nominal) while *uongo* generates *mwongo* (agentive nominal) through different morphological pathways.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-11. The propositional truth / object genuineness distinction is maintained in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus maintains distinct vocabulary for evaluating whether a statement is true and whether an object, document, or person is genuine, even when a single root participates in both domains.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The degree of lexical separation varies: zh maintains the clearest distinction (真实 for factual accuracy of reports vs. 真伪 for object genuineness); he distinguishes *ʾemet* (propositional truth) from *mezuyaf* (forged document); sa distinguishes *satya* (truth of statements) from *pratirūpa* (imitation); el distinguishes *αληθής* (true statement) from *πλαστός* (forged document); en distinguishes *true* from *fake*; tl distinguishes *totoo* from *huwad/peke*; sw distinguishes *kweli* from *bandia*; qu distinguishes *cheqam* (it is true, propositional) from *cheqa qelqa* (authentic document, compound). This is documented in G1, G2, G3, and G6.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In qu and tl, a single root (*cheqa*, *totoo*) participates in both domains, with the distinction maintained through collocational context rather than through distinct lexical items. In sa, the distinction is less elaborated than in other languages; *pratirūpa* (imitation) is the closest term for object inauthenticity but is not as productive as *mezuyaf* (he) or *πλαστός* (el).

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-12. Positive emergence constructions (discovery that something is true or genuine) are more natural than spontaneous-change constructions across most attested languages.**

**Statement.** Across most languages in the corpus, constructions describing the discovery or revelation that something is true or genuine (e.g., *proved true*, *turned out to be genuine*, *was revealed as authentic*) are more natural than constructions describing a spontaneous change into a truth-domain state (e.g., *became true*, *became genuine*). This preference is particularly strong for categorical truth-value predicates.

**Evidence summary.** Supported by F6 naturalness data across 7 of 8 languages. In el, *αποδείχτηκε αληθής* (proved to be true) is [CORE] while *γίνομαι* + truth-domain adjective is [Non-idiomatic] for propositional truth transitions specifically. In en, *proved true* and *turned out to be true* are [CORE] while *became true* is [MARGINAL]. In zh, *他的判断变得正确了* (his judgment became correct) is [MARGINAL]. In he, *hitgalaʾh ke-ʾotenti* (was revealed as authentic) is [CORE]. In tl, *naging totoo* (became true) is [CORE] but the particle *na* construction (*totoo na*) is more colloquial and more frequently attested. In sw, *ulidhihirika* (became evident, *-ika* stative) is [CORE]. In qu, *cheqakay* (to become true) is documented but the *-sqa* participial resultative (*cheqanchasqam*: it has been verified) is more consistently attested. This pattern aligns with AOML's Rule R4 and the provisional REVEAL operator.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In tl, the *naging totoo* construction is [CORE] and is not restricted in the same way as the corresponding constructions in el and en. In sw, the *-ika* stative suffix provides a productive morphological inchoative that is [CORE] for truth-domain predicates. The pattern is strong but not absolute: some languages have productive morphological inchoatives for truth-domain predicates that are [CORE] rather than [MARGINAL].

**Confidence rating.** Medium-high.

**CU-13. Causation of authenticity destruction (forgery, falsification) is more consistently lexicalized than causation of authenticity creation across all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Across all languages in the corpus, causative constructions for destroying or falsifying authenticity (forging a document, counterfeiting goods, adulterating a substance) are more consistently lexicalized and more natural than causative constructions for creating or conferring authenticity.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. Negative-pole causatives: zh *伪造*, *造假*, *掺假*; he *lezayef* (to forge); sa *asatyavādin* (false-speaker, agentive); el *πλαστογραφώ* (to forge); en *forge*, *counterfeit*, *fabricate*, *falsify*; tl *huwad* (fake, used in causative constructions); sw *kughushi* (to commit fraud), *kudanganya* (to deceive); qu *llullachiy* (to cause to lie). Positive-pole causatives for conferring authenticity are less consistently lexicalized: authentication causatives (*核实*, *leʾammet*, *επαληθεύω*, *verify*, *patunayan*, *kuthibitisha*, *cheqanchay*) exist but are oriented toward establishing the epistemic status of a claim about authenticity, not toward creating authenticity itself. F6 data: he *ha-mismakh hafakh le-mezuyaf be-yadav* (the document became forged in his hands) is [UNCERTAIN] — the causative is strongly preferred over the inchoative for forgery. This aligns with AOML's Rule R3.

**Caveats / exceptions.** The distinction between "causing authenticity to be established" (verification causatives, which are [CORE]) and "causing something to be authentic" (which is not attested as natural) is important. Verification causatives operate on the epistemic status of a claim, not on the authenticity property itself. This distinction is maintained in the data but requires careful interpretation.

**Confidence rating.** Medium-high.

**CU-14. Proverbial or semi-fixed expressions asserting that truth is painful, endures, or will eventually be revealed are attested in most languages.**

**Statement.** In most languages in the corpus, there are fixed or semi-fixed expressions encoding the cultural belief that truth is difficult to hear, that truth cannot be permanently suppressed, or that truth will ultimately prevail over falsehood.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in 6 of 8 languages: zh *真金不怕火炼* (true gold fears no fire); he *ʾemet marah* (bitter truth), *ʾemet koʾevet* (painful truth); el *η αλήθεια πονάει* (the truth hurts), *η αλήθεια βγαίνει πάντα στο φως* (truth always comes to light); en *the truth hurts*, *the truth will out*; tl *ang katotohanan ay masakit* (the truth is painful), *ang katotohanan ay lalabas din* (the truth will come out eventually); sw *ukweli unauma* (the truth hurts), *kweli haiozi* (truth does not rot), *ukweli daima hushinda* (truth always prevails). Not clearly documented in sa or qu in the available data.

**Caveats / exceptions.** Not documented in sa or qu. In sa, the closest expressions are philosophical maxims (*satyam eva jayate*: truth alone conquers) rather than proverbial expressions about the painfulness or inevitability of truth. In qu, the *ama llulla* prohibitive triad is a culturally fixed formula but does not encode the same semantic content as the proverbial expressions in other languages. The pattern is strong in 6 of 8 languages but cannot be claimed as universal on the available evidence.

**Confidence rating.** Medium.

**CU-15. The correctness-relative-to-a-standard dimension is lexically distinct from the propositional truth dimension in all attested languages.**

**Statement.** Every language in the corpus maintains at least some lexical distinction between evaluating whether a statement corresponds to the facts (D1) and evaluating whether an answer, procedure, or action conforms to a relevant standard or norm (D3), even when the same root participates in both domains.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in all 8 languages. The degree of lexical separation varies: zh maintains the clearest distinction (真实 for correspondence to reality vs. 正确 for conformity to a standard); he distinguishes *ʾemet* (truth) from *nakhon* (correct, right) and *meduyaq* (precise); sa distinguishes *satya* from *sādhu* (grammatically correct) and *yukta* (appropriate); el distinguishes *αληθής* from *σωστός* (correct) and *ακριβής* (precise); en distinguishes *true* from *correct* and *accurate*; tl distinguishes *totoo* from *tama* (correct) and *wasto* (proper); sw distinguishes *kweli* from *sahihi* (correct, accurate); qu distinguishes *cheqa* from *chiqan* (straight, correct) and *allin* (good, right). This is documented in G1, G2, G3, and G6.

**Caveats / exceptions.** In qu, *allin* covers both moral goodness and practical correctness, merging D3 with moral evaluation in a way not paralleled in most other languages. In tl, *tama* (correct) and *wasto* (proper, conforming to standard) are distinguished by register and degree of normative emphasis, but both are used in contexts that overlap with D1 in some collocations. The distinction is maintained in all 8 languages but the degree of lexical separation varies considerably.

**Confidence rating.** High.

**CU-16. Partial-truth constructions are attested in most languages and are consistently evaluated negatively.**

**Statement.** In most languages in the corpus, there is a fixed or semi-fixed expression for a statement that is technically accurate but misleading through omission (a "half-truth"), and this expression consistently carries a negative evaluative force.

**Evidence summary.** Attested in 7 of 8 languages: zh *半真半假* (half true, half false); he *ḥatsi ʾemet* (half-truth); el *μισή αλήθεια* (half-truth); en *half-truth*; tl *kalahating katotohanan* (half-truth); sw *nusu ukweli* (half-truth). Not documented in qu in the available data. In all 6 languages where the expression is attested, it carries a negative evaluative force: a half-truth is not merely a weaker form of truth but is evaluated as a form of deception or dishonesty.

**Caveats / exceptions.** Not documented in qu or sa in the available data. The absence in qu may reflect a gap in the data rather than a genuine absence in the language. The expression is consistently formed through a "half + truth" compound or phrase in zh, he, el, tl, and sw; en has additional expressions (*a grain of truth*, *economical with the truth*) that encode partial truth through different metaphors.

**Confidence rating.** Medium.

## 2. Major Outliers

---

### 2.1 Language-Level Outliers

**L-OUT-01. Quechua (qu): Grammaticalized evidential morphology as an obligatory category.**

*What diverges.* Quechua is the only language in the corpus with a grammaticalized three-way evidential system (*-m/-mi* direct, *-s/-si* reportative, *-chá* conjectural) that is obligatorily marked on predicates. In all other languages, the source of knowledge is expressed lexically (en *reportedly*, *allegedly*; sw *inasemekana*; tl *daw*) or through specific constructions, but not as an obligatory grammatical category.

*Strength of divergence.* Strong. The grammaticalization of source-of-knowledge marking as an obligatory morphological feature is a typologically significant property that has no parallel in the other 7 languages in the corpus.

*Breadth of divergence.* Broad. The evidential system interacts with all primary truth-domain dimensions (D1–D8) and substitutes for several constructional functions that are expressed through separate constructions in other languages (F-A, F-H, F-L).

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference, not a gap in the data. The evidential system is thoroughly documented in the qu F-phase data.

---

**L-OUT-02. Sanskrit (sa): Elaborated ontological and epistemological sub-domains with no parallel in other languages.**

*What diverges.* Sanskrit is the only language in the corpus that develops a distinct semantic group for the ontological status of being (D11) with named intermediate categories (*mithyā*: neither real nor unreal) and named levels of analysis (*paramārtha*, *vyāvahārika*, *pratibhāsa*). It also develops the most elaborate epistemological sub-domain (D8) through the *pramāṇa* family, distinguishing the means of knowledge, the property of being a valid means, and the debate between intrinsic and extrinsic validity (*svataḥprāmāṇya* vs. *parataḥprāmāṇya*).

*Strength of divergence.* Strong for D11; moderate for D8. The *mithyā* category (neither real nor unreal) introduces a three-valued ontological system that conflicts with AOML's binary CONTR assumption and has no parallel in any other language in the corpus.

*Breadth of divergence.* Moderate. The divergence is concentrated in D11 and the epistemological sub-domain of D8. For D1–D7, sa patterns are broadly consistent with the other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the philosophical traditions documented in the sa data. The *mithyā* category is thoroughly attested in Advaita Vedānta literature.

---

**L-OUT-03. Chinese (zh): Fixed four-character expressions as a productive word-formation strategy.**

*What diverges.* Chinese is the only language in the corpus with a productive fixed four-character expression format (*成语*) that functions as a single evaluative unit in the truth domain. Internal negation (inserting *不* or *非* into the fixed sequence) is a productive strategy for producing antonyms within this format (*名副其实* → *名不副实*; *表里如一* → *表里不一*). This format encodes evaluative meanings that in other languages would require a full clause or a periphrastic construction.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The four-character expression format is a language-specific word-formation strategy, but the evaluative meanings it encodes are not unique to zh; they are expressed through other means in other languages.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the word-formation level; the semantic content and constructional functions are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the morphological properties of Chinese. The four-character expression format is thoroughly documented in the zh F-phase data.

---

**L-OUT-04. Tagalog (tl): Discourse particle system encoding epistemic stances toward truth predicates.**

*What diverges.* Tagalog is the only language in the corpus with a system of discourse particles (*ba*, *na*, *pala*, *nga*, *naman*) that encode epistemic stances toward truth predicates as grammatical particles rather than through separate lexical items or constructions. The *totoo na* construction (change-of-state particle *na* marking confirmation of something previously doubted) is particularly distinctive.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The particle system is a language-specific grammatical feature, but the epistemic stances it encodes are expressed through other means in other languages.

*Breadth of divergence.* Moderate. The particle system interacts with D1, D2, D4, D5, and D8, but the semantic content is broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the grammatical properties of Tagalog. The particle system is thoroughly documented in the tl F-phase data.

---

**L-OUT-05. Swahili (sw): Pervasive Arabic loanword stratum in the truth domain.**

*What diverges.* Swahili is the only language in the corpus where a large proportion of the core truth-domain vocabulary is borrowed from Arabic (*sahihi*, *halisi*, *halali*, *haramu*, *hakika*, *amini*, *shahidi*, *ushahidi*). This creates a distinctive semantic profile in which the legal-religious legitimacy dimension (D7) is expressed through the *halali/haramu* pair, which carries both Islamic legal and broader secular evaluative meanings — a fusion not paralleled in other languages.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate for the loanword stratum; strong for the *halali/haramu* religious-legal fusion.

*Breadth of divergence.* Moderate. The loanword stratum affects the lexical form of truth-domain vocabulary but not the constructional functions, which are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the historical contact between Swahili and Arabic. The loanword stratum is thoroughly documented in the sw F-phase data.

### 2.2 Construction-Level Outliers

**C-OUT-01. The sw *-ika/-eka* stative suffix: morphological distinction between agentive passive and inchoative.**

*What diverges.* Swahili is the only language in the corpus with a morphological distinction between an agentive passive (*-wa*: to be deceived by an agent) and a stative/inchoative (*-ika/-eka*: to be taken in, no agent implied) that is semantically significant in the truth domain. In other languages, this distinction is expressed through separate constructions or lexical choices rather than through dedicated morphological markers.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The distinction is semantically significant and is explicitly documented in the sw F-phase data (*kudanganyika* vs. *kudanganywa*).

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the morphological encoding of the agentive/inchoative distinction; the semantic distinction itself is present in other languages through other means.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the morphological properties of Swahili Bantu morphology.

---

**C-OUT-02. The qu causative pathway asymmetry: *-ncha-* for positive-pole roots vs. *-chi-* for negative-pole root.**

*What diverges.* In Quechua, the *-ncha-* causative verbalizer applies to positive-pole adjectival roots (*cheqa* → *cheqanchay*, *allin* → *allinchay*, *sut'i* → *sut'inchay*) but not to the negative-pole root *llulla*, which uses *-chi-* directly (*llullachiy*). Additionally, no inchoative form (*llullakay*) is documented for *llulla*, while inchoative forms are productive for positive-pole roots (*cheqakay*, *allinkay*). This creates a morphological asymmetry between positive and negative evaluative roots that has no parallel in the other 7 languages.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The asymmetry is clearly documented in the qu F-phase data and is explicitly noted in G4 and G7a.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the morphological encoding of causative and inchoative functions for the negative-pole root; the semantic content is broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the morphological properties of Southern Quechua. The asymmetry is thoroughly documented.

**C-OUT-03. The sw light-verb asymmetry: *sema* for truth-telling vs. *piga* for lying.**

*What diverges.* In Swahili, the act of telling the truth is expressed through *sema ukweli* (speak + truth) while the act of lying is expressed through *piga uongo* (strike/do + falsehood), with no *piga kweli* construction attested. This creates a constructional asymmetry in which the two poles of the truth/falsehood domain are expressed through different light verbs.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The asymmetry is explicitly documented in the sw F-phase data and in G4 and G5.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the light-verb construction for truth-telling and lying; other constructional functions are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference. The absence of *piga kweli* is explicitly noted in the sw data as an attested gap.

---

**C-OUT-04. The he *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry: applies to *-ut* nominalizations but not to corresponding adjectives.**

*What diverges.* In Hebrew, the *ʾi-* prefix negates *-ut* nominalizations (*ʾi-ʾaminut*, *ʾi-sevirut*, *ʾi-diyyuq*) but not the corresponding adjectives, which are negated syntactically with *lo* or with *bilti*. This creates an asymmetry between the adjectival and nominalized negation systems not paralleled in the other 7 languages.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The asymmetry is clearly documented in the he F-phase data and in G4 and G6.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the morphological encoding of negation for *-ut* nominalizations; the semantic content is broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the morphological properties of Modern Hebrew. The *ʾi-* prefix has acquired particular institutional salience in Israeli administrative law (*ʾi-sevirut qitsunit*).

**C-OUT-05. The el *αποδείχτηκε* vs. *γίνομαι* asymmetry for truth-value transitions.**

*What diverges.* In Greek, *αποδείχτηκε* + adjective (proved to be) is [CORE] for truth-value transitions while *γίνομαι* + truth-domain adjective is [Non-idiomatic] for propositional truth transitions specifically. This restriction is more sharply documented in el than in any other language in the corpus.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The F6 data explicitly rates *γίνομαι* + truth-domain adjective as [Non-idiomatic] for propositional truth transitions, while the same construction is [CORE] with *αμφίβολος* (doubtful) and *άκυρος* (invalid).

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The restriction applies specifically to propositional truth adjectives (*αληθινός*, *σωστός*) and not to all truth-domain adjectives. The restriction is consistent with the broader positive-emergence bias (CU-12) but is more sharply documented in el than elsewhere.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This appears to be a genuine structural property of Greek, not a gap in the data. The F6 data is explicit.

### 2.3 Dimension-Specific Outliers

**D-OUT-01. D11 (Ontological status of being) is elaborated as a distinct semantic group only in Sanskrit.**

*What diverges.* Sanskrit is the only language in the corpus that develops D11 (ontological status of being) into a distinct semantic group with named intermediate categories (*mithyā*: neither real nor unreal) and named levels of analysis (*paramārtha*, *vyāvahārika*, *pratibhāsa*). In all other languages, D11 is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary without a dedicated grouping.

*Strength of divergence.* Strong. The *mithyā* category introduces a three-valued ontological system that has no parallel in any other language in the corpus.

*Breadth of divergence.* Moderate. The divergence is concentrated in D11; for D1–D7, sa patterns are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* This is a genuine structural difference reflecting the philosophical traditions documented in the sa data. However, it is possible that other languages have ontological vocabulary that was not captured in the F-phase data collection. The divergence may be partly a reflection of the philosophical register of the sa data.

**D-OUT-02. D9 (Appearance of truth without assertion) is not documented in Swahili.**

*What diverges.* Swahili is the only language in the corpus for which D9 (appearance of truth or genuineness without assertion of actual truth) is not documented as a distinct pattern in the F-phase data. The *parang totoo* (tl), *cheqa hina* (qu), *αληθοφανής* (el), *ābhāsa* (sa), *逼真* (zh), and *plausible* (en) constructions have no documented parallel in sw.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The absence may reflect a gap in the data rather than a genuine absence in the language.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to D9; sw patterns for D1–D8 are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* The absence of D9 documentation in sw is more likely a gap in the data than a genuine structural absence. Swahili has vocabulary for resemblance (*fanana*: to resemble) and appearance (*kuonekana*: to appear) that could in principle be used in D9 constructions. The absence should be treated as a data gap pending further investigation.

**D-OUT-03. D7 (Legal or institutional validity) is not documented as a distinct lexical grouping in Quechua.**

The *taqef/batel* (he), *έγκυρος/άκυρος* (el), *valid/invalid* (en), *halali/haramu* (sw), and *合法/违法* (zh) pairs have no documented parallel in qu.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* The absence of D7 documentation in qu is more likely a gap in the data than a genuine structural absence. Quechua-speaking communities have legal systems and administrative procedures that presumably require vocabulary for legal validity. The absence should be treated as a data gap pending further investigation.

**D-OUT-04. D10 (Correspondence between label and actual nature) is only clearly lexicalized as a distinct grouping in Chinese and Sanskrit.**

*What diverges.* Chinese and Sanskrit are the only languages in the corpus that clearly lexicalize D10 (correspondence between a label or description and the actual nature of the thing) as a distinct semantic grouping. In most other languages, this territory is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary without a dedicated grouping.

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate for zh and sa; weak for other languages. The zh *名副其实/名不副实* pair and the sa *mukhya/gauṇa* distinction are clearly distinct from D1 and D2 vocabulary. In other languages, the distinction is present but not lexicalized as a dedicated grouping.

*Breadth of divergence.* Moderate. The divergence is concentrated in D10; for D1–D7, zh and sa patterns are broadly consistent with other languages.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* The elaboration of D10 in zh and sa may reflect the specific philosophical and rhetorical traditions documented in those languages (the classical Chinese *名/实* distinction; the Sanskrit *mukhya/gauṇa* distinction in Mīmāṃsā). Other languages may have vocabulary for this dimension that was not captured in the F-phase data collection.

**D-OUT-05. The ABSTR × LIFE (verisimilitude nominalization) block claimed by AOML is not confirmed by the empirical data.**

*What diverges.* AOML claims that abstract nominalization of verisimilitude (ABSTR × LIFE) is universally blocked (14:2 ratio across 16 languages). The empirical data from 8 languages shows abstract nominalizations for verisimilitude attested in 5 of 8 languages: el *αληθοφάνεια* [CORE], zh 真实感 [CORE], sa *ābhāsa* (functions as abstract nominal), en *verisimilitude* [MARGINAL], sw *uhalisia* [MARGINAL].

*Strength of divergence.* Moderate. The conflict is real within the available data, but the AOML claim is based on 16 languages while the empirical data here covers 8 languages. The conflict may reflect sampling differences.

*Breadth of divergence.* Narrow. The divergence is confined to the ABSTR × LIFE combination; other constructional functions for D9 are broadly consistent with AOML predictions.

*Structural difference or limited evidence.* The conflict may reflect a difference between the aesthetic verisimilitude sub-domain (LIFE in the narrow sense, where the block may be stronger) and the broader epistemic appearance sub-domain (D9 in the empirical data, where nominalization is more freely attested). The distinction between these two sub-domains is not modeled in AOML.

## 3. Open Questions

---

**OQ-01. Is the agentive asymmetry (negative-pole agents more lexicalized than positive-pole agents) a universal or a strong tendency?**

The finding that negative-pole agents (liar, deceiver, forger) are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-teller) is documented across all 8 languages in the corpus and across unrelated language families. However, the corpus covers only 8 languages, and the asymmetry has not been tested against a broader typological sample. The question is whether this is a genuine universal or a strong tendency with exceptions in languages not yet examined. Additional language coverage — particularly from language families not represented in the current corpus (e.g., Niger-Congo languages other than Swahili, Dravidian, Japonic, Turkic) — would be needed to assess the universality of this pattern.

---

**OQ-02. Is the discourse-framing grammaticalization pattern (truth nominals → discourse particles) a universal or a strong tendency?**

The grammaticalization of truth nominals into discourse-framing constructions is attested in all 8 languages in the corpus. However, the degree of grammaticalization varies considerably, and the question of whether this is a universal tendency or a strong cross-linguistic pattern with exceptions remains open. The pattern may be driven by pragmatic universals (the need to signal frank or corrective speech) that interact with the semantic architecture of the truth domain, but this hypothesis requires testing against a broader typological sample.

---

**OQ-03. What is the status of the ABSTR × LIFE block?**

AOML claims that abstract nominalization of verisimilitude is universally blocked (14:2 ratio across 16 languages). The empirical data from 8 languages shows abstract nominalizations for verisimilitude attested in 5 of 8 languages. The conflict may reflect sampling differences, a distinction between aesthetic verisimilitude (where the block may be stronger) and epistemic appearance (where nominalization is more freely attested), or a genuine weakness in the AOML claim. Resolving this question requires: (a) examining the 16 languages in the AOML database to determine which languages were counted as blocking ABSTR × LIFE; (b) distinguishing between aesthetic verisimilitude (LIFE in the narrow sense) and epistemic appearance (D9 in the empirical data); and (c) testing the block against additional languages.

---

**OQ-04. Does the positive-emergence bias (CU-12) hold universally, or are there languages with productive spontaneous-change constructions for truth-domain predicates?**

The F6 naturalness data across 7 of 8 languages supports a preference for revelation/discovery constructions over spontaneous-change constructions for truth-domain predicates. However, tl and sw have productive morphological inchoatives (*naging totoo*, *ulidhihirika*) that are [CORE] rather than [MARGINAL]. The question is whether the positive-emergence bias is a universal tendency or whether some languages have genuinely productive spontaneous-change constructions for truth-domain predicates. Additional F6 testing across more languages, with explicit comparison of revelation constructions vs. spontaneous-change constructions, would be needed to resolve this question.

---

**OQ-05. Is the D4/D5 distinction (reliability of sources vs. inner sincerity of persons) cross-linguistically robust?**

The empirical data maintains a clear distinction between D4 (reliability of sources and institutions over time) and D5 (inner sincerity of persons). AOML conflates both under SINC. The question is whether this distinction is cross-linguistically robust or whether it is an artifact of the specific languages examined. In tl, *tapat* and *katapatan* merge D4 and D5, suggesting that the distinction is not universal. In he, *neʾeman* covers both faithfulness (D5) and reliability (D4). Additional language coverage would be needed to determine whether the D4/D5 distinction is a strong cross-linguistic tendency or a language-specific elaboration.

---

**OQ-06. What is the cross-linguistic status of D10 (correspondence between label and actual nature)?**

D10 is only clearly lexicalized as a distinct semantic grouping in zh and sa. In most other languages, this territory is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary. The question is whether D10 is a genuinely distinct semantic dimension that is lexicalized in some languages but not others, or whether it is a sub-domain of D1 or D2 that is elaborated in zh and sa due to specific philosophical and rhetorical traditions. Additional language coverage — particularly from languages with strong philosophical or rhetorical traditions that emphasize the relationship between names and things — would be needed to assess the cross-linguistic status of D10.

---

**OQ-07. Is the causative restriction for dispositional traits (Rule R1) universal, or are there languages with productive causatives for sincerity and honesty?**

Rule R1 (CAUSE × DISPOSITIONAL → universally blocked) receives strong support from the empirical data: no language in the corpus lexicalizes a causative for "making someone sincere" or "making someone honest." However, the corpus covers only 8 languages, and the rule has not been tested against a broader typological sample. The question is whether this is a genuine universal or a strong tendency with exceptions. Additional language coverage, with explicit testing of causative constructions for dispositional traits, would be needed to assess the universality of Rule R1.

---

**OQ-08. What is the cross-linguistic distribution of the evidential source-marking function (F-L)?**

The grammaticalized evidential system in qu (*-m/-mi*, *-s/-si*, *-chá*) is the only instance in the corpus of obligatory source-of-knowledge marking as a morphological category. However, many languages not in the current corpus have grammaticalized evidential systems (e.g., Turkish, Tibetan, various Amazonian languages). The question is whether the interaction between evidential morphology and truth-domain vocabulary follows consistent cross-linguistic patterns, and whether the qu patterns are representative of evidential languages more broadly. Extending the corpus to include additional evidential languages would be needed to address this question.

---

**OQ-09. Is the partial-truth construction (CU-16) universal, and does it consistently carry negative evaluative force?**

Partial-truth constructions are attested in 7 of 8 languages in the corpus and consistently carry negative evaluative force. The question is whether this pattern is universal or whether there are languages in which partial truth is evaluated neutrally or positively. The absence of the construction in qu may reflect a data gap or a genuine structural absence. Additional language coverage, with explicit testing of partial-truth constructions and their evaluative force, would be needed to assess the universality of this pattern.

---

**OQ-10. What is the relationship between the truth domain and the evidential domain cross-linguistically?**

The qu evidential system interacts with all primary truth-domain dimensions and substitutes for several constructional functions that are expressed through separate constructions in other languages. The question is whether the interaction between evidential morphology and truth-domain vocabulary follows consistent cross-linguistic patterns, and whether the grammaticalization of evidentiality systematically affects the availability of other constructional functions (F-A, F-H, F-L) in the truth domain. This question requires both broader language coverage and more systematic testing of the interaction between evidential systems and truth-domain vocabulary.

---

**OQ-11. Does the *mithyā* three-valued ontological system in Sanskrit have parallels in other philosophical traditions not represented in the current corpus?**

The sa *mithyā* category (neither real nor unreal) introduces a three-valued ontological system that has no parallel in the other 7 languages in the corpus. The question is whether this is a unique feature of Advaita Vedānta philosophy or whether similar three-valued ontological systems exist in other philosophical traditions and are reflected in the vocabulary of other languages. Languages with strong Buddhist philosophical traditions (e.g., Classical Tibetan, Classical Chinese in Buddhist registers) might have parallel vocabulary. Extending the corpus to include such languages would be needed to address this question.

---

**OQ-12. Is the positive-pole causative block for authenticity (CAUSE(+) × AUTH) universal?**

The empirical data supports the finding that authenticity cannot be manufactured (CAUSE(+) × AUTH not attested as natural) while authenticity can be destroyed or forged (CAUSE(−) × AUTH: forgery causatives are [CORE] in all 8 languages). This aligns with AOML's Rule R3. However, verification causatives (*核实*, *leʾammet*, *kuthibitisha*, *patunayan*) are [CORE] in all 8 languages and operate on the epistemic status of a claim about authenticity. The question is whether the distinction between "causing authenticity to be established" (verification causatives, which are licensed) and "causing something to be authentic" (which is not attested as natural) is cross-linguistically robust, and whether there are languages in which the positive-pole causative for authenticity is more freely licensed. Additional language coverage and more systematic testing of this distinction would be needed.
