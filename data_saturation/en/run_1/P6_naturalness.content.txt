# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
# Language: English (en)

---

**Note on scope:** This step documents how English encodes dynamic truth-related meanings — becoming, causing, and remaining in truth-domain states — using only vocabulary attested in the P1–P5 context. Each subsection treats a distinct vocabulary item or cluster where behavior differs. Where no common expression exists for a given dynamic type, this is stated explicitly. Transliteration is not applicable for English (Latin script native).

---

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1.1 *true* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- *come true* — lexicalized inchoative, restricted to *true* with subjects denoting wishes, predictions, or fears (*Her prediction came true*; *The dream came true*). Documented in P1b (1.13) and P4a (Construction 4). This is the only fully lexicalized inchoative in the domain; no parallel *\*come false*, *\*come accurate*, or *\*come genuine* exists.

- *prove true* — periphrastic inchoative expressing confirmation by events or evidence (*His suspicions proved true*). Documented in P1b (1.16) and P4a (Construction 4). *Prove* here functions as a copular verb of result rather than an agentive verb.

- *turn out to be true* — general periphrastic inchoative, register-neutral (*The rumor turned out to be true*). Documented in P4a (Construction 4).

- *hold true* — expresses the continuation or extension of a truth-domain state to new cases rather than a change of state (*This principle holds true in all contexts*). Documented in P1b (1.15). This is a stability expression rather than a strict inchoative; see Section 3 below.

**Paradigm gap:** No single-word inchoative verb meaning *to become true* exists in English. The periphrastic strategies above are the only attested options.

---

### 1.2 *false* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- *prove false* — periphrastic inchoative expressing disconfirmation by events or evidence (*The hypothesis proved false*). Documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P5 (Opposition 32). Parallel to *prove true* but not lexicalized.

- *turn out to be false* — general periphrastic inchoative (*The account turned out to be false*). Register-neutral.

- *ring false* — expresses the impression of falseness upon hearing or reading rather than a change of state (*The explanation rang false from the start*). Documented in P2b (C10.15) and P5 (Opposition 31). This is an evaluative expression rather than a strict inchoative.

**Paradigm gap:** No lexicalized inchoative parallel to *come true* exists for *false*. *\*Come false* is not attested.

---

### 1.3 *accurate* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- *turn out to be accurate* — general periphrastic inchoative (*The forecast turned out to be accurate*). This is the standard construction; no lexicalized or dedicated form exists.

- *prove accurate* — attested in formal and journalistic registers (*The prediction proved accurate*). Parallel to *prove true*.

**Paradigm gap:** No lexicalized inchoative exists for *accurate*. No dedicated verb meaning *to become accurate* is attested in the P1–P5 data.

---

### 1.4 *genuine* / *authentic* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- No inchoative expressions for *genuine* or *authentic* are attested in the P1–P5 data. The concepts of genuineness and authenticity are not typically framed as states that things enter over time; they are treated as properties that either obtain or do not from the outset.

- The nearest attested construction is the causative *authenticate* (see Section 2.3 below), which describes the act of establishing that something is genuine, not the thing itself becoming genuine.

**Paradigm gap:** No expression meaning *X becomes genuine* or *X becomes authentic* is attested in the data. This gap appears to reflect a conceptual constraint: genuineness is not typically construed as an acquired state.

---

### 1.5 *valid* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- No dedicated inchoative expression for *valid* is attested in the P1–P5 data. The nearest construction is *become valid*, which is grammatical but not a fixed or conventionalized expression in the data.

- In legal contexts, instruments and acts are described as *taking effect* or *coming into force* rather than *becoming valid*, though these are not vocabulary items from the P1–P5 set.

**Paradigm gap:** No lexicalized or conventionalized inchoative for *valid* is attested in the data.

---

### 1.6 *legitimate* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- *become legitimate* — grammatical and attested in political and social discourse, but not a fixed or lexicalized expression in the P1–P5 data.

- The causative *legitimize* (P2a.19.6) implies a prior state of not being legitimate and a transition to legitimacy, but the inchoative perspective (from the subject's point of view) is not separately lexicalized.

**Paradigm gap:** No dedicated inchoative for *legitimate* is attested beyond the general periphrastic *become legitimate*.

---

### 1.7 *credible* / *plausible* — inchoative

**Attested expressions:**

- *gain credibility* — an attested periphrastic construction in which a person or claim acquires believability over time (*The theory gained credibility as evidence accumulated*). This is a nominal construction using the abstract noun *credibility* rather than the adjective *credible* directly.

- *become more plausible* — grammatical and attested in analytical discourse, but not a fixed expression.

- *lend credibility to* (P2b.C17.5) — this is a causative construction (see Section 2 below) rather than an inchoative.

**Paradigm gap:** No lexicalized inchoative for *credible* or *plausible* is attested. The nominal construction *gain credibility* is the closest conventionalized expression for *credible*.

## 2. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 2.1 Causing something to be confirmed as true: *verify*, *prove*, *establish*

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *verify* (P1b.2.1) — *They verified the claim* / *The results verified the hypothesis*. The agent confirms that something is true or genuine. Documented as a causative verb in P4a (Construction 5). Applies to claims, identities, and data.

- *verification* (P1b.2.2) — the nominalization of the process: *The verification of the account took three days*.

- *cross-verify* (P1b.2.20) — *They cross-verified the information against multiple sources*. Expresses verification through multiple independent checks.

- *prove* (in the causative sense) — *The evidence proved the claim true* / *The experiment proved the hypothesis correct*. *Prove* here takes a small clause complement (NP + adjective) and functions as a causative copular verb. Documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P4b.

- *ground truth* (P1b.1.25) — used as a noun denoting verified factual information used as a standard: *The satellite data was checked against ground truth*. Not a causative verb but a nominal expression for the product of verification.

---

### 2.2 Causing something to be false or to appear false: *falsify*, *disprove*, *invalidate*

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *falsify* (P1b.3.6 / P2a.10.4) — *They falsified the documents* / *The data had been falsified*. The agent deliberately alters something to misrepresent the truth. Documented as a causative verb in P4a (Construction 5). Applies to documents, records, and evidence.

- *falsification* (P1b.3.7 / P2a.10.5) — the nominalization: *The falsification of records is a criminal offense*.

- *invalidate* (P2a.6.8) — *The new evidence invalidated the argument* / *The procedural error invalidated the contract*. The agent or event renders something without logical or legal force. Documented in P4a (Construction 5) and P5 (Opposition 27).

- *invalidation* (P2a.6.9) — the nominalization: *The invalidation of the test results required a rerun*.

- *delegitimize* (P2a.19.9) — *The campaign sought to delegitimize the election results*. The agent actively undermines the recognized legitimacy of something. Documented in P5 (Opposition 28).

- *delegitimization* (P2a.19.10) — the nominalization: *The delegitimization of the institution was a deliberate strategy*.

---

### 2.3 Causing something to be recognized as genuine: *authenticate*, *validate*, *legitimize*

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *authenticate* (P2a.5.3) — *The expert authenticated the painting* / *The system authenticates the user*. The agent establishes that something is genuine or that a person is who they claim to be. Documented in P4a (Construction 5).

- *authentication* (P2a.5.4) — the nominalization: *Two-factor authentication is required* / *The authentication of the document took several weeks*.

- *validate* (P2a.6.3) — *The committee validated the results* / *The test validated the hypothesis*. The agent confirms that something meets required standards or has logical or legal force. Documented in P4a (Construction 5).

- *validation* (P2a.6.4) — the nominalization: *The validation of the instrument took six months*.

- *legitimize* (P2a.19.6) — *The election legitimized the new government* / *The treaty legitimized the territorial claim*. The agent or event confers legitimacy on something. Documented in P5 (Opposition 28).

- *legitimization* / *legitimation* (P2a.19.7–19.8) — the nominalizations: *The legitimation of authority requires public consent*.

- *entrust* (P1b.1.36) — *They entrusted the task to a reliable source*. The agent places something in the care of a person deemed trustworthy. This is a causative expression in the reliability sub-domain rather than the truth-evaluation domain proper.

---

### 2.4 Causing something to appear credible or plausible: *lend credibility to*, *strain credibility*

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *lend credibility to* (P2b.C17.5) — *The expert testimony lent credibility to the claim*. The agent or event increases the perceived believability of something. Documented in P3 (Group 5).

- *strain credibility* (P2b.C17.6) — *The account strained credibility*. The subject pushes the limits of believability, causing the audience to doubt. Documented in P3 (Group 5). This is a causative expression in which the subject causes a reduction in perceived credibility.

- *lull into a false sense of security* (P2b.C10.12) — *The early results lulled the team into a false sense of security*. The agent or event causes someone to hold an unwarranted belief in safety or correctness. Documented in P2b. This is a causative expression in the falseness sub-domain.

---

### 2.5 Causing a statement to be corrected: *correct*, *invalidate*, *set the record straight*

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *correct* (P1.7, verbal use) — *The editor corrected the error* / *She corrected the false impression*. The agent rectifies an inaccuracy. Documented in P4a (Construction 5) and P3 (Group 9).

- *correction* (P2a.7.6) — the nominalization: *The newspaper issued a correction*.

- *set the record straight* (P2b.C8.5) — *He set the record straight about what had happened*. The agent corrects a false or misleading account that has been circulating. Documented in P3 (Group 9).

- *stand corrected* (P2b.C7.4) — *I stand corrected*. The subject acknowledges that they have been shown to be in error and accepts the correction. This is a reflexive or passive causative construction in which the subject accepts the result of being corrected.

## 3. STABILITY — REMAINING IN A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 3.1 *hold true* — stability of a principle or generalization

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *hold true* (P1b.1.15) — *This principle holds true in all contexts* / *The same observation holds true for the second group*. Expresses that a truth-domain state continues to apply or remains valid across cases or over time. Documented in P1b and P3 (Group 1). This is the primary stability expression in the domain.

- *remain true* — grammatical and attested but not a fixed expression in the P1–P5 data. Parallel to *hold true* but less conventionalized in this domain.

- *still valid* (P2b.C6.4) — *The argument is still valid* / *The contract is still valid*. Expresses that a legal or logical validity state persists despite the passage of time or changed circumstances. Documented in P2b.

- *remain accurate* — grammatical but not a fixed expression in the P1–P5 data.

---

### 3.2 *self-correcting* — stability through internal correction

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *self-correcting* (P2a.7.9) — *Science is a self-correcting enterprise* / *The system is self-correcting*. Applied to systems and processes that maintain accuracy over time by identifying and rectifying their own errors. Documented in P2a. This is a stability expression that frames accuracy as a dynamic equilibrium rather than a static state.

---

### 3.3 *reliable* / *consistently reliable* — stability of a source or instrument

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- *consistently reliable* (P2b.C16.4) — *The instrument has proven consistently reliable*. Expresses that a reliability state is maintained on a regular and predictable basis. Documented in P2b.

- *proven reliable* (P2b.C16.3) — *The method has been proven reliable over decades of use*. Expresses that reliability has been demonstrated through sustained performance. Documented in P2b.

---

### 3.4 *trustworthy* — stability of a person's character

**Attested expressions from P1–P5:**

- No dedicated stability expression for *trustworthy* is attested in the P1–P5 data beyond the base adjective itself used predicatively (*She has always been trustworthy*). The stability sense is carried by temporal adverbs (*always*, *consistently*, *invariably*) rather than by a dedicated construction.

## 4. CROSS-CUTTING OBSERVATIONS

### 4.1 Asymmetry between positive and negative causative verbs

The P1–P5 data shows a structural asymmetry in the causative domain. Dedicated causative verbs exist for both directions of change:

- **Toward truth/genuineness/validity:** *verify*, *validate*, *authenticate*, *legitimize*, *correct*
- **Away from truth/genuineness/validity:** *falsify*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*, *fabricate*, *counterfeit*

However, the negative-direction verbs (*falsify*, *fabricate*, *counterfeit*) are more strongly associated with deliberate agency and intent than the positive-direction verbs (*verify*, *validate*), which can describe both agentive acts and impersonal processes (*the evidence verified the claim* vs. *the evidence falsified the claim* — both grammatical, but *falsify* more strongly implies human agency in the P1–P5 data).

---

### 4.2 Absence of lexicalized inchoatives for most items

As documented in Section 1, the only fully lexicalized inchoative in the domain is *come true*, which is restricted to *true* with subjects denoting wishes, predictions, or fears. All other items rely on periphrastic constructions (*prove*, *turn out to be*, *become*). This gap is noted explicitly in P4a (Construction 4) and P5 (Opposition 32). The asymmetry between *come true* (lexicalized) and *\*come false* (not attested) is a structural feature of the domain.

---

### 4.3 Nominal constructions for dynamic meanings

Several dynamic meanings in this domain are expressed through nominal rather than verbal constructions:

- *gain credibility* (inchoative for *credible*)
- *ground truth* (product of verification)
- *a grain of truth* / *kernel of truth* (partial truth as a state)
- *moment of truth* (P1b.1.30) — a point at which a truth-domain state is revealed or tested; this is a nominal expression for a dynamic event rather than a state

These nominal constructions supplement the verbal and adjectival patterns and are not reducible to them.

---

### 4.4 The *post-truth* construction as a macro-level stability/change expression

*Post-truth* (P1b.1.39) is distinctive in that it describes not the truth-domain state of a specific proposition or person but the macro-level stability or instability of truth as a social and political norm. It implies a change of state at the level of public discourse (*from a condition in which truth was influential to one in which it is less so*) rather than at the level of individual propositions. No other item in the P1–P5 data operates at this level of abstraction in the dynamic domain.
