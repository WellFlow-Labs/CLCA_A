## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by BECOME (*nimtsa* sub-strategy)

P4a and P4b identify two sub-strategies under the BECOME/Inchoative construction (Pattern 4): (a) *hafakh le-* + predicate, which expresses a change of state, and (b) *nimtsa* + predicate adjective, which P4b explicitly notes is "particularly frequent in legal discourse for recording a change in assessed status." The *nimtsa* sub-strategy (*ha-mismakh nimtsa mezuyaf*, "the document was found/turned out to be forged") carries a meaning closer to REVELATION — the discovery or disclosure of a pre-existing state — than to a genuine change of state. No separate REVELATION construction is identified in P4a or P4b. The *nimtsa* sub-strategy therefore functions as the closest available equivalent to a REVELATION operator, but it does so from within the BECOME construction rather than as a distinct category.

### 2.2 CAUSATION (factual) is root-selective and partially declarative

P4b rates the Causative construction (Pattern 5) as [MARGINAL] due to uneven root coverage. The Hif'il binyan is the primary causative/declarative morphology, but P4b notes that it "most commonly expresses a declarative meaning ('to declare X to be Y') rather than a strictly causative one" for the attested items (*lehatsddiq*, *lehakhshir*). This means that for the roots where a Hif'il is available, the construction covers both CAUSATION (factual) and a declarative/performative function simultaneously. For roots without a productive Hif'il in this domain (e.g., ד-י-ק, ז-י-פ, and the roots of *ʾemet* and *sheqer* directly), no specific causative construction is identified; P4a mentions periphrastic causatives with *legaram* but notes these are not specific to the truth domain.

### 2.3 CAUSATION (character) substituted by active participle construction

The *-an* agentive suffix, which would be the expected morphological strategy for CAUSATION (character) / habitual-agent nominalization, is attested in this domain only for *shaqran* (liar). For all other items — including the truth-telling side of the domain — the active participle used nominally (*dover ʾemet*, *mezayyef*, *medayyeq*, *maʾamin*) substitutes for the *-an* agentive. This creates an asymmetry: the falsity/lying side has a dedicated morphological agentive (*shaqran*), while the truth-telling side relies on a periphrastic participle construction (*dover ʾemet*).

### 2.4 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction

VERIFICATION is expressed through root-specific Hif'il declaratives (*lehakhshir*) and the Pi'el verbal noun *immut*, both of which are restricted in coverage. DISCONFIRMATION is expressed primarily through the analytic negative attribution construction (*lo* + predicate), which is general and unrestricted. The two operators therefore use structurally different constructions: VERIFICATION relies on lexically specific verbal or nominal forms, while DISCONFIRMATION relies on the general negation particle *lo*. They do not share a single construction.

### 2.5 NEGATION is analytic at the clausal level, morphological only in nominals and formal register

The general negation strategy for predicates is analytic: the invariant particle *lo* precedes the predicate with no morphological change to the predicate itself (P4a #2, rated [CORE]). Morphological negation via the *ʾi-* prefix is attested only on *-ut* abstract nominals and is explicitly noted in P4b as restricted to "formal and legal registers." There is no morphological negation strategy for verbal or adjectival predicates in this domain.

### 2.6 Positive/negative asymmetry in adverbial forms

P4a #8 identifies *beʾemet* ("truly, really, honestly") as a grammaticalized sincerity and intensifier marker. No parallel negative adverbial form (e.g., *\*be-sheqer*, "falsely") is identified in P4a or P4b as having the same grammaticalized status. This represents an asymmetry between the truth-positive and truth-negative sides of the adverbial construction.

### 2.7 Concessive/Epistemic Hedge as a distinct discourse-level operator

P4a #9 (Concessive / Epistemic Hedge) is structurally distinct from simple ATTR (Pattern 1) in that it embeds a full clause under *nakhon she-* or *ʾemet she-* and is used specifically in argumentative discourse to acknowledge a truth claim before introducing a counterpoint. P4b notes it is "restricted to argumentative and evaluative discourse contexts." This construction does not map straightforwardly onto any of the ten canonical CLCA operators listed; it is closest to a discourse-level use of ATTR but with a concessive function that is not captured by the ATTR category alone.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **REVELATION**: No dedicated REVELATION construction is identified. The *nimtsa* + predicate sub-strategy of BECOME is the closest functional equivalent but is housed within the BECOME construction, not as a separate operator. This is a **morphosyntactic gap**: the language does not have a distinct syntactic construction for revelation as opposed to change-of-state.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **CAUSATION (factual)**: The Hif'il causative/declarative is absent or unproductive for several roots in the domain (ד-י-ק, ז-י-פ, and the roots of *ʾemet* and *sheqer* directly). No domain-specific periphrastic causative fills this gap. This is a **lexical gap**: the construction type exists in the language but is not lexically instantiated for all relevant roots.
- **CAUSATION (character) — truth-positive side**: No *-an* agentive for truth-telling is attested. The participle construction *dover ʾemet* fills the role but is not a morphologically dedicated agentive. This is a **lexical gap** (no dedicated form) rather than a structural gap (the participle strategy is available).
- **VERIFICATION**: No single dedicated VERIFICATION construction covers the full domain. Coverage depends on root-specific Hif'il or Pi'el availability. This is a **lexical gap**.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the P4a/P4b data. All ten canonical operators can be expressed in Hebrew, though some require substitution or are root-restricted.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (only negative or only positive forms missing)

- **Agentive Nominalization**: The *-an* morphological agentive is present on the falsity/lying side (*shaqran*) but absent on the truth-telling side. The truth-telling side uses the periphrastic *dover ʾemet*. This is an **asymmetric lexical gap**: the morphological form exists for one polarity but not the other.
- **Adverbial Construction**: *beʾemet* is grammaticalized as a sincerity/intensifier marker; no parallel grammaticalized negative adverbial (e.g., "falsely" as a manner adverb with equivalent status) is identified in P4a/P4b. This is a **potential asymmetric lexical gap**, though the data do not confirm or deny the existence of such a form beyond what is reported.
- **Morphological Negation on Nominals**: The *ʾi-* prefix negation is attested only in formal/legal register and only on *-ut* nominals. No parallel morphological negation is available for verbal or adjectival predicates. This is an **asymmetric morphosyntactic restriction**: morphological negation is available for one word class (abstract nominals) but not others.
