# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

**1.**
**Form:** doğru
**Transliteration:** doğru
**Literal meaning:** straight, direct (from Old Turkic *toğru*)
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate

Used to evaluate statements, answers, directions, and conduct as conforming to fact or norm. Participates in productive derivation: *doğruluk* (truthfulness/accuracy), *doğrulamak* (to verify/confirm), *doğrultmak* (to straighten/correct).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** gerçek
**Transliteration:** gerçek
**Literal meaning:** real, genuine (etymology debated; possibly from Persian *gird* + Turkic suffix)
**Typical English translations:** real, true, genuine, actual

Used to assert that something is not illusory, fabricated, or simulated — applied to persons, objects, events, and statements. Contrasts with *sahte* and *yalan*; derives *gerçeklik* (reality), *gerçekleşmek* (to come true/materialize).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** hakikat
**Transliteration:** hakikat
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqīqa* — the essential nature or truth of a thing
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the true nature of something

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal registers to denote deep or fundamental truth, often contrasted with surface appearance. Less colloquial than *gerçek*; appears in expressions like *hakikati söylemek* (to tell the truth).

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** hak
**Transliteration:** hak
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqq* — right, due, truth
**Typical English translations:** right, truth, justice, entitlement

Functions both as a noun denoting what is correct or just and as a predicate in expressions like *haklı* (in the right, correct). Participates in legal, moral, and epistemic contexts; derives *haklı/haksız* (right/wrong), *hakkaniyet* (equity).

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** haklı
**Transliteration:** haklı
**Literal meaning:** *hak* + adjectival suffix *-lı* — "having right/truth"
**Typical English translations:** right, justified, in the right, correct (of a person)

Applied to persons or positions to indicate that their claim, judgment, or action is warranted or correct. Contrasts with *haksız* (unjustified, wrong); used in both moral and epistemic evaluation.

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** yanlış
**Transliteration:** yanlış
**Literal meaning:** from *yan* (side) + *-lış*; "going to the side," hence erroneous
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false

Used to evaluate statements, actions, or beliefs as deviating from what is correct or true. Applies across factual, moral, and procedural contexts; derives *yanlışlık* (error, wrongness), *yanlışlamak* (to falsify, to show as wrong).

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** sahte
**Transliteration:** sahte
**Literal meaning:** from Persian *sākhta* — made, fabricated, artificial
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged, spurious

Applied to objects, documents, identities, and representations that are presented as genuine but are not. Common in legal and commercial contexts: *sahte para* (counterfeit money), *sahte kimlik* (false identity).

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** yalan
**Transliteration:** yalan
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (native Turkic)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untrue

Used as a noun and predicate adjective to characterize statements as deliberately false. Derives *yalancı* (liar), *yalan söylemek* (to tell a lie); contrasts with *doğru* and *gerçek*.

**[CORE]**

**9.**
**Form:** özgün
**Transliteration:** özgün
**Literal meaning:** *öz* (self, essence) + *-gün*; "of one's own essence," hence original
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic, genuine (of creative works or ideas)

Used particularly in aesthetic and intellectual contexts to evaluate works, ideas, or styles as not derivative or imitative. Contrasts with *taklit* (imitation); derives *özgünlük* (originality, authenticity).

**[CORE]**

**10.**
**Form:** özgünlük
**Transliteration:** özgünlük
**Literal meaning:** *özgün* + *-lük* (abstract noun suffix) — the quality of being original/authentic
**Typical English translations:** originality, authenticity, genuineness

Used as an abstract noun in aesthetic, academic, and critical discourse to name the property of being original or authentic. Appears in evaluative judgments about art, literature, and intellectual work.

**11.**
**Form:** gerçeklik
**Transliteration:** gerçeklik
**Literal meaning:** *gerçek* + *-lik* — the quality or state of being real
**Typical English translations:** reality, realness, authenticity, genuineness

Used in philosophical and everyday contexts to refer to the property of being real or to reality as a domain. Appears in expressions evaluating whether something has genuine existence or substance.

**12.**
**Form:** doğruluk
**Transliteration:** doğruluk
**Literal meaning:** *doğru* + *-luk* — the quality of being correct/true
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, accuracy, correctness, integrity

Used to name the property of statements, persons, or measurements as accurate or truthful. Appears in both epistemic contexts (accuracy of information) and moral contexts (honesty of character).

**13.**
**Form:** doğrulamak
**Transliteration:** doğrulamak
**Literal meaning:** *doğru* + causative/factitive *-la-* + infinitive *-mak* — to make true/straight, to verify
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to validate, to authenticate

Used in contexts of checking, confirming, or establishing the truth or correctness of a claim, document, or identity. Common in legal, journalistic, and technical registers.

**14.**
**Form:** tasdik
**Transliteration:** tasdik
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *taṣdīq* — affirmation, attestation, confirmation
**Typical English translations:** attestation, certification, confirmation, authentication

Used in formal and legal contexts to denote official confirmation or authentication of documents, signatures, or facts. Derives *tasdik etmek* (to certify/attest); more formal than *doğrulamak*.

**15.**
**Form:** teyit
**Transliteration:** teyit
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *taʾyīd* — support, corroboration
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, corroboration, verification

Used to indicate that a claim or piece of information has been confirmed by an independent source or check. Common in journalistic and official language: *teyit etmek* (to confirm/corroborate).

**16.**
**Form:** taklit
**Transliteration:** taklit
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *taqlīd* — imitation, following
**Typical English translations:** imitation, copy, counterfeit, fake

Used to evaluate objects, performances, or behaviors as imitative rather than original or genuine. Applied in aesthetic contexts (imitation of a style) and commercial contexts (counterfeit goods).

**17.**
**Form:** sahicilik
**Transliteration:** sahicilik
**Literal meaning:** *sahici* (genuine, real, colloquial) + *-lik* — the quality of being genuine
**Typical English translations:** genuineness, authenticity, realness

A colloquial and informal term for the quality of being authentic or genuine, often applied to persons, relationships, or experiences. Less formal than *özgünlük* or *gerçeklik*; carries an affective, interpersonal nuance.

**18.**
**Form:** sahici
**Transliteration:** sahici
**Literal meaning:** possibly from *sahih* (Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*, sound, valid) + Turkic suffix; or independently formed
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic (colloquial)

Used colloquially to describe persons, emotions, or things as genuinely what they appear to be, without pretense. More informal and affectively warm than *gerçek* or *hakiki*.

**Transliteration:** hakiki
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqīqī* — pertaining to *ḥaqīqa* (true nature); real, genuine
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true

Used as an adjective to evaluate objects, persons, or states as truly what they are claimed to be. Somewhat formal or literary; appears in expressions like *hakiki deri* (genuine leather), *hakiki dost* (true friend).

**20.**
**Form:** sahih
**Transliteration:** sahih
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ* — sound, valid, correct
**Typical English translations:** valid, sound, authentic, correct

Used in religious (especially Islamic), legal, and scholarly contexts to evaluate texts, traditions, or documents as sound and authoritative. Less common in everyday speech; more domain-specific than *doğru*.

Used in legal and political contexts to evaluate actions, claims, or authority as conforming to recognized rules or norms. Derives *meşruiyet* (legitimacy); contrasts with *gayri meşru* (illegitimate).

**Typical English translations:** legitimacy, lawfulness

Used as an abstract noun in political, legal, and philosophical discourse to name the property of being legitimate or rightfully authorized. Central to discussions of political authority and legal validity.

**Typical English translations:** valid, current, accepted, in force

Used to evaluate documents, arguments, rules, or claims as currently accepted, in force, or meeting required standards. Contrasts with *geçersiz* (invalid, void); common in legal and administrative contexts.

**Transliteration:** geçersiz

**Typical English translations:** invalid, void, null, not accepted

Used to declare that a document, argument, or claim does not meet required standards or is no longer in force. Common in legal, administrative, and logical contexts.

**Typical English translations:** misleading, deceptive, fallacious

Used to evaluate statements, appearances, or representations as causing false impressions without necessarily being outright lies. Applied in epistemic, legal, and advertising contexts.

Used to evaluate persons, statements, or appearances as intentionally creating false impressions. Stronger connotation of intentional deception than *yanıltıcı*; used in moral and legal evaluation.

**27.**

**Typical English translations:** sincere, honest, a person of integrity, one whose words match their character

A fixed expression used to evaluate a person as consistent between their inner character and their outward speech — i.e., genuinely honest and not hypocritical. Functions as a predicate or attributive phrase.

Used to evaluate something as presenting itself under a false or altered appearance; implies that the true nature is concealed. Appears in moral and critical contexts to flag misrepresentation.

Used to identify the true, original, or fundamental version of something as opposed to a copy, substitute, or surface appearance. Appears in expressions like *asıl mesele* (the real issue), *asıl nüsha* (the original copy).

**30.**
**Form:** özü
**Transliteration:** özü
**Literal meaning:** *öz* (self, essence, core) + third-person possessive *-ü* — "its essence/core"
**Typical English translations:** its essence, its true nature, its core

Used to refer to the fundamental or essential nature of something, often in evaluative contexts where surface appearance is contrasted with underlying reality. Participates in compounds and fixed expressions: *özünde* (at its core, essentially).
