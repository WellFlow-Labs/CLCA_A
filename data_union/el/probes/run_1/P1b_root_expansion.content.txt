# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** αληθ- / *alith-*
**Transliteration:** alith-
**P1 items participating:** 5 (αληθής, αλήθεια, αληθινός, επαληθεύω, επαλήθευση)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes the primary concept of truth as unconcealment or correspondence with reality. It is the foundational morpheme for propositional truth, factual accuracy, and genuine states of affairs across all registers.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ψευδ- / ψευτ- / *psevd- / psevt-*
**Transliteration:** psevd- / psevt-
**P1 items participating:** 4 (ψεύδος, ψευδής, ψεύτικος, and implicitly ψεύτης underlying ψεύτικος)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes falsehood, deception, and misrepresentation. It covers both the propositional dimension (a false statement) and the agentive dimension (a liar, a deceiver), and is highly productive in compounds and prefixed forms.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** αμφι- + σβητ- / *amfisviт-* (as a compound stem)
**Note:** On reflection, αμφισβητ- is itself a compound and less productive as a standalone root than the two above. The third most productive root across P1 items is more accurately:

**Form:** πιστ- / *pist-*
**Transliteration:** pist-
**P1 items participating:** 3 (αξιόπιστος, επαλήθευση indirectly; more directly: αξιόπιστος, and the underlying πίστις in αξιόπιστος; also διαπίστωση from διαπιστώνω)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes trust, belief, and credibility — the relational dimension of truth-evaluation whereby a claim or person is deemed worthy of confidence. It underlies a large family of terms concerning reliability, faith, and certification.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: αληθ- (*alith-*)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αληθεύω
**Transliteration:** alithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to be truthful
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and literary registers to describe the act of telling the truth or being in a state of truthfulness. Less common in everyday speech than λέω την αλήθεια.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αληθοφανής
**Transliteration:** alithofanís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + φαίνομαι (to appear); "appearing true"
**Natural English translation:** plausible, verisimilar, truth-seeming
**Context of use:** Used in rhetorical, literary, and philosophical contexts to describe something that has the appearance of truth without necessarily being true. Applied to narratives, arguments, and claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια
**Transliteration:** alithofánia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -εια (nominal suffix); "the quality of appearing true"
**Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, plausibility, appearance of truth
**Context of use:** Used in literary criticism and rhetoric to describe the quality of a narrative or argument that makes it seem credible or lifelike. Corresponds to the classical concept of verisimilitude.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** αληθινά
**Transliteration:** alithiná
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + -α (adverbial suffix); "truly, genuinely"
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely
**Context of use:** Common adverbial form used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm the truth of a statement. "Αληθινά δεν το ήξερα" = "I truly didn't know."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** αλήθεια (exclamative)
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth); used as a discourse particle
**Natural English translation:** Really? / Truly? / By the way
**Context of use:** Used as a conversational interjection to express surprise ("Αλήθεια;" = "Really?") or to introduce a new topic ("Αλήθεια, ήθελα να σε ρωτήσω…" = "By the way, I wanted to ask you…"). Extremely frequent in spoken Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** στ' αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** st' alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** εις (to/in) + τη (the) + αλήθεια (truth); "in truth"
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, honestly
**Context of use:** Fixed adverbial phrase used to introduce a sincere or frank statement. "Στ' αλήθεια, δεν ξέρω τι να πω" = "Honestly, I don't know what to say."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Transliteration:** i alíthia íne óti
**Morpheme breakdown:** η αλήθεια (the truth) + είναι (is) + ότι (that)
**Natural English translation:** the truth is that…
**Context of use:** A fixed clausal frame used to introduce a frank or corrective statement, often conceding something the speaker might prefer not to admit. Extremely common in both spoken and written Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** λέω την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** léo tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** λέω (I say) + την αλήθεια (the truth)
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth
**Context of use:** The standard colloquial expression for telling the truth. Used in everyday speech, legal contexts (witness testimony), and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** αλήθεια ή ψέμα
**Transliteration:** alíthia í pséma
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + ή (or) + ψέμα (lie)
**Natural English translation:** truth or lie; true or false
**Context of use:** Used as a binary framing in everyday speech and in game contexts (the game "truth or dare" is sometimes rendered this way). Also used rhetorically to demand a definitive answer.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** αληθής αξία
**Transliteration:** alithís aksía
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθής (true) + αξία (value)
**Natural English translation:** true value, real value
**Context of use:** Used in financial, economic, and philosophical contexts to refer to the actual or intrinsic value of something as opposed to its nominal or market value.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** αληθής αγάπη
**Transliteration:** alithís agápi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθής (true) + αγάπη (love)
**Natural English translation:** true love
**Context of use:** Used in literary, religious, and everyday contexts to describe love that is genuine and deep as opposed to superficial or feigned. Common in both secular and Christian discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** αληθινός φίλος
**Transliteration:** alithinós fílos
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + φίλος (friend)
**Natural English translation:** true friend, genuine friend
**Context of use:** A common collocation used to distinguish a genuine, loyal friend from a superficial acquaintance. Appears frequently in everyday speech and proverbial expressions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** αληθινή ιστορία
**Transliteration:** alithiní istoría
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινή (true, real) + ιστορία (story, history)
**Natural English translation:** true story, real story
**Context of use:** Used to label narratives — in journalism, film, and everyday speech — as based on actual events rather than fiction. "Βασισμένο σε αληθινή ιστορία" = "Based on a true story."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** αληθινός Θεός
**Transliteration:** alithinós Theós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true) + Θεός (God)
**Natural English translation:** the true God
**Context of use:** Used in Christian theological and liturgical discourse to distinguish the God of Christian faith from false gods or idols. Appears in the Nicene Creed and other liturgical texts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** αληθινός εαυτός
**Transliteration:** alithinós eftós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, authentic) + εαυτός (self)
**Natural English translation:** true self, authentic self
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, philosophical, and popular discourse to refer to a person's genuine identity as opposed to the persona they present to others. Common in self-help and therapeutic registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** επαληθεύσιμος
**Transliteration:** epalithéfsimos
**Morpheme breakdown:** επαληθεύω (to verify) + -σιμος (adjectival suffix indicating possibility); "capable of being verified"
**Natural English translation:** verifiable
**Context of use:** Used in scientific, philosophical, and journalistic contexts to describe claims or hypotheses that can in principle be checked against evidence. Related to the concept of falsifiability in philosophy of science.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** μισή αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** misí alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** μισή (half) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** half-truth
**Context of use:** Used in everyday and journalistic discourse to describe a statement that is technically accurate but misleading because it omits relevant information. Carries a negative evaluative force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** πικρή αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** pikrí alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** πικρή (bitter) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** bitter truth, hard truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used to describe a truth that is unpleasant or painful to acknowledge. Common in everyday speech and literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** γυμνή αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** gimní alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** γυμνή (naked, bare) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** naked truth, plain truth
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used to describe truth presented without embellishment or concealment. Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to emphasize directness and frankness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** αλήθεια του Θεού
**Transliteration:** alíthia tu Theú
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + του Θεού (of God)
**Natural English translation:** God's truth, the truth of God
**Context of use:** Used in religious discourse and as an emphatic oath in everyday speech. "Αλήθεια του Θεού!" is used to swear that something is absolutely true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** αναλήθεια
**Transliteration:** analíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αλήθεια (truth); "untruth"
**Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
**Context of use:** A formal or literary term for a falsehood or untruth, used as a more elevated alternative to ψέμα. Appears in philosophical and journalistic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** αναληθής
**Transliteration:** analithís
**Morpheme breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αληθής (true); "untrue"
**Natural English translation:** untrue, false
**Context of use:** A formal adjective used in philosophical and legal contexts to describe a statement or proposition that does not correspond to reality. Less common in everyday speech than ψεύτικος or λανθασμένος.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**23.**
**Form:** αλήθεια και δικαιοσύνη
**Transliteration:** alíthia ke dhikiosíni
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + και (and) + δικαιοσύνη (justice)
**Natural English translation:** truth and justice
**Context of use:** A paired formula used in legal, political, and moral discourse to invoke the twin values of truthfulness and fairness. Appears in official documents, political rhetoric, and ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** αλήθεια και ψέμα
**Transliteration:** alíthia ke pséma
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + και (and) + ψέμα (lie)
**Natural English translation:** truth and lies; truth and falsehood
**Context of use:** A paired formula used to frame a binary opposition between truthfulness and deception. Common in everyday speech, journalism, and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** αλήθεια (ως) πρώτη αρχή
**Transliteration:** alíthia (os) próti archí
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + ως (as) + πρώτη (first) + αρχή (principle)
**Natural English translation:** truth as a first principle
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and ethical discourse to designate truth as a foundational value or axiom. Appears in academic writing and formal argumentation.

---

**26.**
**Form:** αληθοφιλής
**Transliteration:** alithofilis
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + φιλ- (loving); "truth-loving"
**Natural English translation:** truth-loving, veridical
**Context of use:** A learned or literary adjective used to describe a person or disposition oriented toward truth. Rare in everyday speech; found in philosophical and literary texts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**27.**
**Form:** αλήθεια ή τόλμη
**Transliteration:** alíthia í tólmi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + ή (or) + τόλμη (dare)
**Natural English translation:** truth or dare
**Context of use:** The name of the party game "truth or dare" as used in Greek. A direct calque of the English game name, but using the native αλήθεια.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 2: ψευδ- / ψευτ- (*psevd- / psevt-*)

---

**1.**
**Form:** ψεύτης
**Transliteration:** psévtis
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (falsehood) + -της (agentive suffix); "one who lies"
**Natural English translation:** liar
**Context of use:** The standard noun for a liar in modern Greek, used across all registers from everyday insult to legal characterization. "Είσαι ψεύτης" = "You are a liar."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ψεύτρα
**Transliteration:** psévtra
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψεύτης (liar) + -α (feminine suffix); "female liar"
**Natural English translation:** liar (feminine)
**Context of use:** The feminine form of ψεύτης, used in everyday speech to refer to a woman who lies. Carries the same evaluative force as the masculine form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ψέμα
**Transliteration:** pséma
**Morpheme breakdown:** from ψεύδομαι (to lie); colloquial nominal form
**Natural English translation:** lie, falsehood
**Context of use:** The most common colloquial noun for a lie in modern Greek. "Λέει ψέματα" = "He/she tells lies." Preferred over ψεύδος in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** λέω ψέματα
**Transliteration:** léo pséma­ta
**Morpheme breakdown:** λέω (I say/tell) + ψέματα (lies, plural of ψέμα)
**Natural English translation:** to tell lies, to lie
**Context of use:** The standard colloquial expression for the act of lying. Used across all registers and age groups. "Μην λες ψέματα" = "Don't tell lies."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ψεύδομαι
**Transliteration:** psévdhome
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (falsehood) + -ομαι (middle/passive verbal suffix); "to lie, to speak falsely"
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell a falsehood
**Context of use:** The formal or literary verb for lying, used in philosophical, legal, and elevated registers. Less common in everyday speech than λέω ψέματα.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ψευδώνυμο
**Transliteration:** psevdhónimoΨ
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ὄνυμα/ὄνομα (name); "false name"
**Natural English translation:** pseudonym, pen name, alias
**Context of use:** Used in literary, legal, and everyday contexts to refer to a name used in place of one's real name. Authors, activists, and criminals may use a ψευδώνυμο.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ψευδώνυμος
**Transliteration:** psevdhónimoς
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ὄνομα (name) + -ος (adjectival suffix); "bearing a false name"
**Natural English translation:** pseudonymous
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective to describe a person or work that uses or is published under a pseudonym. "Ψευδώνυμος συγγραφέας" = "pseudonymous author."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ψευδαίσθηση
**Transliteration:** psevdhésthisi
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + αίσθηση (perception, sensation); "false perception"
**Natural English translation:** illusion, hallucination, false impression
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, medical, and everyday contexts to describe a false perception or mistaken impression. "Έχει ψευδαισθήσεις" = "He/she has hallucinations/illusions."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ψευδαίσθηση (μεταφορικά)
**Transliteration:** psevdhésthisi
**Morpheme breakdown:** as above
**Natural English translation:** delusion, false belief
**Context of use:** Used metaphorically in everyday and psychological discourse to describe a persistent false belief about oneself or one's situation. "Ζει με ψευδαισθήσεις" = "He/she lives with delusions."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ψευδής κατάθεση
**Transliteration:** psevdhís katáthesi

**Natural English translation:** false testimony, perjury
**Context of use:** A fixed legal collocation referring to testimony given under oath that is knowingly false. Constitutes a criminal offense in Greek law.

**11.**
**Form:** ψευδής καταγγελία
**Transliteration:** psevdhís katangelía

### ROOT 3: πιστ- (*pist-*)

---

**1.**
**Form:** πίστη
**Transliteration:** písti
**Morpheme breakdown:** πιστ- (trust, belief) + -η (nominal suffix); "trust, faith, belief"
**Natural English translation:** faith, trust, belief, loyalty
**Context of use:** The primary noun for faith and trust in Greek, used in religious (Christian faith), interpersonal (trust in a person), and institutional (loyalty to an institution) contexts. One of the most semantically rich terms in the language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** πιστός
**Transliteration:** pistós
**Morpheme breakdown:** πιστ- (trust) + -ός (adjectival suffix); "trustworthy, faithful"
**Natural English translation:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy, reliable
**Context of use:** Used of persons, animals (especially dogs), and translations to indicate faithfulness or reliability. "Πιστός φίλος" = "faithful friend"; "πιστή μετάφραση" = "faithful translation."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** πιστά
**Transliteration:** pistá
**Morpheme breakdown:** πιστός (faithful) + -α (adverbial suffix); "faithfully"
**Natural English translation:** faithfully, loyally, accurately
**Context of use:** Adverbial form used to describe faithful or accurate execution of a task, translation, or representation. "Αποδίδει πιστά το πρωτότυπο" = "It faithfully renders the original."

**4.**

**Transliteration:** pistévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** πιστ- (belief) + -εύω (verbal suffix); "to believe, to trust"
**Natural English translation:** to believe, to think, to trust
**Context of use:** The standard verb for believing in Greek, used in propositional contexts ("πιστεύω ότι…" = "I believe that…"), religious contexts ("πιστεύω στον Θεό" = "I believe in God"), and everyday opinion expression.

**5.**

**Morpheme breakdown:** nominalized form of πιστεύω (to believe); "creed, belief system"

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots identified above but appear in contexts where the contribution of the root seems to shift away from its core truth-evaluation function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**αληθ- root:**

- **αληθινά** (truly) functions as an intensifier in contexts where no truth-claim is being evaluated: "Είναι αληθινά όμορφη" = "She is truly beautiful." The root here marks degree or intensity rather than correspondence with fact.

- **αληθινός** applied to emotions and experiences: "αληθινή χαρά" (true joy), "αληθινός πόνος" (true pain) — the root marks the depth or genuineness of an inner state rather than propositional truth.

- **αληθής αξία** (true value) in financial contexts: the root is applied to quantitative measurement rather than propositional truth.

- **αληθινός Θεός** in theological contexts: the root participates in claims about essential nature and ontological status rather than propositional accuracy.

- **αληθοφάνεια** (verisimilitude) in literary criticism: the root marks the aesthetic quality of appearing true rather than actually being true.

---

**ψευδ-/ψευτ- root:**

- **ψευδαίσθηση** (illusion, hallucination): the root is applied to perceptual experience rather than propositional falsehood.

- **ψευδο-** prefix in compounds such as **ψευδοδημοκρατία**, **ψευδοεπιστήμη**: the root marks a category of institutional or disciplinary identity rather than a specific false statement.

- **ψεύτικος** applied to materials: "ψεύτικο δέρμα" (fake leather, synthetic leather) — the root marks material composition or origin rather than intentional deception.

- **ψεύτικος** applied to emotions in performance contexts: "ψεύτικα δάκρυα" (fake tears) — the root marks performed or simulated affect.

- **ψευδεπίγραφος** in philological contexts: the root marks authorial attribution rather than the truth-value of the text's content.

---

**πιστ- root:**

- **πίστωση** and **πιστωτής** in financial contexts: the root marks a relationship of credit and debt rather than epistemic trust or belief.

- **πιστωτική κάρτα** (credit card): the root participates in a purely financial instrument with no direct epistemic content.

- **πιστός** applied to translations: "πιστή μετάφραση" (faithful translation) — the root marks fidelity of representation rather than personal trustworthiness.

- **πιστός** applied to animals: "πιστό σκυλί" (faithful dog) — the root marks behavioral loyalty rather than epistemic reliability.

- **ολιγοπιστία** and **ολιγόπιστος** in religious contexts: the root marks the quantity or degree of faith rather than the truth-value of a proposition.

- **απιστία** in the sense of marital infidelity: the root marks relational fidelity rather than epistemic credibility.

- **εμπιστευτικός** (confidential): the root marks the restricted circulation of information rather than its truth or reliability.

- **πίστη στα τυφλά** (blind faith): the root appears in a context that explicitly marks the absence of evidential grounding, inverting the normal epistemic function of the root.

- **πιστοποιητικό υγείας** and similar administrative certificates: the root marks formal institutional attestation rather than personal trustworthiness or propositional truth.
