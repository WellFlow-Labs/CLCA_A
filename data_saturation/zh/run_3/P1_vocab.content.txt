# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (single morpheme; functions as root in many compounds)
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that something corresponds to fact or is not counterfeit. Appears as a free morpheme ("这是真的") and as a productive bound root in compounds. [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** false, fake, borrowed (single morpheme; antonym of 真 in most contexts)
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
**Usage:** Contrasts with 真 in evaluations of authenticity, sincerity, and factual accuracy. Used in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts to describe forgeries, lies, or pretense. [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal meaning:** true + solid/actual
**Typical English translations:** truthful, realistic, authentic, factual
**Usage:** Describes statements, representations, or depictions that accurately reflect reality. Common in journalistic, legal, and aesthetic contexts (e.g., "真实报道" — truthful reporting). [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 虚假
**Transliteration:** xūjiǎ
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false
**Typical English translations:** false, fraudulent, fictitious, deceptive
**Usage:** Used in formal and legal registers to characterize statements, claims, or documents that are deliberately misleading or fabricated. Stronger and more formal than 假 alone. [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 正确
**Transliteration:** zhèngquè
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an answer, method, or action conforms to a standard or is free from error. Widely used in educational, technical, and everyday contexts. [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 错误
**Transliteration:** cuòwù
**Literal meaning:** wrong/mistaken + error/mistake
**Typical English translations:** wrong, erroneous, mistaken, incorrect
**Usage:** Describes statements, beliefs, or actions that deviate from what is correct or true. Used in both factual and moral evaluations. [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 准确
**Transliteration:** zhǔnquè
**Literal meaning:** standard/precise + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Emphasizes precision and exactness, often in technical, scientific, or measurement contexts. Slightly more specific than 正确, foregrounding closeness to a target value or description. [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 可靠
**Transliteration:** kěkào
**Literal meaning:** can + lean on/depend on
**Typical English translations:** reliable, dependable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, sources, information, or equipment as worthy of trust or consistent in performance. Common in everyday and professional contexts. [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal meaning:** true + upright/proper
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, true (emphatic)
**Usage:** Emphasizes that something is the authentic or proper instance of a category, often contrasting with something superficial or nominal. Used predicatively and attributively. [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 名副其实
**Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
**Literal meaning:** name + matches + its + substance/reality
**Typical English translations:** living up to one's name, worthy of the title, genuine
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression (成语) asserting that a label or title accurately corresponds to the actual nature of the thing or person. Used in evaluative and commendatory contexts. [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 货真价实
**Transliteration:** huò zhēn jià shí
**Literal meaning:** goods + genuine + price + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** genuine goods at fair prices; the real thing; authentic
**Usage:** Originally a commercial expression asserting authenticity and fair value; extended to describe anything that is genuinely what it claims to be. [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 伪造
**Transliteration:** wěizào
**Literal meaning:** false/counterfeit + to make/fabricate
**Typical English translations:** to forge, to fabricate, to counterfeit
**Usage:** Refers to the deliberate creation of false documents, signatures, currency, or evidence. Primarily used in legal and administrative contexts. [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** true + false/spurious
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness vs. falseness; true or false
**Usage:** A compound that frames the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit, often used in contexts of authentication (art, documents, historical artifacts). [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 辨别真伪
**Transliteration:** biànbié zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** to distinguish + true + false
**Typical English translations:** to distinguish the genuine from the false, to authenticate
**Usage:** A conventionalized verb phrase used in contexts of verification, authentication, and critical evaluation of sources or objects. [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 失实
**Transliteration:** shīshí
**Literal meaning:** lose/depart from + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, contrary to fact, distorted
**Usage:** Used in formal and journalistic registers to characterize reports, statements, or accounts that deviate from the facts, without necessarily implying deliberate deception. [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 属实
**Transliteration:** shǔshí
**Literal meaning:** belong to/be + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** to be true, to be confirmed as factual, verified
**Usage:** A formal expression used in administrative, legal, and journalistic contexts to assert that a claim or allegation has been verified as factual. [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 如实
**Transliteration:** rúshí
**Literal meaning:** as + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** truthfully, as things actually are, accurately
**Usage:** An adverbial expression meaning to report or describe something exactly as it is, without distortion or omission. Common in legal testimony and journalistic contexts ("如实陈述"). [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 本质
**Transliteration:** běnzhì
**Literal meaning:** root/origin + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, fundamental character
**Usage:** Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday contexts to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance. [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 实质
**Transliteration:** shízhì
**Literal meaning:** solid/real + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** substance, essence, real nature
**Usage:** Emphasizes the actual or operative content of something (a law, an agreement, a situation) as opposed to its formal or nominal character. Often used in legal and analytical discourse. [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 名不副实
**Transliteration:** míng bù fù shí
**Literal meaning:** name + not + matches + substance/reality
**Typical English translations:** not living up to one's name, undeserving of the title, nominal only
**Usage:** The negative counterpart of 名副其实; used to criticize a mismatch between a label or reputation and actual qualities. [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 道德
**Transliteration:** dàodé
**Literal meaning:** way/principle + virtue/power
**Typical English translations:** morality, ethics, moral conduct
**Usage:** The standard compound for morality and ethical conduct in Chinese. Used in evaluations of whether behavior conforms to accepted moral norms. [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 合法
**Transliteration:** héfǎ
**Literal meaning:** conform to + law
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legal, legitimate
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an action, document, or status conforms to legal requirements. Widely used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts. [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 违法
**Transliteration:** wéifǎ
**Literal meaning:** violate + law
**Typical English translations:** illegal, unlawful, in violation of the law
**Usage:** The standard term for actions or conduct that contravene legal norms. Used across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 相符
**Transliteration:** xiāngfú
**Literal meaning:** mutually + conform/match
**Typical English translations:** to correspond, to match, to be consistent with
**Usage:** Describes a relationship of correspondence or agreement between two things — a statement and reality, a copy and an original, or a person's conduct and a standard. [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 逼真
**Transliteration:** bīzhēn
**Literal meaning:** approach/press toward + true/real
**Typical English translations:** lifelike, realistic, true to life, convincing
**Usage:** Used in aesthetic contexts to evaluate representations (paintings, performances, descriptions) as closely resembling their subjects. Implies high fidelity of resemblance. [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 失真
**Transliteration:** shīzhēn
**Literal meaning:** lose/depart from + true/real
**Typical English translations:** distortion, loss of fidelity, inaccurate representation
**Usage:** Used in technical contexts (audio, image reproduction) and in evaluative contexts to describe representations that deviate from the original or from reality. [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 诚实
**Transliteration:** chéngshí
**Literal meaning:** sincere/honest + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere
**Usage:** Evaluates a person's character or conduct as marked by truthfulness and absence of deception. Used in moral, educational, and everyday contexts. [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 欺骗
**Transliteration:** qīpiàn
**Literal meaning:** deceive + cheat/trick
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to cheat, to defraud
**Usage:** Describes deliberate acts of misleading others about facts, identity, or intentions. Used across everyday, legal, and moral registers. [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 原真性
**Transliteration:** yuánzhēnxìng
**Literal meaning:** original + true + -ness (nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity (in heritage/conservation contexts)
**Usage:** A specialized term used in cultural heritage, museum studies, and conservation discourse, often as a translation of the concept "authenticity" in international frameworks (e.g., UNESCO documents). Less common outside these domains. [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 确凿
**Transliteration:** quèzáo
**Literal meaning:** certain/definite + solid (second morpheme primarily bound)
**Typical English translations:** conclusive, solid, irrefutable (of evidence)
**Usage:** Used almost exclusively to modify 证据 (evidence) or similar nouns, asserting that evidence is firm and beyond doubt. Primarily a legal and journalistic collocation. [CORE]
