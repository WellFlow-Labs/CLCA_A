## Natural semantic groupings for Sanskrit expressions in P1–P2

Below are usage-based groupings drawn only from the patterns described in the provided Sanskrit material.

---

### 1. Basic truth and falsehood in speaking or stating
These expressions are used most directly for saying that something is true or false, or for telling someone to speak truthfully. They are common in ethical instruction, dialogue, narrative reporting, and straightforward evaluation of utterances.

**Representative expressions**
- **सत्य** (*satya*)
- **सत्यम्** (*satyam*)
- **असत्य** (*asatya*)
- **अनृत** (*anṛta*)
- **मिथ्या** (*mithyā*)
- **मृषा** (*mṛṣā*)
- **सत्यं वद** (*satyaṃ vada*)
- **सत्यं ब्रूते / सत्यं ब्रवीति** (*satyaṃ brūte / satyaṃ bravīti*)
- **सत्यम् एतत्** (*satyam etat*)
- **सत्यम् उक्तम्** (*satyam uktam*)
- **असत्यं वदति** (*asatyaṃ vadati*)
- **अनृतं मा वद** (*anṛtaṃ mā vada*)
- **मिथ्या वदति** (*mithyā vadati*)
- **मृषा वदति** (*mṛṣā vadati*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **सत्यवचन** (*satyavacana*) also fits the group on truthful speech as a moral trait.
- **तथ्य** (*tathya*) can overlap when the focus is factual correctness rather than moral truthfulness.
- **ऋत** (*ṛta*) overlaps only in older or Vedic-style usage.

---

### 2. Truthful speech as a personal or ethical quality
These forms are used not just for a single true statement, but for a person’s habit, commitment, or reputation for speaking truth. They are especially common in praise, moral description, epic characterization, and didactic contexts.

**Representative expressions**
- **सत्यवचन** (*satyavacana*)
- **सत्यवादिन्** (*satyavādin*)
- **सत्यवादी** (*satyavādī*)
- **सत्यभाषण** (*satyabhāṣaṇa*)
- **सत्यभाषिन्** (*satyabhāṣin*)
- **सत्यसंध** (*satyasaṃdha*)
- **सत्यपरायण** (*satyaparāyaṇa*)
- **सत्यनिष्ठ** (*satyaniṣṭha*)
- **सत्यशील** (*satyaśīla*)
- **सत्यपालन** (*satyapālana*)
- **सत्यप्रतिज्ञ** (*satyapratijña*)
- **असत्यवादिन्** (*asatyavādin*)
- **सत्ये स्थितः / सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः** (*satye sthitaḥ / satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ*)
- **सत्यवचने स्थितः** (*satyavacane sthitaḥ*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **सत्य** (*satya*) itself often participates here, but is broader than this group.
- **धर्म्य** (*dharmya*) may overlap when truthful speech is treated as morally proper.
- **अनृत** (*anṛta*) can overlap in moral-religious condemnation of lying.

---

### 3. Factual correctness and accurate reporting
These expressions are used where the concern is whether a statement, description, or report matches the facts or the actual case. They appear in descriptive, learned, reportive, historical, and analytical contexts more than in moral exhortation.

**Representative expressions**
- **तथ्य** (*tathya*)
- **यथार्थ** (*yathārtha*)
- **यथार्थता** (*yathārthatā*)
- **भूतार्थ** (*bhūtārtha*)
- **तथ्यं वचनम्** (*tathyaṃ vacanam*)
- **तथ्यं कथनम्** (*tathyaṃ kathanam*)
- **यथार्थं वर्णयति** (*yathārthaṃ varṇayati*)
- **यथार्थवचन** (*yathārthavacana*)
- **यथार्थवर्णन** (*yathārthavarṇana*)
- **वर्णनस्य यथार्थता** (*varṇanasya yathārthatā*)
- **भूतार्थं वदति** (*bhūtārthaṃ vadati*)
- **भूतार्थवचन** (*bhūtārthavacana*)
- **भूतार्थं निरूपयति** (*bhūtārthaṃ nirūpayati*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **सत्य / सत्यम्** (*satya / satyam*) may also be used for factual truth, but they are not limited to this use.
- **यथार्थज्ञान** (*yathārthajñāna*) overlaps with the group on correct cognition.
- **भूतार्थक** (*bhūtārthaka*) is narrower and more technical.

---

### 4. Correct cognition, understanding, and seeing things as they are
These expressions are used when evaluating knowledge, perception, understanding, or apprehension as correct or incorrect. They are especially common in philosophical, scholastic, and reflective prose.

**Representative expressions**
- **प्रमा** (*pramā*)
- **यथार्थज्ञान** (*yathārthajñāna*)
- **यथार्थदर्शन** (*yathārthadarśana*)
- **यथार्थबोध** (*yathārthabodha*)
- **अयथार्थज्ञान** (*ayathārthajñāna*)
- **मिथ्या ज्ञानम्** (*mithyā jñānam*)
- **सम्यग् ज्ञातम्** (*samyag jñātam*)
- **प्रमा जायते** (*pramā jāyate*)
- **प्रमा भवति** (*pramā bhavati*)
- **ज्ञानस्य यथार्थता** (*jñānasya yathārthatā*)
- **ज्ञानस्य प्रामाण्यम्** (*jñānasya prāmāṇyam*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **यथार्थ** (*yathārtha*) also belongs with factual accuracy and representation.
- **प्रमाण** (*pramāṇa*) overlaps strongly, but usually points to warrant or means rather than the cognition itself.
- **मिथ्या** (*mithyā*) also belongs to the basic falsehood group.

---

### 5. Valid proof, authority, and epistemic warrant
These expressions are used when discussing whether something counts as proof, accepted evidence, valid means of knowledge, or authoritative support. They are especially characteristic of philosophical debate, scholastic prose, legal-style reasoning, and textual evaluation.

**Representative expressions**
- **प्रमाण** (*pramāṇa*)
- **प्रमाणिक** (*pramāṇika*)
- **प्रामाण्य** (*prāmāṇya*)
- **अप्रामाण्य** (*aprāmāṇya*)
- **प्रमाणता** (*pramāṇatā*)
- **प्रमाणत्व** (*pramāṇatva*)
- **प्रमाणभूत** (*pramāṇabhūta*)
- **प्रमाणवचन** (*pramāṇavacana*)
- **प्रमाणसिद्ध** (*pramāṇasiddha*)
- **प्रमाणसिद्धि** (*pramāṇasiddhi*)
- **अप्रमाण** (*apramāṇa*)
- **अप्रमाणिक** (*apramāṇika*)
- **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्** (*pramāṇena siddham*)
- **प्रमाणं भवति** (*pramāṇaṃ bhavati*)
- **प्रमाणं नास्ति** (*pramāṇaṃ nāsti*)
- **प्रमाणतः** (*pramāṇataḥ*)
- **प्रमाणैरवगम्यते** (*pramāṇair avagamyate*)
- **प्रमाणिकः ग्रन्थः** (*pramāṇikaḥ granthaḥ*)
- **प्रमाणिकं वचनम्** (*pramāṇikaṃ vacanam*)
- **शब्दस्य प्रामाण्यम्** (*śabdasya prāmāṇyam*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **प्रमा** (*pramā*) is closely related but refers to valid cognition rather than proof or authority as such.
- **प्रमाता** (*pramātā*) and **प्रमेय** (*prameya*) belong to the same technical family, but are less directly evaluative.
- **प्रमाणपत्र** (*pramāṇapatra*) overlaps only partly, since it is more documentary/administrative.

---

### 6. General correctness, propriety, and justified fit
These expressions are used to judge whether something is proper, fitting, reasonable, justified, or correct in context. They are common in argument, commentary, ethics, practical judgment, and social evaluation, and they are not limited to truth in the narrow sense.

**Representative expressions**
- **सम्यक्** (*samyak*)
- **सम्यक्त्व** (*samyaktva*)
- **युक्त** (*yukta*)
- **उचित** (*ucita*)
- **न्याय्य** (*nyāyya*)
- **धर्म्य** (*dharmya*)
- **सम्यग् उक्तम्** (*samyag uktam*)
- **युक्तं भवति** (*yuktaṃ bhavati*)
- **युक्तम् उक्तम्** (*yuktam uktam*)
- **न युक्तम्** (*na yuktam*)
- **उचितं भवति** (*ucitaṃ bhavati*)
- **न उचितम्** (*na ucitam*)
- **न्याय्यं भवति** (*nyāyyaṃ bhavati*)
- **न्याय्यं मन्यते** (*nyāyyaṃ manyate*)
- **धर्म्यं युद्धम्** (*dharmyaṃ yuddham*)
- **धर्म्यं वचनम्** (*dharmyaṃ vacanam*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **सम्यक्** (*samyak*) can overlap with correct cognition.
- **धर्म्य** (*dharmya*) may overlap with moral truthfulness, but often has broader normative force.
- **युक्त** (*yukta*) sometimes approaches “well-founded” or “reasonable,” not just “proper.”

---

### 7. Essential nature, real form, and fundamental reality
These expressions are used when the concern is what something really is, its own nature, true form, or fundamental principle. They are especially common in philosophical and theological discourse, and less tied to ordinary truth-telling.

**Representative expressions**
- **स्वरूप** (*svarūpa*)
- **तत्त्व** (*tattva*)
- **सत्य** (*satya*) in some philosophical uses
- **स्वरूपं ज्ञातुम्** (*svarūpaṃ jñātum*)
- **स्वरूपेण** (*svarūpeṇa*)
- **स्वरूपतः** (*svarūpataḥ*)
- **तत्त्वं ज्ञातुम्** (*tattvaṃ jñātum*)
- **तत्त्वतः** (*tattvataḥ*)
- **तत्त्वेन** (*tattvena*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **यथार्थ** (*yathārtha*) may overlap when used for what is actually real.
- **सत्य** (*satya*) also overlaps strongly, since it can refer both to truth and to what is ultimately real.
- **भूतार्थ** (*bhūtārtha*) may approach this group in some contexts, but often stays closer to factual actuality.

---

### 8. Resemblance, correspondence, and matching a described type
These expressions are used for likeness, similarity, or being of such a kind, rather than directly for truth or falsity. They matter where correspondence in appearance, character, or fit to a description is being discussed.

**Representative expressions**
- **सदृश** (*sadṛśa*)
- **तादृश** (*tādṛśa*)
- **तस्य सदृशम्** (*tasya sadṛśam*)
- **सदृशं भवति** (*sadṛśaṃ bhavati*)
- **तादृशं न दृश्यते** (*tādṛśaṃ na dṛśyate*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **सदृश** (*sadṛśa*) can come near representational accuracy, but its main use is resemblance.
- **तादृश** (*tādṛśa*) has a weakly distinctive profile and often functions simply as a demonstrative-type adjective.

---

### 9. Older Vedic-style truth linked with right order
This is a smaller and more restricted cluster centered on older usage where truth is tied to rightness or order more broadly. The material itself marks these forms as especially Vedic or archaizing rather than general classical vocabulary.

**Representative expressions**
- **ऋत** (*ṛta*)
- **अनृत** (*anṛta*)
- **ऋतं वद** (*ṛtaṃ vada*)
- **ऋतस्य पन्थाः** (*ṛtasya panthāḥ*)
- **अनृतं वदति** (*anṛtaṃ vadati*)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items**
- **अनृत** (*anṛta*) also belongs with ordinary falsehood and lying.
- **सत्य** (*satya*) often contrasts with this family historically and in usage, but is much broader and more common.

---

## Items with especially broad or unstable grouping

Some expressions do not fit neatly into only one grouping:

- **सत्य / सत्यम्** (*satya / satyam*): used for truth in speech, moral truthfulness, and philosophical reality.
- **यथार्थ** (*yathārtha*): used for factual accuracy, correct cognition, and realistic representation.
- **मिथ्या** (*mithyā*): used for false speech, erroneous cognition, and sometimes unreality.
- **सत्यवचन** (*satyavacana*): can refer both to a true statement and to the ethical practice of truthful speech.
- **धर्म्य** (*dharmya*): often more about righteousness or propriety than truth, but can overlap in evaluative contexts.
- **प्रमा / प्रमाण** (*pramā / pramāṇa*): tightly related, but one centers on valid cognition and the other on proof, warrant, or means of knowledge.

## Items that remain weakly grouped or peripheral
A few items are present in the source material but do not form a strong independent grouping here:

- **अप्रमाद** (*apramāda*): mainly vigilance/carefulness, only indirectly related to correctness or reliability.
- **सम्यक्त्व** (*samyaktva*): abstract and scholastic, but without a strong distinct usage cluster beyond general correctness.
- **प्रमाणपत्र** (*pramāṇapatra*), **प्रमाणलेख** (*pramāṇalekha*): documentary/administrative extensions of the proof-authority family.
- **भूतार्थक** (*bhūtārthaka*): technical and narrower than the broader factuality group.
