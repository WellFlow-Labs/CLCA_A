# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Hebrew (he)
# Layer: P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Nominal predicate sentence: Subject (NP) + Predicate (adjective or noun), with no copula in present tense. Word order flexible; predicate-initial order common for emphasis. Adjective (truth-domain) follows the noun it modifies in attributive position; agreement in gender, number, and definiteness is obligatory. Definite marking appears on both adjective and noun when the NP is definite. Nouns can also modify nouns in construct chains (סמיכות, smikhut).

### Examples
- זֶה אֱמֶת → *ze ʾemet* → "That is true / That is the truth"
- הָעֵד נֶאֱמָן → *ha-ʿed neʾeman* → "The witness is trustworthy"
- חבר אמיתי / *ḥaver ʾamiti* / "a genuine friend"
- עד מהימן / *ʿed meheymen* / "a credible witness"
- חָבֵר אֲמִתִּי / ḥaver ʾamitī / "a true/genuine friend"
- עֵד מְהֵימָן / ʿed meheymen / "a credible witness"

### Notes
Hebrew has no present-tense copula; attribution is expressed by simple juxtaposition of subject and predicate nominal or adjective. Definiteness of the subject NP is marked by the article *ha-*; the predicate adjective agrees in gender and number with the subject.

Hebrew adjectives follow the noun they modify. Definiteness is marked by *ha-* on both noun and adjective: *ha-ʿed ha-meheymen* ("the credible witness"). Gender and number agreement is obligatory: *ʿedut meheymanah* (f.sg.), *meqorot meheymanim* (m.pl.).

Postnominal adjective agrees with head noun in gender, number, and definiteness (e.g., הָעֵד הַמְּהֵימָן, "the credible witness"). Construct-state noun + noun (e.g., עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר, ʿedut sheqer, "false testimony") is an alternative attributive pattern without adjectival agreement.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negation particle *lo* (לֹא) + predicate adjective or noun, preceding the predicate. Subject may be overt or dropped. Negated predicate construction: Noun + *lo* + Adjective (predicative position), or attributive NP with negation at clause level. Hebrew has no negative attributive prefix productively applied to these adjectives; negation is syntactic rather than morphological. The construction *ḥoser* + abstract noun (*ḥoser kanut*, "lack of sincerity") provides an alternative nominal strategy for expressing the absence of a truth-domain quality. The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) productively negates abstract nouns but not adjectives directly.

### Examples
- זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן → *ze lo nakhon* → "That is not correct"
- הָעֵד לֹא מְהֵימָן → *ha-ʿed lo mehiman* → "The witness is not credible"
- המקור לא מהימן / *ha-meqor lo meheymen* / "the source is not reliable"
- זה לא נכון / *ze lo nakhon* / "that is not correct"
- זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן / ze lo nakhon / "that is not correct/wrong"
- חוֹסֶר כַּנּוּת / ḥoser kannut / "lack of sincerity, insincerity"

### Notes
*lo* is the invariant negator for nominal and adjectival predicates in the present tense. It precedes the predicate directly. The construction is fully parallel to affirmative attribution; no additional morphology is required.

There is no productive negative prefix (e.g., *\*lo-meheymen* as a compound attributive) in standard usage.

Predicative negation with לֹא is the primary pattern (לֹא נָכוֹן, לֹא מְהֵימָן, לֹא הָגוּן). The nominal construction חוֹסֶר + abstract noun serves as the standard attributive-equivalent negation for abstract qualities. The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) productively negates abstract nouns (אִי-אֲמִינוּת, ʾi-ʾaminut, "unreliability"; אִי-סְבִירוּת, ʾi-sevirut, "unreasonableness") but not adjectives directly.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Adjective follows the noun it modifies and agrees with it in gender, number, and definiteness. Construct-state (*smikhut*) phrases (Noun + Noun) also function as adnominal modification. A noun (truth-domain) in construct state (*smikhut*) or with *shel* ("of") may modify a head noun, with the truth-domain noun functioning as a genitive modifier specifying a type or property of the head. A prepositional phrase or relative clause may also modify a noun; in the truth domain, the most productive pattern is noun + שֶׁ- (she-, "that/which") clause, or noun + prepositional phrase specifying scope or temporal validity.

### Examples
- מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן → *maqor mehiman* → "a reliable source" (adjective post-nominal, indefinite)
- מִסְמָךְ מְזוּיָּף → *mismakh mezuyaf* → "a forged document" (passive participle as modifier)
- עדות שקר / *ʿedut sheker* / "false testimony" (lit. "testimony of falsehood")
- רגע האמת / *regaʿ ha-ʾemet* / "the moment of truth"
- נָכוֹן לְהַיּוֹם / nakhon le-hayom / "correct as of today" (adjective + prepositional scope phrase)
- גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת / garʿin shel ʾemet / "a kernel of truth" (noun + shel-phrase)

### Notes
Post-nominal adjective agreement is obligatory: gender, number, and definiteness must match the head noun. Passive participles (Pu'al, Huf'al) function freely as adnominal modifiers in this domain, e.g. *meʾummat* (verified), *mutsdddaq* (justified), *mezuyaf* (forged).

The construct state (*smikhut*) is the primary structure: the modifying noun is bare and the head noun carries definiteness when the phrase is definite (*ʾaminut ha-ʿed*, "the credibility of the witness"). The *shel* periphrastic genitive is an alternative, more common in spoken registers. Both patterns are fully productive with truth-domain nouns.

The pattern nakhon le-[date/time] is a productive formula in administrative and journalistic registers specifying the temporal scope of a truth-claim. The shel-genitive (גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת, חֲצִי אֱמֶת) modifies the quantity or degree of truth attributed to a claim.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Verb *lehafokh* (לְהַפֹּךְ) "to become" or *lihyot* (לִהְיוֹת) "to be/become" + predicate adjective, or the Nif'al binyan of relevant roots where available. No dedicated inchoative morphology exists for most items in this domain. Inchoative meaning is also expressed through intransitive verbs (*hitʿarer*, "to be shaken"; *gadal*, "to grow") applied to truth-domain nouns, or through *lehafokh le-* ("to become") with a complement. The hitpaʿel binyan frequently carries inchoative force.

### Examples
- הַמָּקוֹר הָפַךְ לְלֹא אָמִין → *ha-maqor hafakh le-lo ʾamin* → "The source became unreliable"
- הַמִּסְמָךְ נִמְצָא מְזוּיָּף → *ha-mismakh nimtsa mezuyaf* → "The document was found to be / turned out to be forged"
- האמון התערער / *ha-ʾimun hitʿarer* / "the trust was shaken / eroded" (inchoative of trust-loss)
- הספק גדל / *ha-safek gadal* / "the doubt grew"
- זֶה הָפַךְ לְאֱמֶת / ze hafakh le-ʾemet / "this became truth / this turned out to be true"
- הַחוֹזֶה הָפַךְ לְבָטֵל / ha-ḥoze hafakh le-batel / "the contract became void"

### Notes
Hebrew lacks a single dedicated inchoative construction for this semantic domain. The most common strategies are: (a) *hafakh le-* (became) + predicate, and (b) *nimtsa* (was found/turned out) + predicate adjective, which is particularly frequent in legal discourse for recording a change in assessed status.

Hebrew lacks a dedicated inchoative morphological pattern for this semantic domain. Inchoative meaning is expressed through intransitive verbs (*hitʿarer*, "to be shaken"; *gadal*, "to grow") applied to truth-domain nouns, or through the verb *lehafokh le-* ("to become") with a complement. The hitpaʿel binyan frequently carries inchoative force: *lehitgalaʾh ke-mezuyaf* ("to turn out to be a forgery") is a common construction in this domain.

The inchoative is expressed periphrastically with הָפַךְ לְ- (hafakh le-, "became") or נַעֲשָׂה (naʿasah, "became, turned into"). No dedicated inchoative morphological pattern exists for truth-domain adjectives. The construction is compositional and not lexicalized.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Hif'il binyan of the relevant root, where attested. For roots without a productive Hif'il in this domain, periphrastic causatives with *legaram* (לְגָרֹם, "to cause") or *lgaram she-* ("to cause that") + clause are used. Direct morphological causatives exist for some roots.

### Examples
- לְהַצְדִּיק → *lehatsddiq* → "to justify / to declare righteous / to vindicate" (Hif'il of צ-ד-ק)
- לְהַכְשִׁיר → *lehakhshir* → "to certify as fit / to qualify / to render valid" (Hif'il of כ-ש-ר)
- להאמין / *lehaʾamin* / "to believe" (hifʿil of א-מ-נ; also functions as the standard belief verb)
- לאשר / *leʾasher* / "to confirm, to authorize, to cause something to be approved" (hifʿil/piʿel of י-ש-ר)
- לְהַטְעוֹת / lehatʿot / "to mislead, to cause to err" (Hifʿil of ט-ע-ה)
- לְהַצְדִּיק / lehatsdiq / "to justify, to vindicate" (Hifʿil of צ-ד-ק)

### Notes
The Hif'il binyan functions as the primary causative/declarative morphology in Hebrew. In the truth domain it most commonly expresses a declarative meaning ("to declare X to be Y") rather than a strictly causative one. Not all P1 roots have a productive Hif'il in this domain: roots like ד-י-ק and ז-י-פ operate primarily in the Pi'el, and no Hif'il causative is in common use for *ʾemet* or *sheqer* directly.

The hifʿil binyan in Hebrew regularly expresses causative meaning, but for truth-domain roots the causative and non-causative senses have often lexicalized separately. *Lehaʾamin* means "to believe" rather than "to cause to believe"; the causative "to make someone believe" is expressed periphrastically (*lgaram le-mishehu lehaʾamin*). *Letsdiq* (hifʿil of צ-ד-ק, "to justify/vindicate") is a productive morphological causative in the legal domain.

The Hifʿil binyan productively derives causatives: הִטְעָה (hitʿah, "caused to err/misled"), הִצְדִּיק (hitsdiq, "justified/vindicated"), הֶאֱמִין (heʾemin, "caused to believe/believed"). A direct causative "to make [something] true" (e.g., *lehaʾemet) is not a productive or common form; the causative in this domain tends to operate on persons (causing someone to err, to believe, to be justified) rather than on propositions.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Two productive processes: (a) *-ut* suffix attached to an adjectival base, yielding a feminine abstract noun; (b) Pi'el or Hif'il verbal noun (pattern *CiCCuC* or *haCCaCa*) from the relevant root. The suffix -וּת (-ut) is the primary productive abstract nominalizer in modern Hebrew, attaching to adjectives and participial forms. The pattern Adj + -ut is highly regular across the truth domain. The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) combines productively with -ut nominalizations to form negated abstracts.

### Examples
- אֲמִינוּת → *ʾaminut* → "reliability, credibility" (*ʾamin* + *-ut*)
- אִמּוּת → *immut* → "verification, authentication" (Pi'el verbal noun of א-מ-ת)
- אמינות / *ʾaminut* / "credibility, trustworthiness" (from root א-מ-נ)
- מקוריות / *meqoriyut* / "originality, genuineness" (from *meqori*)
- אֲמִינוּת / ʾaminut / "reliability, credibility" (from אָמִין, ʾamin)
- סְבִירוּת / sevirut / "reasonableness" (from סָבִיר, savir)

### Notes
The *-ut* suffix is the most consistently productive nominalization process across all P1 adjectival bases in this domain (e.g., *sevirut*, *legitimiyyut*, *ḥuqiyyut*, *teqinut*, *meqoriyyut*). The *ʾi-* prefix attaches productively to *-ut* nominals to form negated abstracts in formal and legal registers (e.g., *ʾi-sevirut*, *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut*, *ʾi-teqinut*).

The suffix *-ut* is fully productive across the truth-domain adjectives documented in P1–P3: *kanut* ("sincerity"), *meheymanut* ("reliability"), *ʾotentiyut* ("authenticity"), *yesharut* ("uprightness"), *nekhonot* ("correctness/readiness"), *shaqranut* ("mendacity"). Verbal nouns (piʿel pattern: *ziyyuf*, *diyuq*, *ḥiqquy*; hifʿil pattern: *honaʾaʾh*, *ʾishur*) constitute a parallel nominalization strategy for verbal roots.

The -ut suffix is the dominant and productive pattern: מְהֵימָנוּת (meheymanut), עַקְבִּיּוּת (ʿaqbiyut), אֲמִתִּיּוּת (ʾamitiyut), תִּקִינוּת (taqinut), הֲגוּנוּת (hagunot), יְשָׁרוּת (yesharut). The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) combines productively with -ut nominalizations to form negated abstracts (אִי-אֲמִינוּת, אִי-סְבִירוּת, אִי-תִּקִינוּת). Verbal nouns (מַשְׁקַל patterns, e.g., זִיּוּף, דִּיּוּק, סְתִירָה) form a parallel nominalization system for action/process meanings.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
(a) *-an* suffix on root, yielding a masculine agentive noun with habitual/characterological meaning; (b) Qal or Hif'il active participle used nominally. Two distinct agentive patterns are attested: the *-an* suffix for habitual or characterological agents, and the active participle for agents defined by an ongoing or specific act.

### Examples
- שַׁקְרָן → *shaqran* → "liar, habitual liar" (root ש-ק-ר + *-an*)
- מַאֲמִין → *maʾamin* → "believer, one who trusts/believes" (Hif'il active participle of א-מ-נ)
- שקרן / *shaqran* / "liar" (habitual agent; root ש-ק-ר + *-an*)
- מזייף / *mezayef* / "forger" (piʿel active participle; root ז-י-פ)
- שַׁקְרָן / shaqran / "liar, habitual liar" (from root ש-ק-ר)
- דַּיְקָן / dayqan / "a precise/meticulous person, stickler for accuracy" (from root ד-י-ק)

### Notes
The *-an* suffix is the dedicated agentive/characterological nominalizer in Hebrew and is attested in this domain only for *shaqran* (liar) and its feminine *shaqranit*. Active participles (Qal, Pi'el, Hif'il) serve as the general agentive construction for other items: *dover ʾemet* (truth-teller, lit. "one who speaks truth"), *mezayyef* (forger), *medayyeq* (precise person). No *-an* agentive for truth-telling (*ʾemet*) is in common use; the participle construction *dover ʾemet* fills this role.

The *-an* suffix marks a person characterized by habitual performance of the action: *shaqran* ("habitual liar"), *rammʾai* ("habitual deceiver/cheat"), *tsadqan* ("self-righteous person"). The active participle (*mezayef*, *meshaqer*, *merameh*) marks the agent of a specific or ongoing act and is used predicatively (*hu meshaqer*, "he is lying") as well as attributively. The two patterns are not interchangeable: *shaqran* implies character, *meshaqer* implies act.

The -an agentive is productive for negative or characterological evaluations: שַׁקְרָן (liar), בַּטְלָן (idler). A parallel agentive for "truth-teller" is not lexicalized with -an; the function is served periphrastically (מִי שֶׁאוֹמֵר אֱמֶת, "one who tells the truth") or by participial forms (נֶאֱמָן, neʾeman, "faithful/trustworthy one"). The -an agentives productively generate further -anut abstracts (שַׁקְרָנוּת, דַּיְקָנוּת).

## 8. ADVERBIAL CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Preposition *be-* (בְּ, "in/with") + abstract noun, yielding an adverbial phrase of manner or sincerity. These phrases are not argument-internal but frame the entire proposition, occupying sentence-initial or sentence-final adverbial position and modifying the speaker's commitment to the truth or manner of the proposition.

### Examples
- בֶּאֱמֶת → *beʾemet* → "truly, really, honestly" (sincerity/intensifier marker)
- בְּדִיּוּק → *bediyyuq* → "exactly, precisely" (degree/exactness marker)
- בכנות, אני לא יודע / *be-kanut, ʾani lo yodeʿa* / "honestly, I don't know"
- בדיוק עשרה / *be-diyuq ʿasarah* / "exactly ten"
- בֶּאֱמֶת / be-ʾemet / "truly, really, in truth"
- בְּכַנּוּת / be-kannut / "sincerely, honestly, frankly"

### Notes
This is a highly productive pattern in the truth domain. Further attested forms include *bekhannot* (sincerely), *beʾofen ʾamiti* (in a genuine way), and *leʾamitto shel davar* (in actual fact). The *be-* + noun construction grammaticalizes as a manner adverb without additional morphology and is the primary strategy for adverbializing truth-domain nominals in Hebrew.

The pattern *be-* + abstract noun is highly productive in this domain: *be-kanut* ("honestly"), *be-diyuq* ("exactly, precisely"), *be-ʾemet* ("truly, in truth"), *be-yosher* ("with integrity"), *be-mirmah* ("fraudulently, by deceit"). *Be-diyuq* has grammaticalized as a precision adverb and is among the most frequent items in this set.

This pattern is exceptionally productive in the truth domain: בְּדִיּוּק (be-diyyuq, "exactly"), בְּיֹשֶׁר (be-yosher, "with integrity"), בְּתֹקֶף (be-toqef, "by virtue of"), בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים (be-ʾemet u-ve-tamim, "in truth and integrity"). These prepositional adverbials function as sincerity markers, manner adverbials, and discourse particles. Several (especially בֶּאֱמֶת, בְּדִיּוּק) have high token frequency in everyday speech and have acquired pragmatic functions beyond their compositional meanings.

## 9. CONCESSIVE / EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
*nakhon she-* (נָכוֹן שֶׁ-) or *nakhon lehagid she-* + clause, introducing a concession or qualified acknowledgment before a counterpoint. A truth-domain adjective or noun heads a subordinating frame construction that introduces a proposition, typically with the complementizer שֶׁ- (she-, "that"). The construction acknowledges a true or correct point, often before a qualification. Parallel constructions with *ʾemet she-* (אֱמֶת שֶׁ-, "it is true that") also occur in the same discourse function.

### Examples
- נָכוֹן שֶׁהוּא צוֹדֵק, אֲבָל... → *nakhon she-hu tsodeq, aval...* → "It is true that he is right, but..."
- נָכוֹן לְהַגִּיד שֶׁהָיוּ טְעוּיוֹת → *nakhon lehagid she-hayu teʿuyot* → "It is fair to say there were mistakes"
- נָכוֹן שֶׁהוּא נִיסָּה, אֲבָל... / nakhon she-hu nissa, ʾaval... / "It is true that he tried, but..."
- סָבִיר שֶׁהַמַּצָּב יִשְׁתַּנֶּה / savir she-ha-matsav yishtanneh / "It is reasonable that the situation will change"

### Notes
This construction functions as a discourse-level concessive marker, acknowledging a truth claim before introducing a qualification or counterargument. It is structurally distinct from simple attribution (Pattern 1) in that it embeds a full clause under *nakhon* and is used specifically to manage epistemic stance in argumentation.

The pattern [truth-adjective] + שֶׁ- + clause is productive with נָכוֹן (nakhon), סָבִיר (savir), נְכוֹן לְצַיֵּן שֶׁ- (nakhon letsayen she-, "it should be noted that"), and similar items. The נָכוֹן שֶׁ... construction functions specifically as a concessive frame, acknowledging a true premise before introducing a counterpoint; it is among the most frequent discourse patterns in spoken and written Hebrew argumentation.

## 10. TRUTH-PREDICATE FRAME

### Pattern
Fixed sentence frame *ha-ʾemet hi she-* ("the truth is that...") or *nakhon she-* ("it is correct that...") introducing a propositional complement clause. These frames function as epistemic stance markers signaling that the following clause represents the genuine state of affairs.

### Examples
- האמת היא שהמצב החמיר / *ha-ʾemet hi she-ha-matsav heḥmir* / "the truth is that the situation has worsened"
- נכון שאמרת שלא היית שם? / *nakhon she-ʾamarta she-lo hayita sham?* / "is it correct that you said you were not there?"

### Notes
These frames are syntactically distinct from simple predication: they introduce a full embedded clause (*she-* + clause) rather than a nominal or adjectival complement. *Ha-ʾemet hi she-* is used in declarative contexts to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement; *nakhon she-* is used in interrogative contexts, particularly in legal cross-examination, to confirm or challenge a prior assertion. Both are highly frequent across registers.

## 11. VERBAL PERIPHRASIS WITH TRUTH-DOMAIN NOUN AS OBJECT

### Pattern
General-purpose verb (*ledaber*, *lomar*, *legallot*, *lehastir*, *leʿavvet*) + truth-domain noun as direct object, expressing an action performed on or with respect to truth. The truth-domain noun is typically definite (*ha-ʾemet*) when referring to a specific truth, and indefinite (*ʾemet*, *sheker*) when referring to the abstract property.

### Examples
- לדבר אמת / *ledaber ʾemet* / "to tell the truth" (lit. "to speak truth")
- לעוות את האמת / *leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet* / "to distort the truth"

### Notes
This is the primary verbal strategy for expressing truth-related actions in Hebrew, since the truth-domain roots themselves do not always yield productive verbal forms for all meanings. The range of verbs attested with *ha-ʾemet* as object — *legallot* ("to reveal"), *laḥsof* ("to expose"), *lehastir* ("to conceal"), *levarer* ("to ascertain"), *lesalef* ("to falsify"), *liḥyot ʿim* ("to live with") — reflects a systematic conceptual structure in which truth is treated as an object that can be revealed, concealed, distorted, or confronted.
