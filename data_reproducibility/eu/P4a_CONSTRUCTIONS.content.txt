# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Basque (eu)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X da egiazko/gezurrezko/zuzen/oker** (X is true/false/correct/wrong)
Predicative construction using copula **izan** (to be) + adjective in absolutive case.

### Examples
- **Hau egia da**
  - hau egia da
  - "This is truth / This is true"

- **Istorio hori gezurra da**
  - istorio hori gezurra da
  - "That story is a lie / That story is false"

### Notes
Basque uses predicative adjectives/nouns in absolutive case with copula **izan**. The article **-a** typically appears on the predicate nominal. Alternative construction: **X egiazko/gezurrezko Y da** (X is a true/false Y) for attributive use before nouns.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X ez da egiazko/gezurrezko/zuzen/oker** (X is not true/false/correct/wrong)
Negation particle **ez** precedes the copula.

### Examples
- **Hau ez da egia**
  - hau ez da egia
  - "This is not truth / This is not true"

- **Zure esana ez da zuzena**
  - zure esana ez da zuzena
  - "Your statement is not correct"

### Notes
Standard negation places **ez** immediately before the finite verb. The predicate nominal/adjective remains in absolutive case unchanged.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**egiazko/gezurrezko/zuzen/oker + NOUN** (true/false/correct/wrong + noun)
Attributive adjectives use the relational suffix **-ko/-zko** without the article.

### Examples
- **egiazko historia**
  - egiazko historia
  - "true story / real history"

- **gezurrezko agiri**
  - gezurrezko agiri
  - "false document / forged document"

### Notes
Basque distinguishes attributive forms (with **-ko/-zko**, no article) from predicative forms (with article **-a**). The attributive form precedes the noun directly. Alternative: genitive construction **egiaren historia** (story of truth) for different nuance.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**X egiazko/gezurrezko/zuzen/oker bihurtu da** (X became true/false/correct/wrong)
Uses verb **bihurtu** (to become, to turn into) + copula **izan**.

### Examples
- **Istorioa egia bihurtu da**
  - istorioa egia bihurtu da
  - "The story became truth / The story turned out to be true"

- **Zuzena oker bihurtu da**
  - zuzena oker bihurtu da
  - "The correct (thing) became wrong / What was right became wrong"

### Notes
**Bihurtu** is the primary verb for change of state. Alternative construction: **egiaztu** (to be verified/confirmed) for truth-specific inchoative meaning. For errors: **okertu** (to go wrong, to become crooked) is more common than **oker bihurtu**.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**X egiaztu/gezurtatu/zuzendu** (to verify X / to falsify X / to correct X)
Causative/factitive meaning expressed through derivational suffixes **-tu/-du** on root.

### Examples
- **Datuak egiaztatu ditut**
  - datuak egiaztatu ditut
  - "I have verified the data / I verified the data"

- **Akatsa zuzendu behar dugu**
  - akatsa zuzendu behar dugu
  - "We must correct the error"

### Notes
Basque uses derivational morphology rather than periphrastic causatives for these concepts. **Egiaztatu** (verify), **gezurtatu** (falsify), **zuzendu** (correct), **okertu** (make wrong) are lexicalized causative/factitive verbs. No separate analytic causative construction is common for these meanings.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -tasun + -a** (quality/state of being X)
Productive abstract noun formation using suffix **-tasun**.

### Examples
- **egiazkotasuna**
  - egiazkotasuna
  - "truthfulness / authenticity / veracity"

- **zuzentasuna**
  - zuzentasuna
  - "correctness / rightness / justice"

### Notes
The suffix **-tasun** is highly productive for creating abstract quality nouns from adjectives. The resulting form takes the article **-a**. Alternative nominalizations: **-pen** for action/result (e.g., **egiaztapen** "verification"), **-tze** for process (e.g., **egiaztatze** "verifying").

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ROOT + -tzaile** (one who performs action)
Agent noun formation using suffix **-tzaile**.

### Examples
- **zuzentzaile**
  - zuzentzaile
  - "corrector / editor / proofreader"

- **gezurlari**
  - gezurlari
  - "liar / one who lies"

### Notes
**-tzaile** is productive for agent nouns from verbs. For "liar," the compound **gezurlari** (gezur + -lari, where **-lari** indicates habitual agent) is more common than **\*gezurtzaile**. For "truth-teller," no single lexicalized form exists; periphrastic **egia esaten duena** (one who tells truth) is used. **-lari** and **-tzaile** show partial complementary distribution.

---

## 8. VERB + NOUN COLLOCATIONS

### Pattern
**NOUN + VERB** (light verb constructions)
Truth-domain nouns combine with semantically light verbs to express actions.

### Examples
- **egia esan**
  - egia esan
  - "to tell the truth / to speak truth"

- **gezurra esan**
  - gezurra esan
  - "to tell a lie / to lie"

### Notes
Basque extensively uses noun + verb collocations where the verb carries grammatical information and the noun carries semantic weight. Common verbs: **esan** (say), **egin** (do/make), **eman** (give), **jarri** (put/place). Word order is typically NOUN + VERB in neutral contexts. These function as semi-lexicalized units.

---

## 9. GENITIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**NOUN-GEN + NOUN** (possessive/relational constructions)
Genitive case **-ren/-aren** marks possessor or relational head.

### Examples
- **egiaren bila**
  - egiaren bila
  - "in search of truth / seeking truth"

- **egiaren alderdi**
  - egiaren alderdi
  - "aspect of truth / side of truth"

### Notes
Genitive constructions express possession, part-whole relations, and abstract associations. The genitive marker varies by phonological context (**-ren** after consonants, **-aren** after vowels in standard Basque). Common in philosophical and analytical discourse for expressing relationships to abstract concepts.

---

## 10. LOCATIVE/MANNER ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
**ROOT + -an/-ean/-tan** (locative/manner adverbial)
Locative suffix creates adverbial expressions of manner or circumstance.

### Examples
- **egiatan**
  - egiatan
  - "in truth / truly / really"

- **zuzenean**
  - zuzenean
  - "directly / straight / live (broadcast)"

### Notes
The locative suffix **-an/-ean/-tan** (allomorphy conditioned by phonology) creates adverbial expressions. These can indicate physical location, temporal situation, or abstract manner. **Benetan** (truly, really) shows the variant **-tan**. Some forms have specialized meanings (e.g., **zuzenean** for live broadcasts).

---

## 11. INSTRUMENTAL ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
**ROOT + -z/-ez** (instrumental/manner adverbial)
Instrumental case creates manner adverbials.

### Examples
- **zuzentasunez**
  - zuzentasunez
  - "with correctness / rightly / justly"

- **zintzoki** (alternative pattern: ROOT + -ki)
  - zintzoki
  - "honestly / sincerely"

### Notes
Two productive patterns exist: instrumental case **-z/-ez** on nouns, and adverbial suffix **-ki** on adjective roots. The **-ki** pattern is more common for manner adverbs from adjectives (e.g., **egiazki** "truly", **zehazki** "precisely"). Both patterns are productive and semantically similar.

---

## 12. REDUPLICATION FOR EMPHASIS

### Pattern
**ROOT-ROOT** or **WORD-WORD** (emphatic reduplication)
Full or partial reduplication intensifies meaning.

### Examples
- **gezur-gezurra**
  - gezur-gezurra
  - "complete lie / total falsehood / blatant lie"

- **zuzen-zuzen**
  - zuzen-zuzen
  - "very straight / completely correct / directly"

### Notes
Reduplication is a productive emphatic strategy in Basque. Full reduplication (with hyphen) intensifies the base meaning. Common pattern across truth-domain vocabulary: **berdin-berdin** (exactly the same), **ziur-ziur** (absolutely sure). The reduplicated form can function as adjective, adverb, or noun depending on base category.

---

## 13. MODAL NECESSITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**VERB-tu behar** (must/need to VERB)
Modal necessity expressed with **behar** (need, must) following verbal noun.

### Examples
- **zuzendu behar dut**
  - zuzendu behar dut
  - "I must correct / I need to correct"

- **frogatu behar da**
  - frogatu behar da
  - "it must be proven / it needs to be proven"

### Notes
**Behar** functions as a modal auxiliary following the verbal noun (citation form). The construction requires appropriate auxiliary verb agreement. Alternative: **behar du/dute/etc.** for transitive, **behar da/dira** for intransitive. Common in prescriptive and evaluative contexts.

---

## 14. POTENTIAL/EVALUATIVE ADJECTIVES

### Pattern
**ROOT + -garri** (worthy of / able to be X-ed)
Suffix **-garri** creates adjectives indicating potential or worthiness.

### Examples
- **sinesgarria**
  - sinesgarria
  - "believable / credible / plausible"

- **frogagarria**
  - frogagarria
  - "provable / demonstrable / verifiable"

### Notes
**-garri** is highly productive for creating evaluative adjectives from verb roots. The resulting forms indicate that something is worthy of or capable of undergoing the action. Common in epistemic evaluation: **fidagarria** (trustworthy), **zalantzagarria** (doubtful). Takes article **-a** in predicative use, appears without article in attributive position.

---

## 15. PEJORATIVE NOMINALIZATIONS

### Pattern
**ROOT + -keria** (pejorative/collective action nominalization)
Suffix **-keria** creates pejorative nouns indicating habitual or collective negative behavior.

### Examples
- **gezurkeria**
  - gezurkeria
  - "lying / deceit / falsehood (habitual/collective)"

- **faltsukeria**
  - faltsukeria
  - "falseness / deceit / hypocrisy"

### Notes
**-keria** carries negative moral evaluation and often implies habitual or systematic behavior. Particularly productive with deception-related roots: **iruzurkeria** (fraudulence). The suffix combines collective/habitual aspect with pejorative force. Less productive with positive or neutral concepts.

---

## 16. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X bezain egiazko/zuzen** (as true/correct as X)
Comparative equality using **bezain** (as...as).

### Examples
- **Hau hura bezain egia da**
  - hau hura bezain egia da
  - "This is as true as that"

- **Zure erantzuna nirea bezain zuzena da**
  - zure erantzuna nirea bezain zuzena da
  - "Your answer is as correct as mine"

### Notes
**Bezain** creates equative comparisons. For superiority: **X baino egiazkoago** (more true than X) using comparative suffix **-ago** and **baino** (than). Basque lacks synthetic comparative forms for these adjectives; analytic constructions with **gehiago** (more) are also possible but less common.

---

## 17. EVIDENTIAL/APPEARANCE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**X dirudi / itxura du** (X seems / X appears)
Evidential constructions using **dirudi** (seems) or **itxura du** (has appearance).

### Examples
- **Gezurra dirudi**
  - gezurra dirudi
  - "It seems like a lie / It appears false"

- **Egiazko itxura du**
  - egiazko itxura du
  - "It has the appearance of being true / It seems genuine"

### Notes
**Dirudi** (< **da** + **irudi** "seem") is the standard verb for seeming/appearing. **Itxura** (appearance) combines with **eduki** (have) for similar meaning. These constructions mark epistemic stance based on appearance rather than verified knowledge. Common in hedging and cautious assertions.

---

## 18. EXPOSURE/REVELATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**agerian jarri/utzi/egon** (to expose / to leave exposed / to be exposed)
Locative **agerian** (in the open) combines with position/motion verbs.

### Examples
- **Egia agerian jarri dut**
  - egia agerian jarri dut
  - "I have exposed the truth / I put the truth in the open"

- **Gezurra agerian dago**
  - gezurra agerian dago
  - "The lie is exposed / The lie is in the open"

### Notes
**Agerian** (locative of **ageri** "visible, manifest") combines productively with verbs of position and motion: **jarri** (put), **utzi** (leave), **egon** (be/stay). These constructions express revelation, exposure, or manifest visibility. Common in investigative and revelatory contexts.

---

## 19. DOUBT/QUESTIONING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**zalantzan jarri/egon** (to call into question / to be in doubt)
Locative **zalantzan** (in doubt) combines with position verbs.

### Examples
- **Haren zintzotasuna zalantzan jarri dut**
  - haren zintzotasuna zalantzan jarri dut
  - "I have called his/her honesty into question"

- **Oraindik zalantzan nago**
  - oraindik zalantzan nago
  - "I am still in doubt / I am still uncertain"

### Notes
**Zalantzan** (locative of **zalantza** "doubt") combines with **jarri** (put) for active questioning and **egon** (be) for state of doubt. Parallel to **agerian** constructions but expressing epistemic uncertainty rather than manifestation. The construction **zalantza izan** (to have doubt) is also common.

---

## 20. BINOMIAL EXPRESSIONS

### Pattern
**X eta Y** (X and Y) - fixed or semi-fixed pairs
Coordinated pairs expressing contrasts or complementary concepts.

### Examples
- **egia eta gezurra**
  - egia eta gezurra
  - "truth and falsehood / truth and lies"

- **zuzen eta oker**
  - zuzen eta oker
  - "right and wrong / correct and incorrect"

### Notes
Binomial expressions pair contrasting or complementary truth-domain concepts. These function as semi-fixed expressions in moral and epistemological discourse. Order is typically fixed with positive term first. Other examples: **egia ala gezurra** (truth or lie - using disjunctive **ala**).
