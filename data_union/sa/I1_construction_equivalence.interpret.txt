## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 BECOME is not substituted by REVELATION

The BECOME operator is well-served by multiple [CORE] constructions (*jāyate*, *utpadyate*, *nivartate*, *pramāṇa-bhūta*). These constructions encode the arising or cessation of a cognitive state (valid cognition, erroneous cognition) as a process with a grammatical subject. No REVELATION construction is attested: there is no construction in P4a/P4b that encodes the frame "X is found to be true" or "the truth of X comes to light" as a distinct event type. The discourse-marking adverbials (§10: *vastutaḥ*, *paramārthataḥ*, *tattvataḥ*) assert that a proposition holds at a deeper or ultimate level of analysis, but they do not encode the event of a truth being uncovered or revealed. REVELATION is therefore absent rather than substituted.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION is not substituted by DISCOVERY; it is partially available through causative formations

No DISCOVERY construction is attested in P4a/P4b. VERIFICATION is expressible, but only through [MARGINAL] constructions restricted to legal and administrative registers (*satyāpana*, *satyāpayati*) or to modern Sanskrit-influenced usage (*pramāṇīkaraṇa*). In classical philosophical prose, the closest functional equivalent to verification is the epistemological apparatus of *pramāṇa* (valid means of knowledge), but this is encoded as a nominal category (§1 ATTR, §2 NEGATION: *pramāṇaṃ nāsti*) rather than as a verbal or causative construction expressing the act of verifying. The act of verifying is thus not expressed through a [CORE] construction in the philosophical register.

### 2.3 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction

These two operators are expressed through structurally distinct constructions. DISCONFIRMATION is expressed through negative attribution (§2: *na* + predicate nominal, *nāsti*, prefixal *a-*/*an-*), all of which are [CORE]. VERIFICATION is expressed through causative formations (§5), which are [MARGINAL] or register-restricted. The asymmetry is notable: disconfirmation has robust [CORE] expression across registers, while verification lacks a [CORE] construction in classical philosophical prose.

### 2.4 NEGATION: both analytic and morphological strategies are available and productive

Sanskrit employs two parallel negation strategies in the truth domain. The syntactic strategy (*na* + predicate nominal, zero-copula) is used when negation is locally scoped or pragmatically emphatic. The morphological strategy (prefixal *a-*/*an-*) is incorporated directly into the adjective or nominal and tends toward lexicalization (*asatya*, *ayukta*, *anucita*, *apramāṇa*). P4b describes these as functionally parallel and often interchangeable. No consistent semantic distinction between the two strategies is reported; the choice is described as a matter of scope and emphasis rather than meaning. Both strategies are [CORE].

### 2.5 Positive/negative asymmetries in agentive nominalization

Within the agentive nominalization pattern (§7), the *-vādin* suffix shows an observable distributional asymmetry: *satyavādin* (positive) and *asatyavādin* / *mithyāvādin* (negative) are both [CORE] and well-attested. However, the epistemological agent role (the knower of truth) is not filled by a *-vādin* compound; it is filled by *pramātṛ* (suffix *-tṛ*), which is restricted to the epistemological technical register. The speech-domain agentive (*-vādin*, *-bhāṣin*) and the epistemological-domain agentive (*-tṛ*) occupy non-overlapping functional niches. The *-jña* suffix (*ṛta-jña*) occupies a third niche (knowing, not speaking) and is [MARGINAL].

### 2.6 CAUSATION (factual) is narrowly available; CAUSATION (character) is absent

The *āpādaya-* periphrastic causative (§5) is [CORE] but is attested specifically in the context of introducing a fault or defect in an opponent's position (*doṣam āpādayati*). This is a narrow instantiation of factual causation. The morphological causative formations (*satyāpana*, *satyāpayati*) are [MARGINAL] and register-restricted. No construction encodes the sense that a person's character or authority causes a proposition to be true or to be accepted as true. The *pramāṇīkaraṇa* formation (making something into an authority) approaches this meaning but is [MARGINAL] and associated with modern Sanskrit-influenced usage rather than classical philosophical prose.

### 2.7 Discourse-marking adverbials (§10) function as propositional-level operators without a periphrastic equivalent

The *-taḥ* adverbial forms (*vastutaḥ*, *paramārthataḥ*, *tattvataḥ*) function as sentence-level operators asserting that the accompanying proposition corresponds to the actual or ultimate state of affairs. P4b explicitly notes that no periphrastic equivalent ("it is true that…") is reported as a common alternate. These forms are therefore the sole [CORE] device for this propositional-operator function. They are structurally adverbial (derived by the *-taḥ* suffix from nominal bases) but function semantically over the entire proposition.

### 2.8 The concessive construction (§12) is lexically restricted to *satyam*

The concessive use of *satyam* (*satyaṃ tu*, *satyaṃ tathāpi*) is the only truth-domain construction with an explicit lexical restriction: no other truth-domain term participates in this pattern with comparable frequency. *Satyam* in this construction does not predicate truth of a proposition but functions as a discourse particle. This represents a pragmatic specialization of a single lexical item rather than a productive construction type.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **REVELATION** | No construction encodes the event of a truth being uncovered, revealed, or found to be the case. The discourse-marking adverbials (§10) assert ultimate-level truth but do not encode the event of revelation. |
| **CAUSATION (character)** | No construction encodes the sense that a person's character, authority, or status causes a proposition to be true or to be accepted as true. The agentive nominalizations (§7) identify truth-telling agents but do not encode a causative relationship between agent character and propositional truth. |

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **VERIFICATION (classical philosophical register)** | No single [CORE] lexical item or construction in classical philosophical prose encodes the act of verifying a proposition. *Satyāpana* and *satyāpayati* fill this role in legal/administrative Sanskrit but are absent from the philosophical register as [CORE] items. *Pramāṇa* encodes the instrument of valid cognition but not the act of verification as a verbal or causative event. |

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps are identifiable from P4a/P4b data alone. The absence of REVELATION and CAUSATION (character) constructions reflects structural and lexical absences in the attested data; the data do not provide evidence for or against whether these concepts are expressible through extended circumlocution.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

| Asymmetry | Description |
|---|---|
| **VERIFICATION vs. DISCONFIRMATION** | DISCONFIRMATION is fully available through multiple [CORE] constructions (§2). VERIFICATION is only partially available through [MARGINAL], register-restricted constructions (§5). The positive act of confirming/verifying is structurally underrepresented relative to the negative act of denying/disconfirming. |
| **CAUSATION (factual): fault-introduction vs. truth-establishment** | The [CORE] causative construction (*doṣam āpādayati*) is attested specifically for introducing a fault or defect, not for establishing a truth. Causation in the direction of truth-establishment is [MARGINAL] or absent at the [CORE] level. |
| **Agentive nominalization: speech domain vs. epistemic domain** | The *-vādin* and *-bhāṣin* agentive patterns are [CORE] and productive across positive and negative forms in the speech domain. The epistemic-agent role (*pramātṛ*) is [CORE] but restricted to a fixed technical term within a single triad; it is not freely productive across the truth-domain vocabulary. The *-jña* pattern is [MARGINAL]. |
