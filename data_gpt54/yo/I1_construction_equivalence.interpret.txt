## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

The supplied material shows a clear asymmetry between predicative truth attribution and inchoative “become” meanings.

- **ATTR** is directly and clearly available through:
  - **Òtítọ́ ni.**
  - **Ó jẹ́ òtítọ́.**
  - related evaluative predicates **ó tọ́ / ó dájú**

- **BECOME** is explicitly not established:
  - P4a gives **(no common expression identified)**
  - P4b marks this as **[UNCERTAIN]**

Because of that, where a canonical “become true / become evident” operator might be expected, the language data instead uses **REVELATION / manifestation** expressions:
- **Fi òtítọ́ hàn.**
- **Fi ẹ̀rí hàn.**

So in the supplied material, **REVELATION substitutes for BECOME** in practice. This is not because a dedicated become-pattern is shown, but because the attested way to move toward manifest truth/evidence is to “show/reveal” it analytically.

For **CAUSATION (factual)**, there is no dedicated morphological causative. Instead, the language uses analytic multi-word constructions:
- **fi òtítọ́ hàn**
- **fi ẹ̀rí hàn**
- **rí i dájú**

These are best described as **light-verb or analytic carriers** of factual causation / verification-like meaning. The operator is not encoded by a single derivational form in the supplied material.

For **VERIFICATION**, the material does not present a separate canonical construction label, but it does provide two functional routes:
- **rí i dájú** “make sure / ascertain” in the notes to causative
- evidential possession **ní ẹ̀rí** as support for a claim

So **verification is expressed through ascertainment and evidence constructions**, rather than through a distinct dedicated verification morphology.

For **DISCONFIRMATION**, the language uses several non-identical but related strategies:
- direct denial of truth: **Kì í ṣe òtítọ́.**
- explicit falsehood labeling: **Irọ́ ni.**
- broader evaluative rejection: **kò tọ́**
- denial of evidential support: **Kò sí ẹ̀rí.**

Thus **VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction** in the supplied material:
- verification is tied to **evidence / ascertainment**
- disconfirmation is tied to **negation / falsehood labeling / absence of evidence**

For **NEGATION**, the material shows **analytic negation**, not morphological negation:
- **kì í ṣe**
- **kò**
- **kò sí**

No bound or derivational negative truth operator is established in P4a/P4b.

There is also an asymmetry in the nominal domain:
- abstract nominalization is well supported: **òtítọ́, òtọ́, títọ́, ẹ̀rí, òdodo**
- agentive nominalization is only clearly supported for **ẹlẹ́rìí**
- for “liar” or “truth-teller,” P4a explicitly says **NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** in the supplied material

So the nominal system is **strong for abstract nouns**, but **restricted in the agentive truth/falsity subdomain**.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **BECOME**: **MISSING (structural gap)**
  - P4a/P4b explicitly give no common expression identified.
- **CAUSATION (character)**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**
  - No construction of this type is established in the supplied material.
- No dedicated **morphological causative** for truth-domain operations is established.
- No dedicated **morphological negation** for truth-domain operations is established; negation is analytic.

### Lexical Gaps
- Agentive nouns specifically meaning **“liar”** or **“truth-teller”**:
  - **NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS** in the supplied material
- By contrast, **ẹlẹ́rìí** is attested and available.

### Conceptual Gaps
- No conceptual gap is directly established for truth, falsity, evidence, or attribution.
- A blocked or unsupported operation is only clearly shown for **BECOME**, where the supplied material does not establish a dedicated inchoative construction.

### Asymmetric Gaps
- **Positive vs. negative asymmetry**:
  - Positive attribution is directly available: **Òtítọ́ ni. / Ó jẹ́ òtítọ́.**
  - Negative attribution is also directly available: **Kì í ṣe òtítọ́. / Irọ́ ni.**
  - So there is **no major positive/negative gap** at the attribution level.
- **Attribution vs. becoming asymmetry**:
  - Attribution is well supported.
  - Becoming is missing.
- **Abstract vs. agentive nominalization asymmetry**:
  - Abstract nominalization is well supported.
  - Agentive nominalization is only partially supported, with **ẹlẹ́rìí** but not attested “liar/truth-teller” forms.

### Normalized notes on multi-word expressions
- **Òtítọ́ ni** behaves as a **nominal predicate construction**.
- **Ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** behaves as a **verbal predicate construction** with **jẹ́** as the carrier.
- **kì í ṣe òtítọ́** is an **analytic negative predication**.
- **Irọ́ ni** is a **nominal falsehood-labeling predicate**.
- **Ọ̀nà títọ́** and **Ẹlẹ́rìí ojú** behave as **adnominal noun–modifier expressions**.
- **fi òtítọ́ hàn** and **fi ẹ̀rí hàn** are **analytic verbal constructions**; **fi** functions as a light-verb carrier and **hàn** carries the manifestation/revelation component.
- **rí i dájú** is an **analytic verbal expression** for ascertainment/verification-like meaning.
- **ní ẹ̀rí / kò sí ẹ̀rí** are **possessive/existential evidential constructions**.
- **Ní òtítọ́ / L’òtítọ́** are **adverbial discourse-marking expressions**, not core attributive predicates.
