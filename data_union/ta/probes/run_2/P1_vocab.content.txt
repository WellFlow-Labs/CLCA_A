# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

**1.**
**Form:** உண்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai
**Literal meaning:** that which is real/existent; from *uḷ* (to be/exist) + nominal suffix
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuineness

Used to assert that a statement, situation, or thing corresponds to what actually is the case. Appears in both everyday speech and formal philosophical and legal contexts. Productive in derivation: *uṇmaiyāna* (true, genuine), *uṇmaiyāṉ* (truthful person).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** பொய்
**Transliteration:** poy
**Literal meaning:** falsehood (root form; etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, fiction

The primary antonym of *uṇmai*; used to characterize statements, representations, or persons as false or deceptive. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Productive: *poyyan* (liar), *poyyana* (false, fake).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** நேர்மை
**Transliteration:** nēṟmai
**Literal meaning:** straightness, directness; from *nēr* (straight, direct) + *-mai* (abstract nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness, sincerity

Used to evaluate persons and conduct with respect to straightforwardness and absence of deception. Commonly applied in moral and professional contexts. Contrasts with *vaññam* (deceit) and *kāpaṭyam* (hypocrisy).

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** உண்மையான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāna
**Literal meaning:** *uṇmai* + adjectival copular suffix *-āna*
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic

Adjectival form of *uṇmai*; used attributively to evaluate objects, persons, feelings, or statements as genuine or authentic. Extremely frequent in both spoken and written Tamil. Contrasts with *poyyana* (false).

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** சரி
**Transliteration:** cari
**Literal meaning:** correctness, evenness, balance (also used as interjection)
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper

Used to evaluate statements, calculations, actions, or conduct as correct or appropriate. Functions both as a predicate adjective and as a discourse particle confirming accuracy. Productive: *cariyāna* (correct, proper), *caripār* (to check, verify).

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** தவறு
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu
**Literal meaning:** deviation, going astray; from *tavar-* (to err, deviate)
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, wrong, fault

Used to evaluate statements, actions, or conduct as incorrect or morally wrong. Appears in both epistemic contexts (factual error) and moral contexts (wrongdoing). Productive: *tavaṟāna* (wrong, erroneous), *tavaṟu ceytavar* (one who has done wrong).

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** நம்பகமான
**Transliteration:** nampakamāna
**Literal meaning:** *nampakam* (trustworthiness, from *nampu* "to trust/believe") + *-āna*
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable

Used to evaluate persons, sources, institutions, or information with respect to reliability and trustworthiness. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The base noun *nampakam* also occurs independently.

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** நம்பகத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** nampakattaṉmai
**Literal meaning:** *nampakam* (trustworthiness) + *taṉmai* (quality, nature)
**Typical English translations:** credibility, reliability, trustworthiness

A more abstract nominal form used in formal, journalistic, and academic contexts to refer to the property of being credible or reliable. Slightly more formal register than *nampakamāna*. Appears in discussions of evidence, testimony, and institutional conduct.

**[CORE]**

**9.**
**Form:** உண்மைத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmaitaṉmai
**Literal meaning:** *uṇmai* (truth) + *taṉmai* (quality, nature)
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, authenticity, genuineness, veracity

Used in formal and philosophical contexts to refer to the property of being true or genuine. Less frequent than *uṇmai* itself but attested in literary criticism, philosophy, and formal prose. Evaluates both persons and representations.

**10.**
**Form:** நேர்த்தி
**Transliteration:** nēṟtti
**Literal meaning:** from *nēr* (straight, proper); neatness, propriety, correctness of form
**Typical English translations:** propriety, correctness, neatness, precision

Used to evaluate the formal correctness or proper execution of something, including language, craftsmanship, or conduct. More aesthetic and formal in flavor than *cari*. Appears in literary and craft-evaluation contexts.

**11.**
**Form:** கள்ளம்
**Transliteration:** kaḷḷam
**Literal meaning:** deceit, concealment (root *kaḷ-*, related to concealing or stealing)
**Typical English translations:** deceit, duplicity, treachery, guile

Used to evaluate persons or conduct as deceptive or treacherous. Appears in moral and interpersonal evaluative contexts. Contrasts with *nēṟmai*. The compound *kaḷḷamillāta* (without deceit, guileless) is also attested.

**12.**
**Form:** வஞ்சகம்
**Transliteration:** vañcakam
**Literal meaning:** deceit, betrayal; from *vañcu* (to deceive, betray)
**Typical English translations:** treachery, deceit, fraud, betrayal

Used in moral and legal contexts to evaluate conduct or persons as fraudulent or treacherous. Somewhat more formal and literary than *kaḷḷam*. Appears in classical Tamil literature and in legal Tamil.

**[CORE]**

**13.**
**Form:** போலி
**Transliteration:** pōli
**Literal meaning:** resemblance, imitation, counterfeit (from *pōl*, "like, resembling")
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, spurious

Used to evaluate objects, persons, or credentials as not genuine; a counterfeit or impostor. Extremely common in everyday speech and in consumer, legal, and political contexts. Productive: *pōli ṭākṭar* (fake doctor), *pōli āvaṇam* (forged document).

**[CORE]**

**14.**
**Form:** அசல்
**Transliteration:** acal
**Literal meaning:** original, principal (loanword from Arabic *aṣl*, "origin, root")
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic

Used to evaluate documents, objects, or persons as the genuine article rather than a copy or fake. Directly contrasts with *pōli* (fake) and *nakal* (copy). Very common in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** நகல்
**Transliteration:** nakal
**Literal meaning:** copy, duplicate (loanword from Arabic *naqal*, "copy, transfer")
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, transcript

Used in administrative and legal contexts to distinguish a reproduction from the original (*acal*). Evaluatively neutral in itself but relevant to authenticity assessments. Appears in fixed expressions such as *nakal சரி* (certified copy).

**[CORE]**

**16.**
**Form:** சான்று
**Transliteration:** cāṉṟu
**Literal meaning:** that which attests or certifies; from *cāṉṟ-* (to attest, certify, be witnessed)
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, testimony, certificate

Used in legal, epistemic, and evaluative contexts to refer to that which establishes the truth or validity of a claim. Productive: *cāṉṟitam* (certificate), *cāṉṟu peyar* (to certify). Appears in both classical and modern Tamil.

**[CORE]**

**17.**
**Form:** ஆதாரம்
**Transliteration:** ātāram
**Literal meaning:** support, foundation, basis (loanword from Sanskrit *ādhāra*, "support, basis")
**Typical English translations:** evidence, basis, proof, grounds

Used to evaluate whether a claim, action, or position has adequate support or grounding. Common in legal, journalistic, and everyday argumentative contexts. Often appears in the phrase *ātāram illāmal* (without basis/evidence).

**18.**
**Form:** நியாயம்
**Transliteration:** niyāyam
**Literal meaning:** justice, right reasoning, propriety (from Sanskrit *nyāya*, "rule, logic, justice")
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, justification, logic

Used to evaluate conduct, decisions, or arguments as just, fair, or logically sound. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Productive: *niyāyamāna* (just, fair, reasonable), *niyāyam keḷ* (to ask for justice).

**Transliteration:** nēṟmaiyāna

**Typical English translations:** honest, upright, sincere, straightforward

Adjectival form of *nēṟmai*; used to evaluate persons, conduct, or statements as honest and without deception. Extremely common in everyday and formal evaluative contexts. Contrasts with *kaḷḷamāna* (deceitful) and *vañcakamāna* (treacherous).

**20.**
**Form:** உண்மையில்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil
**Literal meaning:** *uṇmai* + locative suffix *-il*; "in truth"
**Typical English translations:** in truth, actually, in reality, truly

A fixed adverbial expression used to assert that what follows is the actual state of affairs, often correcting a misapprehension. Functions as an epistemic marker in discourse. Extremely common in spoken and written Tamil.

**21.**
**Form:** உண்மையிலேயே
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyilēyē
**Literal meaning:** *uṇmaiyil* + emphatic particles *-ē* + *-yē*
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in actual fact

An emphatic form of *uṇmaiyil*, used to stress the genuineness or reality of a state of affairs. Common in spoken Tamil and informal written registers. Marks stronger epistemic commitment than *uṇmaiyil*.

**22.**

**Transliteration:** kapaṭam
**Literal meaning:** deceit, hypocrisy, pretense (from Sanskrit *kapaṭa*, "fraud, deceit")
**Typical English translations:** hypocrisy, pretense, deceit, duplicity

Used to evaluate persons or conduct as hypocritical or pretentious, involving a discrepancy between outward appearance and inner reality. Appears in moral and literary contexts. Related: *kapaṭi* (hypocrite, deceiver).

**23.**
**Form:** மெய்
**Transliteration:** mey
**Literal meaning:** body, substance, that which is real; body as the seat of reality
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the real, body

A classical and literary term for truth or reality, often used in philosophical and poetic contexts. Appears in classical Tamil literature (*Tirukkuṟaḷ* and others) and in formal philosophical discourse. Less common in everyday modern speech than *uṇmai*.

**Typical English translations:** purity, cleanliness, integrity, genuineness

Used to evaluate persons, conduct, or objects as pure, uncontaminated, or morally unblemished. In evaluative contexts, it can carry connotations of moral integrity and authenticity. Appears in moral, religious, and aesthetic contexts.

**Transliteration:** caripār
**Literal meaning:** *cari* (correct) + *pār* (to see, look); "to look for correctness"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to check, to confirm, to validate

A verb used to describe the act of checking or verifying whether something is correct or accurate. Common in everyday, administrative, and technical contexts. Relevant to processes of epistemic evaluation and fact-checking.

**27.**
**Form:** உறுதிப்படுத்து
**Transliteration:** uṟutippaṭuttu
**Literal meaning:** *uṟuti* (firmness, certainty) + *paṭuttu* (causative: to make/cause); "to make certain"
**Typical English translations:** to confirm, to verify, to certify, to establish

Used to describe the act of establishing or confirming the truth, accuracy, or validity of something. Common in formal, legal, journalistic, and administrative contexts. Evaluates the epistemic status of claims or facts.

**28.**
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை
**Transliteration:** nampikkai
**Literal meaning:** *nampu* (to trust, believe) + *-ikkai* (nominal suffix); "the act/state of trusting"
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence

Used to evaluate the relationship between a person and a source, claim, or institution with respect to reliability and credibility. Appears in moral, religious, legal, and everyday contexts. Productive: *nampikkaiyāna* (trustworthy), *nampikkai iḻa* (to lose trust).

**[CORE]**

**29.**
**Form:** நேர்
**Transliteration:** nēr
**Literal meaning:** straight, direct, even, proper
**Typical English translations:** straight, direct, correct, proper, honest

Base form from which *nēṟmai* and *nēṟtti* derive; used independently as an adjective or adverb to evaluate conduct, speech, or direction as direct and without deviation. Appears in compounds and fixed expressions evaluating honesty and correctness.

**30.**
**Form:** உண்மைக்கு புறம்பான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaikkup puṟampāna
**Literal meaning:** *uṇmai* (truth) + dative *-kku* + *puṟam* (outside, exterior) + *-pāna* (adjectival suffix); "that which is outside of truth"
**Typical English translations:** contrary to truth, false, untrue, against the facts

A fixed evaluative expression used in formal and legal Tamil to characterize statements or representations as contrary to the truth. More formal and explicit than *poy* alone. Attested in legal and administrative registers.
