# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + stative verb/adjective (*tọ́*, *gidi*, *dára*) OR Subject + copula *jẹ́* + nominal predicate (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*, *òdodo*)

### Examples
- **Ó tọ́** → *ó tọ́* → "It is correct / it is right"
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Your statement is the truth"

### Notes
The root *tọ́* functions directly as a stative predicate without a copula; *jẹ́* is used when the predicate is a full nominal. Both patterns are productive across the truth-domain vocabulary.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *kò* (negation) + stative verb/adjective OR Subject + *kò jẹ́* + nominal predicate

### Examples
- **Kò tọ́** → *kò tọ́* → "It is not correct / it is wrong"
- **Kò jẹ́ gidi** → *kò jẹ́ gidi* → "It is not genuine / it is not authentic"

### Notes
*kò* is the standard negation marker for stative and copular predicates; it precedes the predicate directly. The pattern is fully parallel to the affirmative construction.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Head noun + modifier (post-nominal position): N + *tọ́* / *rere* / *burúkú* / *gidi* / *ńlá*

### Examples
- **Ìdáhùn tọ́** → *ìdáhùn tọ́* → "Correct answer"
- **Ìwà rere** → *ìwà rere* → "Good character / virtue"

### Notes
Yoruba adjectives and evaluative modifiers follow the noun they modify. The modifier slot is productive: *tọ́*, *rere*, *burúkú*, *gidi*, *ńlá* (great), *jinlẹ̀* (deep) all occupy this position with truth-domain nouns.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*Di* (become) + nominal predicate OR directional motion construction (*padà sí*, *jáde kúrò nínú*) + nominal

### Examples
- **Padà sí ìgbàgbọ́** → *padà sí ìgbàgbọ́* → "Return to faith / come back to belief"
- **Jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀** → *jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀* → "Come out of ignorance / leave ignorance behind"

### Notes
Yoruba does not have a dedicated inchoative morpheme. Change-of-state meaning is expressed through motion verbs (*padà* "return", *jáde kúrò* "come out from") with directional complements, or through *di* (become) + nominal. The motion-based pattern is more frequent in the truth domain.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
*Jẹ́ kí* (let/cause) + NP subject + predicate OR instrumental *fi* + NP + verb

### Examples
- **Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ** → *jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ* → "Let truth be your guide / cause truth to lead you"
- **Fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀** → *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀* → "Speak with truth / speak truthfully" (use truth as instrument of speech)

### Notes
Yoruba lacks a productive causative morpheme in this domain. Causative meaning is expressed periphrastically via *jẹ́ kí* (permissive-causative) or via the instrumental serial verb *fi* (use/employ). The *fi* construction encodes manner rather than strict causation.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ì-* prefix + verbal/adjectival root → abstract noun; also *à-* prefix + root; *o-/ọ-* prefix + root

### Examples
- **Ìmọ̀** → *ìmọ̀* → "Knowledge" (from *mọ̀* "to know")
- **Àtúntọ́** → *àtúntọ́* → "Correction / rectification" (from *tún* "re-" + *tọ́* "correct")

### Notes
The *ì-* prefix is the most productive nominalizer in the truth domain, yielding *ìmọ̀*, *ìgbàgbọ́*, *ìdánwò*, *ìjẹ́rìísí*, *ìtànjẹ́*, etc. The *à-* prefix appears in *àṣìṣe*, *àìmọ̀*, *àtúntọ́*. The *o-/ọ-* prefix appears in *òtítọ́*, *òdodo*. All three yield abstract nouns; the choice of prefix is lexically conditioned rather than semantically contrastive.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*a-* prefix + verb root → agent noun (habitual/characterizing); *olù-* prefix + verb root → agent noun (role/function); *al-* / *ẹ-* prefix + noun → possessive agent

### Examples
- **Aṣírọ́** → *aṣírọ́* → "Liar / one who tells lies" (from *ṣ-* "do" + *irọ́* "lie")
- **Adájọ́** → *adájọ́* → "Judge / one who adjudicates" (from *dájọ́* "pass judgment")

### Notes
Three agentive prefixes are attested: *a-* (habitual agent, often with moral valence: *aṣírọ́*, *aṣìṣe*, *aṣètànjẹ́*), *olù-* (role agent: *olùdánwò* "examiner", *olùtọ́ni* "guide"), and *ẹ-/al-* (possessive agent: *ẹlẹ́rìí* "witness", *olòtítọ́* "truthful person"). The *a-* prefix tends to appear in morally evaluative terms; *olù-* in functional role terms.

---

## 8. FOCUS / EMPHATIC IDENTIFICATION

### Pattern
Predicate nominal or focused constituent + focus marker *ni* (copular-focus); or *ló* (= *ni* + *o*) on focused subject

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ ni** → *òtítọ́ ni* → "It is the truth / that is true" (emphatic)
- **Ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là** → *ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là* → "It is knowledge that saves a person" (focus on *ìmọ̀*)

### Notes
The focus construction is highly productive in truth-domain assertions and proverbs. *ni* follows the focused predicate in identificational sentences; *ló* (subject-focus) marks the focused NP as the unique or contrastive answer to an implicit question. This construction is not derivational but is a primary syntactic strategy for asserting truth-domain claims emphatically.

---

## 9. PROPOSITIONAL COMPLEMENT

### Pattern
Epistemic/evidential verb + *pé* (complementizer) + embedded clause

### Examples
- **Mọ̀ pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *mọ̀ pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Know that it is the truth"
- **Jẹ́rìí pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** → *jẹ́rìí pé ó jẹ́ òtítọ́* → "Testify that it is true / bear witness that it is the truth"

### Notes
*pé* is the standard complementizer introducing propositional content after epistemic verbs (*mọ̀* "know"), evidential verbs (*jẹ́rìí* "testify"), and evaluative predicates (*ó tọ́ pé* "it is right that"). This construction is the primary means of expressing propositional knowledge and testimony in Yoruba.

---

## 10. EXISTENTIAL ASSERTION / DENIAL OF EVIDENCE OR CERTAINTY

### Pattern
Noun phrase + *wà* (exist / there is) OR Noun phrase + *kò sí* (does not exist / there is no)

### Examples
- **Ẹ̀rí kò sí** → *ẹ̀rí kò sí* → "There is no evidence / proof is absent"
- **Ìdánilójú wà** → *ìdánilójú wà* → "There is assurance / confirmation exists"

### Notes
The existential pair *wà* / *kò sí* is a productive construction in legal and evaluative discourse for asserting the presence or absence of evidence, certainty, or verification. It is distinct from the attributive pattern (§1–2) in that it makes an existential rather than predicative claim.
