# G1 — Cross-Language Summary of Observed Semantic Organization

---

## 1. Language Inventory

- **ka — Georgian**: Agglutinative language with rich derivational morphology; truth vocabulary organized around several distinct roots (mart'l-, č'ešmarit'-, namdvil-, ṭq'uil-) with extensive semantic extension into legal, religious, and administrative domains.
- **yo — Yoruba**: Tonal language with productive nominal and agentive derivation; truth vocabulary centers on tọ́ (straight/correct), òtítọ́ (truth), irọ́ (lie), and òdodo (righteousness), with strong integration of moral and factual evaluation.
- **fi — Finnish**: Agglutinative language with highly productive suffixation; truth vocabulary built around tosi/tode- (true/real), oikea (right/correct), väärä (wrong/false), and valhe (lie), with extensive compound formation and grammaticalized discourse markers.
- **eu — Basque**: Agglutinative language with ergative morphology; truth vocabulary centers on egia (truth), gezur (lie), zuzen (straight/correct), and benetako (real/genuine), with productive relational suffixation and notable gaps in causative and agentive derivation.
- **ta — Tamil**: Agglutinative Dravidian language with two major truth roots (uṇmai, mey) and a primary falsehood root (poy), showing register stratification between colloquial and literary/philosophical usage, with extensive compound formation.
- **id — Indonesian**: Isolating language with productive affixation (meN-, ke-...-an, peN-...-an); truth vocabulary centers on benar (true/correct), salah (wrong), jujur (honest), palsu (fake), and asli (genuine), with extensive light verb constructions.
- **tr — Turkish**: Agglutinative language with productive suffixation; truth vocabulary centers on doğru (true/correct/straight), gerçek (real/genuine), hak (right/truth/due), yanlış (wrong), and sahte (fake), with notable polysemy in hak and doğru.
- **ko — Korean**: Agglutinative language with Sino-Korean and native vocabulary strata; truth vocabulary includes jinsil (truth), geojit (falsehood), sasil (fact), jinjja (real), gajja (fake), and sil- (real/actual) compounds, with strong asymmetry between positive and negative agentive forms.

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### Georgian (ka)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Georgian maintains several distinct truth-related roots that partially overlap in usage. The mart'l- root covers correctness, moral rightness, and extends into legal/administrative domains (samamart'ali "law," mart'va "management"). The č'ešmarit'i root emphasizes genuineness and authenticity, appearing frequently in religious and elevated discourse. The namdvil- root focuses on ontological reality and authenticity. The ṭq'uil- root covers falsehood and deception. The sando root covers reliability/trustworthiness as a distinct category. Borrowed terms (realuri, obiekṭuri, subiekṭuri, pakṭi) occupy formal/technical registers.

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The mart'l- root shows exceptional productivity, extending from correctness into management (mart'va), law (samart'ali), orthography (mart'lc'era), and Orthodox Christianity (mart'lmadidebeli). The ṭq'uil- root generates extensive deception vocabulary including reflexive (tviṭmoṭq'ueba "self-deception") and multiple aspectual variants (moṭq'ueba, gaṭq'ueba, šeṭq'ueba). Negative forms use u- prefix (uṭq'uari "truthful," umart'ebulo "incorrect"). Abstract nouns use -oba or -eba suffixes productively.

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Light verb constructions with verbs of speaking (t'qva "say," ambobs "tells") are central. Spatial metaphors for epistemic distance (simart'lestan axlos/shors "close to/far from truth") are attested. Revelation constructions (gamovlinda "was revealed," gamoač'ina "revealed") are productive. No dedicated causative construction for "making true" is identified; the language prefers revelation/verification constructions.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: simart'le/ṭq'uili (truth/lie), ṭq'uili/uṭq'uari (liar/truthful), ṭq'uili/namdvili (false/real). Morphological oppositions using u- (negative) and -iani (possessive). Constructional oppositions: salata/paljastua (conceal/reveal), axlos/shors (close/far from truth).

### Yoruba (yo)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Yoruba organizes truth vocabulary around tọ́ (to be straight/correct), which covers both factual accuracy and moral rightness. The noun òtítọ́ (truth) derives from this root. Falsehood is covered by irọ́ (lie) and purọ́ (to lie). Moral rightness has a distinct term òdodo (righteousness/justice), which is paired with òtítọ́ in fixed binomials. Belief/trust is covered by gbàgbọ́. Genuineness uses gidi and jẹ́jẹ́. The distinction between factual accuracy and moral rightness is less sharply lexicalized than in some other languages, with tọ́ serving both functions.

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The -garri suffix (worthy of) productively creates evaluative adjectives: sinesgarria (believable), fidagarria (reliable), zalantzagarria (doubtful). Agentive forms use oní- prefix (olótítọ́ "truthful person," olódodo "righteous person"). Negative agentives use oní-àì- (aláìgbàgbọ́ "unbeliever"). The verbal system uses serial verb constructions productively (sọ...jáde "tell...out" = reveal). Reduplication intensifies (gidi gidi "very genuine," tọ́tọ́ "very correct").

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Serial verb constructions are a distinctive feature (wá...rí "seek...find"). Focus constructions with ni are primary for contrastive emphasis (Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ "It is truth I spoke"). The causative uses mú (Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ "He made me believe"). No morphological causative for truth-domain terms is identified; light verb constructions dominate.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: òtítọ́/irọ́ (truth/lie), tọ́/kò tọ́ (correct/not correct), ìwà rere/ìwà búburú (good character/bad character). Morphological negation uses àì- prefix (àìtọ́ "incorrectness," àìgbàgbọ́ "unbelief"). Scalar oppositions: irọ́ kékeré/irọ́ ńlá (small lie/big lie).

### Finnish (fi)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Finnish organizes truth vocabulary around tosi/tode- (true/real), oikea (right/correct/genuine), väärä (wrong/false), and valhe (lie). The tosi root shows extensive polysemy: it covers propositional truth, ontological reality (tosielämä "real life"), and functions as a colloquial intensifier (tosi hyvä "really good"). The oikea root covers correctness, direction (right vs. left), genuineness, and legal rights (oikeus). The väärä root covers factual error, moral wrong, and counterfeit. Borrowed terms (objektiivinen, subjektiivinen) occupy academic registers.

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The tode- root generates an exceptionally large derivational family including inchoative (toteutua, todellistua), causative (toteuttaa, todellistaa), and verification verbs (todistaa, todentaa, todeta). The -uus/-yys suffix productively creates abstract nouns (totuus, rehellisyys, luotettavuus). The epä- prefix creates systematic negations (epätosi, epärehellinen, epäluotettava). The vale- prefix creates counterfeit compounds (valepassi, valenimi). The tosi- prefix creates reality compounds (tositarina, tosielämä). The -tön privative suffix creates absence adjectives (virheetön "error-free," vilpitön "sincere").

**Constructional patterns and absences:** The fixed expression pitää paikkansa ("holds its place" = is true) is a distinctive idiom. Discourse markers (sebenarnya, totta puhuen) are grammaticalized from truth roots. The language shows a gap: no natural expression for propositions "becoming true" as state change; Finnish prefers revelation/verification constructions (osoittautua todeksi "prove to be true," paljastua "be revealed"). Translative case (-ksi) marks result states in causative constructions (todistaa oikeaksi "prove right," todistaa vääräksi "prove wrong").

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: tosi/valhe (true/lie), oikea/väärä (right/wrong), aito/väärennös (genuine/forgery). Morphological oppositions using epä- (epätosi, epärehellinen) and -tön (virheetön/virheellinen). Constructional oppositions: pitää paikkansa/ei pidä paikkaansa (is true/is not true), paljastaa/salata (reveal/conceal).

### Basque (eu)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Basque organizes truth vocabulary around egia (truth), gezur (lie), zuzen (straight/correct/direct), and benetako (real/genuine). The zuzen root shows extensive polysemy covering physical straightness, factual correctness, moral rightness, directness (zuzenean "live/directly"), and legal concepts (zuzenbide "law"). The egia root generates a large family of verification and authentication terms. The benetako/itxurazko (real/apparent) opposition covers the reality-appearance distinction. Borrowed terms (faltsua, erreala, fede) are integrated.

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The -zko/-ezko relational suffix productively creates adjectives (egiazkoa "true," gezurrezko "false"). The -tasun suffix creates abstract nouns (egiazkotasuna, zintzotasuna, zuzentasuna). The -garri suffix creates evaluative adjectives (fidagarria "reliable," sinesgarria "believable," frogagarria "provable"). The -tu verbalizer creates verification verbs (egiazkotu, egiaztatu, ziurtatu). Notable gaps: no productive causative for truth-domain terms (the morphological causative -arazi does not combine with truth roots); no simple verb *gezurtu "to lie" (requires light verb construction gezurra esan). Several positive abstract nominals lack negative counterparts (*gezurtasuna not attested).

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Light verb constructions are obligatory for lying (gezurra esan "tell a lie") and truth-telling (egia esan "tell the truth"). Genitive + postposition constructions are productive (egiaren bila "in search of truth," egiaren alde "on the side of truth"). The -etan locative plural creates idiomatic expressions (gezurretan harrapatu "catch in lies," gezurretan ibili "go about lying"). Causative gap is notable: no productive construction for "making X true."

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: egia/gezur (truth/lie), egiazkoa/gezurrezko (true/false), zuzena/okerra (correct/wrong), benetako/itxurazko (real/apparent). Morphological oppositions: -garri/-gaitz (worthy of/unable to be: sinesgarria/sinesgaitza). Several expected oppositions are absent or asymmetric: no dedicated lexical opposite for fidagarria (reliable), zintzoa (honest), or egiazkotasuna (truthfulness).

### Tamil (ta)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Tamil maintains two major truth roots with register stratification: uṇmai (everyday/formal truth) and mey (literary/philosophical truth/body). The poy root covers falsehood across registers. The cari/tappu pair covers correctness/error in everyday contexts. The nērmai root covers moral honesty/integrity as a distinct category. The sattiyam root (from Sanskrit satya) covers solemn/religious truth. The nijam root (colloquial, borrowed) covers everyday factual truth. The mey root shows systematic polysemy between "truth/reality" and "body/physical," generating a large family of non-truth-domain compounds (meyyeḻuttu "consonant," meycilirppu "goosebumps").

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The uṇmai root generates extensive compound vocabulary (uṇmainilai "true state," uṇmaiccāṉṟu "certificate of authenticity," uṇmaivāti "truth-teller"). The poy root generates parallel compounds (poynilai "false state," poyccāṉṟu "false certificate"). Agentive forms show asymmetry: poyyan "liar" (masculine) and poyyāḷ (gender-neutral) exist, but positive agentives use different morphology (uṇmaiyāḷar with honorific suffix). The -āka adverbializer is productive (uṇmaiyāka "truly," poyyāka "falsely," meyyāka "genuinely"). The causative uṇmaippaṭuttu "to verify" uses -paṭuttu, while the falsification causative poyyākku uses -ākku, showing morphological asymmetry.

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Light verb constructions with col (say), pēcu (speak), and kūṟu (state) are central for truth-telling. The existential construction (uṇmai uṇṭu/illai "there is/is no truth") treats truth as a substance that can be present or absent. The discourse marker uṇmaiyil ("in truth, actually") is highly grammaticalized. No productive construction for "making X true" in the sense of causing a proposition to acquire truth value; the language prefers verification/revelation constructions.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: uṇmai/poy (truth/falsehood), cari/tappu (correct/wrong), nērmai/kaḷḷam (honesty/deceit). Morphological oppositions: uṇmaiyāka/poyyāka (truthfully/falsely). Asymmetric oppositions: poy kaṇṇīr "crocodile tears" has no positive counterpart; poy nāṭakam "false drama" has no positive counterpart, reflecting a pattern where deceptive/insincere behavior receives lexical marking while genuine behavior is unmarked.

### Indonesian (id)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Indonesian organizes truth vocabulary around benar (true/correct), salah (wrong/mistaken), jujur (honest), palsu (fake), and asli (genuine). The benar/salah pair covers factual correctness/error. The jujur/bohong pair covers moral honesty/lying. The palsu/asli pair covers authenticity. The nyata root covers ontological reality. The tepat root covers precision/appropriateness. The sah/valid pair covers legal/formal validity. The salah root shows exceptional productivity in compounds (salah paham "misunderstand," salah sangka "misjudge," salah tulis "typo," salah kaprah "widespread error"), and also grammaticalizes as a quantifier (salah satu "one of").

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The meN-...-kan causative is highly productive across truth-domain vocabulary (membenarkan "confirm," menyalahkan "blame," memalsukan "falsify," mengesahkan "validate"). The ke-...-an nominalization creates abstract nouns (kebenaran "truth," kejujuran "honesty," kepalsuan "fakeness"). The peN-...-an nominalization creates process nouns (pembenaran "justification," pemalsuan "forgery"). The se-...-nya pattern creates adverbials (sebenarnya "actually," sejujurnya "honestly," sesungguhnya "truly"). Reduplication intensifies (benar-benar "really," sungguh-sungguh "earnestly"). The salah root grammaticalizes into a quantifier (salah satu "one of"), losing truth-domain semantics entirely.

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Zero copula is the unmarked present-tense predication pattern. The ternyata construction (ter- + nyata) is a grammaticalized evidential marker ("it turns out"). The menjadi construction is the standard inchoative (menjadi kenyataan "become reality"). Authenticity is treated as binary rather than gradable; no natural expression for "becoming more genuine" is attested. The kurang construction creates hedged negations (kurang benar "not quite correct") as a face-saving strategy.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: benar/salah (true/wrong), asli/palsu (genuine/fake), jujur/bohong (honest/lying). Morphological oppositions using tidak (tidak benar, tidak jujur) and epä- equivalent. Constructional oppositions: benar atau salah (true or false), asli atau palsu (genuine or fake). Several expected oppositions are absent: sejati (genuine) and hakiki (essential) lack dedicated antonyms.

### Turkish (tr)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Turkish organizes truth vocabulary around doğru (true/correct/straight/direct), gerçek (real/genuine), hak (right/truth/due/God), yanlış (wrong/false), and sahte (fake). The doğru root shows extensive polysemy covering propositional truth, physical direction, moral rightness, and directness (doğrudan "directly"). The hak root (from Arabic ḥaqq) covers truth, rights, deservingness, and divine names (Hak Teala "God Almighty"), showing the broadest semantic range of any single root. The gerçek root covers ontological reality and genuineness. Borrowed terms from Arabic (hakikat, sahih, meşru) and Persian (sahte) occupy formal and specialized registers.

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The -leş- inchoative suffix combines with truth roots (gerçekleşmek "come true," kesinleşmek "become definite"). The -leştir- causative extends this (gerçekleştirmek "realize/implement," kesinleştirmek "finalize"). The -lık/-lik suffix creates abstract nouns (doğruluk "truth/honesty," gerçeklik "reality," haklılık "rightness"). The -la- denominative creates verification verbs (doğrulamak "verify," yanlışlamak "falsify"). The hak root generates an unusually large family of fixed expressions (hak etmek "deserve," hak vermek "agree with," hak yemek "violate rights," hakkında "about/concerning"). The çıkmak construction (doğru çıkmak "turn out to be true," yanlış çıkmak "turn out to be wrong") is the preferred inchoative pattern for truth-domain terms.

**Constructional patterns and absences:** The çıkmak construction is the primary inchoative for truth revelation (doğru çıkmak, haklı çıkmak). Light verb constructions with etmek, olmak, vermek, and söylemek are central. The postposition hakkında (from hak) is fully grammaticalized for "about/concerning." Alliterative fixed collocations are productive (doğru dürüst, doğru düzgün, yalan dolan). The conditional -sa/-se vs. counterfactual -saydı distinction marks open vs. contrary-to-fact conditions.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: doğru/yanlış (true/wrong), gerçek/sahte (real/fake), haklı/haksız (right/unjust). Morphological oppositions using -sız privative (haksız, tutarsız, geçersiz, uygunsuz). The -lı possessive vs. -sız privative pair is highly productive. Constructional oppositions: doğru çıkmak/yanlış çıkmak (turn out true/false), gerçeği söylemek/gerçeği gizlemek (tell/hide truth).

### Korean (ko)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps:** Korean maintains a Sino-Korean/native vocabulary distinction in the truth domain. Sino-Korean terms (jinsil "truth," sasil "fact," jinjeong "genuine," jeongjik "honest") occupy formal registers, while native terms (jinjja "real," gajja "fake," matda "correct," teullida "wrong") are colloquial. The sil- (實) prefix generates a large family of "actual/real" compounds (silje "reality," silche "substance," siljil "essence," sillyeok "actual ability," siljeok "actual results"). The jin- (眞) prefix generates authenticity compounds (jinsim "sincere heart," jinui "true intention," jinga "true value," jinmyeonmok "true character"). The geojit root covers falsehood and generates extensive compounds (geojinmal "lie," geojitjeungeon "false testimony," geojitjeongbo "false information").

**Derivational and morphological asymmetries:** The -seong suffix creates abstract quality nouns (jinsilseong "truthfulness," sinbingseong "credibility," jinjeongseonng "authenticity"). Agentive forms show strong asymmetry: geojinmaljaengi "liar" is a productive agentive, but no corresponding positive agentive (*jinsiljaengi "truth-teller") is attested. The -투성이 suffix creates saturation expressions (geojittuseongi "full of lies") with no positive counterpart. The -배기 suffix creates emphatic forms (jinjjabaegi "the real deal," gajjabaegi "total fake"). The sil- compounds show a gap: failure terms (silpae "failure," silsu "mistake," silmang "disappointment") use the same sil- root but fall outside the truth-evaluation domain.

**Constructional patterns and absences:** Light verb constructions with malhada (say), balkhida (reveal), sumgida (hide), and hwaginhada (confirm) are central. The balkhyeojida/deureonada (be revealed/emerge) constructions are the primary patterns for truth revelation. Agentive nominalization is strongly asymmetric: the language marks habitual liars but not habitual truth-tellers. The discourse marker sasireun ("in fact, actually") is highly grammaticalized. No productive causative for "making X true" is identified.

**Oppositional structures:** Primary lexical oppositions: jinsil/geojit (truth/falsehood), jinjja/gajja (real/fake), matda/teullida (correct/wrong). Constructional oppositions: jinsireul balkhida/jinsireul sumgida (reveal/hide truth), jinsireul malhada/geojinmareul hada (tell truth/tell lie). Asymmetric oppositions: geojiseobsi "without lies" has no positive counterpart; geojittuseongi "full of lies" has no positive counterpart.

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring Convergences (patterns observed across many languages)

- **Straight/direct as a source domain for truth and correctness.** In Yoruba (tọ́ "straight"), Basque (zuzen "straight/correct"), Turkish (doğru "straight/correct/direct"), and Georgian (sc'ori "straight/correct"), the same physical concept of straightness or directness underlies terms for factual correctness and moral rightness. Finnish (oikea "right") and Indonesian (benar, from a root associated with correctness) show related patterns. This is one of the most widely distributed structural features in the data.

- **Separation of factual accuracy from moral honesty.** All eight languages maintain some distinction between terms for factual truth/correctness and terms for moral honesty/integrity, though the degree of lexical separation varies. Georgian distinguishes č'ešmarit'i (true/genuine) from uṭq'uari (truthful/honest). Yoruba distinguishes òtítọ́ (truth) from òdodo (righteousness). Finnish distinguishes tosi (true) from rehellinen (honest). Basque distinguishes egia (truth) from zintzo (honest). Tamil distinguishes uṇmai (truth) from nērmai (honesty). Indonesian distinguishes benar (correct) from jujur (honest). Turkish distinguishes doğru (correct) from dürüst (honest). Korean distinguishes jinsil (truth) from jeongjik (honest). In all cases, the moral honesty term focuses on character and habitual conduct rather than propositional accuracy.

- **Authenticity/genuineness as a distinct semantic area from propositional truth.** All languages maintain vocabulary for distinguishing genuine from fake objects, identities, or representations, typically using different terms from those used for propositional truth. Georgian uses namdvili (real/genuine) vs. q'albi (fake). Yoruba uses gidi (genuine) vs. irọ́ ìṣe (deceptive action). Finnish uses aito (genuine) vs. väärennös (forgery). Basque uses benetako (real) vs. faltsua (fake). Tamil uses acal (genuine) vs. poy (fake). Indonesian uses asli (genuine) vs. palsu (fake). Turkish uses gerçek/asıl (real/original) vs. sahte (fake). Korean uses jinjja (real) vs. gajja (fake).

- **Light verb constructions as the primary pattern for truth-related speech acts.** Across all languages, the act of telling the truth or lying is expressed through light verb constructions combining a truth/falsehood noun with a speech verb, rather than through a single dedicated verb. Georgian: simart'le t'qva (truth + say). Yoruba: sọ òtítọ́ (say + truth), pa irọ́ (do + lie). Finnish: puhua totuutta (speak + truth), puhua valhetta (speak + lie). Basque: egia esan (truth + say), gezurra esan (lie + say). Tamil: uṇmai col (truth + say), poy col (lie + say). Indonesian: mengatakan kebenaran (say + truth). Turkish: doğruyu söylemek (truth + say), yalan söylemek (lie + say). Korean: jinsireul malhada (truth + say), geojinmareul hada (lie + do).

- **Revelation and concealment as a productive constructional pair.** All languages show productive constructions for truth being revealed or hidden, typically using different verbs with the same truth noun. Georgian: gamovlinda (was revealed) vs. damala (hid). Yoruba: fi òtítọ́ hàn (show truth) vs. ṣe àṣírí (keep secret). Finnish: paljastaa totuus (reveal truth) vs. salata totuus (conceal truth). Basque: egia atera (bring out truth) vs. egia ezkutatu (hide truth). Tamil: uṇmai veḷippaṭu (truth comes out) vs. uṇmaiyai maṟai (hide truth). Indonesian: mengungkap kebenaran (reveal truth) vs. menyembunyikan kebenaran (hide truth). Turkish: gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak (bring truth to light) vs. gerçeği gizlemek (hide truth). Korean: jinsireul balkhida (reveal truth) vs. jinsireul sumgida (hide truth).

- **Absence of productive causative constructions for "making X true."** Across all eight languages, no productive morphological or light verb causative construction for causing a proposition to acquire truth value is identified. All languages prefer constructions involving verification, revelation, or confirmation (establishing that something is true) rather than causation of truth. This gap is explicitly noted in Georgian, Basque, Finnish, Tamil, and Indonesian data, and is consistent across the remaining languages.

- **Discourse markers grammaticalized from truth roots.** All languages show at least one highly frequent discourse marker derived from a truth root that introduces corrections, clarifications, or frank statements. Georgian: mart'la (really, colloquial). Yoruba: ní òtítọ́ (in truth). Finnish: sebenarnya (actually), totta puhuen (truthfully speaking). Basque: egia esan (truth be told), benetan (really). Tamil: uṇmaiyil (in truth, actually). Indonesian: sebenarnya (actually), sejujurnya (honestly). Turkish: doğrusu (to tell the truth), aslında (actually). Korean: sasireun (in fact, actually).

### Major Divergences (patterns observed in few or one language)

- **Exceptional root polysemy spanning truth, rights, and divine names.** Turkish hak (from Arabic ḥaqq) uniquely covers truth, moral rightness, legal rights, deservingness, and divine names (Hak Teala "God Almighty") within a single root. This breadth of polysemy is not matched by any single root in the other seven languages. The hak root generates fixed expressions spanning moral (hak etmek "deserve"), legal (hak sahibi "rights holder"), interpersonal (hak vermek "agree with"), and religious (hakkın rahmetine kavuşmak "to die") domains.

- **Systematic register stratification between two parallel truth roots.** Tamil uniquely maintains two parallel truth roots (uṇmai and mey) with systematic register stratification: uṇmai is the everyday/formal term while mey is literary/philosophical. Both generate parallel derivational families (uṇmaiyāka/meyyāka "truly," uṇmaiyaṟivu/meyyaṟivu "true knowledge"). No other language in the dataset shows this degree of systematic register-based doubling of a core truth root.

- **Grammaticalization of a truth root into a directional/spatial postposition.** Turkish doğru functions as a postposition meaning "toward" (eve doğru "toward home"), and Georgian mart'l- extends into management/governance (mart'va "management," mart'avs "manages"). While several languages show truth roots extending into spatial/directional domains (Yoruba tọ́ "straight," Basque zuzen "direct"), Turkish shows the most complete grammaticalization of a truth root into a postpositional function.

- **Extensive semantic extension of a truth root into administrative and organizational domains.** Georgian mart'l- uniquely extends into management (mart'va "management," mart'uli "managed"), governance (mart'avs "governs"), and organizational contexts (gamamart'uli "well-organized event"). This administrative extension of a truth/correctness root is not matched in the other languages.

- **Productive tosi- and vale- compound prefixes for reality vs. counterfeit.** Finnish uniquely shows two productive compound-forming prefixes derived from truth roots: tosi- (marking factual/authentic: tositarina "true story," tosielämä "real life," tosi-tv "reality TV") and vale- (marking counterfeit/false: valepassi "fake passport," valenimi "pseudonym"). While other languages form similar compounds, Finnish shows the most systematic and productive use of these prefixes.

- **Exceptional productivity of the salah root in Indonesian.** Indonesian salah (wrong) shows unusual productivity in forming compounds for specific error types (salah paham "misunderstand," salah sangka "misjudge," salah tafsir "misinterpret," salah ucap "slip of tongue," salah tulis "typo," salah hitung "miscalculation," salah lihat "see wrongly," salah dengar "mishear," salah langkah "wrong move," salah jalan "wrong path," salah alamat "wrong address," salah urus "mismanagement," salah guna "misuse"). Additionally, salah grammaticalizes as a quantifier (salah satu "one of"), losing truth-domain semantics entirely. This combination of domain-specific compound productivity and grammaticalization is not matched in other languages.

- **Systematic sil- (實) compound family for actual vs. nominal status.** Korean uniquely shows a large productive family of sil- (實) compounds distinguishing actual from nominal status across multiple domains: sillyeok (actual ability), siljeok (actual results), silhyo (actual effect), silje (actual reality), silche (actual substance), siljil (actual quality), silcheon (actual practice), silhaeng (actual execution), silhyeon (actual realization), silmul (actual object), silgam (sense of reality). While other languages have terms for this distinction, Korean shows the most systematic and productive compound family for it.

### Asymmetries, Gaps, and Irregularities

- **Asymmetric agentive nominalization: liars are marked, truth-tellers are not.** Across multiple languages, agentive forms for habitual liars are more lexicalized and productive than corresponding forms for habitual truth-tellers. Georgian: maṭq'uara "liar" (productive) vs. no standard agentive for "truth-teller." Yoruba: apurọ́ "liar" (productive) vs. olótítọ́ "truthful person" (less common). Finnish: no dedicated agentive for either, but negative character is more marked. Basque: gezurtia "liar" (productive) vs. egiazale "truth-lover" (less common, different morphology). Tamil: poyyan "liar" (masculine agentive) vs. uṇmaiyāḷar "truthful person" (honorific, different suffix). Indonesian: pembohong "liar" (productive) vs. no standard agentive for "truth-teller." Turkish: yalancı "liar" (lexicalized) vs. no standard agentive for "truth-teller." Korean: geojinmaljaengi "liar" (productive) vs. no attested *jinsiljaengi. This asymmetry is consistent across all eight languages.

- **Asymmetric abstract nominalization: negative abstract nouns are less productive than positive ones.** Several languages show gaps where positive abstract nouns (truthfulness, honesty) are well-attested but corresponding negative abstract nouns (falseness, dishonesty) are less productive or unattested. Basque: egiazkotasuna "truthfulness" is attested but *gezurtasuna "falseness" is not. Finnish: totuus "truth" is highly productive but no direct abstract nominal for "falseness" parallels it. Korean: jinsilseong "truthfulness" is productive but negative counterparts are less systematic. This pattern is less consistent than the agentive asymmetry but appears in multiple languages.

- **Asymmetric privative constructions: absence of falsehood is marked, absence of truth is not.** Several languages show productive constructions for "without lies/falsehood" but no corresponding construction for "without truth." Georgian: uṭq'uari "without lie" (truthful) is productive. Basque: gezurrezko "false" is productive but no *egiazko-gabe "without truth." Korean: geojiseobsi "without lies" is attested but *jinsilseobsi "without truth" is not. Indonesian: tanpa kebohongan "without lies" is possible but tanpa kebenaran "without truth" is less natural. This asymmetry reflects a pattern where deceptive behavior is marked as an absence of something expected, while truthful behavior is the unmarked default.

- **Grammaticalization of truth roots into non-truth domains.** Several languages show truth roots extending into domains with no clear truth-evaluation function. Finnish tosi- extends into intensification (tosi hyvä "really good") and entertainment genres (tosi-tv "reality TV"). Georgian mart'l- extends into management and governance. Turkish doğru extends into spatial direction (postposition "toward"). Korean sil- extends into failure (silpae "failure," silsu "mistake," silmang "disappointment") and spatial terms (silnae "indoor," siloe "outdoor"). Tamil mey extends into body/physical domain (meyyeḻuttu "consonant," meycilirppu "goosebumps"). These extensions are language-specific and do not form a cross-linguistic pattern.

- **The reality-appearance distinction is lexicalized differently across languages.** While all languages distinguish actual reality from mere appearance, the lexical resources differ substantially. Finnish uses todellinen/näennäinen (real/apparent). Basque uses benetako/itxurazko (real/apparent). Turkish uses gerçek/görünür (real/apparent) and aslında (actually). Indonesian uses nyata/semu (real/apparent) and sebenarnya (actually). Korean uses silje/gyeommo (reality/appearance) and sasireun (actually). Georgian uses namdvili/itxurazko (real/apparent). The grammaticalized discourse markers (sebenarnya, aslında, sasireun, etc.) all derive from truth/reality roots and serve to introduce corrections of apparent vs. actual states, but the specific morphological paths differ.

- **Scalar and gradable truth expressions are rare and marginal across languages.** All languages treat core truth terms (true/false, real/fake) as categorical rather than gradable. Scalar expressions (half-true, more true, very true) are attested but marked as marginal or unusual in multiple languages. Georgian: setengah benar "half true" is noted. Finnish: explicitly notes that truth is treated as binary. Basque: egia-maila "truth-level" is marked as marginal. Korean: explicitly notes that truth-domain terms rarely appear in degree comparisons. Indonesian: setengah benar "half true" is attested but marked. This cross-linguistic tendency to treat truth as categorical rather than scalar is one of the most consistent patterns in the data.

- **Verification and authentication vocabulary is more elaborated than falsification vocabulary.** Across languages, the vocabulary for establishing, confirming, or verifying truth tends to be more morphologically elaborated than the vocabulary for creating or establishing falsehood. Georgian: extensive verification vocabulary (damoc'meba, daadast'ura, egiazkotu) vs. limited falsification vocabulary. Finnish: extensive verification family (todistaa, todentaa, todeta, todistus, todistaja, todistustaakka) vs. limited falsification vocabulary. Basque: extensive verification vocabulary (egiazkotu, egiaztatu, frogatu, ziurtatu) vs. no productive *gezurtu. Tamil: uṇmaippaṭuttu "verify" vs. poyyākku "falsify" (marked as less common). This asymmetry may reflect the institutional contexts (legal, administrative, scientific) that drive elaboration of verification vocabulary.
