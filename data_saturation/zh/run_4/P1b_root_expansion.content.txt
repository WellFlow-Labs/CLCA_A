# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**
**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 participation:** 8 items — 真 (#1), 真实 (#3), 真相 (#8), 真正 (#11), 真诚 (#12), 真伪 (#17), 失真 (#21), 名副其实 (#19, partial)
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts correspondence between a thing and what it genuinely is; marks authenticity, factual accuracy, and sincerity across epistemic, moral, and ontological dimensions.

---

**Root 2**
**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 participation:** 9 items — 真实 (#3), 实质 (#10), 名副其实 (#19), 如实 (#20), 失真 (#21, via 真), 名实 (#28), 失实 (#30), 真相 (#8, indirect via 相/实pairing in usage), 如实 (#20)
**Note on count:** 实 appears directly in: 真实 (#3), 实质 (#10), 名副其实 (#19), 如实 (#20), 失实 (#30), 名实 (#28) — 6 direct participations; additionally it is the second morpheme in 真实 and the operative term in 如实 and 失实. Counted conservatively: **6 direct items**.
**Core meaning contribution:** Denotes substantive reality, factual actuality, and concrete existence; anchors claims to what is the case as opposed to what is nominal, apparent, or empty.

---

**Root 3**
**Form:** 假 (jiǎ) / 伪 (wěi)
**Note:** These two roots are closely related in the P1 data and often co-occur in compounds, but they are distinct morphemes. 假 appears in: 假 (#2), 虚假 (#4), 假冒 (implicit in #27), 真伪 (#17). 伪 appears in: 虚伪 (#13), 伪造 (#16), 真伪 (#17), 辨伪 (#29), 仿冒 (#27, partial). Each participates in approximately 4–5 items. Neither individually matches the frequency of 真 or 实. They are noted here as a paired secondary cluster but are **not selected as primary roots** for full expansion.

**Decision:** Proceed with full expansion of **真 (zhēn)** and **实 (shí)** as the two primary roots. A partial expansion of **假/伪** is appended as a supplementary root given their structural importance to the domain.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT A: 真 (zhēn)

*This root is highly productive. The 25 most common compounds and expressions are listed below; additional forms exist (e.g., technical coinages in optics, photography, and digital media).*

---

**A-01**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal breakdown:** true / genuine (simplex morpheme)
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
**Context of use:** Functions as a free morpheme in colloquial speech (这是真的 "this is real/true") and as a bound root in compounds. Appears across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-02**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** truthful, authentic, real, factual
**Context of use:** Evaluates whether accounts, depictions, or representations correspond to reality. Used in journalism, law, literary criticism, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #3; included here to anchor the expansion.)*

---

**A-03**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance/form
**Natural English translation:** the truth, the true facts, the real situation
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of affairs behind appearances; common in investigative journalism, legal proceedings, and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #8.)*

---

**A-04**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal breakdown:** true + upright/proper
**Natural English translation:** genuine, real, truly
**Context of use:** Emphasizes that something is genuinely what it claims to be, used attributively and adverbially across everyday and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #11.)*

---

**A-05**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true + sincere/honest
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Evaluates sincerity of intentions or expressions in interpersonal and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #12.)*

---

**A-06**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal breakdown:** true + false/counterfeit
**Natural English translation:** genuine vs. fake, authenticity vs. forgery
**Context of use:** Fixed binomial framing the question of authentication; used in art, document, and artifact evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #17.)*

---

**A-07**
**Form:** 失真
**Transliteration:** shīzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** lose + truth/fidelity
**Natural English translation:** distortion, loss of fidelity, inaccurate representation
**Context of use:** Used in audio/visual technical contexts and in evaluative contexts for media or historical representation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
*(Already in P1 #21.)*

---

**A-08**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal breakdown:** true + principle/reason
**Natural English translation:** truth (as a principle), universal truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, ideological, and religious discourse to refer to fundamental or universal truths (e.g., 追求真理 "pursue truth," 真理标准 "criterion of truth"). Distinct from 真相 in that it refers to abstract or universal propositions rather than specific facts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-09**
**Form:** 真知
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhī
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge
**Natural English translation:** genuine knowledge, true understanding
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and educational discourse to distinguish authentic understanding from superficial or mistaken knowledge (e.g., 真知灼见 "genuine insight and clear vision"). Often appears in the compound 真知灼见.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-10**
**Form:** 真知灼见
**Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge + bright/burning + insight/view
**Natural English translation:** genuine insight, penetrating understanding, real wisdom
**Context of use:** A four-character expression used to praise someone's perceptive and well-founded views; common in intellectual, journalistic, and evaluative writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-11**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ

**Natural English translation:** real vs. fake, genuine vs. counterfeit, true or false
**Context of use:** A binomial used to frame the question of authenticity or truth value in everyday and formal contexts (e.g., 分辨真假 "distinguish the real from the fake"). Slightly more colloquial than 真伪.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-12**

**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + nature/property (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, factual accuracy
**Context of use:** Abstract noun used in legal, journalistic, and academic contexts to refer to the property of being truthful or authentic (e.g., 核实真实性 "verify authenticity," 信息的真实性 "the veracity of the information").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-13**

**Transliteration:** zhēn píng shí jù
**Literal breakdown:** true + evidence/proof + solid/real + evidence/basis
**Natural English translation:** solid evidence, concrete proof, hard facts

---

**A-14**

---

**A-15**

**Context of use:** Used to evaluate the sincerity of a person's feelings or intentions in interpersonal and moral contexts (e.g., 真心话 "words from the heart," 真心实意 "genuine and sincere"). Overlaps with 真诚 but is slightly more colloquial.

---

**A-16**

**A-17**

---

**A-18**

**Natural English translation:** portrait, realistic depiction, photo shoot (in modern usage)
**Context of use:** In classical usage, refers to realistic portraiture or depiction. In modern usage, particularly in East Asian popular culture, refers to a photographic portrait session or photo book (写真集). Used in artistic and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-19**

**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious, to take seriously
**Context of use:** Describes an attitude of careful, earnest engagement with a task or matter (e.g., 认真工作 "work conscientiously," 认真对待 "take seriously"). One of the most common everyday uses of 真.

---

**A-20**
**Form:** 当真
**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** treat as + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to take seriously, to take as true, to mean it
**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to ask or assert whether something is being treated as genuine or serious (e.g., 你当真吗？"Are you serious?" 别当真 "Don't take it seriously").

---

**A-21**

**Natural English translation:** to be a stickler, to take things too literally, to nitpick
**Context of use:** Colloquial expression describing someone who insists on strict accuracy or takes things too literally; can be used approvingly (conscientious) or disapprovingly (pedantic).

---

**A-22**

**Transliteration:** zhēn shàn měi
**Literal breakdown:** true + good/virtuous + beautiful
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, and beauty; the true, the good, and the beautiful
**Context of use:** A philosophical and aesthetic triad used in educational, literary, and moral discourse to refer to the three fundamental values. Appears in discussions of aesthetics, ethics, and education.

---

**A-23**
**Form:** 纯真
**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** pure + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** pure, innocent, guileless, unspoiled
**Context of use:** Used to describe a person's character, especially a child's, as untainted and genuine; also used in aesthetic evaluation of art or expression as unaffected and sincere.

**A-24**

---

**A-25**

### ROOT C (Supplementary): 假 (jiǎ) / 伪 (wěi)

*These two roots are treated together as a supplementary expansion given their structural importance as the primary antonyms of 真 and 实 in the truth domain. Each root individually participates in fewer than 10 P1 items; the most common compounds are listed.*

---

**C-01**
**Form:** 假

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots 真, 实, 假, or 伪 in contexts where the root's contribution appears to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From 真 (zhēn):**

- **认真** (rènzhēn, A-19): The root 真 here participates in an expression primarily describing an attitude of earnestness or conscientiousness toward a task, rather than a judgment about correspondence to fact.

- **天真** (tiānzhēn, A-24): The root 真 here participates in an expression describing a quality of natural innocence or naivety, with evaluative valence that can be positive or negative depending on context and the age of the subject.

- **纯真** (chúnzhēn, A-23): The root 真 here participates in an expression describing purity of character or affect, used in aesthetic and moral evaluation of persons and artistic works.

- **逼真** (bīzhēn, A-17): The root 真 here participates in an aesthetic evaluation of the degree to which a representation approximates reality, functioning as a scalar degree term ("how close to real") rather than a binary truth judgment.

- **写真** (xiězhēn, A-18): The root 真 here participates in an expression that in modern usage refers to a photographic portrait session or photo book, a commercial and aesthetic genre, with the truth-correspondence dimension receding.

- **真善美** (zhēn shàn měi, A-22): The root 真 here appears as one term in a philosophical triad alongside goodness and beauty, where its relationship to the other two values raises questions about whether it functions as an epistemic, aesthetic, or axiological term.

- **真金不怕火炼** (A-25): The root 真 here participates in a proverbial expression where 真金 ("true gold") functions as a metaphor for genuine quality or moral integrity, extending beyond factual truth into evaluations of character and worth.

- **真人秀** (zhēnrén xiù, not listed above but noted): The root 真 here participates in the compound for "reality show" (真人 "real person" + 秀 "show"), where the claim to authenticity is itself a genre convention subject to contestation.

- **真值** (zhēnzhí, not listed above but noted): The root 真 here participates in a technical logical and mathematical term meaning "truth value," where it functions within formal systems rather than in natural-language truth evaluation.

---

**From 实 (shí):**

- **落实** (luòshí, B-18): The root 实 here participates in an expression primarily describing the concrete implementation of decisions or policies, where the operative meaning is practical execution rather than correspondence to fact.

- **充实** (chōngshí, B-28): The root 实 here participates in an expression describing substantive fullness or enrichment, applicable to content, arguments, and personal life, where the evaluative dimension is one of density or richness rather than truth.

- **扎实** (zhāshí, B-29): The root 实 here participates in an expression describing thoroughness and groundedness of skills or work, functioning as a quality evaluation rather than a truth-correspondence judgment.

- **务实** (wùshí, B-20): The root 实 here participates in an expression describing a pragmatic orientation toward practical affairs, where the contrast is with idealism or formalism rather than with falsity.

- **踏实** (tāshí, not listed above but noted): The root 实 here participates in an expression describing a feeling of psychological steadiness or groundedness, or a quality of reliable, careful work, where the truth-domain contribution is indirect.

- **实力** (shílì, not listed above but noted): The root 实 here participates in a compound meaning "actual strength" or "capability," where 实 marks the genuine as opposed to nominal nature of a capacity, functioning as an intensifier of authenticity rather than a truth predicate.

- **现实** (xiànshí, not listed above but noted): The root 实 here participates in a compound meaning "reality" or "realistic," used both as a noun (the real world) and as an evaluative adjective (pragmatic, not idealistic), where the boundary between ontological and normative uses is fluid.

- **实用** (shíyòng, not listed above but noted): The root 实 here participates in a compound meaning "practical" or "utilitarian," where the evaluative dimension concerns usefulness rather than truth.

---

**From 假 (jiǎ):**

- **假设** (jiǎshè, C-05) and **假如** (jiǎrú, C-06): The root 假 here participates in conditional and hypothetical constructions where it marks a supposition or counterfactual, functioning as a logical operator rather than a marker of deception or inauthenticity.

- **假期** (jiǎqī, not listed above but noted): The root 假 here participates in a compound meaning "holiday" or "vacation" (from a different etymological sense of 假 meaning "borrowed time" or "leave"), where the truth-domain meaning is entirely absent. This is a homograph/homophone situation: 假 (jiǎ, "false") and 假 (jià, "leave/vacation") are distinguished by tone in speech but share the same character in writing.

---

**From 伪 (wěi):**

- **伪装** (wěizhuāng, C-12): The root 伪 here participates in an expression describing disguise or camouflage, where the emphasis is on concealment of identity rather than on the truth value of a proposition.

- **伪科学** (wěi kēxué, C-14) and **伪命题** (wěi mìngtí, C-18): The root 伪 here functions as a prefix meaning "pseudo-" or "spurious," marking a category as failing to meet the genuine criteria of its claimed type, which extends the root's use from individual artifact authentication into categorical and disciplinary boundary-marking.
