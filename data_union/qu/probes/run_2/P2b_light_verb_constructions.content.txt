# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven constructions that are not reducible to the derivational morphology documented in P2a. Sources are the same as those cited in P1b (Cusihuamán 1976, Soto Ruiz 1976, Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua 2005, documented literary and legal corpora, and colonial-period Quechua texts where relevant). Constructions already documented as fixed collocations in P1b (e.g., *cheqa runa*, *cheqa simi*, *llulla simi*) are cross-referenced here rather than re-documented in full, since they were established there as conventionalized expressions. The present step focuses on constructions involving syntactic combination, periphrasis, and idiomatic extension beyond those already recorded.

A note on the boundary between derivation and construction: Quechua is a highly agglutinative language in which much of what other languages express through multi-word constructions is expressed through suffixation. This means that the inventory of multi-word constructions in the truth-evaluation domain is smaller relative to what would be found in, for example, a Romance language. This is not an absence of data but a structural feature of the language. Where the expected semantic content is expressed derivationally rather than constructionally, this is noted explicitly.

## BASE FORM: cheqa

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqata rimay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-ta rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth
5. **Usage notes:** *Cheqata rimay* uses the accusative-marked adverbial form of *cheqa* with the verb *rimay* (to speak), meaning "to speak truly" or "to speak what is true." This construction is distinct from *cheqanta rimay* (documented in P1), where *cheqan* is a possessed nominal ("its truth"); here *cheqata* functions adverbially. Both constructions are attested and appear to be used in overlapping contexts of moral and legal enjoinment to truthful speech, with *cheqanta rimay* being the more conventionalized fixed expression and *cheqata rimay* the more compositional variant.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta willay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-ta willay
4. **English translation:** to report what is truly the case, to tell what is actually true
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *cheqa* (true) with the nominalized stative *kasqan* (the fact of its being, what it actually is) in the accusative, followed by *willay* (to inform, to report). Used in legal and testimonial contexts to enjoin a witness to report the actual facts of a matter. The construction emphasizes the factual state of affairs rather than the speaker's sincerity.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanman hina rimay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-man hina rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak in accordance with what is actually the case, to speak as things truly are
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *cheqa kasqanman hina* (in accordance with what is truly the case) as an adverbial phrase modifying *rimay* (to speak). The postposition *-man hina* (in accordance with, in the manner of) creates a conformity construction: speech that conforms to the true state of affairs. Attested in formal and legal registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak saying it is true, to affirm as true while speaking
5. **Usage notes:** A converb construction using *nispa* (saying, having said — the converb of *niy*, to say) to embed the assertion of truth within the speech act. The construction characterizes speech as accompanied by an explicit claim of truth. Appears in narrative and testimonial contexts when describing how a statement was made.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Manan cheqachu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n cheqa-chu
4. **English translation:** it is not true, that is not correct, that is false
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative predication of *cheqa*, using the negative *mana* with the negative suffix *-chu* on the predicate. This is the syntactically regular negation of a predicate in Quechua; the construction is not idiomatic but is the expected grammatical form for denying truth. Extremely common in discourse as a direct denial. The evidential *-n* on *mana* marks the speaker's direct knowledge of the falsity.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam, manan llullachu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m, mana-n llulla-chu
4. **English translation:** it is true, it is not a lie; truly, and not deceptive
5. **Usage notes:** A paired assertive construction combining the positive assertion *cheqam* (it is true) with the negative denial *manan llullachu* (it is not a lie). Used in emphatic affirmations, particularly in testimonial and legal contexts, where the speaker reinforces the truth claim by simultaneously denying its opposite. The pairing of positive and negative poles is a documented rhetorical pattern in Quechua discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta yachay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-ta yachay
4. **English translation:** to know what is truly the case, to know the truth of the matter
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *cheqa kasqanta* (the truth of what is the case, accusative) with *yachay* (to know). Used in epistemic contexts to describe knowledge of the actual facts of a matter, as opposed to mere belief or hearsay. Appears in legal and philosophical discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanpi iñiy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-pi iñiy
4. **English translation:** to believe in what is truly the case, to have faith in the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *cheqa kasqanpi* (in the truth of what is the case, locative) with *iñiy* (to believe). Used in religious and philosophical contexts to describe belief grounded in what is actually true. Appears in Christian Quechua catechetical texts and in discussions of epistemically grounded faith.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam nispa niy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m nispa niy
4. **English translation:** to say "it is true," to assert that something is true
5. **Usage notes:** A reported-speech construction using the quotative converb *nispa* (saying) to embed the assertion *cheqam* within a frame verb *niy* (to say). Used in narrative and testimonial contexts when reporting that someone made a truth claim. The construction is the standard way of reporting direct assertions in Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa runam kay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa runa-m kay
4. **English translation:** to be a person of integrity, to be a genuine/upright person
5. **Usage notes:** A predication construction using *cheqa runa* (upright person, documented in P1b) as the predicate nominal with the copular verb *kay* (to be). Used in moral evaluations of persons. The evidential *-m* on *runa* marks the speaker's direct assertion of this characterization. Cross-references the collocation *cheqa runa* documented in P1b.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: cheqam / cheqami

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** cheqam / cheqami
2. **Example:** Cheqam, paym chaypi karqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m, pay-m chay-pi karqa
4. **English translation:** it is true, he/she was there; truly, he/she was indeed there
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse construction in which *cheqam* functions as a sentence-initial affirmative particle before a further assertion, both marked with the direct evidential *-m/-mi*. This pattern — *cheqam* followed by an evidentially marked proposition — is a documented discourse strategy for emphatic affirmation in testimonial and narrative contexts. The initial *cheqam* validates the following proposition.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

1. **Base Form:** cheqam / cheqami
2. **Example:** Cheqam, arím
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m, arí-m
4. **English translation:** it is true, yes indeed; truly, yes
5. **Usage notes:** A paired affirmative construction combining *cheqam* (it is true) with *arím* (yes indeed — *arí* "yes" + evidential *-m*). Used in conversational discourse as an emphatic double affirmation. The pairing of two evidentially marked affirmatives is a documented pattern in Southern Quechua conversational interaction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## BASE FORM: cheqachiy

---

**Construction 13**

1. **Base Form:** cheqachiy
2. **Example:** Cheqachiy atiy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqachiy atiy
4. **English translation:** to be able to verify, to be capable of confirming
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic modal construction combining *cheqachiy* (to verify/confirm) with *atiy* (to be able to, can). Used in legal and administrative contexts when the capacity to verify a claim is at issue. *Atiy* is the standard modal verb for ability in Quechua and combines freely with other infinitives.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

1. **Base Form:** cheqachiy
2. **Example:** Manan cheqachiyta atinichu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n cheqachiy-ta atini-chu
4. **English translation:** I cannot verify it, I am unable to confirm it
5. **Usage notes:** The negated form of the modal construction above, using *mana...chu* negation with *cheqachiyta atiy* (to be able to verify). Used in testimonial contexts when a witness or party cannot confirm a claim. The accusative *-ta* on the infinitive *cheqachiy* is the standard object-marking of infinitives in Quechua modal constructions.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: cheqanta rimay

---

**Note:** *Cheqanta rimay* is itself a conventionalized multi-word expression documented in P1 and P1b. The constructions below involve this expression in larger syntactic frames.

---

**Construction 15**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanta rimay
2. **Example:** Cheqanta rimayta munay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanta rimay-ta munay
4. **English translation:** to want to tell the truth, to wish to speak truthfully
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic volitional construction combining *cheqanta rimay* (to speak the truth) in its nominalized/infinitival accusative form with *munay* (to want, to wish). Used in testimonial and moral contexts when expressing the intention or desire to be truthful. *Munay* is the standard volitional verb in Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanta rimay
2. **Example:** Cheqanta rimanayki tiyan
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanta rima-nayki tiyan
4. **English translation:** you must tell the truth, you are obliged to speak truthfully
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic deontic construction using the obligative nominalization *-na* with second-person possessive *-yki* (your obligation to speak the truth) followed by *tiyan* (it exists, it is necessary — the existential/deontic verb). This is the standard construction for expressing obligation in Quechua. Used in legal and moral contexts to enjoin truthful testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: llulla

---

**Construction 17**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Manan llullachu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n llulla-chu
4. **English translation:** it is not false, it is not a lie, that is not deceptive
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative predication of *llulla*, using *mana...chu* negation. Used in discourse to deny that a statement or person is deceptive. Functions as a positive characterization by negating the negative term, parallel to *manan cheqachu* for the positive term. Extremely common in testimonial and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Llullam, manan cheqachu
3. **Transliteration:** llulla-m, mana-n cheqa-chu
4. **English translation:** it is a lie, it is not true; that is false, and not correct
5. **Usage notes:** The mirror construction of Construction 6 above, pairing the positive assertion of falsehood *llullam* (it is a lie) with the negative denial of truth *manan cheqachu* (it is not true). Used in emphatic denials and in moral condemnation of dishonest speech. The pairing of the two poles is a documented rhetorical pattern.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Llulla nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** llulla nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak calling it false, to speak saying it is a lie
5. **Usage notes:** A converb construction parallel to Construction 4 (*cheqa nispa rimay*), using *nispa* (saying) to embed the characterization of falsehood within a speech act. Used in narrative and testimonial contexts when describing how a statement was characterized as false. Attested in legal and narrative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Llullayta yachay
3. **Transliteration:** llullay-ta yachay
4. **English translation:** to know how to lie, to be skilled at deceiving
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining the infinitive *llullayta* (to lie, accusative) with *yachay* (to know how to). This is the standard Quechua construction for expressing skill or habitual ability: infinitive-*ta* + *yachay*. Documented in P1b as a collocation; documented here as a syntactic construction type. Used in moral condemnation of habitual deceivers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Llulla kasqanta riqsiy
3. **Transliteration:** llulla kasqa-n-ta riqsiy
4. **English translation:** to recognize that it is false, to identify something as a lie
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *llulla kasqanta* (the fact of its being false, accusative) with *riqsiy* (to recognize, to identify). Used in contexts of detecting deception or identifying a false claim. The construction emphasizes recognition of falsehood as a cognitive act of identification.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Example:** Llullayta saqiy
3. **Transliteration:** llullay-ta saqiy
4. **English translation:** to stop lying, to leave off deception, to abandon falsehood
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining the infinitive *llullayta* (to lie, accusative) with *saqiy* (to leave, to abandon, to stop doing). This is the standard Quechua construction for expressing cessation of an activity: infinitive-*ta* + *saqiy*. Used in moral instruction and in contexts of repentance or reform.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## BASE FORM: llullakuy

---

**Construction 23**

1. **Base Form:** llullakuy
2. **Example:** Manan llullakuyta atinichu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n llullakuy-ta atini-chu
4. **English translation:** I cannot bring myself to lie, I am unable to deceive
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic modal construction combining *llullakuyta* (to lie/deceive, accusative infinitive) with the negated modal *manan...atinichu* (I cannot). The reflexive *-ku* in *llullakuy* contributes a sense of internal motivation, so the construction can carry the nuance of being unable to bring oneself to lie. Appears in moral and testimonial contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: llullaq

---

**Construction 24**

1. **Base Form:** llullaq
2. **Example:** Llullaq runam
3. **Transliteration:** llullaq runa-m
4. **English translation:** he/she is a lying person, that person is a liar
5. **Usage notes:** A predication construction using *llullaq runa* (a lying/dishonest person) as a predicate nominal with the evidential *-m* marking direct assertion. Used in moral condemnation and in character assessments. Cross-references the collocation *llulla runa* documented in P1b; *llullaq runa* uses the agentive participial form rather than the bare adjective.

## BASE FORM: riqsiy

---

**Construction 25**

1. **Base Form:** riqsiy
2. **Example:** Riqsinaypaqmi
3. **Transliteration:** riqsi-naypaq-mi
4. **English translation:** so that I may know/recognize, for the purpose of my identifying
5. **Usage notes:** A purposive nominalization construction using *riqsiy* + obligative nominalizer *-na* + first-person possessive *-y* + purposive *-paq* + evidential *-mi*. Used in contexts where identification or recognition is the stated purpose of an action. Attested in legal and administrative contexts where the purpose of an inquiry is to establish identity.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**

1. **Base Form:** riqsiy
2. **Example:** Manan riqsinichunim
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n riqsini-chu-nim
4. **English translation:** I do not know him/her/it, I do not recognize (this person/thing)
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative first-person form of *riqsiy* with direct evidential marking. Used in testimonial contexts when a witness denies acquaintance with or recognition of a person or thing. The construction is syntactically regular but is documented here as a highly conventionalized testimonial formula.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**

1. **Base Form:** riqsiy
2. **Example:** Allintan riqsini
3. **Transliteration:** allin-tan riqsini
4. **English translation:** I know him/her/it well, I recognize (this person/thing) clearly
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining the adverbial *allinta* (well, correctly) with *riqsiy* in the first person, with the direct evidential *-n* on the adverb. Used in testimonial contexts to affirm confident and reliable recognition of a person or thing. The adverb *allinta* modifies the quality of the recognition.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: yachay

---

**Construction 28**

1. **Base Form:** yachay
2. **Example:** Yachanim / Yachaninim
3. **Transliteration:** yachani-m / yachani-ni-m
4. **English translation:** I know (it), I know for certain
5. **Usage notes:** The first-person present form of *yachay* with the direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi*, asserting personal knowledge with direct evidential commitment. Used in testimonial and epistemic contexts to assert that the speaker has direct knowledge of a matter. One of the most common epistemic assertions in Quechua discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**

1. **Base Form:** yachay
2. **Example:** Manan yachanichu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n yachani-chu
4. **English translation:** I do not know, I have no knowledge of it
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative first-person form of *yachay*, used in testimonial and everyday contexts to deny knowledge of a matter. The direct evidential *-n* on *mana* marks the speaker's direct assertion of their own ignorance. A highly conventionalized testimonial formula.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 30**

1. **Base Form:** yachay
2. **Example:** Yachaq kaspa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** yachaq kaspa rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak knowing (what one is talking about), to speak as one who knows
5. **Usage notes:** A converb construction using *kaspa* (the converb of *kay*, to be) to create a conditional/circumstantial frame: "being one who knows, speak." Used in contexts enjoining that speech be grounded in actual knowledge. Appears in legal and moral discourse about the conditions for reliable testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**

1. **Base Form:** yachay
2. **Example:** Mana yachaspa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** mana yachay-spa rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak without knowing, to speak in ignorance
5. **Usage notes:** A converb construction using *mana yachaspa* (not knowing, without having known) as an adverbial frame for *rimay* (to speak). Used to characterize speech that is made without adequate knowledge — a condition that undermines reliability. Appears in legal and moral discourse as a characterization of unreliable testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 32**

1. **Base Form:** yachay
2. **Example:** Yachayta munay
3. **Transliteration:** yachay-ta munay
4. **English translation:** to want to know, to wish to learn
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic volitional construction combining *yachayta* (to know/learn, accusative infinitive) with *munay* (to want). The standard Quechua construction for expressing desire to know something. Used in epistemic and educational contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 34**

2. **Example:** Manan iñinichu
3. **Transliteration:** mana-n iñini-chu
4. **English translation:** I do not believe it, I do not trust (this)
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative first-person form of *iñiy*, used to express disbelief or distrust. The direct evidential *-n* on *mana* marks the speaker's direct assertion of their own disbelief. A highly conventionalized expression in discourse contexts involving credibility assessment.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 35**

1. **Base Form:** iñiy
2. **Example:** Iñiymi, cheqam
3. **Transliteration:** iñiy-mi, cheqa-m
4. **English translation:** I believe it, it is true; I trust it, and it is correct
5. **Usage notes:** A paired construction combining the nominalized/asserted *iñiymi* (I believe it, with evidential) with *cheqam* (it is true). Used in discourse to reinforce a credibility assessment by pairing personal belief with an assertion of truth. Attested in testimonial and religious contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: hina

---

**Construction 41**

1. **Base Form:** hina
2. **Example:** Cheqa hina rimay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa hina rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak as if it were true, to speak in the manner of truth
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *cheqa hina* (as if true, like the real thing — documented in P1b) with *rimay* (to speak). Used to characterize speech that has the appearance of truth without necessarily being true, or alternatively to describe speech that conforms to the truth. The ambiguity between appearance and conformity is present in the construction.

**Construction 42**

2. **Example:** Llulla hina rimay
3. **Transliteration:** llulla hina rimay
4. **English translation:** to speak as if lying, to speak in the manner of a liar
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *llulla hina* (like a liar, as if false) with *rimay* (to speak). Used to characterize speech that has the appearance or manner of deception. Appears in narrative and moral discourse when describing suspicious or apparently dishonest communication.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 43**

1. **Base Form:** hina
2. **Example:** Hinam kasqa
3. **Transliteration:** hina-m kasqa
4. **English translation:** so it was, that is how it turned out to be, it was indeed like that
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *hinam* (it is so) with the past evidential form *kasqa* (it was, it turned out to be — the reportative/inferential past of *kay*). Used in narrative contexts to confirm that events unfolded as described, with the *kasqa* form adding a sense of discovered or reported past. Attested in narrative and testimonial discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM: pantay

---

**Construction 44**

1. **Base Form:** pantay
2. **Example:** Pantasqam kani
3. **Transliteration:** pantasqa-m kani
4. **English translation:** I was mistaken, I am in error, I was wrong

**Construction 45**

5. **Usage notes:** The standard negative first-person form of *pantay*, used to deny that one has made an error. The direct evidential *-n* on *mana* marks the speaker's direct assertion of their own correctness. Used in testimonial and evaluative contexts when defending the accuracy of a claim.

## Constructions involving paired or contrastive use of P1 items

---

**Construction 51**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa / llulla (contrastive pair)
2. **Example:** Cheqachu llullachu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-chu llulla-chu
4. **English translation:** is it true or is it false? is it real or is it a lie?
5. **Usage notes:** A disjunctive question construction using the question suffix *-chu* on both poles of the truth/falsehood opposition. Used in interrogative contexts to ask whether something is true or false. The *-chu* suffix in Quechua marks both yes/no questions and disjunctive alternatives; the double *-chu* construction creates an either/or question. Attested in legal and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 52**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa / llulla (contrastive pair)
2. **Example:** Cheqam, manan llullachu; manan pantanichu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m, mana-n llulla-chu; mana-n pantani-chu
4. **English translation:** it is true, it is not a lie; I have not erred
5. **Usage notes:** An extended testimonial formula combining the positive truth assertion (*cheqam*), the denial of falsehood (*manan llullachu*), and the denial of error (*manan pantanichu*). This three-part construction is attested in legal and testimonial contexts as a formula for asserting the reliability of testimony across multiple dimensions: truth, non-deception, and non-error.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 53**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa / pantay (contrastive pair)
2. **Example:** Cheqachu pantasqachu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-chu pantasqa-chu
4. **English translation:** is it correct or is it mistaken? is it right or is it wrong?
5. **Usage notes:** A disjunctive question construction using *-chu* on both *cheqa* (correct) and *pantasqa* (mistaken), asking whether something is accurate or erroneous. Used in evaluative contexts when assessing the accuracy of a claim or action. Parallel in structure to Construction 51.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary observations

The following observations are drawn directly from the attested constructions documented above and are not interpretive claims beyond what the data support.

**1. Modal periphrasis:** The modal verb *atiy* (to be able to) combines productively with infinitives of truth-evaluation verbs (*cheqachiy*, *sut'ichay*, *pantachiy*) to form ability constructions. This is a general Quechua pattern applied to domain-specific verbs.

**2. Deontic periphrasis:** The obligative nominalization (*-na* + possessive) + *tiyan* construction is attested with truth-evaluation verbs (*rimanayki tiyan*, *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan*) to express obligation. This is a general Quechua deontic pattern.

**3. Converb constructions:** The converb suffix *-spa* and the quotative converb *nispa* combine with truth-evaluation vocabulary to create circumstantial and reported-speech constructions. These are general Quechua syntactic patterns applied to domain-specific vocabulary.

**4. Evidential marking:** Direct evidential *-m/-mi* appears on predicates and adverbs in truth-evaluation constructions, consistent with its general function in Quechua discourse. The pattern of evidential marking is not specific to the truth-evaluation domain but is pervasive within it.

**5. Paired constructions:** Several attested constructions pair positive and negative poles of the truth/falsehood opposition (*cheqam, manan llullachu*; *llullam, manan cheqachu*). This rhetorical pattern of double assertion — affirming one pole while denying the other — is documented across multiple construction types.

**6. Asymmetry between cheqa and llulla in constructions:** The root *cheqa* participates in a wider range of multi-word constructions than *llulla* in the attested record, consistent with the asymmetry noted in P2a. No construction parallel to *cheqam, arím* (Construction 12) is attested for *llulla*. This asymmetry is noted as a pattern in the data.

**7. Structural expression of semantic content:** As noted in the preliminary remarks, much of the semantic content that other languages express through multi-word constructions is expressed in Quechua through derivational suffixation. The inventory of multi-word constructions is accordingly smaller than might be expected from a cross-linguistic perspective, and this reflects the morphological structure of the language rather than an absence of the relevant concepts.
