# CLCA P3 — NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
## Language: Quechua (qu)

Based solely on usage patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. Groupings reflect how terms are used and contrasted in practice. Overlaps are noted; some items remain ungrouped.

---

## GROUP 1 — Affirming that a statement matches fact

**Definition:** Expressions used in everyday assertion to claim that something corresponds to fact or is genuinely the case, including emphatic and interrogative forms used to assert or request confirmation of factual truth. Usage clusters around discourse-level affirmation of statements, often in contrast to falsehood (Group 4).

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqaq (chiqaq) — true, real, genuine
- chiqap (chiqap) — true, truly, real
- cheqaq (cheqaq) — true, correct *(orthographic variant)*
- chiqaqta (chiqaqta) — truly, really, rightly
- chiqaqchu (chiqaqchu) — is it true? really?
- chiqaqmi (chiqaqmi) — it's true (I affirm)
- chiqaqpuni (chiqaqpuni) — definitely true
- chiqaptachu (chiqaptachu) — is it true?
- chiqap kay (chiqap kay) — truth, the truth, reality
- chiqaqpaq (chiqaqpaq) — for real, seriously *(colloquial; weaker attestation)*

**Notes:** -puni and the -mi/-chu enclitic forms straddle this group and grammatical marking of assertion strength; included here because their documented usage centers on affirming/questioning factual truth.

---

## GROUP 2 — Making something clear or setting it right

**Definition:** Verbs used for the act of clarifying, correcting, verifying, or straightening — bringing something into a state of being clear, accurate, or in order. Usage spans pedagogical, legal, administrative, and practical correction contexts, and the same forms cover both physical straightening and factual/moral rectification.

**Representative expressions:**
- sut'inchay (sut'inchay) — to clarify, explain, make evident
- sut'ichay (sut'ichay) — to clarify, explain
- chiqachay (chiqachay) — to correct, straighten, verify, set right
- chiqanchay (chiqanchay) — to straighten, align, rectify
- chiqaqchakuy (chiqaqchakuy) — to verify oneself, make sure
- allinchay (allinchay) — to improve, repair, fix, make right

**Notes:** allinchay overlaps with Group 5 (goodness/evaluation), since its base is the broad evaluative root *allin*. chiqachay/chiqanchay overlap with Group 7 (straightness/path), reflecting the documented physical→moral extension.

---

## GROUP 3 — Stating that something is plainly evident

**Definition:** Expressions used when something is perceptually or epistemically obvious, visible, or self-evidently the case. Usage is documented both for clear perception (clear light) and for facts that are plainly apparent.

**Representative expressions:**
- sut'i (sut'i) — clear, evident, manifest, plain

**Notes:** This is a small group; sut'i stands somewhat apart from the chiqa- truth-affirmation cluster (Group 1) in that its documented usage emphasizes evidentness/visibility rather than correspondence-to-fact. Its derivations (sut'inchay, sut'ichay) sit in Group 2.

---

## GROUP 4 — Naming falsehood, lying, and liars

**Definition:** Expressions used to name a lie, the act of lying or deceiving, or a person who lies — strongly evaluative and morally charged. Usage spans naming the falsehood itself, the conduct, the habitual trait, and deception directed at others, and stands in direct opposition to the truth-affirmation expressions of Group 1.

**Representative expressions:**
- llulla (llulla) — lie, liar, false
- llullakuy (llullakuy) — to lie, deceive
- llullakuq (llullakuq) — liar, deceiver
- llullasapa (llullasapa) — habitual liar, big liar
- llullachiy (llullachiy) — to deceive someone, trick
- llulla simi (llulla simi) — false word, falsehood in speech
- llulla kay (llulla kay) — falseness, mendacity

**Notes:** mana llullaq ("one who does not lie") is built on this root but its documented usage is positive character evaluation (honesty); it sits between this group and Group 6.

---

## GROUP 5 — Judging things and conduct as good/bad or well/poorly done

**Definition:** Broad evaluative expressions used to assess goodness, rightness, quality, or their negation, across moral, practical, and qualitative domains. Usage covers approval in conversation, manner of doing something, craftsmanship, and everyday positive/negative judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- allin (allin) — good, well, right, proper, correct
- mana allin (mana allin) — bad, wrong, not good, improper
- allinmi (allinmi) — it's fine / good / okay
- allillanmi (allillanmi) — I'm fine / it's just fine
- allinta (allinta) — well, properly, thoroughly
- allin ruwasqa (allin ruwasqa) — well made, well done
- allin kay (allin kay) — goodness, well-being, quality
- allinpaq (allinpaq) — for the good, for the best
- chaniyuq (chaniyuq) — valuable, worthy
- chani (chani) — price, value, worth

**Notes:** chani/chaniyuq overlap here because their documented usage spans economic, moral, and aesthetic evaluation. allinchay sits in Group 2 (setting-right action). The greeting/phatic uses (allin p'unchay, allillanmi) shade into Group 9.

---

## GROUP 6 — Describing the moral character of persons

**Definition:** Fixed expressions used to evaluate a person's disposition or character — sincerity, kindness, honesty, goodness. Usage centers on judging individuals (their heart, their conduct, their reliability) rather than the truth of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqaq sunqu (chiqaq sunqu) — sincere, true-hearted, honest
- allin sunqu (allin sunqu) — good-hearted, kind
- allin runa (allin runa) — a good person
- mana llullaq (mana llullaq) — truthful, honest (one who does not lie)
- chiqaq kawsay (chiqaq kawsay) — true/upright life

**Notes:** This group draws from three different roots (chiqa-, allin, llulla) but is unified by the documented usage frame of character evaluation. The recurrent [QUALITY + sunqu] template (P2b) anchors this group. mana llullaq overlaps with Group 4 (root) and this group (usage).

---

## GROUP 7 — Straightness, the right path, and rectitude

**Definition:** Expressions whose documented usage extends physical straightness/directness into moral-legal correctness, justice, and the "right way." Usage appears in moral-religious and administrative discourse about correct direction or conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqan (chiqan) — straight, right, correct, just
- chiqan ñan (chiqan ñan) — the straight path, the right way
- chiqanchay (chiqanchay) — to straighten, align, rectify

**Notes:** Flagged in P1b as a peripheral extension of *chiqa-* away from factual veracity toward spatial/moral senses. chiqanchay overlaps with Group 2 (correction action).

---

## GROUP 8 — Resemblance, sameness, and comparison

**Definition:** Expressions used to assert that things look alike, are identical, or correspond to one another, and the functional elements used to construct comparisons. Usage centers on likeness of appearance and on establishing equivalence or identity between items.

**Representative expressions:**
- rikch'ay (rikch'ay) — appearance, likeness, image
- rikch'akuy (rikch'akuy) — to resemble, look like
- rikch'akuq (rikch'akuq) — similar, resembling, alike
- kikin (kikin) — same, identical, the very same
- kikinchay (kikinchay) — to compare, identify, equate
- hina (hina) — like, as, similar to
- hinallataq (hinallataq) — likewise, similarly
- kikin hina (kikin hina) — just the same, exactly alike

**Notes:** kikin also carries a documented "genuine/authentic" sense ("the very one") that brushes against Group 1, but its dominant comparison usage places it here. hina and hinallataq are flagged in P1 as borderline grammatical/lexical.

---

## GROUP 9 — Conventional greetings and phatic replies

**Definition:** Conventionalized expressions whose documented usage is social/phatic — greetings and standard replies — rather than evaluative or truth-related, despite being built on evaluative roots.

**Representative expressions:**
- allin p'unchay (allin p'unchay) — good day (greeting)
- allillanmi (allillanmi) — I'm fine / it's just fine (reply to greeting)

**Notes:** Both are built on *allin* and overlap with Group 5 in root/literal sense, but their documented usage is phatic convention. Small group.

---

## GROUP 10 — Believing and accepting as true

**Definition:** Expressions used for believing or trusting a statement or person, and (in Christian contexts) religious faith. Usage relates acceptance of truth and reliability to the believer.

**Representative expressions:**
- iñiy (iñiy) — to believe, faith, belief
- iñiq (iñiq) — believer, one who has faith

**Notes:** Small group. Connects to Group 1 (truth) via acceptance-of-truth, but its documented usage centers on the act/state of believing and on religious faith, distinguishing it.

---

## GROUP 11 — Marking error and mistakenness

**Definition:** Expressions used to indicate mistaken belief or action, explicitly distinguished in usage from intentional lying (Group 4). Usage appears in evaluating the accuracy of statements, answers, and conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- pantay (pantay) — to err, make a mistake, be wrong
- pantasqa (pantasqa) — mistaken, wrong, erroneous

**Notes:** P1 explicitly notes pantay is "distinct from intentional lying," so it is kept separate from Group 4. It contrasts with both Group 1 (true) and Group 5 (correct/good), but its documented usage frame is specifically *unintentional* inaccuracy.

---

## Cross-cutting observations (supported by the data)

- **A recurrent contrast pair** runs between Group 1 (truth-affirmation, chiqa-) and Group 4 (falsehood, llulla), made explicit at the construction level by the minimal pair *chiqaq simi* (true word) vs. *llulla simi* (false word).
- **The *chiqa-* root spreads across four groups** (1 truth-affirmation, 2 correction, 6 character, 7 straightness/path), reflecting documented physical→moral→discursive extension. Its members are placed by usage, not by root.
- **The *allin* root spreads across Groups 2, 5, 6, and 9**, reflecting its documented breadth from evaluation to action to phatic convention.
- **The [X + kay] nominalizing frame** (chiqap kay, allin kay, llulla kay) cuts across Groups 1, 5, and 4 respectively; these were placed by their evaluative pole rather than grouped together as a template.

## Items resisting clean grouping

- **chiqaqpaq** ("for real / seriously") — colloquial intensifier; weakly attested; tentatively in Group 1.
- **-puni** — an enclitic marking certainty; relates to Group 1's assertion strength but is grammatical, not lexical; does not group cleanly.
- **kikin** — split between Group 8 (sameness/comparison) and a minor "genuine/authentic" sense touching Group 1.
- **mana llullaq** — split between Group 4 (root) and Group 6 (honesty-as-character usage).

No grouping was forced for the grammatical enclitic material; the evidential system (-mi/-si/-chá), noted as excluded in P1, is not treated here.
