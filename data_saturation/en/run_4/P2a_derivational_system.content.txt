# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested derivational morphology for the 30 lexical items identified in P1. The P1b root expansion has already identified many derived forms in the course of root analysis; those forms are included here where they meet the attestation standard, but the present step focuses on derivations directly from the P1 base forms rather than from roots more broadly. Forms already extensively documented in P1b are noted briefly; forms not previously documented receive fuller treatment.

Derivational morphology is understood here as the formation of new lexical items from a base by affixation, compounding, or conversion, where the resulting form has a distinct lexical entry (distinct meaning, category, or both). Inflectional morphology (plural *-s*, past tense *-ed*, progressive *-ing*, comparative *-er*, superlative *-est*) is excluded unless the inflected form has developed a distinct lexical meaning.

Where a P1 item is itself a derived form (e.g., *truthful* from *truth*), derivations from that item are documented separately.

Gaps are preserved. Where no common derivation is identified, this is stated explicitly.

## P1 Item 1: **true**

---

**1.1**
- **Base Form:** true
- **Derived Form:** truly
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-ly* (adverb-forming suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** in a true or genuine manner; sincerely; used as an intensifier
- **Usage:** Functions as a manner adverb ("she truly believed it") and as a formulaic closing in correspondence ("yours truly"). Appears across all registers and frequency bands.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
- **Base Form:** true
- **Derived Form:** truth
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-th* (Old English abstract noun suffix *-þu*)
- **Natural English translation:** the quality or state of being true; a true statement or fact
- **Usage:** The primary nominal form of the *true* root; independently listed as P1 item 2. The suffix *-th* is no longer productive in English, making this a lexicalized rather than synchronically derived form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
- **Base Form:** true
- **Derived Form:** untrue
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* (negation prefix) + *true*
- **Natural English translation:** not true; false; (of a person or conduct) unfaithful
- **Usage:** Applied to statements to indicate falsity and to persons or conduct to indicate disloyalty or betrayal. Slightly more formal than *false* in some registers; the disloyalty sense is now somewhat archaic.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
- **Base Form:** true
- **Derived Form:** truism
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *-ism* (suffix forming nouns denoting doctrines, practices, or characteristic qualities)
- **Natural English translation:** a statement so obviously true as to be not worth stating; a platitude
- **Usage:** Used in intellectual and journalistic discourse, typically critically, to dismiss a statement as trivially obvious. The suffix *-ism* here does not form a doctrine or movement but a quality-noun; this is an unusual use of the suffix.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
- **Base Form:** true
- **Derived Form:** true-born
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *true* + *born* (participial adjective; compound)
- **Natural English translation:** genuinely of a specified origin or nationality by birth
- **Usage:** Found in literary and historical texts ("a true-born Englishman"). Uncommon in contemporary everyday speech; more frequent in formal, archaic, or ironic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on productivity:** The *true* base generates a small number of direct derivations in contemporary English. The suffix *-th* that produced *truth* is no longer productive. The most productive derivational path runs through *truth* and *truthful* rather than directly from *true*. Compounding with *true* as a modifier (e.g., *true-born*, *true-blue*, *true-hearted*) is attested but not highly productive in contemporary usage.

**2.5**

- **Transliteration:** N/A

**2.6**

- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *truth* + *-ful* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality or habit of being untruthful
- **Usage:** Used in moral evaluation and legal testimony assessment. Less frequent than *dishonesty* but more specific to the speech-act dimension.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on compounds:** *truth* participates in a large number of compounds (*truth-telling*, *truth-teller*, *half-truth*, *truth value*, *truth condition*, *truth table*, *truth claim*, *ground truth*, *home truth*, *gospel truth*, *post-truth*, *truth serum*). These are documented in P1b and are not repeated here in full. They are attested and productive; the *truth* base is among the most compound-productive items in this domain.

**3.4**

- **Transliteration:** N/A

No verbal form derived directly from *accurate* is in common use; the related verb is *verify* or *check*, not *\*accurize* or similar.

## P1 Item 4: **genuine**

---

**4.1**
- **Base Form:** genuine
- **Derived Form:** genuinely
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ly*
- **Natural English translation:** in a genuine manner; sincerely; actually
- **Usage:** Functions as a manner adverb ("she was genuinely surprised") and as an intensifier asserting sincerity or authenticity. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.2**
- **Base Form:** genuine
- **Derived Form:** genuineness
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *genuine* + *-ness*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being genuine; authenticity; sincerity
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts. Less common than *authenticity* in formal discourse; *genuineness* tends to appear in contexts emphasizing sincerity of feeling rather than verified origin.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on gaps:** No common verbal form derived from *genuine* is attested in English (no *\*genuinize* or *\*genuinate*). The verbal function is supplied by *authenticate* (from the parallel root). No negated form *\*ingenuine* is in common use; the negation is typically expressed by *fake*, *spurious*, or *not genuine*. This asymmetry with *authentic* / *inauthentic* is preserved here.

**5.4**

- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *authentic* + *-ation*
- **Natural English translation:** the process of establishing or confirming authenticity

- **Usage:** Used in legal, archival, and digital security contexts. In computing, an *authenticator* is a device or application used to verify identity (e.g., two-factor authentication apps).

---

**5.6**
- **Base Form:** authentic
- **Derived Form:** inauthentic
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *in-* + *authentic*
- **Natural English translation:** not authentic; not genuine; not corresponding to an original
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical, aesthetic, and everyday contexts. In existentialist philosophy, an *inauthentic* life is one lived in conformity with social roles rather than genuine self-determination.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.7**
- **Base Form:** authentic
- **Derived Form:** inauthenticity
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *in-* + *authentic* + *-ity*
- **Natural English translation:** the quality of being inauthentic; lack of genuineness
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical (especially existentialist), aesthetic, and cultural criticism. Less common than *authenticity* but well attested in philosophical and cultural discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.8**
- **Base Form:** authentic
- **Derived Form:** unauthenticated
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** *un-* + *authentic* + *-at(e)* + *-ed*
- **Natural English translation:** not yet authenticated; not verified as genuine
- **Usage:** Used in legal, archival, and digital security contexts to flag documents, identities, or objects whose authenticity has not been confirmed. Common in legal and technical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The *authentic* base is among the most derivationally productive items in this domain, generating adjective, adverb, abstract noun, verb, agent noun, negated adjective, and negated noun forms. The verbal form *authenticate* is itself productive, generating *authentication*, *authenticator*, and *unauthenticated*.

**Note on polysemy and derivational complexity:** *Right* is among the most polysemous items in this domain, and its derivational family reflects this. *Rightful* and *righteous* draw on different senses of the base (legal entitlement and moral virtue respectively). The factual-accuracy sense of *right* ("that's right") does not generate a distinct derivational sub-family; *rightly* and *rightness* cover both the accuracy and moral senses.
