# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X jẹ́ Y** (X be Y) — copular construction with **jẹ́** 'be/affirm'
**Y ni X** (Y FOC X) — focus construction with **ni** copula

### Examples
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí jẹ́ òtítọ́**
  Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí jẹ́ òtítọ́
  "This matter is truth" / "This statement is true"

- **Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀**
  Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
  "Truth FOC word his/her" / "His/her word is truth"

### Notes
Yoruba uses two main copular strategies: **jẹ́** for neutral predication and **ni** for focus/emphasis. The **ni** construction is more emphatic and typically places the predicate first.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X kì í ṣe Y** (X NEG be Y) — negative copula with **kì í ṣe**
**X kọ́** (X NEG.FOC) — negative focus with **kọ́**

### Examples
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kì í ṣe òtítọ́**
  Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kì í ṣe òtítọ́
  "This matter NEG be truth" / "This statement is not true"

- **Irọ́ kọ́**
  Irọ́ kọ́
  "Lie NEG.FOC" / "It's not a lie"

### Notes
Negative copular constructions use **kì í ṣe** for emphatic negation. The negative focus marker **kọ́** directly negates focused predicates and is very common in denials.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**N + ADJ** — noun followed by adjective (e.g., **ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́** 'true statement')
**N + N** — noun-noun compounds (e.g., **òtítọ́ ọkàn** 'sincerity')
**N + tí + CLAUSE** — relative clause modification

### Examples
- **Ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́**
  Ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
  "Word straight/correct" / "True statement"

- **Ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́**
  Ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́
  "Person REL 3SG PROG tell lie" / "One who tells lies"

### Notes
Adjectives follow nouns directly without copula. Relative clauses use **tí** as relativizer. Compound nouns are common for complex truth-domain concepts (e.g., **òtítọ́-inú** 'inner truth/sincerity').

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**di Y** (become Y) — change-of-state verb **di**
**bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí V** (begin to V) — inchoative with **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí**

### Examples
- **Ó di òtítọ́**
  Ó di òtítọ́
  "3SG become truth" / "It became true / It turned out to be true"

- **Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́**
  Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́
  "1SG begin to believe" / "I began to believe"

### Notes
The verb **di** marks change of state. Inchoative aspect with verbs uses **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí** 'begin to'. Note that **di òtítọ́** can mean both "become true" (for propositions) and "turn out to be true" (for beliefs).

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**mú X V** (make X V) — causative with **mú** 'make/cause'
**jẹ́ kí X V** (let/allow X V) — permissive causative

### Examples
- **Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́**
  Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́
  "3SG make 1SG.OBJ believe" / "He/She made me believe"

- **Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ hàn**
  Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ hàn
  "Let truth show" / "Let the truth be revealed"

### Notes
Yoruba uses **mú** for direct causation and **jẹ́ kí** for permissive causation. Truth-domain causatives often involve causing belief (**mú...gbàgbọ́**) or revealing truth (**jẹ́ kí...hàn**).

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ì- + VERB** — abstract nominal prefix (e.g., **ìgbàgbọ́** 'belief')
**VERB-VERB** — gerundive reduplication (e.g., **gbígbàgbọ́** 'believing')

### Examples
- **Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára**
  Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára
  "Belief his/her strong" / "His/her belief is strong"

- **Pípa irọ́ kò dára**
  Pípa irọ́ kò dára
  "Telling lie NEG good" / "Lying is not good"

### Notes
The prefix **ì-** creates abstract state/result nominals from verbs. Gerundive reduplication (copying initial CV) emphasizes the process or action. Both patterns are highly productive in the truth domain.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**oní- + N** (→ **olí-**, **ẹlẹ́-**) — possessor/agent prefix
**a- + VERB** — agent prefix for verbal bases

### Examples
- **Olótítọ́ ni baba mi**
  Olótítọ́ ni baba mi
  "Truthful.person FOC father my" / "My father is a truthful person"

- **Apurọ́ ni ọkùnrin náà**
  Apurọ́ ni ọkùnrin náà
  "Liar FOC man the" / "That man is a liar"

### Notes
**Oní-** (with vowel harmony variants **olí-**, **ẹlẹ́-**) creates agent nouns from nominal bases, meaning 'one who has/possesses X'. **A-** creates agents from verbs. Both are productive for truth-domain character terms.

---

## 8. VERBAL PREDICATION (STATIVE)

### Pattern
**SUBJ + STATIVE.VERB** — simple stative predication
**ó + VERB** (3SG + VERB) — default subject construction

### Examples
- **Ó tọ́**
  Ó tọ́
  "3SG straight/correct" / "It is correct/true"

- **Kò tọ́**
  Kò tọ́
  "NEG straight/correct" / "It is not correct/not true"

### Notes
Stative verbs like **tọ́** 'be straight/correct' function as predicates without copula. The pronoun **ó** serves as default subject for propositions and situations. Negation uses **kò** directly before the verb.

---

## 9. VERBAL PREDICATION (DYNAMIC)

### Pattern
**SUBJ + VERB (+ OBJ)** — basic transitive/intransitive
**SUBJ + VERB + mọ́/sí/fún + OBJ** — extended transitives with applicatives

### Examples
- **Ó purọ́**
  Ó purọ́
  "3SG lie" / "He/She lied"

- **Ó purọ́ mọ́ mi**
  Ó purọ́ mọ́ mi
  "3SG lie to 1SG.OBJ" / "He/She lied to me"

### Notes
Dynamic verbs like **purọ́** 'lie' and **gbàgbọ́** 'believe' take standard SVO order. Applicative markers (**mọ́** 'to/at', **sí** 'to/toward', **fún** 'for/to') introduce additional arguments.

---

## 10. INTERROGATIVE (POLAR QUESTION)

### Pattern
**Ṣé + CLAUSE?** — polar question marker **ṣé**
**...bí?** — clause-final question particle

### Examples
- **Ṣé ó tọ́?**
  Ṣé ó tọ́?
  "Q 3SG correct?" / "Is it correct/true?"

- **Ó gbàgbọ́ bí?**
  Ó gbàgbọ́ bí?
  "3SG believe Q?" / "Does he/she believe?"

### Notes
**Ṣé** is the standard polar question marker, appearing clause-initially. The particle **bí** can also mark yes/no questions, appearing clause-finally. Both are common in truth-verification contexts.

---

## 11. INTERROGATIVE (CONTENT QUESTION)

### Pattern
**kí ni...?** (what FOC...?) — 'what' questions
**ta ni...?** (who FOC...?) — 'who' questions
**èéṣe tí...?** (why REL...?) — 'why' questions

### Examples
- **Kí ni òtítọ́?**
  Kí ni òtítọ́?
  "What FOC truth?" / "What is truth?"

- **Ta ni ó purọ́?**
  Ta ni ó purọ́?
  "Who FOC 3SG lie?" / "Who lied?"

### Notes
Content questions use wh-words with focus marker **ni**. The questioned element typically appears clause-initially in focus position. **Èéṣe tí** 'why' uses the relativizer **tí**.

---

## 12. IMPERATIVE

### Pattern
**VERB (+ OBJ)** — bare verb for positive commands
**má + VERB** — negative imperative with **má**

### Examples
- **Sọ òtítọ́!**
  Sọ òtítọ́!
  "Tell truth!" / "Tell the truth!"

- **Má purọ́!**
  Má purọ́!
  "NEG lie!" / "Don't lie!"

### Notes
Positive imperatives use bare verb stems. Negative imperatives require the prohibitive marker **má**. Second-person subject is null in imperatives.

---

## 13. COMPLEMENTATION

### Pattern
**VERB + pé + CLAUSE** — complementizer **pé** 'that'
**VERB + kí + SUBJUNCTIVE** — subjunctive complement with **kí**

### Examples
- **Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́**
  Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
  "1SG believe that 3SG correct" / "I believe that it is true"

- **Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́**
  Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́
  "3SG fitting SUBJ 2SG tell truth" / "You should tell the truth"

### Notes
Declarative complements use **pé** 'that'. Subjunctive complements (expressing obligation, desire, or purpose) use **kí**. Verbs like **gbàgbọ́** 'believe' and **dájú** 'be certain' take **pé**-complements.

---

## 14. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION (MANNER)

### Pattern
**ní + N** (with N) — instrumental/manner phrase
**láì + VERB** (without VERBing) — negative manner
**bí + CLAUSE** (as/like CLAUSE) — comparative manner

### Examples
- **Ní òtítọ́**
  Ní òtítọ́
  "With truth" / "Truthfully, honestly"

- **Gbàgbọ́ láì rí**
  Gbàgbọ́ láì rí
  "Believe without see" / "Believe without seeing"

### Notes
Manner adverbials use **ní** 'with/in' plus noun. Negative manner uses **láì** 'without' plus verb. The construction **ní + òtítọ́** 'truthfully' is extremely common as a discourse marker.

---

## 15. DEGREE MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**VERB/ADJ + púpọ̀** (very, much) — intensifier
**VERB/ADJ + díẹ̀** (a little) — diminisher
**VERB/ADJ + gan-an** (exactly, absolutely) — emphatic

### Examples
- **Ó tọ́ púpọ̀**
  Ó tọ́ púpọ̀
  "3SG correct very" / "It is very correct/quite true"

- **Ó dájú gan-an**
  Ó dájú gan-an
  "3SG certain exactly" / "It is absolutely certain"

### Notes
Degree modifiers follow the modified predicate. **Púpọ̀** 'much/very' intensifies, **díẹ̀** 'a little' diminishes, and **gan-an** 'exactly' adds emphasis. These are very productive with truth-domain predicates.

---

## 16. REDUPLICATION (EMPHASIS)

### Pattern
**VERB-VERB** or **ADJ-ADJ** — full reduplication for emphasis/intensification

### Examples
- **Tọ́tọ́**
  Tọ́tọ́
  "Straight-straight" / "Very straight, completely correct"

- **Gidi gidi**
  Gidi gidi
  "Genuine-genuine" / "Very genuine, absolutely real"

### Notes
Full reduplication of verbs and adjectives creates emphatic or intensified meanings. Common in truth domain for emphasizing complete correctness (**tọ́tọ́**) or absolute genuineness (**gidi gidi**).

---

## 17. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**X jù Y lọ** (X exceed Y go) — superiority 'X is more than Y'
**X kò tó Y** (X NEG reach Y) — inferiority 'X is less than Y'

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ dára jù irọ́ lọ**
  Òtítọ́ dára jù irọ́ lọ
  "Truth good exceed lie go" / "Truth is better than lies"

- **Irọ́ kékeré kò tó irọ́ ńlá**
  Irọ́ kékeré kò tó irọ́ ńlá
  "Lie small NEG reach lie big" / "A small lie is not as bad as a big lie"

### Notes
Comparatives use **jù...lọ** 'exceed...go' for superiority. The standard of comparison appears between **jù** and **lọ**. Inferiority uses **kò tó** 'not reach'.

---

## 18. POSSESSION

### Pattern
**N + POSS** — possessive pronoun suffix (e.g., **rẹ̀** 'his/her')
**N + ti + NP** — possessive with **ti** 'of'
**oní- + N** — 'one who has N'

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ rẹ̀**
  Òtítọ́ rẹ̀
  "Truth his/her" / "His/her truthfulness"

- **Ìwà ti ọmọlúàbí**
  Ìwà ti ọmọlúàbí
  "Character of well-born.person" / "Character of a virtuous person"

### Notes
Possessive pronouns attach directly to nouns. The particle **ti** 'of' marks possessive relationships between two nouns. **Oní-** creates 'possessor of' agent nouns (see §7).

---

## 19. EXISTENTIAL/LOCATIVE

### Pattern
**N + wà** (N exist/be.located) — existential verb **wà**
**kò sí + N** (NEG exist N) — negative existential

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ wà nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀**
  Òtítọ́ wà nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
  "Truth exist in word his/her" / "There is truth in his/her words"

- **Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀**
  Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀
  "NEG exist lie in 3SG" / "There is no lie in him/her"

### Notes
**Wà** marks existence or location. Negative existentials use **kò sí** 'not exist'. Locative phrases use **ní** 'in/at' plus location (e.g., **nínú** 'inside').

---

## 20. SERIAL VERB CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**V₁ + V₂ (+ OBJ)** — two verbs sharing subject, no conjunction

### Examples
- **Ó sọ òtítọ́ jáde**
  Ó sọ òtítọ́ jáde
  "3SG tell truth come.out" / "He/She revealed the truth"

- **Wá òtítọ́ rí**
  Wá òtítọ́ rí
  "Seek truth find" / "Seek and find the truth"

### Notes
Serial verb constructions involve two or more verbs sharing a subject without overt coordination. Common patterns include **sọ...jáde** 'tell...come.out' (reveal) and **wá...rí** 'seek...find'.

---

## 21. FOCUS CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**X ni + CLAUSE** (X FOC + CLAUSE) — cleft focus with **ni**
**Ní X ni + CLAUSE** (at X FOC + CLAUSE) — locative/temporal focus

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ**
  Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ
  "Truth FOC 1SG tell" / "It is the truth that I told"

- **Ní òtítọ́ ni mo gbàgbọ́**
  Ní òtítọ́ ni mo gbàgbọ́
  "In truth FOC 1SG believe" / "It is in truth that I believe"

### Notes
Focus constructions use **ni** to mark the focused constituent, which appears clause-initially. The remainder of the clause follows. This is the primary strategy for contrastive focus and emphasis.

---

## 22. CONDITIONAL

### Pattern
**bí + CLAUSE₁, CLAUSE₂** (if CLAUSE₁, CLAUSE₂) — conditional with **bí**
**tí + CLAUSE₁, CLAUSE₂** (if CLAUSE₁, CLAUSE₂) — alternative conditional marker

### Examples
- **Bí ó bá tọ́, èmi yóò gbàgbọ́**
  Bí ó bá tọ́, èmi yóò gbàgbọ́
  "If 3SG COND correct, 1SG FUT believe" / "If it is true, I will believe"

- **Tí o bá purọ́, a ó mọ̀**
  Tí o bá purọ́, a ó mọ̀
  "If 2SG COND lie, 1PL FUT know" / "If you lie, we will know"

### Notes
Conditionals use **bí** or **tí** 'if' plus conditional marker **bá** in the protasis. The apodosis often contains future marker **yóò/ó**. Both markers are common in truth-conditional reasoning.

---

## 23. TEMPORAL ASPECT (PROGRESSIVE)

### Pattern
**SUBJ + ń + VERB** — progressive marker **ń**
**SUBJ + ti + VERB** — perfective marker **ti**

### Examples
- **Ó ń pa irọ́**
  Ó ń pa irọ́
  "3SG PROG tell lie" / "He/She is lying"

- **Mo ti gbàgbọ́ tẹ́lẹ̀**
  Mo ti gbàgbọ́ tẹ́lẹ̀
  "1SG PERF believe already" / "I already believed / I have believed"

### Notes
Progressive **ń** marks ongoing action. Perfective **ti** marks completed action with current relevance. Both are productive with truth-domain verbs.

---

## 24. MODAL CONSTRUCTION (OBLIGATION/NECESSITY)

### Pattern
**ó yẹ kí + SUBJUNCTIVE** (it fitting SUBJ...) — 'should, ought to'
**ó dọ́gba kí + SUBJUNCTIVE** (it necessary SUBJ...) — 'must, necessary'

### Examples
- **Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́**
  Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́
  "3SG fitting SUBJ 2SG tell truth" / "You should tell the truth"

- **Kò yẹ kí o purọ́**
  Kò yẹ kí o purọ́
  "NEG fitting SUBJ 2SG lie" / "You should not lie"

### Notes
Modal obligation uses **ó yẹ kí** 'it is fitting that' plus subjunctive clause. Negation of obligation uses **kò yẹ kí**. This is the primary deontic modal construction in the truth domain.

---

## 25. MODAL CONSTRUCTION (ABILITY/POSSIBILITY)

### Pattern
**lè + VERB** — ability/possibility modal **lè** 'can, able'
**ṣe é ṣe** (do it do) — 'it is possible'

### Examples
- **Mo lè sọ òtítọ́**
  Mo lè sọ òtítọ́
  "1SG can tell truth" / "I can tell the truth"

- **Ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó jẹ́ òtítọ́**
  Ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó jẹ́ òtítọ́
  "3SG do it do SUBJ 3SG be truth" / "It is possible that it is true"

### Notes
The modal **lè** 'can' marks ability or possibility. The construction **ṣe é ṣe** (literally 'do it do') expresses epistemic possibility. Both are common in truth-domain reasoning.

---

## 26. EVIDENTIAL/REPORTATIVE

### Pattern
**ó ní pé...** (3SG say that...) — reportative with **ní** 'say'
**bí mo ṣe gbọ́** (as 1SG do hear) — 'as I heard, reportedly'

### Examples
- **Ó ní pé òtítọ́ ni**
  Ó ní pé òtítọ́ ni
  "3SG say that truth FOC" / "He/She says that it is true"

- **Bí mo ṣe gbọ́, ó purọ́**
  Bí mo ṣe gbọ́, ó purọ́
  "As 1SG do hear, 3SG lie" / "As I heard, he/she lied"

### Notes
Reportative evidence uses **ó ní pé** 'he/she says that'. The construction **bí mo ṣe gbọ́** 'as I heard' marks hearsay. Yoruba does not have grammaticalized evidential markers but uses these periphrastic strategies.

---

## 27. QUOTATIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**...pé "QUOTE"** — quotative complementizer **pé**
**...wí pé "QUOTE"** — quotative verb **wí** 'say' plus **pé**

### Examples
- **Ó sọ pé "Òtítọ́ ni"**
  Ó sọ pé "Òtítọ́ ni"
  "3SG tell that 'Truth FOC'" / "He/She said 'It is true'"

- **Mo gbọ́ pé ó purọ́**
  Mo gbọ́ pé ó purọ́
  "1SG hear that 3SG lie" / "I heard that he/she lied"

### Notes
Direct and indirect quotations use **pé** 'that' after verbs of saying (**sọ**, **wí**) and hearing (**gbọ́**). Yoruba does not consistently distinguish direct from indirect speech in form.

---

## 28. REASON/CAUSE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**nítorí (pé)...** (because...) — causal conjunction
**torí pé...** (because that...) — alternative causal marker

### Examples
- **Mo gbàgbọ́ nítorí pé ó tọ́**
  Mo gbàgbọ́ nítorí pé ó tọ́
  "1SG believe because that 3SG correct" / "I believe because it is true"

- **Ó purọ́ torí pé ó bẹ̀rù**
  Ó purọ́ torí pé ó bẹ̀rù
  "3SG lie because that 3SG fear" / "He/She lied because he/she was afraid"

### Notes
Causal clauses use **nítorí (pé)** or **torí pé** 'because (that)'. These introduce reasons or causes for beliefs, statements, or actions in the truth domain.

---

## 29. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé...** (even.though it even be that...) — concessive
**àmọ́...** (but/however...) — adversative conjunction

### Examples
- **Bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé ó purọ́, mo gbàgbọ́**
  Bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé ó purọ́, mo gbàgbọ́
  "Even.though 3SG even be that 3SG lie, 1SG believe" / "Even though he/she lied, I believe"

- **Ó sọ òtítọ́, àmọ́ kò gbàgbọ́**
  Ó sọ òtítọ́, àmọ́ kò gbàgbọ́
  "3SG tell truth, but NEG believe" / "He/She told the truth, but (they) didn't believe"

### Notes
Concessives use **bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé** 'even though'. The adversative **àmọ́** 'but/however' marks contrast. Both are common in complex truth-domain reasoning.

---

## 30. COORDINATION

### Pattern
**X àti Y** (X and Y) — conjunction with **àti**
**X tàbí Y** (X or Y) — disjunction with **tàbí**

### Examples
- **Òtítọ́ àti òdodo**
  Òtítọ́ àti òdodo
  "Truth and righteousness" / "Truth and righteousness"

- **Òtítọ́ tàbí irọ́**
  Òtítọ́ tàbí irọ́
  "Truth or lie" / "Truth or falsehood"

### Notes
**Àti** 'and' coordinates noun phrases and clauses. **Tàbí** 'or' marks disjunction. The pair **òtítọ́ àti òdodo** 'truth and righteousness' is a fixed expression in moral discourse.
