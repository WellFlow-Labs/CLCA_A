# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Preliminary note on Tagalog morphology:** Tagalog derivational morphology operates primarily through prefixes, suffixes, circumfixes, and reduplication, often in combination. The same morphological material can serve multiple functions (e.g., *-an* as locative applicative, as abstract nominalizer, as benefactive marker), and the boundary between derivation and inflection is not always sharp. The forms documented below are restricted to those with clear attestation in the P1 and P1b data or in well-established general usage. Forms generated by regular verbal aspect morphology (e.g., perfective/imperfective alternations) are noted where they are lexically significant but are not treated as independent derivations unless they show semantic specialization.

## Base Form 1: *totoo*

---

**Derived Form 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Derived Form:** katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** katotohanan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-nan* (circumfix; abstract nominalizer); "the state or quality of being *totoo*"
5. **Natural English translation:** truth, the truth, reality
6. **Usage description:** The standard abstract noun for truth across philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday registers. It is the expected translation equivalent of "truth" in Bible translations and legal oaths, and appears in formal moral exhortations (*Sabihin ang katotohanan* — "Tell the truth").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Derived Form:** makatotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** makatotohanan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *maka-* (prefix indicating alignment with or capacity for) + *totohanan* (from *totoo*); "in accordance with or aligned with truth/reality"
5. **Natural English translation:** realistic, true-to-life, truthful, verisimilar
6. **Usage description:** Applied in aesthetic and narrative contexts to evaluate whether a representation corresponds faithfully to reality (*makatotohanang pagkukuwento* — "realistic/truthful storytelling"). Less common in purely propositional contexts than *totoo* itself.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Derived Form:** totoong-totoo
3. **Transliteration:** totoong-totoo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *totoo* + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo* (full reduplication for intensification)
5. **Natural English translation:** very real, absolutely true, genuinely genuine
6. **Usage description:** An intensified form used in emphatic speech to stress that something is unambiguously real or true. Common in emotional or emphatic registers of everyday speech.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *totoo*:** The root does not appear to generate a standard agentive noun (e.g., "one who tells the truth") through direct derivation; that function is served by periphrastic constructions. No causative or applicative verbal form directly from *totoo* is well-attested as a lexicalized item; the verbal function is served by *magsabi ng katotohanan* and similar phrases rather than a derived verb from the root itself.

## Base Form 2: *katotohanan*

---

**Derived Form 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Derived Form:** sa katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** sa katotohanan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *sa* (locative/oblique marker) + *katotohanan*
5. **Natural English translation:** in truth, in reality, truthfully speaking, actually
6. **Usage description:** A fixed discourse connector used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement, equivalent to "in truth" or "actually." Common across spoken and written registers; functions as a pragmatic marker rather than a purely referential phrase.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *katotohanan*:** This form is itself a derivation from *totoo* (see 1.1 above). Its further derivational productivity is limited; it does not generate a standard verbal form or agentive noun directly. The phrase *sa katotohanan* is included here because it represents a grammaticalized discourse use that is distinct from the base nominal function.

## Base Form 3: *tunay*

---

**Derived Form 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** katunayan
3. **Transliteration:** katunayan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an* (circumfix; abstract nominalizer); "the state of being *tunay*; proof"
5. **Natural English translation:** proof, evidence, genuineness, verification
6. **Usage description:** Used both as an abstract noun meaning "genuineness" and, very commonly, as a discourse connector (*sa katunayan* — "in fact / as a matter of fact"). In legal and formal contexts it carries the sense of evidence or proof that establishes something as real.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.2**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** patunayan
3. **Transliteration:** patunayan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pa-* (causative prefix) + *tunay* + *-an* (applicative/locative suffix); "to cause to be proven; to prove to someone"
5. **Natural English translation:** to prove, to demonstrate, to verify
6. **Usage description:** The standard verb for the act of proving or demonstrating something to be true or genuine. Used in legal argumentation, scientific discourse, and everyday persuasion (*Patunayan mo* — "Prove it").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.3**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** pagpapatunay
3. **Transliteration:** pagpapatunay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pag-* (verbal noun prefix) + *pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an*; "the act of causing something to be verified/proven"
5. **Natural English translation:** verification, authentication, confirmation, proof (as an act)
6. **Usage description:** The verbal noun for the act of verifying, authenticating, or proving something. Used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts (*pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "authentication/verification of a document").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.4**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** mapatunayan
3. **Transliteration:** mapatunayan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ma-* (potential/ability prefix) + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "to be able to prove; to be provable"
5. **Natural English translation:** to be able to prove, to be provable, to manage to verify
6. **Usage description:** Used to express the possibility or achievement of proving something. Common in legal and argumentative discourse (*Hindi mapatunayan* — "cannot be proven").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.5**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** napatunayan
3. **Transliteration:** napatunayan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *na-* (perfective/completed aspect marker) + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "has been proven"
5. **Natural English translation:** has been proven, was verified, has been established
6. **Usage description:** The completed-aspect form indicating that something has been successfully proven or verified. Common in legal verdicts, scientific conclusions, and formal declarations.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.6**

1. **Base Form:** tunay
2. **Derived Form:** pinatunayan
3. **Transliteration:** pinatunayan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pi-* (perfective of *pa-* causative) + *tunay* + *-an*; "was proven/caused to be verified"
5. **Natural English translation:** proved, demonstrated, verified (past/completed)
6. **Usage description:** The completed-aspect actor-focus form of *patunayan*, used to state that someone proved or demonstrated something. Common in legal and argumentative contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *tunay*:** The verbal paradigm built on *patunayan* is the most productive derivational cluster from this root. The forms *napatunayan* and *pinatunayan* represent aspect-marked realizations of the same derived verbal stem rather than independent lexical derivations, but are documented here because they carry distinct aspectual meanings that are lexically relevant. The root does not appear to generate a standard agentive noun (e.g., "one who proves") as a lexicalized item; that function is served by periphrastic constructions.

## Base Form 6: *mali*

---

**Derived Form 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** mali
2. **Derived Form:** kamalian
3. **Transliteration:** kamalian
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ka-* + *mali* + *-an* (circumfix; abstract nominalizer); "the state or instance of being *mali*"
5. **Natural English translation:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing
6. **Usage description:** The standard abstract noun for an error or mistake, used in moral, factual, and procedural evaluations (*Aminin ang kamalian* — "Admit the error/mistake").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** mali
2. **Derived Form:** pagkakamali
3. **Transliteration:** pagkakamali
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pag-* + *ka-* + *mali* + *-an*; verbal noun of *magkamali*; "the act or instance of making an error"
5. **Natural English translation:** mistake, error, fault (a specific instance)
6. **Usage description:** The standard nominal form for a specific instance of making a mistake. More specific to a single act of error than *kamalian*, which can refer to the general quality of wrongness. Used across factual, moral, and procedural domains.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.3**

1. **Base Form:** mali
2. **Derived Form:** magkamali
3. **Transliteration:** magkamali
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *mag-* (actor-focus verb prefix) + *ka-* + *mali*; "to make a mistake, to err"
5. **Natural English translation:** to make a mistake, to err, to be wrong
6. **Usage description:** The standard verb for committing an error. Used across all registers and domains (*Nagkamali ako* — "I made a mistake").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *mali*:** The pattern *maling [noun]* (wrong/incorrect [noun]) is highly productive in Tagalog, generating collocations across virtually any domain (see P1b for extensive examples). This productivity is a feature of the adjective's combinatorial behavior with the linker *-ng* rather than a derivational process in the strict morphological sense, and the individual collocations are not documented as separate derivations here. The verbal and nominal derivations documented above (6.1–6.3) represent the morphologically distinct derived forms.

## Base Form 8: *tapat*

---

**Derived Form 8.1**

1. **Base Form:** tapat
2. **Derived Form:** katapatan
3. **Transliteration:** katapatan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ka-* + *tapat* + *-an* (circumfix; abstract nominalizer); "the quality of being *tapat*"
5. **Natural English translation:** honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty
6. **Usage description:** The abstract noun covering both the epistemic sense (honesty in speech) and the relational-moral sense (faithfulness, loyalty). Appears in formal declarations, vows, and moral discourse; its semantic range is broader than English "honesty" alone.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8.2**

1. **Base Form:** tapat
2. **Derived Form:** tapatan
3. **Transliteration:** tapatan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *tapat* + *-an* (applicative/locative suffix); "to be frank/honest with someone; to face someone directly"
5. **Natural English translation:** to speak frankly with, to be honest with, to confront directly
6. **Usage description:** Used to describe the act of speaking honestly and directly with another person. The applicative suffix *-an* marks the person addressed as the object of the action.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8.3**

1. **Base Form:** tapat
2. **Derived Form:** maging tapat
3. **Transliteration:** maging tapat
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *maging* (infinitive/linking verb "to be/become") + *tapat*
5. **Natural English translation:** to be honest, to be sincere, to be straightforward
6. **Usage description:** The standard verbal construction for expressing the state or act of being honest. *Maging tapat* is used in moral exhortations and personal commitments (*Maging tapat ka sa akin* — "Be honest with me").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *tapat*:** The form *maging tapat* (8.3) is a periphrastic construction rather than a morphological derivation in the strict sense. It is included here because it represents the primary verbal expression of the root's meaning and is not replaceable by a morphologically derived verb form. The root *tapat* also has a spatial meaning ("directly facing, opposite") that generates additional collocations unrelated to the honesty/sincerity domain; those are not documented here as they fall outside the scope of the P1 semantic field.

## Summary of Derivational Patterns Observed

The following patterns are directly supported by the forms documented above:

1. **The circumfix *ka-...-an* / *ka-...-han*** is the most consistently attested nominalizing mechanism across this semantic field, generating abstract nouns from adjectival roots: *katotohanan*, *katunayan*, *katapatan*, *kamalian*, *kawastuhan*, *kasiguruhan*, *katumpakan*, *kaangkupan*. This pattern applies to both indigenous roots and integrated borrowings.

2. **The prefix *i-*** generates causative/instrumental verbs from adjectival roots: *itama*, *iwasto*, *iakma*, *iaangkop*. These verbs typically mean "to cause [root state] to obtain; to correct/adjust toward [root state]."

3. **The prefix *pa-* combined with *-an*** generates applicative verbs meaning "to cause to be [root state] for/to someone": *patunayan* and its aspect-marked forms (*mapatunayan*, *napatunayan*, *pinatunayan*).

4. **The prefix *pag-*** combined with root material generates verbal nouns: *pagpapatunay*, *pagwawasto*, *pagsisinungaling*, *pagkakamali*. These are the standard nominalizations of verbal actions.

5. **The suffix *-hin*** generates object-focus verbs from nominal/adjectival roots: *tiwalahin*, *siguraduhin*. This is a regular pattern for verbalizing roots where the object of the action is foregrounded.

6. **Borrowed forms** (*peke*, *huwad*, *ebidensya*, *awtentiko*, *kredible*) show limited or no productive derivation through native Tagalog morphology in this semantic field, with the partial exception of *sigurado* (which has generated *kasiguruhan* and *siguraduhin*) and the marginal case of *lehitimasyon* from *lehitimo*.

7. **Gaps are preserved:** Several base forms (*huwad*, *peke*, *ebidensya*, *awtentiko*, *kredible*) show no attested derivations. The absence of derivation for these forms is not treated as a deficiency to be filled but as a genuine feature of the data.
