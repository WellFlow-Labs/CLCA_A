# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents how Sanskrit encodes dynamic truth-related meanings — becoming, ceasing, causing, and stabilizing — using only vocabulary attested in the CONTEXT (P1 through P5). The analysis proceeds term by term where behavior differs, and by construction type where a pattern applies across multiple items. Asymmetries are preserved throughout: some items participate in dynamic constructions; others do not. Where no common expression exists, this is stated explicitly.

Sanskrit is a highly synthetic language. Many dynamic meanings that analytic languages express periphrastically are handled in Sanskrit through morphological causatives, compounding, or participial constructions. This structural fact shapes the inventory below.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1a. Arising of valid cognition: *pramā jāyate*

**Pattern:** [Truth-related nominal in nominative] + *jāyate* (from root *jan-*, "to be born, to arise")

**Attested example:**
- प्रमा जायते — *pramā jāyate* — "Valid cognition arises / is produced"

**Source:** P2b Construction 20; P4a §4; P4b table entry.

**Notes:** This is the standard formula in *Nyāya* literature for the production of valid cognition as the result of a successful *pramāṇa*. P4b explicitly states that *jāyate* is "not freely substitutable with *bhavati* here" — the verb *jāyate* is the conventional verb for the arising of a cognitive state specifically. The subject is the cognitive result (*pramā*), not the agent.

**Scope:** Restricted to cognitive-state arising. Not attested with *satya*, *ṛta*, *dharma*, or other non-cognitive truth-domain items in this construction.

---

### 1b. Becoming an authority: *pramāṇa-bhūta*

**Pattern:** [Truth-domain nominal] + *bhūta* (past passive participle of root *bhū-*, "to become") as a lexicalized compound

**Attested example:**
- प्रमाणभूतः — *pramāṇabhūtaḥ* — "one who has become an authority / a recognized standard"

**Source:** P1b Root 2 item 3; P2a #21; P4a §4; P4b table entry.

**Notes:** P4b notes that "compounding is the preferred strategy over periphrasis" for this inchoative meaning. The compound *pramāṇa-bhūta* lexicalizes the result of the becoming; the periphrastic *pramāṇaṃ bhavati* ("becomes a *pramāṇa*") is structurally available in Sanskrit but the compound form is the attested one in this domain.

**Scope:** Attested for *pramāṇa*. The *-bhūta* compounding pattern is productive in Sanskrit generally, but no parallel *satya-bhūta*, *dharma-bhūta*, or similar forms are documented in the CONTEXT for this semantic function.

---

### 1c. Becoming established in a truth-related state: *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ*

**Pattern:** Locative of truth-domain nominal + *pratiṣṭhita* (past passive participle of *prati-* + root *sthā-*, "to stand firm, to be established")

**Attested examples:**
- सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः — *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ* — "established in truth; grounded in truth" (P2b #5)
- धर्मे स्थितः — *dharme sthitaḥ* — "established in *dharma*; standing firm in duty" (P2b #26)

**Source:** P2b Constructions 5 and 26; P3 Group 1 and Group 2.

**Notes:** These participial constructions describe a stable resultant state rather than a process of becoming. The past passive participle (*pratiṣṭhita*, *sthita*) encodes the completed transition into the state. The locative of the truth-domain nominal specifies the state in which the subject is established. P3 describes *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ* as "a characterization of the sage or the virtuous person" — a stable achieved state.

**Scope:** Attested for *satya* and *dharma*. The pattern is structurally available for other locative-taking nominals but no further instances are documented in the CONTEXT.

---

### 1d. Becoming determined as *mithyā*: *mithyeti niścitam*

**Pattern:** *mithyā* + *iti* (quotative/predicative particle) + *niścita* (past passive participle of *ni-* + root *ci-*, "to determine, to ascertain")

**Attested example:**
- मिथ्येति निश्चितम् — *mithyeti niścitam* — "It is determined to be false/illusory; it is established as *mithyā*" (P2b #67)

**Source:** P2b Construction 67; P3 Group 5.

**Notes:** This construction encodes the epistemic transition by which something comes to be classified as *mithyā* — the Advaita Vedāntic category of indeterminate ontological status. The *iti* particle marks the predicated characterization; *niścita* encodes the completed determination. This is a becoming-classified construction rather than a becoming-in-the-world construction.

**Scope:** Attested for *mithyā* specifically. The *iti niścitam* pattern is structurally available for other predicates but no parallel instances with other truth-domain items are documented in the CONTEXT.

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH / OPPOSED STATE

### 2a. Deviation from truth or dharma: *dharmāt na calati* / *satyāt na pramaditavyam*

**Pattern:** Ablative of truth-domain nominal + verb of deviation (*cal-*, "to move away"; *pramad-*, "to be negligent, to deviate")

**Attested examples:**
- धर्मात् न चलति — *dharmāt na calati* — "He/she does not deviate from *dharma*" (P2b #27)
- सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम् — *satyāt na pramaditavyam* — "One must not deviate from truth" (P2b #6)
- न्यायात् न विचलति — *nyāyāt na vicalati* — "He/she does not deviate from right method/justice" (P2b #49)

**Source:** P2b Constructions 6, 27, 49; P4a §9; P4b table entry; P5 Opposition 34.

**Notes:** These constructions encode the potential transition away from a truth-related state, consistently in negated form in the attested data. The ablative of separation with verbs of movement (*cal-*, *vical-*, *pramad-*) is the standard Sanskrit construction for expressing deviation from a norm. P5 Opposition 34 notes that the gerundive form (*pramaditavyam*) expresses stronger prohibition than the optative. The positive (non-negated) form — "he deviates from *dharma*" — is structurally available but the negated form is what is documented as formulaic in the CONTEXT.

**Scope:** Attested for *satya*, *dharma*, *nyāya*. The ablative-of-separation pattern is structurally available for other truth-domain nominals but no further formulaic instances are documented.

---

### 2b. Envelopment in falsehood: *anṛtena parivṛttaḥ*

**Pattern:** Instrumental of falsehood-domain nominal + *parivṛtta* (past passive participle of *pari-* + root *vṛt-*, "to surround, to envelop")

**Attested example:**
- अनृतेन परिवृत्तः — *anṛtena parivṛttaḥ* — "Surrounded by / enveloped in falsehood" (P2b #12)

**Source:** P2b Construction 12; P3 Group 2.

**Notes:** P2b marks this construction as [MARGINAL] and describes it as used "in Vedic and post-Vedic literature to characterize a person or state as encompassed by falsehood or disorder." The instrumental of *anṛta* with *parivṛtta* encodes a resultant state of being encompassed by the opposed condition. This is a state-description rather than a process description, but it implies a transition into that state.

**Scope:** Attested for *anṛta* only. No parallel construction with *asatya*, *mithyā*, or *adharma* is documented in the CONTEXT.

---

### 2c. Prospering through wrongdoing: *adharmeṇa vardhate*

**Pattern:** Instrumental of *adharma* + *vardhate* (from root *vṛdh-*, "to grow, to prosper")

**Attested example:**
- अधर्मेण वर्धते — *adharmeṇa vardhate* — "He/she prospers through *adharma* (wrongdoing)" (P2b #29)

**Source:** P2b Construction 29; P3 Group 2.

**Notes:** P2b notes this is attested in *Manusmṛti* 4.174 "in the context of a longer passage about the apparent but ultimately illusory prosperity of the unrighteous." The instrumental of *adharma* encodes the means or instrument of the change of state (prospering). This is a dynamic construction encoding a change of state (*vardhate*) brought about through the opposed truth-domain condition.

**Scope:** Attested for *adharma* only in this construction.

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 3a. Morphological causative: *gamaya* (leading from unreal to real)

**Pattern:** Causative imperative of *gam-* (*gamaya*, "cause to go / lead") with ablative of source (*asataḥ*) and accusative of goal (*sat*)

**Attested example:**
- असतो मा सद्गमय — *asato mā sad gamaya* — "Lead me from the unreal to the real" (P2b #33/34; P4a §5)

**Source:** P2b Constructions 33/34; P4a §5; P4b table entry; P5 Opposition 30.

**Notes:** P4a identifies this as a morphological causative (not periphrastic); P4b notes it is a "fixed liturgical formula from *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*" and that "no periphrastic alternate is attested for this formula." The causative encodes an agent (the deity addressed) causing a subject (*mā*, "me") to transition from the ontological state of *asat* to the ontological state of *sat*. This is the most directly attested causative of a truth-related ontological transition in the CONTEXT.

**Scope:** This specific formula is fixed and liturgical. The morphological causative pattern is fully productive in Sanskrit but no other causative of a truth-domain ontological transition is documented as formulaic in the CONTEXT.

---

### 3b. Denominative causative: *satyāpayati* (verifying / authenticating)

**Pattern:** Denominative causative from *satya* (*satyāpayati*, "causes to be true / verifies")

**Attested example:**
- सत्यापयति — *satyāpayati* — "He/she verifies / authenticates" (P4a §5; P2a #13)

**Source:** P2a #13 (*satyāpana*); P4a §5; P4b table entry.

**Notes:** P4b marks this as [MARGINAL] and notes it is "productive in legal and administrative contexts; not attested as a general philosophical or literary formula." The denominative causative *satyāpayati* encodes causing something to be established as true or verified. The corresponding action noun *satyāpana* ("verification, authentication") and past passive participle *satyāpita* ("verified, authenticated") are also documented in P2a (#13, #5).

**Scope:** Attested for *satya* in legal and administrative contexts. No parallel denominative causative from *ṛta*, *dharma*, *pramāṇa*, or other truth-domain items is documented in the CONTEXT.

---

### 3c. Causing valid cognition: *pramāyāḥ karaṇaṃ pramāṇam*

**Pattern:** Genitive of *pramā* + *karaṇa* (instrument, from root *kṛ-*) + *pramāṇa* as predicate nominal

**Attested example:**
- प्रमायाः करणं प्रमाणम् — *pramāyāḥ karaṇaṃ pramāṇam* — "The instrument of valid cognition is the *pramāṇa*" (P2b #21)

**Source:** P2b Construction 21; P4a §4.

**Notes:** This is a definitional construction encoding the causal-instrumental relationship between *pramāṇa* (the means) and *pramā* (the result). It does not use a verbal causative but encodes causation through the nominal *karaṇa* (instrument). P2b describes it as "a standard technical formula in the epistemological literature." The construction identifies what causes valid cognition to arise rather than describing the process of causing it.

**Scope:** Specific to the *pramāṇa* / *pramā* relationship. No parallel *karaṇa* construction is documented for other truth-domain pairs in the CONTEXT.

---

### 3d. Causing liberation through truth-vision: *tattva-darśanena mokṣaḥ*

**Pattern:** Instrumental of *tattva-darśana* (seeing/vision of the real nature) + *mokṣa* (liberation) as subject

**Attested example:**
- तत्त्वदर्शनेन मोक्षः — *tattva-darśanena mokṣaḥ* — "Liberation through the vision of truth/reality" (P2b #45)

**Source:** P2b Construction 45; P3 Group 8.

**Notes:** The instrumental of *tattva-darśana* encodes the means by which liberation (*mokṣa*) is caused or brought about. This is a nominal causative construction (instrumental of means) rather than a verbal causative. P2b describes it as "a standard formula in soteriological literature." The construction encodes the causal role of truth-cognition in producing a soteriological outcome.

**Scope:** Attested for *tattva* in Vedāntic soteriological contexts. No parallel construction with *satya*, *pramā*, or other truth-domain items producing *mokṣa* is documented in the CONTEXT.

---

### 3e. Causing the removal of false knowledge: *samyag-jñānena mithyājñānaṃ nivartate*

**Pattern:** Instrumental of correct-knowledge nominal + *mithyā-jñāna* as subject + *nivartate* (from *ni-* + root *vṛt-*, "to turn back, to cease")

**Attested example:**
- मिथ्याज्ञानं निवर्तते सम्यग्ज्ञानेन — *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate samyag-jñānena* — "False knowledge is removed by correct knowledge" (P2b #68)

**Source:** P2b Construction 68; P3 Group 4; P5 Opposition 28.

**Notes:** The instrumental of *samyag-jñāna* (correct knowledge — not a P1 item but attested in P2b as the explicit counterpart of *mithyā-jñāna*) encodes the agent or instrument that causes the cessation of false knowledge. The verb *nivartate* encodes the cessation of the false state. P2b describes this as "a standard formula in Advaita Vedānta." The construction encodes causation of a transition out of a false cognitive state.

**Scope:** Attested for *mithyā-jñāna* in Advaita Vedāntic contexts. The parallel construction *bādhena bhrāntiḥ nivartate* (see §4a below) encodes the same causal structure for *bhrānti*.

## 4. CESSATION OF A TRUTH-OPPOSED STATE

### 4a. Cessation of illusion: *bhrāntiḥ nivartate* / *bādhena bhrāntiḥ nivartate*

**Pattern:** *bhrānti* as subject + *nivartate* (from *ni-* + root *vṛt-*, "to turn back, to cease"); optionally with instrumental of *bādhā* (sublation) as the causal instrument

**Attested examples:**
- भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते — *bhrāntiḥ nivartate* — "The illusion ceases; the error is removed" (P2b #58)
- बाधेन भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते — *bādhena bhrāntiḥ nivartate* — "By sublation/contradiction, the illusion ceases" (P2b #59)

**Source:** P2b Constructions 58 and 59; P3 Group 4; P5 Opposition 27.

**Notes:** P2b describes *nivartate* as "the conventional verb for the removal of error in this literature." The two constructions encode (a) the cessation of illusion without specifying a cause, and (b) the cessation of illusion caused by *bādhā* (sublation — the overriding of a cognition by stronger contrary evidence). P2b describes the second as "the mechanism by which cognitive error is removed" in *Nyāya* and Vedāntic epistemology.

**Scope:** Attested for *bhrānti* with *nivartate*. The parallel construction *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate* (§3e above) uses the same verb for *mithyā-jñāna*, indicating that *nivartate* is the general conventional verb for the cessation of false cognitive states in this literature.

---

### 4b. Cessation of erroneous cognition through sublation: *bādhita*

**Pattern:** *bādhita* (past passive participle of root *bādh-*, "to obstruct, to contradict") as predicate adjective

**Attested example:**
- (cognition) बाधितम् — *bādhitam* — "contradicted, sublated, overridden (by a stronger cognition or evidence)" (P1b Root 2 #23; P2a #23)

**Source:** P1b Root 2 item 23; P3 Group 3.

**Notes:** P1b describes *bādhita* as characterizing "a cognition that has been overridden or shown to be erroneous by a subsequent, stronger cognition," with the classic example being "the sublation of the rope-snake illusion upon closer inspection." The past passive participle encodes the completed transition from an erroneous to a corrected cognitive state. P1b notes this is "closely associated with the *pramāṇa* framework."

**Scope:** Attested as a predicate adjective applied to cognitions. The corresponding action noun *bādhā* (P1b Root 2 #24) encodes the process itself.

---

### 4c. Cessation of erroneous cognition: *viparyayāt nivṛttiḥ*

**Pattern:** Ablative of *viparyaya* + *nivṛtti* (cessation, from *ni-* + root *vṛt-*)

**Attested example:**
- विपर्ययात् निवृत्तिः — *viparyayāt nivṛttiḥ* — "Cessation from erroneous cognition; freedom from wrong knowledge" (P2b #61)

**Source:** P2b Construction 61; P3 Group 4.

**Notes:** P2b marks this as [MARGINAL] and describes it as used "in *Sāṃkhya-Yoga* literature to describe the goal of overcoming the five types of mental modification (*vṛtti*)." The ablative of *viparyaya* with *nivṛtti* encodes the cessation of the erroneous cognitive state as a goal. The nominal construction (*nivṛtti* rather than *nivartate*) is used here rather than the verbal construction in §4a.

**Scope:** Attested for *viparyaya* in *Sāṃkhya-Yoga* contexts.

## 5. STABILITY IN A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 5a. Abiding in one's own nature: *svarūpeṇa avatiṣṭhate*

**Pattern:** Instrumental of *svarūpa* + *avatiṣṭhate* (from *ava-* + root *sthā-*, "to stand, to abide")

**Attested example:**
- स्वरूपेण अवतिष्ठते — *svarūpeṇa avatiṣṭhate* — "It stands/abides in its own nature; it exists as it truly is" (P2b #40)

**Source:** P2b Construction 40; P3 Group 8.

**Notes:** P2b notes the *Yogasūtra* 1.3 formula *svarūpe 'vasthānam* (locative variant: "the seer's abiding in its own nature") as the more precisely attested form. The construction encodes stable self-identity in one's true nature — the condition of not being displaced into a false or distorted state. This is the positive counterpart to the deviation constructions in §2a.

**Scope:** Attested for *svarūpa* in *Yoga* philosophical contexts.

---

### 5b. Established in truth: *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ* (cross-reference)

Already documented under §1c above. The same construction (*pratiṣṭhita* + locative) encodes both the transition into and the stable maintenance of a truth-related state; the past passive participle is ambiguous between the two readings, and P2b's description ("firmly grounded in truth") emphasizes the stable state.

---

### 5c. Protecting and being protected by dharma: *dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ*

**Pattern:** *dharma* as subject + *rakṣati* (from root *rakṣ-*, "to protect") + *rakṣita* (past passive participle, "protected") as predicate adjective

**Attested example:**
- धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः — *dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ* — "*Dharma* protects the one who protects it" (P2b #25)

**Source:** P2b Construction 25; P3 Group 2.

**Notes:** P2b describes this as "a well-known maxim from *Manusmṛti* (8.15)." The construction encodes a reciprocal stability relationship: maintaining *dharma* (the truth-related state) causes *dharma* to maintain the agent in turn. This is a dynamic stability construction — the truth-related state is not static but actively sustained through reciprocal action.

**Scope:** Attested for *dharma* only in this specific proverbial form.

## 6. ITEMS FOR WHICH NO DYNAMIC CONSTRUCTION IS DOCUMENTED

The following P1 items appear in the CONTEXT only in static attributive, predicative, or classificatory constructions. No becoming, causing, or ceasing construction is documented for them in the CONTEXT data:

- **ऋत / *ṛta*** (#2): Appears in Vedic epithets, formulaic pairings, and path-expressions; no dynamic construction documented.
- **यथार्थ / *yathārtha*** (#7) and **अयथार्थ / *ayathārtha*** (#8): Appear as attributive adjectives modifying *jñāna*; no becoming or causing construction documented.
- **वास्तव / *vāstava*** (#13): Appears as an attributive adjective; no dynamic construction documented.
- **वस्तु / *vastu*** (#14): Appears in adverbial (*vastutaḥ*) and compound forms; no becoming or causing construction documented beyond the *vastu puruṣatantraṃ na bhavati* formula (P2b #39), which is a static denial rather than a dynamic construction.
- **तत्त्व / *tattva*** (#16): Appears in adverbial (*tattvataḥ*), compound, and soteriological constructions; the *tattva-darśanena mokṣaḥ* construction (§3d) is the only dynamic instance documented.
- **औचित्य / *aucitya*** (#17): Appears in prescriptive and definitional constructions; no becoming or causing construction documented.
- **न्याय / *nyāya*** (#18): Appears in instrumental (*nyāyena siddham*) and ablative (*nyāyāt na vicalati*) constructions; the ablative construction is documented under §2a.
- **साधु / *sādhu*** (#19) and **असाधु / *asādhu*** (#20): Appear in grammatical evaluation formulas; no dynamic construction documented.
- **प्रतिभास / *pratibhāsa*** (#21): Appears in predicative and ontological constructions; no becoming or causing construction documented.
- **विपर्यय / *viparyaya*** (#23): The cessation construction is documented under §4c; no becoming or causing construction documented.
- **आभास / *ābhāsa*** (#24): Appears in denial formulas (*ābhāsamātram na vastu*); no dynamic construction documented.
- **शुद्ध / *śuddha*** (#25): The prescriptive *śuddhiḥ kriyatām* (P2b #65) encodes an action to be performed but is a normative injunction rather than a description of a dynamic process; no becoming or causing construction documented.
- **कृत्रिम / *kṛtrima*** (#27) and **अकृत्रिम / *akṛtrima*** (#28): Appear only as attributive adjectives; no dynamic construction documented.
- **साक्षात् / *sākṣāt*** (#29): Appears as an adverb in perceptual and experiential constructions; no dynamic construction documented.
- **यथावत् / *yathāvat*** (#30): Appears as an adverb in descriptive and normative constructions; no dynamic construction documented.
