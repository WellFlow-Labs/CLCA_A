# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Korean (ko)

## Lexical Items

1. **진실** (jinsil)
   - Literal: "true + real/actual"
   - Typical translations: truth, truthfulness, reality
   - Used to denote factual accuracy and genuine state of affairs. Commonly appears in both everyday and formal contexts when discussing what is actually the case or what corresponds to reality.
   - [CORE]

2. **거짓** (geojit)
   - Literal: "false/fake"
   - Typical translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
   - Refers to statements or representations that are false or deceptive. Used as both noun and adjectival modifier in expressions like "거짓말" (lie, literally "false speech").
   - [CORE]

3. **사실** (sasil)
   - Literal: "matter + real/actual"
   - Typical translations: fact, truth, reality
   - Denotes objective facts or actual circumstances. Frequently used in discourse to mark statements as factual or to introduce verifiable information.
   - [CORE]

4. **진짜** (jinjja)
   - Literal: etymology unclear, possibly "true + thing"
   - Typical translations: real, genuine, authentic
   - Colloquial term for genuineness or authenticity, often contrasted with fake or imitation. Also used as intensifier in informal speech.
   - [CORE]

5. **가짜** (gajja)
   - Literal: "false + thing"
   - Typical translations: fake, counterfeit, imitation
   - Refers to objects or representations that are not genuine or authentic. Commonly used for counterfeit goods or false identities.
   - [CORE]

6. **정확하다** (jeonghwakhada)
   - Literal: "correct/precise + to be"
   - Typical translations: accurate, precise, exact
   - Describes statements, measurements, or representations that correspond exactly to reality or standards. Used across technical, academic, and everyday contexts.
   - [CORE]

7. **옳다** (olda)
   - Literal: basic morpheme
   - Typical translations: right, correct, proper
   - Evaluates correctness in moral, logical, or procedural senses. Used for both factual correctness and ethical rightness.
   - [CORE]

8. **그르다** (geureuda)
   - Literal: basic morpheme
   - Typical translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken
   - Indicates error or incorrectness in judgment, action, or statement. Less common in modern usage than its negative form "그르지 않다" or alternatives.
   - [MARGINAL]

9. **틀리다** (teullida)
   - Literal: "to differ/deviate"
   - Typical translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken
   - Indicates factual error or deviation from correct answer or standard. Commonly used in educational and evaluative contexts.
   - [CORE]

10. **맞다** (matda)
    - Literal: "to fit/match"
    - Typical translations: correct, right, accurate
    - Indicates correctness or correspondence with facts or standards. Used for answers, statements, and physical fit or match.
    - [CORE]

11. **정직** (jeongjik)
    - Literal: "upright + straight"
    - Typical translations: honesty, integrity, uprightness
    - Refers to moral quality of truthfulness and straightforwardness in conduct. Used primarily in ethical and character evaluation contexts.
    - [CORE]

12. **솔직** (soljik)
    - Literal: "straightforward + direct"
    - Typical translations: frank, honest, candid
    - Describes direct and truthful communication without concealment. Emphasizes openness in expression rather than moral character per se.
    - [CORE]

13. **정당하다** (jeongdanghada)
    - Literal: "correct/proper + appropriate + to be"
    - Typical translations: legitimate, justified, rightful
    - Evaluates actions, claims, or positions as properly grounded or morally/legally justified. Common in legal, political, and ethical discourse.
    - [CORE]

14. **타당하다** (tadanghada)
    - Literal: "appropriate + fitting + to be"
    - Typical translations: valid, reasonable, sound
    - Assesses logical validity or reasonableness of arguments, claims, or decisions. Used in analytical and argumentative contexts.
    - [CORE]

15. **진정** (jinjeong)
    - Literal: "true + genuine"
    - Typical translations: genuine, sincere, true
    - Emphasizes authenticity of feelings, intentions, or nature. Often used to distinguish genuine from superficial or pretended states.
    - [CORE]

16. **허위** (heowi)
    - Literal: "empty/false + false"
    - Typical translations: false, fraudulent, untrue
    - Formal term for falsehood, especially in legal contexts. Commonly appears in compounds like "허위사실" (false facts) or "허위진술" (false testimony).
    - [CORE]

17. **참** (cham)
    - Literal: basic morpheme
    - Typical translations: true, real, genuine
    - Native Korean word for truth or genuineness. Used both independently and as prefix meaning "true/real" in compounds.
    - [CORE]

18. **거짓말** (geojinmal)
    - Literal: "false + speech"
    - Typical translations: lie, falsehood
    - Standard term for deliberate false statement. Most common everyday word for lying in Korean.
    - [CORE]

19. **진위** (jinwi)
    - Literal: "true + false"
    - Typical translations: truth or falsehood, authenticity
    - Formal term used when discussing verification of truth versus falsehood. Common in phrases like "진위를 가리다" (to determine truth from falsehood).
    - [CORE]

20. **실제** (silje)
    - Literal: "real/actual + occasion/instance"
    - Typical translations: reality, actuality, practice
    - Refers to actual state of affairs as opposed to theory or appearance. Often contrasts with hypothetical or nominal situations.
    - [CORE]

21. **본질** (bonjil)
    - Literal: "root/origin + quality/substance"
    - Typical translations: essence, nature, substance
    - Philosophical and analytical term for fundamental nature or essential characteristics. Used when discussing what something fundamentally is.
    - [CORE]

22. **진실성** (jinsilseong)
    - Literal: "truth + quality/nature"
    - Typical translations: truthfulness, veracity, authenticity
    - Abstract noun denoting the quality of being truthful or genuine. Used in formal evaluation of statements or character.
    - [CORE]

23. **신빙성** (sinbingseong)
    - Literal: "trust/believe + rely + quality"
    - Typical translations: credibility, reliability, trustworthiness
    - Evaluates whether statements, sources, or evidence can be trusted or believed. Common in journalistic and investigative contexts.
    - [CORE]

24. **확실하다** (hwaksilhada)
    - Literal: "firm/certain + real + to be"
    - Typical translations: certain, sure, definite
    - Indicates high degree of certainty or reliability. Used for both epistemic certainty and factual definiteness.
    - [CORE]

25. **명백하다** (myeongbaekhada)
    - Literal: "bright/clear + white + to be"
    - Typical translations: obvious, clear, evident
    - Describes facts or truths that are clearly apparent or indisputable. Used when evidence or reality is unmistakable.
    - [CORE]

26. **그럴듯하다** (geureoldeuthada)
    - Literal: "be so + seem + to be"
    - Typical translations: plausible, likely, probable
    - Indicates appearance of truth or reasonableness without confirmation. Can suggest something seems true but may not be.
    - [CORE]

27. **진본** (jinbon)
    - Literal: "true + original/root"
    - Typical translations: original, authentic copy, genuine article
    - Refers to authentic original as opposed to copy or forgery. Used primarily for documents, artworks, and artifacts.
    - [CORE]

28. **모조** (mojo)
    - Literal: "imitate + make"
    - Typical translations: imitation, counterfeit, replica
    - Denotes deliberately made copies or imitations. Often used for counterfeit goods or reproductions.
    - [CORE]
