# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

**1. egia**
- **Form:** egia
- **Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
- **Literal meaning:** truth (nominal form; etymology debated, possibly related to *egi-* "real, true")
- **Typical English translations:** truth, true thing, reality
- **Usage:** The primary nominal term for truth in Basque, used across everyday, philosophical, and moral contexts. Appears in constructions such as *egia esan* ("to tell the truth") and *egia da* ("it is true"). Participates in extensive derivational patterns.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2. egiazkoa**
- **Form:** egiazkoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egia* (truth) + *-zko* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article); "of truth, belonging to truth"
- **Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, authentic
- **Usage:** Adjectival form derived from *egia*, used to describe statements, persons, or things as truthful or genuine. Commonly appears in attributive and predicative positions. Distinguishable from bare *egia* by its explicitly adjectival function.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3. benetakoa**
- **Form:** benetakoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *bene-* (possibly from Latin *bene*, "well/good") + *-tako* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic, true
- **Usage:** Used to assert that something is the real or authentic instance of its kind, as opposed to a copy, imitation, or pretense. Frequently used in everyday speech to emphasize authenticity of objects, feelings, or persons. Often interchangeable with *egiazkoa* but with a stronger connotation of genuineness over propositional truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4. gezurra**
- **Form:** gezurra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (nominal form; etymology unclear)
- **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
- **Usage:** The primary term for a lie or falsehood, used in both everyday and moral contexts. Appears in constructions such as *gezurra esan* ("to tell a lie") and *gezurra da* ("it is false/a lie"). Forms a clear semantic opposition with *egia*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5. gezurrezkoa**
- **Form:** gezurrezkoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *gezurr-* (lie) + *-ezko* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** false, lying, deceitful, fake
- **Usage:** Adjectival form derived from *gezurra*, used to characterize statements, persons, or representations as false or deceptive. Parallels the derivational pattern of *egiazkoa*. Used in both moral and descriptive contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**6. zuzena**
- **Form:** zuzena
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *zuzen* (straight, direct, correct, right) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** correct, right, just, upright, straight
- **Usage:** A semantically broad term covering correctness of statements or calculations, moral rightness of conduct, and legal or procedural justice. Used predicatively (*hori zuzena da*, "that is correct/right") and attributively. Participates in legal and ethical discourse extensively.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**7. okerra**
- **Form:** okerra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *oker* (crooked, wrong, mistaken) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** wrong, mistaken, crooked, erroneous
- **Usage:** The primary antonym of *zuzena*, used to describe errors, moral wrongs, and factual incorrectness. Appears in constructions such as *oker nago* ("I am wrong/mistaken") and *bide okerra* ("the wrong path"). Used across everyday, moral, and epistemic contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**8. zehatza**

- **Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate, thorough
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, measurements, descriptions, or accounts as accurate and precise. Commonly appears in technical, scientific, and journalistic registers. Implies a close correspondence between a representation and its object.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**9. fidagarria**
- **Form:** fidagarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *fida-* (trust, from Romance *fiar*) + *-garri* (potential/worthy suffix) + *-a* (definite article); "worthy of trust"
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable
- **Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, or information to indicate that they can be trusted or relied upon. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The suffix *-garri* marks it as a productive derivational form.

**10. sinesgarria**

**13. zilegi**

**14. faltsua**

Commonly used in legal contexts (e.g., *dokumentu faltsua*, "forged document") and everyday speech. Partially overlaps with *gezurrezkoa* but more often applied to objects and identities than to propositions.

**15. autentikoa**

- **Usage:** Used in legal, artistic, and everyday contexts to assert that something is the genuine original rather than a copy or forgery. Appears alongside *benetakoa* in some contexts, though *autentikoa* tends toward formal and legal registers. Common in discussions of documents, artworks, and identity.

**27. itxuraz**

- **Typical English translations:** apparently, seemingly, ostensibly, on the surface
- **Usage:** An adverbial form used to hedge a claim by indicating that something appears to be the case without asserting that it genuinely is. Relevant to epistemic evaluations where appearance and reality may diverge. Common in both spoken and written Basque.
