# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**
**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 participation:** 8 items — 真, 真实, 真相, 真理, 真伪, 真诚, 名副其实, 原形毕露 (participates via semantic field; 实 is the more direct morpheme in the last two, but 真 is the conceptual anchor of the domain)
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts genuine correspondence between a thing and what it claims or appears to be; the primary positive pole of the truth-evaluation domain. Functions as a free morpheme and as a highly productive bound root.

---

**Root 2**
**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 participation:** 10 items — 真实, 事实, 如实, 失实, 属实, 名副其实, 诚实, 信实, 实质, 本质 (participates in 本质 via the semantic field; directly present as morpheme in the others)
**Core meaning contribution:** Contributes the sense of solidity, actuality, and correspondence to what is the case; marks the factual or substantive dimension of truth-evaluation. Appears both as a free morpheme and as a highly productive bound element.

---

**Root 3**
**Form:** 假/伪 (jiǎ/wěi)
**P1 participation:** 7 items — 假, 虚假, 假冒, 仿冒, 伪造, 真伪, 虚伪
**Core meaning contribution:** These two roots function as the negative pole of the domain, marking falsity, spuriousness, and fraudulent misrepresentation. They frequently co-occur (as in 假冒伪劣, 真伪) and overlap substantially in distribution. They are treated together here because their semantic ranges are closely intertwined and their compounds partially overlap; they are distinguished where relevant in Step 2.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: 真 (zhēn)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** truthful, authentic, real, factual
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Describes the quality of correspondence to fact or reality; used in journalism, law, and literary criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance/aspect
**Natural English translation:** the truth, the true facts, the real situation
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Refers to the actual state of affairs behind appearances; common in investigative and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal breakdown:** true + principle/reason
**Natural English translation:** truth (as a universal principle)
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Used in philosophical, ideological, and rhetorical contexts to refer to abstract or universal truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true/genuine + sincere
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Describes genuine sincerity in feeling or intention; applied to persons, apologies, and relationships.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal breakdown:** true + false/spurious
**Natural English translation:** genuine vs. fake, authenticity vs. forgery
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. A binary nominal used in authentication, legal, and documentary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal breakdown:** true + upright/correct
**Natural English translation:** genuine, real, truly, actually
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective or adverb to assert that something is the authentic or actual instance of a category, as opposed to something nominal or superficial. Extremely common across all registers: 真正的朋友 (a true friend), 真正的问题 (the real problem).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** true + false/fake
**Natural English translation:** real vs. fake, genuine vs. counterfeit
**Context of use:** A binary nominal or adjectival compound used to frame the question of authenticity. Appears in constructions like 分不清真假 (unable to tell real from fake) and 真假难辨 (hard to distinguish genuine from fake). Common in everyday, commercial, and evaluative speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 真心
**Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
**Literal breakdown:** true + heart/mind
**Natural English translation:** sincere, wholehearted, genuinely
**Context of use:** Describes sincerity of feeling or intention, often in interpersonal and emotional contexts. Appears in 真心话 (words from the heart, what one truly thinks) and 真心实意 (with genuine sincerity). Common in everyday and literary speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 真话
**Transliteration:** zhēnhuà
**Literal breakdown:** true + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, truthful words, what one really thinks
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that reflects what the speaker genuinely believes or knows to be the case. Contrasts with 假话 (false words, lies) and 套话 (formulaic empty talk). Common in everyday and political discourse: 说真话 (to tell the truth, to speak honestly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 真假难辨
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ nán biàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + false + difficult + distinguish
**Natural English translation:** hard to tell genuine from fake, indistinguishable from the real thing
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used to describe situations where authentic and inauthentic versions of something are extremely difficult to differentiate. Used in everyday, commercial, and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 真知灼见
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhī zhuójiàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge + clear/penetrating + view/insight
**Natural English translation:** genuine insight, penetrating and well-founded views
**Context of use:** A four-character fixed expression (成语) used to praise opinions or analyses that are both accurate and deeply perceptive. Common in intellectual, journalistic, and evaluative writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 真凭实据
**Transliteration:** zhēnpíng shíjù
**Literal breakdown:** true + evidence/proof + solid/real + evidence/basis
**Natural English translation:** solid evidence, concrete proof
**Context of use:** A fixed expression emphasizing that evidence or proof is genuine and substantial. Used in legal, investigative, and argumentative contexts to assert that claims are well-supported.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 真才实学
**Transliteration:** zhēncái shíxué
**Literal breakdown:** true + talent + solid/real + learning/knowledge
**Natural English translation:** genuine talent and solid learning, real ability
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to evaluate whether a person's abilities and knowledge are authentic rather than superficial or merely claimed. Common in educational and professional evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 真实性
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + nature/property (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, veracity, truthfulness (as a property)
**Context of use:** The nominalized form of 真实, used to refer to the property of being truthful or authentic. Appears in assessments of evidence, testimony, documents, and representations: 核实真实性 (verify the authenticity). Common in legal, journalistic, and academic registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 真相大白
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng dàbái
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance/aspect + greatly + clear/white
**Natural English translation:** the truth comes to light, the facts are fully revealed
**Context of use:** A fixed expression describing the moment when the actual facts of a matter become fully known after a period of concealment or uncertainty. Common in journalistic, narrative, and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 认真
**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious; to take seriously
**Context of use:** Describes an attitude of careful, earnest engagement with a task or matter. Extremely common across all registers: 认真工作 (work conscientiously), 认真对待 (take seriously). Also used as a verb: 别认真 (don't take it seriously / don't be so literal about it).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 当真
**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** treat as + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to take seriously, to take as true, to mean it
**Context of use:** Used to indicate that someone treats something as genuinely true or serious, often in contexts where this is unexpected or inadvisable. Contrasts with treating something as a joke: 你别当真 (don't take it seriously). Common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 逼真
**Transliteration:** bīzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** press toward/approach + true/real
**Natural English translation:** lifelike, realistic, true to life, vivid
**Context of use:** Describes a representation, imitation, or performance that closely resembles the real thing. Applied to paintings, photographs, acting, sound effects, and descriptions. Common in aesthetic and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 写真
**Transliteration:** xiězhēn
**Literal breakdown:** write/depict + true/real
**Natural English translation:** portrait, realistic depiction; (in modern usage) photo shoot, portrait photography
**Context of use:** In classical usage, refers to realistic portraiture or depiction. In contemporary usage, particularly common in the context of professional portrait photography or photo books (写真集). Used in artistic and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 纯真
**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** pure + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** pure, innocent, guileless, unspoiled
**Context of use:** Describes a quality of genuine, unspoiled innocence, typically applied to children, emotions, or early states of things. Used in literary, emotional, and evaluative contexts: 纯真的童年 (an innocent childhood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 天真
**Transliteration:** tiānzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** heaven/natural + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** naive, innocent, ingenuous; (of children) naturally pure
**Context of use:** Describes a quality of natural innocence or, in adult contexts, naivety or unrealistic thinking. Applied to children positively and to adults with a range from affectionate to critical: 太天真了 (too naive). Extremely common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 较真
**Transliteration:** jiàozhēn
**Literal breakdown:** compare/be particular about + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to be a stickler, to be pedantic about details, to take things too literally
**Context of use:** Describes the behavior of insisting on strict accuracy or taking something more seriously than the context warrants. Often carries a mildly critical or amused tone. Common in everyday northern Mandarin speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 真人
**Transliteration:** zhēnrén
**Literal breakdown:** true + person
**Natural English translation:** real person; (in Daoist usage) perfected person, realized being
**Context of use:** In everyday usage, refers to an actual living person as opposed to a fictional character or avatar: 真人版 (live-action version). In Daoist philosophical and religious contexts, refers to a spiritually perfected individual. The two uses are contextually distinct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 真迹
**Transliteration:** zhēnjì
**Literal breakdown:** true + trace/mark (handwriting, brushwork)
**Natural English translation:** authentic work, genuine original (of calligraphy or painting)
**Context of use:** Refers specifically to an authentic original work of calligraphy or painting, as opposed to a copy or forgery. Used in art authentication, museum, and connoisseurial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 真本
**Transliteration:** zhēnběn
**Literal breakdown:** true + original/root copy
**Natural English translation:** authentic original edition, genuine text
**Context of use:** Refers to an authentic original edition of a text or manuscript, as opposed to a forgery or later copy. Used in bibliographic, philological, and antiquarian contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 求真
**Transliteration:** qiúzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** seek + true/truth
**Natural English translation:** to seek truth, truth-seeking
**Context of use:** Describes the pursuit of truth or accuracy, used in academic, scientific, and philosophical contexts. Appears in institutional mottos and in discussions of intellectual integrity: 求真务实 (seek truth and be pragmatic).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 求真务实
**Transliteration:** qiúzhēn wùshí
**Literal breakdown:** seek + truth + attend to + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** to seek truth and be pragmatic, truth-seeking and down-to-earth
**Context of use:** A fixed expression widely used in Chinese political and administrative discourse to describe a work style characterized by intellectual honesty and practical effectiveness. Associated particularly with post-Mao reform-era political rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 真善美
**Transliteration:** zhēn shàn měi
**Literal breakdown:** true + good/virtuous + beautiful
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, and beauty (the classical triad)
**Context of use:** A fixed triadic expression referring to the three classical values of truth, moral goodness, and aesthetic beauty. Used in philosophical, educational, and rhetorical contexts to describe ideal human or artistic achievement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 半真半假
**Transliteration:** bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** half + true + half + false
**Natural English translation:** half true, half false; partly genuine, partly fabricated
**Context of use:** Describes a statement or situation that mixes truth and falsehood. Used in everyday and evaluative contexts to characterize ambiguous or misleading claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 真金不怕火炼
**Transliteration:** zhēnjīn bù pà huǒ liàn
**Literal breakdown:** true/genuine + gold + not + fear + fire + smelt/test
**Natural English translation:** true gold fears no fire; genuine quality withstands any test
**Context of use:** A proverb used to assert that what is genuinely good or true will be confirmed rather than destroyed by scrutiny or adversity. Used in moral, evaluative, and rhetorical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note:** 真 is highly productive. Additional attested compounds include 真空 (vacuum), 真菌 (fungus/true fungi), 真皮 (genuine leather; dermis), 真核 (eukaryote), 真数 (antilogarithm), and others in scientific and technical registers. These are listed under Step 3 as peripheral extensions.

### Root 3a: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve one or more of the three central roots (真, 实, 假/伪) but appear in contexts where the root's contribution seems to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**真 (zhēn) — peripheral extensions:**

- **真空** (zhēnkōng, "vacuum"): 真 contributes a sense of completeness or absoluteness rather than truth or genuineness.
- **真菌** (zhēnjūn, "fungi/true fungi"): 真 functions as a taxonomic classifier distinguishing a biological category.
- **真皮** (zhēnpí, "genuine leather; dermis"): In the anatomical sense (dermis), 真 marks a layer of skin rather than authenticity.
- **真核** (zhēnhé, "eukaryote"): 真 functions as a taxonomic marker in biological classification.
- **真气** (zhēnqì, "vital energy, genuine qi"): 真 appears in Daoist and traditional medical discourse to mark a specific type of vital force.
- **认真** (rènzhēn): In the sense of "don't take it seriously" (别认真), the root appears in a context of degree of engagement rather than truth-evaluation.
- **天真** (tiānzhēn): In adult contexts, 真 contributes to a meaning of naivety or unrealistic thinking rather than genuine correspondence to fact.
- **真善美** (zhēn shàn měi): 真 appears as one term in a classical philosophical triad alongside moral goodness and aesthetic beauty, extending beyond factual truth.
- **真人** (zhēnrén) in Daoist usage: 真 marks spiritual perfection or realization rather than factual authenticity.
- **逼真** (bīzhēn): 真 functions as a standard of resemblance in aesthetic evaluation rather than a claim about factual truth.
- **写真** (xiězhēn): 真 marks a mode of artistic representation; in contemporary usage (portrait photography), the truth-evaluation dimension is largely absent.
- **纯真** (chúnzhēn): 真 contributes to a meaning of innocence and unspoiled naturalness rather than factual accuracy.

---

**实 (shí) — peripheral extensions:**

- **落实** (luòshí): 实 contributes a sense of practical implementation and follow-through rather than truth or factual correspondence.
- **务实** (wùshí): 实 marks a pragmatic orientation toward practical matters rather than truth per se.
- **扎实** (zhāshí): 实 contributes a sense of solidity and thoroughness in skill or knowledge rather than factual truth.
- **踏实** (tāshí): 实 contributes to a psychological sense of feeling settled or at ease, as well as a behavioral sense of reliability.
- **充实** (chōngshí): 实 contributes a sense of fullness or enrichment (e.g., 充实生活, enrich one's life) rather than factual correspondence.
- **实力** (shílì, "actual strength, capability"): 实 marks genuine as opposed to nominal capability, but in a domain of power rather than truth.
- **实验** (shíyàn, "experiment"): 实 marks the empirical or practical dimension, but the compound functions primarily in scientific methodology rather than truth-evaluation.
- **虚实** (xūshí) in military strategy: 实 marks actual troop strength or position as opposed to feigned positions; the truth-domain meaning is present but embedded in strategic rather than epistemic contexts.
- **虚实** (xūshí) in aesthetics: 实 marks the "solid" or filled elements in a composition as opposed to empty space; the truth-domain meaning is absent.
- **果实** (guǒshí, "fruit, result"): 实 contributes a sense of tangible outcome or product; the truth-domain meaning is absent.

---

**假 (jiǎ) — peripheral extensions:**

- **假设** (jiǎshè) and **假定** (jiǎdìng): 假 marks a hypothetical or conditional status rather than falsity per se; these are standard logical and scientific terms for propositions assumed without asserting their truth.
- **假如** (jiǎrú) and **假若** (jiǎruò): 假 functions as a conditional particle introducing hypothetical scenarios; the falsity meaning is grammaticalized into conditionality.
- **假借** (jiǎjiè) in classical linguistics: 假 marks the borrowed or extended use of a character for its phonetic value, a technical category in Chinese writing system analysis with no direct connection to deception or falsity.
- **假期** (jiǎqī, "holiday, vacation"): This 假 is a homophone with a different character (假 jiǎ meaning "leave/holiday"), distinct from the 假 (jiǎ) meaning false. Flagged here to note the homophony.

---

**伪 (wěi) — peripheral extensions:**

- **伪政权** (wěi zhèngquán): 伪 marks political illegitimacy rather than factual falsity, operating in a domain of political and historical judgment.
- **伪史** (wěishǐ): 伪 marks historical fabrication, but the term is also used in politically contested contexts where the designation of "false history" is itself a matter of dispute.
- **伪书** (wěishū): 伪 functions as a philological and bibliographic category marker in classical scholarship, with a long technical history in Chinese textual criticism.
