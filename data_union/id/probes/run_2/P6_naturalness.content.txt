# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents how Indonesian encodes dynamic meanings — becoming, causing, and maintaining truth-related states — using only vocabulary attested in the CONTEXT (P1 through P5). Each construction type is examined separately for each relevant lexical item or group where the data supports distinct treatment. Where no common expression is attested in the CONTEXT, this is stated explicitly. Function words, aspect markers (*sudah*, *telah*, *akan*, *sedang*, *belum*), and auxiliaries (*bisa*, *harus*, *mulai*) are added as needed to form natural sentences, but no new truth-domain vocabulary is introduced.

Constructions already documented in P4a and P4b are referenced rather than repeated in full; this step focuses on dynamic (change, causation, stability) readings not already covered there.

---

## 1. X BECOMES A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1a. Something becomes true / becomes reality

**Primary construction:** *menjadi kenyataan*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Prediksi itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.* | "That prediction finally became reality." | [CORE] | Fixed collocation documented in P2b (Construction 6 under *nyata*) and P4a. *Menjadi* is the general inchoative; *kenyataan* is the nominal target state. Subject is typically a prediction, fear, or hope. |
| *Mimpinya menjadi kenyataan.* | "His dream became reality." | [CORE] | Same construction; subject is an aspiration. Extremely common across all registers. |

**Secondary construction:** *terbukti benar*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Dugaan itu terbukti benar.* | "That suspicion turned out to be correct." | [CORE] | *Terbukti* ("to be proven," from *bukti* "evidence" + *ter-* stative prefix) + *benar* as predicate. Documented in P4a under inchoative constructions. Foregrounds the evidentiary process of becoming confirmed. Common in journalistic and legal registers. |
| *Perkiraan para ahli terbukti benar.* | "The experts' estimates turned out to be correct." | [CORE] | Same pattern; subject is a claim or estimate. |

**Note on *benar*:** The inchoative "becomes true" is not expressed by deriving an inchoative directly from *benar* itself (no *\*membenar* or *\*menjadi benar* as a fixed formula). Instead, the construction uses *terbukti benar* (proven to be correct) or *ternyata benar* (turns out to be correct). *Ternyata* ("as it turned out," from *nyata*) is an evidential particle documented in P2b that frequently co-occurs with *benar* in revelatory contexts.

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Ternyata informasi itu benar.* | "It turned out that the information was correct." | [CORE] | *Ternyata* + predicate; introduces a revelation that something is the case. Documented in P2b under *palsu* (Construction 8) as a revelatory construction; the same pattern applies with *benar*. |

### 1b. Something becomes false / fake / invalid

**Primary construction:** *ternyata palsu* / *ternyata tidak benar*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Ternyata itu semua palsu.* | "It turned out that it was all fake." | [CORE] | Documented directly in P2b (Construction 8 under *palsu*). *Ternyata* introduces the revelation of falsity. |
| *Ternyata informasi itu tidak benar.* | "It turned out that the information was not correct." | [CORE] | Same *ternyata* + negated predicate pattern. Common in journalistic corrections and retractions. |

**Construction with *terbukti*:**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Klaim itu terbukti salah.* | "That claim was proven wrong." | [CORE] | *Terbukti* + *salah* as predicate. Parallel to *terbukti benar*; foregrounds evidentiary process. Common in legal and journalistic registers. |
| *Dokumen itu terbukti palsu.* | "That document was proven to be fake." | [CORE] | *Terbukti* + *palsu*; frequent in legal and forensic contexts. |

**Becoming invalid (*tidak sah*):**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Pernikahan itu dinyatakan tidak sah.* | "That marriage was declared invalid." | [CORE] | *Dinyatakan* (passive of *menyatakan*, "to declare") + *tidak sah*. Documented in P1b and P2b. The change to invalidity is expressed through a declarative act rather than a spontaneous change; see Section 3 (causation by authority). |

**Note on *sah*:** There is no attested construction meaning "spontaneously becomes valid" without an authorizing agent. Validity in the *sah* domain is always conferred by an act (see Section 3b). The inchoative reading is therefore absent for *sah* without a causal agent.

### 1c. Something becomes genuine / authentic

**Note:** No fixed inchoative construction meaning "becomes genuine" (*menjadi asli*) is documented in the CONTEXT. The context documents *mengasli* (P2a, Item 3) as a marginal verbal form meaning "to become original again / to revert to the original state," but notes it is "less frequent than other derivatives in this family" and "attested but not common." This gap is preserved.

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *\*Barang itu menjadi asli.* | "That item became genuine." | [UNCERTAIN] | Not attested as a natural construction in the CONTEXT. The concept of an object spontaneously becoming genuine is not a typical real-world scenario, which may explain the absence. |

### 1d. Something becomes revealed / comes to light

**Primary construction:** *terungkap*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya.* | "The truth will come to light in the end." | [CORE] | Documented directly in P4a (Construction 4) and P1b. *Terungkap* is the conventionalized inchoative for truth-revelation; subject is typically *kebenaran* or a concealed fact. |
| *Kebohongannya akhirnya terungkap.* | "His lie finally came to light." | [CORE] | Same construction with *kebohongan* as subject. The lie is the concealed thing that becomes revealed. Common in journalistic and narrative registers. |
| *Penyimpangan itu terungkap setelah penyelidikan.* | "The distortion came to light after the investigation." | [CORE] | *Penyimpangan* (deviation/distortion, documented in P2a) as subject of *terungkap*. |

**Note on *terungkap* vs. *mengungkap*:** *Terungkap* is the spontaneous/stative form ("comes to light on its own"); *mengungkap kebenaran* (documented in P1b and P2b) is the agentive form ("someone reveals the truth"). This active/stative distinction is relevant to the causation section below (Section 3).

---

### 1e. Something becomes a lie / becomes distorted

**Note:** No fixed inchoative construction meaning "becomes a lie" or "becomes distorted" is documented in the CONTEXT. The relevant constructions are causative (someone causes something to be falsified or distorted) rather than spontaneous change. See Section 3.

## 2. X BECOMES [SINCERITY / HONESTY STATE]

### 2a. Becoming honest / coming clean

**Primary construction:** *berjujur* / *mengakui kesalahan*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Akhirnya dia berjujur.* | "He finally came clean / was honest." | [MARGINAL] | *Berjujur* is documented in P2a (Item 12) as attested but marginal, restricted to informal or literary registers. The inchoative reading ("finally became honest") is available but the form itself is not common. |
| *Dia akhirnya mengakui kesalahannya.* | "He finally admitted his mistake." | [CORE] | *Mengakui kesalahan* is documented in P2b (Construction 8 under *salah*) as a stable collocation for acknowledging wrongdoing. This is the standard expression for the transition from concealment to admission. |

**Construction with *ikhlas*:**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Dia belum bisa ikhlas.* | "He is not yet able to accept it." | [CORE] | Documented directly in P2b (Construction 4 under *ikhlas*). Describes the state of not yet having achieved full acceptance; implies a potential future transition. |
| *Lama-lama dia bisa ikhlas juga.* | "Eventually he was able to accept it too." | [CORE] | Extension of the *belum bisa ikhlas* construction to the achieved state; *lama-lama* ("gradually, over time") marks the inchoative process. Common in everyday and emotional discourse. |

## 3. Y CAUSES X TO BE [TRUTH-RELATED STATE]

### 3a. Causing something to be confirmed / justified

**Primary construction:** *membenarkan*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Penyelidikan itu membenarkan klaim tersebut.* | "The investigation confirmed that claim." | [CORE] | *Membenarkan* (causative of *benar*, documented in P2a Item 1 and P4a Construction 5) with an institutional agent. The investigation causes the claim to be in a confirmed state. |
| *Dia membenarkan pernyataan rekannya.* | "He confirmed his colleague's statement." | [CORE] | Personal agent; *membenarkan* in the epistemic confirmation sense. Common in journalistic and everyday registers. |
| *Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu.* | "Nothing can justify that action." | [CORE] | *Membenarkan* in the moral justification sense (documented in P2a as the dual sense of the form). Negated with *tidak ada yang bisa*. |

**Passive counterpart:**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Klaim itu dibenarkan oleh bukti yang ada.* | "That claim was confirmed by the available evidence." | [CORE] | *Dibenarkan* (passive causative, documented in P2a and P4a). Agent (*bukti*, "evidence") expressed with *oleh*. |
| *Tindakan itu tidak dibenarkan.* | "That action is not permitted / not justified." | [CORE] | Documented directly in P2b (Construction 12 under *benar*) and P4b as a standard regulatory formula. |

---

### 3b. Causing something to be valid / ratified

**Primary construction:** *mengesahkan*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang itu kemarin.* | "The parliament ratified that law yesterday." | [CORE] | *Mengesahkan* (causative of *sah*, documented in P2a Item 10 and P4a). Institutional agent causes a document or act to enter the valid state. Standard in legislative reporting. |
| *Notaris mengesahkan dokumen tersebut.* | "The notary authenticated that document." | [CORE] | Same construction with a notarial agent. Documented in P1b. |

**Passive counterpart:**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan.* | "That law has been ratified." | [CORE] | *Disahkan* (passive, documented in P2a and P4a). *Telah* marks completion. Standard formula in legislative and administrative reporting. |
| *Pernikahan mereka dinyatakan sah oleh pengadilan.* | "Their marriage was declared valid by the court." | [CORE] | *Dinyatakan sah* (documented in P2b Construction 3 under *sah*). The court as authorizing agent causes the marriage to enter the valid state through a declarative act. |

**Note on the causation structure for *sah*:** All attested causative constructions for *sah* involve an institutional or authoritative agent (parliament, court, notary, religious official). No construction is documented in the CONTEXT where a non-authoritative agent causes something to become *sah*. This constraint is preserved.

## 4. STABILITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### 4a. Truth / validity persisting or being upheld

**Primary construction:** *menegakkan kebenaran*

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Kita harus terus menegakkan kebenaran.* | "We must continue to uphold the truth." | [CORE] | *Menegakkan kebenaran* (documented in P1b and P2b) describes the active maintenance of truth against forces that would suppress it. *Terus* ("continue") marks ongoing action. Common in moral, religious, and political discourse. |

**Construction with *menepati*:**

| Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Dia selalu menepati janjinya.* | "He always keeps his promises." | [CORE] | *Menepati janji* (documented in P2a Item 7 and P2b) describes the ongoing fulfillment of a commitment. *Selalu* ("always") marks habitual stability. |
| *Janji itu tidak ditepati.* | "That promise was not kept." | [CORE] | Negated passive; the failure to maintain the committed state. Documented in P2a. |

## 5. SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

The following patterns emerge from the data documented above:

**Inchoative (becoming) constructions:**
- Truth-revelation uses *terungkap* (spontaneous) or *terbukti benar* (evidential); no dedicated inchoative morpheme on *benar* itself.
- Reality-realization uses the fixed collocation *menjadi kenyataan*.
- Revelatory discovery uses *ternyata* + predicate for both *benar* and *palsu*.
- Validity (*sah*) does not have a spontaneous inchoative; it is always conferred by an authoritative agent (see causation).
- Genuineness (*asli*) has no attested spontaneous inchoative construction in the CONTEXT.

**Causative constructions:**
- The *me(N)-...-kan* pattern is productive across the truth domain: *membenarkan*, *memalsukan*, *mengesahkan*, *memurnikan*, *mengelirukan*, *mengikhlaskan*.
- The *me(N)-...-i* pattern applies where the target of the action is foregrounded: *membohongi* (to lie to someone), *menepati* (to fulfill a commitment to someone).
- Passive *di-...-kan* and *di-...-i* counterparts are equally productive and frequent, particularly in formal and journalistic registers.
- Causation for *sah* (validity) is institutionally constrained: only authoritative agents (parliament, court, notary) appear as causal agents in attested constructions.

**Stability constructions:**
- Stability is expressed through habitual aspect markers (*selalu*, *setiap hari*, *terus*) combined with the relevant predicate rather than through dedicated stability morphology.
- Active maintenance of truth uses *menegakkan kebenaran*; active maintenance of a commitment uses *menepati janji*.
- Concealment as a maintained state uses *menyembunyikan kebenaran* + *terus*.
- The fixed legal formula *sah dan mengikat* with a temporal phrase expresses the duration of a valid state.
