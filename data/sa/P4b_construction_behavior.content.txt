# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness Rating | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | सत्यं भवति | satyaṃ bhavati | "is true" | [CORE] | Standard copular construction in philosophical and narrative prose. Copula frequently omitted in present tense (satyam alone = "it is true"). Bhavati preferred in explicit predication; asti more existential. |
| | मिथ्या भवति | mithyā bhavati | "is false/illusory" | [CORE] | Identical distribution to satyaṃ bhavati. Common in Vedāntic discourse for characterizing phenomenal reality. Zero-copula variant equally natural. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | सत्यं न भवति | satyaṃ na bhavati | "is not true" | [CORE] | Syntactic negation with na standard for denying predication. Used when explicitly negating a prior assertion or in dialectical contexts. |
| | असत्यं भवति | asatyaṃ bhavati | "is untrue/false" | [CORE] | Morphological negation (a- prefix) creates lexicalized antonym. Asatya functions as independent term with slightly different semantic range than na satya (includes "unreal" not just "not true"). Both constructions productive; choice often stylistic. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | सत्यवचनम् | satyavacanam | "true statement" | [CORE] | Tatpuruṣa compounds overwhelmingly preferred over analytic constructions for adnominal modification. Highly productive across all text types. Compound formation unrestricted with truth-domain terms. |
| | सत्यस्य वचनम् | satyasya vacanam | "utterance of truth" | [MARGINAL] | Genitive construction less common than compound for simple modification. Used when emphasizing possessive/partitive relationship or in metrical contexts requiring analytic form. Semantic difference: genitive suggests "belonging to truth" rather than "characterized by truth." |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | मिथ्या भवति | mithyā bhavati | "becomes false/illusory" | [CORE] | Same form as copular construction; context disambiguates stative vs. inchoative reading. Inchoative sense common in narrative and philosophical argument describing change of epistemic status. |
| | प्रमा जायते | pramā jāyate | "valid knowledge arises" | [CORE] | Jāyate specifically inchoative, emphasizing origination. Restricted to cognitive/epistemic states and abstract entities. Cannot substitute for bhavati in simple copular use. Common in epistemological texts describing knowledge-production. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | (no common expression identified) | — | — | [UNCERTAIN] | Causative morphology (NIC suffix) theoretically available but not attested in corpus for truth-domain predicates. Causation expressed periphrastically through instrumental case or compound verbs (jñāpayati "causes to know" rather than *satyīkaraṇa). |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | सत्यता | satyatā | "truthfulness, truth, reality" | [CORE] | Both -tā and -tva highly productive. Satyatā more frequent in usage; satyatva appears but less common. No clear semantic distinction; may reflect metrical or phonological preferences. Used across all registers. |
| | मिथ्यात्व | mithyātva | "falsity, illusoriness" | [CORE] | Mithyātva standard form (mithyātā rare/unattested). Lexical preferences vary by base: some roots favor -tā, others -tva. Both suffixes denote abstract quality/state without functional difference. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | सत्यवादिन् | satyavādin | "truth-speaker, truthful person" | [CORE] | Highly productive pattern combining truth-term + vādin/jña/kārin. Common in ethical and philosophical characterization of persons. Vādin specifically for speech acts; jña for knowledge; kārin for action. |
| | मिथ्यावादिन् | mithyāvādin | "liar, one who speaks falsely" | [CORE] | Parallel productivity to satyavādin. Used in moral/legal contexts for characterizing deceitful persons. Can take further derivation (mithyāvāditva "state of being a liar"). |
| **8. INSTRUMENTAL MEANS CONSTRUCTION** | प्रमाणेन ज्ञायते | pramāṇena jñāyate | "is known through valid means" | [CORE] | Standard construction in epistemological discourse. Instrumental case unrestricted for expressing means of knowledge. Verb typically passive (jñāyate, gamyate, prāpyate). Extends to causal uses (hetunā "by reason"). |
| | प्रत्यक्षेण ज्ञायते | pratyakṣeṇa jñāyate | "is known by perception" | [CORE] | Identical pattern with specific pramāṇa type. Common in pramāṇa-śāstra for specifying epistemic source. All six pramāṇas (perception, inference, testimony, etc.) appear in this construction. |
| **9. ABLATIVE STANDPOINT CONSTRUCTION** | परमार्थतः | paramārthataḥ | "from the ultimate standpoint, ultimately" | [CORE] | Specialized philosophical construction marking epistemic/metaphysical perspective. Highly productive in Madhyamaka and Advaita texts. Functions adverbially; can modify entire proposition. |
| | व्यवहारतः | vyavahārataḥ | "from the conventional standpoint, practically" | [CORE] | Binary pair with paramārthataḥ in two-truths framework. Equally productive. Also used independently for "practically, in ordinary usage" without explicit contrast to paramārtha. |
| **10. LOCATIVE DOMAIN CONSTRUCTION** | व्यवहारे सत्यम् | vyavahāre satyam | "true at the conventional level" | [CORE] | Locative marks domain of truth-evaluation. Central to two-truths doctrine. Can appear with or without explicit predicate (vyavahāre alone implies "at conventional level"). |
| | परमार्थे सत्यम् | paramārthe satyam | "true at the ultimate level" | [CORE] | Parallel construction to vyavahāre. Forms binary system for relativizing truth-claims to level of analysis. Also used for circumstantial contexts beyond two-truths framework. |
| **11. IMPERATIVE EXHORTATION** | सत्यं वद | satyaṃ vada | "speak truth!" | [CORE] | Direct imperative common in prescriptive/didactic contexts. Appears in Upaniṣadic injunctions, dharmaśāstra, and gnomic literature. Accusative object + imperative verb standard word order. |
| | धर्मं चर | dharmaṃ cara | "practice dharma!" | [CORE] | Same construction with dharma. Shows pattern extends beyond truth-domain to ethical terms generally. Frequent in religious instruction and ritual contexts. |
| **12. OPTATIVE OBLIGATION** | सत्यं ब्रूयात् | satyaṃ brūyāt | "one should speak truth" | [CORE] | Optative mood standard for expressing moral obligation. Common in legal and ethical texts. Less direct than imperative; implies general prescription rather than specific command. |
| | असत्यं न वक्तव्यम् | asatyaṃ na vaktavyam | "falsehood should not be spoken" | [CORE] | Gerundive construction (vaktavya) alternative for deontic modality. Stronger obligation than optative; emphasizes necessity. Both patterns productive; gerundive preferred for prohibitions and formal rules. |
| **13. DUAL CONTRAST CONSTRUCTION** | सत्यासत्ययोः | satyāsatyayoḥ | "of truth and falsehood" | [CORE] | Dual number highly productive for binary oppositions. Genitive dual standard for "between X and Y" or "of X and Y." Common in philosophical texts establishing fundamental contrasts. |
| | व्यवहारपरमार्थयोः | vyavahāraparamārthayoḥ | "of conventional and ultimate [truth]" | [CORE] | Same pattern with two-truths terminology. Shows construction unrestricted across truth-domain terms. Can appear in various cases (dual locative, instrumental) depending on syntactic context. |
| **14. INTERROGATIVE CHALLENGE** | किं प्रमाणम् | kiṃ pramāṇam | "what is the proof?" | [CORE] | Standard construction in philosophical debate for demanding epistemic warrant. Kim for neuter, kaḥ for masculine/feminine. Functions as challenge requiring justification. Common in vāda (debate) literature. |
| | को धर्मः | ko dharmaḥ | "what is dharma?" | [CORE] | Same pattern with masculine noun. Shows construction extends to ethical/normative terms. Frequent in dharmaśāstra and philosophical dialogue for requesting definition or clarification. |
| **15. EMPHATIC AFFIRMATION** | सत्यमेव जयते | satyam eva jayate | "truth alone conquers" | [CORE] | Eva particle provides emphatic/exclusive force. Highly productive across all registers. Can appear with any truth-domain term. Position after noun standard; pre-verbal position possible for verb emphasis. |
| | मिथ्यैव | mithyaiva | "merely false, only illusory" | [CORE] | Eva combines phonologically with preceding word (sandhi). Functions independently as emphatic predicate or modifies verb/copula. Common in Vedāntic texts for emphasizing illusory nature of phenomena. |

## ALTERNATE CONSTRUCTIONS ATTESTED

### Attribution (Construction 1)
- **Zero-copula variant**: satyam (alone) = "it is true" — equally natural in present tense contexts
- **Asti variant**: satyam asti — more existential than predicative; emphasizes being/existence

### Adnominal Modification (Construction 3)
- **Bahuvrīhi compounds**: satya-vāc (having true speech) — less common than tatpuruṣa for simple modification; used for possessive characterization

### Become/Inchoative (Construction 4)
- **Sambhavati variant**: mithyā sambhavati "becomes false" — less common than bhavati; emphasizes coming-into-being
- **Compound verbs**: satya-bhū (become true) — rare; bhavati with predicative adjective strongly preferred

### Instrumental Means (Construction 8)
- **Compound instrumental**: pramāṇa-jñāna (knowledge-by-pramāṇa) — less common than case construction; used in technical compounds

### Standpoint Constructions (9-10)
- **Ablative-locative alternation**: paramārthataḥ ~ paramārthe — both productive; ablative emphasizes source/perspective, locative emphasizes domain/sphere

## GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

1. **High productivity**: Constructions 1-4, 6-11, 13-15 show unrestricted productivity across truth-domain vocabulary
2. **Lexical gaps**: Construction 5 (causative) not attested despite theoretical availability
3. **Morphological vs. syntactic alternation**: Multiple constructions show both options (e.g., a-satya vs. na satya; compound vs. genitive modification)
4. **Register variation**: Imperative/optative constructions (11-12) restricted to prescriptive genres; standpoint constructions (9-10) concentrated in philosophical texts
5. **No asymmetries detected**: Positive and negative terms (satya/mithyā) show parallel distribution across all constructions
