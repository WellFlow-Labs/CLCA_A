# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / BECOMING TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1.1 "X becomes true" — kuwa kweli

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + kweli
**Example:** Habari hiyo imekuwa kweli
**Transliteration:** habari hiyo i-me-kuwa kweli
**English translation:** "That news has become true / turned out to be true"
**Usage notes:** Common construction for situations revealed to be true over time. Uses stative verb kuwa (to be/become) with perfect aspect marker -me-. Frequent in narrative contexts describing verification of claims or predictions coming true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 "X becomes correct" — kuwa sahihi

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + sahihi
**Example:** Jibu lake limekuwa sahihi
**Transliteration:** jibu lake li-me-kuwa sahihi
**English translation:** "His answer has become correct / turned out to be correct"
**Usage notes:** Used when something initially uncertain is confirmed as correct. Common in educational and verification contexts. Less frequent than kuwa kweli but well-attested.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.3 "X becomes genuine/authentic" — kuwa halisi

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + halisi
**Example:** Dhahabu hii imekuwa halisi
**Transliteration:** dhahabu hii i-me-kuwa halisi
**English translation:** "This gold has become genuine / turned out to be genuine"
**Usage notes:** Used when authenticity is established through testing or verification. Common in commercial and verification contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.4 "X becomes certain" — kuwa na hakika

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa na + hakika
**Example:** Sasa nimekuwa na hakika
**Transliteration:** sasa ni-me-kuwa na hakika
**English translation:** "Now I have become certain / I am now certain"
**Usage notes:** Uses possessive construction (kuwa na "to have/possess"). Common for describing acquisition of certainty. Alternative: kuwa na uhakika (with abstract noun).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.5 "X becomes evident/clear" — kuwa dhahiri

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + dhahiri
**Example:** Ukweli umekuwa dhahiri
**Transliteration:** ukweli u-me-kuwa dhahiri
**English translation:** "The truth has become evident / has become clear"
**Usage notes:** Common construction for truth becoming manifest or obvious. Frequent in revelatory contexts and arguments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.6 "X becomes complete/perfect" — kuwa kamili / kukamilika

**Construction:** (a) Subject + kuwa + kamili; (b) Subject + kukamilika
**Example:** (a) Taarifa imekuwa kamili; (b) Taarifa imekamilika
**Transliteration:** (a) taarifa i-me-kuwa kamili; (b) taarifa i-me-kamilika
**English translation:** "The information has become complete"
**Usage notes:** Two patterns: (a) kuwa + adjective; (b) stative verb -kamilika. The stative verb form more common and natural. Used when information or processes reach completion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] for both patterns

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / BECOMING OPPOSED STATE

### 2.1 "X becomes false/a lie" — kuwa uongo

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + uongo
**Example:** Habari hiyo imekuwa uongo
**Transliteration:** habari hiyo i-me-kuwa uongo
**English translation:** "That news has become a lie / turned out to be false"
**Usage notes:** Used when something initially believed true is revealed as false. Common in contexts of disconfirmation and revelation of deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 "X becomes invalid/null" — kuwa batili / kubatilika

**Construction:** (a) Subject + kuwa + batili; (b) Subject + kubatilika
**Example:** (a) Hati imekuwa batili; (b) Hati imebatilika
**Transliteration:** (a) hati i-me-kuwa batili; (b) hati i-me-batilika
**English translation:** "The document has become invalid / has been nullified"
**Usage notes:** Two patterns: (a) kuwa + adjective (less common); (b) stative verb -batilika (more common). Used primarily in legal/administrative contexts for documents losing validity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] for both patterns; -batilika more frequent

---

### 2.3 "X becomes corrupt/rotten" — kubovu

**Construction:** Subject + kubovu
**Example:** Matunda yamebovu
**Transliteration:** matunda ya-me-bovu
**English translation:** "The fruits have rotted / become rotten"
**Usage notes:** Inherent stative/inchoative verb; does not require kuwa. Used for both physical decay and moral corruption. Very common in both literal and metaphorical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 "X becomes incorrect" — kuwa si sahihi

**Construction:** Subject + kuwa + si sahihi
**Example:** Hesabu imekuwa si sahihi
**Transliteration:** hesabu i-me-kuwa si sahihi
**English translation:** "The calculation has become incorrect / turned out to be incorrect"
**Usage notes:** Uses negative copula si with kuwa. Less common than positive counterpart; more natural to say "discovered to be incorrect" using gundua or tambua.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatical but less natural than alternatives

---

### 2.5 "X becomes fake/counterfeit" — NO COMMON EXPRESSION

**Note:** While one can say "X imekuwa ya uongo" (X has become of falsehood), this is not a common or natural expression. More natural to use discovery verbs: "imegundulika kuwa ni ya uongo" (it has been discovered to be fake).
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN] — construction possible but not commonly attested

## 3. CAUSATIVE: "Y causes X to be true/correct"

### 3.1 "Y makes X certain" — hakikisha

**Construction:** Agent + hakikisha + Object
**Example:** Ninahakikisha ukweli wa habari
**Transliteration:** ni-na-hakikisha ukweli wa habari
**English translation:** "I am ensuring/verifying the truth of the news"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation from hakika (certain). Extremely common in instructions, procedures, and verification contexts. Can take clausal complement: hakikisha kwamba... (ensure that...).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 "Y confirms/verifies X" — thibitisha

**Construction:** Agent + thibitisha + Object
**Example:** Wathibitisha ukweli wa taarifa
**Transliteration:** wa-thibitisha ukweli wa taarifa
**English translation:** "They confirm/verify the truth of the information"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation meaning "to cause to be established/firm." Very common in formal, administrative, and legal contexts for verification and confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 "Y makes X complete/perfect" — kamilisha

**Construction:** Agent + kamilisha + Object
**Example:** Amekamilisha taarifa
**Transliteration:** a-me-kamilisha taarifa
**English translation:** "He has completed/perfected the information"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation from kamili (complete). Common in contexts of finishing tasks or bringing things to completion. Can mean both "complete" and "perfect."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 "Y makes X evident/clear" — dhihirisha

**Construction:** Agent + dhihirisha + Object
**Example:** Anadhihirisha ukweli
**Transliteration:** a-na-dhihirisha ukweli
**English translation:** "He is showing/demonstrating/revealing the truth"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation from dhahiri (evident). Common in contexts of demonstration, revelation, and making things clear. Frequent in formal and explanatory discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 "Y makes X straight/correct" — nyofua / nyosha

**Construction:** Agent + nyofua/nyosha + Object
**Example:** Ananyosha njia
**Transliteration:** a-na-nyosha njia
**English translation:** "He is straightening the way / correcting the path"
**Usage notes:** Causative/extended forms from -nyofu (straight). Used for both physical straightening and moral/procedural correction. Nyosha more common than nyofua.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.6 "Y makes X correct" — sahihisha

**Construction:** Agent + sahihisha + Object
**Example:** Sahihisha makosa
**Transliteration:** sahihisha makosa
**English translation:** "Correct the errors"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation from sahihi (correct). Used in educational and editorial contexts for making corrections. Less common than expected; often replaced by -rekebisha (to correct, fix).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but less frequent than alternatives

---

### 3.7 "Y makes X true" — NO COMMON CAUSATIVE FORM

**Note:** There is no common causative derivation *kwelisha or similar. The concept is expressed periphrastically: "kufanya iwe kweli" (to make it be true) or through verification verbs like thibitisha, hakikisha.
**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] — no dedicated causative form attested

---

## 4. CAUSATIVE: "Y causes X to become false/invalid"

### 4.1 "Y invalidates/nullifies X" — batilisha

**Construction:** Agent + batilisha + Object
**Example:** Mahakama imebatilisha hati
**Transliteration:** mahakama i-me-batilisha hati
**English translation:** "The court has invalidated/nullified the document"
**Usage notes:** Causative derivation from batili (invalid). Common in legal and administrative contexts for official cancellation or nullification of documents, decisions, or agreements.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.2 "Y corrupts X" — boza / haribu

**Construction:** Agent + boza/haribu + Object
**Example:** (a) Rushwa inaboza uadilifu; (b) Rushwa inaharibu uadilifu
**Transliteration:** (a) rushwa i-na-boza uadilifu; (b) rushwa i-na-haribu uadilifu
**English translation:** "Bribery corrupts integrity"
**Usage notes:** Two verbs: (a) boza — causative from -bovu (corrupt/rotten), specialized for moral corruption; (b) haribu — general verb "to spoil/ruin," more common. Boza specifically used for moral/ethical corruption.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] for both; haribu more frequent

---

### 4.3 "Y falsifies X" — NO SINGLE COMMON VERB

**Note:** No single common causative verb meaning "to falsify" derived from uongo or batili. Concept expressed through: (a) kubadilisha (to change/alter); (b) kutengeneza (to fabricate); (c) kughushi (to forge/counterfeit). Periphrastic: kufanya iwe ya uongo (to make it be false).
**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] — no dedicated causative form; expressed through other verbs

---

### 4.4 "Y makes X incorrect" — NO COMMON CAUSATIVE FORM

**Note:** No attested causative *sisahihisha or similar. Concept expressed through: (a) kuharibu (to spoil/ruin); (b) kukosea (to make errors in); (c) kubadilisha (to change/alter).
**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] — no dedicated causative form

---

## 5. MAINTAINING TRUTH / STABILITY

### 5.1 "X remains true" — kubaki kuwa kweli

**Construction:** Subject + kubaki + kuwa + kweli
**Example:** Ukweli unabaki kuwa kweli
**Transliteration:** ukweli u-na-baki kuwa kweli
**English translation:** "Truth remains (being) true"
**Usage notes:** Uses verb kubaki (to remain, stay) + infinitive kuwa. Common in philosophical and proverbial discourse about permanence of truth. Can be shortened to "kubaki kweli."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.2 "X stays correct" — kubaki sahihi

**Construction:** Subject + kubaki + sahihi
**Example:** Jibu linabaki sahihi
**Transliteration:** jibu li-na-baki sahihi
**English translation:** "The answer remains correct"
**Usage notes:** Uses kubaki + adjective. Common in contexts where correctness is maintained despite challenges or changes.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.3 "Y preserves/maintains truth" — linda ukweli / hifadhi ukweli

**Construction:** Agent + linda/hifadhi + ukweli
**Example:** (a) Tunalinda ukweli; (b) Tunahifadhi ukweli
**Transliteration:** (a) tu-na-linda ukweli; (b) tu-na-hifadhi ukweli
**English translation:** "We preserve/protect the truth"
**Usage notes:** Two verbs: (a) linda — "to protect, defend" (more common); (b) hifadhi — "to preserve, conserve" (more formal). Both used for maintaining truth against threats or distortion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] for both

---

### 5.4 "Y protects rights" — linda haki

**Construction:** Agent + linda + haki
**Example:** Serikali inalinda haki za raia
**Transliteration:** serikali i-na-linda haki za raia
**English translation:** "The government protects citizens' rights"
**Usage notes:** Very common construction in human rights and legal discourse. Linda (protect) is the standard verb for rights protection.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.5 "Y maintains accuracy" — kudumisha usahihi

**Construction:** Agent + kudumisha + usahihi
**Example:** Wanadumisha usahihi wa taarifa
**Transliteration:** wa-na-dumisha usahihi wa taarifa
**English translation:** "They maintain the accuracy of information"
**Usage notes:** Uses verb kudumisha (to maintain, sustain, preserve). Common in quality control and data management contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Example:** Tuligundua ukweli

**English translation:** "The truth is slowly revealing itself"
**Usage notes:** Reflexive verb jitokeza (to reveal oneself, emerge) + polepole. Common in contexts where truth emerges gradually without active revelation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 15. EXPRESSIONS WITH NO COMMON DYNAMIC FORM

### 15.1 "X becomes reasonable/wise" — LIMITED ATTESTATION

**Note:** While grammatically possible to say "kuwa na busara" (to become with wisdom) or "kuwa mwenye busara" (to become one having wisdom), these are not commonly attested as dynamic expressions. More natural to use acquisition verbs: "kupata busara" (to acquire wisdom).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but not commonly used

---

### 15.2 "X makes Y reasonable" — NO COMMON CAUSATIVE

**Note:** No attested causative form from busara. Concept expressed periphrastically: "kumfanya awe na busara" (to make him be with wisdom) or through teaching/education verbs.
**Epistemic status:** [ABSENT] — no dedicated causative form

---

### 15.3 "X becomes a right/entitlement" — LIMITED USAGE

**Note:** While "kuwa haki" (to become a right) is grammatically possible, it's rarely used. Rights are typically described as existing or being granted rather than becoming. More natural: "kupata haki" (to get rights) or "kuwa na haki" (to have rights).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but uncommon

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Highly Productive Patterns:
1. **kuwa + ADJECTIVE** — very productive for state changes (kuwa kweli, kuwa sahihi, kuwa halisi)
2. **Causative -ish- derivations** — highly productive (hakikisha, thibitisha, kamilisha, dhihirisha, batilisha)
3. **Inherent stative verbs** — common for specific domains (kubovu for corruption, kubatilika for invalidation)
4. **Verb + ukweli/haki constructions** — extremely productive with action verbs

### Limited or Absent Patterns:
1. **No causative from kweli** — no *kwelisha; expressed periphrastically
2. **No causative from busara** — no dedicated form for "make reasonable"
3. **Limited causative from sahihi** — sahihisha exists but less common than alternatives
4. **No single verb for "falsify"** — expressed through multiple verbs depending on context

### Key Semantic Distinctions:
1. **Discovery vs. revelation**: gundua (discover) vs. fichua (reveal/uncover)
2. **Protection vs. preservation**: linda (protect) vs. hifadhi (preserve)
3. **Denial vs. rejection**: kana (deny) vs. kataa (reject) vs. pinga (oppose)
4. **Corruption verbs**: boza (moral corruption) vs. haribu (general spoiling)

### Register Patterns:
- Formal/legal: thibitisha, batilisha, hifadhi
- General/common: hakikisha, linda, onyesha
- Informal: tafuta, gundua, ficha
