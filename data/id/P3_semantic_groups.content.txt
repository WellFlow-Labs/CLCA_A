# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Indonesian (id)

## Group 1: Factual Correctness Evaluation

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, information, or representations correspond to actual facts or reality. These expressions are used when assessing the accuracy of claims, data, or descriptions against what is objectively the case. Common in contexts of verification, fact-checking, and distinguishing accurate from inaccurate information.

**Representative expressions:**
- benar (true, correct)
- salah (wrong, mistaken)
- betul (correct, true)
- tepat (precise, exact)
- akurat (accurate)
- keliru (mistaken, erroneous)
- tidak benar (not true)
- kurang benar (not quite correct)
- data akurat (accurate data)
- informasi akurat (accurate information)
- salah hitung (miscalculation)
- salah tulis (typo, writing error)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- nyata (real, actual) — overlaps with authenticity evaluation
- faktual (factual) — overlaps with evidential basis
- sebenarnya (actually) — functions more as discourse marker

---

## Group 2: Authenticity and Genuineness

**Definition:** Terms distinguishing genuine, original, or authentic items from fake, counterfeit, or imitation ones. These expressions are used primarily for evaluating physical objects, documents, products, and materials rather than statements. Common in commercial, legal, and forensic contexts where establishing authenticity is crucial.

**Representative expressions:**
- asli (original, genuine)
- palsu (fake, counterfeit)
- tulen (pure, genuine)
- sejati (true, genuine)
- uang palsu (counterfeit money)
- dokumen palsu (forged document)
- barang asli (genuine goods)
- emas tulen (pure gold)
- asli atau palsu (genuine or fake)
- keaslian (authenticity)
- pemalsuan (forgery)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- nyata (real, actual) — can refer to both factual truth and material authenticity
- fiktif (fictitious) — primarily about invented vs. real, not fake vs. genuine

## Group 3: Deliberate Deception and Lying

**Definition:** Terms specifically marking intentional falsehood or deception in communication. These expressions emphasize the speaker's intent to mislead rather than mere error, and carry moral judgment about dishonest behavior. Used in contexts of interpersonal trust, moral evaluation, and accusations of dishonesty.

**Representative expressions:**
- bohong (lie, false)
- dusta (lie, falsehood - formal/literary)
- jujur (honest, truthful)
- berbohong (to lie)
- membohongi (to lie to someone)
- pembohong (liar)
- kebohongan (lie, falsehood)
- jangan bohong (don't lie)
- bohong besar (big lie)
- kejujuran (honesty)
- ketidakjujuran (dishonesty)
- jujur saja (to be honest)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- tidak benar (not true) — can indicate either error or deception
- salah (wrong) — typically error rather than deliberate deception

## Group 4: Perceptual and Cognitive Errors

**Definition:** Compound expressions with "salah" marking specific types of unintentional mistakes in perception, understanding, or communication. These terms identify the particular cognitive or perceptual process where error occurred, typically without implying moral fault or deliberate deception.

**Representative expressions:**
- salah paham (misunderstanding)
- salah sangka (misjudgment, wrong suspicion)
- salah tafsir (misinterpretation)
- salah lihat (to see wrongly)
- salah dengar (to mishear)
- salah ucap (slip of the tongue)
- kesalahpahaman (misunderstanding)
- kekeliruan (error, confusion)
- mengelirukan (to confuse, to mislead)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- keliru (mistaken) — general term that can apply to various error types
- salah hitung (miscalculation) — more technical than perceptual

## Group 5: Social Appropriateness and Propriety

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, behaviors, or situations conform to social norms, expectations, or standards of propriety. These expressions assess what is fitting, proper, or reasonable in social contexts, often with prescriptive force about what ought to be done or what is deserved.

**Representative expressions:**
- wajar (reasonable, natural, proper)
- pantas (fitting, appropriate, deserved)
- layak (worthy, proper, suitable)
- patut (proper, ought, should)
- tidak pantas (inappropriate)
- kurang wajar (not quite reasonable)
- kewajaran (reasonableness)
- kepantasan (propriety)
- kelayakan (worthiness, suitability)
- kepatutan (propriety)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- tepat (precise, exact) — can mean both factually accurate and socially appropriate
- sesuai (in accordance with) — overlaps with conformity to standards
- cocok (suitable, matching) — more about fit than propriety

## Group 8: Intensification and Emphasis of Truth

**Definition:** Expressions that intensify or emphasize the genuineness, sincerity, or degree of truth in statements or actions. **Representative expressions:**

- sungguh (truly, really)
- sungguh-sungguh (seriously, earnestly)

## Group 18: Social Discomfort and Awkwardness

**Definition:** Expressions describing feelings or displays of social awkwardness, embarrassment, or discomfort. These terms mark psychological and behavioral states arising from social situations, distinct from factual errors or moral wrongdoing.

**Representative expressions:**
- salah tingkah (awkward, embarrassed, ill at ease)

**Note:** This appears to be a relatively isolated semantic domain with limited representation in the collected data. The expression "salah tingkah" is distinctive in using "salah" to mark emotional/social discomfort rather than error or wrongness.

## Group 19: Comparative and Superlative Modifiers

**Definition:** Grammatical constructions that modify truth-related terms to express degrees, comparisons, or superlatives. These patterns are highly productive across multiple base terms and function to create scalar evaluations of truth, correctness, or appropriateness.

**Representative expressions:**
- tidak [X] (not X) — standard negation
- kurang [X] (not quite X, somewhat not-X) — hedged negation
- sangat [X] (very X) — intensification
- paling [X] (most X) — superlative
- lebih [X] (more X) — comparative
- terlalu [X] (too X, excessively X) — excessive degree

**Note:** This is a grammatical pattern rather than a semantic grouping, but it shows systematic productivity across the domain. Examples include: tidak benar, kurang jujur, sangat akurat, paling tepat, lebih wajar, terlalu jujur.

## Ungrouped or Ambiguous Items

**Items that do not fit cleanly into groups above:**

1. **salah satu** (one of) — Grammaticalized construction where "salah" functions as selector/quantifier, completely divorced from error meaning. Unique semantic shift.

2. **sama-sama** (together, mutually; you're welcome) — Highly grammaticalized with multiple functions, including politeness formula unrelated to sameness.

3. **sama sekali** (at all, completely) — Functions primarily as scope marker in negations, minimal connection to sameness concept.

4. **hakiki** (essential, fundamental, true) — Limited representation in data; appears primarily in philosophical/religious compounds like "kebenaran hakiki."

5. **kebetul** (by chance, coincidentally) — Distinct meaning of coincidence rather than correctness, though derived from "betul."

6. **salah alamat** (wrong address; barking up the wrong tree) — Bridges literal and idiomatic meanings in ways that don't fit neatly with other "salah" compounds.

7. **salah jalan** (wrong way; astray) — Can be both literal (physical direction) and metaphorical (moral path), spanning multiple domains.

8. **dengan [X]** / **secara [X]** constructions — Prepositional/adverbial patterns that are productive but primarily grammatical rather than semantic groupings.

**Cross-cutting observations:**

- Many terms participate in multiple groups (e.g., "tepat" appears in factual correctness, social appropriateness, and conformity to standards)
- The "salah" compounds show remarkable productivity across diverse semantic domains
- Borrowed terms (valid, akurat, faktual, objektif, subjektif) tend to cluster in more technical/formal registers
- Formal/literary terms (dusta, hakiki, sejati, absah) show more restricted distribution than colloquial equivalents
