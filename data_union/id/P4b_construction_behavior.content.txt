| **Noun + *yang* + adjective** | *Alasan yang sah harus disertakan dalam permohonan.* | N/A | "A valid reason must be included in the application." | [CORE] | Common in administrative and legal writing; *yang sah* is a high-frequency collocation in formal documents and regulations. |
| **Noun + *yang* + adjective** | *Informasi yang benar dan akurat.* | Informasi yang benar dan akurat. | "Information that is correct and accurate." | [CORE] | Productive default for postnominal modification; unrestricted across registers and item types. |
| **Noun + *yang* + adjective** | *sumber yang terpercaya* | sumber yang terpercaya | "a trusted/reliable source" | [CORE] | Preferred when modifier is participial or the construction is more explicitly predicative; also common across registers. |
| **Adjective + noun (direct juxtaposition, no *yang*)** | *berita palsu* | berita palsu | "fake news" | [MARGINAL] | Restricted to fixed collocations and technical terms; not the productive default. Not freely generalized to new combinations. |
| **Adjective + noun (direct juxtaposition, no *yang*)** | *uang palsu* | uang palsu | "counterfeit money" | [MARGINAL] | Restricted to fixed collocations and technical terms; not the productive default. Not freely generalized to new combinations. |
| **Adjective + noun (direct juxtaposition, no *yang*)** | *hadis sahih* | hadis sahih | "a sound hadith" | [MARGINAL] | Restricted to fixed collocations and technical terms (*hadis sahih*, *emas murni*, *uang palsu*); not the productive default. Not freely generalized to new combinations. |

**Attested alternate constructions:**
- *yang* construction is the productive default; direct juxtaposition is lexically restricted.
- The *yang* form is preferred for participial modifiers and longer phrases; the bare post-nominal form is preferred for compact noun-modifier compounds. No truth-domain-specific restriction attested beyond this general distributional pattern.
- Prenominal order without *yang* (*berita palsu*, *uang palsu*, *hadis sahih*) attested in P4a notes as lexicalized collocations only, not a productive pattern.

- *menjadi kenyataan* (fixed collocation); *terungkap* and *terbukti* are distinct conventionalized forms within the same semantic space.
- *terbukti* + adjective attested in P4a notes alongside *dinyatakan*. Frequent in legal and journalistic registers.
- *Terbukti* constructions overlap partially with the *ter-* stative passive (§8) but are documented separately on the basis of their resultative-evidential function.

### 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active causative: *meN-* + base + *-kan*** | *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu.* | N/A | "The authorities confirmed that report." | [CORE] | *meN-...-kan* causatives are fully productive and common in formal, journalistic, and legal registers; *membenarkan* in the sense of "to confirm" is a standard journalistic verb. |
| **Active causative: *meN-* + base + *-kan*** | *Ia memalsukan tanda tangan pada dokumen itu.* | N/A | "He forged the signature on that document." | [CORE] | *Memalsukan* is the standard verb for forgery/falsification; frequent in legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. The passive *dipalsukan* is equally common in formal writing. |
| **Active causative: *meN-* + base + *-kan*** | *Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik.* | Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik. | "That statement misled the public." | [CORE] | Productive across the truth-domain inventory; unrestricted in register. Attested forms include: *membenarkan*, *mengelirukan*, *memalsukan*, *mengesahkan*, *memurnikan*, *mengikhlaskan*, *menyesatkan*, *menyatakan*. |
| **Active causative: *meN-* + base + *-kan*: confirm/justify** | *Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu.* | Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu. | "Nothing can justify/sanction that action." | [CORE] | *Membenarkan* carries both 'to confirm' and 'to justify/sanction' readings; context-dependent disambiguation; common in formal and legal registers. |
| **Passive causative: *di-* + base + *-kan*** | *Dokumen itu telah dipalsukan.* | Dokumen itu telah dipalsukan. | "That document has been forged." | [CORE] | Equally productive; particularly frequent in formal, legal, and journalistic registers where the agent is backgrounded or unknown. |

**Restrictions noted:** *Akurat*, *sahih*, *sejati*, and *kredibel* lack attested causative forms in standard usage; the pattern is productive but not universal across truth-domain bases.

**Attested alternate constructions:**
- Active and passive forms are both fully productive and co-attested; choice is governed by discourse-pragmatic factors (agent prominence, register).
- No periphrastic alternatives documented in P4a data for the causative function beyond *me-...-kan*.

- *pembenaran*, *pemalsuan*, *pembuktian*, *pengesahan*, *pernyataan* — *peN-...-an* process/result nominalizations attested in P4a §6 notes as a second nominalization pattern distinct from *ke-...-an* abstract nominalizations.

- *-Itas* suffix applies only to loanword bases and does not compete with *ke-...-an* on native bases. Both strategies are attested; distribution is determined by base origin.
- Distinct from *ke-...-an* abstracts (Construction 8): the two circumfixes are not interchangeable — *pemalsuan* (act of forging) vs. *\*kemalsuan* (unattested); *kebenaran* (truth as quality) vs. *pembenaran* (act of justifying/confirming).

- *orang yang jujur*, *saksi yang terpercaya* — periphrastic alternatives for the truth-telling side of the domain where agentive *peN-* forms are not established; attested in P4a notes.
- *manipulator* — loanword formation attested in P4a; does not follow the *pe(N)-* pattern and is restricted to the specific meaning of manipulation.

- *Sebenarnya* and *pada kenyataannya* function primarily as correction frames; *pada hakikatnya* and *pada esensinya* function as essential-characterization frames; *jujur saja* functions as a candid-admission frame. They are not interchangeable.
