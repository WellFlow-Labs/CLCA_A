# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** 진 (眞)
**Transliteration:** jin
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 진짜 (jinjja), 진위 (jinwi), 진본 (jinbon), 진정성 (jinjeonseong), 진정 (jinjeong) — 5–6 items across probes
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "true," "genuine," "real" — marking that something corresponds to what it actually is, as opposed to being false, imitated, or fabricated. Functions as the primary positive pole of the truth-authenticity axis in the domain. Contributes the sense that something is what it claims or appears to be, across registers from everyday speech to philosophical discourse.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** 실 (實)
**Transliteration:** sil
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 사실 (sasil), 실제 (silje), 본질 (bonjil), 실체 (silche) — 3–6 items across probes
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "real," "actual," "solid," "substantial" — marking that something has genuine existence or substance, as opposed to being empty, theoretical, or merely apparent. Contributes the dimension of actuality and concreteness to truth-related expressions. Anchors the domain of factual and ontological reality — the idea that something genuinely exists or obtains.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** 위 (僞)
**Transliteration:** wi
**P1 items participating:** 위조 (wijo), 위증 (wijeung), 허위 (heowi), 진위 (jinwi) — 3–4 items across probes
**Core meaning contribution:** The Sino-Korean morpheme for "false," "fake," "counterfeit" — marking that something is not what it purports to be, produced or presented deceptively. Functions as the primary negative pole in formal and legal registers of the truth-authenticity domain. Anchors the negative pole of the authenticity domain, particularly in formal, legal, and evaluative contexts.

**Note on Root 3 selection:** The native Korean root 거짓 is highly productive in everyday speech but generates a relatively small number of distinct lexical items compared to the Sino-Korean roots. The Sino-Korean morpheme 위 (僞, "false/fake") — appearing in 위조 (wijo), 위증 (wijeung), 허위 (heowi) — is an alternative candidate for the third root. Both are included in the expansion below. The 위 (僞) morpheme is treated as the third root given its broader morphological productivity across formal registers.

**Root 3a (native Korean):**

**Form:** 거짓
**Transliteration:** geojit
**P1 items participating:** 거짓 (geojit), 거짓말 (geojitmal)
**Core meaning contribution:** Native Korean root for falsehood. Highly productive in everyday speech as a modifier in noun phrases (거짓 + noun). Its most lexicalized compound is 거짓말. Generates fewer morphologically complex compounds than the Sino-Korean roots.

---

**Note on additional root (Probe 2 only):**

**Form:** 정 (正)
**Transliteration:** jeong
**P1 items participating:** 정확 (jeonghwak), 정직 (jeongjik), 공정 (gongjeon) — 4 items directly; 정 appears as a morpheme in 정확, 정직, 공정
**Core meaning contribution:** Correct, upright, proper — the normative sense that something conforms to a standard, whether factual, moral, or procedural.

> **Note on scope:** 진 (眞) is the most productive root in the truth-evaluation domain and generates the widest range of compounds. 실 (實) and 정 (正) are both highly productive but their compounds extend substantially into domains beyond truth evaluation (reality, substance, correctness, justice). All roots are expanded below. Because all roots are highly productive (each exceeding 20 common compounds), the expansion below focuses on the 20–30 most common and representative forms for each, with a note that additional forms exist.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### Root 1: 진 (眞) — jin

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 實(real/solid)
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
**Context of use:** Used across legal, journalistic, moral, and everyday contexts to refer to what is actually the case or to the quality of being truthful. Already listed in P1.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 진실하다
**Transliteration:** jinsil-hada
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 하다(to do/be)
**Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be sincere, to be genuine
**Context of use:** Predicative form used to characterize persons or their conduct as honest and without pretense. Slightly more active in connotation than 진실되다. Common in moral evaluation and interpersonal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 진실성
**Transliteration:** jinsilseong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 性(-ness, quality)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, sincerity as a quality; veracity, truth-value
**Context of use:** Used in evaluative discourse — journalistic, legal, and academic — to assess whether a person, account, or work possesses the quality of being truthful or genuine. Appears in constructions like 진실성을 검증하다 (to verify truthfulness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 진실되다
**Transliteration:** jinsil-doeda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 되다(to become/be)
**Natural English translation:** to be truthful, to be genuine (slightly more stative than 진실하다)
**Context of use:** Used to describe persons or expressions as genuinely truthful; appears in literary and formal registers slightly more than 진실하다, with a nuance of inherent rather than performed quality. Alternative predicative form to 진실하다, with a slightly more passive or resultant nuance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 진실로
**Transliteration:** jinsilro
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 로(adverbial particle)
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, sincerely
**Context of use:** Adverbial expression used to emphasize the genuine or sincere character of a statement or action. Appears in formal speech, religious texts, and literary prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 진실게임
**Transliteration:** jinsil-geim
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + game (loanword)
**Natural English translation:** truth game, truth-or-dare (truth component)
**Context of use:** Used colloquially to refer to a social game in which participants must answer questions truthfully, or more broadly to any situation framed as a test of honesty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 진실의 순간
**Transliteration:** jinsil-ui sungan
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 의(genitive) + 瞬間(moment)
**Natural English translation:** moment of truth
**Context of use:** Used to describe a decisive moment when the true nature of something or someone is revealed. Appears in journalistic, literary, and everyday speech, often as a calque of the Spanish/English expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 진실화해위원회
**Transliteration:** jinsil-hwahae-wiwonhoe
**Literal breakdown:** 眞實(truth) + 和解(reconciliation) + 委員會(commission)
**Natural English translation:** Truth and Reconciliation Commission
**Context of use:** Institutional name used in South Korean political and historical discourse to refer to official bodies investigating past human rights abuses. Refers specifically to the Korean Truth and Reconciliation Commission (진실·화해를 위한 과거사정리위원회 in full). Appears in legal, political, and historical registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 짜(suffix)
**Natural English translation:** real, genuine; really, truly
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 진짜배기
**Transliteration:** jinjja-baegi
**Literal breakdown:** 진짜(genuine) + 배기(suffix indicating the genuine article)
**Natural English translation:** the real thing, the genuine article, the real deal
**Context of use:** Emphatic colloquial form of 진짜, used to stress that something is unambiguously genuine rather than imitation. Common in everyday speech and advertising.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 僞(false)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, truth or falsity
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Used in formal and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 진위를 가리다
**Transliteration:** jinwi-reul garida
**Literal breakdown:** 眞僞(truth or falsity) + 를(object marker) + 가리다(to distinguish, to determine)
**Natural English translation:** to determine the truth or falsity of something, to establish authenticity
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation used in legal, journalistic, and investigative contexts when the task is to establish whether a claim, document, or item is genuine or false. Standard phrasing for the act of adjudicating whether something is genuine.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 진위 여부
**Transliteration:** jinwi yeobu
**Literal breakdown:** 眞僞(truth or falsity) + 與否(whether or not)
**Natural English translation:** whether something is true or false, the question of authenticity
**Context of use:** Fixed nominal phrase used in formal writing and legal discourse to frame the question of whether a claim or item is genuine. Frequently appears in news reporting and official documents. Appears in constructions like 진위 여부를 확인하다 (to verify whether something is genuine).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 진본
**Transliteration:** jinbon
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(genuine) + 本(original/copy)
**Natural English translation:** authentic original, genuine document or artwork; genuine copy (of a document or text)
**Context of use:** Used in archival, legal, and art-historical contexts to refer to an authentic original document or manuscript, as opposed to a copy or forgery. Common in historical and bibliographic discourse. Already listed in P1.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 진본 확인
**Transliteration:** jinbon hwagin
**Literal breakdown:** 眞本(authentic original) + 確認(confirmation/verification)
**Natural English translation:** authentication, verification of authenticity
**Context of use:** Used in archival, museum, and legal contexts to describe the process of confirming that a document or artwork is the genuine original.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 正(correct/proper) + 性(-ness)
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1. Prominent in contemporary discourse on art, politics, and personal relationships.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 진정성 있다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong itda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正性(authenticity) + 있다(to exist/have)
**Natural English translation:** to be authentic, to have genuineness
**Context of use:** Predicative collocation used to evaluate whether a person's expression, political stance, or artistic work reflects genuine inner conviction rather than performance or calculation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**18.**
**Form:** 진정성 없다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong eopda
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正性(authenticity) + 없다(to not exist/have)

**Context of use:** Predicative collocation used critically to characterize expressions, apologies, or political gestures as hollow or performative rather than genuine.

---

**19.**
**Form:** 진정성 있는
**Transliteration:** jinjeonseong inneun
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正性(authenticity) + 있는(having, present participle of 있다)
**Natural English translation:** authentic, genuine, having authenticity
**Context of use:** Used as a prenominal modifier to characterize persons, expressions, or works as possessing genuine authenticity. Common in contemporary discourse about personal integrity, artistic work, and public communication.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 진정
**Transliteration:** jinjeong
**Literal breakdown:** 眞(true) + 正(correct/proper)
**Natural English translation:** genuine, true, real (adjectival/adverbial base); truly, really (adverbial)
**Context of use:** Used as a modifier or adverb meaning "truly" or "genuinely" in formal and literary registers. Also used as a noun meaning "petition" or "appeal" (a different lexical item with the same form). Note: 진정 (眞正) meaning "genuine" is distinct from 진정 (陳情) meaning "petition/appeal," though they share the same phonological form. Already listed in P1.

**21.**
**Form:** 진정한
**Transliteration:** jinjeongan
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正(genuine/true) + 한(adjectival ending)
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real (attributive adjective)
**Context of use:** Attributive modifier used to characterize persons, relationships, values, or states as genuinely what they are claimed to be. Extremely common in literary, political, and everyday discourse: 진정한 친구 (a true friend), 진정한 의미 (the true meaning), 진정한 사랑 (true love).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**22.**
**Form:** 진정으로
**Transliteration:** jinjeongeuro
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正(genuine) + 으로(adverbial particle)
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, sincerely
**Context of use:** Adverbial expression used to emphasize that an action or statement reflects genuine inner conviction. Common in formal apologies, declarations, and literary prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**23.**

**Transliteration:** jinjeonghada
**Literal breakdown:** 眞正(genuine/true) + 하다(to be)
**Natural English translation:** to be genuine, sincere, true
**Context of use:** Predicative form used to evaluate whether a person's feelings, intentions, or expressions are authentic. Common in interpersonal and public discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

> **Note:** The root 진 (眞) is highly productive. Additional forms exist beyond those listed above, including various technical or specialized compounds. Additional compounds include 진여 (眞如, jinyeo, "suchness/tathātā" in Buddhist terminology), 진언 (眞言, jineon, "mantra/true word" in Buddhist ritual contexts), and forms in proper names and specialized vocabulary. The items above represent the most common and clearly attested forms.
