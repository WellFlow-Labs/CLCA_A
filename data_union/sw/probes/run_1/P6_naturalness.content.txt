# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents how Swahili encodes dynamic truth-related meanings: becoming a truth-related state, causing a truth-related state, and the stability or instability of truth-related states. All vocabulary is drawn from the CONTEXT. Where the CONTEXT documents a construction, it is included with its source noted. Where the CONTEXT provides no evidence for a dynamic expression involving a particular item, this is stated explicitly. Expressions are organized by semantic type, then by lexical item within each type.

---

## SECTION 1: "X becomes [truth-related state]"

### 1.1 Becoming evident / coming to light

**Primary construction:** *kudhihirika* (stative-intransitive *-ika* form)

**Form:** *Ukweli ulidhihirika.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli ulidhihirika.
**English translation:** "The truth became evident. / The truth came to light."
**Source:** P4a §4, P4b.
**Notes:** *Kudhihirika* is the primary and most natural expression for truth transitioning from a hidden or unknown state to an evident one. The *-ika* suffix derives an agentless inchoative from the causative base *kudhihirisha* (to make evident). The subject is the truth-related nominal; no agent is expressed. This construction is fully lexicalized and common in narrative, journalistic, and formal registers.

**Additional attested form:** *Ukweli ulidhihirika baadaye.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli ulidhihirika baadaye.
**English translation:** "The truth became evident later. / The truth came to light eventually."
**Notes:** The temporal adverb *baadaye* (later, eventually) is a natural collocate in narrative contexts where truth is revealed after a period of concealment.

---

### 1.2 Becoming certain / becoming sure

**Primary construction:** *kuwa na uhakika* (periphrastic)

**Form:** *Alianza kuwa na uhakika.*
**Transliteration:** Alianza kuwa na uhakika.
**English translation:** "He/she began to be certain. / He/she became sure."
**Source:** P4a §4, P4b (rated [MARGINAL] for the periphrastic form).
**Notes:** The CONTEXT rates the periphrastic *kuwa na uhakika* construction as [MARGINAL] relative to the formulaic *nina/sina uhakika*. It is grammatically well-formed but stylistically marked. The verb *kuanza* (to begin) is a natural inchoative auxiliary in Swahili and is added here as a function word; it is not a truth-domain item but is required to express the becoming reading.

**Note on absence of *-ika* form:** No *-ika* stative-intransitive form built on *uhakika* or *hakika* is documented in the CONTEXT. The inchoative reading for certainty is expressed periphrastically rather than through a lexicalized *-ika* form.

---

### 1.3 Becoming correct / being corrected

**No common "become correct" expression is documented in the CONTEXT.** The CONTEXT documents *kusahihisha* (to correct, to make correct) as a causative, but no *-ika* or other inchoative form meaning "to become correct" is attested. The closest available construction is passive:

**Form:** *Makosa yalisahihishwa.*
**Transliteration:** Makosa yalisahihishwa.
**English translation:** "The errors were corrected."
**Source:** P2a §6 (passive suffix *-wa* on causative stem, parallel to *kuthibitishwa*).
**Notes:** This is a passive construction rather than a true inchoative; an agent (implicit or explicit) is presupposed. No agentless "become correct" form is documented.

---

### 1.4 Becoming legitimate / becoming lawful

**Primary construction:** *kuhalalishwa* (passive of causative) or periphrastic

**Form:** *Jambo hilo lilihalalishwa.*
**Transliteration:** Jambo hilo lilihalalishwa.
**English translation:** "That matter was made lawful. / That thing was legalized."
**Source:** P2a §22 (*kuhalalisha* causative; passive *-wa* suffix by parallel with *kuthibitishwa* documented in P2a §29).
**Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *kuhalalisha* (to legitimize, to legalize) but does not document a *-ika* inchoative form (*kuhalalikika* or similar). The becoming-lawful reading is expressed through the passive of the causative rather than through a dedicated inchoative.

---

### 1.5 Becoming false / becoming a lie

**No common "become false" or "become a lie" expression is documented in the CONTEXT.** *Uongo* and *kudanganya* are not documented with *-ika* or other inchoative forms. The CONTEXT documents *kudanganyika* (to be susceptible to deception, to get deceived), but this applies to a person being deceived, not to a proposition becoming false.

**Form:** *Kudanganyika* (stative-potential)
**Transliteration:** kudanganyika
**English translation:** "to be deceivable; to be susceptible to deception; to get deceived"
**Source:** P2a §16 Derived Form 6.
**Notes:** This form applies to persons (becoming deceived) rather than to propositions (becoming false). It is included here for completeness but does not express the "proposition becomes false" reading.

---

## SECTION 2: "X becomes [non-truth / opposed state]"

### 2.1 Truth becoming hidden / concealed

**Primary construction:** Passive relative clause on *ukweli*

**Form:** *Ukweli ulifichwa.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli ulifichwa.
**English translation:** "The truth was hidden. / The truth was concealed."
**Source:** P1b Root 1 (*ukweli uliofichwa*, "hidden truth"; P2b §2 Construction 7).
**Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *ukweli uliofichwa* as a nominal relative construction ("hidden truth"). The finite past passive *ulifichwa* expresses the event of truth being concealed. The verb *kuficha* (to hide, to conceal) is documented in the CONTEXT as the verb in this construction (P5 Opposition 23). This is the primary expression for truth transitioning to a hidden state.

**Form:** *Ukweli ulifichwa na wenye mamlaka.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli ulifichwa na wenye mamlaka.
**English translation:** "The truth was hidden by those in authority."
**Notes:** The passive construction allows an agent to be expressed with *na* (by). *Wenye mamlaka* (those who have authority) is a natural collocate in contexts of institutional concealment of truth; *mamlaka* (authority, power) is not a truth-domain item but is a common collocate in this context.

---

### 2.2 Certainty becoming doubt

**Primary construction:** *kutia shaka* (phrasal causative, applied to the subject's state)

**Form:** *Uhakika wake ulitoweka; shaka iliingia.*
**Transliteration:** Uhakika wake ulitoweka; shaka iliingia.
**English translation:** "His/her certainty disappeared; doubt entered."
**Notes:** The CONTEXT does not document a single lexicalized expression for "certainty becoming doubt." The closest documented construction is *kutia shaka* (to cast doubt, to cause uncertainty; P2a §26), which is a causative construction (see Section 3 below). The transition from certainty to doubt is expressed periphrastically using general verbs of disappearance (*kutoweka*, to disappear) or entry (*kuingia*, to enter). These are not truth-domain items but are required to express the dynamic reading.

---

### 2.3 Something genuine becoming counterfeit / something lawful becoming forbidden

**No common expressions for these transitions are documented in the CONTEXT.** The CONTEXT documents the states (*halisi*, *bandia*, *halali*, *haramu*) and the causatives (*kuhalalisha*, *kuharamisha*) but does not document inchoative or transitional expressions for objects moving from genuine to counterfeit or from lawful to forbidden status.

---

## SECTION 3: "Y causes X to be [truth-related state]"

### 3.1 Causing truth to be established / confirmed

**Primary construction:** *kuthibitisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Ushahidi ulithibitisha ukweli wa tukio hilo.*
**Transliteration:** Ushahidi ulithibitisha ukweli wa tukio hilo.
**English translation:** "The evidence confirmed the truth of that event. / The evidence established the truth of that incident."
**Source:** P1 (*thibitisha*: "to confirm, to verify, to prove, to authenticate"); P2a §29; P4a §5; P4b.
**Notes:** *Kuthibitisha* is the primary causative verb for causing truth or authenticity to be established. The subject (*ushahidi*, evidence) is the causal agent; *ukweli wa X* is the caused state. This is the most natural and frequent construction for this meaning in formal, legal, and journalistic registers.

**Form:** *Tafadhali thibitisha taarifa hii.*
**Transliteration:** Tafadhali thibitisha taarifa hii.
**English translation:** "Please confirm / verify this report."
**Source:** P4a §5, P4b.
**Notes:** The imperative form with *tafadhali* (please) is a common polite request formula in administrative and journalistic contexts.

---

### 3.2 Causing certainty / making sure

**Primary construction:** *kuhakikisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Hakikisha ukweli wa habari kabla ya kuchapisha.*
**Transliteration:** Hakikisha ukweli wa habari kabla ya kuchapisha.
**English translation:** "Make sure of the accuracy of the news before publishing. / Verify the truth of the information before publishing."
**Source:** P1b Root 2 (*kuhakikisha*: "to make sure, to ensure, to verify, to confirm"); P2a §10; P4a §5; P4b.
**Notes:** *Kuhakikisha* is extremely frequent across all registers. It causes a state of certainty or verified correctness. The CONTEXT notes that it extends into procedural contexts unrelated to truth evaluation (*hakikisha mlango umefungwa*, "make sure the door is closed"), which means it is not restricted to the truth domain.

**Form:** *Hakikisha mlango umefungwa.*
**Transliteration:** Hakikisha mlango umefungwa.
**English translation:** "Make sure the door is closed."
**Source:** P4a §5, P4b.
**Notes:** Included to document the procedural extension of *kuhakikisha* outside the truth domain, as explicitly noted in the CONTEXT.

---

### 3.3 Causing something to be evident / demonstrating

**Primary construction:** *kudhihirisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Uchunguzi ulidhihirisha ukweli uliofichwa.*
**Transliteration:** Uchunguzi ulidhihirisha ukweli uliofichwa.
**English translation:** "The investigation revealed the hidden truth. / The inquiry demonstrated the concealed truth."
**Source:** P2a §28 (*kudhihirisha*: "to reveal, to demonstrate, to make evident, to show clearly"); P4a §5.
**Notes:** *Kudhihirisha* causes a state of evidentiality or visibility. It is the causative counterpart of the inchoative *kudhihirika* (see Section 1.1). The subject is the agent of revelation; the object is the truth-related nominal.

**Form:** *Alidhihirisha ukweli kwa ushahidi.*
**Transliteration:** Alidhihirisha ukweli kwa ushahidi.
**English translation:** "He/she demonstrated the truth with evidence."
**Notes:** The instrumental phrase *kwa ushahidi* (with evidence) is a natural collocate; *kwa* is a function word documented throughout the CONTEXT.

---

### 3.4 Causing something to be correct / correcting

**Primary construction:** *kusahihisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya mwanafunzi.*
**Transliteration:** Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya mwanafunzi.
**English translation:** "The teacher corrected the student's errors."
**Source:** P2a §6 (*kusahihisha*: "to correct, to rectify, to amend, to mark examinations"); P4a §5; P4b.
**Notes:** *Kusahihisha* causes a state of correctness by removing errors. It is most common in educational and editorial contexts. The subject is the agent of correction; the object is the thing being corrected.

**Form:** *Sahihisha jibu lako.*
**Transliteration:** Sahihisha jibu lako.
**English translation:** "Correct your answer."
**Source:** P2a §6 (imperative *sahihisha*); P1b Root 3.
**Notes:** The imperative form is common in classroom and editorial settings.

---

### 3.5 Causing something to be lawful / legitimizing

**Primary construction:** *kuhalalisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Serikali ilihalalisha shughuli hiyo.*
**Transliteration:** Serikali ilihalalisha shughuli hiyo.
**English translation:** "The government legalized that activity. / The government legitimized that practice."
**Source:** P2a §22 (*kuhalalisha*: "to legitimize, to legalize, to authorize, to make lawful").
**Notes:** *Kuhalalisha* causes a state of lawfulness or legitimacy. The subject is the authorizing agent (typically an institution); the object is the activity or thing being made lawful.

---

### 3.6 Causing something to be forbidden / prohibiting

**Primary construction:** *kuharamisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Sheria iliharamisha biashara hiyo.*
**Transliteration:** Sheria iliharamisha biashara hiyo.
**English translation:** "The law prohibited that trade. / The law criminalized that business."
**Source:** P2a §23 (*kuharamisha*: "to prohibit, to make unlawful, to criminalize, to forbid").
**Notes:** *Kuharamisha* causes a state of prohibition or unlawfulness. It is the causative counterpart of *kuhalalisha* (see P5 Opposition 13). The subject is the prohibiting authority; the object is the activity being forbidden.

---

### 3.7 Causing doubt / casting doubt

**Primary construction:** *kutia shaka* (phrasal causative)

**Form:** *Habari mpya ilitia shaka ukweli wa taarifa ya awali.*
**Transliteration:** Habari mpya ilitia shaka ukweli wa taarifa ya awali.
**English translation:** "The new information cast doubt on the truth of the initial report."
**Source:** P2a §26 (*kutia shaka*: "to cast doubt, to cause uncertainty, to make someone doubt").
**Notes:** *Kutia shaka* is a phrasal causative using the light verb *tia* (to put, to apply) with *shaka* as object. It causes a state of doubt or uncertainty. The CONTEXT documents this as the primary construction for introducing doubt; no morphologically derived causative from *shaka* is attested.

---

### 3.8 Causing deception / making someone believe a falsehood

**Primary construction:** *kudanganya* (verb)

**Form:** *Alimkudanganya kwa habari za uongo.*
**Transliteration:** Alimkudanganya kwa habari za uongo.
**English translation:** "He/she deceived him/her with false information."
**Source:** P1 (*kudanganya*: "to deceive, to lie, to mislead, to cheat"); P2a §16.
**Notes:** *Kudanganya* takes a human object (the person deceived) and optionally an instrumental phrase specifying the means of deception. It causes a state of being deceived (*kudanganywa*) in the object.

**Form:** *Alimkudanganya.*
**Transliteration:** Alimkudanganya.
**English translation:** "He/she deceived him/her."
**Notes:** The object prefix *m-* (him/her, class 1) is incorporated into the verb. This is the most common finite form in everyday and legal discourse.

---

### 3.9 Causing trust / making someone believe

**Primary construction:** *kuamini* (verb, with causative reading in context)

**Form:** *Alimfanya aamini ukweli.*
**Transliteration:** Alimfanya aamini ukweli.
**English translation:** "He/she made him/her believe the truth."
**Notes:** The CONTEXT documents *kuamini* (to believe, to trust; P2a §12) but does not document a morphologically derived causative (*kuaminisha* or similar) in the truth domain. The causative reading is expressed periphrastically using *kufanya* (to make, to cause) + subjunctive of *kuamini*. *Kufanya* is a function-word-level causative auxiliary in Swahili and is not a truth-domain item.

---

### 3.10 Causing equalization / making things equal or fair

**Primary construction:** *kusawazisha* (causative verb)

**Form:** *Serikali ilijaribu kusawazisha haki za wananchi wote.*
**Transliteration:** Serikali ilijaribu kusawazisha haki za wananchi wote.
**English translation:** "The government tried to equalize the rights of all citizens."
**Source:** P2a §24 (*kusawazisha*: "to equalize, to balance, to level, to make equal").
**Notes:** *Kusawazisha* causes a state of equality or balance (*usawa*). It is used in social policy, financial, and moral discourse. The subject is the equalizing agent; the object is the thing being equalized.

## SECTION 4: Stability expressions

### 4.1 Truth as stable / enduring

**Primary construction:** *ukweli unaosimama* (relative clause nominal)

**Form:** *Ukweli unaosimama hauwezi kubadilishwa.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli unaosimama hauwezi kubadilishwa.
**English translation:** "Truth that stands cannot be changed. / Enduring truth cannot be altered."
**Source:** P1b Root 1 (*ukweli unaosimama*: "truth that stands, enduring truth, truth that holds up"; rated [MARGINAL]).
**Notes:** The CONTEXT rates *ukweli unaosimama* as [MARGINAL] and associates it with sermon and oratory registers. The relative clause *unaosimama* (that stands, that holds firm; class 11 subject prefix + present relative + *simama*) encodes stability as an ongoing state. The added clause *hauwezi kubadilishwa* (cannot be changed) is constructed from general Swahili grammar and is not a truth-domain item, but it is the natural completion of this stability assertion.

**Form:** *Kweli ni kweli.*
**Transliteration:** Kweli ni kweli.
**English translation:** "Truth is truth. / The truth is the truth."
**Source:** P1b Root 1; P2b §1 Construction 6; P5 Opposition 1 (implicitly).
**Notes:** This tautological copular construction asserts the immutability and self-identity of truth. It is used rhetorically to assert that a fact cannot be denied or altered regardless of circumstances. It is the most common stability assertion in everyday moral and argumentative discourse.

**Form:** *Ukweli utakuwa ukweli daima.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli utakuwa ukweli daima.
**English translation:** "Truth will always be truth. / The truth will always remain the truth."
**Source:** P2b §2 Construction 16 (rated [MARGINAL]).
**Notes:** The future copular construction *utakuwa* (will be; class 11 subject prefix + future + *kuwa*) with *daima* (always) asserts the permanence of truth across time. The CONTEXT rates this as [MARGINAL] and associates it with written and oratorical registers.

---

### 4.2 Truth as unstable / vulnerable to concealment

**Primary construction:** *ukweli uliofichwa* (passive relative nominal)

**Form:** *Ukweli uliofichwa hatimaye ulidhihirika.*
**Transliteration:** Ukweli uliofichwa hatimaye ulidhihirika.
**English translation:** "The hidden truth eventually came to light."
**Source:** P1b Root 1 (*ukweli uliofichwa*); P2b §2 Construction 7; P5 Opposition 23.
**Notes:** This construction combines the instability expression (*ukweli uliofichwa*, truth that was hidden) with the inchoative *ulidhihirika* (became evident; see Section 1.1), expressing a full narrative arc from concealment to revelation. *Hatimaye* (eventually, in the end) is a temporal adverb that is a natural collocate in this narrative pattern.

---

### 4.3 Lies as unstable / self-defeating

**Primary construction:** *Uongo una miguu mifupi.* (proverb)

**Form:** *Uongo una miguu mifupi.*
**Transliteration:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
**English translation:** "A lie has short legs. / Lies don't travel far."
**Source:** P2b §3 Construction 7.
**Notes:** The CONTEXT documents this as "a widely attested Swahili proverb asserting that lies are eventually discovered." It encodes the instability of falsehood: lies cannot sustain themselves indefinitely. This is the primary proverbial expression of the dynamic instability of *uongo* in the CONTEXT.

---

### 4.4 Certainty as a possessed state (stable or unstable)

**Primary construction:** *Nina uhakika* / *Sina uhakika* (possessive epistemic)

**Form:** *Nilikuwa na uhakika, lakini sasa sina uhakika.*
**Transliteration:** Nilikuwa na uhakika, lakini sasa sina uhakika.
**English translation:** "I was certain, but now I am not sure."
**Notes:** The possessive epistemic construction (*nina/sina uhakika*; P4a §10, P4b) can be used in past tense (*nilikuwa na uhakika*, I had certainty) to express a change from a state of certainty to one of uncertainty. *Lakini* (but) and *sasa* (now) are function words. This construction is the primary means of expressing the loss of certainty in everyday discourse.

## SECTION 6: Structural observations

The following observations are directly supported by the data above and are offered without interpretive extension.

**6.1** The *-ika* stative-intransitive suffix is the primary morphological device for expressing "becoming" in this domain, but it is attested for only two items: *kudhihirika* (from *kudhihirisha*) and *kudanganyika* (from *kudanganya*). No *-ika* forms are documented for *ukweli*, *uhakika*, *sahihi*, *halisi*, *bandia*, *halali*, or *haramu*.

**6.2** The causative suffix *-sha*/*-isha* is the primary morphological device for expressing "causing" in this domain, attested for *kuthibitisha*, *kuhakikisha*, *kusahihisha*, *kudhihirisha*, *kuhalalisha*, *kuharamisha*, and *kusawazisha*. The phrasal causative *kutia shaka* covers the one case (*shaka*) where no morphological causative is documented.

**6.3** Stability is expressed primarily through tautological copular constructions (*kweli ni kweli*), proverbial assertions (*Uongo una miguu mifupi*), and relative-clause nominals (*ukweli unaosimama*). No dedicated stability morpheme or construction is attested; stability is encoded through general Swahili grammatical resources applied to truth-domain nominals.

**6.4** The possessive epistemic construction (*nina/sina uhakika*) is the only truth-domain construction that directly encodes a changeable internal state through tense variation (*nilikuwa na uhakika* vs. *sina uhakika*). No parallel possessive construction is documented for *kweli*, *sahihi*, *halisi*, or *uongo*.

**6.5** No "become true" expression built directly on *kweli* or *ukweli* is documented in the CONTEXT. The transition to a state of truth being known is expressed through *kudhihirika* (becoming evident), which is built on *dhahiri* rather than on *kweli* itself.
