# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Forms already fully documented in P1 or P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described at length. Derivations are listed per base form; no attempt is made to impose uniform paradigm structure. Gaps are preserved. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and currency of use.

---

## Base Form 1: ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti

---

**Derived Form 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti
2. **Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
5. **Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
6. **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in philosophical, theological, and everyday discourse. Documented in P1 as item 2; cross-referenced here as the primary nominal derivation of č̣ešmariti.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti
2. **Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტად
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritad
4. **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmariti + adverbial suffix -ad
5. **Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, verily, genuinely
6. **Usage:** Adverb used in formal, literary, and theological registers to affirm the truth of a statement. Documented in P1b supplementary note S1. Less common in everyday speech than namdvilad or martlac.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation:** No verbal derivation from č̣ešmariti is in common use. No agentive, diminutive, or negative prefix form is attested with currency. The derivational range of this root is narrower than that of marṭ-, q̇alb-, or namdvil-.

## Base Form 3: სიმართლე / simartle

---

**Note:** simartle is itself a derivation of the root marṭ- (documented in P1b Root 1). As a base for further derivation, the following is attested:

---

**Derived Form 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე / simartle
2. **Derived Form:** სიმართლის (genitive collocations — see note)
3. **Transliteration:** simartlis
4. **Literal Breakdown:** simartle + genitive suffix -is
5. **Natural English translation:** of truth (in compounds and collocations)
6. **Usage:** The genitive form participates in productive collocations: simartlis tq̇ma (telling the truth), simartlis dadgena (establishing the truth), simartlis ʒieba (seeking the truth), simartlis gamovlena (revealing the truth). These are documented in P1b items 30–33. These are collocational rather than morphologically derived forms.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation:** No derivation of simartle as a base (i.e., using simartle itself as the input to a derivational process) is attested in common use. The root marṭ- is the productive base; simartle does not itself generate further suffixed forms.

(no common derivation identified beyond collocational use of the genitive)

## Base Form 4: მართალი / martali

---

**Derived Form 4.1**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** მართლა
3. **Transliteration:** martla
4. **Literal Breakdown:** marṭ- + -al- + adverbial suffix -a
5. **Natural English translation:** truly, really, indeed; also used as a conversational particle meaning "really?" or "is that so?"
6. **Usage:** Common adverb in everyday spoken Georgian. Functions both as an affirmation of truth and as a discourse particle expressing genuine surprise or seeking confirmation. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 3.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.2**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** მართლაც
3. **Transliteration:** martlac
4. **Literal Breakdown:** marṭ- + -al- + -a (adverbial) + -c (emphatic particle)
5. **Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, really (emphatic)
6. **Usage:** Emphatic adverb used to confirm or reinforce a statement as genuinely true. More emphatic than martla. Common in both spoken and written Georgian. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 4.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.3**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** მართებული
3. **Transliteration:** martebuli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** marṭ- + -eb- (verbal stem extension) + -ul- (participial suffix) + -i
5. **Natural English translation:** correct, appropriate, proper, well-founded, justified
6. **Usage:** Applied to arguments, decisions, conclusions, and actions to indicate that they are well-founded or appropriate. More formal than martali. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 14.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.4**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** მართებულობა
3. **Transliteration:** martebuloba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** martebuli + nominalizing suffix -oba
5. **Natural English translation:** correctness, appropriateness, justifiedness, validity
6. **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the property of being well-founded or appropriate. Used in legal, academic, and administrative contexts. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 15.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.5**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali

3. **Transliteration:** martebulaد
4. **Literal Breakdown:** martebuli + adverbial suffix -ad
5. **Natural English translation:** correctly, appropriately, justifiably, rightly
6. **Usage:** Adverb used in formal and written registers to characterize an action or judgment as having been carried out in a correct or appropriate manner. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 16.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.6**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** გამართლება

5. **Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, acquittal, excuse
6. **Usage:** Used in legal contexts for acquittal, in moral contexts for justification or vindication, and in everyday speech for making excuses. One of the most common derivatives of the root. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 17.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form 4.7**

6. **Usage:** Abstract noun used in legal and constitutional discourse to refer to the quality of being within legal bounds or proportionate to a legitimate aim. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 28.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4.15**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი / martali
2. **Derived Form:** მართლმეტყველება
3. **Transliteration:** martlmetq̇veleba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** marṭl- + metq̇vel- (speaker, one who speaks) + -eba
5. **Natural English translation:** correct speech, orthoepy, proper diction
6. **Usage:** Used in linguistic and educational contexts to refer to correct or standard pronunciation and speech. Less common than martlc̣era but attested in linguistic literature. Documented in P1b Root 1, item 13.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The root marṭ- is exceptionally productive. The derivations above are drawn from P1b documentation. The base form martali is the most accessible entry point for the root in everyday speech, but many of the derived forms listed above derive more directly from the reduced stem marṭl- rather than from martali as such. This is noted without imposing a structural categorization.

## Base Form 6: ყალბი / q̇albi

---

**Derived Form 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Derived Form:** სიყალბე
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albe
4. **Literal Breakdown:** si- (nominal prefix) + q̇alb- + -e (nominal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** falsehood, falsity, forgery, counterfeit quality
6. **Usage:** The primary abstract nominal form of the root. Documented in P1 as item 5. Denotes the quality of being false or counterfeit, applicable to documents, currency, statements, and persons.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Derived Form:** ყალბობა
3. **Transliteration:** q̇alboba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** q̇alb- + nominalizing/verbal suffix -oba
5. **Natural English translation:** forgery, falsification (as an act or practice)
6. **Usage:** Used in legal contexts to refer to the act or practice of forgery or falsification. Also used in everyday speech to describe habitual deceptive behavior. Documented in P1b Root 2, item 3.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.3**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Derived Form:** გაყალბება
3. **Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** ga- (preverb) + q̇alb- + -eba (verbal noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** falsification, forgery (as a completed act); the act of making something false or counterfeit
6. **Usage:** Verbal noun denoting the act of falsifying or forging something. Used in legal and administrative contexts, particularly in criminal law discussions of document fraud and electoral fraud. Distinct from q̇alboba in that gaq̇albeba typically refers to a specific completed act of falsification, while q̇alboba may refer to the practice or habit more generally.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.4**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Derived Form:** გაყალბება არჩევნების
3. **Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba arčevnebis
4. **Literal Breakdown:** gaq̇albeba + arčevnebi (elections, genitive -is)
5. **Natural English translation:** electoral fraud, falsification of elections, rigging of elections
6. **Usage:** Standard collocation in political and legal discourse to describe the manipulation or falsification of electoral results. Widely used in Georgian political journalism and legal proceedings.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.5**

1. **Base Form:** ყალბი / q̇albi
2. **Derived Form:** გაყალბება დოკუმენტების
3. **Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba dokumentebis
4. **Literal Breakdown:** gaq̇albeba + dokumentebi (documents, genitive -is)
5. **Natural English translation:** falsification of documents, document fraud
6. **Usage:** Standard legal collocation used in criminal law to describe the offense of forging or falsifying official documents.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on verbal paradigm:** The verb გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba is the verbal noun (masdar) of the verb გაყალბება (to falsify, to forge). The finite verb forms (e.g., გაყალბებს — he/she will falsify; გაყალბა — he/she falsified) are attested but are inflected verb forms rather than derivations in the morphological sense addressed here. They are noted for completeness but not listed as separate derived forms.

---

**Note on further derivation:** No agentive noun derived directly from q̇albi (e.g., a form meaning "forger" built on this root alone) is in common use as a single lexical item; the concept is typically expressed periphrastically or through collocations. No diminutive or evaluative derivation is attested.

4. **Literal Breakdown:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba

## Base Form 9: სიცრუე / sicrue

---

**Derived Form 9.1**

1. **Base Form:** სიცრუე / sicrue
2. **Derived Form:** ცრუ
3. **Transliteration:** cru
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cru- (root: lie, false) — the bare root form, functioning as an adjective
5. **Natural English translation:** false, lying, deceitful, untrue
6. **Usage:** The adjectival form of the root, used attributively to characterize persons or statements as false or deceitful. Appears in compounds and collocations: cru moq̇me (false witness), cru piroba (false promise). Less common as a standalone predicate than q̇albi or mcdari, but productive in compounds.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9.2**

1. **Base Form:** სიცრუე / sicrue
2. **Derived Form:** ცრუ მოწმე
3. **Transliteration:** cru moq̇me
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cru (false) + moq̇me (witness)
5. **Natural English translation:** false witness, perjurer
6. **Usage:** Used in legal and biblical/theological contexts to describe a person who gives false testimony. Appears in the Ten Commandments in Georgian (the prohibition on bearing false witness) and in legal discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9.3**

1. **Base Form:** სიცრუე / sicrue
2. **Derived Form:** ცრუ წინასწარმეტყველი
3. **Transliteration:** cru c̣inasq̇armetq̇veli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cru (false) + c̣inasq̇armetq̇veli (prophet, one who speaks beforehand)
5. **Natural English translation:** false prophet
6. **Usage:** Used in theological and figurative contexts to describe a person who falsely claims prophetic authority or whose predictions are unreliable. Common in religious texts and in figurative political discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9.4**

1. **Base Form:** სიცრუე / sicrue
2. **Derived Form:** ცრუობა
3. **Transliteration:** cruoba
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cru- + nominalizing suffix -oba
5. **Natural English translation:** lying, deceitfulness, the practice of lying
6. **Usage:** Denotes the practice or habit of lying. Used in moral discourse to characterize a person's disposition toward dishonesty. Less common than sicrue but attested in written Georgian.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9.5**

1. **Base Form:** სიცრუე / sicrue
2. **Derived Form:** ცრუობს
3. **Transliteration:** cruobs
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cru- + verbal suffix -ob- + present tense marker -s
5. **Natural English translation:** he/she lies, he/she is lying (present tense)
6. **Usage:** The finite verb form meaning "to lie" (to tell a lie). Used across all registers in everyday speech. The verbal paradigm built on cru- is productive and widely used. The verbal noun (masdar) form is cruoba (see 9.4).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on verbal paradigm:** The verb cru-ob- (to lie, to tell lies) is fully conjugated across tenses and persons. Finite forms are inflected verb forms rather than derivations in the morphological sense, but the verbal stem cruob- is noted here as it represents a productive extension of the root beyond the nominal sicrue.

## Base Form 10: მცდარი / mcdari

---

**Derived Form 10.1**

1. **Base Form:** მცდარი / mcdari
2. **Derived Form:** შეცდომა
3. **Transliteration:** šecdoma
4. **Literal Breakdown:** še- (preverb) + cdom- (to err) + -a (verbal noun suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** mistake, error, fault
6. **Usage:** The standard noun for an error or mistake, applicable to factual, moral, and practical domains. Shares the root cdar-/cdom- with mcdari. Documented in P1 as item 11. The relationship between mcdari and šecdoma is one of shared root rather than direct derivation of one from the other.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on derivational status:** mcdari (participial adjective: erroneous, mistaken) and šecdoma (verbal noun: mistake, error) share the root cdar-/cdom- but are parallel formations from the same root rather than one being derived from the other. No derivation of mcdari as a base (i.e., using mcdari itself as the input to a further derivational process) is attested in common use. The root is productive but the derivations are built on the verbal stem rather than on the adjectival form mcdari.

(no common derivation of mcdari as base identified)

## Summary of Derivational Gaps

The following P1 base forms show no attested common morphological derivation beyond what is documented in P1 itself or in the entries above:

- **ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba** (Base Form 2): no further derivation
- **სიმართლე / simartle** (Base Form 3): no morphological derivation from simartle as base; root marṭ- is productive
- **სიყალბე / siq̇albe** (Base Form 5): no further derivation from siq̇albe as base
- **ნამდვილობა / namdviloba** (Base Form 8): no further derivation
- **სანდოობა / sandooba** (Base Form 13): no further derivation
- **სარწმუნოება / sarc̣munoeba** (Base Form 23): no further derivation
- **ავთენტურობა / avṭenturoba** (Base Form 21): no further derivation
- **გულწრფელობა / gulc̣rpeloba** (Base Form 16): no further derivation
- **სიზუსტე / sizuste** (Base Form 30): no further derivation

This asymmetry is preserved as documented. Abstract nouns formed with -eba or -oba generally do not themselves serve as bases for further suffixation in Georgian; the productive base remains the adjectival or root form.
