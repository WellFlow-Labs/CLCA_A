# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

**1. egia**
- **Form:** egia
- **Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
- **Literal meaning:** truth (nominal form; etymology debated, possibly related to *egi-* root)
- **Typical English translations:** truth, true fact
- **Usage:** The primary Basque word for truth, used across everyday, philosophical, and legal contexts. It appears in statements like *egia da* ("it is true") and participates in extensive derivational patterns. It is the base for numerous compounds and derived forms.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2. egiazkoa**
- **Form:** egiazkoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** egia (truth) + -zko (adjectival suffix) + -a (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
- **Usage:** Used attributively to describe statements, persons, or things evaluated as truthful or genuine. Contrasts with *gezurrezkoa* (false, lying). Commonly found in both spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3. benetakoa**
- **Form:** benetakoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *bene-* (possibly from Latin *bene*, "well/good") + *-tako* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
- **Usage:** Widely used to assert that something is the real or authentic instance of a kind, as opposed to a copy, imitation, or pretense. Applies to objects, persons, feelings, and situations. Frequently used in everyday speech and commercial contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4. gezurra**
- **Form:** gezurra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (nominal form; *gezur-* + definite *-a*)
- **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
- **Usage:** The standard term for a lie or false statement, used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Appears in expressions such as *gezurra esan* ("to tell a lie"). It is the primary antonym of *egia*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5. faltsua**
- **Form:** faltsua
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** false (from Romance *falso*) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** false, fake, deceitful, insincere
- **Usage:** Used to describe statements, documents, persons, or objects that are false, counterfeit, or deceptive. Has a somewhat stronger connotation of deliberate deception than simple incorrectness. Used in legal and everyday registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6. zuzena**
- **Form:** zuzena
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** straight, direct, correct (*zuzen* + definite *-a*)
- **Typical English translations:** correct, right, straight, just, fair
- **Usage:** Applied to statements, conduct, procedures, and persons to indicate correctness, rightness, or fairness. In moral contexts it carries a sense of uprightness or rectitude. Also used in legal and administrative language for procedural correctness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7. okerra**
- **Form:** okerra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** crooked, bent, wrong (*oker* + definite *-a*)
- **Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, crooked, mistaken
- **Usage:** The standard antonym of *zuzena*, used to describe errors, incorrect statements, or morally wrong conduct. The spatial metaphor of crookedness underlies both the physical and evaluative senses. Widely used across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8. zehatza**
- **Form:** zehatza
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** precise, exact (*zehat-* + definite *-a*)
- **Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate, strict
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, measurements, descriptions, or conduct as accurate and precise. Common in technical, scientific, and administrative language. Also used in everyday speech to commend exactness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9. fidagarria**
- **Form:** fidagarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *fida-* (trust, from Romance) + *-garri* (potential/worthy suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable
- **Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, or information to indicate that they can be trusted or relied upon. Commonly used in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in the productive *-garri* derivational pattern.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10. sinesgarria**
- **Form:** sinesgarria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *sines-* (believe, from *sinetsi*) + *-garri* (worthy of) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** believable, credible, plausible
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate whether a statement, account, or person is credible or worthy of belief. Appears in legal testimony contexts and in everyday assessments of plausibility. Contrasts with *sineskaitza* (incredible, unbelievable).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11. egiazkotasuna**
- **Form:** egiazkotasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egiazko* (true/genuine) + *-tasun* (abstract noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** truthfulness, genuineness, veracity
- **Usage:** An abstract nominal derived from *egiazkoa*, used in philosophical, theological, and formal written contexts to refer to the property of being truthful or genuine. Less common in everyday speech than the adjectival base form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12. balioa**
- **Form:** balioa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** value, worth (*balio* + definite *-a*; from Romance *valor*)
- **Typical English translations:** value, worth, validity
- **Usage:** Used in evaluative contexts to refer to the worth, validity, or standing of an object, action, or claim. In legal contexts it can refer to the validity of a document or act. Participates in expressions like *balio izan* ("to be valid/worth").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13. baliozkoa**
- **Form:** baliozkoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *balio* (value) + *-zko* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, of value
- **Usage:** Used to describe documents, acts, arguments, or claims as legally or formally valid. Common in administrative and legal language. Contrasts with *baliogabea* (invalid, null).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14. baliogabea**
- **Form:** baliogabea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *balio* (value) + *-gabe* (without) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** invalid, null, void, worthless
- **Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe acts, documents, or claims that lack validity or legal force. Also used more broadly to describe things lacking worth or standing. Direct antonym of *baliozkoa*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15. jatorra**
- **Form:** jatorra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *jator* (genuine, of good origin) + *-a* (definite article); *jator* possibly related to *jaio* (to be born)
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, of good stock, decent, honest
- **Usage:** Applied to persons and things to indicate genuine, authentic, or upright character. In colloquial usage it often carries a warm connotation of honest, straightforward decency. Also used to describe authentic or original versions of things.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16. benetakotasuna**
- **Form:** benetakotasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *benetako* (genuine/real) + *-tasun* (abstract noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, realness
- **Usage:** Abstract nominal used in philosophical, aesthetic, and cultural discourse to refer to the property of being authentic or genuine. Appears in discussions of cultural identity, art, and personal integrity. More formal than the adjectival base.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17. itxurakeria**
- **Form:** itxurakeria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *itxura* (appearance, semblance) + *-keria* (pejorative abstract suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** pretense, sham, hypocrisy, mere appearance
- **Usage:** Used to describe conduct or presentation that is all outward show without genuine substance, often with a moral-critical tone. The *-keria* suffix marks it as a pejorative. Common in moral and social criticism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18. itxura**
- **Form:** itxura
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** appearance, semblance, form
- **Typical English translations:** appearance, semblance, look, guise
- **Usage:** Used to refer to the outward appearance or semblance of something, often implicitly contrasted with underlying reality. Appears in evaluative contexts where surface appearance may or may not correspond to genuine nature. Base for several derived forms including *itxurakeria*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19. antzekoa**
- **Form:** antzekoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *antze-* (resemblance, likeness) + *-ko* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, alike
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate resemblance between two things, persons, or states of affairs. Appears in aesthetic and descriptive contexts where likeness or similarity is being assessed. The root *antza* also appears in *antza izan* ("to resemble").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20. kopiakoa**
- **Form:** kopiakoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *kopia* (copy, from Romance) + *-ko* (adjectival/relational suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** copy, reproduction, imitation
- **Usage:** Used to describe an object or document that is a copy rather than an original, relevant in legal, artistic, and commercial evaluative contexts. Contrasts implicitly with *jatorrizkoa* (original). Common in administrative and everyday language.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21. jatorrizkoa**
- **Form:** jatorrizkoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *jatorri* (origin, source) + *-zko* (adjectival suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** original, source, primary
- **Usage:** Used to describe the original version of a document, work, or object as opposed to a copy or derivative. Common in legal, archival, and artistic contexts. Contrasts with *kopiakoa*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22. zintzoa**
- **Form:** zintzoa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** honest, sincere, upright (*zintzo* + definite *-a*)
- **Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, upright, faithful
- **Usage:** Applied to persons and conduct to indicate honesty, sincerity, and moral uprightness. Widely used in everyday moral evaluation and in descriptions of character. One of the most common terms for personal moral integrity in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23. zintzotasuna**
- **Form:** zintzotasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *zintzo* (honest/sincere) + *-tasun* (abstract noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, integrity, uprightness
- **Usage:** Abstract nominal denoting the quality of being honest or sincere. Used in moral, educational, and social discourse. Participates in the standard *-tasun* nominalization pattern.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24. zilegitasuna**
- **Form:** zilegitasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *zilegi* (lawful, permissible) + *-tasun* (abstract noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** legitimacy, lawfulness, permissibility
- **Usage:** Used in legal and political contexts to refer to the legitimacy or lawfulness of an act, authority, or claim. The base adjective *zilegi* is also used independently. Common in formal and institutional discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25. egiaztatzea**
- **Form:** egiaztatzea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egia* (truth) + *-zta-* (verbalizing infix) + *-tze* (verbal noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** verification, verifying, confirming, attesting
- **Usage:** The verbal noun of *egiaztatu* ("to verify, to confirm, to attest"), used in legal, administrative, and scientific contexts to refer to the act of verifying or confirming the truth of something. Appears in procedural and documentary language.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26. egiaztatu**
- **Form:** egiaztatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egia* (truth) + *-zta-* (verbalizing infix) + *-tu* (perfective verbal suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to attest, to certify
- **Usage:** The verb meaning to verify or confirm the truth or accuracy of something. Used in legal, scientific, and administrative contexts. Participates in the same derivational family as *egia* and *egiazkoa*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27. frogatzea**
- **Form:** frogatzea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *froga* (proof, evidence; from Romance *prueba/prova*) + *-tze* (verbal noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** proving, demonstrating, testing
- **Usage:** The verbal noun of *frogatu* ("to prove, to demonstrate, to test"), used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts where evidence or demonstration of truth is at issue. The nominal *froga* ("proof, evidence") is also widely used independently.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28. froga**
- **Form:** froga
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** proof, evidence, test (from Romance *prueba/prova*)
- **Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, test, trial
- **Usage:** Used in legal contexts for evidence or proof, and more broadly for any demonstration or test of a claim. One of the most common terms in legal and argumentative discourse in Basque. Base for *frogatu* and related forms.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29. itxuraz**
- **Form:** itxuraz
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *itxura* (appearance) + *-z* (instrumental/adverbial suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** apparently, seemingly, ostensibly, by appearances
- **Usage:** An adverbial form used to hedge claims by indicating that something is true only by appearance or on the surface, without commitment to underlying reality. Common in spoken and written registers as an epistemic hedge.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30. ustez**
- **Form:** ustez
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *uste* (belief, opinion, supposition) + *-z* (instrumental/adverbial suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** supposedly, allegedly, in one's opinion, presumably
- **Usage:** An adverbial form used to attribute a claim to belief or supposition rather than established fact, often implying that the claim may not be accurate. Widely used in everyday speech and journalism as an epistemic distancing device. Contrasts implicitly with *egiazkoa* or *benetan* (truly, really).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
