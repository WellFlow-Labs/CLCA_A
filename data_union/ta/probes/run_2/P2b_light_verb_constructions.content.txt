# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This document records attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving lexical items identified in P1. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Asymmetry across entries is preserved. Register variation is noted where relevant.

The constructions are organized by P1 base form. Within each entry, construction types are distinguished where the distinction is supported by the data (e.g., verb-object collocations, predicative constructions, fixed idiomatic expressions, comparative constructions). No construction type is assumed to exist for every item.

## 1. Base Form: உண்மை / *uṇmai*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை சொல்லணும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmai collāṇum
**English translation:** (One) must tell the truth / You have to tell the truth
**Usage notes:** A very common deontic construction combining *uṇmai col* (to tell the truth) with the obligative suffix *-āṇum*. Appears in moral instruction, parental speech, and legal contexts. Extremely frequent in spoken Tamil.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை என்னன்னு தெரியலை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaṉṉu teriyalai
**English translation:** (I/We) don't know what the truth is
**Usage notes:** A common spoken construction combining *uṇmai* with the interrogative *eṉṉa* (what) and the negative experiential *teriyalai* (don't know / it's not known). Appears in everyday speech when expressing epistemic uncertainty about the facts of a matter.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை வெளியே வரும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷiyē varum
**English translation:** The truth will come out
**Usage notes:** A fixed proverbial construction asserting the inevitable emergence of hidden facts. Extremely common in everyday speech, narrative, and moral discourse. Functions as a reassurance or warning depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்று கண்டுபிடிக்கணும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu kaṇṭupiṭikkāṇum
**English translation:** (We) need to find out what the truth is
**Usage notes:** A common investigative construction combining *uṇmai* with the embedded question *eṉṉaveṉṟu* (what it is) and the verb *kaṇṭupiṭi* (to find out, to discover). Appears in journalistic, legal, and everyday investigative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** இது உண்மையா இல்லையா என்று தெரியவில்லை
**Transliteration:** itu uṇmaiyā illaiyā eṉṟu teriyavillai
**English translation:** It is not known whether this is true or not
**Usage notes:** A common epistemic construction expressing uncertainty about the truth value of a proposition. The structure *X-ā illai-ā eṉṟu teriyavillai* is a productive pattern in Tamil for expressing uncertainty; *uṇmai* is one of the most frequent fillers. Appears across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மையை மறைக்கிறார்கள்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai maṟaikkiṟārkaḷ
**English translation:** They are concealing the truth
**Usage notes:** A common accusatory construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative (*uṇmaiyai*) with the verb *maṟai* (to conceal, to hide). Appears in journalistic, legal, and political discourse when alleging suppression of facts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மையை ஒப்புக்கொள்ளணும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai oppukkoḷḷāṇum
**English translation:** (One) must acknowledge / admit the truth
**Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative with the verb *oppukkoḷ* (to accept, to acknowledge, to admit). Common in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts where admission of facts is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை தான் வெல்லும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmai tāṉ vellum
**English translation:** Truth alone will prevail / It is truth that will win
**Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. The particle *tāṉ* (alone, only, it is precisely) adds contrastive emphasis. Extremely common in moral and political discourse; appears in speeches, proverbs, and popular culture. Closely related to the Gandhian motto *satyamēva jayatē* in its Tamil cultural function.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** அவர் உண்மை பேசுகிறார்
**Transliteration:** avar uṇmai pēcukiṟār
**English translation:** He/She is speaking the truth
**Usage notes:** A simple predicative construction combining *uṇmai* as a direct object with the verb *pēcu* (to speak). Common in everyday speech and in evaluations of testimony. Contrasts with *poy pēcu* (to speak falsely).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்றால்...
**Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟāl...
**English translation:** The truth is that... / What the truth is, is...
**Usage notes:** A fixed discourse-framing construction used to introduce a clarification or correction of the actual state of affairs. Functions as a topic-introducing frame in spoken and written Tamil. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** இதில் உண்மை இல்லை
**Transliteration:** itil uṇmai illai
**English translation:** There is no truth in this / This has no truth to it
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the existential negative *illai* (there is not) to deny the truth of a claim or statement. Common in everyday speech and in formal rebuttals. The locative *itil* (in this) is the standard framing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** கொஞ்சமாவது உண்மை இருக்கா?
**Transliteration:** koñcamāvatu uṇmai irukkā?
**English translation:** Is there even a little truth (in this)?
**Usage notes:** A rhetorical or genuine question construction combining *uṇmai* with the existential *irukku* (there is) and the scalar particle *koñcamāvatu* (at least a little, even a bit). Common in skeptical or accusatory everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மையை நேருக்கு நேர் சொல்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nērukku nēr col
**English translation:** Tell the truth face to face / Say it directly to (someone's) face
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative with the fixed adverbial *nērukku nēr* (face to face, directly) and the verb *col* (to say). Emphasizes directness and courage in truth-telling. Common in interpersonal and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

**Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
**Example:** உண்மை என்று நிரூபிக்கணும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṟu nirūpikkāṇum
**English translation:** (It) must be proved to be true / (One) must prove that it is true
**Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *uṇmai* with the quotative *eṉṟu* and the verb *nirūpikka* (to prove, to demonstrate). Common in legal and argumentative contexts where the burden of proof is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: பொய் / *poy*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய் சொல்லாதே
**Transliteration:** poy collātē
**English translation:** Don't tell lies / Don't lie
**Usage notes:** A very common prohibitive construction combining *poy* with the verb *col* (to say) and the negative imperative suffix *-ātē*. Appears in moral instruction, parental speech, and everyday interpersonal communication. One of the most frequent uses of *poy* in spoken Tamil.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** அவன் பொய் சொல்கிறான்
**Transliteration:** avaṉ poy colkiṟāṉ
**English translation:** He is lying / He is telling a lie
**Usage notes:** A simple predicative construction combining *poy* as a direct object with the verb *col* (to say). The standard way of asserting that someone is lying in everyday Tamil. Appears across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** இது பொய்யா இல்லை உண்மையா?
**Transliteration:** itu poyyā illai uṇmaiyā?
**English translation:** Is this a lie or the truth?
**Usage notes:** A common binary question construction contrasting *poy* and *uṇmai* directly. The structure *X-ā illai Y-ā* (is it X or Y?) is a productive Tamil interrogative pattern; the *poy*/*uṇmai* pairing is one of the most frequent instantiations. Appears in everyday speech and in formal interrogation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய் சொல்வது தவறு
**Transliteration:** poy coluvatu tavaṟu
**English translation:** Telling lies is wrong
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the verbal noun *coluvatu* (the act of saying) to form a subject, then evaluating it with *tavaṟu* (wrong). A common moral assertion in everyday speech and in moral instruction. The construction type (*V-vatu* + evaluative predicate) is highly productive in Tamil.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய்யில் ஆரம்பித்தது
**Transliteration:** poyyil ārampittatu
**English translation:** It started with a lie / It began in falsehood
**Usage notes:** A construction using *poy* in the locative (*poyyil*, in falsehood) with the verb *ārampi* (to begin, to start). Used in narrative and moral contexts to characterize the origin of a situation as rooted in deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய்யை மூடிமறைக்கிறார்கள்
**Transliteration:** poyyai mūṭimaṟaikkiṟārkaḷ
**English translation:** They are covering up the lie
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *poy* in the accusative with the compound verb *mūṭimaṟai* (to cover and conceal, to cover up). Common in journalistic and political discourse when alleging deliberate concealment of falsehood or wrongdoing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** இது வெறும் பொய்
**Transliteration:** itu veṟum poy
**English translation:** This is a mere lie / This is nothing but a lie
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the scalar particle *veṟum* (mere, only, nothing but) to dismiss a claim as entirely false. Common in everyday speech and in political and legal rebuttals. *veṟum* adds a dismissive or contemptuous force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய் சொல்லி ஏமாற்றினான்
**Transliteration:** poy colli ēmāṟṟiṉāṉ
**English translation:** He deceived (someone) by lying / He lied and cheated
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *poy col* (to tell a lie) in the conjunctive participle form (*colli*) with the verb *ēmāṟṟu* (to deceive, to cheat). The conjunctive participle expresses the means or manner of the main action. Common in everyday narrative and in legal and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய்யை உண்மை என்று நம்பினான்
**Transliteration:** poyyai uṇmai eṉṟu nampināṉ
**English translation:** He believed the lie to be true / He trusted the falsehood as truth
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *poy* in the accusative with the quotative *eṉṟu* and the verb *nampu* (to believe, to trust). Used to describe the epistemic state of someone who has been deceived into accepting a falsehood. Appears in narrative and analytical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

**Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
**Example:** பொய் சொல்வதற்கு வெட்கமில்லையா?
**Transliteration:** poy colvatarku veṭkamillayā?
**English translation:** Are you not ashamed to lie? / Have you no shame in lying?
**Usage notes:** A rhetorical question construction combining the dative infinitive *colvatarku* (for the act of saying) with the noun *veṭkam* (shame) and the existential negative *illai*. Common in moral reproach in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** நேர்மையாக இரு
**Transliteration:** nēṟmaiyāka iru
**English translation:** Be honest / Conduct yourself honestly
**Usage notes:** A very common imperative construction combining the adverbial form *nēṟmaiyāka* with the copular verb *iru* (to be). Used in moral instruction, parental speech, and professional contexts. One of the most frequent uses of *nēṟmai* in spoken Tamil.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** நேர்மை இல்லாத இடத்தில் நம்பிக்கை இல்லை
**Transliteration:** nēṟmai illāta iṭattil nampikkai illai
**English translation:** Where there is no honesty, there is no trust
**Usage notes:** A proverbial construction linking *nēṟmai* and *nampikkai* through a conditional-locative frame. Appears in moral discourse and in discussions of institutional integrity. The structure *X illāta iṭattil Y illai* (where there is no X, there is no Y) is a productive Tamil proverbial pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** நேர்மையோடு நட
**Transliteration:** nēṟmaiyōṭu naṭa
**English translation:** Walk / conduct yourself with honesty
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *nēṟmai* with the sociative suffix *-ōṭu* (with) and the verb *naṭa* (to walk, to conduct oneself). Used in moral instruction and in descriptions of principled conduct. The verb *naṭa* in this context is idiomatic for "to conduct oneself, to behave."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** நேர்மை தான் சிறந்த கொள்கை
**Transliteration:** nēṟmai tāṉ ciṟanta koḷkai
**English translation:** Honesty is the best policy
**Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the value of honesty. The particle *tāṉ* adds emphatic force. This construction is the standard Tamil rendering of the English proverb "honesty is the best policy" and is extremely common in moral and educational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** அவரிடம் நேர்மை இல்லை
**Transliteration:** avaṟiṭam nēṟmai illai
**English translation:** There is no honesty in him/her / He/She lacks honesty
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the existential negative *illai* to deny the presence of honesty in a person. The postposition *-iṭam* (at, in, with — used for persons) is the standard locative for personal attributes. Common in everyday moral evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

**Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nēṟmai*
**Example:** நேர்மையான வழியில் சம்பாதி
**Transliteration:** nēṟmaiyāna vaḻiyil campāti
**English translation:** Earn (money/a living) by honest means
**Usage notes:** A construction combining the adjectival *nēṟmaiyāna* with *vaḻi* (way, means) in the locative and the verb *campāti* (to earn). Common in moral and economic discourse, particularly in contexts of anti-corruption and ethical livelihood.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. Base Form: உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*

---

**Note on scope:** *uṇmaiyāna* is the adjectival form of *uṇmai* and is documented as a derivational form in P2a. Multi-word constructions involving *uṇmaiyāna* as a modifier are documented here where they constitute fixed or semi-fixed collocations beyond simple attributive use.

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*
**Example:** உண்மையான நண்பன் கஷ்டத்தில் தான் தெரியும்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ kaṣṭattil tāṉ teriyum
**English translation:** A true friend is known only in times of hardship
**Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting that genuine friendship is revealed under adversity. Extremely common in everyday moral discourse and in popular culture. The structure is a fixed proverb with *uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ* (true friend) as the subject.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*
**Example:** இது உண்மையான பிரச்சனை
**Transliteration:** itu uṇmaiyāna piraccaṉai
**English translation:** This is a real problem / This is a genuine issue
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using *uṇmaiyāna* to assert the genuine or serious nature of a problem or issue. Common in journalistic, political, and everyday discourse to distinguish real concerns from trivial or fabricated ones.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: சரி / *cari*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
**Example:** சரியா சொன்னே
**Transliteration:** cariyā coṉṉē
**English translation:** You said it right / That's correctly stated
**Usage notes:** A common confirmatory construction combining the adverbial *cariyā* (correctly) with the verb *col* (to say) in the past tense. Used in everyday conversation to confirm the accuracy of a statement. The final *-ē* adds mild emphatic force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
**Example:** சரி பண்ண முடியுமா?
**Transliteration:** cari paṇṇa muṭiyumā?
**English translation:** Can (it) be fixed / corrected?
**Usage notes:** A common question construction combining *cari* with the verb *paṇṇu* (to do, to make; colloquial equivalent of *cey*) in the infinitive and the modal *muṭiyum* (can, is possible). Extremely common in everyday speech when asking about the possibility of correction or repair.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
**Example:** இது சரியா இல்லையா?
**Transliteration:** itu cariyā illaiyā?
**English translation:** Is this correct or not?
**Usage notes:** A binary question construction using the *X-ā illai-ā* pattern to ask about the correctness of something. Extremely common in everyday speech, in educational contexts, and in verification situations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
**Example:** சரி என்று தலையாட்டினான்
**Transliteration:** cari eṉṟu talaiyāṭṭiṉāṉ
**English translation:** He nodded saying "okay" / He nodded in agreement
**Usage notes:** A construction combining *cari* as a quoted agreement particle with the quotative *eṉṟu* and the verb *talaiyāṭṭu* (to nod the head). Common in narrative contexts to describe agreement or confirmation. Reflects the use of *cari* as a discourse particle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
**Example:** சரிபார்த்து சொல்
**Transliteration:** caripārtu col
**English translation:** Check and then say / Verify before speaking
**Usage notes:** A construction combining the conjunctive participle of *caripār* (to verify) with the imperative *col* (say). Used as an instruction to verify information before asserting it. Common in everyday speech and in professional and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 6. Base Form: தவறு / *tavaṟu*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
**Example:** தவறு செய்தால் தண்டிக்கணும்
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu ceytāl taṇṭikkāṇum
**English translation:** If (one) does wrong, (one) must be punished
**Usage notes:** A common conditional-deontic construction combining *tavaṟu cey* (to do wrong) in the conditional with the obligative *taṇṭikka* (to punish). Appears in legal, moral, and political discourse about accountability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
**Example:** தவறை ஒப்புக்கொள்
**Transliteration:** tavaṟai oppukkoḷ
**English translation:** Admit the mistake / Acknowledge the wrong
**Usage notes:** A common imperative construction combining *tavaṟu* in the accusative with the verb *oppukkoḷ* (to accept, to acknowledge). Appears in interpersonal, moral, and legal contexts where admission of error is at issue. Parallel in structure to *uṇmaiyai oppukkoḷ* (Construction 7 under *uṇmai*).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
**Example:** இது என் தவறு இல்லை
**Transliteration:** itu eṉ tavaṟu illai
**English translation:** This is not my mistake / This is not my fault
**Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the existential negative *illai* to deny responsibility for an error. Extremely common in everyday speech and in legal and interpersonal contexts of blame attribution.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
**Example:** தவறு தவறு தான்
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu tavaṟu tāṉ
**English translation:** Wrong is wrong / A mistake is a mistake (regardless of who commits it)
**Usage notes:** A fixed emphatic construction using reduplication of *tavaṟu* with the emphatic particle *tāṉ* to assert that wrongness is an objective property independent of the identity of the wrongdoer. Common in moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
**Example:** தவறை திருத்திக்கோ
**Transliteration:** tavaṟai tiruttikkō
**English translation:** Correct your mistake / Fix the error (for yourself)
