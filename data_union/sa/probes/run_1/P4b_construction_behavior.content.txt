# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST

| **1. ATTRIBUTION** — Nominal sentence, zero-copula, predicate-initial | सत्यं परं धर्मः | *satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ* | "Truth is the highest dharma." | [CORE] | The unmarked form for truth-domain attribution in philosophical and gnomic contexts; predicate-initial order is standard in maxim-style assertions. Restricted to nominal predicates; not used for verbal predication. | Copula-present variant: *mithyā bhavati* (more explicitly propositional, less gnomic); copula follows predicate when present. |
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** — Nominal sentence, zero-copula, predicate-initial | असत्यं पापम् | *asatyaṃ pāpam* | "Untruth is sin." | [CORE] | Same gnomic-assertion context as above; attested in Dharmaśāstra literature. No restriction on negative-domain subjects. | Same as above. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** — *na* + predicate nominal, zero-copula | भ्रमो न प्रमा | *bhramo na pramā* | "Error is not valid cognition." | [CORE] | Standard form for negating a truth-domain predication in philosophical argument, particularly Nyāya epistemological literature. *Na* is placed immediately before the predicate nominal. | Existential negation variant: *nāsti* (*na* + *asti*) used when denying the existence of an epistemic ground rather than denying a property of a subject (see next row). |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** — Existential negation *nāsti* | प्रमाणं नास्ति | *pramāṇaṃ nāsti* | "There is no valid means of knowledge." | [CORE] | Used specifically as a dismissal formula in philosophical debate when the claim is that no relevant epistemic ground exists at all. Distinct in function from property-negation with *na* + zero-copula. | *Na* + zero-copula (property negation, as in row above). |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** — Adjective preceding noun, free modification | यथार्थं ज्ञानम् | *yathārthaṃ jñānam* | "Accurate cognition." | [CORE] | Used when the truth-domain property is predicated contextually or contrastively; attested in Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā epistemological literature. Adjective agrees with noun in gender, number, and case. | Genitive construction (*dharmasya tattvaṃ*); compounding (*satyavacana*, *mithyājñāna*) — the latter treated as lexicalized and handled in P2a. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** — Genitive phrase (*X-sya Y*) | धर्मस्य तत्त्वं निहितं गुहायाम् | *dharmasya tattvaṃ nihitaṃ guhāyām* | "The true nature of dharma is hidden in a cave." | [CORE] | Preferred when the modified noun is the topic and the truth-domain term characterizes a sub-aspect of it; attested in Mahābhārata (Vanaparvan). | Free adjectival modification (see row above); compounding (P2a). |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** — *jāyate* (arising/production) | प्रमा जायते | *pramā jāyate* | "Valid cognition arises." | [CORE] | Marks *pramā* specifically as the output of an epistemic process rather than a standing state; attested in Nyāya epistemological literature. *Jāyate* more specifically marks production or arising than *bhavati*. | *Bhavati* (covers both stative and inchoative readings, less specific); *nivartate* (cessation/inverse transition, see next row). |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** — *nivartate* (cessation, inverse transition) | मिथ्याज्ञानं निवर्तते | *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate* | "False knowledge ceases." | [CORE] | Marks the soteriological removal of *mithyājñāna* in Vedāntic literature; expresses the inverse inchoative transition (cessation rather than arising). Restricted to contexts describing removal of a negative epistemic state. | *Jāyate* (arising, positive transition); *bhavati* (general, non-specific). |
| **5. Restricted to the *satyā-* + causative-of-*āp-* pattern; no parallel morphological causatives on bare *satya-*, *pramā-*, or *dharma-* roots are attested. | Periphrastic causative with *karaṇa* (see next row). |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** — Periphrastic causative (noun + *karaṇa*) | प्रमाणीकरण | *pramāṇīkaraṇa* | "Validation, authentication, certification." | [MARGINAL] | More common in modern Sanskrit-influenced administrative usage than in classical philosophical Sanskrit. The dominant pattern for causative meaning in this domain where morphological causative is unavailable. | Morphological causative (*satyāpana*, see row above). |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** — *-tā* suffix (feminine abstract) | सत्यता | *satyatā* | "Truthfulness, the property of being true." | [CORE] | Fully productive across truth-domain bases in philosophical and ethical discourse. Functions as subject or object of predication, naming the property rather than an instance. Choice between *-tā* and *-tva* is partly stylistic and partly school-conventional; no consistent semantic distinction is supported by the data. | *-tva* suffix (parallel, neuter abstract, see next row); *-ya* after vṛddhi (restricted to *pramāṇa*-family: *prāmāṇya*). |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** — *-tva* suffix (neuter abstract) | मिथ्यात्व | *mithyātva* | "The property of being *mithyā* / illusoriness." | [CORE] | More frequent than *-tā* in technical philosophical argument; central to Advaita Vedānta ontological debate. Fully productive on truth-domain bases. No consistent semantic distinction from *-tā* is supported by the data. | *-tā* suffix (see row above); *-ya* after vṛddhi (*prāmāṇya*, restricted to *pramāṇa*-family). |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** — *-vādin* compound | सत्यवादिन् | *satyavādin* | "One who speaks truth, a truth-teller." | [CORE] | The dominant pattern for truth-domain agents in ethical and epistemological literature; used as an epithet and as a category in discussions of reliable testimony. Both positive (*satyavādin*) and negative (*asatyavādin*, *mithyāvādin*) forms are well-attested. | *Pramātṛ* (agent suffix *-tṛ* on root *mā-*) fills the epistemological knower-of-truth role; *-vādin* does not extend to that function. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** — *-vādin* compound, negative | असत्यवादिन् | *asatyavādin* | "One who speaks falsely, a liar." | [CORE] | Used in ethical and legal literature to characterize an unreliable speaker or witness. Structurally parallel to positive form; both are well-attested. | Same as above. |
| **8. PRESCRIPTIVE / INJUNCTIVE** — Accusative + optative of *brū-* / *vad-* | सत्यं ब्रूयात् | *satyaṃ brūyāt* | "One should speak the truth." | [CORE] | The canonical syntactic form for ethical prescriptions about speech in Dharmaśāstra; attested in Manusmṛti 4.138 and widely across the literature. Restricted to speech-evaluating and conduct-evaluating contexts; does not extend to epistemological or ontological groups. | Imperative form (*satyaṃ vada*, Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.1) used in foundational injunctive contexts; negative prohibitive with *na* + optative (see next row). |
| **8. PRESCRIPTIVE / INJUNCTIVE** — Negative prohibitive: *na* + optative | असत्यं न वदेत् | *asatyaṃ na vadet* | "One should not speak untruth." | [CORE] | Standard prohibitive formula in Dharmaśāstra; structurally parallel to the positive injunction. Restricted to the same speech-evaluating and conduct-evaluating contexts as the positive form. | Positive optative injunction (*satyaṃ brūyāt*, see row above); imperative (*satyaṃ vada*). |
| **9. ABLATIVE DEVIATION** — Ablative + *na calati* / *na pracalati* (negated deviation) | सत्यात् न प्रचलति | *satyāt na pracalati* | "Does not deviate from truth." | [CORE] | Standard formula for expressing steadfast adherence to a normative standard by negating departure from it; attested in Epic literature. Productive across conduct-evaluating and fittingness-evaluating domains. Does not appear in epistemological groups. | Positive deviation: ablative + *cyuta* (past passive participle), expressing the fact of deviation (see next row). |

ABLATIVE DEVIATION** — Ablative + *cyuta* (positive deviation) | औचित्यात् च्युतम् | *aucityāt cyutam* | "Fallen from propriety / deviated from what is fitting." | [CORE] | Standard formula for expressing deviation from a normative standard; attested in Sanskrit aesthetic literature. Inverse of the negated-deviation construction; both patterns are productive across conduct-evaluating and fittingness-evaluating domains. | Negated deviation: ablative + *na pracalati* (see row above). |

### Summary Notes

1. The majority of attested constructions in this domain are [CORE]: they are common, idiomatic, and well-attested across multiple text types and registers.
2. The two [MARGINAL] ratings apply exclusively to the causative constructions (§5), which are restricted by register (legal/administrative rather than philosophical) and, in the case of the periphrastic *karaṇa* pattern, by period (more prominent in modern Sanskrit-influenced usage).
3. No construction in this domain is rated [UNCERTAIN]; all constructions in P4a are supported by identified attestations.
4. The most consistent cross-construction restriction is the boundary between **epistemological/ontological** contexts and **ethical/conduct-evaluating** contexts: the prescriptive/injunctive (§8) and ablative deviation (§9) constructions are explicitly restricted to the latter and do not extend to the former.
