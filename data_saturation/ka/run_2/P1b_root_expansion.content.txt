# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / marṭ-
**Transliteration:** mart-
**P1 participation:** 4 items — martali (#4), simartle (#3), samart̕liani (#19), samart̕lianoba (#20)
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, correctness, and justice as aligned with a standard — covering factual accuracy, moral rectitude, legal legitimacy, and vindication. This root is the primary structural axis of the truth-as-correctness cluster in Georgian.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ჭეშმარ- / č̣ešmar-
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmar-
**P1 participation:** 2 items — č̣ešmariti (#1), č̣ešmariteba (#2)
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as genuine quality or authentic correspondence to reality — the philosophically and theologically marked root for truth in its deepest or most absolute sense. Less productive morphologically than mart-, but semantically central to the domain.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ყალბ- / q̇alb-
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 participation:** 2 items — q̇albi (#6), siq̇albe (#5)
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, counterfeit quality, and inauthenticity — the primary root for the negative pole of the truth domain, covering forgery, fraud, and spurious identity. Highly productive in legal and everyday registers.

---

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: mart- (მართ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართება
**Transliteration:** marteba
**Literal breakdown:** mart- (right, correct) + -eba (verbal/nominal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** rightness, correctness; also: obligation, duty (in the expression marteba aqvs, "it is right/obligatory for him")
**Context of use:** Used in older and formal registers to express that something is correct or fitting. The construction X-s marteba aqvs functions as a modal expression of obligation or appropriateness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** მართებული
**Transliteration:** martebuli
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + -ebuli (participial/adjectival suffix, "that which has been made right")
**Natural English translation:** correct, proper, appropriate, well-founded
**Context of use:** Applied to statements, decisions, and arguments to indicate that they are well-founded or appropriate. Widely used in formal writing, legal reasoning, and academic discourse to evaluate the correctness of a position.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართებულობა
**Transliteration:** martebulobā
**Literal breakdown:** martebuli + -oba (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** correctness, appropriateness, well-foundedness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun derived from martebuli, used in formal and academic contexts to refer to the property of being well-founded or appropriate. Appears in legal and philosophical argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** martla
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + -la (adverbializing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, indeed, in truth
**Context of use:** A high-frequency discourse particle and adverb used to assert or confirm the truth of a statement, or to express genuine surprise. One of the most common words in everyday Georgian speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** martlac
**Literal breakdown:** martla + -c (emphatic/additive particle, "also, even")
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, really (emphatic)
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of martla, used to confirm or reinforce a claim, often in response to a preceding statement or expectation. Extremely frequent in spoken and written Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებელი
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebeli
**Literal breakdown:** martl- (truth, right) + ma- (agentive prefix) + did- (great, glorify) + -ebeli (agentive suffix); "one who glorifies the right/truth rightly"
**Natural English translation:** Orthodox (Christian); literally "right-glorifier"
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for Eastern Orthodox Christianity and its adherents. Used in religious, historical, and cultural contexts. The dominant religious identity term in Georgia.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებლობა
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebloba
**Literal breakdown:** martlmadidebeli + -oba (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** Orthodoxy (Eastern Christian)
**Context of use:** The abstract noun for the Eastern Orthodox faith as a system or institution. Used in theological, historical, and ecclesiastical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულება
**Transliteration:** martlmsajuleba
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + msajul- (judge, from msajuli, "judge") + -eba (nominal suffix); "right-judging"
**Natural English translation:** justice, administration of justice, judiciary
**Context of use:** A formal and legal term for the administration of justice or the judicial process. Used in constitutional, legal, and institutional contexts. Appears in the names of courts and justice-related bodies.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** მართლწერა
**Transliteration:** martlwera
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + wera (writing, from wer-, "to write"); "right-writing"
**Natural English translation:** orthography, correct spelling
**Context of use:** The standard term for orthography and correct spelling in Georgian. Used in educational, editorial, and linguistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მართლწერის წესები
**Transliteration:** martlweris wesesbi
**Literal breakdown:** martlwera (genitive) + weseb- (rules) + -i (nominative plural)
**Natural English translation:** rules of orthography, spelling rules
**Context of use:** Fixed phrase used in educational and editorial contexts to refer to the normative rules governing correct spelling in Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** მართლმეტყველება
**Transliteration:** martlmet̕q̇veleba
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + met̕q̇vel- (speaker, from t̕q̇ven-, "to speak") + -eba; "right-speaking"
**Natural English translation:** correct speech, orthoepy; also: truthful speaking
**Context of use:** Used in linguistic and rhetorical contexts to refer to correct or proper speech, including pronunciation norms. Also appears in older texts with the sense of truthful or righteous speaking.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** მართლი
**Transliteration:** martli
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + -li (adjectival/nominal suffix variant)
**Natural English translation:** truth, the right thing; also used as an adjective meaning "right, correct" in some dialects and older texts
**Context of use:** Appears in older literary Georgian and some dialectal usage as a variant of martali or as a standalone noun for truth/rightness. Less common in modern standard Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**13.**
**Form:** სამართალი
**Transliteration:** samart̕ali
**Literal breakdown:** sa- (circumfix) + mart̕al- (right, just) + -i; "that which pertains to rightness"
**Natural English translation:** law, right, justice (as a system or body of norms)
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for law as a normative system, used in legal, academic, and institutional contexts. Distinct from kanoni (a specific law or rule) in referring to law as a whole domain or system.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** სამართლებრივი
**Transliteration:** samart̕lebrivi
**Literal breakdown:** samart̕al- + -ebriv- (adjectival suffix, "of the nature of") + -i
**Natural English translation:** legal, juridical, of a legal nature
**Context of use:** The standard adjective for describing things that pertain to law or have a legal character. Widely used in legal documents, academic writing, and official discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** სამართლებრივი სახელმწიფო
**Transliteration:** samart̕lebrivi saxelmwip̕o
**Literal breakdown:** samart̕lebrivi (legal) + saxelmwip̕o (state)
**Natural English translation:** rule-of-law state, Rechtsstaat
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase in constitutional and political discourse referring to a state governed by law rather than arbitrary power. Central to Georgian constitutional vocabulary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** უმართლობა
**Transliteration:** umartloba
**Literal breakdown:** u- (privative) + martl- + -oba (nominalizing suffix); "absence of rightness"
**Natural English translation:** injustice, wrongness, unfairness
**Context of use:** Used in moral and everyday discourse to name the condition of being unjust or wrong. Less formal than samart̕lianobis arqona but more expressive in moral condemnation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** მართლსაიდუმლო
**Transliteration:** martlsaidumlo
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + saidumlo (mystery, secret sacrament); "true mystery/sacrament"
**Natural English translation:** true sacrament, genuine mystery (theological)
**Context of use:** Used in theological contexts, particularly in Orthodox Christian discourse, to refer to genuine sacraments as opposed to false or invalid ones. Appears in ecclesiastical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**18.**
**Form:** მართლზომიერი
**Transliteration:** martlzomieri
**Literal breakdown:** martl- + zom- (measure) + -ieri (adjectival suffix); "rightly measured"
**Natural English translation:** proportionate, measured, appropriate in degree
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a measure or action is proportionate to its purpose. Appears in constitutional law discussions of proportionality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** მართლზომიერება
**Transliteration:** martlzomiereba
**Literal breakdown:** martlzomieri + -eba (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** proportionality, proportionateness
**Context of use:** The abstract noun for proportionality as a legal and administrative principle. Used in constitutional law, human rights discourse, and administrative law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** სიმართლის თქმა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis t̕q̇ma
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (genitive) + t̕q̇ma (saying, verbal noun of t̕q̇ven-)
**Natural English translation:** telling the truth, truth-telling
**Context of use:** A highly frequent fixed phrase used in everyday, legal, and moral contexts to describe the act of stating what is true. Appears in oath formulas and moral exhortation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** სიმართლე გამოვიდა
**Transliteration:** simartle gamovida
**Literal breakdown:** simartle (nominative) + gamovida (came out, emerged; perfective of gamosv-)
**Natural English translation:** the truth came out, the truth emerged
**Context of use:** A fixed idiomatic expression used in narrative and everyday speech to describe the revelation of a previously concealed truth. Extremely common in storytelling and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** მართალია, მაგრამ
**Transliteration:** martalia, magram
**Literal breakdown:** martali + -a (copula) + magram (but, however)
**Natural English translation:** it is true, but…; granted, but…
**Context of use:** A highly frequent concessive construction in spoken and written Georgian, used to acknowledge a point before introducing a qualification or counterargument.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** მართალი ადამიანი
**Transliteration:** martali adamiani
**Literal breakdown:** martali (right, just) + adamiani (person, human being)
**Natural English translation:** a just person, a righteous person, an honest person
**Context of use:** Used in moral evaluation of character, in religious contexts (the righteous person), and in everyday speech to describe someone who is honest and fair.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** მართლი გზა
**Transliteration:** martli gza
**Literal breakdown:** martli (right, correct) + gza (road, path, way)
**Natural English translation:** the right path, the straight and narrow
**Context of use:** Used in moral, religious, and metaphorical contexts to refer to the correct course of conduct or life. Appears in religious texts and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** სამართლიანი სასამართლო
**Transliteration:** samart̕liani sasamart̕lo
**Literal breakdown:** samart̕liani (just, fair) + sasamart̕lo (court, from sa- + samart̕al- + -o)
**Natural English translation:** fair trial, fair court
**Context of use:** Used in legal and human rights discourse to describe a judicial proceeding that meets standards of fairness and due process. A key phrase in Georgian constitutional and human rights law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** სასამართლო
**Transliteration:** sasamart̕lo
**Literal breakdown:** sa- + samart̕al- + -o (locative/institutional suffix); "place/institution of justice"
**Natural English translation:** court, tribunal
**Context of use:** The standard term for a court of law in Georgian. Used across all legal registers and in everyday speech when referring to judicial institutions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** მართლდება
**Transliteration:** martldeba
**Literal breakdown:** mart- + -l- + -deba (passive/inchoative verbal suffix); "becomes right/justified"
**Natural English translation:** is justified, is vindicated, is proven right
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to describe the process of being vindicated or proven correct. Also used in theological contexts for justification (as in Lutheran/Protestant theological discourse in Georgian).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** გამართლება
**Transliteration:** gamartleba
**Literal breakdown:** ga- (preverb, completive) + mart- + -l- + -eba; "making right, completing the act of justifying"
**Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, acquittal; also: excuse, rationalization
**Context of use:** Used in legal contexts for acquittal or vindication, in moral contexts for justification of an action, and in everyday speech for making excuses. One of the most productive derivatives of the mart- root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** გამართლება ვერ მოხდა
**Transliteration:** gamartleba ver moxda
**Literal breakdown:** gamartleba (justification) + ver (negation of ability) + moxda (happened, occurred)
**Natural English translation:** justification did not occur; could not be justified; acquittal was not achieved
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral discourse to state that an action or person could not be vindicated or that an expected justification failed to materialize.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** მართლაც რომ
**Transliteration:** martlac rom
**Literal breakdown:** martlac (indeed) + rom (that, if; subordinating conjunction)
**Natural English translation:** if it is indeed the case that…; truly, if…
**Context of use:** A concessive or conditional construction in spoken and written Georgian, used to introduce a clause whose truth is being granted for the sake of argument.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 2: č̣ešmar- (ჭეშმარიტ-)

*Note: This root is less morphologically productive than mart- but is semantically central. The following lists all well-attested derivatives and collocations.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti ɣmerti
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true, genuine) + ɣmerti (God)
**Natural English translation:** the true God
**Context of use:** A fixed theological phrase used in Christian liturgical and doctrinal texts to distinguish the God of Christian faith from false gods or idols. Appears in the Nicene Creed in Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti siq̇varuli
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true) + siq̇varuli (love)
**Natural English translation:** true love, genuine love
**Context of use:** Used in literary, religious, and everyday contexts to distinguish authentic love from its counterfeits or superficial imitations. A common collocation in Georgian poetry and prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი მეგობარი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti megobari
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true, genuine) + megobari (friend)
**Natural English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
**Context of use:** Used in moral and everyday discourse to distinguish a genuinely loyal friend from a superficial or false one. A frequent collocation in proverbs and moral literature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება ითქვა
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba it̕q̇va
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariteba (truth) + it̕q̇va (was said; passive aorist of t̕q̇ven-)
**Natural English translation:** the truth was spoken, truth was told
**Context of use:** A fixed construction used in formal, literary, and rhetorical contexts to assert that what has been stated is true. Appears in legal testimony contexts and in philosophical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტების ძიება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritebis ʒieba
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariteba (genitive) + ʒieba (seeking, searching; verbal noun of ʒieb-)
**Natural English translation:** the search for truth, the pursuit of truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, scientific, and religious discourse to describe the intellectual or spiritual project of seeking truth. A common phrase in academic and theological writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტების გამოვლენა
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritebis gamovlena
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariteba (genitive) + gamovlena (revelation, manifestation; from ga- + mo- + vlen-)
**Natural English translation:** the revelation of truth, the disclosure of truth
**Context of use:** Used in theological, philosophical, and journalistic contexts to describe the act of bringing truth to light. Appears in discussions of divine revelation and investigative disclosure.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი ცოდნა
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti codna
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true) + codna (knowledge; from cod-, "to know")
**Natural English translation:** true knowledge, genuine knowledge
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and theological contexts to distinguish genuine knowledge from mere opinion or false belief. Appears in epistemological and Gnostic-influenced theological discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვნება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti xelovneba
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true, genuine) + xelovneba (art, craft)
**Natural English translation:** true art, genuine art
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic and critical discourse to distinguish authentic artistic expression from imitation, kitsch, or commercial production. Appears in literary criticism and art theory.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი ბუნება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti buneba
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true, genuine) + buneba (nature)
**Natural English translation:** true nature, genuine nature, real nature
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and theological contexts to refer to the essential or authentic nature of a thing or person, as opposed to its appearance or accidental properties.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება გამარჯვებს
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariteba (truth) + gamarjvebs (wins, prevails; present of gamarjveba)
**Natural English translation:** truth prevails, truth wins out
**Context of use:** A proverbial and rhetorical expression asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. Used in moral exhortation, political rhetoric, and literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ნახევარი ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** naxevari č̣ešmariteba
**Literal breakdown:** naxevari (half) + č̣ešmariteba (truth)
**Natural English translation:** a half-truth
**Context of use:** Used in critical and moral discourse to describe a statement that is technically accurate but misleading by omission or emphasis. Appears in journalistic and philosophical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** აბსოლუტური ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** absolut̕uri č̣ešmariteba
**Literal breakdown:** absolut̕uri (absolute; loanword) + č̣ešmariteba (truth)
**Natural English translation:** absolute truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and theological discourse to refer to truth that is unconditional and not relative to perspective or context. A central concept in Georgian philosophical and religious writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** შეფარდებითი ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** šep̕ardebiti č̣ešmariteba
**Literal breakdown:** šep̕ardebiti (relative, relational) + č̣ešmariteba (truth)
**Natural English translation:** relative truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical discourse, particularly in discussions of relativism and epistemology, to contrast with absolute truth.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti mowme
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariti (true, truthful) + mowme (witness)
**Natural English translation:** a truthful witness, a true witness
**Context of use:** Used in legal and religious contexts to describe a witness who testifies truthfully. In theological contexts, also used for martyrs (the Greek martys, "witness," underlies the Georgian usage).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება და სამართლიანობა
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba da samart̕lianoba
**Literal breakdown:** č̣ešmariteba (truth) + da (and) + samart̕lianoba (justice)
**Natural English translation:** truth and justice
**Context of use:** A fixed dyadic phrase used in legal, political, and moral discourse to invoke the twin values of truth and justice. Appears in institutional names, political rhetoric, and ethical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The č̣ešmar- root has fewer than 20 clearly distinct derived forms (as opposed to collocations). The items above represent the full range of well-attested expressions. The root is semantically rich but morphologically less productive than mart-, generating primarily the adjective č̣ešmariti and the abstract noun č̣ešmariteba, with most further expressions being collocations rather than morphological derivatives.*

### ROOT 3: q̇alb- (ყალბ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** ყალბი დოკუმენტი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi dokumenti
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (false, forged) + dokumenti (document; loanword)
**Natural English translation:** forged document, falsified document
**Context of use:** The standard legal and administrative phrase for a forged or falsified document. Appears in criminal law, administrative proceedings, and everyday speech when discussing document fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ყალბი ფული
**Transliteration:** q̇albi p̕uli
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (counterfeit) + p̕uli (money)
**Natural English translation:** counterfeit money, fake currency
**Context of use:** The standard phrase for counterfeit currency. Used in legal, financial, and everyday contexts. Counterfeiting (q̇albi p̕ulis damzadeba) is a specific criminal offense in Georgian law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ყალბი ხელმოწერა
**Transliteration:** q̇albi xelmowera
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (forged) + xelmowera (signature; xel-, "hand" + mowera, "writing")
**Natural English translation:** forged signature
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe a falsified signature on a document. A specific element of forgery offenses in Georgian criminal law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ყალბი ბეჭედი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi bečedi
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (forged) + bečedi (seal, stamp)
**Natural English translation:** forged seal, counterfeit stamp
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe a falsified official seal or stamp. Appears in criminal law relating to document fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ყალბი ინფორმაცია
**Transliteration:** q̇albi inp̕ormacia
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (false) + inp̕ormacia (information; loanword)
**Natural English translation:** false information, disinformation
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, political, and legal contexts to describe deliberately false or misleading information. Increasingly common in discussions of media and information integrity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი სიახლე
**Transliteration:** q̇albi siaxle
**Literal breakdown:** q̇albi (false) + siaxle (news, novelty; si- + axal-, "new" + -e)
**Natural English translation:** fake news, false news
**Context of use:** Used in contemporary media and political discourse to describe deliberately fabricated or misleading news content. A recent but now well-established collocation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ყალბობა
**Transliteration:** q̇alboba
**Literal breakdown:** q̇alb- + -oba (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** falseness, the quality of being fake or counterfeit; also: the act of falsifying
**Context of use:** Used as an abstract noun for the property or practice of being false or counterfeit. Less common than siq̇albe but appears in moral and evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** გაყალბება
**Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where the core truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other semantic territory. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**mart- root:**

- **მართლმადიდებელი** (martlmadidebeli): The mart- root here participates in the name of a religious confession (Eastern Orthodoxy), where the "right/true" component is fused with "glorifying" in a way that makes the truth-domain contribution inseparable from liturgical and doxological function.

- **მართლწერა** (martlwera): The root contributes to the domain of orthographic correctness — the "rightness" of written forms — which is a normative linguistic standard rather than a truth-evaluative judgment about states of affairs.

- **მართლზომიერება** (martlzomiereba): The root combines with a measure-concept to produce a proportionality principle in administrative and constitutional law, where "rightness" functions as a standard of degree rather than a binary truth value.

- **გამართლება** (gamartleba): In everyday usage, this form is frequently used to mean "making excuses" or "rationalizing," where the root's contribution shifts from genuine vindication toward motivated reasoning or self-justification.

- **მართება** (marteba) in the construction X-s marteba aqvs: The root here functions as a modal marker of obligation or fittingness rather than a descriptor of factual correctness.

- **სამართალი** (samart̕ali): The root generates the term for law as a normative system, where "rightness" becomes institutionalized as a body of rules rather than a property of individual statements or persons.

---

**č̣ešmar- root:**

- **ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვნება** (č̣ešmariti xelovneba): The root is applied to aesthetic evaluation of art, where "genuine" or "true" art is distinguished from imitation or commercial production — a domain of aesthetic judgment rather than propositional truth.

- **ჭეშმარიტი ბუნება** (č̣ešmariti buneba): The root is applied to the concept of essential nature, where "true" functions as a marker of essence or fundamental character rather than correspondence to fact.

- **ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე** (č̣ešmariti mowme): In theological usage, this phrase connects to the concept of martyrdom (Greek martys, "witness"), where the root's truth-domain contribution intersects with the domain of sacrificial testimony and religious identity.

---

**q̇alb- root:**

- **ყალბი ნოტა** (q̇albi not̕a): The root is applied to musical performance, where "false" describes a pitch error rather than a propositional falsehood — a domain of technical correctness in aesthetic execution.

- **ყალბი ნაბიჯი** (q̇albi nabiǰi): The root is applied metaphorically to conduct and decision-making, where "false step" describes an error in judgment or strategy rather than a false statement.

- **ყალბი სტარტი** (q̇albi st̕art̕i): The root is applied to procedural invalidity in competitive contexts (sports), where "false" marks a rule violation rather than a truth-evaluative judgment.

- **ყალბი ღიმილი** (q̇albi ɣimili): The root is applied to emotional expression, where "false" marks inauthenticity of affect rather than propositional falsehood — a domain of sincerity and emotional genuineness.

- **ყალბი პათოსი** (q̇albi p̕at̕osi): The root is applied to rhetorical and aesthetic evaluation, where "false" marks manufactured or hollow emotional appeal in literary or oratorical performance.
