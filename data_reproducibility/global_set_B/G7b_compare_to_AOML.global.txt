# G7b — Empirical vs. AOML Constraint Comparison

---

## Preliminary Notes on Alignment Methodology

The empirical matrix (G7a) was derived from F1–F6 data across 8 languages using 17 semantic dimensions (D1–D17) and 17 constructional functions (C1–C17). The AOML matrix uses 7 primary axes (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, VERIF, LIFE), 2 meta-axes (DEG, TEMP), 7 core operators (ATTR, CAUSE, BECOME, RESULT, ABSTR, STRAT, CONTR), 4 levels, and 6 structural rules (R1–R6).

Alignment is attempted where structural correspondence is plausible. Where empirical categories span multiple AOML structures, or where AOML structures have no empirical counterpart, this is noted explicitly. No empirical category is collapsed into an AOML category without recording the loss.

## Summary

### Confirmed Patterns

**1. Universal availability of ATTR, CONTR, and ABSTR across all primary axes.**
The empirical finding that C1 (Attribution), C2 (Negative Attribution), C3 (Adnominal Modification), and C9 (Abstract Nominalization) are ALLOWED for all 17 dimensions aligns with AOML's prediction that ATTR, CONTR, and ABSTR are universally licensed across all primary axes. This is the most robustly confirmed finding in both systems.

**2. Manner adverbials (C12) broadly available.**
Empirical C12 is ALLOWED for 14 of 17 dimensions. AOML licenses adverbial forms of ATTR across all axes. The three exceptions in the empirical data (D14, D16, D17) are rated VARIABLE or INSUFFICIENT DATA rather than BLOCKED, consistent with AOML's prediction of broad availability.

**3. Propositional complement constructions (C13) broadly available.**
Empirical C13 is ALLOWED for 12 of 17 dimensions. AOML licenses clausal complements of ATTR across all axes. The exceptions reflect dimensions where propositional complementation is less documented rather than clearly blocked.

**4. Verification causatives (C6) broadly available for factual targets.**
Empirical C6 is ALLOWED for D1, D3, D9, D12, D15. AOML licenses CAUSE(+) for factual targets (VER, NORM institutionally). The alignment is strong for these dimensions.

**5. Negative agentive nominalization (C10) broadly available.**
Empirical C10 is ALLOWED for D1, D3, D4, D5, D6. AOML licenses ABSTR for negative agentives. The alignment is consistent.

**6. Intensification (C14) universally available.**
Empirical C14 is ALLOWED for all dimensions where tested. AOML licenses DEG[A] as inheriting from the host axis. The alignment is direct.

**7. Discourse markers (C17) broadly available.**
Empirical C17 is ALLOWED for 7 of 8 languages across D1, D4, D8. AOML licenses grammaticalized ATTR constructions. The alignment is consistent.

**8. Dispositional causation block (R1) directionally confirmed.**
Empirical C7 × D4 is BLOCKED. AOML Rule R1 predicts CAUSE × SINC is universally blocked. The empirical data confirms the negative causation direction. The positive causation direction (C6 × D4) is rated INSUFFICIENT DATA, which does not contradict R1.

**9. Positive emergence bias (R4) directionally confirmed.**
Empirical C5 (Inchoative Negative) is consistently more restricted than C4 (Inchoative Positive) across the corpus. AOML Rule R4 predicts this asymmetry. The alignment is directional: the bias is confirmed but not as a universal block.

**10. Deception dimension (D5) is the most constructionally restricted.**
Empirical data shows D5 blocks C4, C6, and partially C7. AOML predicts that SINC (the axis D5 maps to) blocks CAUSE in both directions (R1) and that BECOME(−) is biased/blocked (R4). The alignment is strong for C4 and C6 blocking.

**11. Institutional validity (D9) is the most constructionally symmetric.**
Empirical data shows D9 allows both C6 and C7 (validation and invalidation). AOML licenses CAUSE(+) and CAUSE(−) for NORM (institutional subregion). The alignment is consistent.

**12. Revelation verb substitution aligns with provisional REVEAL operator.**
The empirical pattern of revelation verbs substituting for inchoative constructions in Hebrew, Greek, and Chinese is consistent with AOML's provisional REVEAL operator. The LIFE blocking of REVEAL (constructed things cannot be revealed) is consistent with the empirical data showing that lifelikeness/verisimilitude terms do not participate in revelation constructions.

### Partial or Conditional Matches

**1. C7 (Causative Falsification) × D1 (Correspondence to Fact/Reality): stronger empirical blocking than AOML predicts.**
AOML predicts CAUSE(−) × VER is restricted (not blocked). Empirical data shows BLOCKED in 5 of 8 languages with an explicit structural gap in Quechua. AOML's R3 (polarity asymmetry) applies to AUTH, not VER. The empirical blocking of falsification causatives for factual truth is stronger than AOML's current architecture predicts. AOML may need a VER-specific polarity asymmetry rule parallel to R3 for AUTH.

**2. C4 (Inchoative Positive) × D1: revelation verb substitution not fully modeled.**
AOML predicts BECOME(+) × VER is restricted. Empirical data shows VARIABLE with a systematic substitution pattern (revelation verbs preferred in he, el, zh). AOML's provisional REVEAL operator captures the substitution but its provisional status means this is not a confirmed prediction.

**3. D3 (Correctness of Judgment/Action) spans VER and NORM.**
D3 maps to both VER (factual accuracy component) and NORM (procedural correctness component) in AOML. Several languages use the same term for both functions (Hebrew nakhon, Swahili sahihi). AOML does not model this merger explicitly. The empirical finding that D3 is more naturally inchoative than D1 (Greek γίνεται σωστό is CORE while γίνεται αλήθεια is UNNATURAL) is not predicted by AOML.

**4. D8 (Epistemic Certainty/Doubt) as meta-axis vs. distinct dimension.**
AOML treats certainty/doubt as DEG[VER] (a meta-axis modifier). Empirical data shows certainty/doubt is a distinct lexical cluster in 7 of 8 languages with dedicated vocabulary (Hebrew vadai/safek, Greek βέβαιος/αμφίβολος, Swahili hakika). The empirical elaboration of certainty/doubt as a distinct semantic group suggests it may warrant more than meta-axis status in AOML.

**5. D13 (Resemblance/Similarity) epistemic hedging function not captured by LIFE.**
AOML's LIFE axis covers representational fidelity. Empirical D13 includes an epistemic hedging function (Hebrew domeh li she- = "it seems to me that") that maps better to DEG[VER] or a separate epistemic stance marker than to LIFE. The resemblance-as-hedging function is a distinct empirical pattern not fully captured by AOML.

**6. D14 (Epistemic Source/Evidence Type) is broader than VERIF.**
AOML's VERIF axis covers "the process of proving or confirming a claim." Empirical D14 covers the full range of epistemic source types including perception, inference, testimony, and conjecture (Sanskrit pramāṇa system; Quechua evidential morphology). VERIF captures the verification/confirmation end of D14 but not the source-typing function. The Sanskrit pramāṇa system and Quechua evidential morphology suggest D14 may warrant a dedicated axis or a more elaborated treatment within VERIF.

**7. D15 (Completeness/Sufficiency) has no clear AOML axis.**
D15 spans NORM (procedural completeness), AUTH (complete/genuine article), and a general evaluative dimension. AOML does not have a dedicated axis for completeness/sufficiency. The empirical data shows D15 is broadly attested across all 8 languages with productive inchoative and causative constructions, suggesting it is a genuine semantic dimension not captured by AOML.

**8. ABSTR × LIFE blocking: not fully confirmed.**
AOML predicts ABSTR × LIFE is universally blocked. Empirical data shows Chinese Group 14 (Artistic and Representational Realism: 逼真, 写真) and Greek αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude) as marginal but attested abstract nominalizations in the LIFE domain. The block may be strong but not absolute.

**9. C6 × AUTH: AOML's R3 conflates manufacturing and verifying authenticity.**
AOML Rule R3 states CAUSE(+) × AUTH is blocked (authenticity cannot be manufactured). But empirical data shows authentication causatives (authenticate, patunayan, chiqanchay) are broadly attested and rated ALLOWED. The empirical data distinguishes between manufacturing authenticity (blocked) and verifying/establishing authenticity (allowed). AOML's R3 needs to be refined to distinguish these two operations.

**10. Comparison (C15) more restricted with core truth adjectives than AOML predicts.**
AOML licenses DEG[VER] (comparative form). Empirical data shows comparative constructions with core truth adjectives (true, false) are more restricted than with other adjective types in at least 3 languages (Sanskrit -tara marginal; Quechua lacks morphological comparative; Chinese less common). AOML does not predict this differential restriction.

### Conflicts

**Conflict 1: C7 (Causative Falsification) × D1 (Correspondence to Fact/Reality) — stronger empirical blocking than AOML predicts.**

AOML predicts CAUSE(−) × VER is restricted (not blocked). The empirical data shows BLOCKED in 5 of 8 languages with an explicit structural gap in Quechua (F4: "no construction exists for disconfirmation as a positive operation"). AOML's R3 (polarity asymmetry) applies to AUTH, not VER. The empirical data suggests that a VER-specific polarity asymmetry rule is needed: positive causation of factual truth (verification) is broadly licensed, but negative causation of factual truth (falsification) is blocked or marginal in most languages. This is the strongest conflict between the empirical data and AOML's current architecture.

**Conflict 2: C7 (Causative Falsification) × D5 (Deliberate Deception/Lying) — R1 may be directionally asymmetric.**

AOML Rule R1 predicts CAUSE × SINC is universally blocked in both directions. But empirical data rates C7 × D5 as VARIABLE — causing someone to lie or mislead (pantachiy in Quechua, mislead in English, pantachiq in Sanskrit) is attested in several languages. This suggests that causing deception (negative causation of SINC) may be licensed even if causing sincerity (positive causation of SINC) is blocked. R1 as currently stated does not distinguish between positive and negative causation of dispositional states. The empirical data suggests R1 may need to be reformulated as: CAUSE(+) × SINC is universally blocked; CAUSE(−) × SINC is restricted but not universally blocked.

**Conflict 3: C4 (Inchoative Positive) × D11 (Substance vs. Appearance) — R2 may be too strong.**

AOML Rule R2 blocks BECOME × ESS. But empirical data rates C4 × D11 as VARIABLE, not BLOCKED. Some languages show natural inchoative constructions for revealing or manifesting essential nature (sut'iyay in Quechua, hitgalah in Hebrew). The conflict may reflect that D11 spans both ESS (where R2 applies) and LIFE (where BECOME is less restricted), and the empirical VARIABLE rating averages across both. However, it may also indicate that R2 is too strong — that becoming-manifest of essential nature is licensed even if becoming-essentially-different is blocked. The empirical data does not resolve this definitively but suggests R2 needs refinement.

**Conflict 4: Positive/Negative Agentive Asymmetry — not predicted by AOML.**

AOML licenses ABSTR for both positive and negative agentives without predicting asymmetry. The empirical data shows a strong, consistent asymmetry across all 8 languages and all language families: negative agentives (liars, deceivers) are consistently more elaborated, more colloquial, and more morphologically productive than positive agentives (truth-tellers, honest persons). This asymmetry is not predicted by AOML's current architecture. It may reflect a deeper pattern related to the salience of norm violation versus norm conformity, but AOML does not model this.

### Unmodeled Empirical Findings

**1. The positive/negative agentive asymmetry (C10 >> C11).**
Across all 8 languages, negative agentives are consistently more elaborated than positive agentives. This is one of the most robust findings in the empirical corpus (documented in all 8 languages across all language families). AOML licenses ABSTR for both polarities without predicting this asymmetry. A constraint or tendency capturing this asymmetry is absent from AOML.

**2. The verification/falsification causative asymmetry (C6 >> C7) for VER.**
AOML's R3 captures the polarity asymmetry for AUTH (authenticity cannot be manufactured but can be destroyed). The empirical data shows a parallel asymmetry for VER: verification causatives are broadly available but falsification causatives are blocked or marginal in most languages. AOML does not have a VER-specific polarity asymmetry rule. This is the most significant gap in AOML's coverage of the empirical data.

**3. Discourse marker grammaticalization (C17) as a distinct constructional function.**
The grammaticalization of truth-domain vocabulary into discourse markers (Chinese 说实话, Hebrew האמת היא ש..., Greek στην αλήθεια, English to be honest, Tagalog sa totoo lang, Swahili kwa kweli, Quechua chiqaqmanta rimay) is a distinct constructional function attested in 7 of 8 languages. AOML does not model grammaticalization as a distinct process or constructional function. The empirical data suggests this is a productive and cross-linguistically robust pattern that AOML's architecture does not capture.

**4. The Chinese 假 morpheme's three-way merger (falsity + inauthenticity + hypothetical framing).**
The Chinese 假 morpheme uniquely covers deliberate falsehood (假话), material inauthenticity (假冒), and hypothetical framing (假如, 假设). AOML does not model the integration of hypothetical framing with falsity vocabulary. This is a language-specific finding but it suggests that the relationship between falsity and hypothetical/suppositional framing may be structurally significant in ways AOML does not capture.

**5. The Swahili haki dual function (truth + rights/justice).**
The Swahili term haki covers both propositional truth (haki ni kwamba... = "the truth is that...") and rights/justice (haki za binadamu = "human rights"). AOML treats VER and NORM as separate axes. The empirical data shows that in at least one language, these axes are merged in a single term. AOML does not model axis merger as a cross-linguistic phenomenon.

**6. The Tagalog tama extension to sufficiency/cessation.**
The Tagalog term tama extends from correctness (D3) to sufficiency/cessation (tama na = "that's enough"). AOML does not model semantic extension from truth-domain terms to discourse-level sufficiency markers. This is a language-specific finding but it suggests that the relationship between correctness and sufficiency may be structurally significant.

**7. The Sanskrit pramāṇa system as a distinct epistemic source vocabulary.**
Sanskrit's differentiation of perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna), testimony (śabda), comparison (upamāna), postulation (arthāpatti), and non-perception (anupalabdhi) as distinct epistemic instruments constitutes a semantic cluster not captured by AOML's VERIF axis. AOML treats VERIF as a single axis for "the process of proving or confirming a claim." The pramāṇa system suggests that epistemic source differentiation may warrant a more elaborated treatment within VERIF or a separate axis.

**8. The Quechua evidential morphology as grammaticalized epistemic source marking.**
Quechua's obligatory evidential suffixes (-mi direct, -si hearsay, -cha conjecture) constitute a grammaticalized three-way distinction in epistemic source that is not modeled in AOML. AOML's VERIF axis and TEMP meta-axis do not capture obligatory evidential morphology as a distinct grammatical category. The Quechua data is the strongest empirical evidence for a dedicated evidential dimension in the truth domain.

**9. D15 (Completeness/Sufficiency) has no AOML axis.**
The empirical data shows D15 is a distinct semantic dimension attested in all 8 languages with productive inchoative and causative constructions. AOML does not have a dedicated axis for completeness/sufficiency. This dimension spans NORM, AUTH, and a general evaluative domain in ways that suggest it may warrant dedicated treatment.

**10. D16 (Objectivity vs. Subjectivity) as a language-specific elaboration.**
The empirical data shows D16 is clearly lexicalized only in Chinese and English. AOML does not model this dimension. The language-specific nature of D16 suggests it may not be a universal primitive but rather a culturally or historically specific elaboration of the VER axis. AOML's architecture does not model language-specific elaborations of primary axes.

### Unattested AOML Structures

**1. AOML LIFE axis (Lifelikeness/Representational Fidelity) as a primary axis.**
AOML treats LIFE as a primary axis with its own validation habitat (CONSENSUS, OBSERVATION) and operator licensing (ABSTR blocked). The empirical data shows that lifelikeness/verisimilitude vocabulary is attested in all 8 languages (Chinese Group 14, Greek αληθοφανής, etc.) but is consistently marginal or specialized rather than constituting a primary semantic cluster. The empirical data does not clearly support LIFE as a primary axis of equal status to VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, and VERIF. It may be better modeled as a subregion of AUTH (representational authenticity) or as a distinct but secondary dimension.

**2. AOML ESS axis (Essence) as a primary axis with CAUSE and BECOME blocked.**
AOML treats ESS as a primary axis with CAUSE and BECOME blocked (R2). The empirical data for D11 (Substance vs. Appearance) rates C4 and C6 as VARIABLE rather than BLOCKED. The empirical data does not clearly confirm R2 as a strong constraint. The ESS axis may be better modeled as a subregion of AUTH (essential authenticity) or as a distinct dimension with less strong operator blocking than R2 predicts.

**3. AOML VERIF axis (Verification) as a primary axis distinct from VER.**
AOML treats VERIF as a primary axis for "the process of proving or confirming a claim" distinct from VER (factual correspondence). The empirical data does not clearly support this distinction as a primary axis separation. The empirical data shows verification vocabulary (证实, thibitisha, patunayan, chiqanchay) as a distinct semantic cluster (D14 Epistemic Source, C6 Causative Verification) but these are better modeled as constructional functions applied to VER claims rather than as a separate primary axis. The empirical data suggests VERIF may be better treated as a constructional function (C6) or as a subregion of D14 rather than as a primary axis.

**4. AOML Rule R6 (Temporal Meta-Axis Substitution Prohibition) — provisional and not clearly attested.**
AOML Rule R6 is marked as provisional and did not surface in the CLCA-1.1 global synthesis. The empirical data from the 8-language corpus does not provide clear evidence for or against R6. The temporal aspects of truth-domain constructions (stability, continuation, cessation) are attested in the empirical data (C4/C5 inchoative constructions, C17 discourse markers) but the specific substitution prohibition (TEMP[A] cannot substitute for A in inference) is not directly tested by the empirical matrix.

**5. AOML's deep hypothesis about target-type ontology as structurally prior.**
AOML proposes that the three strongest empirical findings (R1 dispositional causation block, R4 positive emergence bias, cross-linguistic preference for revelation constructions) may all be surface projections of a single deeper constraint about target-type ontology (factual targets are externally producible; epistemic targets are discoverable; dispositional targets are internally cultivated). The empirical data is consistent with this hypothesis but does not provide direct evidence for or against it. The hypothesis is not testable from the constructional data alone.

## Theoretical Discussion

### Where AOML Appears Strongly Supported

**The dispositional causation block (R1) is the most strongly supported AOML prediction.** The empirical data confirms that causation of dispositional states (sincerity, honesty, character) is blocked or marginal across the corpus. The specific finding that C7 × D4 is BLOCKED and that C6 × D4 is INSUFFICIENT DATA (rather than clearly ALLOWED) is consistent with R1. The empirical data from 8 languages does not contradict R1, and the directional confirmation (negative causation of SINC is blocked) is clear.

**The positive emergence bias (R4) is directionally supported.** The empirical finding that C4 (Inchoative Positive) is more broadly available than C5 (Inchoative Negative) across the corpus is consistent with R4. The specific blocking of C5 × D5 (deception does not "become") is the strongest confirmation. The asymmetry is not universal (Chinese, Swahili, and English show symmetric inchoatives) but the directional tendency is consistent with R4.

**The universal availability of ATTR, CONTR, and ABSTR is strongly supported.** The empirical finding that C1, C2, C3, and C9 are ALLOWED for all 17 dimensions aligns with AOML's prediction that these operators are universally licensed. This is the most robustly confirmed finding in both systems.

**The validation habitat framework (Layer 3) is directionally supported.** The empirical data shows that different truth-domain dimensions have different validation requirements (factual correspondence requires observation/experiment; moral rightness requires principle/consensus; sincerity requires testimony/conduct pattern). This is consistent with AOML's validation habitat framework, though the empirical data does not directly test inadmissible method violations.

### Where AOML May Require Refinement

**1. R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry) needs to be extended to VER.**
The empirical data shows a strong polarity asymmetry for VER (verification causatives broadly available; falsification causatives blocked or marginal in 5 of 8 languages) that parallels R3 for AUTH. AOML currently applies R3 only to AUTH. A VER-specific polarity asymmetry rule would be: CAUSE(+) × VER (verification) is broadly licensed; CAUSE(−) × VER (falsification) is blocked or marginal in most languages. This is the most significant gap in AOML's coverage of the empirical data.

**2. R1 (Dispositional Causation Block) may need directional refinement.**
The empirical data suggests that CAUSE(−) × SINC (causing deception, misleading) may be licensed even if CAUSE(+) × SINC (causing sincerity) is blocked. AOML's R1 as currently stated blocks all causation of dispositional states. The empirical data for C7 × D5 (VARIABLE) suggests that causing someone to lie or be deceived is attested in several languages. R1 may need to be reformulated as: CAUSE(+) × SINC is universally blocked; CAUSE(−) × SINC is restricted but not universally blocked.

**3. The positive/negative agentive asymmetry needs to be modeled.**
The empirical finding that negative agentives (C10) are consistently more elaborated than positive agentives (C11) across all 8 languages is not predicted by AOML. This asymmetry may reflect a deeper pattern about the salience of norm violation versus norm conformity, or about the pragmatic utility of identifying norm violators. AOML's architecture does not model this asymmetry. A structural rule or tendency capturing this finding would strengthen AOML's predictive power.

**4. The LIFE axis may need to be reconsidered.**
The empirical data does not clearly support LIFE as a primary axis of equal status to VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, and VERIF. Lifelikeness/verisimilitude vocabulary is consistently marginal or specialized in the empirical corpus. The ABSTR × LIFE blocking prediction is not fully confirmed (Chinese and Greek show marginal abstract nominalizations in this domain). LIFE may be better modeled as a subregion of AUTH (representational authenticity) or as a secondary dimension.

**5. The ESS axis operator blocking (R2) may be too strong.**
The empirical data for D11 (Substance vs. Appearance) rates C4 and C6 as VARIABLE rather than BLOCKED. R2 predicts CAUSE × ESS and BECOME × ESS are blocked. The empirical data suggests that becoming-manifest of essential nature (revelation of essence) may be licensed even if becoming-essentially-different is blocked. R2 may need to be refined to distinguish between: (a) acquiring or losing essential properties (blocked) and (b) manifesting or revealing essential properties (licensed, consistent with provisional REVEAL operator).

**6. The VERIF axis may be better treated as a constructional function.**
The empirical data does not clearly support VERIF as a primary axis distinct from VER. Verification vocabulary is better modeled as a constructional function (C6 Causative Verification) applied to VER claims rather than as a separate primary axis. The empirical data suggests that VERIF may be a subregion of D14 (Epistemic Source/Evidence Type) rather than a primary axis.

**7. D8 (Epistemic Certainty/Doubt) may warrant more than meta-axis status.**
The empirical data shows certainty/doubt is a distinct lexical cluster in 7 of 8 languages with dedicated vocabulary and productive derivation. AOML treats certainty/doubt as DEG[VER] (a meta-axis modifier). The empirical elaboration of certainty/doubt as a distinct semantic group suggests it may warrant a dedicated axis or at minimum a more prominent treatment within the architecture.

### Where Empirical Findings Suggest New Dimensions, Constructional Constraints, or Revised Constraint Strength

**New dimension: Epistemic Source/Evidence Type (D14) as a primary axis.**
The empirical data shows D14 is a distinct semantic dimension with its own lexical elaboration (Sanskrit pramāṇa system, Quechua evidential morphology) that goes beyond what VERIF captures. A dedicated axis for epistemic source differentiation would capture: (a) the distinction between direct observation, inference, testimony, and conjecture; (b) the grammaticalized evidential morphology in Quechua; (c) the technical pramāṇa vocabulary in Sanskrit. This axis would be distinct from VERIF (which covers the process of proving) and from DEG[VER] (which covers degree of confidence).

**New constructional constraint: VER polarity asymmetry rule.**
The empirical data supports a new structural rule parallel to R3 for AUTH: CAUSE(+) × VER (verification) is broadly licensed; CAUSE(−) × VER (falsification) is blocked or marginal in most languages. This rule would explain the strongest conflict between the empirical data and AOML's current architecture.

**New constructional constraint: Positive/Negative Agentive Asymmetry.**
The empirical data supports a new structural tendency: ABSTR applied to the negative pole of truth-domain axes generates more elaborated, more colloquial, and more morphologically productive agentive forms than ABSTR applied to the positive pole. This tendency is attested across all 8 languages and all language families. A structural rule or tendency capturing this finding would strengthen AOML's predictive power.

**New constructional function: Discourse Marker Grammaticalization (C17).**
The empirical data shows that grammaticalization of truth-domain vocabulary into discourse markers is a distinct and cross-linguistically robust constructional function (7 of 8 languages). AOML does not model grammaticalization as a distinct process. A dedicated constructional function for discourse marker grammaticalization would capture this pattern.

**Revised constraint strength: R4 (Positive Emergence Bias) is a tendency, not a universal block.**
The empirical data shows that C5 (Inchoative Negative) is VARIABLE rather than universally blocked. Chinese, Swahili, and English show symmetric inchoatives for both positive and negative truth-domain states. R4 should be reformulated as a strong tendency rather than a near-universal constraint: negative emergence is marked or dispreferred in most languages, but not universally blocked.

**Revised constraint strength: ABSTR × LIFE blocking is not absolute.**
The empirical data shows marginal but attested abstract nominalizations in the LIFE domain (Chinese 逼真, Greek αληθοφάνεια). AOML predicts this is universally blocked. The empirical data suggests the block is strong but not absolute, and may be better characterized as a strong tendency rather than a universal prohibition.

**Revised treatment: D3 (Correctness) as spanning VER and NORM.**
The empirical data shows that correctness of judgment/action spans both VER (factual accuracy) and NORM (procedural correctness) in ways that are not fully captured by treating these as separate axes. Several languages use the same term for both functions. AOML may need to model the VER/NORM overlap in the correctness domain more explicitly, or acknowledge that D3 is a cross-axis dimension rather than a pure instance of either axis.
