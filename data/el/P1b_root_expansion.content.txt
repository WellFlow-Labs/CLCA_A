# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **ψευδ-** (pseud-)
**Native form:** ψευδ-  
**Transliteration:** pseud-  
**P1 items:** 5 (ψέμα, ψεύτης, ψευδής, ψευδώνυμο, and related to πλαστός conceptually but not morphologically)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Falsity, deception, lying; marks something as not corresponding to truth or reality, often with implication of deliberate misrepresentation.

### Root 2: **αληθ-** (alith-)
**Native form:** αληθ-  
**Transliteration:** alith-  
**P1 items:** 2 (αλήθεια, αληθινός)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth, un-hiddenness, genuineness; marks correspondence with reality or facts, authenticity of state or statement.

### Root 3: **ορθ-** (orth-)
**Native form:** ορθ-  
**Transliteration:** orth-  
**P1 items:** 1 (ορθός)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, proper alignment; marks conformity to standard, rule, or correct form.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: ψευδ- (pseud-)

1. **ψεύδομαι** (psévomai)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + -ομαι (middle/passive verb ending)  
   Translation: to lie, to tell lies, to deceive  
   Context: Primary verb for the act of lying or deceiving through false statements. Used across all registers from everyday conversation to formal discourse.  
   [CORE]

2. **ψευδολογία** (psevdología)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + λογ- (speech) + -ία (noun suffix)  
   Translation: lying, mendacity, false speech  
   Context: Formal or literary term for the practice or habit of lying. Used in moral and psychological discourse to describe patterns of deceptive speech.  
   [CORE]

3. **ψευδολόγος** (psevdológos)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + λογ- (speech) + -ος (adjective/noun suffix)  
   Translation: lying, mendacious; liar  
   Context: Adjective or noun marking someone who speaks falsely. More formal or literary than ψεύτης, used in written discourse and moral evaluation.  
   [CORE]

4. **ψευδορκία** (psevdorkía)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + ορκ- (oath) + -ία  
   Translation: perjury, false oath  
   Context: Legal term for the crime of lying under oath. Used in judicial contexts and legal documents to denote false testimony.  
   [CORE]

5. **ψευδόρκος** (psevdórkos)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + ορκ- (oath) + -ος  
   Translation: perjured, forsworn; perjurer  
   Context: Legal term for someone who has committed perjury or broken an oath. Used in legal proceedings and moral condemnation of oath-breaking.  
   [CORE]

6. **ψευδομαρτυρία** (psevdomartyría)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + μαρτυρ- (witness) + -ία  
   Translation: false testimony, false witness  
   Context: Legal and religious term for giving false evidence or testimony. Common in judicial contexts and biblical/religious discourse.  
   [CORE]

7. **ψευδομάρτυρας** (psevdomártyras)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + μάρτυρ- (witness) + -ας  
   Translation: false witness, perjurer  
   Context: Legal and religious term for one who gives false testimony. Used in court proceedings and moral/religious condemnation.  
   [CORE]

8. **ψευδεπίγραφος** (psevdepígrafos)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + επι- (upon) + γραφ- (writing) + -ος  
   Translation: falsely attributed, pseudepigraphic  
   Context: Technical term in textual criticism and religious studies for texts falsely attributed to a particular author. Used in academic and scholarly discourse.  
   [CORE]

9. **ψευδώνυμος** (psevdónymos)  
   Morphemes: ψευδ- + ονυμ- (name) + -ος  
   Translation: pseudonymous, using a false name  
   Context: Adjective form related to ψευδώνυμο, describing someone writing or acting under an assumed name. Used in literary and journalistic contexts.  
   [CORE]

10. **ψευδαίσθηση** (psevdaísthisi)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + αισθη- (perception) + -ση  
    Translation: illusion, false perception, hallucination  
    Context: Psychological and philosophical term for perceptions that do not correspond to reality. Used in psychology, philosophy of mind, and everyday discussion of optical illusions or misperceptions.  
    [CORE]

11. **ψευδοεπιστήμη** (psevdoepistími)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + επιστημ- (science/knowledge) + -η  
    Translation: pseudoscience  
    Context: Academic and critical term for practices or beliefs falsely presented as scientific. Used in scientific discourse and skeptical evaluation of claims.  
    [CORE]

12. **ψευδοϊστορία** (psevdoïstoría)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + ιστορ- (history) + -ία  
    Translation: false history, pseudohistory  
    Context: Academic term for historical narratives that are fabricated or lack proper evidence. Used in historiography and critical evaluation of historical claims.  
    [CORE]

13. **ψευδοφιλόσοφος** (psevdofílósofos)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + φιλοσοφ- (philosopher) + -ος  
    Translation: pseudo-philosopher, false philosopher  
    Context: Polemical term for someone who claims philosophical authority without genuine philosophical understanding. Used in philosophical critique and intellectual debate.  
    [CORE]

14. **ψευδοδιανοούμενος** (psevdodianoúmenos)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + διανοουμεν- (intellectual, participle) + -ος  
    Translation: pseudo-intellectual  
    Context: Critical term for someone who pretends to intellectual sophistication without substance. Used in cultural criticism and social commentary.  
    [CORE]

15. **ψευδοκαλλιτέχνης** (psevdokallitéchnis)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + καλλι- (beauty) + τεχν- (art) + -ης  
    Translation: pseudo-artist, charlatan artist  
    Context: Critical term in art discourse for someone who poses as an artist without genuine artistic merit. Used in art criticism and aesthetic evaluation.  
    [MARGINAL]

16. **ψευδοπροφήτης** (psevdoprofítis)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + προφητ- (prophet) + -ης

    [CORE]

17. **ψευδοδιάγνωση** (psevdodiágnosi)

    [CORE]

18. **ψευδοπαράδειγμα** (psevdoparádegma)

19. **ψευδοεντύπωση** (psevdoentýposi)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + εν- (in) + τυπω- (strike/impress) + -ση  
    Translation: false impression, misleading appearance  
    Context: Common term for an impression or appearance that does not correspond to reality. Used in everyday discourse about misunderstandings and deceptive appearances.  
    [CORE]

20. **ψευδοπληροφορία** (psevdopliροforía)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + πληροφορ- (information) + -ία  
    Translation: misinformation, false information  
    Context: Contemporary term for false or inaccurate information, especially in media and digital contexts. Increasingly common in discussions of news media and online content.  
    [CORE]

21. **ψευδοείδηση** (psevdoéidisi)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + ειδη- (news) + -ση  
    Translation: fake news, false news  
    Context: Contemporary term for deliberately fabricated news stories. Common in media criticism and political discourse about information manipulation.  
    [CORE]

22. **ψευδοπατριωτισμός** (psevdopatriotismós)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + πατριωτ- (patriot) + -ισμ- (ism) + -ός  
    Translation: false patriotism, pseudo-patriotism  
    Context: Political term for superficial or self-serving displays of patriotism. Used in political critique and social commentary.  
    [MARGINAL]

23. **ψευδοδημοκρατία** (psevdodimokratía)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + δημο- (people) + κρατ- (rule) + -ία  
    Translation: pseudo-democracy, sham democracy

    [CORE]

24. **ψευδοανάλυση** (psevdoanálysi)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + ανα- (up/again) + λυ- (loosen) + -ση

    [MARGINAL]

25. **ψευδοεπιχείρημα** (psevdoepichírima)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + επι- (upon) + χειρ- (hand) + -ημα  
    Translation: false argument, fallacious argument  
    Context: Logical and rhetorical term for arguments that appear valid but contain logical flaws. Used in logic, philosophy, and critical thinking instruction.  
    [CORE]

26. **ψευδοσοφία** (psevdosofía)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + σοφ- (wisdom) + -ία  
    Translation: false wisdom, pseudo-wisdom  
    Context: Philosophical and critical term for claims to wisdom that lack genuine understanding. Used in philosophical critique and evaluation of intellectual pretension.  
    [MARGINAL]

27. **ψευδοπρόβλημα** (psevdopróvlima)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + προ- (before) + βλη- (throw) + -μα  
    Translation: pseudo-problem, false problem  
    Context: Philosophical term for problems that appear significant but are based on conceptual confusion. Used in philosophical analysis and problem-solving discourse.  
    [MARGINAL]

28. **ψευδοσύμπτωμα** (psevdosýmptoma)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + συν- (together) + πτω- (fall) + -μα  
    Translation: false symptom, pseudo-symptom  
    Context: Medical and psychological term for symptoms that appear genuine but have no organic basis or are fabricated. Used in clinical diagnosis and psychosomatic medicine.  
    [CORE]

29. **ψευδοκύηση** (psevdokýisi)  
    Morphemes: ψευδ- + κυη- (pregnancy) + -ση  
    Translation: false pregnancy, pseudocyesis  
    Context: Medical term for a condition where someone believes they are pregnant without actual pregnancy. Used in medical and psychological contexts.

30. **ψευδοπόδιο** (psevdopódio)

    [MARGINAL]

**Note:** The ψευδ- root is highly productive with 30+ common compounds. Additional technical and scientific terms exist in specialized domains.

### ROOT 2: αληθ- (alith-)

1. **αληθεύω** (alithévo)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + -ευω (verb suffix)  
   Translation: to tell the truth, to speak truly  
   Context: Verb for the act of telling truth or being truthful. Used across registers from everyday conversation to formal discourse, often in contrast to lying.  
   [CORE]

2. **αληθολογώ** (alithologó)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + λογ- (speech) + -ω  
   Translation: to speak the truth, to be truthful  
   Context: Synonym of αληθεύω, perhaps slightly more formal or emphatic. Used in contexts emphasizing truthful speech as a practice or habit.  
   [CORE]

3. **αλήθευμα** (alíthevma)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + -ευμα (result noun suffix)  
   Translation: truth, true statement  
   Context: Formal or literary term for a true statement or truth. Less common than αλήθεια, used in elevated discourse.  
   [MARGINAL]

4. **αληθοφάνεια** (alithofáneia)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + φαν- (appear) + -εια  
   Translation: verisimilitude, appearance of truth  
   Context: Literary and philosophical term for something that appears true or plausible without necessarily being true. Used in literary criticism and epistemology.  
   [CORE]

5. **αληθοφανής** (alithofanís)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + φαν- (appear) + -ης  
   Translation: verisimilar, truth-like, plausible  
   Context: Adjective for something that has the appearance of truth. Used in literary analysis, rhetoric, and epistemological discussion of plausibility versus truth.  
   [CORE]

6. **αληθώς** (alithós)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + -ως (adverb suffix)  
   Translation: truly, in truth, really  
   Context: Adverb marking truth or reality of a statement. Used for emphasis or confirmation across all registers, from everyday speech to formal writing.  
   [CORE]

7. **αληθολογία** (alithología)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + λογ- (speech) + -ία  
   Translation: truthfulness, truth-telling  
   Context: Formal term for the practice or quality of speaking truth. Used in moral and philosophical discourse about truthfulness as a virtue.  
   [MARGINAL]

8. **αληθοποιώ** (alithopoió)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + ποι- (make) + -ω  
   Translation: to verify, to make true, to confirm  
   Context: Formal or technical term for the process of verifying or confirming something as true. Used in legal, administrative, and scholarly contexts.  
   [CORE]

9. **αληθοποίηση** (alithopoíisi)  
   Morphemes: αληθ- + ποι- (make) + -ηση (action noun)  
   Translation: verification, confirmation, validation  
   Context: Formal term for the act or process of verifying truth. Common in legal, administrative, and academic contexts for validation procedures.  
   [CORE]

10. **αληθοφιλής** (alithofilís)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + φιλ- (love) + -ης  
    Translation: truth-loving, devoted to truth  
    Context: Formal or literary term for someone who loves or seeks truth. Used in philosophical and moral discourse to characterize intellectual virtue.  
    [MARGINAL]

11. **αληθοφιλία** (alithofilía)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + φιλ- (love) + -ία  
    Translation: love of truth, devotion to truth  
    Context: Philosophical term for the disposition or virtue of loving truth. Used in ethical and epistemological discourse about intellectual virtues.  
    [MARGINAL]

12. **αλήθεια;** (alítheia?)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + -εια (as interjection)  
    Translation: really?, truly?, is that so?  
    Context: Colloquial interjection expressing surprise, doubt, or request for confirmation. Common in everyday spoken Greek as a discourse marker.  
    [CORE]

13. **στ' αλήθεια** (st' alítheia)  
    Morphemes: σε (in) + την (the) + αληθ- + -εια  
    Translation: really, truly, in truth  
    Context: Common colloquial expression for emphasis or confirmation. Used in everyday speech to mark sincerity or genuine surprise.  
    [CORE]

14. **κατ' αλήθεια** (kat' alítheia)  
    Morphemes: κατα (according to) + αληθ- + -εια  
    Translation: in truth, truly, actually  
    Context: Formal or emphatic expression marking truth or reality. Used across registers for emphasis or to distinguish truth from appearance.  
    [CORE]

15. **επ' αληθείας** (ep' alithías)  
    Morphemes: επι (upon) + αληθ- + -ειας (genitive)  
    Translation: in truth, truly, honestly  
    Context: Formal or archaic expression marking truthfulness. More common in written or elevated discourse than everyday speech.  
    [MARGINAL]

16. **αληθινά** (alithiná)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + -ινα (adverb from adjective)  
    Translation: truly, really, genuinely  
    Context: Adverb derived from αληθινός, used for emphasis or confirmation. Common in everyday speech and writing across registers.  
    [CORE]

17. **αληθινότητα** (alithinótita)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + -ιν- + -οτητα (abstract noun suffix)  
    Translation: truthfulness, genuineness, authenticity  
    Context: Abstract noun for the quality of being true or genuine. Used in philosophical, aesthetic, and evaluative discourse.  
    [CORE]

18. **αληθοδιψής** (alithodipsís)  
    Morphemes: αληθ- + διψ- (thirst) + -ης  
    Translation: thirsting for truth, truth-seeking  
    Context: Literary or philosophical term for someone who ardently seeks truth. Used in elevated discourse about intellectual or spiritual seeking.  
    [MARGINAL]

**Note:** The αληθ- root has moderate productivity (18 items listed). The root appears in fewer compounds than ψευδ-, with many expressions being prepositional phrases or adverbial forms rather than compound nouns.

### ROOT 3: ορθ- (orth-)

1. **ορθότητα** (orthótita)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + -οτητα (abstract noun suffix)  
   Translation: correctness, rightness, accuracy  
   Context: Abstract noun for the quality of being correct or right. Used across contexts from grammatical correctness to moral rightness.  
   [CORE]

2. **ορθώς** (orthós)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + -ως (adverb suffix)  
   Translation: correctly, rightly, properly  
   Context: Adverb marking correct manner or proper execution. Used across all registers for confirmation or evaluation of correctness.  
   [CORE]

3. **ορθογραφία** (orthografía)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + γραφ- (writing) + -ία  
   Translation: orthography, correct spelling  
   Context: Standard term for correct spelling and writing conventions. Used in education, linguistics, and everyday discussion of written language.  
   [CORE]

4. **ορθογραφικός** (orthografikós)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + γραφ- + -ικος (adjective suffix)  
   Translation: orthographic, spelling (adj.)  
   Context: Adjective relating to spelling and orthography. Common in educational and linguistic contexts.  
   [CORE]

5. **ορθογραφώ** (orthografó)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + γραφ- + -ω  
   Translation: to spell correctly  
   Context: Verb for the act of spelling words correctly. Used in educational contexts and discussions of literacy.  
   [CORE]

6. **ορθοδοξία** (orthodoxía)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + δοξ- (opinion/belief) + -ία  
   Translation: orthodoxy, correct belief  
   Context: Religious and ideological term for adherence to established or correct doctrine. Central in Eastern Orthodox Christianity, also used for conventional views in any domain.  
   [CORE]

7. **ορθόδοξος** (orthódoxos)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + δοξ- + -ος

8. **ορθοπεδικός** (orthopedikós)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + παιδ- (child) + -ικος

   Context: Medical term for the branch of medicine dealing with correction of skeletal deformities. Standard medical terminology.  
   [CORE]

9. **ορθοπεδική** (orthopedikí)  
   Morphemes: ορθ- + παιδ- + -ική (feminine noun from adjective)  
   Translation: orthopedics  
   Context: Medical specialty dealing with musculoskeletal system. Used in healthcare contexts.  
   [CORE]

10. **ορθοπραξία** (orthopraxía)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + πραξ- (action/practice) + -ία  
    Translation: orthopraxy, correct practice  
    Context: Religious and philosophical term for correct practice or conduct, as opposed to correct belief. Used in religious studies and ethical discourse.  
    [MARGINAL]

11. **ορθολογισμός** (orthologismós)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + λογ- (reason) + -ισμος (ism suffix)  
    Translation: rationalism, rationality  
    Context: Philosophical term for reliance on reason and rational principles. Used in philosophy, economics, and general discourse about rational approaches.  
    [CORE]

12. **ορθολογικός** (orthologikós)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + λογ- + -ικος  
    Translation: rational, reasonable  
    Context: Adjective for conformity to reason or rational principles. Used in philosophical, scientific, and everyday evaluative discourse.  
    [CORE]

13. **ορθολογιστής** (orthologistís)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + λογ- + -ιστης (agent noun)  
    Translation: rationalist  
    Context: Philosophical term for someone who emphasizes reason and rational principles. Used in philosophical and intellectual discourse.  
    [MARGINAL]

14. **ορθοφωνία** (orthofōnía)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + φων- (voice/sound) + -ία  
    Translation: correct pronunciation, speech therapy  
    Context: Technical term in linguistics and speech therapy for correct pronunciation or the correction of speech defects. Used in educational and clinical contexts.  
    [CORE]

15. **ορθοεπεία** (orthoepía)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + επ- (upon) + -εια (from έπος "word")

    Context: Linguistic term for correct or standard pronunciation. More technical than ορθοφωνία, used in linguistics and language instruction.

16. **ορθοτομία** (orthotomía)  
    Morphemes: ορθ- + τομ- (cut) + -ία

**Note:** The ορθ- root is highly productive with 30+ compounds. Many extend into technical domains (medical, geometric, biological) where "straight" or "correct" has specialized meanings.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

### Root ψευδ- (pseud-)

**Biological/anatomical extensions:**
- ψευδοπόδιο (pseudopod): The root shifts from deliberate deception to morphological description of temporary cellular projections that resemble but are not true feet.
- ψευδοκύηση (false pregnancy): Medical condition where "false" marks absence of actual pregnancy despite symptoms, not deliberate deception.

**Taxonomic/scientific extensions:**
- ψευδόπτερα (Orthoptera reference in compounds): Used in biological taxonomy where "pseudo-" marks resemblance rather than falsity in moral sense.

**Intensity/degree marking:**
- In compounds like ψευδοδιανοούμενος (pseudo-intellectual), the root functions as a pejorative intensifier marking inadequacy or pretension rather than deliberate lying.

**Artistic/aesthetic judgment:**
- ψευδοκαλλιτέχνης (pseudo-artist): Shifts from truth-falsity to aesthetic authenticity and artistic merit.

---

### Root αληθ- (alith-)

**Discourse marker function:**
- αλήθεια; (really?): Functions as pragmatic discourse marker for surprise or confirmation rather than truth-evaluation.
- στ' αλήθεια (really): Similar pragmatic function as emphasis or surprise marker.

**Plausibility vs. truth:**
- αληθοφάνεια, αληθοφανής (verisimilitude): Mark appearance or plausibility rather than actual truth, introducing distinction between seeming-true and being-true.

**Emotional/spiritual seeking:**
- αληθοδιψής (truth-thirsting): Extends from epistemic domain to emotional or spiritual disposition.

---

### Root ορθ- (orth-)

**Physical/spatial orientation:**
- ορθοστασία (upright posture), ορθοστάτης (standing stone): Root marks physical verticality rather than correctness.
- ορθοπροσωπία (facing forward): Spatial orientation rather than correctness.

**Geometric/mathematical:**
- ορθογώνιος, ορθογώνιο (right-angled, rectangle): "Straight" becomes technical geometric property (90-degree angle).

**Medical/anatomical:**
- ορθοπεδικός (orthopedic), ορθοδοντικός (orthodontic), ορθόπνοια (orthopnea): Root marks physical correction or alignment rather than epistemic correctness.

**Biological taxonomy:**
- ορθόπτερα (Orthoptera): Morphological description (straight wings) in taxonomic classification.

**Rational faculty:**
- ορθολογισμός, ορθολογικός (rationalism, rational): Extends from correctness to faculty of reason itself.

**Moral conduct:**
- ορθοποδώ, ορθοποδία (walk uprightly): Physical metaphor for moral rectitude, extending from correctness to ethical behavior.

**Religious doctrine:**
- ορθοδοξία, ορθόδοξος (orthodoxy, orthodox): Shifts from general correctness to specifically doctrinal correctness and religious identity.
