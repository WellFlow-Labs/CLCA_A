1. **benar**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic adjective; ‘correct, true, right’  
   **Typical English translations:** true; correct; right  

   **Usage:** *Benar* is one of the most common Indonesian words for saying that a statement, answer, or report is correct or true. It is also used in broader evaluative contexts, such as judging an action or decision as right.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **kebenaran**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *ke- + benar + -an*; ‘truth, correctness’  
   **Typical English translations:** truth; correctness; rightness  

   **Usage:** *Kebenaran* is used for truth in general, including factual, moral, religious, and philosophical contexts. It can refer both to the truth of a proposition and to truth as an abstract value or principle.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **membenarkan**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *meN- + benar + -kan*; ‘to make correct; to justify; to confirm as true’  
   **Typical English translations:** to justify; to confirm; to validate; to correct  

   **Usage:** In ordinary usage, *membenarkan* can mean confirming that something is true or correct. It is also very common in moral and legal contexts for ‘justifying’ an act, argument, or decision.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **betul**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic adjective; ‘correct, exact’  
   **Typical English translations:** correct; right; true; exact  

   **Usage:** *Betul* is widely used in speech and writing to affirm that something is correct. It often overlaps with *benar*, though in many contexts it sounds slightly more colloquial or conversational.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **kebetulan**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *ke- + betul + -an*; historically related, but synchronically ‘coincidence’  
   **Typical English translations:** coincidence; by chance  

   **Usage:** Although morphologically related to *betul*, modern *kebetulan* usually means ‘coincidence’ rather than ‘correctness’. Because its current usage does not normally evaluate truth or rightness, it is only a peripheral item here.  
   **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

6. **tepat**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** basic adjective; ‘precise, exact, fitting’  
   **Typical English translations:** accurate; precise; appropriate; exact  

   **Usage:** *Tepat* is used for accuracy in statements, descriptions, measurements, timing, and choices. It also extends to judgments of appropriateness, as in whether a response, policy, or action is fitting.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **ketepatan**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *ke- + tepat + -an*; ‘accuracy, precision, appropriateness’  
   **Typical English translations:** accuracy; precision; exactness; appropriateness  

   **Usage:** *Ketepatan* commonly refers to the accuracy of data, wording, analysis, or identification. It is also used for precision in technical and administrative contexts, and sometimes for the appropriateness of a decision or measure.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **tepat sasaran**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** ‘precise/right + target’  
   **Typical English translations:** well-targeted; on target; accurately directed  

   **Usage:** This fixed expression is common in policy, aid distribution, criticism, and planning. It evaluates whether an action, statement, or intervention reaches the correct object, group, or issue.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **sesuai**  
   **Transliteration:** —  
   **Literal meaning / morphology:** *se- + suai*; ‘in accord, matching’  
   **Typical English translations:** appropriate; in accordance; suitable; consistent with  

   **Usage:** *Sesuai* is used when something matches a rule, fact, expectation, description, or standard. It is especially common in legal, bureaucratic, and formal contexts for conformity with documents, procedures, or reality.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **kesesuaian**

    **Usage:** *Kesesuaian* refers to the degree to which one thing matches another, such as data with evidence, conduct with rules, or a representation with the original. It is frequent in formal evaluation, auditing, and academic writing.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

    **Usage:** *Cocok* is common in everyday speech for whether something suits a person, purpose, or situation. It can also be used for resemblance or matching, such as whether a description, sample, or identification fits.

13. **asli**

    **Typical English translations:** genuine; authentic; original; real  

    **Usage:** *Asli* is widely used to evaluate whether an object, document, artwork, or identity is genuine rather than fake or copied. It also appears in broader senses such as ‘native’ or ‘original’, but genuineness is a central use.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

    **Usage:** *Keaslian* is common in discussions of documents, signatures, artifacts, artworks, and academic work. It evaluates whether something is genuinely what it claims to be, or whether it is original rather than derivative or forged.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **palsu**

    **Typical English translations:** fake; false; counterfeit  

    **Usage:** *Palsu* is used for forged documents, counterfeit goods, false identity, and deceptive representations. It directly contrasts with *asli* in many practical and legal contexts.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
