# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven constructions that are not reducible to single-word derivational forms (documented in P2a). Constructions already documented in P1b as collocations are noted as previously documented rather than re-described in full. Absence of constructions for a given base form is recorded explicitly. The inventory does not assume symmetry across base forms.

## BASE FORM 1: egia

---

**C1.1**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esan, ez nuen espero.
- **Transliteration:** Egia esan, ez nuen espero.
- **English translation:** To tell the truth, I didn't expect it.
- **Usage notes:** *Egia esan* functions as a sentence-initial framing device signaling candor or frankness. It is syntactically detached from the main clause and functions as a discourse marker. Documented in P1b item 3 (*egia esan nahi bada* is the fuller variant); the bare *egia esan* form is the more frequent reduced version.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.2**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia da hori.
- **Transliteration:** Egia da hori.
- **English translation:** That is true.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting truth, with *egia* as predicate nominal and *da* (3sg present of *izan*) as copula. Documented in P1b item 2; noted here as the primary predicative construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.3**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia ala gezurra?
- **Transliteration:** Egia ala gezurra?
- **English translation:** True or false? Truth or lie?
- **Usage notes:** A fixed binary interrogative construction used in educational, game, and rhetorical contexts to pose a truth-value question. The *ala* conjunction marks the exclusive disjunction. Common in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.4**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia da ala gezurra?
- **Transliteration:** Egia da ala gezurra?
- **English translation:** Is it true or false?
- **Usage notes:** The predicative expansion of C1.3, with the copula *da* included. Used in the same contexts as C1.3 but as a full interrogative sentence rather than a nominal pair.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.5**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esateko, ez dakit.
- **Transliteration:** Egia esateko, ez dakit.
- **English translation:** To tell the truth, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic framing construction using the purposive verbal noun (*esateko*, "in order to say"). Functions as a hedging or candor-marking device, parallel to *egia esan* (C1.1) but with a slightly more formal or deliberate tone.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.6**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia baino egia handiagoa
- **Transliteration:** Egia baino egia handiagoa
- **English translation:** A truth greater than truth / truer than true
- **Usage notes:** A comparative intensification construction using the comparative marker *baino* and the comparative suffix *-ago*. Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to assert that something is emphatically or paradoxically true. Attested in literary and oratorical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**C1.7**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia da, baina...
- **Transliteration:** Egia da, baina...
- **English translation:** It is true, but...
- **Usage notes:** A concessive construction in which *egia da* is followed by the adversative conjunction *baina* to introduce a qualification or counterpoint. Extremely common in argumentation and everyday speech as a concession-and-rebuttal structure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.8**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik
- **Transliteration:** Egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik
- **English translation:** The whole truth and nothing but
- **Usage notes:** A formulaic construction echoing oath language, used in legal and rhetorical contexts to assert complete truthfulness. The full Basque oath formula varies by jurisdiction; this is an attested abbreviated form used in everyday and literary registers to invoke the same register.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**C1.9**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egiaren bila dabil.
- **Transliteration:** Egiaren bila dabil.
- **English translation:** He/she is in search of the truth.
- **Usage notes:** The construction *egiaren bila ibili/dabil* uses the postposition *bila* (in search of) with the genitive of *egia*. Documented in P1b item 14 as a collocation; noted here as a productive syntactic construction with the motion verb *ibili*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.10**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia esaten ausartu zen.
- **Transliteration:** Egia esaten ausartu zen.
- **English translation:** He/she dared to tell the truth.
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction combining *egia esan* with the verb *ausartu* (to dare), used to frame truth-telling as an act of courage. Common in narrative and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.11**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia ezkutatu
- **Transliteration:** Egia ezkutatu
- **English translation:** To hide the truth, to conceal the truth
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *ezkutatu* (to hide, to conceal) as the main verb with *egia* as direct object. Used in moral, journalistic, and political discourse to describe the suppression of truth. Common across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.12**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia argitara atera
- **Transliteration:** Egia argitara atera
- **English translation:** To bring the truth to light
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *argitara atera* (to bring to light, literally "to bring out into the light") with *egia* as object. Used in journalistic and investigative contexts. The light metaphor is productive in Basque as in other languages.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.13**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia ukatu
- **Transliteration:** Egia ukatu
- **English translation:** To deny the truth
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *ukatu* (to deny, to refuse) with *egia* as direct object. Used in moral and political discourse. Distinct from *egiari uko egin* (documented in P2a D1.10), which uses the dative construction; *egia ukatu* uses the direct object construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.14**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia jakiteko eskubidea
- **Transliteration:** Egia jakiteko eskubidea
- **English translation:** The right to know the truth
- **Usage notes:** A nominal construction using the purposive verbal noun *jakiteko* (in order to know) and *eskubidea* (the right). Used in journalistic, legal, and political discourse to assert a right to truthful information. Common in media ethics and human rights discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.15**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** Egia eta justizia
- **Transliteration:** Egia eta justizia
- **English translation:** Truth and justice
- **Usage notes:** A fixed paired nominal construction used in political, legal, and transitional justice discourse. Common in the context of truth commissions and post-conflict reconciliation. Attested in Basque political and journalistic writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 2: egiazkoa

---

**C2.1**
- **Base Form:** egiazkoa
- **Example:** Hori egiazkoa da.
- **Transliteration:** Hori egiazkoa da.
- **English translation:** That is genuine / That is true.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction with *egiazkoa* as predicate adjective. Used to assert that something is genuine or truthful. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.2**
- **Base Form:** egiazkoa
- **Example:** Egiazkoa den ala ez
- **Transliteration:** Egiazkoa den ala ez
- **English translation:** Whether it is genuine or not
- **Usage notes:** A subordinate interrogative construction using the relative *den* and the disjunction *ala ez*. Used in evaluative and investigative contexts to frame the question of genuineness. Common in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.3**
- **Base Form:** egiazkoa
- **Example:** Egiazkoa dela ziurtatu
- **Transliteration:** Egiazkoa dela ziurtatu
- **English translation:** To confirm that it is genuine
- **Usage notes:** A complement clause construction with *egiazkoa dela* (that it is genuine) as the embedded clause complement of *ziurtatu* (to confirm, to certify). Used in legal and administrative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

No additional multi-word constructions specific to *egiazkoa* beyond those documented under *egia* (C1) and in P1b have been identified. The attributive uses (*egiazko* + noun) are documented in P1b and P2a and are not repeated here.

## BASE FORM 3: benetakoa

---

**C3.1**
- **Base Form:** benetakoa
- **Example:** Hori benetakoa da.
- **Transliteration:** Hori benetakoa da.
- **English translation:** That is genuine / That is the real thing.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting genuineness or authenticity. Parallel to C2.1 but with a stronger emphasis on authenticity as opposed to imitation. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C3.2**
- **Base Form:** benetakoa
- **Example:** Benetan ala itxuraz?
- **Transliteration:** Benetan ala itxuraz?
- **English translation:** Really or just apparently? / Genuinely or only in appearance?
- **Usage notes:** A binary interrogative construction contrasting the adverbial *benetan* (truly, genuinely) with *itxuraz* (apparently, seemingly). Used in everyday speech to question whether something is genuine or merely superficial. The construction pairs two adverbials from different P1 base forms.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C3.3**
- **Base Form:** benetakoa
- **Example:** Benetakoa dela frogatu
- **Transliteration:** Benetakoa dela frogatu
- **English translation:** To prove that it is genuine
- **Usage notes:** A complement clause construction parallel to C2.3, with *frogatu* (to prove) as the main verb. Used in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts to assert that genuineness has been demonstrated.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C3.4**
- **Base Form:** benetakoa
- **Example:** Benetako arazoa zein den
- **Transliteration:** Benetako arazoa zein den
- **English translation:** What the real problem is
- **Usage notes:** A nominal relative clause construction using *benetako* attributively and *zein den* (what it is) as the embedded interrogative. Used in analytical and argumentative discourse to identify the genuine underlying issue. Common in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 4: gezurra

---

**C4.1**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra esan
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra esan
- **English translation:** To tell a lie, to lie
- **Usage notes:** The standard verbal collocation for the act of lying, with *gezurra* as direct object and *esan* (to say) as the verb. Documented in P2a D4.7; noted here as the primary verbal construction for this base form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.2**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurretan ari da.
- **Transliteration:** Gezurretan ari da.
- **English translation:** He/she is lying. / He/she is engaged in lying.
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic progressive construction using *gezurretan* (in lies, adverbial) and the auxiliary *ari da* (is engaged in). Documented in P2a D4.6; noted here as the primary progressive construction for ongoing lying.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.3**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra da hori.
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra da hori.
- **English translation:** That is a lie. / That is false.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting that something is a lie or falsehood. Direct parallel to *egia da* (C1.2) and its primary antonym in predicative use.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.4**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra eta egia nahastu
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra eta egia nahastu
- **English translation:** To mix truth and lies, to confuse truth and falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *nahastu* (to mix, to confuse) with the paired nominals *gezurra eta egia* as object. Used in moral and epistemological discourse to describe the deliberate or careless blending of true and false elements.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.5**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra harrapatu
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra harrapatu
- **English translation:** To catch someone in a lie, to catch a lie
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *harrapatu* (to catch, to trap) with *gezurra* as object. Used in everyday speech to describe the detection of a lie. Common in spoken registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.6**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra sinestu
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra sinestu
- **English translation:** To believe a lie
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *sinestu* (to believe, variant of *sinetsi*) with *gezurra* as object. Used in everyday and moral discourse to describe the acceptance of a falsehood as true.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.7**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra zabaldu
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra zabaldu
- **English translation:** To spread a lie, to disseminate falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *zabaldu* (to spread, to disseminate) with *gezurra* as object. Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe the propagation of false information. Common in media and political commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.8**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra eta egia bereiztea
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra eta egia bereiztea
- **English translation:** Distinguishing truth from lies, separating truth and falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A nominal construction using *bereiztea* (the act of distinguishing, verbal noun of *bereizi*) with the paired object *gezurra eta egia*. Used in epistemological, journalistic, and educational discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.9**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra txuri
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra txuri
- **English translation:** White lie
- **Usage notes:** A nominal compound using *txuri* (white) as a modifier of *gezurra*, parallel to the English "white lie." Used in everyday moral discourse to describe a lie told to avoid harm or spare feelings. Attested in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C4.10**
- **Base Form:** gezurra
- **Example:** Gezurra bizi da.
- **Transliteration:** Gezurra bizi da.
- **English translation:** The lie lives on. / The lie persists.
- **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using *bizi da* (is alive, lives) with *gezurra* as subject. Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to describe a falsehood that continues to circulate or deceive. Attested in literary and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

- **Transliteration:** Faltsua dela frogatu

## BASE FORM 6: zuzena

---

**C6.1**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzena da hori.
- **Transliteration:** Zuzena da hori.
- **English translation:** That is correct. / That is right.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting correctness or rightness. Used across all registers in evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.2**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzenean emititu
- **Transliteration:** Zuzenean emititu
- **English translation:** To broadcast live
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using the adverbial *zuzenean* (directly, live) with *emititu* (to broadcast, from Romance). The standard expression for live broadcasting in Basque media. Documented in P2a D6.7; noted here as the primary verbal construction for this use.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.3**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzenean hitz egin
- **Transliteration:** Zuzenean hitz egin
- **English translation:** To speak directly, to speak face to face
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *zuzenean* (directly) with *hitz egin* (to speak). Used in everyday and professional contexts to describe direct, unmediated communication.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.4**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Bide zuzena hartu
- **Transliteration:** Bide zuzena hartu
- **English translation:** To take the right path, to take the straight road
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *hartu* (to take) with *bide zuzena* (the right/straight path). Used in both literal (navigation) and figurative (moral, life choices) senses. Common in everyday and literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.5**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzen joatea
- **Transliteration:** Zuzen joatea
- **English translation:** Going straight, going directly
- **Usage notes:** A verbal nominal construction using *zuzen* (straight/directly) as an adverbial modifier of *joatea* (the going, verbal noun of *joan*). Used in both literal and figurative senses. Common in spoken registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.6**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzena eta bidezkoa
- **Transliteration:** Zuzena eta bidezkoa
- **English translation:** Right and proper, just and fair
- **Usage notes:** A fixed paired adjectival construction using *eta* (and) to coordinate *zuzena* (correct/just) and *bidezkoa* (proper, fitting, from *bide* "way/path"). Used in legal and moral discourse to assert that something is both correct and appropriate.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.7**
- **Base Form:** zuzena
- **Example:** Zuzen erantzun
- **Transliteration:** Zuzen erantzun
- **English translation:** To answer correctly
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *zuzen* (correctly) as an adverbial modifier of *erantzun* (to answer). Used in educational and everyday contexts to evaluate the correctness of a response.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 7: okerra

---

**C7.1**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Okerra da hori.
- **Transliteration:** Okerra da hori.
- **English translation:** That is wrong. / That is incorrect.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting wrongness or incorrectness. Direct antonym of *zuzena da* (C6.1). Used across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.2**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Okerreko bidea hartu
- **Transliteration:** Okerreko bidea hartu
- **English translation:** To take the wrong path
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *hartu* (to take) with *okerreko bidea* (the wrong path). Used in both literal and figurative senses, parallel to *bide zuzena hartu* (C6.4). Common in everyday and literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.3**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Oker egon
- **Transliteration:** Oker egon
- **English translation:** To be wrong, to be mistaken
- **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using *oker* (wrong) as a predicate adjective with *egon* (to be, stative). Used in everyday speech to assert that a person is mistaken. Distinct from *okerra da* in that *oker egon* typically refers to a person being in a state of error rather than a proposition being incorrect.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.4**
- **Base Form:** okerra

- **Transliteration:** Oker ibili
- **English translation:** To be going about it wrongly, to be on the wrong track
- **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using *oker* with *ibili* (to go about, to behave). Used in everyday speech to describe someone who is proceeding incorrectly or is mistaken in their approach. Common in spoken registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.5**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Okerra zuzendu
- **Transliteration:** Okerra zuzendu
- **English translation:** To correct the error, to put right what is wrong
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *zuzendu* (to correct, to rectify) with *okerra* as object. Used in educational, administrative, and everyday contexts. The collocation directly pairs the two antonymous P1 base forms in a corrective construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.6**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Oker ulertu
- **Transliteration:** Oker ulertu
- **English translation:** To misunderstand, to understand wrongly
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *oker* (wrongly) as an adverbial modifier of *ulertu* (to understand). Used in everyday speech to describe misunderstanding. Common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.7**
- **Base Form:** okerra
- **Example:** Oker interpretatu
- **Transliteration:** Oker interpretatu
- **English translation:** To misinterpret
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *oker* (wrongly) as an adverbial modifier of *interpretatu* (to interpret, from Romance). Used in analytical, legal, and everyday contexts to describe incorrect interpretation.

## BASE FORM 8: zehatza

---

**C8.1**
- **Base Form:** zehatza
- **Example:** Zehatza da hori.
- **Transliteration:** Zehatza da hori.
- **English translation:** That is precise. / That is accurate.
- **Usage notes:** The basic predicative construction asserting precision or accuracy. Used in technical, scientific, and everyday evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C8.2**
- **Base Form:** zehatza
- **Example:** Zehatz-mehatz esan
- **Transliteration:** Zehatz-mehatz esan
- **English translation:** To say precisely, to state exactly
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using the reduplicated intensifier *zehatz-mehatz* (precisely, exactly, to the letter) with *esan* (to say). The reduplication *zehatz-mehatz* is a fixed form in Basque that intensifies the sense of exactness. Common in spoken and written registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C8.3**
- **Base Form:** zehatza
- **Example:** Zehatz-mehatz bete
- **Transliteration:** Zehatz-mehatz bete
- **English translation:** To fulfill exactly, to comply to the letter
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *zehatz-mehatz* with *bete* (to fulfill, to comply with). Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe exact compliance with a requirement or order.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C8.4**
- **Base Form:** zehatza
- **Example:** Zehazki esateko
- **Transliteration:** Zehazki esateko
- **English translation:** To be precise, to put it precisely, precisely speaking
- **Usage notes:** A framing construction using the adverbial *zehazki* (precisely) with the purposive verbal noun *esateko* (in order to say). Functions as a discourse marker signaling that a more precise formulation follows. Common in formal and technical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C8.5**
- **Base Form:** zehatza
- **Example:** Data zehatza eman
- **Transliteration:** Data zehatza eman
- **English translation:** To give the exact date
- **Usage notes:** A verbal collocation using *eman* (to give) with *data zehatza* (the exact date) as object. Representative of a productive pattern in which *zehatza* modifies a specific type of information (*data*, *kopurua*, *helbidea*, etc.) and *eman* requests or provides it. Common in administrative and everyday language.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
