# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms appear together, which are contrasted with each other, which share typical contexts (legal, religious, journalistic, everyday moral discourse, etc.), and which participate in similar constructions. No theoretical framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

Items that appear in multiple groups are listed under each relevant group and flagged in the overlap section. Items for which no clear grouping is supported by the data are listed at the end without a group assignment.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to evaluate whether a statement or claim corresponds to reality

**Label:** Factual correctness evaluation

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a statement, report, or belief accurately reflects what is actually the case. They appear in contexts of correction, verification, denial, and confirmation — in journalism, everyday conversation, legal testimony, and public communication. The central contrast in this group is between terms that affirm correspondence with reality and terms that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *benar* — used to affirm that a claim is correct; appears in confirmations, corrections, and denials (*tidak benar*, *benar adanya*, *memang benar demikian*)
- *kebenaran* — the nominalized form, used when truth is referred to as an object to be sought, upheld, concealed, or revealed (*mencari kebenaran*, *mengungkap kebenaran*, *menyembunyikan kebenaran*)
- *sebenarnya* — discourse marker introducing a correction or disclosure of what is actually the case
- *sebenar-benarnya* — emphatic form used in formal declarations and testimony
- *salah* — used to deny that a claim is correct; the primary negative counterpart to *benar* in factual contexts (*tidak benar* and *salah* are both used, but *salah* more strongly implies the claim is wrong rather than merely unconfirmed)
- *keliru* — used to indicate that a belief or judgment is based on error or misunderstanding, with less implication of moral fault than *salah*; appears in hedging constructions (*kalau tidak keliru*)
- *akurat* — used in journalistic and technical registers to evaluate data or reporting as free from error (*data yang akurat*, *informasi yang akurat dan terpercaya*)
- *tepat* — used to evaluate whether a description, measurement, or statement fits correctly (*tidak tepat*, *kurang tepat*)
- *nyata* — used to assert that something is factually the case and perceptible, not merely theoretical (*bukti nyata*, *kenyataan di lapangan*)
- *kenyataan* — used when reality is referred to as something to be faced, acknowledged, or contrasted with appearance or expectation (*menghadapi kenyataan*, *menjadi kenyataan*)

**Notes:** *benar* and *keliru* are frequently contrasted in educational and analytical contexts. *akurat* and *tepat* overlap substantially but *akurat* is more restricted to formal and technical registers. *nyata* shades into Group 3 (see below) when it emphasizes perceptibility rather than factual correctness.

## Group 2: Terms used in legal, administrative, and institutional validity contexts

**Label:** Formal and institutional validity

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether an act, document, status, or decision meets the formal requirements for recognition by a legal, administrative, or religious authority. They appear in legislation, court rulings, notarial language, electoral discourse, and family law. The central question in this group is not whether something is factually true but whether it is formally recognized and binding.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sah* — the primary term for legal and administrative validity; appears in fixed formulas (*sah dan mengikat*, *dinyatakan sah*, *pernikahan yang sah*, *tidak sah*, *belum sah*)
- *mengesahkan* — the standard verb for the act of formal ratification or authentication (*mengesahkan undang-undang*, *mengesahkan dokumen*)
- *disahkan* — passive form used in legislative and administrative reporting (*undang-undang disahkan*)
- *pengesahan* — the process nominal for ratification (*pengesahan konstitusi*, *pengesahan dokumen*)
- *keabsahan* — the abstract nominal for the quality of being valid or legitimate (*keabsahan pemilu*, *keabsahan pemerintahan*)
- *absah* — a more formal or literary variant of *sah*, used in elevated legal writing; generates *keabsahan*
- *sahih* — used in Islamic religious scholarship and academic methodology to indicate that something meets the required standards of soundness (*hadis sahih*, *uji kesahihan*)
- *kesahihan* — the nominal form of *sahih*, used in academic research methodology (*uji kesahihan*)
- *valid* — used in academic and scientific registers for the validity of instruments, data, or arguments (*data yang valid*, *validitas instrumen*)
- *validitas* — the borrowed nominal, restricted to academic and technical registers
- *anak sah* / *anak tidak sah* — fixed legal terms for legitimate and illegitimate children
- *sah secara hukum* / *sah secara agama* — constructions distinguishing legal from religious validity, reflecting the dual civil-religious structure of Indonesian family law

**Notes:** *sah*, *sahih*, and *valid* occupy distinct register positions within this group: *sah* is the general-purpose term across legal, administrative, and religious contexts; *sahih* is restricted to Islamic scholarship and academic methodology; *valid* is restricted to academic and scientific registers. *absah* is a formal variant of *sah* with limited independent distribution. The group as a whole is distinguished from Group 1 by the fact that validity here is institutional and procedural rather than a matter of correspondence with reality.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate whether something is genuine, original, or not a copy

**Label:** Genuineness and originality

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether an object, document, person, cultural product, or identity is the original rather than a copy, imitation, or fabrication. They appear in commercial, legal, archival, forensic, and cultural contexts. The central contrast is between the genuine article and something that replicates or substitutes for it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asli* — the primary term; used across all registers to indicate that something is not a copy, forgery, or imitation (*barang asli*, *naskah asli*, *foto asli*, *ijazah asli*, *dijamin asli*, *asli atau palsu?*)
- *keaslian* — the abstract nominal (*keaslian dokumen*, *memastikan keaslian*, *uji keaslian*, *sertifikat keaslian*, *meragukan keaslian*)
- *otentik* — a more formal, register-restricted near-synonym of *asli*, used in legal and academic contexts (*dokumen otentik*)
- *palsu* — the primary negative counterpart; used of objects, documents, and statements that are not genuine and typically involve deliberate deception (*uang palsu*, *berita palsu*, *akun palsu*, *tanda tangan palsu*, *ijazah palsu*)
- *memalsukan* / *dipalsukan* / *pemalsuan* / *pemalsu* — the derivational family of *palsu*, used in legal and journalistic contexts for the act and crime of forgery or falsification
- *tiruan* — used of objects that replicate an original without necessarily implying deceptive intent; contrasts with *palsu* in that imitation is not inherently fraudulent (*barang tiruan*, *kulit tiruan*)
- *meniru* / *peniru* / *peniruan* — the derivational family of *tiru*, used in artistic, legal, and everyday contexts for imitation
- *asli dan sah* — a fixed formula combining genuineness with legal validity, used in notarial language

**Notes:** The three-way contrast *asli* / *tiruan* / *palsu* is well established in Indonesian commercial and legal discourse: *asli* is the genuine original, *tiruan* is an imitation that may be openly labeled as such, and *palsu* is a fraudulent substitute. *otentik* overlaps with *asli* but is more restricted in register and distribution. This group overlaps with Group 2 (*sah*) in contexts where authenticity and legal validity are both required (e.g., *asli dan sah*).

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate the honesty or sincerity of a person or statement

**Label:** Honesty and sincerity in personal conduct

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a person's statements, actions, or motivations are free from deception, concealment, or hidden agenda. They appear in moral discourse, everyday conversation, religious instruction, and character evaluation. The focus is on the person or their inner state rather than on the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**

- *jujur* — the primary term for honesty in personal conduct; used to evaluate persons and statements as free from deception (*orang yang jujur*, *bicara jujur*, *jujur saja*, *tidak jujur*, *terus terang dan jujur*)
- *kejujuran* — the abstract nominal (*kejujuran adalah nilai utama*)
- *bohong* — the primary term for lying; used of persons and statements that deliberately misrepresent reality (*berbohong*, *pembohong*, *kebohongan*, *membohongi*, *jangan bohong*, *ketahuan bohong*, *bohong besar*, *bohong demi kebaikan*)
- *dusta* — a near-synonym of *bohong*, more formal and literary in register, common in religious discourse (*berdusta*, *pendusta*, *dusta belaka*, *tidak berdusta*)
- *tulus* — used to evaluate inner motivation as free from hidden agenda or pretense (*dari hati yang tulus*, *dengan tulus*, *niat yang tulus*, *ketulusan*)
- *ikhlas* — used to evaluate actions as performed without expectation of reward or recognition, particularly in religious contexts; carries a stronger connotation of selfless acceptance than *tulus* (*dengan ikhlas*, *ikhlas menerima*, *tulus ikhlas*, *keikhlasan*, *mengikhlaskan*)
- *sungguh* — used to assert the sincerity or genuineness of a claim or commitment (*sungguh-sungguh*, *bersungguh-sungguh*, *kesungguhan*, *dengan sungguh-sungguh*)

**Notes:** *bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms but occupy different register positions: *bohong* is the everyday term, *dusta* is formal and literary. *tulus* and *ikhlas* are frequently paired (*tulus ikhlas*) and overlap substantially, but *ikhlas* carries a specifically religious connotation of selfless acceptance that *tulus* does not. *sungguh* is primarily an intensifier of sincerity and commitment rather than a term for honesty per se; it shades into Group 1 when used to assert the reality of a claim rather than the sincerity of a person.

## Group 5: Terms used in contexts of deliberate falsification or distortion

**Label:** Deliberate falsification and distortion

**Definition:** These terms are used specifically in contexts where information, data, images, or documents have been intentionally altered, misrepresented, or fabricated. They appear in journalistic, legal, and forensic discourse and carry a strong connotation of intentional deception rather than inadvertent error.

**Representative expressions:**

- *palsu* / *memalsukan* / *pemalsuan* / *pemalsu* — see Group 3; included here because the deliberate deception component is central to *palsu* in a way it is not for *tiruan*
- *manipulasi* / *memanipulasi* / *dimanipulasi* — used for deliberate distortion of data, images, or facts (*memanipulasi data*, *foto yang dimanipulasi*)
- *distorsi* / *mendistorsi* — used in formal and journalistic registers for the misrepresentation or skewing of information; more restricted in register than *manipulasi*
- *menyimpang* / *penyimpangan* — used for conduct, statements, or interpretations that deviate from a correct or standard norm; appears in moral, legal, and doctrinal contexts (*penyimpangan data*, *menyimpang dari kebenaran*)
- *kebohongan* — used in political and journalistic discourse for public lies or deliberate falsehoods (*kebohongan publik*)
- *berita palsu* — the fixed phrase for fake news, appearing in media literacy and legal discourse
- *akun palsu* — the fixed phrase for fraudulent online accounts

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (factual incorrectness) by the element of deliberate intent. *keliru* and *salah* (Group 1) do not imply deliberate deception; *palsu*, *manipulasi*, and *distorsi* do. *menyimpang* occupies a middle position: it can describe inadvertent deviation but is also used for deliberate distortion. *distorsi* is more restricted in register than the other terms in this group.

## Group 6: Terms used in Islamic religious and moral discourse

**Label:** Terms with primary or strong Islamic religious usage

**Definition:** These terms appear with particular frequency or with specialized meanings in Islamic religious contexts — including hadith scholarship, Quranic interpretation, Islamic ethics, and religious practice. While some also appear in secular contexts, their usage in Islamic discourse is either primary or carries distinct connotations not present in secular usage.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahih* — the technical term in hadith criticism for a sound, authentic hadith (*hadis sahih*); the collections *Sahih Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim* are referenced by this term
- *sah* — used for the validity of religious acts (prayer, marriage, fasting) according to Islamic jurisprudence (*sah secara agama*, *pernikahan yang sah*)
- *ikhlas* — from Arabic *ikhlāṣ*; in Islamic discourse specifically denotes performing acts solely for God without expectation of worldly reward; the connotation of selfless religious sincerity is stronger than in secular usage
- *dusta* — from Arabic; more common than *bohong* in Islamic moral and religious texts; appears in hadith and Quranic commentary
- *hakiki* — from Arabic *ḥaqīqī*; used in Islamic philosophical and Sufi discourse to refer to the essential or divine reality underlying appearances (*kebenaran hakiki*)
- *hakikat* — from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*; used in Islamic philosophy and Sufism to refer to the essential truth or inner reality of things (*hakikat manusia*, *pada hakikatnya*, *mencari hakikat*)
- *niat yang benar* — the collocation evaluating whether an action is motivated by correct religious intention (*niat* from Arabic *niyya*)
- *menegakkan kebenaran* — used in Islamic moral discourse for upholding truth against falsehood
- *tulus ikhlas* — the paired formula common in Islamic expressions of gratitude and moral evaluation

**Notes:** This group is defined by context of use rather than by etymology alone. Not all Arabic loanwords in the P1 inventory belong here (e.g., *akurat* from Dutch, *manipulasi* from Dutch, *ijazah* which appears in secular administrative contexts). The terms in this group are those for which Islamic religious usage is either primary or carries distinct semantic weight. Several terms in this group also appear in secular philosophical or formal literary contexts.

---

## Group 7: Terms used in formal and elevated registers with restricted everyday distribution

**Label:** Formal, literary, or technical register restriction

**Definition:** These terms are used in formal writing, legal documents, academic texts, literary prose, or religious scholarship, but are not common in everyday conversational Indonesian. Their meanings are largely covered by more common terms in informal registers, and their distinctiveness lies primarily in register rather than in semantic content.

**Representative expressions:**

- *absah* — formal/literary variant of *sah*; generates *keabsahan* but does not independently generate the verbal and process nominal forms that *sah* does
- *dusta* / *berdusta* / *pendusta* — formal/literary/religious variants of *bohong* / *berbohong* / *pembohong*
- *hakiki* — restricted to philosophical, religious, and literary registers; not used in everyday conversation
- *hakikat* — restricted to formal, philosophical, and religious registers; *pada hakikatnya* functions as a formal discourse marker parallel to *sebenarnya*
- *otentik* — formal variant of *asli*; restricted to legal and academic registers; does not generate the productive collocation network that *asli* does
- *valid* / *validitas* — restricted to academic and scientific registers; in most everyday contexts replaced by *sah* or *sahih*
- *distorsi* — restricted to formal and journalistic registers; in everyday contexts replaced by *menyimpang* or *manipulasi*
- *kredibel* / *kredibilitas* — restricted to formal, journalistic, and political registers

**Notes:** This group is defined by register distribution rather than by semantic content. The terms here are not semantically marginal — they have clear and stable meanings — but their distribution is narrower than their semantic equivalents. Several items in this group are loanwords (*otentik*, *valid*, *distorsi*, *kredibel*) whose register restriction reflects their status as borrowings used primarily in formal or technical writing.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate purity or freedom from contamination, mixture, or corruption

**Label:** Purity and freedom from adulteration

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a substance, motive, doctrine, or quality is free from contamination, mixture, or corruption. They appear in scientific, religious, commercial, and moral contexts. The relevant contrast is between something in its pure or original state and something that has been mixed, diluted, or corrupted.

**Representative expressions:**

- *murni* — the primary term; used of substances (*emas murni*, *air murni*), motives (*motivasi yang murni*), science (*ilmu murni*), and doctrines (*memurnikan ajaran*); derivations include *kemurnian*, *memurnikan*, *pemurnian*
- *asli* — overlaps with this group when used to indicate that something has not been altered or adulterated (*warna asli*, *rasa asli*, *bahan asli*); the overlap is partial — *asli* emphasizes originality and non-derivedness, while *murni* emphasizes freedom from contamination
- *ikhlas* — overlaps with this group in the sense of motivation free from self-interest or hidden agenda; the Arabic root *ikhlāṣ* carries the sense of purification

**Notes:** This group is smaller than others and overlaps substantially with Group 3 (*asli*) and Group 4 (*ikhlas*). The core member is *murni*; the other items are included because they participate in purity-related collocations but their primary groupings are elsewhere.

## Overlapping and Ambiguous Items

The following items appear in multiple groups or present classification difficulties:

**benar** — primarily Group 1 (factual correctness), but also participates in Group 4 contexts (*niat yang benar*, *jalan yang benar*) where it evaluates moral or religious correctness rather than propositional truth, and in Group 6 (*menegakkan kebenaran*). The root is so productive that it appears across multiple domains.

**salah** — primarily Group 1 (factual incorrectness), but also participates in Group 5 contexts when used of deliberate misrepresentation, and in everyday moral evaluation (Group 4) when used to assign fault (*bukan salah saya*, *menyalahkan*). The fixed constructions *salah paham*, *salah kaprah*, *salah sasaran* represent extensions beyond the truth domain.

**nyata** — primarily Group 1 (factual correspondence with reality), but also participates in Group 3 contexts when used to assert that something is real rather than fabricated (*bukti nyata*). The distinction between "factually the case" and "genuinely existing" is not always sharp in usage.

**palsu** — primarily Group 3 (genuineness/originality), but also Group 5 (deliberate falsification) because *palsu* implies intentional deception in a way that *tiruan* does not. The term appears in both the commercial authenticity domain and the legal falsification domain.

**sah** — primarily Group 2 (institutional validity), but also Group 6 (Islamic religious usage) when applied to the validity of religious acts. The term bridges civil and religious validity in Indonesian family law contexts.

**ikhlas** — primarily Group 4 (honesty and sincerity), but also Group 6 (Islamic religious usage) and Group 8 (purity). The term's Arabic etymology and its primary context in Islamic moral discourse make it a member of multiple groups.

**asli** — primarily Group 3 (genuineness/originality), but also Group 8 (purity) when used of unaltered substances or flavors, and in identity and territorial contexts (*penduduk asli*, *bahasa asli*, *tanah asli*) that are not straightforwardly about authenticity of objects.

**sungguh** — primarily Group 4 (sincerity), but also Group 1 when used as an intensifier asserting the reality of a claim rather than the sincerity of a person (*sungguh luar biasa* is a degree intensifier with no clear truth-evaluative content).

**hakikat** / **hakiki** — primarily Group 6 (Islamic religious discourse) and Group 7 (formal register), but *pada hakikatnya* functions as a discourse marker parallel to *sebenarnya* (Group 1), introducing statements about what is essentially the case.

## Items Without a Clear Group Assignment

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above, or their usage patterns as documented do not provide sufficient basis for grouping:

**cermat** — used to evaluate careful, meticulous attention to detail (*kecermatan*, *mencermati*). This is an evaluative term for a quality of conduct (carefulness, precision) rather than for truth, validity, genuineness, or honesty. It appears in the P1 inventory because it participates in accuracy-related discourse, but its usage pattern is distinct from the other terms: it evaluates the process of observation or work rather than the truth-value of a claim or the genuineness of an object. No clear grouping with the other items is supported by the data.

**kredibel** / **kredibilitas** — used to evaluate persons, institutions, or sources as worthy of belief or trust. This term evaluates the reliability of a source rather than the truth of a claim or the validity of a document. It is related to but distinct from the other groups; its usage is restricted to formal, journalistic, and political registers (Group 7), but its semantic content does not align closely enough with any single group to warrant placement there.

**salah kaprah** — a fixed idiomatic construction meaning a widespread misconception that has become accepted as correct. This construction occupies a distinctive position: it describes a state where something *salah* (wrong) has become *kaprah* (common/accepted), which is a specific social-epistemic phenomenon not captured by the other groupings.

**tampil apa adanya** — the fixed construction meaning "to present oneself as one truly is." This expression covers the personal authenticity meaning that in English might be expressed by "being authentic," but it does not use *asli* or any other P1 item directly. It is documented in P2b as the standard periphrastic expression for this meaning. Its relationship to the other items in the inventory is one of semantic proximity rather than shared usage patterns.
