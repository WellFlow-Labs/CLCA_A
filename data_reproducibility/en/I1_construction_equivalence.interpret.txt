## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### REVELATION → BECOME Substitution
English lacks a dedicated REVELATION construction distinct from BECOME. The semantic domain of "discovery of pre-existing truth-value" is expressed through:
- **BECOME verbs with evidential emphasis**: "turn out to be," "prove (to be)"
  - Example: "The story turned out to be false."
  - These emphasize discovery rather than change, but use the same syntactic frame as BECOME
- **VERIFICATION constructions**: Phrasal verbs like "hold up," "check out," "pan out"
  - Example: "His alibi didn't hold up."
  - These incorporate both verification and revelation semantics

The language systematically uses BECOME-frame verbs ("turn out," "prove") to express what other languages might encode as REVELATION. The distinction is lexical (verb choice) rather than constructional.

### VERIFICATION ≈ DISCONFIRMATION Symmetry
English uses the **same resultative construction** for both verification and disconfirmation:
- Verification: "They proved the theory correct."
- Disconfirmation: "They proved the theory incorrect/false."

The construction (Verb + Object + truth-domain adjective) is neutral with respect to positive/negative outcome. The truth-domain adjective itself carries the polarity. No structural asymmetry exists between confirming and disconfirming.

### CAUSATION Asymmetry: Factual vs. Character
English shows a clear **structural gap** for CAUSATION (character):
- **CAUSATION (factual)** is fully productive: "The evidence made his claim true."
- **CAUSATION (character)** has no common expression and requires circumlocution:
  - *"The experience made him a liar" (awkward, requires context)
  - *"The experience caused him to become a liar" (periphrastic)
  - No single construction paralleling "make + object + adjective" exists for agentive properties

This represents a **conceptual gap**: English resists direct causation of habitual truth-domain character traits, preferring to express such changes through BECOME constructions applied to agent nouns.

### NEGATION: Analytic vs. Synthetic Competition
English provides **two parallel strategies** for negation:
- **Analytic**: "not + positive adjective" (fully productive)
- **Synthetic**: "negative prefix + root" (lexically restricted)

The choice is **lexically determined**:
- Some adjectives permit both: "not true" / "untrue"
- Some strongly prefer analytic: "not genuine" >> "ingenuine" (rare/non-standard)
- Some strongly prefer synthetic: "invalid" >> "not valid" (less common)

No systematic semantic or pragmatic distinction governs the choice; it is a matter of lexical convention and register (synthetic forms often more formal).

### Phrasal Verbs as VERIFICATION/REVELATION Equivalents
English employs **idiomatic phrasal verbs** extensively in the truth domain:
- "hold up" (withstand scrutiny)
- "check out" (be verified)
- "bear out" (confirm)
- "pan out" (prove true)
- "turn out" (be revealed as)

These are **more colloquial** than single-word equivalents and often conflate VERIFICATION and REVELATION semantics. They represent a productive subsystem within English truth-domain expression, particularly in spoken registers.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps

1. **REVELATION (dedicated construction)**
   - **Status**: Missing as distinct construction
   - **Substitution**: BECOME verbs with evidential semantics ("turn out to be," "prove") or VERIFICATION phrasal verbs
   - **Note**: The language does not grammatically distinguish "become true" (change of state) from "turn out to be true" (discovery of pre-existing state) at the constructional level

2. **CAUSATION (character)**
   - **Status**: No common expression; circumlocution required
   - **Substitution**: Must use periphrastic constructions ("cause X to become a liar," "make X into a truth-teller")
   - **Note**: Direct causative construction (make/render + object + adjective) does not extend to agentive nominalizations in the truth domain

### Lexical Gaps

1. **Negative prefixes**
   - **Status**: Not all truth-domain adjectives permit negative prefixation
   - **Examples**: "genuine" → *"ingenuine" (non-standard); "reliable" → "unreliable" (standard)
   - **Substitution**: Analytic negation ("not + adjective") fills gaps

2. **Agentive nominalizations**
   - **Status**: Limited productivity; many concepts lack dedicated agent nouns
   - **Examples**: "liar" exists, but no common single word for "one who is always right" or "one who is always accurate"
   - **Substitution**: Compound forms ("truth-teller," "wrongdoer") or periphrasis ("person who tells the truth")

3. **Nominalization suffixes**
   - **Status**: Choice of suffix (-ness, -ity, -cy, -th) is lexically determined and unpredictable
   - **Examples**: "true" → "truth" (not *"trueness" in most contexts); "accurate" → "accuracy" (not *"accurateness")
   - **Note**: This is not a gap per se, but reflects lexical idiosyncrasy rather than productive morphology

### Conceptual Gaps

1. **Direct causation of habitual character traits**
   - **Status**: Blocked operation
   - **Evidence**: No productive construction for "make X (into) a liar/truth-teller" parallel to "make X true/false"
   - **Interpretation**: English conceptually separates factual truth-value (states) from character-based truth-disposition (traits), blocking direct causative application to the latter

### Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Synthetic comparison**
   - **Status**: Rare with truth-domain adjectives
   - **Evidence**: "truer," "falser" are attested but uncommon; most truth-domain adjectives use analytic comparison
   - **Reason**: Core truth-domain adjectives ("true," "false") treated as semantically absolute, resisting gradation
   - **Note**: This is a **semantic restriction** rather than a structural gap; the construction exists but is pragmatically odd with certain adjectives

2. **"Come true" restriction**
   - **Status**: Domain-restricted BECOME construction
   - **Evidence**: Limited to predictions, wishes, dreams; cannot extend to other truth-domain contexts
   - **Example**: "Her prediction came true" (grammatical) vs. *"The rumor came true" (odd; prefer "became true" or "turned out to be true")
   - **Note**: This represents a **lexical-constructional idiom** with restricted distribution

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available

1. **ATTR** (Attribution)
   - Construction: Subject + BE + truth-domain adjective
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive, no restrictions

2. **ADNOM** (Adnominal modification)
   - Construction: Truth-domain adjective + Noun
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive, no restrictions

3. **BECOME** (Inchoative)
   - Construction: Subject + become/turn/prove + truth-domain adjective
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive with standard change-of-state verbs

4. **CAUSATION (factual)**
   - Construction: Agent + make/render + Object + truth-domain adjective
   - Construction: Lexical causatives (falsify, validate, authenticate)
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive in both analytic and lexical forms

5. **VERIFICATION**
   - Construction: Verb + Object + truth-domain adjective (resultative)
   - Construction: Phrasal verbs (hold up, check out, bear out, pan out)
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive

6. **DISCONFIRMATION**
   - Construction: Same as VERIFICATION (Verb + Object + negative truth-domain adjective)
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive, shares construction with VERIFICATION

7. **NEGATION (analytic)**
   - Construction: Subject + BE + NEG + truth-domain adjective
   - Status: [CORE], fully productive without restrictions

8. **NOMINALIZATION (abstract)**
   - Construction: truth-domain adjective + -ness/-ity/-cy/-th
   - Construction: Bare noun forms (truth, error, lie)
   - Status: [CORE], productive (though suffix choice lexically determined)

### Available with Substitution

9. **REVELATION**
   - **Substituted by**: BECOME (with evidential verbs: "turn out to be," "prove") or VERIFICATION (phrasal verbs)
   - Status: No dedicated construction; meaning expressed through other operators
   - Note: Lexical choice (verb selection) rather than constructional distinction

10. **NEGATION (synthetic)**
    - Construction: Subject + BE + negative-prefixed adjective
    - Status: [CORE] but **lexically restricted**; analytic negation substitutes where synthetic unavailable
    - Note: Availability determined by lexical convention, not productive morphology

### Partially Available

11. **BECOME (specialized: "come true")**
    - Construction: Subject + come true
    - Status: [CORE] within restricted domain (predictions, wishes, dreams); [MARGINAL] or blocked elsewhere
    - Note: Lexical-constructional idiom with limited distribution

12. **NOMINALIZATION (agentive)**
    - Construction: truth-domain root + -er/-or (e.g., "liar")
    - Construction: Compound forms (e.g., "truth-teller")
    - Status: [CORE] for established forms like "liar"; [MARGINAL] for compounds
    - Note: Limited productivity; many concepts lack dedicated agent nouns

### Missing Entirely

13. **CAUSATION (character)**
    - Status: **MISSING (structural gap)**
    - Substitution: Circumlocution required ("cause X to become a liar," "make X into a truth-teller")
    - Note: Conceptual blocking; English resists direct causative application to habitual character traits in truth domain

---

## Summary Statement

English provides **robust constructional support** for most CLCA operators in the truth domain, with the following key patterns:

- **Core operators** (ATTR, ADNOM, BECOME, CAUSATION-factual, VERIFICATION, DISCONFIRMATION, NEGATION-analytic, NOMINALIZATION-abstract) are fully productive and unrestricted.

- **REVELATION** is systematically **substituted by BECOME** (through evidential verb choice) or by VERIFICATION constructions (phrasal verbs). No dedicated constructional distinction exists.

- **CAUSATION (character)** represents a **structural and conceptual gap**: the language lacks a common expression for directly causing someone to acquire habitual truth-domain character traits.

- **NEGATION** operates through **dual strategies** (analytic and synthetic), with lexically determined distribution rather than semantic or pragmatic conditioning.

- **Partial availability** characterizes specialized constructions ("come true") and agentive nominalizations, where productivity is restricted by domain or lexical convention.

The language shows **no systematic asymmetries** between positive and negative polarity (VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share constructions; NEGATION is fully productive analytically). The primary gaps are **REVELATION** (substituted) and **CAUSATION (character)** (missing).
