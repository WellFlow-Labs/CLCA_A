# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Methodological note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, domain restriction, and collocational behavior documented in P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns do not support a clean grouping, this is noted explicitly. Items are cited by their transliteration forms; P1 item numbers are given for reference.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to evaluate statements and claims as true or correct

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate statements and claims as true or correct

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a proposition, answer, or assertion is being characterized as accurate or correct. They are used predicatively or attributively to endorse the truth-value of what someone has said or written. They contrast with each other primarily along dimensions of register and degree of precision rather than meaning.

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that martali (#3), sc̣ori (#18), and zusti (#17) all collocate with p'asuxi ("answer") and in'p'ormacia ("information"), and all appear in constructions where a speaker confirms or endorses a claim. The adverbial forms martla, martlac, zustаd, and sc̣orad function as discourse-level confirmations in spoken Georgian. The predicative constructions martali xar, martali var, zustаd asea, and sc̣oria all serve the same interactional function of confirming correctness.

**Representative expressions:**
- მართალი / martali (#3) — "you are right," "that is correct"
- სწორი / sc̣ori (#18) — "correct," especially for factual answers
- ზუსტი / zusti (#17) — "exact," "precise," with emphasis on precision
- მართალია / martalia (P1b) — discourse particle confirming truth
- მართლა / martla (P1b) — adverb asserting truth of a claim
- მართლაც / martlac (P1b) — emphatic confirmation
- ზუსტად ასეა / zustаd asea (P2b) — "that is exactly right"
- სწორია თუ არა / sc̣oria tu ara (P2b) — "is it correct or not"
- სწორად გავიგე / sc̣orad gavige (P2b) — "I understood correctly"
- მართალი ხარ / martali xar (P2b) — "you are right"
- მართალია, მაგრამ... / martalia, magram... (P2b) — concessive acknowledgment of truth

**Notes on internal distinctions:** zusti and sc̣ori both appear with p'asuxi but zusti emphasizes precision of correspondence (e.g., to a measurement or translation), while sc̣ori emphasizes correctness of an answer or decision. martali appears more freely in predicative constructions about persons ("you are right") and carries a broader range including moral uprightness (see Group 3). The adverbial forms martla and martlac function as discourse particles in spoken Georgian in ways that zustad and sc̣orad do not.

## Group 2: Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings to refer to evidence and proof

**Label:** Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings to refer to evidence and proof

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in legal procedural contexts — court proceedings, criminal investigations, administrative decisions — to refer to the material or acts by which claims are established or refuted. They are used in fixed legal collocations and do not typically appear in everyday conversational speech.

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that mt'k'icebuleba (#10) and mt'k'iceba (#9) appear in a dense cluster of legal collocations: mt'k'icebis t'virti ("burden of proof"), mt'k'icebis st'andart'i ("standard of proof"), mt'k'icebulebata šep'aseba ("evaluation of evidence"), mt'k'icebulebata dasašveboba ("admissibility of evidence"), mt'k'icebulebata c̣ardgena ("submission of evidence"). These forms do not appear in the everyday constructions documented for martali, simartle, or namdvili. dadastureba (#20) and gadamomc̣meba (#21) also appear in legal and journalistic verification contexts, though gadamomc̣meba extends into journalism more prominently.

**Representative expressions:**
- მტკიცებულება / mt'k'icebuleba (#10) — "evidence, proof" (legal)
- მტკიცება / mt'k'iceba (#9) — "assertion, act of proving" (legal/philosophical)
- მტკიცების ტვირთი / mt'k'icebis t'virti (P2b) — "burden of proof"
- მტკიცების სტანდარტი / mt'k'icebis st'andart'i (P2b) — "standard of proof"
- მტკიცებულებათა შეფასება / mt'k'icebulebata šep'aseba (P2b) — "evaluation of evidence"
- მტკიცებულებათა დასაშვებობა / mt'k'icebulebata dasašveboba (P2b) — "admissibility of evidence"
- მტკიცებულებათა წარდგენა / mt'k'icebulebata c̣ardgena (P2b) — "submission of evidence"
- დამტკიცებული / damt'k'icebuli (P2a) — "proven, ratified"
- დადასტურება / dadastureba (#20) — "confirmation, verification"
- დადასტურებული / dadasturebuli (P2a) — "confirmed, verified"
- დაუდასტურებელი / daudasturebeli (P2a) — "unconfirmed"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** mt'k'icebuleba refers to the material of proof (evidence as a thing), while mt'k'iceba refers to the act of asserting or proving. dadastureba appears in both legal and journalistic contexts (see also Group 6), making it a candidate for multiple groups. damt'k'iceba (P2a) appears in both legal ratification contexts and argumentative proof contexts, giving it a broader range than mt'k'icebuleba.

## Group 3: Expressions used to characterize persons as honest, upright, or trustworthy

**Label:** Expressions used to characterize persons as honest, upright, or trustworthy

**Definition:** These expressions are applied to persons — rather than to statements or documents — to evaluate their character as honest, reliable, or morally upright. They appear in moral evaluation, literary characterization, and interpersonal assessment. They contrast with expressions that evaluate statements or objects rather than persons.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1 and P2b data show that martali (#3), sarcmuno (#14), and sando (#15) all collocate with person-denoting nouns: martali k'aci ("an honest man"), sarcmuno mc̣me ("a credible witness"), sando adamiani ("a trustworthy person"). mt'q̇uari (#13) is the negative counterpart in this group, applied to persons who lie. The constructions martali var / martali xar (P2b) also apply to persons asserting or being credited with correctness. sarcmuno and sando are distinguished from each other by register (sarcmuno more formal and legal, sando more colloquial) but both apply to persons and sources.

**Representative expressions:**
- მართალი კაცი / martali k'aci (P1b) — "an honest man, a righteous person"
- სარწმუნო მოწმე / sarcmuno mc̣me (P2b) — "a credible witness"
- სანდო ადამიანი / sando adamiani (P2b) — "a trustworthy person"
- სანდო პარტნიორი / sando p'art'niori (P2b) — "a reliable partner"
- მტყუარი / mt'q̇uari (#13) — "liar, untruthful person"
- მტყუარი მოწმე / mt'q̇uari mc̣me (P2b) — "a lying witness"
- მტყუარი აღმოჩნდა / mt'q̇uari aɣmočnda (P2b) — "turned out to be a liar"
- მტყუარობა / mt'q̇uaroba (P2a) — "habitual untruthfulness"
- ჭეშმარიტი მეგობარი / č̣ešmariti megobari (P1b) — "a true friend"
- ჭეშმარიტი ადამიანი / č̣ešmariti adamiani (P1b) — "a genuine/truly human person"
- მართალი გითხრა / martali gitxra (P2b) — discourse marker of personal sincerity
- სიმართლეს ვამბობ / simartles vambob (P2b) — "I am telling the truth"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** martali applied to persons carries a moral-uprightness dimension not present in sc̣ori or zusti. č̣ešmariti applied to persons (č̣ešmariti megobari, č̣ešmariti adamiani) appears in more elevated, literary, or philosophical registers than martali k'aci, which is common in everyday speech. sarcmuno mc̣me is a fixed legal phrase, while sando adamiani is colloquial. mt'q̇uari is the only agentive form in this group and is the primary term for characterizing a person as a liar.

## Group 4: Expressions used to characterize documents, objects, and transactions as forged or fraudulent

**Label:** Expressions used to characterize documents, objects, and transactions as forged or fraudulent

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal, commercial, and administrative contexts to characterize material things — documents, currency, signatures, transactions, companies — as falsified, counterfeit, or fraudulently constructed. They are strongly associated with deliberate human action (forgery, fabrication) rather than with mere error or inaccuracy.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1b and P2b data show that q̇albi (#7) combines productively with a large set of document- and object-denoting nouns: q̇albi dok'ument'i, q̇albi p'uli, q̇albi bank'not'i, q̇albi xelmc̣era, q̇albi mc̣moba, q̇albi angarishi. p'ik't'iuri (#23) appears in a parallel set of legal collocations: p'ik't'iuri garigeba, p'ik't'iuri k'omp'ania, p'ik't'iuri qorcineba, p'ik't'iuri dasaqmeba, p'ik't'iuri misamarti. gaq̇albeba (P2a) is the standard verbal noun for the act of falsification. These expressions do not appear in the everyday moral or interpersonal contexts where sicrue or mt'q̇uari appear.

**Representative expressions:**
- ყალბი / q̇albi (#7) — "false, fake, counterfeit, forged"
- ყალბი დოკუმენტი / q̇albi dok'ument'i (P1b) — "forged document"
- ყალბი ფული / q̇albi p'uli (P1b) — "counterfeit money"
- ყალბი ხელმოწერა / q̇albi xelmc̣era (P1b) — "forged signature"
- ყალბი მოწმობა / q̇albi mc̣moba (P1b) — "forged certificate"
- გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba (P2a) — "falsification, forgery, counterfeiting"
- გაყალბებული / gaq̇albebuli (P2a) — "falsified, forged"
- გამაყალბებელი / gamaq̇albebeli (P2a) — "forger, falsifier"
- ყალბობა / q̇alboba (#8) — "forgery, fraud, falsification"
- ყალბობის ფაქტი / q̇albobis p'aqt'i (P1b) — "the fact of forgery"
- ფიქტიური გარიგება / p'ik't'iuri garigeba (P2b) — "fictitious transaction"
- ფიქტიური კომპანია / p'ik't'iuri k'omp'ania (P2b) — "shell company"
- ფიქტიური ქორწინება / p'ik't'iuri qorcineba (P2b) — "sham marriage"
- სიყალბის დადასტურება / siq̇albis dadastureba (#19) — "confirmation of falsity"
- სიყალბის დამადასტურებელი / siq̇albis damadasturebeli (#30) — "evidence of falsity"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** q̇albi is the native root and applies broadly across documents, currency, persons, and statements. p'ik't'iuri is a borrowing with more restricted use, appearing primarily in commercial and administrative law contexts (transactions, companies, marriages, employment, addresses) rather than in the physical forgery contexts where q̇albi is most at home. The two overlap in legal discourse but are not interchangeable: p'ik't'iuri garigeba is the standard phrase for a sham transaction, while q̇albi dok'ument'i is the standard phrase for a forged document.

## Group 5: Expressions used to assert that something is the genuine or real instance of its kind

**Label:** Expressions used to assert that something is the genuine or real instance of its kind

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that a person, object, or quality is the authentic or real instance of what it is claimed to be, as opposed to an imitation, copy, or pretense. They appear across commercial, aesthetic, everyday, and formal contexts. They do not primarily predicate truth of propositions but rather assert the genuine character of things.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1 and P2b data show that namdvili (#5) and avt'enturi (#24) both appear in contexts asserting genuine character: namdvili oqro ("real gold"), namdvili saxeli ("real name"), avt'enturi t'ekst'i ("authentic text"), avt'enturi asli ("authentic copy"). namdviloba (#6) and avt'enturoba (#25) are the corresponding nominals used in legal and archival authentication proceedings. The adverb namdvilad (P2a) functions as an everyday intensifier asserting genuine reality. These expressions contrast with q̇albi and p'ik't'iuri (Group 4) as their opposites.

**Representative expressions:**
- ნამდვილი / namdvili (#5) — "real, genuine, authentic, actual"
- ნამდვილი ოქრო / namdvili oqro (P2b) — "real gold"
- ნამდვილი სახელი / namdvili saxeli (P2b) — "real name"
- ნამდვილი მიზეზი / namdvili mizezi (P2b) — "the real reason"
- ნამდვილი ამბავი / namdvili ambavi (P2b) — "the real story"
- ნამდვილად / namdvilad (P2a) — "really, genuinely, actually"
- ნამდვილობა / namdviloba (#6) — "authenticity, genuineness"
- ნამდვილობის დადასტურება / namdvilobis dadastureba (P2b) — "confirmation of authenticity"
- ნამდვილობის სერტიფიკატი / namdvilobis sert'ip'ik'at'i (P2b) — "certificate of authenticity"
- ავთენტური / avt'enturi (#24) — "authentic, genuine" (formal/legal)
- ავთენტური ტექსტი / avt'enturi t'ekst'i (P2b) — "authentic text"
- ავთენტური ასლი / avt'enturi asli (P2b) — "authentic copy"
- ავთენტურობა / avt'enturoba (#25) — "authenticity"
- ავთენტურობის დადასტურება / avt'enturobis dadastureba (P2b) — "confirmation of authenticity"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** namdvili is the everyday and colloquial form, appearing freely in spoken Georgian across all domains. avt'enturi is a formal borrowing with restricted use in legal, archival, and cultural contexts. The two overlap in legal authentication contexts (both namdviloba and avt'enturoba appear in authentication proceedings) but namdvili is far more frequent in everyday speech. namdvilad functions as a high-frequency spoken adverb ("really," "actually") in ways that avt'enturad does not.

## Group 6: Expressions used in journalistic and investigative contexts to refer to verification

**Label:** Expressions used in journalistic and investigative contexts to refer to verification

**Definition:** These expressions appear in journalistic, scientific, and investigative contexts to refer to the process of checking whether information is accurate or confirmed. They emphasize the procedural act of checking rather than the outcome of a legal proceeding. They are associated with the practices of news reporting, fact-checking, and research methodology.

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that gadamomc̣meba (#21) and dadastureba (#20) both appear in journalistic constructions: gadamomc̣meba ver moxda ("verification could not be carried out"), dadastureba ver moxda ("confirmation could not be obtained"), damasturebeli vert moxda, op'icialuri dadastureba ("official confirmation"), p'aqt'ebis gadamomc̣meba ("fact-checking"). These constructions are distinct from the legal evidence constructions in Group 2 in that they appear in news reporting contexts and do not involve formal legal procedure. sarcmuno c̣q̇aro ("reliable source") and sando c̣q̇aro ("trusted source") also appear in this cluster as the standard phrases for evaluating information sources in journalism.

**Representative expressions:**
- გადამოწმება / gadamomc̣meba (#21) — "verification, cross-checking"
- ფაქტების გადამოწმება / p'aqt'ebis gadamomc̣meba (P2b) — "fact-checking"
- დამოუკიდებელი გადამოწმება / damouk'idebeli gadamomc̣meba (P2b) — "independent verification"
- გადამოწმება ვერ მოხდა / gadamomc̣meba ver moxda (P2b) — "verification could not be carried out"
- დადასტურება / dadastureba (#20) — "confirmation, verification"
- ოფიციალური დადასტურება / op'icialuri dadastureba (P2b) — "official confirmation"
- დადასტურება ვერ მოხდა / dadastureba ver moxda (P2b) — "confirmation could not be obtained"
- დაუდასტურებელი / daudasturebeli (P2a) — "unconfirmed, unverified"
- სარწმუნო წყარო / sarcmuno c̣q̇aro (P2b) — "a reliable source"
- სანდო წყარო / sando c̣q̇aro (P2b) — "a trusted source"
- სარწმუნო ინფორმაცია / sarcmuno in'p'ormacia (P2b) — "reliable information"
- სანდო ინფორმაცია / sando in'p'ormacia (P2b) — "reliable information"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** gadamomc̣meba implies a more thorough or repeated check (the gada- preverb contributes this sense) and is the primary term in fact-checking discourse. dadastureba is broader and appears in both journalistic and legal-administrative contexts, making it a candidate for both Group 2 and this group. sarcmuno and sando in source-evaluation contexts are more colloquial and journalistic than their appearances in legal proceedings (Group 3).

## Group 7: Expressions used in philosophical and theological registers to assert truth as a fundamental or absolute quality

**Label:** Expressions used in philosophical and theological registers to assert truth as a fundamental or absolute quality

**Definition:** These expressions appear in philosophical writing, theological texts, sermons, and formal intellectual discourse to refer to truth as an abstract, fundamental, or absolute quality rather than as the correctness of a specific claim. They are associated with elevated register and do not typically appear in everyday conversational speech or legal proceedings.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1b and P2b data show that č̣ešmariteba (#2) and č̣ešmariti (#1) appear in a cluster of philosophical and theological constructions not shared by simartle or martali: č̣ešmaritebis dzieba ("the search for truth"), č̣ešmaritebis k'rit'eriumi ("criterion of truth"), č̣ešmaritebis t'eoria ("theory of truth"), č̣ešmariti ɣmerti ("the true God"), č̣ešmariti sarcmunoeba ("the true faith"), č̣ešmariteba ertia ("truth is one"), č̣ešmariteba maradiulia ("truth is eternal"). The adverbial form č̣ešmaritad (P2a) is described as appearing in classical Georgian texts and formal prose. These expressions contrast with the everyday simartle constructions (simartle tqvi, simartle gitxra) which appear in spoken and colloquial contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba (#2) — "truth" (philosophical/theological)
- ჭეშმარიტი / č̣ešmariti (#1) — "true, genuine" (elevated register)
- ჭეშმარიტების ძიება / č̣ešmaritebis dzieba (P1b) — "the search for truth"
- ჭეშმარიტების კრიტერიუმი / č̣ešmaritebis k'rit'eriumi (P1b) — "criterion of truth"
- ჭეშმარიტების თეორია / č̣ešmaritebis t'eoria (P1b) — "theory of truth"
- ჭეშმარიტება ერთია / č̣ešmariteba ertia (P2b) — "truth is one"
- ჭეშმარიტება მარადიულია / č̣ešmariteba maradiulia (P2b) — "truth is eternal"
- ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი / č̣ešmariti ɣmerti (P1b) — "the true God"
- ჭეშმარიტი სარწმუნოება / č̣ešmariti sarcmunoeba (P1b) — "the true faith"
- ჭეშმარიტი ცოდნა / č̣ešmariti codna (P1b) — "true knowledge"
- ჭეშმარიტი ბუნება / č̣ešmariti buneba (P1b) — "true nature"
- ჭეშმარიტად / č̣ešmaritad (P2a) — "truly, verily" (formal/classical)
- ჭეშმარიტება და სამართლიანობა / č̣ešmariteba da samarтlianoba (P1b) — "truth and justice"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** č̣ešmariteba and simartle both translate as "truth" in English but their usage patterns differ: simartle appears in everyday constructions (simartle tqvi, simartle gitxra, simartle mc̣area) while č̣ešmariteba appears in the philosophical and theological constructions listed above. This distinction is directly supported by the P1 entries, which describe simartle as "often preferred in colloquial speech" and č̣ešmariteba as used in "philosophical, religious, and ordinary discourse" with the philosophical and religious uses being the more distinctive ones. The two do overlap in some contexts (both appear in "truth prevails" constructions: simartle gamarjvebs and č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs), which is noted under overlapping items below.

## Group 8: Expressions used in legal contexts to refer to lawfulness and legitimate status

**Label:** Expressions used in legal contexts to refer to lawfulness and legitimate status

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal, constitutional, and administrative contexts to evaluate acts, decisions, persons, and statuses as conforming to or violating law. They are used to characterize the legal validity of actions and claims rather than their factual truth. They are closely associated with institutional and procedural contexts.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1 and P2b data show that k'anonieri (#26) and k'anoniereba (#27) appear in a dense cluster of legal collocations: k'anonieri int'eresi ("legitimate interest"), k'anonieri mp'lobeli ("lawful owner"), k'anonieri c̣armomadgeneli ("legal representative"), k'anonieri dzala ("legal force"), k'anonieri dzala šeudga ("entered into legal force"), k'anonierebis p'rincipi ("principle of legality"), k'anonierebis darɣveva ("violation of legality"). The privative form uk'anono (P2a) is described as "the standard Georgian word for 'illegal' or 'unlawful'" and is highly frequent in legal discourse. These expressions are distinct from the truth-evaluation expressions in Groups 1 and 7 in that they evaluate conformity to law rather than correspondence to fact.

**Representative expressions:**
- კანონიერი / k'anonieri (#26) — "lawful, legal, legitimate"
- კანონიერება / k'anoniereba (#27) — "legality, legitimacy, lawfulness"
- კანონიერი ინტერესი / k'anonieri int'eresi (P2b) — "legitimate interest"
- კანონიერი მფლობელი / k'anonieri mp'lobeli (P2b) — "lawful owner"
- კანონიერი წარმომადგენელი / k'anonieri c̣armomadgeneli (P2b) — "legal representative"
- კანონიერი ძალა შეუდგა / k'anonieri dzala šeudga (P2b) — "entered into legal force"
- კანონიერი გზით / k'anonieri gzit (P2b) — "by lawful means"
- კანონიერების პრინციპი / k'anonierebis p'rincipi (P2b) — "the principle of legality"
- კანონიერების დარღვევა / k'anonierebis darɣveva (P2b) — "violation of legality"
- უკანონო / uk'anono (P2a) — "unlawful, illegal"
- უკანონობა / uk'anonoba (P2a) — "illegality, unlawfulness"
- კანონი / k'anoni (P2a) — "law, statute"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** This group is relatively self-contained within the legal domain. k'anonieri evaluates conformity to law, which is related to but distinct from the truth-evaluation function of the other groups. The connection to truth-related vocabulary is indirect: legal proceedings establish facts (Groups 2, 6) and legal acts must be lawful (this group), but k'anonieri itself does not predicate truth of propositions.

## Group 9: Expressions used to characterize something as merely appearing to be what it is not

**Label:** Expressions used to characterize something as merely appearing to be what it is not

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize a quality, situation, or outcome as presenting a false surface appearance — something that looks like X but is not genuinely X. They carry a critical or skeptical evaluative force and appear in analytical, literary, and political contexts. They differ from the forgery/fraud expressions (Group 4) in that they do not necessarily imply deliberate human deception or legal violation.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1 and P2b data show that močvenebiti (#22) appears in a cluster of constructions where a surface quality is contrasted with an underlying reality: močvenebiti tanxmoba ("apparent agreement"), močvenebiti gamarjveba ("apparent victory"), močvenebiti simšvide ("apparent calm"), močvenebiti samarтlianoba ("apparent justice"). The P1 entry describes it as carrying "a critical or skeptical evaluative force." This pattern is distinct from q̇albi (which implies deliberate fraud) and from namdvili/avt'enturi (which assert genuine character). The expression arsi da p'orma ("substance and form," P2b) and the arsi constructions (arshi, arsit, P2b) also belong here in that they distinguish essential reality from surface appearance, though arsi is a broader term.

**Representative expressions:**
- მოჩვენებითი / močvenebiti (#22) — "apparent, seeming, ostensible, illusory"
- მოჩვენებითი თანხმობა / močvenebiti tanxmoba (P2b) — "apparent agreement"
- მოჩვენებითი გამარჯვება / močvenebiti gamarjveba (P2b) — "apparent victory"
- მოჩვენებითი სიმშვიდე / močvenebiti simšvide (P2b) — "apparent calm"
- მოჩვენებითი სამართლიანობა / močvenebiti samarтlianoba (P2b) — "apparent justice"
- მოჩვენებითად / močvenebitad (P2a) — "apparently, ostensibly, on the surface"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** močvenebiti is the primary expression in this group. The group is smaller than others because the pattern it represents — surface appearance vs. underlying reality — is expressed in Georgian through this one adjective and its derivatives rather than through a large family of related forms. arsi-based expressions (Group 10 below) are related but function differently.

## Group 10: Expressions used to refer to the essential or fundamental nature of something

**Label:** Expressions used to refer to the essential or fundamental nature of something

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal, philosophical, and analytical contexts to refer to the core or fundamental character of a matter, as opposed to its surface features or formal presentation. They are used to direct attention to what something fundamentally is rather than how it appears or is formally described.

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that arsi (#28) appears in a cluster of constructions that all direct attention to fundamental character: saqmis arsi ("the substance of the matter"), p'roblemis arsi ("the essence of the problem"), k'anonis arsi ("the spirit of the law"), arshi ("in essence"), arsit ("in substance"), arsi da p'orma ("substance and form"). The derived forms arsobrivi and arsebiti (P2a) appear in legal contexts (arsobrivi darɣveva, "substantive violation"; arsebiti mnisvneloba, "material significance"). These expressions are used to distinguish what something fundamentally is from how it is formally presented or labeled.

**Representative expressions:**
- არსი / arsi (#28) — "essence, substance, nature, core"
- საქმის არსი / saqmis arsi (P2b) — "the substance of the matter"
- პრობლემის არსი / p'roblemis arsi (P2b) — "the essence of the problem"
- კანონის არსი / k'anonis arsi (P2b) — "the spirit of the law"
- არსში / arshi (P2b) — "in essence, essentially"
- არსით / arsit (P2b) — "in substance, by nature"
- არსი და ფორმა / arsi da p'orma (P2b) — "substance and form"
- არსობრივი / arsobrivi (P2a) — "essential, substantive, fundamental"
- არსებითი / arsebiti (P2a) — "essential, substantial, material"
- არსებითად / arsebitad (P2a) — "essentially, substantially, materially"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** arsobrivi and arsebiti are closely related but arsebiti has broader distribution and appears more frequently in legal contexts (arsebiti mnisvneloba, "material significance"). The arsi group is related to Group 9 (appearance vs. reality) in that both involve a contrast between surface and depth, but arsi expressions are used to identify the genuine core of something rather than to criticize a false appearance.

## Group 11: Expressions used in everyday speech to assert or challenge the truth of a claim

**Label:** Expressions used in everyday speech to assert or challenge the truth of a claim

**Definition:** These expressions appear primarily in spoken, colloquial Georgian as discourse markers, response particles, or formulaic phrases used to assert, confirm, or challenge the truth of what is being said. They are not restricted to specific domains and appear across a wide range of conversational contexts.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1b and P2b data show a cluster of expressions that function as spoken discourse markers: martalia (P1b), martla (P1b), martlac (P1b), simartle gitxra (P2b), martali gitxra (P2b), sicruea (P2b), sicrues ambobs (P2b), simartle tqvi (P2b), simartles vambob (P2b). These expressions are characterized in the data as "extremely frequent in everyday spoken Georgian," "very common in everyday speech," or "among the most frequent" expressions. They contrast with the more formal and domain-specific expressions in Groups 2, 7, and 8.

**Representative expressions:**
- მართალია / martalia (P1b) — "that's right," "it's true" (response particle)
- მართლა / martla (P1b) — "really, truly" (adverb/intensifier)
- მართლაც / martlac (P1b) — "indeed, in fact" (emphatic)
- მართლა? / martla? (P1b) — "really?" (challenge/question)
- სიმართლე თქვი / simartle tqvi (P2b) — "tell the truth" / "you told the truth"
- სიმართლე გითხრა / simartle gitxra (P2b) — "to tell you the truth" (sincerity marker)
- მართალი გითხრა / martali gitxra (P2b) — "to tell you the truth" (sincerity marker)
- სიმართლეს ვამბობ / simartles vambob (P2b) — "I am telling the truth"
- სიცრუეა / sicruea (P2b) — "it is a lie, that is false"
- სიცრუეს ამბობს / sicrues ambobs (P2b) — "is telling a lie"
- ნამდვილად / namdvilad (P2a) — "really, actually" (intensifier)
- ზუსტად ასეა / zustаd asea (P2b) — "that is exactly right"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** martalia and martla function as agreement/confirmation particles in response to prior speech. simartle gitxra and martali gitxra function as sincerity markers introducing a candid statement. sicruea is a direct rebuttal. namdvilad functions as a general intensifier. These forms are united by their function in spoken interaction rather than by a shared root or domain.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single group:

---

**სიმართლე / simartle (#4)**
Appears in Group 7 (alongside č̣ešmariteba as a truth nominal) and Group 11 (in everyday spoken constructions). The P1 entry notes that simartle is "often preferred in colloquial speech" while č̣ešmariteba is more philosophical, but simartle also appears in formal constructions (simartle moitxovs, simartle gamarjvebs). It is the most contextually versatile truth nominal in the data and does not belong exclusively to any single group.

---

**დადასტურება / dadastureba (#20)**
Appears in Group 2 (legal evidence contexts: dadasturebuli, damadasturebeli) and Group 6 (journalistic verification contexts: op'icialuri dadastureba, dadastureba ver moxda). The form is productive across both domains and cannot be assigned to one group exclusively.

---

**სარწმუნო / sarcmuno (#14) and სანდო / sando (#15)**
Both appear in Group 3 (characterizing persons as trustworthy) and Group 6 (evaluating sources in journalistic contexts). sarcmuno also appears in Group 2 (sarcmuno mt'k'icebuleba, "credible evidence"). The distinction between sarcmuno and sando is one of register and domain emphasis rather than meaning, and both straddle the person-evaluation and source-evaluation contexts.

---

**სიყალბე / siq̇albe (#11)**
Appears in Group 4 (fraud/forgery: siq̇albis dadastureba, siq̇albis gamovlena) and could also be placed alongside sicrue (#12) in a group about falsehood as a property of statements. The P1 entry notes that siq̇albe is used in "both epistemic contexts (the falsity of a statement) and moral-legal contexts (fraudulence of conduct)," which means it bridges the document-fraud domain (Group 4) and a more general falsehood domain. It is not assigned exclusively to Group 4.

---

**ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული / č̣ešmariti siq̇varuli (P1b), ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვანი / č̣ešmariti xelovani (P1b), ჭეშმარიტი პატრიოტი / č̣ešmariti p'at'riot'i (P1b)**
These expressions use the č̣ešmar- root (Group 7) but apply it to emotional, aesthetic, and political identity evaluation rather than to philosophical or theological truth. The P1b data flags these as peripheral extensions of the root. They share the pattern of asserting genuine character (Group 5) but use the elevated č̣ešmar- root rather than namdvili. They are noted here as items that sit between Group 5 and Group 7 without belonging cleanly to either.

---

**სიმართლე გამარჯვებს / simartle gamarjvebs (P1b) and ჭეშმარიტება გამარჯვებს / č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs (P1b)**
Both are proverbial/rhetorical expressions asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. They appear in both the everyday simartle register and the elevated č̣ešmariteba register, suggesting that this particular rhetorical formula is shared across registers. They could be placed in Group 7, Group 11, or treated as a distinct rhetorical sub-pattern.

---

**მართლმსაჯულება / martlmsajuleba (P1b), სასამართლო / sasamartlo (P1b), გამართლება / gamartleba (P1b)**
These expressions derive from the mart- root (Groups 1, 3, 11) but their primary use is in the institutional legal domain (justice system, court, acquittal). They are related to Group 8 (lawfulness) by domain but derive from the truth/correctness root rather than the k'anon- root. The P1b data flags them as peripheral extensions of the mart- root. They are not assigned to any group above because their primary meaning contribution is institutional and procedural rather than truth-evaluative.

---

**მართლმადიდებელი / martlmadidebeli (P1b) and მართლწერა / martlc̣era (P1b)**
These expressions derive from the mart- root but apply it to confessional identity (Orthodox Christianity) and orthographic correctness respectively. They do not fit into any of the groups above, which are organized around truth-evaluation, authenticity, fraud, and legal procedure. They are noted here as items that remain ungrouped.

---

**მსგავსება / msgavseba (#29)**
The P1 entry places this in the truth-related vocabulary as relevant to resemblance and likeness (e.g., resemblance of a portrait to its subject). However, the P2a data shows that its primary derivational family (msgavsi, msgavsad, umsgavso, gamsgavseba) is organized around similarity and assimilation rather than truth or authenticity. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above and is noted here as an item that remains ungrouped within the truth-related vocabulary.

---

**სიზუსტე / sizuste (#16) and ზუსტი / zusti (#17)**
These appear in Group 1 (evaluating statements as correct) but their primary domain in the P2b data is measurement, translation, and data accuracy (zusti targmani, zusti monacemebi, sizustis xariši). This gives them a more technical and precision-oriented character than the other Group 1 members. They are included in Group 1 but their technical-precision dimension is noted as a distinguishing feature within that group.
