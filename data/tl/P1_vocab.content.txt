# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

1. **totoo**
   - Literal meaning: true, real
   - Typical English translations: true, real, genuine, actual
   - Usage: Used to affirm the truth or reality of statements, objects, or situations. Commonly appears in everyday speech to distinguish what is genuine from what is false or imaginary.
   - [CORE]

2. **tunay**
   - Literal meaning: genuine, authentic
   - Typical English translations: true, genuine, authentic, real
   - Usage: Emphasizes authenticity and genuineness, often with emotional or qualitative weight. Frequently used to describe sincere feelings, authentic objects, or real identity.
   - [CORE]

3. **mali**
   - Literal meaning: wrong, incorrect
   - Typical English translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
   - Usage: Indicates incorrectness in statements, actions, or judgments. Used across contexts from factual errors to moral wrongdoing.
   - [CORE]

4. **tama**
   - Literal meaning: correct, right
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, proper, accurate
   - Usage: Affirms correctness of statements, appropriateness of actions, or accuracy of information. Widely used in both factual and moral contexts.
   - [CORE]

5. **sinungaling**
   - Literal meaning: liar (derived from "sinungaling" - one who lies)
   - Typical English translations: liar, false, deceitful
   - Usage: Refers to a person who lies or to the quality of being deceitful. Can function as noun or adjective to characterize dishonest persons or statements.
   - [CORE]

6. **kasinungalingan**
   - Literal meaning: ka- + sinungaling + -an (abstract noun formation from "lie")
   - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
   - Usage: Denotes a false statement or the abstract concept of falsehood. Used to identify statements known or believed to be untrue.
   - [CORE]

7. **peke**
   - Literal meaning: fake (loanword from English or Spanish "fake")
   - Typical English translations: fake, counterfeit, imitation, phony
   - Usage: Describes objects, documents, or representations that are fraudulent imitations. Commonly used for counterfeit goods or false credentials.
   - [CORE]

8. **huwad**
   - Literal meaning: false, counterfeit
   - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit, spurious
   - Usage: Indicates falseness or inauthenticity, particularly of objects, documents, or claims. More formal register than "peke."
   - [CORE]

9. **katotohanan**
   - Literal meaning: ka- + totoo + -han (abstract noun: "truth-ness")
   - Typical English translations: truth, reality, fact
   - Usage: Refers to the abstract concept of truth or the true state of affairs. Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts to denote what is factually accurate.
   - [CORE]

10. **wasto**
    - Literal meaning: correct, proper
    - Typical English translations: correct, proper, accurate, right
    - Usage: Indicates correctness or propriety, often with connotations of precision or adherence to standards. Used in formal and technical contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **hustó**
    - Literal meaning: exact, just right (from Spanish "justo")
    - Typical English translations: exact, precise, just right, fitting
    - Usage: Emphasizes exactness, appropriateness, or perfect fit. Used to indicate something meets requirements precisely or is perfectly suitable.
    - [CORE]

12. **totóhanán**
    - Literal meaning: totoo + -han + -an (reciprocal/locative: place/manner of being true)
    - Typical English translations: seriously, in earnest, for real
    - Usage: Adverbial expression indicating seriousness or genuineness of intent. Used to distinguish serious statements or actions from jokes or pretense.
    - [CORE]

13. **kathâ**
    - Literal meaning: fiction, invention
    - Typical English translations: fiction, fabrication, invention, made-up story
    - Usage: Refers to fictional narratives or fabricated accounts. Can denote creative fiction or deliberate falsehoods depending on context.
    - [CORE]

14. **bulaán**
    - Literal meaning: bula (foam/bubble) + -an (place/quality marker)
    - Typical English translations: false, deceitful, lying
    - Usage: Describes persons or things characterized by falsehood or deception. Often used as adjective to characterize dishonest people or false claims.
   - [CORE]

15. **dáyà**
    - Literal meaning: cheat, fraud, deceit
    - Typical English translations: cheat, fraud, deception, trickery
    - Usage: Refers to acts of cheating or deception, particularly in games, transactions, or relationships. Can function as noun or verb root.
    - [CORE]

16. **linlang**
    - Literal meaning: deception, illusion
    - Typical English translations: deception, illusion, delusion, trick
    - Usage: Denotes deceptive appearances or deliberate misleading. Often implies visual or perceptual deception.
    - [CORE]

17. **gawâ-gawâ**
    - Literal meaning: gawa (make/do) reduplicated
    - Typical English translations: made-up, fabricated, artificial, contrived
    - Usage: Indicates something artificially constructed or fabricated, often implying inauthenticity. Used for invented stories, fake emotions, or contrived situations.
    - [CORE]

18. **akma**
    - Literal meaning: fitting, appropriate
    - Typical English translations: appropriate, fitting, suitable, proper
    - Usage: Indicates appropriateness or suitability to context or purpose. Used to evaluate whether something fits requirements or expectations.
    - [CORE]

19. **dapat**
    - Literal meaning: should, ought
    - Typical English translations: should, ought, must, proper
    - Usage: Expresses obligation, propriety, or what is fitting. Used for moral obligations, practical necessities, and normative expectations.
    - [CORE]

20. **makatotohanan**
    - Literal meaning: maka- + totohanan (able to be true/realistic)
    - Typical English translations: realistic, plausible, believable, true-to-life
    - Usage: Describes representations or accounts that correspond to reality or are plausible. Used to evaluate verisimilitude in narratives or depictions.
    - [CORE]

21. **tunay na**
    - Literal meaning: tunay (genuine) + na (linker)
    - Typical English translations: truly, really, genuinely
    - Usage: Adverbial phrase intensifying the genuineness or authenticity of what follows. Functions as emphatic marker of truth or reality.
    - [CORE]

22. **di-totoo**
    - Literal meaning: di- (not) + totoo (true)
    - Typical English translations: untrue, false, not real
    - Usage: Negates truth or reality of statements or objects. Direct negation commonly used in everyday speech.
    - [CORE]

23. **palabás**
    - Literal meaning: pa- + labas (outward show/pretense)
    - Typical English translations: pretense, show, act, facade
    - Usage: Refers to outward show or pretense that conceals true nature. Used to characterize insincere displays or deceptive appearances.
    - [CORE]

24. **kunwari**
    - Literal meaning: pretend, feign (possibly from Spanish "con" + native element)
    - Typical English translations: pretend, feign, as if, supposedly
    - Usage: Indicates pretense or hypothetical scenarios. Used to mark actions or statements as not genuine or as imaginative play.
    - [CORE]

25. **lantád**
    - Literal meaning: exposed, open, evident
    - Typical English translations: evident, obvious, exposed, clear
    - Usage: Describes what is openly visible or clearly evident. Used when truth or reality is manifest and undeniable.
    - [CORE]

26. **lihim**
    - Literal meaning: secret, hidden
    - Typical English translations: secret, hidden, concealed, covert
    - Usage: Refers to what is deliberately kept hidden or not revealed. Contrasts with open truth or public knowledge.
    - [CORE]

27. **sinsero/tapat**
    - Literal meaning: sinsero (from Spanish "sincero"); tapat (native: honest, loyal)
    - Typical English translations: sincere, honest, truthful, faithful
    - Usage: Describes persons or statements characterized by honesty and lack of deception. "Tapat" carries additional connotations of loyalty and faithfulness.
    - [CORE]

28. **kathang-isip**
    - Literal meaning: katha (fiction) + ng + isip (mind/thought)
    - Typical English translations: imaginary, fictional, made-up, figment of imagination
    - Usage: Refers to products of imagination without basis in reality. Used to characterize invented stories or unfounded beliefs.
    - [CORE]
