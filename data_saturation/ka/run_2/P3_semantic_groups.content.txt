# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Methodological note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register distribution, and contextual pairing observed across P1 and P2 documentation. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where the documentation supports multiple groupings for a single item, this is noted explicitly. Where items do not fit any observed grouping, they are listed separately at the end.

---

## Group 1: Terms used in legal proceedings to evaluate documents, signatures, seals, and physical evidence

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate the integrity of physical and documentary evidence in legal and administrative contexts

**Definition:** These expressions appear consistently in legal, notarial, and administrative discourse when the question at issue is whether a document, signature, seal, currency, or other physical object is what it purports to be. They are used by courts, investigators, and administrative bodies to classify items as genuine or falsified. The contrast between the positive and negative poles of this group is highly institutionalized in Georgian criminal and administrative law.

**Representative expressions:**

- ყალბი დოკუმენტი / q̇albi dokumenti (forged document)
- ყალბი ხელმოწერა / q̇albi xelmowera (forged signature)
- ყალბი ბეჭედი / q̇albi bečedi (forged seal)
- ყალბი ფული / q̇albi p̕uli (counterfeit money)
- ყალბი ვალუტა / q̇albi valut̕a (counterfeit currency)
- გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba (falsification, forgery — the act)
- გაყალბებული / gaq̇albebuli (falsified, forged — the result)
- გაყალბებული მტკიცებულება / gaq̇albebuli mt̕k̕icebuleba (falsified evidence)
- სიყალბე / siq̇albe (falseness, inauthenticity — the abstract property)
- სიყალბის ნიშნები / siq̇albis nišnebi (signs of falseness)
- ნამდვილობა / namdviloba (authenticity — the property being verified)
- ნამდვილობის დადასტურება / namdvilobis dadastureba (confirmation of authenticity)
- ნამდვილობის შემოწმება / namdvilobis šemowmeba (authenticity check)
- ავთენტური / avt̕ent̕uri (authentic — formal/archival register)
- ავთენტური ტექსტი / avt̕ent̕uri t̕eqst̕i (authentic text)
- ავთენტური ხელმოწერა / avt̕ent̕uri xelmowera (authentic signature)
- ავთენტურობა / avt̕ent̕uroba (authenticity — formal abstract noun)
- კანონიერი / kanonieri (lawful, rightful — applied to ownership, claims)
- კანონიერება / kanoniereba (lawfulness, legality)
- უკანონო / ukanono (unlawful, illegal)

**Notes:** Within this group, q̇albi and gaq̇albebuli are the primary terms for the negative finding (the item is forged), while namdvili/namdviloba and avt̕ent̕uri/avt̕ent̕uroba are the primary terms for the positive finding (the item is genuine). Avt̕ent̕uri is restricted to formal and archival registers; namdvili covers the same ground in less formal usage. Kanonieri and ukanono appear in this group when the question is whether ownership or a claim is legally valid, rather than whether a physical object is genuine.

## Group 2: Terms used in legal proceedings to evaluate testimony and witnesses

**Label:** Expressions used to assess the truthfulness and reliability of witnesses and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal proceedings — trials, investigations, depositions — when the question is whether a witness is telling the truth and whether their testimony can be relied upon. They are used by courts, lawyers, and investigators to classify witnesses and statements as credible or false. This group is distinct from Group 1 in that it concerns persons and verbal acts rather than physical objects.

**Representative expressions:**

- ყალბი მოწმე / q̇albi mowme (false witness, perjurer)
- ყალბი ჩვენება / q̇albi čveneba (false testimony, perjury)
- ყალბი ბრალდება / q̇albi braldeba (false accusation)
- ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე / č̣ešmariti mowme (truthful witness)
- სარწმუნო მტკიცებულება / sarwmuno mt̕k̕icebuleba (credible evidence)
- სარწმუნო პირი / sarwmuno piri (reliable person)
- სარწმუნო წყარო / sarwmuno wq̇aro (reliable source)
- სარწმუნოდ ითვლება / sarwmunod it̕vleba (is considered reliable)
- სანდო / sando (trustworthy, reliable)
- სანდო წყარო / sando wq̇aro (trustworthy source)
- სანდოობა / sandooba (trustworthiness)
- არასანდო / arasando (untrustworthy)
- დამაჯერებელი მტკიცებულება / damajerebeliˈ mt̕k̕icebuleba (convincing evidence)
- დამაჯერებელი / damajerebeliˈ (convincing, persuasive)
- დამაჯერებელი არ არის / damajerebeliˈ ar aris (is not convincing)
- უტყუარი მტკიცებულება / uẗq̇uari mt̕k̕icebuleba (irrefutable evidence)
- უტყუარი ფაქტი / uẗq̇uari p̕aqt̕i (indubitable fact)
- უტყუარად დადგინდა / uẗq̇uarad dadginda (was established beyond doubt)
- სიმართლის თქმა / simartlis t̕q̇ma (truth-telling — in oath contexts)

**Notes:** Within this group, sarwmuno and sando are near-synonyms but the documentation notes that sando may carry a stronger interpersonal connotation. Damajerebeliˈ is distinguished from sarwmuno by emphasizing the effect on the hearer (persuasive force) rather than an intrinsic property of the source. Uẗq̇uari occupies the strongest position in this group, marking evidence or facts that are beyond any challenge. The group overlaps with Group 1 where the same terms (e.g., gaq̇albebuli mt̕k̕icebuleba) apply to both physical evidence and testimonial evidence.

## Group 3: Terms used in everyday speech to assert or deny that someone is lying

**Label:** Expressions used in everyday moral and interpersonal discourse to accuse, deny, or describe lying

**Definition:** These expressions appear in everyday spoken Georgian when speakers are making direct moral assessments of whether someone is lying or telling the truth. They are used in arguments, storytelling, and moral evaluation of persons. This group is characterized by high frequency in spoken registers and by participation in fixed verbal constructions with t̕q̇va (said) and amboba (to say/tell).

**Representative expressions:**

- ტყუილი / ẗq̇uili (lie — the most colloquial everyday term)
- ტყუილი თქვა / ẗq̇uili t̕q̇va (told a lie)
- ტყუილს ამბობს / ẗq̇uils ambobs (is lying)
- ტყუილი არ ვიცი / ẗq̇uili ar vici (I don't know how to lie)
- ტყუილი-მართალი / ẗq̇uili-martali (half-true, a mixture of truth and lies)
- სიცრუე / sicrue (lie, falsehood — slightly more abstract/formal than ẗq̇uili)
- სიცრუეში გაიჭირა / sicruešiˈ gaič̣ira (got caught in a lie)
- სიმართლეს ვამბობ / simartles vambob (I am telling the truth)
- სიმართლე გითხრა? / simartle git̕xra? (shall I tell you the truth?)
- სიმართლე რომ ვთქვათ / simartle rom vt̕q̇vat̕ (to tell the truth, frankly)
- მართალი ხარ / martali xar (you are right)
- მართალია, მაგრამ / martalia, magram (it is true, but...)
- მართლა / martla (truly, really — discourse particle)
- მართლაც / martlac (indeed, truly — emphatic)

**Notes:** T̈q̇uili and sicrue overlap substantially in this group but ẗq̇uili is more colloquial and more frequent in direct accusation, while sicrue is slightly more abstract and appears more in moral reflection. The simartle constructions in this group (simartles vambob, simartle git̕xra, simartle rom vt̕q̇vat̕) function as performative or discourse-framing devices — they are not simply descriptions of truth-telling but acts of positioning oneself as honest in conversation. Martla and martlac are discourse particles that confirm or emphasize truth and are among the most frequent words in spoken Georgian.

## Group 4: Terms used in proverbial and moral discourse about the nature of truth and lies

**Label:** Expressions appearing in proverbial, literary, and moral discourse about truth and deception as forces or entities

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth and lies are treated as agents or forces with characteristic behaviors — truth prevails, lies breed more lies, lies cannot stand. They are used in moral exhortation, literary writing, and rhetorical argumentation. They are distinct from the legal and interpersonal groups in that they make general claims about the nature of truth and falsehood rather than evaluating specific persons or items.

**Representative expressions:**

- ჭეშმარიტება გამარჯვებს / č̣ešmariteba gamarjvebs (truth prevails)
- ჭეშმარიტება მწარეა / č̣ešmariteba mwarea (the truth is bitter)
- ჭეშმარიტება თვალში გიყრის / č̣ešmariteba t̕valši giq̇ris (the truth stares you in the face)
- სიმართლე თვალში გიყრის / simartle t̕valši giq̇ris (the truth stares you in the face — colloquial variant)
- სიმართლეს ვერ გაექცევი / simartles ver gaekceviˈ (you cannot escape the truth)
- ტყუილი ფეხებზე დგება / ẗq̇uili p̕exebze dgebaˈ (a lie stands on its own feet)
- ტყუილი ტყუილს ბადებს / ẗq̇uili ẗq̇uils badebs (one lie breeds another)
- სიცრუე ფეხებზე დგას / sicrue p̕exebze dgas (a lie stands on its own feet — variant)
- ნახევარი ჭეშმარიტება / naxevari č̣ešmariteba (a half-truth)
- ჭეშმარიტება და სამართლიანობა / č̣ešmariteba da samart̕lianoba (truth and justice — dyadic formula)

**Notes:** The č̣ešmariteba constructions in this group tend to be more formal and literary; the simartle and ẗq̇uili constructions are more colloquial. The "lie stands on its own feet" proverb appears with both ẗq̇uili and sicrue as subject, suggesting the two terms are interchangeable in this register. The dyadic formula č̣ešmariteba da samart̕lianoba belongs here as a rhetorical invocation but also overlaps with Group 6 (institutional and political discourse).

## Group 5: Terms used to distinguish genuine from counterfeit in commercial and everyday contexts

**Label:** Expressions used in commercial, everyday, and material contexts to distinguish real from fake

**Definition:** These expressions appear when speakers are evaluating whether an object — gold, goods, a smile, a performance — is genuine or a counterfeit/imitation. Unlike Group 1, which is institutionalized in legal proceedings, this group covers informal and commercial evaluation of material authenticity. The contrast between namdvili and q̇albi is central.

**Representative expressions:**

- ნამდვილი / namdvili (real, genuine, authentic)
- ნამდვილი ოქრო / namdvili oqro (real gold)
- ნამდვილი მეგობარი / namdvili megobari (a true friend)
- ნამდვილი სახე / namdvili saxe (the true face, one's real nature)
- ნამდვილი მიზეზი / namdvili mizezi (the real reason)
- ნამდვილი პრობლემა / namdvili problema (a real problem)
- ნამდვილად / namdvilad (truly, genuinely, really)
- ეს ნამდვილია / es namdvilia (this is genuine)
- ყალბი ოქრო / q̇albi oqro (fake gold)
- ყალბი ღიმილი / q̇albi ɣimili (a false smile)
- ყალბი ნოტა / q̇albi not̕a (a wrong/false note)
- ყალბი ნაბიჯი / q̇albi nabiǰi (a false step)
- ყალბი ალარმი / q̇albi alarmi (false alarm)
- ყალბი სტარტი / q̇albi st̕art̕i (false start)
- ყალბი ექსპერტი / q̇albi eqsp̕ert̕i (a fake expert)
- ყალბი პირადობა / q̇albi piradoba (false identity)
- ფსევდო- / psevdo- (pseudo- prefix)
- ფსევდომეცნიერი / psevdomecnieri (pseudo-scientist)
- ფსევდომეცნიერება / psevdomecniereba (pseudo-science)
- ფსევდოდემოკრატია / psevdodemocracia (pseudo-democracy)
- თვალთმაქცობა / t̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba (deception through appearance, charlatanism)
- თვალთმაქცი / t̕valt̕maq̇c̕i (deceiver, charlatan)

**Notes:** This group is the broadest in scope. Namdvili is the positive pole and q̇albi is the negative pole across a wide range of material and social contexts. The psevdo- prefix occupies a distinct sub-area within this group: it marks false category membership (a pseudo-scientist is not a scientist) rather than counterfeit material identity (fake gold looks like gold). T̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba and t̕valt̕maq̇c̕i also belong here in that they describe deception through false appearance, but they carry a stronger moral condemnation and are used specifically of persons rather than objects. The q̇albi ɣimili, q̇albi not̕a, q̇albi nabiǰi constructions extend the "false/fake" meaning into domains of emotional expression, musical performance, and conduct — areas where "false" means "not genuine" rather than "deliberately forged."

## Group 6: Terms used in political, constitutional, and institutional discourse

**Label:** Expressions used in political, constitutional, and institutional contexts to evaluate authority, law, and governance

**Definition:** These expressions appear in constitutional law, political science, and institutional discourse when the question is whether authority, governance, or legal arrangements are legitimate, lawful, or just. They are used in formal written registers — constitutional documents, court decisions, political commentary — and are largely absent from everyday spoken Georgian.

**Representative expressions:**

- ლეგიტიმური / legitimuri (legitimate)
- ლეგიტიმურობა / legitimuroba (legitimacy)
- ლეგიტიმაცია / legitimacia (legitimation)
- არალეგიტიმური / aralegitimuri (illegitimate)
- კანონიერი / kanonieri (lawful, legal)
- კანონიერება / kanoniereba (lawfulness, legality)
- უკანონო / ukanono (unlawful, illegal)
- უკანონობა / ukanonoba (unlawfulness)
- სამართალი / samart̕ali (law as a normative system)
- სამართლებრივი / samart̕lebrivi (legal, juridical)
- სამართლებრივი სახელმწიფო / samart̕lebrivi saxelmwip̕o (rule-of-law state)
- სამართლიანი / samart̕liani (just, fair)
- სამართლიანობა / samart̕lianoba (justice, fairness)
- სამართლიანად / samart̕lianad (justly, fairly)
- უსამართლო / usamart̕lo (unjust, unfair)
- უსამართლობა / usamart̕loba (injustice)
- მართლმსაჯულება / martlmsajuleba (administration of justice, judiciary)
- სასამართლო / sasamart̕lo (court, tribunal)
- სამართლიანი სასამართლო განხილვა / samart̕liani sasamart̕lo ganxilva (fair trial)
- მართლზომიერი / martlzomieri (proportionate)
- მართლზომიერება / martlzomiereba (proportionality)
- შესაბამისი / šesabamisi (conforming, consistent with)
- შესაბამისობა / šesabamisoba (conformity, compliance)
- შეუსაბამო / šeusabamo (non-conforming, inconsistent)
- შეუსაბამობა / šeusabamoba (inconsistency, non-conformity)
- ჭეშმარიტება და სამართლიანობა / č̣ešmariteba da samart̕lianoba (truth and justice — dyadic formula)

**Notes:** Legitimuri and kanonieri overlap in this group but are distinguished in the documentation: kanonieri is tied to positive law (does this conform to the rules?), while legitimuri can carry a broader normative dimension (is this authority recognized as valid?). Šesabamisi and its derivatives occupy a distinct sub-area within this group: they are used to evaluate conformity with standards and requirements, which is a procedural rather than substantive form of correctness. Martlzomiereba (proportionality) is a technical constitutional law term that belongs here but is not closely related to the other terms in everyday usage.

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate the correctness of statements, arguments, and reasoning

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether statements, arguments, decisions, or conclusions are correct, well-founded, or accurate

**Definition:** These expressions appear when speakers or writers are assessing the epistemic quality of a claim, argument, or conclusion — whether it is accurate, well-founded, or free from error. They are used across formal and informal registers but are especially prominent in academic, journalistic, and legal argumentation. The contrast in this group is between correctness/accuracy and error/incorrectness, rather than between genuine and counterfeit (Group 5) or truthful and lying (Group 3).

**Representative expressions:**

- ზუსტი / zusti (exact, precise, accurate)
- სიზუსტე / sizuste (exactness, precision, accuracy)
- ზუსტად / zustad (exactly, precisely — also used as a confirmation particle)
- უზუსტობა / uzustoba (inaccuracy, imprecision)
- დაზუსტება / dazusteba (specification, clarification, making precise)
- მართებული / martebuli (correct, well-founded, appropriate)
- მართებულობა / martebulobā (correctness, well-foundedness)
- მცდარი / mcdari (erroneous, mistaken — honest error, not deliberate deception)
- სიმცდარე / simcdare (erroneousness, incorrectness)
- მცრუ / mcru (false, deceptive — formal/literary)
- შესაბამისი / šesabamisi (consistent with, conforming to — when applied to statements)
- სიმართლე ისაა, რომ... / simartle isaa, rom... (the fact is that..., the truth is that...)
- მართლა / martla (truly, really — confirming accuracy)
- მართლაც / martlac (indeed, truly — emphatic confirmation)

**Notes:** Zusti and its derivatives are the primary terms for precision and accuracy in technical and scientific contexts. Martebuli is used specifically for well-foundedness of arguments and decisions — it implies that something has been correctly reasoned or appropriately applied, not merely that it is factually accurate. Mcdari is important within this group because it marks error without implying deliberate deception, distinguishing it from q̇albi (which implies intent to deceive). Šesabamisi overlaps with Group 6 but appears here when the question is whether a statement or conclusion is consistent with the evidence or with a prior claim. Martla and martlac function as confirmation particles in this group, asserting the accuracy of what has just been said.

## Group 8: Terms used in theological and religious discourse

**Label:** Expressions used specifically in Orthodox Christian theological and ecclesiastical contexts

**Definition:** These expressions appear in Georgian Orthodox Christian theological writing, liturgy, and ecclesiastical discourse. Several of them are derived from roots that also appear in secular contexts, but in theological usage they acquire specific meanings related to divine truth, authentic sacraments, and religious identity. This group is identified because the documentation repeatedly notes register shifts into theological usage for several P1 items.

**Representative expressions:**

- ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი / č̣ešmariti ɣmerti (the true God)
- ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba (truth — in its most absolute, philosophical/theological sense)
- ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე / č̣ešmariti mowme (true witness — also: martyr)
- ჭეშმარიტი ცოდნა / č̣ešmariti codna (true knowledge — in Gnostic-influenced theological discourse)
- მართლმადიდებელი / martlmadidebeli (Orthodox Christian; literally "right-glorifier")
- მართლმადიდებლობა / martlmadidebloba (Orthodoxy)
- სარწმუნოება / sarwmunoeba (faith, religion — in theological register)
- სარწმუნოების თავისუფლება / sarwmunoebis t̕avisup̕leba (freedom of religion)
- სარწმუნოების ნიშნით / sarwmunoebis nišnit̕ (on grounds of religion)
- მართლსაიდუმლო / martlsaidumlo (true sacrament — theological)
- ღმერთი მართალია / ɣmerti martalia (God is just/righteous)
- მართლი გზა / martli gza (the right path — in religious/moral usage)

**Notes:** The most significant observation here is the semantic bifurcation of sarwmunoeba: in secular contexts it means credibility/reliability, but in theological contexts it means faith/religion. These are not merely polysemous uses of the same word but distinct registers with distinct collocational patterns. Similarly, č̣ešmariteba in theological usage tends to refer to absolute or divine truth, while in everyday usage it refers to the truth of statements. Martlmadidebeli is the most institutionalized form in this group — it is the standard name for the dominant religious confession in Georgia and is not used outside religious and historical contexts.

## Group 9: Terms used to evaluate sincerity and authenticity of persons and their expressions

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether a person's character, emotions, or expressions are genuine rather than performed or hypocritical

**Definition:** These expressions appear when speakers are assessing whether a person is genuinely what they appear to be — whether their emotions, friendship, or character are authentic rather than performed or deceptive. Unlike Group 1 (documents) or Group 3 (lying), this group concerns the authenticity of persons and their inner states as expressed outwardly. The contrast is between genuine character and performed or hypocritical presentation.

**Representative expressions:**

- გულწრფელი / gulwrp̕eli (sincere, genuine, frank — literally "straight-hearted")
- გულწრფელობა / gulwrp̕eloba (sincerity, genuineness, candor)
- გულწრფელად / gulwrp̕elad (sincerely, genuinely, frankly)
- თვალთმაქცობა / t̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba (deception through appearance, hypocrisy, charlatanism)
- თვალთმაქცი / t̕valt̕maq̇c̕i (deceiver, hypocrite, charlatan)
- ნამდვილი მეგობარი / namdvili megobari (a true friend)
- ჭეშმარიტი მეგობარი / č̣ešmariti megobari (a true friend — more literary)
- ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული / č̣ešmariti siq̇varuli (true love)
- ყალბი ღიმილი / q̇albi ɣimili (a false smile)
- ყალბი პათოსი / q̇albi p̕at̕osi (false pathos, hollow rhetoric)
- მართალი ადამიანი / martali adamiani (a just, honest person)
- ნამდვილი ქართველი / namdvili q̇art̕veli (a real Georgian — authentic identity)
- გარეგნული / garegnuli (outward, superficial, apparent — implying contrast with inner reality)
- გარეგნობა / garegnoba (outward appearance, exterior)
- გარეგნულად / garegnulad (outwardly, superficially)

**Notes:** Gulwrp̕eli and t̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba are the clearest poles of this group: gulwrp̕eli marks genuine inner character expressed outwardly, while t̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba marks deliberate deception through outward performance. Garegnuli belongs here because it marks the outward/inner contrast that underlies the whole group — it is used precisely in contexts where the reliability of surface appearances is in question. The namdvili and č̣ešmariti collocations with megobari and siq̇varuli belong here rather than in Group 5 because the question is not material authenticity but the genuineness of a relationship or emotion.

## Group 10: Terms used in discourse about orthographic, linguistic, and procedural correctness

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate correctness in linguistic, orthographic, and procedural domains

**Definition:** These expressions appear when the question is whether something conforms to a normative standard of correct form — correct spelling, correct speech, correct procedure. The "correctness" here is not factual accuracy (Group 7) or legal validity (Group 6) but conformity to a recognized norm of proper form. This group is smaller and more specialized than the others.

**Representative expressions:**

- მართლწერა / martlwera (orthography, correct spelling)
- მართლწერის წესები / martlweris wesesbi (rules of orthography)
- მართლმეტყველება / martlmet̕q̇veleba (correct speech, orthoepy)
- ზუსტი / zusti (exact, precise — also used for accurate translations and measurements)
- სიზუსტე / sizuste (exactness, precision)
- შესაბამისი / šesabamisi (conforming, consistent with — when applied to procedural compliance)
- მართებული / martebuli (appropriate, correct — when applied to usage or procedure)
- არსებითი სახელი / arsebiti saxeli (noun — grammatical term; literally "essential name")

**Notes:** This group is identified because the mart- root generates a distinct cluster of terms specifically for linguistic and orthographic correctness (martlwera, martlmet̕q̇veleba) that do not appear in the other groups. Zusti appears here when applied to translations and measurements, overlapping with Group 7. Arsebiti saxeli is included as a fixed grammatical term that uses arsebiti in a specialized technical sense distinct from its philosophical usage.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups and cannot be assigned to a single group without distorting the observed usage patterns:

**სიმართლე / simartle** — Appears in Group 3 (everyday assertions of truth-telling), Group 4 (proverbial discourse), and Group 7 (evaluating correctness of statements). The term is the most versatile in the domain and resists single-group assignment. Its discourse-framing constructions (simartle git̕xra, simartle rom vt̕q̇vat̕) are specific to Group 3; its proverbial constructions (simartles ver gaekceviˈ) belong to Group 4; its factual assertion constructions (simartle isaa, rom...) belong to Group 7.

**ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba** — Appears in Group 4 (proverbial/moral discourse), Group 8 (theological discourse), and Group 7 (philosophical discourse about truth). The term is the most philosophically and theologically marked word for truth in Georgian and participates in all three of these registers.

**მართალი / martali** — Appears in Group 3 (everyday assertions: martali xar, martalia magram), Group 6 (legal/moral: martali adamiani), and Group 8 (theological: ɣmerti martalia). The adverbial derivatives martla and martlac belong most clearly to Group 3.

**სარწმუნო / sarwmuno** — Appears in Group 2 (legal evaluation of witnesses and sources) and Group 8 (through its derivative sarwmunoeba in the theological sense of faith/religion). The two registers are clearly distinct in usage.

**შესაბამისი / šesabamisi** — Appears in Group 6 (institutional/legal conformity), Group 7 (consistency of statements with evidence), and Group 10 (procedural correctness). The term is highly productive across formal registers.

**კანონიერი / kanonieri** — Appears in Group 1 (legal validity of documents and claims) and Group 6 (lawfulness of authority and actions). The distinction is between evaluating a specific item (Group 1) and evaluating a system or course of conduct (Group 6).

**ნამდვილი / namdvili** — Appears in Group 5 (commercial/material authenticity), Group 9 (authenticity of persons and relationships), and overlaps with Group 1 through namdviloba (formal authentication). The term is the most productive adjective for genuineness across all registers.

**ყალბი / q̇albi** — Appears in Group 1 (forged documents), Group 2 (false testimony), Group 3 (lying), Group 5 (fake objects), and Group 9 (false emotional expression). The term is the most productive adjective for falseness/fakeness across all registers.

**გამართლება / gamartleba** — Appears in Group 2 (legal acquittal/vindication) and Group 6 (justification of authority or action), and also in everyday usage meaning "making excuses" which does not fit cleanly into any group. The documentation notes this semantic range explicitly.

## Items that do not fit clearly into any group

**მიმსგავსება / mimsgavseba** — The documentation marks this as [MARGINAL] and notes it appears in aesthetic and theological contexts (resemblance to a divine model). It does not participate in any of the observed usage clusters with sufficient frequency to warrant group membership.

**მსგავსი / msgavsi and მსგავსება / msgavseba** — These terms for similarity and resemblance appear in the P1 vocabulary but do not participate in the usage patterns that define any of the groups above. The documentation does not show them collocating with truth/falseness terms or appearing in the legal, moral, or theological contexts that define the other groups. They are noted here as ungrouped.

**უმსგავსო / umsgavso** — The documentation notes that in older literary Georgian this term carries the sense of "unseemly" or "not fitting," which is a normative use of the similarity concept. This is not sufficiently attested in current usage to warrant group placement.

**არსებითი / arsebiti** — In its philosophical sense (essential, fundamental), this term does not fit clearly into any group. Its legal use (arsebiti gansxvaveba, "material difference") overlaps with Group 6, and its grammatical use (arsebiti saxeli, "noun") belongs to Group 10, but the philosophical core meaning is not strongly represented in any group's usage patterns.

**მართება / marteba** (in the modal construction X-s marteba aqvs) — The documentation notes that this construction functions as a modal marker of obligation or fittingness. This is a peripheral use of the mart- root that does not fit the truth/falseness domain of the other groups.

**ტყუილუბრალოდ / ẗq̇uilubralod and ტყუილად / ẗq̇uilad** — These adverbial forms carry the "in vain, for nothing" sense of ẗq̇uili rather than the "lie/falsehood" sense. They do not fit into any of the groups above, which are organized around truth and falseness rather than futility or purposelessness.
