# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

### Construction Type 1: VERB + OBJECT COLLOCATIONS

#### C1.1
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையைச் சொல்லு
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyaic collu
**Translation:** tell the truth, speak the truth
**Usage:** Most common collocation for truth-telling. Used in everyday speech, moral instruction, and interrogation contexts. The accusative marker -ai (phonetically -aic before c-) is obligatory.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.2
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையைப் பேசு
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyaip pēcu
**Translation:** speak the truth, tell the truth
**Usage:** Alternative to uṇmaiyaic collu, equally common in everyday speech. The accusative marker -ai becomes -aip before p-.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.3
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையை மறை
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai maṟai
**Translation:** hide the truth, conceal the truth
**Usage:** Used to describe deliberate concealment of truth. Common in contexts of investigation, deception, and moral judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.4
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையை வெளிப்படுத்து
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai veḷippaṭuttu
**Translation:** reveal the truth, bring the truth to light
**Usage:** Formal expression for revealing or disclosing truth. Common in investigative and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.5
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையை ஏற்று
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai ēṟṟu
**Translation:** accept the truth, acknowledge the truth
**Usage:** Used for the act of accepting or acknowledging truth, especially difficult truths. Common in counseling and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.6
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையைத் தேடு
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyait tēṭu
**Translation:** search for truth, seek truth
**Usage:** Used to describe the pursuit of truth in philosophical, investigative, or spiritual contexts. Common in academic and religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.7
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையை அறி
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai aṟi
**Translation:** know the truth, come to know the truth
**Usage:** Used for gaining knowledge of truth. Common in epistemic and investigative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.8
**Base Form:** உண்மை (uṇmai) - "truth"
**Construction:** உண்மையை உணர்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai uṇar
**Translation:** realize the truth, become aware of the truth
**Usage:** Used for realization or awareness of truth, often with spiritual or philosophical connotations. Common in religious and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.9
**Base Form:** பொய் (poy) - "lie, falsehood"
**Construction:** பொய் சொல்லு
**Transliteration:** poy collu
**Translation:** tell lies, speak falsely
**Usage:** Most common collocation for lying. Used in everyday speech, moral instruction, and accusations of dishonesty. No case marker on poy in this construction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.10
**Base Form:** பொய் (poy) - "lie, falsehood"
**Construction:** பொய் பேசு
**Transliteration:** poy pēcu
**Translation:** speak lies, tell lies
**Usage:** Alternative to poy collu, equally common in everyday speech for lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.11
**Base Form:** பொய் (poy) - "lie, falsehood"
**Construction:** பொய் கூறு
**Transliteration:** poy kūṟu
**Translation:** utter lies, state falsely
**Usage:** More formal variant for speaking lies. Used in written and formal spoken contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### C1.12
**Base Form:** நேர்மை (nērmai) - "honesty, integrity"
**Construction:** நேர்மையைக் காத்து
**Transliteration:** nērmaiyaik kāttu

#### C1.13

**Key observations:**

1. **Noun + Noun compounds** are highly productive, particularly with பொய் (poy) and மெய் (mey), forming specialized terms in legal, philosophical, and everyday domains.

2. **Verb + Object collocations** show clear preferences: உண்மை (uṇmai) commonly collocates with verbs of speaking, hiding, revealing, and seeking; பொய் (poy) primarily with verbs of speaking.

3. **Interrogative constructions** with -ā particle are extremely common in everyday speech for confirmation and verification.

4. **Simple copular constructions** (அது + TERM) are fundamental and extremely frequent in everyday evaluative speech.

5. **Emphatic constructions** using multiple particles (-il-ē-y-ē, -āka-v-ē) are characteristic of Tamil and very common in colloquial speech.

6. Clear **register distinctions**: மெய் (mey) compounds tend toward literary/philosophical registers, while உண்மை (uṇmai) and நிஜம் (nijam) constructions span all registers.
