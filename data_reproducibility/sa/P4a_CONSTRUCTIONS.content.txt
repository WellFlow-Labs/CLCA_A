# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X is true/false**: Predicate nominal construction using copular verb √as (to be) or √bhū (to become) with truth-domain adjectives. Standard structure: NOM + ADJ(NOM) + VERB, though copula often omitted in present tense.

### Examples
- एतत् सत्यम् (अस्ति) / etat satyam (asti) / "this is true"
- तद् वचनं मृषा / tad vacanaṃ mṛṣā / "that statement is false"

### Notes
Copula frequently omitted in present tense nominal sentences. Adverbial forms (mṛṣā, mithyā) function predicatively without requiring copula. Past/future tenses require explicit verbal forms (āsīt, bhaviṣyati).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X is not true/false**: Negation particle na precedes predicate adjective or verb. Structure: NOM + na + ADJ/VERB. Alternative: use of negative-prefixed adjectives (asatya, anṛta, ayukta).

### Examples
- एतत् न सत्यम् / etat na satyam / "this is not true"
- तद् असत्यम् अस्ति / tad asatyam asti / "that is untrue"

### Notes
Two strategies: (1) particle negation of positive term (na satya), (2) use of morphologically negative term (asatya). Both patterns productive. Particle negation more emphatic; morphological negation more categorical.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**True/false X**: Attributive adjective precedes or follows head noun in agreement (case, number, gender). Structure: ADJ(agr) + N or N + ADJ(agr). Truth-domain adjectives function as standard attributive modifiers.

### Examples
- सत्यं वचनम् / satyaṃ vacanam / "true statement"
- मिथ्या ज्ञानम् / mithyā jñānam / "false knowledge"

### Notes
Adjectives agree in case, number, gender with head noun. Adverbial forms (mṛṣā, mithyā) can modify nouns without inflection. Word order flexible but adjective-initial slightly preferred for emphasis.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**Become true/false**: Construction with √bhū (to become) + truth-domain adjective/noun. Structure: NOM + ADJ/N(NOM/INST) + bhū. Expresses change of state or revelation of truth-status.

### Examples
- तत् सत्यं भवति / tat satyaṃ bhavati / "that becomes true / proves true"
- मिथ्या भूतम् / mithyā bhūtam / "became false / proved illusory"

### Notes
√bhū combines with nominative adjective or instrumental abstract noun. Common in contexts of verification, falsification, or revelation. Past participle bhūta frequently used for completed state change.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Make true/false, verify/falsify**: Causative constructions less grammaticalized than in some languages. Primary strategy: √kṛ (to make/do) + truth-domain term. Structure: NOM + ACC + ADJ/N(ACC/INST) + kṛ.

### Examples
- प्रमाणं करोति / pramāṇaṃ karoti / "makes (it) authoritative, validates"
- मृषा करोति / mṛṣā karoti / "makes false, falsifies"

### Notes
No dedicated morphological causative for truth-domain meanings. √kṛ serves as light verb for causative/factitive meanings. Alternative: denominative verbs (pramāṇīkaroti "authenticates") but less common. Verification often expressed through passive constructions with pramāṇena sidh "is established by valid means."

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Truth/falsehood (abstract quality)**: Productive suffixation with -tā (f.) or -tva (n.) on adjectival bases. Structure: ADJ-STEM + -tā/-tva. Creates abstract nouns denoting quality or state.

### Examples
- सत्यता / satyatā / "truthfulness, truth (quality)"
- प्रामाण्यम् / prāmāṇyam / "validity, authoritativeness"

### Notes
Both -tā and -tva highly productive. -tva often preferred in technical philosophical contexts. Some bases take vṛddhi-grade derivatives (pramāṇa → prāmāṇya). Adverbial bases (mṛṣā, mithyā) show limited nominalization (mithyātva attested, *mṛṣātā not common).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Truth-teller/liar**: Agent nouns formed with -in suffix or compound with vādin (speaker). Structure: STEM + -in or STEM + vādin. Denotes person characterized by truth/falsehood in speech.

### Examples
- सत्यवादिन् / satyavādin / "truth-speaker, truthful person"
- मृषावादिन् / mṛṣāvādin / "liar, one who speaks falsely"

### Notes
-vādin compounds highly productive (satya-vādin, asatya-vādin, anṛta-vādin). Simple -in suffix less common for truth-domain (dharmin "righteous person" attested). Agent nouns can also be formed with -tṛ suffix (pramātṛ "knower") but this is more general cognitive agent than specifically truth-related.

---

## 8. VERBAL COMPLEMENTATION (SPEECH VERBS)

### Pattern
**Speak/say truly/falsely**: Truth-domain adverbs or adjectives modify speech verbs. Structure: ADV/ADJ(ACC) + √vad/√brū/√vac (to speak). Accusative object expresses content; manner expressed by adverb or accusative adjective.

### Examples
- सत्यं वदति / satyaṃ vadati / "speaks truth, tells the truth"
- मृषा वदति / mṛṣā vadati / "speaks falsely, lies"

### Notes
Multiple speech verbs: √vad (most common), √brū (more formal), √vac (general). Accusative satya/asatya functions as internal object. Adverbial mṛṣā, mithyā modify verb directly. Construction extremely productive and frequent in ethical/legal contexts.

---

## 9. EPISTEMIC VERB COMPLEMENTATION

### Pattern
**Know/perceive truly/falsely**: Truth-domain adverbs modify cognitive verbs. Structure: ADV + √jñā/√dṛś/√budh (to know/see/understand). Expresses manner of cognition.

### Examples
- यथार्थं जानाति / yathārthaṃ jānāti / "knows accurately, knows truly"
- मिथ्या जानाति / mithyā jānāti / "knows falsely, has false knowledge"

### Notes
Cognitive verbs: √jñā (know), √dṛś (see/perceive), √budh (understand). Manner expressed by adverbs (yathārtha, samyak, mithyā) or instrumental abstracts (yuktyā "by reasoning"). Accusative object expresses content known; manner adverb expresses epistemic quality.

---

## 10. INSTRUMENTAL OF MEANS/MANNER

### Pattern
**By/through truth, truthfully**: Instrumental case expresses means, manner, or accordance. Structure: N/ADJ(INST) + VERB. Extremely productive for expressing conformity to standards or methods.

### Examples
- सत्येन विजयते / satyena vijayate / "conquers by truth, triumphs through truth"
- प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / pramāṇena siddham / "established by valid means, proven"

### Notes
Instrumental case highly productive with truth-domain terms. Expresses: (1) means (pramāṇena "by valid means"), (2) manner (dharmeṇa "righteously"), (3) accordance (nyāyena "according to justice/logic"). Common in both philosophical and ethical discourse.

---

## 11. ABLATIVE OF SOURCE/STANDARD

### Pattern
**From truth/error, due to**: Ablative case expresses source, cause, or standard of deviation. Structure: N(ABL) + VERB/ADJ. Common in causal explanations and prohibitions.

### Examples
- भ्रमात् उत्पन्नम् / bhramāt utpannam / "arisen from error, caused by confusion"
- धर्मात् न चलितव्यम् / dharmāt na chalitavyam / "one should not deviate from dharma"

### Notes
Ablative expresses: (1) causal source (bhramāt "from error"), (2) standard of deviation (satyāt calati "deviates from truth"), (3) origin (pramāṇābhāvāt "due to absence of proof"). Frequent in gerundive prohibitions (na chalitavyam "should not deviate").

---

## 12. GERUNDIVE OBLIGATION

### Pattern
**Should/must (not) speak truth**: Gerundive suffix -tavya/-anīya/-ya on verbal root expresses obligation/necessity. Structure: (na) + ROOT-tavya. Common in ethical prescriptions.

### Examples
- सत्यं वक्तव्यम् / satyaṃ vaktavyam / "truth must be spoken"
- मृषा न वक्तव्यम् / mṛṣā na vaktavyam / "falsehood must not be spoken"

### Notes
Gerundive expresses deontic necessity. Negative na + gerundive = prohibition. Agent in instrumental or genitive. Extremely common in dharmaśāstra and ethical instruction. Alternative: potential participle in -ya (vācya "to be spoken").

---

## 13. CONTRASTIVE COORDINATION

### Pattern
**True or false, truth and falsehood**: Coordination of opposites using vā (or) or ca (and). Structure: X vā Y vā (disjunctive) or X Y ca (conjunctive, often dual). Presents alternatives or paired concepts.

### Examples
- सत्यं वा असत्यं वा / satyaṃ vā asatyaṃ vā / "whether true or false"
- धर्माधर्मौ / dharmādharmau / "dharma and adharma, right and wrong"

### Notes
Two patterns: (1) X vā Y vā for alternatives, (2) dvandva compound in dual for paired opposites (satyāsatye, dharmādharmau). Dual form emphasizes complementary opposition. Common in philosophical analysis presenting exhaustive alternatives.

---

## 14. LOCATIVE OF DOMAIN/SPHERE

### Pattern
**In truth/reality, in dharma**: Locative case expresses domain, sphere, or respect in which something holds. Structure: N(LOC) + VERB/ADJ. Indicates conceptual location or frame of reference.

### Examples
- धर्मे स्थितः / dharme sthitaḥ / "standing in dharma, established in righteousness"
- तत्त्वे / tattve / "in reality, with respect to truth"

### Notes
Locative expresses: (1) sphere of activity (dharme sthā "stand in dharma"), (2) respect/regard (tattvena "in reality"), (3) basis (satye sthā "be grounded in truth"). Common with verbs of standing/remaining (√sthā) for steadfastness in truth/dharma.

---

## 15. INTERROGATIVE CHALLENGE

### Pattern
**What is the proof? How is that true?**: Interrogative constructions challenging truth-claims or demanding justification. Structure: KIM/KATHAM + N/CLAUSE. Common in philosophical debate.

### Examples
- किं प्रमाणम् / kiṃ pramāṇam / "what is the proof/authority?"
- कथं तत् सत्यम् / kathaṃ tat satyam / "how is that true?"

### Notes
Interrogative particles: kim (what), katham (how), kutaḥ (whence/why). Challenges epistemic basis or logical coherence. Extremely common in philosophical dialogue and debate (vāda). Often followed by justificatory response with tathā hi "for thus" or yataḥ "because."

---

## 16. EMPHATIC ASSERTION

### Pattern
**Truth alone, truly indeed**: Emphatic particles eva (alone, indeed) or hi (for, indeed) strengthen truth-claims. Structure: N/ADV + eva/hi. Emphasizes exclusivity or certainty.

### Examples
- सत्यमेव जयते / satyam eva jayate / "truth alone conquers"
- मिथ्यैव / mithyaiva / "falsely indeed, merely illusory"

### Notes
Particle eva emphasizes exclusivity or identity (satya-eva "truth alone"). Particle hi emphasizes certainty or provides justification. Famous Upaniṣadic motto satyam eva jayate uses eva for exclusive emphasis. Common in Vedānta for emphasizing illusory nature (mithyaiva).

---

## 17. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**More true/accurate than**: Comparative expressed through ablative of standard + adjective or through explicit comparison terms. Structure: N(ABL) + ADJ-tara or N(ABL) + adhika/uttara.

### Examples
- अस्मात् सत्यतरम् / asmāt satyataram / "truer than this"
- प्रत्यक्षात् अनुमानम् अल्पप्रामाण्यम् / pratyakṣāt anumānam alpaprāmāṇyam / "inference has less validity than perception"

### Notes
Comparative suffix -tara on adjectives (satya-tara "truer"). Ablative expresses standard of comparison. Philosophical texts debate relative validity (prāmāṇya) of different epistemic sources. Alternative: explicit terms like adhika "more," nyūna "less."

---

## 18. RESULTATIVE/EVIDENTIAL CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**Proven/established as true**: Passive participles with instrumental agent/means express evidential basis. Structure: N(NOM) + INST + PARTICIPLE. Shows how truth-status was established.

### Examples
- प्रत्यक्षेण दृष्टम् / pratyakṣeṇa dṛṣṭam / "seen by perception, directly perceived"
- प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / pramāṇena siddham / "established by valid means, proven"

### Notes
Past passive participles (dṛṣṭa "seen," siddha "established," jñāta "known") with instrumental expressing evidential source. Common in epistemological contexts specifying how knowledge was validated. Multiple pramāṇas can be specified (pratyakṣeṇa, anumānena, śabdena).

---

## 19. CONDITIONAL TRUTH

### Pattern
**If true, then...**: Conditional constructions using yadi...tadā or cet...tarhi. Structure: yadi/cet + PROTASIS, tadā/tarhi + APODOSIS. Expresses hypothetical truth-conditions.

### Examples
- यदि सत्यम् तदा धर्मः / yadi satyam tadā dharmaḥ / "if true, then (it is) dharma"
- सत्ये सति धर्मः भवति / satye sati dharmaḥ bhavati / "truth existing, dharma arises"

### Notes
Two patterns: (1) yadi...tadā with finite verbs, (2) locative absolute (satye sati "truth existing") for conditional. Philosophical texts use conditionals to explore logical relationships between truth, validity, and other values. Alternative: gerund in -tvā for temporal sequence.

---

## 20. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL (ABSENCE OF TRUTH)

### Pattern
**There is no truth/proof**: Negative existential using na + asti or abhāva (absence). Structure: N(GEN) + abhāva or na + N(NOM) + asti. Expresses lack or non-existence.

### Examples
- प्रमाणाभावात् / pramāṇābhāvāt / "due to absence of proof"
- न सत्यम् अस्ति / na satyam asti / "there is no truth"

### Notes
Two strategies: (1) compound with abhāva "absence" (pramāṇa-abhāva), (2) negative particle with existential verb (na asti). Ablative of abhāva-compound expresses causal reasoning from absence. Common in philosophical refutation and skeptical arguments.
