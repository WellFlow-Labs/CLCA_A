# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
**Language: English (en)**

---

**1. true**
- Form: true
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *trēowe* — faithful, trustworthy
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used to predicate accuracy of statements, correspondence with facts, or genuineness of objects and persons. Participates in productive derivation: *truly*, *truth*, *truthful*, *untrue*. Applies across propositional, moral, and identity contexts ("a true friend," "that's true," "true gold").

**2. truth**
- Form: truth
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *trēowth* — the quality of being true
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Nominal form denoting the property of statements, beliefs, or states of affairs that correspond to reality. Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts. Participates in compounds: *truth-telling*, *truth-value*, *half-truth*, *untruth*.

**[CORE]**

**3. accurate**
- Form: accurate
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *accuratus* — done with care, from *ad-* + *cura* (care)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to descriptions, measurements, representations, and reports that correctly correspond to what they describe. Contrasts with *approximate* and *erroneous*. Productive derivation: *accuracy*, *accurately*, *inaccurate*.

**4. genuine**
- Form: genuine
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *genuinus* — native, natural, possibly from *genu* (knee, as in placed on the father's knee to acknowledge paternity)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used of objects, persons, emotions, and documents to indicate that they are what they purport to be, not counterfeit or simulated. Contrasts with *fake*, *spurious*, *artificial*. Productive derivation: *genuinely*, *genuineness*.

**5. authentic**
- Form: authentic
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Greek *authentikos* — original, primary, from *authentes* (one acting on one's own authority)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to objects, documents, persons, and experiences to indicate originality, legitimacy, or correspondence to an original. Common in legal, aesthetic, and philosophical contexts. Productive derivation: *authenticity*, *authenticate*, *authentically*, *inauthenticity*.

**6. valid**
- Form: valid
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *validus* — strong, effective, from *valere* (to be strong)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used in logical contexts to describe arguments whose conclusions follow necessarily from their premises, and in legal contexts to describe documents or acts that meet required conditions. Also used more broadly of claims and concerns. Productive derivation: *validity*, *validate*, *invalid*, *validation*.

**7. correct**
- Form: correct
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *correctus*, past participle of *corrigere* — to set right, from *com-* + *regere* (to guide)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Predicated of answers, beliefs, procedures, and conduct to indicate conformity with a standard, rule, or fact. Applies both to factual accuracy and to normative rightness. Productive derivation: *correctly*, *correction*, *incorrect*, *correctness*.

**8. right**
- Form: right
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old English *riht* — straight, just, proper
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Highly polysemous: used to evaluate statements as accurate, actions as morally or procedurally appropriate, and answers as correct. One of the most frequent evaluative terms in English. Productive derivation: *rightly*, *rightness*, *rightful*, *rights*.

**9. false**
- Form: false
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Latin *falsus* — deceived, feigned, from *fallere* (to deceive)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Predicated of statements, beliefs, appearances, and identities to indicate failure of correspondence with fact or reality, or deliberate misrepresentation. Contrasts directly with *true*. Productive derivation: *falsely*, *falseness*, *falsity*, *falsify*, *falsehood*.

**10. fake**
- Form: fake
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: origin uncertain; possibly from German *fegen* (to sweep, polish) via criminal slang
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Applied to objects, persons, documents, and emotions that are presented as genuine but are not. Common in everyday, journalistic, and legal registers. Functions as adjective, noun, and verb. Productive derivation: *faker*, *fakery*, *faked*.

**[CORE]**

**11. counterfeit**
- Form: counterfeit
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal meaning: Old French *contrefait* — made in imitation, from *contre-* (against, in opposition) + *fait* (made)
- Typical English translations: (source language)

Used specifically of objects — especially currency, documents, and goods — that are made to imitate genuine items with intent to deceive. Carries strong legal connotations. Functions as adjective, noun, and verb. Productive derivation: *counterfeiter*, *counterfeiting*.

Applied to claims, arguments, documents, and objects that appear genuine or well-founded but are not. More formal and domain-specific than *fake*; common in academic, legal, and scholarly registers. Productive derivation: *spuriously*, *spuriousness*.

**21. fidelity**

Denotes the degree of accuracy with which a reproduction, translation, or account corresponds to its original, as well as loyalty in personal and contractual relationships. Common in technical (audio reproduction), legal, and literary contexts. Productive derivation: related to *faithful*; contrasts with *infidelity*.

Applied to arguments, reasoning, judgments, and advice to indicate that they are well-founded, valid, and reliable. In formal logic, a *sound* argument is both valid and has true premises. Productive derivation: *soundly*, *soundness*, *unsound*.

**26. apocryphal**
