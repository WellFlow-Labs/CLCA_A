# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: totoo**

- **Root form:** totoo
- **P1 items participating:** 3 — *totoo* (item 1), *katotohanan* (item 2), *makatotohanan* (item 18/19)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Factual correspondence between a statement or representation and reality; the state of something being the case rather than false, simulated, or supposed. This root anchors the propositional and abstract dimensions of truth.

---

**Root 2: tunay**

- **Root form:** tunay
- **P1 items participating:** 3 direct morphological participants — *tunay* (item 3), *katunayan* (item 4), *pagpapatunay* (item 29); *di-tunay* (item 30) also participates.
- **Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, authenticity, and intrinsic realness of an entity, person, or relationship — emphasizing that something is what it claims or appears to be, rather than an imitation or pretense. Also anchors the evidentiary establishment of something as real.

---

**Root 3: tapat**

- **Root form:** tapat
- **P1 items participating:** 2 — *tapat* (item 13/20), *katapatan* (item 14/21)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Honesty, sincerity, and directness in communication and conduct; the absence of concealment or deception in a person's dealings with others. Also extends to relational faithfulness and loyalty.

**Note on selection:** *totoo* and *tunay* are the two most morphologically productive roots in the P1 data. *tapat* is selected as the third because it is the most productive native root in the honesty/sincerity subdomain and generates a distinct cluster of expressions not covered by *totoo* or *tunay*. *mali* is also moderately productive but its derivational family is smaller and more semantically uniform. *Tiwala* (trust) was considered as a fourth candidate but participates in only 2 P1 items directly (*tiwala*, *mapagkakatiwalaan*, *di-mapagkakatiwalaan*); it is highly productive morphologically but its core domain is trust/confidence rather than truth-evaluation per se.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: totoo

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo
**Literal breakdown:** Root *totoo*; possibly reduplicated base
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Context of use:** Base predicate used to affirm the truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs in everyday speech. Used predicatively to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality (*Totoo ba iyan?* — "Is that true?"). The foundational predicate of truth-assertion in everyday speech. Used across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Transliteration:** katotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-nan* / *-an* (circumfix forming abstract noun); "the state of being *totoo*"
**Natural English translation:** truth, the truth, reality, the true state of affairs
**Context of use:** The standard abstract noun for truth in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse. Used in formal declarations, moral exhortations, and Bible translations. *Sabihin ang katotohanan* ("tell the truth") is a standard fixed phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** makatotohanan
**Literal breakdown:** *maka-* + *totoo* + *-an*; "in accordance with or aligned with truth/reality"
**Natural English translation:** realistic, true-to-life, truthful, verisimilar
**Context of use:** Applied to literary, cinematic, and artistic works to evaluate whether their depictions correspond faithfully to reality. Used in aesthetic, narrative, and moral evaluation to assess whether a representation or depiction faithfully corresponds to reality. Common in film, literary, and journalistic criticism. Less common in purely propositional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** totoong-totoo
**Transliteration:** totoong-totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, really and truly real, genuinely genuine, very real
**Context of use:** Used for emphatic assertion that something is unambiguously real or true, often in response to expressed doubt (*Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro* "This is absolutely real, not a joke"). An intensified form used in emphatic speech to stress that something is unambiguously real or true. Common in spoken Tagalog and informal writing; common in emotional or emphatic registers of everyday speech. *Totoong-totoo ang nangyari* ("what happened was absolutely real").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** totoo naman
**Transliteration:** totoo naman
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *naman* (discourse particle marking contrast, concession, or turn-taking)
**Natural English translation:** it's true though, that's actually true, well, it is real, it really is true
**Context of use:** Used to concede or affirm a truth while managing conversational dynamics — often acknowledging a point made by another speaker (*Totoo naman na mahirap* "It's true though that it's difficult"). Used in conversational Tagalog to concede a point or affirm something while managing the flow of dialogue. The particle *naman* softens or contextualizes the assertion. Extremely common in spoken Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** totoo ba?
**Transliteration:** totoo ba?
**Literal breakdown:** *totoo* + *ba* (interrogative particle)
**Natural English translation:** Is it true? Really? Is that real?
**Context of use:** Standard interrogative formula for questioning the truth or reality of a claim or situation. Extremely common interrogative expression used in everyday conversation to request confirmation of a claim or to express surprise. Functions as a discourse-level truth-check. One of the most frequent utterances in Tagalog conversation when expressing surprise or seeking confirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**7.**
**Form:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** hindi totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *hindi* (negation) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** not true, untrue, not real, false
**Context of use:** Standard negation of *totoo*, used to deny the truth of a statement or the reality of something (*Hindi totoo ang balita* "The news is not true"). Used across all registers to deny the truth or reality of a proposition. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**8.**
**Form:** parang totoo
**Transliteration:** parang totoo
**Literal breakdown:** *parang* (like, as if, seemingly) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** as if it were real, seemingly true, lifelike, seems real, looks genuine
**Context of use:** Used to describe something that resembles reality closely enough to be mistaken for it, or that creates a convincing impression of truth (*Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak* "Her crying seemed real"). Used in everyday evaluative discourse to describe something that has the appearance of being real without asserting that it is. Common in aesthetic evaluation and in descriptions of deception; common in assessments of performances, stories, and objects.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
