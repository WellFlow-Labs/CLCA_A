| 1. Everyday truth / “what is really the case” | This cluster covers forms used when speakers talk about whether something is true, real, or actually the case. | 2. Falsehood, lying, and factual denial | These expressions are used when rejecting truth, accusing someone of lying, or describing something as false, fake, or baseless. They appear in everyday accusation, media language, legal-style criticism, and public argument. | பொய் (poy); பொய்யான (poyyāṉa); பொய் சொல் (poy col); பொய் பேசு (poy pēcu); பொய் குற்றச்சாட்டு (poy kuṟṟaccāṭṭu); பொய் வாக்குறுதி (poy vākkuruṭi); பொய்யாகி போ (poyyāki pō); உண்மையற்ற (uṇmaiyaṟṟa); உண்மைக்கு புறம்பாக (uṇmaikku puṟampāka); உண்மைக்கு மாறாக (uṇmaikku māṟāka); உண்மைக்குப் புறம்பான (uṇmaikkup puṟampāṉa); உண்மைக்கு மாறான (uṇmaikku māṟāṉa) | போலி belongs partly here, but in usage it is often more about non-genuine objects/documents than lying in speech.; உண்மையற்ற can describe claims, depictions, or allegations rather than deliberate lying. | They appear in everyday accusation, media language, legal-style criticism, and public argument. |
| 3. Correctness, propriety, and “okay/right” in practical use | This group centers on **சரி** and related expressions used for answers, actions, procedures, adequacy, and conversational agreement. | 4. Error, mistake, and fault | These forms are used when identifying something as wrong, mistaken, or faulty, whether in information, action, judgment, or conduct. In usage, this group often contrasts directly with **சரி** and sometimes also with moral evaluation. | தவறு (tavaṟu); தவறான (tavaṟāṉa); தவறு செய் (tavaṟu cey); தவறு பண்ணு (tavaṟu paṇṇu); தவறு கண்டுபிடி (tavaṟu kaṇṭupiṭi); தவறு ஒப்பு கொள் (tavaṟu oppu koḷ); தவறுக்கு மன்னிப்பு கேள் (tavaṟukku maṉṉippu kēḷ) | தவறு can refer to factual error or moral fault depending on context.; தவறான overlaps with “incorrect” but may also imply impropriety. | In usage, this group often contrasts directly with **சரி** and sometimes also with moral evaluation. |
| 5. Verification, confirmation, and reliability of information or sources | This cluster gathers expressions used when checking, confirming, establishing, or trusting information, testimony, or sources. These are especially common in administrative, journalistic, academic, and procedural settings. | உறுதி (uṟuti); உறுதியான (uṟudiyāṉa); உறுதி செய் (uṟuti cey); உறுதி அளி (uṟuti aḷi); உறுதி பெறு (uṟuti peṟu); உறுதி படுத்து (uṟuti paṭuttu); நம்பகமான (nampakamāṉa); நம்பகத்தன்மை (nampakattaṉmai); நம்பகமான ஆதாரம் (nampakamāṉa ātāram); நம்பகமான தகவல் (nampakamāṉa takaval); நம்பகமான சாட்சி (nampakamāṉa cāṭci); நம்பகத்தன்மை கொண்ட (nampakattaṉmai koṇṭa); நம்பகத்தன்மையை இழக்கு (nampakattaṉmaiyai iḻakku); நம்பகத்தன்மையை கேள்விக்குட்படுத்து (nampakattaṉmaiyai kēḷvikkkuṭpaṭuttu); சரிபார் (caripār); சரிபார்ப்பு (caripārppu); சரிபார்க்க (caripārkka) | சரிபார் belongs here because it is used for checking correctness, though it is built from the **சரி** family.; உறுதி can refer to certainty, assurance, or confirmation, not only factual verification. | These are especially common in administrative, journalistic, academic, and procedural settings. |
| 6. Matching, agreement, and comparison between versions/accounts | These expressions are used when one thing is checked against another for likeness, agreement, fit, or consistency. They are common in comparison of texts, reports, appearances, and source-based checking. | ஒத்த (otta); ஒற்றுமை (oṟṟumai); ஒத்துப் போ (ottup pō); ஒத்துவரு (ottuvaru); ஒத்துப் பார்க்க (ottup pārkka); ஒற்றுமை காணப்படுகிறது (oṟṟumai kāṇappaṭukiṟatu); ஒற்றுமை இல்லை (oṟṟumai illai); மூலத்துடன் ஒப்பிட்டு பார் (mūlattuṭaṉ oppiṭṭu pār) | ஒத்துவரு may refer to factual agreement, practical suitability, or personal fit.; ஒற்றுமை also has a broader “unity” use outside strict comparison. | They are common in comparison of texts, reports, appearances, and source-based checking. |
| 7. Authentic vs fake objects, documents, and versions | This group is about whether an item is genuine, original, or counterfeit. In usage, these forms are especially common in commerce, bureaucracy, legal reporting, media, and document verification. | போலி (pōli); அசல் (asal); மூலம் (mūlam); போலி ஆவணம் (pōli āvaṇam); போலி சான்றிதழ் (pōli cāṉṟitaḻ); போலி செய்தி (pōli ceyti); போலி கணக்கு (pōli kaṇakku); அசல் நகல் (asal nakal); அசல் பிரதியை காண்பி (asal piratiyai kāṇpi); மூலத்தின் அடிப்படையில் (mūlattiṉ aṭippaṭaiyil); மூலத்துடன் ஒப்பிட்டு பார் (mūlattuṭaṉ oppiṭṭu pār); நிஜமான (nijamāṉa) | போலி செய்தி overlaps with falsehood in public discourse, not just counterfeit objects.; நிஜமான can describe actual situations as well as genuine objects.; மூலம் is broader than authenticity alone; it also means source or basis. | In usage, these forms are especially common in commerce, bureaucracy, legal reporting, media, and document verification. |
| 8. Literary, philosophical, and religious “true/real” language built on மெய் | This cluster contains forms that are not the most ordinary everyday way to say “true,” but are established in formal, literary, philosophical, and religious usage. | 9. Moral fairness, justice, and justified conduct | These expressions are used when evaluating actions, decisions, treatment, or social order in terms of fairness, justice, or righteousness. | 10. Essence, principle, and underlying meaning | These forms are used when speakers or writers focus on the core point, essential substance, or underlying principle of something. They are common in explanation, interpretation, philosophy, and summary-style discourse. | மெய்ப்பொருள் (meypporuḷ); தத்துவம் (tattuvam); சாரம் (cāram); தத்துவம் பேசு (tattuvam pēcu); தத்துவ ரீதியாக (tattuva rītiyāka); சாரம் என்ன? | 11. Figurative representation and non-literal expression | This is a small, more specialized grouping around literary explanation and figurative use. It appears mainly in interpretive or rhetorical discussion rather than ordinary truth-evaluation. | உருவகம் (uruvakam); உருவகமாகப் பயன்படுத்து (uruvakamākap payaṉpaṭuttu); உருவகமாகச் சொல் (uruvakamākac col) | உருவகம் can interact with discussions of surface meaning vs deeper meaning, but its main usage here is literary/rhetorical. | It appears mainly in interpretive or rhetorical discussion rather than ordinary truth-evaluation. |
| 12.
