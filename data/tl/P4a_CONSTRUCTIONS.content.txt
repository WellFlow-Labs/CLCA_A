# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**Predicate-initial construction with topic marker *ang***: The truth-domain term appears in predicate position, followed by the topic marker *ang* and the subject/topic. Basic structure: [PREDICATE] + ang + [TOPIC].

### Examples
- **Totoo ang sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: totoo ang sinabi niya
  - Translation: "What he/she said is true." / "His/her statement is true."

- **Mali ang sagot mo.**
  - Transliteration: mali ang sagot mo
  - Translation: "Your answer is wrong."

### Notes
This is the unmarked attributive construction in Tagalog. The truth-domain term functions as predicate, with the topic marked by *ang*. The construction is non-verbal; no copula is required. Word order is relatively fixed with predicate-initial position.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**Negation with *hindi* preceding the predicate**: The negative particle *hindi* appears before the truth-domain predicate, maintaining the same predicate-topic structure. Structure: hindi + [PREDICATE] + ang + [TOPIC].

### Examples
- **Hindi totoo ang balita.**
  - Transliteration: hindi totoo ang balita
  - Translation: "The news is not true." / "The news is false."

- **Hindi tama ang ginawa mo.**
  - Transliteration: hindi tama ang ginawa mo
  - Translation: "What you did is not right/correct."

### Notes
Standard negation uses *hindi* in pre-predicate position. The alternative negator *di-* can attach directly to the predicate (e.g., *di-totoo*) but is less emphatic. The construction maintains predicate-initial word order even with negation.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**Linker construction with *na/ng***: Truth-domain terms modify nouns using the linker *na* (after vowels) or *-ng* (after consonants). Structure: [MODIFIER] + na/ng + [NOUN] or [NOUN] + na/ng + [MODIFIER].

### Examples
- **tunay na kaibigan**
  - Transliteration: tunay na kaibigan
  - Translation: "true friend" / "genuine friend"

- **maling impormasyon**
  - Transliteration: maling impormasyon
  - Translation: "wrong information" / "false information"

### Notes
The linker *na/ng* connects modifiers to head nouns. Modifier-noun order is more common than noun-modifier for truth-domain terms. The linker form depends on phonological environment: *na* after vowels, *-ng* (enclitic) after consonants.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**Verbal constructions with *mag-* or *ma-* prefixes plus aspectual marking**: Inchoative meaning expressed through verbal derivation with progressive or completive aspect. Common patterns include *nag-* (completed actor voice), *nag-...-an* (progressive), and *magka-* (accidental/resultative).

### Examples
- **Nagtotoo ang panaginip ko.**
  - Transliteration: nagtotoo ang panaginip ko
  - Translation: "My dream is coming true." / "My dream is being realized."

- **Nagkamali ako.**
  - Transliteration: nagkamali ako
  - Translation: "I made a mistake." / "I became wrong/mistaken."

### Notes
The inchoative is expressed through verbal aspect rather than a dedicated "become" construction. *Nagtotoo* (progressive of *magtotoo*) indicates ongoing realization. *Magkamali* uses the *magka-* prefix indicating accidental or resultative change of state. Different roots show different preferred patterns.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Causative prefix *pa-* combined with voice marking**: Causative constructions use the prefix *pa-* with various voice affixes. Common pattern: *pa-* + ROOT + *-in* (patient voice) or *pa-* + ROOT + *-an* (locative voice). Structure varies by intended focus.

### Examples
- **Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo.**
  - Transliteration: patunayan mo ang sinabi mo
  - Translation: "Prove what you said." / "Verify your statement."

- **Pinatotohanan niya ang kanyang salita.**
  - Transliteration: pinatotohanan niya ang kanyang salita
  - Translation: "He/she is proving his/her word true." / "He/she is making his/her word come true."

### Notes
The causative *pa-* combines with voice affixes to create "make/cause to be X" meanings. *Patunayan* (pa- + tunay + -an) means "cause to be proven/genuine." Not all truth-domain roots productively form causatives; *patunayan* is highly lexicalized. The construction requires appropriate voice marking for grammatical focus.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Circumfix *ka-...-an* forming abstract nouns**: The most productive pattern uses the circumfix *ka-...-an* to derive abstract nouns denoting the quality or state. Structure: ka- + ROOT + -an.

### Examples
- **katotohanan**
  - Transliteration: katotohanan
  - Translation: "truth" / "reality" / "the truth"
  - (from *totoo* "true")

- **katapatan**
  - Transliteration: katapatan
  - Translation: "honesty" / "sincerity" / "loyalty"
  - (from *tapat* "honest/faithful")

### Notes
The *ka-...-an* circumfix is highly productive for creating abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives. The resulting forms denote the abstract quality or state rather than instances. Alternative patterns exist: *pagka-* + ROOT (e.g., *pagkatunay* "genuineness") and *pag-* + ROOT (e.g., *pagtotoo* "realization"), but *ka-...-an* is most common for stative qualities.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Lexicalized agent nouns and *mang-...-* derivations**: Agent nouns are often lexicalized (e.g., *sinungaling* "liar") rather than productively derived. Some roots allow *mang-* prefix for habitual agents, though this is limited in the truth domain.

### Examples
- **sinungaling**
  - Transliteration: sinungaling
  - Translation: "liar" / "one who lies"
  - (lexicalized agent noun, not transparently derived)

- **mandaraya**
  - Transliteration: mandaraya
  - Translation: "cheater" / "one who cheats"
  - (from *daya* "cheat" with *mang-* prefix → *man-* before /d/)

### Notes
Unlike abstract nominalization, agentive nominalization is not fully productive in the truth domain. *Sinungaling* is a lexicalized form without transparent derivational morphology. The *mang-* prefix (with phonological variants) can create habitual agent nouns from some roots (*mandaya* → *mandaraya*), but many truth-domain terms lack common agentive forms. There is no general productive pattern equivalent to English "-er" for this semantic field.

---

## 8. EXISTENTIAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Existential predicates *may* (has/there is) and *walang* (without/there is no)**: These existential markers combine with abstract nouns to assert presence or absence. Structure: may/walang + [ABSTRACT NOUN].

### Examples
- **May katotohanan ang sinabi niya.**
  - Transliteration: may katotohanan ang sinabi niya
  - Translation: "What he/she said has truth." / "There is truth to what he/she said."

- **Walang katunayan ang akusasyon.**
  - Transliteration: walang katunayan ang akusasyon
  - Translation: "The accusation has no proof." / "The accusation is without evidence."

### Notes
Existential constructions are highly productive with abstract nouns from the truth domain. *May* asserts existence/possession, *walang* asserts non-existence/lack. These constructions are distinct from simple attribution and often carry pragmatic implications about sufficiency or validity of evidence/truth.

---

## 9. EVIDENTIAL/EPISTEMIC CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Prepositional phrases with *ayon sa* (according to) and *batay sa* (based on)**: These constructions frame statements as grounded in truth, evidence, or facts. Structure: ayon sa / batay sa + [ABSTRACT NOUN].

### Examples
- **Ayon sa katotohanan, wala siyang kasalanan.**
  - Transliteration: ayon sa katotohanan, wala siyang kasalanan
  - Translation: "According to the truth, he/she is not guilty." / "In truth, he/she has no fault."

- **Batay sa katunayan, tama ang desisyon.**
  - Transliteration: batay sa katunayan, tama ang desisyon
  - Translation: "Based on the evidence, the decision is correct."

### Notes
These prepositional constructions function as evidential or epistemic frames, indicating the basis for assertions. They commonly appear with abstract nouns like *katotohanan* (truth) and *katunayan* (evidence/proof). The constructions serve discourse-organizing functions, establishing the grounds for claims.

---

## 10. DISCOURSE MARKER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Particle combinations functioning as discourse markers**: Truth-domain terms combine with particles (*lang*, *naman*, *nga*, *ba*) to create discourse-managing expressions. These often have pragmatic rather than purely semantic functions.

### Examples
- **Sa totoo lang, ayaw ko na.**
  - Transliteration: sa totoo lang, ayaw ko na
  - Translation: "To be honest, I don't want to anymore." / "Honestly, I'm done."

- **Totóhanán, hindi ka ba galit?**
  - Transliteration: totóhanán, hindi ka ba galit
  - Translation: "Seriously, aren't you angry?" / "For real, you're not mad?"

### Notes
These constructions have grammaticalized into discourse markers with specific pragmatic functions: *sa totoo lang* introduces frank admissions, *totóhanán* marks serious intent or questions. The truth-domain semantics contribute to pragmatic meaning (honesty, seriousness) but the constructions function as fixed expressions managing conversational flow rather than making truth-value assertions.

---

## 11. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Comparative with *mas* (more) and superlative with *pinaka-* (most)**: Degree comparison uses the adverb *mas* for comparatives and the prefix *pinaka-* for superlatives. Structure: mas + [ADJECTIVE] or pinaka- + [ADJECTIVE].

### Examples
- **Mas totoo ang kuwento niya kaysa sa iyo.**
  - Transliteration: mas totoo ang kuwento niya kaysa sa iyo
  - Translation: "His/her story is more true than yours." / "His/her account is truer than yours."

- **Ito ang pinakatamang sagot.**
  - Transliteration: ito ang pinakatamang sagot
  - Translation: "This is the most correct answer." / "This is the rightest answer."

### Notes
Tagalog uses analytic comparison rather than inflectional morphology. *Mas* precedes the adjective for comparative degree; *kaysa (sa)* introduces the standard of comparison. *Pinaka-* prefixes to the adjective for superlative meaning. Both patterns are fully productive with truth-domain terms.

---

## 12. INTENSIFICATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Reduplication and intensifying particles**: Intensification achieved through full reduplication with linker (*ADJECTIVE + na + ADJECTIVE*) or intensifying adverbs like *sobrang* (very/extremely). Structure: [ADJ] + na + [ADJ] or sobrang + [ADJ].

### Examples
- **Totoong-totoo ang nangyari.**
  - Transliteration: totoong-totoo ang nangyari
  - Translation: "What happened is very true." / "It's absolutely real what occurred."

- **Sobrang mali ang ginawa niya.**
  - Transliteration: sobrang mali ang ginawa niya
  - Translation: "What he/she did is very wrong." / "His/her action is extremely incorrect."

### Notes
Reduplication with the linker creates emphatic intensification, stressing the high degree or complete nature of the quality. *Sobrang* (from Spanish *sobra* "excess") functions as a degree intensifier. Both patterns are productive across truth-domain adjectives. Reduplication often carries additional connotations of emphasis or removal of doubt.

---

## 13. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Concessive particles *naman* and *man* with truth-domain predicates**: These particles acknowledge a truth while suggesting it doesn't affect the conclusion. Structure: [PREDICATE] + naman/man.

### Examples
- **Totoo naman, pero hindi sapat.**
  - Transliteration: totoo naman, pero hindi sapat
  - Translation: "It is true though, but it's not enough."

- **Tama man, mali pa rin ang paraan.**
  - Transliteration: tama man, mali pa rin ang paraan
  - Translation: "Even if correct, the method is still wrong."

### Notes
*Naman* and *man* mark concessive meaning, acknowledging the truth-value while introducing a contrasting or limiting point. *Naman* is more colloquial; *man* is more formal/literary. These constructions are common in argumentative discourse where partial agreement precedes disagreement or qualification.

---

## 14. CONDITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Conditional marker *kung* (if) with truth-domain predicates**: Standard conditional construction uses *kung* to introduce the protasis. Structure: kung + [PREDICATE], [APODOSIS].

### Examples
- **Kung totoo ang sinabi mo, patawarin kita.**
  - Transliteration: kung totoo ang sinabi mo, patawarin kita
  - Translation: "If what you said is true, I'll forgive you."

- **Kung mali ako, sabihin mo.**
  - Transliteration: kung mali ako, sabihin mo
  - Translation: "If I'm wrong, tell me."

### Notes
*Kung* introduces conditional clauses with truth-domain predicates functioning as conditions. The construction is fully productive. The apodosis (consequent clause) follows, often separated by a comma in writing. This is the standard conditional pattern in Tagalog, not specific to truth-domain terms but commonly used with them in reasoning and argumentation.

---

## 15. INTERROGATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Question particle *ba* following the predicate**: Yes/no questions formed by adding the enclitic particle *ba* after the predicate. Structure: [PREDICATE] + ba + [TOPIC/ARGUMENTS]?

### Examples
- **Totoo ba ang balita?**
  - Transliteration: totoo ba ang balita
  - Translation: "Is the news true?" / "Is it true, the news?"

- **Tama ba ang sagot ko?**
  - Transliteration: tama ba ang sagot ko
  - Translation: "Is my answer correct?" / "Am I right?"

### Notes
The question particle *ba* is enclitic, attaching to the first major constituent (typically the predicate in predicate-initial sentences). This is the standard yes/no question formation in Tagalog. The construction is fully productive with all truth-domain predicates. Rising intonation may accompany *ba* but is not required for question interpretation.

---

## 16. EMPHATIC AFFIRMATION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Affirmative particle *nga* following the predicate**: Emphatic affirmation uses the particle *nga* to confirm or emphasize the truth of the predicate. Structure: [PREDICATE] + nga.

### Examples
- **Tama nga ako!**
  - Transliteration: tama nga ako
  - Translation: "I am right indeed!" / "I was correct after all!"

- **Totoo nga ang sabi nila.**
  - Transliteration: totoo nga ang sabi nila
  - Translation: "What they said is indeed true." / "Their statement is really true."

### Notes
*Nga* is an emphatic particle confirming or validating the predicate, often used when something was previously doubted or questioned. It can express vindication, confirmation of suspicions, or emphatic agreement. The particle is enclitic and follows the predicate or first major constituent.

---

## 17. CESSATION/SUFFICIENCY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Specialized construction with *tama na* (enough/stop)**: The form *tama* combines with the particle *na* (already/now) to express sufficiency or request cessation. Structure: tama na + (optional demonstrative/topic).

### Examples
- **Tama na yan!**
  - Transliteration: tama na yan
  - Translation: "That's enough!" / "Stop that!"

- **Tama na, ayoko na.**
  - Transliteration: tama na, ayoko na
  - Translation: "Enough, I don't want to anymore." / "Stop, I'm done."

### Notes
This represents a semantic extension from correctness to sufficiency/cessation, specific to *tama*. The construction functions as a directive or statement of sufficiency rather than an assertion about correctness. *Na* marks completion or current state. The construction can be made polite with *po*: *tama na po*. This pattern does not extend to other truth-domain terms.

---

## 18. PRONOMINAL ATTRIBUTION CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Truth-domain predicate with pronominal topic**: Predicates combine with pronouns (*ako*, *ka*, *siya*) as topics to attribute truth-values to persons' statements or judgments. Structure: [PREDICATE] + [PRONOUN].

### Examples
- **Mali ka.**
  - Transliteration: mali ka
  - Translation: "You're wrong." / "You are mistaken."

- **Tama ako.**
  - Transliteration: tama ako
  - Translation: "I'm right." / "I'm correct."

### Notes
This construction attributes correctness/incorrectness to persons rather than propositions, implicitly referring to their statements, judgments, or actions. The pronouns function as topics in the standard predicate-topic structure. This is extremely common in conversational Tagalog for agreement/disagreement. The construction is fully productive with evaluative truth-domain terms (*tama*, *mali*, *totoo* in some contexts).
