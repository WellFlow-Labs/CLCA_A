# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note:** This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. Derivational forms documented in P2a are excluded. Where no common construction is identified for a given item, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are drawn from attested usage in Yoruba oral tradition, legal discourse, religious texts, and documented literary and journalistic sources. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and naturalness.

The following general observations are supported by the P1/P1b/P2a data and are stated here to avoid repetition in individual entries:

- Yoruba constructions frequently use the complementizer *pé* to introduce propositional complements.
- The verb *sọ* (to say, speak) is highly productive in constructions involving truth and falsehood.
- Negation is expressed by *kò* (verbal negation) or *àì-* (nominal/adverbial negation).
- The focus particle *ni* and the relativizer *tó* appear in cleft and relative constructions.
- Serial verb constructions are a major syntactic pattern in Yoruba and appear in several entries below.
- Yoruba is an SVO language with post-nominal modification. Many "constructions" in Yoruba are syntactically transparent (verb + object, noun + modifier) but are nonetheless fixed or semi-fixed in usage. These are included where the combination is conventionalized beyond its compositional parts. Purely compositional and non-fixed combinations are excluded.

## Constructions by Base Item

---

### Base: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** speak the truth; tell the truth
**Usage Notes:** The most basic and widely attested verbal construction with *òtítọ́*. The verb *sọ* (to say, to speak) combines with *òtítọ́* as its object to produce the standard expression for truth-telling. Appears in everyday speech, legal contexts, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó sọ òtítọ́ fún mi
**Transliteration:** Ó sọ òtítọ́ fún mi
**English Translation:** He/she told me the truth
**Usage Notes:** The most productive verbal construction with *òtítọ́*. The verb *sọ* (to say/speak) takes *òtítọ́* as its object and *fún* introduces the beneficiary/recipient. Appears across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo.
**Transliteration:** Sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo.
**English Translation:** Always speak the truth. / Tell the truth at all times.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed moral injunction used in parenting, religious instruction, and moral discourse. The construction *sọ òtítọ́ nígbà gbogbo* ("speak truth at all times") is a conventionalized imperative formula appearing in proverb-adjacent speech and moral teaching.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ọ̀nà òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ọ̀nà òtítọ́
**English Translation:** the way of truth; the path of truth
**Usage Notes:** A fixed nominal phrase used in moral and religious discourse to describe the course of conduct characterized by truthfulness and integrity. Appears in proverbs, preaching, and moral instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Dúró sí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** dúró sí òtítọ́
**English Translation:** stand by the truth; hold to the truth; remain committed to the truth
**Usage Notes:** A verbal phrase using *dúró* (to stand, to remain) with the directional *sí* (toward/by) and *òtítọ́*. Used in moral and legal contexts to describe steadfast commitment to truthfulness under pressure.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**Transliteration:** Ó dúró mọ́ òtítọ́.
**English Translation:** He/she stood by the truth. / He/she adhered to the truth.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using *dúró mọ́* ("to stand by, to adhere to") expressing steadfast commitment to truth. Attested in moral, legal, and religious discourse as a description of integrity under pressure.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Gbà òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** gbà òtítọ́
**English Translation:** accept the truth; receive the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbà* (to accept, to receive) with *òtítọ́* as object. Describes the act of acknowledging or accepting a true statement, particularly when it is unwelcome or contrary to one's prior belief. Common in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**English Translation:** it is the truth; that is true (emphatic)
**Usage Notes:** A copular-focus construction using the focus marker *ni* to assert emphatically that something is true. The focus marker *ni* follows the predicate nominal to produce an emphatic identificational assertion. Extremely common in everyday and formal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ kò ní kú
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ kò ní kú
**English Translation:** truth will not die; truth is imperishable
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial expression asserting the indestructibility of truth. *Kò ní* is the negative future construction ("will not"); *kú* means "die." Appears in moral discourse and in legal contexts to assert that the truth will eventually emerge. Widely attested in Yoruba oral tradition and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ló ga
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ló ga
**English Translation:** truth is what is supreme; truth is the highest
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* = *ni* + *o*, focus marker on subject) asserting the supremacy of truth over other values. Appears in proverbs and in moral and legal discourse. The verb *ga* means "to be high, to be supreme."
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀
**Transliteration:** fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀
**English Translation:** speak with truth; speak truthfully
**Usage Notes:** An instrumental construction using *fi* (to use, to employ) + *òtítọ́* + *sọ̀rọ̀* (to speak/talk). Describes the manner of speaking as characterized by truthfulness. Appears in moral instruction and in assessments of testimony.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ẹni tó bá sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó bá sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** whoever speaks the truth; the one who tells the truth
**Usage Notes:** A relative clause construction using *ẹni* (person/one) + *tó bá* (who/whoever, conditional relative) + *sọ òtítọ́*. Used in proverbial and moral discourse to introduce a general statement about truth-tellers. The *bá* element marks the conditional/generic reading.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**English Translation:** truth saves one; truth rescues a person
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction in which *òtítọ́* is the subject of the verb *gbani lọ́wọ́* (to save/rescue a person; *gbà* + *ni* + *lọ́wọ́* "from hand/trouble"). Asserts the protective or salvific function of truth. Appears in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́.
**English Translation:** It is truth that saves one. / Truth is what rescues a person.
**Usage Notes:** A focus construction (*ló* = focus marker on *ni*) asserting the salvific or protective power of truth. Attested in moral and religious discourse; appears in proverb-like formulations and sermon language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ
**Transliteration:** jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ
**English Translation:** let truth be your guide; allow truth to lead you
**Usage Notes:** A hortative construction using *jẹ́ kí* (let/allow) + *òtítọ́* as subject + *jẹ́* (be/become) + *olùdarí* (guide/leader) + *rẹ* (your). Used in moral instruction and in religious preaching to urge commitment to truth as a guiding principle.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ
**English Translation:** It is the truth that he/she spoke; What he/she spoke is the truth
**Usage Notes:** A cleft construction using the focus particle *ni* to topicalize *òtítọ́*. Used in legal and argumentative contexts to assert emphatically that what was said was true. The focus structure places strong emphasis on the truthfulness of the statement.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**English Translation:** There is no truth in his/her words; His/her words contain no truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses the existential negative *kò sí* (there is not) with a locative phrase *nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* (in his/her words). Used in legal and everyday contexts to reject the truthfulness of a statement entirely.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** Let us speak the truth; Let us tell the truth
**Usage Notes:** A hortative construction using *jẹ́ kí* (let, allow that) with first-person plural *a*. Used in moral exhortation, preaching, and deliberative contexts to call for truthful speech. Widely attested in formal and semi-formal registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó jẹ́wọ́ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** Ó jẹ́wọ́ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** He/she confessed the truth; He/she admitted the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses the verb *jẹ́wọ́* (to confess, admit, acknowledge) with *òtítọ́* as object. Used in legal and religious contexts for the act of admitting or confessing what is true, particularly after prior concealment or denial.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni
**English Translation:** It became clear that it is the truth; It turned out to be true
**Usage Notes:** Uses the verb *farahàn* (to become clear, to appear, to be revealed) with the complementizer *pé* introducing the propositional content. Used in narrative and legal contexts when the truth of a matter is established after investigation or deliberation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ẹ bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** Ẹ bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí sọ òtítọ́
**English Translation:** Begin to speak the truth; Start telling the truth
**Usage Notes:** An imperative construction using *bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí* (to begin to) as an aspectual auxiliary. Used in interrogation and moral exhortation contexts to demand that a person start being truthful.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́
**Transliteration:** Ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́
**English Translation:** He/she is concealing the truth; He/she is hiding the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *pa...mọ́* (to keep/conceal) with *òtítọ́* as the object. The progressive marker *ń* indicates ongoing action. Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe deliberate concealment of true information.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn
**Transliteration:** Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn
**English Translation:** He/she revealed the truth; He/she showed the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *fi...hàn* (to use/take...to show/reveal) with *òtítọ́* as the object of *fi*. Used in narrative and legal contexts for the act of bringing the truth to light.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́
**Transliteration:** Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́
**English Translation:** He/she accepted the truth; He/she received the truth
**Usage Notes:** Uses the serial verb construction *gbà...gbọ́* (to accept/receive...to hear/believe) with *òtítọ́* as object. Used in religious and everyday contexts for the act of accepting a true statement or teaching.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Ìwádìí òtítọ́ ń lọ síwájú.
**Transliteration:** Ìwádìí òtítọ́ ń lọ síwájú.
**English Translation:** The investigation of the truth is proceeding. / The truth inquiry is moving forward.
**Usage Notes:** A predicative construction used in journalistic and legal discourse to describe an ongoing truth-finding process. The progressive aspect marker *ń* + motion verb *lọ síwájú* ("go forward") is a conventionalized expression for ongoing progress.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Example:** Òtítọ́ bí ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀, ó ń tọ́ ojú ọ̀nà.
**Transliteration:** Òtítọ́ bí ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀, ó ń tọ́ ojú ọ̀nà.
**English Translation:** Truth is like the divination chain — it points the way correctly.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial comparison (*bí* = "like") attested in Yoruba oral literature, comparing truth to the *ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀* (Ifá divination chain) as a reliable guide. Documents the intersection of truth and divination in Yoruba epistemology.
**Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### Base: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood, untruth)

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** tell a lie; speak a falsehood
**Usage Notes:** The direct parallel to *sọ òtítọ́*; the verb *sọ* (to say/speak) takes *irọ́* as its object. The most basic and widely attested verbal construction with *irọ́*. Appears in everyday speech, legal contexts, and moral condemnation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó sọ irọ́ fún mi
**Transliteration:** Ó sọ irọ́ fún mi
**English Translation:** He/she told me a lie; He/she lied to me
**Usage Notes:** The primary verbal construction with *irọ́*, parallel to *sọ òtítọ́ fún*. Highly productive across all registers. Used in moral condemnation and legal contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Sọ irọ́ fún ènìyàn
**Transliteration:** Sọ irọ́ fún ènìyàn
**English Translation:** To tell a lie to someone / to lie to a person
**Usage Notes:** The standard ditransitive construction for lying to a specific person, using *fún* ("for/to") to introduce the recipient. Attested across all registers in moral instruction and legal testimony.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ò ní gbádùn
**Transliteration:** irọ́ ò ní gbádùn
**English Translation:** falsehood will not prosper; a lie will not succeed
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial expression asserting that lies ultimately fail. *Ò ní* is the negative future; *gbádùn* means "to enjoy, to prosper, to succeed." Appears in moral discourse and in legal contexts to assert the ultimate failure of deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ kò ní gbádùn.
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ kò ní gbádùn.
**English Translation:** A lie will not prosper. / Falsehood will not be enjoyed/succeed.
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial assertion using the negative future construction *kò ní* + verb, asserting that deception ultimately fails. Attested in moral and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúrú
**Transliteration:** irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúrú
**English Translation:** a lie has short legs; falsehood has short legs
**Usage Notes:** A well-known Yoruba proverb asserting that lies cannot travel far — they are eventually exposed. *Ẹsẹ̀* means "leg/foot"; *kúrú* means "short." Appears in moral instruction and in legal discourse about the eventual exposure of false testimony.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúkúrú.
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúkúrú.
**English Translation:** A lie has short legs. / Falsehood has short legs.
**Usage Notes:** A widely attested Yoruba proverb asserting that lies cannot travel far or endure; used in moral instruction and in contexts of exposing deception. The construction is fixed and formulaic.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Má sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** má sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** do not tell a lie; do not speak falsehood
**Usage Notes:** The negative imperative construction using *má* (do not) + *sọ irọ́*. The most direct moral injunction against lying. Appears in moral instruction, religious teaching, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ kò ní dúró
**Transliteration:** irọ́ kò ní dúró
**English Translation:** a lie will not stand; falsehood cannot endure
**Usage Notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the impermanence of falsehood. *Dúró* means "to stand, to remain, to endure." Contrasts with the permanence of truth (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú*). Appears in moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Gbọ́ irọ́
**Transliteration:** gbọ́ irọ́
**English Translation:** believe a lie; accept a falsehood; be taken in by a lie
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbọ́* (to hear, to believe, to accept) with *irọ́* as object. Describes the act of being deceived or of accepting a false statement as true. Appears in moral warnings and in assessments of gullibility or deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**English Translation:** whoever tells a lie will bear guilt/sin; one who lies will be guilty
**Usage Notes:** A conditional moral statement linking the act of lying (*sọ irọ́*) to moral culpability (*jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*, "bear sin/guilt"). Uses the conditional relative *tó bá* and the future marker *á*. Appears in moral and religious instruction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ni ó sọ
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ ni ó sọ
**English Translation:** It is a lie that he/she spoke; What he/she said is a lie
**Usage Notes:** A cleft construction using the focus particle *ni* to topicalize *irọ́*, parallel to *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ*. Used in legal and argumentative contexts to assert emphatically that what was said was false.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**English Translation:** There is no lie in his/her words; His/her words contain no falsehood
**Usage Notes:** Parallel negative existential construction to *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*, used to assert the truthfulness of a statement by denying the presence of falsehood.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó ń pa irọ́
**Transliteration:** Ó ń pa irọ́
**English Translation:** He/she is telling lies; He/she is lying
**Usage Notes:** Uses the verb *pa* (to kill, but idiomatically in this construction: to tell/spin) with *irọ́* as object. The progressive marker *ń* indicates ongoing action. This construction (*pa irọ́*) is a common alternative to *sọ irọ́* and appears frequently in everyday speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ò gbọdọ̀ jẹ́ ọ̀nà rẹ
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ ò gbọdọ̀ jẹ́ ọ̀nà rẹ
**English Translation:** Lying must not be his/her way; Falsehood must not be his/her path
**Usage Notes:** A normative construction using the prohibitive *ò gbọdọ̀* (must not) with the copula *jẹ́* (to be). Used in moral instruction and preaching to prohibit habitual deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó
**Transliteration:** Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó
**English Translation:** He/she used lies to obtain money; He/she obtained money through deception
**Usage Notes:** Uses the instrumental serial verb construction *fi...gbà* (to use...to obtain/receive) with *irọ́* as the instrument. Used in legal and moral contexts to describe fraud or deception for material gain.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Irọ́ ló sọ, kì í ṣe òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** Irọ́ ló sọ, kì í ṣe òtítọ́
**English Translation:** It is a lie he/she spoke, it is not the truth
**Usage Notes:** A contrastive construction using the focus marker *ló* (emphatic form of *ni* + *ó*) and the negative copula *kì í ṣe* (it is not). Used in legal and argumentative contexts to explicitly contrast falsehood with truth.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó ń gbìyànjú láti bo irọ́ rẹ̀ pamọ́

**English Translation:** He/she is trying to conceal his/her lie; He/she is attempting to hide his/her falsehood
**Usage Notes:** Uses *gbìyànjú láti* (to try/attempt to) with the serial verb *bo...pamọ́* (to cover/conceal). Used in legal and narrative contexts to describe deliberate concealment of a deception.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** irọ́

**Transliteration:** A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni
**English Translation:** We know that it is a lie; It is known to be a lie
**Usage Notes:** Uses *mọ̀ pé* (to know that) with the copular focus construction *irọ́ ni* (it is a lie). Used in legal and everyday contexts to assert established knowledge of a falsehood.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Construction 20**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Ó mú irọ́ wá.
**Transliteration:** Ó mú irọ́ wá.
**English Translation:** He/she brought a lie. / He/she came with a falsehood.
**Usage Notes:** A serial verb construction (*mú* = to take/bring + *wá* = to come) used to describe someone who presents a false account or fabricated story. Attested in everyday and legal discourse.

**Construction 21**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Example:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀.
**Transliteration:** Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀.
**English Translation:** There is no lie in it. / It contains no falsehood.
**Usage Notes:** A negated existential construction used to affirm the truthfulness of a statement or account by denying the presence of falsehood. Attested in testimony and affirmation contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---
