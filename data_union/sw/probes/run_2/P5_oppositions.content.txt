# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Opposition Records

---

**Opposition 1**

1. **Term A:** *kweli*
2. **Term B:** *uongo*
3. **Transliteration:** kweli / uongo
4. **English translation:** truth / lie; true / false
5. **Description of contrast:** These two items are explicitly paired in the binary evaluative construction *kweli au uongo* ("true or false?") documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 30) and P2b (Item 1, Construction 5; Item 3, Construction 4). They represent the primary lexical opposition in the truth-evaluation domain, contrasting a statement that corresponds to reality with one that does not.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 2**

1. **Term A:** *ukweli*
2. **Term B:** *uongo*
3. **Transliteration:** ukweli / uongo
4. **English translation:** truth / falsehood; truthfulness / lying
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are abstract *u-* class nominals. *Ukweli* denotes the property or state of being true; *uongo* denotes the property or state of being false or deceptive. The proverbial construction *ukweli nusu ni uongo* ("a half-truth is a lie"), documented in P2b (Item 2, Construction 11), explicitly bridges and contrasts the two. P1 (Item 3) identifies *uongo* as contrasting directly with *ukweli*.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 3**

1. **Term A:** *halisi*
2. **Term B:** *bandia*
3. **Transliteration:** halisi / bandia
4. **English translation:** genuine / fake; authentic / counterfeit
5. **Description of contrast:** These two items are explicitly contrasted in the construction *bidhaa halisi, si bandia* ("genuine goods, not fake"), documented in P2b (Item 4, Construction 3) and P2b (Item 6, Construction 5). *Halisi* evaluates an object as authentic; *bandia* evaluates it as counterfeit or artificial. P3 (Group 1 and Group 2 notes) identifies this pairing as the primary evaluative frame for *halisi* in commercial and legal contexts.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 4**

1. **Term A:** *halali*
2. **Term B:** *haramu*
3. **Transliteration:** halali / haramu
4. **English translation:** lawful / forbidden; permissible / prohibited
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 (Item 30) explicitly identifies *haramu* (from Arabic *ḥarām*) as the standard antonym of *halali* in both religious and legal discourse. P2a (Item 30, Derived Form 2) documents *haramu* as the primary contrastive term, noting it is not a morphological derivation from *halali* but the lexical opposite. The contrast operates in Islamic dietary law, legal permissibility, and moral evaluation.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 5**

1. **Term A:** *ni kweli*
2. **Term B:** *si kweli*
3. **Transliteration:** ni kweli / si kweli
4. **English translation:** it is true / it is not true
5. **Description of contrast:** *Ni* (copula) and *si* (negative copula) are invariant and directly parallel. P1b (Root 1, Items 28–29) documents both forms; P4a (§1 and §2) and P4b describe them as fully parallel constructions. *Ni kweli* asserts truth; *si kweli* denies it. The same pattern extends to *ni sahihi* / *si sahihi*, *ni halisi* / *si halisi*, and *ni halali* / *si halali*.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Opposition 6**

1. **Term A:** *ni sahihi*
2. **Term B:** *hii si sahihi*
3. **Transliteration:** ni sahihi / hii si sahihi
4. **English translation:** it is correct / this is not correct
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b (Item 7, Construction 6) documents *hii si sahihi* as the standard construction for evaluating a statement, procedure, or document as incorrect or invalid. The positive *ni sahihi* is the direct affirmative counterpart. The contrast is constructional, using the *ni* / *si* copular opposition applied specifically to the correctness predicate *sahihi*.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 7**

1. **Term A:** *mwaminifu*
2. **Term B:** *msaliti*
3. **Transliteration:** mwaminifu / msaliti
4. **English translation:** faithful/trustworthy person / traitor/betrayer
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 (Item 20) explicitly notes that *mwaminifu* (a trustworthy, faithful person) contrasts with *msaliti* (traitor, betrayer). Both are agentive *m-* class nominals evaluating persons by their reliability and loyalty. *Mwaminifu* is the positive pole; *msaliti* is the negative pole of the same person-evaluation dimension.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 8**

1. **Term A:** *mwaminifu*
2. **Term B:** *mdanganyifu* / *muongo*
3. **Transliteration:** mwaminifu / mdanganyifu; mwaminifu / muongo
4. **English translation:** trustworthy/faithful person / deceiver; trustworthy person / liar
5. **Description of contrast:** All three are agentive *m-* class nominals characterizing persons by their relationship to honesty. *Mwaminifu* evaluates a person as reliably honest; *mdanganyifu* evaluates a person as habitually deceptive; *muongo* evaluates a person as a liar. P3 (Group 5 notes) identifies *muongo* as belonging to the negative pole of the trustworthiness evaluation group. These are not explicitly paired in a single construction in the data but emerge from the same evaluative dimension across multiple documented contexts.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 9**

1. **Term A:** *shahidi wa haki*
2. **Term B:** *shahidi wa uongo*
3. **Transliteration:** shahidi wa haki / shahidi wa uongo
4. **English translation:** a truthful/just witness / a false witness; a reliable witness / a perjurer
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are genitive nominal constructions with *shahidi* (witness) as head noun, differentiated by the post-nominal genitive modifier (*wa haki* vs. *wa uongo*). P2b (Item 17, Constructions 2 and 4) documents both forms. The contrast is constructional in that the same head noun takes opposing modifiers to produce opposite evaluations of witness reliability.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 10**

1. **Term A:** *ukweli mtupu*
2. **Term B:** *uongo mtupu*
3. **Transliteration:** ukweli mtupu / uongo mtupu
4. **English translation:** the plain truth; nothing but the truth / pure lies; nothing but lies
5. **Description of contrast:** Both constructions use the post-nominal intensifier *mtupu* (pure/mere/nothing but) applied to opposing truth-domain nominals. P1b (Root 1, Item 6) documents *ukweli mtupu*; P2b (Item 3, Construction 5) documents *uongo mtupu*. The parallel structure makes the contrast explicit: the same intensifying modifier is applied to both poles of the truth/falsehood opposition.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 11**

1. **Term A:** *ukweli kamili* / *ukweli wote*
2. **Term B:** *ukweli nusu*
3. **Transliteration:** ukweli kamili / ukweli wote / ukweli nusu
4. **English translation:** the full/complete truth; the whole truth / a half-truth; a partial truth
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b (Root 1, Items 15–16) documents both forms. *Ukweli kamili* and *ukweli wote* assert completeness of disclosure; *ukweli nusu* describes a statement that is technically accurate but misleading through omission. The contrast is constructional, using opposing quantity modifiers (*kamili*/*wote* vs. *nusu*) on the same nominal head. P3 notes that *ukweli nusu* is evaluatively negative, and the construction *ukweli nusu ni uongo* explicitly aligns partial truth with falsehood.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 12**

1. **Term A:** *imani thabiti*
2. **Term B:** *imani potofu*
3. **Transliteration:** imani thabiti / imani potofu
4. **English translation:** firm/steadfast faith / false/misguided faith; unshakeable conviction / erroneous belief
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are nominal collocations with *imani* (faith/belief) as head noun, differentiated by post-nominal modifiers evaluating the quality of the belief. P1b (Root 3, Items 27–28) documents both. *Thabiti* (firm, stable) evaluates the belief positively; *potofu* (erroneous, misguided, from *potoa* "to go astray") evaluates it negatively. P3 (Group 8 notes) identifies this as an internal evaluative dimension within the belief domain.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 13**

1. **Term A:** *kukiuka amana*
2. **Term B:** *kushika amana*
3. **Transliteration:** kukiuka amana / kushika amana
4. **English translation:** to breach a trust; to betray a confidence / to keep a trust; to honor a trust
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are periphrastic verbal constructions with *amana* (trust/something held in trust) as the object noun, differentiated by the main verb. P1b (Root 3, Items 25–26) documents both. *Kukiuka* (to violate/breach) produces a strongly negative evaluation; *kushika* (to hold/keep) produces a positive evaluation. The contrast is constructional, using opposing verbs with the same object.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 14**

1. **Term A:** *kudanganywa* (passive)
2. **Term B:** *kudanganyika* (inchoative)
3. **Transliteration:** kudanganywa / kudanganyika
4. **English translation:** to be deceived (by an agent) / to be taken in; to become deceived (no agent foregrounded)
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are derived from the same verb stem *danganya* but with different suffixes (*-wa* passive vs. *-ika* inchoative/stative). P4a (§4 and §8) and P4b document this as a semantically significant contrast: *kudanganywa* foregrounds an external agent (introduced by *na*); *kudanganyika* presents the subject as entering a state of deception without specifying an agent. P4b explicitly states they are "not interchangeable alternates but distinct constructions with different agent-foregrounding properties."
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 15**

1. **Term A:** *kuamini* (active)
2. **Term B:** *kuaminiwa* (passive)
3. **Transliteration:** kuamini / kuaminiwa
4. **English translation:** to believe/trust (someone or something) / to be trusted; to be found trustworthy
5. **Description of contrast:** The active form *kuamini* describes the act of placing trust or belief in an external object; the passive *kuaminiwa* describes the state of being the recipient of trust from others. P2a (Item 19, Derived Forms 3–4) and P1b (Root 3, Items 5–6) document both. The contrast is morphological (*-wa* passive suffix) and reflects a shift in the direction of the trust relationship: outward (active) vs. inward (passive).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 16**

1. **Term A:** *mtu wa kuaminiwa*
2. **Term B:** *si wa kuaminiwa*
3. **Transliteration:** mtu wa kuaminiwa / si wa kuaminiwa
4. **English translation:** a trustworthy person; a person to be trusted / not to be trusted; untrustworthy
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are relative clause constructions built on *kuaminiwa* (to be trusted), differentiated by the presence or absence of the negative *si*. P1b (Root 3, Items 22–23) and P2b (Item 19, Constructions 3–4) document both. The contrast is constructional: the same passive infinitive construction is negated to produce the opposite person evaluation.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 17**

3. **Transliteration:** kosa la kimakusudi / kosa la bahati mbaya

**Opposition 18**

1. **Term A:** *nakala halisi*
2. **Term B:** *hati bandia*
3. **Transliteration:** nakala halisi / hati bandia
4. **English translation:** a certified true copy; an authenticated copy / a forged document; a fake certificate
5. **Description of contrast:** Both constructions evaluate documents in terms of authenticity, but in opposing directions. P1b (Root 2, Item 27) and P2b (Item 6, Construction 2) document both. P3 (Group 9 notes) explicitly identifies the tension between these two constructions: *nakala halisi* attributes authenticity to a reproduction; *hati bandia* attributes inauthenticity to what appears to be a genuine document. The contrast is lexical at the modifier level (*halisi* vs. *bandia*) within the same document-evaluation domain.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 19**

1. **Term A:** *uhalali wa uchaguzi*
2. **Term B:** *kupinga uhalali*
3. **Transliteration:** uhalali wa uchaguzi / kupinga uhalali
4. **English translation:** the legitimacy of an election / to challenge legitimacy; to contest validity
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b (Root 2, Items 16 and 18) documents both. *Uhalali wa uchaguzi* asserts or refers to the legitimacy of an electoral process; *kupinga uhalali* describes the act of formally contesting that legitimacy. The contrast is constructional: the same nominal (*uhalali*) appears as the object of assertion in one construction and as the object of contestation in the other. P3 (Group 4) notes both as characteristic of post-election political and legal discourse.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 20**

1. **Term A:** *kuthibitisha* (active causative)
2. **Term B:** *kuthibitishwa* (passive)
3. **Transliteration:** kuthibitisha / kuthibitishwa
4. **English translation:** to confirm; to verify; to prove / to be confirmed; to be verified; to be proven
5. **Description of contrast:** The active causative *kuthibitisha* foregrounds the agent performing verification; the passive *kuthibitishwa* foregrounds the claim or document that has been verified. P2a (Item 14, Derived Forms 1–2) and P4a (§5 and §8) document both. The contrast is morphological (*-wa* passive suffix on the causative stem) and reflects the same agent-foregrounding distinction documented for *kudanganywa* / *kudanganyika* (Opposition 14), though here both forms are passive/causative rather than passive/inchoative.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **English translation:** to hide the truth; to conceal the truth / to discover the truth; to uncover the truth

**Opposition 22**

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 23**

1. **Term A:** *uaminifu wa chanzo*
2. **Term B:** *kupinga ushahidi*
3. **Transliteration:** uaminifu wa chanzo / kupinga ushahidi
4. **English translation:** reliability of the source / to challenge evidence; to contest testimony
5. **Description of contrast:** These two constructions represent opposing orientations toward the same epistemic object (information and its basis). P1b (Root 3, Item 18) documents *uaminifu wa chanzo* as an evaluation of source credibility; P2b (Item 16, Construction 6) documents *kupinga ushahidi* as the act of formally contesting evidence. The contrast is not between lexical antonyms but between a credibility-affirming nominal construction and a credibility-contesting verbal construction operating in the same verification domain. This opposition is weaker than the others and is included because both constructions are explicitly documented as part of the same discourse context (journalistic and legal verification).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**Opposition 24**

1. **Term A:** *kukosa imani*
2. **Term B:** *imani thabiti*
3. **Transliteration:** kukosa imani / imani thabiti
4. **English translation:** to lack faith; to be faithless / firm faith; steadfast belief
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b (Root 3, Items 13 and 27) documents both. *Kukosa imani* describes the absence of faith; *imani thabiti* describes faith at its most complete and unwavering. The contrast is between a verbal construction asserting absence (*kukosa* = to lack) and a nominal construction asserting the fullest positive form of the same quality. P3 (Group 8 notes) identifies both as part of the internal evaluative dimension within the belief domain.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Opposition 25**

1. **Term A:** *halali ya kidini*
2. **Term B:** *uhalali wa kisheria*
3. **Transliteration:** halali ya kidini / uhalali wa kisheria
4. **English translation:** religiously permissible; lawful according to religious law / legal legitimacy; lawfulness under formal law
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are genitive constructions built on the *halali/uhalali* base, differentiated by the domain-specifying modifier (*kidini* = religious vs. *kisheria* = legal/formal law). P1b (Root 2, Items 14 and 20) documents both. These are not antonyms but represent two distinct domains of legitimacy evaluation that may conflict in practice (something may be *halali ya kidini* but not *halali ya kisheria* or vice versa). P3 (Group 4 notes) identifies both as instances of the same underlying evaluative question but in different normative frameworks. This is a domain-differentiating contrast rather than a truth-value contrast.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
