# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** אֱמֶת / ʾemet / "truth, truthfulness"
**Term B:** שֶׁקֶר / sheqer / "lie, falsehood, deception"
**Description of contrast:** These are identified in P1 as the primary antonymous pair in the Hebrew truth domain, with sheqer explicitly described as "the primary antonym of ʾemet." The opposition is attested across biblical, legal, and everyday registers and is treated as foundational in the data.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** נָכוֹן / nakhon / "correct, right"
**Term B:** לֹא נָכוֹן / lo nakhon / "incorrect, wrong, not right"
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P2a (item 3.2) and P2b (Construction 3.2) as the standard negation of nakhon, described as "extremely common across all registers." The negated form is treated as having acquired fixed evaluative status.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** תָּקֵף / taqef / "valid, in force, legally binding"
**Term B:** בָּטֵל / batel / "null, void, invalid, annulled"
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly identifies these as contrasting terms (batel is described as contrasting with taqef), and P3 Group 7 describes them as "direct antonyms within this group." The opposition is central to legal and administrative discourse.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** כָּשֵׁר / kasher / "fit, valid, proper"
**Term B:** פָּסוּל / pasul / "disqualified, invalid, unfit, inadmissible"
**Description of contrast:** P3 Group 7 explicitly identifies these as "an antonymous pair primarily in rabbinic and legal-religious contexts." Both terms evaluate fitness or validity against a relevant standard, with kasher affirming and pasul denying that standard.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** מְקוֹרִי / meqorī / "original, genuine"
**Term B:** מְזֻיָּף / mezuyaf / "forged, fake, counterfeit, falsified"
**Description of contrast:** P3 Group 9 groups these as contrasting poles within the genuineness-versus-fabrication domain. P1 item 2 also identifies mezuyaf as contrasting with ʾamitī (genuine), and the same structural contrast applies to meqorī. The opposition concerns the relationship between an object and its purported origin or identity.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** אֲמִתִּי / ʾamitī / "true, genuine, real, authentic"
**Term B:** מְזֻיָּף / mezuyaf / "forged, fake, counterfeit, falsified"
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 2 explicitly states that ʾamitī "contrasts with מזויף (mezuyaf, 'forged/fake')." The opposition is between genuine character and deliberate falsification.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** מְדֻיָּק / meduyaq / "accurate, precise, exact"
**Term B:** מְעֻרְפָּל / meʿurpal / "vague"
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 4 explicitly lists meʿurpal as a contrast term for meduyaq ("Contrasts with מעורפל (meʿurpal, 'vague')"). The opposition concerns the degree of specificity and exactness in a representation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** מְדֻיָּק / meduyaq / "accurate, precise, exact"
**Term B:** מְשֻׁעָר / meshuʿar / "approximate"
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 4 explicitly lists meshuʿar as a contrast term for meduyaq ("Contrasts with... משוער (meshuʿar, 'approximate')"). The opposition concerns the degree of exactness versus approximation in a measurement or description.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** מַטְעֶה / matʿe / "misleading, deceptive"
**Term B:** מוּטְעֶה / mutʿe / "mistaken, misled, erroneous"
**Description of contrast:** P1 items 24–25 and P3 Group 12 distinguish these as occupying different positions in the deception relation: matʿe evaluates the misleading act or agent, while mutʿe evaluates the person or belief that has been misled. They are morphologically related (both from root ט-ע-ה) but occupy agent and patient roles respectively.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** שֶׁקֶר / sheqer / "lie, falsehood" (implying intentional deception)
**Term B:** מוּטְעֶה / mutʿe / "mistaken, erroneous" (without implication of intentional deception)
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 24 explicitly states that mutʿe "does not necessarily imply intentional deception," distinguishing it from sheqer. The opposition concerns the presence or absence of intent to deceive in the production of a false or incorrect statement.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** מַטְעֶה / matʿe / "misleading" (technically true but deceptive in effect)
**Term B:** שֶׁקֶר / sheqer / "lie, falsehood" (false statement)
**Description of contrast:** P1 item 25 explicitly states that "a misleading statement may be literally true," distinguishing matʿe from sheqer. The opposition concerns whether the deceptive effect is achieved through a false statement or through a technically true but misleading one.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** אָמִין / ʾamin / "reliable, trustworthy, dependable"
**Term B:** אִי-אֲמִינוּת / ʾi-ʾaminut / "unreliability, lack of credibility"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 19.2) and P1b document ʾi-ʾaminut as the negated abstract derived from ʾamin. The opposition is between the positive quality of reliability and its negation, with the prefix אִי- serving as the productive negator of the -ut abstract.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** מְהֵימָן / meheymen / "reliable, credible, trustworthy"
**Term B:** לֹא מְהֵימָן / lo meheymen / "not reliable, not credible, untrustworthy"
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P2b (Construction 18.3) as the standard negation of meheymen. The opposition is predicative and is the primary way of negating credibility evaluations in legal and journalistic contexts.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** סָבִיר / savir / "reasonable, plausible"
**Term B:** אִי-סְבִירוּת / ʾi-sevirut / "unreasonableness"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 17.2) and P3 Group 11 document ʾi-sevirut as the negated abstract of sevirut (from savir). The opposition is central to Israeli administrative law, where courts may strike down decisions on grounds of ʾi-sevirut.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** עַקְבִּי / ʿaqbī / "consistent, coherent"
**Term B:** סוֹתֵר / soter / "contradicting, contradictory, inconsistent with"
**Description of contrast:** P3 Group 10 describes ʿaqbī and soter as "near-antonyms in usage: עַקְבִּי evaluates consistency positively, while סוֹתֵר evaluates contradiction negatively." The opposition concerns the logical relationship between multiple claims or positions.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** עַקְבִּיּוּת / ʿaqbiyut / "consistency, coherence"
**Term B:** אִי-עַקְבִּיּוּת / ʾi-ʿaqbiyut / "inconsistency, incoherence"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 22.2) documents ʾi-ʿaqbiyut as the negated abstract of ʿaqbiyut, formed by the productive prefix אִי-. The opposition is used in journalistic and political discourse to evaluate whether positions are consistent over time.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** תִּקִינוּת / taqinut / "proper functioning, regularity, compliance"
**Term B:** אִי-תִּקִינוּת / ʾi-taqinut / "improper functioning, irregularity, non-compliance"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 14.2) documents ʾi-taqinut as the negated abstract of taqinut, used in administrative and regulatory discourse to evaluate procedural non-compliance.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** תֹּקֶף / toqef / "validity, legal force"
**Term B:** אִי-תֹּקֶף / ʾi-toqef / "invalidity, lack of legal force"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 15.2) documents ʾi-toqef as the negated form of toqef, used in legal contexts to evaluate whether a document or decision lacks legal validity. Described as "less common than the positive form toqef but attested in legal discourse."
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** דִּיּוּק / diyyuq / "precision, exactness, accuracy"
**Term B:** אִי-דִּיּוּק / ʾi-diyyuq / "inaccuracy, imprecision, lack of precision"
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 4.6) documents ʾi-diyyuq as the negated form of diyyuq, used in scientific, journalistic, and legal contexts to evaluate a statement or measurement as lacking required precision.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** אָמוֹן / ʾamon / "trust, confidence"
**Term B:** אִי-אָמוּן / ʾi-ʾamon / "distrust, no-confidence, lack of trust"
**Description of contrast:** P1b (א-מ-נ, item 17) documents ʾi-ʾamon as the negated form of ʾamon, used in political and institutional contexts (most prominently in הַצְבָּעַת אִי-אָמוּן, "vote of no confidence") and in interpersonal contexts to describe a breakdown of trust.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** בְּכַוָּנָה / be-khavvanah / "intentionally, deliberately, on purpose"
**Term B:** בִּשְׁגָגָה / bi-shogeg / "inadvertently, unintentionally"
**Description of contrast:** P1b (כ-ו-נ, item 11) explicitly identifies bi-shogeg as the contrast term for be-khavvanah ("Contrasts with בשוגג (bi-shogeg, 'inadvertently')"). The opposition is central to legal and moral evaluation of whether an act was deliberate.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** מְקוֹרִי / meqorī / "original, genuine" (emphasizing origin)
**Term B:** אוֹתֶנְטִי / ʾotentī / "authentic, genuine" (emphasizing character)
**Description of contrast:** P3 Group 9 notes that meqorī "emphasizes origin rather than genuineness of character," while ʾotentī concerns genuineness of character. P1 item 6 states they are "sometimes interchangeable" but distinguishable by this emphasis. This is a partial, non-antonymous contrast within a shared semantic space.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** שַׁקְרָן / shaqran / "liar, habitual liar" (agentive, negative)
**Term B:** (no lexicalized positive counterpart with -an suffix)
**Description of contrast:** P4a (Construction 7) and P4b explicitly note the asymmetry: "the -an pattern is productive for negative characterological agents (שַׁקְרָן) but lacks a lexicalized positive counterpart; positive agentive meaning is expressed periphrastically" (e.g., מִי שֶׁאוֹמֵר אֱמֶת, "one who tells the truth"). This is a one-sided gap in the agentive nominalization system.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** לְגַלּוֹת אֶת הָאֱמֶת / legallot ʾet ha-ʾemet / "to reveal the truth"
**Term B:** לְהַסְתִּיר אֶת הָאֱמֶת / lehastir ʾet ha-ʾemet / "to hide the truth, to conceal the truth"
**Description of contrast:** P2b (Constructions 1.6 and 1.7) explicitly presents these as contrasting constructions: legallot ʾet ha-ʾemet "implies prior concealment," while lehastir ʾet ha-ʾemet describes deliberate withholding of true information. The two constructions are described as contrasting in the same note.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** כָּל הָאֱמֶת / kol ha-ʾemet / "the whole truth"
**Term B:** חֲצִי אֱמֶת / ḥatsi ʾemet / "a half-truth"
**Description of contrast:** P2b (Constructions 1.10 and 1.12) presents these as contrasting constructions within the truth-quantity domain. kol ha-ʾemet emphasizes that nothing true has been withheld; ḥatsi ʾemet evaluates a statement as misleading because it omits relevant information. P3 Group 12 also identifies ḥatsi ʾemet as "a form of misleading by omission."
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** נֶאֱמָן לָעוּבְדוֹת / neʾeman la-ʿuvdot / "faithful to the facts"
**Term B:** לְעַוֵּת אֶת הָאֱמֶת / leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet / "to distort the truth"
**Description of contrast:** P1b (א-מ-נ, item 36) and P2b (Construction 1.19) document these as contrasting evaluations of how a representation relates to reality: neʾeman la-ʿuvdot affirms accurate correspondence, while leʿavvet ʾet ha-ʾemet evaluates a misleading or distorted presentation. The contrast is constructional rather than lexical, as the two expressions do not share morphological structure.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 27

**Term A:** בָּטֵל מֵעִיקָּרוֹ / batel me-ʿiqqaro / "void from the outset, null ab initio"
**Term B:** חוֹזֶה תָּקֵף / ḥoze taqef / "a valid contract"
**Description of contrast:** P2b (Constructions 15.4 and 16.2) documents these as contrasting legal evaluations of a contract's status. batel me-ʿiqqaro specifies invalidity from inception; ḥoze taqef affirms current legal force. The contrast is constructional and domain-specific to contract law.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 28

**Term A:** מְהֵימָן / meheymen / "reliable, credible" (more formal, legal register)
**Term B:** אָמִין / ʾamin / "reliable, trustworthy" (more colloquial register)
**Description of contrast:** P1 items 18–19 and P3 Group 4 document these as near-synonyms with register differences: "meheymen somewhat more formal/legal, ʾamin more colloquial." This is a register-based contrast within a shared semantic space rather than an antonymous opposition.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** יֹשֶׁר / yosher / "integrity, uprightness" (more formal, abstract)
**Term B:** יְשָׁרוּת / yesharut / "straightforwardness, honesty, directness" (more colloquial)
**Description of contrast:** P2a (item 10.2) explicitly states that yesharut is "somewhat more colloquial than yosher and tends to emphasize directness of manner rather than moral integrity per se." Both derive from the same root י-ש-ר but differ in register and semantic emphasis.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** נָכוֹן שֶׁ... / nakhon she-... / "it is true that..., admittedly..." (concessive frame)
**Term B:** סָבִיר שֶׁ... / savir she-... / "it is reasonable that..." (epistemic frame)
**Description of contrast:** P4b explicitly distinguishes these two instances of the same construction type: nakhon she- "functions specifically as a concessive frame, acknowledging a true premise before introducing a counterpoint," while savir she- "functions as epistemic frame rather than concessive frame." Both use the [truth-adjective + שֶׁ- + clause] pattern but serve different discourse functions.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** בָּדוּי / baduy / "fabricated, invented, fictitious" (without implication of criminal intent)
**Term B:** מְזֻיָּף / mezuyaf / "forged, fake, counterfeit" (implying deliberate criminal falsification)
**Description of contrast:** P3 Group 9 explicitly distinguishes these: "מְזֻיָּף implies deliberate criminal falsification; בָּדוּי implies fabrication without necessarily implying criminal intent." Both evaluate something as not genuine, but differ in the degree and nature of intentionality attributed.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** אֱמוּנָה תְּמִימָה / ʾemunah temimah / "wholehearted faith, complete trust" (used approvingly)
**Term B:** אֱמוּנָה תְּמִימָה / ʾemunah temimah / "naive belief, blind credulity" (used critically)
**Description of contrast:** P1b (א-מ-נ, item 15) explicitly documents that this single expression "can be used approvingly (sincere faith) or critically (blind credulity) depending on context." This is an internal evaluative contrast within a single form rather than an opposition between two distinct forms.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 33

**Term A:** צֶדֶק / tsedeq / "justice, righteousness" (systemic/moral rightness; formal or political register)
**Term B:** הָגוּן / hagun / "decent, fair, proper" (immediate social fairness; colloquial register)
**Description of contrast:** P3 explicitly distinguishes these in the notes to Groups 6 and 8: "Group 8 expressions tend to appear in more formal, religious, or political discourse and concern systemic or moral rightness, while Group 6 expressions are more colloquial and concern immediate social fairness." The contrast is one of scope and register rather than antonymy.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** תָּקֵף עַד / taqef ʿad / "valid until" (affirming current validity with expiry)
**Term B:** לֹא תָּקֵף / lo taqef / "not valid, expired"
**Description of contrast:** P2b (Constructions 15.6 and 15.3) documents these as contrasting administrative formulas: taqef ʿad specifies the boundary of validity, while lo taqef evaluates a document as having passed that boundary. The contrast is constructional and domain-specific to administrative and commercial contexts.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
