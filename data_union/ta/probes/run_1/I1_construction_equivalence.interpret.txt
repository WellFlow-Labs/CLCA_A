## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION and BECOME: Partial Overlap and Functional Differentiation

Two constructions in Tamil straddle the boundary between BECOME and REVELATION. The *āku* construction (*uṇmaiyākiviṭṭatu*, "that has turned out to be true") is a general change-of-state construction and maps cleanly onto BECOME. The *teriya varu* construction (*uṇmai teriya vantatu*, "the truth became known") is described in P4a as specific to the revelation-of-hidden-truth pattern, treating truth as an entity moving from concealment to visibility. This construction appears in both the BECOME section (P4a §4) and the REVELATION section (P4a §9 and P4b §9), and is also the verbal component of the evidential/source-marking construction (P4b §8).

The functional distinction is as follows: *āku* expresses a general change of state and does not presuppose prior concealment; *teriya varu* presupposes that the truth was previously hidden and expresses its coming into visibility. The *veḷiyē vantatu* construction (*uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu*, "the truth came out") is the most clearly intransitive REVELATION construction, with truth as subject and no case marking. These two revelation constructions (*teriya vantatu* and *veḷiyē vantatu*) are attested as distinct but semantically overlapping. Neither substitutes for BECOME in the general sense; rather, *teriya varu* occupies a position between BECOME and REVELATION, and is best described as a REVELATION construction that also encodes change of state.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and REVELATION: Partial Substitution

The evidential/source-marking construction (P4b §8) uses *teriya vantatu* as its verbal component, embedding the REVELATION construction within a source-credibility frame (*nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu teriya vantatu*). This means that VERIFICATION in Tamil is expressed not through a dedicated verification verb but through a REVELATION construction augmented by source-marking (ablative *-iliruntu*) and credibility evaluation (*nampakamāna*). VERIFICATION is therefore **partially substituted by REVELATION** in this language, with the source-marking and credibility components doing the additional work that a dedicated VERIFICATION operator would otherwise carry.

No single dedicated VERIFICATION verb (equivalent to "verify," "confirm," "corroborate") is attested in the P4a/P4b data. The proving/establishing function is covered by *meyppiкka*, *nilaināṭṭu*, and *nirūpi*, but these map more directly onto CAUSATION (factual) — the act of making something true or demonstrating it — rather than onto VERIFICATION as an epistemic check against an independent standard.

### 2.3 CAUSATION (factual) and VERIFICATION: Functional Proximity

The constructions attested for CAUSATION (factual) — *meyppiкka*, *nilaināṭṭu*, *nirūpi* — carry the proving and establishing function. These constructions are semantically close to VERIFICATION in that they involve demonstrating the truth of a claim, but they are framed as acts of making-true or establishing rather than as acts of checking against evidence. The distinction is that CAUSATION (factual) in Tamil is agentive and directed (someone proves something), while VERIFICATION as a distinct operator would involve an evaluative check. The data do not provide a construction that clearly separates these two functions; the proving constructions serve both.

### 2.4 NEGATION: Analytic, Not Morphological; Two Non-Interchangeable Strategies

Tamil negation in the truth domain is entirely analytic. Two distinct participial strategies are attested: *illāta* (lacking) and *aṟṟa* (cut off from, privative). These are not interchangeable: *illāta* is broader and more common in spoken registers; *aṟṟa* tends to attach to quality/attribute nouns and is more common in formal and written registers. No single morphological negation suffix covers the entire domain. This means that the choice of negation strategy is partly lexically conditioned and partly register-sensitive, and no unified NEGATION morpheme exists for this domain.

### 2.5 Asymmetry: Concealment and Revelation as Structural Complements

The concealment/revelation construction pair (P4a §9, P4b §9) shows a clear structural asymmetry: the concealment construction is transitive with accusative marking on *uṇmai* (*uṇmaiyai maṟaikkiṟārkaḷ*), while the revelation construction is intransitive with *uṇmai* as subject and no case marking (*uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu*). This asymmetry is not a gap but a grammatically systematic distinction: concealment is construed as an action done to truth (truth as patient), while revelation is construed as truth acting on its own (truth as agent/subject). The proverbial extension to *poy* (*poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum*) confirms that this intransitive revelation structure is not restricted to *uṇmai* and extends to other domain items.

### 2.6 Asymmetry: Causative Derivation Available Only from *mey*, Not from *uṇmai*

The morphological causative *meyppiкka* is derived from the classical root *mey*, not from the more common lexical item *uṇmai*. No parallel morphological causative *\*uṇmaippiкka* is attested. This means that the morphological causative strategy is available for the classical/literary form of "truth" but not for the everyday form. The proving function for *uṇmai* is expressed periphrastically (*uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭiṉār*). This is an asymmetric gap within the CAUSATION (factual) category: the morphological strategy is lexically restricted to one root.

### 2.7 CAUSATION (character): No Attested Construction

No construction is attested in the P4a/P4b data for causing a change in a person's character with respect to honesty or truthfulness (e.g., making someone honest, causing someone to become a liar). The agentive nominalization constructions (*poy colpavaṉ*, *nampikkaiāḷar*) describe agents but do not express causation of a character state. This is a morphosyntactic gap: the function is not expressed by any attested construction in the data.

### 2.8 DISCONFIRMATION: No Attested Construction

No construction is attested for the act of disconfirming, refuting, or falsifying a claim. The negative attribution constructions (*illāta*, *aṟṟa*) describe the absence of a quality in a referent but do not express the speech act or epistemic act of disconfirmation. This is a morphosyntactic gap in the data as provided.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps (Morphosyntactic Gaps)

- **CAUSATION (character):** No construction is attested for causing a change in a person's character state with respect to honesty or truthfulness. The agentive nominalization constructions are not equivalent. **MISSING (structural gap).**
- **DISCONFIRMATION:** No construction is attested for the act of refuting or falsifying a claim. Negative attribution (*illāta*, *aṟṟa*) is not equivalent to disconfirmation. **MISSING (structural gap).**
- **Unified NEGATION morpheme:** No single morphological negation suffix covers the entire truth domain. Two analytic strategies coexist with non-overlapping distributions. This is not a gap in the function but a gap in morphological unification. **Morphosyntactic gap (analytic only; no unified morpheme).**

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **Dedicated VERIFICATION verb:** No single lexical item equivalent to "verify," "confirm," or "corroborate" is attested. The function is distributed across *meyppiкka*, *nilaināṭṭu*, *nirūpi* (proving/establishing) and the evidential source-marking construction. **Lexical gap; function covered by distributed constructions.**
- **Dedicated DISCONFIRMATION verb:** No single lexical item equivalent to "refute," "disprove," or "falsify" is attested in the data. **Lexical gap.**
- **Morphological causative from *uṇmai*:** No form *\*uṇmaippiкka* or equivalent is attested. The causative derivation is available only from *mey*. **Lexical/morphological gap (root-restricted).**

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data as provided. All major truth-domain concepts (truth, falsehood, honesty, evidence, credibility, concealment, revelation, proof) are expressible in Tamil, though some require circumlocution or periphrastic constructions.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

- **Causative derivation:** Morphological causative is available for *mey* (classical "truth") but not for *uṇmai* (common "truth"). The positive proving function is morphologically expressible only from one of the two primary truth roots. **Asymmetric gap: morphological causative available for classical root only.**
- **Agentive nominalization:** The participial relative clause strategy (*poy colpavaṉ*, liar) is productive and [CORE]-rated. The corresponding positive agent (*uṇmai colpavaṉ*, truth-teller) is structurally available by the same pattern but is not independently attested as a lexicalized form in the data; *-vāti* and *piriyar* forms for the positive agent are rated [MARGINAL]. **Asymmetric gap: negative agent (liar) is more lexically established than positive agent (truth-teller) in common registers.**
- **Nominalization suffix distribution:** *uṇmai* and *nērmai* are themselves *-mai* nominals and do not take a further *-mai* suffix; *poy* and *mey* do take *-mai* (*poymai*, *meymmai*). This creates an asymmetry in the availability of secondary *-mai* nominalization across domain items. **Asymmetric gap: secondary *-mai* nominalization blocked for items already ending in *-mai*.**
