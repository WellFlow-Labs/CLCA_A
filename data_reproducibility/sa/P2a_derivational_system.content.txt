# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Derivational Morphology Analysis

---

### BASE: सत्य / satya

#### Derived Form 1: सत्यता / satyatā
- **Transliteration:** satyatā
- **Literal breakdown:** satya + -tā (abstract suffix)
- **Translation:** truthfulness, truth, veracity
- **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality or state of being true. Common in philosophical and ordinary discourse for the property of truth itself.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: सत्यत्व / satyatva
- **Transliteration:** satyatva
- **Literal breakdown:** satya + -tva (abstract suffix)
- **Translation:** truthfulness, truth, the state of being true
- **Usage:** Alternative abstract formation to satyatā, functionally equivalent. Both -tā and -tva suffixes create abstract nouns in Sanskrit, with -tva often preferred in more technical philosophical contexts.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 3: सात्य / sātya
- **Transliteration:** sātya
- **Literal breakdown:** satya + -ya (secondary derivative suffix)
- **Translation:** relating to truth, truthful
- **Usage:** Adjectival derivative emphasizing connection to or derivation from truth. Less common than base form satya.
- **Status:** [MARGINAL]

#### Derived Form 2: असत्यत्व / asatyatva

- **Status:** [CORE]

### BASE: मृषा / mṛṣā

**Note:** मृषा functions primarily as an indeclinable adverb. Derivational morphology is limited.

#### Derived Form 1: मृषावादिन् / mṛṣāvādin
- **Transliteration:** mṛṣāvādin
- **Literal breakdown:** mṛṣā (falsely) + vādin (speaking)

### BASE: प्रमाण / pramāṇa

#### Derived Form 1: प्रामाण्य / prāmāṇya
- **Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
- **Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ya (abstract suffix with vṛddhi)
- **Translation:** validity, authoritativeness, state of being a valid means
- **Usage:** Technical abstract noun denoting the property of epistemic validity. Central term in epistemological discussions. Already listed in P1 but included here to show derivational relationship.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: प्रामाणिक / prāmāṇika
- **Transliteration:** prāmāṇika
- **Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ika (adjectival suffix with vṛddhi)
- **Translation:** authoritative, valid, reliable, authentic
- **Usage:** Adjectival form describing what possesses validity or authority. Common in both technical and ordinary usage for reliable sources.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 3: प्रमाणीकरण / pramāṇīkaraṇa
- **Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa
- **Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ī- + karaṇa (making, doing)
- **Translation:** validation, authentication, verification
- **Usage:** Denominative verbal noun denoting the act of validating or authenticating. Already noted in P1b but represents clear derivational pattern.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 4: प्रमाणीकृत / pramāṇīkṛta
- **Transliteration:** pramāṇīkṛta
- **Literal breakdown:** pramāṇa + -ī- + kṛta (made, done)
- **Translation:** validated, authenticated, certified
- **Usage:** Past participle from denominative verb, describing what has been validated. Already noted in P1b.
- **Status:** [CORE]

## Summary Notes on Derivational Patterns

### Productive Patterns Observed:

1. **Abstract noun formation with -tā and -tva**: Highly productive across most adjectival bases (satya → satyatā/satyatva, yukta → yuktatā/yuktatva, etc.). Both suffixes are functionally equivalent, with -tva sometimes preferred in technical philosophical contexts.

2. **Adjectival formation with -ika (with vṛddhi)**: Very productive for creating relational adjectives from nouns (dharma → dhārmika, nyāya → naiyāyika, tattva → tāttvika, vastu → vāstavika).

3. **Possessive adjectives with -in and -vat**: Common pattern for indicating possession of a quality (dharma → dharmin, rūpa → rūpin/rūpavat).

4. **Adverbial formation with -tas**: Productive for creating manner adverbs from nouns (rūpa → rūpatas, tattva → tattvatas, vastu → vastutaḥ).

5. **Action nouns from verbal roots with -ana and -ti**: Standard pattern for creating abstract nouns from roots (√bhram → bhramaṇa/bhrānti, √chal → chalana).

### Limited Productivity:

1. **Indeclinable adverbs** (mṛṣā, mithyā, samyak): Show limited derivational morphology due to their grammatical category. Abstract formations are possible but less common.

2. **Compound terms** (yathārtha, svarūpa): Derivational morphology applies to the compound as a whole rather than showing internal derivation.

3. **Archaic Vedic terms** (ṛta): Show limited productivity in classical Sanskrit, with most derivatives being archaic or specialized.

### Asymmetries and Gaps:

- Not all bases show equal derivational productivity
- Some expected parallel formations are unattested (e.g., *ṛtatā is not common while ṛtatva exists)
- Adverbial bases show restricted derivational possibilities
- Some derivatives are marginal or uncertain in attestation
