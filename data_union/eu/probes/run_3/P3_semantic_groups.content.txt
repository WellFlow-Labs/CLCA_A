# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from observed patterns in P1 and P2: which items co-occur, which are explicitly contrasted, which share syntactic environments, which appear in the same registers, and which are defined in terms of each other. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented. Items that appear in multiple groups are listed under each relevant group with a note. Items that do not fit any observed grouping are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Paired truth-assertion and falsity-assertion formulas

**Label:** Paired truth-assertion and falsity-assertion formulas

**Definition:** These expressions function as the primary predicative formulas for asserting that something is true or false in Basque. They appear in direct parallel syntactic environments (*egia da* / *gezurra da*), are explicitly contrasted with each other in P1 and P2, and together constitute the baseline binary for propositional evaluation in everyday, legal, and philosophical discourse. The pair is so tightly linked that derivational families, collocations, and discourse functions mirror each other systematically.

**Representative expressions:**

- *egia* (P1 item 1) — the primary nominal for truth
- *egia da* (P1b 1.2; P2b 1.2) — "it is true"
- *egia esan* (P1b 1.1; P2b 1.1) — "to tell the truth"
- *egia hutsa* (P2b 1.18) — "the plain truth"
- *egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik* (P2b 1.4) — "the whole truth and nothing but the truth"
- *egia esateko* (P2b 1.13) — discourse marker, "to tell the truth / truth be told"
- *gezurra* (P1 item 4) — the primary nominal for falsehood
- *gezurra da* (P1b 1.22; P2b 4.2) — "it is false / it is a lie"
- *gezurra esan* (P1b 1.21; P2b 4.1) — "to tell a lie"
- *gezurra zabaldu* (P2b 4.10) — "to spread a lie"
- *gezurra eta egia nahastu* (P2b 4.7) — "to mix truth and lies"
- *egia da, baina...* (P2b 1.15) — concessive construction
- *egia da ala ez?* (P2b 1.11) — binary truth question formula

**Notes on internal structure:** The parallelism is morphological (*egia*/*gezurra* both take *esan*, *da*, *aitortu*, *gorde*/*harrapatu*), derivational (*egiazkoa*/*gezurrezkoa*; *egiatasuna*/*gezurtasuna*), and discursive (both appear in legal oath formulas, both serve as predicative complements). The construction *gezurra eta egia nahastu* presupposes the pair as a contrast set.

## Group 2: Adjectival forms used to characterize statements, objects, or persons as true/false

**Label:** Adjectival characterization of truth or falsity

**Definition:** These are adjectival forms derived from the truth/falsity roots that are used attributively or predicatively to characterize statements, objects, or persons as truthful, genuine, false, or fake. They are distinguished from the nominal forms in Group 1 by their explicitly adjectival function and their range of application (they can modify nouns directly). They appear in attributive position before nouns and in predicative constructions, and they participate in the *-tasun* abstract nominalization pattern.

**Representative expressions:**

- *egiazkoa* (P1 item 2) — "true, truthful, genuine" (adjectival)
- *egiazko* (P1b 1.4) — indefinite attributive form
- *egiazkotasuna* (P1b 1.5; P2a 1.2) — "truthfulness, genuineness" (abstract nominal)
- *egiazki* (P1b 1.20; P2a 1.4) — "truly, genuinely" (adverbial)
- *egiazko informazioa zabaldu* (P2b 2.1) — "to spread true information"
- *egiazko adiskidea* (P2b 2.2) — "a true friend"
- *egiazko arrazoia* (P2b 2.3) — "the real reason"
- *gezurrezkoa* (P1 item 5) — "false, lying, deceptive" (adjectival)
- *gezurrezko* (P1b 1.23) — indefinite attributive form
- *gezurrezko lekukotasuna* (P1b 1.24; P2b 5.1) — "false testimony, perjury"
- *gezurrezko informazioa zabaldu* (P2b 5.2) — "to spread false information"
- *gezurrezko agindua* (P2b 5.3) — "a false promise"
- *gezurtasuna* (P1b 1.26; P2a 4.4) — "falseness, mendacity"

**Notes:** *egiazkoa* and *gezurrezkoa* are the adjectival counterparts of *egia* and *gezurra* respectively, and their usage patterns mirror each other. The distinction between Group 1 and Group 2 is functional: Group 1 items are used as nominals in predicative formulas; Group 2 items are used as adjectives modifying nouns or as predicative adjectives. Some items (e.g., *egia da* vs. *egiazkoa da*) can appear in similar syntactic positions but with different nuances documented in P1.

## Group 3: Expressions used to assert or question authenticity of objects and identities

**Label:** Authenticity assertion for objects and identities

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to assert, question, or deny that an object, document, artwork, or identity is the genuine original rather than a copy, forgery, or assumed identity. They appear predominantly in legal, administrative, and artistic contexts, and they co-occur with terms for documents, artworks, signatures, and identities. They are distinguished from Group 2 by their focus on object-authenticity rather than propositional truth.

**Representative expressions:**

- *benetakoa* (P1 item 3) — "real, genuine, authentic"
- *benetako* (P2a 3.2) — indefinite attributive form
- *benetakotasuna* (P2a 3.1) — "genuineness, authenticity"
- *benetako izena* (P2b 3.3) — "the real name"
- *benetakoa al da?* (P2b 3.5) — "Is it real? Is it genuine?"
- *autentikoa* (P1 item 15) — "authentic, genuine"
- *autentikotasuna* (P2a 15.1) — "authenticity"
- *autentifikatu* (P2a 15.2) — "to authenticate"
- *autentifikazioa* (P2a 15.3) — "authentication"
- *autentikoa dela egiaztatu* (P2b 15.1) — "to verify that it is authentic"
- *autentikotasuna zalantzan jarri* (P2b 15.3) — "to call authenticity into question"
- *arte lan autentikoa* (P2b 15.2) — "an authentic work of art"
- *faltsua* (P1 item 14) — "false, fake, counterfeit"
- *dokumentu faltsua* (P2b 14.1) — "a forged document"
- *nortasun faltsua* (P2b 14.2) — "false identity"
- *faltsifikatu* (P2a 14.3) — "to falsify, to forge"
- *faltsifikazioa* (P2a 14.4) — "falsification, forgery"
- *egiaztatua* (P1 item 25) — "verified, certified"
- *egiaztagiri* (P2a 1.11) — "certificate of verification"
- *ofizialki egiaztatua* (P2b 25.2) — "officially verified"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** Within this group, *benetakoa* and *autentikoa* are near-synonyms but *autentikoa* tends toward formal and legal registers while *benetakoa* is more common in everyday speech (as documented in P1). *faltsua* is the primary antonym for object-authenticity in this group, while *gezurrezkoa* (Group 2) is more commonly used for propositional falsity. *egiaztatua* and *autentifikatu* both appear in legal certification contexts and frequently co-occur (see P2b 15.1).

## Group 4: Expressions used in verification and proof contexts

**Label:** Verification and proof expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the establishment of truth or fact through evidence, testing, or official procedure is at issue. They appear in legal, scientific, journalistic, and administrative registers, and they co-occur with terms for evidence, documents, data, and official procedures. They are distinguished from Groups 1–3 by their focus on the *process* of establishing truth rather than on asserting or characterizing it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *egiaztatu* (P1b 1.6; P2a 1.5) — "to verify, to certify, to confirm"
- *egiaztatze* (P1b 1.7; P2a 1.7) — "the process of verifying"
- *egiaztapen* (P1b 1.8; P2a 1.8) — "verification, attestation (as result)"
- *egiaztagarria* (P1b 1.9; P2a 1.9) — "verifiable"
- *egiaztagaitza* (P1b 1.10; P2a 1.10) — "unverifiable"
- *egiaztagiri* (P2a 1.11) — "certificate of verification"
- *frogatu* (P1 item 26) — "to prove, to demonstrate, to test"
- *froga* (P1b 1.28; P2a 26.1) — "proof, evidence"
- *frogagarria* (P1b 1.30; P2a 26.2) — "provable, demonstrable"
- *frogarik gabe* (P1b 1.29; P2a 26.3) — "without proof, unproven"
- *frogazko* (P2a 26.4) — "evidentiary, relating to proof"
- *frogabidea* (P2a 26.5) — "means of proof"
- *frogatugabea* (P2a 26.6) — "unproven"
- *fidagarria dela frogatu* (P2b 9.3) — "to prove that it is reliable"
- *faltsua dela frogatu* (P2b 14.3) — "to prove that it is false"
- *autentikoa dela egiaztatu* (P2b 15.1) — "to verify that it is authentic"
- *datuak egiaztatu* (P1b 1.6, example) — "to verify the data"

**Notes:** *egiaztatu* and *frogatu* are the two primary verbs in this group. As documented in P1 and P2, *egiaztatu* tends toward official certification and administrative verification, while *frogatu* tends toward demonstration through evidence in legal and scientific contexts. Both appear in constructions with *dela* subordinate clauses (e.g., *fidagarria dela frogatu*, *autentikoa dela egiaztatu*), which is a shared syntactic pattern. *egiaztagarria* and *frogagarria* are parallel *-garri* forms within this group.

## Group 5: Expressions for correctness and error in statements and actions

**Label:** Correctness and error in statements and actions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, calculation, procedure, or action is correct or incorrect. They appear in epistemic, educational, and procedural contexts, and they participate in constructions for asserting, admitting, or correcting error. They are distinguished from the truth/falsity group (Group 1) by their focus on correctness relative to a standard or procedure rather than on propositional truth per se.

**Representative expressions:**

- *zuzena* (P1 item 6) — "correct, right, just, upright"
- *zuzentasuna* (P1b 3.3; P2a 6.1) — "correctness, rightness, justice"
- *zuzendu* (P1b 3.4; P2a 6.2) — "to correct, to rectify"
- *zuzenketa* (P1b 3.5; P2a 6.3) — "correction, amendment"
- *zuzentzailea* (P1b 3.14; P2a 6.5) — "corrector, proofreader"
- *zuzentzapen* (P2a 6.11) — "correction, amendment (formal)"
- *hori zuzena da* (P2b 6.1) — "that is correct"
- *zuzena dela uste dut* (P2b 6.5) — "I think it is correct"
- *zuzen ala oker* (P2b 6.6) — "right or wrong"
- *zuzena den ala ez den* (P2b 6.7) — "whether it is correct or not"
- *okerra* (P1 item 7) — "wrong, mistaken, erroneous"
- *okerreko* (P1b 3.24; P2a 7.1) — "wrong, erroneous" (attributive)
- *okertu* (P1b 3.22; P2a 7.2) — "to go wrong, to err"
- *okertasuna* (P1b 3.23; P2a 7.3) — "wrongness, error"
- *okerkeria* (P2a 7.4) — "wrongdoing, misconduct"
- *oker nago* (P2b 7.1) — "I am wrong / I am mistaken"
- *oker zaude* (P2b 7.2) — "you are wrong"
- *oker ez banago* (P2b 7.6) — "if I am not mistaken"
- *oker ibili* (P1b 3.25; P2b 7.3) — "to be on the wrong track"
- *okerreko bidea hartu* (P2b 7.4) — "to take the wrong path"
- *zuzen jarri* (P1b 3.26) — "to set straight, to put right"
- *zehatz-mehatz bete* (P2b 8.3) — "to comply with to the letter"

**Notes:** *zuzena* and *okerra* are explicitly defined as antonyms in P1 and appear together in the fixed binary *zuzen ala oker* (P2b 6.6). The correction sub-cluster (*zuzendu*, *zuzenketa*, *zuzentzailea*) is a distinct functional subset within this group, appearing in educational and editorial contexts. *oker ez banago* is a hedging formula that belongs both here and in Group 8 (epistemic hedging).

## Group 6: Expressions for precision and exactness of description or measurement

**Label:** Precision and exactness of description or measurement

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, description, or account is sufficiently precise or exact. They appear predominantly in scientific, technical, journalistic, and administrative registers, and they co-occur with terms for data, measurements, dates, and specifications. They are distinguished from Group 5 (correctness) by their focus on the degree of correspondence between a representation and its object, rather than on binary correctness.

**Representative expressions:**

- *zehatza* (P1 item 8) — "exact, precise, accurate, thorough"
- *zehaztasuna* (P2a 8.1) — "exactness, precision, accuracy"
- *zehaztu* (P2a 8.2) — "to specify, to determine precisely"
- *zehaztapen* (P2a 8.3) — "specification, precise determination"
- *zehaztugabea* (P2a 8.4) — "unspecified, imprecise"
- *zehazki* (P2a 8.5) — "exactly, precisely" (adverb)
- *zehaztasun falta* (P2a 8.6) — "lack of precision"
- *zehatz esateko* (P2b 8.1) — "to be precise / precisely speaking" (discourse marker)
- *zehatz-mehatz* (P2b 8.2) — "exactly, in every detail" (reduplicated intensifier)
- *zehaztasun handiz* (P2b 8.4) — "with great precision"
- *zehazki esanda* (P2b 8.5) — "precisely stated / strictly speaking" (discourse marker)
- *datu zehatzak eman* (P2b 8.6) — "to provide precise data"

**Notes:** *zehatz esateko* and *zehazki esanda* function as discourse markers for introducing precise formulations, parallel to *egia esateko* in Group 1. The reduplicated form *zehatz-mehatz* (P2b 8.2) is a lexicalized intensifier specific to this group. This group has no clear antonym item in P1, though *zehaztugabea* and *zehaztasun falta* function as negative poles within the group.

## Group 7: Expressions for reliability and credibility of sources and testimony

**Label:** Reliability and credibility of sources and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person, source, institution, or piece of testimony can be trusted or believed. They appear in journalistic, legal, and institutional contexts, and they frequently co-occur with terms for sources (*iturri*), testimony (*lekukotasuna*), and evidence. They are distinguished from Group 3 (authenticity of objects) and Group 4 (verification) by their focus on the epistemic standing of agents and sources rather than on objects or procedures.

**Representative expressions:**

- *fidagarria* (P1 item 9) — "reliable, trustworthy, dependable"
- *fidagarritasuna* (P2a 9.1) — "reliability, trustworthiness"
- *ez-fidagarria* (P2a 9.3) — "unreliable, untrustworthy"
- *fidatu* (P2a 9.4) — "to trust, to rely on"
- *iturri fidagarria* (P2b 9.1) — "a reliable source"
- *iturri fidagarrien arabera* (P2b 9.2) — "according to reliable sources"
- *fidagarritasun falta* (P2a 9.2) — "lack of reliability"
- *sinesgarria* (P1 item 10) — "credible, believable, plausible"
- *sinesgarritasuna* (P2a 10.1) — "credibility, believability"
- *ez-sinesgarria* (P2a 10.2) — "incredible, unbelievable, implausible"
- *sinetsi* (P2a 10.3) — "to believe, to trust"
- *sinesgarria iruditzen zait* (P2b 10.1) — "it seems credible to me"
- *sinesgarritasuna galdu* (P2b 10.3) — "to lose credibility"
- *sinesgarritasuna irabazi* (P2b 10.4) — "to gain credibility"
- *sinesgarria ez den istorioa* (P2b 10.2) — "a story that is not credible"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** As documented in P1, *fidagarria* focuses on reliability (whether something can be depended upon) while *sinesgarria* focuses on believability (whether something is worthy of belief). Both appear in legal testimony contexts and journalistic source evaluation, but *fidagarria* more commonly applies to sources and institutions while *sinesgarria* more commonly applies to accounts and claims. The *-garri* suffix is shared by both, marking them as parallel derivational forms. *sinetsi* (the base verb of *sinesgarria*) also connects this group to *sinesmena* (P2a 10.4), which extends into religious belief contexts.

## Group 8: Epistemic hedging expressions

**Label:** Epistemic hedging expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to mark a claim as based on appearance, belief, or supposition rather than established fact, or to introduce a statement while acknowledging the possibility of error. They appear in both spoken and written registers and function as discourse-level qualifiers. They are distinguished from the other groups by their function as hedges on the speaker's commitment to a proposition rather than as characterizations of the proposition itself.

**Representative expressions:**

- *itxuraz* (P1 item 27) — "apparently, seemingly, ostensibly"
- *itxurazko* (P1b 2.3) — "apparent, seeming" (attributive)
- *itxurazko adostasuna* (P1b 2.16) — "apparent consensus"
- *itxura batean* (P1b 2.13) — "at first glance, on the surface"
- *itxura guztien arabera* (P1b 2.12, example) — "by all appearances"
- *itxuraz besterik ez* (P2b 16.7) — "only in appearance"
- *ustez* (P1 item 28) — "supposedly, allegedly, presumably"
- *ustezko* (P2a 28.6) — "supposed, alleged, presumed" (attributive)
- *ustezko erruduna* (P2a 28.6, example) — "the alleged culprit"
- *antza ematen du* (P2a 17.3; P2b 17.2) — "it seems, it appears"
- *antza du* (P2a 17.2; P2b 17.1) — "it resembles, it seems"
- *sinesgarria iruditzen zait* (P2b 10.1) — "it seems credible to me"
- *oker ez banago* (P2b 7.6) — "if I am not mistaken"
- *egia dirudien arren* (P2b 1.16) — "although it seems true"
- *itxura batean ondo dirudi, baina...* (P2b 16.4) — "at first glance it looks fine, but..."

**Notes:** *itxuraz* and *ustez* are the two primary single-word hedges in this group. As documented in P1, *itxuraz* hedges based on appearance while *ustez* hedges based on belief or supposition. Both are adverbial forms (instrumental *-z*) of their respective base nominals. *antza ematen du* functions as a periphrastic hedge parallel to *itxuraz* but with a slightly different construction. *oker ez banago* is a conditional hedge that also belongs to Group 5. The construction *itxura batean ondo dirudi, baina...* (P2b 16.4) is a formulaic pattern that combines hedging with concession.

## Group 9: Appearance-versus-reality expressions

**Label:** Appearance-versus-reality expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to draw a contrast between how something appears and what it actually is, or to characterize something as existing only as surface appearance without underlying substance. They appear in evaluative, literary, and social commentary contexts, and many of them are built on the *itxura-* root. They are distinguished from Group 8 (hedging) by their focus on the contrast itself rather than on the speaker's epistemic position.

**Representative expressions:**

- *itxurakoa* (P1 item 16) — "apparent, seeming, superficial, of mere appearance"
- *itxura egin* (P1b 2.4; P2b 16.1) — "to pretend, to feign"
- *itxurak gorde* (P1b 2.6; P2b 16.2) — "to keep up appearances"
- *itxurak engainatzen du* (P1b 2.15; P2b 16.3) — "appearances are deceiving"
- *itxura hutsean* (P1b 2.17; P2b 16.5) — "in mere appearance only"
- *itxurakeria* (P2a 16.1) — "pretense, superficiality"
- *itxuragilea* (P1 item 24) — "pretender, impostor, maker of appearances"
- *itxuratu* (P2a 16.3) — "to give an appearance to, to disguise"
- *itxura batean ondo dirudi, baina...* (P2b 16.4) — "at first glance it looks fine, but..."
- *itxuraz besterik ez* (P2b 16.7) — "only in appearance"
- *benetakoa* (P1 item 3) — used as the positive pole contrasting with mere appearance
- *benetako helburua* (P2b 3.4) — "the real goal" (as opposed to stated goal)
- *egiazko arrazoia* (P2b 2.3) — "the real reason" (as opposed to apparent reason)

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 3 (authenticity) and Group 8 (hedging). The overlap with Group 3 is through *benetakoa*, which appears both as an authenticity term (Group 3) and as the positive pole in appearance-versus-reality contrasts (this group). The overlap with Group 8 is through *itxuraz* and *itxura batean*, which function both as hedges and as markers of the appearance side of the contrast. *itxuragilea* (P1 item 24, epistemic status [MARGINAL]) belongs here rather than in Group 8 because it characterizes an agent rather than hedging a proposition.

## Group 10: Expressions used in legal validity and permissibility contexts

**Label:** Legal validity and permissibility expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts to assert or deny that a document, act, claim, or authority has legal standing, is officially valid, or is permitted. They appear in legal texts, administrative procedures, and formal discourse, and they co-occur with terms for documents, contracts, authority, and law. They are distinguished from Group 4 (verification) by their focus on the legal status of something rather than on the process of establishing its truth.

**Representative expressions:**

- *baliozko* (P1 item 11) — "valid, legitimate, of value"
- *baliozko agiria* (P2b 11.1) — "a valid document"
- *baliozko kontratuak* (P2b 11.2) — "valid contracts"
- *baliozkotu* (P2a 11.3) — "to validate, to make valid"
- *baliogabea* (P2a 11.4) — "invalid, null and void"
- *baliogabetu* (P2a 11.5) — "to invalidate, to nullify"
- *baliotasuna* (P2a 11.6) — "validity, worth"
- *baliogabea dela adierazi* (P2b 11.3) — "to declare it null and void"
- *balio juridikoa* (P2b 11.4) — "legal value, juridical validity"
- *legitimoa* (P1 item 12) — "legitimate, lawful, rightful"
- *legitimotasuna* (P2a 12.1) — "legitimacy"
- *legitimatu* (P2a 12.2) — "to legitimize"
- *botere legitimoa* (P2b 12.1) — "legitimate power"
- *legitimotasuna zalantzan jarri* (P2b 12.3) — "to call legitimacy into question"
- *zilegi* (P1 item 13) — "lawful, permissible, licit"
- *zilegitasuna* (P2a 13.2) — "lawfulness, permissibility"
- *zilegi da* (P2b 13.1) — "it is lawful / it is permissible"
- *zilegi al da?* (P2b 13.2) — "Is it lawful?"
- *ez-zilegia* (P2a 13.4) — "unlawful, impermissible"
- *legez zilegi* (P2b 13.4) — "legally permissible"
- *zuzenbidea* (P1b 3.10; P2a 6.10) — "law, jurisprudence"

**Notes on internal distinctions:** Within this group, *baliozko* focuses on formal validity of documents and acts, *legitimoa* focuses on the legitimacy of authority and claims (particularly in political contexts), and *zilegi* focuses on permissibility of actions. As documented in P1, *legitimoa* is a loanword used alongside native *zilegi*; they partially overlap but *legitimoa* is more common in political discourse and *zilegi* in ethical and everyday legal discourse. *zuzenbidea* (law as a discipline) is included here as a peripheral member because it is derived from *zuzen-* and appears in legal collocations, though it is primarily an institutional term.

## Group 11: Expressions for personal moral integrity and honesty

**Label:** Personal moral integrity and honesty expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize persons as honest, sincere, upright, or loyal in their conduct and character. They appear in everyday moral evaluation, relational discourse, and institutional contexts, and they focus on the character of agents rather than on the truth-value of propositions or the validity of documents. They are distinguished from Group 1 (propositional truth) and Group 3 (object authenticity) by their application to persons and their focus on character traits.

**Representative expressions:**

- *zintzoa* (P1 item 20) — "honest, upright, sincere, well-behaved"
- *zintzotasuna* (P2a 20.1) — "honesty, uprightness, integrity"
- *zintzoki* (P2a 20.2) — "honestly, sincerely" (adverb)
- *zintzoki esan* (P2b 20.2) — "to say honestly"
- *zintzoa da* (P2b 20.1) — "he/she is honest"
- *zintzoa izatea* (P2b 20.3) — "being honest"
- *leiala* (P1 item 21) — "loyal, faithful, true"
- *leialatasuna* (P2a 21.1) — "loyalty, faithfulness"
- *leialki* (P2a 21.2) — "loyally, faithfully"
- *leial egon* (P2b 21.1) — "to remain loyal"
- *leialatasuna erakutsi* (P2b 21.3) — "to show loyalty"
- *leialatasuna galdu* (P2b 21.4) — "to lose loyalty"
- *gezurtia* (P1b 1.25; P2a 4.3) — "liar (habitual)" — negative pole
- *iruzurti* (P2a 23.4) — "habitually deceptive" — negative pole

**Notes:** *zintzoa* and *leiala* are the two primary positive terms in this group. As documented in P1, *zintzoa* focuses on personal sincerity and moral uprightness while *leiala* focuses on faithfulness to commitments and persons. Both are distinguished from *egiazkoa* (Group 2) by their application to persons and character rather than to propositions. *gezurtia* and *iruzurti* are the negative-pole character terms in this group; both use the *-ti* suffix marking habitual character traits, as documented in P2a.

## Group 12: Expressions for fraud, deception, and corruption

**Label:** Fraud, deception, and corruption expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize conduct, persons, or institutions as engaging in deliberate deception, fraud, or moral corruption. They appear in legal, journalistic, and political contexts, and they focus on intentional wrongdoing rather than mere error or falsity. They are distinguished from Group 5 (error and incorrectness) by their implication of deliberate intent, and from Group 2 (propositional falsity) by their focus on conduct rather than statements.

**Representative expressions:**

- *iruzurra* (P1 item 23) — "fraud, deceit, cheat, swindle"
- *iruzur egin* (P2b 23.1) — "to commit fraud, to cheat"
- *iruzurgilea* (P2a 23.1) — "fraudster, swindler"
- *iruzurrezkoa* (P2a 23.2) — "fraudulent, deceptive"
- *iruzurrezko jarduna* (P2b 23.4) — "fraudulent conduct"
- *iruzurra salatu* (P2b 23.2) — "to denounce fraud"
- *iruzurra egotzi* (P2b 23.3) — "to accuse of fraud"
- *ustelkeria* (P1 item 22) — "corruption, rottenness, moral decay"
- *ustelkeria politikoa* (P2b 22.1) — "political corruption"
- *ustelkeria salatu* (P2b 22.2) — "to denounce corruption"
- *ustelkeria kasuak* (P2b 22.3) — "corruption cases"
- *ustelkeriaren aurkako borroka* (P2b 22.4) — "the fight against corruption"
- *ustela* (P2a 22.1) — "rotten, corrupt"
- *usteldu* (P2a 22.2) — "to rot, to become corrupt"
- *faltsukeria* (P2a 14.1) — "falseness, deceitfulness, duplicity"
- *faltsifikatu* (P2a 14.3) — "to falsify, to forge"
- *faltsifikazioa* (P2a 14.4) — "falsification, forgery"
- *itxuragilea* (P1 item 24) — "pretender, impostor"
- *itxurakeria* (P2a 16.1) — "pretense, superficiality"

**Notes:** *iruzurra* and *ustelkeria* are the two primary terms in this group. As documented in P1, *iruzurra* focuses on specific acts of fraud or deception while *ustelkeria* focuses on systemic or institutional corruption. Both appear with *salatu* ("to denounce") in parallel constructions (P2b 22.2, 23.2). *faltsifikatu* and *faltsifikazioa* belong here as well as in Group 3 (authenticity), since forgery involves both inauthenticity and deliberate deception. *itxuragilea* and *itxurakeria* are peripheral members, appearing in this group when the appearance-making involves deliberate deception of others.

## Group 13: Resemblance and similarity expressions

**Label:** Resemblance and similarity expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize the degree of likeness or similarity between two entities, without asserting identity or authenticity. They appear in comparative, descriptive, and evaluative contexts, and they are relevant to assessments of copies, translations, and approximations. They are distinguished from Group 3 (authenticity) by their focus on degree of resemblance rather than on genuine-versus-fake status.

**Representative expressions:**

- *antzekoa* (P1 item 17) — "similar, resembling, alike"
- *antzekotasuna* (P2a 17.1) — "similarity, resemblance"
- *antza* (P2a 17.2) — "resemblance, likeness"
- *antza du* (P2b 17.1) — "it resembles, it looks like"
- *antza ematen du* (P2a 17.3; P2b 17.2) — "it seems, it appears"
- *antz handia du* (P2b 17.5) — "there is a strong resemblance"
- *antzera* (P2a 17.4) — "similarly, in a similar way"
- *antzeko kasua* (P2b 17.3) — "a similar case"
- *antzeko zerbait* (P2b 17.4) — "something similar"
- *berdintsua* (P1 item 18) — "approximately equal, similar, equivalent"
- *berdina* (P2a 18.1) — "the same, identical, equal"
- *berdintasuna* (P2a 18.2) — "equality, sameness"
- *berdindu* (P2a 18.3) — "to equalize, to make equal"
- *berdinki* (P2a 18.4) — "equally, in the same way"
- *gauza berdina da* (P2b 18.1) — "it is the same thing"
- *ia berdina* (P2b 18.2) — "almost the same"
- *berdin-berdin* (P2b 18.4) — "exactly the same" (reduplicated intensifier)

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *antzekoa* and *berdintsua* differ in degree: *antzekoa* marks resemblance or likeness, while *berdintsua* (with the approximative *-tsu* suffix) marks approximate equality. *berdina* (without *-tsu*) marks exact identity and is the positive pole of the scale. The fixed idiomatic construction *berdin dio* (P2b 18.3, "it doesn't matter / it's all the same") is a lexicalized use of *berdin* that does not belong to this group's core resemblance function. *antza ematen du* (P2b 17.2) also belongs to Group 8 (hedging) as an epistemic hedge.

## Group 14: Appropriateness and suitability expressions

**Label:** Appropriateness and suitability expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something is fitting, suitable, or appropriate for a given purpose, context, or standard. They appear in aesthetic, practical, and normative evaluative contexts, and they focus on fit between a thing and its context rather than on truth or correctness per se. They are distinguished from Group 5 (correctness) by their focus on contextual fit rather than conformity to a standard of accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**

- *egokia* (P1 item 19) — "appropriate, suitable, fitting, proper"
- *egokitasuna* (P2a 19.1) — "appropriateness, suitability"
- *egokitu* (P2a 19.2) — "to adapt, to adjust, to make appropriate"
- *egokitze* (P2a 19.3) — "adaptation, adjustment"
- *egokiera* (P2a 19.4) — "opportunity, suitable moment"
- *egokia da* (P2b 19.1) — "it is appropriate"
- *egokia iruditzen zait* (P2b 19.2) — "it seems appropriate to me"
- *testuingurura egokitua* (P2b 19.3) — "adapted to the context"
- *egokia den ala ez den* (P2b 19.4) — "whether it is appropriate or not"

**Notes:** This group is smaller and more internally uniform than most others. *egokia* does not have a single primary antonym in P1; the negative is typically expressed through *ez da egokia* ("it is not appropriate") rather than through a dedicated antonym. The semantic extension of *egokiera* to "opportunity" (P2a 19.4) represents a shift away from the core appropriateness meaning and is noted as a peripheral use.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups or present classification difficulties:

**benetakoa** (P1 item 3): Appears in Group 3 (authenticity of objects) and Group 9 (appearance-versus-reality). In Group 3 it functions as an authenticity term contrasting with *faltsua* and *autentikoa*; in Group 9 it functions as the positive pole contrasting with *itxurakoa* and related appearance terms. The distinction depends on context: when applied to objects and documents, Group 3; when used to identify the "real" underlying reality behind appearances, Group 9.

**itxuraz** (P1 item 27): Appears in Group 8 (hedging) and Group 9 (appearance-versus-reality). As a hedging adverb it belongs to Group 8; as a marker of the appearance side of the appearance-reality contrast it belongs to Group 9. The two functions are not always distinguishable in context.

**antza ematen du** (P2a 17.3; P2b 17.2): Appears in Group 8 (hedging) and Group 13 (resemblance). When used to express that something appears to be the case without asserting it, it functions as a hedge (Group 8); when used to express resemblance between two entities, it belongs to Group 13.

**faltsua** (P1 item 14) and *faltsifikatu* (P2a 14.3): Appear in Group 3 (authenticity) and Group 12 (fraud and deception). *faltsua* applied to documents and objects belongs to Group 3; *faltsifikatu* and *faltsifikazioa* belong to Group 12 when the focus is on the deliberate act of forgery.

**itxuragilea** (P1 item 24): Appears in Group 9 (appearance-versus-reality) and Group 12 (fraud and deception). As a characterization of a person who creates false appearances it belongs to Group 9; when the false appearances are created to deceive others it belongs to Group 12. The item is marked [MARGINAL] in P1.

**oker ez banago** (P2b 7.6): Appears in Group 5 (correctness and error) and Group 8 (hedging). It is a conditional hedge (Group 8) built on the wrongness/error vocabulary (Group 5).

**zuzentasuna** (P1b 3.3; P2a 6.1): Appears in Group 5 (correctness) and Group 10 (legal validity). In epistemic and educational contexts it belongs to Group 5; in legal and moral justice contexts (*zuzentasun soziala*, *zuzentasun prozedurala*) it belongs to Group 10. The semantic breadth of *zuzen-* documented in P1 and P1b means this item straddles multiple groups.

**gezurtia** (P1b 1.25; P2a 4.3): Appears in Group 2 (adjectival falsity) and Group 11 (personal moral integrity). As a characterization of a person as a habitual liar it belongs to Group 11 (character evaluation); as a derivation of *gezurra* it is formally connected to Group 2.

## Items that do not fit clearly into any group

**ustekabea / ustekabeko / ustekabean** (P2a 28.2–28.4): These forms are derived from *uste-* (the belief/supposition root underlying *ustez*, Group 8) but their meaning — "unexpected, by surprise" — does not fit the hedging function of Group 8 or any other group identified here. They represent a semantic extension of the belief root toward the domain of expectation and surprise that is not shared by other items in the inventory.

**ustekeria** (P2a 28.5): "Unfounded belief, prejudice, baseless opinion." This pejorative form of *uste-* characterizes beliefs held without justification. It is related to Group 8 (hedging) through the *uste-* root but functions as a critical characterization of others' beliefs rather than as a hedge on one's own claims. No other item in the inventory occupies this function.

**egokiera** (P2a 19.4): "Opportunity, suitable moment." This is listed as a peripheral member of Group 14 (appropriateness) but its primary meaning — an opportune occasion — is sufficiently distinct from the core appropriateness function that it does not fit cleanly. It is noted here as an item whose usage has shifted away from the core group meaning.

**berdin dio** (P2b 18.3): "It doesn't matter / it's all the same." This is a lexicalized idiomatic construction built on *berdin* (the equality root underlying Group 13) but its function — expressing indifference to outcomes — does not fit the resemblance and similarity function of Group 13. It is noted in P2b as a lexicalized idiomatic use distinct from the literal equality meaning.

**sinesmena** (P2a 10.4): "Belief, faith, creed." This form is derived from the *sines-* root (underlying *sinesgarria*, Group 7) but its primary use in religious and philosophical contexts — referring to a system of beliefs or the capacity for belief — is distinct from the credibility-evaluation function of Group 7. It is not clearly assignable to any group in this inventory.

**egiazale / egiazalea** (P1b 1.17–1.18; P2a 1.12): "Truth-lover, truth-seeker." These marginal forms characterize a person devoted to truth-seeking. They are derived from *egia-* (Groups 1 and 2) but their function — characterizing a person by their relationship to truth — does not fit the propositional or adjectival functions of those groups, nor does it fit Group 11 (personal moral integrity) cleanly, since truth-seeking is an epistemic rather than a moral character trait in the documented usage.
