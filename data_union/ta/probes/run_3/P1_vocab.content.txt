# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

**1.**
**Form:** உண்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai
**Literal meaning:** that which is real/existent; from *uḷ* (to be/exist) + nominal suffix *-mai*
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuineness
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality. Participates in productive derivation: *uṇmaiyāṉa* (true, genuine), *uṇmaiyāka* (truly, honestly). Frequently appears in moral and religious discourse as a core virtue term.
**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** பொய்
**Transliteration:** poy
**Literal meaning:** falsehood (root form; etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, deception
**Usage:** The primary antonym of *uṇmai*; used to characterize statements, persons, or representations as false or deceptive. Appears in compounds such as *poy-sākṣi* (false witness) and in moral condemnation across literary and colloquial registers. Productive in adjectival and adverbial derivation.
**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** நேர்மை
**Transliteration:** nērmai
**Literal meaning:** straightness, directness; from *nēr* (straight, direct) + *-mai*
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness, straightforwardness
**Usage:** Refers to a quality of character or conduct marked by transparency and absence of deception. Commonly used in evaluations of persons and their behavior in moral, professional, and civic contexts. Distinct from *uṇmai* in that it foregrounds conduct and disposition rather than propositional truth.
**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** உண்மையான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa
**Literal meaning:** that which is of/pertaining to *uṇmai*; adjectival form via *-āṉa*
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** Adjectival form of *uṇmai*, used attributively to modify nouns denoting statements, persons, objects, or feelings. Widely used in both spoken and written Tamil. Contrasts with *poyyāṉa* (false) in parallel constructions.
**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** சரி
**Transliteration:** cari
**Literal meaning:** correctness, rightness (also used as interjection)
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, calculations, actions, or decisions as correct or appropriate. Extremely frequent in colloquial speech as both predicate and response particle. Also appears in compounds such as *caritāṉ* (it is indeed correct) and in formal written usage to mean "proper" or "accurate."
**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** தவறு
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu
**Literal meaning:** deviation, going astray; from root *tavar-* (to err, deviate)
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, wrong, fault
**Usage:** Used to characterize statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong. Appears in both epistemic contexts (factual error) and moral contexts (wrongdoing, fault). Productive in derivation: *tavaṟāṉa* (erroneous, wrong), *tavaṟu ceytāl* (if one errs/does wrong).
**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** நம்பகமான
**Transliteration:** nampakamāṉa
**Literal meaning:** worthy of trust/belief; from *nampu* (to trust, believe) + *-akam* + *-āṉa*
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable
**Usage:** Used to evaluate persons, sources, institutions, or information as worthy of trust. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The base noun *nampakam* also occurs independently in formal registers.
**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** நம்பகத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** nampakat-taṉmai
**Literal meaning:** the quality/nature of being trustworthy; *nampakam* + *taṉmai* (nature, quality)
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the property of being reliable or credible, used in formal written Tamil including legal, academic, and journalistic discourse. Often appears in discussions of evidence, testimony, or institutional conduct. Less frequent in spoken registers than *nampakamāṉa*.
**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** நேர்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** nēr-taṉmai
**Literal meaning:** the quality of being straight/direct; *nēr* + *taṉmai*
**Typical English translations:** straightforwardness, directness, integrity
**Usage:** Used in formal and literary registers to describe a quality of character or conduct. Overlaps with *nērmai* but tends to appear in more explicitly philosophical or literary contexts. Less frequent in everyday speech.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** உண்மைத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai-t-taṉmai
**Literal meaning:** the quality/nature of truth; *uṇmai* + *taṉmai*
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, authenticity, genuineness
**Usage:** Abstract noun used in formal and philosophical discourse to denote the property of being true or authentic. Appears in literary criticism and philosophical writing when evaluating whether something genuinely possesses a quality. Less common in everyday speech than *uṇmai* alone.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** போலி
**Transliteration:** pōli
**Literal meaning:** imitation, counterfeit, fake (root form)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, spurious, sham
**Usage:** Used to characterize objects, persons, documents, or credentials as not genuine — copies or imitations presented as authentic. Common in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts: *pōli āvaṇam* (forged document), *pōli māṭṭu* (fake cattle/goods). Productive in nominal compounds.
**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** கள்ளம்
**Transliteration:** kaḷḷam
**Literal meaning:** deceit, fraud, concealment (classical root)
**Typical English translations:** deceit, fraud, duplicity, treachery
**Usage:** A classical and literary term for deceptive intent or fraudulent conduct, used in moral and legal evaluations. Appears in classical Tamil literature (Sangam and later) as a moral failing. Still used in formal and literary written Tamil; less common in everyday colloquial speech.
**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** வஞ்சகம்
**Transliteration:** vañcakam
**Literal meaning:** treachery, deceit (Sanskrit-origin loanword via Tamil literary tradition)
**Typical English translations:** treachery, deceit, fraud, duplicity
**Usage:** Used in literary, formal, and legal registers to denote deliberate deception or betrayal. Overlaps with *kaḷḷam* but carries a stronger connotation of intentional betrayal of trust. Appears in classical literature and continues in formal written Tamil.
**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** நியாயம்
**Transliteration:** niyāyam
**Literal meaning:** justice, rightness, reason (from Sanskrit *nyāya*)
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, legitimacy, reason
**Usage:** Used to evaluate actions, decisions, or claims as just, fair, or rationally grounded. Appears in legal, moral, and everyday contexts: *itu niyāyamā?* (Is this right/fair?). Also used in philosophical contexts to mean logical reasoning or valid argument, reflecting its Sanskrit heritage.
**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** நியாயமான
**Transliteration:** niyāyamāṉa
**Literal meaning:** that which is of/pertaining to *niyāyam*; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** just, fair, reasonable, legitimate, proper
**Usage:** Adjectival form of *niyāyam*, used to evaluate conduct, claims, prices, or decisions as fair or justified. Extremely common in spoken and written Tamil across registers. Contrasts with *anyāyamāṉa* (unjust, unfair).
**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** அன்யாயம்
**Transliteration:** anyāyam
**Literal meaning:** injustice, wrongness; Sanskrit *anyāya* (contrary to *nyāya*)
**Typical English translations:** injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness
**Usage:** Used to characterize actions, decisions, or situations as unjust or morally wrong. Common in moral complaint and legal discourse. Functions as the direct antonym of *niyāyam* in evaluative contexts.
**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** உண்மையிலேயே
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyilēyē
**Literal meaning:** truly, in truth indeed; *uṇmai* + locative *-il* + emphatic *-ē* + *-yē*
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in fact
**Usage:** Emphatic adverbial expression used to assert the genuine or actual nature of a state of affairs. Common in spoken Tamil to reinforce the truth of a claim or the sincerity of a statement. Functions as an intensifier in evaluative assertions.
**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** உண்மையான அர்த்தத்தில்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa arttattil
**Literal meaning:** in the true/genuine meaning/sense; *uṇmaiyāṉa* + *arttam* (meaning, sense) + locative
**Typical English translations:** in the true sense, in the genuine meaning, properly speaking
**Usage:** A fixed or semi-fixed expression used in formal and philosophical discourse to specify that a term or concept is being used in its proper or genuine sense. Appears in written Tamil in definitional and analytical contexts. Marks a distinction between nominal and genuine instantiation of a category.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** உண்மைத் தன்மையுள்ள
**Transliteration:** uṇmai-t-taṉmaiyuḷḷa
**Literal meaning:** possessing the quality of truth/genuineness; *uṇmai* + *taṉmai* + *uḷḷa* (having)
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine, truthful (attributive)
**Usage:** Attributive participial phrase used in formal written Tamil to characterize persons, works, or representations as genuinely possessing a quality. Appears in literary criticism and philosophical writing. Less lexicalized than *uṇmaiyāṉa* and more compositionally transparent.
**[MARGINAL]**

**20.**
**Form:** சாட்சி
**Transliteration:** cāṭci
**Literal meaning:** witness, testimony (from Sanskrit *sākṣin*)
**Typical English translations:** witness, testimony, evidence
**Usage:** Used in legal and religious contexts to denote a witness or the act of bearing witness. Evaluative in legal contexts where the reliability and truthfulness of testimony is at issue: *poy-cāṭci* (false witness/perjury). Appears in classical Tamil literature as well as modern legal usage.
**[CORE]**

---

**21.**
**Form:** ஆதாரம்
**Transliteration:** ātāram
**Literal meaning:** support, basis, foundation (from Sanskrit *ādhāra*)
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, basis, support, grounds
**Usage:** Used in legal, academic, and everyday contexts to denote evidence or grounds for a claim or judgment. Evaluative in that claims are assessed as well- or ill-founded depending on the *ātāram* available. Common in spoken and written Tamil: *ātāram illāmal* (without evidence/basis).
**[CORE]**

---

**22.**
**Form:** நிரூபணம்
**Transliteration:** nirūpaṇam
**Literal meaning:** proof, demonstration, determination (from Sanskrit *nirūpaṇa*)
**Typical English translations:** proof, demonstration, verification, establishment (of a fact)
**Usage:** Used in formal, legal, and philosophical contexts to denote the act or result of proving or demonstrating something conclusively. Appears in legal discourse (*nirūpaṇam ceyta* — having proved) and in philosophical writing. Less common in everyday speech than *ātāram*.
**[CORE]**

---

**23.**
**Form:** சரிபார்த்தல்
**Transliteration:** caripārtttal
**Literal meaning:** act of checking/verifying; *cari* (correct) + *pār* (to see/look) + verbal noun suffix
**Typical English translations:** verification, checking, confirmation
**Usage:** Verbal noun denoting the act of checking or verifying whether something is correct or accurate. Common in administrative, technical, and everyday contexts. The finite verb form *caripārkkiṟēṉ* (I am checking/verifying) is widely used in spoken Tamil.
**[CORE]**

---

**24.**
**Form:** உண்மைப்படுத்துதல்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaippaṭuttutal
**Literal meaning:** act of making true/real; *uṇmai* + causative *-paṭuttu-* + verbal noun
**Typical English translations:** verification, authentication, confirmation, substantiation
**Usage:** Used in formal and administrative contexts to denote the act of authenticating or verifying a document, claim, or identity. Appears in official Tamil in contexts such as document authentication. More formal than *caripārtttal* and specifically implies establishing truth or genuineness.
**[MARGINAL]**

**25.**
**Form:** தரம்
**Transliteration:** taram
**Literal meaning:** quality, grade, standard (from Sanskrit *tara* via Tamil)
**Typical English translations:** quality, standard, grade, caliber
**Usage:** Used to evaluate objects, persons, or performances with respect to a standard of excellence or adequacy. Common in commercial, aesthetic, and everyday evaluative contexts: *taram kuṟainta* (of reduced/inferior quality). Participates in compounds such as *tarakkuṟaivu* (decline in quality).
**[CORE]**

---

**26.**
**Form:** தரமான
**Transliteration:** taramāṉa
**Literal meaning:** of quality, up to standard; *taram* + *-āṉa*
**Typical English translations:** quality (attributive), of good standard, proper, up to the mark
**Usage:** Adjectival form of *taram*, used to evaluate objects, services, or work as meeting an acceptable or high standard. Extremely common in commercial and everyday speech. Contrasts with *taram kuṟainta* or *taramillāta* (substandard, of poor quality).
**[CORE]**

---

**27.**
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை
**Transliteration:** nampikkai
**Literal meaning:** trust, belief, faith; from *nampu* (to trust/believe) + *-ikkai* (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence, credence
**Usage:** Denotes the state of trusting or believing in a person, institution, or proposition. Used in moral, religious, and everyday contexts to evaluate the reliability of persons or the warranted acceptance of claims. Productive in derivation: *nampikkaiyāṉa* (trustworthy), *nampikkai iḻattal* (loss of trust).
**[CORE]**

---

**28.**
**Form:** நேர்மையற்ற
**Transliteration:** nērmaiyaṟṟa
**Literal meaning:** lacking *nērmai* (honesty/integrity); *nērmai* + *aṟṟa* (privative participial)
**Typical English translations:** dishonest, lacking integrity, unprincipled
**Usage:** Privative adjectival form used to characterize persons or conduct as lacking honesty or integrity. Common in moral evaluation in both spoken and written Tamil. Contrasts directly with *nērmayāṉa* in parallel evaluative constructions.
**[CORE]**

---

**29.**
**Form:** உண்மைக்கு மாறான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaikkum māṟāṉa
**Literal meaning:** contrary to truth; *uṇmai* + dative + *māṟu* (contrary, opposite) + *-āṉa*
**Typical English translations:** contrary to truth, false, untrue, at variance with the facts
**Usage:** A compositional but conventionalized expression used in formal and legal Tamil to characterize statements or representations as contrary to fact. Appears in legal documents and formal written registers. More explicit and formal than simply using *poy* (false).
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**30.**
**Form:** அசல்
**Transliteration:** acal
**Literal meaning:** original, genuine article (from Arabic *aṣl*, via Tamil usage)
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic (of documents, objects)
**Usage:** Used primarily in legal and administrative contexts to distinguish an original document or object from a copy or forgery. Common in everyday speech as well: *acal āvaṇam* (original document). Contrasts directly with *pōli* (fake, counterfeit) and *nakal* (copy).
**[CORE]**
