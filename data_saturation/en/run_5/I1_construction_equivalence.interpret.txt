## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION and BECOME share the *prove* construction

The P4a and P4b data do not provide separate constructions for BECOME (a state coming into being) and REVELATION (a state being discovered or disclosed). Both meanings are expressed by the same surface form: *prove* + predicate adjective (*The rumor proved false*). The verb *prove* carries both the inchoative implication (the state came to obtain) and the evidential implication (this was established through testing or demonstration). No dedicated REVELATION verb or morpheme is attested in the data that is distinct from the inchoative *prove*/*turn out to be* construction. The two operators are therefore not formally distinguished in English as documented here; *prove* serves both functions simultaneously. *Turn out to be* similarly conflates the two. The only construction that is purely inchoative without an evidential component is *become* + adjective, but this form is not illustrated with truth-domain examples in P4a and is listed only as a background option.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are asymmetric

The data show a directional asymmetry between VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION. VERIFICATION has dedicated lexical causatives: *verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*. These are described as common in formal, technical, scientific, and legal registers. DISCONFIRMATION has no parallel dedicated verb for neutral evidential contexts. *Falsify* is the closest candidate, but P4b notes that in non-logical contexts it implies deliberate distortion rather than neutral evidential disconfirmation. The disconfirmatory direction is therefore expressed by the BECOME/*prove* construction (*proved false*, *proved incorrect*) or by the CAUSATION periphrastic (*rendered invalid*, *made false*), both of which are shared with other operators. This is an asymmetric lexical gap: the positive (confirmatory) direction has dedicated vocabulary; the negative (disconfirmatory) direction does not, and relies on constructions that also serve other functions.

### 2.3 NEGATION is both analytic and morphological, with asymmetric coverage

English employs two negation strategies documented in P4a §2. Analytic negation (*not* + adjective) is universal: it applies to every item in the P1 inventory without exception and is the default in spoken and informal registers. Synthetic (morphological) negation via derivational prefix is available for most but not all P1 items. The gap is lexically specific: *\*unfalse* and *\*unfraudulent* are unattested. For items where a prefixed form exists, the two strategies are described as largely interchangeable in propositional content, with register and emphasis differences. The analytic strategy therefore functions as a universal fallback that fills all gaps left by the morphological strategy. There is no case in the data where negation of a P1 adjective is unavailable; the gap is in the morphological strategy only, not in the negation operator as a whole.

### 2.4 CAUSATION (character) is not formally distinguished from CAUSATION (factual)

The P4a and P4b data describe a single causative construction frame — periphrastic *make*/*render* + object + adjective, and lexical causatives — without distinguishing between causation applied to propositions or evidence (factual) and causation applied to persons or sources (character). The same frame (*render* + NP + adjective) can in principle apply to either target. The agentive nominalizations (*truth-teller*, *falsifier*) documented in P4a §7 are the closest constructions that encode character-directed evaluation, but they are rated [MARGINAL] and are not productive across the P1 inventory. No dedicated construction for character-directed causation is identified in the data.

### 2.5 DISCOVERY does not substitute for CAUSATION in the data

The data do not document a DISCOVERY operator as a distinct construction type. The predicate complement with epistemic verb (P4a §8) — *The court found the testimony credible*; *Investigators established that the document was fraudulent* — comes closest to encoding discovery or determination, but this construction is classified in P4a as a distinct pattern encoding both the truth-domain evaluation and the epistemic agent. It does not substitute for CAUSATION; it is a separate construction type that encodes the agent of evaluation rather than the cause of a state change.

### 2.6 Compounding is restricted to *truth* and *false*

The compound noun construction (P4a §10) is noted as specific to *truth* and *false* within the P1 inventory. Other P1 adjectives (*accurate*, *valid*, *sincere*, *credible*, etc.) do not participate productively in this pattern. This is a lexically restricted asymmetry within the NOMINALIZATION and ADNOM domains: the compounding resource is available for two items only.

### 2.7 Discourse-marker constructions are pragmatically distinct

The discourse-marker construction (P4a §9) does not map onto any of the ten CLCA operators listed above. It does not predicate truth of a proposition, does not cause or verify a state, and does not nominalize a property. It functions as a speaker-stance marker. It is included in the P4a inventory but falls outside the operator mapping task; it is noted here for completeness and to confirm that it has been considered and excluded from the mapping table on principled grounds derived from the P4a notes themselves.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
**None identified.** All ten CLCA operators can be expressed in English using attested constructions documented in P4a and P4b. No operator requires a construction type that is entirely absent from the language's grammatical resources.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization across the P1 inventory.** No productive agentive nominal series exists for truth-domain adjectives. *Truth-teller* is a compound restricted to one culturally specific meaning. *Falsifier*, *authenticator*, *validator*, *verifier* are marginal and restricted to technical registers. The agentive for a person characterized by lack of credibility or honesty is expressed periphrastically (*an unreliable witness*, *a dishonest person*) with no dedicated nominal form derived from P1 roots.

2. **Prefixed negative forms for *false* and *fraudulent*.** *\*Unfalse* and *\*unfraudulent* are unattested. Analytic negation (*not false*, *not fraudulent*) fills this gap without structural cost, so the lexical gap does not produce a functional gap.

3. **Dedicated neutral disconfirmation verb.** No single verb parallel to *verify* exists for the disconfirmatory direction in neutral evidential contexts. *Falsify* is available but carries the additional implication of deliberate distortion in non-logical use. The gap is filled by the *prove* + negative adjective construction and by periphrastic causatives.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps
**None identified from the data.** No CLCA operator is blocked as a conceptual category in English. All ten operators correspond to meanings that English can express. The data provide no evidence that any operator is conceptually unavailable or systematically avoided.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

1. **VERIFICATION vs. DISCONFIRMATION (positive/negative asymmetry).** VERIFICATION has dedicated lexical causatives (*verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*) rated [CORE] in formal registers. DISCONFIRMATION lacks a parallel dedicated verb for neutral evidential contexts; *falsify* is available but semantically skewed toward deliberate distortion. The positive (truth-confirming) direction is lexically richer than the negative (truth-denying) direction.

2. **Synthetic negation coverage.** Prefixed negative forms are available for most but not all P1 items. The gap is on the negative side of specific items (*false*, *fraudulent*) only; no item lacks a positive form while having a negative form.

3. ***Come true* restriction.** The inchoative idiom *come true* is available only for the positive truth direction (hopes, predictions, dreams coming to be true) and does not extend to other P1 adjectives or to the negative direction. No parallel idiom *come false* is attested.
