# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope:** Quechua is a dialect continuum with significant lexical variation across varieties (Cusco-Collao, Ayacucho, Ecuadorian, etc.). Items below are drawn primarily from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka), the best-documented varieties. Variety-specific distribution is noted where relevant. Transliteration follows the standardized Quechua orthography established by the Peruvian Ministry of Education (normalized three-vowel system), which is the dominant academic convention for Southern Quechua.

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "true, real, correct, straight"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, correct, genuine, right

Used as an adjective or adverb to predicate truth or correctness of statements, and genuineness or authenticity of persons and things. Appears in constructions such as *cheqam* (it is true, with evidential suffix *-m/-mi*) and *cheqa runa* (a genuine/upright person). Participates in extensive derivation: *cheqakayta* (to be true/real), *cheqachiy* (to verify, to confirm).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqam / cheqami
**Transliteration:** cheqam / cheqami
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + validating evidential clitic *-m/-mi*; "it is true (I assert this directly)"
**Typical English translations:** it is true, indeed, certainly, that is correct

A fixed assertive expression used to affirm the truth of a proposition with direct evidential commitment from the speaker. The evidential clitic *-mi* marks that the speaker has direct knowledge of what is asserted. Commonly used in discourse to confirm or validate a prior statement.

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + agentive/participial suffix *-q*; "one who is true, that which is true/real"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic

Functions as an adjective meaning "true" or "real," often with a stronger or more emphatic sense than bare *cheqa* in some contexts. Used to describe persons of integrity and statements that are factually accurate. Attested in both Cusco and Ayacucho varieties.

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** cheqachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqachiy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y*; "to cause to be true/correct, to verify"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct

Used in contexts of verification, confirmation, or correction of a statement or situation. Can refer to the act of establishing that something is accurate or to correcting an error. Attested in legal and administrative Quechua contexts as well as everyday speech.

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** cheqan
**Transliteration:** cheqan
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive or nominalizing); "its truth, the right/correct one"
**Typical English translations:** the truth, the correct one, what is right

Used to refer to the truth of a matter or the correct version of something. Appears in expressions like *cheqanta rimay* (to speak the truth). The possessive reading ("its truth") and the nominalizing reading overlap in usage.

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** cheqanta rimay
**Transliteration:** cheqanta rimay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa-n-ta (truth-POSS-ACC) + rimay (to speak); "to speak the truth"
**Typical English translations:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully

A conventionalized verbal expression used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to enjoin or describe truthful speech. Appears in testimony contexts and in moral instruction. Functions as a fixed collocation rather than a free compositional phrase.

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "false, lying, deceptive"
**Typical English translations:** false, lying, deceptive, fake, untrue

The primary term for falsehood and deception, used of persons (a liar), statements (a false claim), and objects (a fake or counterfeit). Contrasts directly with *cheqa*. Participates in derivation: *llullakuy* (to lie, to deceive oneself or others), *llullaq* (liar).

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** llulla + reflexive/middle *-ku* + infinitive *-y*; "to lie, to deceive"
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods

The standard verb for the act of lying or deceiving. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in legal contexts. The reflexive suffix *-ku* here marks the action as self-directed or internally motivated rather than purely causative.

**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** llullaq
**Transliteration:** llullaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** llulla + agentive *-q*; "one who lies, a liar"
**Typical English translations:** liar, deceiver, false person

Used as a noun or adjective to characterize a person as habitually dishonest. Carries strong moral condemnation in Quechua-speaking communities. Attested widely across Southern Quechua varieties.

**[CORE]**

**10.**
**Form:** qunqoriy
**Transliteration:** qunqoriy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** uncertain root; "to kneel, to submit" — included here only as potentially relevant to contexts of sworn testimony
**Typical English translations:** to kneel, to submit

**Note:** This item is included with caution. Its relevance to truth/verification contexts is indirect (kneeling as a posture of sworn affirmation in some Andean ritual and legal contexts). The connection is contextual rather than lexically encoded.

**11.**
**Form:** riqsiy
**Transliteration:** riqsiy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root verb; "to know (by acquaintance), to recognize, to identify"
**Typical English translations:** to know, to recognize, to identify, to be acquainted with

Refers to knowledge by acquaintance or recognition, as opposed to propositional knowledge (*yachay*). Used in contexts of identifying whether something or someone is genuine or correctly identified. Relevant to evaluations of identity and authenticity.

**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** yachay
**Transliteration:** yachay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root verb/noun; "to know, to learn; knowledge"
**Typical English translations:** to know, to learn; knowledge, understanding

The primary term for propositional knowledge and learning. Used in epistemic contexts to assert or deny that something is known to be the case. Relevant to evaluations of reliability and accuracy insofar as knowledge grounds correct assertion.

**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** iñiy
**Transliteration:** iñiy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root verb; "to believe, to trust, to have faith in"
**Typical English translations:** to believe, to trust, to have faith

Used for belief in propositions and trust in persons. Relevant to reliability evaluations: *iñinapaq* (trustworthy, believable). Contrasts with *yachay* in that *iñiy* does not entail certainty or direct knowledge.

**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** iñinapaq
**Transliteration:** iñinapaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** iñiy (to believe) + *-na* (nominalizer/infinitive) + *-paq* (purposive/benefactive); "worthy of being believed"
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, believable, credible, reliable

Used to evaluate persons or statements as deserving of belief or trust. Functions as a predicate adjective in reliability assessments. Attested in both everyday and formal registers.

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** mana iñinapaq
**Transliteration:** mana iñinapaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** mana (negation) + iñinapaq (worthy of belief); "not worthy of being believed"
**Typical English translations:** untrustworthy, unbelievable, unreliable, incredible

The negated form of *iñinapaq*, used to characterize persons or statements as lacking credibility. Functions as a fixed negative evaluative expression. Attested in discourse contexts involving testimony and character assessment.

**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "good, well, correct, proper"
**Typical English translations:** good, well, correct, proper, right

A broad evaluative term covering moral goodness, correctness, and appropriateness. Used to evaluate conduct, persons, and states of affairs. Participates in extensive derivation: *allinyay* (to improve, to become correct), *allinchay* (to fix, to correct, to make right).

**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** allinchay
**Transliteration:** allinchay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** allin + causative *-cha* + infinitive *-y*; "to make good/correct, to fix, to repair"
**Typical English translations:** to correct, to fix, to repair, to make right, to improve

Used in contexts of correcting errors, repairing objects, and rectifying conduct or situations. Relevant to accuracy and correctness evaluations as the action of bringing something into conformity with what is right or proper.

**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** mana allin
**Transliteration:** mana allin
**Literal meaning/morphology:** mana (negation) + allin (good/correct); "not good, not correct"
**Typical English translations:** bad, wrong, incorrect, improper, not right

The standard negated evaluative expression for badness, wrongness, or incorrectness. Used across moral, practical, and accuracy-related contexts. Functions as a fixed collocation in evaluative discourse.

**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** hina
**Transliteration:** hina
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "like, similar to, as, in the manner of"
**Typical English translations:** like, similar to, as if, resembling, in the manner of

Used in comparisons and resemblance statements. Relevant to evaluations of whether something matches, resembles, or corresponds to something else. Also used in evidential-like constructions suggesting appearance versus reality (*hina* can mark that something appears to be the case without certainty).

**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** hinam
**Transliteration:** hinam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** hina + validating evidential *-m/-mi*; "it is so, it is like that, that is how it is"
**Typical English translations:** it is so, that is right, that is how it is, indeed

A fixed assertive expression confirming that something is as described or as expected. Used to validate a prior description or characterization. Contrasts with *manahinam* (it is not so, that is not right).

**[CORE]**

**21.**
**Form:** chiqap
**Transliteration:** chiqap
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root or derived form; "true, real, genuine, exact" — variant or near-synonym of *cheqa* in some varieties
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, exact, precise

Attested in some Southern Quechua varieties (including Cusco) as a near-synonym of *cheqa* with possible emphasis on exactness or precision. The relationship between *chiqap* and *cheqa* varies by variety and context. Some sources treat *chiqap* as more emphatic or more specifically "exact."

**22.**
**Form:** pura
**Transliteration:** pura
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "pure, only, among (members of a group), itself"
**Typical English translations:** pure, only, itself, among (peers), exclusively

Used in contexts emphasizing that something is purely or exclusively what it is, without admixture or pretense. Relevant to authenticity and identity evaluations in some constructions. Also functions as a postposition meaning "among."

**23.**
**Form:** qispiy
**Transliteration:** qispiy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root verb; "to be free, to be saved, to escape, to be clear/pure"
**Typical English translations:** to be free, to be saved, to be clear, to be pure, to escape

In some contexts, used to describe a state of being free from fault, guilt, or impurity — relevant to moral and legal evaluations of innocence or blamelessness. The connection to truth/accuracy evaluation is indirect and contextual.

**24.**
**Form:** pantay
**Transliteration:** pantay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root verb; "to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong, to go astray"
**Typical English translations:** to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong, to go astray

The standard verb for being in error or making a mistake, used of persons, statements, and actions. Directly relevant to accuracy evaluations as the condition of being incorrect. Contrasts with *cheqa* and *allin* in evaluative contexts.

**25.**

**Transliteration:** pantasqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** pantay + past participle *-sqa*; "having erred, mistaken, wrong"
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error

Used as an adjective to characterize a person, statement, or action as mistaken or incorrect. Appears in evaluative judgments about accuracy and correctness. Attested across Southern Quechua varieties.

**[CORE]**

**26.**
**Form:** sut'i
**Transliteration:** sut'i
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "clear, plain, explicit, evident, distinct"
**Typical English translations:** clear, plain, explicit, evident, distinct, unambiguous

Used to evaluate statements, explanations, and representations as clear, unambiguous, or evident. Relevant to accuracy and reliability insofar as clarity is associated with correct and transparent communication. Also used of identity: *sut'i sutin* (its clear/true name).

**Transliteration:** sut'ichay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** sut'i + causative *-cha* + infinitive *-y*; "to make clear, to clarify, to explain plainly"
**Typical English translations:** to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify

Used in contexts of clarification, specification, and disambiguation. Relevant to accuracy evaluations as the act of making a representation more precise or correct. Attested in both everyday and formal registers.

**28.**
**Form:** qillqasqa
**Transliteration:** qillqasqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** qillqay (to write) + past participle *-sqa*; "written, that which is written"
**Typical English translations:** written, document, written record, text

Included here as relevant to legal and administrative contexts where written records are evaluated for accuracy and authenticity. The term itself does not encode evaluation but is the standard term for written documents subject to such evaluation.

**Typical English translations:** to strengthen, to confirm, to reinforce, to validate

In some contexts used to mean confirming or validating a statement or decision, giving it force or authority. The connection to truth/accuracy evaluation is indirect; the primary meaning is strengthening or reinforcing.

**30.**
**Form:** runa
**Transliteration:** runa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "person, human being, (Quechua) person"
**Typical English translations:** person, human being, man, indigenous person

Included here specifically in the collocation *cheqa runa* (a true/genuine/upright person), where it participates in moral evaluation of persons. As a standalone item, *runa* does not encode evaluation, but it is the standard nominal base for person-directed moral assessments in Quechua.
