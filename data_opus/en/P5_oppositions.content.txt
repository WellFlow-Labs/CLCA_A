## Language: English (en)

The following oppositions are extracted solely from patterns attested in the CONTEXT (P1–P4b). Each is grounded in explicit contrastive data (antonym pairings, prefix negations, suppletion notes, or constructional contrasts).

### OPPOSITION 1
1. **Term A:** true
2. **Term B:** false
3. **Transliteration:** true / false
4. **English translation:** corresponding to fact / not corresponding to fact
5. **Description of contrast:** The central statement-truth antonym pair, repeatedly contrasted across P1, P3 Group 1, and P4a/P4b as positive/negative poles ("that is true" vs "that is false"). *false* is a suppletive (non-derived) antonym, not a prefixed form.

## Summary Notes (data-supported)

- **Dominant pattern:** morphological negation by lexically-unpredictable prefix (*un-*, *in-*, *im-*, *il-*, *dis-*) is the most frequent opposition type, attested across Groups 1, 4, 6, 7, 8.
- **Suppletion:** the highest-frequency native items use suppletive antonyms (*true/false*, *real-genuine/fake*, *right/wrong*, *truth-teller/liar*) rather than, or in competition with, prefixed forms.
- **Constructional oppositions** exist independently of lexical ones: *ring true / ring false*, affirmative vs. negative attribution, and the prefixed-vs-suppletive strategy contrast.
- **Asymmetries (not forced into balance):** the Authentication Nominal Frame has no negative pole; *just* takes *un-* as adjective but *in-* as noun; *false* generates fewer idioms than *true* (P2b/P3).
- Marginal-status oppositions involve items whose dominant sense lies outside the truth domain (*incredible* = "amazing") or whose forms are themselves marginal (*inexact*).
