## 1. Language Inventory

- **zh — Chinese**
  Broad inventory covering everyday true/false, formal verification, correctness, accuracy, credibility, authenticity, realism, essence/substance, and legitimacy.

- **he — Hebrew**
  Rich split between truth, correctness, precision, credibility, authenticity, correspondence, justice/rightness, propriety, and essence.

- **sa — Sanskrit**
  Strongly differentiated lexicon for truth, falsehood, correct cognition, proof/validity, propriety, essence, and resemblance, with many scholastic and philosophical forms.

- **el — Greek**
  Broad system distinguishing truth, correctness, precision, validity, authenticity, reality, substance, fidelity, falsification, sincerity, and plausibility.

- **en — English**
  Large and differentiated inventory separating truth, correctness, precision, validity, reliability, authenticity, lawfulness, legitimacy, fairness, and fidelity.

- **tl — Tagalog**
  Core contrasts among truth, correctness, precision, certainty, trustworthiness, honesty, genuineness, fakery, support/basis, and verification.

- **sw — Swahili**
  Core contrasts among truth, falsehood, correctness, authenticity, fakery, trustworthiness, belief, justice, validity/lawfulness, and evidentness.

- **qu — Quechua**
  Smaller but clearly structured system distinguishing truth/reality, falsehood/lying, mistake/error, rightness/propriety, identity/difference, belief/trust, guilt, and worth.

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### zh — Chinese
Main distinctions are especially fine-grained. The data separates everyday **真 / 假**, formal factual verification (**属实, 不实, 失实, 如实**), deception/fabrication (**虚假, 伪造**), correctness (**正确 / 错误**), precision (**准确, 失真**), credibility (**可信, 可靠**), commercial authenticity (**真品, 假货, 冒牌**), title/reputation matching reality (**名副其实**), lifelike resemblance (**仿真, 逼真**), original-style authenticity (**原汁原味, 道地**), and essence/substance (**本质, 实质**) plus legitimacy (**正当**). Several items overlap across these zones, especially **真实**, **真**, **真正**, **货真价实**, **正宗**, and **失真**.

Derivationally, Chinese shows many productive abstract nouns with **-性** and **-度**, many adverbial **-地** forms, and many lexical compounds. There is asymmetry in that some domains are highly productive (**准确性, 可靠性, 可信度**) while idiomatic items like **名副其实** and **货真价实** lack common derivations.

Constructionally, Chinese strongly prefers ordinary attribution and negation, but also has specialized official verification formulas with **属实 / 经查属实 / 查证属实 / 是否属实**. Dynamic patterns are common with **变得**, **越来越**, **使/让**, but not for all items: **属实** notably resists ordinary “become” patterns and instead uses verification constructions. Caused falsity is often expressed lexically by verbs like **伪造** or phrases like **发布虚假信息**, rather than by general adjectival causatives.

Oppositionally, Chinese has many clear lexical pairs: **真/假**, **真实/虚假**, **正确/错误**, **属实/失实**, **属实/不实**. It also has productive negation oppositions (**可信/不可信**, **可靠/不可靠**, **准确/不准确**) and contrasts between actual reality and resemblance (**仿真/逼真** vs **真/真实**).

### he — Hebrew
Hebrew distinguishes everyday truth/falsity (**אמת, נכון, לא נכון, שקר**), softer precision-based correction (**מדויק, לא מדויק, דיוק**), credibility/reliability (**מהימן, אמין, מהימנות, אמינות**), genuineness/authenticity (**אמיתי, אותנטי, מקורי**), forgery/counterfeit (**זיוף, מזויף**), correspondence and sameness (**דומה ל־, זהה, תואם, תואם למציאות**), formal falsehood labels (**כוזב, שקרי**), being right/justified (**צודק, צדק, מוצדק**), justice/fairness (**צדק** cluster), propriety (**ראוי**), and essence (**מהות**).

Derivationally, Hebrew shows many abstract nouns and negative forms, but not all bases are equally productive. There are strong families around **אמת**, **דיוק**, **אמין/אמינות**, **מהימן/מהימנות**, and **צדק**. A notable asymmetry is that the data explicitly reports no common inchoative construction across the truth domain. Another is that **נכונות** is semantically split, often meaning readiness/willingness rather than correctness.

Constructionally, Hebrew relies heavily on simple attribution and negation, support-verb constructions (**לומר את האמת, לעשות צדק**), discourse markers (**למען האמת, ליתר דיוק**), and comparison/correspondence constructions (**זהה ל־, תואם ל־**). Dynamic meaning is often expressed not as “become true” but through revelation (**לגלות את האמת, להוציא את האמת לאור**), vindication (**יצא צודק**), justification (**להצדיק**), testing (**בדיקת אמינות, מבחן האמת**), or precision-improvement (**לדייק**). The tables explicitly note absence of a common general inchoative pattern.

Oppositionally, Hebrew has strong pairs such as **אמת/שקר**, **נכון/לא נכון**, **מדויק/לא מדויק**, **אמין/לא אמין**, **מהימן/לא מהימן**, **אותנטי/לא אותנטי**, **מקורי/מזויף**, **תואם למציאות/אינו תואם למציאות**. It also shows repeated usage contrasts rather than strict antonymy, such as **צודק** vs **נכון**, and **אמין** vs **מהימן**.

### sa — Sanskrit
Sanskrit shows a broad lexical split among basic truth/falsehood (**सत्य, सत्यम्, असत्य, मिथ्या, मृषा, अनृत**), factual correspondence (**तथ्य, यथार्थ, भूतार्थ**), correct cognition (**प्रमा, यथार्थज्ञान, मिथ्या ज्ञानम्**), proof/validity/authority (**प्रमाण, प्रामाण्य, प्रमाणिक**), general correctness/propriety (**सम्यक्, युक्त, उचित, न्याय्य, धर्म्य**), essence/reality (**स्वरूप, तत्त्व**), resemblance (**सदृश, तादृश**), and older Vedic truth/order (**ऋत / अनृत**). Overlap is especially visible around **सत्य**, **यथार्थ**, and the **प्रमा/प्रमाण** family.

Derivationally, Sanskrit is highly elaborated. It has many abstract nouns (**-ता, -त्व**), agentive forms (**सत्यवादिन्, असत्यवादिन्**), privative opposites (**असत्य, अयथार्थ, अप्रामाण्य, अप्रमाणिक**), and large scholastic families around **यथार्थ** and **प्रमाण**. A notable asymmetry is that some domains are richly derivational (**प्रमाण** family especially), while others have no common further derivation in the dataset.

Constructionally, Sanskrit strongly uses speech-verb constructions (**सत्यं वद, मिथ्या वदति, असत्यं वदति, अनृतं वदति**), genitive evaluation constructions (**वाक्यस्य सत्यता, ज्ञानस्य प्रामाण्यम्**), instrumental grounds (**प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**), and adverbial standpoint markers (**यथार्थतः, तत्त्वतः, स्वरूपतः**). Dynamic patterns are limited and domain-specific: **सत्यतां गच्छति** is only marginal, while dynamicity is clearer in cognition (**प्रमा जायते**) and proof/establishment (**प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**). The tables explicitly note no single general causative “make true/make valid.”

Oppositionally, Sanskrit has many clear morphological pairs: **सत्य/असत्य**, **ऋत/अनृत**, **यथार्थ/अयथार्थ**, **यथार्थज्ञान/अयथार्थज्ञान**, **प्रामाण्य/अप्रामाण्य**, **प्रमाणिक/अप्रमाणिक**, **सत्यवचन/असत्यवचन**, **सत्यवादिन्/असत्यवादिन्**. It also contrasts truthful speech with false speech through parallel constructions.

### el — Greek
Greek distinguishes statement truth (**αλήθεια, αληθής, ψευδής, ψέμα**), everyday correctness (**σωστός**), formal correctness (**ορθός, ορθότητα**), precision (**ακρίβεια, ακριβής**), validity/reliability/authority (**έγκυρος, εγκυρότητα, έγκριτος**), authenticity/genuineness (**αυθεντικός, γνήσιος**), reality (**πραγματικός, πραγματικότητα**), substance/essence (**ουσία, ουσιώδης, ουσιαστικός, κατ’ ουσίαν**), fidelity to an original (**πιστός, πιστότητα, απαράλλακτος, κατά γράμμα**), falsification/forgery (**παραποιημένος, πλαστός**), sincerity (**ειλικρινής**), and appearance-of-truth (**αληθοφανής, αληθοφάνεια**).

Derivationally, Greek has many regular abstract nouns and adverbs, and many negative forms. Some families are especially rich (**αλήθεια**, **ορθός**, **ουσία**). A notable asymmetry is that some specialized **ορθο-** derivatives are morphologically related but semantically peripheral to truth/correctness. Another is that there is no common dedicated causative for “make valid/authentic/genuine.”

Constructionally, Greek uses ordinary attribution and negation, but also result/discovery constructions such as **αποδεικνύεται ψευδής** and **αποδείχθηκε πλαστή**. Dynamic truth is expressed more naturally through **αληθεύει** or evidential/resultative patterns than through a single general “become true” verb. Falsification is expressed with lexical verbs (**παραποιώ, πλαστογραφώ**). Discourse-level shifts are important with **στην πραγματικότητα**, **στην ουσία**, **κατ’ ουσίαν**, **επί της ουσίας**.

Oppositionally, Greek has strong pairs such as **αλήθεια/ψέμα**, **αληθές/ψευδές**, **αληθής/ψευδής**, **αυθεντικός/γνήσιος** vs **πλαστός**, **γνήσια υπογραφή/πλαστή υπογραφή**, **ουσία/τύπος**, **κατ’ ουσίαν/κατά γράμμα**, **halisi-like reality** vs appearance-side notions. It also has repeated usage contrasts like **σωστός** vs **ορθός**, and **αληθινός** vs **αληθής**.

### en — English
English distinguishes basic truth/falsity (**truth, true, untrue, false**), answer/procedure correctness (**correct, right**), precision (**accurate, exact, precise**), genuineness/authenticity/reality (**genuine, authentic, real**), validity/soundness (**valid, sound**), reliability/trustworthiness/credibility (**reliable, trustworthy, credible**), fidelity to an original (**faithful, true to life**), fake/counterfeit/forgery (**fake, counterfeit, forgery**), appearance-of-truth (**semblance, verisimilitude, ring true**), propriety (**proper**), legitimacy/lawfulness (**legitimate, lawful**), and fairness/justice (**just, fair**).

Derivationally, English shows extensive negative morphology (**untrue, incorrect, inaccurate, invalid, unreliable, untrustworthy, inauthentic, illegitimate, unlawful, unjust, unfair**) and many abstract nouns (**truthfulness, accuracy, authenticity, validity, reliability, credibility, precision, legitimacy, justice, fairness**). A notable asymmetry is that negative agentive or negative lexical items are often more entrenched than positive counterparts, e.g. **liar** is much more ordinary than **truth-teller**.

Constructionally, English has many domain-specific dynamic patterns rather than one unified system: **prove true, turn out true, come true** for truth-confirmation; **get it right, set right, put right, correct** for correction; **authenticate** for authenticity; **validate/invalidate** for validity; **hold true, remain valid, remain faithful** for stability. The tables explicitly note that English does not use one single dynamic verb across the whole domain.

Oppositionally, English has many clear morphological pairs and also several constructional contrasts: positive vs negative attribution, **tell the truth** vs lying expressions, **seem credible / ring true** vs **be true**, **faithful to** vs simple propositional truth, and **lawful** vs broader **right/proper**. It also clearly separates precision terms from broader truth/correctness terms.

### tl — Tagalog
Tagalog distinguishes truth/reality (**totoo, katotohanan**), correctness (**tama, wasto**), precision (**tumpak, eksakto**), certainty (**tiyak, sigurado**), trustworthiness/reliability (**mapagkakatiwalaan, maaasahan**), honesty/sincerity (**tapat, matapat**), genuineness/authenticity (**tunay, totoong**), fake/counterfeit (**peke, pekeng, huwad**), suitability (**akma, angkop**), support/basis (**salig, saligan, walang saligan**), and checked/verified status (**beripikado, beripikahin**). Some forms overlap strongly, especially **totoo / tunay / totoong**, **tama / wasto**, and **tapat / matapat**.

Derivationally, Tagalog shows productive ligatured attributive forms (**totoong, wastong, pekeng**), abstract nominalization (**katotohanan, saligan**), and a notable dynamic family around **totoo**: **magkatotoo, nagkatotoo, totohanin**. A strong asymmetry is that many other bases do not show similarly supported derivational families in the dataset.

Constructionally, Tagalog relies heavily on predicate-initial attribution without a copula, interrogative **ba** patterns, discourse frames (**sa totoo lang, sa katotohanan**), basis constructions (**salig sa, may saligan, walang saligan**), and verification constructions (**beripikahin, beripikadong**). Dynamicity is concentrated in a few areas: **magkatotoo / nagkatotoo** for “come true/turn out true,” **wastuhin** as a limited corrective verb, and **beripikahin** for procedural checking. The tables explicitly note absence of a common general causative “make true.”

Oppositionally, Tagalog has clear contrasts such as **totoo/peke**, **tunay/peke**, **tunay/huwad**, **may saligan/walang saligan**, positive vs negative **tama** and **tiyak** constructions, and colloquial seriousness contrasts within the **totoo** family (**Huwag mong totohanin... / Totohanin mo...**).

### sw — Swahili
Swahili distinguishes truth/falsity (**kweli, ukweli, uongo, -kweli, -ongo**), correctness/accuracy (**sahihi, usahihi**), genuineness/reality (**halisi, uhalisia, asili, ya asili**), fake/counterfeit (**bandia, feki**), trustworthiness/credibility (**aminifu, uaminifu, kuaminika, aminika**), belief/trust (**kuamini, imani** family), justice/rights (**haki, -enye haki**), validity/lawfulness/prohibition (**batili, halali, haramu**), evidentness (**dhahiri**), and resemblance/example (**-fanana, mfano**). Overlap is visible especially between truth and genuineness, and between trustworthiness and belief.

Derivationally, Swahili shows strong noun-class and abstract-noun morphology: **ukweli, usahihi, uhalisia, uaminifu**, person nouns like **mkweli, mwongo, mwaminifu**, and negative credibility forms like **asiyeaminika, kutoaminika**. A notable asymmetry is that some domains are richly derivational (**kweli/uongo**, **amini** family), while others remain mostly lexical.

Constructionally, Swahili strongly uses simple predication with **ni / si**, light-verb constructions (**sema ukweli, kusema uongo**), genitive quality constructions (**habari za uongo, bidhaa ya asili**), and relative-clause credibility constructions (**chanzo kinachoaminika**). Dynamic patterns are limited: **kuaminika** is the clearest state-change-compatible form, and **kutia imani** is the nearest causative-like pattern, but the tables explicitly note no common general dynamic verb for “become true/correct/authentic.”

Oppositionally, Swahili has strong pairs such as **kweli/uongo**, **-kweli/-ongo**, **ni kweli/si kweli**, **ni kweli/ni uongo**, **halali/haramu**, **sahihi/si sahihi**, and authenticity contrasts like **halisi/bandia** and **ya asili/bandia**. It also contrasts accurate information with false information and credible sources with non-credible ones.

### qu — Quechua
Quechua distinguishes truth/reality (**chiqaq, chiqap, chiqaq kay**), falsehood/lying (**llulla, llullakuy, llullasqa**), mistake/error (**pantay, pantasqa, pantachiy**), rightness/propriety (**allin, allinmi, mana allin**), identity/difference/resemblance (**chiqan, kikin, kuska, rikch’akuy, riqsiy**), belief/trust (**iñiy, iñisqa**), guilt/fault (**hucha, huchayuq**), and worth/esteem (**chanin, chaninnaq, yupaychay, sumaq**). The system is smaller than in several other languages but still clearly differentiated.

Derivationally, Quechua shows participial and agentive formations (**pantasqa, llullasqa, llullaq, iñisqa, huchayuq**), causatives (**pantachiy**), and some fixed combinations (**chiqaq kay, llulla runa, llulla simi**). A notable asymmetry is that the data gives no common inchoative expressions for “become true/false/wrong”; dynamic morphology is clearer for causing error than for entering truth states.

Constructionally, the data highlights simple predicative evaluation (**allinmi, pantasqa, mana allin**), adverbial truth assertion (**chiqap**), speech-specific noun phrases (**chiqaq simi, llulla simi**), and causatives of error (**pantachiy, pantayman churay**). The F6 table repeatedly notes absence of common expressions for “become true,” “become false,” or similar transitions.

Oppositionally, Quechua has clear contrasts such as **chiqaq/llulla**, **chiqaq simi/llulla simi**, **allin/mana allin**, **chiqan/kikin**, and mistake vs deception (**pantay/pantasqa** vs **llulla/llullasqa**). It also contrasts reality with mere seeming through **chiqaq kay** vs **rikch’akuy**.

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring convergences
- Many languages clearly separate at least three areas:  
  **(a)** truth/falsity of statements,  
  **(b)** correctness/right answer/proper procedure, and  
  **(c)** genuineness/authenticity vs fake/counterfeit.  
  This is especially clear in **zh, he, el, en, tl, sw**, and also visible in **sa** and **qu**.

- Many languages also distinguish **precision/accuracy** from broader truth or correctness. This is strongly visible in **zh, he, el, en, tl, sw**, and in more specialized form in **sa**.

- Several languages distinguish **credibility/reliability/trustworthiness** from direct truth. This is especially strong in **zh, he, el, en, tl, sw**, and present in **sa** and **qu** through belief/trust or validity-related forms.

- Many languages have a separate area for **moral or normative rightness** rather than factual truth: **zh 正当**, **he צדק / ראוי**, **sa न्याय्य / धर्म्य / उचित**, **el δικαιοσύνη-related and propriety-related forms**, **en just / fair / lawful / legitimate**, **sw haki / halali / haramu**, **qu hucha / huchayuq / allin**.

- Across many languages, **truth-telling and lying are often expressed by speech constructions**, not only by adjectives:  
  **zh 如实回答 / 真话 / 假话**, **he לומר את האמת / לספר שקר**, **sa सत्यं वद / मिथ्या वदति**, **el λέω την αλήθεια / λέω ψέματα**, **en tell the truth**, **sw sema ukweli / kusema uongo**, **qu llullakuy / speech-specific phrases**.

- Many languages show **specialized expressions for authenticity of objects/documents/identity** rather than using the same words as statement-truth: **zh 真品/假货/冒牌/伪造**, **he מקורי/אותנטי/מזויף/זיוף**, **el αυθεντικός/γνήσιος/πλαστός/παραποιημένος**, **en authentic/genuine/fake/counterfeit/forgery**, **tl tunay/peke/huwad/beripikado**, **sw halisi/bandia/feki/ya asili**.

- Many languages use **discourse markers meaning roughly “to tell the truth / actually / in fact”**: **zh 说真的 / 如实**, **he למען האמת / האמת היא ש־ / לאמיתו של דבר**, **el η αλήθεια είναι ότι / στην πραγματικότητα / στην ουσία**, **en truth be told / in truth**, **tl sa totoo lang / sa katotohanan**, **sw kwa kweli / kweli ni kwamba**.

### Major divergences
- **Chinese** has an unusually fine-grained split between everyday truth, official verification, deception/fraud, precision, credibility, realism, original-style authenticity, essence/substance, and legitimacy.

- **Sanskrit** stands out for its large technical-philosophical system around **correct cognition** and **proof/validity** (**प्रमा, प्रमाण, प्रामाण्य**), much more elaborated than in most other languages.

- **Hebrew** stands out for the explicit absence of a common general inchoative pattern in the truth domain; dynamic meaning is instead routed through revelation, vindication, testing, and precision-improvement.

- **Greek** shows a particularly strong split among **truth**, **correctness**, **precision**, **validity**, **authenticity**, **reality**, **substance**, and **fidelity to an original**, with many discourse-level “substance vs wording” expressions.

- **Tagalog** stands out for concentrating dynamic truth-change in the **totoo** family (**magkatotoo, nagkatotoo**) while many other nearby domains remain mainly stative.

- **Swahili** stands out for the central role of **noun-class and relative-clause constructions** in this domain, especially for credibility (**chanzo kinachoaminika**) and quality/genitive expressions (**habari za uongo, bidhaa ya asili**).

- **Quechua** differs in having a smaller system with strong contrasts between **truth**, **lying**, **mistake**, and **rightness**, but with repeated absence of common “become true/false” expressions in the supplied material.

### Asymmetries, gaps, or irregularities
- In several languages, **negative or falsehood terms are more lexicalized than positive person terms**. This is especially clear in **en** (*liar* vs less common *truth-teller*), and also visible in other languages where “truthful person” is more descriptive or morally marked.

- Many languages show **no single general dynamic verb covering the whole truth domain**. This is explicitly noted for **he, sa, en, tl, sw, qu**, and partly for **el**. Instead, different subdomains use different verbs or constructions.

- Several languages have **specialized verification or evidential-result constructions** rather than ordinary “become true” patterns:  
  **zh 属实 / 经查属实**, **he להוציא את האמת לאור / מבחן האמת / בדיקת אמינות**, **el αποδεικνύεται ψευδής / αποδείχθηκε πλαστή**, **en prove true / authenticate / validate**, **tl beripikahin / beripikado**.

- Some languages show **broad items that cut across multiple areas and resist neat grouping**:  
  **zh 真, 真实, 真正**, **he אמת, נכון, אמיתי, צדק**, **sa सत्य, यथार्थ**, **el αληθινός, πραγματικός, ουσιαστικός**, **tl totoo, wasto, tapat**, **sw kweli, -kweli**, **qu allin**.

- Some languages have **specialized “appearance of truth” or resemblance zones**, but not all do. This is especially clear in **zh 仿真/逼真**, **he דומה / זהה / תואם**, **el αληθοφανής / αληθοφάνεια**, **en ring true / verisimilitude / semblance**, and more weakly in **sw -fanana** and **qu rikch’akuy**.

- Some languages have **distinct “substance/essence vs form/appearance” vocabularies**, while others do not show this strongly in the supplied material. This is especially strong in **zh 本质/实质**, **he מהות**, **sa स्वरूप/तत्त्व**, **el ουσία / κατ’ ουσίαν**, and **en** only more diffusely through lexical items like *real*, *essence*, *proper*, *valid* in the provided set.

- A few languages show **specialized colloquial shifts away from truth into seriousness or stance**: especially **tl totohanin / totohanan**, and to a lesser extent discourse uses like **zh 说真的**, **he באמת**, **el πραγματικά**, **en really**, **sw kwa kweli**.
