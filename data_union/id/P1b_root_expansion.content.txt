# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **benar** (Latin script; no separate native script)

**Participation in P1 items:** 3 items directly (*benar* entry 1; *kebenaran* item 24; *sebenarnya* item 25); additionally the root underlies *membenarkan* mentioned in derivational notes across multiple entries; derivatives cited across multiple entries (*kebenaran*, *membenarkan*).

**Core meaning contribution:** The primary positive pole of factual and moral correctness in Indonesian. Serves as the base for the most productive derivational family in the truth-evaluation domain, generating nouns, verbs, adverbs, and fixed phrases covering truth, justification, confirmation, and rectification. The root anchors the affirmative side of the truth/correctness axis and is highly productive morphologically across formal, legal, moral, and everyday registers.

---

### Root 2: **sah** (Latin script; from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*) / **sahih** (from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*)

**Participation in P1 items:** 4 items directly (*sah* entry 10; *sahih* entry 9; *absah* entry 11; *mengesahkan* mentioned in derivational notes of items 9 and 10); *keabsahan* cited under both *sah* and *absah*; the Arabic root underlies all three.

**Core meaning contribution:** Validity, legitimacy, and formal correctness — the evaluation of whether an act, document, status, or claim meets the conditions required for recognition as binding, sound, or authoritative. Operates across legal, religious, administrative, and procedural domains. The three P1 items represent three registers (everyday-formal, formal-legal, Islamic-scholarly) of the same underlying semantic contribution.

---

### Root 3: **salah** (Latin script)

**Participation in P1 items:** 2 items directly (*salah*, *kesalahan*, *menyalahkan* mentioned in derivation); also implicitly present in compounds such as *salah paham*, *salah kaprah*, etc.

**Core meaning contribution:** Deviation from correctness, fact, or proper conduct — whether through error, misjudgment, or moral fault. The root anchors the negative pole of the correctness axis and is highly productive in compounds expressing specific types of error or misattribution.

---

### Root 4: **asli** (Latin script) / **palsu** (Latin script) — paired root cluster

> **Note on selection:** *asli* and *palsu* form a tightly coupled antonymic pair that together anchor the authenticity/inauthenticity axis in the P1 data. Each is individually productive. They are treated here as a paired cluster with complementary derivational families, each expanded separately below.

**Participation in P1 items:** 3 items directly (*asli* item 3; *palsu* item 4; *tiruan* item 18 contrasted with *asli*); derivational mentions include *memalsukan*, *pemalsuan*. The root *asli* also contrasts structurally with *palsu* and *tiruan*, anchoring the authenticity/genuineness pole of the domain.

**Core meaning contribution:** The axis of genuine-vs.-counterfeit, original-vs.-fabricated, used across legal, commercial, cultural, and identity contexts.

---

### Root 5: **bukti** (Latin script)

**Participation in P1 items:** 1 direct item (*terbukti* entry 23); *bukti* cited as base noun; *membuktikan* cited in derivation.

**Core meaning contribution:** Evidence and proof — the grounding of truth claims in demonstrable support. Highly productive in legal, journalistic, scientific, and procedural contexts. Its derivational family most directly concerns the epistemic process of establishing truth rather than merely attributing it.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: **benar**

---

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *benar* (root — correct, true)
**Natural English translation:** true, correct, right
**Context of use:** Base adjective/predicate used to affirm factual accuracy or moral correctness in everyday and formal speech. The unmarked positive evaluator of truth in Indonesian. Appears across all registers from casual conversation to formal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ke-* (nominalizer circumfix) + *benar* + *-an*
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, rightness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse to refer to the property of being true or correct, or to a specific true fact. *Mencari kebenaran* (to seek the truth) is a common collocation. Already listed in P1 (item 24); included here as the central nominal of the family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *mem-* (active verb prefix) + *benar* + *-kan* (causative/applicative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to justify, to validate, to correct (something/someone)
**Context of use:** Used in institutional, journalistic, and interpersonal contexts to mean either "to confirm that something is true" or "to justify/excuse an action." *Pihak berwenang membenarkan laporan itu* (The authorities confirmed the report). Also used in moral contexts: *tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu* (nothing can justify that action). The dual sense (epistemic confirmation vs. moral justification) is well established.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** dibenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *benar* + *-kan*
**Natural English translation:** to be confirmed; to be permitted, to be justified
**Context of use:** Passive form of *membenarkan*. Widely used in legal and administrative contexts: *tidak dibenarkan* (not permitted, not justified) is a standard formula in regulations and official documents. *Hal ini tidak dibenarkan oleh hukum* (This is not sanctioned by law).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *se-* (equative/intensifying prefix) + *benar* + *-nya* (nominalizing/specifying suffix)
**Natural English translation:** actually, in truth, in fact, to tell the truth, really
**Context of use:** Discourse marker used to introduce a correction, clarification, or revelation of what is actually the case, often contrasting with a prior assumption. Extremely common in spoken and written Indonesian. *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju* (Actually, I disagree). Already listed in P1 (item 25).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** sebenar-benarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *se-* + *benar* + reduplication of *benar* + *-nya*
**Natural English translation:** the absolute truth, truly and completely, in all honesty, in all truth
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of *sebenarnya*, used in formal declarations, oaths, and solemn statements to assert that what follows is the complete and unqualified truth. Common in legal testimony and formal written declarations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** benar-benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *benar* + full reduplication
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely, absolutely
**Context of use:** Intensifying adverb used to emphasize the degree or genuineness of a quality or action. *Dia benar-benar tidak tahu* (He truly did not know). Very common in everyday speech and writing across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** pembenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *pem-* (agentive/process nominalizer) + *benar* + *-an*
**Natural English translation:** justification, confirmation, validation, rationalization
**Context of use:** Used in moral, psychological, legal, and philosophical discourse to refer to the act or process of justifying or confirming something. *Pembenaran diri* (self-justification) is a common collocation in psychological and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 2: **sah** (and its variants *absah*, *sahih*)

---

**1.**
**Form:** sah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sah* (root — valid, lawful; from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*)
**Natural English translation:** valid, legitimate, lawful, official
**Context of use:** Base adjective/predicate used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to evaluate whether an act or status meets required conditions. *Pernikahan itu sah* (The marriage is valid/legal). Already listed in P1 (item 10).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** mengesahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *meng-* (active verb prefix) + *e* (linking vowel) + *sah* + *-kan* (causative suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to validate, to ratify, to legalize, to authenticate, to endorse
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and political contexts to describe the act of officially approving or ratifying a document, law, or decision. *DPR mengesahkan undang-undang baru* (The parliament ratified the new law). Standard term in legislative and notarial language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** disahkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *di-* (passive prefix) + *sah* + *-kan*
**Natural English translation:** to be ratified, to be validated, to be legalized
**Context of use:** Passive form of *mengesahkan*, used in official announcements, legal documents, and news reporting. *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan* (The law has been ratified). Extremely common in legislative and administrative language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** pengesahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *peng-* (process nominalizer) + *e* + *sah* + *-an*
**Natural English translation:** ratification, validation, legalization, authentication
**Context of use:** Noun referring to the act or process of official validation or ratification. Used in legal, administrative, and political contexts. *Pengesahan undang-undang* (ratification of a law) is a standard phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** keabsahan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ke-* + *absah* (variant of *sah*) + *-an*
**Natural English translation:** validity, legitimacy, lawfulness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun used in legal, political, academic, and philosophical contexts to refer to the property of being valid or legitimate. *Keabsahan kontrak itu dipertanyakan* (The validity of the contract was questioned). *Keabsahan pemilu* (validity/legitimacy of an election) is a common collocation in political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**

**Transliteration:** N/A

**Natural English translation:** invalid, illegitimate, unlawful, void, null and void
**Context of use:** Standard negation used in legal, administrative, and religious contexts to declare that an act, document, or status does not meet required conditions. *Pernikahan itu tidak sah* (That marriage is invalid). A standard legal and religious formula.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
