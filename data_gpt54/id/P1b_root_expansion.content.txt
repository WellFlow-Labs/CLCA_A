## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **benar** — transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
(*benar, kebenaran, membenarkan*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the sense of correctness, truth, or rightness, both for statements and for judgments about actions or decisions.

### 2. **tepat** — transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
(*tepat, ketepatan, tepat sasaran*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the sense of exactness, precision, and fittingness; often used for accuracy, appropriateness, and correct targeting.

### 3. **sah / absah / valid** — transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 5 P1 items total as a closely overlapping validity cluster  
(*sah, absah, keabsahan, valid, validitas*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the sense of validity, legitimacy, and formal acceptability under rules, procedures, law, or technical standards.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

## Root: **benar**

Below are common derived forms, compounds, collocations, and fixed expressions built from **benar**. This root is moderately productive; common usage is concentrated in a limited set of high-frequency forms.

### 1. **benar**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** basic root  
**English:** true; correct; right  
**Context of use:** Used very widely to affirm that a statement, answer, or judgment is correct. It also appears in evaluative contexts for what is morally or practically right.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **kebenaran**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *ke- + benar + -an*  
**English:** truth; correctness; rightness  
**Context of use:** Used for truth as an abstract noun in factual, moral, religious, and philosophical discourse. It can refer to both propositional truth and truth as a value.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **membenarkan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + benar + -kan*  
**English:** to justify; to confirm; to validate; to correct  
**Context of use:** Commonly used when someone confirms that something is true, or when an action or argument is said to be justified. It is frequent in legal, moral, and argumentative contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **pembenaran**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *peN- + benar + -an*  
**English:** justification  
**Context of use:** Used for the act or basis of justifying a claim, action, or decision. It is common in formal writing, ethics, law, and public debate.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **membetulkan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + betul + -kan*  
**English:** to correct; to set right; to straighten/fix  
**Context of use:** Although built from *betul* rather than *benar*, it belongs to the same correctness area in usage. It is used both for correcting mistakes in speech or writing and for physically fixing or straightening something.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **pembetulan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *peN- + betul + -an*  
**English:** correction; rectification  
**Context of use:** Common in editing, administration, and public notices for correcting errors in names, data, documents, or statements. It can also refer to practical repair or adjustment.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **kebenaran umum**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + umum*  
**English:** general truth; accepted truth  
**Context of use:** Used in expository, philosophical, and educational writing for a truth regarded as broadly accepted or widely applicable.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **kebenaran mutlak**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + mutlak*  
**English:** absolute truth  
**Context of use:** Common in philosophical and religious discussion where truth is treated as unconditional or not relative to perspective.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **kebenaran relatif**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + relatif*  
**English:** relative truth  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, social, and intellectual discourse when discussing truth as dependent on standpoint, context, or framework.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **kebenaran ilmiah**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + ilmiah*  
**English:** scientific truth  
**Context of use:** Used in academic and educational contexts for truth established through scientific method, evidence, and testing.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11. **kebenaran historis**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + historis*  
**English:** historical truth  
**Context of use:** Used in historiography, journalism, and public memory debates for truth concerning past events and their documentation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12. **kebenaran faktual**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + faktual*  
**English:** factual truth  
**Context of use:** Common in journalism, legal reasoning, and public communication where emphasis is on correspondence with verifiable facts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13. **kebenaran hakiki**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + hakiki*  
**English:** essential truth; ultimate truth  
**Context of use:** Found in philosophical and religious discourse for truth understood as fundamental or ultimate rather than merely surface-level.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14. **kebenaran sejati**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + sejati*  
**English:** true truth; genuine truth  
**Context of use:** Used in elevated, religious, or reflective style to emphasize truth in a deep or authentic sense.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 15. **benar-benar**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** reduplication of *benar*  
**English:** really; truly; genuinely  
**Context of use:** Very common in everyday Indonesian as an intensifier. It often no longer directly evaluates truth, but strengthens the speaker’s assertion or emotion.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 16. **sebenarnya**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *se- + benar + -nya*  
**English:** actually; in fact; the truth is  
**Context of use:** Extremely common in speech and writing to introduce what is really the case, often contrasting appearance with reality or correcting a misunderstanding.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17. **kebenarannya**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kebenaran + -nya*  
**English:** its truth; the truth of it  
**Context of use:** Used when discussing whether a report, claim, rumor, or statement is true. It is common in journalism and formal evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18. **membenarkan diri**

**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + benar + -kan + diri*

**Context of use:** Used when a person defends or excuses their own conduct. It is common in moral criticism and interpersonal discourse.

**Context of use:** Common in reporting and analysis when evidence supports a prior suspicion, expectation, or hypothesis.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 20. **membenarkan kabar / berita**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + benar + -kan + kabar/berita*  
**English:** to confirm news / a report  
**Context of use:** Frequent in journalism and official statements when an authority confirms that circulating information is true.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21. **yang benar**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *yang + benar*  
**English:** the correct one; what is right; really?  
**Context of use:** Used both referentially and idiomatically. In conversation it can also express surprise or ask for confirmation, as in “really?”  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 22. **benar saja**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *benar + saja*  
**English:** sure enough; indeed  
**Context of use:** Used in narrative and conversation when an expectation or suspicion turns out to be correct.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 23. **tidak benar**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *tidak + benar*  
**English:** not true; incorrect  
**Context of use:** A basic and very common negated form used to deny the truth or correctness of a statement.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24. **kurang benar**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *kurang + benar*  
**English:** not quite correct  
**Context of use:** Used as a softened evaluation in academic, editorial, and polite corrective contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** This root has relatively few highly lexicalized compounds beyond the core derivational family; many additional uses are ordinary syntactic combinations rather than fixed expressions.

## Root: **tepat**

This root is productive and widely used across accuracy, timing, targeting, and appropriateness. Additional forms exist beyond the list below.

### 1. **tepat**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** basic root  
**English:** accurate; precise; exact; appropriate  
**Context of use:** Used for statements, measurements, timing, choices, and judgments that are exact or fitting. It is common in both everyday and formal registers.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **ketepatan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *ke- + tepat + -an*  
**English:** accuracy; precision; appropriateness  
**Context of use:** Used in technical, academic, and administrative contexts for the degree of exactness or fit. It can refer to wording, data, timing, or decisions.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **tepat sasaran**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *tepat + sasaran*  
**English:** well-targeted; on target  
**Context of use:** Very common in policy, aid distribution, planning, and criticism. It evaluates whether an action reaches the intended group, object, or issue.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **ketepatan sasaran**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *ketepatan + sasaran*  
**English:** targeting accuracy; precision of targeting  
**Context of use:** Common in policy evaluation, welfare distribution, and program assessment. It refers to whether benefits or interventions reach the correct recipients.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **tepat waktu**
**Transliteration:** —

**Context of use:** Very common in everyday and formal usage for arriving, submitting, paying, or acting at the proper time.

### 6. **ketepatan waktu**

## Root cluster: **sah / absah / valid**

These forms are not one native derivational root, but in the P1 set they function as the main validity cluster. Because the task asks for the most central building blocks in the domain, this cluster is expanded together. It is highly productive in formal usage; many additional combinations exist.

### 1. **sah**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** basic root  
**English:** valid; legitimate; lawful  
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, political, and religious contexts for acts, documents, statuses, and procedures recognized as valid.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **mengesahkan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + sah + -kan*  
**English:** to legalize; to ratify; to validate; to enact formally  
**Context of use:** Very common in law, government, and administration when an authority formally approves a law, result, document, or status.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **pengesahan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *peN- + sah + -an*  
**English:** ratification; legalization; formal approval  
**Context of use:** Used for the formal act of validating a regulation, agreement, identity, result, or institutional decision.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **keabsahan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *ke- + absah + -an*  
**English:** validity; legitimacy  
**Context of use:** Formal noun used in legal, academic, and evidential contexts for whether something is valid under relevant standards or procedures.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **absah**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** basic root  
**English:** valid; legitimate  
**Context of use:** More formal than *sah* in many contexts. It is common in legal and bureaucratic writing for documents, evidence, and procedures.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **mengabsahkan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *meN- + absah + -kan*  
**English:** to validate; to legitimize  
**Context of use:** Used in formal legal, institutional, and academic contexts when something is made valid or recognized as legitimate.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **pengabsahan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal breakdown:** *peN- + absah + -an*

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **valid**

**Note:** This cluster is highly productive in formal Indonesian; many additional combinations exist, especially with legal nouns (*akta, perjanjian, putusan, izin*) and research nouns (*instrumen, konstruk, butir, data*).

## Step 3 — Peripheral extensions

The following expressions show meaning/function shifts or extensions away from narrow truth/falsity evaluation and should be flagged:

- **benar-benar** — often functions mainly as an intensifier rather than a truth judgment.
- **yang benar** — can function as a discourse marker of surprise or confirmation-seeking.
- **membetulkan / pembetulan** — often extend into physical fixing, straightening, or repair.
- **tepat waktu** — centers on timing rather than truth.
- **tepat guna / teknologi tepat guna** — centers on suitability and practical appropriateness.
- **tepat janji** — centers on character and promise-keeping.
- **tepat langkah** — centers on strategic appropriateness.
- **orang yang tepat / tempat yang tepat / waktu yang tepat** — often concern suitability rather than factual correctness.
- **anak sah** — legal-social status use.
- **sah menurut agama** — religious-procedural validity.
- **valid secara statistik** — technical-methodological validity rather than ordinary truth.
