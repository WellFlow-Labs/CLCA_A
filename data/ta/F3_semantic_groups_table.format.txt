# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — F3 Semantic Groups Table
# Language: Tamil (ta)

| Group Label | Definition | Representative Expressions | Overlapping / Ambiguous Items | Boundary Notes |
|------------|------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| GROUP 1: Basic Truth-Falsehood Opposition | This group contains the primary everyday terms used to assert or deny the truth-value of statements, claims, or situations. These terms function as direct opposites in colloquial and formal discourse, appearing in parallel constructions and serving as the default vocabulary for truth-evaluation in everyday speech. | உண்மை (uṇmai) "truth"; பொய் (poy) "falsehood, lie"; உண்மையான (uṇmaiyāṉa) "true, genuine"; பொய்யான (poyyāṉa) "false, fake"; நிஜம் (nijam) "truth, reality" | உண்மையான (uṇmaiyāṉa) also appears in Group 10; பொய்யான (poyyāṉa) also appears in Group 10 | These terms appear in direct opposition (உண்மை vs. பொய்), in parallel verbal constructions (உண்மை சொல் vs. பொய் சொல்), and in interrogative-confirmatory exchanges. They are the most frequent terms in everyday truth-evaluation. |
| GROUP 2: Correctness and Error | This group contains terms used to evaluate the accuracy or appropriateness of statements, actions, calculations, or judgments. These terms focus on whether something is done properly or matches expected standards, rather than on moral truthfulness or factual reality. They are particularly common in pedagogical, technical, and everyday problem-solving contexts. | சரி (cari) "correct, right"; தப்பு (tappu) "mistake, wrong"; சரியான (cariyāṉa) "correct, proper"; தவறு (tavaṟu) "mistake, error"; பொருத்தம் (poruttam) "appropriateness, suitability, fit"; துல்லியம் (tulliyam) "accuracy, precision" | பொருத்தம் (poruttam) can overlap with truth-evaluation when discussing whether statements "fit" or correspond to reality | These terms appear in evaluative contexts about accuracy, appropriateness, and proper execution. Unlike Group 1 terms, these focus on correctness-of-fit rather than truth-of-claim. |
| GROUP 3: Moral Honesty and Character | This group contains terms that evaluate the moral character of persons, particularly regarding their disposition toward truthfulness and deception. These terms characterize people rather than statements, emphasizing habitual behavior and ethical qualities. They are common in character descriptions, moral instruction, and ethical discourse. | நேர்மை (nērmai) "honesty, integrity, uprightness"; நேர்மையான (nērmaiyāṉa) "honest, upright"; கள்ளம் (kaḷḷam) "deceit, fraud"; கள்ளம் கபடம் இல்லாத (kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta) "without deceit or cunning, guileless"; பொய்யாமை (poyyāmai) "truthfulness, absence of falsehood"; பொய்யன் (poyyan) "liar, deceiver"; மெய்யன் (meyyan) "truthful person"; உண்மைவாதி (uṇmaivāti) "truth-teller, one who advocates truth" | None noted | These terms appear in character evaluations, moral instruction, and descriptions of personal integrity. They focus on dispositional qualities and habitual behavior rather than individual truth-claims. |
| GROUP 4: Elevated/Literary Truth Terms | This group contains terms for truth and reality that carry elevated, literary, or philosophical register. These terms appear more frequently in classical literature, religious texts, and formal philosophical discourse than in everyday speech. They often carry connotations of ultimate, essential, or transcendent truth. | மெய் (mey) "truth, reality"; மெய்ம்மை (meymai) "truth, reality"; மெய்யான (meyyāṉa) "true, genuine"; சத்தியம் (cattiyam) "truth, oath"; மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் (meyññāṉam) "true knowledge, spiritual wisdom, enlightenment"; மெய்ப்பொருள் (meypporuḷ) "true meaning, ultimate reality"; மெய்யியல் (meyyiyal) "metaphysics, ontology" | சத்தியம் (cattiyam) also appears in Group 10 | These terms appear in classical Tamil literature, religious texts, philosophical discourse, and formal writing. They are less common in everyday colloquial speech, where உண்மை (uṇmai) is preferred. மெய் (mey) shows significant polysemy between "truth/reality" and "body/physical." |
| GROUP 5: Verification and Authentication | This group contains terms and constructions related to establishing, confirming, or certifying the truth of claims through evidence, documentation, or formal processes. These terms are particularly common in administrative, legal, and investigative contexts where truth must be officially established or documented. | மெய்ப்பு (meyppu) "proof, verification, confirmation"; உண்மைப்படுத்து (uṇmaippaṭuttu) "to verify, to authenticate"; உண்மைச்சான்று (uṇmaiccāṉṟu) "certificate of authenticity, proof of truth"; உண்மைப்பிரமாணம் (uṇmaippramāṇam) "proof of truth, authoritative evidence"; நம்பகம் (nampakam) "reliability, trustworthiness, credibility"; உறுதிப்படுத்து (uṟutippaṭuttu) "to confirm, to verify"; சோதி (cōti) "to test, to verify"; கண்டறி (kaṇṭaṟi) "to discover, to ascertain" | மெய்ப்பு (meyppu) also appears in Group 10 | These terms appear in contexts requiring formal establishment of truth: legal proceedings, administrative documentation, scientific verification, and investigative work. They emphasize procedural and evidentiary aspects of truth-establishment. |
| GROUP 6: Revelation and Concealment | This group contains terms and constructions related to making truth visible or keeping it hidden. These expressions focus on the dynamic processes of truth emerging, being disclosed, or being concealed, rather than on static truth-values. They are common in investigative, journalistic, and narrative contexts. | உண்மை வெளிப்படு (uṇmai veḷippaṭu) "the truth comes out, is revealed"; உண்மையை வெளிப்படுத்து (uṇmaiyai veḷippaṭuttu) "to reveal the truth"; உண்மை வெளியாகு (uṇmai veḷiyāku) "the truth comes out"; உண்மையை மறை (uṇmaiyai maṟai) "to hide the truth"; உண்மையை மூடி மறை (uṇmaiyai mūṭi maṟai) "to cover up the truth"; உண்மை தெரி (uṇmai teri) "the truth becomes known"; உண்மை தெரிய வரு (uṇmai teriya varu) "the truth comes to be known gradually" | உண்மை தெரி appears in both Group 6 and Group 7 | These constructions appear in investigative journalism, legal discovery, confessions, exposés, and narrative revelations. They emphasize the temporal and agentive aspects of truth-disclosure or concealment. |
| GROUP 7: Discovery and Knowledge of Truth | This group contains terms and constructions related to coming to know, finding out, or understanding truth through investigation, inquiry, or realization. These expressions emphasize the epistemic process of acquiring knowledge of truth rather than the truth itself. | உண்மை தெரி (uṇmai teri) "to know the truth"; உண்மையைத் தெரிந்துகொள் (uṇmaiyait terintukoḷ) "to find out the truth"; உண்மையைக் கண்டுபிடி (uṇmaiyaik kaṇṭupiṭi) "to discover the truth"; உண்மை அறி (uṇmai aṟi) "to know the truth"; உண்மையை உணர் (uṇmaiyai uṇar) "to realize the truth"; உண்மையறிவு (uṇmaiyaṟivu) "knowledge of truth"; உண்மையுணர்வு (uṇmaiyuṇarvu) "awareness of truth"; மெய் உணர் (mey uṇar) "to realize truth, attain enlightenment" | உண்மை தெரி appears in both Group 6 and Group 7 | These expressions appear in contexts of investigation, learning, spiritual realization, and epistemic achievement. They focus on the knower's relationship to truth rather than on truth's properties. Register variation: தெரி (teri) is colloquial, அறி (aṟi) is more formal/literary, உணர் (uṇar) often carries connotations of deeper realization or spiritual insight. |
| GROUP 8: Acceptance and Denial of Truth | This group contains terms and constructions related to accepting, acknowledging, or rejecting truth claims. These expressions focus on the volitional and social acts of accepting or denying truth rather than on knowing or discovering it. They are common in interpersonal, legal, and psychological contexts. | உண்மையை ஏற்று (uṇmaiyai ēṟṟu) "to accept the truth"; உண்மையை ஒத்துக்கொள் (uṇmaiyai ottukkoḷ) "to admit the truth, confess"; உண்மையை மறு (uṇmaiyai maṟu) "to deny the truth"; உண்மையை நம்பு (uṇmaiyai nampu) "to believe the truth"; ஏற்புடைமை (ēṟpuṭaimai) "acceptability, validity" | None noted | These constructions appear in contexts of confession, admission, denial, belief formation, and interpersonal truth-claims. They emphasize the social and psychological dimensions of truth-acceptance. |
| GROUP 9: Speech Acts of Truth-Telling and Lying | This group contains constructions that combine truth-domain terms with verbs of speaking to create expressions for the acts of telling truth or lying. These are among the most common everyday constructions in the truth domain, appearing across all registers. | உண்மை சொல் (uṇmai col) "to tell the truth"; பொய் சொல் (poy col) "to tell a lie"; உண்மை பேசு (uṇmai pēcu) "to speak the truth"; பொய் பேசு (poy pēcu) "to speak falsely"; உண்மையைச் சொல்லு (uṇmaiyaic collu) "to tell the truth"; பொய் சொல்லாதே (poy collātē) "don't lie"; உண்மையைக் கூறு (uṇmaiyaik kūṟu) "to state the truth" | None noted | These constructions are fundamental to everyday discourse about truthfulness. They appear in commands, requests, moral instruction, and descriptions of speech behavior. The choice between சொல் (col), பேசு (pēcu), and கூறு (kūṟu) reflects register differences. |
| GROUP 10: Legal and Testimonial Truth | This group contains terms specifically associated with legal contexts, testimony, oaths, and formal declarations. These terms carry weight of legal or sacred obligation and appear primarily in judicial, administrative, and religious contexts. | சத்தியம் (cattiyam) "truth, oath, vow"; பொய்ச்சாட்சி (poyccāṭci) "false testimony, perjury"; பொய்யாணை (poyyāṇai) "false oath"; பொய்யறிக்கை (poyyaṟikkai) "false declaration"; பொய்க்குற்றம் (poykuṟṟam) "false accusation"; பொய்வழக்கு (poyvaḻakku) "false case, fraudulent lawsuit"; உண்மைப்பிரமாணம் (uṇmaippramāṇam) "authoritative proof, evidence" | சத்தியம் (cattiyam) also appears in Group 4; மெய்ப்பு (meyppu) also appears in Group 5 | These terms appear in legal proceedings, court testimony, official declarations, and religious oath-taking. They carry implications of formal obligation and legal consequences for falsehood. |
| GROUP 11: Documentary and Material Authenticity | This group contains terms related to the authenticity, genuineness, or counterfeit nature of physical objects, documents, and material goods. These terms evaluate whether objects are genuine or fake rather than whether statements are true or false. | அசல் (acal) "original, genuine, authentic"; பொய்ப்பொருள் (poypporuḷ) "fake object, counterfeit item"; உண்மைப்பொருள் (uṇmaipporuḷ) "genuine object, real substance"; பொய்யடையாளம் (poyaṭaiyāḷam) "false identity, fake identification"; பொய்யெழுத்து (poyeḻuttu) "false document, forged writing"; பொய்ச்சான்று (poyccāṉṟu) "false certificate, forged document"; உண்மைச்சான்று (uṇmaiccāṉṟu) "certificate of authenticity" | உண்மையான (uṇmaiyāṉa) also appears in Group 1; பொய்யான (poyyāṉa) also appears in Group 1 | These terms appear in commercial contexts (distinguishing genuine from counterfeit goods), security contexts (identifying fake documents), and authentication processes. They extend truth-vocabulary to material objects. |
| GROUP 12: Narrative and Factual Truth | This group contains terms and constructions that distinguish factual narratives from fictional ones, or actual events from imagined ones. These expressions are common in storytelling, journalism, and historical discourse. | உண்மைக்கதை (uṇmaikkatai) "true story, factual narrative"; பொய்க்கதை (poykkatai) "false story, fiction"; உண்மைச்சம்பவம் (uṇmaiccampavam) "true event, actual incident"; நடப்பு (naṭappu) "actual, current, happening"; உண்மைநிலை (uṇmainilai) "true state, actual situation"; உண்மைநிலவரம் (uṇmainilavaṟam) "true state of affairs, factual report" | None noted | These terms appear in journalism, historical writing, storytelling, and contexts where factual accuracy of narratives is at issue. They distinguish what actually happened from what is claimed, imagined, or fabricated. |
| GROUP 13: Philosophical and Metaphysical Truth | This group contains terms related to ultimate reality, essential nature, and philosophical truth. These terms appear primarily in philosophical, religious, and metaphysical discourse, often concerning the nature of reality itself rather than the truth of particular claims. | மெய்ப்பொருள் (meypporuḷ) "true meaning, ultimate reality, essence"; மெய்யியல் (meyyiyal) "metaphysics, ontology"; மெய்ஞ்ஞானம் (meyññāṉam) "true knowledge, spiritual wisdom, enlightenment"; இயல்பு (iyalpu) "nature, natural state, essence"; மெய்க்காட்சி (meykkāṭci) "vision of truth, true appearance"; மெய் உணர் (mey uṇar) "to realize truth, attain enlightenment" | None noted | These terms appear in philosophical texts, religious literature, and discussions of ultimate reality or essential nature. They often concern transcendent or absolute truth rather than everyday factual truth. |
| GROUP 14: Epistemic Modality and Appearance | This group contains constructions that express how things appear, seem, or are perceived in relation to truth. These expressions mark epistemic uncertainty or the gap between appearance and reality. | உண்மை போல் (uṇmai pōl) "like truth, as if true, seemingly true"; பொய் போல் (poy pōl) "like a lie, as if false, seemingly false"; பொய்யுணர்வு (poyyuṇarvu) "false perception, illusion"; பொய்நிலை (poynilai) "false state, deceptive condition" | None noted | These constructions appear when speakers express uncertainty, describe appearances that may be deceptive, or mark the distinction between how things seem and how they are. They are common in evaluative and cautious discourse. |
| GROUP 15: Emphasis and Confirmation | This group contains constructions used to emphasize, confirm, or assert truth with particular force. These are primarily discourse-level expressions that strengthen truth-claims or seek emphatic confirmation. | உண்மைதான் (uṇmaitāṉ) "it is indeed true"; நிஜம்தான் (nijamtāṉ) "it's really true"; உண்மையா? (uṇmaiyā?) "is it true? really?"; நிஜமா? (nijamā?) "really? for real?"; உண்மையில் (uṇmaiyil) "truly, really, actually, in fact" | None noted | These expressions function as discourse markers to emphasize truth-claims, seek confirmation, or express surprise. They are very common in conversational Tamil and serve pragmatic functions beyond simple truth-assertion. |
| (Items with Ambiguous or Multiple Group Membership) | Items that could reasonably belong to multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single identified group. | நியாயம் (niyāyam) "justice, fairness, rightness, reasonableness" — could belong to Group 2 when evaluating whether actions are right or proper, but has strong moral and legal dimensions; பொருந்து (poruntu) "to fit, to match, to be suitable" — appears in contexts of both correctness-evaluation (Group 2) and truth-correspondence, but primary meaning concerns fitting or matching; தகுதி (takuti) "fitness, worthiness, qualification" — evaluates merit and qualification rather than truth or correctness; உண்மைக்கண் (uṇmaikkaṇ) "eye of truth, insight" — literary metaphor marked as [MARGINAL], does not fit cleanly into any group | None noted | நியாயம் evaluates moral/legal rightness rather than factual truth or simple correctness. பொருந்து may represent a distinct semantic area not fully captured by the identified groups. தகுதி represents evaluation of a different kind. உண்மைக்கண் is a literary metaphor rather than a functional term in everyday truth-discourse. |
| (Ungrouped Items) | Items that do not participate in truth-domain semantics or belong to different semantic domains. | மெய்யெழுத்து (meyyeḻuttu) "consonant (in grammar)" — grammatical term that has shifted entirely to technical linguistic meaning; மெய்ப்பாடு (meyppāṭu) "bodily suffering, physical experience" — uses மெய் in "body" sense, belongs to physical experience domain; மெய்யடக்கம் (meyyaṭakkam) "bodily restraint, physical modesty" — uses மெய் in "body" sense, belongs to physical conduct domain; மெய்சிலிர்ப்பு (meycilirppu) "goosebumps, bodily thrill" — describes physical sensations, uses மெய் in "body" sense; மெய்ம்மறந்து (meymaṟantu) "forgetting oneself, losing bodily awareness" — idiomatic expression using மெய் in "body" sense | None noted | These items use மெய் (mey) in its "body" sense rather than "truth" sense, or represent domain extensions to technical/specialized meanings. They do not participate in truth-evaluation semantics. |
