# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## 1.
**Form:** òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalized form related to the root *tọ* (to be straight, correct, proper); the *ò-* prefix nominalizes
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, accuracy, fact

**Usage description:** Used to assert that a statement or claim corresponds to reality. Appears in both everyday speech and formal/legal contexts, e.g., *sọ òtítọ́* ("speak the truth"). Contrasts with *irọ́* (falsehood).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 2.
**Form:** irọ́
**Transliteration:** irọ́
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Noun; root possibly related to *rọ* (to be soft, yielding); conventionally understood as the antonym of *òtítọ́*
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, deception

**Usage description:** Refers to a statement or representation that is false or deliberately misleading. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in legal contexts of perjury or fraud. *Sọ irọ́* ("tell a lie") is a highly productive collocation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 3.
**Form:** òdodo
**Transliteration:** òdodo
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalized form; reduplication of *do* or related to *dodo*; semantically associated with uprightness and moral correctness
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, fairness, rectitude

**Usage description:** Used in moral and legal discourse to describe conduct or outcomes that conform to right standards. Appears in religious and ethical contexts, including Yoruba traditional religion and Christian/Islamic usage. Distinct from *òtítọ́* in that it emphasizes moral rightness rather than factual accuracy.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 4.
**Form:** ìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalization of *mọ̀* (to know); *ì-* is a nominalizing prefix
**Typical English translations:** knowledge, understanding, awareness

**Usage description:** Refers to the state of knowing or being informed about something. Used in epistemological and practical contexts to evaluate whether a person has accurate information. Relevant to assessments of reliability and credibility of testimony.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 5.
**Form:** gbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** gbàgbọ́
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Verb; *gbà* (to accept, receive) + *gbọ́* (to hear, to believe); compound meaning "to accept as heard/true"
**Typical English translations:** to believe, to trust, to have faith in

**Usage description:** Describes the cognitive act of accepting a proposition or person as reliable or true. Used in both religious contexts (faith) and everyday contexts of trusting testimony. The object can be a person, statement, or claim.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 6.
**Form:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalization of *gbàgbọ́*; *ì-* nominalizing prefix
**Typical English translations:** belief, faith, trust, conviction

**Usage description:** The nominal form of *gbàgbọ́*, referring to the state or act of believing. Widely used in religious discourse (particularly Christian) but also in secular contexts of epistemic commitment. Evaluates the reliability or credibility of what is believed.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 7.
**Form:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Transliteration:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalization of *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (to lean on, to rely on); *ì-* nominalizing prefix; *gbẹ́* (to lean) + *kẹ̀lé* (on, against)
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliance, dependability, confidence in

**Usage description:** Refers specifically to reliance on a person or thing as dependable or trustworthy. Used to evaluate whether a source, person, or institution is reliable. Distinct from *ìgbàgbọ́* in emphasizing practical dependability over propositional belief.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 8.
**Form:** ẹ̀rí
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Noun; evidence, witness, testimony; related to *rí* (to see)
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, testimony, witness

**Usage description:** Used in legal and evaluative contexts to refer to evidence that supports or establishes the truth of a claim. *Ẹ̀rí ojú* ("eye-witness evidence") is a common collocation. Central to assessments of factual accuracy in judicial settings.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 9.
**Form:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, witness) + *ojú* (eye, face); literally "eye-evidence" or "face-witness"
**Typical English translations:** eyewitness testimony, direct evidence, firsthand witness

**Usage description:** A fixed expression referring to evidence based on direct visual observation. Used in legal and everyday contexts to distinguish firsthand from hearsay evidence. Carries strong epistemic weight as a marker of reliable testimony.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 10.
**Form:** jẹ́rìí
**Transliteration:** jẹ́rìí
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Verb; to bear witness, to testify; related to *ẹ̀rí* (evidence/witness); possibly from Arabic *shahida* via Islamic influence, or native derivation
**Typical English translations:** to testify, to bear witness, to attest

**Usage description:** Used in legal and religious contexts for the act of giving testimony or bearing witness to the truth of something. Appears in Islamic religious usage (*jẹ́rìí* to the oneness of God) and in court contexts. Evaluates the act of affirming truth through personal attestation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 11.
**Form:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́rìísí
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Nominalization of *jẹ́rìí* + causative/completive suffix *-sí*; *ì-* nominalizing prefix
**Typical English translations:** certification, attestation, verification, confirmation

**Usage description:** Refers to the formal act or document of certifying or confirming the truth, authenticity, or validity of something. Used in administrative, legal, and academic contexts. Evaluates whether something has been officially verified as genuine or correct.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 12.
**Form:** tọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** tọ̀nà
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** *tọ* (straight, correct, proper) + *nà* (path, way); literally "straight path" or "correct way"
**Typical English translations:** right path, correct way, proper conduct

**Usage description:** Used in moral and religious contexts to describe the correct or righteous course of action or belief. Evaluates conduct or orientation against a standard of what is right or proper. Common in Islamic and Christian religious discourse in Yoruba.

## 13.
**Form:** tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Verb/adjective root; to be straight, correct, proper, right
**Typical English translations:** to be correct, to be right, to be proper, to be straight

**Usage description:** A core evaluative root used to assess whether something conforms to a standard of correctness or propriety. Appears in numerous compounds and collocations. Used for both factual correctness (*ó tọ́* — "it is correct") and moral rightness.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 14.
**Form:** ìwà
**Transliteration:** ìwà
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Noun; *ì-* nominalizing prefix + *wà* (to exist, to be); literally "the manner of being" or "existence-quality"
**Typical English translations:** character, conduct, nature, moral character, behavior

**Usage description:** Refers to the fundamental character or nature of a person, evaluated morally. Central to Yoruba ethical discourse; *ìwà rere* ("good character") is a foundational moral concept. Used to assess what a person fundamentally is, not merely what they do.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 15.
**Form:** ìwà rere
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** *ìwà* (character, nature) + *rere* (good, fine, excellent); "good character"
**Typical English translations:** good character, virtue, moral excellence, integrity

**Usage description:** A fixed expression that is the central concept of Yoruba ethics, referring to the possession of genuine moral goodness as a fundamental quality of a person. Used to evaluate persons against the highest moral standard. Widely attested in proverbs, philosophical discourse, and everyday moral evaluation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 16.
**Form:** ìwà búburú
**Transliteration:** ìwà búburú
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** *ìwà* (character, nature) + *búburú* (bad, evil, wicked); "bad character"
**Typical English translations:** bad character, wickedness, moral corruption, vice

**Usage description:** The antonym of *ìwà rere*, used to evaluate a person as fundamentally morally deficient. Appears in moral condemnation and in proverbs contrasting good and bad conduct. Evaluates the fundamental nature of a person rather than isolated acts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 17.
**Form:** gidi
**Transliteration:** gidi
**Literal meaning/morphological breakdown:** Adjective/adverb; genuine, real, authentic; possibly from *gidi* (solid, firm, real)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true, actual

**Usage description:** Used to assert that something is the real or authentic version of what it purports to be, as opposed to a fake or imitation. Appears in both everyday speech and commercial contexts. *Nǹkan gidi* ("a real/genuine thing") contrasts with counterfeits or pretenses.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Usage description:** Refers to established cultural practices or norms against which conduct is evaluated for propriety or correctness. Used in assessments of whether behavior conforms to recognized standards. Relevant to evaluations of rightness in cultural and social contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 19.
**Form:** ẹ̀tọ́

**Usage description:** Refers to the act of testing or examining something to determine its true nature, quality, or correctness. Used in academic, scientific, and legal contexts. Evaluates whether something meets a standard through systematic examination.
