# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P1b/P2a/P2b documentation: which terms appear in the same contexts, which are contrasted with each other, which share typical collocates, and which are restricted to particular registers or domains. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns do not support a clear grouping, items are left ungrouped or noted as ambiguous.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that a statement corresponds to the facts

**Label:** Predicated of statements and claims for truth-value assessment

**Definition:** These terms are used primarily as predicates applied to propositions, statements, testimony, and claims to evaluate whether they correspond to reality. They appear characteristically in constructions of the form *[statement/claim] + κρίθηκε/αποδείχθηκε/είναι + [term]*, and in legal, journalistic, and formal registers where the truth or falsity of an assertion is at issue. They contrast directly with each other as positive and negative poles.

**Representative expressions:**

- αληθής / alithís — *Ο ισχυρισμός αποδείχθηκε αληθής* ("The claim proved to be true"); *Η κατάθεσή του κρίθηκε αληθής*
- ψευδής / psevdís — *Ο ισχυρισμός κρίθηκε ψευδής*; *ψευδής κατάθεση* ("false testimony"); *αποδεδειγμένως ψευδής*
- ψεύδος / psévdos — *Αυτό είναι ένα ξεκάθαρο ψεύδος* ("This is an outright falsehood"); *διαδίδει ψεύδη*
- αποδεδειγμένος / apodedeignménos — *αποδεδειγμένα ψευδής ισχυρισμός*; *αποδεδειγμένως*
- αδιαμφισβήτητος / adiamfisvitiitos — *αδιαμφισβήτητο γεγονός*; used to assert that a claim is beyond challenge
- αμφίβολος / amfívolos — *Το αποτέλεσμα παραμένει αμφίβολο*; marks a claim as uncertain rather than established
- αμφισβητήσιμος / amfisvitiísimos — *Τα συμπεράσματα είναι αμφισβητήσιμα*; marks a claim as open to active contestation
- εν γνώσει ψευδής — legal formula for a knowingly false statement

**Notes:** αληθής and ψευδής are the most symmetrical pair in this group, appearing in parallel legal constructions. αποδεδειγμένος and αδιαμφισβήτητος add a dimension of established or unchallengeable status. αμφίβολος and αμφισβητήσιμος occupy the uncertain middle, with αμφίβολος leaning toward epistemic uncertainty and αμφισβητήσιμος toward the possibility of active dispute.

## Group 2: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts to assess the standing of documents, acts, and decisions

**Label:** Validity and legal standing of formal instruments

**Definition:** These terms are used in legal, administrative, and official contexts to evaluate whether a document, decision, vote, contract, or procedural act has proper standing and force. They appear in constructions such as *[document/act] + κρίθηκε/παραμένει/θεωρείται + [term]* and in fixed administrative formulas. The central contrast is between έγκυρος and άκυρος.

**Representative expressions:**

- έγκυρος / éngiros — *Το συμβόλαιο παραμένει έγκυρο*; *Η ψήφος κρίθηκε έγκυρη*; *Έγκυρο έως…*
- άκυρος / ákiros (derived from έγκυρος) — *Άκυρο εισιτήριο*; *άκυρη ψήφος*; direct antonym of έγκυρος
- ακυρώνω / akiróno — *ακυρώνω μια απόφαση*; the act of rendering something invalid
- ακύρωση / akíro̱si — *ακύρωση σύμβασης*; *ακύρωση πτήσης*
- νόμιμος / nómimos — *νόμιμος εκπρόσωπος*; *νόμιμος κληρονόμος*; *κατά νόμιμο τρόπο*
- παράνομος / paránomος — direct antonym of νόμιμος; *παράνομη δραστηριότητα*
- νομιμοποίηση / nomimopoíisi — *νομιμοποίηση εξουσίας*
- πιστοποίηση / pistopoíisi — *πιστοποίηση γνησιότητας*; *πιστοποίηση ποιότητας*
- πιστοποιητικό / pistopoiitikó — *πιστοποιητικό γέννησης*; the document that certifies
- γνησιοποίηση / gnisiopoíisi — *γνησιοποίηση υπογραφής*; official certification of a signature as genuine
- γνήσιο αντίγραφο — *πιστοποιείται ως γνήσιο αντίγραφο*; fixed administrative formula

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 1 (truth of statements) and Group 3 (genuineness of objects) but is distinguished by its focus on formal standing and procedural validity rather than factual truth or material authenticity. The terms here are characteristically used by authorities (courts, notaries, administrative bodies) to confer or withdraw official status.

## Group 3: Terms used to assert or deny that an object, person, or work is what it purports to be

**Label:** Genuineness and authenticity of things and persons

**Definition:** These terms are applied to physical objects, artworks, documents, products, and persons to indicate whether they are what they appear or claim to be, as opposed to being copies, imitations, or counterfeits. They appear in contexts of authentication, commercial fraud, art criticism, and character evaluation. The central contrast is between terms asserting genuine origin and terms asserting fabrication or imitation.

**Representative expressions:**

- γνήσιος / gnísios — *γνήσιο προϊόν*; *Πρόκειται για γνήσιο έργο*; *Δεν είναι γνήσιο — είναι απομίμηση*
- γνησιότητα / gnisiótita — *έλεγχος γνησιότητας*; *Αμφισβητείται η γνησιότητά του*
- αυθεντικός / afthentikós — *Το έργο πιστοποιήθηκε ως αυθεντικό*; *Αμφισβητείται η αυθεντικότητα της υπογραφής*
- αυθεντικότητα / afthentikótita — *η αυθεντικότητα ενός έργου τέχνης*
- πλαστός / plastós — *πλαστό διαβατήριο*; *πλαστή ταυτότητα*; *Αποδείχθηκε ότι η υπογραφή ήταν πλαστή*
- πλαστογραφία / plastografía — the legal term for document forgery
- νόθος / nóthos — *νόθα προϊόντα*; applied to adulterated goods and spurious texts
- νοθεία / nothía — *νοθεία τροφίμων*; adulteration of products
- νοθεύω / nothévo — *νοθεύω τρόφιμα*; *νοθεύω εκλογές*
- ψεύτικος / pséftikos — *ψεύτικο νόμισμα*; broader and more colloquial than πλαστός
- ψευδεπίγραφος / psevdepígrafos — applied to texts falsely attributed to an author; philological register

**Notes:** γνήσιος and αυθεντικός overlap considerably in this group but show some distributional differences: γνήσιος is more common in commercial and administrative contexts (products, signatures, copies), while αυθεντικός is more common in aesthetic and personal authenticity contexts. πλαστός is specifically associated with deliberate falsification (forgery), while νόθος covers both adulteration and spurious attribution. ψεύτικος is the most colloquial and broadest term.

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate persons and sources as worthy or unworthy of trust

**Label:** Credibility and trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Definition:** These terms are applied to persons, witnesses, institutions, and sources of information to evaluate whether they can be trusted or believed. They appear in contexts of journalistic source evaluation, legal witness assessment, and everyday character judgment. The central contrast is between αξιόπιστος and αναξιόπιστος.

**Representative expressions:**

- αξιόπιστος / aksiópistos — *αξιόπιστη πηγή*; *Δεν κρίνεται αξιόπιστος ως μάρτυρας*; *Αποδεικνύεται αξιόπιστος στην πράξη*
- αξιοπιστία / aksiopistía — *η αξιοπιστία μιας πηγής*
- αναξιόπιστος / anaksiópistos — *αναξιόπιστος μάρτυρας*; *Η κατάθεσή του κρίθηκε αναξιόπιστη*
- αναξιοπιστία / anaksiopistía — abstract noun for lack of credibility
- εμπιστοσύνη / empistoséni — *Έχω εμπιστοσύνη σε κάποιον*; *κλονίζω την εμπιστοσύνη*; *κατάχρηση εμπιστοσύνης*
- εμπιστεύομαι / empistévome — *Εμπιστεύομαι τον φίλο μου*
- δυσπιστία / dispistía — *Αντιμετωπίζω κάτι με δυσπιστία*; disposition of distrust
- δύσπιστος / díspistos — *Είναι πολύ δύσπιστος*; applied to a person who is hard to convince
- πιστός / pistós — *πιστός φίλος*; in the sense of faithful/reliable
- ψήφος εμπιστοσύνης / psífos empistosénis — parliamentary vote of confidence
- ψήφος δυσπιστίας / psífos dispistías — parliamentary vote of no confidence

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (truth of statements) by its focus on the trustworthiness of a source or person rather than the truth-value of a proposition. The terms here are relational — they describe a relationship between a person and those who evaluate them — whereas Group 1 terms are applied to propositions. εμπιστοσύνη and its family extend into interpersonal, financial, and political domains beyond epistemic evaluation.

## Group 5: Terms used to distinguish what is substantive from what is merely formal

**Label:** Substance versus form in evaluation of acts and situations

**Definition:** These terms appear characteristically in contrast with each other to distinguish between what is genuinely or fundamentally the case and what merely satisfies formal or surface requirements. They are used in legal, administrative, political, and everyday discourse, often in explicit opposition: *τυπικά… αλλά ουσιαστικά όχι*. The contrast is between the level of form and the level of substance.

**Representative expressions:**

- ουσιαστικός / usiástikos — *ουσιαστική διαφορά*; *ουσιαστική παράβαση*; *Δεν υπάρχει ουσιαστική διαφορά*
- ουσιαστικά / usiástika — *ουσιαστικά δεν άλλαξε τίποτα*; very common discourse marker
- ουσία / usía — *στην ουσία*; *κατ' ουσίαν*
- κατ' ουσίαν / kat' usían — *κατ' ουσίαν και τύπον*; legal and philosophical formula
- τυπικός / tipikós — *τυπική συμμόρφωση*; *Είναι απλώς τυπικό*; *Τυπικά μόνο*
- τυπικά / tipikά — *Τυπικά συμμορφώθηκε αλλά ουσιαστικά όχι*
- τυπικότητα / tipikótita — *Είναι απλή τυπικότητα*
- άτυπος / átipos — *άτυπη συμφωνία*; *άτυπη εκπαίδευση*; informal, lacking formal status
- φαινομενικός / fenomenikós — *φαινομενική επιτυχία*; *φαινομενική αντίφαση*; what appears to be the case at the surface
- φαινομενικά / fenomenikά — *φαινομενικά ήταν ήρεμος*; hedging adverb indicating appearance vs. reality
- κατά φαινόμενον / katá fenómenon — philosophical formula contrasting with κατ' ουσίαν

**Notes:** This group is defined by the contrast between its members rather than by a single shared property. ουσιαστικός and τυπικός are the core pair, appearing in explicit opposition. φαινομενικός and κατά φαινόμενον participate in the same contrast but are more specifically about appearance versus underlying reality, and are more common in philosophical and analytical registers. εικονικός (Group 6) overlaps here in some uses (εικονικός διευθυντής, εικονική συναλλαγή).

---

## Group 6: Terms used to describe things that have the form of something without being that thing

**Label:** Simulation, nominal status, and the pseudo- pattern

**Definition:** These terms are applied to things, persons, or institutions that present the outward form or appearance of something without possessing its genuine substance or properties. They include the highly productive ψευδο- prefix family and the term εικονικός in its non-virtual senses. They appear in critical, dismissive, or analytical registers.

**Representative expressions:**

- ψευδοεπιστήμη / psevdoepistími — *ψευδοεπιστημονικός*; claims that mimic scientific form without scientific validity
- ψευδοδημοκρατία / psevdodimokratía — *ψευδοδημοκρατικός*; a system with democratic form but not democratic substance
- ψευδοδιανοούμενος / psevdodianoúmenos — a person who affects intellectual depth without genuine substance
- ψευδοπροφήτης / psevdoprofítis — one who falsely claims prophetic authority
- ψευδοδιλήμμα / psevdodilíma — a false dilemma; a logical fallacy presenting false alternatives
- ψευδεπίγραφος / psevdepígrafos — a text falsely attributed to an author
- εικονικός / eikonikós — *εικονική συναλλαγή* (fictitious transaction); *εικονικός διευθυντής* (nominal director); *εικονική δίκη* (mock trial)
- ψευδαίσθηση / psevdésthisi — *ψευδαίσθηση ελέγχου*; a false perception or illusion

**Notes:** The ψευδο- prefix is highly productive and generates new compounds freely. The items listed are the most attested. εικονικός overlaps with this group in its legal and administrative uses (fictitious transaction, nominal director) but belongs to Group 5 in its virtual reality use. ψευδαίσθηση is somewhat distinct in that it refers to perceptual experience rather than institutional or propositional simulation, but it participates in the same pattern of "false appearance of X."

## Group 7: Terms used in discourse management to signal the speaker's epistemic stance

**Label:** Discourse markers signaling truth, frankness, or precision

**Definition:** These are fixed expressions or adverbial forms that function in spoken and written discourse to signal the speaker's relationship to what they are saying — asserting sincerity, introducing a correction, hedging a claim, or requesting confirmation. They are not primarily used to predicate truth of a proposition but to frame the speaker's own utterance.

**Representative expressions:**

- αλήθεια; / alíthia? — standalone interrogative: "Really?"; requests confirmation or expresses surprise
- αλήθεια να λέμε / alíthia na léme — "to tell the truth, let's be honest"; introduces a frank statement
- η αλήθεια είναι ότι / i alíthia íne óti — "the truth is that…"; introduces a corrective or frank assessment
- για την αλήθεια / ya tin alíthia — "to be honest, truthfully"; discourse framing device
- αληθινά / alithiná — "truly, really"; used to intensify or affirm sincerity
- ουσιαστικά / usiástika — "essentially, basically"; identifies the core of a matter
- φαινομενικά / fenomenikά — "apparently, seemingly"; hedges a claim as based on appearance
- για να είμαι ακριβής / ya na íme akrivís — "to be precise…"; introduces a more exact formulation
- αν κατάλαβα σωστά / an katálava sostá — "if I understood correctly…"; checks comprehension
- ακριβώς! / akrivós! — "Exactly!"; affirms a preceding statement
- σωστά! / sostá! — "Right! Correct!"; affirms a preceding statement
- χωρίς αμφιβολία / horís amfivolía — "without doubt"; asserts confidence in a claim
- πέρα από κάθε αμφιβολία / péra apó káthe amfivolía — "beyond any doubt"; stronger assertion of certainty

**Notes:** This group is defined by function in discourse rather than by semantic content. The items here are not primarily used to describe the world but to manage the speaker's relationship to their own utterances. Several items from other groups (αλήθεια, αληθινά, ακριβώς, σωστά) appear here in specialized discourse-management uses that are distinct from their primary predicative or attributive uses.

---

## Group 8: Terms used in the domain of evidence, proof, and substantiation

**Label:** Evidence, proof, and the establishment of claims

**Definition:** These terms are used in contexts where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it has been established through evidence, documentation, or argument. They appear in legal, scientific, journalistic, and academic registers and are associated with processes of verification and challenge.

**Representative expressions:**

- αποδεδειγμένος / apodedeignménos — *αποδεδειγμένα ψευδής*; *αποδεδειγμένως*
- αποδεικνύω / apodeiknío — *αποδεικνύω την αθωότητά μου*; *Δεν έχει αποδειχθεί τίποτα*
- απόδειξη / apódiksi — *απόδειξη ενοχής*; *το βάρος της απόδειξης*
- αναπόδεικτος / anapódiktos — *αναπόδεικτος ισχυρισμός*; an unproven claim
- τεκμηριωμένος / tekmirio̱ménos — *Ο ισχυρισμός δεν είναι τεκμηριωμένος*; *βασισμένο σε τεκμηριωμένα στοιχεία*
- τεκμηριώνω / tekmirio̱no — *τεκμηριώνω έναν ισχυρισμό*
- τεκμήριο / tekmírio — *τεκμήριο αθωότητας*; *τεκμήρια ενοχής*
- τεκμηρίωση / tekmirio̱si — *η τεκμηρίωση μιας θέσης*
- αδιαμφισβήτητος / adiamfisvitiitos — used when a claim is asserted to be beyond challenge; overlaps with Group 1
- αμφισβητώ / amfisviτó — *αμφισβητώ μια απόφαση*; the act of challenging an established claim
- αμφισβήτηση / amfisvitiisi — *υπό αμφισβήτηση*; the state of being contested

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 1 (truth of statements) but is distinguished by its focus on the process and status of establishing claims rather than on truth-value per se. The distinction is between *is it true?* (Group 1) and *has it been shown to be true, and by what standard?* (Group 8). τεκμηριωμένος is more associated with documentation and evidence-gathering; αποδεδειγμένος with demonstration and proof.

---

## Group 9: Terms used in the domain of accuracy and correspondence to a standard

**Label:** Accuracy, precision, and correctness relative to a standard

**Definition:** These terms are applied to descriptions, measurements, translations, answers, and procedures to indicate that they correspond closely to a fact, standard, or requirement. They appear in educational, technical, legal, and everyday contexts. They are distinguished from truth-predication (Group 1) by their focus on degree of correspondence and conformity to a standard rather than simple true/false evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**

- ακριβής / akrivís — *ακριβής μετάφραση*; *Η περιγραφή δεν είναι ακριβής*; *κατά το δυνατόν ακριβής*
- ακρίβεια / akrívia — *η ακρίβεια μιας μέτρησης*; accuracy/precision
- ακριβώς / akrivós — "exactly, precisely"; also used as an affirmative
- ακριβολογία / akrivología — precision of language
- σωστός / sostós — *Η σωστή απάντηση*; *Κάνω σωστά;*; correctness in everyday contexts
- σωστά / sostá — *Το έκανες σωστά*; adverbial form
- ορθός / orthós — *κατά την ορθή ερμηνεία*; *υπό την ορθή έννοια του όρου*; more formal register
- ορθώς / orthós — *Ορθώς εκτιμά την κατάσταση*; formal adverb
- ορθογραφία / orthografía — correct spelling; a compound showing the productive use of ορθ-
- πιστός / pistós — *πιστή μετάφραση*; faithful/accurate in the sense of close correspondence
- πιστότητα / pistótita — *πιστότητα μετάφρασης*; fidelity of translation

**Notes:** σωστός, ακριβής, and ορθός overlap considerably in this group but show register differences: σωστός is the most colloquial and broadly applicable; ακριβής emphasizes precision and close correspondence; ορθός is the most formal and is associated with conformity to a norm or standard. πιστός and πιστότητα participate in this group specifically in translation and reproduction contexts, where the relevant standard is the source text.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items show usage patterns that place them in more than one group, or that do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**αληθινός / alithinós**

Participates in Group 1 (truth of statements: *Αυτό είναι αληθινό*), Group 3 (genuineness of persons and things: *αληθινός φίλος*, *αληθινή αγάπη*), and Group 7 (discourse management: *αληθινά* as a sincerity marker). The term is the most semantically diffuse in the αληθ- family, covering factual truth, relational authenticity, and personal sincerity without a clear dominant domain.

---

**εικονικός / eikonikós**

Participates in Group 6 (simulation: *εικονική συναλλαγή*, *εικονικός διευθυντής*) and Group 5 (form vs. substance: *εικονικός* as "nominal"). Its contemporary primary use — *εικονική πραγματικότητα* (virtual reality) — does not fit cleanly into either group, as it refers to a technologically simulated environment rather than to deception or mere formality. This use is not well captured by any of the groups identified.

---

**πιστός / pistós**

Participates in Group 4 (trustworthiness: *πιστός φίλος*), Group 9 (accuracy: *πιστή μετάφραση*), and in religious contexts (a believer) that do not fit any of the groups above. The term's range across faithfulness, accuracy, and religious belief reflects the breadth of the πιστ- root rather than a unified usage pattern.

---

**αληθοφάνεια / alithofánia** and **αληθοφανής / alithofanís**

These terms are used in literary and rhetorical contexts to evaluate the appearance of truth in narrative and argument. They do not fit cleanly into Group 1 (truth of statements) because the relevant question is not whether something is true but whether it appears true. They do not fit Group 5 (form vs. substance) because the evaluation is aesthetic and rhetorical rather than legal or administrative. They are closest to Group 6 (simulation) in that they concern the production of an effect of truth, but they are not used critically or dismissively. No group above captures this usage pattern cleanly.

---

**εικαζόμενος / ikazómenos** and **εικασία / ikasía**

These terms are used to mark a claim as conjectured or supposed rather than established. They overlap with Group 1 (truth-value assessment) and Group 8 (evidence and proof) but are specifically about the epistemic status of a claim as unverified conjecture. Their register (formal, journalistic, legal) and their function (marking alleged but unestablished status) are distinct enough that they could form a small group with εικαζόμενος, εικός, and related terms, but the data do not show sufficient clustering to justify a separate group.

---

**Items not grouped**

The following items from P1 and P2 do not show usage patterns that place them clearly in any of the groups identified:

- **εικός / ikós**: Primarily a classical and formal philosophical term for probability or plausibility. Its contemporary use is too restricted to show clear grouping with other items.
- **κατά φαινόμενον / katá fenómenon**: A philosophical formula that participates in the form/substance contrast (Group 5) but is too restricted in register to be a representative member.
- **αλήθεια ενώπιον Θεού / alíthia enó̱pion Theoú**: A religious and legal oath formula. Its use is too specialized and formulaic to fit the usage-based groups identified.
- **αλήθεια ως αποκάλυψη / alíthia o̱s apokalipsi**: A philosophical and theological collocation. Its use is restricted to Heideggerian philosophy and theology and does not show clustering with other items in practice.
- **ψευδοεπιστημονισμός / psevdoepistimoniismós**: Documented as marginal; too rare to show clear grouping.
- **εικοτολογία / ikotología**: A technical classical rhetoric term; too restricted in use to group.
