# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: -kweli**

- **Root form:** kweli
- **P1 items participating:** 4 (*kweli*, *ukweli*, *kweli kweli*, *haki ya kweli*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Truth, genuine assertion, factual correctness. The primary Swahili root for truth as correspondence to reality. Functions as noun, adverb, and intensifier. Generates abstract nominalizations and reduplicated emphatic forms. Serves as the baseline against which false or uncertain claims are measured. It is the most semantically central root in the Swahili truth-evaluation domain.

---

**Root 2: ḥaqq / hakika family (Arabic ح-ق-ق)**

- **Root form:** haki / hakika / uhakika
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*haki*, *hakika*, *uhakika*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, entitlement, and certainty understood as grounded in reality. This Arabic root cluster covers truth as moral and legal right, as factual certainty, and as the underlying nature of things. It is the most semantically wide-ranging root in the domain.

---

**Root 3: -sahihi (Arabic ص-ح-ح)**

- **Root form:** sahihi / usahihi
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*sahihi*, *usahihi*, and participates in *thibitisha/uthibitisho* collocations)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Correctness and precision as evaluable properties of statements, documents, calculations, and procedures. This root anchors the domain of technical and procedural accuracy. Evaluates whether something conforms to a standard — factual, procedural, or formal. It is highly productive in administrative, legal, educational, and technical registers.

---

**Root 4: halisi / halali (Arabic-derived genuineness/legitimacy cluster)**

- **Root form:** halisi / uhalisi / halali / uhalali
- **P1 items participating:** 5 (*halisi*, *hali halisi*, *uhalisi*, *halali*, *uhalali*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, purity, and legitimacy — covering both the sense of being unmixed/authentic (from Arabic *khāliṣ*) and the sense of being lawfully permitted (from Arabic *ḥalāl*). These two Arabic-derived strands converge in Swahili around the evaluation of whether something is real, pure, or rightfully valid. Bridges truth-evaluation and ontological/aesthetic judgment.

---

**Root 5: -amini / amini**

- **Root form:** amini / uaminifu / mwaminifu
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*amini*, *uaminifu*, *mwaminifu*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Trust, belief, and faithfulness — the epistemic and moral evaluation of whether a person or claim is credible and reliable. Generates a productive derivational family covering persons, qualities, and acts of believing or trusting.

---

**Note on scope:** The roots *-kweli*, *haki/hakika*, *sahihi*, *halisi/halali*, and *amini* are selected as the central roots for expansion. The root *-danganya* (deceive) and the cluster around *ushahidi* are also productive but participate in fewer P1 items and are noted where relevant. The Arabic-derived roots *amini/uaminifu*, *halisi/uhalisi*, and *thibitisha/uthibitisho* are also productive in this domain. The *haki/hakika* root is highly productive but its primary semantic weight falls on rights/justice rather than truth per se; it is noted in Step 3 where relevant.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: -kweli

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root *-kweli*; bare form functioning as noun or adverb
**Natural English translation:** truth; truly; really
**Context of use:** Used as a standalone assertion of truth or as an adverb modifying a claim. Appears in everyday speech, moral discourse, and legal testimony (*Ninasema kweli* "I am speaking the truth"). Also used as a discourse particle to invite or confirm agreement: *kweli?* ("really?").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ukweli
**Transliteration:** ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract prefix) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; veracity; accuracy
**Context of use:** The standard abstract nominal form used in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse when referring to truth as a property or value. Used in philosophical, legal, and moral discourse to refer to the property of being true or the truth of a matter (*ukweli wa jambo* "the truth of the matter"). Already listed in P1; included here as the primary nominal derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** kweli kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** Reduplication of *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** really truly; genuinely; for real; in actual fact; truly; in all honesty
**Context of use:** Intensified form used in spoken Swahili to emphasize sincerity or factual accuracy. Emphatic intensifier in spoken and written Swahili, used to stress the sincerity or accuracy of an assertion. Already listed in P1; included here as the primary intensified derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** kwa kweli
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* (prepositional particle: by, with, in) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest; indeed
**Context of use:** Adverbial phrase used to frame a statement as sincere or factually grounded. Common in both spoken and written registers, including religious and moral discourse: *kwa kweli nakuambia* ("I tell you truly"). An adverbial phrase used to assert that something is said or done sincerely and honestly (*kwa kweli, hii ni tatizo kubwa*, "truly, this is a serious problem").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** kwa kweli kweli
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* + reduplicated *kweli kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truly and sincerely; in all honesty; absolutely for real; really and truly; genuinely; absolutely
**Context of use:** Further intensified adverbial phrase used in emphatic spoken assertions. More colloquial than *kwa kweli*; used when a speaker wishes to stress complete sincerity or factual certainty. More emphatic than *kwa kweli*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** kweli kabisa
**Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* + *kabisa* (completely, absolutely; from Arabic *كبيسة*)
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true; completely true; the absolute truth; that's absolutely right
**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to assert that something is entirely and without qualification true. Used as an emphatic affirmation in spoken and written Swahili. Confirms the complete accuracy of a statement or claim. *Kabisa* functions as an intensifier across many Swahili evaluative expressions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ukweli wa mambo
**Transliteration:** ukweli wa mambo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *wa* (associative particle) + *mambo* (things, matters, affairs; plural of *jambo*)
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter; the facts of the case; how things really are
**Context of use:** Used in discourse to refer to the actual state of affairs, as opposed to appearances or claims. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Used in legal, journalistic, and analytical contexts to refer to the actual facts underlying a situation or dispute.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ukweli mtupu
**Transliteration:** ukweli mtupu
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *mtupu* (pure, mere, nothing but; class 11 agreement)
**Natural English translation:** the plain truth; the bare truth; nothing but the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to assert that a statement contains no admixture of falsehood or embellishment (*Ninasema ukweli mtupu* "I am telling the plain truth"). Equivalent in function to English "the plain truth." Parallels the legal formula "the truth, the whole truth."
