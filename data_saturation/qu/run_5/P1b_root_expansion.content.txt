# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** This expansion follows the same scope and orthographic conventions as P1: primary documentation from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka), with variety-specific notes where relevant. Epistemic statuses reflect attestation confidence based on available lexicographic and descriptive sources (Cerrón-Palomino, Parker, Cusihuamán, Itier, and the Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua). Where a form is attested in one variety but not confirmed across the continuum, this is noted.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: cheqa**

- **Root form:** cheqa
- **P1 items participating:** 9 (cheqa, cheqaq, cheqaqsi, cheqaqmi, cheqan, cheqaqa, qelqa cheqa, cheqa kay, cheqanchay, mana cheqa, cheqaq simi)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary semantic anchor of the truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua. Contributes the meaning of truth, correctness, genuineness, and reality. It is the base from which the most productive derivational network in this domain is built, participating in nominal, verbal, adjectival, and evidential forms.

---

**Root 2: llulla**

- **Root form:** llulla
- **P1 items participating:** 4 (llulla, llullakuy, llullaq, llulla simi)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary semantic anchor of the falsity/deception domain, directly antonymous to cheqa. Contributes the meaning of falsehood, deception, and dishonesty. Less derivationally productive than cheqa but forms a coherent cluster of forms for evaluating dishonest speech and character.

---

**Root 3: panta-**

- **Root form:** panta-
- **P1 items participating:** 2 (pantay, pantasqa)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Contributes the meaning of error, mistake, and being wrong — distinct from deliberate deception (llulla) and from mere absence of truth (mana cheqa). Covers the domain of inadvertent incorrectness, misidentification, and going astray. Moderately productive derivationally.

**Note on selection:** *allin* (P1 item 12) is a high-frequency root with broad evaluative scope, but its participation in the truth-evaluation domain specifically is secondary (it covers goodness, correctness, and propriety across many domains). It is noted here but not selected as a primary root for expansion, as its truth-domain contribution is less central than cheqa, llulla, or panta-. The expansion below focuses on the three roots identified above.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: cheqa — Full Expansion

---

**1.**
- **Form:** cheqa
- **Transliteration:** cheqa
- **Morpheme breakdown:** Root; adjective/noun base
- **Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, correct
- **Context of use:** The base form used predicatively and attributively to evaluate statements, persons, objects, and states of affairs as true or genuine. Appears in everyday speech, moral evaluation, and formal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
- **Form:** cheqaq
- **Transliteration:** cheqaq
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (agentive/nominalizing suffix) → "one who is true / that which is true"
- **Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine, real
- **Context of use:** Used predicatively or nominally to characterize a person as honest or a statement as truthful. Appears in moral evaluation of character and in assessment of testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Form:** cheqaqmi
- **Transliteration:** cheqaqmi
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + *-mi* (direct evidential suffix) → "it is indeed true (I know directly)"
- **Natural English translation:** it is truly so, it is indeed true, certainly
- **Context of use:** Asserts truth on the basis of the speaker's direct knowledge or experience. Used in everyday assertion and in formal testimony to mark first-hand epistemic grounding.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Form:** cheqaqsi
- **Transliteration:** cheqaqsi
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + *-si* (reportative evidential suffix) → "reportedly true"
- **Natural English translation:** reportedly true, said to be true, allegedly genuine
- **Context of use:** Marks a proposition as true according to hearsay or reported evidence. Used in narration of events not directly witnessed and in evaluation of second-hand testimony.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
- **Form:** cheqaqchus
- **Transliteration:** cheqaqchus
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + *-chus* (dubitative/interrogative suffix) → "is it true? / perhaps true"
- **Natural English translation:** is it really true?, perhaps true, I wonder if it is genuine
- **Context of use:** Expresses doubt or uncertainty about whether something is true or genuine. Used in everyday questioning and in contexts of evaluating uncertain claims. Attested in Southern Quechua as part of the systematic evidential paradigm.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
- **Form:** cheqan
- **Transliteration:** cheqan
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive/nominalizing suffix) → "its truth / what is right"
- **Natural English translation:** what is right, what is correct, the truth of the matter, justice
- **Context of use:** Used in moral and legal contexts to refer to what is proper or just in a given situation. Appears in evaluations of whether a judgment or course of action is correct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
- **Form:** cheqaqa
- **Transliteration:** cheqaqa
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-qa* (topic marker) → "as for the truth / the truth is..."
- **Natural English translation:** the truth is..., in truth, actually, in reality
- **Context of use:** Introduces a corrective or clarifying statement, signaling a shift from an apparent or reported state of affairs to what is actually the case. Used in everyday discourse and in formal correction of errors.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
- **Form:** cheqanchay
- **Transliteration:** cheqanchay
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha* (causative/verbalizer) + *-y* (infinitive) → "to make true / to verify / to correct"
- **Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to correct, to establish as true
- **Context of use:** Used in contexts of checking accuracy, confirming facts, or rectifying a false or mistaken state of affairs. Appears in administrative, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
- **Form:** cheqanchasqa
- **Transliteration:** cheqanchasqa
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqancha- + *-sqa* (past participle/resultative) → "that which has been verified / confirmed / corrected"
- **Natural English translation:** verified, confirmed, corrected, established as true
- **Context of use:** Adjectival/participial form used to characterize a statement, document, or fact as having been verified or officially confirmed. Appears in legal and administrative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
- **Form:** cheqanchakuy
- **Transliteration:** cheqanchakuy
- **Morpheme breakdown:** cheqancha- + *-ku* (reflexive/middle) + *-y* (infinitive) → "to verify for oneself / to confirm oneself"
- **Natural English translation:** to verify for oneself, to confirm (intransitive), to make sure
- **Context of use:** The reflexive/middle form of cheqanchay, used when the subject verifies or confirms something for their own benefit or in a self-directed manner. Attested in Southern Quechua.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**

- **Transliteration:** cheqa kay

- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**Note:** The cheqa root is highly productive. ## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are cases where the identified roots appear in contexts where their contribution seems to shift from the core truth-evaluation domain. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**cheqa in degree/intensity contexts:**
- *cheqaqpuni* (item 29 above) uses cheqaq with the emphatic suffix *-puni*, which in other contexts marks degree or absolute extent (e.g., "always," "definitely," "without exception"). The cheqa root here participates in a degree-marking construction.

**cheqa in path/direction contexts:**
- *cheqa ñan* ("true path," item 21 above) applies cheqa to a physical or metaphorical path. In some attested uses, cheqa ñan refers to a straight or direct road (physical straightness) as well as a morally correct course of conduct.

**cheqa in identity/selfhood contexts:**
- *cheqa runa* ("true person," item 17 above) applies cheqa to the concept of personhood and human identity, not merely to propositions or objects. The root here participates in evaluation of what constitutes a proper or complete human being.

**cheqa in religious/cosmological contexts:**
- *cheqa Dios* (item 23 above) and *cheqa yupaychay* (item 22 above) apply cheqa in contexts of religious authenticity and worship, where the contrast is between genuine and false spiritual objects or practices.

**cheqa in aesthetic/representational contexts:**
- *cheqa hina* (item 28 above) applies cheqa in a comparative construction evaluating resemblance to truth or reality, which appears in contexts of evaluating representations, performances, or appearances.

**llulla in object/material contexts:**
- *llulla qelqa* (item 12, llulla expansion) applies llulla to written documents, where the root contributes the meaning of forgery or fabrication rather than spoken falsehood.

**llullayay in process/change contexts:**
- *llullayay* (item 11, llulla expansion) uses the inceptive/stative verbalizer *-ya-* with llulla, producing a form describing a process of becoming false or deceptive. The root here participates in a change-of-state construction.

**panta- in physical/spatial contexts:**
- The root panta- in some attested uses extends to physical going astray or losing one's way (e.g., losing a path), not only to propositional or moral error. This spatial use is attested alongside the epistemic and moral uses.

**panta- in causative/agency contexts:**
- *pantachiy* (item 4, panta- expansion) places the panta- root in a causative construction where the relevant evaluation shifts from the error itself to the act of inducing error in another agent.

**cheqanchay in administrative/procedural contexts:**
- *cheqanchay* and *cheqanchasqa* appear in administrative and legal contexts where "verification" or "confirmation" is a procedural act with institutional force, not merely an epistemic judgment about truth.
