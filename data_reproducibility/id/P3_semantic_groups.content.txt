# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Indonesian (id)

## IDENTIFIED USAGE-BASED GROUPS

---

## GROUP 1: Factual Correctness of Statements

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, claims, or propositions correspond to facts or reality. These expressions are used in contexts where speakers assess the truth-value of assertions, distinguish accurate from inaccurate information, or verify factual claims. Commonly appear in discourse about information verification, fact-checking, and epistemic evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- benar (true, correct)
- salah (wrong, incorrect)
- betul (correct, right)
- keliru (mistaken, erroneous)
- tidak benar (not true)
- benar atau salah (true or false)
- faktual (factual)
- akurat (accurate)
- tepat (accurate, precise)
- sebenarnya (actually, in fact)
- ternyata (it turns out, apparently)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- nyata (real, actual) — overlaps with existence evaluation
- bohong (lie) — overlaps with intentional deception group
- tepat (precise) — overlaps with appropriateness evaluation

---

## GROUP 2: Intentional Deception vs. Honesty

**Definition:** Terms specifically evaluating whether speech acts involve deliberate deception or truthfulness. Distinguished from mere factual error by emphasis on speaker's intent and moral character. Used in contexts of trust evaluation, moral judgment of communication, and interpersonal credibility assessment.

**Representative expressions:**
- bohong (lie, to lie)
- jujur (honest, truthful)
- dusta (lie, falsehood - literary)
- kebohongan (lie, deception)
- berbohong (to lie)
- membohongi (to lie to)
- pembohong (liar)
- kejujuran (honesty)
- jujur saja (to be honest)
- jangan bohong (don't lie)
- bohong besar (big lie)
- penuh dusta (full of lies)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- palsu (fake) — overlaps with authenticity of objects
- benar (true) — overlaps with factual correctness

---

## GROUP 3: Authenticity of Objects and Documents

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether physical objects, documents, or products are genuine originals versus counterfeits, forgeries, or imitations. Used primarily in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts involving authentication, verification of provenance, and detection of fraud.

**Representative expressions:**
- asli (genuine, authentic, original)
- palsu (fake, counterfeit, forged)
- tulen (pure, genuine)
- keaslian (authenticity, genuineness)
- memalsu (to forge, to counterfeit)
- pemalsuan (forgery, counterfeiting)
- pemalsu (forger, counterfeiter)
- uang palsu (counterfeit money)
- dokumen palsu (forged document)
- asli atau palsu (genuine or fake)
- emas tulen (pure gold)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- sejati (true, genuine) — more abstract/philosophical usage
- nyata (real) — overlaps with existence evaluation

---

## GROUP 4: Legal and Procedural Validity

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, documents, or statuses conform to legal requirements, official procedures, or institutional rules. Used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts to assess legitimacy, enforceability, and compliance with established norms and regulations.

**Representative expressions:**
- sah (valid, legal, legitimate)
- absah (valid, legitimate)
- batal (void, cancelled, invalid)
- valid (valid - technical)
- sahih (valid, authentic - especially religious)
- mengesahkan (to validate, to legalize)
- pengesahan (validation, legalization)
- keabsahan (validity, legitimacy)
- membatalkan (to cancel, to annul)
- pembatalan (cancellation, annulment)
- sah atau tidak (valid or not)
- batal demi hukum (null and void by law)
- sah secara hukum (legally valid)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- benar (correct) — overlaps with procedural correctness
- tepat (appropriate) — overlaps with procedural appropriateness

---

## GROUP 5: Existence and Reality vs. Illusion

**Definition:** Terms distinguishing what actually exists or is real from what is imaginary, hypothetical, or illusory. Used in contexts contrasting tangible reality with fantasy, discussing manifestation of evidence, or asserting actual existence versus mere appearance or assumption.

**Representative expressions:**
- nyata (real, actual, tangible)
- kenyataan (reality, fact)
- sejati (true, genuine - elevated)
- sungguh (truly, really, genuinely)
- nyata-nyata (clearly, obviously)
- kenyataan atau khayalan (reality or fantasy)
- dalam kenyataan (in reality)
- sesungguhnya (truly, in reality)
- hakikat (essence, true nature)
- sebenarnya (actually, in truth)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- benar (true) — overlaps with factual correctness
- asli (genuine) — overlaps with authenticity of objects

## GROUP 6: Similarity and Identity Relations

**Definition:** Terms used to compare entities and evaluate degrees of resemblance, equivalence, or sameness. Used in comparative contexts, matching operations, and discussions of likeness ranging from exact identity to approximate similarity.

**Representative expressions:**
- sama (same, equal, identical)
- mirip (similar, resembling)
- kemiripan (similarity, resemblance)
- kesamaan (similarity, equality)
- menyamakan (to equate, to make equal)
- sama dengan (same as, equal to)
- mirip dengan (similar to)
- sama persis (exactly the same)
- mirip sekali (very similar)
- tidak sama (not the same)
- sama saja (just the same)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- cocok (matching, suitable) — overlaps with appropriateness
- tepat (exact) — overlaps with precision

---

## GROUP 7: Appropriateness and Suitability

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether something is fitting, suitable, or appropriate for a particular purpose, context, or situation. Used in contexts of matching, compatibility assessment, and judgments about what is proper or fitting given circumstances. Includes both functional fit and social/moral propriety.

**Representative expressions:**
- cocok (suitable, matching, fitting)
- pas (exact fit, just right)
- tepat (appropriate, precise, exact)
- wajar (reasonable, natural, proper)
- pantas (appropriate, fitting, deserved)
- kecocokan (compatibility, suitability)
- kepantasan (propriety, appropriateness)
- kewajaran (reasonableness, naturalness)
- mencocokkan (to match, to fit)
- tepat waktu (on time, punctual)
- tepat sasaran (on target)
- cocok sekali (very suitable)
- kurang pantas (somewhat inappropriate)
- wajar saja (it's natural, no wonder)
- pantas saja (no wonder, serves right)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- benar (correct) — overlaps with correctness evaluation
- sesuai (in accordance with) — not in P1 but related concept

## GROUP 8: Error and Mistake Attribution

**Definition:** Terms used to identify and attribute errors, mistakes, or faults, particularly in contexts of responsibility assignment and blame. Distinguished from simple incorrectness by emphasis on accountability and causal attribution of wrongness.

**Representative expressions:**
- salah (wrong, at fault)
- keliru (mistaken, erroneous)
- khilaf (error, lapse - formal)
- kesalahan (error, mistake, fault)
- menyalahkan (to blame, to fault)
- salah paham (misunderstanding)
- salah sangka (mistaken assumption)
- salah kaprah (common misconception)
- terkeliru (mistaken accidentally)
- tersalah (wrongly done accidentally)
- siapa yang salah (who's at fault)
- kalau tidak salah (if I'm not mistaken)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- tidak benar (not true) — overlaps with factual incorrectness
- batal (void) — overlaps with legal invalidity

## GROUP 9: Precision and Exactness

**Definition:** Terms emphasizing exact measurement, temporal punctuality, or precise correspondence. Used in technical, scientific, and temporal contexts where exactness and accuracy of specification are paramount.

**Representative expressions:**
- tepat (precise, exact)
- akurat (accurate)
- pas (exact, fitting precisely)
- ketepatan (accuracy, precision, exactness)
- keakuratan (accuracy, precision)
- tepat waktu (on time, punctual)
- pas sekali (exactly right)
- akurat sekali (very accurate)
- tepat di (exactly at)
- sangat akurat (very accurate)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- benar (correct) — overlaps with general correctness
- cocok (fitting) — overlaps with suitability

---

## GROUP 10: Intensification and Emphasis Constructions

**Definition:** Reduplicated and compound forms used to intensify, emphasize, or strengthen the force of evaluation. These constructions function primarily as discourse intensifiers rather than introducing new semantic content.

**Representative expressions:**
- benar-benar (really, truly, very)
- betul-betul (really, truly)
- sungguh-sungguh (seriously, earnestly)
- nyata-nyata (clearly, obviously)
- sama sekali tidak (not at all)
- benar sekali (exactly right)
- tepat sekali (exactly right)
- sangat wajar (very reasonable)
- mirip sekali (very similar)
- cocok sekali (very suitable)

**Note:** These are primarily grammatical/pragmatic rather than lexical-semantic groupings.

---

## GROUP 11: Discourse Markers for Truth Claims

**Definition:** Expressions that function as discourse markers to introduce corrections, reveal actual states of affairs, or frame statements as frank admissions. Used to manage information flow and epistemic stance in conversation.

**Representative expressions:**
- sebenarnya (actually, in fact)
- sesungguhnya (truly, in reality)
- sebetulnya (actually, in fact)
- ternyata (it turns out, apparently)
- jujur saja (to be honest)
- sejujurnya (honestly)
- pada hakikatnya (essentially, in essence)
- dalam kenyataan (in reality)

**Note:** These items function more as discourse-pragmatic markers than as predicates of evaluation.

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with multiple strong affiliations:

**tepat** — Functions across at least three domains:
- Precision/exactness (tepat waktu, measurements)
- Appropriateness (tepat sasaran, suitable choice)
- Factual accuracy (jawaban tepat)

**benar** — Extremely broad usage:
- Factual correctness of statements
- Procedural correctness
- Moral rightness
- General affirmation/agreement

**nyata** — Spans multiple domains:
- Existence/reality (dunia nyata)
- Tangible evidence (bukti nyata)
- Actual occurrence (kenyataan)

**sama** — Multiple functions:
- Identity/equality relation
- Discourse particle (sama-sama = you're welcome)
- Reciprocal marker (sesama = fellow, mutual)

### Items with specialized or restricted usage:

**salah satu** (one of) — Grammaticalized into quantifier function, semantically distant from "wrong"

**bohong-bohongan** (fake, pretend) — Specialized playful/non-serious meaning distinct from actual lying

**bercocok tanam** (to cultivate/farm) — Semantic shift from "matching" to agricultural domain

**pas-pasan** (barely enough) — Specialized economic/adequacy meaning

### Items requiring more data:

**kebenar-benaran** (excessive correctness) — Uncertain status, limited attestation
**ketulen** (purity) — Rare nominalization, uncertain productivity
**terbenar** (most correct) — Marginal superlative form

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Cross-cutting distinctions observed:

1. **Object of evaluation:**
   - Statements/propositions (benar, salah, faktual)
   - Physical objects (asli, palsu, tulen)
   - Legal/procedural status (sah, batal, valid)
   - Persons/character (jujur, pembohong)

2. **Intentionality dimension:**
   - Intentional deception (bohong, dusta, palsu)
   - Unintentional error (keliru, khilaf, tersalah)
   - Neutral evaluation (benar, salah, tepat)

3. **Register stratification:**
   - Colloquial (betul, bohong, pas)
   - Neutral (benar, salah, sama)
   - Formal/literary (dusta, sejati, hakikat)
   - Technical (valid, faktual, akurat)

4. **Gradability:**
   - Gradable (mirip, tepat, akurat, cocok)
   - Binary (sah/batal, asli/palsu, benar/salah)

### Asymmetries noted:

- The authenticity domain (asli/palsu) is primarily object-focused with limited extension to abstract evaluation
- The legal validity domain (sah/batal) shows highly specialized constructions not paralleled in other domains
- The similarity domain (sama/mirip) shows rich grammaticalization patterns (sama-sama, sesama) not found with other bases
- Intentional deception terms (bohong, dusta) form a coherent group but lack a clear positive counterpart beyond general "jujur"

### Items resisting clear grouping:

Several high-frequency items (benar, tepat, nyata, sama) show such broad and varied usage that they participate in multiple groupings without clearly belonging to any single one. This appears to reflect genuine polysemy and functional breadth rather than analytical inadequacy.
