Below is a construction-focused inventory using P1 items as the **base forms** and listing **attested multi-word constructions** only. I avoid single-word derivations already handled in P2a.

---

## 1. Base Form: **სიმართლე**

### 1. **სიმართლის თქმა**
- **Transliteration:** *simartlis tkma*
- **English translation:** telling the truth; to tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** Very common expression for speaking truthfully. Used both literally and in moral or conversational evaluation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **სიმართლე გითხრათ / სიმართლე რომ გითხრათ**
- **Transliteration:** *simartle gitxrat / simartle rom gitxrat*
- **English translation:** to tell you the truth; honestly
- **Usage notes:** Common discourse-marking phrase introducing a candid opinion, admission, or correction. Neutral and widely used in speech and writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **სიმართლე ითქვას**
- **Transliteration:** *simartle itqvas*
- **English translation:** truth be told
- **Usage notes:** A common impersonal formula used to introduce a frank assessment. Slightly more rhetorical or literary than direct conversational phrasing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **სიმართლეს შეესაბამება**
- **Transliteration:** *simartles sheesabameba*
- **English translation:** corresponds to the truth; is true
- **Usage notes:** Used in formal, journalistic, legal, and evaluative contexts for whether a statement matches reality. More formal than everyday “is right/true.”
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **სიმართლეს არ შეესაბამება**
- **Transliteration:** *simartles ar sheesabameba*
- **English translation:** does not correspond to the truth; is untrue
- **Usage notes:** Common in denials, corrections, media statements, and official language. Often used to reject claims as factually false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **სიმართლე გაირკვა**
- **Transliteration:** *simartle gairkva*
- **English translation:** the truth came out; the truth became clear
- **Usage notes:** Used when hidden facts are revealed or uncertainty is resolved. Common in narrative, journalism, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **სიმართლე დადგინდა**
- **Transliteration:** *simartle dadginda*
- **English translation:** the truth was established
- **Usage notes:** Common in investigative, legal, and formal contexts. Stronger and more institutional than everyday revelation formulas.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **სიმართლის დადგენა**
- **Transliteration:** *simartlis dadgena*
- **English translation:** establishment of the truth; determining the truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in law, investigation, scholarship, and journalism. Refers to the process of finding what really happened or what is factually correct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **სიმართლის ძიება**
- **Transliteration:** *simartlis dzieba*
- **English translation:** search for truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in philosophical, journalistic, ethical, and literary contexts. It can refer to factual inquiry or broader moral/intellectual pursuit.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **სიმართლე თვალებში ჩახედა**
- **Transliteration:** *simartle tvalebshi chakheda*
- **English translation:** looked the truth in the eye; faced the truth
- **Usage notes:** Idiomatic expression for confronting reality honestly. Used in literary and journalistic language and also understandable in general usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## 2. Base Form: **მართალი**

### 1. **მართალი ხარ**
- **Transliteration:** *martali xar*
- **English translation:** you are right
- **Usage notes:** Extremely common conversational phrase for agreement or acknowledgment that another speaker is correct.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **მართალი იყო**
- **Transliteration:** *martali iq’o*
- **English translation:** was right
- **Usage notes:** Used retrospectively about a person, warning, judgment, or prediction. Very common in everyday speech and narrative.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **მართალი რომ ვთქვა**
- **Transliteration:** *martali rom vtkva*
- **English translation:** to tell the truth; honestly speaking
- **Usage notes:** Common discourse phrase introducing a frank opinion. Close in function to **სიმართლე რომ გითხრათ**, but built on the adjective.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **მართალი გითხრათ**
- **Transliteration:** *martali gitxrat*
- **English translation:** to tell you the truth
- **Usage notes:** Very common candidness marker in speech and writing. Natural in both neutral and mildly informal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **მართალი სიტყვა**
- **Transliteration:** *martali sit’q’va*
- **English translation:** true word; truthful statement
- **Usage notes:** Used for sincere or truthful speech, often with moral or rhetorical force. More common in literary, emphatic, or evaluative usage than in plain factual reporting.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **მართალი კაცი**
- **Transliteration:** *martali k’atsi*
- **English translation:** righteous man; honest person
- **Usage notes:** Refers to a morally upright or fair person. Common in moral, religious, and literary contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **მართალი საქმე**
- **Transliteration:** *martali sakme*
- **English translation:** just cause; right cause
- **Usage notes:** Used for a morally justified cause, action, or position. Common in rhetorical, ethical, and public discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **მართალი პასუხი**
- **Transliteration:** *martali pasuxi*
- **English translation:** correct answer
- **Usage notes:** Natural in educational and conversational contexts, though **სწორი პასუხი** is often more neutral and common for school-type correctness. This one remains attested and understandable.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
