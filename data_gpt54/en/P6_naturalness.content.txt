## 1. BECOMING TRUE / CONFIRMED TRUE
### Pattern
**NP + prove true / turn out true / become true**; also **come true** for predictions, hopes, fears, wishes, and dreams

### Examples
- *The rumor proved true.* → N/A → “The rumor proved true.”
- *Her prediction came true.* → N/A → “Her prediction came true.”

### Notes
English has clear and common dynamic expressions for later confirmation that something is true.  
*Prove true* is common for claims, rumors, suspicions, and reports.  
*Come true* is especially fixed for predictions, hopes, fears, wishes, and dreams.  
*Become true* is possible, but less idiomatic than *come true* or *prove true* in many cases.

### Examples
- *They got it right.* → N/A → “They got it right.”
- *He put the record right.* → N/A → “He put the record right.”

These are especially common for answers, reports, interpretations, and situations needing correction.

### Examples

## 10. CAUSING SOMETHING TO BE TRUE
### Pattern
**Y + prove + NP + true**

### Examples
- *The new evidence proved the claim true.* → N/A → “The new evidence proved the claim true.”
- *The facts proved the rumor true.* → N/A → “The facts proved the rumor true.”

### Notes
This is one of the clearest causative-like constructions in the domain.  
It does not mean that Y makes the claim true in the world; rather, Y establishes or shows it to be true.  
English does not have one general productive verb meaning “cause to be true” across all truth-related meanings in the provided vocabulary.

## 17. APPEARANCE OF TRUTH AS A DYNAMIC JUDGMENT
### Pattern
**NP + ring true / seem credible / seem genuine / have a ring of truth**

### Examples
- *His story rings true.* → N/A → “His story rings true.”
- *The excuse seems credible.* → N/A → “The excuse seems credible.”

### Notes
These are dynamic in discourse because they encode an evaluation reached by a speaker, often before full verification.  
They do not mark actual change in the object itself, but a judgment of plausibility, authenticity, or believability.

## 18. NO SINGLE GENERAL DYNAMIC SYSTEM
### Pattern
**No common expression identified**

### Examples
- *No common expression identified for a single general verb meaning “become true/correct/valid/authentic/reliable” across all subdomains.* → N/A → “No common expression identified.”
- *No common expression identified for a single general verb meaning “cause to become true/correct/valid/authentic” across all subdomains.* → N/A → “No common expression identified.”

### Notes
English does not use one unified dynamic verb across the whole truth-related domain.  
Instead, it uses different constructions for different subdomains:

- **authenticate** for authenticity
- **validate / invalidate** for validity
- **hold true / remain faithful / remain valid** for stability
