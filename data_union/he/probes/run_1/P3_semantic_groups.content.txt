# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, register distribution, and contextual pairing observed across P1 and P1b/P2 documentation. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. Where items appear in multiple groups, this is noted explicitly. Items that do not fit cleanly into any group are listed at the end. Group sizes are uneven by design; this reflects the data.

---

## Group 1 — Everyday correctness confirmation

**Definition:** These expressions are used in everyday spoken Hebrew to confirm or deny that a statement, answer, or fact is correct. They function primarily as responses or evaluations in conversational exchange, without strong moral or philosophical weight. They are the default register-neutral tools for asserting or denying factual accuracy in ordinary discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *nakhon* (נכון) — the standard response confirming correctness
- *ze nakhon* (זֶה נָכוֹן) — "that is correct"
- *ze lo nakhon* (זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן) — "that is not correct"
- *nakhon she-* (נָכוֹן שֶׁ-) — "it is true that / admittedly"
- *nakhon lehagid* (נָכוֹן לְהַגִּיד) — "to be fair / it is right to say"
- *nakhon leʿakhshav* (נָכוֹן לְעַכְשָׁו) — "correct as of now"
- *nakhon letaʾarikh* (נָכוֹן לְתַאֲרִיךְ) — "correct as of [date]"
- *lo bediyyuq nakhon* (לֹא בִּדְיוּק נָכוֹן) — "not exactly correct"
- *ze ʾemet* (זֶה אֱמֶת) — "that is true"
- *ze lo ʾemet* (זֶה לֹא אֱמֶת) — "that is not true"
- *atta tsodeq / at tsoddeqet* (אַתָּה צוֹדֵק / אַתְּ צוֹדֶקֶת) — "you are right"
- *beʾemet* (בֶּאֱמֶת) — "really, truly" (as everyday intensifier/sincerity marker)
- *bediyyuq* (בְּדִיּוּק) — "exactly, precisely"

**Notes:** *ze ʾemet* and *ze lo ʾemet* overlap with Group 2 (moral/philosophical truth), but in everyday spoken usage they function primarily as correctness confirmations rather than philosophical assertions. *atta tsodeq* is the most colloquial form; it draws on the root צ-ד-ק but in this usage has no moral or justice connotation. *beʾemet* in this group functions as a degree marker or sincerity signal rather than a propositional claim.

## Group 2 — Moral and philosophical truth claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used when *ʾemet* is invoked as a weighty moral, philosophical, or religious value — not merely as a description of factual accuracy but as a standard of genuine correspondence between word and reality, or between a person's conduct and an ideal. They appear in elevated, religious, legal, and philosophical registers and carry normative force beyond simple correctness.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾemet* (אֱמֶת) — truth as a moral/philosophical value
- *ʾamiti* (אֲמִתִּי) — true, real, genuine (with moral weight)
- *ʾamitiyyut* (אֲמִתִּיּוּת) — truthfulness as an abstract quality
- *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* (אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה) — truth and faithfulness (liturgical/legal)
- *ʾemet va-shalom* (אֱמֶת וָשָׁלוֹם) — truth and peace (biblical)
- *ʾemet u-mishpat* (אֱמֶת וּמִשְׁפָּט) — truth and justice (biblical/legal)
- *ʾemet leʾammittah* (אֱמֶת לְאַמִּתָּהּ) — truth in its truest sense (rabbinic/legal)
- *leʾamitto shel davar* (לֶאֱמִתּוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר) — in actual fact (formal/legal)
- *dover ʾemet* (דּוֹבֵר אֱמֶת) — one who speaks the truth (moral characterization)
- *ledabber ʾemet* (לְדַבֵּר אֱמֶת) — to speak the truth
- *lomar et ha-ʾemet* (לוֹמַר אֶת הָאֱמֶת) — to tell the truth
- *ḥotam shel ʾemet* (חוֹתָם שֶׁל אֱמֶת) — seal of truth (literary/religious)
- *ʾemet nitshit* (אֱמֶת נִצְחִית) — eternal truth
- *ʾemet merubbat panim* (אֱמֶת מְרֻבַּת פָּנִים) — truth with many faces (rabbinic)
- *midvar sheqer tirḥaq* (מִדְבַּר שֶׁקֶר תִּרְחָק) — distance yourself from falsehood (biblical injunction)
- *kanut* (כַּנּוּת) — sincerity, honesty
- *bekhannot* (בְּכַנּוּת) — sincerely, honestly
- *yosher* (יֹשֶׁר) — integrity, uprightness
- *yashar* (יָשָׁר) — upright, honest
- *yesharut* (יְשָׁרוּת) — uprightness, honesty

**Notes:** This group overlaps substantially with Group 3 (legal/testimonial truth) and Group 5 (personal character evaluation). The distinguishing feature here is the invocation of *ʾemet* or related terms as a value in itself, not merely as a procedural or evaluative standard. *kanut* and *yosher* belong here because their primary usage is in moral evaluation of persons and conduct, but they also appear in Group 5.

## Group 3 — Legal and testimonial credibility

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in legal proceedings, judicial decisions, and formal evaluations of testimony and evidence. They assess whether a witness, source, or document can be relied upon in a formal adjudicative context. They are distinguished from everyday reliability assessments by their institutional register and their connection to formal legal consequences.

**Representative expressions:**
- *mehiman* (מְהֵימָן) — trustworthy, credible (formal/legal)
- *mehimanut* (מְהֵימָנוּת) — credibility, trustworthiness (legal abstract noun)
- *nimtsa mehiman* (נִמְצָא מְהֵימָן) — found to be credible (judicial formula)
- *nimtsa lo mehiman* (נִמְצָא לֹא מְהֵימָן) — found not to be credible (judicial formula)
- *lo mehiman* (לֹא מְהֵימָן) — not credible (legal)
- *ʿed neʾeman* (עֵד נֶאֱמָן) — reliable witness (legal/religious)
- *ʿed ʾemet* (עֵד אֱמֶת) — truthful witness (legal/religious)
- *ʿed sheqer* (עֵד שֶׁקֶר) — false witness, perjurer (legal/religious)
- *ʾaminut* (אֲמִינוּת) — credibility, reliability (legal/journalistic)
- *beḥinat ha-ʾaminut* (בְּחִינַת הָאֲמִינוּת) — examination of credibility (legal formula)
- *mivḥan ha-ʾaminut* (מִבְחַן הָאֲמִינוּת) — the credibility test
- *ḥoser ʾaminut* (חוֹסֶר אֲמִינוּת) — lack of credibility
- *pegiʿa ba-ʾaminut* (פְּגִיעָה בָּאֲמִינוּת) — damage to credibility
- *maqor mehiman* (מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן) — reliable source (legal/journalistic)
- *maqor ʾamin* (מָקוֹר אָמִין) — reliable source (slightly less formal)
- *ʿed ʾamin* (עֵד אָמִין) — reliable witness
- *savir* (סָבִיר) — reasonable, plausible (legal standard)
- *sevirut* (סְבִירוּת) — reasonableness (legal standard)
- *ʾi-sevirut* (אִי-סְבִירוּת) — unreasonableness (administrative law)
- *bilti savir* (בִּלְתִּי סָבִיר) — unreasonable
- *sevara* (סְבָרָה) — reasoned argument, logical inference (Talmudic/legal)
- *pasul* (פָּסוּל) — disqualified, inadmissible
- *pesulut* (פְּסוּלוּת) — invalidity, inadmissibility
- *lifsol* (לִפְסֹל) — to disqualify, to rule inadmissible
- *pissul* (פִּסּוּל) — disqualification, ruling of inadmissibility
- *kasher* (כָּשֵׁר) — fit, valid, above suspicion (in legal/general use)
- *kashrut* (כַּשְׁרוּת) — fitness, validity (in legal/general use)

**Notes:** *savir* and *sevirut* are particularly prominent in Israeli administrative law, where *ʾi-sevirut qitsunit* (extreme unreasonableness) is a key standard for judicial review. *kasher* and *pasul* form a direct antonym pair in this group. *mehiman* is more formal and more specifically legal than *ʾamin*; both appear here but with different register weights. *ʾaminut* overlaps with Group 4 (journalistic/source reliability).

## Group 4 — Source and information reliability

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a source of information — a person, institution, document, or dataset — can be trusted to provide accurate information. They appear in journalistic, intelligence, and media-literacy contexts, and are distinguished from Group 3 by their focus on information flow rather than formal legal proceedings.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾamin* (אָמִין) — reliable, credible (general)
- *ʾaminut* (אֲמִינוּת) — reliability, credibility
- *ʾaminut meyda* (אֲמִינוּת מֵידָע) — information reliability
- *lo ʾamin* (לֹא אָמִין) — unreliable
- *nirʾe ʾamin* (נִרְאֶה אָמִין) — seems reliable
- *paḥot ʾamin* (פָּחוֹת אָמִין) — less reliable
- *maqor ʾamin* (מָקוֹר אָמִין) — reliable source
- *maqor mehiman* (מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן) — reliable source (more formal)
- *musad mehiman* (מוּסָד מְהֵימָן) — reliable institution
- *mashber ʾaminut* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֲמִינוּת) — credibility crisis
- *mashber ʾemun* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן) — crisis of trust
- *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* (אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר) — public trust
- *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן) — faithful, trustworthy, reliable
- *neʾemanut* (נֶאֱמָנוּת) — loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity
- *lo neʾeman* (לֹא נֶאֱמָן) — untrustworthy

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 3 (legal credibility) at *maqor mehiman* and *ʾaminut*, and with Group 6 (interpersonal trust and loyalty) at *neʾeman* and *neʾemanut*. The distinguishing feature here is the focus on information sources and institutional credibility rather than personal loyalty or formal legal proceedings.

## Group 5 — Personal character evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person's moral character — whether they are honest, sincere, upright, or decent in their conduct and dealings with others. They appear in everyday moral discourse, interpersonal evaluation, and character description, and are applied to persons rather than to statements, documents, or institutions.

**Representative expressions:**
- *yashar* (יָשָׁר) — upright, honest, straightforward
- *yosher* (יֹשֶׁר) — integrity, uprightness
- *yesharut* (יְשָׁרוּת) — uprightness, honesty
- *kanut* (כַּנּוּת) — sincerity, honesty
- *kan* (כָּן) — sincere, honest (adjectival base)
- *bekhannot* (בְּכַנּוּת) — sincerely, honestly
- *mitokh kanut* (מִתּוֹךְ כַּנּוּת) — out of sincerity
- *lemaʿan ha-kanut* (לְמַעַן הַכַּנּוּת) — for the sake of honesty
- *ḥoser kanut* (חֹסֶר כַּנּוּת) — lack of sincerity
- *hagun* (הָגוּן) — decent, proper, respectable
- *hagunot* (הֲגוּנוּת) — decency, propriety
- *lo hagun* (לֹא הָגוּן) — improper, indecent, unfair
- *ʾadam yashar* (אָדָם יָשָׁר) — an upright person
- *shaqran* (שַׁקְרָן) — liar, habitual liar
- *shaqranit* (שַׁקְרָנִית) — liar (feminine)
- *shaqranut* (שַׁקְרָנוּת) — habitual lying, mendacity
- *dover ʾemet* (דּוֹבֵר אֱמֶת) — one who speaks the truth (moral characterization)
- *dover sheqer* (דּוֹבֵר שֶׁקֶר) — one who speaks falsehood (moral characterization)
- *tsaddiq* (צַדִּיק) — righteous person
- *tsaddiqut* (צַדִּיקוּת) — righteousness as a quality
- *tsaddiq gamur* (צַדִּיק גָּמוּר) — completely righteous person
- *tsaddiq bedoro* (צַדִּיק בְּדוֹרוֹ) — righteous in his generation
- *tsaddiq bi-Sdom* (צַדִּיק בִּסְדוֹם) — a righteous person in Sodom (idiomatic)
- *tsaddiq ve-rashaʿ* (צַדִּיק וְרָשָׁע) — the righteous and the wicked (binary)
- *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן) — faithful, loyal, trustworthy (of persons)
- *neʾeman le-* (נֶאֱמָן לְ-) — faithful to, loyal to

**Notes:** *neʾeman* and *neʾemanut* appear in both this group and Group 4 (source reliability) and Group 6 (interpersonal trust). *hagun* and *lo hagun* are primarily used in everyday interpersonal and social evaluation; *lo hagun* is particularly frequent as a fixed complaint about unfair treatment. *tsaddiq* in Hasidic usage denotes a spiritual leader, which is a distinct usage not fully captured here.

## Group 6 — Interpersonal trust and loyalty

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe the trust, confidence, or loyalty that exists between persons, or between a person and an institution, principle, or commitment. They focus on the relational dimension of reliability — whether someone can be counted on, whether they remain faithful to a person or cause — rather than on the accuracy of information or the correctness of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾemuna* (אֱמוּנָה) — faith, faithfulness, trust
- *ʾemun* (אֱמוּן) — trust, confidence (institutional/relational)
- *neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן) — faithful, loyal, trustworthy
- *neʾemanut* (נֶאֱמָנוּת) — loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity
- *neʾeman le-* (נֶאֱמָן לְ-) — faithful to, loyal to
- *lo neʾeman* (לֹא נֶאֱמָן) — untrustworthy, disloyal
- *heʾemin* / *lehaʾamin* (הֶאֱמִין / לְהַאֲמִין) — to believe, to trust
- *maʾamin* (מַאֲמִין) — believer, one who trusts
- *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* (אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר) — public trust
- *mashber ʾemun* (מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן) — crisis of trust
- *hatshaʾrat ʾemunim* (הַצְהָרַת אֱמוּנִים) — oath of allegiance, declaration of loyalty
- *ʾamana* (אֲמָנָה) — agreement, covenant, compact (grounded in mutual trust)
- *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* (אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה) — truth and faithfulness (fixed phrase)
- *boḥen ʾemunim* (בּוֹחֵן אֱמוּנִים) — tester of loyalties (literary/biblical)
- *ʾemuna ʿiveret* (אֱמוּנָה עִוֶּרֶת) — blind faith (critical usage)
- *ʾemunat shaʾv* (אֱמוּנַת שָׁוְא) — false belief, misplaced trust

**Notes:** *ʾemuna* spans both this group and Group 7 (religious belief), depending on context. *ʾamana* belongs here because its binding force derives from mutual trust, but it also has a formal legal dimension. *ʾemuna ʿiveret* and *ʾemunat shaʾv* are critical evaluations of trust/belief that belong here because they evaluate the quality of the relational commitment rather than the content of a belief.

## Group 7 — Religious belief and faith

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in religious contexts to describe belief in God, adherence to religious doctrine, or the quality of religious commitment. They are distinguished from Group 6 (interpersonal trust) by their specifically theological or liturgical register, though the underlying root is shared.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾemuna* (אֱמוּנָה) — religious faith, belief
- *ʾemuna datit* (אֱמוּנָה דָּתִית) — religious faith
- *maʾamin* (מַאֲמִין) — believer (religious)
- *maʾaminim* (מַאֲמִינִים) — the faithful, believers (plural)
- *lehaʾamin be-* (לְהַאֲמִין בְּ-) — to believe in (God, doctrine)
- *ʾamen* (אָמֵן) — amen (liturgical affirmation)
- *tsiddduq ha-din* (צִדּוּק הַדִּין) — justification of the divine judgment (liturgical)
- *middat ha-tsedeq* (מִדַּת הַצֶּדֶק) — the attribute of justice (theological)
- *tsaddiq yesod ʿolam* (צַדִּיק יְסוֹד עוֹלָם) — the righteous person is the foundation of the world (Hasidic/religious)
- *ḥotam shel ʾemet* (חוֹתָם שֶׁל אֱמֶת) — seal of truth (rabbinic)

**Notes:** *ʾemuna* is the central item shared between this group and Group 6. The distinction is contextual: in Group 6, *ʾemuna* describes relational trust between persons or toward institutions; in this group, it describes specifically religious commitment. *tsiddduq ha-din* and *middat ha-tsedeq* are exclusively theological/liturgical and do not appear in other groups.

## Group 8 — Document and object authenticity

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, artwork, or credential is genuinely what it purports to be — the original rather than a copy, or authentic rather than forged. They appear in legal, commercial, administrative, and cultural contexts where the material genuineness of an object is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ʾotenti* (אוֹתֶנְטִי) — authentic, genuine
- *ʾotentiyyut* (אוֹתֶנְטִיּוּת) — authenticity
- *lo ʾotenti* (לֹא אוֹתֶנְטִי) — inauthentic
- *nirʾe ʾotenti* (נִרְאֶה אוֹתֶנְטִי) — seems authentic
- *ʾotenti leḥallutin* (אוֹתֶנְטִי לְחַלּוּטִין) — completely authentic
- *ḥomer ʾotenti* (חוֹמֶר אוֹתֶנְטִי) — authentic material (historical/documentary)
- *meqori* (מְקוֹרִי) — original, genuine
- *meqoriyyut* (מְקוֹרִיּוּת) — originality, genuineness
- *ʿoteq meqori* (עֹתֶק מְקוֹרִי) — original copy/document
- *ha-maqor ha-meqori* (הַמָּקוֹר הַמְּקוֹרִי) — the original source
- *lo meqori* (לֹא מְקוֹרִי) — not original, derivative
- *mezuyaf* (מְזוּיָּף) — forged, falsified, counterfeit
- *ziyyuf* (זִיּוּף) — forgery, falsification
- *lezayyef* (לְזַיֵּף) — to forge, to falsify
- *mezayyef* (מְזַיֵּף) — forger, counterfeiter
- *ziyyuf mismakhim* (זִיּוּף מִסְמָכִים) — document forgery
- *ziyyuf ḥatima* (זִיּוּף חֲתִימָה) — signature forgery
- *mismakh mezuyaf* (מִסְמָךְ מְזוּיָּף) — forged document
- *kesef mezuyaf* (כֶּסֶף מְזוּיָּף) — counterfeit money
- *teʿuda mezuyyefet* (תְּעוּדָה מְזוּיֶּפֶת) — forged certificate
- *ḥatima mezuyyefet* (חֲתִימָה מְזוּיֶּפֶת) — forged signature
- *nimtsa mezuyaf* (נִמְצָא מְזוּיָּף) — found to be forged
- *ʿaverat ziyyuf* (עֲבֵרַת זִיּוּף) — the offense of forgery
- *ḥashud beziyyuf* (חָשׁוּד בְּזִיּוּף) — suspected of forgery
- *leʾammet* (לְאַמֵּת) — to verify, to authenticate
- *immut* (אִמּוּת) — verification, authentication
- *meʾummat* (מְאֻמָּת) — verified, authenticated
- *immut zehut* (אִמּוּת זֶהוּת) — identity verification

**Notes:** *ʾotenti* and *meqori* overlap in this group but with a distinction visible in usage: *ʾotenti* emphasizes genuineness (not an imitation), while *meqori* emphasizes origin (the original rather than a copy). *mezuyaf* is the direct antonym of both. *leʾammet* and *immut* belong here because their primary modern use is the procedural verification of documents and identities, though they derive from the root א-מ-ת (truth).

## Group 9 — Precision and exactness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate the degree of correspondence between a statement, measurement, or representation and the reality it describes, with emphasis on exactness rather than mere correctness. They appear in scientific, technical, journalistic, and legal contexts where the degree of precision matters, not just whether something is right or wrong.

**Representative expressions:**
- *meduyaq* (מְדוּיָּק) — precise, exact, accurate
- *diyyuq* (דִּיּוּק) — precision, exactness
- *ledayyeq* (לְדַיֵּק) — to be precise, to specify exactly
- *medayyeq* (מְדַיֵּק) — precise person; being precise
- *ʾi-diyyuq* (אִי-דִּיּוּק) — imprecision, inaccuracy
- *bediyyuq* (בְּדִיּוּק) — exactly, precisely (adverb)
- *ḥoser diyyuq* (חֹסֶר דִּיּוּק) — lack of precision
- *lo meduyaq* (לֹא מְדוּיָּק) — not precise, inaccurate
- *meduyaq beyoter* (מְדוּיָּק בְּיוֹתֵר) — most precise
- *meduyaq yoter* (מְדוּיָּק יוֹתֵר) — more precise
- *lihyot meduyaq* (לִהְיוֹת מְדוּיָּק) — to be precise (parenthetical)
- *nekhonot* (נְכוֹנוּת) — correctness, accuracy (abstract)

**Notes:** *nekhonot* is placed here rather than in Group 1 because as an abstract noun it tends to appear in more formal evaluative contexts where the degree of accuracy is being assessed, rather than in simple conversational confirmation. *bediyyuq* overlaps with Group 1 as an everyday intensifier but belongs primarily here when used to specify exact correspondence.

## Group 10 — Legal validity and formal status

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a document, contract, license, decision, or action has the formal legal status required for it to be operative or recognized. They appear in legal and administrative contexts and are concerned with formal validity rather than moral rightness or factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- *taqef* (תָּקֵף) — valid, in force
- *toqef* (תֹּקֶף) — validity, legal force
- *lo taqef* (לֹא תָּקֵף) — invalid, expired
- *letaqqef* (לְתַקֵּף) — to validate, to ratify
- *ʾi-teqifut* (אִי-תְּקִיפוּת) — invalidity, lack of legal force
- *ḥuqi* (חוּקִי) — legal, lawful
- *ḥuqiyyut* (חוּקִיּוּת) — legality, lawfulness
- *ʾi-ḥuqiyyut* (אִי-חוּקִיּוּת) — illegality
- *bilti ḥuqi* (בִּלְתִּי חוּקִי) — illegal, unlawful
- *legitimi* (לֶגִיטִימִי) — legitimate, valid, justified
- *legitimiyyut* (לֶגִיטִימִיּוּת) — legitimacy
- *ʾi-legitimiyyut* (אִי-לֶגִיטִימִיּוּת) — illegitimacy
- *bilti legitimi* (בִּלְתִּי לֶגִיטִימִי) — illegitimate
- *taqin* (תָּקִין) — proper, in order, functioning correctly
- *teqinut* (תְּקִינוּת) — proper functioning, regularity
- *ʾi-teqinut* (אִי-תְּקִינוּת) — improper functioning, irregularity
- *lo taqin* (לֹא תָּקִין) — not in order, not functioning properly
- *kasher* (כָּשֵׁר) — fit, valid, above suspicion (formal/legal use)
- *kashrut* (כַּשְׁרוּת) — fitness, validity (formal/legal use)
- *lehakhshir* (לְהַכְשִׁיר) — to qualify, to certify as fit, to authorize
- *mukhshar* (מֻכְשָׁר) — qualified, certified, fit
- *pasul* (פָּסוּל) — disqualified, invalid, inadmissible
- *pesulut* (פְּסוּלוּת) — invalidity, disqualification
- *lifsol* (לִפְסֹל) — to disqualify, to invalidate
- *haʾamana* (הַאֲמָנָה) — accreditation, authorization
- *kitvey haʾamana* (כִּתְבֵי הַאֲמָנָה) — letters of accreditation

**Notes:** *kasher* and *pasul* form a direct antonym pair in this group and also in Group 3 (legal credibility). *taqin* is distinguished from *taqef* in usage: *taqef* evaluates whether something is currently in legal force, while *taqin* evaluates whether something is functioning or structured as it should be. *legitimi* and *ḥuqi* overlap in meaning but differ in register and origin: *ḥuqi* is specifically about conformity to law, while *legitimi* can extend to political and moral justification beyond strict legality.

## Group 11 — Justice and rightness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether conduct, judgments, social arrangements, or outcomes conform to standards of rightness — moral, legal, or social. They appear in moral, legal, religious, and political discourse and are concerned with whether things are as they should be according to a normative standard, rather than whether they are factually accurate or formally valid.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tsedeq* (צֶדֶק) — justice, righteousness
- *tsedaqa* (צְדָקָה) — righteousness; charity (in modern use)
- *tsedaqa u-mishpat* (צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט) — righteousness and justice (biblical)
- *tsedeq ḥevrati* (צֶדֶק חֶבְרָתִי) — social justice
- *tsedeq metaqen* (צֶדֶק מְתַקֵּן) — restorative/corrective justice
- *tsedeq meḥalleq* (צֶדֶק מְחַלֵּק) — distributive justice
- *tsedeq maʿavar* (צֶדֶק מַעֲבָר) — transitional justice
- *tsedeq ʿiver* (צֶדֶק עִוֵּר) — blind justice
- *mishpat tsedeq* (מִשְׁפַּט צֶדֶק) — just judgment, fair trial
- *ʿasiyyat tsedeq* (עֲשִׂיַּת צֶדֶק) — doing justice
- *laʿasot tsedeq* (לַעֲשׂוֹת צֶדֶק) — to do justice
- *lidrosh tsedeq* (לִדְרֹשׁ צֶדֶק) — to demand justice
- *eyn tsedeq* (אֵין צֶדֶק) — there is no justice
- *bet mishpat shel tsedeq* (בֵּית מִשְׁפַּט שֶׁל צֶדֶק) — court of justice
- *mutsdddaq* (מוּצְדָּק) — justified, warranted
- *lo mutsdddaq* (לֹא מוּצְדָּק) — unjustified, unwarranted
- *lehatsddiq* (לְהַצְדִּיק) — to justify, to vindicate
- *hatsdaqa* (הַצְדָּקָה) — justification, vindication
- *hatsdaqa ʿatsmit* (הַצְדָּקָה עַצְמִית) — self-justification
- *tsiddduq* (צִדּוּק) — justification, vindication
- *litsddoq* (לִצְדֹּק) — to be right (in a claim or argument)
- *yosher* (יֹשֶׁר) — integrity, fairness (in legal equity contexts)
- *hagun* (הָגוּן) — decent, fair, proper
- *lo hagun* (לֹא הָגוּן) — unfair, improper

**Notes:** *litsddoq* in its everyday colloquial use ("you are right") belongs primarily to Group 1, but in its moral/legal sense of being justified it belongs here. *yosher* appears in both Group 5 (personal character) and here, because in legal Hebrew it functions as a principle of equity or good faith. *hagun* and *lo hagun* appear in both Group 5 and here; *lo hagun* in particular is used both to describe a person's character and to evaluate the fairness of a situation or outcome.

## Group 12 — Falsehood and deception

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize statements, persons, or acts as false, deceptive, or dishonest. They define the negative pole against which truth, reliability, and authenticity are measured. They appear across moral, legal, journalistic, and everyday registers.

**Representative expressions:**
- *sheqer* (שֶׁקֶר) — falsehood, lie
- *leshaqer* (לְשַׁקֵּר) — to lie
- *shaqran* (שַׁקְרָן) — liar
- *shaqranit* (שַׁקְרָנִית) — liar (feminine)
- *shaqranut* (שַׁקְרָנוּת) — habitual lying, mendacity
- *shiqri* (שִׁקְרִי) — false, mendacious (adjective)
- *ze sheqer* (זֶה שֶׁקֶר) — that is a lie
- *sheqer muḥlat* (שֶׁקֶר מוּחְלָט) — outright lie
- *sheqer lavan* (שֶׁקֶר לָבָן) — white lie
- *sheqer va-khazav* (שֶׁקֶר וָכָזָב) — lies and falsehood (rhetorical binomial)
- *ʿolam ha-sheqer* (עוֹלַם הַשֶּׁקֶר) — the world of falsehood
- *lehappits sheqer* (לְהַפִּיץ שֶׁקֶר) — to spread lies
- *dover sheqer* (דּוֹבֵר שֶׁקֶר) — one who speaks falsehood
- *ʿed sheqer* (עֵד שֶׁקֶר) — false witness
- *midvar sheqer tirḥaq* (מִדְבַּר שֶׁקֶר תִּרְחָק) — distance yourself from falsehood
- *ziyyuf* (זִיּוּף) — forgery, falsification
- *mezuyaf* (מְזוּיָּף) — forged, counterfeit
- *ziyyuf histori* (זִיּוּף הִיסְטוֹרִי) — historical falsification
- *ʿiyyut ha-ʾemet* (עִיּוּת הָאֱמֶת) — distortion of the truth
- *leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet* (לְעַוֵּת אֶת הָאֱמֶת) — to distort the truth
- *lehastir et ha-ʾemet* (לְהַסְתִּיר אֶת הָאֱמֶת) — to hide the truth
- *ḥatsi ʾemet* (חֲצִי אֱמֶת) — half-truth
- *ʾemuna ʿiveret* (אֱמוּנָה עִוֶּרֶת) — blind faith (as a form of epistemic failure)
- *ʾemunat shaʾv* (אֱמוּנַת שָׁוְא) — false belief, misplaced trust

**Notes:** *ziyyuf* and *mezuyaf* appear in both this group and Group 8 (document authenticity); they are placed here as well because they characterize an act or product as deliberately false, not merely as non-original. *ḥatsi ʾemet* is placed here because its primary evaluative force is negative — it describes a form of deception through selective truth-telling. *ʾemuna ʿiveret* and *ʾemunat shaʾv* are placed here as evaluations of epistemic failure, though they derive from the trust/faith vocabulary of Group 6.

## Group 13 — Essence and genuine identity

**Definition:** These expressions are used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday discourse to distinguish what something genuinely or fundamentally is from what it merely appears to be, or to emphasize the intrinsic nature of a thing or person. They appear when the question is not whether a statement is accurate but whether something is real, genuine, or truly itself.

**Representative expressions:**
- *mahut* (מַהוּת) — essence, nature
- *mahuti* (מַהוּתִי) — essential, substantive
- *mahutiyyut* (מַהוּתִיּוּת) — essentiality; essentialism
- *ʿetsem* (עֶצֶם) — essence, the very thing itself
- *ʿatsmi* (עַצְמִי) — self-, intrinsic
- *ʿatsmiyyut* (עַצְמִיּוּת) — selfhood, individuality, autonomy
- *ʾamiti* (אֲמִתִּי) — true, real, genuine (in ontological sense)
- *ha-sippur ha-ʾamiti* (הַסִּיפּוּר הָאֲמִתִּי) — the real story
- *ha-shem ha-ʾamiti* (הַשֵּׁם הָאֲמִתִּי) — the real name
- *sibba ʾamitit* (סִיבָּה אֲמִתִּית) — the real reason
- *ḥaver ʾamiti* (חָבֵר אֲמִתִּי) — a true friend
- *ʾahava ʾamitit* (אַהֲבָה אֲמִתִּית) — true love
- *beʾofen ʾamiti* (בְּאֹפֶן אֲמִתִּי) — in a genuine way
- *lo ʾamiti* (לֹא אֲמִתִּי) — not genuine, fake
- *ʾotenti* (אוֹתֶנְטִי) — authentic, genuine (in identity/cultural sense)
- *ʾotentiyyut* (אוֹתֶנְטִיּוּת) — authenticity (as a quality of identity)

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 8 (document authenticity) at *ʾotenti* and *ʾotentiyyut*, but the usage pattern here is different: in Group 8, authenticity is evaluated against a standard of material genuineness (not forged); in this group, it is evaluated against a standard of genuine identity or essential nature (truly itself, not performed or derivative). *ʾamiti* appears in both Group 2 (moral truth) and here; the distinction is that in Group 2 it carries normative weight, while here it is used to distinguish genuine from apparent identity.

## Group 14 — Resemblance and similarity

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate the degree of resemblance between two things — whether a copy resembles an original, whether two cases are analogous, or whether something imitates something else. They appear in legal analogy, aesthetic evaluation, and everyday comparison.

**Representative expressions:**
- *dome* (דּוֹמֶה) — similar, resembling
- *dimyon* (דִּמְיוֹן) — resemblance, similarity
- *lehiddamot* (לְהִדָּמוֹת) — to resemble, to be similar to
- *damuy* (דָּמוּי) — resembling, simulated, imitation
- *meqori* (מְקוֹרִי) — original (implicitly contrasted with imitation)
- *ḥoser meqoriyyut* (חֹסֶר מְקוֹרִיּוּת) — lack of originality

**Notes:** This is a smaller group. *meqori* and *ḥoser meqoriyyut* are included here because their primary evaluative function in aesthetic and intellectual contexts is to distinguish the original from the derivative — a contrast that presupposes a resemblance relationship. *damuy* is notable because it describes something that resembles but is not identical to the original, and is used in technical contexts (imitation leather, humanoid) as well as evaluative ones.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items appear in multiple groups and cannot be cleanly assigned to one:

- ***ʾemet* (אֱמֶת):** Appears in Groups 1, 2, 3, and 12. Its usage spans everyday correctness confirmation, philosophical/moral truth, legal testimony, and the definition of falsehood's negative pole. It is the most semantically distributed item in the set.

- ***neʾeman* (נֶאֱמָן):** Appears in Groups 4, 5, and 6. Used for source reliability (journalistic), personal character (honest, loyal), and interpersonal/institutional trust. The context determines which dimension is primary.

- ***ʾemuna* (אֱמוּנָה):** Appears in Groups 6 and 7. In secular contexts it describes relational trust and confidence; in religious contexts it describes faith in God or religious doctrine. The word is the same; the register determines the group.

- ***kasher* (כָּשֵׁר) / *pasul* (פָּסוּל):** Appear in Groups 3 and 10. In Group 3 they evaluate the admissibility of testimony; in Group 10 they evaluate the formal validity of documents, actions, or persons in a broader legal/administrative sense.

- ***yosher* (יֹשֶׁר):** Appears in Groups 2, 5, and 11. As a moral quality of persons it belongs to Groups 2 and 5; as a legal principle of equity or good faith it belongs to Group 11.

- ***hagun* / *lo hagun* (הָגוּן / לֹא הָגוּן):** Appear in Groups 5 and 11. *hagun* evaluates personal decency (Group 5); *lo hagun* is frequently used to evaluate the fairness of a situation or outcome (Group 11), not only a person's character.

- ***legitimi* (לֶגִיטִימִי):** Appears in Groups 10 and 11. When it evaluates formal legal status it belongs to Group 10; when it evaluates political or moral justification it belongs to Group 11.

- ***litsddoq* / *atta tsodeq* (לִצְדֹּק / אַתָּה צוֹדֵק):** In everyday colloquial use these belong to Group 1 (correctness confirmation); in moral or legal argumentation they belong to Group 11 (justice and rightness).

- ***ʾotenti* (אוֹתֶנְטִי):** Appears in Groups 8 and 13. In Group 8 it evaluates material genuineness (not forged); in Group 13 it evaluates genuine identity or cultural authenticity.

- ***ziyyuf* / *mezuyaf* (זִיּוּף / מְזוּיָּף):** Appear in Groups 8 and 12. In Group 8 they describe the material condition of a forged object; in Group 12 they characterize an act of deliberate deception.

## Items that do not fit cleanly into any group

- ***ʾomen* / *ʾomenet* (אוֹמֵן / אוֹמֶנֶת):** These terms (trainer, foster parent, guardian) derive from root א-מ-נ but their primary usage is in contexts of upbringing and training, not in any of the truth/reliability/justice domains documented above. They are noted in P1b as a peripheral extension of the root. No group in this analysis captures their usage pattern.

- ***ʾemun* (אֵמוּן) in the sense of training/apprenticeship:** The homophonous *ʾemun* meaning training or upbringing (distinct from *ʾemun* meaning trust) similarly falls outside all groups here.

- ***hakhshara* (הַכְשָׁרָה):** In its primary modern use (professional training, vocational preparation), this term has moved away from the fitness/validity sense of *kasher* and does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

- ***yishshur* (יִשּׁוּר):** In its primary modern administrative use (approval, authorization), this term has shifted from the straightening/rectifying sense of *yashar* and sits at the edge of Group 10 (legal validity) without fully belonging there.

- ***tsedaqa* (צְדָקָה) in its modern sense of charity:** In modern Hebrew, *tsedaqa* primarily means charitable giving. This usage does not fit into Group 11 (justice and rightness) as it is currently used, though its etymological connection to *tsedeq* is clear. It is noted here as an item whose modern primary usage falls outside the groups documented.

- ***dimyon* (דִּמְיוֹן) in the sense of imagination:** The imagination sense of *dimyon* (distinct from its resemblance sense) falls outside all groups here. Only the resemblance sense is captured in Group 14.
