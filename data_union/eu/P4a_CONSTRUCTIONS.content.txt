# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Predicative: NP/NOUN/ADJECTIVE + copula *da* (3sg of *izan*)
The truth-domain adjective or nominal appears as predicate complement with the copula *da*; the copula is obligatory and the truth-term does not inflect for the subject. Word order is flexible but predicate-before-copula is canonical.

### Examples
- *Egia da* → N/A → "It is true."
- *Gezurra da* → N/A → "It is a lie / It is false."
- *Egia da hori.* → Egia da hori. → "That is true."
- *Egiazkoa da agiri hori.* → Egiazkoa da agiri hori. → "That document is genuine."
- *Zilegi da* → N/A → "It is lawful / It is permissible"

### Notes
Basque predicative attribution uses *da* (existential/equative copula *izan*) for inherent properties; stative *egon* is used for contingent states (see §4 below). The predicate nominal or adjective appears without case marking in this position.

The "subject" NP (the proposition being evaluated) is typically implicit or expressed as a clause. *Egia da* and *gezurra da* function as the primary binary poles of propositional truth attribution in everyday speech.

Both nominal (*egia*) and adjectival (*egiazkoa*, *benetakoa*, *baliozkoa*, *sinesgarria*, *zuzena*) bases participate in this pattern without structural difference.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negation particle *ez* precedes the finite verb: NOUN/ADJECTIVE + *ez* + *da* + truth-term (or *ez da* + truth-term).
Alternatively, *ez-* prefixes the adjective itself to form a lexicalized negative: *ez-* + ADJECTIVE.

### Examples
- *Ez da egia* → N/A → "It is not true."
- *Ez da egia hori.* → Ez da egia hori. → "That is not true."
- *Ez da sinesgarria.* → Ez da sinesgarria. → "It is not credible."
- *Ziurra ez da* → N/A → "It is not certain." (also: *ziurra ez* as reduced form)
- *Ez-fidagarria da* → N/A → "It is unreliable / It is not trustworthy"

### Notes
*Ez* immediately precedes the finite verb (*da*); the truth-term remains in predicate position. This is the standard negation pattern for all copular attributions in Basque and applies uniformly across the truth-domain vocabulary.

Two distinct strategies are attested: syntactic negation (*ez da*) is productive and applies to any predicate; lexical negation (*ez-* prefix) is more restricted and yields semi-lexicalized forms such as *ez-fidagarria*, *ez-sinesgarria*, *ez-zilegia*. Both strategies are documented in P2a.

The construction *oker ez banago* ("if I am not mistaken") shows the same preverbal negation pattern in a conditional frame, functioning as an epistemic hedge.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Attributive modifier precedes the noun in its indefinite form (article *-a* dropped): [truth-term-*zko* / truth-term-*ko* / truth-term-*ezko* / truth-term-*tako*] + [Noun]
The suffixes *-zko*, *-ko*, *-ezko*, and *-tako* derive relational adjectives from nominals; the resulting form is invariant before the head noun.

### Examples
- *Egiazko lekukotasuna* → Egiazko lekukotasuna → "True / genuine testimony"
- *Gezurrezko agiria* → N/A → "A false / forged document."
- *Benetako arazoa* → Benetako arazoa → "The real problem"
- *Egiazko informazioa* → N/A → "True information / Genuine information"
- *Gezurrezko lekukotasuna* → N/A → "False testimony / Perjury"

### Notes
Basque is consistently head-final in nominal modification: the modifier precedes the noun. The indefinite attributive form (e.g., *egiazko*, *gezurrezko*, *benetako*, *ustezko*, *jatorrizko*, *itxurazko*, *baliozko*) is obligatory in prenominal position; the definite form (*egiazkoa*, *gezurrezkoa*) appears only in predicative or standalone nominal positions. This is a systematic morphological alternation, not lexical suppletion.

The definite article *-a* attaches to the head noun, not to the modifier. Relational adjectives (*egiazko*, *gezurrezko*, *benetako*, *itxurazko*) are the primary adnominal modifiers in the truth domain.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Root adjective/noun + *-tu* verbal suffix, used intransitively; or periphrastic verb *bihurtu* ("to become, to turn into") with a truth-domain nominal or adjectival as complement: [Subject] + [truth-term] + *bihurtu da*. The resulting state is expressed by ADJECTIVE + *egon*.

### Examples
- *Zintzotu* → N/A → "To become honest; to reform."
- *Okertu* → N/A → "To go wrong; to become distorted; to err."
- *Egia bihurtu da.* → Egia bihurtu da. → "It has become true / It turned out to be true."
- *Okertu egin da gauza hori.* → Okertu egin da gauza hori. → "That thing has gone wrong."
- *Gauzak okertu dira* → N/A → "Things have gone wrong / Things have become crooked"
- *Oker nago* → N/A → "I am wrong / I am mistaken" (resultant state with *egon*)

### Notes
The suffix *-tu* on an adjectival base yields a verb that can be read as inchoative ("to become X") or causative ("to make X") depending on argument structure (see §5). Intransitive use (*zintzotu da*, "he/she has become honest") yields the inchoative reading; transitive use (*zintzotu du*, "he/she has made [someone] honest") yields the causative. Context and argument structure disambiguate.

The periphrastic *bihurtu* construction is the general inchoative strategy and applies broadly. The suffixed *-tu* inchoative is lexically restricted: *okertu* (to go wrong, become crooked) is well-attested; *zilegitu* (to become legitimate) is marginal. No *-tu* inchoative from *egia* or *gezurra* is attested.

The *egon* construction marks the resultant state as contingent and reversible, contrasting with *izan* for inherent properties.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
The same *-tu* verbal suffix used for inchoatives (§4) also yields causative/transitive readings when a causee argument is present. The suffix *-arazi* attaches to the verbal stem to form a productive morphological causative: [Verb-stem] + *-arazi*. Periphrastic causatives use *eragin* or *behartu* with an infinitival complement.

### Examples
- *Gezurra sinetsarazi* → N/A → "To make someone believe a lie; to get someone to believe a falsehood."
- *Egiaztatu* → N/A → "To cause to be verified; to have [something] confirmed." (transitive, institutionally causative in use)
- *Egiaztarazi zion.* → Egiaztarazi zion. → "He/she made him/her verify it / caused it to be verified."
- *Egia esan eragin zion.* → Egia esan eragin zion. → "He/she made him/her tell the truth."
- *Akatsak zuzendu* → N/A → "To correct the errors" (causative: agent acts on object)
- *Epaia baliogabetu* → N/A → "To nullify the judgment" (causative: agent causes invalidity)

### Notes
The productive causative morpheme is *-arazi* (e.g., *sinets-arazi*, "to cause to believe"). The *-tu* verbs (*egiaztatu*, *ziurtatu*, *zuzendu*) are transitive and carry an implicit causative meaning ("to make true/certain/correct") but are not formed with *-arazi*. Both patterns are attested in the truth domain.

Morphological *-arazi* causatives are grammatically productive in Basque but are relatively low-frequency for truth-domain verbs in the data; periphrastic causatives with *eragin* or *behartu* + infinitive are more commonly attested in this domain. No causative from nominal bases (*egia*, *gezurra*) via *-arazi* is documented in the data.

The *-tu* verbs (*zuzendu*, *okertu*, *usteldu*, *baliogabetu*, *egokitu*) are polyfunctional: transitive use with an explicit object yields causative meaning; intransitive use yields inchoative meaning. The distinction is syntactic, not morphological.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Adjective/verb stem + *-tasun* (abstract nominal suffix) + *-a* (definite article), yielding nouns denoting the quality or state expressed by the base.
A secondary pattern uses *-pen* on verbal stems to yield result nominals; a further pattern uses *-keta* for action/result nominals.

### Examples
- *Ziurtasun* → N/A → "Certainty; sureness; assurance."
- *Zintzotasun* → N/A → "Honesty; uprightness; sincerity."
- *Zintzotasuna* → Zintzotasuna → "Honesty, sincerity, integrity"
- *Fidagarritasuna* → Fidagarritasuna → "Reliability, trustworthiness"
- *Egiatasuna* → N/A → "Truthfulness / The quality of being true"
- *Egiaztapen* → N/A → "Verification / Attestation (as result or document)"

### Notes
The suffix *-tasun* is the primary and highly productive abstract nominalizer in Basque. It attaches to adjectives (*ziur*, *zintzo*, *garden*, *zuzen*, *fidagarri*, *sinesgarri*) to yield abstract nouns. The *-tasun* suffix applies uniformly to all adjectival bases in the data: *egiazkotasuna*, *benetakotasuna*, *faltsutasuna*, *zuzentasuna*, *zehaztasuna*, *sinesgarritasuna*, *jatortasuna*, *antzekotasuna*, *baliogabetasuna*, *gezurtasuna*, *legitimotasuna*, *zilegitasuna*, *autentikotasuna*, *leialatasuna*.

A secondary pattern, the *-pen* suffix, derives result-oriented nominals from verbal stems (*egiaztapen*, *frogapen*, *zehaztapen*), denoting the result or outcome rather than the property.

The *-tze* suffix (see §8) denotes the ongoing process or act, while *-pen* nominals denote the result or outcome. A further pattern uses *-keta* for action/result nominals (as in *zuzenketa*). All three nominalization types are attested in this domain and are not interchangeable.

These nominals are terminal in derivational chains: no further suffixal derivation from *-tasun* forms has been identified.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Three productive patterns are attested:
**(i)** Verbal stem + *-tzaile* (agentive suffix for professional/role agents) + *-a* (definite)
**(ii)** Root + *-gile* (maker/doer agent suffix) + *-a* (definite)
**(iii)** Nominal root + *-ti* (characterizing/habitual suffix marking habitual tendency) + *-a* (definite)

### Examples
- *Zuzentzaile* → N/A → "Corrector; editor; proofreader." (*zuzendu* + *-tzaile*)
- *Gezurti* → N/A → "Liar; lying person." (*gezur* + *-ti*)
- *Gezurtia* → Gezurtia → "The liar" (habitual liar; from *gezur-* + *-ti* + *-a*)
- *Iruzurgilea* → N/A → "Fraudster / Swindler" (*iruzur* + *-gile* + *-a*)
- *Egia esaten duena* → Egia esaten duena → "The one who tells the truth" (periphrastic; no single-word form attested)

### Notes
Three distinct agentive patterns are attested in this domain: (i) *-tzaile* is the productive agentive suffix for professional/role actors (*zuzentzaile*, "one who corrects"); (ii) *-gile* for maker/doer agents (*iruzurgilea*, "fraudster"; *itxuragilea*, "pretender/impostor"); (iii) *-ti* is a characterizing suffix marking habitual tendency, used specifically for moral character evaluation (*gezurti*, "one given to lying"). The *-ti* type implies a stable character trait rather than a role or act, and is the primary pattern for moral character evaluation in this domain.

The *-ti* agentive is asymmetric in this domain: *gezurti* (liar) is a fully lexicalized common word, but no corresponding *egiazkoti* or *egiati* (truth-teller) is attested. The truth-telling agent is expressed periphrastically via relative clauses (*egia esaten duena*) or by character adjectives (*zintzoa*, *jatorra*) rather than by a dedicated agentive nominal.

No *-tzaile* form meaning "truth-teller" (*egiazale* or similar) has been identified as broadly attested; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*egia esaten duena*, "one who tells the truth"). *Zuzendari* (director) uses the variant agentive *-dari* but has shifted to an organizational rather than correctional meaning.

A further characterological pattern uses *-ti* for *iruzurti* ("habitually deceptive").

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Verbal stem + *-tze* (process/action nominalization suffix) + optional case suffixes or definite *-a*.

### Examples
- *Egiaztatzea* → Egiaztatzea → "The verifying, verification (as process)"
- *Frogatzea* → Frogatzea → "The proving, demonstration (as process)"
- *Egiaztatze-prozesua* → N/A → "The verification process"
- *Egokitze* → N/A → "Adaptation / The process of adapting"

### Notes
The *-tze* suffix nominalizes a verbal stem to yield a process nominal (the ongoing activity), contrasting with *-pen* (result nominal, §6) and *-keta* (action/result nominal, as in *zuzenketa*). All three nominalization types are attested in this domain and are not interchangeable: *egiaztatze* (process), *egiaztapen* (result/document), *egiaztagiri* (the physical certificate).

Verbal nouns in *-tze* function as syntactic subjects and objects freely (*frogatzea zaila da*, "proving it is difficult"; *frogatzeko zama*, "burden of proof").

## 9. PRIVATIVE / NEGATED-PROPERTY CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Two competing strategies are attested:
**(a)** Suffix *-gabe* attaches to a nominal root: [Root] + *-gabe* + *-a* → property-lacking nominal
**(b)** *ezin* (impossibility marker) precedes the verbal stem: [Verb-stem] + *ezin* + *-a* → "impossible to [verb]"

### Examples
- *Baliogabea* → Baliogabea → "Invalid, null, without value" (*balio* + *-gabe* + *-a*)
- *Frogaezina* → Frogaezina → "Unprovable, impossible to prove" (*froga* + *ezin* + *-a*)

### Notes
The *-gabe* privative is productive for nominal roots (*baliogabea*, *egigabea*) and for verbal stems (*zehaztugabea*, *egiaztatu gabe*). The *ezin* construction is specific to verbal roots and expresses impossibility rather than mere absence (*egiaztaezina*, *frogaezina*). The antonym of *-garri* adjectivals (§10 below) is preferentially formed with *-gaitz* rather than *-gabe* (*fidagaitza*, *sineskaitza*).

---

## 10. POTENTIAL / WORTHY-OF ADJECTIVAL

### Pattern
Suffix *-garri* attaches to a verbal stem to form an adjectival meaning "worthy of" or "capable of being [verbed]": [Verb-stem] + *-garri* + *-a*
The negative counterpart uses *-gaitz* (difficult, resistant): [Verb-stem] + *-gaitz* + *-a*

### Examples
- *Frogagarria* → Frogagarria → "Provable, demonstrable"
- *Fidagaitza* → Fidagaitza → "Unreliable, hard to trust"

### Notes
The *-garri* / *-gaitz* pair is productive across the epistemic sub-domain: *egiaztagarria* (verifiable) / *egiaztaezina* (unverifiable, via *ezin*); *frogagarria* / *frogaezina*; *fidagarria* / *fidagaitza*; *sinesgarria* / *sineskaitza*. The negative of *-garri* forms uses *-gaitz* (or *-kaitz*) rather than *-gabe*, a pattern consistent across all attested examples in the data.

## 11. TRANSLATIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Participial/adjectival form + *-tzat* + evaluative verb (*jo*, *hartu*, *onartu*)
The suffix *-tzat* marks the translative case ("as X, for X"); combined with verbs of considering or accepting, it expresses treating something as having a given status.

### Examples
- *Egiazkotzat jo* → N/A → "To consider as true; to take as genuine."
- *Baliozkotzat jo* → N/A → "To consider as valid; to regard as legitimate."

### Notes
This construction is productive across the truth domain and is particularly common in legal and administrative registers where formal attribution of a status (true, valid, false) is required without necessarily asserting it as fact. It is structurally distinct from simple predication (*egia da*) in that it marks the attribution as a judgment or institutional act rather than a direct assertion.

## 12. EPISTEMIC HEDGE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Two sub-patterns are attested:
**(a)** Parenthetical adverb or nominal phrase functioning as a sentence-level epistemic modifier: *ziurrenik*, *antza denez*, *antza du*, and discourse-initial frames.
**(b)** Base nominal + instrumental suffix *-z* → adverbial hedge: *itxuraz* (appearance-based hedge), *ustez* (belief-based hedge).

### Examples
- *Ziurrenik etorriko da* → N/A → "He/she will probably come; most likely he/she will come."
- *Antza denez, egia da* → N/A → "Apparently it is true; it would seem to be true."
- *Itxuraz ondo dago* → N/A → "Apparently it is fine / It seems to be fine"
- *Ustez erruduna da* → N/A → "Supposedly he/she is guilty / Allegedly guilty"

### Notes
Basque expresses epistemic hedging through adverbs (*ziurrenik*, "probably/certainly") and parenthetical nominal constructions (*antza denez*, "as it appears"; *antza du*, "it seems"). These are not inflectional mood distinctions but lexical devices. *Ziurrenik* is notable for being derived from *ziur* ("certain") yet functioning in practice as a probability marker rather than a certainty marker.

Both *itxuraz* and *ustez* are formed by the instrumental *-z* suffix on their base nominals (*itxura*, "appearance"; *uste*, "belief/supposition"). They function as sentence-level epistemic hedges, marking the speaker's reduced commitment to the proposition. A parallel attributive form exists for each: *itxurazko* and *ustezko* (adnominal, §3). This is a productive pattern: the instrumental *-z* on epistemic nominals yields adverbial hedges.

## 13. PEJORATIVE ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Noun or adjective stem + *-keria* (nominal suffix marking reprehensible habitual practice).

### Examples
- *Gezurkeria* → N/A → "Lying; deceitfulness; dishonesty as a habitual practice."
- *Itxurakeria* → N/A → "Pretense; affectation; hypocrisy; keeping up appearances."
- *Ustelkeria* → N/A → "Corruption / Moral decay" (*ustel* + *-keria*)

### Notes
The suffix *-keria* consistently marks a habitual or systematic moral failing across the truth domain: *gezurkeria* (habitual lying), *faltsukeria* (habitual insincerity), *okerkeria* (habitual wrongdoing), *itxurakeria* (habitual pretense), *ustelkeria* (corruption/moral decay), *bidegabekeria*, *ustekeria*, *egiazkeria*.

The *-keria* suffix is a distinct nominalizer from *-tasun* (§6): where *-tasun* yields a neutral abstract property nominal, *-keria* yields a pejorative nominal implying a pattern of blameworthy conduct. This suffix carries an inherently pejorative and behavioral connotation and is distinct from *-tasun* (abstract quality). This pattern is productive and semantically consistent across all instances.

## 14. LIGHT-VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *EGIN* AND *ESAN*

### Pattern
Truth-domain nominal + light verb *egin* ("to do/make") or *esan* ("to say/tell").

### Examples
- *Gezurra esan* → N/A → "To tell a lie" (*gezurra* + *esan*)
- *Iruzurra egin* → N/A → "To commit fraud / To cheat" (*iruzurra* + *egin*)

### Notes
Light-verb constructions with *esan* are used for speech-act expressions (*egia esan*, *gezurra esan*, *zintzoki esan*); those with *egin* are used for action expressions (*iruzurra egin*, *itxura egin*). This is the primary verbal strategy for truth-domain nominals that lack a dedicated *-tu* verb. The nominal bears absolutive case and the light verb carries all tense/agreement morphology.
