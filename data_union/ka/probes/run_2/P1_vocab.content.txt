# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgia (2002), as used in Georgian passports and official romanization, supplemented by standard academic convention for Georgian studies. Applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine quality) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and propositions, as well as genuineness of persons or things. Appears in philosophical, theological, and everyday contexts. Participates in derivation: č̣ešmariteba (truth, as abstract noun).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in Georgian, used across philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday registers. Commonly appears as the subject or object of predications about what is the case. Central to Georgian theological and philosophical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominal prefix) + martal- (right, correct, just) + -e (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, justice
**Usage:** Overlaps with č̣ešmariteba but is more commonly used in everyday speech for factual truth and in legal or moral contexts for rightness and justice. The phrase simartle tkva ("told the truth") is highly frequent. Also used in the sense of what is factually accurate in a dispute.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** martal- (right, correct) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, truthful, just, innocent
**Usage:** Applied to persons (a truthful or just person), statements (a correct claim), and verdicts (a just outcome). In legal contexts can carry the sense of innocent or vindicated. Closely related to simartle through shared root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -e
**Typical English translations:** falseness, falsehood, forgery, inauthenticity
**Usage:** Used for the abstract quality of being false or counterfeit, applicable to documents, currency, statements, and persons. Frequently appears in legal contexts involving forgery or fraud. Antonym of simartle and č̣ešmariteba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + adjectival suffix -i; borrowed from Persian/Arabic via Turkish
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged
**Usage:** The standard adjective for counterfeit or forged items (q̇albi dokumenti, "forged document") and false statements. Also used colloquially for inauthentic behavior or persons. Productive in derivation: siq̇albe, q̇alboba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** nam- (perfective preverb functioning as intensifier in this lexicalized form) + dvil- (root related to being, existence) + -i
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** The primary adjective for authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, and states of affairs. Namdvili oqro ("real gold"), namdvili megobari ("true friend"). Widely used across registers and participates in productive derivation (namdviloba, "genuineness, authenticity").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, realness
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from namdvili, used in legal, administrative, and philosophical contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in discussions of document verification and identity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (lie, false) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The standard noun for a lie or falsehood in everyday and moral discourse. Contrasts with simartle. Used in moral evaluation of persons and statements. The verb tkuili tkva ("told a lie") is a near-synonym construction; sicrue is the more abstract nominal form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ტყუილი
**Transliteration:** ẗq̇uili
**Literal meaning:** ẗq̇uil- (lie, falsehood) + nominal suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The most common everyday word for a lie, used in direct moral evaluation of statements and persons. Slightly more colloquial than sicrue. The verb ẗq̇uili tkva is a standard fixed expression meaning "told a lie." Also used adverbially in the sense of "in vain, for nothing."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarwmuno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (circumfix, "worthy of") + rwmun- (belief, trust, faith) + -o
**Typical English translations:** credible, reliable, trustworthy, believable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, testimony, and evidence to indicate that they merit belief or trust. Frequently used in legal and journalistic contexts. Participates in derivation: sarwmunoba ("credibility, reliability").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** სარწმუნოება
**Transliteration:** sarwmunoeba
**Literal meaning:** sarwmuno + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** credibility, reliability, trustworthiness; also faith, religion (in theological register)
**Usage:** In secular contexts, denotes the property of being credible or trustworthy. In theological and ecclesiastical contexts, the dominant meaning shifts to faith or religion. The semantic range is broad and context-dependent.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** დამაჯერებელი
**Transliteration:** damajerebeliˈ
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + majer- (causative of "believe") + -ebeli (agentive/adjectival suffix, "capable of causing")
**Typical English translations:** convincing, persuasive, credible, plausible
**Usage:** Applied to arguments, evidence, persons, and performances to indicate that they are capable of producing belief or conviction. Commonly used in legal, rhetorical, and critical contexts. Distinct from sarwmuno in emphasizing the effect on a hearer rather than an intrinsic property.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** სანდო
**Transliteration:** sando
**Literal meaning:** sa- + nd- (root of trust, confidence) + -o
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** Applied primarily to persons and sources to indicate reliability and trustworthiness. Frequently used in contexts of evaluating witnesses, informants, and institutions. Closely synonymous with sarwmuno but may carry a slightly stronger interpersonal connotation of personal trust.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** უტყუარი
**Transliteration:** uẗq̇uari
**Literal meaning:** u- (privative prefix, "without") + ẗq̇uar- (lie, falsehood) + -i
**Typical English translations:** infallible, indubitable, certain, irrefutable
**Usage:** Used to describe evidence, proofs, or facts that are beyond doubt or cannot be falsified. Appears in legal and philosophical contexts. The privative formation explicitly marks the absence of falsehood or error.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avt̕ent̕uri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek/Latin authenticus via European languages + adjectival suffix -uri
**Typical English translations:** authentic
**Usage:** A loanword used primarily in formal, legal, archival, and academic contexts to describe documents, texts, or objects whose origin and integrity are verified. Less common in everyday speech than namdvili, which covers similar ground in informal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ლეგიტიმური
**Transliteration:** legitimuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin/European legitimus + adjectival suffix -uri
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, valid
**Usage:** Used in legal, political, and institutional contexts to evaluate whether authority, claims, or actions conform to recognized rules or norms. Distinct from mere legality in sometimes carrying a normative or moral dimension.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** კანონიერი
**Transliteration:** kanonieri
**Literal meaning:** kanon- (law, rule; from Greek kanon) + -ieri (adjectival suffix, "having the quality of")
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legal, legitimate, rightful
**Usage:** The standard adjective for legality and lawfulness in Georgian legal discourse. Applied to actions, claims, ownership, and authority. Overlaps with legitimuri but is more specifically tied to positive law rather than broader normative legitimacy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** სამართლიანი
**Transliteration:** samart̕liani
**Literal meaning:** samart̕al- (law, justice, right) + -iani (adjectival suffix, "characterized by")
**Typical English translations:** just, fair, righteous, equitable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, decisions, distributions, and systems to evaluate them as conforming to standards of justice or fairness. Central to moral and legal evaluation in Georgian. Participates in productive derivation: samart̕lianoba ("justice, fairness").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** სამართლიანობა
**Transliteration:** samart̕lianoba
**Literal meaning:** samart̕liani + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, righteousness, equity
**Usage:** The abstract noun for justice and fairness, used in philosophical, legal, and political discourse. A central term in Georgian ethical and legal vocabulary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** მიმსგავსება
**Transliteration:** mimsgavseba
**Literal meaning:** mi- (preverb) + msgavs- (similar, resembling) + -eba (verbal/nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, likening, assimilation (to a model)
**Usage:** Refers to the process or result of making something resemble something else, or the state of resemblance. Used in aesthetic and theological contexts (e.g., resemblance to a divine model). Less common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** მსგავსი
**Transliteration:** msgavsi
**Literal meaning:** msgavs- (similar, like) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** similar, like, resembling
**Usage:** The standard adjective for similarity and resemblance, used across registers. Applied to persons, objects, and situations. Participates in derivation: msgavseba ("similarity, resemblance, likeness").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** გულწრფელი
**Transliteration:** gulwrp̕eli
**Literal meaning:** gul- (heart) + wrp̕el- (straight, sincere) + -i; literally "straight-hearted"
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, frank, candid
**Usage:** Applied to persons and expressions to indicate sincerity and absence of deception. Used in moral evaluation of character and in assessing the authenticity of emotional or verbal expression. Contrasts with terms implying hypocrisy or pretense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** თვალთმაქცობა
**Transliteration:** t̕valt̕maq̇c̕oba
**Literal meaning:** t̕val- (eye) + t̕maq̇c̕- (deceiver, juggler) + -oba (nominalizing suffix); literally "eye-deceiving"
**Typical English translations:** deception, hypocrisy, pretense, charlatanism
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception through appearance or performance, including hypocrisy and charlatanism. Used in moral condemnation of persons who present a false appearance. The morphological structure explicitly encodes visual deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** შესაბამისი
**Transliteration:** šesabamisi
**Literal meaning:** še- (preverb) + sabam- (correspondence, conformity) + -isi (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** corresponding, conforming, appropriate, consistent with
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether something conforms to or corresponds with a standard, rule, or expectation. Appears in legal, administrative, and evaluative contexts. Relevant to assessments of whether conduct or representations match what is required or claimed.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** zust- (exact, precise; loanword from Persian/Turkish) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, translations, statements, and reproductions to indicate precision and accuracy. Widely used across technical, scientific, and everyday registers. Participates in derivation: zustoba, zustad ("precisely, exactly" as adverb).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- + -e
**Typical English translations:** exactness, precision, accuracy
**Usage:** Abstract noun for the property of being exact or accurate. Used in technical, scientific, and evaluative contexts. Closely related to zusti through shared root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** გარეგნული
**Transliteration:** garegnuli
**Literal meaning:** gareg- (outer, external) + -nuli (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** external, outward, superficial, apparent
**Usage:** Used to mark a contrast between outward appearance and inner reality or essence. Relevant in contexts where the reliability of surface appearances is evaluated. Often implicitly contrasted with šinagani ("internal, inner").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** არსებითი
**Transliteration:** arsebiti
**Literal meaning:** arseb- (being, existence, essence; from arsi, "essence") + -iti (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** essential, substantial, fundamental, material
**Usage:** Applied to qualities, differences, and facts to indicate that they pertain to the fundamental nature or essence of something. Used in philosophical, legal, and evaluative contexts. In legal usage, arsebiti gansxvaveba ("material difference") is a recognized expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** ფსევდო-
**Transliteration:** psevdo-
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek pseudos (false) + productive prefix in Georgian
**Typical English translations:** pseudo-, false, spurious (as prefix)
**Usage:** A productive borrowed prefix attached to nouns to indicate that something falsely presents itself as belonging to a category. Used in academic, journalistic, and critical registers (e.g., psevdomecnieri, "pseudo-scientist"). Marks inauthenticity of classification or identity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
