# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## OPPOSITION 1: òtítọ́ vs. irọ́

**Term A:** òtítọ́  
**Term B:** irọ́  
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ vs. irọ́  
**Translation:** truth, truthfulness vs. lie, falsehood  
**Description of contrast:** Primary lexical opposition between truth and falsehood. These are the core nouns used across all contexts to denote true versus false content. Both appear in parallel constructions (sọ òtítọ́ "tell truth" vs. pa irọ́ "tell lie") and proverbial expressions (òtítọ́ kọjá irọ́ "truth surpasses lies").  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 2: tọ́ vs. purọ́

**Term A:** tọ́  
**Term B:** purọ́  
**Transliteration:** tọ́ vs. purọ́  
**Translation:** to be correct, to be true vs. to lie, to be false  
**Description of contrast:** Primary verbal opposition for truth versus falsehood. **Tọ́** is a stative verb evaluating correctness, while **purọ́** is an active verb describing the act of lying or being false. Both function as main predicates in simple clauses.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 3: tọ́ vs. kò tọ́

**Term A:** tọ́  
**Term B:** kò tọ́  
**Transliteration:** tọ́ vs. kò tọ́  
**Translation:** to be correct/true vs. to not be correct/true, to be wrong  
**Description of contrast:** Morphological negation using preverbal negator **kò**. This is the standard way to express incorrectness or untruth. The negative form **kò tọ́** is as frequent and natural as the positive **tọ́**.  
**Contrast type:** morphological  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 4: òtítọ́ vs. àìtọ́

**Term A:** òtítọ́  
**Term B:** àìtọ́  
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ vs. àìtọ́  
**Translation:** truth, correctness vs. incorrectness, untruth  
**Description of contrast:** Nominal opposition between positive abstract noun **òtítọ́** and negative abstract noun **àìtọ́** (formed with negative prefix **àì-**). Both denote states or qualities rather than acts. **Àìtọ́** is less common than **irọ́** but used in formal/analytical discourse.  
**Contrast type:** morphological  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 15: sọ òtítọ́ vs. pa irọ́

**Term A:** sọ òtítọ́  
**Term B:** pa irọ́  
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́ vs. pa irọ́  
**Translation:** to tell the truth vs. to tell a lie  
**Description of contrast:** Constructional opposition using different verbs (**sọ** "speak" vs. **pa** "kill/strike") with contrasting objects (**òtítọ́** vs. **irọ́**). These are the standard expressions for truth-telling versus lie-telling. Note the asymmetry: truth uses **sọ** (speak) while lies use **pa** (conventionalized with irọ́).  
**Contrast type:** constructional  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY

**Total oppositions identified:** 30

**Distribution by contrast type:**
- Lexical: 6 oppositions
- Morphological: 13 oppositions  
- Constructional: 11 oppositions

**Distribution by epistemic status:**
- [CORE]: 28 oppositions
- [MARGINAL]: 1 opposition (ìyẹ in opposition 12)
- [UNCERTAIN]: 2 oppositions (irọ́ dùn in opposition 24, antonym of gidi in opposition 28)

**Key observations:**

1. **Morphological negation is highly productive**: The preverbal negator **kò** and the nominal negative prefix **àì-** create systematic oppositions across the truth domain. This is the most regular pattern.

2. **Lexical oppositions are limited but central**: The core lexical pairs (òtítọ́/irọ́, tọ́/purọ́, rere/búburú) are fundamental to the domain, but there are relatively few such pairs compared to morphological oppositions.

3. **Constructional asymmetries exist**: Some oppositions show structural asymmetry, such as **ọmọ òtítọ́** (compound) vs. **apurọ́** (derived form), or **sọ òtítọ́** (speak truth) vs. **pa irọ́** (strike/tell lie) using different verbs.

4. **No opposition for some expected contrasts**: The data shows no attested lexical opposition for "fake/counterfeit" as antonym of **gidi**, and no separate lexical item for "doubt" (expressed through negation of **dájú**).

5. **Emphatic particles create polar oppositions**: Particles like **títí** (completely) and **rárá** (at all) combine with positive and negative predicates to create emphatic polar oppositions.

6. **Truth is conceptualized as binary**: Most oppositions are categorical (true/false, correct/incorrect) rather than scalar, reflecting the binary conceptualization of truth in Yoruba.
