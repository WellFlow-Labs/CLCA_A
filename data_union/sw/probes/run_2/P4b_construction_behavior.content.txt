# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Construction Usage Table

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes | Attested Alternates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Attribution** (*ni* + truth predicate) | *Habari hii ni kweli.* | Habari hii ni kweli. | "This news is true." | [CORE] | Extremely common across all registers — conversational, journalistic, legal. No restriction on subject type; *ni* is invariant across noun classes. | *Hati hii ni halisi.* (same pattern, different predicate) |
| **1. Attribution** (*ni* + truth predicate) | *Hati hii ni halisi.* | Hati hii ni halisi. | "This document is genuine." | [CORE] | Frequent in commercial and legal contexts when authenticating documents or goods. | *Hati hii ni sahihi.* (near-synonymous in document contexts) |
| **2. Negative Attribution** (*si* + truth predicate) | *Hii si kweli.* | Hii si kweli. | "This is not true." | [CORE] | Direct negation of attribution; fully parallel to *ni* construction. Occurs in all registers including informal speech. | *Hii si sahihi.* (same pattern) |
| **2. Negative Attribution** (*si* + truth predicate) | *Bidhaa hizi si halisi; ni bandia.* | Bidhaa hizi si halisi; ni bandia. | "These goods are not genuine; they are fake." | [CORE] | The contrastive pairing *halisi … bandia* is characteristic of commercial and legal discourse; the two-clause structure reinforces the denial with a positive counter-assertion. | *Hizi si za kweli; ni za bandia.* (genitive variant, same semantic content) |
| **3a. Adnominal — bare adjective modifier** | *Dhahabu halisi* | Dhahabu halisi | "Genuine gold." | [CORE] | Standard noun-phrase pattern for attributive modification; no linker required. Frequent in commercial, legal, and everyday descriptive contexts. No agreement morphology on the modifier. | *Dhahabu sahihi* (near-synonymous in some contexts) |
| **3b. Adnominal — genitive modifier** | *Shahidi wa uongo* | Shahidi wa uongo | "A false witness." | [CORE] | Required when the modifier is a noun rather than a bare adjective-type item; linker (*wa/ya/la/za* etc.) must agree with the head noun's class. Frequent in legal and religious discourse. | *Ushahidi wa uongo* (same pattern, different head noun class → *wa* retained here by class agreement) |
| **4. Become / Inchoative** (*-ika/-eka*) | *Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi.* | Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi. | "The truth became evident after the investigation." | [CORE] | Common in narrative and journalistic contexts where a state of affairs is presented as emerging without foregrounding an external agent. Restricted to bases that admit the *-ika/-eka* derivation; not attested for *kweli* or *halisi* directly. | — |
| **4. Become / Inchoative** (*-ika/-eka*) | *Alidanganyika kwa urahisi.* | Alidanganyika kwa urahisi. | "He/she was easily taken in / became deceived." | [CORE] | Presents the subject as entering a state of deception without specifying an agent; common in narrative contexts. Distinct from the passive (*kudanganywa*), which foregrounds an external agent. | *Alidanganywa* (passive, agent-foregrounding alternate — see §8) |
| **5. Causative** (*-sha/-isha*) | *Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya wanafunzi.* | Mwalimu alisahihisha makosa ya wanafunzi. | "The teacher corrected the students' errors." | [CORE] | Standard in educational and editorial contexts. The causative suffix is productive across truth-domain bases that admit an external agent. No causative from *kweli* itself is attested. | *Mwalimu alirudi makosa* (non-causative periphrastic, less precise) |
| **5. Causative** (*-sha/-isha*) | *Serikali inahalalisha mkataba huo.* | Serikali inahalalisha mkataba huo. | "The government is legitimizing that contract." | [CORE] | Characteristic of legal and political discourse; the causative construction foregrounds the agent's authority to bring about a legitimate state. *Kuthibitisha* functions similarly in verification contexts. | *Serikali inathibitisha mkataba huo* (verification rather than legitimization) |
| **6. Abstract Nominalization** (*u-* prefix) | *Ukweli wa jambo hili haujulikani.* | Ukweli wa jambo hili haujulikani. | "The truth of this matter is not known." | [CORE] | Highly productive pattern; *u-* nominals are the standard form for abstract reference to truth-domain properties. Appear as heads of genitive phrases specifying domain. Common across all formal registers. | *Kweli ya jambo hili* (less formal, bare noun variant attested but less common for abstract reference) |
| **6. Abstract Nominalization** (*u-* prefix) | *Uhalali wa uchaguzi unapingwa mahakamani.* | Uhalali wa uchaguzi unapingwa mahakamani. | "The legitimacy of the election is being contested in court." | [CORE] | Characteristic of legal and political discourse; *uhalali* is the standard nominal for legitimacy claims in formal contexts. The genitive phrase specifying domain (*wa uchaguzi*) is a regular and frequent extension. | *Haki ya uchaguzi* (partially overlapping in meaning but distinct in focus) |
| **7a. Agentive Nominalization** (*m-/mw-* + stem) | *Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu.* | Yeye ni muongo wa hali ya juu. | "He/she is a consummate liar." | [CORE] | Lexicalized agentive nominals (*muongo*, *mwaminifu*, *mdanganyifu*) are common and idiomatic. The *-fu* suffix marks habitual or characterological quality. Plural formed by *wa-* replacement (*waongo*, *waaminifu*). | *Yeye ni mdanganyifu* (alternate agentive nominal, slightly different nuance — deceiver vs. liar) |
| **7b. Agentive Nominalization** (relative clause periphrasis) | *Tunahitaji mtu wa kuaminiwa.* | Tunahitaji mtu wa kuaminiwa. | "We need a trustworthy person / a person to be trusted." | [CORE] | Used where no lexicalized agentive nominal exists (e.g., no single-word "truth-teller"). The periphrastic form is natural and not marked as unusual. Common in everyday and formal speech. | *Tunahitaji mtu anayesema kweli* (alternate periphrastic — relative clause with active verb, also attested per P4a) |
| **8. Passive** (*-wa* suffix) | *Habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi.* | Habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi. | "The news has been confirmed by multiple sources." | [CORE] | Standard construction in journalistic and legal discourse for reporting verification outcomes. Agent introduced by *na*; agent expression is optional. Highly productive across all transitive and causative truth-domain verbs. | *Vyanzo vingi vimethibitisha habari* (active alternate, agent-foregrounding) |
| **8. Passive** (*-wa* suffix) | *Nilidanganywa na maneno yake.* | Nilidanganywa na maneno yake. | "I was deceived by his/her words." | [CORE] | Common in personal narrative and testimony contexts. Distinct from the inchoative *alidanganyika* (§4) in that the agent (*na maneno yake*) is expressed. The contrast between passive and inchoative is semantically significant in this domain. | *Alidanganyika* (inchoative alternate — no agent expressed, see §4) |
| **9. Adverbial Truth-Asserting Phrase** (*kwa* + noun) | *Kwa kweli, sikujua chochote.* | Kwa kweli, sikujua chochote. | "Honestly, I knew nothing." | [CORE] | Common sentence-initial sincerity marker across all registers including informal speech. Reduplication (*kwa kweli kweli*) intensifies the sincerity claim and is also widely attested. Not morphologically integrated into the verb complex. | *Kwa kweli kweli, sikujua chochote* (reduplicated intensified variant) |
| **9. Adverbial Truth-Asserting Phrase** (*kwa* + noun) | *Kwa dhahiri, tatizo hili ni kubwa.* | Kwa dhahiri, tatizo hili ni kubwa. | "Evidently, this problem is serious." | [CORE] | Functions as an evidential marker of self-evidence rather than a sincerity marker; characteristic of argumentative and expository discourse. Sentence-initial position is typical. | *Ni dhahiri kwamba* (alternate evidential construction — see §10, more formal) |
| **10. Evidential / Self-Evidence Predicate** (*ni dhahiri kwamba*) | *Ni dhahiri kwamba alikosea.* | Ni dhahiri kwamba alikosea. | "It is evident that he/she erred." | [CORE] | Characteristic of argumentative and legal discourse where the speaker presents a conclusion as requiring no further support. Takes a *kwamba*-complement clause; intensifier *kabisa* may be inserted. | *Kwa dhahiri, alikosea* (adverbial alternate — see §9, less formally structured) |
| **10. Evidential / Self-Evidence Predicate** (*ni dhahiri kwamba*) | *Ni dhahiri kabisa kwamba ushahidi unatosha.* | Ni dhahiri kabisa kwamba ushahidi unatosha. | "It is absolutely clear that the evidence is sufficient." | [CORE] | The intensifier *kabisa* is inserted between *dhahiri* and *kwamba*; this is an attested and natural extension of the base construction. Particularly common in formal argumentation and legal summation. | *Ni wazi kabisa kwamba* (near-synonymous with *wazi* 'clear/open', attested in similar contexts though *wazi* is not a core truth-domain item per P4a) |
| **11. Periphrastic Verbal Construction** (light verb + truth noun) | *Kusema ukweli daima ni wajibu.* | Kusema ukweli daima ni wajibu. | "To always tell the truth is a duty." | [CORE] | Standard pattern compensating for the absence of lexicalized single-word verbs for "truth-tell," "evidence," etc. The object noun carries semantic content; the light verb specifies action type. Productive and not restricted to fixed collocations. | — |
| **11. Periphrastic Verbal Construction** (light verb + truth noun) | *Walikusanya ushahidi kwa miezi mingi.* | Walikusanya ushahidi kwa miezi mingi. | "They gathered evidence for many months." | [CORE] | Common in journalistic, legal, and investigative contexts. The range of light verbs (*sema*, *toa*, *tafuta*, *ficha*, *potosha*, *kukusanya*, *pinga*) is broad; the pattern is productive across action types in the truth domain. | — |

## Summary Notes

**No constructions rated [MARGINAL] or [UNCERTAIN]:** All eleven construction types identified in P4a are rated [CORE] based on the evidence presented. Each is described in P4a as common, productive, or characteristic of identifiable registers, and no construction is flagged in P4a as restricted, rare, or doubtful. This rating reflects the P4a data as given; it does not constitute an independent claim about frequency outside that data.

**Passive vs. Inchoative distinction (§4 / §8):** The contrast between *kudanganyika* (inchoative, no agent) and *kudanganywa* (passive, agent expressible) is semantically significant and both forms are [CORE]; they are not interchangeable alternates but distinct constructions with different agent-foregrounding properties.

**Absence of attested alternates for §11:** No alternate constructions are listed for the periphrastic verbal construction because P4a does not attest single-word lexicalized verbs for the relevant actions ("to truth-tell," "to evidence-gather"); the periphrastic pattern is the primary attested form for these meanings.
