# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** true, correct
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate

Used to affirm that a statement, fact, or claim corresponds to reality or is without error. Also used in moral and evaluative contexts to mean "right" or "proper." Participates in derivation: *kebenaran* (truth, correctness), *membenarkan* (to justify, to confirm), *sebenarnya* (actually, in truth).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** salah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** wrong, mistaken
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false

The primary antonym of *benar*, used to evaluate statements, actions, or attributions as incorrect or erroneous. Appears in moral contexts (wrongdoing) and factual contexts (incorrect information). Also used in legal contexts. Productive in derivation: *kesalahan* (error, fault, wrongdoing), *menyalahkan* (to blame, to fault).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** asli
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** original, native, genuine
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, original, real

Applied to objects, documents, persons, and cultural items to indicate they are not copies, forgeries, or imitations. Commonly used in legal, commercial, aesthetic, and cultural contexts. Contrasts with *palsu* (fake) and *tiruan* (imitation).

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** palsu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** false, fake (from Portuguese *falso*)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged, spurious

Used to evaluate objects, documents, currency, goods, identities, or claims as not genuine or deliberately deceptive. Carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception than *salah*. Also used of statements or claims that are deliberately untrue. Appears in legal contexts (pemalsuan — forgery, falsification) and everyday evaluation of inauthenticity. Productive: *memalsukan* (to falsify, to forge), *pemalsuan* (falsification, forgery).

**[CORE]**

**5.**
**Form:** nyata
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** real, manifest, evident
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, evident, concrete, tangible

Indicates that something is genuinely present, perceptible, or factually existing rather than imagined, hypothetical, or supposed. Also used to emphasize that a claim or situation is clearly the case. Used in both philosophical and everyday registers. Related forms: *kenyataan* (reality, fact), *menyatakan* (to state, to declare).

**[CORE]**

**6.**
**Form:** sungguh
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** truly, earnestly
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, seriously

Used as an intensifier to assert sincerity or reality of a claim or state. Often used in expressions of genuine feeling or to assert that something is not pretended. Appears in the fixed expression *sungguh-sungguh* (in earnest, genuinely, in all seriousness). Also used in *bersungguh-sungguh* (to be earnest, to be serious about something).

**[CORE]**

**7.**
**Form:** sejati
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine, real (from *se-* — intensifier + *jati* — true, genuine; *jati* also means teak wood, connoting hardness/solidity)
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic

Evaluates persons, qualities, or states as genuinely and fully what they are claimed or appear to be — often with a sense of depth or completeness. Used in moral, philosophical, and literary registers, often with a philosophical or elevated tone. Commonly appears in compounds: *teman sejati* (true friend), *cinta sejati* (true love), *pahlawan sejati* (true hero). Less productive derivationally than *benar* or *asli*.

**[CORE]**

**8.**
**Form:** sah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid, lawful (from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*)
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, lawful, official

Primarily used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to indicate that an act, document, or status meets required conditions and is recognized as binding or proper. Contrasts with *tidak sah* (invalid, illegitimate). Productive: *mengesahkan* (to validate, to ratify, to legalize), *pengesahan* (ratification, validation), *keabsahan* (validity, legitimacy).

**[CORE]**

**9.**
**Form:** absah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** valid, legitimate (from Arabic *ṣaḥḥ*, variant of *sah*)
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, lawful

A near-synonym of *sah*, used in formal and legal registers to evaluate whether something meets the conditions for recognition or legitimacy. Slightly more formal in distribution than *sah*. The relationship between *absah* and *sah* is not fully transparent to all speakers. Related: *keabsahan* (validity, legitimacy).

**10.**
**Form:** tepat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** precise, exact, fitting
**Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate, correct, appropriate

Evaluates whether something corresponds exactly to a standard, target, or requirement — used for measurements, timing, word choice, and judgments. Also used to evaluate whether an action or decision is fitting for a given situation. Productive: *ketepatan* (precision, accuracy), *menepati* (to fulfill, to keep to).

**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise

Used in technical, journalistic, and scientific contexts to evaluate data, reports, or measurements as free from error and closely corresponding to fact. More domain-specific than *tepat* and less common in everyday speech. Related: *keakuratan* (accuracy).

**12.**
**Form:** jujur
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** honest, straightforward
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere, candid, frank

Evaluates a person's conduct or statements as free from deception or concealment. A central term in moral evaluation, used in moral, interpersonal, and institutional contexts. Productive: *kejujuran* (honesty), *ketidakjujuran* (dishonesty), *berjujur* (to be honest, to confess).

**13.**
**Form:** bohong
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** lie, untruth
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, untrue, untruthful

Used to evaluate a statement or person as deliberately untruthful. Functions as both noun (a lie) and predicate adjective (it is false/a lie). Contrasts with *jujur*. Related: *berbohong* (to lie), *pembohong* (liar), *membohongi* (to deceive, to lie to someone), *kebohongan* (lie, falsehood).

A near-synonym of *bohong*, somewhat more formal or literary in register. Used in moral and religious discourse to evaluate statements or conduct as deliberately deceptive. Related: *berdusta* (to lie), *mendustakan* (to deny, to call a liar), *pendusta* (liar).

Used to evaluate the inner motivation or intention behind an action or statement as free from hidden agenda or pretense. Often applied to feelings, intentions, and expressions. Related form: *ketulusan* (sincerity).

**Typical English translations:** sincere, wholehearted, selfless, accepting

Used to describe actions or attitudes performed without expectation of reward or recognition, particularly in religious and moral contexts. Carries a stronger connotation of selfless acceptance than *tulus*. Related form: *keikhlasan* (sincerity, selflessness).

**Typical English translations:** pure, genuine, unadulterated, sheer

Applied to substances, motives, and abstract qualities to indicate freedom from contamination, mixture, or corruption. Used in both physical and figurative senses. Related form: *kemurnian* (purity).

**18.**
**Form:** tiruan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** imitation, copy (from *tiru* — to imitate + *-an* nominalizer)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, copy, replica, fake

Evaluates an object as a reproduction or copy rather than an original. Used in commercial, artistic, and legal contexts. Does not necessarily imply deceptive intent, unlike *palsu*; *tiruan* may be acknowledged openly. Contrasts with *asli*. Base: *meniru* (to imitate, to copy), *peniru* (imitator), *peniruan* (imitation, copying).

**19.**
**Form:** otentik
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** authentic (from Dutch *authentiek*)
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine

Used in formal, academic, legal, and journalistic registers to evaluate documents, artworks, or statements as genuine and not fabricated or falsified. Less common in everyday speech than *asli*. *Keaslian* is the more common nominal form in informal contexts; *otentisitas* is used in academic registers.

Used primarily in Islamic religious and scholarly discourse to evaluate hadith (prophetic traditions) or religious texts as meeting criteria of authenticity and reliability through established chains of transmission. Evaluates the degree of resemblance between two entities — used in contexts of identification, comparison, and assessment of likeness. **31.**
**Form:** serupa
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** of the same form/kind (from *se-* — same + *rupa* — form, appearance)
**Typical English translations:** similar, alike, resembling

Near-synonym of *mirip*, evaluating resemblance or likeness between entities. Slightly more formal in register and more explicitly grounded in the notion of shared form or appearance. *Rupa* (form, appearance) is independently productive in compounds and derivations. Related: *keserupaan* (similarity, resemblance).

**32.**
**Form:** hakikat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** essence, fundamental nature (from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*)
**Typical English translations:** essence, true nature, fundamental reality, truth

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance. Appears in evaluative contexts where the question is whether something's true nature has been correctly identified. Often appears in the phrase *pada hakikatnya* (in essence, fundamentally). Related: *hakiki*.

**33.**
**Form:** esensi
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** essence (from Dutch *essentie*)
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature

Near-synonym of *hakikat* in philosophical and formal registers, used to refer to the fundamental nature of something. More common in academic and secular discourse; *hakikat* is more common in religious and traditional contexts. More neutral in register and less tied to philosophical or religious tradition than *hakikat*. Related: *esensial* (essential).

**Typical English translations:** true, real, essential, fundamental

Used in philosophical, religious, and formal literary contexts to indicate the essential or fundamental nature of something, as opposed to its surface appearance. Related to *hakikat* (essence, true nature). More restricted to formal registers.

The nominalized form of *benar*, used to refer to truth as an abstract quality or to the correctness of a specific claim or state of affairs. Widely used in moral, legal, philosophical, and everyday contexts. A highly productive and central form.

**Typical English translations:** actually, in truth, in fact, really

A fixed adverbial form derived from *benar*, used to introduce a correction, clarification, or disclosure of what is actually the case. Extremely common in spoken and written Indonesian. Functions as a discourse marker signaling contrast with prior assumption.
