# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: English (en)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Subject + BE + truth-domain adjective | That statement is true. | That statement is true. | "The claim corresponds to reality." | [CORE] | The unmarked, most frequent construction for truth-domain predication. Used across all registers and contexts without restriction. |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested in data.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Subject + BE + NEG + truth-domain adjective | The document is not authentic. | The document is not authentic. | "The document lacks authenticity." | [CORE] | Standard analytic negation, fully productive with all truth-domain adjectives. No restrictions observed. |
| Subject + BE + negative-prefixed adjective | The claim is untrue. | The claim is untrue. | "The claim is false." | [CORE] | Synthetic negation available for many but not all adjectives. Lexically determined which adjectives permit negative prefixes (un-, in-, dis-). |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested in data.

**Additional notes:** Choice between analytic and synthetic negation depends on lexical availability and register preferences. Some adjectives strongly prefer one pattern over the other.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Subject + become/turn/prove + truth-domain adjective | The rumor became true. | The rumor became true. | "The rumor was realized as fact." | [CORE] | General inchoative construction using change-of-state verbs. "Become" is neutral; "turn" is more colloquial; "prove" emphasizes evidential basis. |
| Subject + come true | Her prediction came true. | Her prediction came true. | "Her prediction was realized in reality." | [CORE] | Specialized idiomatic construction limited to predictions, wishes, dreams, and similar future-oriented propositions. Not productive beyond these contexts. |

**Alternate constructions:** "turn out to be + adjective" (emphasizes discovery rather than change).

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| Agent + make + Object + truth-domain adjective | The evidence made his claim true. | The evidence made his claim true. | "The evidence caused his claim to be true." | [CORE] | Standard analytic causative construction. "Make" is neutral register, fully productive with truth-domain adjectives. |
| Agent + render + Object + truth-domain adjective | The forgery rendered the document invalid. | The forgery rendered the document invalid. | "The forgery caused the document to become invalid." | [CORE] | Formal register variant of analytic causative. More common in written and technical contexts than in speech. |

**Alternate constructions:** Lexical causatives (falsify, validate, authenticate, legitimize) incorporate causative meaning without analytic construction.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| truth-domain root + -er/-or | a liar | a liar | "one who habitually tells falsehoods" | [CORE] | Limited productivity in truth domain. "Liar" is the most common and well-established form. |
| Compound forms | a truth-teller | a truth-teller | "one who habitually tells the truth" | [MARGINAL] | Productive but less frequent than simple forms. Used when no established lexical item exists. |

**Alternate constructions:** Agentive forms derived from verbs rather than adjectives (e.g., "falsifier" from "falsify").

**Additional notes:** Many truth-domain concepts lack dedicated agent nouns. Compounds fill gaps but are less lexicalized.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**Most productive constructions [CORE]:**
- Basic attribution (Subject + BE + adjective)
- Negative attribution (both analytic and synthetic)
- Adnominal modification
- Epistemic qualification
- Interrogative constructions
- It-clefts

**Restricted or less common constructions [MARGINAL]:**
- Agentive nominalization through compounding
- Synthetic comparative forms with truth-domain adjectives
- Wh-clefts

**No [UNCERTAIN] ratings assigned:** All constructions in P4a are well-attested and clearly grammatical in English.

**Key restrictions observed:**
- Synthetic comparison limited due to absolute semantics of core truth-domain adjectives
- Agentive nominalization not fully productive; gaps filled by compounds
- "Come true" construction restricted to predictions, wishes, dreams
- Negative prefix availability lexically determined
- Choice of nominalization suffix lexically determined
