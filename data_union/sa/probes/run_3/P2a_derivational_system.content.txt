# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST, consistent with P1 and P1b.

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This output documents attested derivational morphology for the 30 P1 lexical items. Coverage is drawn from the P1 and P1b outputs and from the attested Sanskrit grammatical and philosophical record. Forms already fully documented in P1b (e.g., *satyatā*, *satyavādin*, *pramāṇatā*, *apramāṇa*, *apramā*, *anumāna*, *prāmāṇya*) are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full. Asymmetry is preserved throughout: some items generate extensive derivational families; others generate few or none. No paradigm completion has been performed.

## P1 Item 1 — सत्य / *satya*

---

**1.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यता
**Transliteration:** satyatā
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the property of being true"
**Natural English Translation:** truthfulness, the quality of being true
**Usage:** Used in epistemological and ethical literature to designate the abstract property of being true or truthful, as predicated of statements, persons, or cognitions. Appears in *Nyāya* commentaries and *Dharmaśāstra*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 8.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यवादिन्
**Transliteration:** satyavādin
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*)
**Natural English Translation:** one who speaks the truth, a truth-teller
**Usage:** Used in ethical, legal, and epic literature to characterize a person whose speech is reliably truthful; appears as a term of praise in *Dharmaśāstra* witness evaluation. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 9.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सात्य
**Transliteration:** sātya
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + secondary adjectival suffix *-a* with vṛddhi of initial syllable; "relating to or derived from *satya*"
**Natural English Translation:** relating to truth; of the nature of truth
**Usage:** Appears in Vedic and post-Vedic literature as a secondary adjectival formation from *satya*, used to characterize what pertains to or partakes of truth. Less frequent than *satya* itself.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**4.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यापन
**Transliteration:** satyāpana
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + causative/denominative suffix *-āpana*; "causing to be true, verification"
**Natural English Translation:** verification, confirmation, authentication
**Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to designate the act of verifying or confirming that something is true or genuine. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 13.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यापित
**Transliteration:** satyāpita
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + causative past passive participle suffix *-āpita*; "caused to be true, verified"
**Natural English Translation:** verified, authenticated, confirmed
**Usage:** The past passive participle corresponding to *satyāpana*; used in legal and administrative contexts to characterize something that has been officially verified or authenticated.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यवाक्य
**Transliteration:** satya-vākya
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vākya* (utterance, from root *vac-*)
**Natural English Translation:** a true statement, a truthful utterance
**Usage:** Used in logical, legal, and ethical contexts to designate an utterance that corresponds to the facts; appears in discussions of testimony (*śabda-pramāṇa*). (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 10.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्यव्रत
**Transliteration:** satya-vrata
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vrata* (vow, observance)
**Natural English Translation:** one who has taken a vow of truthfulness; the vow of truth
**Usage:** Used in epic and *Purāṇic* literature to designate a person committed to speaking only truth as a religious or ethical observance; also used as a proper name. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 11.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Derived Form:** सत्याग्रह
**Transliteration:** satyāgraha
**Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence, from *ā-* + root *grah-*)
**Natural English Translation:** insistence on truth; truth-force
**Usage:** Coined by Mohandas Gandhi as a political and ethical term designating nonviolent resistance grounded in commitment to truth; widely used in modern Indian political discourse. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 14.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Usage:** Used in *Yogasūtra* 1.48 to designate the highest form of wisdom (*prajñā*) that directly bears or carries truth; also used as an epithet of Vedic speech. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 3, item 11.)

## P1 Item 5 — प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**18.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** प्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + secondary suffix *-ya* with vṛddhi; "the property of being a *pramāṇa*"
**Natural English Translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the status of being a valid means of knowledge
**Usage:** Central to debates in *Mīmāṃsā* and *Nyāya* about how validity is established and recognized; used as the abstract noun for the property that makes something a *pramāṇa*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 13.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमाणता
**Transliteration:** pramāṇatā
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + abstract suffix *-tā*
**Natural English Translation:** the property of being a valid means of knowledge; epistemic validity
**Usage:** Used in *Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*, and Buddhist epistemological literature to designate the abstract property a source must possess to count as genuinely knowledge-yielding. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 2.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** अप्रमाण
**Transliteration:** apramāṇa
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramāṇa*
**Natural English Translation:** invalid means of knowledge; lacking epistemic warrant
**Usage:** Used in epistemological literature to designate a source or means of cognition that fails to meet the criteria for validity; also used in general contexts to characterize a person or text as lacking authority. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 1.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमाणभूत
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-bhūta
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *bhūta* (become, being, from root *bhū-*)
**Natural English Translation:** one who has become an authority; a recognized standard
**Usage:** Used to characterize a person, text, or tradition recognized as an authoritative standard; appears in philosophical and legal literature. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 3.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** पramāṇābhāsa
**Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāsa
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *ābhāsa* (semblance, pseudo-)
**Natural English Translation:** a pseudo-valid means of knowledge; an apparent but not genuine epistemic source
**Usage:** Used in epistemological literature to designate something that resembles a valid means of knowledge but fails to meet the criteria for genuine validity; parallel to *hetvābhāsa*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 6.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमाणसिद्ध
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa-siddha
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *siddha* (established, proven, from root *sidh-*)
**Natural English Translation:** established by valid means of knowledge; epistemically warranted
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and legal literature to characterize a claim or fact established through a recognized valid means of knowledge, as opposed to what is merely asserted. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 22.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमाणीकरण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa
**Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + denominative *-ī-* + *karaṇa* (making, from root *kṛ-*)
**Natural English Translation:** authentication, certification, validation
**Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to designate the act of authenticating or certifying something as valid or authoritative; appears in modern Sanskrit administrative usage. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 21.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** svataḥ-prāmāṇya
**Literal Breakdown:** *svataḥ* (from itself, intrinsically) + *prāmāṇya* (validity)
**Natural English Translation:** intrinsic validity; the view that cognitions are self-validating
**Usage:** A central technical term in *Mīmāṃsā* epistemology designating the doctrine that valid cognitions carry their validity intrinsically; contrasted with *parataḥ-prāmāṇya*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 11.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Derived Form:** परतःप्रामाण्य
**Transliteration:** parataḥ-prāmāṇya
**Literal Breakdown:** *parataḥ* (from another, extrinsically) + *prāmāṇya* (validity)
**Natural English Translation:** extrinsic validity; the view that cognitions require external confirmation
**Usage:** The *Nyāya* position on epistemic validity, contrasted with the *Mīmāṃsā* doctrine of *svataḥ-prāmāṇya*; used in debates about the source and criterion of epistemic validity. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 12.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 6 — प्रमा / *pramā*

---

**27.**
**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
**Derived Form:** अप्रमा
**Transliteration:** apramā
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *pramā* (valid cognition)
**Natural English Translation:** erroneous cognition, invalid cognition, false apprehension
**Usage:** Used in *Nyāya* epistemological literature as the general term for any cognition that fails to accurately represent its object; covers *bhrānti*, *saṃśaya*, and *tarka* as species. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 7.)

---

**28.**
**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमातृ
**Transliteration:** pramātṛ
**Literal Breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* + agent suffix *-tṛ*; "the one who knows validly"
**Natural English Translation:** the knowing subject, the cognizer, the epistemic agent
**Usage:** Used in epistemological literature to designate the subject who undergoes valid cognition, as one of the three components of the knowledge-situation (*pramātṛ*, *pramāṇa*, *prameya*). (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 8.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
**Derived Form:** प्रमेय
**Transliteration:** prameya
**Literal Breakdown:** *pra-* + *mā-* + gerundive suffix *-ya*; "that which is to be known/measured"
**Natural English Translation:** the object of valid cognition; the knowable
**Usage:** Used in *Nyāya* and related epistemological literature to designate the object of valid cognition; the *Nyāyasūtra* lists twelve *prameyas* as objects whose knowledge leads to liberation. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 9.)
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)

**Natural English Translation:** the quality of being righteous; the nature of duty or law
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and legal literature to designate the abstract property of conforming to *dharma*; appears in Buddhist literature also to designate the nature of phenomena (*dharmatā* as the nature of *dharmas*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**36.**
**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Derived Form:** धर्मिन्

**Natural English Translation:** one who possesses *dharma*; (in logic) the subject of a property, the property-bearer
**Usage:** In *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* logic, *dharmin* designates the subject or locus that bears a property (*dharma*), as opposed to the property itself; this technical logical usage is distinct from the ethical sense.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**37.**
**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Derived Form:** धर्मशास्त्र
**Transliteration:** dharma-śāstra

**Natural English Translation:** the science or treatise of *dharma*; the body of legal and ethical literature
**Usage:** Designates the genre of Sanskrit legal and ethical literature that codifies duties, rights, and norms; includes texts such as *Manusmṛti* and *Yājñavalkyasmṛti*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**38.**
**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Derived Form:** धर्मराज
**Transliteration:** dharma-rāja
**Literal Breakdown:** *dharma* + *rāja* (king, from root *rāj-*)
**Natural English Translation:** king of *dharma*; an epithet of Yudhiṣṭhira and of Yama
**Usage:** Used in epic literature as an epithet of Yudhiṣṭhira (the most righteous of the Pāṇḍavas) and of Yama (the god of death, who judges the dead according to their *dharma*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]
