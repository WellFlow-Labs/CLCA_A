## 1. ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**NP + copula (often zero in present) + adjective/noun** expressing truth, correctness, reliability, authenticity, or correspondence.

### Examples
- זה נכון. → *ze nakhon* → “That is true/correct.”
- המקור מהימן. → *ha-maqor meheman* → “The source is reliable.”

### Notes
Present-tense Hebrew commonly uses zero copula, so attribution is often just **subject + predicate**. Predicates in this domain are typically adjectives/participles such as **נכון, אמין, מהימן, מדויק, אותנטי, זהה, תואם**.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
### Pattern
**NP + לא / אינו + predicate** for falsity, inaccuracy, unreliability, inauthenticity, or non-correspondence.

### Examples
- זה לא נכון. → *ze lo nakhon* → “That is not true/correct.”
- התיאור אינו תואם למציאות. → *ha-teʾur eino toʾem la-metsiʾut* → “The description does not correspond to reality.”

### Notes
Negation is usually with **לא** in ordinary speech and often **אינו/אינה** in more formal style. Hebrew also uses lexical negatives such as **כוזב, שקרי, מזויף**, but simple clausal negation is highly productive.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION
### Pattern
**Noun + adjective/participle** modifying a statement, source, document, testimony, product, etc.

### Examples
- סיפור אמיתי → *sipur amiti* → “a true story”
- מסמך מזויף → *mismakh mezuyaf* → “a forged document”

### Notes
The modifier normally follows the noun and agrees with it. This pattern is central for truth-related evaluation: **טענה שקרית, עדות מהימנה, תיאור מדויק, אוכל אותנטי**.

## 5. CAUSATIVE
### Pattern
**Verb of causing/revealing/justifying + truth-domain noun/adjective**, especially with **להצדיק** “justify” and truth-revelation verbs.

### Examples
- להוציא את האמת לאור → *lehotsi et ha-ʾemet la-ʾor* → “to bring the truth to light”
- להצדיק את הטענה → *lehatsdiq et ha-taʿanah* → “to justify the claim”

### Notes
A direct causative morphology is clearly attested with **להצדיק** from the צדק root. Elsewhere causation is often lexical/periphrastic, especially with verbs like **להוציא**, **להוכיח**, **לגלות** plus a truth noun or proposition.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION
### Pattern
**Abstract noun** naming truth, reliability, precision, justice/rightness, essence, or forgery.

### Examples
- אמת → *ʾemet* → “truth”
- אמינות → *ʾaminut* → “reliability / credibility”

### Notes
Hebrew uses lexical abstract nouns extensively in this domain: **אמת, דיוק, אמינות, מהימנות, צדק, הצדקה, מהות, זיוף**. These function as heads of NP constructions such as **מהימנות המקור** and **חוסר דיוק**.

## 8. LIGHT-VERB / SUPPORT-VERB CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**Verb + truth-domain noun**, especially with **אמת, צדק, שקר, אמינות**.

### Examples
- לומר את האמת → *lomar et ha-ʾemet* → “to tell the truth”
- לעשות צדק → *laʿasot tsedeq* → “to do justice”

### Notes
This is one of the strongest construction types in the data. Hebrew often packages truth-related meanings in verb+noun combinations rather than in single verbs.

## 9. DISCOURSE-MARKER CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**Fixed phrase with אמת / דיוק** introducing clarification, candor, or correction.

### Examples
- למען האמת → *lemaʿan ha-ʾemet* → “to tell the truth / honestly”
- ליתר דיוק → *le-yeter diyuq* → “more precisely”

### Notes
These are structurally frozen or semi-frozen discourse frames. They do not simply predicate truth; they manage stance, correction, and reformulation.

## 10. CORRESPONDENCE / COMPARISON CONSTRUCTIONS
### Pattern
**Predicate + complement with ל־ / את** expressing resemblance, identity, or match.

### Examples
- זהה ל־ → *zehe le-* → “identical to—”
- תואם את העובדות → *toʾem et ha-uvdot* → “matches the facts”

### Notes
Hebrew distinguishes graded relations here: **דומה ל־** for resemblance, **זהה ל־** for identity, and **תואם ל־ / תואם את** for correspondence/compliance. These are central for truth-as-match meanings.

## 11. FORMAL FALSEHOOD LABELING
### Pattern
**Noun + formal adjective of falsity** in legal/journalistic style.

### Examples
- הצהרה כוזבת → *hatsaharah kozevet* → “false declaration”
- טענה שקרית → *taʿanah sheqrit* → “false claim”

### Notes
This pattern is especially characteristic of formal written Hebrew. It contrasts with everyday clausal negation like **זה לא נכון** and is used for institutionally evaluated claims, testimony, and representations.
