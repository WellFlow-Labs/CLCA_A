1. **진실**
   - **Transliteration:** jinsil
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 眞實 “true + real”
   - **Typical English translations:** truth; reality; genuineness
   - **Usage:** 진실 is a very common noun for “truth,” especially when contrasting truth with lies, concealment, or distortion. It is used in everyday speech as well as journalism, law, and moral discussion, as in 진실을 밝히다 “to reveal the truth.”
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **참**
   - **Transliteration:** cham
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** native Korean adjective/adverb stem meaning “true; really”
   - **Typical English translations:** true; genuine; real; really
   - **Usage:** 참 appears both as an adjective-like modifier in compounds and as an adverb meaning “really.” In evaluative contexts it can mark something as genuine or authentic, as in 참사랑 “true love” or 참된 마음 “a sincere/true heart.”
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **참되다**
   - **Transliteration:** chamdoeda
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 참 “true” + 되다 “become/be”
   - **Typical English translations:** be true; be genuine; be sincere; be right
   - **Usage:** 참되다 is used for persons, attitudes, teachings, or values judged to be genuinely right or sincere. It is more literary or formal than plain everyday predicates, and often appears in moral, religious, or philosophical contexts.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **진정하다**
   - **Transliteration:** jinjeonghada
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 眞正하다 “be truly correct/genuine”
   - **Typical English translations:** be genuine; be authentic; be true
   - **Usage:** 진정하다 is a formal adjective used to describe something as genuinely of a certain kind, often in writing or elevated speech. It commonly appears in forms like 진정한 사랑 “true love” or 진정한 의미 “the true meaning.”
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **진짜**
   - **Transliteration:** jinjja
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** lexicalized form; no transparent modern breakdown for most speakers
   - **Typical English translations:** real; genuine; authentic; for real
   - **Usage:** 진짜 is extremely common in spoken Korean for distinguishing the genuine from the fake, and also as an adverb meaning “really.” It applies to objects, feelings, and situations, though in formal writing it is often replaced by more formal terms like 진정한 or 진실한.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **가짜**
   - **Transliteration:** gajja
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** lexical item meaning “fake, imitation”
   - **Typical English translations:** fake; false; counterfeit
   - **Usage:** 가짜 is the ordinary opposite of 진짜 in many contexts involving objects, documents, claims, or appearances. It is widely used for counterfeit goods, forged materials, and figuratively for insincere or bogus persons or performances.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **사실**
   - **Transliteration:** sasil
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 事實 “matter/event + reality”
   - **Typical English translations:** fact; truth; actuality
   - **Usage:** 사실 refers to what is factually the case and is common in both everyday and formal discourse. It is used when evaluating whether a statement corresponds to reality, as in 사실 여부 “whether something is true.”
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **사실이다**
   - **Transliteration:** sasirida
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 사실 “fact” + copula
   - **Typical English translations:** be true; be a fact
   - **Usage:** 사실이다 is a standard way to assert that a proposition is factually correct. It is common in argumentation, reporting, and legal or public discourse when confirming the truth of a claim.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **허위**
   - **Transliteration:** heowi
   - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 虛僞 “empty/false + deceit”
   - **Typical English translations:** falsehood; falsity; fraudulent; untrue
   - **Usage:** 허위 is a formal term used especially in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts for false statements, false reporting, or fraudulent representation. It often appears in compounds such as 허위 사실 “false facts/false allegations” and 허위 신고 “false report.”
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **거짓**
    - **Transliteration:** geojit
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** native Korean noun/adnominal form meaning “lie; falsehood”
    - **Typical English translations:** lie; falsehood; false
    - **Usage:** 거짓 is a basic and very common term for falsity, whether in speech, representation, or appearance. It appears in expressions like 거짓말 “lie” and 거짓 정보 “false information,” and can describe both deliberate lying and simple untruth.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **정확하다**
    - **Transliteration:** jeonghwakhada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 正確하다 “correct + exact”
    - **Typical English translations:** be accurate; be exact; be precise
    - **Usage:** 정확하다 is used for measurements, descriptions, statements, memory, timing, and representation when judged for accuracy. It is one of the most common evaluative terms for correspondence to fact or exact detail.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **정확성**
    - **Transliteration:** jeonghwakseong
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 正確性 “accuracy/exactness + quality”
    - **Typical English translations:** accuracy; precision; exactness
    - **Usage:** 정확성 is the noun used when discussing the degree of accuracy of information, translation, reporting, or technical output. It is common in academic, media, and professional contexts where reliability of representation is assessed.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **올바르다**
    - **Transliteration:** olbareuda
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** native Korean adjective meaning “be upright/correct”
    - **Typical English translations:** be correct; be right; be proper
    - **Usage:** 올바르다 is widely used for judgments about conduct, attitude, method, language use, and moral correctness. It can refer both to factual or procedural rightness and to ethical propriety, depending on context.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **옳다**
    - **Transliteration:** olta
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** native Korean adjective meaning “be right”
    - **Typical English translations:** be right; be correct; be just
    - **Usage:** 옳다 is a basic evaluative predicate for saying that a judgment, action, or principle is right. It is common in moral reasoning, argument, and education, and can cover both correctness and ethical rightness.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **정당하다**
    - **Transliteration:** jeongdanghada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 正當하다 “proper/rightful”
    - **Typical English translations:** be just; be legitimate; be justified; be proper
    - **Usage:** 정당하다 is used when evaluating actions, claims, punishment, authority, or procedures as justified or legitimate. It is especially common in legal, political, and ethical discussion rather than in ordinary factual assessment.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **타당하다**
    - **Transliteration:** tadanghada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 妥當하다 “appropriate; valid”
    - **Typical English translations:** be valid; be sound; be reasonable; be appropriate
    - **Usage:** 타당하다 is often used for arguments, interpretations, decisions, and grounds that are judged intellectually or procedurally well-founded. It is common in academic, legal, and formal evaluative writing.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **유효하다**
    - **Transliteration:** yuhyohada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 有效하다 “have effect/validity”
    - **Typical English translations:** be valid; be effective; be in force
    - **Usage:** 유효하다 is used for contracts, documents, votes, licenses, arguments, and methods when they count as valid or operative. In legal and administrative settings it often contrasts with 무효하다 “be invalid.”
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **무효**
    - **Transliteration:** muhyo
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 無效 “without validity/effect”
    - **Typical English translations:** invalidity; nullity; invalid
    - **Usage:** 무효 is a common formal term for something lacking legal or procedural validity. It appears in expressions like 무효 판결 “judgment of invalidity” and 무효표 “invalid ballot,” and can also be used more broadly for something that does not count.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **신뢰하다**
    - **Transliteration:** silloehada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 信賴하다 “trust + rely”
    - **Typical English translations:** trust; rely on
    - **Usage:** 신뢰하다 is used for trusting persons, institutions, testimony, systems, or information sources. It evaluates reliability rather than truth directly, and is common in social, political, and professional contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **신뢰성**
    - **Transliteration:** silloeseong
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 信賴性 “trustworthiness/reliability + quality”
    - **Typical English translations:** reliability; trustworthiness; credibility
    - **Usage:** 신뢰성 is used for data, research, devices, witnesses, media, and institutions when discussing how dependable they are. It is especially frequent in technical, academic, and evaluative discourse.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **믿을 만하다**
    - **Transliteration:** mideul manhada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 믿다 “believe” + -(으)ㄹ 만하다 “worth being / sufficiently”
    - **Typical English translations:** be trustworthy; be reliable; be believable
    - **Usage:** 믿을 만하다 is a fixed evaluative expression for saying that a person, report, or source is reliable enough to trust. It is common in everyday speech and writing and often sounds less formal than 신뢰할 수 있다.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **진실하다**
    - **Transliteration:** jinsilhada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 眞實하다 “be truthful/true”
    - **Typical English translations:** be truthful; be sincere; be true
    - **Usage:** 진실하다 is used mainly for persons, speech, or attitudes judged to be truthful or sincere. It is less common than the noun 진실, but still well attested in literary, moral, and descriptive contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **진실성**
    - **Transliteration:** jinsilseong
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 眞實性 “truthfulness/genuineness + quality”
    - **Typical English translations:** sincerity; truthfulness; authenticity
    - **Usage:** 진실성 refers to the quality of being genuine or truthful, especially in a person’s attitude, expression, or artistic or political stance. It is used in criticism and public discourse when evaluating whether something feels honest rather than contrived.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **본질**
    - **Transliteration:** bonjil
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 本質 “root/origin + substance/quality”
    - **Typical English translations:** essence; nature; substance
    - **Usage:** 본질 is used when discussing what something fundamentally is, beyond appearance or incidental features. It is common in philosophy, criticism, education, and everyday abstract discussion, as in 문제의 본질 “the essence of the problem.”
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **실체**
    - **Transliteration:** silche
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 實體 “real + body/entity”
    - **Typical English translations:** substance; reality; actual entity; true nature
    - **Usage:** 실체 refers to the actual substance or real state of something, often contrasted with appearance, rumor, or abstraction. It is used in philosophy, law, and journalism, for example in expressions like 사건의 실체 “the true substance of the case.”
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **닮다**
    - **Transliteration:** damda
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** native Korean verb meaning “resemble”
    - **Typical English translations:** resemble; look like; take after
    - **Usage:** 닮다 is the ordinary verb for resemblance in appearance, character, or style. It is relevant when evaluating whether one thing faithfully resembles another, including persons, artworks, and representations.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

27. **흡사하다**
    - **Transliteration:** heupsahada
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 恰似하다 “closely resemble”
    - **Typical English translations:** be very similar; closely resemble; be almost identical
    - **Usage:** 흡사하다 is a somewhat formal or literary term for strong resemblance. It is often used in descriptive writing, criticism, or reporting when one thing appears strikingly like another.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

28. **모사**
    - **Transliteration:** mosa
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 模寫 “imitate + copy/depict”
    - **Typical English translations:** copying; imitation; reproduction
    - **Usage:** 모사 is used in art, representation, and sometimes criminal or forensic contexts for copying or reproducing something closely. It can be neutral in artistic training but may also imply imitation that raises questions of authenticity or originality.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

29. **위작**
    - **Transliteration:** wijak
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 僞作 “false + work”
    - **Typical English translations:** forgery; fake work; counterfeit artwork
    - **Usage:** 위작 is a domain-specific but well-attested term used especially in art and cultural-property contexts for forged works falsely presented as genuine. It directly concerns judgments of authenticity and authorship.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

30. **정품**
    - **Transliteration:** jeongpum
    - **Literal meaning / breakdown:** 正品 “proper/genuine + product”
    - **Typical English translations:** genuine product; authentic item
    - **Usage:** 정품 is widely used in commerce to distinguish authentic branded goods from counterfeits. It is especially common in consumer contexts involving luxury goods, electronics, cosmetics, and certification of genuineness.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
