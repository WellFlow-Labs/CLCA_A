# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1 and P1b)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This document records attested derivational morphology for the P1 lexical items. The inventory draws on forms documented in classical Sanskrit literature, philosophical treatises, grammatical texts, legal literature, and (where noted) modern Sanskrit-medium usage. Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The constraint against paradigm completion is observed throughout: only forms with genuine attestation or strong philological support are included. Gaps are preserved and noted explicitly.

The organization follows the P1 item list. For each P1 item, derivations are listed as attested. Where no common derivation beyond what P1b already documents is identified, this is stated explicitly.

## P1 Item 1: सत्य / *satya*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यता
3. **Transliteration:** satyatā
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state or property of being true"
5. **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, truth as an abstract property
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical and ethical discourse to name the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from particular true statements or acts. Appears in characterizations of persons and propositions. Appears in *Nyāya* commentaries and *Dharmaśāstra*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 8.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यत्व
3. **Transliteration:** satyatva
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *-tva* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being true"
5. **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, the property of being true
6. **Usage:** Parallel to *satyatā* but formed with the *-tva* suffix; used in philosophical prose to name the abstract property of truth. Both *satyatā* and *satyatva* are attested, with *satyatā* somewhat more common in ethical contexts and *satyatva* more common in technical philosophical argument. A more technical philosophical variant appearing in Navya-Nyāya logical literature to refer to the property of truth as a formal category subject to definition and analysis.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सात्य
3. **Transliteration:** sātya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + secondary adjectival derivation (vṛddhi of initial syllable); "relating to or characterized by truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** relating to truth, of the nature of truth
6. **Usage:** Attested as a secondary adjectival form in some philosophical and grammatical contexts, though less common than *satya* itself used adjectivally. The form is morphologically regular but its independent attestation is limited. Appears in Vedic and post-Vedic literature as a secondary adjectival formation from *satya*, used to characterize what pertains to or partakes of truth.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यापन
3. **Transliteration:** satyāpana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *āpana* (causative action noun from root *āp-*, to reach/attain); "causing truth to be established, verification"
5. **Natural English translation:** verification, attestation, confirmation
6. **Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for the act of verifying or attesting to the truth of a statement or document. Standard in modern Sanskrit-medium official usage. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, items 7 and 13.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यापित
3. **Transliteration:** satyāpita
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *āpita* (past passive participle of causative of *āp-*); "caused to be verified, attested"
5. **Natural English translation:** verified, attested, certified
6. **Usage:** Used in legal and administrative documents to indicate that a statement or copy has been officially verified. Standard in modern Sanskrit-influenced official language. The past passive participle form corresponding to *satyāpana*; appears on official documents to indicate that a statement has been verified. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 8.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यवादिन्
3. **Transliteration:** satyavādin
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* (agent noun from root *vad-*, to speak); "one who speaks truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully
6. **Usage:** Used in epic and Dharmaśāstra literature to characterize a person whose speech is reliably truthful; appears as an epithet of virtuous figures and as a category in discussions of reliable testimony. Used as a term of praise in *Dharmaśāstra* witness evaluation. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, items 2 and 9.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यवचन
3. **Transliteration:** satyavacana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vacana* (speech, utterance, from root *vac-*); "truthful utterance"
5. **Natural English translation:** truthful speech, speaking the truth
6. **Usage:** Used in ethical and legal contexts to designate the act or practice of speaking truthfully; appears in Dharmaśāstra enumerations of virtuous conduct and in vow formulas. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 3.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यव्रत
3. **Transliteration:** satyavrata
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vrata* (vow, observance); "vow of truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** vow of truthfulness; one who has taken a vow of truth
6. **Usage:** Used in epic and Purāṇic literature both as a compound noun naming the vow of always speaking truth and as an epithet of figures who have taken such a vow; also functions as a proper name. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, items 4 and 11.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यसंध
3. **Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* (bound, pledged, from root *dhā-* with *sam-*); "bound to truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** true to one's word, faithful to one's pledge
6. **Usage:** Used in epic literature as an epithet of heroes and kings who keep their promises; evaluates reliability and fidelity to commitments as a form of truthfulness. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 5.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यनिष्ठ
3. **Transliteration:** satyaniṣṭha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *niṣṭha* (devoted to, grounded in, from root *sthā-*); "grounded in truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** devoted to truth, steadfast in truthfulness
6. **Usage:** Used in ethical and religious literature to characterize a person whose conduct and character are firmly grounded in truthfulness; appears in enumerations of virtuous qualities. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 6.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 11**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यप्रतिज्ञ
3. **Transliteration:** satyapratijna
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijñā* (pledge, promise, from *prati-* + root *jñā-*); "whose pledge is truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** one whose promises are true, one who keeps his word
6. **Usage:** Used in epic literature as an epithet of figures renowned for keeping their pledges; evaluates the reliability of a person's commitments as a form of truthfulness. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 12.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यशोधन
3. **Transliteration:** satyaśodhana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *śodhana* (purification, investigation, from root *śudh-*); "investigation of truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** investigation of truth, truth-seeking, verification
6. **Usage:** Used in modern Sanskrit and Sanskrit-influenced discourse for the process of investigating or establishing the truth of a matter; appears in academic and journalistic contexts. Used in modern Sanskrit-influenced discourse, including Gandhi's autobiography (*Satya-śodhana* / *My Experiments with Truth*), to denote the systematic pursuit of truth. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 30.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 13**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यग्रह
3. **Transliteration:** satyagraha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *graha* (grasping, holding, from root *grah-*); "holding to truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** holding to truth, truth-force
6. **Usage:** Coined or crystallized by Gandhi as a term for nonviolent resistance grounded in truth; widely used in political, historical, and ethical discourse in modern Indian languages and internationally. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 9.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 14**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्याग्रह
3. **Transliteration:** satyāgraha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence, from *ā-* + root *grah-*); "holding firmly to truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** insistence on truth, truth-force
6. **Usage:** Systematized by Mohandas Gandhi to denote nonviolent resistance grounded in the force of truth; used in political, ethical, and historical discourse. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 14.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 15**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यवाक्य
3. **Transliteration:** satya-vākya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *vākya* (utterance, from root *vac-*)
5. **Natural English translation:** a true statement, a truthful utterance
6. **Usage:** Used in logical, legal, and ethical contexts to designate an utterance that corresponds to the facts; appears in discussions of testimony (*śabda-pramāṇa*). (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 1, item 10.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 16**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** असत्य
3. **Transliteration:** asatya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *satya*; "not true"
5. **Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
6. **Usage:** Used across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as the direct negation of *satya*, denoting a false statement or the quality of being untrue. Common in moral prescriptions against lying.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The base *satya* is highly productive in compounding. The forms above represent the most clearly derivational patterns. Additional compounds (*satyaśīla*, *satyaparāyaṇa*) exist but are not separately documented here as they do not introduce additional derivational morphology beyond compounding.

## P1 Item 2: ऋत / *ṛta*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतधामन्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtadhāman
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *dhāman* (abode, seat, from root *dhā-*); "whose abode is cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one whose dwelling is cosmic order; an epithet of Varuṇa and other Vedic deities
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities associated with cosmic order, particularly Varuṇa; evaluates divine nature in terms of conformity to *ṛta*. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*. (Cross-referenced from P1b supplementary expansion, item 1; P1b Root 3, item 1.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतावन्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtāvan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *-van* (possessive suffix); "possessing ṛta, conforming to cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who possesses or upholds cosmic order
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities and persons who uphold or embody *ṛta*; evaluates conformity to cosmic and moral order. Appears in the *Ṛgveda* and *Atharvaveda*. (Cross-referenced from P1b supplementary expansion, item 2; P1b Root 3, item 2.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतज्ञ
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtajña
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *jña* (knowing, from root *jñā-*); "knowing cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who knows cosmic order, knower of the right
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic literature for one who has knowledge of *ṛta*; evaluates knowledge in terms of conformity to the right order of things. Appears in the *Ṛgveda*. (Cross-referenced from P1b supplementary expansion, item 3; P1b Root 3, item 3.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतस्य पथा
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtasya pathā
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛtasya* (genitive of *ṛta*) + *pathā* (instrumental of *path*, path); "by the path of cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** by the path of truth or right order
6. **Usage:** A fixed phrase in Vedic hymns (Ṛgveda) designating the way of cosmic order along which the gods and the righteous travel; used in ritual and cosmological contexts. (Cross-referenced from P1b supplementary expansion, item 4.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतचित्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtacit
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *cit* (perceiving, knowing, from root *cit-*); "perceiving cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who perceives or knows cosmic order
6. **Usage:** Attested in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities and seers who have insight into *ṛta*; the form is Vedic and does not extend into classical Sanskrit.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतप्रजात
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtaprajāta
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *prajāta* (born, produced, from *pra-* + root *jan-*); "born of cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** born of right order, produced by cosmic truth
6. **Usage:** Attested in Vedic hymns as a characterization of divine beings or sacred fires understood as arising from *ṛta*; confined to Vedic usage.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतवान्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtavān
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + possessive suffix *-vat* (masculine nominative *-vān*); "one who possesses cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who embodies right order, righteous
6. **Usage:** Appears in Vedic literature as a characterization of persons or deities who uphold or embody *ṛta*; the form is confined to archaic Vedic contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 8**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतधीति
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtadhīti
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *dhīti* (thought, insight, from root *dhī-*); "thought/insight in accordance with cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** right-minded thought, insight aligned with cosmic order
6. **Usage:** A Vedic compound appearing in the Ṛgveda to characterize inspired or correct thought; confined to Vedic poetic contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 9**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतंभरा
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtaṃbharā
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛtam* (accusative of *ṛta*) + *bharā* (bearing, from root *bhṛ-*); "truth-bearing"
5. **Natural English translation:** truth-bearing; an epithet of a special type of wisdom in Yoga philosophy
6. **Usage:** Used in *Yogasūtra* 1.48 to designate the highest form of wisdom (*prajñā*) that directly bears or carries truth; also used as an epithet of Vedic speech. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 3, item 11.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The root *ṛta* is derivationally productive primarily within Vedic Sanskrit. Its compound formation in post-Vedic classical Sanskrit is limited. The forms listed above represent the main attested expressions; no further common derivations are identified.

## P1 Item 3: अनृत / *anṛta*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** अनृतवचन
3. **Transliteration:** anṛtavacana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *anṛta* + *vacana* (speech, utterance, from root *vac-*); "speech contrary to cosmic order, false speech"
5. **Natural English translation:** false speech, lying, speech contrary to right order
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic and Dharmaśāstra literature for the category of false or wrongful speech; appears in enumerations of wrongful acts of speech alongside other violations of right conduct. Appears in *Dharmaśāstra* lists of prohibited conduct. (Cross-referenced from P1b supplementary expansion, item 5; P1b Root 3, item 10.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** आनृत्य
3. **Transliteration:** ānṛtya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *anṛta* + secondary suffix *-ya* (with *vṛddhi*); "the state or quality of being *anṛta*"
5. **Natural English translation:** falsehood, the quality of being false or contrary to order
6. **Usage:** Appears in Vedic and early post-Vedic legal literature to denote the abstract quality of falsehood or disorder; less common than *anṛta* itself and confined to archaic or formal registers. Used in contexts discussing the sin of lying.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** अनृतभाषिन्
3. **Transliteration:** anṛta-bhāṣin
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *anṛta* + *bhāṣin* (speaking, from root *bhāṣ-*)
5. **Natural English translation:** one who speaks falsehood, a liar
6. **Usage:** Used in ethical and legal literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks falsely; appears in *Dharmaśāstra* discussions of witness disqualification. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 3, item 9.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation:** *Anṛta* is less derivationally productive than *asatya* in post-Vedic Sanskrit. The form functions primarily as a simple negation of *ṛta* in Vedic and early classical contexts.

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form 3**

---

**Note:** *Asatya* generates a derivational family parallel to *satya* but smaller in extent. The forms listed above represent the main attested expressions. No further common derivations are identified.

## P1 Item 5: प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रामाण्य
3. **Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ya* (abstract adjectival suffix, with vṛddhi of initial syllable); "the property of being a valid means of knowledge"
5. **Natural English translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the property of being a genuine means of knowledge
6. **Usage:** A central technical term in Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya epistemology for the property that makes a cognition or means of knowledge valid; debates about whether *prāmāṇya* is intrinsic (*svataḥ*) or extrinsic (*parataḥ*) are among the most important in Sanskrit epistemology. Used as the abstract noun for the property that makes something a *pramāṇa*. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 3; P1b Root 2, item 13.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** अप्रामाण्य
3. **Transliteration:** aprāmāṇya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *prāmāṇya*; "invalidity, lack of epistemic authority"
5. **Natural English translation:** invalidity, lack of epistemic authority
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological literature for the property of a cognition or means of knowledge that fails to be valid; appears in discussions of how invalidity is recognized and what causes it. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 6.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमाणित
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇita
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ita* (past passive participle suffix); "made authoritative, validated"
5. **Natural English translation:** validated, authenticated, certified, proven
6. **Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for something that has been officially validated or proven; appears in modern Sanskrit-influenced official language and in philosophical contexts for a claim that has been established. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, item 17.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमाणीकरण
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ī-* (denominative) + *karaṇa* (making, from root *kṛ-*); "making into an authority, validating"
5. **Natural English translation:** validation, authentication, certification
6. **Usage:** Used in modern Sanskrit-influenced administrative and legal language for the act of officially validating or authenticating a document, claim, or person. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, items 16 and 21.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमाणभूत
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇabhūta
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *bhūta* (become, being, past passive participle of root *bhū-*); "having become an authority"
5. **Natural English translation:** authoritative, one who has become a valid authority
6. **Usage:** Used in Buddhist epistemology as an epithet of the Buddha, characterizing him as one whose cognitions are reliably valid; Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika* opens with the characterization of the Buddha as *pramāṇabhūta*. Also used more broadly for any authoritative source or person. (Cross-referenced from P1b Root 2, items 3 and 10.)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमाणाभास
3. **Transliteration:** pramāṇābhāsa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *ābhāsa* (semblance, appearance
