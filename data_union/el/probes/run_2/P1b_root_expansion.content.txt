# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** ψευδ- / ΨΕΥΔ-
**Transliteration:** psevd-
**P1 items participating:** 3 — ψεύδος (14), ψευδής (15), ψεύτικος (13)
**Core meaning contribution:** Falsehood, fabrication, the condition of not being what one claims or appears to be. This root generates the primary vocabulary of lying, deception, and falsification across all registers.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** αληθ- / ΑΛΗΘ-
**Transliteration:** alith-
**P1 items participating:** 3 — αληθής (1), αλήθεια (2), αληθινός (3)
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth, unconcealment, correspondence to reality. This root is the primary positive pole of the truth domain in Greek, generating the central noun, adjective, and verb for truth across all registers.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** -πιστ- / -ΠΙΣΤ- (within the compound base ἄξιος + πίστις)
**Transliteration:** -pist-
**P1 items participating:** 2 — αξιόπιστος (16), αναξιόπιστος (17)

*Note: The root πιστ- (from πίστις, faith/trust) is highly productive across Greek but contributes only 2 items directly to P1. The root αξι- (from ἄξιος, worthy) is similarly productive but contributes only 2 items. Neither alone reaches the frequency of ψευδ- or αληθ-.*

*Revised selection: In place of a third root of comparable P1 frequency, the expansion below covers **ψευδ-** and **αληθ-** as the two primary roots, and adds **πιστ-** as the third root on the grounds that it underlies the credibility/trust sub-domain and generates a large attested family.*

**Core meaning contribution:** Trust, belief, credibility — the epistemic relation between a person and a claim or source. Generates vocabulary of reliability, faith, persuasion, and proof.

## Step 2 — Root Expansions

---

### ROOT 1: ψευδ- (psevd-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** ψεύτης
**Transliteration:** pséftis
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψεύδ- + agent suffix -της; "one who produces falsehood"
**Natural English translation:** liar
**Context of use:** Common everyday noun applied to a person who habitually or characteristically lies. Used across informal and formal registers; appears in moral judgments and legal characterizations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ψεύτρα
**Transliteration:** péftra
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψεύδ- + feminine agent suffix -τρα; "female liar"
**Natural English translation:** liar (feminine)
**Context of use:** Feminine form of ψεύτης, used in everyday speech to refer to a woman who lies. Fully colloquial and common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ψεύδομαι
**Transliteration:** psévomai
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψεύδ- + middle/deponent verbal suffix; "to produce falsehood (for oneself)"
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell a lie
**Context of use:** The primary verb for lying in Greek, used across all registers. Appears in legal testimony contexts (*ψεύδομαι ενώπιον δικαστηρίου*, "to lie before a court") and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ψευδής
**Transliteration:** psevdís
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψεύδ- + adjectival suffix -ής; "of the nature of falsehood"
**Natural English translation:** false, untrue
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 15. Included here to anchor the derivational family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ψευδώς
**Transliteration:** psevdós
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδής + adverbial suffix -ώς; "in a false manner"
**Natural English translation:** falsely, untruly
**Context of use:** Adverbial form of ψευδής. Used in formal and legal registers: *κατηγορήθηκε ψευδώς* ("he was falsely accused"). Less common in everyday speech, where *ψεύτικα* is preferred.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ψευδής κατάθεση
**Transliteration:** psevdís katáthesi
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδής (false) + κατάθεση (deposition, testimony)
**Natural English translation:** false testimony, perjury
**Context of use:** A fixed legal collocation referring to the act of giving false testimony under oath. Constitutes a criminal offense under Greek law; appears routinely in legal documents and journalism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ψευδής καταγγελία
**Transliteration:** psevdís katangelía
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδής (false) + καταγγελία (complaint, denunciation)
**Natural English translation:** false accusation, false complaint
**Context of use:** Legal collocation referring to a complaint or denunciation that is knowingly false. Used in criminal law contexts and journalistic reporting on legal proceedings.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ψευδής δήλωση
**Transliteration:** psevdís dílo̱si
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδής (false) + δήλωση (declaration, statement)
**Natural English translation:** false declaration, false statement
**Context of use:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to refer to a written or oral declaration that contains falsehoods. Appears in tax law, immigration law, and official documentation contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ψευδαίσθηση
**Transliteration:** psevdésthisi
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + αίσθηση (perception, sensation); "false perception"
**Natural English translation:** illusion, hallucination, false impression
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, medical, and everyday registers to refer to a false perception or mistaken impression. *Έχω ψευδαισθήσεις* means "I have hallucinations/illusions."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ψευδώνυμο
**Transliteration:** psevdó̱nimo
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ὄνυμα/όνομα (name); "false name"
**Natural English translation:** pseudonym, pen name, alias
**Context of use:** Applied to a false or assumed name used by an author, public figure, or criminal. Common in literary, journalistic, and legal contexts. *Γράφει με ψευδώνυμο* means "he/she writes under a pseudonym."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ψευδεπίγραφος
**Transliteration:** psevdepígrafos
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + ἐπίγραφος (inscribed, titled); "falsely attributed/titled"
**Natural English translation:** pseudepigraphical, falsely attributed
**Context of use:** Used in philological, biblical, and literary-historical scholarship to refer to a text attributed to an author who did not write it. *Ψευδεπίγραφα κείμενα* are texts of disputed or false attribution.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστήμη
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistími
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- (false, pseudo-) + επιστήμη (science, knowledge); "false science"
**Natural English translation:** pseudoscience
**Context of use:** Used in scientific, journalistic, and educational contexts to refer to claims or practices that present themselves as scientific but lack scientific validity. Common in contemporary discourse on misinformation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστημονικός
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistimoniikós
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + επιστημονικός (scientific); "falsely scientific"
**Natural English translation:** pseudoscientific
**Context of use:** Adjectival form applied to claims, methods, or publications that mimic scientific form without scientific validity. Used in academic and journalistic criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ψευδοδιανοούμενος
**Transliteration:** psevdodianoúmenos
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + διανοούμενος (intellectual); "false intellectual"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-intellectual
**Context of use:** Applied dismissively to a person who affects intellectual depth or learning without genuine substance. Used in cultural criticism and everyday evaluative speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ψευδαίσθηση ελέγχου
**Transliteration:** psevdésthisi elénhou
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδαίσθηση (illusion) + ελέγχου (of control, genitive)
**Natural English translation:** illusion of control
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used in psychology and behavioral science to refer to the cognitive bias of believing one has more control over outcomes than one actually does. Appears in academic and popular psychology writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστήμονας
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistímonas
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + επιστήμονας (scientist); "false scientist"
**Natural English translation:** pseudoscientist
**Context of use:** Applied to a person who claims scientific authority or expertise without genuine scientific credentials or methodology. Used in critical and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ψευδοπροφήτης
**Transliteration:** psevdoprofítis
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + προφήτης (prophet); "false prophet"
**Natural English translation:** false prophet
**Context of use:** Used in religious, biblical, and metaphorical contexts to refer to someone who falsely claims prophetic authority or whose predictions prove wrong. Appears in theological writing and figurative everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ψευδοδιλήμμα
**Transliteration:** psevdodilíma
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + δίλημμα (dilemma); "false dilemma"
**Natural English translation:** false dilemma
**Context of use:** A term in logic and rhetoric referring to the fallacy of presenting only two options when more exist. Used in philosophy, rhetoric, and critical thinking education.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπώνυμος
**Transliteration:** psevdoepó̱nimos
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + επώνυμος (surname, named); "falsely named"
**Natural English translation:** using a false surname, pseudonymous
**Context of use:** Applied to persons or publications using a false surname or identity. Used in legal and literary contexts, sometimes interchangeable with ψευδώνυμο in broader usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστολή
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistolí
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + επιστολή (letter); "false letter"
**Natural English translation:** forged letter, pseudonymous letter
**Context of use:** Used in philological and historical scholarship to refer to a letter falsely attributed to a historical figure. Appears in classical studies and biblical scholarship.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ψευδοαρχαίος
**Transliteration:** psevdoarhaíos
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + αρχαίος (ancient); "falsely ancient"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-archaic, falsely ancient
**Context of use:** Applied to objects, texts, or linguistic forms that are presented as ancient but are not. Used in archaeology, art history, and philology.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** ψευδοκλασικός
**Transliteration:** psevdoklasikós
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + κλασικός (classical); "falsely classical"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-classical
**Context of use:** Applied in art history and architectural criticism to works that imitate classical forms without genuine classical origin or quality.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**23.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστημονισμός
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistimoniismós
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + επιστημονισμός (scientism); "false scientism"
**Natural English translation:** pseudoscientism
**Context of use:** A more abstract term used in philosophical and sociological criticism to refer to the systematic misuse of scientific language or prestige. Less common than ψευδοεπιστήμη.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**24.**
**Form:** ψευδοδημοκρατία
**Transliteration:** psevdodimokratía
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + δημοκρατία (democracy); "false democracy"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-democracy, sham democracy
**Context of use:** Used in political science and journalism to refer to a political system that has the formal appearance of democracy without its substantive features. Common in contemporary political commentary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ψευδοδημοκρατικός
**Transliteration:** psevdodimokratikós
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδο- + δημοκρατικός (democratic); "falsely democratic"
**Natural English translation:** pseudo-democratic
**Context of use:** Adjectival form applied to institutions, procedures, or regimes that simulate democratic form without democratic substance. Used in political analysis and journalism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ψευδοεπιστήμονες της διατροφής
**Transliteration:** psevdoepistímones tis diatrofís
**Morpheme breakdown:** ψευδοεπιστήμονες (pseudoscientists) + της διατροφής (of nutrition)
**Natural English translation:** nutrition pseudoscientists
**Context of use:** A collocation used in health journalism and medical commentary to refer to persons who make false or unsubstantiated claims about nutrition under the guise of scientific authority. Cited here as an example of the productive ψευδο- + domain-noun pattern.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

*Note: The ψευδο- prefix is highly productive in Greek and can combine with virtually any domain noun to produce a "false/pseudo-X" compound. The items above represent the most common and well-attested formations. Additional forms exist (ψευδοφιλόσοφος, ψευδοθεραπεία, ψευδοθρησκεία, etc.) following the same pattern.*

### ROOT 2: αληθ- (alith-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- + nominal suffix -εια; "the state of being unconcealed"
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 2. Included here to anchor the derivational family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αληθεύω
**Transliteration:** alithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- + verbal suffix -εύω; "to be truthful, to tell the truth"
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, to be truthful, to be accurate
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, literary, and formal registers to indicate the act of speaking truthfully or the state of being accurate. *Αληθεύει ότι…* means "it is true that…" in formal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια
**Transliteration:** alithofánia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθο- (truth-) + φαίνομαι (to appear); "truth-appearing"
**Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, appearance of truth, plausibility
**Context of use:** Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophy to refer to the quality of appearing true or lifelike. Applied to narrative, argument, and representation. *Η αληθοφάνεια ενός μυθιστορήματος* means "the verisimilitude of a novel."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** αληθοφανής
**Transliteration:** alithofanís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθο- + φαίνομαι + adjectival suffix -ής; "appearing true"
**Natural English translation:** verisimilar, plausible, lifelike, credible
**Context of use:** Applied to narratives, arguments, and representations that have the appearance of truth or reality. Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophical discussion of probability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια αφήγησης
**Transliteration:** alithofánia afígisis
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude) + αφήγησης (of narration, genitive)
**Natural English translation:** narrative verisimilitude
**Context of use:** A collocation used in literary theory and narratology to refer to the quality of a narrative that makes it seem true or realistic. Common in academic literary criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** λέω την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** léo̱ tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** λέω (I say) + την (the, accusative) + αλήθεια (truth)
**Natural English translation:** I tell the truth, I am telling the truth
**Context of use:** The most common fixed expression for asserting truthfulness in everyday Greek. Used across all registers and age groups; functions as both a sincere assertion and a rhetorical marker.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Transliteration:** i alíthia íne óti
**Morpheme breakdown:** η αλήθεια (the truth) + είναι (is) + ότι (that)
**Natural English translation:** the truth is that…
**Context of use:** A very common discourse marker used to introduce a frank or corrective statement. Functions to signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment, often contrasting with a previous claim or expectation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** για την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** ya tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** για (for) + την αλήθεια (the truth)
**Natural English translation:** for the sake of truth, to be honest, truthfully
**Context of use:** A fixed prepositional phrase used as a discourse marker to signal that the speaker is about to say something frank or corrective. Common in everyday speech and informal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** αλήθεια;
**Transliteration:** alíthia?
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) used as an interrogative particle
**Natural English translation:** Really? Truly? Is that so?
**Context of use:** Used as a standalone interrogative in everyday speech to express surprise or request confirmation. Equivalent to "really?" in English. Extremely common in informal conversation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** αλήθεια να λέμε
**Transliteration:** alíthia na léme
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + να (subjunctive particle) + λέμε (we say)
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, honestly speaking, let's be honest
**Context of use:** A fixed colloquial expression used to introduce a frank or candid statement. Functions as a hedging or framing device in everyday conversation and informal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** αλήθεια ή τόλμη
**Transliteration:** alíthia í tólmi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth) + ή (or) + τόλμη (dare)
**Natural English translation:** truth or dare
**Context of use:** The name of the well-known party game, used in everyday speech and media. Included as an attested fixed expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** αληθινά
**Transliteration:** alithiná
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός + adverbial suffix -ά; "truly, genuinely"
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely
**Context of use:** Adverbial form of αληθινός, used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm a statement. *Αληθινά δεν το ήξερα* means "I truly didn't know." Also used as a discourse marker of sincerity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** αληθινός φίλος
**Transliteration:** alithinós fílos
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + φίλος (friend)
**Natural English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
**Context of use:** A very common collocation used to distinguish a genuine, loyal friend from a superficial or false one. Appears in everyday speech, literature, and moral discourse.

---

**14.**
**Form:** αληθινή αγάπη
**Transliteration:** alithiní agápi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινή (true, genuine, feminine) + αγάπη (love)
**Natural English translation:** true love, genuine love
**Context of use:** A common collocation in everyday speech, literature, and popular culture to refer to love that is genuine and deep as opposed to superficial or pretended.

---

**15.**
**Form:** αληθινή ιστορία
**Transliteration:** alithiní istoría
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινή (true) + ιστορία (story, history)
**Natural English translation:** true story, real story
**Context of use:** Applied to narratives, films, and accounts that are based on actual events. Common in journalism, publishing, and everyday speech. *Βασισμένο σε αληθινή ιστορία* means "based on a true story."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** αληθινός εαυτός
**Transliteration:** alithinós eaftós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + εαυτός (self)

**Context of use:** Used in psychological, philosophical, and popular self-help discourse to refer to a person's genuine identity as opposed to a performed or socially constructed persona.

### ROOT 3: πιστ- (pist-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** πίστη
**Transliteration:** písti

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the three roots operating in contexts where the connection to truth-evaluation, credibility, or falsification is not straightforwardly the primary function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**ψευδ- extensions:**

- **ψευδαίσθηση** appears in contexts of perceptual experience and cognitive psychology where the primary frame is perception rather than assertion or deception.
- **ψευδώνυμο** functions primarily as a neutral label for an assumed name in literary and professional contexts, without necessarily implying deception or moral fault.
- **ψευδοκλασικός** and **ψευδοαρχαίος** operate in aesthetic evaluation where the primary judgment concerns stylistic imitation rather than truth-value.
- **ψευδοδημοκρατία** and **ψευδοδημοκρατικός** function in political classification where the primary frame is institutional form rather than propositional truth.

---

**αληθ- extensions:**

- **αληθινός** in *αληθινός φίλος*, *αληθινή αγάπη* operates in the domain of relational and emotional authenticity, where the contrast is with superficiality or pretense rather than with factual falsehood.
- **αληθινός Θεός** operates in theological ontology, where the contrast is with false gods rather than with false propositions.
- **αληθοφάνεια** and **αληθοφανής** operate in aesthetic and rhetorical evaluation, where the primary concern is the effect of reality rather than actual truth.
- **αληθεύω** in *αληθεύει ότι…* functions as an impersonal truth-predicate closer to logical or propositional usage than to the experiential or relational uses of αληθινός.
- **αλήθεια** used as a standalone interrogative (*αλήθεια;*) functions as a discourse particle expressing surprise or requesting confirmation, with no direct propositional content.
- **αλήθεια να λέμε** functions as a hedging or framing device in discourse management.
- **πικρή αλήθεια** and **η αλήθεια πονάει** operate in the domain of emotional and moral experience, where truth is framed in terms of affect rather than epistemology.

---

**πιστ- extensions:**

- **πίστωση** and **πιστωτής** and **πιστωτική κάρτα** operate in financial and economic contexts where the primary frame is credit and debt rather than belief or credibility.
- **πιστός** in *πιστή μετάφραση* operates in the domain of translation fidelity and textual accuracy, where the primary concern is correspondence rather than trust.
- **πιστότητα** in *πιστότητα ήχου* (audio fidelity, hi-fi) operates in a technical-acoustic context.
- **εμπιστευτικός** and **εμπιστευτικά** operate in the domain of information privacy and confidentiality, where the primary frame is access restriction rather than truth-evaluation.
- **αυτοπεποίθηση** operates in the domain of psychological self-assessment and personal character, where the primary frame is confidence rather than belief in propositions.
- **πεποίθηση** in legal contexts (*θρησκευτική πεποίθηση*, religious conviction) operates in the domain of protected belief and identity rather than epistemological evaluation.
- **ψήφος εμπιστοσύνης** and **ψήφος δυσπιστίας** operate in the domain of parliamentary procedure and political legitimacy.
- **κατάχρηση εμπιστοσύνης** operates in the domain of legal wrongdoing and fiduciary duty.
