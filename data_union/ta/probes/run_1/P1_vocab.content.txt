# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (Tamil), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** உண்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Derived from *uḷ* (to be/exist) + nominalizing suffix; "that which is"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuineness
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal contexts to assert that a statement or state of affairs corresponds to reality. Participates in productive compounds such as *uṇmaiyāna* (true/genuine, attributive) and *uṇmaiyil* (in truth, adverbial). Contrasts with *poyymai* (falsehood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** பொய்
**Transliteration:** poy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Monomorphemic root; "falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, falsity
**Usage:** Applied to statements, persons, and representations judged to be false or deceptive. Functions as both noun and attributive modifier (*poy-yāna*, false). Appears in moral and legal condemnation of deceptive conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** நேர்மை
**Transliteration:** nērmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nēr* (straight, direct) + *-mai* (abstract nominalizer); "straightness"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness, straightforwardness
**Usage:** Describes a person's character or conduct as honest and non-deceptive. Commonly used in moral evaluation of individuals in personal, professional, and civic contexts. Contrasts with *vaஞcaṉai* (deceit) and *kāppaṭṭam* (hypocrisy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** உண்மையான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāna
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* + *-āna* (adjectival copular suffix); "that which is truth"
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** Attributive adjective modifying nouns to indicate genuineness or factual accuracy. Used of objects (genuine goods), persons (a true friend), and propositions (a true statement). Highly productive in contemporary spoken and written Tamil.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** நம்பகமான
**Transliteration:** nampakamāna
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nampu* (to trust/believe) + *-akam* (nominalizing infix) + *-āna* (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, and information judged worthy of trust. Frequently used in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The base noun *nampakam* also occurs independently.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை
**Transliteration:** nampikkai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *nampu* (to trust) + *-ikkai* (action/state nominalizer); "the act or state of trusting"
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence
**Usage:** Refers to the epistemic or affective state of trusting a person, institution, or claim. Used in both religious contexts (faith) and secular evaluative contexts (confidence in reliability). Participates in compounds such as *nampikkaiyiṉmai* (lack of trust).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** சரி
**Transliteration:** cari
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly from Sanskrit *sari* (equal, correct); "correctness, rightness"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper
**Usage:** Extremely common in spoken Tamil to affirm that something is correct, appropriate, or in order. Used to evaluate statements, actions, calculations, and conduct. Also functions as a discourse particle confirming agreement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** தவறு
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *tavar* (to err, deviate); "error, deviation"
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, wrong, fault
**Usage:** Applied to incorrect statements, mistaken beliefs, and morally wrong actions. Contrasts with *cari* (correct) in evaluative contexts. Used in both epistemic (factual error) and moral (wrongdoing) registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** உண்மைத்தன்மை
**Transliteration:** uṇmait-taṉmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* (truth) + *taṉmai* (nature, quality); "the quality of being true"
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, genuineness
**Usage:** A more abstract nominal form used in philosophical, literary, and formal evaluative discourse to refer to the property of being true or authentic. Less common in casual speech than *uṇmai* alone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** அசல்
**Transliteration:** acal
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *aṣl* (origin, root); "original, source"
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic (of objects)
**Usage:** Primarily used to distinguish genuine articles from copies or counterfeits, especially in commercial and legal contexts. Contrasts with *nakal* (copy, duplicate). Common in everyday speech when evaluating goods.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** நகல்
**Transliteration:** nakal
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic/Persian *naqal* (copy, transfer); "copy, duplicate"
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, imitation, counterfeit
**Usage:** Used to describe reproductions or imitations of an original, often in contexts where authenticity is being evaluated. Can carry neutral (certified copy of a document) or negative (fake) connotations depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** போலி
**Transliteration:** pōli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly onomatopoeic or from *pōl* (resemblance); "resemblance, imitation"
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, spurious, imitation, sham
**Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, or credentials that falsely claim to be genuine. Carries a strongly negative evaluative charge. Used in legal, commercial, and moral condemnation of fraud and misrepresentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** உண்மையில்
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* + *-il* (locative case suffix); "in truth"
**Typical English translations:** in truth, actually, in reality, truly
**Usage:** Adverbial expression used to assert that what follows reflects the actual state of affairs, often correcting a misconception or contrasting appearance with reality. Common in both spoken and written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** நேர்
**Transliteration:** nēr
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Monomorphemic root; "straight, direct, exact"
**Typical English translations:** straight, direct, exact, correct, proper
**Usage:** Used attributively and in compounds to indicate directness, exactness, or correctness. Appears in *nēr-mai* (honesty), *nēr-mukaṃ* (face-to-face), and evaluative phrases about accuracy or proper conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** நியாயம்
**Transliteration:** niyāyam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Sanskrit *nyāya* (rule, logic, justice); "justice, reason, rightness"
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, reasonableness, justification
**Usage:** Used in moral and legal contexts to evaluate whether an action, decision, or claim is just or well-founded. Also used in everyday speech to assert that something is fair or reasonable. Participates in compounds such as *niyāyamāna* (just, fair).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** அநியாயம்
**Transliteration:** aniyāyam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Sanskrit privative *a-* + *nyāya*; "absence of justice/rightness"
**Typical English translations:** injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness, unreasonableness
**Usage:** Direct antonym of *niyāyam*, used to condemn actions, decisions, or situations as unjust or morally wrong. Common in moral complaint and legal argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** சட்டப்படி
**Transliteration:** caṭṭappaṭi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *caṭṭam* (law) + *-paṭi* (according to, in accordance with); "according to law"
**Typical English translations:** legally, lawfully, according to law, by right
**Usage:** Fixed expression used to evaluate whether an action or claim is legally valid or sanctioned. Common in legal and administrative discourse. Contrasts implicitly with *caṭṭavirutti* (illegal conduct).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** தகுதி
**Transliteration:** takuti
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From *taku* (to be fitting/suitable) + *-ti* (nominalizer); "fitness, suitability"
**Typical English translations:** qualification, eligibility, merit, worthiness, fitness
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether a person or thing meets the required standard for a role, claim, or judgment. Common in legal, professional, and moral contexts. Participates in *takutiyāna* (qualified, worthy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** தகுதியற்ற
**Transliteration:** takutiyaṟṟa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *takuti* + *aṟṟa* (privative participial suffix); "lacking fitness"
**Typical English translations:** unqualified, ineligible, unworthy, unfit
**Usage:** Evaluative term applied to persons or things that fail to meet a required standard. Used in legal disqualification, moral condemnation, and professional assessment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** உண்மைக்கு மாறான
**Transliteration:** uṇmaikkum māṟāna
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uṇmai* (truth) + dative + *māṟāna* (contrary to, deviating from); "contrary to truth"
**Typical English translations:** contrary to fact, false, untrue, counterfactual
**Usage:** Fixed evaluative expression used in legal and formal written contexts to characterize a statement or representation as factually false. More formal than simply *poy* (lie).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** கற்பனை
**Transliteration:** kaṟpaṉai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *kaṟpu* (imagination, invention) + *-aṉai* (nominalizer); "that which is imagined/invented"
**Typical English translations:** fiction, imagination, fabrication, invention
**Usage:** Used to characterize a claim or narrative as invented rather than factual. Can be neutral (creative fiction) or negative (fabrication). Relevant in evaluating the factual status of representations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** வஞ்சனை
**Transliteration:** vañcaṉai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From *vañcu* (to deceive) + *-aṉai* (nominalizer); "deception"
**Typical English translations:** deceit, deception, fraud, treachery
**Usage:** Used in moral and legal contexts to characterize deliberate misleading of another person. Carries strong negative moral charge. Appears in legal terminology for fraud and in literary condemnation of betrayal.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** மெய்
**Transliteration:** mey
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Monomorphemic classical root; "body, substance, reality"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the real, body (in some contexts)
**Usage:** Classical and literary term for truth or reality, often used in philosophical and poetic contexts. Appears in compounds such as *meyññāṉam* (true knowledge) and *meypporuḷ* (true meaning/essence). Less common in everyday spoken Tamil than *uṇmai*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** மெய்ப்பிக்க
**Transliteration:** meyppiкka
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *mey* (truth/reality) + causative *-ppi-* + infinitive *-kka*; "to cause to be real/true"
**Typical English translations:** to prove, to verify, to demonstrate, to establish (a fact)
**Usage:** Used in legal and argumentative contexts for the act of proving or establishing the truth of a claim. The noun form *meyppiтtal* (proving, verification) also occurs in formal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** சாட்சி
**Transliteration:** cāṭci
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Sanskrit *sākṣin* (witness, one who sees directly); "witness, testimony"
**Typical English translations:** witness, testimony, evidence
**Usage:** Used in legal contexts for a witness or the testimony they provide. Evaluatively relevant as the basis for establishing truth in legal proceedings. Compounds include *cāṭciyam* (evidence, attestation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ஆதாரம்
**Transliteration:** ātāram
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Sanskrit *ādhāra* (support, basis, foundation); "basis, support"
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, basis, foundation, support
**Usage:** Used to refer to the evidentiary or factual basis for a claim, judgment, or belief. Common in legal, journalistic, and everyday argumentative contexts. Evaluatively central in assessing whether a claim is well-supported.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** இயல்பு
**Transliteration:** iyalpu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *iyal* (to be natural, to function naturally) + *-pu* (nominalizer); "natural state, inherent nature"
**Typical English translations:** nature, inherent quality, natural character, essence
**Usage:** Used to describe what something fundamentally or naturally is, relevant in philosophical and evaluative contexts about essential properties. Appears in discussions of whether something conforms to or deviates from its true nature.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** இலக்கணம்
**Transliteration:** ilakkaṇam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Sanskrit *lakṣaṇa* (mark, characteristic, definition); "defining characteristic, grammar, definition"
**Typical English translations:** definition, characteristic, grammar, proper form
**Usage:** In philosophical and evaluative contexts, refers to the defining characteristics that make something what it is. Used to assess whether something conforms to its proper definition or standard. Also the standard term for grammar.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** பொருத்தம்
**Transliteration:** poruttam
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *poruntu* (to fit, to be appropriate) + *-am* (nominalizer); "fittingness, appropriateness"
**Typical English translations:** appropriateness, suitability, fit, relevance, correspondence
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether something is fitting, appropriate, or corresponds correctly to a standard or context. Relevant in aesthetic, moral, and logical evaluation of whether things match or accord with each other.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** தன்மை
**Transliteration:** taṉmai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *taṉ* (self, own) + *-mai* (abstract nominalizer); "own-ness, inherent quality"
**Typical English translations:** nature, quality, characteristic, property, essence
**Usage:** Used to refer to the inherent or essential quality of a person or thing, relevant in evaluative contexts about what something truly or fundamentally is. Appears productively in compounds such as *uṇmait-taṉmai* (truthfulness) and *nērmai-taṉmai* (quality of honesty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
