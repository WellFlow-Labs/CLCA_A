# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Derivational Morphology in Chinese

Chinese morphology differs substantially from Indo-European derivational systems. The forms documented here represent attested morphological operations that produce new lexical items from P1 bases: primarily suffixation with nominalizing or verbalizing suffixes (化, 性, 者, 感, 主义), and a small number of prefixation and reduplication patterns. Compounding is the dominant word-formation process in Chinese but is treated in P1b as root expansion rather than derivation proper. Only forms where a P1 item functions as a direct base for a morphological operation — not merely as a component in a new compound — are included here. Where the boundary is unclear, this is noted.

Chinese morphology is predominantly compounding-based rather than affixational. Productive derivational affixes are few and their status as "derivational morphology" proper (as opposed to compounding) is a matter of ongoing linguistic debate. The following documents only those affixational and quasi-affixational processes that are genuinely attested and broadly recognized in the literature on Chinese word formation.

The derivational processes identified below are:

1. **Nominalization via -性 (-xìng)**
2. **Nominalization/Verbalization via -化 (-huà)**
3. **Nominalization/Agentivization via -者 (-zhě)**
4. **Experiential/Sensory Nominalization via -感 (-gǎn)**
5. **Ideological/Doctrinal Nominalization via -主义 (-zhǔyì)**
6. **Negation via 非- (fēi-)**
7. **Negation via 不- (bù-) in lexicalized forms**
8. **Negation via 无- (wú-) in lexicalized forms**
9. **Reduplication**

Gaps are preserved. Many P1 items yield no common derivation by these criteria.

## Section 1: Nominalization via -性 (-xìng)

**Suffix description:** -性 derives abstract property nouns from adjectives or noun-adjective compounds, roughly equivalent to English *-ness*, *-ity*, or *-hood*. It is highly productive in academic, legal, and technical registers. Not all adjectives or compounds accept -性; acceptability is partly lexically conditioned.

---

**1.**
- **Base Form:** 真实 (zhēnshí)
- **Derived Form:** 真实性
- **Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
- **Literal Breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ness/-ity (nominalizing suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, factual accuracy (as a property)
- **Usage:** The nominalized form of 真实, used to name the property of being truthful or authentic as an abstract quality subject to evaluation or measurement. Extremely common in legal, journalistic, literary-critical, and heritage discourse: 新闻报道的真实性 (the truthfulness of news reporting), 历史真实性 (historical authenticity), 证据的真实性 (the authenticity of evidence), 核实其真实性 (to verify its authenticity/accuracy). This is one of the most productive and well-established 性-nominalizations in the truth domain.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**2.**

- **Natural English Translation:** falseness, spuriousness, fraudulent nature, the quality of being fictitious or deceptive
- **Usage:** Nominalizes 虚假 to name the abstract property of being false or deceptive, used in legal, philosophical, and analytical discourse: 陈述的虚假性 (the falseness of a statement), 广告的虚假性 (the fraudulent nature of the advertisement). Less frequent than 真实性 but attested in regulatory and academic writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Gaps noted for -性:**
- 假 (jiǎ) alone does not productively take -性 to yield 假性 as a general abstract noun; 假性 exists only as a bound medical modifier (e.g., 假性近视, pseudomyopia), parallel to 真性 above, and is domain-restricted.
- 错误 (cuòwù): the property of being erroneous is typically expressed by 错误 itself or by 错误率 (error rate) rather than 错误性, though 错误性 is marginally attested (see item 20 above).
- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) yields 欺骗性 (see item 13 above); 欺骗者 (see Section 3).
- 伪造 (wěizào), 假冒 (jiǎmào), 仿冒 (fǎngmào), 鉴定 (jiàndìng), 核实 (héshí), 伪装 (wěizhuāng) do not productively take -性 in standard usage.
- The four-character expressions (成语) 名副其实, 名不副实, 表里如一, 货真价实, 真相大白, etc. do not take -性.
- 真正 (zhēnzhèng): the form 真正性 does not appear to be in common use; 真实性 serves the nominalization function in this semantic area.
- 逼真 (bīzhēn): the form 逼真性 does not appear to be in common use.

---

**Supplementary Note: 假性**

- **Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
- **Derived Form:** 假性
- **Transliteration:** jiǎxìng
- **Literal Breakdown:** false + -ness/-ity / false + nature
- **Natural English Translation:** pseudo-, false (as a medical or technical prefix-like modifier); spurious quality
- **Usage:** In medical and technical Chinese, 假性 functions as a bound modifier meaning "pseudo-" or "false," appearing in terms such as 假性近视 (pseudomyopia), 假性肥大 (pseudohypertrophy). It is debatable whether this represents suffixation of 假 or a compound with 性 functioning as a noun meaning "nature/type." The pattern is productive in medical terminology but the analysis as derivation is uncertain.
- **Epistemic Status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Section 2: Nominalization/Verbalization via -化 (-huà)

**Suffix description:** -化 derives verbs meaning "to become X" or "to make X" and corresponding process nouns, roughly equivalent to English *-ize/-ization* or *-ify/-ification*. It is highly productive in academic, political, and technical registers. Its applicability to the P1 items is assessed below.

---

**1.**
- **Base Form:** 真实 (zhēnshí)
- **Derived Form:** 真实化
- **Transliteration:** zhēnshíhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ize (verbalizing/nominalizing suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** to make realistic, to render authentic, realization (in the sense of making real)
- **Usage:** Used in literary, cinematic, aesthetic, and media contexts to describe the process of making a representation more faithful to reality or more realistic: 人物形象的真实化 (the rendering of character images as more realistic), 角色真实化 (making characters more realistic), 场景真实化 (rendering scenes more authentic). Less common than the base form; appears primarily in written critical discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.**
- **Base Form:** 客观 (kèguān)
- **Derived Form:** 客观化
- **Transliteration:** kèguānhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** guest/external + observe/view + -ize
- **Natural English Translation:** to objectify, to render objective, objectification (in the epistemological sense)
- **Usage:** Describes the process of making something more objective or of treating something as an external object of study. Used in philosophical and social-scientific discourse: 知识的客观化 (the objectification of knowledge). Less common than 客观性.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**3.**
- **Base Form:** 主观 (zhǔguān)
- **Derived Form:** 主观化
- **Transliteration:** zhǔguānhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** master/internal + observe/view + -ize
- **Natural English Translation:** to subjectivize, to render subjective
- **Usage:** Describes the process of introducing subjective elements into what might otherwise be treated as objective. Used in philosophical and critical discourse. Less common than 主观性 and appears primarily in academic writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**4.**
- **Base Form:** 正确 (zhèngquè)
- **Derived Form:** 正确化
- **Transliteration:** zhèngquèhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** correct/right + -ize
- **Natural English Translation:** to make correct; correction; rectification
- **Usage:** Attested in administrative and educational discourse to mean the process of making something correct or bringing it into conformity with a standard: 答案正确化 (making answers correct). Less common than 纠正 (to correct) for the same meaning; 正确化 is more formal and process-oriented.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**5.**
- **Base Form:** 虚假 (xūjiǎ)
- **Derived Form:** 虚假化
- **Transliteration:** xūjiǎhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** empty/hollow + false + -ize
- **Natural English Translation:** to become falsified; falsification (as a process)
- **Usage:** Attested in critical and analytical discourse to describe a process by which something becomes false or is rendered fraudulent: 信息虚假化 (the falsification of information). Not highly frequent but attested in journalistic and academic writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**6.**
- **Base Form:** 本质 (běnzhì)
- **Derived Form:** 本质化
- **Transliteration:** běnzhìhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** root/origin + substance/nature + -ize
- **Natural English Translation:** to essentialize; essentialization
- **Usage:** Used in critical theory and social science discourse to describe the act of treating contingent or constructed characteristics as essential or fixed: 对文化的本质化理解 (an essentialized understanding of culture). Corresponds to the English critical theory term "essentialize/essentialization." Attestation is limited; not in common general use.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**7.**
- **Base Form:** 现实 (xiànshí)
- **Derived Form:** 现实化
- **Transliteration:** xiànshíhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** present/manifest + real/actual + -ize (verbalizing suffix 化)
- **Natural English Translation:** to make real, to realize, to actualize; to become realistic
- **Usage:** Used to describe the process of making something concrete or actual, as opposed to keeping it at the level of abstraction or ideal: 将计划现实化 (to actualize a plan), 将目标现实化 (to actualize a goal), 理想现实化 (to make ideals real). Overlaps with 实现 (to realize/achieve) but emphasizes the process of concretization. Less common than 实现; attested in formal and analytical contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
- **Base Form:** 合法 (héfǎ)
- **Derived Form:** 合法化
- **Transliteration:** héfǎhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** conform to + law + -ize (verbalizing suffix 化)
- **Natural English Translation:** to legalize, to legitimize; legalization
- **Usage:** Used in legal, political, and social discourse to describe the process of making something legally permissible or officially recognized: 大麻合法化 (legalization of cannabis), 将非正式行为合法化 (to legalize informal practices). Productive and well attested across registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
- **Base Form:** 道德 (dàodé)
- **Derived Form:** 道德化
- **Transliteration:** dàodéhuà
- **Literal Breakdown:** way/principle + virtue/power + -ize (verbalizing suffix 化)
- **Natural English Translation:** to moralize, to make moral; moralization
- **Usage:** Used in sociological, critical, and everyday contexts to describe the process of framing something in moral terms or imposing moral standards on a domain: 将问题道德化 (to moralize an issue), 过度道德化 (excessive moralization). Carries a somewhat critical connotation in contemporary usage, suggesting inappropriate or excessive application of moral judgment.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Gaps noted for -化:**
- 真 (zhēn), 假 (jiǎ), 可靠 (kěkào), 准确 (zhǔnquè), 诚实 (chéngshí), 错误 (cuòwù), 欺骗 (qīpiàn), 伪造 (wěizào), 假冒 (jiǎmào), 鉴定 (jiàndìng), 核实 (héshí), 伪装 (wěizhuāng), 失真 (shīzhēn), 失实 (shīshí) do not productively take -化 in standard usage to yield commonly used derived forms in the truth-evaluation domain.
- The four-character expressions do not take -化.
- 造假化, 欺骗化, 伪造化 are not attested as standard forms.

## Section 3: Agentive/Participant Nominalization via -者 (-zhě)

**Suffix description:** -者 derives nouns denoting persons who perform an action or hold a role, roughly equivalent to English *-er*, *-or*, or *one who*. It attaches primarily to verbs and verbal compounds. It is productive in formal and written registers.

---

**1.**
- **Base Form:** 欺骗 (qīpiàn)
- **Derived Form:** 欺骗者
- **Transliteration:** qīpiànzhě
- **Literal Breakdown:** deceive + cheat + -er (agentive suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** deceiver; one who deceives
- **Usage:** Refers to a person who engages in deception. Used in moral, legal, and analytical discourse. Less common than 骗子 (piànzi, swindler/cheat) in everyday speech but attested in formal and written contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**2.**
- **Base Form:** 伪造 (wěizào)
- **Derived Form:** 伪造者
- **Transliteration:** wěizàozhě
- **Literal Breakdown:** false/spurious + make/fabricate + -er (agentive suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** forger; fabricator; counterfeiter; one who forges
- **Usage:** Refers to a person who forges documents, signatures, currency, or objects. Used in legal and journalistic contexts: 文件伪造者 (document forger), 货币伪造者 (currency counterfeiter). Attested in legal texts and journalistic reporting on forgery cases.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
- **Base Form:** 伪装 (wěizhuāng)
- **Derived Form:** 伪装者
- **Transliteration:** wěizhuāngzhě
- **Literal Breakdown:** false/spurious + dress/disguise + -er (agentive suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** one who disguises; impostor; masquerader
- **Usage:** Refers to a person who conceals their true identity or nature through disguise or pretense. Attested in literary, military, and political discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
- **Base Form:** 假冒 (jiǎmào)
- **Derived Form:** 假冒者
- **Transliteration:** jiǎmàozhě
- **Literal Breakdown:** false/fake + pass off as + -er (agentive suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** impersonator; one who passes off fakes as genuine
- **Usage:** Refers to a person who impersonates another or passes off counterfeit goods as genuine. Attested in legal
