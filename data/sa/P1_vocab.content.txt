# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

## Lexical Items

### 1
**Form:** सत्य  
**Transliteration:** satya  
**Literal meaning:** from √as "to be" + -tya suffix; "that which is"  
**Typical English translations:** truth, true, real, genuine  
**Usage description:** Used across all registers to denote truth, reality, or what genuinely exists. Appears in philosophical discourse (satya vs. mithyā), moral contexts (satya-vāda "truth-speaking"), and legal/ritual settings where authenticity is at issue.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2
**Form:** असत्य  
**Transliteration:** asatya  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative prefix) + satya; "not-being, non-existent"  
**Typical English translations:** false, untrue, unreal  
**Usage description:** Direct negation of satya, used to characterize false statements, unreal appearances, or inauthentic claims. Common in philosophical and ethical discourse contrasting truth and falsehood.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3
**Form:** ऋत  
**Transliteration:** ṛta  
**Literal meaning:** from √ṛ "to go, move rightly"; cosmic order, right  
**Typical English translations:** cosmic order, truth, right, law  
**Usage description:** Vedic term denoting cosmic and moral order, truth as alignment with universal law. Used primarily in archaic/religious contexts to describe what is fundamentally right or properly ordered.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 4
**Form:** अनृत  
**Transliteration:** anṛta  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative) + ṛta; "not-right, disorder"  
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, untruth, disorder, wrong  
**Usage description:** Negation of ṛta, denoting falsehood or violation of cosmic/moral order. Appears in Vedic and classical texts as opposite of truth and righteousness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 5
**Form:** तथ्य  
**Transliteration:** tathya  
**Literal meaning:** from tathā "thus, so" + -ya suffix; "being thus, corresponding to fact"  
**Typical English translations:** true, factual, real, accurate  
**Usage description:** Emphasizes correspondence to actual state of affairs or facts. Used in contexts requiring verification or factual accuracy, often in narrative or descriptive discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6
**Form:** मिथ्या  
**Transliteration:** mithyā  
**Literal meaning:** etymology uncertain; possibly from mithah "mutually, wrongly"  
**Typical English translations:** false, illusory, unreal, erroneous  
**Usage description:** Central term in philosophical discourse (especially Vedānta) for what is false, illusory, or lacking ultimate reality. Used to characterize mistaken cognitions, false appearances, and unreliable representations.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7
**Form:** प्रमाण  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa  
**Literal meaning:** pra- (forth) + √mā "to measure" + -ana; "means of right knowledge"  
**Typical English translations:** valid means of knowledge, proof, authority, standard  
**Usage description:** Technical term in epistemology for valid instruments of knowledge (perception, inference, testimony, etc.). Used broadly for standards of verification, authoritative sources, and reliable evidence.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8
**Form:** प्रमा  
**Transliteration:** pramā  
**Literal meaning:** pra- + √mā; "right knowledge, valid cognition"  
**Typical English translations:** valid knowledge, true cognition, correct understanding  
**Usage description:** Denotes the result of valid cognition, knowledge that corresponds to reality. Distinguished from erroneous cognition (bhrama) in epistemological discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9
**Form:** भ्रम  
**Transliteration:** bhrama  
**Literal meaning:** from √bhram "to wander, err"; error, confusion  
**Typical English translations:** error, illusion, mistake, false cognition  
**Usage description:** Denotes erroneous cognition or mistaken perception (e.g., seeing rope as snake). Central to epistemological discussions of knowledge versus error.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10
**Form:** भ्रान्ति  
**Transliteration:** bhrānti  
**Literal meaning:** from √bhram + -ti suffix; "wandering, error"  
**Typical English translations:** error, delusion, mistake, confusion  
**Usage description:** Similar to bhrama, denotes mistaken cognition or confusion. Used interchangeably with bhrama in many contexts, though sometimes distinguished in technical philosophical usage.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11
**Form:** यथार्थ  
**Transliteration:** yathārtha  
**Literal meaning:** yathā "as, like" + artha "object, meaning"; "according to the object"  
**Typical English translations:** true, accurate, real, corresponding to reality  
**Usage description:** Denotes correspondence between cognition and object, or between representation and reality. Used for accurate statements, true knowledge, and genuine understanding.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12
**Form:** अयथार्थ  
**Transliteration:** ayathārtha  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative) + yathārtha; "not according to the object"  
**Typical English translations:** untrue, inaccurate, not corresponding to reality  
**Usage description:** Negation of yathārtha, denoting lack of correspondence between cognition and reality. Used for false statements or erroneous representations.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13
**Form:** धर्म  
**Transliteration:** dharma  
**Literal meaning:** from √dhṛ "to hold, support" + -ma; "that which upholds"  
**Typical English translations:** duty, righteousness, law, religion, proper conduct  
**Usage description:** Multivalent term denoting right conduct, moral/religious duty, law, or essential nature. Used in moral, legal, and philosophical contexts to evaluate conduct and character as right or proper.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14
**Form:** अधर्म  
**Transliteration:** adharma  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative) + dharma; "not-dharma"  
**Typical English translations:** unrighteousness, wrong conduct, injustice, vice  
**Usage description:** Negation of dharma, denoting wrong conduct, violation of duty, or moral transgression. Used in ethical and legal discourse to characterize improper actions.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 15
**Form:** साधु  
**Transliteration:** sādhu  
**Literal meaning:** from √sādh "to accomplish, succeed"; "good, proper"  
**Typical English translations:** good, virtuous, proper, correct, well done  
**Usage description:** Evaluative term for what is good, proper, or well-executed. Used for moral character, correct conduct, and proper usage (especially grammatical correctness).  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 16
**Form:** असाधु  
**Transliteration:** asādhu  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative) + sādhu; "not-good, improper"  
**Typical English translations:** bad, improper, incorrect, wicked  
**Usage description:** Negation of sādhu, denoting what is improper, incorrect, or morally bad. Used in ethical and grammatical contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17
**Form:** सम्यक्  
**Transliteration:** samyak  
**Literal meaning:** sam- (together, complete) + √añc "to go"; "going together, complete"  
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper, complete, true  
**Usage description:** Adverbial/adjectival form denoting correctness, completeness, or propriety. Common in Buddhist contexts (samyag-dṛṣṭi "right view") and general usage for correct understanding or proper action.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18
**Form:** मिथ्यात्व  
**Transliteration:** mithyātva  
**Literal meaning:** mithyā + -tva (abstract suffix); "falseness, illusoriness"  
**Typical English translations:** falsity, illusoriness, unreality  
**Usage description:** Abstract noun denoting the quality or state of being false or illusory. Technical term in Vedānta philosophy for the ontological status of appearance versus reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 19
**Form:** सत्यता  
**Transliteration:** satyatā  
**Literal meaning:** satya + -tā (abstract suffix); "truthfulness, reality"  
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, truth, reality, genuineness  
**Usage description:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being true or real. Used for truthfulness as virtue and reality as ontological category.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 20
**Form:** विपर्यय  
**Transliteration:** viparyaya  
**Literal meaning:** vi- (apart) + pari- (around) + √i "to go" + -aya; "going wrongly"  
**Typical English translations:** error, perversion, reversal, mistaken cognition  
**Usage description:** Technical term in Yoga and epistemology for erroneous cognition or mistaken understanding. Denotes knowledge that reverses or perverts the true nature of things.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21
**Form:** आभास  
**Transliteration:** ābhāsa  
**Literal meaning:** ā- (toward) + √bhās "to shine" + -a; "shining forth, appearance"  
**Typical English translations:** appearance, semblance, reflection, illusion  
**Usage description:** Denotes appearance or semblance, often contrasted with reality. Used in philosophical contexts for illusory appearances or mere semblances lacking genuine reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 22
**Form:** व्यवहार  
**Transliteration:** vyavahāra  
**Literal meaning:** vi- (apart) + ava- (down) + √hṛ "to carry" + -a; "transaction, practice"  
**Typical English translations:** conventional usage, practical reality, transaction, behavior  
**Usage description:** Denotes conventional or practical level of reality and discourse, contrasted with ultimate truth (paramārtha). Used for everyday transactions, linguistic conventions, and empirical reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 23
**Form:** परमार्थ  
**Transliteration:** paramārtha  
**Literal meaning:** parama "highest" + artha "meaning, object"; "highest meaning/reality"  
**Typical English translations:** ultimate truth, highest reality, absolute meaning  
**Usage description:** Denotes ultimate or absolute truth/reality, contrasted with conventional truth (vyavahāra). Central to Buddhist and Vedāntic discussions of two levels of truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24
**Form:** अविद्या  
**Transliteration:** avidyā  
**Literal meaning:** a- (negative) + vidyā "knowledge"; "non-knowledge, ignorance"  
**Typical English translations:** ignorance, nescience, false knowledge  
**Usage description:** Fundamental term for ignorance or false knowledge, especially metaphysical ignorance of reality. In Vedānta, the root cause of bondage and misperception of reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 25
**Form:** विद्या  
**Transliteration:** vidyā  
**Literal meaning:** from √vid "to know" + -yā; "knowledge"  
**Typical English translations:** knowledge, learning, wisdom, science  
**Usage description:** General term for knowledge, learning, or wisdom. Contrasted with avidyā (ignorance) and used for both mundane learning and liberating knowledge.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 26
**Form:** सदृश  
**Transliteration:** sadṛśa  
**Literal meaning:** sa- (with) + dṛśa (from √dṛś "to see"); "having same appearance"  
**Typical English translations:** similar, like, resembling, analogous  
**Usage description:** Denotes similarity or resemblance between things. Used in comparative contexts and discussions of analogy, likeness, or correspondence.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 27
**Form:** स्वरूप  
**Transliteration:** svarūpa  
**Literal meaning:** sva "own" + rūpa "form"; "own-form"  
**Typical English translations:** essential nature, true form, inherent character  
**Usage description:** Denotes the essential nature or true form of something, what it fundamentally is. Used in philosophical discourse to distinguish essential from accidental properties.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 28
**Form:** अनुमान  
**Transliteration:** anumāna  
**Literal meaning:** anu- (after) + √mā "to measure" + -ana; "measuring after"  
**Typical English translations:** inference, reasoning, deduction  
**Usage description:** Technical term for inference as a means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa). Denotes knowledge derived through reasoning from perceived evidence to unperceived conclusion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 29
**Form:** प्रत्यक्ष  
**Transliteration:** pratyakṣa  
**Literal meaning:** prati- (toward) + akṣa "eye"; "before the eyes"  
**Typical English translations:** perception, direct cognition, evident  
**Usage description:** Technical term for direct perception as a means of valid knowledge. Denotes immediate, non-inferential cognition through sense organs, considered most reliable pramāṇa.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 30
**Form:** शब्द  
**Transliteration:** śabda  
**Literal meaning:** from √śabd "to sound"; "word, sound"  
**Typical English translations:** word, verbal testimony, scriptural authority, sound  
**Usage description:** As epistemological term, denotes verbal testimony or authoritative statement as means of knowledge. Includes both ordinary reliable testimony and scriptural revelation (āgama).  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
