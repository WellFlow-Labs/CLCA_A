# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / BECOMING TRUTH

### Pattern
**X egia bihurtu** (X become truth) — periphrastic inchoative construction using "bihurtu" (to become, turn into). The truth-domain noun appears in absolutive case (unmarked).

### Examples
- **Hori egia bihurtu da**
  - hori egia bihurtu da
  - "That has become truth / That has turned into truth"

### Notes
Grammatically possible but semantically restricted. More natural to say something is revealed as true (egia dela agertu) or proven true (egia dela frogatu) than to say it "becomes" true. Truth is typically discovered or established rather than created or transformed.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but pragmatically restricted

---

## 2. BECOMING A LIE / BECOMING FALSE

### Pattern
**X gezur bihurtu** (X become lie) — parallel construction to "egia bihurtu" using the same verb "bihurtu."

### Examples
- **Hori gezur bihurtu da**
  - hori gezur bihurtu da
  - "That has become a lie / That has turned into a lie"

### Notes
Even more restricted than "egia bihurtu." Semantically odd since lies are typically told rather than transformed into. More natural to say something is revealed as false (gezurra dela agertu) or proven false (gezurra dela frogatu).

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but pragmatically very restricted

---

## 3. BECOMING CORRECT / BECOMING RIGHT

### Pattern
**X zuzen bihurtu** (X become correct) — periphrastic inchoative with "bihurtu."

### Examples
- **Erantzuna zuzen bihurtu da**
  - erantzuna zuzen bihurtu da
  - "The answer has become correct"

### Notes
Semantically restricted. Answers or statements don't typically "become" correct; they are correct or incorrect from the start. More natural to say something is corrected (zuzendu) or proven correct (zuzen dela frogatu).

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but pragmatically restricted

---

## 4. BECOMING WRONG / GOING WRONG

### Pattern
**X okertu** (X go wrong / become wrong) — dedicated intransitive verb derived from "oker" with verbalizing suffix -tu.

### Examples
- **Dena okertu da**
  - dena okertu da
  - "Everything has gone wrong"

- **Kalkuluak okertu dira**
  - kalkuluak okertu dira
  - "The calculations have gone wrong"

### Notes
This is a CORE, fully productive construction. Unlike "zuzen bihurtu," the verb "okertu" is natural and common. It describes processes of deterioration, error, or going wrong. Typically intransitive — things go wrong rather than being made wrong.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — fully productive and natural

---

## 5. BECOMING GENUINE / BECOMING REAL

### Pattern
**X benetako bihurtu** (X become real) — periphrastic construction with adjectival predicate.

### Examples
- **Amets bat benetako bihurtu da**
  - amets bat benetako bihurtu da
  - "A dream has become real"

### Notes
Grammatically possible and semantically more natural than "egia bihurtu." Used when something imagined or planned becomes actual reality. The adjectival form "benetako" is used rather than a noun.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — grammatically and pragmatically acceptable

## 6. BECOMING APPARENT / BECOMING EVIDENT

### Pattern
**X agertu** (X appear / become evident) — dedicated verb from root "ager-" (appear, be visible) with verbalizing suffix -tu.

### Examples
- **Egia agertu da**
  - egia agertu da
  - "The truth has appeared / The truth has become evident"

- **Gezurra agertu da**
  - gezurra agertu da
  - "The lie has appeared / The lie has become evident"

### Notes
This is a CORE construction for expressing how truth or falsehood becomes visible or manifest. The verb "agertu" is fully productive and natural with truth-domain subjects. It describes revelation or manifestation rather than transformation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — fully productive and natural

## 7. BECOMING CERTAIN / BECOMING SURE

### Pattern
**X ziur bihurtu** (X become certain) — periphrastic construction, but more commonly expressed through stative constructions.

### Examples
- **Ziur bihurtu naiz**
  - ziur bihurtu naiz
  - "I have become certain"

### Notes
Grammatically possible but less common than stative expressions like "ziur nago" (I am certain) or "ziurtatu dut" (I have made certain / I have verified). The inchoative is possible but not the preferred construction.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] — grammatically possible but less preferred than alternatives

## 8. BECOMING DOUBTFUL / COMING INTO DOUBT

### Pattern
**X zalantzan jarri** (X put in doubt) — construction using locative case + verb "jarri" (to put). Typically passive or reflexive for inchoative meaning.

### Examples
- **Hori zalantzan jarri da**
  - hori zalantzan jarri da
  - "That has been put in doubt / That has become doubtful"

### Notes
This is a CORE construction for expressing how something becomes doubtful or questionable. The locative case "zalantzan" (in doubt) combines with "jarri" (put) to create the inchoative meaning. Very common in critical and analytical discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — fully productive and natural

## 9. VERIFICATION / MAKING CERTAIN (CAUSATIVE-LIKE)

### Pattern
**X egiazkotu / egiaztatu** (verify X / authenticate X) — dedicated transitive verbs meaning to establish as true or authentic.

### Examples
- **Dokumentua egiazkotu dut**
  - dokumentua egiazkotu dut
  - "I have verified the document / I have authenticated the document"

- **Datuak egiaztatu ditugu**
  - datuak egiaztatu ditugu
  - "We have verified the data"

### Notes
These are CORE transitive verbs that function similarly to causatives — they establish or make something (be recognized as) true. However, they are not morphological causatives but dedicated verification verbs. Very common in administrative, legal, and scientific contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — fully productive and natural

## 12. FALSIFYING / MAKING FALSE (CAUSATIVE-LIKE)

### Pattern
**X faltsutu** (falsify X / forge X) — transitive verb from borrowed root "faltsu" (false) with verbalizing suffix -tu.

### Examples
- **Dokumentua faltsutu dute**
  - dokumentua faltsutu dute
  - "They have falsified the document / They have forged the document"

- **Sinadura faltsutu**
  - sinadura faltsutu
  - "to forge a signature"

### Notes
This is a CORE transitive verb meaning to falsify or forge. Note that this derives from the borrowed root "faltsu" rather than the native "gezur." No verb *gezurtu (to make into a lie) exists. Common in legal and forensic contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE] — fully productive and natural

## 29. NO MORPHOLOGICAL CAUSATIVE WITH TRUTH-DOMAIN ROOTS

### Pattern
**ROOT + -arazi** (morphological causative suffix)

### Examples
NO COMMON EXPRESSIONS IDENTIFIED

### Notes
The morphological causative suffix -arazi does not productively combine with truth-domain roots to create causative meanings. There are no attested forms like:
- *egiaarazi (to cause to be true)
- *gezurarazi (to cause to be a lie)
- *zuzendarazi (to cause to be correct) — note: "zuzendaritza" exists but means "direction/management," not causative
- *okerarazi (to cause to go wrong)

Instead, Basque uses dedicated transitive verbs (egiazkotu, ziurtatu, zuzendu, faltsutu) or light verb constructions to express causative-like meanings in the truth domain.

**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN] — no productive pattern identified

## 30. NO SIMPLE VERB "TO LIE"

### Pattern
**gezurtu** (hypothetical simple verb "to lie")

### Examples
NO COMMON EXPRESSIONS IDENTIFIED

### Notes
There is no attested simple verb *gezurtu meaning "to lie" or "to make into a lie." The concept of lying is expressed through the light verb construction "gezurra esan" (to tell a lie), which is obligatory and fully idiomatic. This represents a significant lexical gap in the verbal system.

Alternative expressions for lying:
- gezurra esan (to tell a lie) — [CORE] standard construction
- gezurretan ibili (to go about lying) — [CORE] habitual
- gezurrak asmatu (to invent lies) — [CORE] fabrication

**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN] — no simple verb exists; light verb construction is obligatory

## SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

### PRODUCTIVE INCHOATIVE PATTERNS:

1. **X agertu** (X appear/become evident) — [CORE]
   - Natural with truth-domain subjects
   - Expresses revelation or manifestation

2. **X okertu** (X go wrong) — [CORE]
   - Dedicated intransitive verb
   - Natural and common for deterioration/error

3. **X zalantzan jarri** (X put in doubt) — [CORE]
   - Passive/reflexive for inchoative meaning
   - Very common in critical discourse

4. **X benetako bihurtu** (X become real) — [CORE]
   - Grammatically and pragmatically acceptable
   - Used for dreams/plans becoming reality

### MARGINAL INCHOATIVE PATTERNS:

1. **X egia bihurtu** (X become truth) — [MARGINAL]
   - Grammatically possible but pragmatically restricted
   - Truth is discovered/established, not created

2. **X gezur bihurtu** (X become lie) — [MARGINAL]
   - Even more restricted than "egia bihurtu"
   - Lies are told, not transformed into

3. **X zuzen bihurtu** (X become correct) — [MARGINAL]
   - Grammatically possible but pragmatically restricted
   - Correctness is established, not transformed

4. **X ziur bihurtu** (X become certain) — [MARGINAL]
   - Less preferred than stative "ziur egon" or "ziurtatu"

### PRODUCTIVE CAUSATIVE-LIKE PATTERNS:

1. **Dedicated transitive verbs:**
   - egiazkotu/egiaztatu (verify, authenticate) — [CORE]
   - ziurtatu (make certain, ensure) — [CORE]
   - zuzendu (correct, straighten) — [CORE]
   - faltsutu (falsify, forge) — [CORE]
   - frogatu (prove, test, demonstrate) — [CORE]

2. **Light verb constructions with dynamic verbs:**
   - egia atera (bring out truth) — [CORE]
   - egia ezkutatu (hide truth) — [CORE]
   - egia aurkitu (find truth) — [CORE]
   - egia onartu (accept truth) — [CORE]
   - egia ukatu (deny truth) — [CORE]
   - gezurra atera (expose lie) — [CORE]
   - gezurra asmatu (invent lie) — [CORE]
   - zalantza kendu (remove doubt) — [CORE]
   - fede eman (give credence) — [CORE]

3. **Locative + transitive verb:**
   - X zalantzan jarri (put X in doubt) — [CORE]

4. **Locative + "catch" verb:**
   - gezurretan harrapatu (catch in lies) — [CORE]

### PRODUCTIVE STABILITY/PERSISTENCE PATTERNS:

1. **Dative + "hold" verb:**
   - egiari eusten (hold to truth) — [CORE]

2. **Stative constructions:**
   - ziur egon (remain certain) — [CORE]

3. **Motion verb constructions:**
   - zintzo ibili (behave honestly) — [CORE]
   - gezurretan ibili (go about lying) — [CORE]

### SIGNIFICANT GAPS:

1. **No morphological causative** with -arazi suffix for truth-domain roots

2. **No simple verb "to lie"** (*gezurtu) — obligatory light verb construction "gezurra esan"

3. **No simple verb "to make false"** from native "gezur" — uses borrowed "faltsutu" instead

4. **Limited inchoative constructions** — truth is typically discovered/established rather than created/transformed

### KEY ASYMMETRIES:

1. **"oker" supports dedicated intransitive verb** (okertu) but **"zuzen" does not** (*zuzentu means "to correct," not "to become correct")

2. **"egia" supports extensive light verb constructions** for dynamic meanings, but **"gezur" shows fewer options**

3. **Verification has multiple dedicated verbs** (egiazkotu, egiaztatu, ziurtatu, frogatu) but **falsification relies on borrowed root** (faltsutu)

4. **Truth-revelation is highly elaborated** (atera, aurkitu, agertu) while **lie-creation is less elaborated** (primarily asmatu)

These patterns reflect actual usage in Basque where truth is conceptualized as something discovered, revealed, or established through evidence, while lies are told or fabricated through speech acts. The language shows rich resources for expressing verification and revelation but limited resources for expressing transformation or creation of truth-values.
