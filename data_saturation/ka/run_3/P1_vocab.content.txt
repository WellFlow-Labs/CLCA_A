# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgian Romanization (as used in Georgian academic and official contexts), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine quality) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and propositions, and also to characterize persons or things as genuine or authentic. Appears in philosophical, theological, and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in productive derivation (see č̣ešmariteba below).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, genuineness
**Usage:** The standard nominal form for "truth" as an abstract property of statements or states of affairs. Widely used in philosophical, religious, and ordinary discourse. Contrasts with mtkuaroba (falsehood) and t'yupiloba (deception).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** root mart- (straight, right, correct) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, true, just, honest
**Usage:** Applied to persons (an honest or upright person), statements (a correct or true claim), and conduct (rightful behavior). Covers both epistemic correctness and moral uprightness, making it semantically broad. Commonly used in everyday speech and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominal prefix) + mart- + -le (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, justice, rightness
**Usage:** The primary everyday word for "truth" in Georgian, used interchangeably with č̣ešmariteba in many contexts but often preferred in colloquial speech. Also carries connotations of fairness and justice. Frequently appears in the phrase simartle tkvit ("tell the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** na- (participial/resultative prefix) + mdvili (from root mdv-, to place/settle) + -i; roughly "that which has been established/settled"
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** Used to assert that something is the real or genuine instance of a kind, as opposed to a copy, imitation, or pretense. Common in commercial, aesthetic, and everyday contexts (e.g., namdvili oqro, "real gold"). Does not typically predicate truth of propositions directly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, reality
**Usage:** The nominal form derived from namdvili, used to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in legal, archival, and philosophical contexts when the authenticity of documents, objects, or claims is under evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Persian/Arabic (qalb, "heart; counterfeit"); adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged, spurious
**Usage:** Used to characterize documents, currency, objects, or statements as counterfeit or fraudulent. Strongly associated with deliberate deception or forgery. Appears frequently in legal and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ყალბობა
**Transliteration:** q̇alboba
**Literal meaning:** q̇albi + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** falseness, forgery, fraud, falsification
**Usage:** The nominal form of q̇albi, referring to the act or state of being counterfeit or fraudulent. Used in legal discourse to describe forgery or falsification of documents and evidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** მტკიცება
**Transliteration:** mt'k'iceba
**Literal meaning:** mt'k'ice (firm, solid, strong) + nominalizing suffix -ba; "making firm/solid"
**Typical English translations:** assertion, affirmation, proof, claim, allegation
**Usage:** Refers to the act of asserting or affirming a proposition, as well as to the act of proving or establishing something. Used in legal contexts for claims and in philosophical contexts for assertions. Participates in the compound mt'k'icebuleba ("proof, evidence").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მტკიცებულება
**Transliteration:** mt'k'icebuleba
**Literal meaning:** mt'k'iceba + -ul- (resultative participial infix) + -eba; "that which has been established/proven"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, demonstration
**Usage:** The standard legal and philosophical term for evidence or proof. Used in court proceedings to refer to admissible evidence and in argumentation to refer to demonstrative proof. Closely related to but distinct from mt'k'iceba (the act of asserting/proving).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominal prefix) + q̇alb- + -e (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, falseness, fraud
**Usage:** An abstract nominal referring to the property of being false or fraudulent. Used in both epistemic contexts (the falsity of a statement) and moral-legal contexts (fraudulence of conduct). Contrasts directly with simartle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (root associated with lying/falsehood) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** Refers specifically to a lie or deliberate untruth, with a stronger connotation of intentional deception than siq̇albe. Used in moral and everyday contexts to characterize statements made with intent to deceive. Closely related to mtkuari ("liar").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** მტყუარი
**Transliteration:** mt'q̇uari
**Literal meaning:** mt'q̇u- (root of lying) + agentive suffix -ari
**Typical English translations:** liar, false (person), untruthful
**Usage:** An agentive noun or adjective characterizing a person who lies or a statement that is untrue. Used in moral evaluation of persons and in characterizing false testimony. Participates in the derivational family including mtkuaroba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarcmuno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (prefix indicating fitness/capacity) + rcmun- (root of belief/trust) + -o (adjectival suffix); "worthy of belief/trust"
**Typical English translations:** credible, reliable, trustworthy, believable
**Usage:** Used to evaluate sources, witnesses, documents, or statements as worthy of belief or trust. Common in legal contexts (sarcmuno mowme, "credible witness") and in everyday evaluation of reliability. Contrasts with usarcmuno ("incredible, unreliable").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** სანდო
**Transliteration:** sando
**Literal meaning:** sa- + nd- (root related to trust, cf. ndoba "trust") + -o
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, and institutions to indicate that they can be relied upon. Somewhat more colloquial than sarcmuno and tends to emphasize practical reliability over epistemic credibility. Frequently used in journalism and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- (from Persian/Turkish zust, "exact, precise") + -e
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness, correctness
**Usage:** Refers to the property of being accurate or precise, applied to measurements, translations, reproductions, and statements. Used in scientific, technical, and everyday evaluative contexts. Contrasts with uზუსტობა (inaccuracy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** zust- + adjectival suffix -i (see sizuste above)
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate, correct
**Usage:** The adjectival form corresponding to sizuste. Applied to descriptions, translations, reproductions, and measurements. Commonly used to evaluate whether a representation faithfully corresponds to what it represents.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** სწორი
**Transliteration:** sc̣ori
**Literal meaning:** sc̣or- (straight, level, correct) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, straight, proper
**Usage:** Used to evaluate answers, decisions, conduct, and statements as correct or proper. Overlaps with martali in the sense of "correct" but is more commonly used for factual correctness and less for moral uprightness. Very frequent in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** სიყალბის დადასტურება
**Transliteration:** siq̇albis dadastureba
**Literal meaning:** siq̇albe (genitive: siq̇albis) + dadastureba ("confirmation, verification, attestation")
**Typical English translations:** confirmation of falsity, verification of fraud, attestation of forgery
**Usage:** A fixed nominal phrase used in legal and administrative contexts to refer to the formal establishment or confirmation that something is false or fraudulent. Appears in legal documents and proceedings.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**20.**
**Form:** დადასტურება
**Transliteration:** dadastureba
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + dastur- (from Persian dastur, "rule, permission, confirmation") + -eba (verbal noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, verification, attestation, corroboration
**Usage:** Refers to the act of confirming, verifying, or attesting to the truth or validity of something. Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts. Participates in productive verbal derivation (daadasturebs, "he/she confirms").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** გადამოწმება
**Transliteration:** gadamomc̣meba
**Literal meaning:** gada- (preverb indicating thoroughness/repetition) + mo- (preverb) + mc̣m- (root of checking/verifying) + -eba
**Typical English translations:** verification, cross-checking, re-examination
**Usage:** Refers to the act of checking or verifying something, often by consulting an additional source or repeating a check. Used in journalistic, scientific, and legal contexts. Implies a more thorough or repeated verification than simple momc̣meba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** მოჩვენებითი
**Transliteration:** močvenebiti
**Literal meaning:** mo- + čven- (root of showing/appearing) + -ebit- + -i; "of the nature of mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible, illusory, spurious
**Usage:** Used to characterize something that merely appears to be what it is claimed to be, without actually being so. Applied to arguments, qualities, and identities. Carries a critical or skeptical evaluative force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** ფიქტიური
**Transliteration:** p'ik't'iuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin/Russian fictivnyj/fictif; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** fictitious, fictional, fabricated, sham
**Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to characterize entities, transactions, or claims as fabricated or not genuinely existing. Common in legal discourse (p'ik't'iuri garigebа, "fictitious transaction"). A borrowing with restricted domain use.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avt'enturi
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek/European authentic; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** An international borrowing used in legal, archival, and cultural contexts to assert the authenticity of documents, artworks, or texts. Overlaps with namdvili but is more formal and domain-specific. Increasingly common in contemporary Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ავთენტურობა
**Transliteration:** avt'enturoba
**Literal meaning:** avt'enturi + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity
**Usage:** The nominal form of avt'enturi, used in legal, archival, and philosophical contexts to refer to the property of being authentic. Appears in discussions of document authentication and cultural heritage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** კანონიერი
**Transliteration:** k'anonieri
**Literal meaning:** k'anon- (from Greek kanon, "rule, law") + -ieri (adjectival suffix indicating conformity)
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legal, legitimate, rightful
**Usage:** Used to evaluate conduct, claims, and statuses as conforming to law or legitimate rule. Appears extensively in legal contexts. Contrasts with ук'аnonо (unlawful) and participates in productive derivation (k'anoniereba, "legality, legitimacy").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** კანონიერება
**Transliteration:** k'anoniereba
**Literal meaning:** k'anonieri + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** legality, legitimacy, lawfulness
**Usage:** The nominal form of k'anonieri, referring to the property of being lawful or legitimate. Used in legal and political discourse to evaluate the validity of acts, decisions, and claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** არსი
**Transliteration:** arsi
**Literal meaning:** from Persian/Arabic asl ("origin, essence") via Georgian phonological adaptation; + nominal suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** essence, substance, nature, core, what something fundamentally is
**Usage:** Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts to refer to the fundamental nature or essential character of something. Appears in phrases evaluating whether something corresponds to its true nature or essential definition. Participates in compounds and derivations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** მსგავსება
**Transliteration:** msgavseba
**Literal meaning:** msgavs- (similar, resembling) + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Refers to the property of resembling or being similar to something else. Used in aesthetic, legal (e.g., resemblance of a portrait to its subject), and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in productive derivation from the adjective msgavsi.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** siq̇albis damadasturebeliTransliteration
**Transliteration:** siq̇albis damadasturebeliTransliteration
**Transliteration:** siq̇albis damadasturebeli
