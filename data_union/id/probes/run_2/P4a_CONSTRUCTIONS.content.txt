# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *benar* / *salah* / *palsu* / *sah* / *jujur* (predicate adjective, no copula)

### Examples
- *Informasi itu benar.* → Informasi itu benar. → "That information is correct."
- *Dokumen ini palsu.* → Dokumen ini palsu. → "This document is fake."

### Notes
Indonesian has no overt copula in nominal/adjectival predication. The truth-domain adjective follows the subject NP directly. Negation inserts *tidak* (for adjectives/verbs) or *bukan* (for nouns) before the predicate: *Informasi itu tidak benar*; *Ini bukan yang asli*.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *tidak* + adjective (for scalar/gradable predicates); Subject + *bukan* + NP (for categorical denial)

### Examples
- *Berita itu tidak akurat.* → Berita itu tidak akurat. → "That news is not accurate."
- *Ini bukan asli.* → Ini bukan asli. → "This is not genuine."

### Notes
*Tidak* negates adjectival and verbal predicates; *bukan* negates nominal predicates and is used for categorical denial of identity or category membership. Both are attested with truth-domain items. Softened negation uses *kurang* ("less, not quite"): *kurang tepat* ("not quite right"), *kurang benar* ("not entirely correct").

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Noun + *yang* + adjective (postnominal relative clause); adjective + noun is also attested for some fixed collocations

### Examples
- *Informasi yang benar dan akurat.* → Informasi yang benar dan akurat. → "Information that is correct and accurate."
- *Hadis sahih.* → Hadis sahih. → "A sound hadith." (direct juxtaposition, no *yang*, fixed technical term)

### Notes
The standard modifier position in Indonesian is postnominal, using the relativizer *yang*. Direct adjective–noun juxtaposition without *yang* occurs in fixed collocations and technical terms (*hadis sahih*, *emas murni*, *uang palsu*) but is not the productive default. Both patterns are well attested across the P1 inventory.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*menjadi* + noun/adjective (general inchoative); *terungkap* / *terbukti* for truth-coming-to-light constructions

### Examples
- *Kebohongan itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan yang diketahui publik.* → "That lie eventually became a reality known to the public."
- *Kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya.* → "The truth will come to light in the end."

### Notes
The general inchoative is *menjadi* + NP/adjective. For truth-domain meanings, the most conventionalized inchoative constructions involve *terungkap* ("to be revealed, to come to light") and *terbukti* ("to be proven") with *kebenaran* as subject. *Menjadi kenyataan* ("to become reality") is a fixed inchoative collocation.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
*me(N)-* + base + *-kan* (active causative); *di-* + base + *-kan* (passive causative)

### Examples
- *Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik.* → "That statement misled the public."
- *Dokumen itu telah dipalsukan.* → "That document has been forged."

### Notes
The suffix *-kan* on a verbal base regularly produces a causative or transitivizing reading. This pattern is productive across the P1 inventory: *membenarkan* (to confirm/justify), *mengelirukan* (to mislead), *memalsukan* (to forge), *mengesahkan* (to ratify), *memurnikan* (to purify), *mengikhlaskan* (to cause to accept). The passive *di-...-kan* counterpart is equally productive and frequent, especially in formal and journalistic registers.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ke-* + base + *-an* (circumfix; produces abstract quality nouns)

### Examples
- *Kejujuran adalah nilai yang dijunjung tinggi.* → "Honesty is a highly valued principle."
- *Keabsahan pemilu itu dipertanyakan.* → "The legitimacy of that election was called into question."

### Notes
The circumfix *ke-...-an* is the primary abstract nominalizer in Indonesian and applies productively across the P1 inventory: *kebenaran* (truth), *kesalahan* (error), *keaslian* (authenticity), *kejujuran* (honesty), *kebohongan* (falsehood), *keabsahan* (legitimacy), *kemurnian* (purity), *ketulusan* (sincerity), *keikhlasan* (selfless sincerity), *kenyataan* (reality), *ketepatan* (accuracy/precision), *keakuratan* (accuracy). Loanwords show limited integration: *validitas* (from Dutch/Latin) is used instead of *\*kevalidan* in technical registers; *\*keotentikan* is marginal.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*pe(N)-* + base (agentive prefix; produces "one who does X")

### Examples
- *Dia dikenal sebagai pembohong ulung.* → "He is known as a skilled liar."
- *Pemalsu dokumen itu akhirnya ditangkap.* → "The document forger was finally arrested."

### Notes
The prefix *pe(N)-* produces agentive nominals. Attested forms in the truth domain include *pembohong* (liar), *pendusta* (liar, formal/religious register), *pemalsu* (forger/counterfeiter), *peniru* (imitator). Notably absent or marginal: *\*pembenar* (one who confirms/justifies) is not in common use; *\*penjujur* (honest person) does not exist — the concept is expressed periphrastically as *orang yang jujur*. This gap is asymmetric: agentive nominals are well established for negative roles (liar, forger) but not for positive ones in this domain.

---

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*pe(N)-* + base + *-an* (circumfix; produces process/event nouns)

### Examples
- *Pemalsuan dokumen adalah tindak pidana.* → "Document forgery is a criminal act."
- *Pengesahan undang-undang itu berlangsung kemarin.* → "The ratification of that law took place yesterday."

### Notes
The circumfix *pe(N)-...-an* produces nominals referring to the act or process of doing something, distinct from the abstract quality nouns produced by *ke-...-an*. Attested forms include *pemalsuan* (forgery/falsification), *pengesahan* (ratification/authentication), *pembenaran* (justification/confirmation), *pemurnian* (purification), *peniruan* (imitation), *penyimpangan* (deviation/distortion). This pattern is productive across the inventory and is particularly frequent in legal and journalistic registers.

---

## 9. DISCOURSE-MARKER CONSTRUCTIONS (CORRECTION / DISCLOSURE)

### Pattern
*Sebenarnya* / *Pada hakikatnya* + clause (sentence-initial; introduces correction or disclosure of what is actually the case)

### Examples
- *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju dengan keputusan itu.* → "Actually, I don't agree with that decision."
- *Pada hakikatnya, masalah ini jauh lebih kompleks.* → "In essence, this problem is far more complex."

### Notes
*Sebenarnya* (*se-benar-nya*) is the high-frequency everyday discourse marker for correction and disclosure; *pada hakikatnya* is its formal/philosophical counterpart, introducing statements about essential rather than surface reality. Both function as sentence-initial pragmatic operators. *Sebenar-benarnya* is the emphatic variant of *sebenarnya*, used in formal declarations and sworn testimony.

---

## 10. PARTIAL CONCESSION CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
*Ada* + *benar* + *-nya* (existential + adjective + enclitic *-nya*); also *memang benar* + clause

### Examples
- *Ada benarnya juga pendapat itu.* → "There is something to that view too."
- *Memang benar bahwa situasinya sulit.* → "It is indeed true that the situation is difficult."

### Notes
*Ada benarnya* is a fixed partial-concession construction acknowledging limited validity of a claim without full endorsement; *juga* ("also") frequently co-occurs to soften further. *Memang benar* is a confirmatory construction used to concede or affirm a point, often in argumentative contexts. Both are highly frequent in conversational and editorial discourse and have no single-word equivalents.

---

## 11. INTENSIFIED ASSERTION

### Pattern
Full reduplication: *benar-benar* / *sungguh-sungguh* / *nyata-nyata* + predicate

### Examples
- *Dia benar-benar tidak tahu tentang hal itu.* → "He truly did not know about that."
- *Tindakan itu nyata-nyata melanggar hukum.* → "That action clearly and manifestly violated the law."

### Notes
Full reduplication of truth-domain adjectives produces intensified adverbials. *Benar-benar* intensifies degree or reality; *sungguh-sungguh* intensifies sincerity and earnestness; *nyata-nyata* intensifies evidential certainty ("manifestly, clearly"). The pattern is productive for this semantic class. *Sebenar-benarnya* (*se-* + reduplicated *benar* + *-nya*) is a related emphatic form restricted to formal declarations.
