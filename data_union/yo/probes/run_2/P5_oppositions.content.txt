# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** òtítọ́
**Term B:** irọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ / irọ́
**English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** These two items are explicitly described in P1 as antonyms and appear in directly parallel syntactic frames: *sọ òtítọ́* (tell the truth) vs. *sọ irọ́* (tell a lie); *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* vs. *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*; *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* vs. *Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*. The contrast is between a statement that corresponds to reality and one that does not.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** òtítọ́
**Term B:** àìtọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ / àìtọ́
**English translation:** truth, correctness / incorrectness, wrongness, impropriety
**Description of contrast:** *àìtọ́* is formed by negating the root *tọ́* from which *òtítọ́* is derived (*à-* + *ì-* + *tọ́*), producing a nominal that denotes the absence or negation of correctness. The contrast is between the state of being correct and the state of not being correct, operating at the level of the shared root rather than as a direct lexical pair.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** ó tọ́
**Term B:** kò tọ́
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́ / kò tọ́
**English translation:** it is correct / it is not correct; it is right / it is wrong; it is proper / it is improper
**Description of contrast:** These are the affirmative and negated forms of the stative predicate *tọ́*, documented in P1b and P2b as a highly productive evaluative pair used across factual, moral, procedural, and social domains. They appear in directly parallel constructions: *Ó tọ́ pé kí a sọ òtítọ́* vs. *Kò tọ́ pé kí o sọ irọ́*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** ìwà rere
**Term B:** ìwà búburú
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / ìwà búburú
**English translation:** good character, virtue / bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
**Description of contrast:** These two compounds share the head noun *ìwà* (character, nature) and differ only in the modifying adjective (*rere* — good vs. *búburú* — bad). They are explicitly described in P1 as antonyms and appear in parallel evaluative constructions: *Ó ní ìwà rere* vs. *Kò ní ìwà rere*; *Ìwà rere ló ń gbé ènìyàn ga* vs. *Ìwà búburú rẹ̀ ló pa á*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Term B:** àìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ / àìgbàgbọ́
**English translation:** belief, faith, trust / unbelief, disbelief, lack of faith
**Description of contrast:** *àìgbàgbọ́* is the morphological negation of *ìgbàgbọ́*, formed with the negating prefix *àì-*. The contrast is between the state of holding a belief or faith and the state of not holding it. Both forms are documented as productive in religious and philosophical discourse, with corresponding agentive forms *olùgbàgbọ́* (believer) and *aláìgbàgbọ́* (unbeliever).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** olùgbàgbọ́
**Term B:** aláìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** olùgbàgbọ́ / aláìgbàgbọ́
**English translation:** believer, one who has faith / unbeliever, one who lacks faith
**Description of contrast:** These are the agentive nominals derived from *gbàgbọ́* (to believe) and its negation, respectively. They are explicitly described in P2a as antonyms used in religious discourse. The contrast is between a person characterized by belief and one characterized by its absence.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** ìmọ̀
**Term B:** àìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ / àìmọ̀
**English translation:** knowledge, understanding, awareness / ignorance, lack of knowledge, unawareness
**Description of contrast:** *àìmọ̀* is the morphological negation of *ìmọ̀*, formed with *àì-*. The contrast is between possessing relevant knowledge and lacking it, and is used in legal and evaluative contexts to assess whether a person's testimony or conduct is epistemically grounded. The pair appears in directly parallel constructions: *Ó ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀* vs. *Kò ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú
**Term B:** ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú / ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**English translation:** eyewitness, direct witness / false witness, perjurer
**Description of contrast:** Both compounds share the head noun *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness) and differ in the modifying noun: *ojú* (eye) marks a witness whose testimony is based on direct observation, while *irọ́* (lie) marks a witness whose testimony is false. The contrast is between a witness whose account is reliable and one whose account is deliberately false.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** ẹ̀rí ojú (ẹ̀rí tààrà)
**Term B:** ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú / ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn
**English translation:** eyewitness evidence, direct evidence / hearsay evidence, secondhand testimony
**Description of contrast:** Both are compounds with the head noun *ẹ̀rí* (evidence) and are explicitly contrasted in P1b: *ẹ̀rí ojú* refers to evidence based on direct visual observation, while *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn* refers to evidence based on what was heard from another person. The contrast is between firsthand and secondhand evidentiary basis.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** ẹ̀rí tààrà
**Term B:** ẹ̀rí àyíká
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí tààrà / ẹ̀rí àyíká
**English translation:** direct evidence / circumstantial evidence
**Description of contrast:** Both are compounds with the head noun *ẹ̀rí* (evidence) documented in P1b and P2a as a legal distinction. *tààrà* (direct, straight) marks evidence that directly establishes a fact; *àyíká* (surrounding, circumstantial) marks evidence that supports a conclusion by inference. The contrast is between evidence that directly proves and evidence that implies.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo
**Term B:** ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo / ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́
**English translation:** his/her certificate is genuine/valid / his/her certificate is false/fraudulent
**Description of contrast:** These are directly parallel copular constructions documented in P2b, differing only in the nominal predicate: *òdodo* (genuine, righteous) vs. *irọ́* (lie, falsehood). The contrast applies the truth/falsehood opposition (Opposition 1) to the domain of formal certification, evaluating whether a credential is authentic or forged.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** sọ òtítọ́
**Term B:** sọ irọ́ / pa irọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́ / sọ irọ́, pa irọ́
**English translation:** to tell the truth / to tell a lie
**Description of contrast:** These verbal collocations share the verb *sọ* (to say/speak) and contrast in their objects (*òtítọ́* vs. *irọ́*). *pa irọ́* is documented in P2b as a common alternative to *sọ irọ́* with the same meaning. The contrast is between the speech act of truthful assertion and the speech act of deceptive assertion.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

## Opposition 17

The contrast is between conforming to the correct moral or religious orientation and deviating from it.

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** nǹkan gidi
**Term B:** (implied: counterfeit, imitation)
**Transliteration:** nǹkan gidi / (no single attested antonym)
**English translation:** the real thing, a genuine thing / (fake, imitation — not lexicalized as a single term in the data)
**Description of contrast:** *gidi* (genuine, real, authentic) is consistently described in P1 and P2b as contrasting with counterfeits or pretenses, and the construction *kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* (it is not the real thing) is attested. However, no single lexicalized antonym to *gidi* or *nǹkan gidi* is documented in the data. The opposition is constructionally present (affirmative vs. negative existential/copular) but is not a balanced lexical pair.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** ìwà rere
**Term B:** àbùkù
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / àbùkù
**English translation:** good character, virtue, moral excellence / blemish, defect, disgrace, fault
**Description of contrast:** These two items are not explicitly paired as antonyms in the data, but P1 describes *àbùkù* as marking a person or thing that falls short of what it should genuinely be, and places it in the same semantic group (Group 4) as *ìwà rere* and *ìwà búburú*. The contrast is between possessing the quality one should have and being marked by a deficiency or flaw. The opposition is not fully symmetrical: *ìwà rere* is a positive characterization of a person's fundamental nature, while *àbùkù* marks a specific deficiency that may apply to persons or objects.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** gbàgbọ́ pé (propositional belief construction)
**Term B:** kò gbàgbọ́ (negated belief construction)
**Transliteration:** gbàgbọ́ pé... / kò gbàgbọ́...
**English translation:** to believe that... / to not believe; to disbelieve; to doubt; to reject
**Description of contrast:** These are the affirmative and negated forms of the propositional belief construction documented in P2b. *gbàgbọ́ pé* introduces a believed proposition; *kò gbàgbọ́* expresses rejection of a claim or testimony. The contrast is between epistemic acceptance and epistemic rejection of a proposition or account.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** ìjẹ́rìí (the act of testifying)
**Term B:** ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí (refusal to testify)
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́rìí / ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí
**English translation:** testimony, the act of witnessing / he/she refused to testify; he/she declined to bear witness
**Description of contrast:** These are not a morphological pair but a constructional contrast documented in P2b: the nominal *ìjẹ́rìí* (the act of testifying) is set against the construction *kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí* (to refuse to testify), which uses the verb *kọ̀* (to refuse, decline) with the infinitival *láti*. The contrast is between performing the act of testimony and withholding it.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára
**Term B:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára / ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀
**English translation:** his/her faith is strong / his/her belief has no foundation; his/her faith lacks a basis
**Description of contrast:** These are parallel evaluative constructions documented in P2b that assess the epistemic quality of a belief from opposite directions: *lágbára* (to be strong, firm) evaluates a belief positively, while *kò ní ìpìlẹ̀* (has no foundation) evaluates it negatively. The contrast is between a well-grounded, firm belief and an unfounded one.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
