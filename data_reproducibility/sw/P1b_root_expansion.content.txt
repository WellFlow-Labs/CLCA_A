# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

### Root 1: **-kweli** (truth)
- **Form:** -kweli
- **Participates in:** 5 P1 items (#1 kweli, #2 ukweli, #5 -a kweli, #22 -a kweli kabisa, and implicitly in truth-related discourse)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes truth, truthfulness, genuineness, and veracity as fundamental concepts

### Root 2: **haki** (right/justice/truth)
- **Form:** haki (from Arabic ḥaqq)
- **Participates in:** 3 P1 items (#6 haki, #10 -enye haki, #25 -a haki)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes rightness, justice, legitimacy, and truth as interrelated concepts of what is correct and just

### Root 3: **-sahihi** (correct/accurate)
- **Form:** -sahihi (from Arabic ṣaḥīḥ)
- **Participates in:** 2 P1 items (#3 sahihi, #24 usahihi)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes correctness, accuracy, and validity in statements, procedures, and documents

## STEP 2 — EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1: **-kweli** EXPANSIONS

1. **kwa kweli**
   - Literal: by/with truth
   - Translation: truly, really, indeed, in truth
   - Context: Adverbial phrase used to emphasize sincerity or truthfulness of statements. Very common in everyday speech to affirm what one is saying ("kwa kweli, sikujua" = "truly, I didn't know").
   - [CORE]

2. **kweli kweli**
   - Literal: truth truth (reduplication)
   - Translation: really truly, very true, absolutely true
   - Context: Emphatic reduplication used to intensify truthfulness claims. Common in informal speech to express strong affirmation or surprise at truth ("ni kweli kweli!" = "it's really true!").
   - [CORE]

3. **ni kweli**
   - Literal: it-is truth
   - Translation: it's true, that's right
   - Context: Standard affirmative response or statement confirming truth of propositions. Used constantly in conversation to validate what has been said.
   - [CORE]

4. **si kweli**
   - Literal: not truth
   - Translation: it's not true, that's false
   - Context: Negation used to deny truth of statements. Common in disagreements and corrections ("si kweli kabisa" = "not true at all").
   - [CORE]

5. **ukweli wa jambo**
   - Literal: truth of matter
   - Translation: the truth of the matter, the facts of the case
   - Context: Used in discussions and investigations to refer to actual facts or reality of situations. Common in legal and formal discourse.
   - [CORE]

6. **ukweli wa mambo**
   - Literal: truth of things/matters
   - Translation: the truth about things, the reality of matters
   - Context: Broader reference to truth about multiple issues or general state of affairs. Used in explanatory and analytical contexts.
   - [CORE]

7. **ukweli ulio wazi**
   - Literal: truth which-is open/clear
   - Translation: obvious truth, clear truth, self-evident truth
   - Context: Refers to truths that are manifest or undeniable. Used in arguments to point out what should be apparent to all.
   - [CORE]

8. **ukweli halisi**
   - Literal: truth genuine
   - Translation: genuine truth, real truth, actual truth
   - Context: Emphasizes authenticity of truth claims, distinguishing from apparent or claimed truth. Used when establishing what is really true.
   - [CORE]

9. **ukweli wa historia**
   - Literal: truth of history
   - Translation: historical truth, historical fact
   - Context: Refers to factual accuracy in historical accounts. Used in academic and educational contexts discussing historical veracity.
   - [CORE]

10. **ukweli wa Mungu**
    - Literal: truth of God
    - Translation: God's truth, divine truth
    - Context: Religious expression referring to truth as revealed or guaranteed by God. Common in Christian and Islamic religious discourse.
    - [CORE]

11. **kusema kweli**
    - Literal: to-speak truth
    - Translation: to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
    - Context: Verbal phrase describing act of truthful speech. Used in moral instruction and when requesting honesty ("sema kweli" = "tell the truth").
    - [CORE]

12. **kuambia kweli**
    - Literal: to-tell truth
    - Translation: to tell the truth (to someone)
    - Context: Similar to kusema kweli but with applicative extension indicating telling truth to someone. Common in confessions and honest disclosures.
    - [CORE]

13. **kuongea kweli**
    - Literal: to-talk truth
    - Translation: to speak truthfully, to talk honestly
    - Context: Describes habitual or characteristic truthful speech. Used in character evaluations ("yeye huongea kweli" = "he speaks truthfully").
    - [CORE]

14. **mwenye kweli**

    - [CORE]

15. **mtu wa kweli**

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### Root -kweli extensions:

1. **Authenticity/genuineness domain:** The root -kweli extends beyond propositional truth to mark authenticity of objects (dhahabu ya kweli "genuine gold"), genuineness of relationships (rafiki wa kweli "true friend"), and authenticity of emotions (upendo wa kweli "true love"). The root shifts from epistemic evaluation to ontological/qualitative evaluation.

2. **Identity/naming domain:** In expressions like "jina la kweli" (real name), the root marks actual identity as opposed to aliases or nicknames, extending from truth-of-statements to truth-of-identity.

3. **Causation/explanation domain:** In "sababu ya kweli" (real reason) and "maana ya kweli" (true meaning), the root marks genuine causes and meanings as opposed to apparent or stated ones, extending into explanatory and interpretive functions.

4. **Magnitude/significance domain:** In "ukweli mkubwa/mdogo" (great/small truth), the root participates in scalar evaluations of importance, extending beyond truth/falsity to significance assessment.

5. **Epistemic modality domain:** In "ukweli usio na shaka" (undoubted truth), the root combines with doubt-negation to mark certainty levels, extending into modal epistemic territory.

### Root haki extensions:

1. **Entitlement/rights domain:** The root haki extensively marks legal and moral entitlements (haki za binadamu "human rights," haki ya kumiliki "property right"), shifting from truth-evaluation to normative claims about what is due or deserved.

2. **Justice/fairness domain:** In expressions like "toa haki" (render justice) and "vunja haki" (violate rights), the root marks justice as action or state rather than truth-evaluation, extending into moral and legal action domains.

3. **Truth-claim domain:** In "haki ni kwamba..." (the truth is that...), the root functions as truth-marker similar to ukweli, showing overlap between justice/rightness and truth concepts.

4. **Possession/ownership domain:** In possessive constructions (haki yake, haki yangu), the root marks what belongs to or is due to someone, extending into ownership and entitlement territory.

5. **Legal/customary distinction:** In "haki ya kisheria" (legal right) vs. "haki ya kimila" (customary right), the root participates in distinguishing types of normative systems, extending into legal taxonomy.

### Root -sahihi extensions:

1. **Procedural correctness domain:** In expressions like "njia sahihi" (correct way) and "namna sahihi" (proper manner), the root marks procedural appropriateness beyond factual accuracy, extending into normative evaluation of methods.

2. **Temporal/spatial accuracy domain:** In "wakati sahihi" (correct time) and "mahali sahihi" (correct place), the root marks accuracy of temporal and spatial specifications, extending from propositional to indexical accuracy.

3. **Authentication/validation domain:** In "saini sahihi" (valid signature), "hati sahihi" (valid document), and "nakala sahihi" (certified copy), the root marks official validity and authentication status, extending into administrative and legal validation functions.

4. **Translation/interpretation domain:** In "tafsiri sahihi" (correct translation) and "ufafanuzi sahihi" (correct interpretation), the root evaluates accuracy of meaning transfer and interpretation, extending into hermeneutic territory.

5. **Quality assurance domain:** In verbal expressions like "hakikisha usahihi" (ensure accuracy) and "angalia usahihi" (check accuracy), the root participates in quality control and verification procedures, extending into procedural and institutional practices.
