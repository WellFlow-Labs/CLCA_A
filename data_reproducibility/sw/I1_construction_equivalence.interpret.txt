## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION → BECOME Substitution
The language shows **no dedicated REVELATION construction** (e.g., "it turned out to be true," "it was revealed to be false"). Instead, the general inchoative verb *kuwa* + predicate serves this function:
- *Kuwa kweli* can mean both "to become true" and "to turn out to be true"
- No morphological or lexical distinction exists between change-of-state and epistemic revelation

This represents a **systematic substitution** where BECOME absorbs REVELATION functions.

### 2.2 CAUSATION (factual) → VERIFICATION Substitution
The causative suffix *-ish-/-iz-* productively derives verification verbs rather than pure factual causatives:
- *Hakikisha* (< *hakika* "certain") = "to verify/ensure" (not "to make true")
- *Thibitisha* (< *thabiti* "firm/stable") = "to confirm/verify/validate" (not "to make true")

The language **lacks a productive pattern** for "make X true" or "make X false" as direct causation of truth-value. Instead, causative morphology is recruited for **verification and validation actions** (epistemic causation rather than factual causation).

### 2.3 VERIFICATION ≈ DISCONFIRMATION (Shared Morphology)
Both verification and disconfirmation use the **same causative suffix**:
- Verification: *hakikisha*, *thibitisha* (cause certainty/confirmation)
- Disconfirmation: *batilisha* (cause invalidity/nullification)

The morphological pattern is identical; only the root semantics differ (positive vs. negative validation).

### 2.4 NEGATION: Analytic Strategy
Negation is **analytic** (copula replacement) rather than morphological:
- Affirmative: *ni* → Negative: *si*
- No verbal negation morphology applied to truth predicates themselves
- Pattern: *NP si PRED* (not *NP ni si-PRED* or similar)

### 2.5 CAUSATION (character): Circumlocution Required
No productive construction exists for "make someone truthful/honest" or "make someone a liar":
- Agentive nominalization exists: *mdanganyifu* "liar/deceiver"
- But **no causative derivation** from this form
- Would require periphrastic expression: *kufanya mtu awe mdanganyifu* "to make someone be a liar" (not attested in data)

This represents a **conceptual gap** in the causative system: character causation is not grammaticalized.

### 2.6 Asymmetries
**No systematic asymmetries** between positive and negative forms:
- Both *kweli* (true) and *uongo* (false) participate equally in all constructions
- Both *sahihi* (correct) and negated forms are equally productive
- Causative derivations exist for both validation (*thibitisha*) and invalidation (*batilisha*)

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
1. **REVELATION**: No dedicated construction for epistemic revelation distinct from general becoming
   - Function absorbed by *kuwa* + predicate
   - No morphological marking distinguishing "become true" from "turn out to be true"

2. **CAUSATION (character)**: No productive morphosyntactic pattern for causing someone to become truthful/deceptive
   - Agentive nouns exist (*mdanganyifu* "liar")
   - But no causative derivation from these forms
   - Would require full periphrastic clause

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps
1. **Factual causatives**: No simple verbs meaning "make true" or "make false"
   - Causative morphology redirected to verification domain
   - *Hakikisha* means "verify/ensure" not "make certain/true"

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps
1. **Character causation**: The semantic domain of "causing someone to acquire truth-related character traits" is not grammaticalized
   - Cannot derive *mdanganyifu-ish-a* "to make someone a liar"
   - Blocked derivational pattern

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps
**None identified**. The language shows remarkable symmetry:
- Positive and negative predicates behave identically
- Truth and falsity domains equally productive
- Verification and disconfirmation share morphological patterns

## 5. Additional Observations

### 5.1 Multi-word Expression Analysis
Several constructions involve multi-word expressions that function as unified operators:

1. **Adverbial modification** (*kwa kweli*, *kwa hakika*): 
   - Syntactically: prepositional phrases
   - Functionally: manner adverbs
   - Behavior: modify entire clauses, not just verbs

2. **Complementizer constructions** (*ni kweli kwamba*):
   - Syntactically: copula + predicate + complementizer + clause
   - Functionally: propositional attitude operator
   - Behavior: takes clausal complement

3. **Verbal object constructions** (*kusema kweli*):
   - Syntactically: verb + direct object
   - Functionally: lexicalized verbal expressions
   - Behavior: *kweli* functions as incorporated object, not free noun

### 5.2 Light Verb Patterns
The verb *kuwa* "to be/become" serves as a **light verb carrier** for:
- Inchoative semantics: *kuwa kweli* "become true"
- Revelation semantics: *kuwa kweli* "turn out to be true"
- Stative semantics: *kuwa na hakika* "be certain"

This represents a **single morphosyntactic frame** serving multiple semantic operators.

### 5.3 Causative Polysemy
The causative suffix *-ish-/-iz-* shows **systematic polysemy**:
- Primary function: verification/validation (*hakikisha*, *thibitisha*)
- Secondary function: completion (*kamilisha* "complete/perfect")
- Blocked function: factual causation (*make true/false*)

This suggests the causative has been **semantically specialized** in the truth domain toward epistemic rather than factual operations.
