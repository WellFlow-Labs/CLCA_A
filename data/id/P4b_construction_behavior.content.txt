# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Epistemic Status | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|-------------|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | cerita itu benar | cerita itu benar | "that story is true" | [CORE] | Extremely common in all registers. Zero copula is obligatory in present tense affirmative; copula *adalah* possible but marked/emphatic. No restrictions observed. |
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | dokumen ini palsu | dokumen ini palsu | "this document is fake" | [CORE] | Same pattern as above. Productive across all truth-domain adjectives without exception. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | informasi itu tidak akurat | informasi itu tidak akurat | "that information is not accurate" | [CORE] | Standard negation for adjectival predicates. *Tidak* obligatory; zero negation ungrammatical. Used in all registers. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | dia bukan pembohong | dia bukan pembohong | "he/she is not a liar" | [CORE] | *Bukan* obligatory for nominal predicates; *tidak* would be ungrammatical here. Clear distributional split between *tidak* (adjectives) and *bukan* (nouns). |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | kebenaran hakiki | kebenaran hakiki | "essential truth, fundamental truth" | [CORE] | Highly productive. Adjective-noun order fixed; noun-adjective order ungrammatical except with borrowed Romance adjectives in technical registers. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | bukti nyata | bukti nyata | "tangible evidence, concrete proof" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common collocation in legal and investigative contexts. No restrictions. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | menjadi nyata | menjadi nyata | "to become real, to be realized" | [CORE] | Standard inchoative verb. Used in all registers. Combines freely with both adjectives and nouns. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | menjadi kenyataan | menjadi kenyataan | "to become reality, to come true" | [CORE] | Fixed collocation, extremely common in narrative and aspirational contexts. Preferred over *menjadi nyata* for abstract realization. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | membenarkan | meN- + benar + -kan | "to confirm, to verify, to validate" | [CORE] | Highly productive morphological pattern. Used across all registers. Passive *dibenarkan* equally common. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | menyalahkan | meN- + salah + -kan | "to blame, to fault, to accuse" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in conflict and attribution contexts. No restrictions observed. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | kebenaran | ke- + benar + -an | "truth, correctness, veracity" | [CORE] | Extremely productive derivational pattern. Creates abstract nouns from virtually all truth-domain adjectives. Used in all registers. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | kejujuran | ke- + jujur + -an | "honesty, truthfulness, integrity" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in moral and ethical discourse. Possessive forms (*kejujurannya*) frequent. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | pembohong | peN- + bohong | "liar, deceiver" | [CORE] | Productive for creating agent nouns. Carries strong negative moral judgment for deception terms. Common in all registers. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | pendusta | peN- + dusta | "liar, one who tells falsehoods" | [MARGINAL] | More formal/literary than *pembohong*. Restricted to written and elevated registers. Less frequent in conversational Indonesian. |
| **8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION** | pembenaran | peN- + benar + -an | "justification, validation, confirmation" | [CORE] | Productive pattern for process/action nouns. Common in formal, legal, and academic registers. Derived from corresponding *meN-...-kan* verbs. |
| **8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION** | pemalsuan | peN- + palsu + -an | "forgery, counterfeiting, falsification" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Frequent in legal and criminal contexts. No restrictions observed. |
| **9. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION** | sebenarnya | se- + benar + -nya | "actually, in fact, in truth" | [CORE] | Extremely common discourse marker in all registers. Functions to introduce corrections or reveal actual facts. One of the most frequent adverbials in conversational Indonesian. |
| **9. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION** | sesungguhnya | se- + sungguh + -nya | "truly, verily, in truth" | [CORE] | Common but slightly more formal than *sebenarnya*. Frequent in narrative and religious discourse. Also functions as discourse marker. |
| **10. INTENSIFICATION BY REDUPLICATION** | benar-benar | benar + benar | "really, truly, genuinely, completely" | [CORE] | Extremely productive pattern for intensification. Used across all registers. Can function as both adverb and emphatic modifier. |
| **10. INTENSIFICATION BY REDUPLICATION** | sungguh-sungguh | sungguh + sungguh | "seriously, earnestly, genuinely" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in contexts emphasizing sincerity or seriousness. Productive with verbal prefixes (*bersungguh-sungguh*). |
| **11. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | lebih benar | lebih benar (daripada yang lain) | "more correct (than the other)" | [CORE] | Standard comparative construction. *Daripada* phrase often omitted when contextually clear. Productive across all adjectives. |
| **11. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | lebih jujur | lebih jujur | "more honest, more truthful" | [CORE] | Same pattern. No restrictions. Used in all registers. |
| **12. SUPERLATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | paling benar | paling benar | "most correct, truest" | [CORE] | Standard superlative construction. Productive across all adjectives. Used in all registers. |
| **12. SUPERLATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | paling tepat | paling tepat | "most accurate, most appropriate" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in contexts requiring precision or selection among alternatives. |
| **13. INTERROGATIVE VERIFICATION** | apa benar | apa benar | "is it true, is it correct" | [CORE] | Very common yes/no question formula in conversational Indonesian. Can be intensified with particles (*apa benar ya*). *Apakah* used in formal registers. |
| **13. INTERROGATIVE VERIFICATION** | apa sungguh | apa sungguh | "is it really, are you serious" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Expresses surprise or seeks confirmation of unexpected information. Common in conversational contexts. |
| **14. CONDITIONAL TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS** | kalau benar | kalau benar | "if it's true, if correct" | [CORE] | Standard conditional construction. *Kalau* more colloquial, *jika* more formal. Productive across all truth-domain terms. |
| **14. CONDITIONAL TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS** | kalau tidak salah | kalau tidak salah | "if not mistaken, if I'm not wrong" | [CORE] | Fixed epistemic hedge formula. Extremely common in conversational Indonesian to soften assertions. Functions as discourse marker. |
| **15. BINARY OPPOSITION CONSTRUCTIONS** | benar atau salah | benar atau salah | "true or false, right or wrong" | [CORE] | Fixed phrase common in tests, logical reasoning, and decision contexts. Emphasizes binary choice between truth-values. |
| **15. BINARY OPPOSITION CONSTRUCTIONS** | asli atau palsu | asli atau palsu | "genuine or fake, authentic or counterfeit" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in authentication and verification contexts. Fixed phrase with antonymous pair. |
| **16. PREPOSITIONAL MANNER CONSTRUCTIONS** | dengan jujur | dengan jujur | "honestly, with honesty, truthfully" | [CORE] | Productive manner construction. *Dengan* more colloquial, used across all registers. Combines freely with truth-domain adjectives. |
| **16. PREPOSITIONAL MANNER CONSTRUCTIONS** | secara faktual | secara faktual | "factually, in a factual manner" | [CORE] | More formal than *dengan* construction. Particularly common with borrowed/technical terms. Frequent in academic and official registers. |
| **17. EVIDENTIAL RESULT CONSTRUCTIONS** | ternyata | ter- + nyata | "it turns out, apparently, evidently" | [CORE] | Extremely common discourse marker introducing new information contradicting prior belief. One of the most frequent evidential markers in Indonesian. |
| **17. EVIDENTIAL RESULT CONSTRUCTIONS** | terbukti bohong | ter- + bukti + bohong | "proven false, proven to be a lie" | [CORE] | *Terbukti* combines productively with truth-predicates to mark demonstrated truth-value. Common in investigative and legal contexts. |
| **18. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH TRUTH NOUNS** | mencari kebenaran | mencari kebenaran | "to seek truth, to search for truth" | [CORE] | Productive pattern with verbs of seeking, revealing, upholding. Common in philosophical, investigative, and religious discourse. |
| **18. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH TRUTH NOUNS** | menghadapi kenyataan | menghadapi kenyataan | "to face reality, to confront facts" | [CORE] | Fixed collocation, very common in psychological and counseling contexts. *Menghadapi* combines productively with truth-nouns. |
| **19. COMPOUND ERROR CONSTRUCTIONS** | salah paham | salah paham | "to misunderstand, misunderstanding" | [CORE] | Highly productive pattern specifying error domain. Functions as both verb and noun. Extremely common in conversational and written Indonesian. |
| **19. COMPOUND ERROR CONSTRUCTIONS** | salah hitung | salah hitung | "miscalculation, calculation error" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in mathematical and accounting contexts. Pattern extends to *salah lihat*, *salah dengar*, *salah ucap*, etc. |
| **20. EMPHATIC CONFIRMATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | benar sekali | benar sekali | "very true, absolutely correct, exactly right" | [CORE] | Very common emphatic affirmation in conversational responses. *Sekali* follows adjective; order fixed. Used across all registers. |
| **20. EMPHATIC CONFIRMATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | bohong belaka | bohong belaka | "nothing but lies, entirely false" | [CORE] | *Belaka* emphasizes totality, often with negative connotation. Less frequent than *sekali* pattern but productive. |
| **21. RELATIVIZATION WITH TRUTH PREDICATES** | yang benar | yang benar | "the correct one, what is right" | [CORE] | Highly productive nominalization/relativization pattern. Used to identify correct alternatives. Can function as exclamation (*Yang benar saja!* "Really?!"). |
| **21. RELATIVIZATION WITH TRUTH PREDICATES** | yang sesungguhnya | yang sesungguhnya | "the real one, what is truly, the actual" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Emphasizes actual reality versus appearance. Common in narrative and investigative contexts. |
| **22. PASSIVE APPLICATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | dibohongi | di- + bohong + -i | "to be lied to, to be deceived" | [CORE] | Productive passive applicative marking patient/victim of deception. *-i* applicative with *bohong*, *-kan* causative with *salah*. Common in all registers. |
| **22. PASSIVE APPLICATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS** | disalahkan | di- + salah + -kan | "to be blamed, to be faulted" | [CORE] | Same passive pattern with causative *-kan*. Very common in conflict and attribution contexts. No restrictions observed. |
| **23. HEDGED NEGATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | kurang benar | kurang benar | "not quite correct, somewhat inaccurate" | [CORE] | Productive polite/hedged negation pattern. Common in formal discourse and face-saving contexts. Softens direct contradiction. |
| **23. HEDGED NEGATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | kurang jujur | kurang jujur | "not quite honest, somewhat dishonest" | [CORE] | Same pattern. Functions as euphemistic negation. Productive across all truth-domain adjectives. |
| **24. EXISTENTIAL TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS** | benar adanya | benar adanya | "it is true, it truly exists, it is indeed the case" | [MARGINAL] | Fixed phrase, not productive beyond *benar*. Common in formal statements and testimony. Pattern does not extend to other truth-domain roots. |
| **25. VALIDATION AND AUTHORIZATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | mengesahkan | meN- + sah + -kan | "to validate, to legalize, to authorize, to ratify" | [CORE] | Primary verb for legal/official validation. Common in legal and administrative contexts. Passive *disahkan* equally frequent. |
| **25. VALIDATION AND AUTHORIZATION CONSTRUCTIONS** | memvalidasi | meN- + valid + -asi | "to validate" | [CORE] | Borrowed pattern with suffix *-asi* (from -ation). Common in technical and administrative contexts. Coexists with native *mengesahkan*. |

## ALTERNATE CONSTRUCTIONS ATTESTED

### For Construction 2 (Negative Attribution):
- **Hedged variant**: *kurang* + ADJ (see Construction 23) — softer negation
- **Emphatic variant**: *sama sekali tidak* + ADJ — "not at all, absolutely not"

### For Construction 9 (Adverbial Modification):
- **Superlative variant**: *se-* + REDUP + *-nya* (e.g., *sebenar-benarnya* "most truly") — combines reduplication with adverbial pattern

### For Construction 13 (Interrogative Verification):
- **Formal variant**: *apakah* + ADJ — more formal than *apa*
- **Tag question variant**: ADJ + *kan?* (e.g., *benar kan?* "it's true, right?")

### For Construction 14 (Conditional Truth):
- **Formal variant**: *jika* replaces *kalau* in written/formal registers
- **Concessive variant**: *meskipun/walaupun* + ADJ — "even if/although X"

### For Construction 20 (Emphatic Confirmation):
- **Variant intensifier**: *sangat* + ADJ — "very X" (precedes rather than follows)
- **Colloquial variant**: ADJ + *banget* — informal intensifier (follows adjective)

## GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

1. **Register variation**: Clear split between colloquial (*kalau*, *dengan*, *apa*) and formal (*jika*, *secara*, *apakah*) variants in several constructions.

2. **Productivity**: Constructions 1-3, 5-6, 8, 10-13, 16, 19, 22-23 show unrestricted productivity across truth-domain vocabulary.

3. **Fixed phrases**: Constructions 14 (*kalau tidak salah*), 15 (binary pairs), 18 (*menghadapi kenyataan*), 24 (*benar adanya*) show lexical restriction.

4. **Morphological complexity**: Indonesian shows rich derivational morphology (ke-...-an, peN-...-an, meN-...-kan, ter-) all highly productive in truth domain.

5. **Zero copula**: Construction 1 demonstrates obligatory zero copula in present tense affirmative, distinguishing Indonesian from copula-requiring languages.
