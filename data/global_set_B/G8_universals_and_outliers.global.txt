# G8 — CANDIDATE UNIVERSALS, OUTLIERS, AND OPEN QUESTIONS

## 1. CANDIDATE UNIVERSALS / STRONG TENDENCIES

### 1.1 Constructional Universals

#### CU-1: Basic Predication Universally Licensed
**Statement**: All languages allow basic predicative attribution of truth-domain properties to subjects through copular or stative constructions.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages without exception (zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu)
- F4 construction tables document full productivity across all dimensions
- G7a matrix shows ALLOWED across all dimensions × F1 (Basic Predication)
- No restrictions observed on subject type or truth-domain term

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Formal realization varies (zero copula vs. obligatory copula, word order)
- Quechua adds obligatory evidential marking but predication itself is unrestricted
- No exceptions to the functional availability

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
This represents the most fundamental constructional function. No language can express truth-domain meanings without basic predication.

---

#### CU-2: Morphological Negation Universally Productive
**Statement**: All languages show highly productive morphological negation of truth-domain terms, creating systematic oppositions (true/not-true, correct/incorrect, honest/dishonest).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 不, he לא, sa अ-/अन-, el α-/δεν, en un-/in-/dis-, tl hindi/di-, sw mana/si, qu mana)
- G7a matrix shows ALLOWED across all dimensions × F2 (Negation)
- G6 documents morphological negation as universal strategy
- Coexists with lexical antonyms but extends to derived forms

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Strategy varies (prefix vs. proclitic vs. negative copula)
- Some languages prefer lexical antonyms for basic oppositions (true/false) but use morphological negation for derived terms
- No exceptions to productivity itself

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal across all sampled languages from unrelated families. Represents fundamental grammatical resource for creating oppositions.

---

#### CU-3: Abstract Nominalization Universally Productive
**Statement**: All languages show productive derivation of abstract quality nouns from truth-domain terms (truth, honesty, accuracy, authenticity as abstract concepts).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages with varying formal strategies
- Suffixation (zh -性, he -וּת, sa -त्व/-ता, el -ότητα/-ία, en -ness/-ity)
- Circumfixation (tl ka-...-an, sw u-...-a)
- Periphrasis (qu nominalized copula)
- G7a matrix shows ALLOWED across all dimensions × F10
- F2a tables document extensive derivational families

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Formal strategies vary by language type
- AOML notes ESS and LIFE show restrictions on ABSTR, but current empirical data doesn't clearly test these
- No exceptions to general productivity

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal functional need for abstract quality nouns. Essential for formal/philosophical discourse across all languages.

---

#### CU-4: Adnominal Modification Universally Productive
**Statement**: All languages productively modify nouns with truth-domain terms (true story, false witness, genuine friend) through language-specific strategies.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages without restrictions
- Direct prenominal adjectives (zh, el, en)
- Associative/genitive constructions (he, tl, sw, qu)
- G7a matrix shows ALLOWED across all dimensions × F3
- F4 tables document full productivity

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Formal strategy correlates with general language typology (isolating/fusional vs. Semitic/Austronesian/Bantu)
- Word order varies (prenominal vs. postnominal)
- No exceptions to functional availability

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal across all languages. Represents fundamental need to characterize entities with truth-domain properties.

---

#### CU-5: "Speak Truth/Lies" Constructions Universally Conventionalized
**Statement**: All languages show highly conventionalized verb-object constructions for "tell the truth" and "tell lies," despite structural variation.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 说实话/说谎, he לומר את האמת/לספר שקרים, sa सत्यं वद, el λέω την αλήθεια/λέω ψέματα, en tell the truth/tell lies, tl magsabi ng totoo/magsinungaling, sw sema ukweli/sema uongo, qu chiqanta rimay/llullata rimay)
- G5 notes: "Universal pattern with language-specific verbs"
- G7a matrix documents as F12 (Light Verb Constructions)
- Extremely high frequency across all registers

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Structural variation: definiteness marking, case marking, verb choice
- Tagalog uses different construction for lying (dedicated verb magsinungaling)
- No exceptions to functional conventionalization

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal pragmatic importance of truth-telling speech acts. Represents one of the most stable cross-linguistic patterns in the domain.

---

#### CU-6: Analytic Comparison Only (No Synthetic Forms)
**Statement**: No language in the sample shows synthetic comparative or superlative morphology for truth-domain terms; all use analytic strategies (more/most + adjective).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 更/最, he יותר/הכי, sa separate constructions, el πιο, en more/most, tl mas/pinaka-, sw zaidi, qu aswan)
- G4 notes: "No synthetic forms attested"
- G7a matrix shows universal analytic comparison
- F4 tables document productivity

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Some languages have synthetic comparison for other adjectives (English good/better/best) but not for truth-domain
- Sanskrit uses separate comparative constructions rather than affixation
- No exceptions observed in truth-domain

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal pattern across all 8 languages. Truth-domain terms resist synthetic comparison even in languages that have it for other adjectives.

### 1.2 Semantic Universals

#### SU-1: Propositional Truth vs. Material Authenticity Distinction
**Statement**: Most languages (6/8) lexically distinguish propositional truth (whether statements correspond to facts) from material authenticity (whether objects are genuine originals).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Clear lexical distinction in zh (实 vs. 真), he (אמת vs. אמיתי), el (αλήθεια vs. γνήσιος/αυθεντικός), en (true vs. genuine/authentic), tl (totoo vs. tunay), sw (kweli vs. halisi)
- G3 identifies this as Dimension 1 vs. Dimension 2
- G2 notes: "6/8 languages show dedicated lexical opposition"
- Semantic groups in F3 tables consistently separate these domains

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit सत्य (satya) and Quechua chiqan cover both domains without clear internal lexical distinction
- Hebrew אמיתי derived from truth root but functionally distinct
- The distinction may be constructional rather than lexical in sa and qu

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong recurrent pattern (6/8 languages) but not universal. The two exceptions (sa, qu) suggest the distinction can be handled constructionally rather than requiring separate lexemes.

---

#### SU-2: Deception vs. Error Universally Distinguished
**Statement**: All languages lexically distinguish deliberate falsehood/deception (involving intent to mislead) from unintentional error/mistake (involving lack of knowledge or judgment failure).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 欺骗 vs. 错误, he שקר vs. טעות, sa मिथ्या vs. भ्रम, el ψέμα vs. λάθος, en lie vs. error, tl kasinungalingan vs. pagkakamali, sw uongo vs. makosa, qu llulla vs. pantay)
- G3 identifies as Dimension 6 vs. Dimension 7
- G2 notes: "Universal semantic groups for deception vs. error"
- Semantic groups in F3 tables universally separate these

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Some terms may be ambiguous between intentional and unintentional (context-dependent)
- The boundary may be gradient rather than categorical in some cases
- No exceptions to the basic lexical distinction

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal across all sampled languages. Represents fundamental moral distinction between deliberate and accidental falsehood.

---

#### SU-3: Evidence/Proof vs. Truth/Claim Universally Distinguished
**Statement**: All languages maintain clear distinction between evidential support (evidence, proof, testimony) and propositional content (truth, facts, claims).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 证据 vs. 事实, he ראיה vs. אמת, sa हेतु/प्रमाण vs. सत्य, el απόδειξη vs. αλήθεια, en evidence vs. truth, tl katunayan vs. katotohanan, sw ushahidi vs. ukweli, qu takyay vs. chiqan)
- G3 identifies as Dimension 20
- G2 notes: "Universal distinction fundamental to argumentation"
- Semantic groups consistently separate evidential from propositional

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Some terms may span both (Tagalog katunayan "proof/evidence/fact")
- The distinction may be less elaborated in some languages
- No exceptions to basic functional distinction

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal across all languages. Fundamental to argumentative and legal discourse. Distinguishing what is claimed from what supports the claim appears universally necessary.

---

#### SU-4: Honesty/Sincerity (Character) vs. Factual Accuracy Distinguished
**Statement**: Most languages (6/8) lexically distinguish evaluation of persons' habitual truthfulness or sincere character from evaluation of whether specific statements correspond to facts.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Clear lexical distinction in zh (诚实 vs. 真实), he (כנה vs. אמת), el (ειλικρινής vs. αλήθεια), en (honest vs. true), tl (tapat vs. totoo), sw (uses halisi/sinsero vs. kweli)
- G3 identifies as Dimension 5 vs. Dimension 1
- G2 notes: "6/8 languages clearly lexicalize"
- Semantic groups consistently separate character from content

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit uses सत्यवादिन् (satyavādin) "truth-speaker" (compound) rather than dedicated sincerity term
- Quechua uses chiqan runa "truthful person" and chiqan sunqu "true heart" (compounds) rather than dedicated lexeme
- The distinction may be constructional rather than lexical in sa and qu

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong recurrent pattern (6/8 languages) but not universal. Reflects different evaluation targets (character traits vs. propositional content).

### 1.3 Asymmetry Universals

#### AU-1: Negative Agentive Productive, Positive Agentive Blocked
**Statement**: Agentive nominalization for "liar/deceiver" is productive across all languages; for "truth-teller/honest person" it is blocked or uses circumlocution in most languages (6/8).

**Evidence Summary**:
- **Productive negative**: All 8 languages (he שקרן, sa मिथ्यावादिन्, el ψεύτης, en liar, tl sinungaling, sw mwongo, qu llullakuq, zh uses circumlocution but has dedicated forms like 骗子)
- **Blocked positive**: 6/8 languages (zh circumlocution, he participial, el gap, en marginal compound, tl gap, sw possessive)
- **Exceptions**: sa सत्यवादिन् [CORE], qu chiqan rimaq [CORE - compound]
- G6 documents as "strongest universal blocking pattern in the data"
- G7a matrix shows F9 BLOCKED across all dimensions

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit and Quechua show productive formation for both poles
- English liar is suppletive (not regular derivation from false)
- The asymmetry is in productivity, not absolute absence

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Systematic asymmetry across 6/8 languages from unrelated families. Represents universal cognitive/social bias toward marking negative moral agents.

---

#### AU-2: Causative Verification Productive, Causative Falsification Blocked
**Statement**: Causative derivations meaning "make true/verify/confirm" are productive in most languages (6/8); causatives meaning "make false/falsify" are blocked or use completely different strategies in all languages (8/8).

**Evidence Summary**:
- **Verification productive**: 6/8 languages (he hif'il, el -ώνω, tl pa-, sw -isha, qu -cha-, plus zh resultative compounds)
- **Falsification blocked**: 8/8 languages lack productive causative from false/lie roots
- G6 documents: "No language shows productive causative 'make false' from false/lie roots"
- G7a matrix shows F5 ALLOWED (6/8) vs. F6 BLOCKED (8/8)
- Languages use deception verbs instead (deceive, mislead, corrupt)

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Greek διαψεύδω diapsévdo exists but marked [MARGINAL]
- English falsify uses different root (Latin), not derived from false
- Chinese uses resultative compounds rather than affixal causatives
- The asymmetry is in derivational strategy, not functional absence

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Universal asymmetry across all 8 languages. Verification conceptualized as active process; falsification achieved through alternative means (deception, corruption, forgery).

---

#### AU-3: Revelation Intransitive, Concealment Transitive
**Statement**: Truth revelation constructions are typically intransitive or passive (truth emerges on its own); truth concealment constructions are typically transitive and agentive (someone hides truth).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in 6/8 languages (zh 真相大白 intransitive vs. 隐瞒真相 transitive, he האמת תצא לאור intransitive vs. להסתיר transitive, el βγαίνει intransitive vs. κρύβω transitive, en "truth will out" vs. "hide truth", tl lumabas vs. itago, sw unadhihirika passive vs. ficha transitive)
- G6 documents as "strong recurrent pattern"
- F5 opposition tables show systematic constructional asymmetry
- Less prominent in sa and qu

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit and Quechua show less elaborated revelation metaphors
- Some languages allow transitive revelation (reveal the truth) alongside intransitive
- The asymmetry is in preferred/unmarked constructions

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong pattern in 6/8 languages. Reflects conceptual metaphor: truth has inherent tendency to emerge; concealment requires active suppression.

---

#### AU-4: Positive Emergence Natural, Negative Emergence Blocked
**Statement**: Positive state emergence (become good, become complete) is productive; negative state emergence (become false, become wrong) is blocked or highly restricted across languages.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in 7/8 languages for blocking of negative emergence
- "Become good/better" productive where tested (zh 变好, he להשתפר, el γίνομαι καλύτερος, en improve, qu allinyay)
- "Become false" blocked or marginal (zh 变假 rare, he uses different root, el marginal, en *come false ungrammatical, tl marginal, sw uses revelation, qu marginal)
- G6 documents as systematic asymmetry
- G7b confirms alignment with AOML Rule R4

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Distinction between state emergence (blocked) and action performance (allowed): "make mistakes" is productive
- Some languages allow "become false" in limited contexts (epistemic change: "turned out to be false")
- Sanskrit भवति shows context-dependent inchoative for both poles

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong pattern across 7/8 languages. Requires clarification: applies to autonomous state emergence, not action performance or epistemic discovery.

---

#### AU-5: Inchoative "Become True" Restricted, Revelation Preferred
**Statement**: Simple inchoative constructions for "become true" are restricted or marginal; languages prefer revelation/discovery constructions (truth emerged, came to light, was revealed).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Restricted in 7/8 languages (zh 变真 marginal, he uses התברר "became clear", el γίνεται αληθινό marginal, en *become true marginal/come true restricted, tl marginal, sw uses revelation, qu chiqanyay marginal)
- Revelation constructions productive in 6/8 languages
- G6 documents preference for revelation over inchoative
- F6 naturalness data shows systematic pattern

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit भवति allows inchoative reading more readily
- "Come true" productive in English but lexically restricted to this phrase
- Some languages allow inchoative for epistemic change ("turned out to be true")

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong pattern across 7/8 languages. Truth conceptualized as pre-existing and revealed rather than coming into being.

### 1.4 Lexical Universals

#### LU-1: Core Truth-Falsehood Opposition Universally Lexicalized
**Statement**: All languages show dedicated lexical opposition between truth and falsehood for propositional content (true/false, truth/lie).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages (zh 真/假, he אמת/שקר, sa सत्य/मिथ्या, el αλήθεια/ψέμα, en true/false, tl totoo/kasinungalingan, sw kweli/uongo, qu chiqan/llulla)
- G2 notes: "Primary lexical opposition in all languages"
- F5 opposition tables document systematic contrasts
- Both poles equally productive in derivations

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Some languages have multiple terms (sa सत्य/असत्य and सत्य/मिथ्या)
- Tagalog kasinungalingan is derived abstract noun, not simple adjective
- No exceptions to basic lexical distinction

**Confidence Rating**: **HIGH**
Most fundamental lexical opposition in the domain. Universal across all sampled languages.

---

#### LU-2: Dedicated Deception Vocabulary Universally Present
**Statement**: All languages show dedicated vocabulary for deception, lying, and trickery, often more elaborate than honesty vocabulary.

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in all 8 languages with multiple terms in most
- Chinese: 欺骗, 诈骗, 蒙骗, 哄骗
- Greek: ψέμα, απάτη, δόλος, υποκρισία
- Tagalog: kasinungalingan, daya, linlang, palabás, kunwari
- English: lie, deceive, trick, cheat, fraud
- G6 notes: "Deception vocabulary often more elaborate than honesty vocabulary"

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Degree of elaboration varies across languages
- Some languages show equally elaborate honesty vocabulary
- Pattern is tendency, not absolute rule

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong tendency across multiple languages. Reflects cognitive salience of deception and social need to identify/warn about dishonesty.

### 1.5 Discourse/Pragmatic Universals

#### PU-1: Discourse Marker Grammaticalization from Truth Vocabulary
**Statement**: Truth-domain terms grammaticalize into discourse markers (introducing frank statements, seeking confirmation, expressing surprise) in all living languages sampled (7/8, excluding classical Sanskrit).

**Evidence Summary**:
- Attested in 7/8 languages (zh 说真的, he באמת?, el αλήθεια?, en "to be honest"/"really?", tl "totoo lang", sw "kwa kweli", qu chiqap)
- G5 documents as "universal pragmatic pathway active in living languages"
- G7a matrix shows F17 ALLOWED in 7/8
- Functions: introduce frank statements, seek confirmation, express surprise

**Caveats/Exceptions**:
- Sanskrit (classical language) lacks this pragmatic grammaticalization
- Degree of grammaticalization varies (some fully grammaticalized, others still compositional)
- Specific functions vary across languages

**Confidence Rating**: **MEDIUM-HIGH**
Strong pattern in all living languages. Represents active grammaticalization pathway: truth-assertion → speaker stance marker → discourse management particle.

## 2. MAJOR OUTLIERS

### 2.1 Language-Level Outliers

#### L-OUT-1: Sanskrit — Philosophical Elaboration
**What Diverges**: Sanskrit shows unique elaboration of philosophical/epistemological dimensions not systematically lexicalized in other languages.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Two-truths doctrine vocabulary**: व्यवहार/परमार्थ (conventional/ultimate truth) with dedicated adverbials व्यवहारतः/परमार्थतः — **UNIQUE** to Sanskrit
2. **Epistemological validity framework**: प्रमाण-śāstra system with six pramāṇas (perception, inference, testimony, etc.) — **UNIQUE** systematic elaboration
3. **Dual number for oppositions**: Productive use of dual genitive for binary contrasts (सत्यासत्ययोः) — shared only with Hebrew, but more systematic in Sanskrit
4. **Ablative standpoint marking**: -तः suffix marking philosophical perspective — **UNIQUE** systematic use

**Strength of Divergence**: **STRONG**
These are not marginal features but central to Sanskrit philosophical vocabulary. Represent systematic elaborations absent in other languages.

**Scope**: **BROAD**
Affects multiple dimensions (D9 conventional/ultimate truth, D10 epistemological validity) and multiple constructions (ablative standpoint, dual oppositions).

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects Sanskrit's role as classical philosophical metalanguage
- Represents cultural/historical elaboration rather than universal semantic structure
- Other languages distinguish evidence from truth (universal) but lack systematic pramāṇa framework
- G7b notes: "Language-specific philosophical elaboration"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TRUE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE**
Not due to limited evidence. Sanskrit genuinely elaborates philosophical dimensions other languages don't systematically lexicalize.

---

#### L-OUT-2: Quechua — Evidential Integration
**What Diverges**: Quechua shows obligatory grammatical evidential marking integrated with truth vocabulary; all other languages lack grammatical evidentials.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Obligatory evidentials**: -mi (direct knowledge), -si (reportative), -cha (conjectural) required in main clauses
2. **Integration with truth terms**: chiqapmi "it is truly so (I know directly)", chiqapsi "it is reportedly true", chiqapcha "it seems to be true"
3. **Grammatical rather than lexical**: Evidentials are inflectional morphology, not optional adverbs

**Strength of Divergence**: **STRONG**
Typologically rare feature. No other sampled language shows grammatical evidentials.

**Scope**: **BROAD**
Affects all main clause predications, not just truth-domain. But integration with truth vocabulary creates unique patterns.

**Interpretation**:
- Represents typological feature of Quechuan family
- Other languages mark epistemic source through optional modals/adverbs
- G4 notes: "Typologically rare feature specific to Quechua and related languages"
- G7b notes: "Quechua pattern unique in sample"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TRUE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE**
Grammatical evidentials are typological feature, not data gap. Quechua genuinely differs from other sampled languages.

---

#### L-OUT-3: Greek — Pseudo-X Productivity
**What Diverges**: Greek shows uniquely extensive ψευδ- (pseud-) compounding for creating critical/polemical "false pretender to category" terms.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Academic/intellectual**: ψευδοεπιστήμη "pseudoscience", ψευδοφιλόσοφος "pseudo-philosopher", ψευδοδιανοούμενος "pseudo-intellectual"
2. **Political**: ψευδοδημοκρατία "pseudo-democracy", ψευδοπατριωτισμός "false patriotism"
3. **Media**: ψευδοπληροφορία "misinformation", ψευδοείδηση "fake news"
4. **Extreme productivity**: Pattern extends to numerous domains

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE-STRONG**
English borrows the pattern (pseudoscience) but shows much less productivity. Other languages lack equivalent systematic pattern.

**Scope**: **NARROW**
Affects only pseudo-X compounding, not core truth-domain structure. Represents specialized elaboration for critical discourse.

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects Greek's role in academic and intellectual discourse
- Represents cultural/historical development of critical vocabulary
- G2 notes: "Greek-specific elaboration for critical/polemical discourse"
- G4 notes: "Language-specific elaboration"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TRUE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE**
Not due to limited evidence. Greek genuinely shows unique productivity in this pattern.

---

#### L-OUT-4: Chinese — Resultative Verb Compounds
**What Diverges**: Chinese uses resultative verb compounds (改正 "change-correct", 证实 "prove-solid") as preferred strategy where other languages use affixal causatives.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Resultative compounds**: 改正 gǎizhèng, 落实 luòshí, 证实 zhèngshí, 坐实 zuòshí
2. **Preferred over periphrasis**: Resultative compounds more common than 使...成为 constructions
3. **Characteristic morphological strategy**: Extends beyond truth-domain to general Chinese morphology

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Other languages use affixal causatives (6 languages) or periphrastic constructions (English). Chinese strategy is typologically distinct but functionally equivalent.

**Scope**: **NARROW**
Affects causative constructions (F5, F7) but not core semantic structure. Represents morphological typology difference.

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects general Chinese morphological strategy (isolating language with compounding)
- Functionally equivalent to affixal causatives in other languages
- G4 notes: "Characteristic Chinese morphological strategy not paralleled elsewhere"
- G5 notes: "Typological substitution: compounding replaces affixation"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TYPOLOGICAL VARIATION**
Not structural difference in semantic geometry but in morphological realization of universal function (causative verification).

---

#### L-OUT-5: Tagalog — Particle System Elaboration
**What Diverges**: Tagalog shows extensive particle system (ba, nga, lang, naman, pa) modulating illocutionary force of truth-domain terms; other languages use separate adverbs or discourse markers.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Interrogative**: ba (totoo ba? "is it true?")
2. **Emphatic**: nga (totoo nga "it's really true")
3. **Limitative**: lang (totoo lang "honestly, just the truth")
4. **Concessive**: naman (totoo naman "it is true though")
5. **Additive**: pa (totoo pa "even more true")

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Quechua shows some parallel particle system (-chu, -mi/-si/-cha, -pas) but less extensive. Other languages use separate adverbs.

**Scope**: **MODERATE**
Affects discourse management and pragmatic modulation but not core semantic structure.

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects Austronesian typology (particle-rich languages)
- Functionally similar to discourse markers in other languages but grammatically integrated
- G4 notes: "Agglutinative/particle-rich language strategy"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TYPOLOGICAL VARIATION**
Represents morphological strategy difference, not semantic difference. Other languages achieve similar pragmatic functions through separate words.

### 2.2 Construction-Level Outliers

#### C-OUT-1: English "Come True" Lexical Restriction
**What Diverges**: English shows fixed expression "come true" for realization of predictions/hopes, but blocks *come false and marks *become true as awkward.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Fixed expression**: "His dreams came true" [CORE]
2. **Blocked negative**: *"The rumor came false" [UNATTESTED]
3. **Marked alternative**: "became true" [MARGINAL]
4. **Unique restriction**: Other languages allow more flexible inchoative constructions

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Other languages show restrictions on inchoatives but not this specific lexical fossilization.

**Scope**: **NARROW**
Affects only inchoative constructions (F4) for truth-domain. Doesn't affect other constructional functions.

**Interpretation**:
- Represents lexical fossilization of specific metaphor (dreams/predictions coming true)
- Other languages use more productive inchoative or revelation constructions
- F6 documents as [CORE] for "come true" but [UNATTESTED] for *come false
- G6 notes: "Extreme asymmetry: come true fixed expression; *come false blocked"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **LEXICAL IDIOSYNCRASY**
Likely historical accident of English lexicalization rather than deep structural principle. Other languages don't show this specific restriction.

---

#### C-OUT-2: Tagalog "Tama Na" Semantic Shift
**What Diverges**: Tagalog tama shows unique semantic extension from "correct" to "enough, stop" in the construction "tama na."

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Semantic shift**: tama "correct" → tama na "enough, stop"
2. **Highly conventionalized**: "Tama na yan!" "That's enough! Stop that!"
3. **Unique to Tagalog**: No parallel in other languages

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Other languages don't show this specific semantic extension from correctness to cessation.

**Scope**: **NARROW**
Affects only specific construction (tama na). Doesn't affect core correctness meanings of tama.

**Interpretation**:
- Represents language-specific grammaticalization pathway
- Possibly from "it is correct (to stop now)" → "enough, stop"
- F1 notes: "Semantic shift from correctness to sufficiency"
- G2 notes: "Semantic extension to cessation domain"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **LANGUAGE-SPECIFIC GRAMMATICALIZATION**
Historical development specific to Tagalog. Not structural principle but diachronic pathway.

---

#### C-OUT-3: Hebrew Binyan Voice Alternations
**What Diverges**: Hebrew shows Semitic root-and-pattern morphology creating systematic voice alternations (pa'al → pi'el → hif'il → huf'al) not paralleled in other languages.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Templatic morphology**: Root צ-ד-ק → הצדיק (causative) → מוצדק (passive causative)
2. **Systematic voice system**: Multiple binyanim create valency/voice alternations
3. **Unique to Semitic**: No equivalent in other sampled languages

**Strength of Divergence**: **STRONG**
Represents fundamentally different morphological typology (root-and-pattern vs. affixation/compounding).

**Scope**: **BROAD**
Affects all verbal derivations, not just truth-domain. Represents general morphological typology.

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects Semitic morphological structure
- Functionally equivalent to affixation in other languages (creates causatives, passives, etc.)
- G4 notes: "Semitic morphological typology"
- G5 notes: "No equivalent in other languages, which use affixation or separate lexemes"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **TYPOLOGICAL VARIATION**
Not semantic difference but morphological realization difference. Hebrew achieves same functions (causatives, passives) through different formal means.

### 2.3 Dimension-Specific Outliers

#### D-OUT-1: Chinese Substantiality Dimension (实/虚)
**What Diverges**: Chinese uniquely elaborates substantiality vs. emptiness dimension (实 shí "solid, substantial" vs. 虚 xū "empty, hollow") with extensive vocabulary and fixed expressions.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Systematic opposition**: 实/虚 spans concrete reality, practical implementation, and substance vs. show
2. **Fixed expressions**: 华而不实 "flashy but lacking substance", 脚踏实地 "feet on solid ground", 虚虚实实 "mix of feint and reality"
3. **Extensive derivations**: 实际, 实在, 实力, 实质, 实体, etc.

**Strength of Divergence**: **STRONG**
Quechua shows some parallel (hunt'a/ch'usaq "full/empty") but less systematic. Other languages lack equivalent elaboration.

**Scope**: **MODERATE**
Affects one semantic dimension (D8) but that dimension is extensively elaborated in Chinese.

**Interpretation**:
- May reflect cultural emphasis on substance vs. appearance
- G2 notes: "No other language shows equivalent systematic elaboration"
- G3 identifies as Dimension 4 but notes: "Strongly lexicalized in Chinese and Quechua; weaker in other languages"
- G7b notes: "Empirical dimension not captured by AOML axis set"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **CULTURAL/LINGUISTIC ELABORATION**
Likely represents cultural emphasis rather than universal dimension. Quechua parallel suggests it's possible but not necessary cross-linguistically.

---

#### D-OUT-2: Hebrew Truth-Justice Conflation (צדק)
**What Diverges**: Hebrew צדק (tsedek) uniquely conflates factual correctness ("being right" in arguments) with moral righteousness and justice.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Single root spans**: "You're right" (צודק tsodek) and "justice/righteousness" (צדק tsedek)
2. **Extensive derivational family**: All from single root צ-ד-ק
3. **Shared with Swahili**: Swahili haki (from Arabic ḥaqq) shows similar conflation

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Swahili shows parallel pattern (possibly through Arabic influence). Other 6 languages maintain separation.

**Scope**: **MODERATE**
Affects mapping between factual and moral domains (D3 vs. D19) but doesn't prevent expressing both meanings.

**Interpretation**:
- May reflect Semitic/Arabic conceptual linking of truth and justice
- Other languages separate: zh 正确 vs. 正义, el αλήθεια vs. δικαιοσύνη, en true vs. just
- G2 notes: "Hebrew and Swahili (via Arabic influence) conceptually link factual correctness with moral/legal rightness"
- G3 notes: "Culturally variable boundary"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **CULTURAL/LINGUISTIC VARIATION**
Represents different semantic organization, not universal constraint. Both separated and conflated patterns are viable.

#### D-OUT-3: Greek Technical Domain Extensions (ορθ-)
**What Diverges**: Greek ορθ- (orth-) root uniquely extends into multiple technical domains (medical, linguistic, geometric) beyond core correctness meanings.

**Specific Divergences**:
1. **Medical**: ορθοπεδική "orthopedics", ορθοδοντική "orthodontics"
2. **Linguistic**: ορθογραφία "orthography", ορθοεπεία "orthoepy"
3. **Geometric**: ορθογώνιος "right-angled", ορθογώνιο "rectangle"
4. **Philosophical**: ορθολογισμός "rationalism"
5. **Religious**: ορθοδοξία "orthodoxy"

**Strength of Divergence**: **MODERATE**
Chinese shows some parallel (正 zhèng extends to spatial/temporal precision, 真 zhēn to biological terms) but less systematic. Other languages show minimal technical extension.

**Scope**: **MODERATE**
Affects technical vocabulary but not everyday truth-domain core. Represents specialized elaboration.

**Interpretation**:
- Reflects Greek's role in scientific and academic terminology
- Many terms borrowed into other languages (orthography, orthodoxy, orthopedics)
- G2 notes: "Extensive semantic extension from 'straight/correct' into specialized technical vocabularies"
- G4 notes: "Language-specific elaboration"

**Structural vs. Evidential**: **CULTURAL/HISTORICAL ELABORATION**
Represents historical development of technical vocabulary, not universal semantic structure.

## 3. OPEN QUESTIONS

**Current Status**: Both models defensible; requires targeted investigation.

**Why Important**: Affects strength of Rule R1 and whether indirect causation is structurally allowed.

**Current Status**: **CONFLICT** between AOML universal blocking and empirical variable restriction. Requires sample reconciliation or rule clarification.

#### OQ-6: Authenticity Causation Asymmetry (Rule R3)
**Question**: Does authenticity block positive causation (can't manufacture authenticity) but allow negative causation (can forge/destroy)?

**Current Evidence**:

- Empirical data shows verification productive but conflates verification (establishing pre-existing authenticity) with creation (manufacturing authenticity de novo)

**Why Important**: Affects whether Rule R3 is correct and whether verification equals causation.

**What's Needed**:
- Distinguish "verify authenticity" (establish pre-existing) from "create authenticity" (manufacture de novo)
- Test whether languages allow "make something authentic" (not just verify it)
- Examine forgery constructions to confirm negative causation is licensed

**Current Status**: Partial support for Rule R3 but requires clarification of verification vs. creation distinction.
