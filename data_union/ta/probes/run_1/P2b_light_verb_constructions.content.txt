# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested multi-word constructions involving lexical items identified in P1. Only constructions that are periphrastic, idiomatic, or syntactically-driven are included here; morphologically derived forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Asymmetries in coverage reflect the data, not an attempt to achieve uniform documentation.

---

## Item 1: உண்மை / *uṇmai*

*uṇmai* is the most constructionally productive item in this inventory. It participates in a wide range of periphrastic verbal phrases, fixed evaluative expressions, and idiomatic collocations across registers.

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai col
4. **English translation:** tell the truth, speak truthfully
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic and frequent verbal construction with *uṇmai*; used as an imperative, infinitive, or nominalized form across all registers. Appears in everyday speech, legal instruction, and moral exhortation. The verb *col* (to say/tell) is the default verbal complement of *uṇmai* in this construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை பேசு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai pēcu
4. **English translation:** speak the truth, talk honestly
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to *uṇmai col* but with *pēcu* (to speak), which carries a slightly more sustained or conversational sense. Used in moral instruction and everyday speech. The two constructions (*uṇmai col* and *uṇmai pēcu*) are largely interchangeable in most contexts, though *pēcu* may emphasize the ongoing act of speaking rather than a single utterance.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை மறை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai maṟai
4. **English translation:** hide the truth, conceal the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction (*uṇmai* + *-ai*) with the verb *maṟai* (to hide, conceal); used in legal and moral condemnation of deliberate suppression of true information. The accusative marking on *uṇmai* is obligatory in this construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை அறி
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai aṟi
4. **English translation:** know the truth, find out the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *aṟi* (to know, to come to know); used across registers to describe the epistemic act of arriving at knowledge of what is true. Common in investigative, legal, and philosophical contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை உணர்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai uṇar
4. **English translation:** realize the truth, come to feel/know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *uṇar* (to feel, to realize, to become aware of); distinct from *uṇmaiyai aṟi* in that *uṇar* implies a more experiential or affective process of coming to know, rather than purely cognitive acquisition. Common in literary and reflective writing.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை தெரிஞ்சுக்கோ
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai teriñcukkō
4. **English translation:** find out the truth (for yourself), get to know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Colloquial spoken Tamil construction with *teri* (to become known/visible) in the reflexive-benefactive form *teriñcukko*; used in everyday speech, often with an implication that the hearer should investigate or discover the truth themselves. More colloquial than *uṇmaiyai aṟi*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu
4. **English translation:** the truth came out, the truth emerged
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction in which *uṇmai* is the subject and *veḷiyē va* (to come outside, to emerge) is the predicate; used in narrative, journalistic, and everyday speech to describe the revelation of previously hidden truth. The construction treats truth as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai teriya vantatu
4. **English translation:** the truth became known, the truth came to light
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction with *teriya va* (to come to be known/visible); closely related to 1.7 but with *teri* emphasizing the becoming-known aspect rather than the coming-out aspect. Common in colloquial and journalistic Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭu
4. **English translation:** establish the truth, prove the truth definitively
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *nilaināṭṭu* (to establish firmly, to make stand); used in legal and formal argumentative contexts for the act of definitively proving that something is true. More emphatic than *meyppiкka* in its implication of permanence.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.10**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai
4. **English translation:** it is not known what the truth is, the truth is unclear
5. **Usage notes:** Embedded question construction (*uṇmai eṉṉa* = what the truth is) with the negative *teriyavillai* (is not known); used in everyday and journalistic Tamil to express epistemic uncertainty about what is actually true. A natural way to express agnosticism about the facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையில் என்ன நடந்தது என்று யாருக்கும் தெரியாது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil eṉṉa naṭantatu eṉṟu yārukum teriyātu
4. **English translation:** nobody knows what actually happened, what really happened is unknown to anyone
5. **Usage notes:** Extended construction using *uṇmaiyil* as an adverbial frame for an embedded question; illustrates the use of *uṇmaiyil* to introduce a contrast between appearance and reality in complex sentence structures. Common in journalistic and narrative Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மைதான்
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaitāṉ
4. **English translation:** that is indeed true, that is the truth (emphatic)
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *uṇmai* as predicate nominal and *-tāṉ* as emphatic particle; used in spoken Tamil to affirm a statement as true, often in response to a challenge or to emphasize agreement. Extremely common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மையா?
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaiyā?
4. **English translation:** is that true? is that really so?
5. **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction with *uṇmai* as predicate nominal and the question particle *-ā*; the standard way to ask whether a statement is true in spoken Tamil. Extremely common across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்றாலும் சொல்ல வேண்டாம்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṟālum colla vēṇṭām
4. **English translation:** even if it is true, (you) need not say it; even true things need not be said
5. **Usage notes:** Concessive conditional construction (*eṉṟālum* = even if it is said/even though); used in pragmatic and moral instruction about the limits of truth-telling, expressing the idea that truth alone does not always justify utterance. Appears in proverb-like and advisory speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.15**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை கசக்கும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai kacakkum
4. **English translation:** the truth is bitter, truth tastes bitter
5. **Usage notes:** Idiomatic construction using *kacakku* (to be bitter, to taste bitter); a widely used expression in Tamil to convey that true statements are often unpleasant to hear. Functions as a proverbial observation and appears in everyday speech, literature, and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.16**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை எப்போதும் வெல்லும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eppōtum vellum
4. **English translation:** truth always wins, truth always prevails
5. **Usage notes:** Proverbial construction with *vell* (to win, to prevail); a widely attested moral and motivational expression in Tamil, appearing in literature, political speech, and everyday moral discourse. Reflects a culturally salient belief in the ultimate triumph of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.17**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை தெரியும் வரை காத்திரு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai teriyum varai kāttiru
4. **English translation:** wait until the truth is known, wait until the truth comes out
5. **Usage notes:** Temporal subordinate clause construction (*teriyum varai* = until it becomes known) used in everyday and narrative Tamil to advise patience pending the revelation of facts. Common in spoken Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.18**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை ஒப்புக்கொள்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai oppukkoḷ
4. **English translation:** admit the truth, acknowledge the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *oppukkoḷ* (to accept, to admit, to acknowledge); used in legal, interpersonal, and moral contexts for the act of admitting what is true, especially when previously denied. Common in legal discourse and everyday confrontational speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.19**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை திரிக்காதே
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai tirikāte
4. **English translation:** don't distort the truth, don't twist the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *tiri* (to twist, to distort) in the negative imperative; used in moral and legal condemnation of misrepresentation that falls short of outright lying but involves distortion of facts. Common in journalistic and political discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.20**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை முழுவதும் சொல்லவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai muḻuvatum collavillai
4. **English translation:** (he/she) did not tell the whole truth, the full truth was not told
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *muḻuvatum* (completely, fully) as an adverbial modifier; used in legal and moral contexts to characterize partial disclosure as a form of deception. The construction implies that selective truth-telling is itself a form of concealment.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 2: பொய் / *poy*

---

**Construction 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** poy col
4. **English translation:** tell a lie, say something false
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic verbal construction with *poy*; parallel in structure to *uṇmai col*. Used across all registers in moral condemnation, legal testimony, and everyday speech. The verb *col* is the default verbal complement.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் பேசு
3. **Transliteration:** poy pēcu
4. **English translation:** speak falsely, talk dishonestly
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to *poy col* with *pēcu* (to speak); used in moral condemnation of habitual or sustained dishonest speech. The distinction between *poy col* and *poy pēcu* mirrors that between *uṇmai col* and *uṇmai pēcu*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்லாதே
3. **Transliteration:** poy collāte
4. **English translation:** don't lie, don't tell lies
5. **Usage notes:** Negative imperative construction; one of the most common moral injunctions in Tamil, used in child-rearing, moral instruction, and everyday speech. Appears in proverbs and didactic literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.4**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** அது பொய்தான்
3. **Transliteration:** atu poytāṉ
4. **English translation:** that is indeed a lie, that is false (emphatic)
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *poy* as predicate nominal and *-tāṉ* as emphatic particle; used in spoken Tamil to assert that a statement is false, often in direct contradiction of a claim. Parallel in structure to *atu uṇmaitāṉ*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.5**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்வது தவறு
3. **Transliteration:** poy coluvatu tavaṟu
4. **English translation:** telling lies is wrong, lying is a mistake/sin
5. **Usage notes:** Nominalized construction (*poy coluvatu* = the act of telling lies) as subject with *tavaṟu* as predicate; used in moral instruction and didactic contexts to assert the wrongness of lying. Common in Tamil moral literature and everyday moral speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.6**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்லி தப்பிக்க முயற்சிக்கிறான்
3. **Transliteration:** poy colli tappikka muyaṟcikkiṟāṉ
4. **English translation:** he is trying to escape by lying, he is attempting to get away with it through lies
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial participial construction (*poy colli* = having said a lie / by lying) used to describe lying as an instrumental means to another end; common in legal and narrative contexts describing deceptive conduct.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.7**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சாட்சி சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** poy cāṭci col
4. **English translation:** give false testimony, bear false witness
5. **Usage notes:** Construction combining *poy* (false) as attributive modifier with *cāṭci* (testimony/witness) and the verb *col*; used specifically in legal contexts to characterize perjury or false testimony. A fixed collocation in legal Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.8**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய்யில் வாழ்வது கஷ்டம்
3. **Transliteration:** poyyil vāḻvatu kaṣṭam
4. **English translation:** living in falsehood is difficult, it is hard to live a lie
5. **Usage notes:** Locative construction (*poyyil* = in falsehood/in lies) with *vāḻ* (to live); used in moral and literary contexts to express the difficulty or unsustainability of a life built on deception. Appears in literary and philosophical Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 2.9**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் ஒரு நாள் வெளியே வரும்
3. **Transliteration:** poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum
4. **English translation:** a lie will come out one day, falsehood will eventually be exposed
5. **Usage notes:** Proverbial construction parallel in structure to *uṇmai vellum*; used in moral discourse to assert that deception cannot be sustained indefinitely. Common in everyday moral speech and Tamil proverb tradition.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 3: நேர்மை / *nērmai*

---

**Construction 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையாக நட
3. **Transliteration:** nērmaiyāka naṭa
4. **English translation:** behave honestly, conduct yourself with integrity
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction with *nērmaiyāka* (*nērmai* + *-āka*, adverbial suffix) and the verb *naṭa* (to walk, to conduct oneself, to behave); used in moral instruction and everyday speech to enjoin honest conduct. The *-āka* adverbial is the standard way to derive manner adverbials from nominals in Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையாக பதில் சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** nērmaiyāka patil col
4. **English translation:** answer honestly, give an honest reply
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction with *nērmaiyāka* modifying the verbal phrase *patil col* (to give an answer); used in everyday and formal contexts to request or enjoin honest responses. Common in interview, legal, and pedagogical contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையாக இரு
3. **Transliteration:** nērmaiyāka iru
4. **English translation:** be honest, be straightforward
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction with *nērmaiyāka* and the copular verb *iru* (to be); the standard way to enjoin a state of honesty rather than a specific honest act. Common in moral instruction and everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.4**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** அவர் நேர்மையான ஆள்
3. **Transliteration:** avar nērmaiyāna āḷ
4. **English translation:** he/she is an honest person, he/she is a person of integrity
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *nērmaiyāna* as attributive adjective modifying *āḷ* (person); the standard way to characterize a person as honest in Tamil. Extremely common in character evaluation across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.5**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையே சிறந்த கொள்கை
3. **Transliteration:** nērmaiyē ciṟanta koḷkai
4. **English translation:** honesty is the best policy
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *nērmai* + emphatic *-ē* as subject and *ciṟanta koḷkai* (best policy) as predicate; a widely attested proverbial expression in Tamil, corresponding to the English proverb. Common in moral instruction and everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.6**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையில் சிறிதும் குறைவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** nērmaiyil ciṟitum kuṟaivillai
4. **English translation:** there is not the slightest lack of honesty, (his/her) integrity is complete
5. **Usage notes:** Locative construction (*nērmaiyil* = in honesty/in terms of honesty) with the negative *kuṟaivillai* (there is no lack); used in formal character evaluation and testimonials to assert complete integrity. More formal than everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Item 4: நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*

---

**Construction 4.1**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கை வை
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkai vai
4. **English translation:** place trust (in), have faith (in), trust
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic verbal construction with *nampikkai*; *vai* (to place, to put) is the standard verbal complement for expressing the act of trusting. The object of trust takes the dative case (*-il* or *-iṭam*). Extremely common across religious, interpersonal, and institutional contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.2**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கை இழ
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkai iḻa
4. **English translation:** lose trust, lose faith, lose confidence
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *iḻa* (to lose); used in interpersonal, political, and religious contexts to describe the loss of trust or faith. The subject may be the person who loses trust or, in passive-like constructions, the institution that loses the trust of others.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.3**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கை கொடு
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkai koṭu
4. **English translation:** give confidence, inspire trust, give assurance
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *koṭu* (to give); used to describe the act of providing reassurance or inspiring confidence in another person. Common in motivational, pedagogical, and interpersonal contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.4**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கை துரோகம் செய்
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkai turōkam cey
4. **English translation:** betray trust, commit a breach of faith
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *turōkam* (betrayal) and *cey* (to do/commit); used in moral and legal contexts to characterize the violation of trust. *nampikkai turōkam* functions as a compound nominal object of *cey*. Common in legal discourse on breach of fiduciary duty.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.5**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கை போச்சு
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkai pōccu
4. **English translation:** trust is gone, faith has been lost (colloquial)
5. **Usage notes:** Colloquial construction with *pō* (to go, to be lost) in the perfective; used in everyday spoken Tamil to express that trust has been destroyed or has disappeared. More colloquial and emotionally immediate than *nampikkai iḻa*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.6**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** நம்பிக்கையோடு செய்
3. **Transliteration:** nampikkaiyōṭu cey
4. **English translation:** do (something) with confidence, act with faith
5. **Usage notes:** Sociative/comitative construction (*nampikkai* + *-ōṭu* = with trust/confidence) with *cey* (to do); used to describe acting with confidence or faith. Common in motivational and religious contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4.7**

1. **Base Form:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai*
2. **Example:** அவர் மீது நம்பிக்கை இல்லை
3. **Transliteration:** avar mītu nampikkai illai
4. **English translation:** there is no trust in him/her, I/we don't trust him/her

## Item 5: சரி / *cari*

*cari* functions primarily as an uninflected evaluative particle and discourse marker. Its constructional behavior is largely pragmatic rather than syntactically complex.*

---

**Construction 5.1**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** அது சரிதான்
3. **Transliteration:** atu caritāṉ
4. **English translation:** that is indeed correct, that is right (emphatic)
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *cari* as predicate and *-tāṉ* as emphatic particle; used in spoken Tamil to affirm that something is correct or appropriate. Parallel in structure to *atu uṇmaitāṉ* but focused on correctness rather than truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.2**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** சரியா சொன்னே
3. **Transliteration:** cariyā coṉṉē
4. **English translation:** you said it correctly, you got it right
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *cari* + *-ā* (adverbial suffix) modifying the verb *col* (to say); used in spoken Tamil to affirm that a statement was accurate. The *-ā* adverbial form of *cari* (*cariyā*) is common in colloquial speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.3**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** சரி, போகலாம்
3. **Transliteration:** cari, pōkalām
4. **English translation:** okay, let's go; alright, we can go
5. **Usage notes:** Discourse particle use of *cari* as a sentence-initial agreement or acceptance marker; this is the most frequent use of *cari* in spoken Tamil, functioning as a pragmatic particle rather than a predicative or attributive element. Included here as a distinct constructional pattern.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.4**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** சரியில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** cariyillai
4. **English translation:** it is not right, it is not okay, something is wrong
5. **Usage notes:** Negative existential construction (*cari* + *illai* = correctness does not exist); used in everyday speech to indicate that something is wrong, incorrect, or not in order. Also used to indicate that a person is unwell (*உடம்பு சரியில்லை*, the body is not right = I am not well).

**Construction 5.5**

5. **Usage notes:** Attributive construction with *uṇmaikkum māṟāna* modifying *vākkumūlam* (statement, declaration); used in legal contexts to characterize a formal statement as factually false. A standard legal formulation in Tamil court language.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 18: கற்பனை / *kaṟpaṉai*

---

**Construction 18.1**

5. **Usage notes:** Attributive construction with *kaṟpaṉai* modifying *katai* (story); used in literary and media contexts to classify a narrative as fictional rather than factual. Common in book and film descriptions.

**Construction 18.2**

3. **Transliteration:** itu veṟum kaṟpaṉai
4. **English translation:** this is mere imagination, this is pure fabrication
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *veṟum* (mere, only, bare) as an intensifying modifier; used to dismiss a claim or narrative as entirely invented and without factual basis. Common in everyday speech and legal dismissal of unsupported claims.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 18.3**

1. **Base Form:** கற்பனை / *kaṟpaṉai*

5. **Usage notes:** Dative construction (*kaṟpaṉaikkum eṭṭāta* = unreachable even to imagination) used as an attributive phrase modifying *uṇmai*; an idiomatic expression used in literary and journalistic Tamil to describe a reality so extraordinary that it exceeds what could be imagined. Common in Tamil literary and journalistic writing.

## Item 19: வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*

---

**Construction 19.1**

1. **Base Form:** வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*
2. **Example:** வஞ்சனை செய்
3. **Transliteration:** vañcaṉai cey
4. **English translation:** commit deception, practice deceit, defraud
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *cey* (to do/commit); the standard verbal construction for the act of deceiving. Used in moral and legal condemnation of deliberate deception. Common in legal Tamil and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19.2**

1. **Base Form:** வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*

3. **Transliteration:** vañcaṉaiyillāta maṉacu
4. **English translation:** a heart without deceit, a guileless mind
5. **Usage notes:** Privative relative clause construction (*vañcaṉai illāta* = that which lacks deceit) modifying *maṉacu* (heart, mind); used in literary and moral contexts to describe a person of genuine sincerity. Common in Tamil devotional and literary writing.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 19.3**

1. **Base Form:** வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*
2. **Example:** வஞ்சனையாக நட
3. **Transliteration:** vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa
4. **English translation:** behave deceitfully, act in a deceptive manner
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction with *vañcaṉaiyāka* (*vañcaṉai* + *-āka*, adverbial suffix) and *naṭa* (to conduct oneself); used in moral and legal condemnation of deceptive conduct. Parallel in structure to *nērmaiyāka naṭa*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
