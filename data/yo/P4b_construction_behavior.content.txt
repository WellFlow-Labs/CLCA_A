# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness Rating | Usage Notes |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **1. ATTRIBUTION** | Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí jẹ́ òtítọ́ | Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí jẹ́ òtítọ́ | "This statement is true" | [CORE] | The **jẹ́** construction is neutral and unmarked for attribution. Used in declarative statements without emphasis. |
| | Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ | Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ | "His/her word is truth" | [CORE] | The **ni** construction is emphatic/contrastive, placing predicate first. Very common in assertions and rebuttals. |
| **2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION** | Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kì í ṣe òtítọ́ | Ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kì í ṣe òtítọ́ | "This statement is not true" | [CORE] | **Kì í ṣe** is the standard emphatic negative copula. Used for strong denials and corrections. |
| | Irọ́ kọ́ | Irọ́ kọ́ | "It's not a lie" | [CORE] | **Kọ́** is extremely common in conversational denials and rebuttals. Often used alone as a one-word denial. |
| **3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION** | Ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ | Ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́ | "True statement" | [CORE] | Direct N+ADJ order is standard and productive. No copula required for attributive modification. |
| | Ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́ | Ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́ | "One who tells lies" | [CORE] | Relative clauses with **tí** are highly productive. Used for defining and identifying referents. |
| **4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE** | Ó di òtítọ́ | Ó di òtítọ́ | "It became true / It turned out to be true" | [CORE] | **Di** marks change of state. Common in narrative contexts and when describing verification or realization. |
| | Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ | Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ | "I began to believe" | [CORE] | **Bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí** is the standard inchoative marker. Used with all verb types to mark inception of action or state. |
| **5. CAUSATIVE** | Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ | Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ | "He/She made me believe" | [CORE] | **Mú** marks direct causation. Common with psychological verbs in truth domain (cause to believe, know, understand). |
| | Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ hàn | Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ hàn | "Let the truth be revealed" | [CORE] | **Jẹ́ kí** marks permissive/hortative causation. Frequent in exhortations and prayers regarding truth revelation. |
| **6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION** | Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára | Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára | "His/her belief is strong" | [CORE] | **Ì-** prefix creates state/result nominals. Highly productive with truth-domain verbs (ìgbàgbọ́, ìdájú, etc.). |
| | Pípa irọ́ kò dára | Pípa irọ́ kò dára | "Lying is not good" | [CORE] | Gerundive reduplication emphasizes process/action. Common in moral evaluations and general statements. |
| **7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION** | Olótítọ́ ni baba mi | Olótítọ́ ni baba mi | "My father is a truthful person" | [CORE] | **Oní-** (→ **olí-**, **ẹlẹ́-**) creates character/agent nouns. Very productive for truth-domain character terms. |
| | Apurọ́ ni ọkùnrin náà | Apurọ́ ni ọkùnrin náà | "That man is a liar" | [CORE] | **A-** prefix creates agent nouns from verbs. **Apurọ́** 'liar' is extremely common in moral discourse. |
| **8. VERBAL PREDICATION (STATIVE)** | Ó tọ́ | Ó tọ́ | "It is correct/true" | [CORE] | Stative verbs function as predicates without copula. **Ó** serves as default subject for propositions. Very frequent. |
| | Kò tọ́ | Kò tọ́ | "It is not correct/not true" | [CORE] | **Kò** negates stative predicates directly. Standard negation pattern for truth evaluations. |
| **9. VERBAL PREDICATION (DYNAMIC)** | Ó purọ́ | Ó purọ́ | "He/She lied" | [CORE] | Basic SVO order. **Purọ́** 'lie' is extremely common in moral discourse and accusations. |
| | Ó purọ́ mọ́ mi | Ó purọ́ mọ́ mi | "He/She lied to me" | [CORE] | Applicative **mọ́** introduces recipient/target. Productive with verbs of communication and deception. |
| **10. INTERROGATIVE (POLAR QUESTION)** | Ṣé ó tọ́? | Ṣé ó tọ́? | "Is it correct/true?" | [CORE] | **Ṣé** is the standard polar question marker. Very common in verification and confirmation contexts. |
| | Ó gbàgbọ́ bí? | Ó gbàgbọ́ bí? | "Does he/she believe?" | [CORE] | Clause-final **bí** is also common. Both **ṣé** and **bí** are freely used in truth-domain questions. |
| **11. INTERROGATIVE (CONTENT QUESTION)** | Kí ni òtítọ́? | Kí ni òtítọ́? | "What is truth?" | [CORE] | **Kí ni** 'what FOC' is standard for content questions. Famous philosophical question in Yoruba discourse. |
| | Ta ni ó purọ́? | Ta ni ó purọ́? | "Who lied?" | [CORE] | **Ta ni** 'who FOC' for person questions. Common in investigations and accusations. |
| **12. IMPERATIVE** | Sọ òtítọ́! | Sọ òtítọ́! | "Tell the truth!" | [CORE] | Bare verb for positive commands. **Sọ òtítọ́** is an extremely common moral injunction. |
| | Má purọ́! | Má purọ́! | "Don't lie!" | [CORE] | **Má** marks prohibitive. **Má purọ́** is one of the most basic moral commands in Yoruba culture. |
| **13. COMPLEMENTATION** | Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́ | Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́ | "I believe that it is true" | [CORE] | **Pé** 'that' introduces declarative complements. Standard with epistemic verbs (gbàgbọ́, mọ̀, rò). |
| | Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́ | Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́ | "You should tell the truth" | [CORE] | **Kí** introduces subjunctive complements. Common with deontic predicates expressing obligation. |
| **14. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION (MANNER)** | Ní òtítọ́ | Ní òtítọ́ | "Truthfully, honestly" | [CORE] | **Ní òtítọ́** is extremely common as both manner adverbial and discourse marker. Used to emphasize sincerity. |
| | Gbàgbọ́ láì rí | Gbàgbọ́ láì rí | "Believe without seeing" | [CORE] | **Láì** 'without' plus verb creates negative manner. Common in religious and philosophical discourse. |
| **15. DEGREE MODIFICATION** | Ó tọ́ púpọ̀ | Ó tọ́ púpọ̀ | "It is very correct/quite true" | [CORE] | **Púpọ̀** 'very/much' is the standard intensifier. Follows predicate. Very productive. |
| | Ó dájú gan-an | Ó dájú gan-an | "It is absolutely certain" | [CORE] | **Gan-an** 'exactly/absolutely' adds emphatic certainty. Common in strong assertions. |
| **16. REDUPLICATION (EMPHASIS)** | Tọ́tọ́ | Tọ́tọ́ | "Very straight, completely correct" | [CORE] | Full reduplication intensifies meaning. **Tọ́tọ́** is common in emphatic truth assertions. |
| | Gidi gidi | Gidi gidi | "Very genuine, absolutely real" | [CORE] | **Gidi gidi** is a fixed expression for absolute genuineness. Very common in colloquial speech. |
| **17. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTION** | Òtítọ́ dára jù irọ́ lọ | Òtítọ́ dára jù irọ́ lọ | "Truth is better than lies" | [CORE] | **Jù...lọ** 'exceed...go' is standard comparative. Common in moral evaluations and proverbs. |
| | Irọ́ kékeré kò tó irọ́ ńlá | Irọ́ kékeré kò tó irọ́ ńlá | "A small lie is not as bad as a big lie" | [CORE] | **Kò tó** 'not reach' marks inferiority. Used in comparative moral reasoning. |
| **18. POSSESSION** | Òtítọ́ rẹ̀ | Òtítọ́ rẹ̀ | "His/her truthfulness" | [CORE] | Possessive pronouns attach directly. Very common for expressing personal qualities and attributes. |
| | Ìwà ti ọmọlúàbí | Ìwà ti ọmọlúàbí | "Character of a virtuous person" | [CORE] | **Ti** 'of' marks N-N possession. Standard in formal and proverbial discourse. |
| **19. EXISTENTIAL/LOCATIVE** | Òtítọ́ wà nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ | Òtítọ́ wà nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ | "There is truth in his/her words" | [CORE] | **Wà** marks existence/location. Common metaphor for truth as substance that can be present or absent. |
| | Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀ | Kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀ | "There is no lie in him/her" | [CORE] | **Kò sí** 'not exist' is standard negative existential. Common in character assessments. |
| **20. SERIAL VERB CONSTRUCTION** | Ó sọ òtítọ́ jáde | Ó sọ òtítọ́ jáde | "He/She revealed the truth" | [CORE] | **Sọ...jáde** 'tell...come.out' is a fixed serial for 'reveal'. Very common in truth-revelation contexts. |
| | Wá òtítọ́ rí | Wá òtítọ́ rí | "Seek and find the truth" | [CORE] | **Wá...rí** 'seek...find' is common in hortative and philosophical discourse about truth-seeking. |
| **21. FOCUS CONSTRUCTION** | Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ | Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ | "It is the truth that I told" | [CORE] | Cleft focus with **ni** is primary strategy for contrastive emphasis. Extremely common in assertions and rebuttals. |
| | Ní òtítọ́ ni mo gbàgbọ́ | Ní òtítọ́ ni mo gbàgbọ́ | "It is in truth that I believe" | [CORE] | Locative/manner focus with **ní...ni**. Used to emphasize the manner or basis of belief. |
| **22. CONDITIONAL** | Bí ó bá tọ́, èmi yóò gbàgbọ́ | Bí ó bá tọ́, èmi yóò gbàgbọ́ | "If it is true, I will believe" | [CORE] | **Bí...bá** conditional is standard. Very common in truth-conditional reasoning and negotiations. |
| | Tí o bá purọ́, a ó mọ̀ | Tí o bá purọ́, a ó mọ̀ | "If you lie, we will know" | [CORE] | **Tí...bá** is alternative conditional marker. Both **bí** and **tí** are freely used. |
| **23. TEMPORAL ASPECT (PROGRESSIVE)** | Ó ń pa irọ́ | Ó ń pa irọ́ | "He/She is lying" | [CORE] | Progressive **ń** marks ongoing action. Common in accusations and observations of deceptive behavior. |
| | Mo ti gbàgbọ́ tẹ́lẹ̀ | Mo ti gbàgbọ́ tẹ́lẹ̀ | "I already believed / I have believed" | [CORE] | Perfective **ti** marks completed action with current relevance. Often used with **tẹ́lẹ̀** 'already'. |
| **24. MODAL CONSTRUCTION (OBLIGATION/NECESSITY)** | Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́ | Ó yẹ kí o sọ òtítọ́ | "You should tell the truth" | [CORE] | **Ó yẹ kí** 'it is fitting that' is primary deontic modal. Extremely common in moral instruction. |
| | Kò yẹ kí o purọ́ | Kò yẹ kí o purọ́ | "You should not lie" | [CORE] | Negated obligation with **kò yẹ kí**. Standard form for moral prohibitions. |
| **25. MODAL CONSTRUCTION (ABILITY/POSSIBILITY)** | Mo lè sọ òtítọ́ | Mo lè sọ òtítọ́ | "I can tell the truth" | [CORE] | **Lè** 'can' marks ability/possibility. Common in assertions of capacity or willingness. |
| | Ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó jẹ́ òtítọ́ | Ó ṣe é ṣe kí ó jẹ́ òtítọ́ | "It is possible that it is true" | [CORE] | **Ṣe é ṣe** marks epistemic possibility. Common in hedged assertions and uncertainty expressions. |
| **26. EVIDENTIAL/REPORTATIVE** | Ó ní pé òtítọ́ ni | Ó ní pé òtítọ́ ni | "He/She says that it is true" | [CORE] | **Ó ní pé** 'he/she says that' is standard reportative. Very common in gossip and testimony contexts. |
| | Bí mo ṣe gbọ́, ó purọ́ | Bí mo ṣe gbọ́, ó purọ́ | "As I heard, he/she lied" | [CORE] | **Bí mo ṣe gbọ́** marks hearsay evidence. Common in reporting unverified information. |
| **27. QUOTATIVE CONSTRUCTION** | Ó sọ pé "Òtítọ́ ni" | Ó sọ pé "Òtítọ́ ni" | "He/She said 'It is true'" | [CORE] | **Sọ pé** introduces quotations. No formal distinction between direct and indirect speech in most contexts. |
| | Mo gbọ́ pé ó purọ́ | Mo gbọ́ pé ó purọ́ | "I heard that he/she lied" | [CORE] | **Gbọ́ pé** 'hear that' for reported information. Very common in gossip and testimony. |
| **28. REASON/CAUSE CONSTRUCTION** | Mo gbàgbọ́ nítorí pé ó tọ́ | Mo gbàgbọ́ nítorí pé ó tọ́ | "I believe because it is true" | [CORE] | **Nítorí pé** 'because that' is standard causal marker. Common in explanations and justifications. |
| | Ó purọ́ torí pé ó bẹ̀rù | Ó purọ́ torí pé ó bẹ̀rù | "He/She lied because he/she was afraid" | [CORE] | **Torí pé** is alternative causal marker. Both forms are freely used in causal explanations. |
| **29. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTION** | Bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé ó purọ́, mo gbàgbọ́ | Bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé ó purọ́, mo gbàgbọ́ | "Even though he/she lied, I believe" | [CORE] | **Bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé** marks concessive. Common in complex reasoning about trust despite evidence. |
| | Ó sọ òtítọ́, àmọ́ kò gbàgbọ́ | Ó sọ òtítọ́, àmọ́ kò gbàgbọ́ | "He/She told the truth, but (they) didn't believe" | [CORE] | **Àmọ́** 'but/however' marks adversative. Very common in narrative and argumentative discourse. |
| **30. COORDINATION** | Òtítọ́ àti òdodo | Òtítọ́ àti òdodo | "Truth and righteousness" | [CORE] | **Àti** 'and' is standard coordinator. **Òtítọ́ àti òdodo** is a fixed expression in moral discourse. |
| | Òtítọ́ tàbí irọ́ | Òtítọ́ tàbí irọ́ | "Truth or falsehood" | [CORE] | **Tàbí** 'or' marks disjunction. Common in binary truth-value contexts and philosophical discourse. |

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### High-Frequency Constructions
The following constructions appear to be extremely productive and central to truth-domain discourse:
- Focus construction with **ni** (§21)
- Imperatives **Sọ òtítọ́!** and **Má purọ́!** (§12)
- Complementation with **pé** after epistemic verbs (§13)
- Deontic modal **ó yẹ kí** (§24)
- Stative predication with **tọ́** (§8)

### Fixed Expressions
Several constructions appear as fixed or semi-fixed expressions:
- **Ní òtítọ́** 'truthfully' (§14)
- **Òtítọ́ àti òdodo** 'truth and righteousness' (§30)
- **Gidi gidi** 'very genuine' (§16)
- **Sọ òtítọ́ jáde** 'reveal the truth' (§20)

### No Marginal or Uncertain Constructions Identified
All 30 constructions documented in P4a appear to be [CORE] based on the data provided. No constructions were marked as restricted, doubtful, or requiring special contexts beyond normal discourse patterns.
