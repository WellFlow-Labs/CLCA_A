# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[NP subject] + εἶναι/είναι (copula) + [truth-domain adjective (predicative)]

### Examples
- Ο ισχυρισμός είναι αληθής. / *O ishirismós íne alithís.* / "The claim is true."
- Η κατάθεση κρίθηκε ψευδής. / *I katáthesi krítike psevdís.* / "The testimony was judged false."

### Notes
Greek uses a copular predicative construction as the primary attribution pattern; the adjective agrees in gender, number, and case with the subject NP. Attributive (prenominal) position is possible but less frequent for αληθής/ψευδής; αληθινός and σωστός are more common attributively (*αληθινή ιστορία*, *σωστή απάντηση*).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[NP subject] + δεν + είναι + [truth-domain adjective] — OR — [NP subject] + κρίθηκε/αποδείχθηκε + [negated adjective phrase]

### Examples
- Δεν είναι αληθινό αυτό που λες. / *Den íne alithinó aftó pu les.* / "What you are saying is not true."
- Ο ισχυρισμός δεν είναι τεκμηριωμένος. / *O ishirismós den íne tekmirio̱ménos.* / "The claim is not substantiated."

### Notes
Sentential negation is formed by preverbal δεν; the adjective itself is not morphologically negated. Privative-prefix forms (αναξιόπιστος, αδιαμφισβήτητος) exist as lexicalized negatives but are not the productive negation strategy for most adjectives in this domain.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
[truth-domain adjective] + [head noun] (prenominal, agreeing in gender/number/case) — OR — [head noun] + [genitive NP] for nominal modifiers

### Examples
- αληθινή ιστορία / *alithiní istoría* / "true story"
- ψευδής κατάθεση / *psevdís katáthesi* / "false testimony"

### Notes
Adjectives in this domain freely occupy prenominal attributive position with full agreement inflection. Nominal modifiers (e.g., τεκμήριο αθωότητας, "presumption of innocence") use a following genitive NP rather than a compound or prefix.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
[NP subject] + αποδεικνύεται / αποδείχθηκε / κρίνεται / κρίθηκε + [truth-domain adjective] — expressing transition to an evaluated state

### Examples
- Ο ισχυρισμός αποδείχθηκε αληθής. / *O ishirismós apodíhthike alithís.* / "The claim proved to be true."
- Αποδείχθηκε αναξιόπιστος. / *Apodíhthike anaksiópistos.* / "He proved to be unreliable."

### Notes
Greek lacks a single dedicated inchoative copula for this domain. The transition-to-state meaning is expressed by passive or middle forms of αποδεικνύω ("to prove/demonstrate") or κρίνω ("to judge"), which simultaneously encode the process of evaluation and its result. A bare γίνεται ("becomes") construction is not idiomatic here.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
(no productive morphological causative identified for truth-domain adjectives)

Periphrastic alternatives:
- κάνω + [NP] + να + [subjunctive verb] — general causative
- νοθεύω / πλαστογραφώ / ακυρώνω — lexical causatives within specific sub-domains

### Examples
- Νόθευσε τα εκλογικά αποτελέσματα. / *Nóthefse ta eklogika apotelesmata.* / "He falsified the election results." [lexical causative: cause to become spurious]
- Ακύρωσε τη σύμβαση. / *Akíro̱se ti símvasi.* / "He annulled the contract." [lexical causative: cause to become invalid]

### Notes
Greek has no productive morphological causative suffix applicable across truth-domain adjectives. Causative meanings are expressed lexically: νοθεύω (cause to be adulterated/spurious), πλαστογραφώ (cause to be forged), ακυρώνω (cause to be invalid), νομιμοποιώ (cause to be legitimate). A general periphrastic causative with κάνω exists but is not idiomatic in this domain.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[adjectival base] + -εια / -ία / -τητα / -οσύνη → abstract noun denoting the property or state

### Examples
- αλήθεια / *alíthia* / "truth" (from αληθής + -εια)
- αξιοπιστία / *aksiopistía* / "credibility, reliability" (from αξιόπιστος + -ία)

### Notes
Three suffixes are productive in this domain: -εια (αλήθεια, ακρίβεια), -ία (αξιοπιστία, αμφιβολία, νομιμότητα via -τητα), and -τητα (γνησιότητα, αυθεντικότητα, εγκυρότητα, σωστότητα, ορθότητα). The choice among suffixes is lexically determined, not rule-governed. All three yield fully productive abstract nouns used across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
[verbal/nominal base] + -τής (masc.) / -τρια or -τρα (fem.) → agent noun; OR compound [ψευδο-] + [role noun]

### Examples
- ψεύτης / *pséftis* / "liar" (agent of ψεύδομαι; masc.); ψεύτρα / *pséftra* / (fem.)
- πλαστογράφος / *plastográfos* / "forger" (agent of πλαστογραφώ)

### Notes
Agentive nominalization for truth-domain roles is lexically restricted. ψεύτης/ψεύτρα (liar) is the primary everyday agent noun; πλαστογράφος (forger) is the legal term. No common agentive noun for "truth-teller" is in everyday use; the function is covered periphrastically (*αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια*) or by the adjective αληθινός applied to persons. The ψευδο- prefix generates pseudo-agent compounds (ψευδοεπιστήμονας, ψευδοπροφήτης) following a productive pattern.

## 8. EVIDENTIAL / EPISTEMIC-STATUS MARKING

### Pattern
[evidential adverb or fixed phrase] + [proposition] — marking the speaker's epistemic commitment or the evidential basis of a claim

### Examples
- Αποδεδειγμένως ψευδής ισχυρισμός. / *Apodedeignménos psevdís ishirismós.* / "A demonstrably false claim."
- Εικαζόμενος δράστης. / *Ikazómenos drástis.* / "The alleged perpetrator."

### Notes
Greek marks evidential and epistemic status through adverbs derived from truth-domain adjectives (αποδεδειγμένως, αδιαμφισβήτητα, φαινομενικά) and through participial modifiers (εικαζόμενος, τεκμηριωμένος). These form a productive paradigm: the adverb or participle precedes or modifies the predicate/NP and signals whether a claim is proven, alleged, apparent, or beyond dispute. This pattern is especially productive in legal and journalistic registers.

---

## 9. DISCOURSE-FRAMING CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
[fixed phrase] + [proposition] — speaker-oriented frame introducing a frank, corrective, or precision-marking statement

### Examples
- Η αλήθεια είναι ότι δεν υπάρχουν αποδείξεις. / *I alíthia íne óti den ipárhun apodíksis.* / "The truth is that there is no evidence."
- Για να είμαι ακριβής, δεν το είπε ποτέ. / *Ya na íme akrivís, den to ípe poté.* / "To be precise, he never said it."

### Notes
A set of fixed expressions functions as discourse-level operators rather than propositional predicates: η αλήθεια είναι ότι, για να είμαι ακριβής, αλήθεια να λέμε, στην ουσία, ουσιαστικά. These are syntactically parenthetical or sentence-initial and signal the speaker's epistemic stance (frankness, precision, correction) toward the following proposition. They do not predicate truth of an external claim but frame the speaker's own utterance.

---

## 10. PSEUDO- COMPOUND PATTERN

### Pattern
ψευδο- (prefix) + [domain noun or adjective] → compound denoting something that has the form but not the substance of the base category

### Examples
- ψευδοεπιστήμη / *psevdoepistími* / "pseudoscience"
- ψευδοδημοκρατία / *psevdodimokratía* / "pseudo-democracy, sham democracy"

### Notes
ψευδο- is a fully productive combining form in contemporary Greek, freely attaching to nouns and adjectives across domains (science, politics, religion, aesthetics, logic) to produce compounds meaning "false/sham X." The pattern is open-ended; new formations are coined as needed. This is the primary structural resource for expressing the concept of something that simulates a category without instantiating it.
