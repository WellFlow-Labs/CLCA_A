# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

### METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis documents only attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Constructions are included only if they:
1. Involve syntactic combination beyond simple derivational morphology
2. Show some degree of conventionalization or idiomaticity
3. Are attested in natural usage

Constructions are organized by semantic/functional domains rather than by base form, as many constructions involve multiple P1 items.

---

## DOMAIN 1: TRUTH-TELLING AND ASSERTION

### Construction 1: Truth-telling
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + sọ (to speak)
**Example:** Mo sọ òtítọ́ fún ọ
**Transliteration:** mo sọ òtítọ́ fún ọ
**Translation:** I told you the truth / I spoke truthfully to you
**Usage:** Standard construction for asserting that one has told the truth. The verb sọ (speak) combines with òtítọ́ as direct object. Extremely common in everyday discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 2: Truth-speaking (alternative word order)
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + sọ (to speak)
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ni mo sọ
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni mo sọ
**Translation:** It is the truth that I spoke / I spoke the truth (emphatic)
**Usage:** Focus construction emphasizing òtítọ́. The particle ni marks focus. Used when emphatically asserting truthfulness, often in response to doubt.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 3: Swearing to truth
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + búra (to swear)
**Example:** Mo búra lórí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** mo búra lórí òtítọ́
**Translation:** I swear on the truth / I swear truthfully
**Usage:** Construction used when making solemn oaths or assertions. The preposition lórí (on/upon) connects búra and òtítọ́. Common in legal and serious interpersonal contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 4: Speaking in truth
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + sọ (to speak)
**Example:** Ní òtítọ́ ni mo sọ ọ́
**Transliteration:** ní òtítọ́ ni mo sọ ọ́
**Translation:** In truth I spoke it / I spoke it truthfully
**Usage:** Construction emphasizing the manner of speaking. The preposition ní (in/with) marks òtítọ́ as manner adverbial. Used for emphatic truth claims.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 5: Truthful witness
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + jẹ́rìí (witness/testimony)
**Example:** Ó jẹ́rìí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ó jẹ́rìí òtítọ́
**Translation:** He/she bore truthful witness / gave true testimony
**Usage:** Legal and religious construction for giving truthful testimony. òtítọ́ modifies jẹ́rìí as attributive. Common in court and religious contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 6: Adverbial truth assertion
**Base Form:** nitootọ́ (truly)
**Example:** Nitootọ́, mo rí i
**Transliteration:** nitootọ́, mo rí i
**Translation:** Truly, I saw it / I really saw it
**Usage:** Sentence-initial adverbial construction emphasizing truthfulness of entire statement. Very common in conversational assertion and emphasis.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 7: Truth confirmation
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + ni (copula/focus marker)
**Example:** Òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**Translation:** It is true / That's the truth
**Usage:** Minimal construction confirming truth of previous statement. Extremely common as conversational response or agreement marker.
**Status:** [CORE]

## DOMAIN 2: LIE-TELLING AND DECEPTION

### Construction 8: Lie-telling
**Base Form:** irọ́ (lie) + pa (to tell/kill)
**Example:** Ó pa irọ́
**Transliteration:** ó pa irọ́
**Translation:** He/she told a lie / lied
**Usage:** Standard construction for lie-telling. The verb pa (literally "to kill/strike") is conventionally used with irọ́. Very common in everyday speech.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 9: Lie-speaking (alternative)
**Base Form:** irọ́ (lie) + sọ (to speak)
**Example:** Ó sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** ó sọ irọ́
**Translation:** He/she spoke a lie / told a lie
**Usage:** Alternative construction using sọ (speak) instead of pa. Less common than "pa irọ́" but fully acceptable. Used across registers.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 10: Habitual liar
**Base Form:** irọ́ (lie) + pa (to tell)
**Example:** Ó máa ń pa irọ́
**Transliteration:** ó máa ń pa irọ́
**Translation:** He/she habitually tells lies / is a liar
**Usage:** Construction using habitual aspect markers (máa ń) to describe characteristic behavior. Common in character evaluation.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 11: Lying verb
**Base Form:** purọ́ (to lie)
**Example:** Ó purọ́
**Transliteration:** ó purọ́
**Translation:** He/she lied / is lying
**Usage:** Simple verbal construction. Most direct way to accuse someone of lying. Common in confrontational contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 12: Emphatic lie denial
**Base Form:** irọ́ (lie) + ni (copula)
**Example:** Irọ́ ni!
**Transliteration:** irọ́ ni
**Translation:** It's a lie! / That's false!
**Usage:** Emphatic construction rejecting a statement as false. Very common as conversational interjection or protest.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 13: Mixing truth and lies
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + irọ́ (lie) + pò (to mix)
**Example:** Ó pò òtítọ́ àti irọ́ pọ̀
**Transliteration:** ó pò òtítọ́ àti irọ́ pọ̀
**Translation:** He/she mixed truth and lies together
**Usage:** Construction describing partial deception or distortion. The verb pò (to mix) takes both nouns as objects with àti (and) coordinating. Used when describing manipulation of truth.
**Status:** [CORE]

## DOMAIN 3: VERIFICATION AND CONFIRMATION

### Construction 14: Truth verification
**Base Form:** òtítọ́ (truth) + wádìí (to investigate)
**Example:** Mo wádìí òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ náà
**Transliteration:** mo wádìí òtítọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ náà
**Translation:** I investigated the truth of that matter
**Usage:** Construction for describing investigation or verification. òtítọ́ modifies ọ̀rọ̀ (matter/word) in genitive-like relationship. Common in investigative and judicial contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 15: Certainty assertion
**Base Form:** dájú (to be certain)
**Example:** Mo dájú pé ó tọ́
**Transliteration:** mo dájú pé ó tọ́
**Translation:** I am certain that it is true
**Usage:** Construction using complementizer pé to introduce embedded clause. Very common for expressing epistemic certainty about propositions.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 16: Certainty question
**Base Form:** dájú (to be certain)
**Example:** Ṣé o dájú?
**Transliteration:** ṣé o dájú
**Translation:** Are you certain? / Are you sure?
**Usage:** Interrogative construction using question particle ṣé. Extremely common in conversation when seeking confirmation or challenging claims.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 17: Making certain
**Base Form:** dájú (to be certain)
**Example:** Jẹ́ kí n dájú
**Transliteration:** jẹ́ kí n dájú
**Translation:** Let me make sure / Let me verify
**Usage:** Construction using jussive/permissive jẹ́ kí (let). Common when requesting time or permission to verify something.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 18: Assurance-giving
**Base Form:** ìdájú (certainty) + fún (to give)
**Example:** Mo fún ọ ní ìdájú
**Transliteration:** mo fún ọ ní ìdájú
**Translation:** I give you assurance / I assure you
**Usage:** Construction for providing assurance to someone. Uses benefactive fún with ní marking the thing given. Common in reassurance and commitment contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 19: Belief expression
**Base Form:** gbàgbọ́ (to believe)
**Example:** Mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
**Transliteration:** mo gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
**Translation:** I believe that it is true
**Usage:** Construction using complementizer pé for embedded proposition. Standard way to express propositional belief. Very common across all registers.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 20: Belief in someone/something
**Base Form:** gbàgbọ́ (to believe)
**Example:** Mo gbàgbọ́ nínú rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** mo gbàgbọ́ nínú rẹ̀
**Translation:** I believe in you / I trust you
**Usage:** Construction using preposition nínú (in/inside) for belief in persons or entities. Common in interpersonal and religious contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

**Example:** Ṣé ó tọ́?
**Transliteration:** ṣé ó tọ́
**Translation:** Is it correct? / Is it true?
**Usage:** Interrogative construction with question particle ṣé. Very common for seeking confirmation or verification.

### Construction 23: Negative correctness
**Base Form:** tọ́ (to be correct)
**Example:** Kò tọ́ rárá
**Transliteration:** kò tọ́ rárá
**Translation:** It is not correct at all / It is completely wrong
**Usage:** Negative construction with emphatic particle rárá (at all). Common for strong denial or correction.

### Construction 24: Exactness emphasis
**Base Form:** ṣe déédé (to be exact)
**Example:** Ó ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ó ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀
**Translation:** It is exactly so / It is just like that
**Usage:** Construction using demonstrative bẹ́ẹ̀ (like that/so) to confirm exact correspondence. Common in confirmations and descriptions.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 25: Precision requirement
**Base Form:** pé (to be complete/exact)
**Example:** Kò pé bẹ́ẹ̀
**Transliteration:** kò pé bẹ́ẹ̀
**Translation:** It is not quite like that / It is not exactly so
**Usage:** Negative construction indicating lack of complete correspondence. Used when correcting or qualifying statements.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 26: Matching/correspondence
**Base Form:** bá mu (to correspond)
**Example:** Ó bá ọ̀rọ̀ náà mu
**Transliteration:** ó bá ọ̀rọ̀ náà mu
**Translation:** It corresponds to that matter / It matches that statement
**Usage:** Construction with direct object between bá and mu. Common in comparative and verificational contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

### Construction 27: Non-correspondence
**Base Form:** bá mu (to correspond)
**Example:** Kò bá a mu
**Transliteration:** kò bá a mu
**Translation:** It does not correspond to it / It doesn't match
**Usage:** Negative construction indicating mismatch or disagreement. Common when identifying discrepancies.
**Status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** ó jọ ọ́ púpọ̀

**Transliteration:** kò jọ ọ́ rárá

**Transliteration:** wọ́n jọra

**Status:** [CORE]

## CONSTRUCTIONS NOT COMMONLY ATTESTED

The following semantic/functional patterns were investigated but **no common fixed constructions were identified**:

1. **Degrees of truth**: No conventional construction for "more true" or "less true" (truth is typically binary in Yoruba discourse)

2. **Truth-value assignment**: No fixed construction equivalent to "assign truth value to" (this is expressed through simple predication: "ó tọ́" or "kò tọ́")

3. **Partial truth**: No conventional fixed expression for "partially true" (typically expressed through mixing construction: "ó pò òtítọ́ àti irọ́ pọ̀")

4. **Approximate truth**: No fixed construction for "approximately true" (approximation is expressed through resemblance: "ó jọ òtítọ́")

5. **Literal vs. figurative truth**: No conventional construction distinguishing these (context and discourse markers handle this distinction)

6. **Propositional attitude reports beyond belief**: No fixed constructions for "know that," "doubt that," etc. beyond "gbàgbọ́ pé" (these use simple complementation with pé)

7. **Evidential source marking**: No grammaticalized evidential constructions (evidential information expressed through discourse context and optional source phrases)

8. **Counterfactual truth**: No fixed construction for counterfactual conditionals related to truth (standard conditional constructions used)
