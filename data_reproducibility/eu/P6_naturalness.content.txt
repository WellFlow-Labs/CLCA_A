# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / VERIFIED

### Pattern
**X egiaztu da / X egiazkotu da** (X has been verified / X has become verified)
Intransitive perfect construction using the verb **egiaztatu** (to verify) in its intransitive/passive form.

### Examples
- **Albistea egiaztatu da**
  - albistea egiaztatu da
  - "The news has been verified / The news turned out to be true"

- **Susmo hura egiaztu zen**
  - susmo hura egiaztu zen
  - "That suspicion was verified / That suspicion turned out to be true"

### Notes
The verb **egiaztatu** functions both transitively (to verify something) and intransitively (to be verified, to turn out to be true). The intransitive use with **izan** auxiliary expresses the change of state from unverified to verified. This is the most natural expression for something becoming established as true.

**Alternative:** The generic inchoative **egia bihurtu da** (became truth) exists but is less common and less idiomatic than **egiaztatu da**.

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / EXPOSED AS FALSE

### Pattern
**X gezurra zela frogatu da / X gezurra dela agertu da** (It was proven that X is a lie / It appeared that X is a lie)
Complementizer construction with **zela/dela** (that it is/was) + main verb expressing revelation.

### Examples
- **Haren esana gezurra zela frogatu zen**
  - haren esana gezurra zela frogatu zen
  - "It was proven that his/her statement was a lie / His/her statement was proven to be a lie"

- **Istorioa gezurra dela agertu da**
  - istorioa gezurra dela agertu da
  - "It has appeared that the story is a lie / The story turned out to be a lie"

### Notes
Basque does not have a simple intransitive verb **\*gezurtu** meaning "to become false" parallel to **egiaztatu**. Instead, it uses complementizer constructions with verbs like **frogatu** (to prove), **agertu** (to appear, become evident), or **ikusi** (to see). The verb **gezurtatu** exists but means "to lie, to falsify" (transitive action) rather than "to become false."

**No common expression exists** for a simple intransitive "X became false" without specifying how the falsity was revealed.

## 3. BECOMING CORRECT

### Pattern
**X zuzendu da** (X has been corrected / X became correct)
Intransitive perfect construction using **zuzendu** (to correct, to straighten).

### Examples
- **Akatsa zuzendu da**
  - akatsa zuzendu da
  - "The error has been corrected / The error was corrected"

- **Egoera zuzendu zen**
  - egoera zuzendu zen
  - "The situation was corrected / The situation was set right"

### Notes
The verb **zuzendu** functions both transitively (to correct something) and intransitively (to be corrected, to become straight/correct). The intransitive use expresses change from incorrect to correct state. This is the standard expression for correction processes.

## 4. BECOMING WRONG / GOING WRONG

### Pattern
**X okertu da** (X has gone wrong / X became wrong)
Intransitive perfect construction using **okertu** (to go wrong, to become crooked).

### Examples
- **Plana okertu da**
  - plana okertu da
  - "The plan has gone wrong / The plan went wrong"

- **Dena okertu zen**
  - dena okertu zen
  - "Everything went wrong / Everything became wrong"

### Notes
The verb **okertu** is primarily intransitive, expressing the process of going wrong or becoming crooked. Unlike **zuzendu**, which is commonly transitive, **okertu** more naturally describes spontaneous deterioration or error. For transitive "to make wrong," periphrastic constructions are more common.

## 5. BECOMING EVIDENT / MANIFEST

### Pattern
**X agerian jarri da / X agertu da** (X has been exposed / X appeared)
Intransitive construction using **agertu** (to appear, become visible) or passive of **agerian jarri** (to expose).

### Examples
- **Egia agerian jarri da**
  - egia agerian jarri da
  - "The truth has been exposed / The truth was brought to light"

- **Gezurra agertu da**
  - gezurra agertu da
  - "The lie appeared / The lie became evident"

### Notes
**Agertu** is the standard intransitive verb for appearing or becoming visible/evident. The construction **agerian jarri** (literally "put in the open") can be used intransitively with **izan** auxiliary to express passive meaning (was exposed). Both express change from hidden to manifest state.

## 6. BECOMING HIDDEN / CONCEALED

### Pattern
**X ezkutatu da / X ezkutuan jarri da** (X has been hidden / X was put in hiding)
Intransitive/passive construction using **ezkutatu** (to hide) or **ezkutuan jarri** (to put in hiding).

### Examples
- **Egia ezkutatu da**
  - egia ezkutatu da
  - "The truth has been hidden / The truth was concealed"

- **Informazioa ezkutuan jarri zen**
  - informazioa ezkutuan jarri zen
  - "The information was put in hiding / The information was concealed"

### Notes
**Ezkutatu** functions both transitively (to hide something) and intransitively (to be hidden). The construction **ezkutuan jarri** (put in hiding) parallels **agerian jarri** (put in the open) and can be used intransitively with **izan** for passive meaning. These express change from visible to hidden state.

### Examples

- **Haren parte-hartzea ziurtatu zen**
  - haren parte-hartzea ziurtatu zen
  - "His/her participation was confirmed / His/her participation became certain"

### Notes
**Ziurtatu** functions both transitively (to make sure of something, to confirm) and intransitively (to be confirmed, to become certain). This is the standard expression for something becoming established as certain. The generic inchoative **ziur bihurtu** is less common.

## 8. BECOMING DOUBTFUL / QUESTIONED

### Pattern
**X zalantzan jarri da** (X has been called into question / X was questioned)
Passive construction using **zalantzan jarri** (to call into question).

### Examples
- **Haren zintzotasuna zalantzan jarri da**
  - haren zintzotasuna zalantzan jarri da
  - "His/her honesty has been called into question / His/her honesty was questioned"

- **Teoria zalantzan jarri zen**
  - teoria zalantzan jarri zen
  - "The theory was called into question / The theory became doubtful"

### Notes
The construction **zalantzan jarri** (literally "put in doubt") is used intransitively with **izan** auxiliary to express passive meaning. This is the standard way to express something becoming subject to doubt. **No simple intransitive verb exists** meaning "to become doubtful" without the locative construction.

## 9. BECOMING SIMILAR / RESEMBLING

### Pattern
**X antzetu da / X Y bezain antzeko bihurtu da** (X resembled / X became similar to Y)
Intransitive construction using **antzetu** (to resemble) or **bihurtu** (to become) + **antzeko** (similar).

### Examples
- **Bi istorioak elkarri antzetu zaizkio**
  - bi istorioak elkarri antzetu zaizkio
  - "The two stories have come to resemble each other"

- **Kopia originala bezain antzeko bihurtu da**
  - kopia originala bezain antzeko bihurtu da
  - "The copy became as similar as the original / The copy became very similar to the original"

### Notes
**Antzetu** is an intransitive verb meaning to resemble or become similar, though it is less common than the adjectival forms **antzeko/antzekoa**. The construction with **bihurtu** + **antzeko** is more frequently attested. For expressing resemblance, stative constructions (**X Y bezain antzekoa da** "X is similar to Y") are more common than inchoative ones.

## 10. BECOMING EQUAL / SAME

### Pattern
**X berdindu da / X Y-rekin berdindu da** (X equalized / X became equal with Y)
Intransitive construction using **berdindu** (to equalize, to become equal).

### Examples
- **Puntuazioak berdindu dira**
  - puntuazioak berdindu dira
  - "The scores have equalized / The scores became equal"

- **Bi kopiak berdindu dira**
  - bi kopiak berdindu dira
  - "The two copies became equal / The two copies became identical"

### Notes
**Berdindu** functions both transitively (to equalize something, to make equal) and intransitively (to become equal, to equalize). Common in sports contexts for tying scores (**berdinketa** "tie, equalization") and in social contexts for equalization processes. The intransitive use is fully productive.

## 11. BECOMING APPROPRIATE / SUITABLE

### Pattern
**X egokitu da / X Y-ra egokitu da** (X adapted / X adapted to Y)
Intransitive construction using **egokitu** (to adapt, to suit, to fit).

### Examples
- **Arauak egoerara egokitu dira**

**Egokitu** is commonly intransitive, expressing adaptation or becoming suitable. The allative case **-ra** marks the target of adaptation. This verb emphasizes process of adjustment rather than simple state change. Transitive use (to adapt something) is also common.

## 13. CAUSING VERIFICATION / MAKING TRUE

### Pattern
**Y-k X egiaztatu du** (Y has verified X / Y made X verified)
Transitive construction with ergative agent and absolutive patient.

### Examples
- **Ikertzaileak datuak egiaztatu ditu**
  - ikertzaileak datuak egiaztatu ditu
  - "The researchers have verified the data / The researchers confirmed the data"

- **Frogek teoria egiaztatu dute**
  - frogek teoria egiaztatu dute
  - "The proofs have verified the theory / The proofs confirmed the theory"

### Notes
**Egiaztatu** in transitive use expresses causation of verification. The agent (marked with ergative **-k**) causes the patient (absolutive) to become verified. This is the standard causative expression for establishing truth. The auxiliary **du/ditu** agrees with both agent and patient.

---

## 14. CAUSING FALSIFICATION / MAKING FALSE

### Pattern
**Y-k X gezurtatu du / Y-k X faltsutu du** (Y falsified X / Y made X false)
Transitive construction with ergative agent.

### Examples
- **Norbaitek dokumentua faltsutu du**
  - norbaitek dokumentua faltsutu du
  - "Someone has falsified the document / Someone made the document false"

- **Testigoak lekukotza gezurtatu zuen**
  - testigoak lekukotza gezurtatu zuen
  - "The witness falsified the testimony / The witness made the testimony false"

### Notes
Both **gezurtatu** (to lie, to falsify) and **faltsutu** (to falsify, to counterfeit) express causation of falseness. **Faltsutu** is more common for documents and objects, while **gezurtatu** can apply to statements. These are transitive verbs requiring ergative agents. The action is deliberate falsification rather than mere error.

## 15. CAUSING CORRECTION / MAKING CORRECT

### Pattern
**Y-k X zuzendu du** (Y corrected X / Y made X correct)
Transitive construction with ergative agent.

### Examples
- **Irakasleak akatsa zuzendu du**
  - irakasleak akatsa zuzendu du
  - "The teacher has corrected the error / The teacher made the error correct"

- **Zuzenketa-programak testua zuzendu du**
  - zuzenketa-programak testua zuzendu du
  - "The correction program has corrected the text / The correction program made the text correct"

### Notes
**Zuzendu** in transitive use expresses causation of correction. The agent actively corrects or straightens the patient. This is the standard causative expression for making something correct. Common across educational, editorial, and technical contexts.

---

## 16. CAUSING ERROR / MAKING WRONG

### Pattern
**Y-k X okertu du** (Y made X wrong / Y caused X to go wrong)
Transitive construction with ergative agent (less common than intransitive).

### Examples
- **Aldaketak plana okertu du**
  - aldaketak plana okertu du
  - "The changes have made the plan wrong / The changes caused the plan to go wrong"

### Notes
**Okertu** is less commonly transitive than **zuzendu**. The intransitive use (X went wrong) is more natural. For expressing causation of error, periphrastic constructions or other verbs are often preferred. **No highly frequent transitive causative exists** for making something wrong parallel to **zuzendu** for making correct.

**Alternative:** Periphrastic constructions like **Y-k egin du X oker joan dadin** (Y made it so that X would go wrong) are more natural for expressing causation of error.

## 24. REMAINING TRUE / STAYING TRUE

### Pattern
**X egia izaten jarraitu du / X egiazko izaten jarraitu du** (X continued being true / X remained true)
Continuative construction using **jarraitu** (to continue) + imperfective participle **izaten**.

### Examples
- **Teoria egia izaten jarraitu du**
  - teoria egia izaten jarraitu du
  - "The theory continued being true / The theory remained true"

- **Haren esana egiazko izaten jarraitu zen**
  - haren esana egiazko izaten jarraitu zen
  - "His/her statement remained true / His/her statement continued being true"

### Notes
Basque expresses continuation of state using **jarraitu** (continue) + imperfective participle (**izaten** from **izan** "to be"). This construction emphasizes ongoing maintenance of the true state. Alternative: **X egia da oraindik** (X is still true) using the adverb **oraindik** (still) for simpler stative continuation.

## 32. CEASING TO BE TRUE / STOPPING BEING TRUE

### Pattern
**X egia izateari utzi dio / X egiazko izateari utzi dio** (X stopped being true / X ceased being true)
Cessative construction using **utzi** (to leave, to stop) + dative of verbal noun **izateari** (to being).

### Examples
- **Teoria egia izateari utzi dio**
  - teoria egia izateari utzi dio
  - "The theory stopped being true / The theory ceased being true"

- **Haren esana egiazko izateari utzi zion**
  - haren esana egiazko izateari utzi zion
  - "His/her statement stopped being true / His/her statement ceased being true"

### Notes
Basque expresses cessation using **utzi** (stop, leave) + dative of verbal noun. This construction emphasizes the ending of a state. The auxiliary agrees with the subject. This is the standard pattern for expressing cessation of states.

**Alternative:** **X ez da gehiago egia** (X is no longer true) using the negative copula + **gehiago** (more, longer) for simpler expression of cessation.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Distribution of expression types:

1. **Becoming constructions (inchoative):** 12 patterns identified
   - Most use intransitive/passive forms of transitive verbs (egiaztatu, zuzendu, ziurtatu, etc.)
   - Some use generic inchoative **bihurtu** (become) but this is less common/idiomatic
   - Locative + verb constructions common (agerian jarri, zalantzan jarri, ezkutuan jarri)

2. **Causative constructions:** 12 patterns identified
   - Primarily use transitive forms of the same verbs used intransitively for becoming
   - Ergative-absolutive alignment: agent marked with **-k**, patient in absolutive
   - Highly productive across truth-domain vocabulary

3. **Continuative constructions (remaining/staying):** 9 patterns identified
   - Standard pattern: **jarraitu** (continue) + imperfective participle **izaten** or adjective/locative
   - Alternative: **oraindik** (still) + stative predicate for simpler expression
   - Fully productive across all truth-domain states

4. **Cessative constructions (stopping):** 2 patterns identified
   - Standard pattern: **utzi** (stop, leave) + dative of verbal noun **izateari**
   - Alternative: **ez da gehiago** (is no longer) + predicate
   - Less elaborated than continuative constructions in provided data

5. **Preventive/permissive/facilitative constructions:** 6 patterns identified
   - Use verbs like **eragotzi/galarazi** (prevent), **baimendu/onartu** (permit), **erraztu/lagundu** (facilitate), **zaildu/oztopatu** (hinder)
   - Take verbal noun (nominalized infinitive) as object
   - Productive pattern for expressing modulation of processes

6. **Aspectual modifications:** 3 patterns identified
   - Gradual: **pixkanaka, poliki-poliki, apurka-apurka**
   - Sudden: **bat-batean, ustekabean, berehala**
   - Partial: **neurri batean, zati batean, partzialki**
   - Expressed through adverbial modification rather than verbal morphology

### Key asymmetries:

1. **Verification vs. falsification:**
   - **Egiaztatu** has both transitive (verify) and intransitive (be verified, turn out true) uses
   - **Gezurtatu** is primarily transitive (to lie, to falsify); no simple intransitive "become false"
   - Falsification requires complementizer constructions or revelation verbs

2. **Correction vs. error:**
   - **Zuzendu** is commonly both transitive and intransitive
   - **Okertu** is primarily intransitive (go wrong); transitive use less common
   - Causation of error often requires periphrastic constructions

3. **Exposure vs. concealment:**
   - Both **agerian jarri** (expose) and **ezkutatu** (hide) have transitive and intransitive uses
   - Parallel locative constructions: **agerian** (in the open) vs. **ezkutuan** (in hiding)
   - Symmetric pattern for visibility/concealment dynamics

4. **Certainty vs. doubt:**
   - **Ziurtatu** has both transitive (confirm) and intransitive (be confirmed) uses
   - **Zalantzan jarri** (call into question) is the standard expression for creating doubt
   - No simple verb **\*zalantzatu** parallel to **ziurtatu**

### Morphological patterns:

- The suffix **-tu/-du** creates both transitive causatives and intransitive inchoatives depending on the verb
- Locative + **jarri** (put) constructions are highly productive for both causative and inchoative meanings
- The auxiliary system (**du/ditu** for transitive, **da/dira** for intransitive) clearly marks transitivity
- Ergative-absolutive alignment is consistent across all causative constructions

### Lexical gaps:

- No simple intransitive verb for "become false" (requires complementizer or revelation constructions)
- No simple transitive verb for "make wrong" parallel to **zuzendu** "make correct"
- No simple verb **\*zalantzatu** "to doubt" parallel to **ziurtatu** "to confirm"
- These gaps are filled by periphrastic or constructional means
