## Summary Notes

1. The majority of attested constructions in this domain are [CORE]: they are common, idiomatic, and well-attested across multiple text types and registers.

2. The [MARGINAL] ratings apply to: the morphological causative constructions (§5: *satyāpana*, *satyāpayati*), which are restricted by register (legal/administrative rather than philosophical) and, in the case of the periphrastic *karaṇa* pattern (*pramāṇīkaraṇa*), by period (more prominent in modern Sanskrit-influenced usage); and the *-jña* agentive nominalization (§7: *ṛta-jña*), which shows observable domain restriction relative to the other attested agentive patterns.

3. No construction in this inventory is rated [UNCERTAIN]; all patterns are supported by identified attestations.

4. The most consistent cross-construction restriction is the boundary between **epistemological/ontological** contexts and **ethical/conduct-evaluating** contexts: the prescriptive/injunctive (§8) and ablative deviation (§9) constructions are explicitly restricted to the latter and do not extend to the former.

5. The concessive construction (§12) is the only pattern with an explicit lexical restriction: *satyam* is the sole truth-domain term attested in this role with consistent frequency.

6. The agentive *-tṛ* nominalization (§7: *pramātṛ*) is register-restricted to epistemological technical prose and is not freely productive across the vocabulary domain, distinguishing it from the *-vādin* compound pattern, which is productive across legal, ethical, and philosophical registers.

7. The *-vādin* and *-bhāṣin* agentive suffixes (§7) show an observable distributional pattern: *-vādin* is more frequent in the *satya*-family, while *-bhāṣin* is more frequent in the *anṛta*-family; this distribution is observable but not absolute.

8. Within the abstract nominalization patterns (§6), *mithyātva* carries a specific technical meaning in Advaita Vedānta (neither real nor unreal) beyond the general nominalization function, making it non-freely interchangeable with *mithyātā* in Advaita contexts specifically, even though no consistent semantic distinction between *-tā* and *-tva* is supported by the data generally.

9. Alternate constructions listed are drawn exclusively from examples attested within the probe data; no additional alternates have been generated.
