# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The scope excludes derivational forms already documented in P2a. Constructions are included only where there is positive evidence of natural, attested use. Where a construction type does not exist or is not established for a given item, this is recorded explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

Constructions are organized by P1 base form. Within each base form, constructions are grouped by structural type where multiple types are present.

---

## 1. Base Form: **benar**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benar atau tidak, kabar itu sudah tersebar luas.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** True or not, the news has already spread widely.
**Usage Notes:** Concessive framing using the *benar atau tidak* binary to bracket a claim whose truth is set aside while a consequence is asserted. Common in journalistic and conversational registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benar atau tidak, hal itu perlu diselidiki.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** True or not, the matter needs to be investigated.
**Usage Notes:** A suspension-of-judgment construction using *benar atau tidak* (true or not) to bracket the truth value of a claim while asserting that it warrants attention regardless. Common in journalistic and investigative discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kalau benar demikian, kita harus segera bertindak.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If that is indeed the case, we must act immediately.
**Usage Notes:** Conditional construction *kalau benar* (if true / if indeed) used to introduce a course of action contingent on the truth of a prior claim. Common in spoken and written deliberative discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kalau benar demikian, maka...
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If that is indeed the case, then...
**Usage Notes:** A conditional construction used in argumentation and formal discourse to introduce a consequence that follows if a prior claim is accepted as true. *Kalau benar demikian* (if truly so) is a fixed conditional opener. Common in legal reasoning, journalistic commentary, and formal debate.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Memang benar bahwa angka kemiskinan menurun.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is indeed true that poverty rates have declined.
**Usage Notes:** *Memang benar bahwa* is a fixed concessive-affirmative frame used to acknowledge a fact before introducing a qualification or contrast. *Memang* reinforces the truth claim. Common in formal argumentation and journalism.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Memang benar bahwa harga terus naik.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is indeed true that prices keep rising.
**Usage Notes:** A confirmation construction using *memang* (indeed, as expected) + *benar* + *bahwa* (that) to introduce a factual claim that is being affirmed, often in response to a prior assertion or in the context of acknowledging an uncomfortable truth. *Memang benar* functions as a fixed affirmative unit.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Memang benar demikian.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is indeed the case. / That is indeed so.
**Usage Notes:** A concessive or confirmatory construction combining *memang* (indeed, as expected) with *benar* and the demonstrative *demikian* (thus, so). Used in formal and journalistic registers to confirm a claim, often after it has been questioned or disputed.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benarkah ia yang melakukan itu?
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Is it really true that he did that? / Did he really do that?
**Usage Notes:** *Benar* + *-kah* (interrogative enclitic) forms a yes/no question challenging or seeking confirmation of a truth claim. Common in spoken and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Ia benar dalam hal ini.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He is right about this / correct on this point.
**Usage Notes:** *Benar dalam hal* (correct/right in the matter of) restricts the scope of a truth attribution to a specific domain or point. Common in debate, commentary, and academic writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Tidak ada yang benar-benar tahu apa yang terjadi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** No one truly knows what happened.
**Usage Notes:** *Tidak ada yang benar-benar* (no one truly / nothing really) combines negation with the intensifier *benar-benar* to assert the complete absence of genuine knowledge or certainty. Common in journalistic and narrative registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Tidak ada yang benar-benar tahu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Nobody really knows. / No one truly knows.
**Usage Notes:** A construction combining negation (*tidak ada yang*) with the intensifier *benar-benar* to express that genuine knowledge of something is absent. Common in journalistic and conversational registers when discussing uncertain matters.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Sejauh yang saya tahu, hal itu benar adanya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** As far as I know, that is indeed the case.
**Usage Notes:** *Sejauh yang saya tahu* (as far as I know) is an epistemic hedge that qualifies the speaker's commitment to the truth claim introduced by *benar adanya*. Common in spoken and written registers where the speaker signals limited but genuine confidence.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Pada dasarnya benar, tetapi ada beberapa detail yang perlu dikoreksi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Essentially correct, but there are some details that need to be corrected.
**Usage Notes:** *Pada dasarnya benar* (essentially/fundamentally correct) is a partial concession construction used in academic, journalistic, and deliberative contexts to acknowledge the core truth of a claim while reserving the right to qualify it.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Saya rasa ada benarnya juga pendapat itu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** I think there is something to that view as well.
**Usage Notes:** *Ada benarnya* (there is some truth/correctness to it) is a partial concession expression used in conversational and deliberative contexts to acknowledge partial validity without full endorsement. The addition of *juga* (also) softens the concession further.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Saya rasa ada benarnya pendapat itu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** I think there is some truth to that opinion.
**Usage Notes:** A hedged partial-endorsement construction combining *saya rasa* (I think/feel) + *ada benarnya* (there is some truth to it) + the evaluated item. The hedge *saya rasa* marks the speaker's epistemic tentativeness. Common in conversational and opinion discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Itu benar, tapi tidak lengkap.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is true, but incomplete.
**Usage Notes:** *Benar, tapi* (true, but) is a standard concessive construction used to acknowledge a truth claim while introducing a qualification. Extremely common in argumentation, debate, and commentary across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Demi kebenaran dan keadilan, ia bersedia bersaksi.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** For the sake of truth and justice, he was willing to testify.
**Usage Notes:** *Demi kebenaran dan keadilan* (for the sake of truth and justice) is a fixed collocation used in legal, moral, and political discourse to invoke truth and justice as paired values motivating an action. The pairing of *kebenaran* and *keadilan* is stable and frequent. Also appears as the shorter formula *Demi kebenaran dan keadilan* in formal speeches, court proceedings, and activist discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kebenarannya belum dapat dipastikan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Its truth has not yet been established / confirmed.
**Usage Notes:** *Kebenaran* + *-nya* (its truth) + *belum dapat dipastikan* (cannot yet be confirmed) is a standard journalistic and legal formula for suspending judgment on an unverified claim. Common in news reporting and official statements.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Itu benar adanya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is genuinely the case. / That is true as it stands.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed predicate construction used to assert that something is factually and genuinely so, often in formal or emphatic contexts. The element *adanya* (*ada* + *-nya*) adds a sense of existential factuality; the construction as a whole is more emphatic than bare *itu benar*. Common in formal speech and writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Ada benarnya juga.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** There is something to that. / That has a point. / That's not entirely wrong.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed concessive construction used to partially acknowledge the validity of a claim or argument without fully endorsing it. The particle *juga* ("also, too") softens the concession further. Extremely common in conversational and editorial discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Yang benar saja.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Come on, be serious. / Really? / Tell the truth now.
**Usage Notes:** A colloquial fixed expression used to challenge someone to be honest or to express mild disbelief at a claim. The construction is pragmatically ambiguous between a challenge to honesty and an expression of incredulity. Restricted to informal spoken registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Benar atau salah, itu pilihannya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Right or wrong, that is his/her choice.
**Usage Notes:** The binary formula *benar atau salah* (true or false / right or wrong) used in a concessive frame. The formula itself is fixed; its embedding in larger constructions is syntactically free. Common in moral and deliberative discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kalau memang benar, buktikan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If it is indeed true, prove it.
**Usage Notes:** A conditional challenge construction common in journalistic, legal, and political discourse. The combination *kalau memang benar* is a semi-fixed frame used to demand evidence for a claim.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Actually, I disagree. / In truth, I don't agree.
**Usage Notes:** *Sebenarnya* functioning as a sentence-initial discourse marker introducing a correction or disclosure. This is the canonical use of the form; included here as a construction rather than a derivation because its function is pragmatic-discoursal rather than purely morphological.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Atas nama kebenaran.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** In the name of truth.
**Usage Notes:** A prepositional formula used in moral, religious, and political rhetoric to invoke truth as a justifying principle for an action or stance. Appears in formal speeches, editorials, and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** The truth will come out in the end. / Truth will eventually be revealed.
**Usage Notes:** A semi-proverbial construction expressing the belief that truth cannot be permanently concealed. The verb *terungkap* (to be revealed, to come to light) is the standard collocate for *kebenaran* in this construction. Common in journalistic and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Tidak dibenarkan untuk...
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is not permitted to... / One is not justified in...
**Usage Notes:** A fixed regulatory formula using the passive *dibenarkan* in negated form to prohibit or disallow an action. Standard in official regulations, institutional rules, and legal documents. The deontic (permissive/prohibitive) use of *benar* is well established in this construction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Tidak dapat dibenarkan dengan alasan apa pun.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It cannot be justified by any reason whatsoever.
**Usage Notes:** A modal negation construction used in legal, moral, and journalistic discourse to declare that an action or claim is wholly without justification. *Tidak dapat dibenarkan* (cannot be justified/confirmed) is a fixed evaluative unit; the addition of *dengan alasan apa pun* (by any reason whatsoever) intensifies the rejection.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 29**
**Base Form:** benar
**Example:** Sudah terbukti benar.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It has already been proven true. / It has been verified as correct.
**Usage Notes:** A resultative construction combining *sudah* (already) + *terbukti* (proven, verified) + *benar* (true) to indicate that the truth of something has been established through evidence. Common in journalistic, legal, and scientific discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **salah**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Kalau saya tidak salah, pertemuan itu dijadwalkan hari Kamis.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If I am not mistaken, the meeting is scheduled for Thursday.
**Usage Notes:** *Kalau saya tidak salah* (if I am not mistaken) is a standard epistemic hedge used in spoken and written Indonesian to qualify a claim the speaker is not fully certain about. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Ia mengakui bahwa ia salah.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He admitted that he was wrong.
**Usage Notes:** *Mengakui bahwa ia salah* (to admit that one is wrong) is a standard construction for expressing acknowledgment of error or fault. *Mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake) is the nominalized variant. Both are common in interpersonal, legal, and moral discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Bukan salah saya kalau informasinya tidak akurat.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It is not my fault if the information is inaccurate.
**Usage Notes:** *Bukan salah* + possessive (not [someone's] fault) is a standard construction for deflecting blame. Common in interpersonal and institutional discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Siapa yang salah dalam kasus ini masih diperdebatkan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Who is at fault in this case is still being debated.
**Usage Notes:** *Siapa yang salah* (who is wrong/at fault) is a standard interrogative frame used in legal, journalistic, and interpersonal contexts to open the question of culpability. *Yang salah* (the one who is wrong/at fault) is a productive relative-clause construction that can be nominalized: *yang salah harus bertanggung jawab* (the one who is wrong must take responsibility). Common in news reporting and legal commentary.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah besar jika kita mengabaikan masalah ini.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It would be a serious mistake to ignore this problem.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah besar* (a big/serious mistake) is a fixed intensified construction used in moral, political, and practical discourse to characterize an error as grave or consequential. Common in editorial and advisory writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Tidak ada yang salah dengan pendekatan itu.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** There is nothing wrong with that approach.
**Usage Notes:** *Tidak ada yang salah dengan* (there is nothing wrong with) is a standard construction for defending a practice or position against implied criticism. Common in argumentative and advisory discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Ia salah mengerti maksud saya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He misunderstood my meaning.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah* + verb (to wrongly/mistakenly do something) is a productive construction where *salah* functions as a pre-verbal modifier meaning "wrongly" or "mistakenly." *Salah mengerti* (to misunderstand), *salah membaca* (to misread), *salah mengira* (to mistakenly think) are all attested. Common across registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah langkah bisa berakibat fatal.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** A wrong step could have fatal consequences.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah langkah* (wrong step, misstep) is a fixed compound used in practical, political, and moral discourse to describe a consequential error in judgment or action. Common in advisory and journalistic writing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Itu bukan salah satu alasan yang saya maksud.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is not one of the reasons I meant.
**Usage Notes:** *Salah satu* (one of) in its grammaticalized partitive use, combined with negation. The negated form *bukan salah satu* (not one of) is equally common. The partitive meaning of *salah* in this construction is fully grammaticalized. The truth-evaluative meaning of *salah* is absent in this construction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah satu dari mereka.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** One of them.
**Usage Notes:** The construction *salah satu* (literally "one wrong" or "one of") is a fully grammaticalized quantifier meaning "one of (a set)." The truth-evaluative meaning of *salah* is absent in this construction.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah paham.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Misunderstanding. / To misunderstand.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed compound construction (*salah* + *paham*, "understanding") meaning "misunderstanding." Functions as both a noun and a verb (*terjadi salah paham*, "a misunderstanding occurred"; *saya salah paham*, "I misunderstood"). Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah kaprah.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** A widespread misconception. / A commonly accepted error.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed idiomatic construction meaning a mistaken belief or practice that has become so widespread it is accepted as correct. *Kaprah* means "common, widespread." Used in journalistic, educational, and analytical discourse to identify entrenched errors.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah sasaran.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Off-target. / Misdirected. / Hitting the wrong target.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *sasaran*, "target") meaning that an action, policy, or criticism is directed at the wrong target or fails to address the right issue. Common in journalistic and policy discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah tafsir.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Misinterpretation. / To misinterpret.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *tafsir*, "interpretation") meaning misinterpretation. Used in legal, religious, and academic contexts to describe incorrect readings of texts, laws, or statements.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Bukan salah saya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** It's not my fault. / I'm not to blame.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed denial-of-responsibility construction. *Bukan salah* + possessive is a standard formula for disclaiming fault. Extremely common in everyday, legal, and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Mengakui kesalahan.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** To admit one's mistake. / To acknowledge wrongdoing.
**Usage Notes:** A stable verb-object construction (*mengakui* "to admit/acknowledge" + *kesalahan* "mistake/fault") used in moral, legal, and political discourse. *Mengakui kesalahan* is a standard formula in apologies, confessions, and accountability discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah dalam bertindak.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Wrong in one's actions. / Mistaken in acting.
**Usage Notes:** A predicative construction using *salah* as a predicate adjective followed by a prepositional phrase specifying the domain of error. Syntactically productive; the pattern *salah dalam* + verbal noun is common in moral and legal evaluation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Salah alamat.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Wrong address. / Barking up the wrong tree. / You've got the wrong person/place.
**Usage Notes:** A fixed construction (*salah* + *alamat*, "address") used both literally (wrong address) and figuratively (directing a complaint, accusation, or request to the wrong person or place). The figurative use is common in everyday and journalistic registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Itu bukan salah saya.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is not my fault. / That is not my mistake.
**Usage Notes:** A denial-of-responsibility construction using *bukan salah* + possessive to reject attribution of fault. *Bukan salah saya* is a fixed unit in interpersonal and legal discourse. The construction *bukan salah X* (not X's fault) is fully productive with any possessive.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Tidak ada salahnya mencoba.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** There is no harm in trying. / It is not wrong to try.
**Usage Notes:** A permission-granting construction using *tidak ada salahnya* + infinitive to suggest that a proposed action is unobjectionable. *Tidak ada salahnya* is a fixed unit; the following verb phrase is open. Common in motivational and advisory discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Jangan salahkan diri sendiri.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Don't blame yourself.
**Usage Notes:** An imperative construction using *jangan* (don't) + *salahkan* (blame) + reflexive *diri sendiri* (oneself). A common expression in interpersonal and psychological discourse. The construction *jangan salahkan* + object is fully productive.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Kalau salah, harus diakui.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** If wrong, it must be acknowledged. / If mistaken, one must admit it.
**Usage Notes:** A conditional moral construction used in ethical and interpersonal discourse to assert the obligation to acknowledge error. *Kalau salah* (if wrong) is a fixed conditional opener in moral argumentation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Itu salah besar.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That is a serious mistake. / That is gravely wrong.
**Usage Notes:** An intensified error-evaluation construction using *salah* + *besar* (big, great) to characterize an error or moral wrong as serious. *Salah besar* is a fixed evaluative unit used in moral, legal, and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
**Base Form:** salah
**Example:** Bukan berarti dia salah.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** That does not mean he is wrong. / It does not follow that he is at fault.
**Usage Notes:** A logical-denial construction using *bukan berarti* (does not mean) + *salah* to block an inference from a prior claim to a conclusion of fault or error. Common in argumentative and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: **asli**

---

**Construction 1**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Apakah dokumen ini asli atau palsu?
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** Is this document genuine or fake?
**Usage Notes:** *Asli atau palsu* (genuine or fake) is the standard binary opposition used in legal, commercial, and administrative contexts to frame the question of authenticity. The pairing is stable and highly frequent.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Barang ini dijamin asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** This item is guaranteed to be genuine.
**Usage Notes:** *Dijamin asli* (guaranteed genuine) is a common commercial formula used in retail and online commerce to assert authenticity. The passive *dijamin* (guaranteed) combined with *asli* is a stable collocation in commercial language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Ia orang Jawa asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** He is a genuine/native Javanese person.
**Usage Notes:** *[Ethnonym/origin] + asli* is a construction used to assert that a person is a native or authentic member of an ethnic, regional, or national group, as opposed to someone with partial or adopted affiliation. Common in identity and demographic discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Versi aslinya berbeda dari yang beredar sekarang.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** The original version differs from what is currently circulating.
**Usage Notes:** *Versi aslinya* (the original version of it) is a common construction in journalistic, legal, and academic contexts to distinguish an original document, text, or recording from later versions or copies.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Produk ini bukan produk asli.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** This product is not a genuine product.
**Usage Notes:** *Bukan [noun] asli* (not a genuine [noun]) is a standard negation construction used in commercial and legal contexts to identify counterfeit or inauthentic goods. Common in consumer protection and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Sertifikat asli harus diserahkan kepada panitia.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** The original certificate must be submitted to the committee.
**Usage Notes:** *[Document noun] + asli* (original [document]) is a standard administrative construction distinguishing the original document from copies (*salinan* or *fotokopi*). Extremely common in administrative and legal language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
**Base Form:** asli
**Example:** Dijamin asli.
