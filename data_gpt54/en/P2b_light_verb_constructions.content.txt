Below is a focused inventory of **attested multi-word constructions** in English built on or extending the **P1 base forms**. I exclude simple derivational forms already handled in P2a, and I avoid unattested gap-filling.

## 1. Base Form: **truth**

### 1. **tell the truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** Extremely common everyday and legal expression meaning to speak honestly and accurately. It is central in moral, interpersonal, and testimonial contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **the truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the truth
- **Usage notes:** Very common noun phrase referring to what is really the case. It often carries rhetorical force, especially in contrastive contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **the whole truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** the whole truth
- **Usage notes:** Common phrase emphasizing completeness as well as accuracy. It is especially familiar from legal oaths and serious interpersonal exchanges.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **nothing but the truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** Fixed phrase most familiar from courtroom oath language, often occurring in “the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth.” It stresses exclusion of falsehood or embellishment.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **truth be told**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** truth be told; honestly
- **Usage notes:** Common discourse marker introducing a candid admission. It is frequent in speech and informal-to-neutral writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **in truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** in truth; in fact
- **Usage notes:** Established but somewhat formal or literary phrase marking what is really the case. It is less common in casual conversation than *actually* or *in fact*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **there is some truth in**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** there is some truth in X
- **Usage notes:** Common evaluative construction used to concede partial correctness to a claim, criticism, or stereotype. It is frequent in argument and commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **have a ring of truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** have a ring of truth
- **Usage notes:** Idiomatic expression for sounding believable or authentic. It overlaps semantically with *ring true* but is built on the noun *truth*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **moment of truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** moment of truth
- **Usage notes:** Common idiom for the decisive point when reality is revealed or tested. It is widely used in journalism, sports, business, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **home truth**
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English translation:** home truth; uncomfortable truth
- **Usage notes:** Established chiefly in British English for a blunt or unpleasant truth someone needs to hear. It is idiomatic and somewhat marked regionally.
