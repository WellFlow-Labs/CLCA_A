## 2. Narrative Analysis

### Patterns Recurring Across Unrelated Language Families

**Abstract noun formation from truth-domain roots is universal in the corpus.** Every language has a morphological or syntactic strategy for creating abstract nouns from truth-domain adjectives or roots. The specific device varies by morphological type: agglutinative suffixes in Hebrew (-ות), Sanskrit (-tā, -tva), Greek (-ότητα, -εια), and English (-y, -ness, -ity); a noun-class prefix in Swahili (u-); a circumfix in Tagalog (ka-...-an); a copula nominalization in Quechua (chiqan kay); and a suffix restricted to disyllabic bases in Chinese (-性). Despite the typological diversity of these strategies, the functional outcome is the same: a nominal form denoting the quality or state of being true, false, correct, honest, etc. This convergence across unrelated families (Sino-Tibetan, Semitic, Indo-European, Austronesian, Niger-Congo, Quechuan) is robust.

**Morphological negation as the dominant antonym-forming strategy is universal.** In all eight languages, the primary strategy for expressing the opposite of a truth-domain term is morphological negation rather than a dedicated lexical antonym. Sanskrit's a-/an- privative is the most systematic, generating fully lexicalized antonyms for virtually every truth-domain term. English uses three distinct prefixes (un-, in-, dis-) with partially unpredictable distribution. Tagalog and Quechua use free negative particles (hindi, mana) rather than bound morphemes, but the functional result is the same. The consistency of this pattern across unrelated families suggests that the truth domain is organized around a small set of positive terms, with negative counterparts derived rather than independently lexicalized.

**The agentive asymmetry (liar lexicalized; truth-teller not) is attested across at least five unrelated families.** In Chinese, Greek, English, Swahili, and Quechua, there is no single derived word for "truth-teller" while "liar" is fully lexicalized. Hebrew שקרן, Sanskrit mithyāvādin, Greek ψεύτης, English liar, Tagalog sinungaling, Swahili mwongo, and Quechua llullakuq are all attested as core vocabulary items. The positive counterparts — "one who habitually tells the truth" — require compounds or periphrasis in most languages. This asymmetry is not a derivational accident: it reflects a consistent pattern across unrelated families where the negative disposition (lying) is more salient as a character type than the positive disposition (truth-telling). The pattern is documented in Sino-Tibetan (zh), Indo-European (el, en, sa), Semitic (he), Austronesian (tl), Niger-Congo (sw), and Quechuan (qu).

**Discourse marker grammaticalization from truth terms is attested across at least seven languages.** In Chinese (说真的), Hebrew (באמת?), Greek (αλήθεια?), English (to be honest), Tagalog (sa totoo lang), Swahili (kwa kweli), and Quechua (chiqap, k'ari), truth-domain terms or phrases have undergone pragmatic grammaticalization to function as discourse markers introducing frank statements, expressing surprise, or seeking confirmation. The degree of grammaticalization varies, but the pattern of recruiting truth vocabulary for discourse management is consistent across unrelated families.

**Compounding of truth-domain roots with speech/communication verbs is universal.** In all eight languages, the most frequent constructions in the truth domain involve a truth-domain term combined with a verb of speaking or communicating: Chinese 说实话, Hebrew לומר את האמת, Sanskrit satyaṃ vada, Greek λέω την αλήθεια, English tell the truth, Tagalog chiqanta rimay, Swahili sema ukweli, Quechua chiqanta rimay. This pattern reflects the fundamental connection between truth and speech acts across all languages in the corpus.

### Striking Asymmetries

**The inchoative asymmetry (becoming true/good is lexicalized; becoming false/bad is not) is attested across multiple unrelated families.** This is one of the most consistent morphological asymmetries in the corpus:

- Quechua has allinyay (become good, -ya- inchoative) but no attested parallel for deterioration.
- English "come true" is a fixed expression; *come false is ungrammatical.
- Greek γίνομαι αληθινός (become true) is natural; the reverse is less attested.
- Hebrew התברר (it became clear/turned out to be true) is the standard inchoative for truth-revelation; no parallel for "turning out to be false" is as grammaticalized.
- Chinese 变得真实 (become authentic) is core; 变假 (become fake) is marginal.

This asymmetry is documented across Quechuan, Indo-European (en, el, he), and Sino-Tibetan (zh) — unrelated families. It is not a derivational accident but a consistent pattern suggesting that positive change toward truth/goodness is more readily grammaticalized than negative change.

**The causative asymmetry (making true/correct is lexicalized; making false is more restricted) is attested across multiple families.** In all eight languages, causative forms for establishing, verifying, or correcting truth are well-attested (zh 改正/落实/证实, he הוכיח/אימת, sa sahihisha/hakikisha, el βεβαιώνω/αποδεικνύω, en verify/authenticate/validate, tl patunayan, sw sahihisha/hakikisha, qu chiqanchay). By contrast, causative forms for making false or causing falsity are more restricted:

- English "make X true" is largely ungrammatical; dedicated verbs (falsify, forge) are required for the negative direction.
- Hebrew has no common direct causative "to make X true" or "to make X false."
- Quechua chiqanchay (verify/authenticate) is attested; no parallel *llullachay (make false) is documented.
- Greek lacks a simple causative for making true; ακυρώνω (invalidate) covers the negative direction for validity but not for truth generally.

The negative causative (making false) tends to be expressed through deception verbs (deceive, forge, falsify) rather than through direct causatives of the falsity adjective. This asymmetry is documented across Indo-European (en, el, he), Quechuan (qu), and Austronesian (tl).

**The degree nominalization asymmetry is a single-language observation.** Chinese alone has a dedicated suffix (-度) for creating degree nouns from truth-domain adjectives (真实度, 可靠度, 准确度), distinct from quality nouns (-性). No other language in the corpus has a dedicated degree nominalization suffix for this domain. This is a Chinese-specific feature, not a cross-linguistic pattern.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**The "become false/bad" inchoative is blocked or marginal in multiple languages.** As noted above, the inchoative pathway from truth-domain adjectives is one-directional in several languages: positive change (becoming true, becoming good, becoming complete) is lexicalized while negative change (becoming false, becoming bad, becoming empty) requires periphrasis or is unattested. This is a one-way pathway documented across unrelated families.

**The "truth-teller" agentive is blocked in multiple languages.** The agentive nominalization pathway from truth-domain roots generates "liar" in all eight languages but fails to generate "truth-teller" as a single derived word in at least five. This is a consistent one-way pathway: the negative agentive is lexicalized; the positive agentive is not.

**The -度 degree suffix in Chinese is blocked for monosyllabic bases.** The Chinese -度 suffix (真实度, 可靠度, 准确度) is restricted to disyllabic bases; *真度 and *假度 are unattested. Similarly, the -性 quality suffix is restricted to disyllabic bases (*真性, *假度 are unattested). This is a morphological blocking condition specific to Chinese.

**The Sanskrit causative for truth-domain predicates is blocked.** Sanskrit causative morphology (NIC suffix) is theoretically available but not attested in the corpus for truth-domain predicates. Causation is expressed periphrastically (through instrumental case or compound verbs) rather than through direct causative derivation. This is a blocked pathway in Sanskrit that is filled by other strategies in other languages.

### Productive, Restricted, Lexicalized/Fossilized, and Unclear Patterns

**Productive patterns** (generating new forms freely):
- Morphological negation of truth-domain adjectives in all languages
- Abstract noun formation from truth-domain roots in all languages
- Causative derivation for verification/correction in zh (-isha in sw, -cha- in qu, pa- in tl)
- The ψευδ- pseudo-X compound system in Greek
- The -isha causative in Swahili applied to Arabic loanwords (sahihisha, hakikisha, kamilisha)
- The ka-...-an circumfix in Tagalog for truth-domain abstract nouns
- The evidential suffixes in Quechua applied to truth-domain assertions

**Restricted patterns** (attested but not freely productive):
- Chinese -性 and -度 suffixes (restricted to disyllabic bases)
- Chinese AABB reduplication (restricted to disyllabic bases; 实实在在 is core, but *真真假假 as a new formation would be unusual)
- Hebrew emphatic reduplication (אמת לאמיתה is attested but the pattern is not freely productive with other truth terms)
- Quechua -lla limitative with truth terms (chiqanlla, allinlla, llullalla are attested; extension to other truth terms is possible but not documented)
- Tagalog reduplication for emphasis (totoong-totoo, tunay na tunay are core; extension to other truth terms is possible but not all are documented)

**Lexicalized/fossilized patterns** (forms that are fixed and no longer transparently compositional):
- Chinese chengyu (四字格): 真金不怕火炼, 名副其实, 表里如一, 华而不实 — these are fixed four-character expressions that are not productively generated by current morphological rules
- Hebrew אמת לאמיתה (truth to-its-truth) — a fixed emphatic expression; the pattern is not freely productive
- Greek αλήθεια? as a surprise marker — the discourse marker function is grammaticalized and no longer compositional
- English "come true" — fixed expression; the pattern does not extend to other truth-domain adjectives
- Tagalog sa totoo lang — highly grammaticalized discourse marker; the compositional meaning (in the truth only) is backgrounded
- Swahili sawa sawa — pragmatic discourse marker; the reduplication is fossilized in this function
- Quechua k'ari — marked as marginal in the F1 table; its status as a productive emphatic particle vs. a fossilized form is unclear

**Unclear or weakly attested patterns**:
- Sanskrit causative morphology for truth-domain predicates: theoretically available but not attested; the absence may reflect a gap in the corpus rather than a genuine blocking
- Quechua chiqanyay (becoming true, -ya- inchoative): marked as marginal; it is unclear whether this is a productive form or a marginal/nonce formation
- Hebrew אמת ושלום (truth and peace) as a fixed pairing: marked as marginal; its status as a productive construction vs. a fixed liturgical formula is unclear
- Swahili reduplication (kweli kweli, sawa sawa): marked as marginal and informal; the productivity of this pattern for other truth-domain terms is not documented
- Chinese 真真 (AA reduplication, truly): marked as marginal; it is unclear whether this is a productive pattern or a literary/archaic form
- The Sanskrit dual genitive construction (satyāsatyayoḥ, pramābhramayoḥ): attested as a productive pattern for expressing binary oppositions, but its status as a derivational strategy vs. a syntactic pattern is unclear
