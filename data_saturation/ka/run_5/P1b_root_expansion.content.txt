# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / martal- (მართალ-)
**Transliteration:** mart- / martal-
**P1 participation:** 3 items directly (martali #4, simartle #3, arasc̣ori #12 via semantic field); the root martal- underlies martali and simartle, and the broader mart- stem underlies additional derivatives identified below.
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, correctness, and justice conceived as a unified property — covering factual accuracy, moral uprightness, and legal legitimacy. This root is the most morphologically productive native root in the truth-evaluation domain.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ყალბ- (q̇alb-)
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 participation:** 3 items (q̇albi #6, siq̇albe #5, gaq̇albeba #23)
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, counterfeit quality, and forgery — covering both the property of being false and the act of making something false. This root (borrowed from Persian/Arabic via Turkish) is the primary lexical vehicle for inauthenticity and deliberate falsification.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ჭეშმარიტ- (č̣ešmarit-)
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmarit-
**P1 participation:** 2 items directly (č̣ešmariti #1, č̣ešmariteba #2), but the root is the primary philosophical and theological vehicle for truth as a property of propositions and states of affairs.
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth conceived as a deep or essential quality — the genuine, the real, the veridical. Less productive morphologically than martal- but carries higher register weight in philosophical and religious discourse.

**Note on root selection:** The root rc̣mun- (to believe/trust), underlying sarc̣muno (#13) and damajerebelilit (#25), is also moderately productive and is noted in Step 3 where relevant. It is not selected as a primary root because its P1 participation (2 items) is lower and its core meaning is epistemic attitude rather than truth-evaluation proper.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: მართ- / მართალ- (mart- / martal-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართალია
**Transliteration:** martalia
**Morpheme breakdown:** martali + copula -a (3sg present)
**Natural English translation:** it is true; you are right; that is correct
**Context of use:** The single most common everyday assertion of truth or correctness in spoken Georgian. Used to concede a point in argument, confirm a fact, or validate a speaker's claim.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** martla
**Morpheme breakdown:** martal- + adverbial reduction (syncope of -al- > -l-)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, indeed, in fact
**Context of use:** High-frequency adverb used to assert or emphasize that something is genuinely the case. Appears in both spoken and written registers as an intensifying truth marker.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** martlac
**Morpheme breakdown:** martla + emphatic particle -c
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Stronger emphatic form of martla, used to confirm or underscore a claim, often in response to doubt or surprise. Very common in spoken Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლაც კი
**Transliteration:** martlac ki
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlac + emphatic particle ki
**Natural English translation:** indeed, really and truly, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Further-reinforced emphatic form, used in contexts of strong affirmation or when countering skepticism. More emphatic than martlac alone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** მართლი
**Transliteration:** martli
**Morpheme breakdown:** mart- + adjectival suffix -li
**Natural English translation:** righteous, just, upright
**Context of use:** Used primarily in religious and moral discourse to characterize a person as righteous or just. Appears frequently in biblical and liturgical Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** მართლება
**Transliteration:** martleba
**Morpheme breakdown:** martl- + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, being proven right
**Context of use:** Used in theological contexts (justification before God) and in legal and moral contexts (vindication of a person or claim). Appears in both classical and modern Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** გამართლება
**Transliteration:** gamartleba
**Morpheme breakdown:** ga- (preverb, completive) + martl- + -eba (verbal noun)
**Natural English translation:** justification, acquittal, vindication, proving right
**Context of use:** The standard legal and moral term for acquittal or vindication. Used in court proceedings (acquittal of a defendant), in moral argument (justification of an action), and in everyday speech (proving that one was right).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** გამართლება (მოქმედების)
**Transliteration:** gamartleba (moqmedebis)
**Morpheme breakdown:** gamartleba + genitive of moqmedeba (action)
**Natural English translation:** justification of an action
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation in legal and ethical discourse referring to the justification or defense of a particular act. Appears in legal briefs and philosophical argument.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** გამართლდა
**Transliteration:** gamartlda
**Morpheme breakdown:** ga- + martl- + -d- (passive/intransitive marker) + -a (past tense)
**Natural English translation:** was vindicated; proved to be right; turned out to be correct
**Context of use:** Verbal form used to report that a prediction, person, or claim was subsequently shown to be correct. Common in journalistic and everyday narrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** გაუმართლა
**Transliteration:** gaumartla
**Morpheme breakdown:** ga- + u- (benefactive/dative marker) + martl- + -a (past)
**Natural English translation:** was lucky; it worked out for (someone)
**Context of use:** Idiomatic expression meaning that things turned out well for someone, often through luck rather than merit. A significant semantic extension of the root into the domain of fortune and outcome.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულება
**Transliteration:** martlmsajuleba
**Morpheme breakdown:** martl- + msajul- (judge, from msajuli) + -eba
**Natural English translation:** justice, administration of justice, judiciary
**Context of use:** The standard formal term for the justice system and the administration of justice. Used in legal, constitutional, and political discourse. Appears in the names of courts and legal institutions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულების სისტემა
**Transliteration:** martlmsajulebis sistema
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlmsajuleba (genitive -is) + sistema (system, borrowed)
**Natural English translation:** justice system, judicial system
**Context of use:** Standard collocation in legal and political discourse referring to the institutional apparatus of justice. Appears in policy documents, legal commentary, and journalism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** მართლწერა
**Transliteration:** martlc̣era
**Morpheme breakdown:** martl- + c̣era (writing, from c̣erva, to write)
**Natural English translation:** orthography, correct spelling
**Context of use:** The standard term for orthography and correct spelling in Georgian. Used in educational, editorial, and linguistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** მართლწერის წესები
**Transliteration:** martlc̣eris c̣esebi
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlc̣era (genitive -is) + c̣esebi (rules, plural of c̣esi)
**Natural English translation:** rules of orthography, spelling rules
**Context of use:** Standard collocation in educational and editorial contexts referring to the normative rules of correct spelling. Appears in grammar books and style guides.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებელი
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebeli
**Morpheme breakdown:** martl- + madidebeli (glorifying, from madideba, to glorify) + -i
**Natural English translation:** Orthodox (Christian); one who glorifies rightly/truly
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for Eastern Orthodox Christianity and its adherents. Used in religious, historical, and cultural contexts. The compound encodes the idea of correct or true glorification of God.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებლობა
**Transliteration:** martlmadideblobа
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlmadidebeli + nominalizing suffix -oba (with stem reduction)
**Natural English translation:** Orthodoxy, Eastern Orthodox Christianity
**Context of use:** The abstract noun for the Orthodox Christian faith and tradition. Used in theological, historical, and cultural discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებელი ეკლესია
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebeli eklesia
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlmadidebeli + eklesia (church, borrowed from Greek)
**Natural English translation:** the Orthodox Church
**Context of use:** Standard collocation referring to the Georgian Orthodox Church or Eastern Orthodox churches generally. Appears in religious, historical, and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** სამართალი
**Transliteration:** samartal i
**Morpheme breakdown:** sa- (relational prefix) + martal- + -i
**Natural English translation:** law, right, justice (as a system or domain)
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian word for law as a system or domain of study and practice. Used in legal education, jurisprudence, and formal discourse. Distinct from kanoni (specific law/statute).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** სამართლის ფაკულტეტი
**Transliteration:** samartal is p̕akult̕et̕i
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartali (genitive -is) + p̕akult̕et̕i (faculty, borrowed)
**Natural English translation:** faculty of law, law school
**Context of use:** Standard collocation for a university law faculty. Appears in academic and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** სამართლებრივი
**Transliteration:** samartalebr ivi
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartali + -ebr- (manner/relational infix) + -ivi (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** legal, juridical, lawful
**Context of use:** The standard adjective meaning "pertaining to law" or "legal." Used extensively in legal documents, legislation, and administrative texts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** სამართლებრივი სახელმწიფო
**Transliteration:** samartalebr ivi saxelmwipo
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartalebr ivi + saxelmwipo (state)
**Natural English translation:** rule-of-law state, Rechtsstaat
**Context of use:** Key term in constitutional and political discourse referring to a state governed by law. The Georgian equivalent of the German Rechtsstaat concept.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** სამართლიანი
**Transliteration:** samartaliani
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartali + -ani (adjectival suffix indicating possession of a quality)
**Natural English translation:** fair, just, equitable
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate persons, decisions, distributions, and systems as fair or just. One of the most common evaluative adjectives in moral and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** სამართლიანობა
**Transliteration:** samartalianoba
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartaliani + -oba (nominalization)
**Natural English translation:** fairness, justice, equity
**Context of use:** The abstract noun for fairness and justice as a quality or value. Used in moral philosophy, political theory, and everyday moral argument.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** სამართლიანად
**Transliteration:** samartalianad
**Morpheme breakdown:** samartaliani + adverbial suffix -ad
**Natural English translation:** fairly, justly, rightly
**Context of use:** Adverbial form used to characterize actions or judgments as carried out in a fair or just manner. Common in moral and legal evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** უსამართლო
**Transliteration:** usamartlo
**Morpheme breakdown:** u- (negation) + samartali + -o
**Natural English translation:** unjust, unfair, inequitable
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate decisions, systems, persons, or situations as lacking justice or fairness. Common in moral, political, and everyday evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** უსამართლობა
**Transliteration:** usamartloba
**Morpheme breakdown:** usamartlo + -ba (nominalization, variant of -oba)
**Natural English translation:** injustice, unfairness
**Context of use:** The abstract noun for injustice. Used in moral, political, and legal discourse to name the condition or instance of being treated unjustly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** მართებული
**Transliteration:** martebuli
**Morpheme breakdown:** mart- + -ebuli (resultative participle suffix)
**Natural English translation:** correct, proper, appropriate, well-founded
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate statements, decisions, and actions as correct or well-founded. Slightly more formal than sc̣ori; common in written and official registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** მართებულია
**Transliteration:** martebulia
**Morpheme breakdown:** martebuli + copula -a
**Natural English translation:** it is correct; it is appropriate; it is proper
**Context of use:** Predicative form used to assert that something is correct or appropriate. Common in formal written discourse, official documents, and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** მართებულად
**Transliteration:** martebulaad
**Morpheme breakdown:** martebuli + adverbial suffix -ad
**Natural English translation:** correctly, properly, appropriately
**Context of use:** Adverbial form used to characterize actions or judgments as carried out correctly. Appears in formal and written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** გამართული
**Transliteration:** gamartuli
**Morpheme breakdown:** ga- (completive preverb) + mart- + -uli (resultative participle)
**Natural English translation:** well-organized, properly arranged, correct (of language/text), in good order
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate texts, speech, systems, or arrangements as properly organized or correct. A common term in editorial and linguistic contexts for well-formed language.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Additional note:** The mart-/martal- root is highly productive. Further forms exist (e.g., damartaleba, marteba in archaic usage, compound legal terms) beyond the 30 listed here. The above represents the most common and well-attested expressions.

### ROOT 3: ჭეშმარიტ- (č̣ešmarit-)

**Context of use:** Collocation used in theological and philosophical contexts to assert the truth-value of a statement or utterance. Appears in biblical commentary and philosophical argument.

**2.**

**Natural English translation:** the true God
**Context of use:** Theological collocation used in Christian doctrine to distinguish the one true God from false gods or idols. Appears in liturgical texts, creeds, and theological writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი სარწმუნოება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti sarc̣muneba
**Morpheme breakdown:** č̣ešmariti + sarc̣muneba (faith, belief)
**Natural English translation:** the true faith, true religion
