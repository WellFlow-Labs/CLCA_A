# Language: Greek (el)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### 1. Base: **αλήθεια** (alítheia) — truth

#### Derived Form 1.1
**Derived form:** αληθινός (alithinós)  
**Transliteration:** alithinós  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- (truth root) + -ινός (adjectival suffix marking material/quality)  
**Translation:** true, genuine, real, authentic  
**Usage:** Adjectival derivation marking something as conforming to reality or being authentic. Used attributively and predicatively across all registers.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.2
**Derived form:** αληθεύω (alithévo)  
**Transliteration:** alithévo  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- + -εύω (denominative verb suffix)  
**Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truly  
**Usage:** Verbal derivation for the act of telling truth. Common across registers, often in contrast to lying.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.3
**Derived form:** αληθώς (alithós)  
**Transliteration:** alithós  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθ- + -ώς (adverbial suffix)  
**Translation:** truly, in truth, really  
**Usage:** Adverbial derivation used for emphasis or confirmation of truth. Functions across all registers.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.4
**Derived form:** αληθινά (alithiná)  
**Transliteration:** alithiná  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθιν- (from αληθινός) + -ά (adverbial suffix)  
**Translation:** truly, really, genuinely  
**Usage:** Secondary adverbial derivation from the adjective αληθινός. Common in everyday speech and writing.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 1.5
**Derived form:** αληθινότητα (alithinótita)  
**Transliteration:** alithinótita  
**Literal breakdown:** αληθιν- + -ότητα (abstract noun suffix)  
**Translation:** truthfulness, genuineness, authenticity  
**Usage:** Abstract noun derivation marking the quality of being true or genuine. Used in philosophical and evaluative discourse.  
**Status:** [CORE]

### 2. Base: **ψέμα** (pséma) — lie

#### Derived Form 2.1
**Derived form:** ψεματάκι (psematáki)  
**Transliteration:** psematáki  
**Literal breakdown:** ψεμα- + -τάκι (diminutive suffix)  
**Translation:** little lie, white lie, fib  
**Usage:** Diminutive form suggesting a minor or harmless lie. Common in everyday speech to minimize the seriousness of a falsehood.  
**Status:** [CORE]

**Note:** The base ψέμα does not productively generate agent nouns or adjectives; these come from the related verb ψεύδομαι and root ψευδ-.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productivity Patterns

1. **Highly productive suffixes:**
   - **-ότητα** (abstract noun): Appears with nearly all adjectival bases to create quality nouns (αληθινότητα, σωστότητα, ορθότητα, γνησιότητα, etc.)
   - **-ά** (adverb): Productive with adjectival bases (σωστά, αληθινά, πραγματικά, etc.)
   - **-ώς** (adverb): Alternative adverbial suffix, often more formal (αληθώς, ορθώς, δικαίως, etc.)
   - **-εύω** (denominative verb): Creates verbs from nouns/adjectives (αληθεύω, σωστεύω, λαθεύω, etc.)
   - **-ώνω** (causative verb): Creates causative verbs (ορθώνω, δικαιώνω, βεβαιώνω, ακυρώνω)

2. **Asymmetries:**
   - Truth vocabulary (αλήθεια, αληθινός) shows moderate derivational productivity
   - Falsity vocabulary (ψέμα, ψεύτης, ψευδής) shows split patterns: ψέμα has limited derivations, while ψευδ- root is highly productive in compounds
   - Correctness terms (σωστός, ορθός) show parallel derivational patterns
   - Justice terms (δίκαιος, άδικος) show rich derivational families

3. **Gaps:**
   - Some bases marked [MARGINAL] in P1 (κίβδηλος, νόθος, καταχρηστικός) show limited derivational productivity
   - Compound forms like ψευδώνυμο typically do not generate further derivations
   - Some semantic domains rely on root compounding (ψευδ-, ορθ-) rather than suffix derivation

4. **Register variation:**
   - Adverbial forms in -ώς often more formal than -ά forms
   - Abstract nouns in -ότητα span all registers
   - Some derivations (e.g., from νόθος, κίβδηλος) restricted to formal/literary registers
