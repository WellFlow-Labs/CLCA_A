# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions are extracted only where the CONTEXT explicitly documents a contrast, antonym relationship, or constructional asymmetry. Where the CONTEXT notes a contrast without fully elaborating it, that contrast is included with a note on the basis of inclusion. No oppositions have been inferred beyond what the data supports. Where the CONTEXT documents an asymmetry (e.g., one term has a construction that the other lacks), this is recorded as a constructional contrast rather than a lexical one.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** kweli
**Term B:** uongo
**Transliteration:** kweli / uongo
**English translation:** truth / falsehood, lie; true / false
**Description of contrast:** These are the primary lexical antonyms in the Swahili truth-evaluation domain. They are explicitly contrasted in fixed constructions (*kweli au uongo*, "true or false"), in paired imperatives (*niambie ukweli, usiniambie uongo*), and in proverbial assertions (*kweli hushinda uongo*). They are explicitly paired in the binary evaluative construction *kweli au uongo* ("true or false?") documented in P1b (Root 1, Item 30) and P2b (Item 1, Construction 5; Item 3, Construction 4). P3 identifies them as the core contrasting pair across propositional truth assertion and deception characterization.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** ukweli
**Term B:** uongo
**Transliteration:** ukweli / uongo
**English translation:** truth, truthfulness / lie, falsehood, deception
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly identifies *uongo* as the antonym of *ukweli* (P1: "Antonym of *ukweli*"). Both are abstract nominals in noun class 11/14 formed with the *u-* prefix; they are the primary polar opposites in the propositional truth domain. The proverbial construction *ukweli nusu ni uongo* ("a half-truth is a lie"), documented in P2b (Item 2, Construction 11), explicitly bridges and contrasts the two. They are contrasted in constructions such as *kuna tofauti kubwa kati ya kweli na uongo* and *uhalisi dhidi ya uongo*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** halisi
**Term B:** bandia
**Transliteration:** halisi / bandia
**English translation:** genuine, authentic, real / fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly identifies *bandia* as the antonym of *halisi* (P1: "Antonym of *halisi*"). *Halisi* evaluates an object as genuine; *bandia* evaluates it as not genuine. The contrast is directly illustrated by the paired expressions *bidhaa halisi* (genuine goods) and *bidhaa bandia* (fake goods) documented in P2b. They are explicitly opposed in the construction *hii ni halisi, si bandia* ("this is genuine, not fake") and in the binary frame *halisi au bandia — jinsi ya kutofautisha* ("genuine or fake — how to tell the difference"). P3 identifies them as "the core contrasting pair" in Group 3, and P1 notes that *bandia* "contrasts directly with *halisi*."
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** halali
**Term B:** haramu
**Transliteration:** halali / haramu
**English translation:** lawful, legitimate, permissible / forbidden, unlawful, illicit, wrong
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT explicitly identifies *haramu* as the antonym of *halali* (P1: "Functions as the antonym of *halali*"). Both are Arabic-derived adjectives used in religious-legal and broader moral evaluation; they occupy opposite poles of the permissibility dimension. P1 (Item 30) explicitly identifies *haramu* (from Arabic *ḥarām*) as the standard antonym of *halali* in both religious and legal discourse. P2a (Item 30, Derived Form 2) documents *haramu* as the primary contrastive term, noting it is not a morphological derivation from *halali* but the lexical opposite. The contrast operates in Islamic dietary law, legal permissibility, and moral evaluation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** kuthibitisha
**Term B:** kudanganya
**Transliteration:** kuthibitisha / kudanganya
**English translation:** to confirm; to verify; to prove; to authenticate / to deceive; to lie; to mislead; to cheat
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both verbs as central to the truth domain (P1, P2a). *Kuthibitisha* describes the act of establishing truth or authenticity; *kudanganya* describes the act of creating false impressions. They are not morphologically related but occupy opposite functional positions in the domain of acts bearing on truth.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** kudhihirisha
**Term B:** kuficha
**Transliteration:** kudhihirisha / kuficha
**English translation:** to reveal; to demonstrate; to make evident; to show clearly / to hide; to conceal
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents *kudhihirisha* (P2a §28) and documents *kuficha* as the verb in the passive relative construction *ukweli uliofichwa* ("hidden truth; concealed truth"; P1b Root 1, P2b §2 Construction 7). Both verbs take *ukweli* as a natural object and appear in constructions documented in P1b and P2b (*kudhihirisha ukweli mbele ya wote*; *ukweli uliofichwa*). P2a documents *kudhihirisha* as a causative derivation; *kuficha* is its contextual antonym in the revelation-concealment domain. The two verbs express opposite acts with respect to making truth visible or invisible.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** uaminifu
**Term B:** udanganyifu
**Transliteration:** uaminifu / udanganyifu
**English translation:** faithfulness; trustworthiness; loyalty; integrity; reliability / deception; deceitfulness; dishonesty; fraud
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both abstract nominals (P1, P2a). They are morphologically parallel (*u-* abstract prefix + adjectival stem ending in *-fu*) and occupy opposite positions in the evaluation of character: *uaminifu* denotes the quality of being trustworthy; *udanganyifu* denotes the quality of being deceptive. Both are *u-* class 14 abstract nominals evaluating character, and they occupy opposing positions in the character-evaluation domain documented in P3 Groups 6 and 7. The construction *mtu asiye na uaminifu hawezi kuaminiwa* (P2b) implicitly invokes this opposition by defining the untrustworthy person through the absence of *uaminifu*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** mwaminifu
**Term B:** mdanganyifu
**Transliteration:** mwaminifu / mdanganyifu
**English translation:** a faithful/trustworthy person; a believer; a loyal person / a deceiver; a liar; a dishonest person; a fraud
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both agentive nominals (P2a §12, §16). They are morphologically parallel (*m-* class 1 prefix + adjectival stem) and occupy opposite positions in the evaluation of personal trustworthiness: *mwaminifu* denotes a person who can be trusted; *mdanganyifu* denotes a person who deceives. Both are documented in P2a as core agentive nominalizations. The opposition is structurally parallel: same prefix, contrasting stems.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** mwaminifu
**Term B:** mdanganyifu / muongo
**Transliteration:** mwaminifu / mdanganyifu; mwaminifu / muongo
**English translation:** trustworthy/faithful person / deceiver; trustworthy person / liar
**Description of contrast:** All three are agentive *m-* class nominals characterizing persons by their relationship to honesty. *Mwaminifu* evaluates a person as reliably honest; *mdanganyifu* evaluates a person as habitually deceptive; *muongo* evaluates a person as a liar. P3 (Group 5 notes) identifies *muongo* as belonging to the negative pole of the trustworthiness evaluation group. These are not explicitly paired in a single construction in the data but emerge from the same evaluative dimension across multiple documented contexts.

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** mwaminifu
**Term B:** mwongo
**Transliteration:** mwaminifu / mwongo
**English translation:** a faithful/trustworthy person / a liar
**Description of contrast:** A second agentive opposition in the character-evaluation domain, contrasting the trustworthy person with the liar specifically. Both are *m-* class 1 human nominals. *Mwongo* is derived from the *-ongo* stem (falsehood/deception) and is documented in P2a; *mwaminifu* is derived from *amini* (to trust/believe). The construction *mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo* (P2b) implicitly links the truthful person to the absence of lying, paralleling this opposition.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** kudanganywa
**Term B:** kudanganyika
**Transliteration:** kudanganywa / kudanganyika
**English translation:** to be deceived (by an agent) / to be taken in; to become deceived (no agent foregrounded)
**Description of contrast:** Both are derived from the same verb stem *danganya* but with different suffixes (*-wa* passive vs. *-ika* inchoative/stative). P4a (§4 and §8) and P4b document this as a semantically significant contrast: *kudanganywa* foregrounds an external agent (introduced by *na*); *kudanganyika* presents the subject as entering a state of deception without specifying an agent. P4b explicitly states they are "not interchangeable alternates but distinct constructions with different agent-foregrounding properties."
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** kuamini
**Term B:** kuaminiwa
**Transliteration:** kuamini / kuaminiwa
**English translation:** to believe/trust (someone or something) / to be trusted; to be found trustworthy
**Description of contrast:** The active form *kuamini* describes the act of placing trust or belief in an external object; the passive *kuaminiwa* describes the state of being the recipient of trust from others. P2a (Item 19, Derived Forms 3–4) and P1b (Root 3, Items 5–6) document both. The contrast is morphological (*-wa* passive suffix) and reflects a shift in the direction of the trust relationship: outward (active) vs. inward (passive).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** kuthibitisha
**Term B:** kuthibitishwa
**Transliteration:** kuthibitisha / kuthibitishwa
**English translation:** to confirm; to verify; to prove / to be confirmed; to be verified; to be proven
**Description of contrast:** The active causative *kuthibitisha* foregrounds the agent performing verification; the passive *kuthibitishwa* foregrounds the claim or document that has been verified. P2a (Item 14, Derived Forms 1–2) and P4a (§5 and §8) document both. The contrast is morphological (*-wa* passive suffix on the causative stem) and reflects the same agent-foregrounding distinction documented for *kudanganywa* / *kudanganyika* (Opposition 14), though here both forms are passive/causative rather than passive/inchoative.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** kuamini
**Term B:** kudanganya
**Transliteration:** kuamini / kudanganya
**English translation:** to believe, to trust / to deceive, to mislead
**Description of contrast:** A verbal opposition between the act of trusting/believing and the act of deceiving. *Kuamini* is documented in P2a as a core verbal form; *kudanganya* is documented in P1 and P2a. The construction *siwezi kumwamini tena* (P2b) describes the breakdown of trust, which is the interpersonal consequence of *kudanganya*. The two verbs occupy opposing positions in the trust-deception domain.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** kuaminika
**Term B:** kudanganyika
**Transliteration:** kuaminika / kudanganyika
**English translation:** to be trustworthy, to be credible / to be deceived, to fall for deception
**Description of contrast:** Both are stative forms with the *-ka*/*-ika* suffix (documented in P2a), but they occupy opposing positions: *kuaminika* describes the state of being worthy of trust, while *kudanganyika* describes the state of having been deceived or being susceptible to deception. The structural parallelism (same suffix, contrasting stems) makes this a morphological opposition.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** kudhihirika
**Term B:** kufichika
**Transliteration:** kudhihirika / kufichika
**English translation:** to become evident, to come to light / to be hideable, to remain hidden
**Description of contrast:** The stative/intransitive counterparts of the causative pair in Opposition 7. *Kudhihirika* (stative of *dhahiri*, documented in P2a) means to become manifest without an explicit agent; *kufichika* (stative of *kuficha*) means to be capable of being hidden or to remain concealed. The opposition is attested in the proverbial construction *ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele* (P2b), where *haufichiki* is the negative stative of *kuficha*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** kufanana na
**Term B:** kutofautiana
**Transliteration:** kufanana na / kutofautiana
**English translation:** to resemble, to be similar to / to differ from each other
**Description of contrast:** The verbal counterpart of the *fanana* / *tofauti* opposition, with the reciprocal suffix *-ana* on *kutofautiana* marking mutual difference. Both are documented in P2a as core derivations. The structural contrast is between a verb of similarity (*kufanana na*, requiring a comitative *na*-phrase) and a verb of mutual difference (*kutofautiana*, with the reciprocal *-ana* suffix encoding the mutual relation internally).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** Hii ni kweli.
**Term B:** Si kweli.
**Transliteration:** Hii ni kweli. / Si kweli.
**English translation:** "This is true." / "It is not true. / That is false."
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both the affirmative copular construction (*ni* + *kweli*) and its negative counterpart (*si* + *kweli*) as the primary means of propositional assertion and denial respectively (P4a §1–2, P4b). The contrast is between the invariant copula *ni* and its suppletive negative *si*. P1b (Root 1, Items 28–29) documents both forms; P4a (§1 and §2) and P4b describe them as fully parallel constructions. The same pattern extends to *ni sahihi* / *si sahihi*, *ni halisi* / *si halisi*, and *ni halali* / *si halali*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** Ni sahihi.
**Term B:** Si sahihi. / hii si sahihi
**Transliteration:** Ni sahihi. / Si sahihi.; hii si sahihi
**English translation:** "It is correct." / "It is incorrect. / That is not right. / This is not correct."
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both the affirmative and negative copular constructions with *sahihi* as predicate (P1b Root 3, P2b §6, P4a §1–2, P4b). P2b (Item 7, Construction 6) documents *hii si sahihi* as the standard construction for evaluating a statement, procedure, or document as incorrect or invalid. The contrast is the same *ni* / *si* opposition as in Opposition 21, applied to the correctness dimension. The opposition is attested in educational contexts (*jibu lako si sahihi — jaribu tena*) and in formal verification contexts (*taarifa hii imethibitishwa kuwa sahihi*).
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** pesa bandia
**Term B:** pesa za ghushi
**Transliteration:** pesa bandia / pesa za ghushi
**English translation:** "counterfeit money; fake currency" / "counterfeit money; fraudulent currency"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both expressions as referring to counterfeit currency (P2b §13 Construction 1, §14 Construction 4) and notes that *ghushi* "overlaps with *bandia* but carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or criminal fraud" (P1). ## Opposition 33

**Term A:** sahihi (correctness/accuracy sense)
**Term B:** sahihi (signature sense)
**Transliteration:** sahihi / sahihi
**English translation:** "correct; accurate; right" / "signature"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents these as two distinct usage clusters of the same form (P3 Groups 3 and 7, P1b Root 3 peripheral extensions: "The use of *sahihi* to mean 'signature' represents a semantic extension rather than a morphological derivation"). P1b (Step 3) and P2a document that *sahihi* has developed a distinct lexical meaning — "signature" — in administrative and legal contexts, where it refers to an authenticating mark rather than to the property of correctness. P3 places the two uses in different groups (Group 4 for correctness; Group 5 for document authentication). The contrast is between the evaluative-correctness use and the document-authentication use of the same lexical item.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 37

**Term A:** kwa kweli
**Term B:** kwa uaminifu
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli / kwa uaminifu
**English translation:** "truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest" / "faithfully; trustworthily; with integrity; honestly"
**Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both adverbial framing constructions (P1b Root 1, P2b §11 Construction 4, P4a §8). The CONTEXT does not explicitly identify these as contrasting, but documents them as parallel constructions with distinct semantic orientations within the same pattern.
