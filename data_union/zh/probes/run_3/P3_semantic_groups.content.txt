# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from observed patterns in the P1 and P2 data: which items co-occur in the same contexts, which items are contrasted with each other, which items share typical collocates, and which items appear in the same institutional registers. No external framework has been imposed. Items that do not fit clearly into any group are listed at the end. Some items appear in more than one group; this is noted explicitly.

---

## GROUP 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that something is genuine rather than counterfeit or fabricated

**Label:** Genuineness vs. fakeness in objects, goods, and documents

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the central question is whether a physical object, document, credential, or product is what it presents itself as being — as opposed to being a forgery, imitation, or counterfeit. They appear most densely in commercial, legal, and authentication contexts. The contrast between 真 and 假/伪 is the organizing axis of this group, but the items are grouped here because of their shared deployment in verification and anti-counterfeiting discourse rather than because of any assumed conceptual unity.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真 (zhēn) — "genuine, real" — the positive pole in authenticity evaluation of objects
- 假 (jiǎ) — "fake, counterfeit" — the negative pole
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — "genuine or fake" — frames the authentication question as a binary
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — "to distinguish genuine from fake" — the standard verb phrase for authentication activity
- 鉴定真伪 (jiàndìng zhēnwěi) — "to authenticate" — professional appraisal context
- 难辨真伪 / 真伪难辨 (nán biàn zhēnwěi / zhēnwěi nán biàn) — "hard to tell genuine from fake"
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — "true or false, genuine or fake" — everyday version of 真伪
- 是真是假 (shì zhēn shì jiǎ) — "is it real or fake?" — interrogative frame
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — "to pass off fakes as genuine" — describes the act of deceptive substitution
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — "counterfeit, to impersonate" — commercial and legal term
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎmào wěiliè) — "counterfeit and substandard goods" — fixed regulatory category
- 假冒伪劣产品 (jiǎmào wěiliè chǎnpǐn) — "fake and shoddy products" — canonical noun phrase in consumer law
- 伪造 (wěizào) — "to forge, to fabricate" — deliberate creation of false documents or evidence
- 伪造文件 (wěizào wénjiàn) — "to forge documents"
- 伪造证据 (wěizào zhèngjù) — "to fabricate evidence"
- 伪造者 (wěizàozhě) — "forger, counterfeiter"
- 打假 (dǎ jiǎ) — "to crack down on counterfeits" — enforcement and regulatory context
- 辨真假 (biàn zhēnjiǎ) — "to tell real from fake" — colloquial version of 辨别真伪
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) — "genuine goods at fair prices; the real thing" — originally commercial, now general
- 货真价实的好东西 (huò zhēn jià shí de hǎo dōngxi) — attributive construction asserting authenticity
- 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) — "solid evidence, concrete proof" — evidentiary authenticity
- 确凿 (quèzáo) — "conclusive, irrefutable" — used almost exclusively with 证据 (evidence)
- 证据确凿 (zhèngjù quèzáo) — "the evidence is conclusive"
- 有确凿证据证明 (yǒu quèzáo zhèngjù zhèngmíng) — "there is conclusive evidence proving"

**Notes:** 真正 (zhēnzhèng) and 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) overlap with this group but are more typically used in evaluating persons, titles, and categories rather than physical objects or documents; see Group 2. 虚假 (xūjiǎ) overlaps with this group but is more strongly associated with statements and information than with physical goods; see Group 3.

## GROUP 2: Expressions used to evaluate whether something or someone genuinely merits a label, title, or category

**Label:** Correspondence between name/label and actual nature

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether a person, institution, or thing actually lives up to the description, title, or reputation applied to it. They appear in evaluative, commendatory, and critical contexts — praising something as genuinely what it claims to be, or criticizing a mismatch between designation and reality. The classical philosophical pairing of 名 (name) and 实 (reality/substance) is the background of several items in this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真正 (zhēnzhèng) — "genuine, real (emphatic)" — asserts that something fully meets the criteria of a category
- 真正意义上的 (zhēnzhèng yìyì shàng de) — "in the true sense of the word" — distinguishes genuine instances from nominal ones
- 算不上真正的 (suàn bu shàng zhēnzhèng de) — "does not qualify as a real X" — denial of category membership
- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — "living up to one's name" — commendatory assertion of correspondence
- 名副其实的专家 (míng fù qí shí de zhuānjiā) — "a genuine expert" — attributive use
- 可以说是名副其实 (kěyǐ shuō shì míng fù qí shí) — "one can say it truly lives up to its name"
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — "not living up to one's name" — critical assertion of mismatch
- 名不副实的头衔 (míng bù fù shí de tóuxián) — "an undeserved title"
- 名实 (míngshí) — "name and reality" — the classical analytical pairing
- 名实相符 (míng shí xiāngfú) — "name and reality correspond"
- 相符 (xiāngfú) — "to correspond, to match" — general correspondence between two things
- 与事实相符 (yǔ shìshí xiāngfú) — "consistent with the facts"
- 本质 (běnzhì) — "essence, fundamental nature" — what something actually is beneath its label
- 实质 (shízhì) — "substance, real nature" — operative content as opposed to formal character
- 实质上 (shízhì shàng) — "in substance, essentially"
- 触及实质 (chùjí shízhì) — "to get to the substance, to address the real issue"
- 揭示本质 (jiēshì běnzhì) — "to reveal the essence"
- 看清本质 (kànqīng běnzhì) — "to see clearly the true nature"
- 从本质上说 (cóng běnzhì shàng shuō) — "essentially speaking, fundamentally"
- 言过其实 (yán guò qí shí) — "to exaggerate, to say more than the facts warrant" — mismatch between claim and reality

**Notes:** 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) overlaps with this group (asserting that something is genuinely what it claims to be) but is more strongly associated with commercial and object-authenticity contexts (Group 1). 真实 (zhēnshí) overlaps with this group in some uses but is more typically associated with the accuracy of representations and reports (Group 3).

## GROUP 3: Expressions used in evaluating the accuracy or truthfulness of statements, reports, and information

**Label:** Accuracy and truthfulness of statements and representations

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether a statement, report, account, or piece of information accurately reflects what is actually the case. They appear most densely in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts, and they are typically applied to propositions, claims, and representations rather than to physical objects. Several items in this group are specifically associated with the institutional demand for accurate reporting.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真实 (zhēnshí) — "truthful, factual, accurate" — the standard evaluative term for accurate representation
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — "truthfulness, factual accuracy, veracity" — nominalized property
- 真实报道 (zhēnshí bàodào) — "truthful reporting" — journalistic context
- 还原真实 (huányuán zhēnshí) — "to restore the truth, to recover the true picture"
- 贴近真实 (tiējìn zhēnshí) — "to be close to reality, to approach the truth"
- 反映真实情况 (fǎnyìng zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "to reflect the true situation"
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — "false, fraudulent, fictitious" — formal/legal term for false statements and information
- 虚假性 (xūjiǎxìng) — "falseness, fraudulence"
- 散布虚假信息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to spread false information"
- 发布虚假广告 (fābù xūjiǎ guǎnggào) — "to publish false advertising"
- 制造虚假繁荣 (zhìzào xūjiǎ fánróng) — "to manufacture a false prosperity"
- 失实 (shīshí) — "inaccurate, departing from the facts" — without necessarily implying deliberate deception
- 报道失实 (bàodào shīshí) — "the report is inaccurate"
- 严重失实 (yánzhòng shīshí) — "seriously inaccurate"
- 属实 (shǔshí) — "confirmed as factual, verified" — formal assertion that a claim is true
- 经查属实 (jīng chá shǔshí) — "verified upon investigation"
- 情况属实 (qíngkuàng shǔshí) — "the situation is confirmed as factual"
- 如实 (rúshí) — "truthfully, as things actually are" — adverbial, describing manner of reporting
- 如实陈述 (rúshí chénshù) — "to state truthfully"
- 如实反映 (rúshí fǎnyìng) — "to accurately reflect"
- 如实回答 (rúshí huídá) — "to answer truthfully"
- 正确 (zhèngquè) — "correct, right, accurate" — evaluates conformity to a standard
- 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) — "correctness, accuracy"
- 错误 (cuòwù) — "wrong, erroneous, mistaken"
- 错误性 (cuòwùxìng) — "erroneousness"
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — "accurate, precise, exact" — emphasizes closeness to a target value
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — "accuracy, precision"
- 表述准确 (biǎoshù zhǔnquè) — "the formulation is accurate"
- 把握准确 (bǎwò zhǔnquè) — "to grasp accurately"
- 事实 (shìshí) — "fact, the facts" — the standard term for factual reality
- 事实上 (shìshí shàng) — "in fact, as a matter of fact"
- 尊重事实 (zūnzhòng shìshí) — "to respect the facts"
- 歪曲事实 (wāiqū shìshí) — "to distort the facts"
- 捏造事实 (niēzào shìshí) — "to fabricate facts"
- 以事实为依据 (yǐ shìshí wéi yījù) — "to take facts as the basis"
- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — "the truth of a matter, the real situation" — what lies behind appearances
- 真话 (zhēnhuà) — "truthful words, honest speech"
- 实话 (shíhuà) — "honest words, straight talk"
- 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) — "to tell the truth"
- 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō) — "to tell it like it is"
- 真有其事 (zhēn yǒu qí shì) — "it really happened, it's a true story"
- 这是真的 (zhè shì zhēn de) — "this is true/real"
- 不是真的 (bù shì zhēn de) — "it's not true/real"
- 千真万确 (qiān zhēn wàn què) — "absolutely true, beyond all doubt"
- 半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ) — "half true and half false"
- 真真假假 (zhēn zhēn jiǎ jiǎ) — "a mixture of truth and falsehood"
- 失真 (shīzhēn) — "distortion, loss of fidelity" — representation departs from original
- 逼真 (bīzhēn) — "lifelike, true to life" — representation closely resembles its subject
- 逼真传神 (bīzhēn chuánshén) — "realistic and spirit-capturing"
- 模仿得逼真 (mófǎng de bīzhēn) — "imitated so realistically"
- 写真 (xiězhēn) — "realistic depiction; portrait photography" — fidelity of representation
- 写实 (xiěshí) — "realistic (in art or literature)"
- 写实主义 (xiěshí zhǔyì) — "realism as an artistic doctrine"
- 写实性 (xiěshíxìng) — "the quality of being realistic"
- 实录 (shílù) — "faithful record, veritable records" — historiographical fidelity

**Notes:** 失真 and 逼真 are primarily used in aesthetic and technical contexts (audio fidelity, image reproduction, artistic representation) and could be considered a sub-cluster within this group. 真相 is particularly associated with the idea that the truth is hidden and must be revealed, which distinguishes it from 事实 (which simply denotes factual content) and 真实 (which evaluates accuracy of representation). 正确 and 准确 are included here but also overlap with Group 4 (correctness of answers and methods), where the evaluative standard is conformity to a rule or target rather than correspondence to reality.

## GROUP 4: Expressions used in verification and confirmation processes

**Label:** Checking, verifying, and confirming claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where a claim, piece of information, or allegation is subjected to a formal or informal process of checking, and where the outcome is an assertion that the claim has (or has not) been confirmed. They appear most densely in journalistic, administrative, and legal contexts. The shared feature is the procedural or epistemic act of verification rather than the content of what is verified.

**Representative expressions:**

- 核实 (héshí) — "to verify, to confirm, to check the facts"
- 经核实 (jīng héshí) — "upon verification, having been verified"
- 无法核实 (wúfǎ héshí) — "cannot be verified"
- 证实 (zhèngshí) — "to confirm, to verify, to prove to be true"
- 经调查证实 (jīng diàochá zhèngshí) — "confirmed by investigation"
- 得到证实 (dédào zhèngshí) — "to be confirmed"
- 查实 (cháshí) — "to investigate and confirm"
- 坐实 (zuòshí) — "to nail down a fact, to establish as confirmed" — colloquial
- 属实 (shǔshí) — "confirmed as factual" — formal outcome of verification
- 经查属实 (jīng chá shǔshí) — "verified upon investigation"
- 实证 (shízhèng) — "empirical evidence; to verify empirically"
- 可靠 (kěkào) — "reliable, dependable" — evaluates sources and information as worthy of trust
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) — "reliability, dependability"
- 来源可靠 (láiyuán kěkào) — "the source is reliable"
- 消息可靠 (xiāoxi kěkào) — "the information is reliable"
- 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) — "solid evidence, concrete proof"
- 确凿 (quèzáo) — "conclusive, irrefutable" — evaluates evidence as beyond doubt
- 证据确凿 (zhèngjù quèzáo) — "the evidence is conclusive"
- 有确凿证据证明 (yǒu quèzáo zhèngjù zhèngmíng) — "there is conclusive evidence proving"
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — "to distinguish genuine from fake" — authentication as verification
- 鉴定真伪 (jiàndìng zhēnwěi) — "to authenticate" — professional verification

**Notes:** This group overlaps substantially with Group 1 (authentication of objects) and Group 3 (accuracy of statements). The items are grouped here because they share a focus on the *process* of checking and the *outcome* of confirmation, rather than on the content being evaluated. 属实 appears in both Group 3 and this group because it functions both as a description of a statement's truth-value and as the formal outcome of a verification procedure.

## GROUP 5: Expressions used in contexts of deliberate deception and falsification

**Label:** Deliberate deception, fraud, and falsification

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the focus is on an agent who intentionally misleads others or creates false appearances. They appear in legal, moral, and regulatory contexts, and they typically imply agency and intent in a way that distinguishes them from expressions that merely describe inaccuracy (such as 失实). Several items in this group are associated with specific legal offenses or administrative prohibitions.

**Representative expressions:**

- 欺骗 (qīpiàn) — "to deceive, to cheat, to defraud"
- 欺骗性 (qīpiànxìng) — "deceptiveness"
- 欺骗者 (qīpiànzhě) — "deceiver, cheat"
- 欺骗消费者 (qīpiàn xiāofèizhě) — "to deceive consumers"
- 受到欺骗 (shòudào qīpiàn) — "to be deceived"
- 不得欺骗 (bùdé qīpiàn) — "must not deceive" — prohibition frame
- 伪造 (wěizào) — "to forge, to fabricate"
- 伪造文件 (wěizào wénjiàn) — "to forge documents"
- 伪造证据 (wěizào zhèngjù) — "to fabricate evidence"
- 伪造者 (wěizàozhě) — "forger, counterfeiter"
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — "false, fraudulent, fictitious" — formal/legal characterization of deceptive content
- 散布虚假信息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to spread false information"
- 发布虚假广告 (fābù xūjiǎ guǎnggào) — "to publish false advertising"
- 弄虚作假 (nòng xū zuò jiǎ) — "to engage in fraud and falsification"
- 严禁弄虚作假 (yánjìn nòng xū zuò jiǎ) — "strictly prohibit falsification"
- 作假 (zuòjiǎ) — "to falsify, to cheat"
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — "counterfeit, to impersonate"
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎmào wěiliè) — "counterfeit and substandard"
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — "to pass off fakes as genuine"
- 捏造事实 (niēzào shìshí) — "to fabricate facts"
- 歪曲事实 (wāiqū shìshí) — "to distort the facts"
- 假象 (jiǎxiàng) — "false appearance, misleading phenomenon"
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — "false pretense, feigned intention"
- 虚情假意 (xū qíng jiǎ yì) — "false sentiments and feigned intentions"
- 假仁假义 (jiǎ rén jiǎ yì) — "false benevolence and feigned righteousness"
- 假公济私 (jiǎ gōng jì sī) — "to use public office for private gain"
- 借职务之便假公济私 (jiè zhíwù zhī biàn jiǎ gōng jì sī) — "to exploit one's position for private gain"
- 装假 (zhuāng jiǎ) — "to put on a false front, to fake it"

**Notes:** 假话 (jiǎhuà) — "lies, false statements" — overlaps with this group and with Group 3. It is listed under Group 3 because it describes the content of a statement rather than the act of deception, but it is closely associated with the deception context. 虚假 appears in both this group and Group 3; it is included here because of its strong association with deliberate fraud in legal and regulatory discourse, and in Group 3 because it also describes the property of being false (without necessarily implying intent).

## GROUP 6: Expressions used in moral evaluation of persons and conduct

**Label:** Moral evaluation of honesty, sincerity, and integrity in persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person's character or conduct as honest, sincere, or their opposites. They appear in moral, educational, and interpersonal contexts, and they are typically applied to persons or to the manner of a person's conduct rather than to propositions or objects. The evaluative standard is a moral one (is this person honest? sincere? trustworthy?) rather than a purely epistemic one (is this statement accurate?).

**Representative expressions:**

- 诚实 (chéngshí) — "honest, truthful, sincere"
- 诚实性 (chéngshíxìng) — "honesty, the quality of being truthful"
- 诚实守信 (chéngshí shǒuxìn) — "honest and trustworthy" — moral principle pairing
- 为人诚实 (wéirén chéngshí) — "to be an honest person"
- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — "sincere, genuine, heartfelt"
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — "sincere, wholehearted, genuine in feeling"
- 真话 (zhēnhuà) — "truthful words, honest speech" — overlaps with Group 3
- 说真的 (shuō zhēn de) — "to be honest, honestly speaking"
- 道德 (dàodé) — "morality, ethics, moral conduct"
- 道德化 (dàodéhuà) — "to moralize; moralization"
- 道德上说不过去 (dàodé shàng shuō bu guòqù) — "morally indefensible"
- 有违道德 (yǒu wéi dàodé) — "contrary to morality"
- 虚情假意 (xū qíng jiǎ yì) — "false sentiments and feigned intentions" — negative pole
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — "false pretense, feigned intention" — negative pole
- 假仁假义 (jiǎ rén jiǎ yì) — "false benevolence and feigned righteousness" — negative pole
- 纯真 (chúnzhēn) — "pure and genuine, innocent, unspoiled"
- 天真 (tiānzhēn) — "naive, innocent, naturally genuine"
- 真善美 (zhēn shàn měi) — "truth, goodness, and beauty" — philosophical triad of values
- 认真 (rènzhēn) — "serious, earnest, conscientious"
- 老实 (lǎoshí) — "honest, straightforward, well-behaved"
- 朴实 (pǔshí) — "plain, simple, unpretentious, down-to-earth"
- 踏实 (tāshí) — "steady, grounded, reliable; at ease"

**Notes:** 认真 (rènzhēn), 老实 (lǎoshí), 朴实 (pǔshí), and 踏实 (tāshí) are included here because they appear in character evaluations alongside 诚实 and 真诚, but their connection to the truth/falsity domain is indirect (as noted in P1b Step 3). They are included in this group rather than left ungrouped because their usage pattern — evaluating a person's manner of engagement or character — is shared with the other items here. 天真 (tiānzhēn) is similarly included with the caveat that it can carry a critical connotation (naivety) in adult contexts.

## GROUP 7: Expressions used in contexts of empirical inquiry, practical reasoning, and policy implementation

**Label:** Empirical grounding, practical reality, and implementation

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the concern is with engaging with actual conditions rather than with ideology, theory, or wishful thinking, and with ensuring that plans and policies are actually carried out. They appear most densely in political, administrative, and analytical discourse. Several items in this group are associated with a specific Chinese political-epistemological tradition of empirical, non-dogmatic reasoning.

**Representative expressions:**

- 实事求是 (shíshì qiúshì) — "to seek truth from facts; to be realistic and empirical"
- 坚持实事求是 (jiānchí shíshì qiúshì) — "to uphold the principle of seeking truth from facts"
- 实事求是地说 (shíshì qiúshì de shuō) — "to say it as it actually is"
- 现实 (xiànshí) — "reality, the real world; realistic, practical"
- 现实性 (xiànshíxìng) — "the quality of being realistic or feasible"
- 面对现实 (miànduì xiànshí) — "to face reality"
- 脱离现实 (tuōlí xiànshí) — "divorced from reality"
- 实际 (shíjì) — "actual, practical, real; in practice"
- 实际性 (shíjìxìng) — "practicality, the quality of being actual"
- 从实际出发 (cóng shíjì chūfā) — "to proceed from actual conditions"
- 脱离实际 (tuōlí shíjì) — "out of touch with actual conditions"
- 务实 (wùshí) — "pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth"
- 务实性 (wùshíxìng) — "pragmatism, the quality of being practical"
- 务实的态度 (wùshí de tàidù) — "a pragmatic attitude"
- 实用 (shíyòng) — "practical, functional, utilitarian"
- 实用性 (shíyòngxìng) — "practicality, utility"
- 实用主义 (shíyòng zhǔyì) — "pragmatism (as a philosophical doctrine)"
- 落实 (luòshí) — "to implement, to put into practice; to verify"
- 落实政策 (luòshí zhèngcè) — "to implement policy"
- 抓落实 (zhuā luòshí) — "to ensure implementation"
- 扎实 (zhāshí) — "solid, thorough, well-grounded (of skills or work)"
- 充实 (chōngshí) — "to enrich, to make more substantial; rich, full"
- 实力 (shílì) — "actual strength, real capability"
- 实证 (shízhèng) — "empirical evidence; positivism"

**Notes:** This group is organized around the shared usage pattern of contrasting actual conditions and genuine capability with nominal, theoretical, or merely formal ones. 实用主义 is included because it is the standard translation of philosophical pragmatism and is used in the same discourse contexts as 务实 and 实际. 扎实, 充实, and 踏实 appear here and in Group 6; they are included in both because they describe both a quality of work/knowledge (Group 7) and a quality of character (Group 6).

## GROUP 8: Expressions used in legal and regulatory contexts to assert or deny lawfulness

**Label:** Legal compliance and violation

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in legal, administrative, and regulatory contexts to evaluate whether conduct, documents, or status conform to or violate legal norms. They are distinguished from the broader truth/accuracy cluster by their specific reference to law (法) as the evaluative standard.

**Representative expressions:**

- 合法 (héfǎ) — "lawful, legal, legitimate"
- 合法性 (héfǎxìng) — "legality, legitimacy, lawfulness"
- 合法化 (héfǎhuà) — "to legalize, to legitimize"
- 合法权益 (héfǎ quányì) — "lawful rights and interests"
- 依法合规 (yīfǎ héguī) — "in accordance with law and regulations"
- 违法 (wéifǎ) — "illegal, unlawful, in violation of the law"
- 违法性 (wéifǎxìng) — "illegality, unlawfulness"
- 违法行为 (wéifǎ xíngwéi) — "illegal conduct"
- 依法查处违法行为 (yīfǎ cháchǔ wéifǎ xíngwéi) — "to handle illegal conduct according to law"

**Notes:** This group is relatively self-contained. Several items from Group 5 (伪造, 假冒伪劣, 弄虚作假) also appear in legal contexts, but they are grouped under deliberate deception because their primary evaluative dimension is the falsity/deception axis rather than the legal/illegal axis. 道德 (Group 6) sometimes appears alongside 合法/违法 in evaluative discourse, but the two groups are kept separate because the evaluative standards (moral norms vs. legal norms) are consistently distinguished in Chinese usage.

## GROUP 9: Expressions used in hypothetical and conditional reasoning

**Label:** Hypothetical conditions and stipulated assumptions

**Definition:** These expressions use the character 假 (jiǎ) not to assert that something is false or counterfeit, but to introduce a stipulated or imagined condition. They appear in logical, scientific, and everyday reasoning contexts. They are grouped together here because they share a usage pattern that is distinct from all other groups — the 假 root functions as a marker of hypotheticality rather than of falsity or inauthenticity.

**Representative expressions:**

- 假设 (jiǎshè) — "hypothesis, assumption; to hypothesize"
- 假设性 (jiǎshèxìng) — "hypothetical nature, the quality of being hypothetical"
- 假如 (jiǎrú) — "if, supposing that"
- 假若 (jiǎruò) — "if, supposing that" (slightly more literary)
- 假定 (jiǎdìng) — "to assume, to suppose; assumption"

**Notes:** These items are grouped together primarily because they are distinguished from the rest of the 假 cluster by their non-falsity function. They do not fit naturally into any of the other groups. The relationship between this hypothetical use of 假 and its falsity/counterfeit use is noted in P1b Step 3 but is not interpreted here beyond the usage observation.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

The following items from P1 and P2 overlap across multiple groups in ways that cannot be resolved by usage patterns alone, or occupy positions that are genuinely intermediate.

---

**真话 (zhēnhuà) and 实话 (shíhuà):**
Both appear in Group 3 (accuracy of statements) and Group 6 (moral evaluation of persons). In practice, 真话 and 实话 are used interchangeably in many contexts ("说真话/说实话" — to tell the truth), but 实话 is slightly more associated with candid, unvarnished communication of facts (Group 3), while 真话 carries a slightly stronger moral valence of speaking from genuine conviction (Group 6). The distinction is not consistent enough to assign them to separate groups.

---

**假话 (jiǎhuà):**
Overlaps between Group 3 (false statements) and Group 5 (deliberate deception). 假话 describes the content of a false statement (Group 3) but is typically used in contexts where deliberate untruth is implied (Group 5). It is not listed as a primary item in either group above because its placement depends on context.

---

**虚假 (xūjiǎ):**
Appears in both Group 3 (false statements and information) and Group 5 (deliberate deception). In legal and regulatory discourse, 虚假 consistently implies deliberate fraud; in some analytical contexts it can describe falseness without implying intent. Both placements are supported by usage.

---

**真理 (zhēnlǐ):**
Does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. 真理 is used in philosophical, political, and ideological discourse to refer to fundamental or universal truth as a principle ("真理标准" — the criterion of truth). This usage pattern is distinct from the accuracy-of-statements cluster (Group 3), the genuineness-of-objects cluster (Group 1), and the moral-evaluation cluster (Group 6). It is not grouped here because no other items in P1 or P2 share its specific usage pattern closely enough to form a group.

---

**真善美 (zhēn shàn měi):**
Appears in Group 6 (moral evaluation) but its usage in philosophical and aesthetic discourse — as a triad of values — is not fully captured by that group. It is included in Group 6 as the closest fit, but it occupies a somewhat distinct position as a value-theoretical expression.

---

**实质性 (shízhìxìng):**
As noted in P2a, this form is attested primarily as an attributive adjective in fixed collocations ("实质性进展" — substantive progress) rather than as a full nominalization. It does not fit cleanly into any group and is noted here as an item whose usage pattern is too restricted to assign.

---

**弄假成真 (nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn):**
This expression describes a situation where a pretense becomes reality — a process rather than a static evaluation. It overlaps with Group 1 (genuineness) and Group 5 (deception) but does not fit either fully, since it describes an unintended outcome rather than a deliberate act of deception or an evaluation of authenticity. It is not assigned to any group.

---

**真人 (zhēnrén):**
In everyday usage ("真人秀" — reality show; "真人版" — live-action version), 真人 belongs loosely to Group 1 or Group 3 (asserting that something involves real persons rather than fictional or animated ones). In Daoist usage, it refers to a spiritually perfected person, which is a distinct domain not represented elsewhere in the P1/P2 data. Neither usage is central enough to the groups above to assign it clearly.

---

**传真 (chuánzhēn):**
A technical term for fax/facsimile. The 真 root reflects the original concept of transmitting a true copy, but in contemporary usage 传真 is a lexicalized technical term with no active connection to the truth/authenticity domain. It is not assigned to any group.

---

**真菌 (zhēnjūn) and 真空 (zhēnkōng):**
Both are technical/scientific terms where 真 functions as a taxonomic or physical classifier rather than as an epistemic or authenticity marker. They are not assigned to any group.

---

**写真 (xiězhēn):**
In contemporary usage, primarily a genre label for portrait photography. Its connection to the fidelity-of-representation cluster (Group 3) is etymological rather than active in current usage. Not assigned to any group.

---

**假期 (jiàqī), 请假 (qǐngjià), 放假 (fàngjià):**
These items use the jià pronunciation and the vacation/leave meaning, which is functionally separate from all other groups in this analysis. They are not assigned to any group.

---

**假借 (jiǎjiè) in the linguistic sense:**
The use of 假借 to refer to phonetic loan characters in classical Chinese writing system analysis is a specialized technical usage with no close neighbors in the P1/P2 data. Not assigned to any group.

---

**原真性 (yuánzhēnxìng):**
A specialized term in cultural heritage and conservation discourse. While it belongs semantically to the genuineness cluster, its usage is restricted to a specific institutional domain (UNESCO frameworks, museum studies) that is not represented by any other item in the P1/P2 data. It is noted as a marginal item without group assignment.

---

**名实 (míngshí):**
Assigned to Group 2 (correspondence between name and reality), but its usage in classical philosophical discourse (School of Names, 名家) is more specialized than the other items in that group. It is included in Group 2 as the closest fit.

---

- **真菌 (zhēnjūn)** — biological taxonomic term
- **真空 (zhēnkōng)** — physical/technical term (vacuum)
- **传真 (chuánzhēn)** — technical term (fax)
- **写真 (xiězhēn)** — contemporary genre label (portrait photography)
- **假期 (jiàqī), 请假 (qǐngjià), 放假 (fàngjià)** — vacation/leave domain
- **假借 (jiǎjiè)** in the linguistic sense — writing system analysis
- **原真性 (yuánzhēnxìng)** — specialized heritage conservation term
