## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 VERIFICATION as CAUSATION
Swahili does not maintain a distinct VERIFICATION construction separate from CAUSATION. The causative suffix *-ish-* serves both functions:
- **Factual causation**: *sahihisha* "make correct/verify", *hakikisha* "make certain/ensure"
- **Verification**: *thibitisha* "confirm/verify", *sahihisha* "verify/correct"

The same morphological pattern (ROOT + -ish- + -a) covers both "making something true" and "verifying that something is true." The passive forms (*thibitishwa*, *sahihishwa*) are particularly common in verification contexts, as shown in Construction 22.

Construction 24 (instrumental *kwa* + evidence term) provides an additional verification strategy by specifying the means of confirmation, but this is a manner specification rather than a dedicated verification operator.

### 2.2 DISCONFIRMATION as NEGATION + CAUSATION
Swahili lacks a dedicated DISCONFIRMATION construction. Instead, it employs:
- **Simple negation** (Construction 2): *si kweli* "is not true"
- **Negated causative/verification**: Implied forms like *haijathibitishwa* "has not been confirmed"

The language does not have a specific morphological or syntactic pattern meaning "prove false" or "disconfirm" distinct from "negate" or "fail to verify."

### 2.3 CAUSATION (character) as BECOME
For character attribution (making someone a liar, making someone truthful), Swahili strongly prefers the BECOME construction over causative morphology:
- **Attested**: *Amekuwa mwongo* "He/she has become a liar" (Construction 4)
- **Not attested**: A causative form meaning "make (someone) a liar"

This represents a functional gap where BECOME substitutes for character-oriented CAUSATION. The causative suffix *-ish-* applies productively to factual/state changes but not to character transformation.

### 2.4 REVELATION Gap
No construction in P4a or P4b corresponds to REVELATION (becoming known, coming to light, being discovered). The language has:
- **BECOME constructions** for state change (*kamilika* "become complete")
- **Passive constructions** for actions done to truth-terms (*thibitishwa* "be confirmed")
- **No dedicated pattern** for truth emerging or being revealed

This appears to be a structural gap. While circumlocution is possible (e.g., *ukweli umejulikana* "the truth has become known" using the stative form of *jua* "know"), this is not a conventionalized construction in the truth domain.

### 2.5 Negation Strategy: Analytic
Swahili negation is consistently analytic in the truth domain:
- **Copular negation**: Separate negative copula *si* (Construction 2)
- **Verbal negation**: Negative prefixes on verbs (*ha-*, *si-*) but not incorporated into truth-terms
- **Privative**: Separate preposition *bila* (Construction 16)

No morphological negation attaches directly to truth-terms themselves (no *un-true* equivalent).

### 2.6 Asymmetries
**Truth vs. Falsity**: The constructions apply symmetrically to both positive truth-terms (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *ukweli*) and negative ones (*uongo*, *bandia*). No structural asymmetry observed.

**Positive vs. Negative Forms**: All major constructions (attribution, adnominal, causative, nominalization) work equally in affirmative and negative polarity.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
- **REVELATION**: No construction for "X becomes known/revealed/discovered." The language lacks a dedicated pattern for truth emerging or coming to light. Circumlocution possible but not conventionalized.

### 3.2 Functional Gaps (Substitution Required)
- **CAUSATION (character)**: No causative construction for character transformation ("make someone a liar"). **SUBSTITUTED BY BECOME** (*kuwa mwongo*).
- **VERIFICATION**: No distinct verification construction. **SUBSTITUTED BY CAUSATION** (causative suffix *-ish-* serves both functions).
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated disconfirmation construction. **SUBSTITUTED BY NEGATION + CAUSATION** (negative copula or negated verification forms).

### 3.3 Lexical Gaps
None identified. The language has rich lexical resources for truth-domain concepts (multiple terms for truth, falsity, correctness, genuineness, etc.).

### 3.4 Conceptual Gaps
None identified. All truth-domain concepts appear expressible, though some require substitution or circumlocution.

### 3.5 Asymmetric Gaps
None identified. Constructions apply symmetrically across:
- Truth vs. falsity terms
- Positive vs. negative polarity
- Abstract vs. concrete reference

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### 4.1 Fully Available
- **ATTR**: Copular construction (ni/si + TRUTH-TERM)
- **ADNOM**: Associative and direct modification patterns
- **BECOME**: Stative extension (-ik-) and periphrastic (*kuwa*) strategies
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Causative suffix (-ish-/-sh-)
- **NEGATION**: Analytic negation (negative copula *si*, privative *bila*)
- **NOMINALIZATION**: Abstract (u- prefix) and agentive (m-/wa- prefix) patterns

### 4.2 Available with Substitution
- **VERIFICATION**: Expressed through CAUSATION constructions (causative suffix *-ish-* in verification sense: *thibitisha*, *hakikisha*, *sahihisha*)
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Expressed through NEGATION + CAUSATION (negative copula or negated causative/verification forms)
- **CAUSATION (character)**: Expressed through BECOME construction (*kuwa* + character term)

### 4.3 Partially Available
None. All expressible operators have clear constructional realizations, even if through substitution.

### 4.4 Missing Entirely
- **REVELATION**: No conventionalized construction for truth emerging, becoming known, or being discovered. Circumlocution possible (*ukweli umejulikana* "the truth has become known") but not a standard truth-domain construction pattern.

## 5. Additional Observations

### 5.1 Multi-Functional Constructions
Several constructions serve multiple CLCA operators:

**Construction 5 (Causative)** serves:
- CAUSATION (factual): *sahihisha* "make correct"
- VERIFICATION: *thibitisha* "confirm/verify"

**Construction 2 (Negative Copular)** serves:
- NEGATION: *si kweli* "is not true"
- DISCONFIRMATION (partial): *si sahihi* "is not correct"

**Construction 4 (BECOME)** serves:
- BECOME: *kamilika* "become complete"
- CAUSATION (character): *kuwa mwongo* "become a liar"

### 5.2 Construction Density
Swahili shows high construction density in certain functional areas:
- **Manner specification**: Multiple constructions (10, 24) for expressing how truth is told/verified
- **Intensification**: Multiple strategies (9, 14, 18) for emphasizing truth-value
- **Domain specification**: Productive pattern (19) for distinguishing truth types

These do not map to canonical CLCA operators but represent language-specific elaborations of the truth domain.

### 5.3 Morphological Productivity
The causative suffix *-ish-* shows exceptional productivity across the truth domain, creating forms for:
- Correction: *sahihisha*
- Verification: *hakikisha*, *thibitisha*
- Completion: *kamilisha*
- Manifestation: *dhihirisha*
- Clarification: *safisha* (metaphorical extension)

This single morphological pattern carries much of the functional load for both CAUSATION and VERIFICATION operators.
