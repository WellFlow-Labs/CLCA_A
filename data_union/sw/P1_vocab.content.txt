# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Swahili (sw)
# Layer: P1_vocab

---

**Note on script and transliteration:** Swahili is standardly written in Latin script; no separate transliteration column is required. Morphological breakdowns are provided where relevant.

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *-kweli*; functions as noun and adverb. Etymology debated; possibly from Arabic *qawl* ("speech/saying") or via other borrowing pathway.
**Typical English translations:** truth, true, truly, really, indeed
**Usage:** Used to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality or fact, or to intensify an assertion. Functions both as a noun (*kweli ni kwamba…* "the truth is that…") and as an adverb or intensifier. Appears in legal, moral, religious, and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in derivation: *ukweli* (the truth, as abstract noun). Attested in statements like *hii ni kweli* ("this is true").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ukweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (noun class 11/14 prefix, abstract) + *kweli*; abstract nominalization of *kweli*
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity, accuracy, fact
**Usage:** The nominalized abstract form, used when referring to truth as a property or concept. Used in philosophical, legal, and moral discourse (*ukweli wa jambo*, "the truth of a matter"). Contrasts with *uongo* (falsehood). Highly productive as a noun.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** uongo
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *-ongo* (root associated with falsehood, deception)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, untruth
**Usage:** The standard term for a false or deceptive statement, and for the quality of being false. Used in moral condemnation of dishonesty and in legal contexts regarding false testimony (*kusema uongo*, "to tell a lie"). Direct antonym of *ukweli*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** halisi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *خالص* (khāliṣ), meaning pure, genuine, unmixed
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, pure
**Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, or qualities to indicate authenticity or lack of adulteration. Used in commercial contexts (*dhahabu halisi*, "genuine gold"), in evaluations of character, and in broader evaluative discourse about identity. Contrasts with *bandia*. Participates in the nominal form *uhalisi*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** uhalisi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *halisi* (genuine, real)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, reality, realism
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or real, or reality itself. Used in philosophical, aesthetic, and everyday contexts (*uhalisi wa hali*, "the reality of the situation"). Used in literary and artistic criticism, including discussions of literary realism (*uhalisi wa kisanaa*, "artistic realism"; *uhalisi* as a literary movement). Also used in everyday evaluation of authenticity.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**6.**
**Form:** uhalisia
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *halisi* + *-a* (nominalizing/adjectivizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** realism, authenticity (in aesthetic or philosophical sense)
**Usage:** Used particularly in literary and artistic criticism to denote the quality of faithfully representing reality. Somewhat more domain-specific than *uhalisi* but well attested in Swahili literary discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**7.**
**Form:** sahihi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *صحيح* (ṣaḥīḥ), meaning correct, sound, valid
**Typical English translations:** correct, accurate, right, exact, valid
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, calculations, documents, and procedures as accurate or correct (*jibu sahihi*, "correct answer"; *hati sahihi*, "valid document"). Extremely common in educational, administrative, and legal contexts. Also used as a confirmation marker meaning "exactly" or "that's right." Also used as a verb (*kusahihisha*, to correct).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** usahihi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *sahihi*
**Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, precision, validity
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from *sahihi*, referring to the property of being accurate or correct. Used in formal and technical registers when evaluating the precision of information, measurements, or claims, including legal and scientific contexts (*usahihi wa taarifa*, "accuracy of a report").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** haki
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Borrowing from Arabic *حق* (ḥaqq), meaning right, truth, due
**Typical English translations:** right, justice, truth, entitlement, fairness, due
**Usage:** Covers a wide semantic range including moral rightness, legal rights, and factual truth. Used in legal discourse (*haki za binadamu*, "human rights"), moral evaluation, and everyday assertions of correctness (*haki kabisa*, "absolutely right/true"; *haki ni haki*, "right is right/truth is truth"). Participates in *uhaki* and compounds.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** haki ya
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *haki* (right/truth) + *ya* (of, genitive linker)
**Typical English translations:** the right of, the truth of, it is true that
**Usage:** A fixed genitive construction used to assert the truth or legitimacy of something. Functions in evaluative assertions (*haki ya Mungu*, "by God's truth"; used as an oath or strong assertion of truth).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**11.**
**Form:** uhakika
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *hakika* (from Arabic *حقيقة*, ḥaqīqa, reality, certainty)
**Typical English translations:** certainty, sureness, reliability, assurance
**Usage:** Refers to the state of being certain or reliable. Used when evaluating whether information or a source can be trusted or confirmed. Contrasts with *shaka* (doubt).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** hakika
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حقيقة* (ḥaqīqa), reality, truth
**Typical English translations:** certainty, sureness; also used adverbially: certainly, for sure
**Usage:** Used both as a noun (a certainty) and as an adverb or discourse particle affirming that something is definitely the case. Common in everyday speech and in formal assertions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** uaminifu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *aminifu* (*-amini-* from Arabic *أمين*, amīn, trustworthy + *-fu* adjectival/nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** faithfulness, trustworthiness, loyalty, integrity, reliability, honesty, fidelity
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being trustworthy or faithful, applied to persons, institutions, or representations. Used in moral evaluation of character, in assessments of whether a source or account can be relied upon, and in religious and professional contexts (*uaminifu wa mfanyakazi*, "an employee's reliability").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** amini
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *أمين* (amīn), trustworthy, faithful; functions as verb *kuamini* (to believe, to trust)
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, faithful, reliable; also used as a verb: to believe, to trust, to have faith in
**Usage:** Functions as an adjective evaluating persons or things as reliable and honest, and as a verb (*kuamini*: to believe, to trust). Used in evaluating whether something is credible or whether a person is trustworthy (*siwezi kumwamini*, "I cannot trust him/her"). Central to moral and religious discourse on trust and credibility. Participates in productive derivation: *uaminifu*, *mwaminifu* (faithful person).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** mwaminifu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *m-* (class 1 person prefix) + *amini* + *-fu*; one who is trustworthy
**Typical English translations:** faithful person, trustworthy person, reliable person
**Usage:** Refers to a person evaluated as reliably honest or faithful. Used in moral and relational contexts to commend character. Contrasts with *msaliti* (traitor, betrayer).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** bandia
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Possibly from Arabic *بندي* or related form, or possibly from *banda* (shed, makeshift structure) with suffix; etymology uncertain. Meaning: fabricated, artificial.
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation, forged
**Usage:** Applied to objects, documents, or representations that are not genuine. Common in commercial and legal contexts (*pesa bandia*, "counterfeit money"; *bidhaa bandia*, "fake goods"). Antonym of *halisi*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ghushi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *غش* (ghishsh), fraud, adulteration
**Typical English translations:** fraud, forgery, adulteration, fake, counterfeit
**Usage:** Used in legal and commercial contexts to refer to fraudulent goods, forged documents, or deceptive practices. Overlaps with *bandia* but carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or criminal fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** udanganyifu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *danganya* (to deceive) + *-ifu* (adjectival/nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** deception, deceitfulness, dishonesty, fraud, fraudulence, trickery
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality or act of deceiving. Used in moral condemnation and legal contexts. Derived from the verb *kudanganya* (to deceive, to lie). Appears in formal and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** kudanganya
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *danganya* (root: to deceive); etymology of root uncertain
**Typical English translations:** to deceive, to lie, to mislead, to cheat
**Usage:** Verb used to describe the act of making false representations or misleading someone. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts (*alikudanganya*, "he/she deceived you"). Productive root: *danganyifu* (deceptive), *udanganyifu* (deception), *mdanganyifu* (deceiver).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ulaghai
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *laghai* (fraud, trickery; from Arabic *laghā*, "vain/worthless speech")
**Typical English translations:** fraud, trickery, swindling, deceit
**Usage:** Refers specifically to fraudulent or swindling conduct. Used in legal and moral contexts. Somewhat more specific than *udanganyifu*, carrying connotations of deliberate material fraud (*kesi ya ulaghai*, "a fraud case").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** kweli kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Reduplication of *kweli* (truth)
**Typical English translations:** really truly, genuinely, in actual fact, for real, truly, in truth
**Usage:** Reduplicated intensified form of *kweli*, used to emphasize that something is genuinely or emphatically true. Common in spoken and written Swahili as an emphatic marker of sincerity or accuracy (*hii ni kweli kweli*, "this is really true"). Reduplication as an intensifying strategy is productive in Swahili.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** asili
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *أصل* (aṣl), origin, root, foundation, essence
**Typical English translations:** origin, nature, original, authentic, indigenous, essence, native
**Usage:** Refers to the original or fundamental nature of something, or to what something essentially is. Used in evaluations of authenticity (*lugha ya asili*, "native/original language") and in philosophical or cultural discussions of essence and origin (*asili yake ni nzuri*, "his/her nature is good").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** wa asili
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *wa* (associative particle) + *asili* (origin, nature)
**Typical English translations:** original, native, natural, authentic, indigenous
**Usage:** Fixed adjectival expression meaning "of the original kind" or "native to." Used to distinguish genuine or original items, persons, or practices from imitations or introduced ones. Common in cultural and commercial evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** uasili
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract noun prefix) + *asili* (origin/essence)
**Typical English translations:** originality, nativeness, authenticity, essential nature
**Usage:** The abstract nominalization of *asili*, referring to the quality of being original or of one's essential nature. Used in aesthetic and philosophical contexts, though somewhat less frequently than *asili* itself.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**25.**
**Form:** haki ya kweli
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *haki* (right, truth) + *ya* (associative particle) + *kweli* (truth)
**Typical English translations:** the real truth, genuine right, true justice, the genuine right, the true fact
**Usage:** A fixed or semi-fixed expression used for emphasis, asserting that something is genuinely true or genuinely just. Combines two high-frequency evaluative terms. Appears in spoken and written Swahili when a speaker wishes to stress the authenticity of a claim or the legitimacy of a right, often in assertive or argumentative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**26.**
**Form:** uhalali
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *halali* (from Arabic *حلال*, ḥalāl, lawful, permissible)
**Typical English translations:** legitimacy, lawfulness, legality, permissibility
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being lawful or legitimate. Used in legal and religious evaluations of acts, contracts, or claims (*uhalali wa mkataba*, "the legality/validity of a contract"). Distinct from but related to *haki* (right, justice).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** halali
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حلال* (ḥalāl), lawful, permitted
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legitimate, permissible, rightful
**Usage:** Used to evaluate actions, goods, or claims as legally or morally permissible. Has both Islamic legal connotations (permitted by religious law) and broader secular uses meaning "legitimate" or "rightfully obtained." Contrasts with *haramu* (forbidden, illicit). Participates in derivation with *uhalali*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** haramu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *حرام* (ḥarām), forbidden, unlawful
**Typical English translations:** forbidden, unlawful, illicit, wrong
**Usage:** Evaluates actions, goods, or conduct as prohibited or morally/legally wrong. Used in religious, moral, and legal registers. Functions as the antonym of *halali*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** sawa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root *-sawa*; from Arabic *sawā'* (equal, alike); meaning equal, level, correct
**Typical English translations:** correct, equal, right, okay, same, fair
**Usage:** Used to confirm that something is correct, equal, or in order. In evaluative contexts, functions as an affirmation of accuracy or fairness. Also used in comparisons to indicate equivalence or resemblance. Functions as an adjective, adverb, and discourse particle confirming accuracy or agreement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** usawa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *sawa*
**Typical English translations:** equality, balance, fairness, equivalence
**Usage:** Abstract noun referring to the quality of being equal, balanced, or fair. Used in moral and legal discourse on justice and equity, and in evaluations of proportionality or correspondence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** sawa sawa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Reduplication of *sawa* (equal, correct)
**Typical English translations:** exactly, precisely, correctly, just right
**Usage:** An intensified reduplicated form of *sawa*, used to assert exact correctness or precise equivalence. Common in evaluations of measurements, statements, and representations for accuracy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Form:** shaka
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *شك* (shakk), doubt, uncertainty
**Typical English translations:** doubt, uncertainty, suspicion, hesitation
**Usage:** Refers to a state of uncertainty or lack of confidence in the truth or reliability of something. Used in epistemic evaluations and in legal contexts (*bila shaka*, "without doubt"). Contrasts with *uhakika*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**
**Form:** bila shaka
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *bila* (without) + *shaka* (doubt)
**Typical English translations:** without doubt, certainly, undoubtedly, beyond question
**Usage:** Fixed expression used to assert that something is unquestionably true or certain. Common in both spoken and written Swahili as a strong affirmation of reliability or truth.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** dhahiri
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *ظاهر* (ẓāhir), apparent, manifest, evident
**Typical English translations:** evident, obvious, apparent, manifest, clear
**Usage:** Used to evaluate something as plainly or visibly true, real, or the case. Applied to facts, qualities, or states that are considered self-evident or openly observable. Appears in argumentative and legal contexts where evidence is assessed as unambiguous (*ni dhahiri kwamba…*, "it is evident that…"). Also used adverbially: *dhahiri kabisa* ("quite obviously").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** thibitisha
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *ثبت* (thabbata), to confirm, establish firmly; Swahili causative form (*-isha*)
**Typical English translations:** to confirm, to verify, to prove, to authenticate, to establish, to validate
**Usage:** Verb used in legal, administrative, scientific, and everyday contexts to mean the act of confirming or verifying the truth or authenticity of something (*thibitisha utambulisho wako*, "verify your identity"). Productive: *uthibitisho* (confirmation, verification, proof).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**36.**
**Form:** uthibitisho
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *thibitish-* + *-o* (nominalizing suffix); from same Arabic root as *thibitisha*
**Typical English translations:** confirmation, verification, proof, authentication, attestation, validation
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to the act or result of confirming or verifying something. Used in legal, administrative, and academic contexts when documentary or procedural proof of authenticity or correctness is required (*uthibitisho wa hati*, "authentication of a document").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**37.**
**Form:** ushahidi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *shahidi* (witness); from Arabic *shāhid* (witness)
**Typical English translations:** evidence, testimony, witness, proof
**Usage:** Refers to evidence or testimony used to evaluate the truth of a claim. Central to legal discourse in Swahili-speaking contexts; also used in religious and moral evaluations of conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**38.**
**Form:** shahidi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *shāhid* (witness, one who testifies)
**Typical English translations:** witness, testifier
**Usage:** A person who provides testimony or evidence regarding the truth of events or claims. Used in legal proceedings and in religious contexts. Participates in derivation with *ushahidi*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**39.**
**Form:** nakala
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *naqala* (to copy, to transfer); copy, transcript
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, transcript, reproduction
**Usage:** Used in evaluations of whether a document or object is an original or a reproduction. Relevant in legal and aesthetic contexts where authenticity is assessed (*nakala ya hati*, "copy of a document").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**40.**
**Form:** hali halisi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *hali* (state, condition) + *halisi* (genuine, real); the real/actual state
**Typical English translations:** the actual situation, the real state of affairs, reality
**Usage:** A fixed nominal phrase used to evaluate or describe what is genuinely the case, as opposed to appearances or claims. Common in journalistic and analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**41.**
**Form:** heshima
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *ḥishma* (modesty, dignity, respect)
**Typical English translations:** respect, dignity, honor, esteem
**Usage:** Used in moral evaluations of persons and conduct with respect to whether proper regard is shown. Relevant in assessments of rightness of behavior in social and ethical contexts (*kukosa heshima*, "to lack respect/to be disrespectful").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**42.**
**Form:** makosa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *ma-* (plural prefix, class 6) + *kosa* (error, fault, mistake)
**Typical English translations:** errors, mistakes, faults, wrongs
**Usage:** Plural of *kosa*; used in evaluations of incorrect statements, faulty conduct, or moral wrongdoing. Appears in legal, moral, and epistemic contexts (*bila makosa*, "without errors/faults"; *kufanya makosa*, "to make mistakes/errors"; *makosa ya kisheria*, "legal offenses").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**43.**
**Form:** kosa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Error, fault, sin; singular of *makosa*. Etymology of root debated (possibly from Arabic *qaṣwa* or related root).
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fault, wrong, sin, offense
**Usage:** The singular form; used to evaluate a single act, statement, or judgment as incorrect or morally wrong. Can refer to factual errors, moral wrongs, or legal offenses (*kosa kubwa*, "a serious mistake/offense"). Participates in productive derivation: *kukosa* (to err, to lack), *makosa* (plural), *kukosea* (to wrong someone).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**44.**
**Form:** bora
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *bāri'* (excellent, outstanding); meaning best, excellent, superior
**Typical English translations:** best, excellent, superior, ideal, better
**Usage:** Used in evaluative contexts to characterize something as of high quality or as the best option. Appears in comparative and superlative constructions (*bora zaidi*, "even better") and in aesthetic and practical evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**45.**
**Form:** kasoro
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *qaṣūr* (deficiency, shortcoming); meaning defect, flaw, shortcoming
**Typical English translations:** defect, flaw, fault, shortcoming, imperfection
**Usage:** Used to identify a deficiency or flaw in an object, person, or representation. Appears in quality assessment, legal, and moral contexts (*bidhaa yenye kasoro*, "a defective product").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**46.**
**Form:** mfano
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *m-* (noun class 3 prefix) + *fano* (likeness, resemblance; cf. *fanana*, "to resemble")
**Typical English translations:** example, likeness, resemblance, model, parable
**Usage:** Used in contexts of resemblance and representation, including evaluations of how closely something resembles something else. Also appears in aesthetic and didactic contexts (*mfano mzuri*, "a good example/likeness").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**47.**
**Form:** fanana
**Literal meaning/morphology:** To resemble, to be alike (verb; related to *mfano*)
**Typical English translations:** to resemble, to be similar, to be alike, to match
**Usage:** Used to evaluate the degree of resemblance between two things or persons. Appears in aesthetic, legal (e.g., identifying persons or objects), and everyday contexts (*vinafanana sana*, "they resemble each other closely").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**48.**
**Form:** tofauti
**Literal meaning/morphology:** From Arabic *tafāwut* (difference, disparity); meaning difference, different
**Typical English translations:** difference, different, distinct, discrepancy
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether two things differ, including in contexts where accuracy or resemblance is at issue. Appears in comparative evaluations and in identifying discrepancies (*kuna tofauti*, "there is a difference/discrepancy").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**49.**
**Form:** uongo mtupu
**Literal meaning/morphology:** *uongo* (falsehood) + *mtupu* (pure, mere, nothing but)
**Typical English translations:** pure falsehood, outright lie, nothing but lies
**Usage:** Fixed expression that intensifies the characterization of something as entirely false. *Mtupu* functions as an intensifier meaning "nothing but" or "sheer." Attested in spoken and written Swahili in moral and argumentative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
