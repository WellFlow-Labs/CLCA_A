# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

This step documents how English encodes dynamic truth-related meanings: change of state (becoming true/false/valid/etc.), causation of truth-related states, and stability or persistence of such states. All vocabulary is drawn from the CONTEXT (P1–P5 and P1b). Auxiliary verbs, aspect markers, copulas, and function words are added as needed for grammatical well-formedness.

The analysis treats each truth-domain term separately where behavior differs. Where no common expression exists for a given term or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Patterns are reported from attested data in the CONTEXT; no constructions are invented beyond what the CONTEXT supports.

Three main subsection types are addressed:
1. **Becoming** — change into a truth-related state
2. **Causing** — bringing about a truth-related state in something else
3. **Stability / Persistence** — remaining in or losing a truth-related state

A fourth subsection addresses **dynamic expressions not fitting the above types** that are attested in the CONTEXT.

---

## 1. BECOMING — Change into a Truth-Related State

### 1.1 General Pattern

English has no dedicated morphological inchoative for the truth domain. Change of state is expressed periphrastically. The CONTEXT (P4a §4) identifies three main frames:

- **PROVE + adjective** (evidential inchoative: retrospective confirmation)
- **TURN OUT TO BE + adjective** (discovery inchoative: often implies surprise)
- **BECOME + adjective** (general inchoative: grammatical but marginal for most items)
- **COME TRUE** (fixed idiom: restricted to wishes, predictions, fears)

These frames are not equally available across all truth-domain adjectives. Availability is documented item by item below.

## 2. CAUSING — Bringing About a Truth-Related State

### 2.1 General Pattern

The CONTEXT (P4a §5) identifies two main causative strategies:

- **Derivational verbal causative**: dedicated verbs derived from truth-domain adjectives (*validate*, *authenticate*, *verify*, *legitimize*, *correct*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*)
- **Periphrastic causative**: *make + NP + adjective* (marginal for most items); nominalization paraphrases (*confirm the validity of*, *establish the authenticity of*)

Lexical gaps are documented: *\*genuinize*, *\*accuratize*, *\*plausibilize*, *\*spuriousize* are unattested. Where no derivational causative exists, the periphrastic or nominalization strategy is used.

## 3. STABILITY AND PERSISTENCE — Remaining in or Losing a Truth-Related State

### 3.1 General Pattern

English encodes the persistence of truth-related states primarily through:

- **REMAIN + adjective** (explicit stability)
- **HOLD + adjective** (*hold true*, *hold valid*) — semi-fixed
- **CONTINUE TO BE + adjective** (explicit continuity)
- **NO LONGER + adjective** (explicit loss of state)
- **LOSE + nominalization** (*lose credibility*, *lose validity*)
- **UNDERMINE / DAMAGE + nominalization** (gradual erosion of state)

## 4. OTHER DYNAMIC EXPRESSIONS

### 4.1 Expressions of Gradual Change

Several truth-domain terms participate in degree-modifier constructions that encode gradual change rather than discrete state transitions. These are documented here as a distinct dynamic type not captured by the become/cause/remain framework.

| Construction | Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BECOME + COMPARATIVE + *accurate* | *The model became more accurate over time.* | The model improved in accuracy over time. | [CORE] | Documented in §1.2 above; *accurate* is particularly natural in gradual-change constructions because accuracy is explicitly graded. |
| BECOME + INCREASINGLY + *implausible* | *The alibi became increasingly implausible.* | The alibi grew progressively harder to believe. | [CORE] | Documented in §1.2 above; *increasingly* encodes gradual change. |
| BECOME + MORE + *credible* | *The theory became more credible as evidence accumulated.* | The theory grew more worthy of belief as evidence accumulated. | [CORE] | Documented in §1.2 above. |
| GROW + *plausible* / *implausible* | *The scenario grew more plausible as details emerged.* | The scenario became more believable as details emerged. | [CORE] | *Grow* is a near-synonym of *become* in gradual-change constructions; natural with graded truth-domain adjectives. |

---

### 4.2 Expressions of Revelation and Discovery

The CONTEXT documents a cluster of expressions in which a truth-related property is revealed or discovered rather than acquired or conferred. These are distinct from both the inchoative and causative frames.

| Construction | Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| THE TRUTH WILL OUT | *The truth will out eventually.* | The truth will eventually be revealed. | [CORE] | Fixed proverbial expression; documented in P2b §2.25. Encodes the inevitability of truth's emergence. |
| MOMENT OF TRUTH | *The moment of truth arrived when the results were announced.* | The critical point at which the true nature of the situation was revealed arrived. | [CORE] | Fixed phrase; documented in P1b Root 2 item 14 and P2b §2.10. Encodes a discrete point of revelation. |
| COME TO LIGHT | *The fraud came to light after the audit.* | The fraud was revealed after the audit. | [CORE] | Not morphologically derived from a P1 truth-domain adjective but attested throughout the CONTEXT as a collocate of the fraud/deception cluster; encodes the emergence of hidden truth. |
| RING TRUE / RING FALSE | *His explanation rang true / rang false.* | His explanation seemed genuine / seemed implausible. | [CORE] | Fixed verbal idioms; documented in P2b §1.2–1.3 and P4a §9. Encode the perceptual discovery of a truth-related property. |

### 4.3 Expressions of Conferral and Withdrawal of Epistemic Status

The CONTEXT documents a cluster of expressions in which epistemic or institutional status is conferred on or withdrawn from a claim, person, or institution by an external agent. These are distinct from the derivational causatives in §2 in that they use general-purpose verbs (*lend*, *confer*, *strip*, *withdraw*) rather than dedicated truth-domain verbs.

| Construction | Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LEND + *credibility* / *authenticity* / *verisimilitude* / *plausibility* | *The expert testimony lent credibility to the claim.* | The expert testimony caused the claim to be more worthy of belief. | [CORE] | Documented in P2b §12.6, §22.2; §2 above. *Lend* is the most productive general-purpose verb for conferring epistemic status. |
| CONFER + *legitimacy* | *The referendum conferred legitimacy on the new constitution.* | The referendum caused the new constitution to be recognized as legitimate. | [CORE] | Documented in §2 above. *Confer* is the most natural verb for conferring institutional status. |
| STRIP + NP + OF + *legitimacy* / *credibility* | *The scandal stripped the government of its legitimacy.* | The scandal caused the government to lose its recognized status as legitimate. | [CORE] | Causative of loss of institutional status; natural in political and journalistic contexts. |
| UNDERMINE + *credibility* / *reliability* / *veracity* | *The inconsistency undermined her credibility.* | The inconsistency gradually caused her to be less worthy of belief. | [CORE] | Documented in §2 and §3 above. *Undermine* encodes gradual erosion rather than sudden loss. |

### 4.4 The *post-truth* Construction as a Dynamic Expression

The CONTEXT (P1b Root 2 item 30, P3 Group 10, P5 Opposition 46) documents *post-truth* as a construction that encodes a claimed historical change in the status of truth itself — a shift from a condition in which truth functions as a stable epistemic standard to one in which it does not. This is a unique dynamic expression in the domain: it does not describe a change in the truth-status of a particular claim or person but a change in the social and epistemic role of truth as a category.

| Construction | Example | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| POST-TRUTH + noun | *We are living in a post-truth era.* | We are living in a period in which objective facts are less influential than emotional appeals. | [CORE] | Documented in P1b Root 2 item 30 and P3 Group 10. The *post-* prefix encodes a temporal change of state at the level of discourse norms rather than individual propositions. |

### 4.5 Summary of Causative Gaps

The following truth-domain adjectives from P1 have no attested derivational causative verb in English. For each, the available periphrastic strategy is noted.

| Adjective | Gap | Available Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| *true* | No *\*trueify* | *verify*, *confirm*, *make X true* (marginal) |
| *accurate* | No *\*accuratize* | *verify*, *correct*, *make X accurate* |
| *genuine* | No *\*genuinize* | *establish as genuine*, *prove genuine* |
| *credible* | No *\*credibilize* | *lend credibility to*, *establish as credible* |
| *sincere* | No *\*sincerize* | *make X sincere* (marginal) |
| *truthful* | No *\*truthfulize* | *make X truthful* (marginal) |
| *plausible* | No *\*plausibilize* | *lend plausibility to*, *make X plausible* |
| *sound* | No *\*soundify* | *make X sound* (marginal) |
| *reliable* | No *\*reliabilize* | *establish as reliable* |
| *faithful* | No *\*faithfulize* | *render faithful* (marginal), *translate faithfully* |
| *bogus* | No *\*bogusify* | no natural periphrastic causative identified |
| *spurious* | No *\*spuriousize* | no natural periphrastic causative identified |
| *apocryphal* | No *\*apocryphalify* | no natural periphrastic causative identified |

> **Note on *bogus*, *spurious*, and *apocryphal*:** No natural periphrastic causative construction is identified for these three items in the CONTEXT. One does not naturally "cause" something to be bogus, spurious, or apocryphal in the way one can cause something to be valid or authentic; these properties are typically discovered or assigned retrospectively rather than brought about by an agent. This asymmetry is preserved without normalization.
