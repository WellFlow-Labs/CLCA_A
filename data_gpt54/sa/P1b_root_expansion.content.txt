## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **सत्य / सत्** — *satya / sat*
**Participates in:** 5 P1 items  
(*सत्य, सत्यम्, सत्यता, सत्यवचन, असत्य*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the central sense of truth, reality, genuineness, and truthfulness in speech or character.

### 2. **प्रमा / प्रमाण** — *pramā / pramāṇa*
**Participates in:** 4 P1 items  
(*प्रमाण, प्रमा, प्रमाणिक, प्रामाण्य*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the epistemic sense of valid cognition, proof, authority, warrant, and reliability.

### 3. **यथार्थ / अर्थ** — *yathārtha / artha*
**Participates in:** 3 P1 items directly by stem/family  
(*यथार्थ, यथार्थता, भूतार्थ*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the sense of accordance with the object, actual state of affairs, factuality, real purport, or accurate representation.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

### Root 1: **सत्य / सत्** — *satya / sat*

This root is highly productive. Below are the most common and well-attested expressions; additional forms exist.

---

**सत्य**  
**Transliteration:** satya  
**Literal breakdown:** from *sat* “being, real” + *-ya*  
**Translation:** truth; true  
**Context of use:** A basic and pervasive word for truth, truthful speech, or what is real. Used in ordinary, ethical, literary, and philosophical contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यम्**  
**Transliteration:** satyam  
**Literal breakdown:** neuter of *satya*  
**Translation:** truth; it is true  
**Context of use:** Common in literary, scriptural, and philosophical Sanskrit as a noun or predicative expression. Often used in maxims and formal assertions.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यता**  
**Transliteration:** satyatā  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā*  
**Translation:** truth; truthfulness; correctness  
**Context of use:** Used in abstract discussion of the quality of being true, especially in scholastic or reflective prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यवचन**  
**Transliteration:** satyavacana  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* “true” + *vacana* “speech, utterance”  
**Translation:** truthful speech; true statement  
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and literary contexts for speech that accords with fact or duty. Also used in praise of honest persons.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यवादिन्**  
**Transliteration:** satyavādin  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *vādin* “speaker”  
**Translation:** truth-speaker; truthful person  
**Context of use:** A common ethical and descriptive term for one who speaks truth. Found in narrative, didactic, and religious texts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यवादी**  
**Transliteration:** satyavādī  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *vādī* “speaker, one who says”  
**Translation:** truthful; one who tells the truth  
**Context of use:** Very common in later and modern Sanskritized usage for a truthful person. Often overlaps with *satyavādin*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यभाषण**  
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣaṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *bhāṣaṇa* “speaking”  
**Translation:** truthful speaking  
**Context of use:** Used in moral and didactic contexts, especially where truthfulness is treated as a virtue of speech.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यभाषिन्**  
**Transliteration:** satyabhāṣin  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *bhāṣin* “speaker”  
**Translation:** truthful speaker  
**Context of use:** A descriptive and ethical expression for one who habitually speaks truth. Common in praise and character evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यसंध**  
**Transliteration:** satyasaṃdha  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *saṃdha* “firm, faithful to”  
**Translation:** true to one’s word; faithful to truth  
**Context of use:** Common in epic and kāvya for a person who keeps promises and remains faithful to truth. Often used of kings and heroes.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यपरायण**  
**Transliteration:** satyaparāyaṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *parāyaṇa* “devoted to”  
**Translation:** devoted to truth  
**Context of use:** Used in ethical, religious, and laudatory contexts for a person deeply committed to truthfulness.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यनिष्ठ**  
**Transliteration:** satyaniṣṭha  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *niṣṭha* “steadfast, grounded in”  
**Translation:** steadfast in truth; truthful  
**Context of use:** Common in moral and character evaluation. It often emphasizes stable commitment rather than a single true statement.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यशील**  
**Transliteration:** satyaśīla  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *śīla* “conduct, character”  
**Translation:** of truthful character  
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and literary description of persons whose disposition is truthful.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यधर्म**  
**Transliteration:** satyadharma  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *dharma*  
**Translation:** truth as duty; true righteousness  
**Context of use:** Found in religious and moral discourse where truthfulness is treated as a form of dharma or as joined with righteousness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्यपालन**  
**Transliteration:** satyapālana  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *pālana* “keeping, maintaining”  
**Translation:** observance of truth; keeping truth  
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and religious contexts for maintaining truthfulness or keeping one’s truthful commitment.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यप्रतिज्ञ**  
**Transliteration:** satyapratijña  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *pratijñā/pratijña* “vow, pledge”  
**Translation:** true to one’s vow; whose pledge is true  
**Context of use:** Common in epic and literary usage for one whose promise is fulfilled. It often shades into reliability and vow-keeping.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**सत्यप्रतिश्रवस्**  
**Transliteration:** satyapratiśravas  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *pratiśravas* “promise, assurance”  
**Translation:** true to one’s promise  
**Context of use:** A literary and elevated expression, especially in epic diction, for one whose assurance proves true.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्यलोक**  
**Transliteration:** satyaloka  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *loka* “world”  
**Translation:** Satya-world; highest heavenly realm  
**Context of use:** A cosmological and religious term, especially in Purāṇic and theological discourse. Its use is established but not directly about truth evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्ययुग**  
**Transliteration:** satyayuga  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *yuga* “age”  
**Translation:** the age of truth  
**Context of use:** A standard cosmological term for the first and most righteous age in Hindu time cycles. It is very common but not a direct truth-judgment term.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्याग्रह**  
**Transliteration:** satyāgraha  
**Literal breakdown:** *satya* + *āgraha* “firm insistence, holding fast”  
**Translation:** insistence on truth; satyagraha  
**Context of use:** A major modern Indic political-ethical term, also used in Sanskritized discourse. It refers to principled adherence to truth, especially in nonviolent resistance.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्संग**  
**Transliteration:** satsaṅga  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* “good, true, real” + *saṅga* “association”  
**Translation:** association with the good; holy company  
**Context of use:** Very common in religious discourse for fellowship with the virtuous or spiritually elevated. The root shifts toward goodness and spiritual worth.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्पुरुष**  
**Transliteration:** satpuruṣa  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *puruṣa* “person”  
**Translation:** good man; virtuous person  
**Context of use:** Common in ethical and literary contexts for a morally good or noble person. The sense is often moral rather than strictly truth-related.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्कार**  
**Transliteration:** satkāra  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *kāra* “making, doing; honor”  
**Translation:** honor; respectful reception  
**Context of use:** Common in social and literary usage for respectful treatment or hospitality. This is a well-attested extension beyond truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्कृत**  
**Transliteration:** satkṛta  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *kṛta* “done, made”  
**Translation:** honored; well treated  
**Context of use:** Used for a person who has been respectfully received or honored. The root contributes positive value rather than truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्कर्म**  
**Transliteration:** satkarma  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *karma* “action”  
**Translation:** good deed; virtuous act  
**Context of use:** Common in moral and religious contexts for meritorious action. This is a productive ethical extension of *sat*.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्पथ**  
**Transliteration:** satpatha  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *patha* “path”  
**Translation:** right path; good path  
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse for the proper or virtuous course of life.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सत्स्वरूप**  
**Transliteration:** satsvarūpa  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* + *svarūpa* “own form”  
**Translation:** of true nature; truly real in nature  
**Context of use:** Found in philosophical and theological discourse for what has real or true being.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**सच्चिदानन्द**  
**Transliteration:** saccidānanda  
**Literal breakdown:** *sat* “being” + *cit* “consciousness” + *ānanda* “bliss”  
**Translation:** being-consciousness-bliss  
**Context of use:** A major theological-philosophical expression, especially in Vedānta, for ultimate reality. The root here contributes ontological being rather than ordinary truthfulness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**असत्य**  
**Transliteration:** asatya  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *satya*  
**Translation:** false; untrue  
**Context of use:** A standard antonym of *satya*, used for falsehood in speech, character, or representation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**असत्यवचन**  
**Transliteration:** asatyavacana  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *satya* + *vacana*  
**Translation:** false statement; untruthful speech  
**Context of use:** Used in ethical and juridical contexts for false utterance or lying.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**असत्यवादिन्**  
**Transliteration:** asatyavādin  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *satya* + *vādin*  
**Translation:** speaker of falsehood; liar  
**Context of use:** Used in moral, narrative, and polemical contexts for one who speaks falsely.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 2: **प्रमा / प्रमाण** — *pramā / pramāṇa*

This root family is highly productive in philosophical and learned usage. Below are the most common and well-attested expressions; additional forms exist.

---

**प्रमाण**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *pra-* + √*mā* “measure”  
**Translation:** proof; valid means of knowledge; authority; standard  
**Context of use:** A central term in epistemology, logic, scriptural interpretation, and argument. Also used more broadly for evidence, measure, or standard.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमा**  
**Transliteration:** pramā  
**Literal breakdown:** same root family; “correct cognition”  
**Translation:** valid cognition; true knowledge  
**Context of use:** A technical philosophical term for cognition that is true or veridical. Common in epistemological discussion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाता**  
**Transliteration:** pramātā  
**Literal breakdown:** from *pramā* + agentive suffix  
**Translation:** knower; valid cognizer  
**Context of use:** Used in epistemology for the subject who possesses valid cognition. Common in discussions of knowledge structure.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमेय**  
**Transliteration:** prameya  
**Literal breakdown:** from same root family; “that which is to be known/measured”  
**Translation:** object of valid knowledge; knowable object  
**Context of use:** A standard epistemological term paired with *pramāṇa* and *pramātā*. Also used in scholastic enumeration of categories.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमिति**  
**Transliteration:** pramiti  
**Literal breakdown:** from same root family  
**Translation:** valid cognition; ascertainment  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and logical texts for determinate valid knowledge or ascertainment.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणिक**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇika  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ika*  
**Translation:** authoritative; authentic; trustworthy  
**Context of use:** Used for texts, persons, documents, testimony, or evidence regarded as reliable or authoritative.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रामाण्य**  
**Transliteration:** prāmāṇya  
**Literal breakdown:** abstract noun from *pramāṇa* family  
**Translation:** validity; authority; trustworthiness  
**Context of use:** A major epistemological term for the validity-status of cognition or testimony. Also used in textual and doctrinal evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**अप्रामाण्य**  
**Transliteration:** aprāmāṇya  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *prāmāṇya*  
**Translation:** invalidity; lack of authority  
**Context of use:** Used in epistemology and debate for the failure of a cognition, source, or argument to count as valid.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणता**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇatā  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-tā*  
**Translation:** validity; status as proof or standard  
**Context of use:** Used in scholastic and philosophical prose for the condition of being a valid means or standard.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणत्व**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇatva  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-tva*  
**Translation:** the state of being a pramāṇa; evidentiary validity  
**Context of use:** Common in technical philosophical argument, especially when discussing whether something qualifies as a valid means of knowledge.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणभूत**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇabhūta  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *bhūta* “become, being”  
**Translation:** authoritative; serving as valid authority  
**Context of use:** Used of persons, texts, or statements treated as authoritative. Common in scholastic and religious writing.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणपुरुष**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇapuruṣa  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *puruṣa*  
**Translation:** authoritative person  
**Context of use:** Used for a person whose testimony or judgment is accepted as reliable. Appears in legal, scholastic, and religious contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**प्रमाणवचन**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇavacana  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *vacana*  
**Translation:** authoritative statement  
**Context of use:** Used for a statement accepted as evidentially or doctrinally authoritative. Common in commentary and debate.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणशास्त्र**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇaśāstra  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *śāstra* “treatise, science”  
**Translation:** science of valid knowledge; epistemological treatise  
**Context of use:** Used for the branch of philosophy dealing with means of valid cognition, especially in Nyāya and Buddhist traditions.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणसमुच्चय**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasamuccaya  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *samuccaya* “collection, compendium”  
**Translation:** compendium of pramāṇas  
**Context of use:** A title type and technical expression in philosophical literature. It refers to systematic treatment of means of valid knowledge.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**प्रमाणसिद्ध**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasiddha  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *siddha* “established”  
**Translation:** established by valid proof  
**Context of use:** Used in argument and commentary for a claim shown by accepted evidence or valid means of knowledge.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणसिद्धि**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇasiddhi  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *siddhi* “establishment, proof”  
**Translation:** establishment by proof; proof of validity  
**Context of use:** Used in technical philosophical contexts for demonstration through valid means. Also appears in titles and chapter headings.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणतः**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇataḥ  
**Literal breakdown:** from *pramāṇa* in adverbial use  
**Translation:** by proof; authoritatively; on valid grounds  
**Context of use:** A common scholastic adverb in argumentation, indicating that something is established by valid evidence.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणेन**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇena  
**Literal breakdown:** instrumental of *pramāṇa* used adverbially  
**Translation:** by means of proof; by valid evidence  
**Context of use:** Frequently used in philosophical and legal-style reasoning. Though formally inflectional in origin, it functions as a regular collocational expression in argument.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**प्रमाणीकृत**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkṛta  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇī* + *kṛta* / “made authoritative, authenticated”  
**Translation:** authenticated; validated  
**Context of use:** Used for documents, readings, claims, or procedures that have been confirmed or regularized as authoritative.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणीकरण**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇīkaraṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇī* + *karaṇa* “making”  
**Translation:** authentication; validation  
**Context of use:** Common in administrative, textual, and technical contexts for the act of validating or certifying something.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणपत्र**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇapatra  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *patra* “document”  
**Translation:** certificate; documentary proof  
**Context of use:** Common in administrative and legal usage for a certificate or official document serving as proof.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाणलेख**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇalekha  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *lekha* “writing, document”  
**Translation:** documentary evidence; attested document  
**Context of use:** Used in documentary, legal, and archival contexts for a written record serving as proof.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**प्रमाणदृष्ट**  
**Transliteration:** pramāṇadṛṣṭa  
**Literal breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *dṛṣṭa* “seen, recognized”  
**Translation:** recognized by authority/proof  
**Context of use:** Used in scholastic and exegetical contexts for something accepted on the basis of valid authority.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**अप्रमाण**  
**Transliteration:** apramāṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *pramāṇa*  
**Translation:** not authoritative; invalid as proof  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and legal-style reasoning for what does not count as valid evidence or standard.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**अप्रमाणिक**  
**Transliteration:** apramāṇika  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *pramāṇika*  
**Translation:** unauthoritative; unreliable; inauthentic  
**Context of use:** Used for texts, claims, witnesses, or documents lacking accepted authority or authenticity.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**प्रमाद**  
**Transliteration:** pramāda  
**Literal breakdown:** same broader root family in historical derivation, but distinct lexical development  
**Translation:** carelessness; heedlessness; error  
**Context of use:** Very common in ethical and religious discourse for negligence or lack of vigilance. Its meaning is no longer centered on valid cognition.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**अप्रमाद**  
**Transliteration:** apramāda  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *pramāda*  
**Translation:** vigilance; carefulness; non-negligence  
**Context of use:** Common in Buddhist, ethical, and religious discourse for careful attentiveness. It is adjacent to reliability but not directly a truth term.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### Root 3: **यथार्थ / अर्थ** — *yathārtha / artha*

This family is productive but less tightly unified than the two above. Below are the most common truth-relevant and closely related expressions.

---

**यथार्थ**  
**Transliteration:** yathārtha  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathā* “as” + *artha* “object, matter, meaning”  
**Translation:** real; accurate; true; actual  
**Context of use:** A central term for correspondence to reality, factual accuracy, or exact representation. Common in philosophy, literary criticism, and descriptive prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थता**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthatā  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *-tā*  
**Translation:** reality; accuracy; truth; exactness  
**Context of use:** Used in abstract discussion of the quality of being accurate or corresponding to fact. Common in analytical and philosophical contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थत्व**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthatva  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *-tva*  
**Translation:** actuality; correctness; realism  
**Context of use:** Used in scholastic and philosophical prose for the state of being real or accurate.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थज्ञान**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthajñāna  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *jñāna* “knowledge”  
**Translation:** true knowledge; accurate cognition  
**Context of use:** Common in philosophical discourse for cognition that corresponds to reality. Often contrasted with error or illusion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थदर्शन**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthadarśana  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *darśana* “seeing, view”  
**Translation:** seeing things as they are; accurate perception  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and religious contexts for correct apprehension of reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थवचन**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthavacana  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *vacana*  
**Translation:** accurate statement; statement of fact  
**Context of use:** Used for speech or writing that correctly represents the matter at hand. Common in learned and descriptive contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थवर्णन**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthavarṇana  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *varṇana* “description”  
**Translation:** accurate description; realistic depiction  
**Context of use:** Used in literary, historical, and descriptive contexts for representation faithful to the actual state of affairs.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थनिरूपण**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthanirūpaṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *nirūpaṇa* “determination, exposition”  
**Translation:** accurate exposition; correct determination  
**Context of use:** Common in scholastic and commentarial writing for precise exposition of a matter as it really is.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थबोध**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthabodha  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *bodha* “understanding”  
**Translation:** correct understanding; true apprehension  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and pedagogical contexts for understanding that matches reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थानुभव**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthānubhava  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *anubhava* “experience”  
**Translation:** veridical experience; true experience of the real  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and religious discourse for experience that is not illusory or mistaken.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**यथार्थनामन् / यथार्थनाम**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthanāman / yathārthanāma  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *nāman* “name”  
**Translation:** aptly named; truly named  
**Context of use:** Used when a name correctly fits the thing named. More literary and exegetical than everyday.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**यथार्थचित्रण**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthacitraṇa  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *citraṇa* “depiction”  
**Translation:** realistic depiction  
**Context of use:** Used in literary and artistic criticism for depiction faithful to reality. This is especially relevant in representational evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**यथार्थवाद**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthavāda  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *vāda* “doctrine, discourse”  
**Translation:** realism  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophy and literary theory for realism or a realist doctrine. It extends from truth correspondence into doctrinal and aesthetic usage.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**यथार्थवादी**  
**Transliteration:** yathārthavādī  
**Literal breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *vādī*  
**Translation:** realist; one who speaks realistically  
**Context of use:** Used for philosophers, critics, or speakers committed to realism or factuality.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**अयथार्थ**  
**Transliteration:** ayathārtha  
**Literal breakdown:** *a-* + *yathārtha*  
**Translation:** unreal; inaccurate; false
