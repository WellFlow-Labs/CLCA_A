# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Derivations are documented only where the derived form is independently attested in use, not merely constructible by rule. Where a form appears in P1b root expansion, it is included here if it represents a genuine derivational relationship; its prior appearance is noted. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved without comment beyond factual observation.

## 1. Base Form: **kweli**

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Derived Form:** ukweli
- **Transliteration:** ukweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract nominal prefix) + *kweli*
- **Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; veracity
- **Usage:** The standard abstract nominal form of *kweli*, used in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse when referring to truth as a property or value. This is the primary and most productive nominal derivation from this root.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation from kweli:**
No agentive, causative, or verbal derivations from *kweli* are identified. The root does not appear to generate a verb stem, a diminutive, or an adjectival form beyond its bare use. The *u-* nominalization (*ukweli*) is the only morphologically productive derivation attested.

- **Natural English translation:** to make sure; to ensure; to verify; to confirm; to ascertain
- **Usage:** Causative verbal derivation, extremely common in everyday, administrative, and legal Swahili. Used to describe the act of making certain that something is the case or of verifying a fact or condition. This is among the most productive derivations in this domain.

**Note on the morphological relationship between hakika and kuhakikisha:**
The causative *-sha* suffix attaches to a stem *hakikish-* that shows internal modification relative to the base *hakika*. This is consistent with the behavior of Arabic-derived bases in Swahili verbal derivation and does not represent an irregular form.

## 12. Base Form: **amini**

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base Form:** amini
- **Derived Form:** kuamini
- **Transliteration:** kuamini
- **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *amini*
- **Natural English translation:** to believe; to trust; to have faith in
- **Usage:** Verbal form used in religious, moral, and everyday discourse to describe the act of believing or trusting. Central to both Islamic and Christian Swahili discourse on faith and credibility.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base Form:** amini
- **Derived Form:** aminifu
- **Transliteration:** aminifu
- **Literal Breakdown:** *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival/qualitative suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** trustworthy; faithful; reliable; honest
- **Usage:** Adjectival form used to evaluate persons, institutions, or representations as reliably honest and faithful. The *-fu* suffix derives qualitative adjectives from nominal or verbal bases in Swahili.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base Form:** amini (via *aminifu*)
- **Derived Form:** uaminifu
- **Transliteration:** uaminifu
- **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract nominal prefix) + *aminifu*
- **Natural English translation:** faithfulness; trustworthiness; loyalty; integrity; reliability
- **Usage:** Abstract nominal form referring to the quality of being trustworthy or faithful. Used in moral evaluation of character and in assessments of whether a source or account can be relied upon. Already listed in P1.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base Form:** amini (via *kuamini*)
- **Derived Form:** mwaminifu
- **Transliteration:** mwaminifu
- **Literal Breakdown:** *mw-* (class 1 singular prefix, before vowel) + *aminifu*
- **Natural English translation:** a faithful/trustworthy person; a believer; a loyal person
- **Usage:** Agentive nominal referring to a person who is faithful, trustworthy, or a believer. Used in religious contexts (a faithful believer) and in moral evaluation of character.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base Form:** amini (via *kuamini*)
- **Derived Form:** waaminifu
- **Transliteration:** waaminifu
- **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (class 2 plural prefix) + *aminifu*
- **Natural English translation:** faithful/trustworthy people; believers; loyal persons (plural)
- **Usage:** Plural agentive nominal. Used in religious and moral discourse to refer to a group of faithful or trustworthy persons.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base Form:** amini (via *kuamini*)
- **Derived Form:** imani
- **Transliteration:** imani
- **Literal Breakdown:** Nominal form from the same Arabic root (*أمن*); *i-* nominal prefix variant
- **Natural English translation:** faith; belief; trust; conviction
- **Usage:** The primary nominal form for faith or belief, used extensively in religious, moral, and everyday discourse. While sharing the Arabic root with *amini*, *imani* functions as an independent lexical item in Swahili rather than a transparent morphological derivation from *amini* within Swahili grammar.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on imani:** The relationship between *amini* and *imani* in Swahili is one of shared Arabic etymology (*أمن*, *أمين*, *إيمان*) rather than synchronic Swahili morphological derivation. Both are borrowed forms. The connection is documented here because speakers and lexicographers consistently treat them as related, but the derivational relationship is historical rather than productive within Swahili.

- **Usage:** Nominal form in class 3 (rather than the abstract class 11/14 *u-* form), referring to a specific instance or document of confirmation or proof. Used in legal and administrative contexts to refer to a concrete certificate or piece of evidence.

## Summary Observations

The following observations are directly supported by the data above and are offered without interpretive extension:

1. The *u-* abstract nominalization prefix is the most consistently attested derivational process across this domain, applying to *kweli*, *halisi*, *sahihi*, *hakika*, *aminifu*, *halali*, *sawa*, and *thibitisha*. It is absent or unattested as a common form for *bandia*, *ghushi*, *shaka*, and *asili*.

2. The causative suffix *-sha* / *-isha* generates productive verbal forms from *sahihi* (*kusahihisha*), *hakika* (*kuhakikisha*), *halali* (*kuhalalisha*), *haramu* (*kuharamisha*), *sawa* (*kusawazisha*), *dhahiri* (*kudhihirisha*), and *thibitisha* (already causative in form). It is not attested as productive for *kweli*, *uongo*, *bandia*, *shaka*, or *asili*.

3. The agentive nominal pattern *m-/wa-* + stem + *-aji* is attested for *sahihi* (*msahihishaji/wasahihishaji*) and *danganya* (*mdanganyifu/wadanganyifu*), and the simpler *m-/wa-* + stem pattern for *ghushi* (*mghushi/waghushi*) and *amini* (*mwaminifu/waaminifu*).

4. The passive suffix *-wa* and stative-potential suffix *-ika* are attested for *kudanganya* (*kudanganywa*, *kudanganyika*) and *dhahiri* (*kudhihirika*), but not identified for most other bases in this domain.

5. Several P1 items (*bandia*, *kweli kweli*, *bila shaka*, *haki ya kweli*, *wa asili*) show no attested morphological derivations, either because they are phrasal, because they function primarily as modifiers, or because the root does not participate in Swahili derivational morphology productively.
