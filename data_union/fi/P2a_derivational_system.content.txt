# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Finnish (fi)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested derivational morphology for the P1 base items. The P1b root expansion has already documented derivations from the three central roots (tosi/tode-, oikea/oikeu-, väärä/vääre-) in considerable detail. This step therefore focuses on the remaining P1 items not covered as base forms in P1b, while cross-referencing P1b material where a P1 item is itself a derived form that generates further derivations. Forms already fully documented in P1b are noted with a cross-reference rather than repeated in full.

---

## Item 1: tosi

**Base Form:** tosi
**Note:** Derivations from this base are documented in P1b Root 1. Cross-reference: P1b Root 1, items 1–30 and peripheral extensions. The most directly derived forms are: *totuus* (truth), *todellinen* (real), *todellisuus* (reality), *todistaa* (to prove/testify), *todentaa* (to verify), *todennäköinen* (probable). The stem alternation *tosi* → *tode-* is irregular and must be learned; it does not follow a fully predictable pattern. The root is nonetheless highly productive, generating adverbs, adjectives, abstract nouns, and verbs. The forms *todella*, *todellinen*, *todellisuus*, *todistaa*, *todentaa* are all in common use across registers.

**Note on *tosi*:** This base shows unusually high compounding productivity, particularly with nouns (*tosimaailma*, *tosielämä*, *tositapahtuma*, *tosiseikka*, *tosipohjainen*). The verbal derivation *todistaa* is itself highly productive (see P1 item 21 and its own derivational network below).

**Note on *totuus*:** Derivational productivity from *totuus* falls into two distinct clusters: (a) genitive-compound forms (*totuuden-* + *mukainen/vastainen*) used in legal and evaluative registers, and (b) direct compounds (*totuus* + noun) concentrated in technical philosophical and logical vocabulary. The *-llinen* derivation (*totuudellinen*) is marginal, suggesting that this adjectival suffix is not fully productive on this base. No common adjectival or verbal derivation directly from *totuus* as a base (rather than from *tosi/tode-*) has been identified beyond those listed.

## Item 3: aito

**Base Form:** aito

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitous
3. **Transliteration:** aitous
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -us (nominalizing suffix); note: *aitous* uses *-us* with the stem
5. **Natural English translation:** authenticity; genuineness
6. **Usage:** The standard nominal form for authenticity as an abstract property, used in philosophical, cultural, and commercial discourse (*tuotteen aitous* — "the authenticity of the product"; *tunteiden aitous* — "the genuineness of the emotions"). Appears in both evaluative and philosophical contexts. Already documented as P1 item 4; cross-referenced here as the primary nominal derivation from *aito*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aidosti
3. **Transliteration:** aidosti
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito (stem *aido-*) + -sti (adverbial suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** genuinely; authentically; truly
6. **Usage:** Used as a sentence adverb or modifier to assert that something is done or experienced in a genuine manner (*aidosti kiinnostunut* — "genuinely interested"; *aidosti suomalainen* — "authentically Finnish"; *aidosti erilainen* — "genuinely different"). Common in everyday, journalistic, and marketing registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitoutua
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -utua (reflexive/inchoative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to become authentic; to become genuine
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical and psychological discourse to describe a process by which something or someone comes to be or becomes authentic (*ihminen voi aitoutua kokemusten kautta* — "a person can become authentic through experiences"). Less common than *aitous* but attested in existentialist and humanistic contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitouttaa
3. **Transliteration:** aitouttaa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -uttaa (causative suffix); also analysed as *aitous* + causative *-ttaa*
5. **Natural English translation:** to authenticate; to make authentic; to certify as genuine
6. **Usage:** Used in administrative and commercial contexts to refer to the act of certifying or confirming that something is genuine (*aitouttaa asiakirja* — "to authenticate a document"). Less common than *todentaa* in administrative usage; found in art authentication and archival contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** epäaito
3. **Transliteration:** epäaito
4. **Literal Breakdown:** epä- (negation prefix) + aito
5. **Natural English translation:** inauthentic; not genuine; fake; spurious
6. **Usage:** The direct negation of *aito*. Applied to objects, emotions, and conduct. Used in philosophical, aesthetic, and everyday contexts (*epäaito käytös* — "inauthentic behavior"). Less common than *väärä* or *jäljitelmä* for physical fakes, but used for personal or existential inauthenticity. Already documented as P1 item 29; cross-referenced here as the primary prefixal negation of *aito*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** epäaitous
3. **Transliteration:** epäaitous
4. **Literal Breakdown:** epä- + aitous
5. **Natural English translation:** inauthenticity; lack of genuineness; spuriousness
6. **Usage:** The nominal form of *epäaito*, used in philosophical and cultural discourse to refer to the quality of being inauthentic (*epäaitous on eksistentialistinen käsite* — "inauthenticity is an existentialist concept"). Used in philosophical and aesthetic contexts to name the quality of being inauthentic. Less frequent than *epäaito* itself.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aidonkaltainen
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *aidon* (genitive of *aito*) + *kaltainen* (resembling, of the kind of)
5. **Natural English translation:** genuine-seeming; resembling the authentic; authentic-looking
6. **Usage:** Used to describe something that resembles or appears to be genuine without necessarily being so. Appears in evaluative and critical contexts, including art authentication and consumer protection.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Note on *aito*:** The derivational network of *aito* is relatively limited compared to *tosi* or *oikea*. The primary productive pattern is the *epä-* negation (*epäaito*, *epäaitous*). The *-us* abstract nominal (*aitous*) is well-attested. No verbal derivation directly from *aito* is identified; the related verbal concept is expressed periphrastically or through *todentaa*.
