## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**Total oppositions identified:** 35

**Distribution by type:**
- Lexical oppositions: 9 clear + 5 partial/uncertain = 14 total
- Morphological oppositions: 12 (primarily *lo* negation, plus *bilti-* and *i-* prefixes)
- Constructional oppositions: 9

**Key patterns:**

1. **Morphological negation dominance**: The most productive opposition pattern is simple *lo* negation, which applies to virtually all truth-domain adjectives and creates natural antonyms.

2. **Lexical gaps**: Several conceptual oppositions exist in Hebrew usage but are not attested in the collected data (e.g., *avel* "injustice," *boged* "traitor," *tsavu'a* "hypocrite"). These are marked as [UNCERTAIN] or partial oppositions.

3. **Register-based constructional variation**: Multiple constructional oppositions reflect register differences (colloquial vs. formal) rather than semantic oppositions per se.

4. **Primary lexical pair**: *Emet* vs. *sheker* is the fundamental lexical opposition, with all other oppositions being either derived (through negation) or domain-specific.

5. **Scalar vs. binary**: Some oppositions are truly binary (truth/falsehood, yes/no), while others are scalar (big mistake/small mistake, very correct/somewhat correct).

6. **Intentionality distinction**: Hebrew marks the opposition between intentional falsehood (*sheker*) and unintentional error (*ta'ut*) lexically, not just through adverbial modification.
