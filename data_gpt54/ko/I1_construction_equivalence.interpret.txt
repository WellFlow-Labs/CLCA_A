## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### Revelation replacing Becoming
Korean has a direct BECOME pattern with **되다**, as in **계약이 무효가 되었다**. However, in truth/falsity contexts, the language also uses **드러나다**, as in **그 주장은 거짓으로 드러났다** and **그 조직의 실체가 드러났다**. In the provided materials, **드러나다** does not simply mark change of state; it presents the status as becoming known or exposed. Descriptively, REVELATION often substitutes for BECOME when the relevant meaning is “turned out to be” rather than simple status change.

### Verification replacing Causation
The materials explicitly note that there is no single productive causative morphology dedicated to “make true/false/valid” across this domain. Instead, Korean uses lexical verbs such as **확인하다, 밝히다, 규명하다, 무효로 하다, 정정하다**. This means that where a canonical CAUSATION operator might be expected, Korean often uses VERIFICATION or REVELATION constructions instead, especially with **확인하다** and **밝히다**. Descriptively, verification/discovery procedures frequently stand in for dedicated factual causation.

### Negation: analytic rather than morphological
NEGATION is described as usually formed analytically with **아니다** or **-지 않다**. The examples **그 말은 사실이 아니다** and the note on **A-지 않다** show this clearly. Korean also uses lexical alternatives such as **사실과 다르다** and **허위이다**, but these are not described as dedicated morphological negation. So the negative operator is primarily analytic.

### Verification and Disconfirmation overlap
VERIFICATION is clearly expressed by **확인하다**, including **N 여부를 확인하다**. DISCONFIRMATION does not appear as a single dedicated construction in the materials. Instead, it is expressed through:
- analytic negation: **사실이 아니다**
- mismatch: **사실과 다르다**
- formal falsity labeling: **허위이다**
- revelation of falsity: **거짓으로 드러나다**
- correction/invalidation: **정정하다**, **무효로 하다**

So VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share one single construction, but they do overlap procedurally through the same lexical field of checking, revealing, and correcting.

### Positive/negative asymmetry
There is a clear asymmetry between positive and negative forms:
- Positive attribution is straightforward: **사실이다, 옳다, 정확하다, 유효하다**
- Negative meanings have several routes: **아니다, -지 않다, 사실과 다르다, 허위이다**
This makes the negative side more differentiated by register and discourse function.

### Agentive asymmetry
The materials explicitly note a lexicalized negative agent noun **거짓말쟁이** “liar,” but no equally standard productive positive counterpart meaning “truth-teller.” Positive meanings are expressed descriptively with **정직한 사람**, **진실한 사람**. This is a lexical asymmetry, not a complete absence of positive description.

### Multi-word expression normalization
Several operators are carried by multi-word expressions rather than single lexical items:
- **무효로 하다**: complement + light verb **하다**; functions verbally as causative invalidation
- **사실과 다르다**: noun phrase + comparative/mismatch predicate; functions as predicate of disconfirmation/negative attribution
- **부인할 수 없는 사실이다**: clausal/adnominal modifier + noun + copula; functions as assertive evidential attribution
- **N 여부를 확인하다**: nominal complement + lexical verb; functions verbally as verification
- **거짓으로 드러나다**: complement + intransitive revelation verb; functions verbally, often substituting for becoming/disconfirmation

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **CAUSATION (character)**: **MISSING (structural gap)**
  - No construction is provided for causing someone to become truthful/deceptive as a truth-domain operator.
- No other canonical category is fully absent in the provided materials.

### Lexical Gaps
- No equally standard productive positive counterpart to **거짓말쟁이**
  - P4a explicitly notes no equally standard truth-domain counterpart meaning “truth-teller.”
  - Positive alternatives are descriptive phrases: **정직한 사람**, **진실한 사람**.
- No single productive lexical causative meaning “make true/false/valid” across the domain
  - Instead, Korean uses several lexical/periphrastic verbs: **밝히다, 확인하다, 무효로 하다, 정정하다**.

### Conceptual Gaps
- No dedicated operator in the provided materials for “cause truth/falsity as character” distinct from descriptive labeling.
- No dedicated single disconfirmation operator isolated from negation, mismatch, revelation, and correction; the function is distributed across several constructions.

### Asymmetric Gaps
- Negative agentive is lexicalized: **거짓말쟁이**
- Positive agentive counterpart is not equally lexicalized; only descriptive phrases are given.
- Positive attribution has direct simple predicates; negative/disconfirming meanings are spread across several analytic and lexical constructions.
- BECOME is available with **되다**, but falsity/truth often appears through **드러나다**, creating an asymmetry toward revelation in contested truth contexts.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully available
- **ATTR**
- **ADNOM**
- **REVELATION**
- **VERIFICATION**
- **NEGATION**
- **NOMINALIZATION**

### Available with substitution
- **BECOME**
  - Available via **되다**
  - Frequently substituted by **드러나다** when the meaning is “turn out / be revealed as”
- **DISCONFIRMATION**
  - Expressed through **사실과 다르다**, **허위이다**, **거짓으로 드러나다**, **정정하다**, and analytic negation
  - No single dedicated construction isolated in the materials

### Partially available
- **CAUSATION (factual)**
  - Expressed through circumlocution and lexical/periphrastic verbs: **밝히다, 확인하다, 무효로 하다, 정정하다**
  - No dedicated productive causative morphology for this semantic domain

### Missing entirely
- **CAUSATION (character)**
  - **MISSING (structural gap)** in the provided materials

### Final normalized list
- **Fully available:** ATTR, ADNOM, REVELATION, VERIFICATION, NEGATION, NOMINALIZATION
- **Available with substitution:** BECOME, DISCONFIRMATION
- **Partially available:** CAUSATION (factual)
- **Missing entirely:** CAUSATION (character)
