# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

1. **kweli**
   - Literal meaning: truth, truthfulness
   - Typical English translations: true, truth, real, really, indeed
   - Usage: Used to affirm the accuracy or genuineness of statements and to express agreement with factual claims. Commonly appears in both everyday conversation and formal discourse to mark veracity.
   - [CORE]

2. **ukweli**
   - Literal meaning: u- (noun class prefix) + -kweli (truth root)
   - Typical English translations: truth, reality, fact
   - Usage: Nominal form referring to the state or quality of being true. Frequently used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts when discussing what is factually accurate or real.
   - [CORE]

3. **sahihi**
   - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ṣaḥīḥ) correct, sound
   - Typical English translations: correct, accurate, right, proper, authentic
   - Usage: Indicates correctness of information, accuracy of representations, or authenticity of documents. Widely used in educational, administrative, and everyday contexts to confirm accuracy.
   - [CORE]

4. **uongo**
   - Literal meaning: u- (noun class prefix) + -ongo (lie/falsehood root)
   - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
   - Usage: Refers to deliberate falsehood or deception. Used in moral and everyday discourse to characterize statements or representations as false or deceptive.
   - [CORE]

5. **-a kweli**
   - Literal meaning: of truth (associative construction)
   - Typical English translations: true, genuine, real, authentic
   - Usage: Adjectival phrase modifying nouns to indicate genuineness or authenticity. Common in expressions like "mtu wa kweli" (genuine person) or "habari za kweli" (true news).
   - [CORE]

6. **haki**
   - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ḥaqq) right, justice, truth
   - Typical English translations: right, justice, truth, entitlement, fairness
   - Usage: Used in legal, moral, and social contexts to refer to rights, justice, or what is morally correct. Frequently appears in discussions of fairness and legitimate claims.
   - [CORE]

7. **sawa**
   - Literal meaning: equal, same, level
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, okay, equal, same, appropriate
   - Usage: Indicates correctness, appropriateness, or agreement with a standard. Commonly used in everyday speech to confirm that something is right or acceptable.
   - [CORE]

8. **halisi**
   - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ḫāliṣ) pure, genuine
   - Typical English translations: genuine, authentic, real, pure, sincere
   - Usage: Emphasizes authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, or qualities. Used to distinguish real from fake or to indicate sincerity.
   - [CORE]

9. **-enye ukweli**
   - Literal meaning: having truth (possessive construction)
   - Typical English translations: truthful, veracious, true
   - Usage: Describes persons or statements as possessing the quality of truthfulness. Used attributively to characterize reliability or veracity.
   - [CORE]

10. **dhahiri**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ẓāhir) apparent, evident
    - Typical English translations: clear, evident, obvious, apparent, manifest
    - Usage: Indicates that something is clearly true or evident to observation. Used when the truth or reality of something is plainly visible or demonstrable.
    - [CORE]

11. **hakika**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ḥaqīqa) certainty, reality
    - Typical English translations: certain, sure, definite, indeed, really
    - Usage: Expresses certainty about the truth of a statement or the reality of a situation. Commonly used to emphasize confidence in accuracy or to confirm facts.
    - [CORE]

12. **bandia**
    - Literal meaning: artificial, manufactured
    - Typical English translations: fake, artificial, counterfeit, false, imitation
    - Usage: Characterizes objects or representations as not genuine or authentic. Used to identify counterfeits, imitations, or things that falsely represent themselves.
    - [CORE]

13. **-a uongo**
    - Literal meaning: of falsehood (associative construction)
    - Typical English translations: false, lying, deceptive, untrue
    - Usage: Adjectival phrase indicating falseness or deceptiveness. Applied to statements, persons, or representations that are characterized by untruth.
    - [CORE]

14. **hoja**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ḥujja) argument, proof
    - Typical English translations: argument, proof, evidence, reason
    - Usage: Refers to arguments or evidence presented to establish truth or correctness. Common in legal, academic, and debate contexts where claims require justification.
    - [CORE]

15. **ushahidi**
    - Literal meaning: u- (noun class prefix) + shahidi (witness) + abstract suffix
    - Typical English translations: evidence, testimony, witness, proof
    - Usage: Denotes evidence or testimony used to establish facts or truth. Frequently used in legal contexts and when discussing verification of claims.
    - [CORE]

16. **kamili**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic kāmil) complete, perfect
    - Typical English translations: complete, perfect, whole, entire, accurate
    - Usage: Indicates completeness or perfection, often implying accuracy or correctness in representation. Used to characterize things that fully meet standards or requirements.
    - [CORE]

17. **adili**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ʿādil) just, fair
    - Typical English translations: just, fair, equitable, righteous
    - Usage: Describes actions, persons, or judgments as morally right or fair. Common in ethical and legal discourse concerning proper conduct and fairness.
    - [CORE]

18. **-potoka**
    - Literal meaning: to go astray, to deviate
    - Typical English translations: to err, to be wrong, to go astray, to be mistaken
    - Usage: Indicates deviation from truth, correctness, or proper path. Used for both factual errors and moral transgressions.
    - [CORE]

19. **makosa**
    - Literal meaning: ma- (plural prefix) + kosa (error/mistake)
    - Typical English translations: mistakes, errors, faults, wrongs
    - Usage: Refers to errors, mistakes, or moral wrongs. Used in contexts ranging from factual inaccuracies to moral failings or offenses.
    - [CORE]

20. **safi**
    - Literal meaning: clean, pure
    - Typical English translations: clean, pure, clear, genuine, perfect
    - Usage: Beyond physical cleanliness, indicates purity, genuineness, or correctness. Used colloquially to affirm that something is right, clear, or without problems.
    - [CORE]

21. **madhubuti**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic maḍbūṭ) firm, precise
    - Typical English translations: reliable, accurate, precise, firm, trustworthy
    - Usage: Characterizes information, persons, or objects as reliable and accurate. Used when emphasizing dependability and precision.
    - [CORE]

22. **thibitisha**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic thabata) to confirm, to establish firmly
    - Typical English translations: to confirm, to verify, to establish, to prove
    - Usage: Refers to the act of confirming or establishing the truth of something. Common in contexts requiring verification or validation of claims.
    - [CORE]

23. **uhalisia**
    - Literal meaning: u- (noun class prefix) + halisi (genuine) + -a (abstract suffix)
    - Typical English translations: authenticity, genuineness, reality
    - Usage: Abstract noun referring to the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in discussions of authenticity and reality versus appearance.
    - [CORE]

24. **-danganya**
    - Literal meaning: to deceive, to trick
    - Typical English translations: to deceive, to lie, to trick, to cheat, to mislead
    - Usage: Describes the act of deliberately misleading or presenting false information. Common in moral discourse about dishonesty and deception.
    - [CORE]

25. **hukumu**
    - Literal meaning: (from Arabic ḥukm) judgment, ruling
    - Typical English translations: judgment, verdict, ruling, sentence
    - Usage: Refers to judicial or evaluative judgments about rightness, guilt, or correctness. Used in legal contexts and when making authoritative determinations.
    - [CORE]
