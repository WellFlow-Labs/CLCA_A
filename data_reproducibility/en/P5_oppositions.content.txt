## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**Total oppositions identified:** 60 (including 4 cases where no clear opposition exists)

**Distribution by contrast type:**
- **Morphological:** 38 oppositions (primarily using un-, in-, dis-, il- prefixes)
- **Lexical:** 15 oppositions (independent lexical items)
- **Constructional:** 7 oppositions (same construction with contrasting elements)

**Distribution by epistemic status:**
- **[CORE]:** 56 oppositions
- **[MARGINAL]:** 4 oppositions (veracious/mendacious, veracity/mendacity, falsifiable/unfalsifiable, and their derivatives)

**Key patterns observed:**

1. **Morphological negation dominates:** The majority of oppositions are formed through productive negative prefixation rather than independent lexical items.

2. **Multiple prefixes compete:** un- (Germanic), in-/il- (Latin), and dis- (Latin) all appear, with choice lexically determined by the base form's etymology and phonological properties.

3. **Asymmetries exist:** Not all positive terms have corresponding negative forms (e.g., "authenticate" lacks *"inauthenticate"; "bona fide" has no morphological negation).

4. **Lexical oppositions cluster in core vocabulary:** The most basic and frequent terms (true/false, right/wrong, real/fake) use independent lexical items rather than morphological negation.

5. **Constructional oppositions are limited:** Only 7 constructional oppositions identified, primarily involving phrasal verbs and fixed expressions with contrasting adjectives.

6. **Technical terms show standard patterns:** Even specialized terms (false positive/negative, falsifiable/unfalsifiable) follow the same morphological negation patterns as everyday vocabulary.

7. **Register stratification:** Formal/literary oppositions (veracious/mendacious) are marked as [MARGINAL] due to restricted usage compared to everyday equivalents (truthful/lying, honest/dishonest).
