# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X doğru/gerçek/yanlış (olmak)** — Copular construction with truth-value adjective as predicate. The copula *olmak* (to be) is often omitted in present tense, appearing as zero copula with third person subjects.

### Examples
- **Bu doğru** / bu doğru
  "This is true" (zero copula, present tense)
  
- **Söylediğin gerçek değil** / söylediğin gerçek değil
  "What you said is not true/real" (negative copula *değil*)

### Notes
Turkish uses zero copula for present tense positive attribution with third person. Negative attribution requires *değil*. Past and other tenses require overt *olmak*: *Bu doğruydu* "This was true." The adjectives *doğru*, *gerçek*, *yanlış*, *sahte* all function as predicates in this pattern.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X doğru/gerçek değil** — Negative copula *değil* follows the predicate adjective directly. No overt copula *olmak* appears in present tense negation.

### Examples
- **Bu doğru değil** / bu doğru değil
  "This is not true/correct"
  
- **Gerçek değil, yalan** / gerçek değil, yalan
  "It's not true, it's a lie"

### Notes
Turkish negative copula *değil* is invariant and follows the predicate. It contrasts with affirmative zero copula. For past tense: *doğru değildi* "was not true." The pattern applies uniformly across all truth-value adjectives.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + [NOUN]** — Truth-value adjectives directly precede nouns without intervening copula or relativization. No agreement marking.

### Examples
- **doğru bilgi** / doğru bilgi
  "correct information, true information"
  
- **yanlış anlama** / yanlış anlama
  "misunderstanding, wrong understanding"

### Notes
Turkish adjectives precede nouns and show no agreement. Truth-value terms freely modify abstract nouns (*bilgi* "information," *haber* "news," *söz* "word") and concrete nouns (*para* "money" in *sahte para* "counterfeit money"). The pattern is highly productive across all truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**X-e dönüşmek** / **X hâline gelmek** / **X çıkmak** — Multiple strategies for inchoative meaning: (1) dative + *dönüşmek* "transform into," (2) *hâline gelmek* "come to be in the state of," (3) *çıkmak* "emerge/turn out to be."

### Examples
- **Gerçeğe dönüştü** / gerçeğe dönüştü
  "It turned into reality" (dative + *dönüşmek*)
  
- **Doğru çıktı** / doğru çıktı
  "It turned out to be true" (*çıkmak* pattern)

### Notes
Turkish has multiple inchoative strategies. The *çıkmak* pattern (*doğru çıkmak*, *yanlış çıkmak*, *sahte çıkmak*) is particularly common for truth-value terms, expressing "turn out to be X." The *gerçekleşmek* pattern (gerçek + inchoative *-leş-*) specifically means "come true, be realized" and is highly productive.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Causative morphology limited; light verb constructions predominate** — Turkish has productive causative suffix *-(t)tır/-(t)tir*, but truth-domain uses light verbs (*etmek*, *yapmak*, *göstermek*) more than morphological causatives.

### Examples
- **Gerçekleştirmek** / gerçekleştirmek
  "to realize, to make real" (gerçek + *-leş-* inchoative + *-tir-* causative)
  
- **Doğru göstermek** / doğru göstermek
  "to show to be true, to demonstrate truth" (light verb construction)

### Notes
The main morphological causative in this domain is *gerçekleştirmek* (causative of *gerçekleşmek* "come true"). Most causative meanings use light verb constructions: *doğru kabul etmek* "to accept as true," *gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak* "to reveal the truth." The pattern *X göstermek* "show X" is common for demonstrative causation.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**[ADJ/NOUN] + -lık/-lik/-luk/-lük** — Highly productive abstract noun suffix with vowel harmony. Creates quality/state nouns from adjectives and abstract nouns.

### Examples
- **doğruluk** / doğruluk
  "truth, truthfulness, correctness" (doğru + *-luk*)
  
- **gerçeklik** / gerçeklik
  "reality, realness" (gerçek + *-lik*)

### Notes
The *-lık/-lik* suffix is the primary abstract nominalization strategy in Turkish truth vocabulary. Highly productive: *haklılık* "rightness," *haksızlık* "injustice," *güvenilirlik* "reliability," *tutarlılık* "consistency." The suffix shows four-way vowel harmony (*-lık/-lik/-luk/-lük*) based on the stem's vowels.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Limited agentive morphology; periphrastic constructions predominate** — Turkish has agentive suffix *-cı/-ci/-cu/-cü* but it rarely applies to truth-domain terms. Agent roles expressed periphrastically.

### Examples
- **yalancı** / yalancı
  "liar" (yalan + *-cı* agentive)
  
- **doğru söyleyen (kişi)** / doğru söyleyen (kişi)
  "truth-teller, one who tells the truth" (participle + optional *kişi* "person")

### Notes
Only *yalancı* "liar" shows productive agentive morphology in this domain. Truth-tellers and other agents are expressed with participles: *doğru söyleyen* "one who tells truth," *yalan söyleyen* "one who tells lies." The pattern *[VERB]-en kişi* "person who VERBs" is the standard periphrastic agentive construction. No dedicated morphological form exists for "truth-teller."

---

## 8. VERBAL NOUN FORMATION (ACTION NOMINALIZATION)

### Pattern
**[VERB] + -ma/-me** — Productive verbal noun suffix creates action/process nouns from verbs. Shows two-way vowel harmony.

### Examples
- **doğrulama** / doğrulama
  "verification, confirmation" (doğrula- + *-ma*)
  
- **gerçekleşme** / gerçekleşme
  "realization, occurrence" (gerçekleş- + *-me*)

### Notes
The *-ma/-me* suffix creates action nouns from verbs throughout the truth domain: *yanılma* "error, being mistaken," *aldatma* "deception," *inandırma* "convincing." This pattern is distinct from abstract quality nouns (*-lık/-lik*). Verbal nouns denote processes or acts, while *-lık/-lik* nouns denote states or qualities.

---

## 9. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *etmek*

### Pattern
**[NOUN] + etmek** — Highly productive pattern combining abstract nouns (often Arabic/Persian loans) with light verb *etmek* "to do/make" to create verbal expressions.

### Examples
- **hak etmek** / hak etmek
  "to deserve, to merit" (hak + etmek)
  
- **kabul etmek** / kabul etmek
  "to accept" (kabul + etmek, in *doğru kabul etmek* "accept as true")

### Notes
Light verb *etmek* is extremely productive with truth-domain nouns: *haksızlık etmek* "to wrong someone," *inkâr etmek* "to deny," *teslim etmek* "to acknowledge." This pattern is the primary strategy for verbalizing Arabic and Persian loan nouns. Native Turkish nouns typically take derivational morphology instead.

---

## 10. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *olmak*

### Pattern
**[NOUN/ADJ] + olmak** — Light verb *olmak* "to be/become" forms copular constructions (stative) and inchoative constructions (change of state).

### Examples
- **doğru olmak** / doğru olmak
  "to be true" (copular: stative)
  
- **gerçek olmak** / gerçek olmak
  "to be real" (copular: stative)

### Notes
*Olmak* functions both as copula (stative: *X doğru olmak* "X is true") and as inchoative (change: *gerçek olmak* can mean "become real" in some contexts). The copula is often zero in present tense third person (*Bu doğru* "This is true"), but overt in other tenses (*doğruydu* "was true") and with first/second person (*doğruyum* "I am right").

---

## 11. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *çıkmak*

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + çıkmak** — Light verb *çıkmak* "to come out, emerge" combines with truth-value adjectives to express "turn out to be X" or "be revealed as X."

### Examples
- **doğru çıkmak** / doğru çıkmak
  "to turn out to be true, to prove correct"
  
- **sahte çıkmak** / sahte çıkmak
  "to turn out to be fake, to be revealed as counterfeit"

### Notes
The *çıkmak* pattern is highly productive with truth-value adjectives: *haklı çıkmak* "be proven right," *haksız çıkmak* "be proven wrong," *yanlış çıkmak* "turn out wrong." This construction emphasizes the revelation or discovery of a truth-value that was previously uncertain or hidden. It is distinct from simple copular *olmak*.

---

## 12. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *vermek*

### Pattern
**[NOUN-POSS-ACC] + vermek** — Light verb *vermek* "to give" combines with possessed accusative forms to express granting, acknowledging, or doing justice to something.

### Examples
- **hak vermek** / hak vermek
  "to agree with, to acknowledge someone is right" (hak + vermek)
  
- **hakkını vermek** / hakkını vermek
  "to give credit, to do justice to" (hak + 3SG.POSS + ACC + vermek)

### Notes
The *vermek* pattern with *hak* is particularly productive: *hak vermek* "agree with/side with," *hakkını vermek* "give due credit." The construction often involves possessive marking on the noun (*hakkını* = hak-ı-nı "his/her/its right-POSS-ACC"). This pattern expresses acknowledgment or granting of what is due.

---

## 13. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *almak*

### Pattern
**[NOUN-POSS-ACC] + almak** — Light verb *almak* "to take" combines with possessed accusative forms to express obtaining or claiming what is due.

### Examples
- **hakkını almak** / hakkını almak
  "to get one's due, to obtain justice" (hak + 3SG.POSS + ACC + almak)
  
- **intikamını almak** / intikamını almak
  "to take revenge" (not truth-specific, but shows pattern)

### Notes
The *almak* pattern is less productive in truth domain than *vermek*, but *hakkını almak* "get one's due" is common. The pattern emphasizes active claiming or obtaining of what one is entitled to, contrasting with *vermek* which emphasizes granting or acknowledging.

---

## 14. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *söylemek*

### Pattern
**[NOUN-ACC] + söylemek** — Light verb *söylemek* "to say/tell" combines with accusative-marked truth-value nouns to express truthful or false speech.

### Examples
- **doğruyu söylemek** / doğruyu söylemek
  "to tell the truth" (doğru + ACC + söylemek)
  
- **yalan söylemek** / yalan söylemek
  "to tell a lie" (yalan + söylemek, no accusative marking)

### Notes
The pattern shows variation in accusative marking: *doğruyu söylemek* (with ACC *-yu*) vs. *yalan söylemek* (no ACC). The construction *doğrusunu söylemek* (doğru + 3SG.POSS + ACC) means "to tell the truth, to be frank" and is idiomatic. *Gerçeği söylemek* "tell the truth" also follows this pattern.

---

## 15. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *bulmak*

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + bulmak** — Light verb *bulmak* "to find" combines with adjectives to express judgment or evaluation as finding something to have a quality.

### Examples
- **doğru bulmak** / doğru bulmak
  "to find correct, to approve of, to consider right"
  
- **uygun bulmak** / uygun bulmak
  "to find appropriate, to consider suitable"

### Notes
The *bulmak* pattern expresses subjective evaluation or judgment. *Doğru bulmak* means "to judge as correct/right" rather than "to discover truth." Similar constructions include *uygun bulmak* "find appropriate," *yerinde bulmak* "find apt/justified." This pattern emphasizes the evaluator's judgment.

---

## 16. LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *görmek*

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + görmek** — Light verb *görmek* "to see" combines with adjectives to express judgment, similar to *bulmak* but slightly more formal.

### Examples
- **uygun görmek** / uygun görmek
  "to deem appropriate, to consider suitable"
  
- **doğru görmek** / doğru görmek
  "to consider right, to deem correct"

### Notes
The *görmek* pattern is semantically similar to *bulmak* but tends toward more formal register. *Uygun görmek* "deem appropriate" is particularly common in official and formal contexts. The pattern emphasizes the act of judgment or evaluation.

---

## 17. POSTPOSITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *hakkında*

### Pattern
**[NOUN-GEN] + hakkında** — Grammaticalized postposition *hakkında* "about, concerning" derives historically from *hak* "right" but no longer functions in truth-evaluation.

### Examples
- **bu konu hakkında** / bu konu hakkında
  "about this subject" (bu konu + hakkında)
  
- **gerçek hakkında** / gerçek hakkında
  "about the truth/reality" (gerçek + hakkında)

### Notes
*Hakkında* is fully grammaticalized as a postposition meaning "about, concerning" and does not participate in truth-evaluation constructions. It derives from *hak-ı-n-da* (right-POSS-GEN-LOC) but is synchronically opaque. This represents complete semantic bleaching from "right" to "aboutness."

---

## 18. ABLATIVE ADVERBIAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[NOUN/ADJ] + -dan/-den/-tan/-ten** — Ablative case marker creates adverbs from nouns and adjectives, particularly common with truth-value terms.

### Examples
- **gerçekten** / gerçekten
  "really, truly" (gerçek + ABL *-ten*)
  
- **doğrudan** / doğrudan
  "directly" (doğru + ABL *-dan*)

### Notes
The ablative creates manner adverbs from truth-value terms: *gerçekten* "really," *hakikaten* "truly," *doğrudan* "directly." The pattern is productive but somewhat lexicalized—not all truth-terms freely form ablative adverbs. *Yalandan* "falsely, pretending" shows the pattern with *yalan* "lie."

---

## 19. INSTRUMENTAL ADVERBIAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[NOUN-POSS] + -yla/-yle** — Instrumental case on possessed forms creates manner adverbials.

### Examples
- **hakkıyla** / hakkıyla
  "properly, duly, as it deserves" (hak + 3SG.POSS + INST)
  
- **tam anlamıyla** / tam anlamıyla
  "in the full sense, completely" (tam anlam + 3SG.POSS + INST)

### Notes
The instrumental pattern creates manner adverbials emphasizing proper or complete manner. *Hakkıyla* (from *hak* "right/due") means "properly, giving it its due." The pattern often involves possessive marking before the instrumental case. This construction is productive with abstract nouns.

---

## 20. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + -dan daha + [ADJ]** — Standard Turkish comparative uses ablative *-dan* "from/than" + *daha* "more" + adjective. Truth-value adjectives participate but with limited productivity.

### Examples
- **yalandan daha kötü** / yalandan daha kötü
  "worse than a lie" (yalan + ABL + daha + kötü)
  
- **gerçekten daha garip** / gerçekten daha garip
  "stranger than reality/truth" (gerçek + ABL + daha + garip)

### Notes
Truth-value adjectives rarely appear as the compared element (*daha doğru* "more true" is uncommon), but frequently appear in the standard of comparison (*gerçekten daha X* "more X than reality"). Turkish does not have dedicated comparative forms for truth-value terms; they are treated as absolute rather than gradable in most contexts.

---

## 21. EQUATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *gibi*

### Pattern
**[NOUN] + gibi** — Postposition *gibi* "like, as" creates similes and approximations. Common with truth-value nouns.

### Examples
- **gerçek gibi** / gerçek gibi
  "like real, as if real, realistic"
  
- **yalan gibi** / yalan gibi
  "like a lie, unbelievable, incredible"

### Notes
The *gibi* pattern creates similes with truth-value nouns. *Gerçek gibi* "like real" evaluates verisimilitude. *Yalan gibi* "like a lie" idiomatically means "unbelievable, incredible" (often for surprising truths). The pattern is productive and common in everyday speech.

---

## 22. QUESTION PARTICLE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + mı/mi/mu/mü** — Question particle with four-way vowel harmony creates yes/no questions. Productive with truth-value adjectives.

### Examples
- **Doğru mu?** / doğru mu
  "Is it true? Is it correct?"
  
- **Gerçek mi?** / gerçek mi
  "Is it real? Is it true?"

### Notes
The question particle *mı/mi/mu/mü* attaches to truth-value adjectives to create verification questions. The pattern *doğru mu yanlış mı* "true or false?" uses double question particles for alternative questions. This is the standard interrogative strategy for truth-value assessment in Turkish.

---

## 23. EMPHATIC REDUPLICATION

### Pattern
**[WORD] + [WORD]** or **[WORD] + alliterative variant** — Turkish uses various reduplication patterns for emphasis, including exact reduplication and alliterative/rhyming variants.

### Examples
- **doğru düzgün** / doğru düzgün
  "proper, decent" (alliterative pair: doğru + düzgün)
  
- **yalan dolan** / yalan dolan
  "full of lies" (rhyming pair: yalan + dolan)

### Notes
Turkish uses alliterative and rhyming pairs for emphasis in truth domain: *doğru düzgün*, *doğru dürüst*, *yalan yanlış*, *yalan dolan*. These are partially lexicalized collocations rather than productive patterns. The second element may be semantically related (*düzgün* "proper") or phonologically motivated (*dolan* from *dolmak* "to fill").

---

## 24. NEGATIVE POLARITY CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ] + değil** (negative copula) or **[VERB-NEG]** — Turkish marks negation on predicates (copula *değil*) or verbs (negative suffix *-ma/-me*).

### Examples
- **Doğru değil** / doğru değil
  "It's not true" (doğru + negative copula)
  
- **Gerçek olamaz** / gerçek olamaz
  "It can't be real" (gerçek + ol-a-maz be-ABIL-NEG)

### Notes
Negative copula *değil* is invariant and follows predicates. Verbal negation uses *-ma/-me* before tense/mood markers: *doğrulamadı* "didn't verify," *gerçekleşmedi* "didn't come true." The pattern *X olamaz* "cannot be X" expresses impossibility or disbelief regarding truth-value.

---

## 25. CONDITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[VERB-COND] / [ADJ + ise]** — Conditional marked by *-sa/-se* on verbs or *ise* (often cliticized as *-ysa/-yse*) after nominals/adjectives.

### Examples
- **Doğruysa** / doğruysa
  "If it's true" (doğru + conditional *-ysa*)
  
- **Gerçek olsaydı** / gerçek olsaydı
  "If it were real" (gerçek + ol-sa-ydı be-COND-PAST, counterfactual)

### Notes
Conditional *-sa/-se* attaches to verb stems; *ise* (often reduced to *-ysa/-yse*) attaches to nominals and adjectives. The pattern *doğruysa* "if true" is common in conditional reasoning. Past conditional *-saydı* creates counterfactuals: *gerçek olsaydı* "if it were real (but it's not)."

---

## 26. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[ADJ/VERB] + -sa/-se + bile/dahi** — Conditional marker + concessive particle *bile* "even" or *dahi* (formal) creates "even if" constructions.

### Examples
- **Doğru olsa bile** / doğru olsa bile
  "Even if it's true" (doğru + ol-sa + bile)
  
- **Gerçek olsa dahi** / gerçek olsa dahi
  "Even if it were real" (gerçek + ol-sa + dahi, formal)

### Notes
Concessive constructions use conditional *-sa/-se* plus particle *bile* (colloquial) or *dahi* (formal). The pattern expresses "even if X is true/real, Y still holds." This construction is common in argumentative contexts where truth-value is acknowledged but deemed insufficient.

---

## 27. CAUSAL/REASON CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[CLAUSE] + için** or **[CLAUSE-dığı için]** — Postposition *için* "for, because of" or nominalized clause + *için* expresses reason/cause.

### Examples
- **Doğru olduğu için** / doğru olduğu için
  "Because it's true" (doğru + ol-duğu + için)
  
- **Gerçek için** / gerçek için
  "For the truth, for reality" (gerçek + için)

### Notes
Causal constructions use *için* "for, because of" with nominalized clauses (*-dığı için*) or bare nouns. The pattern *X olduğu için* "because X is (the case)" is standard for causal reasoning. *Gerçek için* "for the truth" expresses purpose or dedication to truth.

---

## 28. TEMPORAL CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *zaman*

### Pattern
**[CLAUSE-dığı] + zaman** — Nominalized clause + *zaman* "time, when" creates temporal subordinate clauses.

### Examples
- **Gerçeği öğrendiği zaman** / gerçeği öğrendiği zaman
  "When he/she learned the truth" (gerçek-i öğren-diği zaman)
  
- **Doğru olduğu zaman** / doğru olduğu zaman
  "When it's true" (doğru ol-duğu zaman)

### Notes
Temporal clauses use nominalized verbs (*-dığı*) + *zaman* "time/when." The pattern is productive with truth-domain verbs: *gerçekleştiği zaman* "when it comes true," *doğrulandığı zaman* "when it's verified." This is the standard temporal subordination strategy in Turkish.

---

## 29. RELATIVE CLAUSE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[VERB-PARTICIPLE] + [NOUN]** — Participles (*-an/-en*, *-dık/-dik*, *-acak/-ecek*) function as relative clauses, preceding the head noun.

### Examples
- **doğru söyleyen kişi** / doğru söyleyen kişi
  "person who tells the truth" (doğru söyle-yen kişi)
  
- **gerçeği bilen** / gerçeği bilen
  "one who knows the truth" (gerçek-i bil-en)

### Notes
Turkish uses participial relative clauses that precede head nouns. The aorist participle *-an/-en* indicates habitual/characteristic action: *doğru söyleyen* "truth-telling (one)." Past participle *-dık/-dik* indicates completed action. No relative pronoun exists; the participle directly modifies the noun.

---

## 30. COMPLEMENT CLAUSE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**[CLAUSE-dığını] + [VERB]** — Accusative-marked nominalized clause functions as complement to verbs of knowing, saying, believing.

### Examples
- **Doğru olduğunu biliyorum** / doğru olduğunu biliyorum
  "I know that it's true" (doğru ol-duğu-nu bil-iyor-um)
  
- **Gerçek olduğunu söyledi** / gerçek olduğunu söyledi
  "He/she said that it's real" (gerçek ol-duğu-nu söyle-di)

### Notes
Complement clauses use nominalized verbs with accusative case (*-dığını*) before matrix verbs like *bilmek* "know," *söylemek* "say," *düşünmek* "think." The pattern is highly productive: *yanlış olduğunu anladım* "I understood that it was wrong." This is the standard complementation strategy for propositional attitudes in Turkish.
