# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 lexical items. "Multi-word constructions" are understood here as syntactically separable expressions — periphrastic predicates, fixed collocations, idiomatic phrases, and syntactically-driven patterns — that are not reducible to single-word derivations (documented in P2a) or to simple compositional noun phrases. The boundary between a "construction" and a "collocation" is not always sharp; where a multi-word sequence shows fixedness, non-compositionality, or domain-specific conventionalization, it is included.

The following categories are represented where attested:
- **Verb-object collocations** (N + 을/를 + V): e.g., 진실을 말하다
- **Predicative collocations** (N + 이/가 + Adj/V): e.g., 신빙성이 있다
- **Adverbial collocations** (N + particle + adverbial function)
- **Idiomatic expressions** (fixed multi-word units with non-compositional or semi-compositional meaning)
- **Syntactic frames** (productive patterns in which the base form participates)

Constructions already documented in P1b (root expansion) are cross-referenced but not repeated in full. The focus here is on constructions not yet captured in prior steps.

Absence is recorded explicitly where no common construction is identified for a given base item.

## Constructions by P1 Base Item

---

### 1. Base Form: 진실 (jinsil)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 말하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul malhada
4. **English Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** The most basic and frequent verbal construction with 진실. Used across all registers in moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Appears in both affirmative and negative forms (진실을 말하지 않다, to not tell the truth). Cross-referenced from P1b.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 밝히다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul balkhida
4. **English Translation:** to reveal the truth, to bring the truth to light
5. **Usage Notes:** A highly conventionalized collocation used in investigative, journalistic, and legal contexts to describe the act of uncovering or disclosing what is actually the case. 밝히다 (to illuminate, to clarify) is the preferred verb in this collocation; contrasts with 진실을 말하다 in that it implies prior concealment. Cross-referenced from P1b.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 숨기다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul sumgida
4. **English Translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe deliberate concealment of what is actually the case. Common in journalistic, legal, narrative, and interpersonal contexts. Contrasts with 진실을 밝히다 as the negative counterpart; frequently appears in opposition to it.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 알다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul alda
4. **English Translation:** to know the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Verb-object collocation describing the epistemic state of knowing what is actually the case. Common across all registers; frequently appears in narrative and investigative contexts. Appears in constructions such as 진실을 알고 싶다 (to want to know the truth) and 진실을 알게 되다 (to come to know the truth).
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 외면하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul oemyeonhada
4. **English Translation:** to turn away from the truth, to ignore the truth, to avert one's eyes from the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to characterize willful avoidance of an uncomfortable or inconvenient truth. 외면하다 (to turn one's face away) carries a moral dimension of culpable avoidance. Common in political, ethical, journalistic, and moral contexts. Carries a critical or accusatory tone.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실을 왜곡하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eul waegokada
4. **English Translation:** to distort the truth, to twist the truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe the misrepresentation of facts. 왜곡하다 (to distort, to warp) implies deliberate or systematic misrepresentation rather than simple error. The pairing of 진실 with 왜곡하다 is highly conventionalized.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실이 밝혀지다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-i balkhyeojida
4. **English Translation:** the truth comes to light, the truth is revealed, the truth is uncovered
5. **Usage Notes:** Passive/resultative construction describing the emergence of truth, typically after a period of concealment or uncertainty. The passive/spontaneous counterpart to 진실을 밝히다. Common in narrative, journalistic, and legal contexts. The subject is 진실 rather than an agent. Carries a slightly more agentive implication than 진실이 드러나다 — the truth is uncovered through investigation or disclosure rather than simply emerging.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실이 드러나다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-i deureonada
4. **English Translation:** the truth emerges, the truth is exposed, the truth comes to light
5. **Usage Notes:** Predicative collocation describing the emergence of truth, often in contexts where it had previously been concealed. Similar to 진실이 밝혀지다 but 드러나다 (to be exposed, to surface) carries a slightly stronger implication of something previously hidden becoming visible. Common in investigative, journalistic, and narrative contexts. The subject is 진실 and the verb 드러나다 is intransitive.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실과 거짓을 가리다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-gwa geojit-eul garida
4. **English Translation:** to distinguish truth from falsehood, to separate truth from lies
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed collocation framing the epistemic task of discrimination between true and false. 가리다 (to distinguish, to determine) is the standard verb in this construction. Appears in legal, journalistic, and philosophical contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실과 거짓을 구별하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil gwa geojit-eul gubyeolhada
4. **English Translation:** to distinguish truth from falsehood
5. **Usage Notes:** Verb-object collocation framing the epistemic task of differentiating true from false claims. Common in educational, philosophical, and journalistic discourse. The paired structure 진실과 거짓 (truth and falsehood) is itself a fixed nominal pair.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실과 거짓 사이
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-gwa geojit sai
4. **English Translation:** between truth and falsehood
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed framing construction used in titles, discourse, and analytical contexts to position something in the space between truth and lie. The pairing 진실과 거짓 is highly conventionalized as a polar opposition.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실의 순간
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-ui sungan
4. **English Translation:** moment of truth
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe a decisive moment when the true nature of something or someone is revealed. Likely influenced by the Spanish/English expression "moment of truth." Used in everyday and journalistic Korean to describe situations where the true state of affairs becomes apparent or a critical decision must be made. Documented in P1b; cross-referenced here as a fixed nominal construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실은 하나다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-eun hanada
4. **English Translation:** there is only one truth, the truth is singular
5. **Usage Notes:** A formulaic assertion used in investigative, legal, and popular culture contexts (notably associated with the detective fiction genre in Korean popular culture) to assert that facts are determinate and singular. Functions as a rhetorical claim rather than a philosophical proposition in most uses.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실만이 살 길이다
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil-mani sal gil-ida
4. **English Translation:** truth is the only way to survive, only the truth will save us
5. **Usage Notes:** Proverbial expression used in moral and political discourse to assert that honesty is the only viable course of action in a difficult situation. The construction is formulaic and appears in public speech and media commentary.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**

1. **Base Form:** 진실 (jinsil)
2. **Example:** 진실 규명
3. **Transliteration:** jinsil gyumyeong
4. **English Translation:** truth investigation, clarification of the truth, truth-finding
5. **Usage Notes:** Noun-noun compound used in political, legal, and historical contexts to refer to formal processes of establishing what actually happened. Cross-referenced from P1b.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 2. Base Form: 사실 (sasil)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 확인하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul hwagin-hada
4. **English Translation:** to verify the facts, to confirm the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard collocation for the act of checking whether reported information is accurate. Common in journalistic, administrative, legal, and everyday contexts. The basis for the compound 사실확인 (fact-checking) documented in P1b; here as a verbal construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실에 근거하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-e geungeo-hada
4. **English Translation:** to be based on facts, to be grounded in fact
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to characterize a claim, argument, or representation as having factual foundation. Common in academic, journalistic, and legal discourse. The prepositional phrase 사실에 (on/in fact) combines with the verb 근거하다 (to be grounded in). Connects 사실 and 근거 from P1.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 왜곡하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul waegokada
4. **English Translation:** to distort the facts, to misrepresent the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and political discourse to describe deliberate misrepresentation of factual information. Parallel in structure to 진실을 왜곡하다 but focuses on specific facts rather than truth as an abstract value. Connects 사실 and 왜곡 from P1.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 직시하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul jiksihada
4. **English Translation:** to face the facts squarely, to look the facts in the face
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the act of confronting reality without avoidance or self-deception. 직시하다 (to look directly at) implies a deliberate, unflinching engagement with what is actually the case. Common in motivational, political, and analytical discourse. Carries a connotation of unflinching acknowledgment.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실이 아니다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-i anida
4. **English Translation:** it is not a fact, that is not true, that is not the case
5. **Usage Notes:** The standard negation construction for factual claims. Extremely common across all registers as a denial or correction. More neutral in register than 거짓말이다 (that is a lie). Fully compositional but highly conventionalized as a denial formula.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실로 드러나다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-ro deureonada
4. **English Translation:** to turn out to be true, to be revealed as fact
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the confirmation of a claim that was previously uncertain or disputed. The construction marks a transition from alleged or suspected to established. Common in journalistic and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실로 밝혀지다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-ro balkhyeojida
4. **English Translation:** to turn out to be true, to be revealed as fact
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in journalistic and everyday contexts to describe the confirmation of a claim as factually accurate. The construction 사실로 + 밝혀지다/드러나다 is highly conventionalized.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실 여부를 따지다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil yeobu-reul ttajida
4. **English Translation:** to examine whether something is factual, to question the factual basis of something
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in evaluative, investigative, legal, and journalistic contexts to describe the act of scrutinizing whether a claim is actually true. 따지다 (to examine closely, to question) implies critical scrutiny. 여부 (whether or not) is a productive element in formal Korean discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 말하자면
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul malhajamyeon
4. **English Translation:** to tell the truth, if I'm being honest, speaking factually, the fact of the matter is
5. **Usage Notes:** Discourse-framing construction used to introduce a frank or corrective statement. Functions as a hedging or truth-marking device at the beginning of an utterance, signaling that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment. Common in spoken and written registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 엄연한 사실
3. **Transliteration:** eomyeonhan sasil
4. **English Translation:** an undeniable fact, a plain fact, an incontrovertible fact
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed nominal collocation using 엄연한 (undeniable/plain/manifest) as an intensifying modifier. Used to assert that something is unambiguously factual and cannot be disputed. Common in argumentative and rhetorical contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실과 다르다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-gwa dareuda
4. **English Translation:** to differ from the facts, to be contrary to the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** A standard construction used in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts to characterize a claim as factually incorrect without necessarily implying deliberate deception. Appears in formal corrections and rebuttals.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실을 은폐하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil-eul eunpye hada
4. **English Translation:** to conceal the facts, to cover up the facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Verb-object collocation describing deliberate suppression of factual information. Common in journalistic, legal, and political discourse; carries a stronger implication of institutional or deliberate concealment than 사실을 숨기다.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**

1. **Base Form:** 사실 (sasil)
2. **Example:** 사실관계를 파악하다
3. **Transliteration:** sasil gwangye-reul paakada
4. **English Translation:** to grasp the factual circumstances, to establish the facts of the case
5. **Usage Notes:** Verb-object collocation used in legal and journalistic contexts to describe the act of determining the factual basis of a situation. 사실관계 is documented in P1b; this collocation with 파악하다 is its most common verbal partner.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 3. Base Form: 진짜 (jinjja)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜인지 가짜인지 모르다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja-inji gajja-inji moreuda
4. **English Translation:** not knowing whether it is real or fake, unable to tell the genuine from the counterfeit
5. **Usage Notes:** Fixed interrogative-embedded construction used to express uncertainty about the authenticity of an item, person, or claim. The paired 진짜/가짜 structure is formulaic. Common in everyday speech and consumer contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜 같다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja gatda
4. **English Translation:** it seems real, it looks genuine, it appears to be the real thing, it appears authentic
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to express the appearance of genuineness without full commitment to its being genuine. 같다 (to seem, to appear like) marks epistemic tentativeness. Common in everyday evaluative speech about objects, performances, and claims. The -같다 construction is productive with many nominal bases.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜로 받아들이다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja-ro badadeulida
4. **English Translation:** to take as genuine, to accept as real
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the cognitive act of treating something as authentic or true. Common in contexts of credulity, trust, or deliberate acceptance. The construction 진짜로 (as genuine/real) functions adverbially. Appears in contexts where the speaker notes that something false was mistakenly accepted as genuine.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜 실력을 발휘하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja sillyeok-eul balhwihada
4. **English Translation:** to show one's true ability, to demonstrate one's real skill
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe the display of genuine competence as opposed to performance or pretense. 진짜 functions as an attributive modifier emphasizing authenticity of the quality being displayed. Common in sports, professional, and educational contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 이게 진짜야?
3. **Transliteration:** ige jinjjaya?
4. **English Translation:** Is this real? Is this for real?
5. **Usage Notes:** A highly frequent colloquial interrogative construction used to express surprise or disbelief and to question the authenticity or truth of something. Functions both as a genuine question and as an exclamation of surprise. Extremely common in spoken Korean.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 진짜 (jinjja)
2. **Example:** 진짜 맞다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja matda
4. **English Translation:** it's really true, that's genuinely correct
5. **Usage Notes:** Colloquial emphatic collocation used to assert that something is genuinely the case. 진짜 functions as an adverbial intensifier modifying 맞다 (to be correct/right). Extremely common in informal spoken Korean.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on 진짜:** Beyond the constructions above, 진짜 functions very productively as an intensifying adverb (진짜 좋다, "really good"; 진짜 힘들다, "really difficult") across all registers. This adverbial use is fully productive and not a fixed construction; it is noted here but not enumerated as individual constructions. The base form functions as both adjective and adverb and participates freely in nominal and adverbial positions. Its multi-word constructions are largely productive syntactic combinations rather than fixed idioms. The constructions above represent the most frequently attested fixed or semi-fixed patterns.

### 4. Base Form: 가짜 (gajja)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜로 판명되다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-ro panmyeongdoeda
4. **English Translation:** to be determined to be fake, to be found to be counterfeit
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal, journalistic, and commercial contexts to describe the official or investigative determination that an item or claim is not genuine. 판명되다 (to be determined, to be found to be) marks an authoritative conclusion.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜를 진짜로 속이다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-reul jinjja-ro sogida
4. **English Translation:** to pass off a fake as genuine, to deceive by presenting a counterfeit as real
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of fraudulent misrepresentation of an inauthentic item as genuine. The construction 가짜를 진짜로 (fake as real) is a fixed contrastive frame. Common in consumer fraud and art forgery contexts.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜 뉴스를 퍼뜨리다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja nyuseu-reul peotteurida
4. **English Translation:** to spread fake news, to disseminate false information
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in contemporary media and political discourse to describe the deliberate circulation of fabricated or misleading news content. 퍼뜨리다 (to spread, to disseminate) is the standard verb in this construction.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜인 줄 알다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-in jul alda
4. **English Translation:** to know that it is fake, to be aware that it is not genuine
5. **Usage Notes:** Used to describe epistemic awareness of inauthenticity. The construction -인 줄 알다 (to know that something is ~) is a standard epistemic frame. Common in legal contexts where knowledge of falsity is relevant to culpability.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜인 줄 몰랐다
3. **Transliteration:** gajja-in jul mollassda
4. **English Translation:** I didn't know it was fake, I was unaware it was counterfeit
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to assert ignorance of inauthenticity, often as a defense or explanation. The construction 인 줄 몰랐다 (didn't know that it was) is a productive syntactic pattern applied here to 가짜.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 가짜 뉴스
3. **Transliteration:** gajja nyuseu
4. **English Translation:** fake news
5. **Usage Notes:** Nominal collocation referring to deliberately false or misleading news content. Entered Korean public discourse prominently from approximately 2016–2017 onward, largely parallel to international usage. Now well-established in journalistic and political discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** 가짜 (gajja)
2. **Example:** 진짜와 가짜를 구별하다
3. **Transliteration:** jinjja wa gajja-reul gubyeolhada
4. **English Translation:** to distinguish the genuine from the fake
5. **Usage Notes:** Verb-object collocation framing the task of authenticity discrimination. The paired nominal structure 진짜와 가짜 (the genuine and the fake) is a fixed nominal pair that appears frequently in this and related constructions.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### 5. Base Form: 정확 (jeonghwak)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확히 말하면
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwaki malhamyeon
4. **English Translation:** to be precise, strictly speaking, to put it exactly
5. **Usage Notes:** Discourse-framing construction used to introduce a more precise or corrective formulation of a claim. Functions as a precision marker at the beginning of a clause. Common in academic, journalistic, and everyday speech. Cross-referenced from P2a.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확히 말하자면
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwaki malhajamyeon
4. **English Translation:** to be precise, to put it exactly, strictly speaking
5. **Usage Notes:** A fixed discourse-framing construction used to introduce a more precise or corrective formulation. Functions as a pragmatic marker signaling increased precision. Common across registers.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확한 정보를 제공하다
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwakan jeongbo-reul jegonghada
4. **English Translation:** to provide accurate information
5. **Usage Notes:** Standard collocation in journalistic, administrative, public health, and public communication contexts. 정확한 (accurate, attributive form of 정확하다) modifies 정보 (information); 제공하다 (to provide) is the standard verb. The collocation 정확한 정보 is itself highly conventionalized (cross-referenced from P1b).
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확한 사실을 파악하다
3. **Transliteration:** jeonghwakan sasil-eul paakada
4. **English Translation:** to grasp the accurate facts, to ascertain the precise facts
5. **Usage Notes:** Used in investigative and analytical contexts to describe the act of establishing what is precisely the case. 파악하다 (to grasp, to ascertain) implies active cognitive effort. Common in journalistic and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** 정확 (jeonghwak)
2. **Example:** 정확도가 높다 / 정확도가 낮다
