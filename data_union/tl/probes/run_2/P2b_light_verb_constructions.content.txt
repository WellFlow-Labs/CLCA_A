# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Preliminary note:** This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve the P1 lexical items and are not reducible to the derivational forms documented in P2a. The boundary between a "construction" and a "collocation" is not always sharp in Tagalog; the criterion applied here is that the multi-word unit carries a meaning or function not fully predictable from its parts alone, or that it represents a fixed or semi-fixed expression with stable discourse function. Purely compositional phrases (e.g., *totoo ang sagot* — "the answer is true") are not documented as constructions unless they show fixedness or pragmatic specialization. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded.

Forms from P1b root expansion that are multi-word phrases are reviewed here; those that qualify as constructions under the above criterion are documented. Those that are purely compositional noun phrases (e.g., *totoong kwento*, *maling datos*) are noted as not qualifying unless they show idiomatic or pragmatic specialization beyond their compositional meaning.

## Base Form: *totoo*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Totoo ba iyan?
3. **Transliteration:** Totoo ba iyan?
4. **English translation:** Is that true? / Is that real?
5. **Usage notes:** The construction *totoo ba* + demonstrative/noun phrase is the standard interrogative formula for challenging or requesting confirmation of a claim. The particle *ba* marks the utterance as a yes/no question; the construction as a whole functions as a fixed epistemic inquiry formula across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Totoo na.
3. **Transliteration:** Totoo na.
4. **English translation:** It's true now. / It's actually happening. / It's become real.
5. **Usage notes:** The combination of *totoo* with the particle *na* (marking change of state or current relevance) is a fixed conversational formula used to mark that something previously doubted, anticipated, or hypothetical has been confirmed or has come to pass. The pragmatic force is distinct from simple predication with *totoo*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Totoo naman.
3. **Transliteration:** Totoo naman.
4. **English translation:** It's true, though. / Well, that's actually true. / That is real, after all.
5. **Usage notes:** The particle *naman* introduces a concessive or turn-managing function; this construction is used to concede a point in dialogue, often after a preceding challenge or contrary claim. The combination is pragmatically fixed as a concessive acknowledgment formula.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Hindi totoo iyan.
3. **Transliteration:** Hindi totoo iyan.
4. **English translation:** That is not true. / That is false.
5. **Usage notes:** The negation *hindi* + *totoo* is the standard formula for denying the truth of a proposition. While compositionally transparent, it is the fixed and expected form for formal and informal denial of truth-claims and appears in legal denials, press statements, and everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** totoo sa buhay
3. **Transliteration:** totoo sa buhay
4. **English translation:** true to life, realistic, lifelike
5. **Usage notes:** This construction applies *totoo* to aesthetic or narrative evaluation rather than propositional truth; it is used to characterize depictions, stories, or portrayals as faithfully reflecting lived experience. The phrase functions as a semi-fixed evaluative expression in literary and media discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** totoo sa sarili
3. **Transliteration:** totoo sa sarili
4. **English translation:** true to oneself, authentic to one's own nature
5. **Usage notes:** This construction extends *totoo* into the domain of personal authenticity and identity; it evaluates whether a person acts in accordance with their genuine values rather than performing for others. It appears in self-help, literary, and moral discourse and is not reducible to simple propositional truth-predication.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Kung totoo man...
3. **Transliteration:** Kung totoo man...
4. **English translation:** Even if it were true... / If it is indeed true...
5. **Usage notes:** The construction *kung totoo man* is a fixed conditional-concessive formula used in argumentation to grant a premise hypothetically while proceeding to challenge its implications. The particle *man* adds a concessive nuance ("even if"). Common in formal argumentation and everyday debate.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Ang totoo nito...
3. **Transliteration:** Ang totoo nito...
4. **English translation:** The truth of the matter is... / What is really true about this is...
5. **Usage notes:** This construction uses *totoo* as a nominalized head (*ang totoo*) followed by a genitive phrase (*nito* — "of this"), functioning as a discourse-initial formula for introducing a corrective or clarifying statement. It is pragmatically equivalent to "the fact of the matter is" and is common in both spoken and written Tagalog.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** totoo
2. **Example:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
3. **Transliteration:** Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.
4. **English translation:** The truth is, I don't know. / Honestly, I don't know.
5. **Usage notes:** *Ang totoo* used as a sentence-initial discourse marker, followed by a comma pause, functions as a hedging or honesty-signaling formula before a potentially face-threatening admission. This use is pragmatically fixed and common across registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on purely compositional phrases from P1b:** The phrases *totoong kwento* ("true story"), *totoong pangyayari* ("real event"), and *totoo ang lahat* ("it's all true") are compositionally transparent noun phrases or predications and do not qualify as constructions under the criterion applied here. They are not documented as separate constructions.

## Base Form: *katotohanan*

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** Sabihin ang katotohanan.
3. **Transliteration:** Sabihin ang katotohanan.
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** This is the standard imperative formula for demanding truth-telling, used in legal oaths, moral exhortations, and everyday speech. The construction is fixed in form and function; it is the expected phrasing in courtroom and confessional contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** sa katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** sa katotohanan
4. **English translation:** in truth, in reality, actually, truthfully speaking
5. **Usage notes:** This prepositional phrase functions as a grammaticalized discourse connector introducing a corrective or clarifying statement. Its pragmatic function as a sentence-initial or clause-initial marker is distinct from its compositional meaning and is stable across registers. (Also documented in P2a as a grammaticalized use; included here as a multi-word construction for completeness.)
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** walang katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** walang katotohanan
4. **English translation:** without truth, having no truth, baseless, groundless
5. **Usage notes:** The construction *wala* + *-ng* + *katotohanan* is a fixed evaluative formula used to characterize a claim, rumor, or allegation as entirely without factual basis. Common in formal denials and journalistic fact-checking; the formula is stable and expected in these contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** may katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** may katotohanan
4. **English translation:** there is truth in it, it has some truth, partly true
5. **Usage notes:** The existential construction *may* + *katotohanan* is used to concede partial truth in a claim without fully endorsing it. It functions as a nuanced evaluative formula in discourse where a claim is neither fully accepted nor fully rejected.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** pagtatago ng katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** pagtatago ng katotohanan
4. **English translation:** concealment of the truth, hiding the truth
5. **Usage notes:** This nominal construction characterizes the act of withholding or suppressing true information. It appears in legal, moral, and journalistic discourse and functions as a fixed accusatory or descriptive formula.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** pagsasabi ng katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** pagsasabi ng katotohanan
4. **English translation:** telling the truth, speaking the truth
5. **Usage notes:** This nominal construction designates the act of truth-telling as a moral or legal duty. It appears in moral instruction, legal contexts, and religious discourse (*Ang pagsasabi ng katotohanan ay isang tungkulin* — "Telling the truth is a duty") and is the standard nominal formula for this act.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** paghahanap ng katotohanan
3. **Transliteration:** paghahanap ng katotohanan
4. **English translation:** search for truth, seeking the truth
5. **Usage notes:** This nominal construction characterizes the pursuit of truth as an activity or vocation. It is common in philosophical, journalistic, and religious discourse and functions as a fixed formula for describing investigative or intellectual endeavor.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**

1. **Base Form:** katotohanan
2. **Example:** Ang katotohanan ay mananatiling katotohanan.
3. **Transliteration:** Ang katotohanan ay mananatiling katotohanan.
4. **English translation:** The truth will remain the truth. / Truth will always be truth.
5. **Usage notes:** This is a fixed rhetorical formula used to assert the permanence or inviolability of truth, typically in moral or political argumentation. It functions as a proverb-like declaration and is attested in formal speeches, editorial writing, and religious discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Base Form: *tama*

---

**Construction 30**

1. **Base Form:** tama
2. **Example:** Tama ka.
3. **Transliteration:** Tama ka.
4. **English translation:** You are right. / You are correct.
5. **Usage notes:** The predication *tama* + second-person pronoun is the standard formula for affirming another person's correctness in dialogue. It is the expected and fixed form for this function across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 31**

1. **Base Form:** tama
2. **Example:** Tama na.
3. **Transliteration:** Tama na.
4. **English translation:** That's enough. / Stop. / That will do.
5. **Usage notes:** The construction *tama* + *na* has a pragmatically specialized meaning distinct from simple correctness-predication: it signals that a sufficient point has been reached and further action or speech should cease. This is a fixed idiomatic formula used to halt an action, argument, or behavior, and its meaning is not fully predictable from *tama* ("correct") + *na* ("now/already") alone.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 32**

1. **Base Form:** tama
2. **Example:** Tama ba?
3. **Transliteration:** Tama ba?
4. **English translation:** Is that right? / Is that correct? / Am I right?
5. **Usage notes:** The interrogative formula *tama ba* is used to seek confirmation of a claim or action. It is the standard formula for this function and appears across all registers, from classroom to courtroom.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 33**

1. **Base Form:** tama
2. **Example:** nang tama
3. **Transliteration:** nang tama
4. **English translation:** correctly, in the right way, properly
5. **Usage notes:** The adverbial construction *nang* + *tama* is the standard formula for expressing that an action is performed correctly or in the proper manner. *Nang* is the adverbial linker used before adjectives functioning as manner adverbials; this is a regular syntactic pattern but the construction is the fixed and expected form for this adverbial function.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 34**

1. **Base Form:** tama
2. **Example:** Hindi naman mali, tama nga.
3. **Transliteration:** Hindi naman mali, tama nga.
4. **English translation:** It's not wrong, it's actually correct. / It's not wrong — in fact, it's right.
5. **Usage notes:** The construction *tama nga* (with the particle *nga* adding emphasis or confirmation) is used to affirm correctness with added force, often in response to a challenge or doubt. The particle *nga* signals that the correctness is being asserted as established or evident.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary of Patterns Observed

The following patterns are directly supported by the constructions documented above:

1. **Predication with demonstrative or pronoun** (*totoo ba iyan*, *mali ka*, *peke iyan*) is the standard formula for asserting or querying the truth-value, correctness, or authenticity of a referent. This pattern is attested across nearly all P1 base forms.

2. **Negation with *hindi*** (*hindi totoo*, *hindi mali*, *hindi ako sigurado*) generates the standard denial or uncertainty formula for each base form. This is a regular syntactic pattern but the resulting constructions are the fixed and expected forms for their functions.

3. **The particle *na*** combines with several base forms (*totoo na*, *tama na*, *sigurado na*) to generate pragmatically specialized constructions marking change of state, sufficiency, or current relevance. The meaning of these constructions is not fully predictable from the base form alone.

4. **The particle *naman*** combines with base forms (*totoo naman*) to generate concessive or turn-managing formulas in dialogue.

5. **The particle *nga*** combines with base forms (*tama nga*) to generate confirmatory or emphatic formulas.

6. **The construction *wala* + *-ng* + [base form]** (*walang katotohanan*, *walang katunayan*, *walang pruweba*, *walang ebidensya*) is a productive formula for asserting the absence of truth, proof, or evidence. This pattern is attested across multiple P1 forms in the evidence/proof cluster.

7. **The construction *may* + [base form]** (*may tiwala*, *may katotohanan*, *may katunayan*, *may pruweba*) is the corresponding existential formula for asserting the presence of trust, truth, or evidence.

8. **The pattern *[base form] na [noun]*** generates fixed evaluative collocations across the *tunay* cluster (*tunay na kaibigan*, *tunay na pagmamahal*, *tunay na mukha*) and the *mali* cluster (*maling akala*, *maling impormasyon*, *maling paniniwala*). These collocations are the standard formulas for their evaluative functions.

9. **Discourse connector formulas** (*sa katotohanan*, *sa katunayan*, *ang totoo*) represent grammaticalized uses of nominal forms as sentence-initial pragmatic markers. Their pragmatic function is distinct from their compositional meaning.

10. **Domain-specific technical collocations** (*ebidensyang pisikal*, *ebidensyang sirkumstansyal*, *orihinal na kopya*, *awtentikong dokumento*, *lehitimong anak*) are fixed technical terms in legal and administrative discourse. These show the highest degree of fixedness and the narrowest register distribution.

11. **Asymmetry is preserved:** Several base forms (*lehitimasyon*, *pagsisinungaling*, *kawastuhan*, *katumpakan*) show no fixed multi-word constructions beyond their use as head nouns. This absence is recorded rather than filled.
