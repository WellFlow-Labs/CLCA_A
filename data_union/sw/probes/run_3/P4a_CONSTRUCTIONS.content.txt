# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *ni* (copula) + truth-domain adjective/noun

### Examples
- *Hii ni kweli.* → Hii ni kweli. → "This is true."
- *Taarifa hii ni sahihi.* → Taarifa hii ni sahihi. → "This report is accurate."

### Notes
Swahili uses the invariant copula *ni* for attribution. The truth-domain item (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *halisi*, *dhahiri*) follows directly; there is no agreement morphology on the predicate nominal/adjective in this construction.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + *si* (negative copula) + truth-domain adjective/noun

### Examples
- *Hii si kweli.* → Hii si kweli. → "This is not true."
- *Jibu lako si sahihi.* → Jibu lako si sahihi. → "Your answer is not correct."

### Notes
*Si* is the direct negative counterpart of *ni* and is likewise invariant. The pairs *ni kweli / si kweli* and *ni sahihi / si sahihi* function as the canonical binary truth-evaluation frames in Swahili.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Noun + truth-domain modifier (post-nominal position); or *wa/ya* + truth-domain noun

### Examples
- *dhahabu halisi* → dhahabu halisi → "genuine gold" (noun + attributive adjective)
- *ukweli wa jambo* → ukweli wa jambo → "the truth of the matter" (noun + *wa* + noun)

### Notes
Attributive adjectives and nouns used as modifiers follow the head noun in Swahili. The associative particle (*wa*, *ya*, *la*, etc.) agrees with the noun class of the head; *wa* is the most frequent in this domain. The pattern *X wa kweli* ("true/genuine X") is highly productive: *upendo wa kweli* ("true love"), *rafiki wa kweli* ("true friend"), *mtu wa kweli* ("truthful person").

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Stative/intransitive verbal form: verb stem + *-ka* or *-ika* suffix (with *ku-* infinitive prefix)

### Examples
- *Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi.* → Ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi. → "The truth became evident after the investigation."
- *Alidanganyika.* → Alidanganyika. → "He/she was deceived / fell for it."

### Notes
The suffix *-ka* / *-ika* on a verb stem produces a stative or resultant-state reading ("to become/come to be in a state"). Attested for *dhahiri* (*kudhihirika*, "to become evident") and *danganya* (*kudanganyika*, "to come to be deceived"). No inchoative form is attested for *kweli* or *sahihi* as bases.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Verb stem + *-sha* / *-isha* causative suffix (with *ku-* infinitive prefix)

### Examples
- *Matendo yake yalidhihirisha tabia yake.* → Matendo yake yalidhihirisha tabia yake. → "His actions revealed / made evident his character."
- *Sahihisha makosa kabla ya kuwasilisha.* → Sahihisha makosa kabla ya kuwasilisha. → "Correct the errors before submitting."

### Notes
The causative suffix *-sha* / *-isha* is productive for roots that permit verbal derivation: *dhahiri* → *kudhihirisha* ("to make evident, to demonstrate"), *sahihi* → *kusahihisha* ("to correct, to make accurate"), *fanana* → *kufananisha* ("to compare, to liken"), *tofauti* → *kutofautisha* ("to distinguish"). The roots *kweli* and *halisi* do not show well-attested causative forms in common usage.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*u-* (class 14 abstract noun prefix) + root (± additional suffix)

### Examples
- *Usahihi wa taarifa unahitajika.* → Usahihi wa taarifa unahitajika. → "The accuracy of the report is required."
- *Udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani.* → Udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani. → "His/her deception was exposed publicly."

### Notes
The *u-* prefix is the primary nominalizing strategy for abstract properties in Swahili and is highly productive across this domain: *ukweli* ("truth"), *usahihi* ("accuracy"), *uhalisi* ("reality/authenticity"), *uaminifu* ("faithfulness"), *udanganyifu* ("deception"), *ulaghai* ("fraud"), *ubora* ("quality"), *utofauti* ("difference"), *udhahiri* ("obviousness"). A further suffix *-o* nominalizes verbal actions: *uthibitisho* ("verification/confirmation"), *udhihirisho* ("demonstration/manifestation").

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*m-* (class 1 human singular prefix) + root (± suffix); plural *wa-*

### Examples
- *Yeye ni mwongo — hasemi ukweli kamwe.* → Yeye ni mwongo — hasemi ukweli kamwe. → "He/she is a liar — never speaks the truth."
- *Yeye ni mdanganyifu wa hali ya juu.* → Yeye ni mdanganyifu wa hali ya juu. → "He/she is a thoroughgoing deceiver."

### Notes
The *m-* / *wa-* prefix pair (singular/plural, noun class 1/2) derives human-agent nouns. Attested pairs in this domain: *mwongo / waongo* ("liar/s"), *mdanganyifu / wadanganyifu* ("deceiver/s"), *mlaghai / walaghai* ("fraudster/s"), *mwaminifu / waaminifu* ("faithful person/people"), *mkosoaji / wakosoaji* ("critic/s"), *mkosaji / wakosaji* ("offender/s"). No agentive nominalization for a "truth-teller" as a fixed lexical item is attested; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*mtu wa kweli*, "a person of truth").

---

## 8. COMPLEMENTIZER FRAMING (additional pattern)

### Pattern
Copular or evidential frame + *kwamba* / *kuwa* + embedded clause

### Examples
- *Ukweli ni kwamba hakuna ushahidi.* → Ukweli ni kwamba hakuna ushahidi. → "The truth is that there is no evidence."
- *Ni dhahiri kwamba alikuwa anajua.* → Ni dhahiri kwamba alikuwa anajua. → "It is evident that he/she knew."

### Notes
The complementizers *kwamba* and *kuwa* ("that") introduce embedded propositional content after a truth-domain predicate. The frames *ukweli ni kwamba...* and *ni dhahiri kwamba...* are highly productive in journalistic, legal, and argumentative discourse for introducing asserted or evidentially grounded propositions.

---

## 9. PARENTHETICAL SINCERITY FRAME (additional pattern)

### Pattern
Infinitive phrase (*kusema ukweli* / *kwa kweli*) + comma + main clause

### Examples
- *Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo.* → Kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo. → "To tell the truth, I didn't like that."
- *Kwa kweli, sijui.* → Kwa kweli, sijui. → "Honestly, I don't know."

### Notes
These sentence-initial phrases function as discourse markers signaling speaker sincerity rather than as predicates about a specific proposition. *Kusema ukweli* (infinitive: "to speak the truth") and *kwa kweli* (prepositional: "in truth") are the two most frequent forms; *kwa kweli kweli* is an intensified variant.

---

## 10. PASSIVE VERIFICATION / EXPOSURE (additional pattern)

### Pattern
Subject + passive verb (*-wa* suffix) + optional agentive *na*-phrase or adverb

### Examples
- *Hati ilithibitishwa na mamlaka.* → Hati ilithibitishwa na mamlaka. → "The document was authenticated by the authorities."
- *Uongo wake ulidhihirika.* → Uongo wake ulidhihirika. → "His/her lie was exposed / became evident."

### Notes
The passive suffix *-wa* is productive across this domain: *kuthibitishwa* ("to be verified"), *kudanganywa* ("to be deceived"), *kufunuliwa* ("to be revealed"). The stative *-ka*/*-ika* (Pattern 4) and the passive *-wa* overlap in expressing resultant states but differ in agentivity: *-wa* implies an external agent; *-ka*/*-ika* does not.
