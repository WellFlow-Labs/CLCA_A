# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Korean (ko)

---

**Transliteration standard:** Revised Romanization of Korean (국립국어원 표준 로마자 표기법), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 실 (實, real/actual)
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality

Used to describe statements or situations that correspond to what actually is the case. Appears in moral, legal, and everyday contexts, often in opposition to 거짓 (geojit, falsehood). Participates in compounds such as 진실성 (jinsilseong, truthfulness/veracity).
[CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 사실
**Transliteration:** sasil
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 사 (事, matter/affair) + 실 (實, real/actual)
**Typical English translations:** fact, actuality, truth (in the sense of factual accuracy)

Refers to something that has actually occurred or is the case, as opposed to opinion or fabrication. Widely used in legal, journalistic, and everyday speech to ground claims in verifiable reality. Also functions adverbially meaning "in fact" or "actually."
[CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 거짓
**Transliteration:** geojit
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** native Korean root; etymology debated
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth

Denotes a statement or representation that does not correspond to reality. Used in both moral condemnation of lying and neutral description of inaccuracy. Participates in compounds such as 거짓말 (geojitmal, lie/false speech) and 거짓말쟁이 (geojitmaljaengi, liar).
[CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 거짓말
**Transliteration:** geojitmal
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 거짓 (geojit, falsehood) + 말 (mal, speech/word)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood

A conventionalized compound specifically denoting a false utterance or deliberate deception. Extremely common in everyday moral evaluation of speech acts. The derived verb 거짓말하다 (geojitmal-hada) means "to lie."
[CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** colloquial/emphatic form; likely derived from 진 (眞, genuine); exact morphology of 짜 debated
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic; (colloquially) really, truly

Used to assert that something is the genuine article as opposed to a copy, fake, or pretense. Extremely frequent in everyday speech for evaluating objects, persons, and situations. Contrasts directly with 가짜 (gajja, fake/counterfeit).
[CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 가짜
**Transliteration:** gajja
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 가 (假, false/temporary/fake) + 짜 (colloquial suffix, parallel to 진짜)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, phony

Denotes something that appears to be genuine but is not, whether an object, credential, or identity. Used in consumer, legal, and everyday moral contexts. Directly contrasts with 진짜 (jinjja).
[CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 진정
**Transliteration:** jinjeong
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 정 (正, correct/upright) or 정 (情, feeling/sincerity, depending on context)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, true, real, sincere; (adverbially) truly, really

Used to evaluate whether something is authentic in character or feeling, often with an emphasis on sincerity rather than mere surface appearance. Appears in expressions like 진정한 사랑 (jinjeonghan sarang, true love) and 진정성 (jinjeongseong, authenticity/genuineness). Participates in productive adjectival and nominal derivation.
[CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeongseong
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 진정 (jinjeong, genuine/true) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity, nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity

A nominalized form evaluating whether a person, act, or expression reflects genuine inner conviction or character. Prominent in contemporary public discourse, criticism, and ethical evaluation. Often used to question or affirm the credibility of public figures or artistic works.
[CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 정확
**Transliteration:** jeonghwak
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 정 (正, correct/upright) + 확 (確, certain/firm)
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness; (adjectival form 정확한) accurate, precise, exact

Evaluates whether a statement, measurement, or representation corresponds correctly and precisely to its referent. Used in scientific, legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts. Participates in derivation: 정확성 (jeonghwakseong, accuracy), 정확히 (jeonghwaki, accurately/exactly).
[CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 올바르다
**Transliteration:** olbareuda
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 올 (right/straight, native Korean root) + 바르다 (bareuda, to be straight/upright/correct)
**Typical English translations:** to be right, correct, proper, upright

Evaluates conduct, reasoning, or values as morally or normatively correct. Used in ethical and educational contexts to describe proper behavior or sound judgment. The adjectival form 올바른 (olbareun) is very common in moral and civic discourse.
[CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 옳다
**Transliteration:** olta
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** native Korean root; to be right/correct
**Typical English translations:** to be right, correct, proper, just

Used to evaluate propositions, actions, or judgments as correct or morally right. Appears in both epistemic contexts (this answer is right) and moral contexts (this action is right). Contrasts with 그르다 (geureuda, to be wrong/incorrect).
[CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 그르다
**Transliteration:** geureuda
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** native Korean root; to be wrong/incorrect
**Typical English translations:** to be wrong, incorrect, improper

The direct antonym of 옳다 (olta), used to evaluate propositions or conduct as incorrect or morally wrong. Somewhat more formal or literary in register than 틀리다 (teullida). Appears in moral and philosophical discourse.
[CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 틀리다
**Transliteration:** teullida
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** native Korean root; to be wrong, to differ, to be off
**Typical English translations:** to be wrong, incorrect, mistaken; to differ

Evaluates a statement, answer, or calculation as factually or logically incorrect. More commonly used in everyday speech than 그르다 (geureuda) for factual incorrectness. Note: sometimes confused with 다르다 (dareuda, to be different), but 틀리다 carries a normative sense of error.
[CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 정직
**Transliteration:** jeongjik
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 정 (正, correct/upright) + 직 (直, straight/honest)
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, straightforwardness

Evaluates a person or conduct as honest and non-deceptive. Used in moral, educational, and professional contexts. Participates in derivation: 정직하다 (jeongjik-hada, to be honest), 정직성 (jeongjikseong, honesty as a quality).
[CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 신뢰
**Transliteration:** sinloe
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 신 (信, trust/faith) + 뢰 (賴, rely on/depend on)
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, credibility

Evaluates whether a person, institution, or source can be depended upon to be accurate and honest. Used in interpersonal, institutional, and legal contexts. Participates in derivation: 신뢰성 (sinloeseong, reliability/trustworthiness), 신뢰하다 (sinloe-hada, to trust).
[CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 신뢰성
**Transliteration:** sinloeseong
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 신뢰 (sinloe, trust/reliability) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility

A nominalized form used to evaluate whether a source, testimony, or system can be relied upon for accurate information. Prominent in legal, academic, and journalistic discourse. Closely related to but distinct from 진정성 (jinjeongseong), which emphasizes sincerity over reliability.
[CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 신빙성
**Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 신 (信, trust/credibility) + 빙 (憑, rely on/evidence) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**Typical English translations:** credibility, plausibility, trustworthiness (of testimony or evidence)

Used specifically to evaluate whether a claim, testimony, or account is credible and worthy of belief. Particularly common in legal and investigative contexts. Somewhat more formal and domain-specific than 신뢰성 (sinloeseong).
[CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 위조
**Transliteration:** wijo
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 위 (僞, false/counterfeit) + 조 (造, to make/manufacture)
**Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeiting, fabrication

Denotes the act of producing a false document, object, or signature intended to deceive. Used primarily in legal and criminal contexts. Participates in derivation: 위조하다 (wijo-hada, to forge/counterfeit), 위조품 (wijopum, counterfeit goods).
[CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 허위
**Transliteration:** heowi
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 허 (虛, empty/false) + 위 (僞, false/counterfeit)
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, misrepresentation

Used in formal, legal, and journalistic contexts to evaluate statements or representations as false or misleading. More formal than 거짓 (geojit). Appears in legal compounds such as 허위진술 (heowi jinsul, false statement) and 허위광고 (heowi gwanggo, false advertising).
[CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 위 (僞, false/counterfeit)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness vs. falsity; truth or falsehood

Used to frame the question of whether something is genuine or false, particularly for documents, artworks, or claims. Typically appears in the construction 진위를 가리다 (jinwireul garida, to determine authenticity/truth). Common in legal, art-historical, and investigative contexts.
[CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 본질
**Transliteration:** bonjil
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 본 (本, root/origin/fundamental) + 질 (質, substance/nature/quality)
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, substance

Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday contexts to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface appearance or accidental properties. Appears in constructions like 본질적으로 (bonjiljeogeuro, essentially/fundamentally). Relevant to evaluations of what something truly is.
[CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 실체
**Transliteration:** silche
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 체 (體, body/substance/form)
**Typical English translations:** substance, true nature, real entity, actuality

Refers to the actual or underlying reality of something, as opposed to appearance or representation. Used in philosophical, legal, and journalistic contexts to probe what something really is. Appears in expressions like 실체를 밝히다 (silchereul balkida, to reveal the true nature of something).
[CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 타당
**Transliteration:** tadang
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 타 (妥, appropriate/fitting) + 당 (當, proper/right/fitting)
**Typical English translations:** validity, appropriateness, reasonableness; (adjectival form 타당한) valid, reasonable, sound

Evaluates whether an argument, claim, or action is logically sound or normatively appropriate. Used in academic, legal, and deliberative contexts. Participates in derivation: 타당성 (tadangseong, validity/soundness), 타당하다 (tadang-hada, to be valid/reasonable).
[CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 타당성
**Transliteration:** tadangseong
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 타당 (tadang, valid/appropriate) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**Typical English translations:** validity, soundness, reasonableness

A nominalized form used to evaluate the logical or normative soundness of an argument, policy, or claim. Prominent in academic, legal, and policy discourse. Often appears in the construction 타당성을 검토하다 (tadangseong-eul geomto-hada, to examine the validity of something).
[CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 공정
**Transliteration:** gongjeon
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 공 (公, public/impartial) + 정 (正, correct/upright/just)
**Typical English translations:** fairness, impartiality, justice

Evaluates whether a process, judgment, or treatment is impartial and free from bias. Used in legal, institutional, and everyday moral contexts. Participates in derivation: 공정성 (gongjeonseong, fairness/impartiality), 공정하다 (gongjeon-hada, to be fair/impartial).
[CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 객관적
**Transliteration:** gaekgwanjeok
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 객관 (gaekgwan, 客觀, objective view) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix -ic/-al)
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial, unbiased

Evaluates a judgment, description, or method as based on facts independent of personal perspective. Used in academic, journalistic, and everyday contexts. Contrasts with 주관적 (jugwanjeok, subjective). Participates in derivation: 객관성 (gaekgwanseong, objectivity).
[CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 주관적
**Transliteration:** jugwanjeok
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 주관 (jugwan, 主觀, subjective view) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** subjective, personal, biased (in some contexts)

Evaluates a judgment or perception as based on personal perspective rather than independent fact. Used in academic, aesthetic, and everyday contexts, sometimes neutrally and sometimes critically. Contrasts with 객관적 (gaekgwanjeok, objective).
[CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 모조
**Transliteration:** mojo
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 모 (模, model/imitate) + 조 (造, make/manufacture)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, copy, counterfeit

Denotes an object made to resemble another, often without authorization or with intent to deceive. Used in consumer, legal, and art contexts. Appears in compounds such as 모조품 (mojopum, imitation goods/replica). Distinct from 위조 (wijo) in that 모조 does not always imply criminal intent.
[CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 표절
**Transliteration:** pyojeol
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 표 (剽, to steal/plagiarize) + 절 (竊, to steal secretly)
**Typical English translations:** plagiarism, copying without attribution

Evaluates a work or act as involving the unacknowledged appropriation of another's intellectual or creative output. Used in academic, literary, and legal contexts. Participates in derivation: 표절하다 (pyojeol-hada, to plagiarize), 표절작 (pyojeoljak, plagiarized work).
[CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 왜곡
**Transliteration:** waegok
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** 왜 (歪, crooked/distorted) + 곡 (曲, bent/curved/distorted)
**Typical English translations:** distortion, misrepresentation, falsification

Evaluates a representation, account, or interpretation as having been altered or skewed away from accurate correspondence to reality. Used in journalistic, historical, legal, and political contexts. Participates in derivation: 왜곡하다 (waegok-hada, to distort/misrepresent), 역사 왜곡 (yeoksa waegok, historical distortion).
[CORE]
