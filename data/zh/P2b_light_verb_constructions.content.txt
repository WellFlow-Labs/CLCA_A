# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## PRELIMINARY NOTE ON CHINESE MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

Chinese exhibits rich multi-word constructions including:
1. **Idiomatic expressions** (成语 chéngyǔ and other fixed phrases)
2. **Periphrastic constructions** (analytical expressions)
3. **Collocational patterns** (semi-fixed word combinations)
4. **Syntactic frames** (productive patterns with specific lexical items)

This analysis documents **attested constructions** involving P1 lexical items, excluding derivational forms already covered in P2a.

## CONSTRUCTION TYPE 1: IDIOMATIC EXPRESSIONS (成语 AND FIXED PHRASES)

### 1.1 Based on 真 (zhēn)

#### Construction 1.1.1
**Base Form**: 真 (zhēn) - true, real, genuine

**Example**: 弄假成真 (nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn)
- **Transliteration**: nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn
- **Literal**: make + false + become + true
- **Translation**: what starts as pretense becomes real; fake it till you make it
- **Usage**: Describes situations where something that begins as false or pretended eventually becomes genuine. Common in discussions of relationships or situations that evolve unexpectedly.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.2
**Example**: 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn)
- **Transliteration**: qù wěi cún zhēn
- **Literal**: remove + false + preserve + true
- **Translation**: eliminate the false and retain the true
- **Usage**: Describes the process of critical analysis or investigation to separate truth from falsehood. Common in academic and investigative contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.3
**Example**: 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn)
- **Transliteration**: yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn
- **Literal**: use + false + confuse + true
- **Translation**: pass off the fake as genuine; confuse the false with the true
- **Usage**: Describes counterfeits or imitations so good they can be mistaken for the real thing. Common in discussions of forgery or deception.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.4
**Example**: 真假难辨 (zhēn jiǎ nán biàn)
- **Transliteration**: zhēn jiǎ nán biàn
- **Literal**: true + false + difficult + distinguish
- **Translation**: difficult to distinguish true from false
- **Usage**: Describes situations where it's hard to tell what's genuine from what's fake. Common in contexts of uncertainty or sophisticated deception.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.5
**Example**: 半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ)
- **Transliteration**: bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
- **Literal**: half + true + half + false
- **Translation**: half true and half false; partly true
- **Usage**: Describes statements or situations that mix truth with falsehood. Common in evaluating credibility of claims.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.6
**Example**: 真心实意 (zhēn xīn shí yì)
- **Transliteration**: zhēn xīn shí yì
- **Literal**: true + heart + solid + intention
- **Translation**: wholehearted, sincere, genuine
- **Usage**: Emphasizes complete sincerity and genuine intention. Common in describing honest attitudes or commitments.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.1.7
**Example**: 真相大白 (zhēn xiàng dà bái)
- **Transliteration**: zhēn xiàng dà bái
- **Literal**: true + appearance + great + white/clear
- **Translation**: the truth comes to light; the facts are revealed
- **Usage**: Describes the moment when hidden truth is finally revealed. Common in investigative and narrative contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### 1.2 Based on 实 (shí)

#### Construction 1.2.1
**Base Form**: 实 (shí) - solid, real, actual

**Example**: 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí)
- **Transliteration**: míng fù qí shí
- **Literal**: name + match + its + reality
- **Translation**: worthy of the name; live up to one's reputation
- **Usage**: Evaluates whether something truly corresponds to its name or reputation. Common in assessments of whether claims match reality.
- **Status**: [CORE] (already in P1, included for completeness)

#### Construction 1.2.2
**Example**: 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí)
- **Transliteration**: míng bù fù shí
- **Literal**: name + not + match + reality
- **Translation**: unworthy of the name; not live up to reputation
- **Usage**: Criticizes discrepancy between name/reputation and actual reality. Common in negative evaluations.
- **Status**: [CORE] (already in P1, included for completeness)

#### Construction 1.2.3
**Example**: 华而不实 (huá ér bù shí)
- **Transliteration**: huá ér bù shí
- **Literal**: flowery + but + not + solid
- **Translation**: flashy but lacking substance; all show and no substance
- **Usage**: Criticizes things that appear impressive but lack real substance or value. Common in critical evaluations of style over substance.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2.4
**Example**: 脚踏实地 (jiǎo tà shí dì)
- **Transliteration**: jiǎo tà shí dì
- **Literal**: foot + step + solid + ground
- **Translation**: down-to-earth, practical, realistic
- **Usage**: Praises practical, realistic approach to work or life. Common in character evaluation and advice.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2.5
**Example**: 实事求是 (shí shì qiú shì)
- **Transliteration**: shí shì qiú shì
- **Literal**: solid + matter + seek + be
- **Translation**: seek truth from facts; be realistic and pragmatic
- **Usage**: Important political and philosophical principle emphasizing empirical investigation and realistic assessment. Central to Chinese Communist Party ideology.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2.6
**Example**: 虚虚实实 (xū xū shí shí)
- **Transliteration**: xū xū shí shí
- **Literal**: empty + empty + solid + solid (reduplication)
- **Translation**: mix of feint and reality; deceptive mixture of true and false
- **Usage**: Describes strategic deception mixing real and fake elements. Common in military strategy and competitive contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2.7
**Example**: 查无实据 (chá wú shí jù)
- **Transliteration**: chá wú shí jù
- **Literal**: investigate + not have + solid + evidence
- **Translation**: investigation reveals no solid evidence
- **Usage**: Legal and investigative term indicating lack of concrete evidence after investigation. Common in legal contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 1.2.8
**Example**: 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù)
- **Transliteration**: zhēn píng shí jù
- **Literal**: true + evidence + solid + proof
- **Translation**: conclusive evidence, solid proof
- **Usage**: Legal term for reliable, concrete evidence. Emphasizes both authenticity and substantiality.
- **Status**: [CORE] (already in P1b, included for completeness)

## CONSTRUCTION TYPE 2: PERIPHRASTIC CONSTRUCTIONS

### 2.1 Verification/Confirmation Constructions

#### Construction 2.1.1
**Base Form**: 真 (zhēn) - true, real

**Example**: 是真的吗？(shì zhēn de ma?)
- **Transliteration**: shì zhēn de ma?
- **Literal**: be + true + [nominalizer] + [question particle]
- **Translation**: Is it true? Is it real?
- **Usage**: Common question form for verifying truth or authenticity. Extremely frequent in everyday conversation.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.1.2
**Example**: 这是真的 (zhè shì zhēn de)
- **Transliteration**: zhè shì zhēn de
- **Literal**: this + be + true + [nominalizer]
- **Translation**: This is true/real
- **Usage**: Common assertion of truth or authenticity. Used to confirm or emphasize veracity.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.1.3
**Base Form**: 假 (jiǎ) - false, fake

**Example**: 这是假的 (zhè shì jiǎ de)
- **Transliteration**: zhè shì jiǎ de
- **Literal**: this + be + false + [nominalizer]
- **Translation**: This is fake/false
- **Usage**: Common assertion that something is counterfeit or false. Used in authentication contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### 2.2 Correctness Assessment Constructions

#### Construction 2.2.1
**Base Form**: 对 (duì) - correct, right

**Example**: 对不对？(duì bu duì?)
- **Transliteration**: duì bu duì?
- **Literal**: correct + not + correct
- **Translation**: Right? Is that correct?
- **Usage**: Very common tag question seeking confirmation or agreement. Used extensively in conversation.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.2.2
**Example**: 你说得对 (nǐ shuō de duì)
- **Transliteration**: nǐ shuō de duì
- **Literal**: you + say + [particle] + correct
- **Translation**: You're right; What you said is correct
- **Usage**: Common way to agree with someone's statement. Frequent in discussions and conversations.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.2.3
**Base Form**: 错 (cuò) - wrong, mistaken

**Example**: 你错了 (nǐ cuò le)
- **Transliteration**: nǐ cuò le
- **Literal**: you + wrong + [perfective particle]
- **Translation**: You're wrong; You made a mistake
- **Usage**: Direct way to indicate someone is mistaken. Common in corrections and disagreements.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.2.4
**Example**: 哪里错了？(nǎlǐ cuò le?)
- **Transliteration**: nǎlǐ cuò le?
- **Literal**: where + wrong + [perfective particle]
- **Translation**: Where is the mistake? What's wrong?
- **Usage**: Question asking for identification of error. Common in educational and problem-solving contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### 2.3 Truth-Telling Constructions

#### Construction 2.3.1
**Base Form**: 实话 (shíhuà) - truth, honest words

**Example**: 说实话 (shuō shíhuà)
- **Transliteration**: shuō shíhuà
- **Literal**: speak + truth/honest words
- **Translation**: to tell the truth; to be honest
- **Usage**: Very common phrase introducing honest statements, often admitting something. Frequently used as discourse marker.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.3.2
**Example**: 老实说 (lǎoshí shuō)
- **Transliteration**: lǎoshí shuō
- **Literal**: honest + say
- **Translation**: honestly speaking; to be frank
- **Usage**: Similar to 说实话, introduces frank or honest statements. Common in conversational contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.3.3
**Example**: 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō)
- **Transliteration**: shíhuà shíshuō
- **Literal**: honest words + honestly say
- **Translation**: speak the plain truth; tell it like it is
- **Usage**: Emphasizes complete honesty without embellishment. Also title of famous Chinese TV talk show.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### 2.4 Deception/Lying Constructions

#### Construction 2.4.1

- **Transliteration**: sā huǎng
- **Literal**: scatter/spread + lie
- **Translation**: to lie, to tell lies
- **Usage**: Colloquial variant of 说谎, equally common. Same meaning and usage.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.4.3
**Base Form**: 欺骗 (qīpiàn) - deceive, cheat

- **Transliteration**: qīpiàn gǎnqíng
- **Literal**: deceive + feelings/emotions
- **Translation**: to deceive someone's feelings; to toy with someone's affections
- **Usage**: Common phrase for emotional deception in relationships. Carries strong negative moral judgment.
- **Status**: [CORE]

- **Status**: [CORE] (already listed in 1.1.4)

### 2.6 Reality/Actuality Constructions

#### Construction 2.6.1

- **Transliteration**: shìshí shàng

- **Translation**: in fact, actually, as a matter of fact

- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.6.2
**Example**: 面对事实 (miànduì shìshí)
- **Transliteration**: miànduì shìshí
- **Literal**: face + fact
- **Translation**: to face facts; to face reality
- **Usage**: Common phrase urging acceptance of reality. Used in advice and confrontational contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.6.3
**Example**: 事实胜于雄辩 (shìshí shèng yú xióngbiàn)
- **Transliteration**: shìshí shèng yú xióngbiàn
- **Literal**: facts + surpass + eloquent argument
- **Translation**: facts speak louder than eloquence; facts are more convincing than words
- **Usage**: Proverb emphasizing that facts are more persuasive than rhetoric. Common in argumentative contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.6.4
**Base Form**: 实际 (shíjì) - actual, practical

**Example**: 实际上 (shíjì shàng)
- **Transliteration**: shíjì shàng
- **Literal**: actual + on/above
- **Translation**: actually, in fact, in reality
- **Usage**: Very common adverbial phrase similar to 事实上, introducing actual state of affairs. Used across all registers.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### Construction 2.6.5
**Example**: 从实际出发 (cóng shíjì chūfā)
- **Transliteration**: cóng shíjì chūfā
- **Literal**: from + actual + set out
- **Translation**: proceed from reality; start from actual conditions
- **Usage**: Political and planning phrase emphasizing realistic, practical approach. Common in policy discussions.
- **Status**: [CORE]

## SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

### Most Productive Construction Types:
1. **Idiomatic expressions (成语)**: Rich inventory, especially for 真, 实, 正
2. **Periphrastic constructions**: Common for verification, assessment, and truth-telling
3. **Collocational patterns**: Strong preferences for specific verb-noun combinations
4. **Discourse markers**: Frequent use of truth-domain terms as pragmatic markers

### Asymmetries Observed:
- 真 and 实 participate in more idiomatic expressions than other roots
- 对/错 have fewer fixed idioms but more productive syntactic patterns
- Truth-revelation verbs (揭露, 查明, 隐瞒) strongly collocate with 真相
- Honesty terms (诚实, 老实) frequently appear in character evaluation collocations

### Register Variation:
- Formal register: 确实, 事实上, 符合实际
- Colloquial register: 说真的, 真的吗, 老实说
- Literary/classical: Four-character idioms (成语)
- Political: 实事求是, 正确处理, 从实际出发
