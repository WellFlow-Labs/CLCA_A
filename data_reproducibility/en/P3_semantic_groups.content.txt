# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: English (en)

## IDENTIFIED GROUPS

### GROUP 1: Correspondence to Facts or Reality

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, beliefs, or representations correspond to actual facts or reality. These expressions are primarily applied to propositions, claims, descriptions, and accounts to assess their factual accuracy. They contrast with terms indicating lack of correspondence or deviation from facts.

**Representative expressions:**
- true
- false
- accurate
- inaccurate
- correct
- incorrect
- right (in epistemic sense: "the right answer")
- wrong (in epistemic sense: "the wrong answer")
- erroneous
- mistaken
- veridical
- truthful (when applied to statements)
- untruthful (when applied to statements)

**Typical contexts:** Educational assessment, factual verification, scientific reporting, everyday claims about states of affairs.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "right" and "wrong" — also used for moral evaluation and practical appropriateness
- "truthful" — can apply to persons (character) or statements (content)
- "honest" — overlaps but emphasizes speaker's intention rather than statement's correspondence

---

### GROUP 2: Authenticity and Genuineness of Objects

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether physical objects, artifacts, or documents are original, authentic, or genuine rather than fake, forged, or substitute versions. These expressions distinguish real instances from imitations, reproductions, or fraudulent copies. Common in commercial, artistic, and legal contexts involving verification of provenance or originality.

**Representative expressions:**
- genuine
- authentic
- real (when applied to objects: "real gold")
- counterfeit
- fake
- bogus
- spurious (when applied to objects)
- ersatz
- false (in compounds: "false teeth," "false bottom")
- the genuine article
- the real thing
- the real deal
- bona fide

**Typical contexts:** Art authentication, currency verification, commercial transactions, historical document verification, consumer protection.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "real" — also used for existence/non-existence and sincerity
- "authentic" — also used for personal authenticity and cultural authenticity
- "legitimate" — overlaps but emphasizes legal authorization rather than physical genuineness
- "true" — occasionally used for objects ("true gold") but less common than "genuine" or "real"

---

### GROUP 3: Sincerity and Honesty of Persons

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether persons are truthful, sincere, or honest in their communications and conduct, or whether they are deceptive, insincere, or dishonest. These expressions focus on the speaker's or actor's intentions, character, and alignment between inner states and outward expressions rather than merely the factual accuracy of statements.

**Representative expressions:**
- honest
- dishonest
- sincere
- insincere
- truthful (when applied to persons)
- untruthful (when applied to persons)
- mendacious
- veracious (when applied to persons)
- to be honest
- honestly
- sincerely
- truthfully
- honest to God
- keep it real

**Typical contexts:** Character evaluation, moral judgment, personal relationships, credibility assessment.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "truthful" — can apply to statements (factual accuracy) or persons (character)
- "genuine" — can apply to persons' sincerity or objects' authenticity
- "real" — in "keep it real" overlaps with authenticity of self-presentation
- "faithful" — overlaps but emphasizes loyalty as well as honesty

---

### GROUP 4: Validity of Arguments and Reasoning

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate the logical soundness of arguments, reasoning, or inferences. These expressions assess whether conclusions follow properly from premises or whether reasoning contains logical errors. Primarily technical usage in logic, philosophy, and critical analysis, though some terms appear in educated general discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- valid
- invalid
- sound
- unsound
- fallacious
- fallacy
- spurious (when applied to reasoning)
- veridical (in philosophical contexts)

**Typical contexts:** Logical analysis, philosophical argument, academic discourse, critical thinking instruction.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "sound" — also used for physical integrity and financial stability
- "correct" — can apply to logical validity but more commonly to factual accuracy
- "right" — occasionally used for valid reasoning but less technical than "valid"

---

### GROUP 5: Legitimacy and Legal/Social Authorization

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether claims, authority, actions, or status conform to law, established rules, or accepted social standards. These expressions assess proper authorization, legal standing, or conformity to institutional norms rather than factual truth or logical validity. Common in legal, political, and administrative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- legitimate
- illegitimate
- legitimacy
- legitimize
- lawful
- unlawful
- rightful
- wrongful
- valid (when applied to documents, licenses, tickets)
- invalid (when applied to documents, licenses, tickets)
- bona fide (in legal contexts)
- false imprisonment
- false arrest
- no longer valid

**Typical contexts:** Legal proceedings, political authority, administrative procedures, inheritance disputes, professional licensing.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "valid" — used for both logical soundness and legal/administrative authorization
- "legitimate" — can also mean "reasonable" or "justified" beyond strict legal sense
- "right" and "wrong" — overlap in moral and legal domains
- "authentic" — occasionally used for legal authorization but primarily for genuineness

---

### GROUP 6: Reliability and Consistency

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether sources, instruments, persons, or methods consistently produce accurate results or dependable performance over time. These expressions emphasize consistency, dependability, and trustworthiness rather than single-instance accuracy. Common in technical, scientific, and practical contexts involving repeated performance.

**Representative expressions:**
- reliable
- unreliable
- reliability
- unreliability
- dependable
- consistent
- sound (when applied to judgment or methods)
- tried and true

**Typical contexts:** Scientific measurement, equipment evaluation, source assessment, character judgment, methodology evaluation.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "sound" — also used for logical validity and physical integrity
- "faithful" — overlaps when applied to accurate reproduction over time
- "accurate" — single-instance accuracy vs. consistent reliability
- "trustworthy" — overlaps but emphasizes moral character as well as reliability

---

### GROUP 7: Faithful Reproduction and Accuracy of Representation

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether representations, translations, copies, or portrayals accurately preserve or reproduce an original. These expressions assess the degree of correspondence between a representation and what it represents, emphasizing preservation of essential features. Common in artistic, literary, and documentary contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- faithful
- faithfully
- unfaithful
- accurate (when applied to representations)
- true to life
- true to form
- realistic
- lifelike
- authentic (when applied to portrayals)

**Typical contexts:** Translation evaluation, artistic representation, biographical accuracy, documentary filmmaking, historical recreation.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "accurate" — can apply to measurements or representations
- "faithful" — also used for personal loyalty
- "authentic" — can apply to original objects or accurate portrayals
- "true" — occasionally used for accurate representation

---

### GROUP 8: Appropriateness and Fittingness

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether descriptions, comparisons, responses, or actions are particularly suitable, fitting, or appropriate to their context. These expressions assess contextual appropriateness rather than factual accuracy or moral correctness. Common in aesthetic judgment and social evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- apt
- aptly
- inapt
- fitting
- appropriate
- suitable
- false note (metaphorical)
- ring true (intuitive fittingness)
- ring false (intuitive unfittingness)

**Typical contexts:** Literary criticism, aesthetic judgment, social appropriateness, rhetorical evaluation.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "right" — can indicate appropriateness as well as correctness
- "correct" — sometimes used for social appropriateness ("correct behavior")
- "true" in "ring true" — intuitive sense of fittingness rather than factual accuracy

---

### GROUP 9: Moral Rightness and Wrongness

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether actions, decisions, or conduct are morally right or wrong, just or unjust. These expressions make moral judgments about behavior rather than assessing factual accuracy or logical validity. They often appear in contexts of moral deliberation, ethical judgment, and character evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- right (in moral sense: "the right thing to do")
- wrong (in moral sense: "it's wrong to lie")
- righteous
- righteousness
- wrongdoing
- wrongdoer
- do right by
- do wrong
- serve someone right
- two wrongs don't make a right

**Typical contexts:** Moral deliberation, ethical judgment, character evaluation, justice assessment.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "right" and "wrong" — used for both factual correctness and moral evaluation
- "honest" and "dishonest" — overlap between moral character and truthfulness
- "just" and "unjust" — closely related but not in P1/P2 data
- "legitimate" — can carry moral as well as legal connotations

---

### GROUP 10: Existence and Reality vs. Non-existence

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish what actually exists or occurs in reality from what is imaginary, fictional, hypothetical, or virtual. These expressions contrast the actual world with mental constructs, simulations, or theoretical possibilities. Common in philosophical discourse and everyday distinctions between reality and imagination.

**Representative expressions:**
- real (in existential sense)
- unreal
- reality
- unreality
- actual
- in real life
- real world
- for real

**Typical contexts:** Philosophical ontology, everyday reality-checking, virtual vs. physical distinctions, dream vs. waking states.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "real" — also used for authenticity of objects and sincerity of persons
- "true" — occasionally used for existence ("true love exists") but less common
- "genuine" — primarily for authenticity but can overlap with existence claims

---

### GROUP 11: Realization and Actualization

**Definition:** Terms and expressions used to describe the process of something potential, hoped-for, or predicted becoming actual or real. These expressions focus on the transition from possibility to actuality rather than static truth or existence. Common in narrative contexts and discussions of goals and predictions.

**Representative expressions:**
- come true
- realize
- realization
- make real
- actualize

**Typical contexts:** Narrative about wishes and dreams, goal achievement, prediction fulfillment, project completion.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "realize" — also means "become aware of" (epistemic sense)
- "real" — static existence vs. dynamic realization

---

### GROUP 12: Deceptive Appearances and Misleading Indicators

**Definition:** Terms and expressions used to describe situations, signs, or indicators that appear to suggest one thing but actually indicate something else, or that prove to be mistaken or premature. These expressions emphasize the gap between appearance and reality or between initial indication and actual outcome. Common in contexts of warning, disappointment, or correction.

**Representative expressions:**
- false alarm
- false start
- false dawn
- false positive
- false negative
- false flag
- too good to be true
- false economy
- false move
- false step

**Typical contexts:** Emergency response, medical testing, sports, political analysis, practical warnings.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "false" — used in many specialized compounds with distinct meanings
- "spurious" — can indicate deceptive appearance or actual falsity

---

### GROUP 13: Epistemic Hedging and Qualification

**Definition:** Expressions used to qualify or hedge statements, indicating uncertainty, tentativeness, or invitation for correction. These phrases mark the speaker's epistemic stance as provisional rather than asserting full confidence. Common in polite discourse and careful communication.

**Representative expressions:**
- if I'm not mistaken
- unless I'm mistaken
- correct me if I'm wrong
- if truth be told
- truth be told
- to tell (you) the truth
- to be honest

**Typical contexts:** Polite conversation, careful assertion, invitation for correction, frank admission.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "to be honest" and "to tell the truth" — function as both hedges and discourse markers introducing frank statements
- These expressions often have pragmatic functions beyond their literal meanings

---

### GROUP 14: Emphasis and Intensification of Truth Claims

**Definition:** Expressions used to emphasize or intensify the truth, genuineness, or seriousness of a statement. These phrases strengthen truth claims or signal a shift to more serious or authentic communication. Common in conversational contexts where credibility or seriousness needs emphasis.

**Representative expressions:**
- really
- truly
- for real
- honest to God
- honest to goodness
- the honest truth
- the plain truth
- the whole truth
- gospel truth
- in truth

**Typical contexts:** Conversational emphasis, credibility assertion, serious communication, oath-taking.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- "really" — also functions as degree intensifier ("really big")
- "for real" — can be question or assertion
- Many of these have both literal and pragmatic/discourse functions

---

### GROUP 15: Technical Testing and Diagnostic Terms

**Definition:** Specialized terms used in testing, diagnostics, and measurement contexts to describe types of errors or results. These expressions classify outcomes in systematic testing procedures, particularly distinguishing correct identifications from various types of errors. Primarily technical usage in medical, scientific, and quality control contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- false positive
- false negative
- accurate (in measurement contexts)
- accuracy (in measurement contexts)
- reliable (in testing contexts)
- reliability (in testing contexts)
- valid (in testing contexts)
- validity (in testing contexts)

**Typical contexts:** Medical testing, scientific measurement, quality control, statistical analysis, diagnostic procedures.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- These terms have technical meanings in testing contexts that differ from their everyday usage
- "accurate," "reliable," and "valid" have distinct technical meanings in measurement theory

---

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with Multiple Group Memberships

**right / wrong**
- Appears in Groups 1 (factual correctness), 5 (legal/moral legitimacy), and 9 (moral evaluation)
- Usage context determines which sense is active
- No single grouping captures all uses

**real**
- Appears in Groups 2 (authenticity of objects), 3 (sincerity in "keep it real"), and 10 (existence)
- Highly polysemous with distinct usage patterns in different contexts

**valid**
- Appears in Groups 4 (logical validity) and 5 (legal/administrative authorization)
- Technical meanings differ significantly between contexts

**sound**
- Appears in Groups 4 (logical soundness) and 6 (reliability)
- Also used for physical integrity (not grouped here)

**faithful**
- Appears in Groups 6 (reliability) and 7 (accurate reproduction)
- Also used for personal loyalty (not grouped here)

**authentic**
- Appears in Groups 2 (object authenticity) and 7 (accurate portrayal)
- Also used for personal authenticity (not fully captured)

**truthful**
- Appears in Groups 1 (factual accuracy) and 3 (personal honesty)
- Application to statements vs. persons determines grouping

### Items Not Clearly Grouped

**apocryphal**
- Indicates doubtful authenticity of stories or claims
- Overlaps with Groups 1 and 2 but has specialized historical/textual usage
- Not clearly aligned with other terms in those groups

**veridical**
- Technical philosophical term for accurate perception
- Overlaps with Group 1 but highly specialized usage
- Limited to academic discourse

**mendacious / mendacity**
- Formal terms for lying
- Overlaps with Group 3 but much more formal register
- Limited usage compared to "dishonest" or "lying"

**veracious / veracity**
- Formal terms for truthfulness
- Overlaps with Groups 1 and 3 but elevated register
- "Veracity" more common than "veracious"

**ersatz**
- Borrowed term for inferior substitutes
- Related to Group 2 but emphasizes inferiority rather than mere inauthenticity
- Distinct connotations not fully captured by authenticity grouping

**bogus**
- Informal term for fake or fraudulent
- Overlaps with Group 2 but more colloquial
- Less specific than "counterfeit" or "fake"

### Compound Expressions with Specialized Meanings

Many compound expressions (e.g., "false imprisonment," "false arrest," "false bottom," "false teeth," "false friend") have specialized meanings that don't align neatly with the core senses of their component words. These are documented in P2b but don't form coherent semantic groups beyond their individual specialized uses.

---

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Polysemy and Context-Dependence

The most frequent and basic terms ("true," "false," "right," "wrong," "real") show extensive polysemy, appearing in multiple groups depending on context. Their meanings are highly context-dependent, and usage patterns rather than core meanings determine appropriate grouping.

### Register and Formality Stratification

Several groups show internal stratification by register:
- Group 1: "true/false" (neutral) vs. "veridical" (technical)
- Group 3: "honest" (common) vs. "mendacious" (formal/literary)
- Group 2: "fake" (informal) vs. "spurious" (formal)

### Domain-Specific Specialization

Some groups are strongly associated with particular domains:
- Group 4: Logic and philosophy
- Group 5: Legal and administrative contexts
- Group 15: Medical and scientific testing
- Group 7: Arts and translation

### Asymmetries in Positive/Negative Pairs

Not all positive terms have corresponding negative terms with equal frequency or productivity:
- "true" is more productive than "false" in compounds
- "honest" and "dishonest" are equally common, but "sincere/insincere" show less balance
- "legitimate/illegitimate" are balanced, but "genuine" lacks a common negative counterpart (not *"ungenuine")

### Pragmatic vs. Semantic Groupings

Groups 13 and 14 are based primarily on pragmatic function (hedging, emphasis) rather than semantic content, reflecting the importance of discourse functions in this semantic domain.
