# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

**Scope note:** This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, collocational, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where a construction type has no common attested representative for a given base, this is stated explicitly. Constructions are organized by P1 base form.

## Base Form 1: உண்மை / *uṇmai*

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை சொல்லு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai collu
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth / Say the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Imperative construction combining *uṇmai* as a nominal object with the verb *col* (to say/tell). Extremely common in spoken Tamil across all registers and age groups, used in direct address, moral instruction, and legal contexts. The finite forms *uṇmai collukiṟāṉ* (he is telling the truth), *uṇmai colluvēṉ* (I will tell the truth) are equally productive.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை பேசு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai pēcu
4. **English translation:** Speak the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Imperative construction with *pēcu* (to speak). Functionally equivalent to 1.1 but *pēcu* carries a slightly more sustained or deliberate sense of speaking. Appears in proverbs and moral exhortation: *uṇmai pēcu, nīti vāḻu* (speak truth, live justly). Common across spoken and written registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu
4. **English translation:** The truth came to light / The truth was revealed
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction using the verb *veḷippaṭu* (to come out, become visible, be revealed). Common in journalistic and narrative Tamil to describe the emergence of a previously concealed truth. The verbal noun form *uṇmai veḷippaṭutal* is documented in P1b; this is the finite past form in active use.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை மறைக்கப்பட்டது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu
4. **English translation:** The truth was concealed / The truth was suppressed
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction using *maṟai* (to conceal) in the Tamil passive (*-kkap-paṭu*). Used in legal, journalistic, and moral discourse to characterize deliberate suppression of truth. The active form *uṇmai maṟaittāṉ* (he concealed the truth) is equally common.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai teriya vantatu
4. **English translation:** The truth became known / The truth came out
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *teri* (to be known/apparent) + *va* (to come) in a purposive-resultative pattern. Common in spoken Tamil and popular narrative to describe the eventual disclosure of hidden truth. Slightly more colloquial than *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* (1.3).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai
4. **English translation:** It is not known what the truth is / The truth is not clear
5. **Usage notes:** A common epistemic construction embedding *uṇmai* in an indirect question (*eṉṉa* — what) with the negative *teriyavillai* (is not known/apparent). Used in everyday and journalistic Tamil to express uncertainty about the actual state of affairs.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை மூடி மறைத்தார்கள்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai mūṭi maṟaittārkaḷ
4. **English translation:** They covered up the truth / They concealed the truth (by covering it)
5. **Usage notes:** A compound verb construction using *mūṭu* (to cover) + *maṟai* (to conceal) in sequence, intensifying the sense of deliberate suppression. The double verb construction is a natural Tamil pattern for expressing thoroughness of an action. Used in political and journalistic discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை நிலை என்ன?
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai nilai eṉṉa?
4. **English translation:** What is the actual state of affairs? / What is the true situation?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction using *uṇmai nilai* (true state/condition) as a nominal phrase. Common in journalistic and administrative Tamil when questioning the accuracy of official accounts or reports. The construction presupposes a gap between appearance and reality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை அறிய வேண்டும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai aṟiya vēṇṭum
4. **English translation:** One must know the truth / It is necessary to know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction using *aṟi* (to know) in the infinitive + *vēṇṭum* (must, it is necessary). Common in philosophical, investigative, and moral discourse expressing the obligation or necessity of attaining knowledge of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.10**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மையா?
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaiyā?
4. **English translation:** Is that true? / Is that the truth?
5. **Usage notes:** A simple interrogative construction using *uṇmai* as a predicate nominal with the question particle *-ā*. Extremely common in spoken Tamil as a basic truth-checking question. The affirmative response *ām, uṇmai* (yes, it is true) is equally frequent.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மைதான்
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaitāṉ
4. **English translation:** That is indeed the truth / That is certainly true
5. **Usage notes:** An emphatic affirmative construction using *uṇmai* as predicate nominal + emphatic particle *tāṉ*. Common in spoken Tamil to confirm or concede the truth of a statement. The particle *tāṉ* adds a sense of acknowledgment or concession.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை ஒத்துக்கொண்டான்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai ottukkōṇṭāṉ
4. **English translation:** He admitted the truth / He acknowledged the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *ottukkō* (to admit, acknowledge, concede) with *uṇmai* as accusative object. Common in legal, interpersonal, and narrative contexts to describe the act of admitting a true state of affairs. The construction implies prior concealment or denial.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை நம்பவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nampavil̲l̲ai
4. **English translation:** (He/she/they) did not believe the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *nampu* (to believe/trust) with *uṇmai* as accusative object in the negative. Used in narrative and moral discourse to describe the failure or refusal to accept a true state of affairs. Highlights the distinction between truth and its acceptance.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை எதிர்கொள்ள வேண்டும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai etiṟkoḷḷa vēṇṭum
4. **English translation:** One must face the truth / It is necessary to confront the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction using *etiṟkoḷ* (to face, confront) + *vēṇṭum*. Used in moral, psychological, and journalistic discourse to express the obligation to acknowledge difficult truths. Less common than 1.9 but well-attested in written Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Base Form 2: பொய் / *poy*

---

**Construction 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்லாதே
3. **Transliteration:** poy collāte
4. **English translation:** Don't tell lies / Don't lie
5. **Usage notes:** Negative imperative construction with *col* (to say/tell) + negative imperative *-āte*. Extremely common in spoken Tamil in moral instruction, parenting, and everyday address. The positive imperative *poy collāte* (don't lie) is more frequent than the positive *poy collu* (tell a lie) in instructional contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் பேசுகிறான்
3. **Transliteration:** poy pēcukiṟāṉ
4. **English translation:** He is lying / He is speaking falsely
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *pēcu* (to speak) with *poy* as a nominal object or adverbial modifier. Common in spoken and written Tamil to characterize ongoing deceptive speech. The past form *poy pēcināṉ* (he lied/spoke falsely) is equally common.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் சொல்வது தவறு
3. **Transliteration:** poy coluvatu tavaṟu
4. **English translation:** Telling lies is wrong / Lying is a mistake/fault
5. **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the verbal noun *poy coluvatu* (the act of telling lies) as subject with *tavaṟu* (wrong, mistake) as predicate. Common in moral instruction and ethical discourse. Represents a standard Tamil pattern for expressing moral evaluations of actions.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.4**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய்யில் வாழ்கிறான்
3. **Transliteration:** poyyil vāḻkiṟāṉ
4. **English translation:** He lives in falsehood / He lives by lies
5. **Usage notes:** A locative construction using *poy* + locative *-il* with the verb *vāḻ* (to live). Used in literary and moral discourse to characterize a person whose life or conduct is pervasively deceptive. Less common in everyday speech; appears in literary and formal registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.5**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் கட்டிவிட்டான்
3. **Transliteration:** poy kaṭṭiviṭṭāṉ
4. **English translation:** He fabricated a lie / He constructed a falsehood
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *kaṭṭu* (to build, construct, fabricate) + completive *viṭu* with *poy* as object. Used in spoken and written Tamil to describe the deliberate construction of a false account. The compound verb *kaṭṭiviṭu* emphasizes completion and deliberateness of the act.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.6**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய் மேல் பொய் சொல்கிறான்
3. **Transliteration:** poy mēl poy colkiṟāṉ
4. **English translation:** He tells lie upon lie / He keeps piling up lies
5. **Usage notes:** A reduplicated construction using *poy mēl poy* (lie upon lie) to express accumulation or compounding of falsehoods. Common in spoken Tamil and in narrative to characterize habitual or escalating deception. The pattern *X mēl X* (X upon X) is a productive Tamil construction for expressing accumulation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.7**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** அது பொய்யில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** atu poyyillai
4. **English translation:** That is not a lie / That is not false
5. **Usage notes:** A simple negative predicative construction using *poy* + negative existential *illai*. Common in spoken Tamil as a denial of a charge of lying or as an assertion that something is genuine. Functions as the negative counterpart of *atu uṇmaitāṉ* (1.11).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.8**

1. **Base Form:** பொய் / *poy*
2. **Example:** பொய்யை மூடி மறைத்தான்
3. **Transliteration:** poyyai mūṭi maṟaittāṉ
4. **English translation:** He covered up the lie / He concealed the falsehood
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to construction 1.7 but with *poy* as the object rather than *uṇmai*. Used to describe the concealment of a deception rather than the concealment of truth. Attested in narrative and journalistic Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Base Form 3: நேர்மை / *nērmai*

---

**Construction 3.1**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையாக பேசு
3. **Transliteration:** nērmayāka pēcu
4. **English translation:** Speak honestly / Speak with integrity
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction using the *-āka* form of *nērmai* with the verb *pēcu* (to speak). Common in everyday and formal speech as an instruction or request for candid communication. The finite forms *nērmayāka pēcukiṟāṉ* (he speaks honestly) and *nērmayāka pēcuvēṉ* (I will speak honestly) are equally productive.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.2**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மை காட்டினான்
3. **Transliteration:** nērmai kāṭṭiṉāṉ
4. **English translation:** He showed honesty / He demonstrated integrity
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *kāṭṭu* (to show, display) with *nērmai* as object. Common in evaluative discourse to describe the demonstration of honest conduct in a specific situation. The verbal noun form *nērmai kāṭṭutal* is documented in P1b; this is the finite past form.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.3**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மை கடைப்பிடிக்கிறார்
3. **Transliteration:** nērmai kaṭaippiṭikkiṟār
4. **English translation:** He/she adheres to honesty / He/she maintains integrity
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *kaṭaippiṭi* (to follow, adhere to, observe) with *nērmai* as object. Used in moral and professional discourse to describe the sustained practice of honest conduct. The honorific third-person form *-ār* is used here, appropriate for formal or respectful reference.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.4**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மை இல்லாமல் வாழ்வது கஷ்டம்
3. **Transliteration:** nērmai illāmal vāḻvatu kaṣṭam
4. **English translation:** It is difficult to live without honesty / Living without integrity is hard
5. **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the negative converb *nērmai illāmal* (without honesty) with the verbal noun *vāḻvatu* (the act of living) as subject and *kaṣṭam* (difficulty, hardship) as predicate. Used in moral and philosophical discourse. Represents a common Tamil pattern for expressing the difficulty of a mode of life.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.5**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** அவரிடம் நேர்மை இல்லை
3. **Transliteration:** avaṟiṭam nērmai illai
4. **English translation:** There is no honesty in him/her / He/she lacks integrity
5. **Usage notes:** A simple existential negative construction using *nērmai* + *illai* (does not exist, is absent). Common in everyday moral evaluation of persons. The locative-dative *-iṭam* marks the person in whom the quality is absent.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3.6**

1. **Base Form:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Example:** நேர்மையுடன் பணி செய்கிறார்
3. **Transliteration:** nērmayuṭaṉ paṇi ceykiṟār
4. **English translation:** He/she works with integrity / He/she performs duties honestly
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using the sociative *nērmayuṭaṉ* (with honesty) with the verb *paṇi cey* (to do work/service). Common in professional and civic discourse to characterize honest performance of duties. The sociative construction is a productive Tamil pattern for expressing the manner of an action.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Base Form 4: உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāṉa*

**Note:** *uṇmaiyāṉa* functions primarily as an attributive modifier and does not independently anchor multi-word constructions beyond those already captured under *uṇmai* (Base Form 1). The following construction is noted as it involves *uṇmaiyāṉa* in a fixed evaluative frame not reducible to the base nominal.

---

**Construction 4.1**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāṉa*
2. **Example:** உண்மையான அர்த்தத்தில் சொல்வதென்றால்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa arttattil colvatēṉṟāl
4. **English translation:** If one is to say it in the true sense / Properly speaking / In the genuine meaning of the term
5. **Usage notes:** A semi-fixed framing construction used in formal and philosophical written Tamil to introduce a definition or clarification that specifies the strict or proper application of a concept. The conditional *-eṉṟāl* (if one says/if we say) adds a hedging or definitional frame. Documented in P1 (item 18) and P1b; this is the full finite construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Base Form 5: சரி / *cari*

**Construction 5.1**

2. **Example:** சரியாக புரிந்துகொண்டாயா?
3. **Transliteration:** cariyāka purinthukōṇṭāyā?
4. **English translation:** Did you understand correctly? / Have you got it right?
5. **Usage notes:** A common interrogative construction using the adverbial *cariyāka* (correctly) with the verb *purinthukō* (to understand, comprehend). Extremely common in spoken Tamil in educational, instructional, and everyday communicative contexts to check comprehension.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.2**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*

4. **English translation:** I have checked / I have verified it
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *caripār* (to check/verify) + completive *viṭu* in the past tense. The completive *viṭu* signals that the checking is finished. Common in administrative, technical, and everyday contexts to report the completion of a verification task.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.3**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** அது சரிதானா?
3. **Transliteration:** atu caritāṉā?
4. **English translation:** Is that really correct? / Is that actually right?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction using *cari* + emphatic *tāṉ* + question particle *-ā*. Common in spoken Tamil to express mild doubt or to seek confirmation of a claim. The *tāṉ* adds a nuance of seeking reassurance or expressing slight surprise.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.4**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** சரி செய்துவிட்டார்கள்
3. **Transliteration:** cari ceytuviṭṭārkaḷ
4. **English translation:** They have fixed it / They have corrected it / They have set it right
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *cari cey* (to correct, fix) + completive *viṭu* in the past tense. Common in everyday and administrative Tamil to report the completion of a correction or repair. The completive *viṭu* signals finality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.5**

1. **Base Form:** சரி / *cari*
2. **Example:** அது சரியில்லை என்று தெரிகிறது
3. **Transliteration:** atu cariyillai eṉṟu terikiraṭu
4. **English translation:** It appears that is not right / It seems that is incorrect
5. **Usage notes:** A reported-evidential construction embedding *atu cariyillai* (that is not right) in an indirect speech frame with *eṉṟu* (saying/that) + *teri* (to be apparent/known). Common in spoken Tamil to express an inference or perception that something is incorrect, with a degree of epistemic hedging.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Base Form 6: தவறு / *tavaṟu*

---

**Construction 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
2. **Example:** தவறு செய்துவிட்டேன்
3. **Transliteration:** tavaṟu ceytuviṭṭēṉ
4. **English translation:** I have made a mistake / I have done wrong

**Construction 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** தவறு / *tavaṟu*
2. **Example:** தவறை ஒத்துக்கொண்டான்

## Base Form 20: சாட்சி / *cāṭci*

---

**Construction 20.1**

1. **Base Form:** சாட்சி / *cāṭci*
2. **Example:** சாட்சி சொல்கிறான்
3. **Transliteration:** cāṭci colkiṟāṉ
4. **English translation:** He is giving testimony / He is bearing witness
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *cāṭci col* (to give testimony, to bear witness) as a light verb construction. Common in legal Tamil to describe the act of testifying. The finite forms across tenses are all productive: *cāṭci colluvāṉ* (he will testify), *cāṭci collināṉ* (he testified).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20.2**

1. **Base Form:** சாட்சி / *cāṭci*
2. **Example:** சாட்சி கொடுத்தான்
3. **Transliteration:** cāṭci koṭuttāṉ
4. **English translation:** He gave testimony / He provided evidence
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *cāṭci koṭu* (to give testimony/evidence) as a light verb construction. Functionally equivalent to *cāṭci col* (20.1) in most legal contexts, but *koṭu* (to give) may emphasize the formal submission of evidence more than *col* (to say/tell). Both constructions are common in legal Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20.3**

1. **Base Form:** சாட்சி / *cāṭci*
2. **Example:** சாட்சி விசாரணை நடந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** cāṭci vicāraṇai naṭantatu
4. **English translation:** The examination of witnesses took place / The witness examination was conducted
5. **Usage notes:** A nominal phrase construction using *cāṭci vicāraṇai* (witness examination) as subject with *naṭa* (to take place, occur) as verb. Common in legal journalism and court reporting in Tamil. The phrase *cāṭci vicāraṇai* is a fixed compound in legal Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20.4**

1. **Base Form:** சாட்சி / *cāṭci*
2. **Example:** பொய்ச் சாட்சி சொன்னான்
3. **Transliteration:** poy-c-cāṭci coṉṉāṉ
4. **English translation:** He gave false testimony / He committed perjury
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *poy-c-cāṭci* (false testimony/perjury) as object with *col* (to say/tell) in the past tense. Common in legal and moral discourse to characterize the act of perjury. The compound *poy-c-cāṭci* is documented in P1 (items 2 and 20) and P2a (form 20.2).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20.5**

1. **Base Form:** சாட்சி / *cāṭci*
2. **Example:** சாட்சி இல்லாமல் நிரூபிக்க முடியாது
3. **Transliteration:** cāṭci illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu
4. **English translation:** It cannot be proven without a witness / Without testimony it is impossible to prove
5. **Usage notes:** A negative modal construction using *cāṭci illāmal* (without a witness/testimony) as a negative converb with *nirūpikka muṭiyātu* (it is not possible to prove). Common in legal discourse to assert the necessity of witness testimony for proof. The construction *X illāmal Y muṭiyātu* (without X, Y is not possible) is a productive Tamil pattern for expressing necessary conditions.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
