## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **totoo / totoo**
- **Participates in:** 6 P1 items  
  (*totoo, katotohanan, totohanan, totoong, sa totoo lang,* and indirectly the same stem in the truth-family)
- **Core meaning contribution:** truth, realness, genuineness, actuality

### 2. **wasto / wasto**
- **Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
  (*wasto, wastong, wasto ba*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** correctness, propriety, proper form or procedure

### 3. **salig / salig**
- **Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
  (*salig, saligan, walang saligan*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** basis, grounding, reliance, support

## Step 2 — Root expansion

## Root: **totoo / totoo**

Note: this root is productive, but the commonly used set is not extremely large. The list below focuses on the most regular and well-attested expressions.

### 1. **totoo**
- **Transliteration:** totoo
- **Breakdown:** root form
- **English:** true; real
- **Context:** Used predicatively for factual truth or realness: *Totoo iyan.* Also used in contrast with falsehood, pretense, or fiction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **totoong**
- **Transliteration:** totoong
- **Breakdown:** *totoo* + ligature *-ng*
- **English:** true; real; genuine
- **Context:** Common attributive form before nouns: *totoong balita, totoong pangalan*. Used in both everyday and formal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-han*
- **English:** truth; reality; fact
- **Context:** Standard abstract noun in journalism, law, philosophy, religion, and ordinary discussion: *ang katotohanan*. Often refers to the truth of a matter or reality itself.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **katotohananan**
- **Transliteration:** katotohananan
- **Breakdown:** not included; not a standard common form
- **English:** —
- **Context:** Not a regular common expression; excluded from expansion.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### 5. **sa katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** sa katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** *sa* + *katotohanan*
- **English:** in truth; in fact
- **Context:** Common discourse marker in formal and semi-formal speech and writing. Used to introduce a correction, clarification, or stronger assertion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **ang katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** ang katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** *ang* + *katotohanan*
- **English:** the truth
- **Context:** Very common nominal phrase in legal, journalistic, moral, and religious discourse. Used when referring to “the truth” as a definite matter.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **buong katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** buong katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** *buo-ng* + *katotohanan*
- **English:** the whole truth
- **Context:** Common in testimony, confession, journalism, and moral discourse. Used when stressing completeness rather than partial disclosure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **pawang katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** pawang katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** *pawang* + *katotohanan*
- **English:** nothing but the truth
- **Context:** Used in formal or rhetorical contexts, especially in testimony or emphatic assertion. Less colloquial than *buong katotohanan*.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 9. **katotohanan ng pangyayari**
- **Transliteration:** katotohanan ng pangyayari
- **Breakdown:** *katotohanan* + *ng* + *pangyayari*
- **English:** the truth of what happened; factual truth of the घटना
- **Context:** Common in legal, investigative, and journalistic contexts when establishing what actually occurred.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **katotohanan ng buhay**
- **Transliteration:** katotohanan ng buhay
- **Breakdown:** *katotohanan* + *ng* + *buhay*
- **English:** truth of life; reality of life
- **Context:** Used in reflective, literary, philosophical, and everyday speech for hard realities or enduring facts about life.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11. **mga katotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** mga katotohanan
- **Breakdown:** plural marker + *katotohanan*
- **English:** facts; truths
- **Context:** Common in expository, academic, and journalistic writing. Refers to established facts or multiple truths under discussion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12. **sa totoo lang**
- **Transliteration:** sa totoo lang
- **Breakdown:** *sa* + *totoo* + *lang*
- **English:** honestly; to tell the truth
- **Context:** Very common discourse marker in conversation. Introduces candid opinion, admission, or correction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13. **kung tutuusin**
- **Transliteration:** kung tutuusin
- **Breakdown:** from reduplicated/derived form of *totoo* family; idiomatic
- **English:** if you really think about it; actually; when it comes down to it
- **Context:** Common conversational and essayistic expression used to introduce a more exact or candid assessment. Its connection to the *totoo* family is historical/idiomatic rather than transparent to all speakers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 14. **totohanan**
- **Transliteration:** totohanan
- **Breakdown:** from *totoo* + nominal morphology
- **English:** seriousness; for real; in earnest
- **Context:** Used in contrast with joking, play, or pretense. Often appears in colloquial expressions rather than as a standalone abstract noun.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 15. **sa totohanan**
- **Transliteration:** sa totohanan
- **Breakdown:** *sa* + *totohanan*
- **English:** seriously; for real; in earnest
- **Context:** Colloquial expression used when shifting from joking to seriousness. Less central than *sa totoo lang* and more restricted in use.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 16. **magkatotoo**
- **Transliteration:** magkatotoo
- **Breakdown:** *magka-* + *totoo*
- **English:** come true; turn out to be true
- **Context:** Very common for hopes, fears, predictions, rumors, or prophecies: *Sana magkatotoo*. Used for eventual realization or confirmation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17. **nagkatotoo**
- **Transliteration:** nagkatotoo
- **Breakdown:** *nagka-* + *totoo*
- **English:** came true; turned out to be true
- **Context:** Used for events, predictions, suspicions, and expectations that were later confirmed or realized.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18. **magkakatotoo**
- **Transliteration:** magkakatotoo
- **Breakdown:** future/aspectual form of *magkatotoo*
- **English:** will come true
- **Context:** Common in speech about hopes, plans, or predictions. Since this is an inflected/aspectual form, it is not expanded further.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### 19. **nagkakatotoo**
- **Transliteration:** nagkakatotoo
- **Breakdown:** progressive/aspectual form of *magkatotoo*
- **English:** is coming true
- **Context:** Used for something in the process of being realized. As an inflected/aspectual form, it is not counted as a separate lexical expansion item.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### 20. **totohanin**
- **Transliteration:** totohanin
- **Breakdown:** *totoo* + *-hin*
- **English:** take seriously; do for real
- **Context:** Common colloquial verb, especially in warnings or challenges: *Huwag mong totohanin* / *Totohanin mo*. Often concerns seriousness rather than factual truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21. **tinotohanan**
- **Transliteration:** tinotohanan
- **Breakdown:** verbal form from *totohanan*
- **English:** treated as serious / made real
- **Context:** Exists in use, but mostly as an inflected verbal form rather than an independent lexical item. Not expanded as a separate entry.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### 22. **totohanan na**
- **Transliteration:** totohanan na
- **Breakdown:** *totohanan* + *na*
- **English:** now seriously; for real now
- **Context:** Common colloquial phrase in playful or confrontational interaction when shifting from joking to seriousness.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 23. **totoong buhay**
- **Transliteration:** totoong buhay
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *buhay*
- **English:** real life
- **Context:** Used in contrast with fiction, fantasy, role-play, or online presentation. Common in media and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24. **totoong pangalan**
- **Transliteration:** totoong pangalan
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *pangalan*
- **English:** real name
- **Context:** Common in administrative, legal, and online-identity contexts. Used to distinguish legal or actual identity from aliases.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 25. **totoong balita**
- **Transliteration:** totoong balita
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *balita*
- **English:** real news; true news
- **Context:** Used in media-literacy and everyday discussion to distinguish verified reporting from false or fabricated reports.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 26. **totoong nangyari**
- **Transliteration:** totoong nangyari
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *nangyari*
- **English:** what really happened; the true event
- **Context:** Common in testimony, narration, and argument when distinguishing actual events from rumor or distortion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 27. **totoong kaibigan**
- **Transliteration:** totoong kaibigan
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *kaibigan*
- **English:** true friend; genuine friend
- **Context:** Common in moral and interpersonal evaluation. Focuses on sincerity and authenticity in relationships.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 28. **totoong pag-ibig**
- **Transliteration:** totoong pag-ibig
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *pag-ibig*
- **English:** true love
- **Context:** Common in literary, popular, and everyday romantic discourse. Often emphasizes genuineness rather than factual truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 29. **totoong tao**
- **Transliteration:** totoong tao
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *tao*
- **English:** real person
- **Context:** Used in contrast with fictional characters, bots, impersonation, or symbolic figures. Common in digital and conversational contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 30. **totoong kuwento**
- **Transliteration:** totoong kuwento
- **Breakdown:** *totoong* + *kuwento*
- **English:** true story
- **Context:** Common in media, storytelling, and advertising. Marks a narrative as based on actual events.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Root: **wasto / wasto**

This root has relatively few very common lexical expansions. Fewer than 5 highly fixed derivatives are widely used beyond straightforward collocation.

### 1. **wasto**
- **Transliteration:** wasto
- **Breakdown:** root form
- **English:** correct; proper; appropriate
- **Context:** Used predicatively for correctness in grammar, procedure, conduct, and reasoning. More formal than *tama* in many contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **wastong**
- **Transliteration:** wastong
- **Breakdown:** *wasto* + ligature *-ng*
- **English:** proper; correct
- **Context:** Common attributive form before nouns in educational, bureaucratic, and public-information language.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **wasto ba**
- **Transliteration:** wasto ba
- **Breakdown:** *wasto* + question particle *ba*
- **English:** is it correct?; is it proper?
- **Context:** Used in evaluative or instructional settings when asking whether a form, answer, or action is proper.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 4. **wastong gamit**
- **Transliteration:** wastong gamit
- **Breakdown:** *wastong* + *gamit*
- **English:** proper use; correct usage
- **Context:** Very common in education, manuals, and public guidance. Often used for language, tools, medicine, and equipment.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **wastong baybay**
- **Transliteration:** wastong baybay
- **Breakdown:** *wastong* + *baybay*
- **English:** correct spelling
- **Context:** Common in school, dictionaries, and language instruction. Refers to orthographic correctness.

### 6. **wastong gramatika**

## Root: **salig / salig**

This root is moderately productive in formal and literary registers. Several common expressions are fixed and institutional.

### 1. **salig**
- **Transliteration:** salig
- **Breakdown:** root form
- **English:** trust; reliance; basis
- **Context:** Usually encountered in formal or literary usage rather than casual speech. It refers to reliance, confidence, or grounding.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 2. **saligan**
- **Transliteration:** saligan
- **Breakdown:** *salig* + *-an*
- **English:** basis; foundation; grounds
- **Context:** Common in legal, academic, and argumentative language for the basis of a claim, accusation, or principle.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **walang saligan**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligan
- **Breakdown:** *wala-ng* + *saligan*
- **English:** baseless; unfounded; groundless
- **Context:** Very common in legal, journalistic, and public argument. Used for accusations, rumors, assumptions, and conclusions lacking support.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **may saligan**
- **Transliteration:** may saligan
- **Breakdown:** *may* + *saligan*
- **English:** well-founded; has basis
- **Context:** Used in argument, law, and analysis to indicate that a claim or suspicion has support or grounds.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5. **walang saligang**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligang
- **Breakdown:** *walang* + *saligan* + ligatured attributive use
- **English:** baseless; unfounded
- **Context:** Common before nouns, especially *akusasyon, paratang, balita, haka-haka*. Very frequent in formal writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. **walang saligang akusasyon**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligang akusasyon
- **Breakdown:** *walang saligang* + *akusasyon*
- **English:** baseless accusation
- **Context:** Common in legal and political discourse. Used to reject allegations as unsupported.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. **walang saligang paratang**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligang paratang
- **Breakdown:** *walang saligang* + *paratang*
- **English:** unfounded allegation
- **Context:** Common in journalism, law, and public statements. Similar to *akusasyon* but often more rhetorical.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **walang saligang balita**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligang balita
- **Breakdown:** *walang saligang* + *balita*
- **English:** unfounded report; baseless news
- **Context:** Used in media criticism and public advisories for reports lacking evidentiary basis.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **walang saligang haka-haka**
- **Transliteration:** walang saligang haka-haka
- **Breakdown:** *walang saligang* + *haka-haka*
- **English:** baseless speculation
- **Context:** Common in journalism and argumentation. Used to dismiss conjecture unsupported by evidence.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **salig sa**
- **Transliteration:** salig sa
- **Breakdown:** *salig* + *sa*
- **English:** based on; grounded in
- **Context:** Common formal expression introducing the basis of a judgment, policy, or statement: *salig sa ebidensiya*. Often overlaps with *batay sa*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11. **nakasalig sa**
- **Transliteration:** nakasalig sa
- **Breakdown:** *naka-* + *salig* + *sa*
- **English:** based on; founded on; dependent on
- **Context:** Used in academic, legal, and analytical writing for systems, arguments, or decisions resting on a stated basis.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12. **ipinagsasalig**
- **Transliteration:** ipinagsasalig
- **Breakdown:** verbal derivation from *salig*
- **English:** entrusts; places reliance in
- **Context:** Attested in formal/literary use, especially in religious or elevated registers, but not common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 13. **mapananaligan**
- **Transliteration:** mapananaligan
- **Breakdown:** derivation from *panalig/salig* family
- **English:** trustworthy; reliable; something one can rely on
- **Context:** Used in formal, literary, and religious registers for persons, testimony, or sources worthy of reliance.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 14. **pananalig**
- **Transliteration:** pananalig
- **Breakdown:** related derivation in the *salig* family
- **English:** faith; trust; belief
- **Context:** Very common in religious and elevated discourse. Refers more to faith or trust than evidential grounding.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 15. **manalig**
- **Transliteration:** manalig
- **Breakdown:** verbal derivation in the same family
- **English:** believe; have faith; trust
- **Context:** Common in religious exhortation and literary style. Focuses on trusting or believing rather than factual verification.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 16. **saligang batas**
- **Transliteration:** saligang batas
- **Breakdown:** *saligan* + ligature *-ng* + *batas*
- **English:** constitution
- **Context:** Standard formal term for the Constitution. Here the sense is “fundamental law” or foundational law.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17. **saligang prinsipyo**
- **Transliteration:** saligang prinsipyo
- **Breakdown:** *saligan* + ligature *-ng* + *prinsipyo*
- **English:** foundational principle
- **Context:** Used in philosophy, law, education, and policy writing for a principle serving as a basis.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18. **saligang kaisipan**
- **Transliteration:** saligang kaisipan
- **Breakdown:** *saligan* + ligature *-ng* + *kaisipan*
- **English:** central/foundational idea
- **Context:** Used in academic writing, literary criticism, and pedagogy for an underlying or grounding idea.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 19. **walang pinagsasaligan**
- **Transliteration:** walang pinagsasaligan
- **Breakdown:** *wala-ng* + verbal nominal from *salig*
- **English:** without any basis; having nothing to rest on
- **Context:** Used in formal argument and criticism, though less fixed than *walang saligan*. Often appears in legalistic or rhetorical prose.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 20. **may pinagsasaligan**
- **Transliteration:** may pinagsasaligan
- **Breakdown:** *may* + verbal nominal from *salig*
- **English:** has some basis; has something to rest on
- **Context:** Used in formal argument to indicate that a claim or suspicion is not arbitrary.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
