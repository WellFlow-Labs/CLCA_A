# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

All oppositions below are drawn directly from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4. No opposition has been inferred or constructed beyond what the data explicitly support. Where the data describe a pair as "directly opposed," "antonym," "negative counterpart," "contrasts with," or "parallel construction," this is treated as evidence of an opposition. Where overlap exists between oppositions, this is noted rather than resolved.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *òtítọ́*
**Term B:** *irọ́*
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ / irọ́
**English Translation:** truth, truthfulness / lie, falsehood
**Description of Contrast:** P1 explicitly identifies *irọ́* as "the direct antonym of *òtítọ́*." The two terms appear in parallel verbal constructions (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), in parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú* / *irọ́ kò ní dúró*), and in parallel negative future assertions about their respective fates. P3 identifies them as "the most consistently paired terms in the entire dataset."
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *sọ òtítọ́*
**Term B:** *sọ irọ́*
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́ / sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** speak the truth / tell a lie
**Description of Contrast:** Both constructions share the same verb (*sọ*, to say/speak) and differ only in the object noun. P2b documents them as parallel constructions (items 1 and 12) and P3 identifies them as a "matched pair across moral, legal, and everyday discourse."
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** *òtítọ́ kò ní kú*
**Term B:** *irọ́ kò ní dúró*
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ kò ní kú / irọ́ kò ní dúró
**English Translation:** truth will not die / a lie will not stand
**Description of Contrast:** Two proverbial constructions documented in P2b (items 6 and 16) that assert the permanence of truth and the impermanence of falsehood respectively. Both use the negative future construction *kò ní* but with different predicates (*kú* "die" vs. *dúró* "stand/endure"), producing a structural parallel with opposing semantic outcomes.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** *ó tọ́*
**Term B:** *kò tọ́*
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́ / kò tọ́
**English Translation:** it is correct / it is not correct
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as the affirmative and negative predicative forms of the root *tọ́* (items 56 and 57), described as "fully parallel" and "extremely common in everyday and formal discourse." P4a and P4b identify them as the core attributive and negative attributive construction pair in the truth domain.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** *ó tọ́ pé*
**Term B:** *kò tọ́ pé*
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́ pé / kò tọ́ pé
**English Translation:** it is right that / it is not right that
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as the propositional-complement versions of the *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* pair (items 58 and 59), extending the correctness evaluation to embedded clauses. The contrast is structurally parallel to Opposition 4 but operates at the level of propositional content rather than simple predication.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** *ìmọ̀*
**Term B:** *àìmọ̀*
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ / àìmọ̀
**English Translation:** knowledge, understanding / ignorance, lack of knowledge
**Description of Contrast:** P1 identifies *àìmọ̀* as formed by the negation prefix *àì-* on the root *mọ̀*, and describes it as "contrasts with *àìmọ̀* (ignorance)" in the entry for *ìmọ̀*. P3 identifies the two as "the core opposition" in Group 4. The morphological negation is transparent and productive.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** *ìwà rere*
**Term B:** *ìwà burúkú*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / ìwà burúkú
**English Translation:** good character, virtue / bad character, vice
**Description of Contrast:** P1 identifies *ìwà burúkú* as "the antonym of *ìwà rere*." Both are documented as P1 base items (18 and 19) and as the "core opposition" in P3 Group 6. They share the same head noun (*ìwà*) and differ only in the evaluative modifier (*rere* "good" vs. *burúkú* "bad/evil").
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́*
**Term B:** *ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́ / ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn
**English Translation:** good character saves/helps a person / bad character kills/destroys a person
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as parallel proverbial constructions (items 72 and 73) with the same subject-predicate structure but opposing subjects (*ìwà rere* vs. *ìwà burúkú*) and opposing outcomes (saving vs. destroying). Both use the habitual marker *ń*.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** *olòtítọ́*
**Term B:** *aṣírọ́*
**Transliteration:** olòtítọ́ / aṣírọ́
**English Translation:** truthful person, one who tells the truth / liar, one who tells lies
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as agentive nominalizations characterizing persons by their habitual truth-telling or lying behavior (items 1 and 7). They are derived from the opposed base terms *òtítọ́* and *irọ́* respectively and function as the person-level counterparts of Opposition 1.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** *aláìrọ́*
**Term B:** *aṣírọ́*
**Transliteration:** aláìrọ́ / aṣírọ́
**English Translation:** one who does not lie, honest person / liar, one who tells lies
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as agentive forms derived from *irọ́* (items 6 and 7): *aláìrọ́* via the negation prefix *àì-* (one who does not lie) and *aṣírọ́* via the action prefix *aṣ-* (one who does lie). They are morphologically related to the same base but produce opposed evaluations of a person's honesty.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** *olùgbàgbọ́*
**Term B:** *aláìgbàgbọ́*
**Transliteration:** olùgbàgbọ́ / aláìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** believer, one who has faith / unbeliever, one who lacks faith
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as derivations from *gbàgbọ́* (items 17 and 16): *olùgbàgbọ́* via the *olù-* agent prefix and *aláìgbàgbọ́* via the *al-* + *àì-* negation prefix. P2a describes them as contrasting identity categories in religious communities.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú*
**Term B:** *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́*
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú / ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**English Translation:** eyewitness, direct witness / false witness, perjurer
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as compounds formed on the base *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness), with *ojú* (eye) marking the most credible witness type and *irọ́* (lie) marking the negation of the witness role's truth-function (items 22 and 23). P3 notes that *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* "combines a term from Group 3 with a term from Group 1, marking the violation of the witness role's truth-function."
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** *ẹ̀rí ojú*
**Term B:** *ẹ̀rí etí*
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú / ẹ̀rí etí
**English Translation:** eyewitness evidence, direct evidence / hearsay evidence, testimony based on what was heard
**Description of Contrast:** P1b documents *ẹ̀rí etí* as contrasting with *ẹ̀rí ojú* (Root 3, item 2), noting that hearsay evidence "is generally considered less reliable in Yoruba legal discourse." Both are compounds on *ẹ̀rí* (evidence) modified by a sensory organ (*ojú* "eye" vs. *etí* "ear"), producing a hierarchy of evidential reliability.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** *ẹ̀rí tó péye wà*
**Term B:** *ẹ̀rí kò sí*
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí tó péye wà / ẹ̀rí kò sí
**English Translation:** there is sufficient evidence / there is no evidence
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as existential constructions used in legal proceedings (items 43 and 44): the first asserts that the evidentiary threshold has been met, the second asserts the complete absence of evidence. Both use the existential frame but with opposing polarity (*wà* "exists" vs. *kò sí* "does not exist").
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** *ìdánilójú wà*
**Term B:** *ìdánilójú kò sí*
**Transliteration:** ìdánilójú wà / ìdánilójú kò sí
**English Translation:** there is assurance / there is no assurance
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as the affirmative and negative existential constructions for *ìdánilójú* (items 88 and 89), described as counterparts used in legal and evaluative contexts to assert the presence or absence of adequate verification. The pattern is structurally parallel to Opposition 14.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** *kọjá ìdánwò*
**Term B:** *kùnà ìdánwò*
**Transliteration:** kọjá ìdánwò / kùnà ìdánwò
**English Translation:** pass a test / fail a test
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as verbal constructions with *ìdánwò* as object (items 91 and 92), explicitly noting that they contrast with each other. They share the same object but differ in the verb (*kọjá* "pass/cross" vs. *kùnà* "fail/not succeed"), producing opposed evaluations of the outcome of a test.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** *àṣìṣe*
**Term B:** *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*
**Transliteration:** àṣìṣe / ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**English Translation:** error, mistake, fault / sin, guilt, offense
**Description of Contrast:** P3 Group 10 notes that *àṣìṣe* "can describe factual errors without implying moral guilt" while *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* "is more strongly associated with religious discourse and moral culpability." The two terms participate in the same constructional frames (*jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* / *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *darí àṣìṣe jì* / *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì*) but differ in the degree of moral culpability implied.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe*
**Term B:** *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*
**Transliteration:** jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe / jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀
**English Translation:** confess/admit an error / confess sin
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as constructions using the same verb *jẹ̀wọ́* (to confess/admit) with different objects (items 63 and 67). P3 notes the parallel explicitly. The contrast is not between the constructions themselves but between the objects, reflecting the distinction in Opposition 17 at the constructional level.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** *gidi*
**Term B:** *àgàbàgebè*
**Transliteration:** gidi / àgàbàgebè
**English Translation:** genuine, real, authentic / pretense, hypocrisy, sham
**Description of Contrast:** P3 Group 5 identifies *àgàbàgebè* as "the primary term for the opposite of authenticity in this group," with *gidi* and *ojúlówó* as the authenticity terms it opposes. P1 describes *àgàbàgebè* as implying "that the outward appearance does not correspond to inner reality," which is the defining concern of the group containing *gidi*.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** *ojúlówó*
**Term B:** *àgàbàgebè*
**Transliteration:** ojúlówó / àgàbàgebè
**English Translation:** genuine, authentic, legitimate / pretense, hypocrisy, sham
**Description of Contrast:** P3 Group 5 places both terms in the same group defined by the contrast between authentic presentation and performative falseness, with *àgàbàgebè* as the opposing term to the authenticity terms including *ojúlówó*. The contrast is between genuine possession of a quality and its outward performance without inner reality.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** *ìtànjẹ́*
**Term B:** *àṣìṣe*
**Transliteration:** ìtànjẹ́ / àṣìṣe
**English Translation:** deception, fraud, deceit / error, mistake, fault
**Description of Contrast:** P1 explicitly distinguishes *ìtànjẹ́* from *àṣìṣe* by noting that *ìtànjẹ́* "implies intentional misrepresentation, distinguishing it from mere error (*àṣìṣe*)." The contrast is between deliberate deception and unintentional error, not between truth and falsehood per se.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** *ìmọ̀ àti ìgbàgbọ́* (as a paired construction)
**Term B:** (implicit: the distinction between the two terms within the pair)
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ àti ìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** knowledge and belief
**Description of Contrast:** P1b (Root 2, item 24) documents this construction as used "to distinguish or relate the epistemic states of knowing and believing," appearing "in theological discussions about the relationship between faith and reason." P3 notes it as "explicitly contrasting this group [Group 7] with Group 4, treating knowing and believing as distinct but related stances." The opposition is between *ìmọ̀* (epistemic grounding in genuine knowledge) and *ìgbàgbọ́* (acceptance as true without necessarily having that grounding).
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** *òdodo*
**Term B:** *àìdodo*
**Transliteration:** òdodo / àìdodo
**English Translation:** justice, righteousness, fairness / injustice, unrighteousness, unfairness
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *àìdodo* as "the negated counterpart of *òdodo*," formed via the *àì-* negation prefix (item 10), and describes it as used "to evaluate conduct, decisions, or persons as unjust or unrighteous."
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** *dọ̀gba*
**Term B:** *àìdọ̀gba*
**Transliteration:** dọ̀gba / àìdọ̀gba
**English Translation:** equal, equivalent, matching / inequality, disparity, non-equivalence
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *àìdọ̀gba* as the negated form of *dọ̀gba* (item 55), used "to evaluate a situation as characterized by inequality or disparity." The morphological negation via *àì-* is transparent and the contrast is between correspondence/equivalence and its absence.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term B:** (implicit: superficial or absent belief)
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ jinlẹ̀
**English Translation:** deep faith, profound belief
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *ìgbàgbọ́ jinlẹ̀* as used "to distinguish superficial from genuine conviction" (item 18). The contrast is between deep/genuine faith and its shallow or absent counterpart, though no single lexical term for the opposing pole is documented in the data.

## Opposition 27

**Term A:** *alẹ̀ṣẹ̀*
**Term B:** *àìṣẹ̀*
**Transliteration:** alẹ̀ṣẹ̀ / àìṣẹ̀
**English Translation:** sinner, guilty person / sinlessness, innocence
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as derivations from *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (items 33 and 34): *alẹ̀ṣẹ̀* as the agentive form (one who has sin) and *àìṣẹ̀* as the negated form (not having sinned). P2a describes *àìṣẹ̀* as used "in assessments of innocence."
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 28

**Term A:** *aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*
**Term B:** *ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé*
**Transliteration:** aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé / ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**English Translation:** untrustworthy person, one who cannot be relied upon / a person who can be relied upon
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* as "a strong negative character assessment" (item 19) and P2b documents *ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* as the positive counterpart (item 39). The contrast is between a person who is and is not worthy of reliance, expressed through morphological negation in one form and a modal relative clause in the other.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** *ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú*
**Term B:** (implicit: faith with works, which is living)
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú
**English Translation:** faith without works is dead
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents this construction (item 37) as asserting that *ìgbàgbọ́* (faith) without *ìṣe* (works/action) is *òkú* (dead). The construction encodes an internal opposition between faith as mere belief-state and faith as expressed in action, with *òkú* (dead) implying the existence of a living counterpart. No single lexical term for the opposing pole (faith with works) is documented as a fixed expression.

*(no single opposing construction identified as a fixed form in the data)*

**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** *ìtọ́*
**Term B:** *àìtọ́*
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ / àìtọ́
**English Translation:** straightness, correctness / incorrectness, wrongness
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents *àìtọ́* as "the negated counterpart of *òtọ́/òtítọ́*," formed via the *àì-* negation prefix on the root *tọ́* (item 2). P1b also documents *ẹ̀rí àìtọ́* (evidence of incorrectness) as a compound using this negated form (Root 3, item 18). The morphological negation is transparent.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** *ẹni tó mọ̀*
**Term B:** *ẹni tí kò mọ̀*
**Transliteration:** ẹni tó mọ̀ / ẹni tí kò mọ̀
**English Translation:** one who knows, a knowledgeable person / one who does not know, an uninformed person
**Description of Contrast:** P1b documents both as relative clause constructions (Root 2, items 12 and 13) that identify persons by their epistemic status. The contrast is between having and lacking the relevant knowledge, expressed through the positive and negative forms of the relative clause on the same verb *mọ̀*.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** *ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là*
**Term B:** *àìmọ̀ kò ní gbani là*
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ ló ń gbani là / àìmọ̀ kò ní gbani là
**English Translation:** it is knowledge that saves/enriches a person / ignorance will not save/enrich a person
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as proverbial constructions (items 26 and 31), explicitly describing the second as "the negative counterpart" of the first. Both use the same predicate (*gbani là*, to save/enrich a person) but with opposing subjects (*ìmọ̀* vs. *àìmọ̀*) and opposing polarity (affirmative focus construction vs. negative future).
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]
