# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**NP + copula (*olla*) + PRED-ADJ (nominative/partitive)**

The predicate adjective agrees in case with the subject. For truth-value attribution, the partitive *totta* (from *tosi*) is obligatory in the standard construction; other adjectives use nominative.

### Examples
- *Se on totta.* → Se on totta. → "It is true."
- *Väite on perusteeton.* → Väite on perusteeton. → "The claim is unfounded."

### Notes
The partitive *totta* (not nominative *tosi*) is required in the copular truth-attribution construction *olla totta*; this is a lexically conditioned case selection unique to this item. For other adjectives (*väärä*, *aito*, *rehellinen*, *luotettava*, *uskottava*), the nominative predicate adjective is used normally: *se on väärä*, *lähde on luotettava*.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**NP + *ei ole* + PRED-ADJ (nominative) / *ei pidä paikkansa***

Negation of the copula (*ei ole*) with predicate adjective in nominative; or the idiomatic *ei pidä paikkansa* (does not hold) as a fixed negative truth-attribution.

### Examples
- *Tämä allekirjoitus ei ole aito.* → Tämä allekirjoitus ei ole aito. → "This signature is not genuine."
- *Väite ei pidä paikkaansa.* → Väite ei pidä paikkaansa. → "The claim is not true / does not hold."

### Notes
*Ei pidä paikkaansa* (literally "does not hold its place") is the idiomatic near-synonym of *ei ole totta* and is preferred in evaluative assessment of specific claims across all registers. The negative of *olla totta* is *ei ole totta* (partitive retained): *se ei ole totta* — "it is not true."

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**ADJ (nominative, agreeing in number and case) + NP**

Attributive adjectives precede the noun and agree with it in case and number. No separate attributive form exists; the same form is used predicatively and attributively.

### Examples
- *todenmukainen kuvaus* → todenmukainen kuvaus → "a true-to-life / accurate description"
- *perusteeton syytös* → perusteeton syytös → "an unfounded accusation"

### Notes
Finnish attributive adjectives are prenominal and inflect with the noun in all cases: *perusteettomasta syytöksestä* (from an unfounded accusation), *todenmukaisessa kuvauksessa* (in an accurate description). No distinction between attributive and predicative form exists morphologically.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
***osoittautua* + TRANSLATIVE (-ksi form of adjective or noun)**

The verb *osoittautua* (to turn out to be, to prove to be) takes a translative complement expressing the state that comes to be established. This is the standard inchoative construction for truth-domain adjectives.

### Examples
- *Ennuste osoittautui oikeaksi.* → Ennuste osoittautui oikeaksi. → "The forecast turned out to be correct."
- *Maalaus osoittautui väärennökseksi.* → Maalaus osoittautui väärennökseksi. → "The painting turned out to be a forgery."

### Notes
The translative case (*-ksi*) is obligatory with *osoittautua* and with other change-of-state verbs (*tulla*, *käydä*) in this function. The pattern covers the full range of truth-domain adjectives and nouns: *osoittautua aidoksi* (to prove genuine), *osoittautua vääräksi* (to prove false), *osoittautua luotettavaksi* (to prove reliable).

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Causative verb derived by *-entää* / *-istää* / *-ttaa* suffix on adjectival base**

Finnish encodes causation morphologically. The relevant causatives in this domain are: *väärentää* (to make false/forge, from *väärä*), *vääristää* (to distort/skew, from *väärä*), *oikeuttaa* (to justify/entitle, from *oikeus*). No single productive causative pattern covers all truth-domain adjectives; each is lexically specific.

### Examples
- *Hän väärensi passin.* → Hän väärensi passin. → "He/she forged the passport." (*väärentää* = cause to be false/counterfeit)
- *Monopoli vääristää kilpailua.* → Monopoli vääristää kilpailua. → "A monopoly distorts competition." (*vääristää* = cause to be skewed/distorted)

### Notes
*Väärentää* (producing a counterfeit object, implying intent) and *vääristää* (distorting or skewing, not necessarily implying intent) are morphologically parallel causatives from *väärä* but are semantically and legally distinct. No productive causative from *tosi*, *aito*, or *luotettava* is attested; those concepts are expressed periphrastically (*todistaa aidoksi*, *tehdä luotettavaksi*).

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ADJ-stem + *-uus* / *-yys* (back/front vowel harmony)**

The suffix *-uus* / *-yys* derives abstract nouns from adjectives throughout this domain. It is fully productive on all P1 adjectives. The resulting nouns name the property or quality denoted by the adjective.

### Examples
- *tarkkuus* → tarkkuus → "accuracy; precision" (from *tarkka*)
- *totuudenmukaisuus* → totuudenmukaisuus → "truthfulness; conformity to truth" (from *totuudenmukainen*)

### Notes
The suffix triggers consonant gradation where applicable (*tarkka* → *tark-* + *-kuus* → *tarkkuus*). Compound adjectives nominalize as a unit: *totuudenmukainen* → *totuudenmukaisuus*; *oikeudenmukainen* → *oikeudenmukaisuus*. The *-uus*/*-yys* nominalization is the primary mechanism for abstract noun formation across the entire truth-domain vocabulary.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Verb-stem + *-ja* / *-jä* (back/front vowel harmony)**

The suffix *-ja* / *-jä* derives agent nouns from verbs. In this domain, the primary attested agentives are built on *todistaa* and *väärentää*.

### Examples
- *todistaja* → todistaja → "witness; testifier" (from *todistaa*)
- *väärentäjä* → väärentäjä → "forger; counterfeiter" (from *väärentää*)

### Notes
No agentive nominalization from *tosi*, *totuus*, *aito*, or *rehellinen* is attested as a common lexical item; the concepts "truth-teller" and "liar" are expressed periphrastically (*henkilö, joka puhuu totta* — a person who tells the truth) or by separate lexemes (*valehtelija* — liar, from *valehdella*). The agentive *tarkastaja* (inspector/auditor, from *tarkastaa*) and *tarkkailija* (observer/monitor, from *tarkkailla*) are productive in the checking-and-verification domain.

---

## 8. RESULT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Verb-stem + *-us* / *-ys***

The suffix *-us* / *-ys* derives result nouns naming the product or outcome of the verbal action. It is distinct from the *-uus*/*-yys* quality nominalization and from the *-minen* verbal noun.

### Examples
- *todistus* → todistus → "proof; testimony; certificate" (from *todistaa*)
- *väärennös* → väärennös → "forgery; counterfeit" (from *väärentää*, with allomorphic *-ös*)

### Notes
The result nominal *todistus* is exceptionally productive as a compounding base, generating an extensive legal vocabulary (*todistustaakka*, *todistusaineisto*, *todistusvoima*, etc.). The allomorph *-ös* in *väärennös* is conditioned by the stem. The result nominal is semantically distinct from the verbal noun in *-minen*: *todistus* names the product (a certificate, a piece of testimony), while *todistaminen* names the act (the act of proving or testifying).

---

## 9. VERBAL NOUN (ACTION NOMINALIZATION)

### Pattern
**Verb-stem + *-minen***

The suffix *-minen* derives verbal nouns naming the act or process denoted by the verb. It is fully productive on all verbs in Finnish and is used throughout this domain for naming acts and processes.

### Examples
- *väärentäminen* → väärentäminen → "forgery; falsification; the act of forging" (from *väärentää*)
- *todentaminen* → todentaminen → "verification; authentication; the act of verifying" (from *todentaa*)

### Notes
The *-minen* verbal noun is used in legal language to name criminal acts (*asiakirjan väärentäminen* — document forgery) and in technical and administrative language to name processes (*kaksivaiheinen todentaminen* — two-factor authentication). It is syntactically nominal but retains the argument structure of the verb: *asiakirjan väärentäminen* (the forging of the document) parallels *väärentää asiakirja*.

## 10. CERTIFICATION CONSTRUCTION (TRANSLATIVE PREDICATION)

### Pattern
***todistaa* + NP-accusative + TRANSLATIVE (*-ksi*)**

A periphrastic construction in which *todistaa* (to certify/prove) takes a direct object and a translative complement expressing the state being certified. This construction is used for official certification and for proving or disproving claims.

### Examples
- *Notaari todistaa kopion oikeaksi.* → Notaari todistaa kopion oikeaksi. → "The notary certifies the copy as correct."
- *Tutkimus todisti teorian vääräksi.* → Tutkimus todisti teorian vääräksi. → "The study proved the theory false."

### Notes
The translative case (*-ksi*) marks the resultant state: *oikeaksi* (as correct), *vääräksi* (as false), *aidoksi* (as genuine). This construction is structurally parallel to the *osoittautua* + translative inchoative (Pattern 4) but is transitive: an external agent certifies or proves the object to be in a certain state. It is standard in notarial, legal, and scientific registers.

---

## 11. CONFORMITY COMPOUND ADJECTIVE

### Pattern
**NP-genitive + *mukainen* (conforming to)**

A productive compounding pattern in which the genitive of a noun combines with *mukainen* (conforming to, in accordance with) to form an adjective meaning "conforming to X." Abstract nouns from the truth domain participate regularly.

### Examples
- *totuudenmukainen* → totuudenmukainen → "conforming to truth; truthful; accurate" (*totuuden* + *mukainen*)
- *todenmukainen* → todenmukainen → "true-to-life; realistic; verisimilar" (*toden* + *mukainen*)

### Notes
The corresponding abstract noun is formed by adding *-uus*: *totuudenmukaisuus*, *todenmukaisuus*. The antonym pattern uses *vastainen* (contrary to) in place of *mukainen*: *totuudenvastainen* (contrary to truth), *totuudenvastaisuus* (untruthfulness). Both *mukainen* and *vastainen* compounds are productive across the domain.
