# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: English (en)
# Input: P1 base lexical items (#1–#30)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This document records attested derivational morphology for each of the 30 base items identified in P1. The inventory is drawn from the derivational families cited in P1 entries, the root expansions in P1b, and additional attested forms. Where P1 or P1b already documented a derived form, that form is included here with a cross-reference. No forms are generated by paradigm completion. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved as found.

Coverage note: P1b documented derivational families for the three central roots (*ver-*, *true/truth*, *fals-*) in considerable detail. Those forms are incorporated here under their respective base items without duplication of the full P1b analysis. The present document organizes all derivational data by P1 base item rather than by root.

## Item 1 — true

---

**1.1**
- Base Form: true
- Derived Form: truth
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: OE *trēowe* + *-þ* (abstract noun suffix)
- Natural English translation: the quality or state of being true; that which is true
- Usage: The primary nominalization of *true* and the central noun of the truth-evaluation domain. Used across all registers. "Tell me the truth."
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.2**
- Base Form: true
- Derived Form: truly
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ly*
- Natural English translation: in a true manner; genuinely; used as an intensifier
- Usage: Adverbial form used in general speech and writing, including in formulaic closings (*yours truly*). "I truly believe this."
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.3**
- Base Form: true
- Derived Form: truism
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ism*
- Natural English translation: a statement so obviously true as to be not worth stating; a platitude
- Usage: Used in intellectual and general discourse to dismiss a claim as trivially obvious. "It is a truism that prevention is better than cure."
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.4**
- Base Form: true
- Derived Form: untrue
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *un-* + *true*
- Natural English translation: not true; false
- Usage: Used in general speech as a somewhat softer alternative to *false*. "That statement is simply untrue."
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.5**
- Base Form: true
- Derived Form: trueness
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *true* + *-ness*
- Natural English translation: the quality of being true or accurate; alignment
- Usage: Attested but less common than *truth*. Used in technical contexts (e.g., the trueness of a wheel or surface) and occasionally in general evaluative use. "The trueness of the bearing surface must be checked."
- Epistemic status: **[MARGINAL]**

---

> **Note:** *Truth* (1.1) is itself a highly productive base generating further derivations documented under Item 1 of the P1b root expansion (truth-telling, truth-teller, truth value, truth table, truth condition, half-truth, home truth, ground truth, post-truth, truth commission, truth serum, moment of truth, untruth, untruthful, truthfulness). Those forms are not repeated here but are considered part of the derivational family of *true* via *truth*.

**3.2**

- Derived Form: genuineness
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal Breakdown: *genuine* + *-ness*
- Natural English translation: the quality of being genuine; authenticity; sincerity
- Usage: Used in general and evaluative contexts. Less common than *authenticity* in many registers. "The genuineness of his concern was not in doubt."

> **Note:** No common nominalization *genuinity* is attested in standard use. *Genuineness* is the standard abstract noun. No prefixed negative form (*ingenuine*, *ungenuine*) is in common use; *not genuine*, *fake*, or *counterfeit* serve this function.
