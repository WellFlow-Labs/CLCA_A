# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1

**Form:** *tọ́* (also appearing as *-tọ-* in compounds)
**Transliteration:** tọ́
**P1 participation:** 4 items directly — *òtítọ́* (1), *òtọ́* (13), *ìtọ́ni* (14), and morphologically embedded in *òtítọ́* as the core stem
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, rightness, accuracy. This root provides the evaluative criterion of correspondence to a standard — whether a path, statement, measurement, or action is straight/correct rather than deviant/wrong.

---

### Root 2

**Form:** *mọ̀*
**Transliteration:** mọ̀
**P1 participation:** 3 items — *ìmọ̀* (4), *àìmọ̀* (5), and as a component in evaluative constructions throughout the domain
**Core meaning contribution:** Knowing, recognizing, being acquainted with. This root anchors the epistemic grounding dimension of truth-evaluation — whether a person has the cognitive basis for a claim.

---

### Root 3

**Form:** *ẹ̀rí* / *jẹ́rìí*
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí / jẹ́rìí
**P1 participation:** 4 items — *ẹ̀rí* (8), *ẹ̀rí ojú* (9), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (10), *jẹ́rìí* (11)
**Core meaning contribution:** Witnessing, attesting, evidencing. This root cluster covers the evidentiary and testimonial dimension of truth — the process by which truth claims are confirmed through witness or proof.

---

**Note on selection rationale:** The root *tọ́* was prioritized over *ìwà* (character) and *dá* (establish/make) because it is the morphological core of the most semantically central item in the domain (*òtítọ́*, "truth") and generates the widest range of truth-evaluative derivatives. *Mọ̀* was selected because it anchors the epistemic grounding dimension. The *ẹ̀rí/jẹ́rìí* cluster was selected because it covers the evidentiary and testimonial dimension, which is highly productive in Yoruba legal and religious discourse. The root *dá* (appearing in *ìdánilójú*, *ìdánwò*, *ìdájọ́*) is noted as a strong candidate for a fourth root but is not expanded here as the instruction specifies 1–3 roots.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: *tọ́* — Straightness, Correctness, Accuracy

---

**1.**
**Form:** ó tọ́
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ó* (3sg subject pronoun) + *tọ́* (be correct/straight)
**Natural English translation:** It is correct; it is right; that is proper
**Context of use:** The most basic predicative use of the root; used in everyday speech to affirm that a statement, action, or situation is correct or appropriate. Functions as a general affirmative evaluative judgment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** tọ́ sí
**Transliteration:** tọ́ sí
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/straight) + *sí* (toward/to)
**Natural English translation:** to be directed correctly toward; to be appropriate to; to be fitting for
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether an action, response, or statement is appropriately directed or suited to a situation. Common in moral and practical assessments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ìtọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straightness/correctness)
**Natural English translation:** straightness, correctness, rightness
**Context of use:** The bare nominalization of the root; used in philosophical and moral discourse to refer abstractly to the quality of being correct or straight. Less common than *òtítọ́* in everyday speech but appears in formal and literary registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ọ̀nà tọ́tọ́
**Transliteration:** ọ̀nà tọ́tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ọ̀nà* (path/way/road) + *tọ́tọ́* (reduplicated form of *tọ́*; very straight/correct)
**Natural English translation:** the straight path; the correct way; the right road
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse to describe the path of righteous or correct conduct. Appears in proverbs and in Christian and Islamic Yoruba preaching as a metaphor for moral rectitude.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sọ̀nà
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straight/correct) + *sọ̀nà* (on the path/way); "directing correctly on the path"
**Natural English translation:** guidance, direction, leading aright
**Context of use:** Used in educational, religious, and moral contexts to describe the act of guiding someone along the correct path. Common in pastoral and pedagogical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** tọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** tọ́ ni
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/right) + *ni* (it is / copula-focus marker)
**Natural English translation:** it is indeed correct; that is right (emphatic)
**Context of use:** An emphatic affirmation used in conversation and debate to confirm that a statement is accurate or a judgment is sound. The focus marker *ni* adds emphasis.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ìtọ́jú
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́jú
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straight/correct/careful) + *jú* (surpass/over); "careful oversight, tending correctly"
**Natural English translation:** care, maintenance, upkeep, tending
**Context of use:** Used to describe the careful and correct maintenance of a person, object, or institution. Common in medical, agricultural, and interpersonal contexts. The *tọ́* component here carries the sense of careful correctness in handling.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** tọ́ ara rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** tọ́ ara rẹ̀
**Literal breakdown:** *tọ́* (correct/straighten) + *ara* (body/self) + *rẹ̀* (his/her/its, possessive)
**Natural English translation:** to correct oneself; to straighten oneself out; to reform
**Context of use:** Used in moral and behavioral contexts to describe self-correction or self-reform. Appears in moral instruction and in contexts of personal accountability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** àtúntọ́
**Transliteration:** àtúntọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *à-* (nominalizer) + *tún* (again/re-) + *tọ́* (correct/straighten); "re-correcting, straightening again"
**Natural English translation:** correction, rectification, reform, amendment
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and moral contexts to describe the act of correcting something that was previously wrong or misaligned. Appears in discussions of legal reform and institutional correction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ìtúntọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtúntọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tún* (again/re-) + *tọ́* (correct); variant nominalization of the same compound
**Natural English translation:** correction, rectification, revision
**Context of use:** Functionally similar to *àtúntọ́*; used in editorial, administrative, and legal contexts to refer to the process of revising or correcting a document, decision, or course of action.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** tọ́ọ̀tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́ọ̀tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** Reduplicated/intensified form of *tọ́*; "truly correct, genuinely right"
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, really, indeed (adverbial intensifier)
**Context of use:** Used as an adverbial intensifier to affirm that something is genuinely or truly the case. Appears in everyday speech and in formal discourse to add emphasis to a truth claim.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "correct knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** correct knowledge, accurate understanding, right information
**Context of use:** Used in educational and epistemic contexts to distinguish genuine and accurate knowledge from mistaken belief or misinformation. Appears in academic and religious discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
**Transliteration:** ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ọ̀rọ̀* (word/matter/speech) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "correct word/speech"
**Natural English translation:** correct speech, right word, accurate statement
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate a statement or utterance as accurate and appropriate. Appears in legal testimony contexts and in everyday assessments of whether someone has spoken accurately.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ìwà tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìwà tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwà* (character/conduct) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "correct/right character"
**Natural English translation:** upright character, correct conduct, proper behavior
**Context of use:** Used in moral evaluation to describe a person whose conduct is consistently correct and proper. Appears in character assessments and in proverbs about moral uprightness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ìdánilójú tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìdánilójú tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìdánilójú* (assurance/verification) + *tọ́* (correct); "correct verification"
**Natural English translation:** proper verification, correct confirmation
**Context of use:** Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe verification that has been carried out correctly and according to proper procedure.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ìlànà tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìlànà tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìlànà* (procedure/rule/pattern) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "correct procedure"
**Natural English translation:** correct procedure, proper rule, right method
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and educational contexts to evaluate whether a process or method conforms to the correct standard.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ìdáhùn tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìdáhùn tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìdáhùn* (answer/response) + *tọ́* (correct); "correct answer"
**Natural English translation:** correct answer, right response
**Context of use:** Used in educational and evaluative contexts to describe an answer that is accurate and meets the required standard. Extremely common in school and examination discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ìwọ̀n tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìwọ̀n tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìwọ̀n* (measurement/weight/proportion) + *tọ́* (correct/accurate); "correct measurement"
**Natural English translation:** accurate measurement, correct weight, proper proportion
**Context of use:** Used in commercial, scientific, and legal contexts to evaluate whether a measurement or quantity is accurate. Relevant in market contexts where fair measurement is a moral and legal issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìsọ̀rọ̀* (speaking/speech act) + *tọ́* (correct); "correct speaking"
**Natural English translation:** speaking correctly, accurate speech, truthful utterance
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate the accuracy and appropriateness of a person's speech. Appears in moral and legal assessments of testimony and in everyday evaluations of communication.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ìgbésẹ̀ tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìgbésẹ̀ tọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìgbésẹ̀* (step/action/move) + *tọ́* (correct/right); "correct step/action"
**Natural English translation:** correct action, right step, proper move
**Context of use:** Used in moral, strategic, and procedural contexts to evaluate whether an action taken was the appropriate and correct one. Common in deliberative and advisory discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ìtọ́sí
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́sí
**Literal breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (correct/straight) + *sí* (toward); "directing correctly toward"
**Natural English translation:** direction toward, orientation, alignment
**Context of use:** Used in contexts describing the correct orientation or alignment of something toward a goal or standard. Appears in religious and philosophical discourse about moral orientation.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ àṣà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ àṣà
**Literal breakdown:** *ìtọ́* (correctness/guidance) + *àṣà* (custom/tradition/culture); "correct tradition, guiding custom"
**Natural English translation:** correct tradition, proper custom, cultural guidance
**Context of use:** Used in discussions of cultural practice to evaluate whether a custom or tradition is being correctly observed or transmitted. Appears in ethnographic and normative discourse about Yoruba cultural practice.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**23.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ ọmọ
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ ọmọ
**Literal breakdown:** *ìtọ́* (guidance/correction) + *ọmọ* (child); "guiding/correcting a child"
**Natural English translation:** child-rearing, upbringing, moral guidance of a child

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ ẹ̀kọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ ẹ̀kọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìtọ́* (guidance/correction) + *ẹ̀kọ́* (lesson/teaching/education); "correct teaching, guiding education"
**Natural English translation:** correct teaching, proper instruction, educational guidance
**Context of use:** Used in educational contexts to describe instruction that correctly guides students. Appears in discussions of curriculum and pedagogy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ìtọ́ ìwà
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́ ìwà
**Literal breakdown:** *ìtọ́* (guidance/correction) + *ìwà* (character/conduct); "correcting/guiding character"

**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious contexts to describe the process of correcting or guiding a person's character toward what is right. Appears in pastoral and educational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The root *tọ́* is highly productive. The above represents the most common and well-attested compounds and collocations. Additional forms exist, particularly in technical and specialized registers (e.g., medical, legal, and administrative Yoruba), but are less commonly attested in general usage.

### ROOT 2: *mọ̀* — Knowing, Recognizing, Being Acquainted With

---

**1.**
**Form:** mọ̀
**Transliteration:** mọ̀
**Literal breakdown:** *mọ̀* (base verb: to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with)
**Natural English translation:** to know, to recognize, to be aware of
**Context of use:** The bare verbal root; used in all contexts where knowledge, recognition, or awareness is predicated of a subject. The most basic epistemic verb in Yoruba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ jinlẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ jinlẹ̀
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *jinlẹ̀* (deep); "deep knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** deep knowledge, profound understanding, expertise
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate a person's knowledge as thorough and profound rather than superficial. Appears in academic, professional, and philosophical discourse to distinguish expert knowledge from casual familiarity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ gígbòòrò
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ gígbòòrò
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *gígbòòrò* (wide/broad); "broad knowledge"
**Natural English translation:** broad knowledge, wide learning, extensive understanding
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate a person's knowledge as wide-ranging across many domains. Appears in assessments of scholars, elders, and educated persons.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ ẹ̀kọ́
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ ẹ̀kọ́
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *ẹ̀kọ́* (lesson/education/learning); "knowledge from education"
**Natural English translation:** academic knowledge, learned knowledge, educational attainment
**Context of use:** Used in educational contexts to describe knowledge acquired through formal instruction. Distinguishes learned knowledge from experiential or traditional knowledge.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ àṣà
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ àṣà
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *àṣà* (custom/tradition/culture); "knowledge of custom/culture"
**Natural English translation:** cultural knowledge, knowledge of tradition, customary knowledge
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate whether a person has adequate knowledge of Yoruba cultural practices and traditions. Appears in discussions of cultural competence and in assessments of elders and community leaders.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ ìṣègùn
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ ìṣègùn

**Natural English translation:** medical knowledge, knowledge of healing, pharmacological knowledge
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate the expertise of healers, herbalists (*onísègùn*), and medical practitioners. Appears in assessments of traditional and modern medical competence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ìmọ̀ Ọlọ́run
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ Ọlọ́run
**Literal breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *Ọlọ́run* (God); "knowledge of God"
**Natural English translation:** knowledge of God, divine knowledge, theological understanding
**Context of use:** Used in religious contexts to describe knowledge of divine nature and will. Appears in Christian and Islamic Yoruba discourse as a valued epistemic and spiritual attainment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**

### ROOT 3: *ẹ̀rí* / *jẹ́rìí* — Witnessing, Attesting, Evidencing

---

**Note on this root cluster:** *ẹ̀rí* (noun: evidence, witness, testimony) and *jẹ́rìí* (verb: to bear witness, to testify) are treated together as they share a common semantic core and are morphologically related. The verbal form *jẹ́rìí* appears to incorporate *ẹ̀rí* as its core element. Both are highly productive in legal, religious, and evaluative discourse.

**Literal breakdown:** *ẹ̀rí* (evidence/witness) + *ojú* (eye); "eye-evidence"
**Natural English translation:** eyewitness testimony, direct evidence, firsthand witness
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 9); included here for completeness of the root expansion. The most commonly cited compound of *ẹ̀rí* in legal discourse.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions are flagged as instances where the identified roots appear in contexts where their contribution to truth-evaluation is not straightforward. No interpretation or categorization is offered.

---

**Root *tọ́*:**

- *ìtọ́jú* (item 7 above): The root *tọ́* appears in the sense of careful, correct handling of a person or object — a context of maintenance and care rather than truth-evaluation or moral correctness per se.

- *tọ́ ara rẹ̀* (item 8 above): The root appears in a reflexive context of self-correction or self-reform, where the target of "straightening" is the self rather than a statement or external standard.

- *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* (item 5 above): The root appears in a navigational/directional metaphor where "straightness" applies to a path rather than to a proposition or moral act.

- *ìwọ̀n tọ́* (item 18 above): The root appears in a metrological context — correct measurement — where the criterion of correctness is quantitative accuracy rather than propositional truth or moral rightness.

- *tọ́ọ̀tọ́* (item 11 above): The reduplicated form functions as an adverbial intensifier ("truly, genuinely") in contexts that may not involve truth-evaluation at all, but simply emphasis or degree-marking.

---

**Root *mọ̀*:**

- *ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ* (item 19 above): The root *mọ̀* appears as the first element of the standard Yoruba word for "technology," where the knowledge component has been lexicalized into a domain-specific technical term rather than functioning as an epistemic evaluator.

- *mọ̀ ẹni* (item 15 above): The root appears in a context of social acquaintance and personal identification rather than propositional knowledge or truth-evaluation.

- *ìmọ̀ àgbàyanu* (item 27 above): The root appears in a context of aesthetic or admiring evaluation of knowledge, where the evaluative dimension is one of degree or impressiveness rather than truth or accuracy.

- *àìmọ̀ ọ̀nà* (item 22 above): The root appears in a navigational context (not knowing a physical path) that may be literal rather than epistemic or moral.

---

**Root *ẹ̀rí/jẹ́rìí*:**

- *ẹ̀rí ìgbésí ayé* (item 25 above): The root appears in a context where a person's entire life and conduct is described as constituting "witness" or "testimony" — a use that extends the evidential function of the root into the domain of character and identity.

- *ẹ̀rí ìṣẹ̀dá* (item 24 above): The root appears in a theological context where natural phenomena are described as "evidence" — a use that extends the testimonial function of the root into the domain of natural theology and cosmological argument.

- *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (item 16 above): The root appears in a compound that negates its own core function — a "witness" who produces falsehood rather than truth — flagging a use where the root's truth-evaluative contribution is inverted rather than affirmed.

- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbàgbọ́* (item 13 above): The root appears in a context where attestation is directed toward a religious belief rather than toward an empirical fact — a use where the object of testimony is a matter of faith rather than observable evidence.
