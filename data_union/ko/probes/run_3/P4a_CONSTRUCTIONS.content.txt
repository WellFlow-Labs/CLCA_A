# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a Constructions
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**N이/가 + Adj** (predicative attribution via copular adjective)
The truth-domain adjective or nominal predicate follows the subject with subject particle 이/가. Korean has no copula separate from the predicate adjective itself; the adjective inflects for tense and mood directly.

### Examples
- 그 말이 사실이다 → *geu mali sasirida* → "That statement is a fact / That is true."
- 그의 주장이 타당하다 → *geuui jujang i tadanghada* → "His claim is valid/reasonable."

### Notes
The most productive frame for truth-domain attribution. Nominal predicates (사실이다, 진실이다) use the copula 이다; adjectival predicates (타당하다, 정확하다, 옳다) inflect directly. Both patterns are common; the choice depends on whether the base form is a noun or adjective.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**N이/가 + Neg-Adj** or **N이/가 + 아니다**
Negation is achieved either by prefixing 부-/비-/무- to the predicate base, or by using the negative copula 아니다 with nominal predicates. Verbal negation uses 안 or -지 않다.

### Examples
- 그 정보가 정확하지 않다 → *geu jeongboga jeonghwakaji anta* → "That information is not accurate."
- 그것은 사실이 아니다 → *geugeoseun sasiri anida* → "That is not a fact / That is not true."

### Notes
Two structurally distinct negation strategies coexist: morphological (부정확하다, 무효하다) and syntactic (-지 않다, 아니다). The syntactic strategy is more broadly productive and more common in spoken registers; morphological negation tends toward formal and written use.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**Adj-stem + -(으)ㄴ + N** (adnominal form of adjective modifying noun)
Korean adjectives precede the nouns they modify in their adnominal form, marked by the suffix -(으)ㄴ for stative adjectives. Sino-Korean bases take -한 (from 하다); native Korean adjectives take -은/-ㄴ directly from the stem.

### Examples
- 정확한 정보 → *jeonghwakhan jeongbo* → "accurate information"
- 올바른 판단 → *olbareun pandan* → "a correct/right judgment"

### Notes
This is the primary attributive position in Korean; there is no postnominal adjectival modification. The adnominal suffix differs by base type: Sino-Korean -하다 adjectives use -한, native Korean adjectives use -(으)ㄴ directly on the stem. Both patterns are fully productive across the truth-domain vocabulary.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**N + 이/가 + 되다** or **N + (으)로 + 되다 / 판명되다**
Inchoative meaning ("becomes X," "turns out to be X") is expressed periphrastically. The verb 되다 ("to become") follows the nominal predicate with subject particle, or the nominal appears in the -로 instrumental frame with result verbs such as 판명되다 (to be determined to be) and 밝혀지다 (to be revealed as).

### Examples
- 계약이 무효가 되다 → *gyeyagi muhyoga doeda* → "The contract becomes void."
- 가짜로 판명되다 → *gajjaro panmyeong doeda* → "To be determined to be fake."

### Notes
Korean lacks a single inchoative morpheme; the pattern is periphrastic. 되다 is the general inchoative verb; 판명되다 and 밝혀지다 are more specific to contexts of investigation and verification, where the change of state results from an epistemic process rather than a natural transition.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**N + 을/를 + 무효화하다 / 합법화하다 / 정당화하다** (causative via -화하다 suffix)
A productive causative-like pattern in the truth domain uses the suffix -화하다 (化하다, "to make into / to cause to become") attached to Sino-Korean nominal bases. This produces transitive verbs meaning "to render X" or "to cause something to become X."

### Examples
- 계약을 무효화하다 → *gyeyageul muhyohwa hada* → "To invalidate the contract / to render the contract void."
- 마리화나를 합법화하다 → *marihwanareul hapbeophwa hada* → "To legalize marijuana."

### Notes
The -화하다 pattern is the closest Korean equivalent to a morphological causative in this domain. It is productive with Sino-Korean bases (무효화, 합법화, 정당화, 실체화) but not with native Korean bases (no *진짜화하다, *옳화하다). A separate periphrastic causative using -게 하다 exists but is not specific to the truth domain.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Base + -성 (性)** or **Verb-stem + -(으)ㅁ / -기**
Abstract quality nominalizations in the truth domain are formed primarily by the Sino-Korean suffix -성 (性, "nature/quality") attached to Sino-Korean bases. Native Korean adjectives nominalize via -(으)ㅁ on the verb stem. Both yield nouns referring to the property or quality in the abstract.

### Examples
- 정확성을 높이다 → *jeonghwakseong eul nopida* → "To improve accuracy / to raise the level of precision."
- 올바름의 기준 → *olbareum ui gijun* → "The standard of rightness/correctness."

### Notes
-성 is highly productive with Sino-Korean bases in this domain (진실성, 타당성, 유효성, 합법성, 신뢰성, 신빙성, 진정성, 허위성). Native Korean adjective stems nominalize via -(으)ㅁ (올바름, 옳음, 그름), but these forms are less common in everyday use and tend toward philosophical or literary registers.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Base + -자 (者)** (Sino-Korean agentive) or **Base + -쟁이** (native Korean colloquial agentive)
Agentive nominalizations referring to persons defined by their relationship to truth or falsehood are formed by -자 (者, formal) or -쟁이 (colloquial, habitual agent). The domain is asymmetric: liar-type agents are well-lexicalized; truth-teller agents are not.

### Examples
- 거짓말쟁이 → *geojitmaljaengi* → "Liar" (habitual; colloquial)
- 위선자 → *wiseonja* → "Hypocrite" (person of false virtue; formal)

### Notes
The asymmetry is structurally significant: Korean has no common agentive nominalization for "truth-teller" parallel to 거짓말쟁이. The concept is expressed periphrastically (진실을 말하는 사람, "a person who tells the truth") rather than by a single lexicalized form. Agentive -자 forms in this domain (위선자, 위조범) tend toward the negative/false pole.

---

## 8. VERB-OBJECT COLLOCATION (LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTION)

### Pattern
**N + 을/를 + V** where V is a semantically light or collocationally fixed verb
A large proportion of truth-domain predication in Korean is expressed through verb-object collocations in which a nominal base takes a collocationally fixed verb. The nominal carries the semantic content; the verb (밝히다, 숨기다, 쌓다, 잃다, 왜곡하다, etc.) specifies the action type.

### Examples
- 진실을 밝히다 → *jinsilеul balkhida* → "To reveal the truth."
- 신뢰를 잃다 → *sinloereul ilta* → "To lose trust/credibility."

### Notes
This pattern is the primary means of expressing truth-domain actions in Korean. The same nominal base participates in multiple collocations with different verbs (진실을 밝히다 / 숨기다 / 왜곡하다 / 외면하다), and the verb choice encodes the specific action. This is structurally distinct from derivational morphology and from simple predication; it constitutes the most productive construction type in the domain.

---

## 9. SCALAR PREDICATIVE COLLOCATION

### Pattern
**N + 이/가 + 있다 / 없다** or **N + 이/가 + 높다 / 낮다**
Several truth-domain nominals — particularly those denoting properties or qualities — are predicated not through adjectives but through the existential verbs 있다 (to exist/have) and 없다 (to not exist/lack), or through scalar adjectives 높다 (high) and 낮다 (low). This frames the property as something possessed in greater or lesser degree.

### Examples
- 신빙성이 있다 / 없다 → *sinbyeongseong i itda / eopda* → "To be credible / to lack credibility."
- 신뢰도가 높다 → *sinloedo ga nopda* → "To have high credibility/trustworthiness."

### Notes
This construction is specific to nominal bases that denote assessable properties (신빙성, 타당성, 진정성, 근거, 신뢰도). It is not available for all truth-domain nominals — 사실이 있다 does not mean "to be factual" in the same way. The 있다/없다 frame treats the property as a binary possession; the 높다/낮다 frame treats it as a scalar quantity. Both are productive in evaluative and journalistic discourse.
