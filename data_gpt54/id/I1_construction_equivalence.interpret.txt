## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Indonesian shows a clear distinction between ordinary attribution, change-of-state, and evidential/result-discovery.

For **BECOME**, the regular overt construction is **menjadi + ADJ/N**, as in *Data itu menjadi tidak valid setelah diubah.* This is the direct change-of-state pattern.

For **REVELATION**, Indonesian commonly uses **terbukti + ADJ**, as in *Dokumen itu terbukti palsu.* P4a and P4b describe this not as simple becoming, but as a confirmed or discovered result. So REVELATION does not replace BECOME across the board; rather, it covers a different function: established discovery or proof. In this sense, **REVELATION is available alongside BECOME**, not as its full replacement. Where the intended meaning is “turned out/proved to be,” Indonesian prefers REVELATION-type forms such as *terbukti*.

For **VERIFICATION**, Indonesian does not show one single dedicated construction in the provided material. Instead, verification meanings are distributed across:
- lexicalized **meN- + root + -kan** verbs such as *membenarkan* and *mengesahkan*,
- and evaluative/evidential passive forms such as *dianggap valid* or *dipastikan + ADJ* within the pattern listed in P4a/P4b.

So **VERIFICATION is expressed through lexicalized factual/validative verbs and evidential passive operators**, rather than by one uniform operator.

For **DISCONFIRMATION**, no separate dedicated construction is identified in P4a/P4b. Instead, Indonesian uses:
- ordinary **NEGATION**, e.g. *Kabar itu tidak benar.*,
- or falsity/authenticity predicates under REVELATION/evidential passive, e.g. *Dokumen itu terbukti palsu.*, *Tanda tangan itu diduga palsu.*

So **DISCONFIRMATION is largely substituted by NEGATION and by REVELATION/evidential evaluation with falsity predicates**.

**NEGATION is analytic, not morphological.** The main markers are separate words before the predicate: *tidak*, *kurang*, and *belum*. This is explicit in P4a/P4b.

**VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share one single construction.**
- Verification is expressed by lexicalized confirmative/validative verbs and evaluative passive.
- Disconfirmation is expressed by analytic negation and falsity predicates, sometimes in evaluative passive.
There is overlap only at the broad level of institutional evaluation, not a single shared form.

There is also an asymmetry between **positive confirmation** and **negative rejection**:
- positive confirmation often uses specialized lexical verbs such as *membenarkan* and *mengesahkan*;
- negative rejection is more directly handled by analytic negation (*tidak benar*) or by predicates like *palsu*.

For **CAUSATION**, the language does not present a single general truth-domain causative construction. P4a/P4b explicitly note that the pattern is **root-specific** and lexicalized. Thus factual causation is available, but only through particular verbs. No corresponding productive pattern is identified for **CAUSATION (character)**.

### Normalization of multi-word expressions
- **benar, betul, tepat, sesuai, sah, valid, akurat, asli, palsu**: predicate adjectives or adjective-like predicates in bare predication.
- **tidak / kurang / belum + ADJ**: analytic negation or scalar/status operators preceding an adjectival predicate.
- **N yang + ADJ**: adnominal modification with **yang** linking noun and adjective.
- **menjadi + ADJ/N**: light verb/carrier **menjadi** marks change-of-state; the truth-domain content is in the following adjective/noun.
- **terbukti + ADJ**: verbal evidential/resultative carrier **terbukti** plus adjectival predicate.
- **dianggap / dinyatakan / dipastikan / diduga / terbukti + ADJ**: passive verbal carriers of evaluation/evidential stance; the truth-domain property is expressed by the following adjective.
- **benar bahwa ... / tidak benar bahwa ...**: predicate adjective plus clausal complement.
- **ke- + ADJ + -an** forms: nominalizations.
- **membenarkan, mengesahkan**: lexicalized derived verbs functioning as verbal operators for confirmation/ratification.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **CAUSATION (character): MISSING (structural gap)**
  - P4a/P4b provide no common expression for agentive/character-type derivation in this semantic area.
- No single fully general truth-domain causative construction:
  - factual causation exists, but only through root-specific lexicalized verbs rather than one uniform morphosyntactic pattern.

### Lexical Gaps
- **Agentive nominalization (liar, truth-teller)**: no common productive expression identified.
  - P4a/P4b explicitly state **(no common expression identified)**.
  - This is a lexical gap in the provided dataset, and also reflects absence of a productive derivational pattern.

### Conceptual Gaps
- No conceptual gap is directly identified in P4a/P4b for attribution, negation, adnominal modification, becoming, revelation, or nominalization.
- The main limitation is not conceptual absence, but **lack of a single dedicated construction** for some operators.

### Asymmetric Gaps
- **Verification vs. disconfirmation asymmetry**:
  - verification often uses specialized lexical verbs (*membenarkan, mengesahkan*);
  - disconfirmation is more often expressed by analytic negation (*tidak benar*) or falsity predicates (*palsu*).
- **Factual causation vs. character causation asymmetry**:
  - factual causation is partially available through lexicalized verbs;
  - character causation is missing in the provided material.
- **BECOME vs. REVELATION asymmetry**:
  - both are available, but REVELATION is especially associated with evidence-based result (*terbukti*), not general change-of-state.
