# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## PRELIMINARY NOTE ON CHINESE DERIVATIONAL MORPHOLOGY

Chinese derivational morphology differs fundamentally from inflectional languages. The language primarily uses:
1. **Compounding** (combining existing morphemes)
2. **Affixation** (limited productive affixes)
3. **Reduplication** (for intensification or other effects)
4. **Conversion** (category shift without formal marking)

True derivational affixes are relatively few. Most word formation occurs through compounding, which has already been extensively documented in P1 and P1b. The following documents **additional derivational patterns** not already covered.

## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS IDENTIFIED

### PATTERN 1: Nominalization with -性 (-xìng) "nature, quality"

This suffix derives abstract nouns denoting qualities or properties from adjectives or stative verbs.

#### 1.1 From 真实 (zhēnshí)

**Base Form**: 真实 (zhēnshí) - true, real, authentic

**Derived Form**: 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng)
- **Transliteration**: zhēnshíxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + real + nature/quality
- **Translation**: authenticity, truthfulness, veracity
- **Usage**: Abstract noun used in academic, legal, and technical contexts to discuss the quality of being authentic or true. Common in phrases like "质疑真实性" (question the authenticity).
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.2 From 可靠 (kěkào)

**Base Form**: 可靠 (kěkào) - reliable, dependable

**Derived Form**: 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng)
- **Transliteration**: kěkàoxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: can + lean on + nature/quality
- **Translation**: reliability, dependability
- **Usage**: Technical and formal term for the quality of being reliable. Common in engineering, statistics, and quality assessment contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.3 From 正当 (zhèngdàng)

**Base Form**: 正当 (zhèngdàng) - legitimate, proper, justified

**Derived Form**: 正当性 (zhèngdàngxìng)
- **Transliteration**: zhèngdàngxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: upright/correct + appropriate + nature/quality
- **Translation**: legitimacy, justification
- **Usage**: Legal and political term for the quality of being legitimate or justified. Used in discussions of authority, rights, and actions.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.4 From 合理 (hélǐ)

**Base Form**: 合理 (hélǐ) - reasonable, rational

**Derived Form**: 合理性 (hélǐxìng)
- **Transliteration**: hélǐxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: accord + principle/reason + nature/quality
- **Translation**: rationality, reasonableness
- **Usage**: Philosophical and analytical term for the quality of being reasonable or rational. Common in academic and critical discourse.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.5 From 实用 (shíyòng)

**Base Form**: 实用 (shíyòng) - practical, pragmatic

**Derived Form**: 实用性 (shíyòngxìng)
- **Transliteration**: shíyòngxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: real + use + nature/quality
- **Translation**: practicality, utility
- **Usage**: Evaluative term for the quality of being practical or useful. Common in product reviews and functional assessments.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.6 From 正常 (zhèngcháng)

**Base Form**: 正常 (zhèngcháng) - normal, regular

**Derived Form**: 正常性 (zhèngchángxìng)
- **Transliteration**: zhèngchángxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: correct/upright + constant + nature/quality
- **Translation**: normality, normalcy
- **Usage**: Medical and psychological term for the quality of being normal. Less common than the base form.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 1.7 From 准确 (zhǔnquè)

**Base Form**: 准确 (zhǔnquè) - accurate, precise

**Derived Form**: 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng)
- **Transliteration**: zhǔnquèxìng
- **Literal Breakdown**: standard + definite + nature/quality
- **Translation**: accuracy, precision
- **Usage**: Technical and scientific term for the quality of being accurate. Common in measurement, data analysis, and quality control contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### PATTERN 2: Nominalization with -度 (-dù) "degree, extent"

This suffix derives nouns indicating degree or extent of a quality.

#### 2.1 From 真实 (zhēnshí)

**Base Form**: 真实 (zhēnshí) - true, real, authentic

**Derived Form**: 真实度 (zhēnshídù)
- **Transliteration**: zhēnshídù
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + real + degree
- **Translation**: degree of authenticity, level of realism
- **Usage**: Used to measure or evaluate how authentic or realistic something is. Common in discussions of simulations, representations, or artistic works.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 2.2 From 可靠 (kěkào)

**Base Form**: 可靠 (kěkào) - reliable, dependable

**Derived Form**: 可靠度 (kěkàodù)
- **Transliteration**: kěkàodù
- **Literal Breakdown**: can + lean on + degree
- **Translation**: degree of reliability, reliability level
- **Usage**: Technical term for measuring reliability, especially in engineering and statistics. Often used interchangeably with 可靠性 but emphasizes measurable degree.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 2.3 From 准确 (zhǔnquè)

**Base Form**: 准确 (zhǔnquè) - accurate, precise

**Derived Form**: 准确度 (zhǔnquèdù)
- **Transliteration**: zhǔnquèdù
- **Literal Breakdown**: standard + definite + degree
- **Translation**: degree of accuracy, accuracy level
- **Usage**: Technical term for measuring accuracy. Common in scientific and technical contexts where precision is quantified.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### PATTERN 3: Adverbialization with 地 (de)

This particle converts adjectives to adverbs (manner adverbials). However, many truth-domain terms already function adverbially without this marker, or use it optionally.

#### 3.1 From 真实 (zhēnshí)

**Base Form**: 真实 (zhēnshí) - true, real, authentic

**Derived Form**: 真实地 (zhēnshí de)
- **Transliteration**: zhēnshí de
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + real + [adverbial particle]
- **Translation**: truly, authentically, realistically
- **Usage**: Adverbial form describing manner of action as authentic or realistic. Example: "真实地反映情况" (truly reflect the situation). The particle is often optional.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 3.2 From 诚实 (chéngshí)

**Base Form**: 诚实 (chéngshí) - honest, truthful

**Derived Form**: 诚实地 (chéngshí de)
- **Transliteration**: chéngshí de
- **Literal Breakdown**: sincere + real + [adverbial particle]
- **Translation**: honestly, truthfully
- **Usage**: Adverbial form describing manner of speaking or acting. Example: "诚实地回答" (answer honestly).
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 3.3 From 正确 (zhèngquè)

**Base Form**: 正确 (zhèngquè) - correct, right

**Derived Form**: 正确地 (zhèngquè de)
- **Transliteration**: zhèngquè de
- **Literal Breakdown**: upright/correct + definite + [adverbial particle]
- **Translation**: correctly, rightly
- **Usage**: Adverbial form describing manner of doing something. Example: "正确地理解" (understand correctly).
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Note**: The 地 (de) particle is highly productive and can attach to virtually any adjective. Only representative examples are listed here. Many speakers also omit this particle in casual speech.

### PATTERN 4: Reduplication for Intensification/Distribution

Chinese uses reduplication for various functions including intensification, distribution, and softening.

#### 4.1 From 实 (shí)

**Base Form**: 实 (shí) - solid, real, actual

**Derived Form**: 实实在在 (shíshízàizài)
- **Transliteration**: shíshízàizài
- **Literal Breakdown**: solid + solid + exist + exist (AABB reduplication)
- **Translation**: really and truly, genuinely, down-to-earth
- **Usage**: Emphatic form stressing genuineness, honesty, or substantiality. Common in everyday speech to emphasize authenticity or sincerity.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 4.2 From 真 (zhēn)

**Base Form**: 真 (zhēn) - true, real, genuine

**Derived Form**: 真真 (zhēnzhēn)
- **Transliteration**: zhēnzhēn
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + true (AA reduplication)
- **Translation**: really, truly, indeed
- **Usage**: Literary or emphatic form meaning "really" or "truly." Less common in modern speech than 真的 or 真正. Often appears in classical or literary contexts.
- **Status**: [MARGINAL]

#### 4.3 From 正 (zhèng)

**Base Form**: 正 (zhèng) - correct, upright

**Derived Form**: 正正 (zhèngzhèng)
- **Transliteration**: zhèngzhèng
- **Literal Breakdown**: correct + correct (AA reduplication)
- **Translation**: exactly, precisely, just right
- **Usage**: Emphasizes exactness or precision. Example: "正正好好" (just right, exactly right). Less common as standalone form.
- **Status**: [MARGINAL]

### PATTERN 5: Negation with 不 (bù) "not"

While negation is grammatical rather than derivational, certain negated forms have lexicalized into distinct lexical items.

#### 5.1 From 正常 (zhèngcháng)

**Base Form**: 正常 (zhèngcháng) - normal, regular

**Derived Form**: 不正常 (bù zhèngcháng)
- **Transliteration**: bù zhèngcháng
- **Literal Breakdown**: not + correct/upright + constant
- **Translation**: abnormal, irregular, unusual
- **Usage**: Common term for what is abnormal or irregular. While compositional, it functions as a lexicalized unit in medical and everyday contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 5.2 From 真实 (zhēnshí)

**Base Form**: 真实 (zhēnshí) - true, real, authentic

**Derived Form**: 不真实 (bù zhēnshí)
- **Transliteration**: bù zhēnshí
- **Literal Breakdown**: not + true + real
- **Translation**: unreal, inauthentic, false
- **Usage**: Describes something as not authentic or realistic. Fully compositional but commonly used as a unit.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Note**: Negation with 不 is fully productive and compositional. These examples are included because they function as common lexical units, but the pattern itself is grammatical rather than derivational.

### PATTERN 6: Verbalization with Resultative Complements

Some truth-domain terms combine with verbal elements to create resultative verb compounds.

#### 6.1 From 正 (zhèng)

**Base Form**: 正 (zhèng) - correct, upright

**Derived Form**: 纠正 (jiūzhèng)
- **Transliteration**: jiūzhèng
- **Literal Breakdown**: rectify/correct + correct/upright
- **Translation**: correct, rectify, redress
- **Usage**: Common verb meaning to correct errors or improper situations. The first element 纠 means "rectify/correct," creating a resultative compound.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Derived Form**: 改正 (gǎizhèng)
- **Transliteration**: gǎizhèng
- **Literal Breakdown**: change + correct/upright
- **Translation**: correct, amend, put right
- **Usage**: Common verb for correcting mistakes. The first element 改 means "change," indicating the action that results in correctness.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Derived Form**: 修正 (xiūzhèng)
- **Transliteration**: xiūzhèng
- **Literal Breakdown**: repair/revise + correct/upright
- **Translation**: revise, amend, correct
- **Usage**: Formal verb for revising or amending. The first element 修 means "repair/revise," suggesting careful correction.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 6.2 From 实 (shí)

**Base Form**: 实 (shí) - solid, real, actual

**Derived Form**: 充实 (chōngshí)
- **Transliteration**: chōngshí
- **Literal Breakdown**: fill + solid
- **Translation**: enrich, substantiate; substantial, fulfilling
- **Usage**: Functions both as verb (to enrich/fill out) and adjective (substantial/fulfilling). The first element 充 means "fill," creating a resultative meaning.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Derived Form**: 落实 (luòshí)
- **Transliteration**: luòshí
- **Literal Breakdown**: fall/settle + solid
- **Translation**: implement, carry out, put into effect
- **Usage**: Common verb meaning to implement or actualize plans/policies. The first element 落 means "fall/settle," suggesting making something concrete.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Derived Form**: 坐实 (zuòshí)
- **Transliteration**: zuòshí
- **Literal Breakdown**: sit/establish + solid
- **Translation**: confirm, establish as fact
- **Usage**: Verb meaning to confirm or establish something as factual. Often used in legal or investigative contexts.
- **Status**: [CORE]

### PATTERN 7: Attributive Modification with 的 (de)

The particle 的 (de) converts various elements into attributive modifiers. While grammatical, certain combinations have lexicalized.

#### 7.1 From 真 (zhēn)

**Base Form**: 真 (zhēn) - true, real, genuine

**Derived Form**: 真的 (zhēn de)
- **Transliteration**: zhēn de
- **Literal Breakdown**: true + [attributive/nominalizing particle]
- **Translation**: real one, genuine one; really, truly
- **Usage**: Functions both as noun phrase (the real/genuine one) and as adverb (really, truly). Extremely common in spoken Chinese. Example: "这是真的" (This is real/true).
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 7.2 From 假 (jiǎ)

**Base Form**: 假 (jiǎ) - false, fake

**Derived Form**: 假的 (jiǎ de)
- **Transliteration**: jiǎ de
- **Literal Breakdown**: false + [attributive/nominalizing particle]
- **Translation**: fake one, counterfeit one
- **Usage**: Noun phrase meaning "the fake/counterfeit one." Common in distinguishing genuine from fake items.
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Note**: The 的 particle is fully productive. These forms are included because they function as common lexical units in discourse.

### PATTERN 8: Causative/Resultative with 化 (-huà) "-ize, -ify"

This suffix creates verbs indicating transformation or process.

#### 8.1 From 实 (shí)

**Base Form**: 实 (shí) - solid, real, actual

**Derived Form**: 实化 (shíhuà)
- **Transliteration**: shíhuà
- **Literal Breakdown**: solid/real + -ize
- **Translation**: materialize, actualize, make concrete
- **Usage**: Technical or formal verb meaning to make something concrete or actual. Less common than 实现 but used in specialized contexts.
- **Status**: [MARGINAL]

**Note**: The -化 suffix is productive but does not commonly attach to most truth-domain terms. Most truth-domain concepts do not readily undergo this type of transformation derivation.

---

### PATTERN 9: Agentive/Instrumental Nominalization

Chinese has limited agentive derivation compared to languages with productive agentive suffixes.

**Finding**: No productive agentive or instrumental derivation identified for truth-domain terms.

The concepts in this domain (truth, falsity, correctness) do not typically generate agent nouns (e.g., *"truth-er," *"falsifier" as productive patterns). While 欺骗者 (deceiver) exists, it uses the general agentive suffix -者, which is not specific to this semantic domain.

---

### PATTERN 10: Diminutive/Augmentative Forms

**Finding**: No diminutive or augmentative derivation identified for truth-domain terms.

Chinese does not have productive diminutive or augmentative affixes that apply to abstract quality terms like those in the truth domain.

### PATTERN 11: Conversion (Category Shift Without Formal Marking)

Many Chinese words undergo category shift without overt marking.

#### 11.1 From 真 (zhēn)

**Base Form**: 真 (zhēn) - [adjective] true, real, genuine

**Derived Form**: 真 (zhēn) - [adverb]
- **Transliteration**: zhēn
- **Translation**: really, truly, very
- **Usage**: Functions as intensifying adverb without formal marking. Example: "真好" (really good), "真大" (really big). Extremely common in spoken Chinese.
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 11.2 From 假 (jiǎ)

**Base Form**: 假 (jiǎ) - [adjective] false, fake

**Derived Form**: 假 (jiǎ) - [verb]
- **Transliteration**: jiǎ
- **Translation**: to fake, to pretend, to feign
- **Usage**: Functions as verb meaning to pretend or feign. Example: "假装" (pretend), "假死" (feign death).
- **Status**: [CORE]

#### 11.3 From 对 (duì)

**Base Form**: 对 (duì) - [adjective] correct, right

**Derived Form**: 对 (duì) - [verb]
- **Transliteration**: duì
- **Translation**: to be correct, to check/verify
- **Usage**: Functions as verb in contexts like "对答案" (check answers), "对一对" (verify/check).
- **Status**: [CORE]

**Note**: Category conversion is extremely common in Chinese and often occurs without formal marking. Only the most salient examples are listed.

## SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

### Productive Derivational Patterns Identified:

1. **-性 (-xìng) nominalization**: Highly productive for creating abstract quality nouns from adjectives [CORE pattern]

2. **-度 (-dù) nominalization**: Productive for creating degree/extent nouns, especially in technical contexts [CORE pattern]

3. **地 (de) adverbialization**: Fully productive but often optional; converts adjectives to manner adverbs [CORE pattern]

4. **Reduplication**: Limited productivity; used for emphasis and intensification [MARGINAL pattern]

5. **Resultative verb compounds**: Moderately productive, especially with 正 and 实 [CORE pattern]

6. **Category conversion**: Highly productive but unmarked; common for adjective-to-adverb and adjective-to-verb shifts [CORE pattern]

### Patterns Not Found:

- Agentive nominalization (no productive pattern specific to truth domain)
- Instrumental nominalization (not applicable to abstract qualities)
- Diminutive/augmentative forms (not applicable to abstract qualities)
- Causative -化 derivation (limited application in truth domain)

### Notes on Asymmetry:

- The suffix -性 applies broadly across truth-domain adjectives
- The suffix -度 applies primarily to gradable qualities (真实, 可靠, 准确)
- Resultative compounds are more productive with 正 and 实 than with 真
- Reduplication is limited and often stylistically marked
- Most word formation in this domain occurs through compounding (already documented in P1/P1b) rather than affixation
