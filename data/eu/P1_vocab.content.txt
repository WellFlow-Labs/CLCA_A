# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Basque (eu)

## Lexical Items

### 1. egia
**Form:** egia  
**Literal meaning:** truth  
**Typical English translations:** truth, true, reality

Used as the primary noun for truth in all contexts, from everyday statements to philosophical discourse. Can appear in constructions like "egia esan" (to tell the truth) and "egiatan" (truly, in truth). The adjective form "egiazko" means true/real.

**[CORE]**

---

### 2. gezur
**Form:** gezur  
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood  
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, falsehood

The standard term for lie or falsehood, used both as noun and in adjectival constructions. Commonly appears in phrases like "gezurra esan" (to tell a lie) and "gezurrezko" (false, fake). Directly opposes "egia" in semantic space.

**[CORE]**

---

### 3. benetako
**Form:** benetako  
**Literal meaning:** from bene- (true/real) + -tako (relational suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic, true

Adjectival form used to describe something as genuine or authentic. Frequently used to distinguish real instances from imitations or false representations. Related to "benetan" (really, truly).

**[CORE]**

---

### 4. egiazkoa
**Form:** egiazkoa  
**Literal meaning:** from egia (truth) + -zko (relational suffix) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine

Adjectival form derived from "egia," used to characterize statements, objects, or persons as truthful or genuine. Can function nominally when referring to "the true one" or "the genuine article."

**[CORE]**

---

### 5. zuzena
**Form:** zuzena  
**Literal meaning:** straight, correct  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, straight, just

Polysemous term covering physical straightness, correctness of statements or answers, and moral rightness. Used in mathematical, ethical, and everyday evaluative contexts. The root "zuzen" is highly productive.

**[CORE]**

---

### 6. okerra
**Form:** okerra  
**Literal meaning:** twisted, wrong  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, crooked, unjust

Opposite of "zuzena," covering both physical crookedness and incorrectness in statements, actions, or moral judgments. Used across evaluative domains from simple errors to ethical wrongdoing.

**[CORE]**

---

### 7. fidagarria
**Form:** fidagarria  
**Literal meaning:** from fida- (trust) + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable

Describes persons, sources, or objects as worthy of trust or confidence. Commonly used in evaluating testimony, information sources, and character. The root "fida" relates to trust and confidence.

**[CORE]**

---

### 8. sinesgarria
**Form:** sinesgarria  
**Literal meaning:** from sines- (believe) + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** believable, credible, plausible

Used to evaluate whether statements, accounts, or representations are believable or credible. Focuses on epistemic plausibility rather than moral trustworthiness, though contexts may overlap.

**[CORE]**

---

### 9. ziurra
**Form:** ziurra  
**Literal meaning:** certain, sure  
**Typical English translations:** certain, sure, secure, reliable

Expresses certainty, security, or reliability. Used both for epistemic certainty ("ziur nago" = I am certain) and for characterizing things as secure or reliable. Highly frequent in everyday and formal discourse.

**[CORE]**

---

### 10. faltsua
**Form:** faltsua  
**Literal meaning:** false (borrowed from Latin falsus)  
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, insincere

Describes statements, objects, or persons as false, fake, or insincere. Used for counterfeit goods, false statements, and inauthentic behavior. Borrowed term but fully integrated into Basque.

**[CORE]**

---

### 11. antzekoa
**Form:** antzekoa  
**Literal meaning:** from antze (resemblance) + -ko (relational) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, like

Used to express resemblance or similarity between things. Common in comparative evaluations and descriptions of likeness. The root "antze" means resemblance or appearance.

**[CORE]**

---

### 12. itxura
**Form:** itxura  
**Literal meaning:** appearance, aspect  
**Typical English translations:** appearance, look, aspect, semblance

Refers to outward appearance or how something seems. Often used in constructions evaluating whether appearance matches reality, as in "itxuraz" (apparently, seemingly). Can imply surface versus substance distinctions.

**[CORE]**

---

### 13. egiazkotu
**Form:** egiazkotu  
**Literal meaning:** from egia (truth) + -zko + -tu (verbalizer)  
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to authenticate

Verb meaning to verify, confirm, or establish as true. Used in contexts requiring validation or authentication of statements, documents, or facts. Productive derivation from "egia."

**[CORE]**

---

### 14. frogatu
**Form:** frogatu  
**Literal meaning:** to prove, to test  
**Typical English translations:** to prove, to test, to demonstrate

Verb used for proving, testing, or demonstrating truth or quality. Common in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts where evidence or demonstration is required. Can also mean to try or taste.

**[CORE]**

---

### 15. iruzurra
**Form:** iruzurra  
**Literal meaning:** fraud, deceit  
**Typical English translations:** fraud, deceit, deception, swindle

Noun for fraud or deceit, particularly in contexts of deliberate deception for gain. Used in legal and moral contexts. The adjective "iruzurtia" means fraudulent or deceitful.

**[CORE]**

---

### 16. zintzoa
**Form:** zintzoa  
**Literal meaning:** honest, sincere  
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, upright, fair

Describes persons or conduct as honest, sincere, or upright. Used in moral and character evaluations. The noun "zintzotasuna" means honesty or sincerity. Positive moral term with broad application.

**[CORE]**

---

### 17. zintzo
**Form:** zintzo  
**Literal meaning:** honest, sincere (uninflected form)  
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, upright

Uninflected adjectival form of "zintzoa," used predicatively or in certain constructions. Same semantic range as "zintzoa," evaluating honesty and sincerity in persons and actions.

**[CORE]**

---

### 18. benetakoa
**Form:** benetakoa  
**Literal meaning:** from bene- (true) + -tako (relational) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** the real one, the genuine one, authentic

Nominalized form of "benetako," referring to the genuine or real instance of something. Used to distinguish authentic items from copies or false versions. Common in everyday evaluative discourse.

**[CORE]**

---

### 19. egiazkotasuna
**Form:** egiazkotasuna  
**Literal meaning:** from egiazko (true) + -tasun (abstract noun suffix) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity

Abstract noun denoting the quality of being true, truthful, or authentic. Used in formal and philosophical contexts to discuss truth as a property or quality. Productive nominal derivation.

**[CORE]**

---

### 20. gezurtia
**Form:** gezurtia  
**Literal meaning:** from gezur (lie) + -ti (adjectival suffix) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** lying, false, deceitful, mendacious

Adjectival form describing persons or things as characterized by lying or falsehood. Used to evaluate character or habitual behavior rather than single instances. Related to "gezurti" (liar).

**[CORE]**

---

### 21. egiazkotatu
**Form:** egiazkotatu  
**Literal meaning:** from egiazko (true) + -ta- + -tu (verbalizer)  
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to authenticate, to certify

Variant verbal form with similar meaning to "egiazkotu," used for verification and authentication. May carry slightly more formal or technical connotations in some contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

### 22. benetan
**Form:** benetan  
**Literal meaning:** from bene- (true) + -tan (locative/manner suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** really, truly, indeed, in truth

Adverbial expression meaning really or truly, used to emphasize truth or reality of statements. Very common in spoken and written discourse. Functions as intensifier and truth-marker.

**[CORE]**

---

### 23. egiatan
**Form:** egiatan  
**Literal meaning:** from egia (truth) + -tan (locative/manner suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** in truth, truly, really, actually

Adverbial phrase meaning "in truth" or "truly," used to mark statements as truthful or to emphasize reality. Similar function to "benetan" but may be slightly more formal or emphatic.

**[CORE]**

---

### 24. itxurazko
**Form:** itxurazko  
**Literal meaning:** from itxura (appearance) + -zko (relational suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, superficial

Adjectival form indicating something is based on appearance rather than reality. Often implies contrast between surface and substance. Used in evaluations distinguishing seeming from being.

**[CORE]**

---

### 25. ageria
**Form:** ageria  
**Literal meaning:** from ager- (appear, be visible) + -i + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** evident, obvious, manifest, clear

Describes something as evident, obvious, or manifest. Used when truth or reality is clearly visible or demonstrable. Common in both everyday and formal discourse about what is apparent.

**[CORE]**

---

### 26. zalantzagarria
**Form:** zalantzagarria  
**Literal meaning:** from zalantza (doubt) + -garri (worthy of) + -a (article)  
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, questionable, dubious

Describes statements, claims, or situations as worthy of doubt or questionable. Used in epistemic evaluations where certainty is lacking or reliability is questioned. Productive derivation from "zalantza."

**[CORE]**

---

### 27. egiazkoa den
**Form:** egiazkoa den  
**Literal meaning:** that which is true (relative clause: egiazko + -a + den "is")  
**Typical English translations:** what is true, that which is true

Fixed relative clause construction used to refer to what is true or genuine. Common in philosophical and everyday discourse when discussing truth or reality as a category.

**[CORE]**

---

### 28. gezurrezko
**Form:** gezurrezko  
**Literal meaning:** from gezur (lie) + -ezko (relational suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, lying, mendacious

Adjectival form indicating something is characterized by falsehood or is fake. Used for false statements, counterfeit objects, and deceptive representations. Parallel formation to "egiazkoa."

**[CORE]**

---

### 29. zuzendu
**Form:** zuzendu  
**Literal meaning:** from zuzen (correct/straight) + -du (verbalizer)  
**Typical English translations:** to correct, to straighten, to rectify

Verb meaning to correct, straighten, or rectify. Used for correcting errors, straightening physical objects, and rectifying wrongs. Productive derivation showing polysemy of "zuzen."

**[CORE]**

---

### 30. fede
**Form:** fede  
**Literal meaning:** faith, trust (borrowed from Latin fides)  
**Typical English translations:** faith, trust, belief, confidence

Noun denoting faith, trust, or confidence, particularly in religious contexts but also in secular trust relationships. Used in expressions like "fede ona" (good faith). Borrowed but fully integrated.

**[CORE]**
