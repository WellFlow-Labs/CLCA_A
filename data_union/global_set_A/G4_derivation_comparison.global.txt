## 2. Narrative Analysis

### Patterns Recurring Across Unrelated Language Families

**Abstract property nominalization is universal in the dataset.** Every language produces abstract nouns naming truth-domain qualities through a suffix or circumfix on an adjectival or verbal base. The specific morphological mechanism varies — Turkish *-luk*, Finnish *-uus*, Basque *-tasun*, Tamil *-mai*, Indonesian *ke-...-an* circumfix, Korean *-seong*, Georgian *-eba*, Yoruba *ì-* prefix — but the functional outcome is the same: a nominal that names the property as an abstract entity and participates in evaluative constructions. This pattern is productive in all eight languages and shows no signs of fossilization; new forms can be created as needed.

**The agentive asymmetry (liar lexicalized, truth-teller not) is consistent across all eight languages and all language families represented.** Georgian (South Caucasian), Yoruba (Niger-Congo), Finnish (Finno-Ugric), Basque (language isolate), Tamil (Dravidian), Indonesian (Austronesian), Turkish (Turkic), and Korean (Koreanic) all have lexicalized single-word agentives for habitual liars and forgers, but none has a parallel lexicalized agentive for habitual truth-tellers. The derivational process that generates *yalancı* (liar), *aṣírọ́* (liar), *valehtelija* (liar), *gezurti* (liar), *poyyaṉ* (liar), *pembohong* (liar), *거짓말쟁이* (liar) does not generate a corresponding positive-role agentive in any of the eight languages. The concept of "truth-teller" is expressed periphrastically in all eight languages. This is not a gap in documentation; the F4 construction tables for all eight languages explicitly note the absence and describe the periphrastic alternatives. The asymmetry is consistent across unrelated language families.

**The spatial metaphor of straightness underlying truth and correctness is attested in all eight languages.** Georgian *marṭl-* (straight/right), Yoruba *tọ́* (straight/correct), Finnish *oikea* (right, also directional), Basque *zuzen* (straight/correct), Tamil *nēr* (straight/direct), Indonesian *benar* (correct), Turkish *doğru* (straight/correct/true), and Korean *olbareuda* (upright/correct) all show a root or base that combines spatial straightness with epistemic or moral correctness. The antonym in several languages is also spatial: Turkish *eğri* (crooked/dishonest), Basque *oker* (crooked/wrong). This convergence across unrelated language families is the most consistently attested morphological/semantic pattern in the dataset.

**The intransitive/passive framing of truth-revelation is documented in all eight languages.** In every language, truth can be the grammatical subject of an intransitive or passive verb expressing emergence or disclosure. This constructional pattern — treating truth as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility — is consistent across all eight languages and all language families. The specific verbs vary (Georgian *gamovida*, Yoruba *farahàn*, Finnish *tulee ilmi*, Basque *argitara atera*, Tamil *veḷippaṭṭatu*, Indonesian *terungkap*, Turkish *ortaya çıkmak*, Korean *드러나다*), but the structural pattern is the same.

**The discourse-framing adverbial derived from a truth base is attested in seven of eight languages.** Georgian *marṭla* (really/truly), Finnish *totta puhuen* (to tell the truth), Basque *egia esan behar* (truth be told), Tamil *uṇmaiyil* (in truth/actually), Indonesian *sebenarnya* (actually/in truth), Turkish *aslında* (actually/in fact), and Korean *사실을 말하자면* (to tell the truth) all function as sentence-level discourse markers introducing corrections, frank statements, or contrasts with appearance. The degree of grammaticalization varies — Indonesian *sebenarnya* and Turkish *aslında* are fully grammaticalized, while others are more compositional — but the functional pattern is consistent across unrelated language families.

### Striking Asymmetries

**The most striking asymmetry is the one-way blocked pathway for truth-teller agentives.** As documented above, all eight languages have lexicalized agentives for liars and forgers but none for truth-tellers. This is not a gap in documentation; it is consistently documented as an absence across the F4 construction tables and F7 agentive nominalization sections for all eight languages. The asymmetry is consistent across unrelated language families and appears to reflect a cross-linguistic pattern in how the truth domain is lexicalized: the negative pole (habitual deception) is more salient as a characterological category than the positive pole (habitual truth-telling).

**The degree of morphological productivity of the primary truth root varies enormously across languages.** Turkish *doğru* and Georgian *marṭl-* generate the largest documented derivational families, extending into technical domains (Turkish: *doğrusal*, linear; *doğrultu*, direction; *doğrulanabilirlik*, verifiability), religious domains (Georgian: *marṭlmadidebeli*, Orthodox Christian; *marṭlsaidumlo*, true sacrament), and institutional domains (Georgian: *marṭlmsajuleba*, justice system; *marṭlc̣era*, orthography). By contrast, Yoruba *tọ́* and Tamil *uṇmai* generate smaller derivational families, with the Tamil family concentrated in adjectival, adverbial, and verbal noun forms rather than compound institutional terms. This asymmetry is not explained by language family or morphological type alone; it reflects the specific historical and cultural elaboration of the truth domain in each language.

**The negative agentive derivation is more productive than the positive agentive derivation in all eight languages.** Not only is the truth-teller agentive absent, but the forger/deceiver agentive is more elaborated than any positive-role agentive in the truth domain. Finnish has *väärentäjä* (forger), *valehtelija* (liar), and *pettäjä* (deceiver) but no parallel positive-role agentives. Korean has *위조범* (forger), *거짓말쟁이* (liar), and *위선자* (hypocrite) but no parallel positive-role agentives. This asymmetry is consistent across all eight languages.

**The *-hada/-doeda* stative/agentive distinction in Korean is unique in the dataset.** Korean documents a systematic semantic distinction between *jinsil-hada* (to be truthful, active/performed quality) and *jinsil-doeda* (to be truthful, inherent/resultant quality), and between *wijo-hada* (to forge, agentive) and *wijo-doeda* (to be forged, passive/resultant). This morphological distinction between performed and inherent quality is not documented in any other language in the dataset. It is a productive pattern in Korean that applies across the truth domain, and the case of *mosun-doeda* (to be contradictory) — where only the *-doeda* form is attested and *\*mosun-hada* is not — shows that the distinction can be morphologically asymmetric within the same language.

### One-Way or Blocked Derivational Pathways

**The truth-teller agentive pathway is blocked in all eight languages.** The agentive derivation productive for negative roles does not generate truth-tellers. This is the most consistently documented blocked pathway in the dataset, attested across all eight languages and all language families represented.

**The *-hada* predicative suffix is blocked for *-seong* quality nominals in Korean.** Korean *-seong* nominals (*jinjeongseong*, *sinbyeongseong*, *tadangseong*) cannot take *-hada* as a predicative suffix; they require the existential *있다/없다* frame instead. This is a morphological restriction documented in the F4 construction tables for Korean and has no parallel in other languages.

**The *-garri* potential suffix in Basque does not extend to all truth-domain bases.** While *egiaztagarria* (verifiable), *fidagarria* (trustworthy), *sinesgarria* (believable), and *frogagarria* (provable) are all attested, the suffix does not apply freely to all truth-domain bases. The data documents this as a restricted rather than fully productive pattern.

**The *-itas* suffix in Indonesian applies only to loanword bases.** Indonesian *validitas*, *kredibilitas*, and *otentisitas* use the *-itas* suffix, but this suffix does not extend to native Indonesian roots (*\*benarittas*, *\*asliitas* are not attested). This is a morphological restriction documented in the F2a derivation table for Indonesian.

**The causative-inchoative chain (*-laş-/-leş-*) in Turkish is restricted to bases that have already undergone *-laş-* derivation.** *Meşrulaştırmak* (to legitimize) is attested because *meşrulaşmak* (to become legitimate) is attested, but the chain does not apply freely to all truth-domain adjectives. *\*Doğrulaştırmak* (to make more correct) is not documented as a standard form; *doğrulamak* (to verify) uses a different derivational path (*-la-* directly on the base).

### Productivity, Restriction, Lexicalization, and Unclear Attestation

**Productive patterns** (can generate new forms as needed, not restricted to a closed set):
- Abstract property nominalization via suffix (all 8 languages)
- Adverbial derivation from truth base (all 8 languages)
- Verbal noun / process nominalization (all 8 languages)
- Negative agentive nominalization for liars and forgers (all 8 languages)
- Privative/negative nominal (all 8 languages)
- Indonesian *salah* + [noun/verb] misaction compounding
- Turkish *doğru-* and *yanlış-* derivational families
- Basque *-garri/-gaitz/-ezin* potential/difficult/impossible adjectival series (within the set of verbal stems that accept it)
- Korean *-seong* abstract quality nominalization
- Finnish *totuuden-mukainen/vastainen* compound adjective pattern

**Restricted patterns** (attested but not freely productive across all bases):
- Compound adjective with *mukainen/vastainen* (Finnish, restricted to genitive nominal bases)
- Causative-inchoative chain *-laş-/-leş-tir-* (Turkish, restricted to bases that have undergone *-laş-* derivation)
- *-garri* potential suffix (Basque, restricted to verbal stems; not all truth-domain bases accept it)
- *-itas* suffix (Indonesian, restricted to loanword bases)
- Pejorative *-keria* suffix (Basque, restricted to a near-closed set of bases)
- Korean *-화하다* causative (restricted to Sino-Korean bases; not available for native Korean bases)
- Nominal compounding of truth + falsity to frame a binary question (ka, eu, ko — restricted to specific lexicalized compounds)

**Lexicalized / fossilized patterns** (forms that were once productive but are now fixed):
- Georgian *sarwmunoeba* (credibility/faith) — the bifurcation into secular and theological meanings is a lexicalized semantic split, not a productive derivational pattern
- Indonesian *sebenarnya* (actually/in truth) — fully grammaticalized as a discourse marker; the compositional meaning (*se-benar-nya*, "in its truth") is no longer active
- Turkish *aslında* (actually/in fact) — fully grammaticalized as a discourse marker; the compositional meaning (*asl-ında*, "in its origin") is no longer synchronically active
- Basque *ziurrenik* (probably/most likely) — the semantic shift from "certainly" to "probably" is a lexicalized change; the form is no longer compositionally interpreted as expressing certainty
- Korean *틀림없다* (certainly, without error) — lexicalized as a fixed expression of certainty; the compositional meaning (without deviation) is backgrounded in everyday use
