## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 CAUSATION Merger
Indonesian does not distinguish **CAUSATION (factual)** from **CAUSATION (character)** constructionally. The *meN-...-kan* causative pattern (Construction 5) serves both functions:
- Factual causation: *membenarkan* "to confirm/verify" (making a statement true)
- Character causation: *menyalahkan* "to blame" (attributing wrongness to a person)

The semantic distinction is carried by the lexical root and context, not by distinct morphosyntactic patterns. This represents a **constructional merger** where one operator handles both domains.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION Identity
**VERIFICATION** and **DISCONFIRMATION** share the same construction in Indonesian. The resultative *terbukti* "proven" (Construction 17) combines with both positive and negative predicates:
- Verification: *terbukti benar* "proven true"
- Disconfirmation: *terbukti bohong* "proven false"

Polarity is marked on the predicate, not through distinct constructions. This differs from languages where verification and falsification use different operators.

Interrogative verification (*apa benar* "is it true?", Construction 13) similarly accepts both positive and negative predicates without structural change.

### 2.3 REVELATION vs. BECOME Distinction
Indonesian maintains a clear distinction between **BECOME** and **REVELATION**:
- **BECOME** (*menjadi*, Construction 4): marks change of state without evidential commitment
  - *menjadi nyata* "to become real" (neutral inchoative)
- **REVELATION** (*ternyata*, Construction 17): marks evidential discovery of contrary-to-expectation information
  - *ternyata* "it turns out" (evidential surprise)

These do not substitute for each other. *Ternyata* functions primarily as a discourse marker introducing new information that contradicts prior belief, while *menjadi* is a standard inchoative verb. The *ter-* prefix has resultative/evidential semantics distinct from pure change-of-state.

### 2.4 Negation Asymmetry: Two-Tier System
Indonesian exhibits a **two-tier negation system** for truth-domain predicates:
- **Direct negation**: *tidak* + ADJ (Construction 2)
  - *tidak benar* "not true"
- **Hedged negation**: *kurang* + ADJ (Construction 23)
  - *kurang benar* "not quite correct"

The hedged form is not merely emphatic variation but serves distinct pragmatic functions (politeness, face-saving, partial negation). This creates an asymmetry where negative polarity has two constructional options while positive polarity has one.

Additionally, negation distinguishes adjectival (*tidak*) from nominal (*bukan*) predicates:
- *tidak akurat* "not accurate" (adjective)
- *bukan pembohong* "not a liar" (noun)

### 2.5 Nominalization Proliferation
Indonesian shows **three distinct nominalization constructions** (Constructions 6, 7, 8) where other languages might use one:
- Abstract quality: *ke-...-an* → *kebenaran* "truth"
- Agent: *peN-* → *pembohong* "liar"
- Process: *peN-...-an* → *pembenaran* "justification"

These are not interchangeable and cannot substitute for each other. The language requires selecting the appropriate nominalization type based on semantic function.

### 2.6 Adverbial Modification Specialization
Construction 9 (*se-...-nya*) creates adverbials that function primarily as **discourse markers** rather than manner adverbs:
- *sebenarnya* "actually, in fact" (introduces corrections)
- *sesungguhnya* "truly, verily" (emphasizes veracity)

These do not substitute for manner constructions (*dengan/secara* + ADJ, Construction 16), which express how an action is performed. The language maintains this functional distinction constructionally.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
**NONE IDENTIFIED**

All canonical CLCA operators have constructional realizations in Indonesian, though CAUSATION (factual) and CAUSATION (character) merge into a single construction.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps
**Construction 24 (Existential Truth)**: The pattern *benar adanya* "it is true/it truly exists" is a **fixed phrase** not productive beyond the root *benar*. Attempts to extend this pattern to other truth-domain roots (*\*jujur adanya*, *\*akurat adanya*) are not attested. This represents a **lexical restriction** rather than a productive construction.

**Construction 7 (Agentive Nominalization)**: *Pendusta* "liar" (from *dusta* "falsehood") is marked as [MARGINAL] and restricted to formal/literary registers. The productive agentive form is *pembohong* (from *bohong* "lie"). This suggests **register-based lexical restriction** where certain derivations are blocked in colloquial speech.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps
**NONE IDENTIFIED**

No evidence of conceptual operations that Indonesian systematically blocks or cannot express.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

#### 3.4.1 Positive/Negative Asymmetry in Intensification
Construction 20 shows asymmetry in intensifier distribution:
- Positive intensification: *benar sekali* "very true" [CORE, highly frequent]
- Negative intensification: *bohong belaka* "nothing but lies" [CORE, less frequent]

The intensifier *sekali* is highly productive with positive truth-predicates but less common with negative ones. The intensifier *belaka* appears primarily with negative predicates and carries negative connotation. This represents **distributional asymmetry** in intensification strategies.

#### 3.4.2 Binary Opposition Lexicalization
Construction 15 (Binary Opposition) shows **lexical fixedness**:
- *benar atau salah* "true or false" [fixed phrase]
- *asli atau palsu* "genuine or fake" [fixed phrase]

These are not freely productive. While the pattern *[ADJ₁] atau [ADJ₂]* exists in Indonesian, the truth-domain binary pairs function as **fixed collocations** rather than productive constructions. Substituting other antonymous pairs (*\*tepat atau tidak tepat*) is grammatical but not conventionalized.

#### 3.4.3 Light Verb Construction Selectivity
Construction 18 shows **verb-noun selectivity**:
- *mencari kebenaran* "to seek truth" [CORE]
- *menghadapi kenyataan* "to face reality" [CORE, fixed collocation]

Not all verbs combine freely with all truth-nominalizations. *Menghadapi* "to face" strongly prefers *kenyataan* "reality" over other truth-nouns (*\?menghadapi kebenaran* "to face truth" is marginal). This represents **collocational restriction** rather than free productivity.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### 4.1 Fully Available Operators
The following CLCA operators have direct, productive constructional equivalents in Indonesian:

- **ATTR**: Construction 1 (zero copula predication)
- **ADNOM**: Construction 3 (adjective-noun modification)
- **BECOME**: Construction 4 (*menjadi* inchoative)
- **REVELATION**: Construction 17 (*ter-* evidential/resultative)
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Construction 5 (*meN-...-kan* causative)
- **NEGATION**: Construction 2 (*tidak/bukan* negation)
- **NOMINALIZATION**: Constructions 6, 7, 8 (three-way distinction)

### 4.2 Available with Substitution
- **CAUSATION (character)**: **SUBSTITUTED BY Construction 5** (*meN-...-kan*)
  - No distinct construction; merged with factual causation
  - Semantic distinction carried by lexical root, not morphosyntax

### 4.3 Available with Constructional Identity
- **VERIFICATION** and **DISCONFIRMATION**: Share Construction 17 (*terbukti* resultative) and Construction 13 (*apa* interrogative)
  - Polarity marked on predicate, not through distinct operators
  - Functionally equivalent constructions for both operations

### 4.4 Partially Available (with Restrictions)
**NONE**

All attested constructions are either fully productive or show only lexical/collocational restrictions (not constructional unavailability).

### 4.5 Missing Entirely
**NONE**

All canonical CLCA operators have constructional realizations, though some merge (CAUSATION) or share constructions (VERIFICATION/DISCONFIRMATION).

## 5. Additional Observations

### 5.1 Morphological Richness
Indonesian demonstrates **high morphological productivity** in the truth domain through:
- Derivational affixation: *ke-...-an*, *peN-*, *peN-...-an*, *meN-...-kan*, *ter-*, *se-...-nya*
- Reduplication: Full reduplication for intensification (Construction 10)
- Compounding: *salah* + NOUN/VERB compounds (Construction 19)

This contrasts with more analytic languages where truth-domain operations rely primarily on syntax.

### 5.2 Register Stratification
Several constructions show **clear register splits**:
- Colloquial: *kalau*, *dengan*, *apa*, *banget*
- Formal: *jika*, *secara*, *apakah*, *sangat*

This stratification is systematic across multiple constructions (14, 16, 13, 20), suggesting register is a significant organizing principle in Indonesian truth-domain grammar.

### 5.3 Discourse Marker Specialization
Multiple constructions (9, 13, 14, 17, 21) have **discourse marker functions** beyond their core grammatical roles:
- *sebenarnya* "actually" (introduces corrections)
- *ternyata* "it turns out" (introduces contrary-to-expectation information)
- *kalau tidak salah* "if I'm not mistaken" (epistemic hedge)
- *yang benar saja!* "really?!" (exclamation)

This suggests Indonesian grammaticalizes truth-domain vocabulary into discourse management functions more extensively than some other languages.

### 5.4 Zero Copula Obligatoriness
Construction 1 demonstrates **obligatory zero copula** in present tense affirmative predication. Use of copula *adalah* is marked/emphatic, not neutral. This distinguishes Indonesian from copula-requiring languages and affects how ATTR constructions are realized.
