## Step 1 — Central roots

Below are the roots/stems that recur most clearly across the P1 set.

1. **gerçek** — *gerçek*  
   **Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
   (**gerçek, gerçekçi, gerçekçilik/related only one listed as gerçekçi; counting only listed P1 items gives 2 directly, but as a stem it is one of the clearest recurring bases**)  
   **Core meaning contribution:** real, actual, non-fabricated; marks correspondence to actuality or genuine existence.

2. **doğru / doğr-** — *doğru / doğr-*  
   **Participates in:** 2 P1 items  
   (**doğru, doğruluk**)  
   **Core meaning contribution:** correct, right, straight; marks correctness, truth, accuracy, or proper alignment.

3. **hak / hakikat / hakik-** — *hak / hakikat / hakik-*  
   **Participates in:** 4 P1 items if the family is taken together  
   (**hakikat, hakiki, haklı, plus semantically and historically related hak-based formations**)  
   **Core meaning contribution:** truth, right, rightful claim, essential reality, legitimacy/justification.

Because the P1 list is semantically broad and not built from a single derivational family, recurrence is somewhat dispersed. Other visible productive bases include **öz** (*öz, özgün, özgünlük*) and **güven** (*güvenilir, güvenilirlik*), but the three above are the most central for root expansion in the truth-evaluation domain.

## 1. Root: **gerçek** — *gerçek*

### 1. **gerçek**
**Transliteration:** gerçek  
**Literal breakdown:** simplex stem  
**English:** real; actual; true  
**Context:** Very common in everyday speech and writing for things, events, motives, and states that are actual rather than apparent or invented. Also used contrastively against *sahte*, *uydurma*, or *hayalî*.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **gerçekten**
**Transliteration:** gerçekten  
**Literal breakdown:** gerçek + -ten  
**English:** really; truly; in fact  
**Context:** Very common adverb in speech and writing. It can mark sincerity, emphasis, or factuality, and often functions discourse-pragmatically rather than strictly truth-conditionally.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **gerçeklik**
**Transliteration:** gerçeklik  
**Literal breakdown:** gerçek + -lik  
**English:** reality; realness  
**Context:** Common in philosophy, criticism, psychology, and general prose. Used for actuality, the nature of the real, or the relation between representation and the world.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **gerçekçi**
**Transliteration:** gerçekçi  
**Literal breakdown:** gerçek + -çi

## 3. Root: **hak / hakikat / hakik-** — *hak / hakikat / hakik-*

This family is internally mixed: some forms center on **truth/reality** (*hakikat, hakiki*), others on **right/claim/justice** (*haklı, hak, meşru-adjacent semantic area*). Since the task asks for root expansion from central building blocks, I list common attested forms from this family while keeping the forms themselves distinct.

### 1. **hakikat**
**Transliteration:** hakikat  
**Literal breakdown:** Arabic-origin noun  
**English:** truth; reality  
**Context:** Common in literary, philosophical, religious, and formal discourse. Often refers to essential truth, deeper reality, or ultimate truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **hakiki**
**Transliteration:** hakiki  
**Literal breakdown:** hakikat + adjectival formation  
**English:** genuine; real; authentic

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **hakikatli**
**Transliteration:** hakikatli  
**Literal breakdown:** hakikat + -li
