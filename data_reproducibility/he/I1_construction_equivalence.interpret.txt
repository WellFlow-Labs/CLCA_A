## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### REVELATION substitutes for BECOME
Hebrew systematically avoids direct inchoative constructions with truth-domain terms. The construction "X became true/false" is marked as [MARGINAL] in P4b and described as "not natural in Hebrew." Instead, the language uses **verbs of revelation and discovery** (*hitgalah* "was revealed", *hitbarar* "became clear", *nimtsa* "was found") to express change-of-state in the truth domain. 

P4a explicitly states: "Change of state is more idiomatically expressed through verbs of revelation (*hitgalah* 'was revealed'), discovery (*nimtsa* 'was found'), or clarification (*hitbarar* 'became clear'). The construction 'X became true/false' is not natural in Hebrew; instead, one says 'it turned out that X' (*hitbarar she-X*)."

This represents a **conceptual substitution**: the language conceptualizes truth-emergence as revelation/discovery rather than as simple state-change.

### VERIFICATION overlaps with CAUSATION (factual)
The verb *hokhiakh* "prove" serves double duty: it expresses both causing something to be recognized as true (causation) and establishing truth through evidence (verification). Hebrew does not maintain a clear constructional distinction between "making something true" and "proving something true" — both are expressed through the same periphrastic construction with *hokhiakh*.

P4a notes: "Causation is expressed through verbs like *hokhiakh* 'prove,' *shiknea* 'convince,' or *gilah* 'reveal.'" These same verbs appear in verification contexts.

### DISCONFIRMATION lacks dedicated construction
While verification has a clear expression through *hokhiakh* "prove", disconfirmation has no parallel dedicated construction. P4b does not list any [CORE] construction for disconfirmation. Instead, speakers must use:
- Negation + verification: *lo hokhiakh* "didn't prove"
- Refutation verbs: *hikhkhish* "deny/refute" (not listed in P4a constructions)
- Showing falsity: *hir'ah she-lo* "showed that not"

This represents a **structural asymmetry**: positive verification is constructionally supported, negative disconfirmation requires circumlocution.

### Analytic NEGATION dominates
Hebrew negation is overwhelmingly analytic, using the particle *lo* before predicates. The negative copula *ein/eino* exists but is marked [MARGINAL] and "formal/literary" in P4b. The prefix *bilti-* creates lexicalized negative adjectives (*bilti amin* "unreliable") but is not productive across all truth-terms.

P4a states: "*Lo* is the standard colloquial negator; *ein/eino* (inflected negative copula) is more formal."

### Demonstrative preference in ATTRIBUTION
While attribution can use full NP subjects, P4b notes that "Demonstrative subjects (*ze*, *zot*) are extremely common in truth-evaluation contexts" and "*Ze/zot* + truth-term is the unmarked way to evaluate propositions in conversation, far more common than full NP subjects."

This represents a **discourse-pragmatic preference** rather than a structural gap, but it affects the typical realization of the ATTR construction.

### Asymmetry in agentive nominalization
P4a notes: "No common agentive noun exists for 'truth-teller' — this is expressed periphrastically (*mi she-omer emet* 'one who tells truth')." 

Hebrew has productive agentive nominalizations for negative truth-domain agents (*shakran* "liar", *mezayef* "forger") but lacks a simple lexical item for positive truth-tellers. This is a **lexical asymmetry** rather than a constructional gap — the nominalization pattern exists, but the specific lexeme is missing.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps

1. **BECOME (inchoative)**: No natural direct construction for "X became true/false". P4a explicitly states this is "not natural in Hebrew." The construction *ha-davar ne'esah barur* is marked [MARGINAL]. **Substituted by REVELATION verbs.**

2. **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated construction parallel to verification. No [CORE] pattern listed in P4b for establishing falsity. Requires circumlocution through negation + verification or separate refutation verbs.

3. **CAUSATION (factual) — direct "make true/false"**: No construction for directly causing truth-value change. P4a notes: "Many truth-domain terms lack dedicated causative forms; causation is expressed through verbs like *hokhiakh* 'prove,' *shiknea* 'convince,' or *gilah* 'reveal.'" The language has causative morphology (hif'il stem) but it doesn't apply to core truth-terms like *emet* "truth" or *sheker* "falsehood" to mean "make true/false."

### Lexical Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization for "truth-teller"**: P4a explicitly states "No common agentive noun exists for 'truth-teller.'" Must use periphrastic *mi she-omer emet* "one who tells truth."

2. **Dedicated disconfirmation verb**: While *hokhiakh* "prove" exists for verification, no parallel single verb for disconfirmation is listed in P4a constructions.

### Conceptual Gaps

1. **Direct inchoative with truth-values**: The concept of truth-values changing state directly (becoming true/false) is not expressed naturally. P4a states: "The construction 'X became true/false' is not natural in Hebrew." This suggests a conceptual preference for viewing truth-emergence as revelation rather than state-change.

### Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Verification vs. Disconfirmation**: Positive verification has clear constructional support (*hokhiakh* "prove"), while negative disconfirmation lacks parallel dedicated construction.

2. **Agentive nominalizations**: Negative agents (*shakran* "liar", *mezayef* "forger") have productive lexical forms, while positive truth-teller lacks simple lexeme.

3. **Causation**: Character-based causation (misleading, deceiving) has productive hif'il forms (*hit'ah* "mislead"), while factual causation (making true/false) lacks direct construction.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available
- **ATTR**: Core construction with zero copula in present tense. Extremely frequent.
- **ADNOM**: Core construction with standard adjective-noun agreement.
- **NEGATION**: Core construction with analytic particle *lo*.
- **NOMINALIZATION**: Both abstract (*-ut* suffix) and agentive (*-an* suffix, participles) highly productive.
- **REVELATION**: Core construction through dedicated verbs (*hitgalah*, *hitbarar*, *gilah*).
- **CAUSATION (character)**: Available through hif'il causative stem (*hit'ah* "mislead", *hitsdik* "justify").

### Available with Substitution
- **BECOME**: **Substituted by REVELATION**. Direct inchoative marked [MARGINAL]; revelation verbs serve this function naturally.
- **VERIFICATION**: Available through *hokhiakh* "prove" but overlaps functionally with CAUSATION (factual).

### Partially Available
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: Available through periphrastic constructions (*hokhiakh she-* "prove that") but lacks direct "make X true/false" construction. Causative morphology exists but doesn't apply to core truth-value terms.

### Missing Entirely
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated construction. Requires circumlocution through negation + verification or separate refutation verbs not integrated into the core construction system.

---

## Summary Statement

Hebrew shows a **well-developed constructional system** for truth-domain operations with the following characteristics:

1. **Strong support** for attribution, adnominal modification, negation, and nominalization.

2. **Systematic substitution** of REVELATION for BECOME, reflecting a conceptual preference for viewing truth-emergence as discovery/revelation rather than state-change.

3. **Partial gaps** in causation, where character-based causation (misleading) is morphologically supported but factual causation (making true/false) requires periphrasis.

4. **Structural asymmetry** between verification (well-supported) and disconfirmation (requiring circumlocution).

5. **Lexical asymmetries** favoring negative agents (liar) over positive agents (truth-teller).

The language's construction inventory reflects a **revelation-centered** rather than **state-change-centered** conceptualization of truth-domain dynamics.
