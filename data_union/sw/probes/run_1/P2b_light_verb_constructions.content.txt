# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions are included only where there is positive evidence of attestation; absence is recorded explicitly. Phrasal constructions already documented in P1b root expansion are noted as prior-attested where they appear here, but are not duplicated in full. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven constructions not already covered.

A construction is included here if it meets the following criteria: (a) it involves a P1 item as a core component; (b) it is multi-word; (c) it is not a simple nominal modification phrase of the type *X wa Y* unless that phrase has idiomatic or fixed status beyond compositional meaning; (d) it is not a derivational form documented in P2a.

## 1. Base Form: **kweli**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli? / Kweli!
- **Transliteration:** Kweli? / Kweli!
- **English translation:** Really? / Really!
- **Usage notes:** Used as a one-word discourse particle functioning as a question tag or exclamation of surprise. Included here because it functions as a pragmatic construction in dialogue, not merely as a propositional assertion. Extremely common in spoken Swahili.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli
- **English translation:** truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest
- **Usage notes:** Adverbial prepositional phrase used to frame a statement as sincere or factually grounded. Prior-attested in P1b. Included here as a confirmed periphrastic construction.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kwa kweli kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli kweli
- **English translation:** truly and sincerely; in all honesty; absolutely for real
- **Usage notes:** Further intensified adverbial phrase combining the prepositional particle *kwa* with the reduplicated form *kweli kweli*. Prior-attested in P1b. More colloquial than *kwa kweli*; used in emphatic spoken assertions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli kabisa
- **Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
- **English translation:** absolutely true; completely true; the absolute truth
- **Usage notes:** Intensified nominal/adverbial phrase using *kabisa* (completely, absolutely) as a postposed intensifier. Prior-attested in P1b. Common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli au uongo
- **Transliteration:** kweli au uongo
- **English translation:** true or false; truth or lie
- **Usage notes:** Paired contrastive construction used in educational true/false formats, everyday challenges to a statement's accuracy, and philosophical discussion. Prior-attested in P1b.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** kweli ni kweli
- **Transliteration:** kweli ni kweli
- **English translation:** truth is truth; the truth is the truth
- **Usage notes:** Copular tautological construction used as a rhetorical affirmation that a fact cannot be denied or altered. Prior-attested in P1b. Functions as a proverb-like assertion in argument and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Nakuambia kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Nakuambia kweli.
- **English translation:** I am telling you the truth. / I tell you truly.
- **Usage notes:** Periphrastic assertion construction combining *kuambia* (to tell) with *kweli* as object. Used in everyday interpersonal discourse to assert sincerity. The verb *kuambia* takes a human object (*ku-* = you) and *kweli* as the content object.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Sema kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Sema kweli.
- **English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *sema* (speak/say) with *kweli* as direct object. Prior-attested in P1b as *sema ukweli*; this shorter form with bare *kweli* rather than *ukweli* is also attested in everyday speech, particularly in informal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Hii ni kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Hii ni kweli.
- **English translation:** This is true. / This is the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Basic copular predication using *ni* (copula) with *kweli* as predicate. Extremely common in everyday discourse as a simple assertion of truth. The demonstrative *hii* (this) can be replaced by other subjects.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Si kweli.
- **Transliteration:** Si kweli.
- **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not true. / That's false.
- **Usage notes:** Negative copular construction using *si* (negative copula) with *kweli* as predicate. Extremely common in everyday discourse as a denial or refutation. Functionally equivalent to "that's not true."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Example:** Kweli kabla ya uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Kweli kabla ya uongo.
- **English translation:** Truth before falsehood.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial or slogan-type construction asserting the priority of truth over falsehood. Attested in moral and religious discourse. The construction *X kabla ya Y* (X before Y) is syntactically regular but this specific pairing has semi-fixed status.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## 2. Base Form: **ukweli**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli wa mambo
- **Transliteration:** ukweli wa mambo
- **English translation:** the truth of the matter; the facts of the case; how things really are
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction using the associative particle *wa* to link *ukweli* with *mambo* (things, matters). Prior-attested in P1b. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli mtupu
- **Transliteration:** ukweli mtupu
- **English translation:** the plain truth; the bare truth; nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal phrase with postposed adjective *mtupu* (pure, mere, nothing but) in class 11 agreement. Prior-attested in P1b. Used in legal and moral contexts to assert a statement contains no falsehood or embellishment.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli mzima
- **Transliteration:** ukweli mzima
- **English translation:** the whole truth; the full truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal phrase with postposed adjective *mzima* (whole, complete) in class 11 agreement. Prior-attested in P1b. Used in legal and moral contexts to insist on complete disclosure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli na uwazi
- **Transliteration:** ukweli na uwazi
- **English translation:** truth and transparency; honesty and openness
- **Usage notes:** Paired nominal construction using *na* (and) to link *ukweli* with *uwazi* (openness, transparency). Prior-attested in P1b. Common in governance, journalism, and civil society discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli na haki
- **Transliteration:** ukweli na haki
- **English translation:** truth and justice; truth and rights
- **Usage notes:** Paired nominal construction linking *ukweli* with *haki*. Prior-attested in P1b. Common in legal, political, and moral discourse and in the names of advocacy organizations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli unauma
- **Transliteration:** ukweli unauma
- **English translation:** the truth hurts
- **Usage notes:** Subject-verb construction with *ukweli* as subject and *unauma* (it hurts/stings; class 11 subject prefix + present tense + *uma*) as predicate. Prior-attested in P1b as a widely used proverbial expression.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli uliofichwa
- **Transliteration:** ukweli uliofichwa
- **English translation:** hidden truth; concealed truth; suppressed truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal relative clause construction: *ukweli* + relative clause *uliofichwa* (that was hidden; passive relative). Prior-attested in P1b. Used in investigative journalism and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** nusu ukweli
- **Transliteration:** nusu ukweli
- **English translation:** half-truth; partial truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal construction with *nusu* (half) as preposed modifier. Prior-attested in P1b. Used in media criticism and political commentary to describe a technically accurate but misleading statement.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** Tafuta ukweli.
- **Transliteration:** Tafuta ukweli.
- **English translation:** Seek the truth. / Search for the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *tafuta* (seek, search for) with *ukweli* as direct object. Prior-attested in P1b. Used in journalistic, philosophical, and religious discourse; also appears as a motto.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** Sema ukweli.
- **Transliteration:** Sema ukweli.
- **English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *sema* (speak/say) with *ukweli* as direct object. Prior-attested in P1b. Used in moral instruction, legal proceedings, and everyday confrontations.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** Kusema ukweli ni wajibu.
- **Transliteration:** Kusema ukweli ni wajibu.
- **English translation:** Telling the truth is a duty. / To tell the truth is an obligation.
- **Usage notes:** Infinitival subject construction: *kusema ukweli* (to tell the truth) functions as the subject of the copular predication *ni wajibu* (is a duty/obligation). Prior-attested in P1b. Used in moral and legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** Mwambie ukweli.
- **Transliteration:** Mwambie ukweli.
- **English translation:** Tell him/her the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Directive construction with object prefix *mw-* (class 1, him/her) on the verb *ambie* (subjunctive of *kuambia*, to tell) and *ukweli* as content object. Prior-attested in P1b. Common in everyday interpersonal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli wa kihistoria
- **Transliteration:** ukweli wa kihistoria
- **English translation:** historical truth; historical accuracy
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *ki-* adjectival prefix on *historia* (history). Prior-attested in P1b. Used in academic, journalistic, and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli wa kisayansi
- **Transliteration:** ukweli wa kisayansi
- **English translation:** scientific truth; empirical truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *ki-* adjectival prefix on *sayansi* (science). Prior-attested in P1b. Used in academic and educational discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** ukweli wa kidini
- **Transliteration:** ukweli wa kidini
- **English translation:** religious truth; doctrinal truth
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *ki-* adjectival prefix on *dini* (religion). Prior-attested in P1b. Used in theological and interfaith discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Base Form:** ukweli
- **Example:** Ukweli utakuwa ukweli daima.
- **Transliteration:** Ukweli utakuwa ukweli daima.
- **English translation:** Truth will always be truth. / The truth will always remain the truth.
- **Usage notes:** Copular future construction using *utakuwa* (will be; class 11 subject prefix + future + *kuwa*) with *daima* (always) as adverb. Used in moral and rhetorical discourse to assert the permanence of truth. Attested in written and oratorical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## 3. Base Form: **uongo**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Hii ni uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Hii ni uongo.
- **English translation:** This is a lie. / This is false. / This is untrue.
- **Usage notes:** Basic copular predication using *ni* (copula) with *uongo* as predicate. Extremely common in everyday discourse as a denial or accusation of falsehood.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Sema uongo
- **Transliteration:** Sema uongo
- **English translation:** Tell a lie. / Say something false.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *sema* (speak/say) with *uongo* as direct object. Used in everyday discourse, often in conditional or hypothetical contexts, and in moral instruction as a negative example.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Piga uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Piga uongo.
- **English translation:** Tell a lie. / Lie.
- **Usage notes:** Periphrastic verbal construction using *piga* (to strike, to do; a general-purpose light verb in Swahili) with *uongo* as object. This construction is the most colloquial way to express the act of lying in everyday spoken Swahili, more common in informal registers than *kudanganya*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Anapiga uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Anapiga uongo.
- **English translation:** He/she is lying. / He/she tells lies.
- **Usage notes:** Inflected form of the *piga uongo* construction with third-person singular subject prefix *a-* and present tense marker *-na-*. Illustrates the productivity of this periphrastic construction in finite verbal contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** kweli au uongo
- **Transliteration:** kweli au uongo
- **English translation:** true or false; truth or lie
- **Usage notes:** Paired contrastive construction. Already documented under *kweli* (Construction 5 above). Included here as it equally involves *uongo* as a core component.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** uongo mtupu
- **Transliteration:** uongo mtupu
- **English translation:** a complete lie; pure falsehood; nothing but lies
- **Usage notes:** Nominal phrase with postposed adjective *mtupu* (pure, mere, nothing but) in class 11 agreement. Parallel in structure to *ukweli mtupu*. Used in everyday and moral discourse to assert that a statement is entirely false.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
- **Transliteration:** Uongo una miguu mifupi.
- **English translation:** A lie has short legs. / Lies don't travel far.
- **Usage notes:** A widely attested Swahili proverb asserting that lies are eventually discovered. Uses *una* (has; class 11 subject prefix + *-na-* + *-a*) with *miguu mifupi* (short legs) as predicate. Attested in moral and didactic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Uongo ni dhambi.
- **Transliteration:** Uongo ni dhambi.
- **English translation:** Lying is a sin. / Falsehood is a sin.
- **Usage notes:** Copular moral predication using *ni* (copula) with *dhambi* (sin; from Arabic *ذنب*) as predicate. Used in religious and moral instruction. Attested in both Islamic and Christian Swahili discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** Usiamini uongo.
- **Transliteration:** Usiamini uongo.
- **English translation:** Don't believe lies. / Do not trust falsehood.
- **Usage notes:** Negative imperative construction using *usi-* (negative imperative prefix, 2nd person singular) + *amini* (believe/trust) with *uongo* as object. Used in moral instruction and everyday cautionary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Example:** nusu uongo
- **Transliteration:** nusu uongo
- **English translation:** a half-lie; partial falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Nominal construction with *nusu* (half) as preposed modifier, parallel to *nusu ukweli*. Used in evaluative discourse to describe a statement that is partially false or misleading. Less common than *nusu ukweli* but attested.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## 4. Base Form: **halisi**

---

**Construction 1**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** dhahabu halisi
- **Transliteration:** dhahabu halisi
- **English translation:** genuine gold; real gold; pure gold
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *halisi* as postposed adjective modifying *dhahabu* (gold; from Arabic *ذهب*). A highly typical and frequently cited context of use for *halisi* in commercial and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** bidhaa halisi
- **Transliteration:** bidhaa halisi
- **English translation:** genuine goods; authentic products; real merchandise
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *halisi* modifying *bidhaa* (goods, merchandise; from Arabic *بضاعة*). Used in commercial contexts to distinguish genuine products from counterfeits. Contrasts with *bidhaa bandia* (fake goods).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** Hii ni halisi.
- **Transliteration:** Hii ni halisi.
- **English translation:** This is genuine. / This is authentic. / This is real.
- **Usage notes:** Copular predication using *ni* with *halisi* as predicate adjective. Used in commercial and everyday evaluative contexts to assert the authenticity of an item.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** Si halisi.
- **Transliteration:** Si halisi.
- **English translation:** It is not genuine. / It is not authentic. / It is fake.
- **Usage notes:** Negative copular construction using *si* with *halisi* as predicate. Used in commercial and everyday evaluative contexts to assert that an item is not authentic.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** mtu halisi
- **Transliteration:** mtu halisi
- **English translation:** a genuine person; a person of integrity; a real person
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *halisi* modifying *mtu* (person). Used in character evaluation to describe someone who is authentic and without pretense. Common in everyday moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Base Form:** halisi
- **Example:** upendo halisi
- **Transliteration:** upendo halisi
- **English translation:** genuine love; real love; true love
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction with *halisi* modifying *upendo* (love; *u-* + *pendo*). Used in everyday and literary discourse to distinguish sincere affection from pretense.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: **uhalisi**

---

- **Transliteration:** uhalisi wa maisha
- **English translation:** the reality of life; the realities of life; life as it really is

- **Transliteration:** uhalisi wa hali
- **English translation:** the reality of the situation; the actual state of affairs
- **Usage notes:** Nominal modification construction linking *uhalisi* with *hali* (situation, condition; from Arabic *حال*). Used in journalistic and everyday discourse to refer to the actual state of a situation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Base Form:** uhalisi
- **Example:** Ukabili uhalisi.
- **Transliteration:** Ukabili uhalisi.
- **English translation:** Face reality. / Confront the truth of the situation.
- **Usage notes:** Imperative construction using *kabili* (to face, to confront; from Arabic *قابل*) with *uhalisi* as direct object. Used in everyday and motivational discourse to urge someone to accept or deal with actual circumstances.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Base Form:** uhalisi

- **Transliteration:** uhalisi wa kisanaa / uhalisi katika fasihi
