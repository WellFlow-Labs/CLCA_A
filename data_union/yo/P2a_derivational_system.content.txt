# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Yoruba (yo)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

**Preliminary note on Yoruba derivational morphology:** Yoruba derivational morphology operates primarily through prefixation (nominalization via *i-*, *ì-*, *à-*, *o-*, *ọ-*, *ẹ-*) and compounding. Suffixation is rare as a productive derivational process. The boundary between derivation and compounding is not always sharp. Verbal roots are typically bare; nominalization is the most productive derivational process. The negating prefix *àì-* (combining *à-* + *ì-*) is productive across nominal forms. Not all roots participate equally in all processes. Adjectival and adverbial modification is largely achieved through juxtaposition rather than affixation. Reduplication functions as an intensifying or iterative process in some forms. The forms documented below are restricted to those with clear attestation in the P1 and P1b data or in well-documented Yoruba lexicographic and linguistic sources. Paradigm completion is not attempted. Gaps are preserved without completion.

## Derived Forms by Base Item

### Base: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**1.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** àìtọ́
**Transliteration:** àìtọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (negating prefix) + *ì-* (nominalizing) + *tọ́* (correct, straight); "the state of not being correct"
**Natural English Translation:** incorrectness, impropriety, wrongness
**Usage:** Used in moral and evaluative contexts to refer to the quality of being incorrect or improper. Appears in everyday speech and moral condemnation as a general negation of correctness, though less frequent than *irọ́* for the specific sense of falsehood. Also appears in legal and moral assessments and in the compound *ẹ̀rí àìtọ́*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** olòtítọ́
**Transliteration:** olòtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *olò-* (person-prefix, variant of *ẹni tó ni* "one who has/possesses") + *títọ́* (truthfulness); "one who possesses truth / a truthful person"
**Natural English Translation:** truthful person, honest person, one who tells the truth
**Usage:** Used to describe a person whose habitual conduct is characterized by truthfulness. Appears in moral assessments and in character evaluations in everyday and formal discourse. Also appears in proverbs contrasting truthful and deceptive persons.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** òtítọ́ pátápátá
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ pátápátá
**Literal Breakdown:** *òtítọ́* (truth) + *pátápátá* (completely, entirely); "complete truth"
**Natural English Translation:** the whole truth, complete truth, nothing but the truth
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts to assert that a statement is entirely truthful without omission or distortion. Appears in oath-taking and formal testimony contexts, parallel to the English legal formula.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ọ̀rọ̀* (word, speech, matter) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "word/matter of truth"
**Natural English Translation:** true statement, truthful speech, words of truth
**Usage:** A productive nominal compound used to evaluate a statement or account as truthful. Appears in everyday speech, preaching, and legal contexts where the truthfulness of an utterance is being affirmed.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, speak) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "to speak truth"
**Natural English Translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
**Usage:** The most productive verbal collocation with *òtítọ́*, used across all registers to describe the act of making a truthful statement. Appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and moral instruction. The standard verbal collocation for truth-telling; used in legal oaths, moral instruction, everyday assertions, and religious discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìsọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìsọ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizing prefix) + *sọ* (to say) + *òtítọ́* (truth); "the act of speaking truth"
**Natural English Translation:** truth-telling, the act of speaking truthfully
**Usage:** The nominalized form of *sọ òtítọ́*, used to refer to truth-telling as a practice or virtue. Appears in moral and educational discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìtọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizer) + *tọ́* (straight/correct); bare nominalization of the root
**Natural English Translation:** straightness, correctness, rightness
**Usage:** The bare nominalization of the root *tọ́*; used in philosophical and moral discourse to refer abstractly to the quality of being correct. Less frequent in everyday speech than *òtítọ́* but attested in formal and literary registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** tọ́tọ́
**Transliteration:** tọ́tọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** Reduplication of *tọ́*; intensified form
**Natural English Translation:** very straight, very correct, truly right
**Usage:** The reduplicated form of the root, used as an intensifier in adjectival and adverbial function. Appears in *ọ̀nà tọ́tọ́* ("the very straight/correct path"). Reduplication here signals intensification rather than a new lexical category.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** àtúntọ́
**Transliteration:** àtúntọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (nominalizer) + *tún* (again, re-) + *tọ́* (correct/straighten); "re-correcting"
**Natural English Translation:** correction, rectification, reform, amendment
**Usage:** Used in administrative, legal, and moral contexts to describe the act of correcting something previously wrong. The *tún* element marks iterative or restorative action.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**Literal Breakdown:** *òtítọ́* (truth) + *ni* (copula/focus marker)
**Natural English Translation:** it is the truth; that is true
**Usage:** Fixed assertive expression used to affirm the truth of a statement; appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and religious affirmation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìjẹ́* (acknowledgment, admission; from *jẹ*, to accept/consume) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** acknowledgment of the truth, admission of truth
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts for the act of formally admitting or acknowledging that something is true; appears in court proceedings and religious confession contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìwádìí* (investigation, inquiry) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** investigation of the truth, truth inquiry, fact-finding
**Usage:** Used in legal, journalistic, and administrative contexts for the process of formally investigating what is true; appears in news media and official inquiry language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ènìyàn* (person) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** a truthful person, a person of truth
**Usage:** Attributive nominal construction used in character evaluation to describe someone consistently honest in speech and conduct. Appears in social and moral assessment contexts across registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *òtítọ́*:** No common suffixed or verbal derivation from *òtítọ́* itself (as distinct from its root *tọ́*) is identified. The word functions primarily as a nominal base for collocations rather than as a morphological base for further affixation. *òtítọ́* participates in productive genitive/attributive constructions of the pattern [NOUN + *òtítọ́*] and [VERB + *òtítọ́*], but these are syntactic collocations rather than morphological derivations in the strict sense.

### Base: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

---

**14.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ irọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, speak) + *irọ́* (lie, falsehood); "to speak a lie"
**Natural English Translation:** to tell a lie, to lie, to speak falsely
**Usage:** The primary verbal collocation with *irọ́*, highly productive across all registers. Used in moral condemnation, legal contexts, and everyday speech to describe the act of making a false statement. The direct antonym of *sọ òtítọ́*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** ìsọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** ìsọ irọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ì-* (nominalizing prefix) + *sọ* (to say) + *irọ́* (lie); "the act of speaking a lie"
**Natural English Translation:** lying, the act of telling lies, deception through speech
**Usage:** The nominalized form of *sọ irọ́*, used to refer to lying as a practice or act. Appears in moral and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** aláìrọ́
**Transliteration:** aláìrọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (agentive prefix, from *eni tó ní*) + *àì-* (negating) + *irọ́* (lie); "one who does not lie, one without falsehood"
**Natural English Translation:** truthful person, one who does not lie, honest person
**Usage:** Used as a nominal to describe a person who does not lie. Appears in moral commendation and in proverbs. Less common than *olòtítọ́* but attested in evaluative discourse. Also covers, in some usages, the sense of "one who is genuine/authentic."
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**17.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** aṣírọ́
**Transliteration:** aṣírọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (person-nominalizer) + *ṣ-* (do/make) + *irọ́* (lie); "one who makes/tells lies"
**Natural English Translation:** liar, one who tells lies, fabricator
**Usage:** Used to describe a habitual liar. Appears in moral condemnation and in legal contexts where a witness's credibility is being challenged. A strong term of moral criticism.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** olóirọ́
**Transliteration:** olóirọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *oní-* / *olú-* (agentive, "one who has/does") + *irọ́* (lie); "one who has lies, a liar"
**Natural English Translation:** liar, one who tells lies, deceiver
**Usage:** The standard agentive nominal for a habitual liar. Used in moral condemnation and in legal contexts to characterize a witness or party as unreliable. Appears in proverbs and everyday speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** irọ́ pátápátá
**Transliteration:** irọ́ pátápátá
**Literal Breakdown:** *irọ́* (lie) + *pátápátá* (completely, entirely, totally); "complete/total lie"
**Natural English Translation:** complete lie, total falsehood, outright fabrication
**Usage:** Used to intensify the evaluation of a statement as entirely false, with no element of truth. The adverbial *pátápátá* is a productive intensifier in Yoruba. Appears in everyday and legal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** irọ́ funfun
**Transliteration:** irọ́ funfun
**Literal Breakdown:** *irọ́* (lie) + *funfun* (white, clean, pure); "white lie, clean lie"
**Natural English Translation:** white lie, harmless lie, minor falsehood
**Usage:** Used in everyday speech to distinguish a minor or socially motivated falsehood from a serious or malicious lie. The collocation is attested in contemporary Yoruba usage, including in media and informal discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**21.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** irọ́ tótóbi
**Transliteration:** irọ́ tótóbi
**Literal Breakdown:** *irọ́* (lie) + *tó* (that, which) + *tóbi* (is big, is great); "a big/great lie"
**Natural English Translation:** a great lie, a serious falsehood, a major deception
**Usage:** Used in everyday and legal speech to characterize a falsehood as particularly serious or consequential. Appears in moral condemnation and in legal argument about the gravity of perjury.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Base Form:** irọ́
**Derived Form:** ènìyàn irọ́
**Transliteration:** ènìyàn irọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ènìyàn* (person) + *irọ́* (lie, falsehood)
**Natural English Translation:** a lying person, a liar
**Usage:** Attributive nominal construction used in character evaluation to describe someone who habitually tells lies; appears in social and moral condemnation contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on *irọ́*:** The root does not appear to generate a large paradigm of prefixed nominalizations in the way that *tọ́* or *mọ̀* do. The primary derivational products are agentive forms (the liar) and intensified forms. No affixal derivation from *irọ́* as a morphological base is identified beyond the forms above. No causative or other derivational forms are identified with confidence.

### Base: **òdodo** (justice, righteousness, fairness)

---

**23.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** alòdodo
**Transliteration:** alòdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *òdodo* (justice/righteousness); "one who possesses/practices justice"
**Natural English Translation:** just person, righteous person, one who acts with fairness
**Usage:** Used to describe a person characterized by just and righteous conduct. Appears in moral praise and in assessments of judges, leaders, and community figures.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** àìdodo
**Transliteration:** àìdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (negating prefix) + *ì-* (nominalizing) + *dodo* (righteousness/justice root); "absence of righteousness/justice"
**Natural English Translation:** injustice, unrighteousness, unfairness, lack of fairness
**Usage:** Used in moral and legal discourse to refer to the absence or violation of justice. Appears in condemnation of unjust conduct or rulings. Functions as the negated counterpart of *òdodo*.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**25.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** ìdájọ́ òdodo
**Transliteration:** ìdájọ́ òdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìdájọ́* (judgment, verdict) + *òdodo* (justice, righteousness); "righteous judgment"
**Natural English Translation:** just judgment, righteous verdict, fair ruling
**Usage:** Used in legal and religious discourse to evaluate a judgment as conforming to the standard of justice and righteousness. Appears in court contexts and in religious discourse about divine judgment.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** ìṣe òdodo
**Transliteration:** ìṣe òdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìṣe* (action, deed, doing) + *òdodo* (justice, righteousness); "doing of righteousness/justice"
**Natural English Translation:** righteous action, just conduct, doing what is right
**Usage:** Used in moral and religious discourse to refer to the practice of justice or righteousness. Appears in Christian and Islamic Yoruba discourse and in everyday moral evaluation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Base Form:** òdodo
**Derived Form:** aláàdodo
**Transliteration:** aláàdodo
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (agentive) + *àà-* (intensifier/possessive) + *dodo* / *òdodo*; "one who possesses/embodies righteousness"
**Natural English Translation:** righteous person, just person, one who embodies justice
**Usage:** Used in moral commendation to describe a person who consistently acts with justice and righteousness. Appears in religious and ethical discourse.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on *òdodo*:** The reduplication in *òdodo* itself is already a morphological feature of the base form. The morphological base of *òdodo* is debated, and this limits confident identification of further derivations. Further derivational productivity beyond agentive and negated forms is not well-attested in available data.

### Base: **ìmọ̀** (knowledge, understanding, awareness)

---

**28.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** mọ̀
**Transliteration:** mọ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** Root verb; to know, to be aware of, to recognize
**Natural English Translation:** to know, to be aware of, to recognize
**Usage:** The verbal base from which *ìmọ̀* is derived. Used in all registers to express the state of knowing. Relevant to truth evaluation as the basic predicate of epistemic access.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** amọ̀na
**Transliteration:** amọ̀na
**Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (person-nominalizer) + *mọ̀* (know) + *na* (the way/path, contracted); "one who knows the way"
**Natural English Translation:** guide, one who knows the way, knowledgeable leader
**Usage:** Used to describe a person who has the knowledge to guide others. Appears in contexts of leadership, navigation (literal and metaphorical), and expertise. The agentive prefix *a-* combines with the verbal root.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀-ẹ̀rọ
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *ẹ̀rọ* (machine/mechanism/device); "knowledge of machines"
**Natural English Translation:** technology, technical knowledge, engineering
**Usage:** The standard Yoruba term for technology. A lexicalized compound in which the *ìmọ̀* element has been incorporated into a domain-specific term. Highly productive as a base for further compounds.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** aláìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** aláìmọ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *al-* (person-prefix) + *àì-* (negation) + *mọ̀* (know); "one who does not know"
**Natural English Translation:** ignorant person, one who lacks knowledge, uninformed person
**Usage:** Used to describe a person characterized by ignorance or lack of knowledge. Appears in moral and educational discourse, sometimes as a mitigating descriptor and sometimes as a criticism, sometimes with a tone of condemnation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** olùkọ́
**Transliteration:** olùkọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *olù-* (person-prefix for agent of action) + *kọ́* (teach/instruct); "one who teaches"

**Note:** This form derives from *kọ́* (to teach) rather than directly from *ìmọ̀*, but is included here because it is the standard agentive counterpart in the knowledge-transmission domain. It is the person who transmits *ìmọ̀*. The derivation is from *kọ́*, not *mọ̀*.

**Natural English Translation:** teacher, instructor, educator
**Usage:** The standard Yoruba term for a teacher or instructor. Used in educational and social contexts to describe the person responsible for transmitting knowledge.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**

**Natural English Translation:** knowledgeable person, one who knows, expert
**Usage:** Used to refer to a person with knowledge or expertise in a domain. Appears in everyday speech and in contexts where the credibility of a source is being evaluated.
