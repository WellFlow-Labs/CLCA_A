# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

---

### BASE: kweli (truth - adverbial/adjectival)

#### Derived Form 1: ukweli
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Derived Form:** ukweli
- **Transliteration:** ukweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** u- (noun class 11/14 abstract prefix) + -kweli (truth root)
- **Translation:** truth, reality, fact (as abstract noun)
- **Usage:** Nominalizes the truth concept into an abstract noun referring to the state or quality of being true. This is the standard abstract noun form used in formal and philosophical discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: -a kweli
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Derived Form:** -a kweli
- **Transliteration:** -a kweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** -a (associative marker) + kweli (truth)
- **Translation:** of truth, true, genuine (adjectival phrase)
- **Usage:** Creates an adjectival phrase through the associative construction. Modifies nouns to indicate genuineness or truthfulness (e.g., "mtu wa kweli" - genuine person).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 3: -enye ukweli
- **Base Form:** ukweli (derived from kweli)
- **Derived Form:** -enye ukweli
- **Transliteration:** -enye ukweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** -enye (possessive/having) + ukweli (truth)
- **Translation:** having truth, truthful, veracious
- **Usage:** Creates an adjectival construction indicating possession of the quality of truthfulness. Used attributively to describe persons or statements.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 4: kwa kweli
- **Base Form:** kweli
- **Derived Form:** kwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** kwa kweli
- **Literal Breakdown:** kwa (instrumental/manner preposition) + kweli (truth)
- **Translation:** truly, really, indeed, in truth
- **Usage:** Forms an adverbial phrase through prepositional construction. Extremely productive and common in everyday speech for emphasis and affirmation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

### BASE: ukweli (truth - abstract noun)

#### Note on further derivation:
The abstract noun "ukweli" itself is already a derivation from "kweli" (see above). It participates in numerous phrasal constructions (documented in P1b) but does not undergo further morphological derivation. The associative construction "-a kweli" derives from the base "kweli" rather than from "ukweli."

### BASE: uongo (lie, falsehood - abstract noun)

#### Derived Form 1: mwongo
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Derived Form:** mwongo
- **Transliteration:** mwongo
- **Literal Breakdown:** m- (noun class 1 person prefix) + -ongo (lie root)
- **Translation:** liar, one who lies
- **Usage:** Derives a personal noun from the abstract noun/root, referring to a person characterized by lying. Strong moral condemnation in most contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 2: waongo
- **Base Form:** mwongo
- **Derived Form:** waongo
- **Transliteration:** waongo
- **Literal Breakdown:** wa- (noun class 2 plural person prefix) + -ongo (lie root)
- **Translation:** liars, those who lie
- **Usage:** Plural form of mwongo. Regular noun class agreement pattern.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 3: -a uongo
- **Base Form:** uongo
- **Derived Form:** -a uongo
- **Transliteration:** -a uongo
- **Literal Breakdown:** -a (associative marker) + uongo (lie)
- **Translation:** of falsehood, false, lying, deceptive
- **Usage:** Associative construction creating an adjectival phrase. Used to characterize statements, persons, or representations as false.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

#### Derived Form 4: uongo (verbal noun from -onga)
**Note:** The relationship between "uongo" (noun) and the verb "-onga" (to lie, deceive) is complex. While "uongo" can be analyzed as u- + -ongo (verbal root), the verb "-onga" itself is independently attested:

- **Base Form:** -onga (verb root)
- **Derived Form:** uongo
- **Transliteration:** uongo
- **Literal Breakdown:** u- (noun class 11/14 abstract prefix) + -ongo (lie/deceive root)
- **Translation:** lying, falsehood, deception (verbal noun)
- **Usage:** This represents the deverbal noun formation from the verb "-onga." The directionality of derivation (verb→noun vs. noun→verb) is historically complex.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productivity Patterns Observed:

1. **Abstract nominalization with u-:** Highly productive across Arabic-derived adjectives and some verbs (ukweli, usahihi, uhalisia, uhakika, ukamili, uadilifu, usafi, etc.). This is a core Swahili derivational pattern.

2. **Causative formation with -ish-/-sh-:** Very productive for creating verbs meaning "to make X" or "to cause to be X" (sahihisha, hakikisha, kamilisha, safisha, thibitisha, etc.). Regular passive forms with -wa are consistently attested.

3. **Stative/neuter formation with -ik-:** Attested for some bases (kamilika, danganyika) but not universally productive.

4. **Agentive/quality suffix -ifu:** Productive for creating person nouns and abstract quality nouns from verbs (mdanganyifu/udanganyifu, uadilifu).

5. **Associative construction -a + noun:** Productive for creating adjectival phrases, though often less common than direct adjectival use of the base form.

6. **Person prefix derivation (m-/wa-):** Productive for creating agent nouns from roots (mwongo/waongo, mhukumu/wahukumu).

### Asymmetries and Gaps:

- Some bases show extensive derivational possibilities (e.g., -danganya, kamili, hakika) while others are more limited (e.g., madhubuti, haki).
- Arabic-derived forms show varying degrees of integration into Swahili derivational morphology.
- Some expected forms may exist but are not commonly used or are replaced by alternative constructions.
- Verbal bases show richer derivational possibilities than nominal or adjectival bases.
