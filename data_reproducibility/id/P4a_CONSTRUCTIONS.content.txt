# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] + [TRUTH-TERM]**
Simple juxtaposition of noun phrase and truth-domain adjective/stative verb. No copula required in present tense. Optional copula "adalah" in formal register.

### Examples
- **cerita itu benar**
  cerita itu benar
  "that story is true"

- **dokumen ini palsu**
  dokumen ini palsu
  "this document is fake"

### Notes
Indonesian allows zero copula for predicate adjectives. The truth-term directly follows the subject NP. Demonstratives (itu, ini) commonly appear with definite subjects.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**[NP] + tidak + [TRUTH-TERM]**
Standard negation with "tidak" preceding the truth-term predicate.

### Examples
- **laporan itu tidak akurat**
  laporan itu tidak akurat
  "that report is not accurate"

- **dia tidak jujur**
  dia tidak jujur
  "he/she is not honest"

### Notes
"Tidak" is the standard negator for adjectives and stative verbs. Alternative negators include "bukan" (for nominal predicates) and "belum" (not yet), but "tidak" is default for truth-domain terms.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-TERM] + [NOUN]**
Truth-term precedes the noun it modifies without intervening particle. Relative clause alternative: **[NOUN] + yang + [TRUTH-TERM]**.

### Examples
- **berita palsu**
  berita palsu
  "fake news"

- **orang jujur**
  orang jujur
  "honest person"

### Notes
Direct prenominal modification is standard. Post-nominal modification with relativizer "yang" is also common (e.g., "orang yang jujur"). No agreement marking.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**menjadi + [TRUTH-TERM]**
Inchoative verb "menjadi" (become) + truth-term indicates change of state.

### Examples
- **cerita itu menjadi nyata**
  cerita itu menjadi nyata
  "that story became real"

- **dokumen menjadi sah**
  dokumen menjadi sah
  "the document became valid"

### Notes
"Menjadi" is the standard inchoative/copular verb for change of state. Alternative constructions include "menjadi lebih [TRUTH-TERM]" (become more X) for gradable terms.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**meN- + [ROOT] + -kan**
Causative prefix meN- (with nasal assimilation) + root + suffix -kan. Transitive construction: **[AGENT] + meN-ROOT-kan + [PATIENT]**.

### Examples
- **dia membenarkan laporan itu**
  dia membenarkan laporan itu
  "he/she confirmed that report"

- **pemerintah mengesahkan undang-undang**
  pemerintah mengesahkan undang-undang
  "the government ratified the law"

### Notes
The meN-...-kan causative is highly productive for truth-domain terms. Semantic range includes: make X true/valid, confirm as X, declare X. Not all roots support this derivation (e.g., *memiripi is unattested).

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**ke- + [ROOT] + -an**
Circumfix ke-...-an derives abstract quality nouns from truth-domain roots.

### Examples
- **kebenaran itu penting**
  kebenaran itu penting
  "truth/honesty is important"

- **keaslian produk diragukan**
  keaslian produk diragukan
  "the authenticity of the product is doubted"

### Notes
Nearly universal pattern for deriving abstract nouns denoting qualities or states. The derived noun can function as subject, object, or in possessive constructions. Highly productive across all truth-domain terms.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**peN- + [ROOT]** (for agent nouns)
Prefix peN- (with nasal assimilation) derives agent nouns. Primarily productive with deception/error roots.

### Examples
- **pembohong itu tertangkap**
  pembohong itu tertangkap
  "the liar was caught"

- **pemalsu dokumen dihukum**
  pemalsu dokumen dihukum
  "the document forger was punished"

### Notes
Agent nominalization is productive primarily for negative/deceptive terms (pembohong "liar," pemalsu "forger," pendusta "deceiver"). Positive counterparts are rare or use different strategies (e.g., "orang jujur" = honest person, not *penjujur). Asymmetry reflects focus on marking deviant agents.

---

## 8. INTENSIFICATION

### Pattern
**[ROOT] + REDUPLICATION** or **[ROOT] + sekali/banget**
Reduplication or intensifying adverbs strengthen the evaluation.

### Examples
- **benar-benar penting**
  benar-benar penting
  "really important"

- **mirip sekali dengan ayahnya**
  mirip sekali dengan ayahnya
  "very similar to his father"

### Notes
Reduplication (benar-benar, sungguh-sungguh) creates adverbial intensifiers. "Sekali" (very) and colloquial "banget" (really) follow the truth-term. Reduplication is highly productive and often shifts meaning toward emphasis rather than truth-evaluation.

---

## 9. HEDGING/MITIGATION

### Pattern
**kurang + [TRUTH-TERM]**
"Kurang" (less, lacking) creates softened negative evaluation.

### Examples
- **jawaban itu kurang tepat**
  jawaban itu kurang tepat
  "that answer is not quite right"

- **tindakan kurang pantas**
  tindakan kurang pantas
  "somewhat inappropriate action"

### Notes
"Kurang" provides polite or hedged negation, suggesting partial rather than absolute failure to meet standard. Common in formal discourse and polite disagreement. More diplomatic than direct "tidak."

---

## 10. EVIDENTIAL/DISCOVERY

### Pattern
**ter- + [ROOT]**
Prefix ter- marks accidental, resultative, or discovered states.

### Examples
- **ternyata dia benar**
  ternyata dia benar
  "it turns out he/she was right"

- **tersalah ambil barang**
  tersalah ambil barang
  "took the wrong item by mistake"

### Notes
"Ternyata" (from ter-+nyata) has grammaticalized as evidential marker meaning "it turns out, apparently." Other ter- forms indicate accidental or unintentional states (tersalah, terkeliru). Limited productivity but semantically important for marking epistemic discovery.

---

## 11. CONTRASTIVE PAIRING

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-TERM₁] + atau + [TRUTH-TERM₂]**
Disjunctive coordination presents binary alternatives for evaluation.

### Examples
- **benar atau salah**
  benar atau salah
  "true or false"

- **asli atau palsu**
  asli atau palsu
  "genuine or fake"

### Notes
Contrastive pairs are formulaic in questions, tests, and evaluative contexts. Common pairs: benar/salah, asli/palsu, sah/batal, jujur/bohong. The construction frames evaluation as binary choice.

---

## 12. PASSIVE VOICE

### Pattern
**di- + [ROOT] + (-kan)**
Passive prefix di- (+ optional -kan suffix) with agent optionally marked by "oleh."

### Examples
- **dokumen itu dipalsukan**
  dokumen itu dipalsukan
  "that document was forged"

- **laporan disahkan oleh pemerintah**
  laporan disahkan oleh pemerintah
  "the report was validated by the government"

### Notes
Passive voice is extremely common in Indonesian, often preferred over active. The di- prefix attaches to verbal forms (especially meN-...-kan causatives). Agent phrases with "oleh" are optional and often omitted.

---

## 13. COMPARATIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**lebih + [TRUTH-TERM] + (daripada [STANDARD])**
"Lebih" (more) + truth-term creates comparative. Standard of comparison introduced by "daripada."

### Examples
- **lebih akurat daripada sebelumnya**
  lebih akurat daripada sebelumnya
  "more accurate than before"

- **lebih mirip dengan ibunya**
  lebih mirip dengan ibunya
  "more similar to his/her mother"

### Notes
Analytic comparative with "lebih" is standard for gradable terms. Not all truth-domain terms are gradable (e.g., *lebih asli is marginal). Superlative uses "paling" (most) or "ter-" prefix.

---

## 14. DISCOURSE MARKER FUNCTION

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-TERM] + saja/nya**
Truth-terms with particles "saja" or "-nya" function as discourse markers introducing corrections or frank statements.

### Examples
- **jujur saja, saya tidak setuju**
  jujur saja, saya tidak setuju
  "to be honest, I don't agree"

- **sebenarnya dia tidak datang**
  sebenarnya dia tidak datang
  "actually he/she didn't come"

### Notes
Several truth-terms have grammaticalized into discourse markers: jujur saja (to be honest), sebenarnya/sebetulnya (actually), sesungguhnya (truly). These frame epistemic stance or introduce corrections rather than directly evaluating truth.

---

## 15. RECIPROCAL/COLLECTIVE CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
**se- + [ROOT]** or **ber- + [ROOT] + -an**
Prefix se- creates reciprocal/collective meaning; ber-...-an marks mutual action.

### Examples
- **sesama manusia harus jujur**
  sesama manusia harus jujur
  "fellow humans must be honest"

- **bertepatan dengan hari raya**
  bertepatan dengan hari raya
  "coinciding with the holiday"

### Notes
"Sesama" (from se-+sama) means "fellow, mutual, one another." "Bertepatan" indicates temporal coincidence. These constructions are limited to specific roots (primarily "sama" and "tepat") and show semantic specialization beyond simple truth-evaluation.

---

## 16. FIXED IDIOMATIC EXPRESSIONS

### Pattern
**[TRUTH-TERM] + [FIXED NOUN/VERB]**
Lexicalized collocations with specialized meanings.

### Examples
- **salah kaprah**
  salah kaprah
  "common misconception" (lit. wrong-widespread)

- **cinta sejati**
  cinta sejati
  "true love"

### Notes
Numerous fixed expressions show semantic drift from compositional meaning: salah kaprah (widespread error), salah tingkah (awkward/embarrassed), salah satu (one of). These require lexical listing as their meanings are not fully predictable from components.
