# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 NATURALNESS (Change, Causation, and Stability)
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / CORRECT

### Pattern
**Subject + נעשה/הפך ל- + truth-related adjective/noun**
Inchoative constructions indicating transition to truth/correctness.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Expression:** הדבר נעשה ברור
- **Transliteration:** hadavar na'ase barur
- **Translation:** "the thing became clear"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common construction. נעשה (nif'al of עשה "do/make") functions as inchoative "became." Used when something unclear becomes evident. Frequent in explanatory and narrative contexts.

#### Example 2
- **Expression:** זה הפך לאמת
- **Transliteration:** ze hafakh le'emet
- **Translation:** "this became truth / this turned into truth"
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]
- **Notes:** Less common than other constructions. הפך ל- "turned into" is explicit change-of-state marker. More natural to say זה התברר שזה אמת "it became clear that this is true" than to predicate "becoming truth" directly.

#### Example 3
- **Expression:** התברר שזה נכון
- **Transliteration:** hitbarer sheze nakhon
- **Translation:** "it became clear that this is correct"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Extremely common construction. התברר (hitpa'el of ברר "clarify") is the standard verb for "it became clear/evident." Takes complement clause with ש-. Used constantly in narrative and explanatory discourse.

#### Example 4
- **Expression:** הסיפור התברר כאמיתי
- **Transliteration:** hasipur hitbarer ke'amiti
- **Translation:** "the story turned out to be true"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction with התברר + כ- "as." Used when something is revealed or proven to be true. Frequent in investigative and narrative contexts.

#### Example 5
- **Expression:** הדברים התבררו
- **Transliteration:** hadvarim hitbareru
- **Translation:** "things became clear / matters were clarified"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common intransitive use of התברר. Used when situation or facts become clear without specifying what exactly became clear. Frequent in narrative conclusions.

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / INCORRECT

### Pattern
**Subject + נעשה/הפך ל- + falsity-related adjective/noun**
Inchoative constructions indicating transition to falsity/incorrectness.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Expression:** זה הפך לשקר
- **Transliteration:** ze hafakh lesheker
- **Translation:** "this became a lie / this turned into a lie"
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]
- **Notes:** Uncommon construction. More natural to say זה התברר כשקר "it turned out to be a lie" or זה הפך להיות שקר "this became (a) lie." Direct predication of "becoming falsehood" is marked.

#### Example 2
- **Expression:** התברר שזה שקר
- **Transliteration:** hitbarer sheze sheker
- **Translation:** "it became clear that this is a lie"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common construction, parallel to התברר שזה נכון. Standard way to express discovery of falsehood. Used constantly in corrections and revelations.

#### Example 3
- **Expression:** התברר שזה לא נכון
- **Transliteration:** hitbarer sheze lo nakhon
- **Translation:** "it became clear that this is not correct"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Extremely common construction. Often preferred over positive assertion of falsehood (שקר). Used in corrections and clarifications across all registers.

#### Example 4
- **Expression:** הטענה התבררה כשקרית
- **Transliteration:** hata'ana hitbarera kashikriת
- **Translation:** "the claim turned out to be false"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction with התברר + כ- + adjective שקרי "false." Used in formal and investigative contexts when claims are disproven.

#### Example 5
- **Expression:** הדבר התברר כלא נכון
- **Transliteration:** hadavar hitbarer kelo nakhon
- **Translation:** "the thing turned out to be incorrect"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common construction. Negation with לא נכון often preferred over positive falsity terms. Used across all registers.

## 8. CAUSING TO BE TRUE / MAKING TRUE

### Pattern
**Agent + causative verb + object + truth-related state**
Causative constructions where agent makes something true or correct.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Expression:** הוא הוכיח את הטענה
- **Transliteration:** hu hokhiaḥ et hata'ana
- **Translation:** "he proved the claim"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common causative construction. הוכיח (hif'il) "proved" = "caused to be proven/true." Used in logical, scientific, and legal contexts. Does not directly predicate "making true" but "demonstrating truth."

#### Example 2
- **Expression:** הוא ביסס את הטענה
- **Transliteration:** hu bises et hata'ana
- **Translation:** "he established/substantiated the claim"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction. ביסס (pi'el) "established/grounded" indicates providing foundation for truth. Used in academic and argumentative contexts.

#### Example 3
- **Expression:** הוא אימת את הדברים
- **Transliteration:** hu imet et hadvarim
- **Translation:** "he verified the things/statements"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction. אימת (pi'el from root א-מ-ת) "verified/confirmed" = "caused to be confirmed as true." Used in verification and validation contexts.

#### Example 4
- **Expression:** הוא תיקן את הטעות
- **Transliteration:** hu tiken et hata'ut
- **Translation:** "he corrected the mistake"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common construction. תיקן "corrected" = "caused to be correct." Used when errors are fixed. Does not use truth-vocabulary directly but achieves truth-related causation.

#### Example 5
- **Expression:** (no common expression for "making something true")
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Translation:** N/A
- **Naturalness:** N/A
- **Notes:** **Hebrew does not have a common direct causative "to make X true" construction.** Truth is typically proven/demonstrated (הוכיח), verified (אימת), or established (ביסס), but not "made true" in the sense of causing truth to come into being. This reflects a realist conception where truth is discovered/demonstrated rather than created.

- **Transliteration:** hu hikhish et ha'uvdot
- **Translation:** "he denied the facts"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction. הכחיש (hif'il) "denied" = "caused to be treated as false." Used when facts are rejected or contradicted.

## 10. CAUSING TO BELIEVE / MAKING BELIEVE

### Pattern
**Agent + causative verb + object + belief state**
Causative constructions where agent causes someone to believe.

### Examples

#### Example 1
- **Expression:** הוא שכנע אותי
- **Transliteration:** hu shikhne'a oti
- **Translation:** "he convinced me"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common causative construction. שכנע (pi'el) "convinced" = "caused to believe/accept." Used across all contexts. Can take complement clause: שכנע אותי ש- "convinced me that..."

#### Example 2
- **Expression:** הוא גרם לי להאמין
- **Transliteration:** hu garam li leha'amin
- **Translation:** "he caused me to believe"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common analytic causative. גרם ל- "caused to" + infinitive להאמין "to believe." More explicit causation than שכנע. Used when causation is emphasized.

#### Example 3
- **Expression:** הוא האמין לי
- **Transliteration:** hu he'emin li
- **Translation:** "he believed me / he trusted me"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction but **NOT causative**. האמין ל- "believed (someone)" is intransitive belief, not causing belief. The hif'il form here is not causative but indicates belief-relation. Important distinction.

#### Example 4
- **Expression:** הוא הטעה אותי
- **Transliteration:** hu hit'a oti
- **Translation:** "he misled me / he deceived me"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Very common causative construction. הטעה (hif'il) "misled" = "caused to believe falsely." Used when deception causes false belief. Common in all registers.

#### Example 5
- **Expression:** הוא שידל אותי
- **Transliteration:** hu shidel oti
- **Translation:** "he persuaded me"
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]
- **Notes:** Common construction. שידל (pi'el) "persuaded" = "caused to be persuaded/convinced." Similar to שכנע but often implies more effort or manipulation. Used across registers.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Patterns Identified

**Total subsections:** 30

**Highly productive patterns:**
1. **התברר constructions** (sections 1, 2, 6, 7, 24, 25): Extremely common for "becoming clear/evident" with various complements
2. **Causative hif'il verbs** (sections 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15): Standard morphological causatives
3. **Loss constructions with איבד** (sections 5, 17, 22, 23, 25, 29): Very productive for expressing loss of truth-related states
4. **Revelation/concealment verbs** (sections 18, 19): Consistent metaphorical pattern treating truth as hidden object
5. **Maintenance verbs** (sections 16, 28): Common constructions for preserving truth-related states

### Key Findings

1. **Hebrew strongly prefers התברר "it became clear" over direct "becoming true" constructions.** Truth is typically revealed/clarified rather than created or made true.

2. **No common direct causative "to make X true" exists.** Truth is proven (הוכיח), verified (אימת), or established (ביסס), but not "made true" in the sense of causing truth to come into being.

3. **Morphological causatives (hif'il, pi'el) are highly productive** for causing clarity, belief, doubt, and other epistemic states.

4. **Metaphorical consistency:** Truth is systematically conceptualized as:
   - Hidden object (revealed, concealed, uncovered)
   - Path (deviated from, adhered to, pursued)
   - Possession (lost, maintained, kept)
   - Building (established, undermined, fortified)

5. **Gradual change is commonly expressed** through הלך ו- "went and" + verb construction, indicating progressive processes.

6. **Negation patterns are highly productive:** Most positive constructions have natural negated counterparts (נכון/לא נכון, אמין/לא אמין, etc.).

7. **Register variation:** Some constructions are formal/literary (ביקש את האמת, העלים את האמת) while others are colloquial (חיפש את האמת, הסתיר את האמת).

8. **Binyan system crucial:** Voice alternations (active/passive) and causative formations through binyan patterns are central to expressing change and causation in truth domain.

### Constructions NOT Found

1. **Direct "making true" causative** - Hebrew lacks a common expression parallel to English "make X true"
2. **"Becoming a truth" constructions** - Truth is not typically predicated as something one becomes
3. **Synthetic inchoatives** - Hebrew uses analytic constructions (נעשה, הפך ל-) rather than synthetic inchoative morphology
4. **Truth as agent** - While "truth came to light" exists, truth rarely appears as active agent in Hebrew constructions
