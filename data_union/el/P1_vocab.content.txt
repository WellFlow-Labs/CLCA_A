# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (privative) + λήθη (concealment, forgetting); "unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, veridical
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements, propositions, claims, and persons to indicate correspondence with reality or fact. Appears in both everyday and philosophical registers. Participates in a productive derivational family (αλήθεια, αληθινός, αληθεύω).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Literal meaning:** nominal form of αληθής; "the state of being unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality
**Usage:** The primary noun for truth in Greek, used across everyday, legal, philosophical, and religious contexts. Can function as an exclamation ("αλήθεια;" = "Really?") and as a subject of predication ("η αλήθεια είναι ότι…"). Frequently appears in fixed expressions such as λέω την αλήθεια ("I tell the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Literal meaning:** adjectival extension of αλήθεια / αληθής; "of the nature of truth"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Applied to persons, objects, feelings, and states of affairs to indicate that they are what they purport to be, as opposed to false or simulated. Often used to distinguish something genuine from a copy, imitation, or false version (e.g., αληθινός φίλος, "a true/genuine friend"). Slightly more colloquial than αληθής and more commonly used in modern spoken Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** γνήσιος
**Transliteration:** gníssios
**Literal meaning:** from γένος (birth, kind); "of legitimate birth," hence "genuine"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, legitimate, true, pure
**Usage:** Used of objects, documents, persons, and relationships to indicate authenticity or legitimacy of origin. Contrasts with νόθος (spurious, illegitimate). Common in legal contexts (e.g., γνήσιο αντίγραφο, "certified/genuine copy") and in evaluating artworks or products (e.g., γνήσιο προϊόν, "genuine product").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** αυθεντικός
**Transliteration:** afthentikós
**Literal meaning:** from αυθέντης (one who acts on their own authority); "authoritative, original"
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine, original
**Usage:** Used in aesthetic, legal, and everyday contexts to indicate that something is original rather than a copy or forgery. Also applied to persons to mean that they behave in a manner consistent with their true character, or to indicate sincerity or lack of pretense. Widely used in cultural, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ακριβής
**Transliteration:** akrivís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (intensive) + κρίβανος or related to ἄκρος / ἀκριβόω; "exact, precise"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, correct
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, descriptions, translations, and accounts to indicate that they correspond closely to what they represent or to the facts. Commonly used in scientific, journalistic, technical, and everyday evaluative contexts. Ακριβής μετάφραση means "accurate translation."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ορθός
**Transliteration:** orthós
**Literal meaning:** "straight, upright, correct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, orthodox
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, procedures, beliefs, and conduct as conforming to a standard or norm. Appears in more formal, technical, and philosophical registers. Appears in compounds such as ορθογραφία (correct spelling/writing) and ορθόδοξος (right-believing/of correct opinion). In moral contexts it can indicate rightness of action.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** σωστός
**Transliteration:** sostós
**Literal meaning:** from σώζω (to save, preserve); "preserved intact," hence "correct, sound"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, accurate, sound
**Usage:** The most common colloquial term for "correct" in modern Greek, used of answers, behavior, persons, procedures, and objects. Overlaps with ορθός but is more frequent in informal registers. Η σωστή απάντηση means "the correct answer."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** λανθασμένος
**Transliteration:** lanthazmenos
**Literal meaning:** past participle of λανθάνομαι (to be mistaken, to err)
**Typical English translations:** wrong, mistaken, erroneous, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to beliefs, statements, calculations, and actions to indicate that they are in error. Common in everyday and educational contexts as the standard antonym of σωστός.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** έγκυρος
**Transliteration:** éngiros
**Literal meaning:** from ἐν- (in) + κῦρος (authority, validity); "having authority"
**Typical English translations:** valid, authoritative, reliable, credible, legitimate
**Usage:** Used in legal, administrative, and epistemic contexts to indicate that a document, argument, decision, or source carries recognized authority or meets formal requirements. A passport, contract, or election may be described as έγκυρος. Contrasts with άκυρος ("invalid, null"). Έγκυρο συμβόλαιο means "valid contract."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** νόμιμος
**Transliteration:** nómimos
**Literal meaning:** from νόμος (law); "according to law"
**Typical English translations:** legal, lawful, legitimate
**Usage:** Indicates conformity with legal norms or established rules. Applied to persons, actions, and documents. Contrasts with παράνομος (illegal).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** αξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** aksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + πίστις (trust, faith); "worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, and testimonies to indicate that they merit confidence and can be trusted or believed. Frequently used in journalistic and legal contexts when evaluating the credibility of witnesses or information sources. Αξιόπιστη πηγή means "reliable source."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** αναξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** anaksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** privative ἀν- + αξιόπιστος; "not worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** unreliable, untrustworthy, incredible
**Usage:** The direct negation of αξιόπιστος, applied to persons, sources, and claims. Used in evaluative contexts where credibility is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** αξιόλογος
**Transliteration:** aksiólogos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + λόγος (word, account, reason); "worthy of account"
**Typical English translations:** noteworthy, significant, considerable, creditable, of merit
**Usage:** Used to evaluate persons, achievements, or works as deserving recognition or serious consideration. Applied to persons, works, or achievements to indicate that they are genuinely worthy of attention or merit. Carries a positive evaluative force and appears in both formal and informal registers, as well as academic and critical registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** νόθος
**Transliteration:** nóthos
**Literal meaning:** "of illegitimate birth," hence "spurious, counterfeit"
**Typical English translations:** spurious, illegitimate, counterfeit, false, adulterated
**Usage:** Historically used of illegitimate children; extended to texts, documents, and objects to indicate that they are not what they claim to be. Used in philological and legal contexts (e.g., a νόθο κείμενο is a text of doubtful authenticity). In textual criticism, applied to works falsely attributed to an author.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** πλαστός
**Transliteration:** plastós
**Literal meaning:** from πλάσσω / πλάθω (to mold, fabricate); "fabricated, molded"
**Typical English translations:** forged, counterfeit, fake, fabricated
**Usage:** Applied to documents, signatures, currency, and identities to indicate deliberate falsification. Implies deliberate fabrication. Πλαστή ταυτότητα is a forged identity document; πλαστό διαβατήριο means "forged passport." Commonly used in legal and criminal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ψεύτικος
**Transliteration:** psévtikos / pséftikos
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύτης (liar) / ψεύδος (falsehood, lie); "of the nature of a lie, false"
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, lying
**Usage:** A common colloquial adjective applied to objects, persons, and statements to indicate that they are not genuine or truthful. Less formal than πλαστός and broader in application. Contrasts with αληθινός and γνήσιος in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ψευδής
**Transliteration:** psevdís
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύδος (falsehood); "characterized by falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** false, untrue, mendacious, lying, spurious
**Usage:** More formal than ψεύτικος; used of statements, testimony, documents, and representations in legal and philosophical contexts to indicate falsity or deceptiveness. A ψευδής κατάθεση is false testimony. Participates in compounds such as ψευδώνυμο (pseudonym).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** ψεύδος
**Transliteration:** psévdos
**Literal meaning:** "falsehood, lie"
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth
**Usage:** The primary noun for a falsehood or lie, used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts. Participates in a large derivational family: ψεύτης (liar), ψεύδομαι (to lie), ψευδής (false). Also appears in the compound ψευδώνυμο and in compounds such as ψευδοεπιστήμη ("pseudoscience"). In everyday speech the plural ψέματα is the colloquial form (λέω ψέματα, "I tell lies").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** αμφίβολος
**Transliteration:** amfívolos
**Literal meaning:** ἀμφί (on both sides) + βάλλω (to throw); "thrown both ways, doubtful"
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, dubious, questionable, ambiguous
**Usage:** Applied to persons, situations, claims, and cases whose reliability, truth, or quality is in question or uncertain. An αμφίβολη υπόθεση is a dubious case or affair. Used in both everyday and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** αμφισβητήσιμος
**Transliteration:** amfisvitiísimos
**Literal meaning:** from αμφισβητώ (to dispute, contest); "disputable, contestable"
**Typical English translations:** disputable, contestable, questionable, debatable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, titles, rights, and facts that are open to challenge or have not been definitively established. More formal than αμφίβολος and more specifically tied to the possibility of active contestation. Frequently used in legal and academic contexts to flag that a proposition or right is not settled.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** αμφισβητώ
**Transliteration:** amfisviтó
**Literal meaning:** ἀμφί (on both sides) + σβητός; conventionally "to dispute, contest"
**Typical English translations:** to dispute, to contest, to challenge, to question
**Usage:** A verb used to indicate that a claim, right, or fact is being actively challenged. Used in legal proceedings (αμφισβητώ μια κατάθεση) and in everyday argument. Productive derivational base for αμφισβήτηση, αμφισβητήσιμος.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** αδιαμφισβήτητος
**Transliteration:** adiamfisvitiitos
**Literal meaning:** ἀ- (privative) + διαμφισβητέω (to dispute thoroughly); "not disputable"
**Typical English translations:** indisputable, unquestionable, undeniable
**Usage:** Applied to facts, rights, and truths that are held to be beyond reasonable challenge. Used for emphasis in legal, rhetorical, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** αποδεδειγμένος
**Transliteration:** apodedeignménos
**Literal meaning:** perfect passive participle of ἀποδείκνυμι (to demonstrate, prove); "having been demonstrated"
**Typical English translations:** proven, demonstrated, established
**Usage:** Applied to facts, claims, and propositions that have been shown to be true through evidence or argument. Used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** τεκμηριωμένος
**Transliteration:** tekmirio̱ménos
**Literal meaning:** from τεκμήριον (evidence, proof); "supported by evidence"
**Typical English translations:** documented, evidenced, substantiated
**Usage:** Applied to claims, arguments, and positions that are backed by concrete evidence or documentation. Common in journalistic, academic, and legal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** επαληθεύω
**Transliteration:** epalithévo
**Literal meaning:** ἐπί (upon, toward) + αληθεύω (to be true); "to verify, confirm as true"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to validate, to corroborate
**Usage:** Used in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts to indicate the process of checking whether a claim, result, or document is accurate or genuine. The noun επαλήθευση is used for the act of verification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** επαλήθευση
**Transliteration:** epalíthefsi
**Literal meaning:** nominal form of επαληθεύω; "the act of verifying"
**Typical English translations:** verification, confirmation, validation
**Usage:** Used in scientific, legal, and administrative contexts to refer to the process or result of checking accuracy or truth. Appears in formal documents and procedures.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** διαψεύδω
**Transliteration:** diapsévdo
**Literal meaning:** διά (through, thoroughly) + ψεύδω (to deceive, to falsify); "to refute, to give the lie to"
**Typical English translations:** to refute, to deny, to disprove, to contradict
**Usage:** Used when a claim, rumor, or allegation is officially or demonstrably shown to be false. Common in journalistic and official contexts (e.g., η κυβέρνηση διέψευσε τις φήμες, "the government denied/refuted the rumors").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** διαπίστωση
**Transliteration:** diapístoси
**Literal meaning:** from διαπιστώνω (to ascertain, establish); "the act of ascertaining"
**Typical English translations:** finding, ascertainment, determination, observation
**Usage:** Used to refer to the result of an investigation or observation that establishes a fact. Common in official reports, medical contexts, and everyday speech when someone states what they have found to be the case.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** τεκμήριο
**Transliteration:** tekmírio
**Literal meaning:** from τεκμαίρομαι (to infer, judge by signs); "sign, proof, evidence"
**Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, indication, token
**Usage:** Used in legal and argumentative contexts to refer to a piece of evidence or an indicator that supports a conclusion. A τεκμήριο is typically indirect evidence from which a fact is inferred, distinguishing it from direct proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** απόδειξη
**Transliteration:** apódhiksi
**Literal meaning:** from αποδεικνύω (to demonstrate, prove); "demonstration, proof"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, receipt, demonstration
**Usage:** Used in logical, legal, and everyday contexts to refer to something that proves or demonstrates a claim. In commercial contexts it also means "receipt." The dual usage reflects the general sense of something that establishes a fact.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**32.**
**Form:** εικονικός
**Transliteration:** eikonikós / ikonikós
**Literal meaning:** from εικόνα / εἰκών (image, icon); "of or like an image"
**Typical English translations:** simulated, virtual, nominal, figurative; also: iconic
**Usage:** In modern usage, applied to simulated or nominal states of affairs (e.g., εικονική πραγματικότητα = virtual reality). Can also mean "nominal" or "token" in legal and financial contexts, indicating something that resembles but is not the real thing. In legal contexts, an εικονική πράξη is a sham transaction. The evaluative force is that something appears to be what it is not.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**33.**
**Form:** φαινομενικός
**Transliteration:** fenomenikós
**Literal meaning:** from φαινόμενον (that which appears); "of or pertaining to appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible
**Usage:** Applied to qualities or states that appear to be the case but may not correspond to underlying reality. Φαινομενική επιτυχία means "apparent success."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**34.**
**Form:** ουσιαστικός
**Transliteration:** usiástikos / ussiastikós
**Literal meaning:** from οὐσία (essence, substance, being); "of or pertaining to essence"
**Typical English translations:** essential, substantive, real, fundamental, material
**Usage:** Used to distinguish what is genuinely or fundamentally the case from what is merely formal or superficial. In legal contexts, ουσιαστικό δίκαιο means "substantive law" as opposed to procedural law. Also used in everyday speech to mean "real" or "meaningful." Ουσιαστική διαφορά means "a substantive difference."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**35.**
**Form:** ουσία
**Transliteration:** ussía
**Literal meaning:** from εἶναι (to be); "being, essence, substance"
**Typical English translations:** essence, substance, matter, gist
**Usage:** Used in philosophical contexts for the fundamental nature of a thing, and in everyday contexts for the essential point or substance of a matter (e.g., η ουσία του ζητήματος, "the substance/gist of the matter"). Also used in legal contexts for "substance" as opposed to form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**36.**
**Form:** τυπικός
**Transliteration:** tipikós
**Literal meaning:** from τύπος (type, form, stamp); "of or pertaining to form or type"
**Typical English translations:** formal, nominal, typical, perfunctory
**Usage:** Often used in contrast to ουσιαστικός to indicate that something satisfies formal requirements without genuine substance. Τυπική συμμόρφωση means "formal compliance."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**37.**
**Form:** κατ' ουσίαν
**Transliteration:** kat' usían
**Literal meaning:** κατά (according to, in respect of) + οὐσία (essence); "in respect of essence"
**Typical English translations:** essentially, substantially, in substance
**Usage:** A fixed prepositional expression used to indicate that something is true or the case at the level of substance or essence, as opposed to mere form. Common in legal and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**38.**
**Form:** ομοιότητα
**Transliteration:** omióтita
**Literal meaning:** from όμοιος (similar, like); "likeness, resemblance"
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness
**Usage:** Used to describe the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant in aesthetic evaluation (resemblance of a portrait to its subject) and in legal contexts (resemblance of a trademark to another). Participates in evaluative judgments about representation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**39.**
**Form:** αντίγραφο
**Transliteration:** andígrafo
**Literal meaning:** ἀντί (against, in place of) + γράφω (to write); "written in place of"
**Typical English translations:** copy, reproduction, transcript
**Usage:** Used of documents, artworks, and files to indicate that something is a reproduction rather than the original. The distinction between αντίγραφο and πρωτότυπο (original) is central to legal and archival evaluation of authenticity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**40.**
**Form:** πρωτότυπο
**Transliteration:** protótipo
**Literal meaning:** πρῶτος (first) + τύπος (type, impression); "first impression, original"
**Typical English translations:** original, prototype
**Usage:** Used to designate the original version of a document, artwork, or text as opposed to copies or translations. In aesthetic and legal contexts, the πρωτότυπο carries special authority or value.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**41.**
**Form:** παραποίηση
**Transliteration:** parapiísi
**Literal meaning:** from παραποιώ (to alter, falsify); "alteration, falsification"
**Typical English translations:** falsification, adulteration, distortion, tampering
**Usage:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to refer to the deliberate alteration of a document, substance, or account so as to misrepresent it. A παραποίηση εγγράφου is the falsification of a document, a criminal offense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**42.**
**Form:** δήθεν
**Transliteration:** díthен
**Literal meaning:** from δή (indeed, just) + θεν (suffix of origin/manner); "supposedly, allegedly"
**Typical English translations:** supposedly, allegedly, so-called, ostensibly
**Usage:** A particle or adverb expressing skepticism or irony about a claim — that something is presented as being the case but the speaker doubts it. Frequently used with scare-quote force in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**43.**
**Form:** τάχα
**Transliteration:** tácha
**Literal meaning:** originally "quickly, perhaps"; in modern usage, "supposedly, as if"
**Typical English translations:** supposedly, allegedly, as if, ostensibly
**Usage:** Used colloquially to cast doubt on a claim or to indicate that something is presented as true but is not believed by the speaker. Often interchangeable with δήθεν in informal registers, though with slightly different pragmatic nuances.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**44.**
**Form:** φερόμενος ως
**Transliteration:** ferómenos os
**Literal meaning:** present participle of φέρω (to carry, bear) + ως (as); "being carried/presented as"
**Typical English translations:** alleged, purported, so-called, presented as
**Usage:** A fixed participial expression used in legal, journalistic, and formal contexts to indicate that something is described or presented as having a certain identity or status without that status being confirmed (e.g., φερόμενος ως ιδιοκτήτης, "the purported owner").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**45.**
**Form:** κατά φαινόμενον
**Transliteration:** katá fenómenon
**Literal meaning:** κατά (according to) + φαινόμενον (appearance); "according to appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparently, ostensibly, in appearance
**Usage:** A fixed prepositional expression contrasting with κατ' ουσίαν, used to indicate that something holds only at the level of appearance. Found in philosophical and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**46.**
**Form:** εικαζόμενος
**Transliteration:** eikazómenos / ikazómenos
**Literal meaning:** present passive participle of εἰκάζω (to conjecture, liken); "conjectured, supposed"
**Typical English translations:** alleged, supposed, presumed, putative, conjectured
**Usage:** Used in legal and journalistic contexts to describe a person or fact whose status has not been established (e.g., ο εικαζόμενος δράστης, "the alleged perpetrator"). Marks epistemic reservation about a claim.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**47.**
**Form:** εικός
**Transliteration:** ikós
**Literal meaning:** from ἔοικα (to seem, resemble); "that which seems likely, probable"
**Typical English translations:** probable, likely, plausible, verisimilar
**Usage:** Found primarily in classical and formal philosophical registers to indicate what is probable or plausible as opposed to what is certain or demonstrated. Less common in contemporary everyday Greek but retained in set expressions and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**48.**
**Form:** αναληθής
**Transliteration:** analithís
**Literal meaning:** ἀν- (privative) + αληθής; "not true"
**Typical English translations:** untrue, false
**Usage:** A formal or literary negation of αληθής, used in philosophical and written contexts. Less common in everyday speech than ψευδής; tends to appear in contexts where the negation of truth-value is being discussed explicitly.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**49.**
**Form:** κίβδηλος
**Transliteration:** kívdhilos / kívdilos
**Literal meaning:** "adulterated, counterfeit" (of coins originally); "base, spurious"
**Typical English translations:** counterfeit, spurious, base, debased, adulterated
**Usage:** Originally applied to debased or counterfeit coinage; extended metaphorically to persons, arguments, and values considered inauthentic or morally debased. Used in formal, literary, and philosophical registers to describe something that appears genuine but is not. Less common in everyday Modern Greek; more frequent in written and elevated registers than πλαστός or ψεύτικος.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**50.**
**Form:** εικαστικός
**Transliteration:** eikastikós
**Literal meaning:** from εικάζω (to represent by likeness, to conjecture); "of or pertaining to representation by likeness"
**Typical English translations:** visual (arts), representational, figurative
**Usage:** In Modern Greek, primarily used in the fixed expression εικαστικές τέχνες ("visual/plastic arts"), referring to arts that produce visible representations. The term carries the sense of arts concerned with likeness and representation, though its evaluative dimension (resemblance, fidelity of representation) is more prominent in older or technical usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
