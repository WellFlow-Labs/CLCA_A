# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which terms co-occur in the same contexts, which terms are contrasted with each other, which terms appear in the same regulatory or discourse registers, and which terms share characteristic verb collocates or construction types. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data. Items that do not fit cleanly are listed at the end with explicit notes.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that something is genuine rather than counterfeit, fabricated, or imitated

**Label:** Genuineness vs. fakeness in objects, goods, and documents

**Definition:** These terms are used in contexts where the central question is whether a physical object, document, credential, or product is what it presents itself as being — as opposed to being a forgery, imitation, or counterfeit. They appear most densely in commercial, legal, and authentication contexts. The contrast between 真 and 假/伪 is the organizing axis of this group, but the items are grouped here because of their shared deployment in verification and anti-counterfeiting discourse.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真 (zhēn) — the basic positive pole; "genuine, real"; contrasts with 假
- 假 (jiǎ) — the basic negative pole; "fake, not genuine"; contrasts with 真
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — the binomial framing the everyday question of genuineness; 辨别真假, 真假难辨
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — frames the question of genuine vs. spurious, especially for documents, artworks, and texts; 辨别真伪, 鉴别真伪, 难辨真伪; more restricted to formal authentication contexts than 真假
- 是真是假 (shì zhēn shì jiǎ) — interrogative frame: "is it real or fake?"
- 真假难分 (zhēnjiǎ nán fēn) — "hard to tell real from fake"
- 真伪难辨 (zhēnwěi nán biàn) — "difficult to distinguish genuine from counterfeit"
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — "to distinguish genuine from fake" — the standard verb phrase for authentication activity
- 辨真假 (biàn zhēnjiǎ) — "to tell real from fake" — colloquial version of 辨别真伪
- 鉴定真伪 (jiàndìng zhēnwěi) — "to authenticate" — professional appraisal context
- 真品 (zhēnpǐn) — "genuine article, authentic product"
- 真迹 (zhēnjì) — "authentic original work" (calligraphy, painting); used specifically for objects being authenticated, not for statements or persons
- 假货 (jiǎhuò) — "fake goods, counterfeit merchandise"
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — passing off as genuine or authorized; 假冒商品; commercial and legal term; names both the state (a counterfeit object) and the act (deliberate misrepresentation)
- 仿冒 (fǎngmào) — "to counterfeit, imitation fraud"; more specifically associated with trademark and branded goods than 假冒
- 伪造 (wěizào) — the deliberate manufacture of a false document, signature, or object; 伪造文件, 伪造签名, 伪造证据; criminal law term; implies deliberate fabrication and is primarily legal in register
- 伪造者 (wěizàozhě) — "forger, counterfeiter"
- 伪劣 (wěiliè) — fake and inferior; 伪劣产品
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎ mào wěi liè) — standard regulatory phrase for counterfeit and substandard goods
- 假冒伪劣产品 (jiǎmào wěiliè chǎnpǐn) — "fake and shoddy products" — canonical noun phrase in consumer law
- 打假 (dǎ jiǎ) — to combat counterfeits; anti-counterfeiting efforts; enforcement and regulatory context
- 造假 (zào jiǎ) — to manufacture fakes or falsify; 数据造假, 学术造假
- 作假 (zuò jiǎ) — to falsify; to cheat; 考试作假, 账目作假
- 掺假 (chān jiǎ) — to adulterate; to mix in fake material
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — using fakes to confuse the genuine; describes the act of deceptive substitution
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) — affirms that goods are authentic and prices honest; extended to mean genuinely what it claims to be; originally commercial, now general
- 货真价实的好东西 (huò zhēn jià shí de hǎo dōngxi) — attributive construction asserting authenticity
- 真金不怕火炼 (zhēnjīn bù pà huǒ liàn) — "true gold fears no fire; genuine quality withstands testing"
- 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn) — eliminating the false and retaining the genuine
- 真伪莫辨 (zhēn wěi mò biàn) — impossible to tell genuine from fake
- 道地 (dàodì) — colloquial affirmation of genuine regional or cultural authenticity; 道地药材, 道地的北京话
- 原真性 (yuánzhēnxìng) — specialized term for authenticity in heritage conservation contexts; usage restricted to a specific institutional domain (UNESCO frameworks, museum studies)
- 鉴定 (jiàndìng) — expert evaluation to determine authenticity; 进行鉴定, 经专家鉴定, 送去鉴定
- 鉴定书 (jiàndìngshū) — the document produced by an authentication process
- 辨伪 (biànwěi) — scholarly or expert detection of forgeries
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — the act of distinguishing genuine from fake
- 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) — solid, genuine evidence backing a claim; evidentiary authenticity
- 确凿 (quèzáo) — "conclusive, irrefutable" — used almost exclusively with 证据 (evidence)
- 证据确凿 (zhèngjù quèzáo) — "the evidence is conclusive"
- 有确凿证据证明 (yǒu quèzáo zhèngjù zhèngmíng) — "there is conclusive evidence proving"
- 真本 (zhēnběn) — marginal, domain-restricted term from bibliographic scholarship; fits partly in this group but usage is too restricted to place confidently

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 辨别真假 / 辨别真伪
- 鉴别真伪
- 打假 / 造假 / 作假 / 掺假
- 以假乱真
- 货真价实
- 去伪存真

**Notes:** 真伪 is more restricted to formal authentication contexts (art, antiques, legal documents); 真假 is broader and more everyday. 伪造 implies deliberate fabrication and is primarily legal in register; 假货 and 造假 are more general. 仿冒 is more specifically associated with trademark and branded goods; 假冒 is broader. 真迹 and 真品 are used specifically for objects being authenticated, not for statements or persons. 真正 and 名副其实 overlap with this group but are more typically used in evaluating persons, titles, and categories rather than physical objects or documents; see Group 2. 虚假 overlaps with this group but is more strongly associated with statements and information than with physical goods; see Group 3. 原真性 is noted as a marginal item whose usage is restricted to heritage conservation discourse.

## Group 2: Terms used to evaluate whether something or someone genuinely merits a label, title, or category

**Label:** Correspondence between name/label and actual nature

**Definition:** These terms are used when the question is whether a person, institution, or thing actually lives up to the description, title, or reputation applied to it. They appear in evaluative, commendatory, and critical contexts — praising something as genuinely what it claims to be, or criticizing a mismatch between designation and reality. The classical philosophical pairing of 名 (name) and 实 (reality/substance) is the background of several items in this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真正 (zhēnzhèng) — intensifies the claim that something is genuinely what it appears to be; 真正的朋友, 真正理解; functions as a general intensifier of genuineness across the domain
- 真正意义上的 (zhēnzhèng yìyì shàng de) — "in the true sense of the word" — distinguishes genuine instances from nominal ones
- 算不上真正的 (suàn bu shàng zhēnzhèng de) — "does not qualify as a real X" — denial of category membership
- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — the label accurately corresponds to the actual qualities; 名副其实的专家; commendatory assertion of correspondence
- 名副其实的专家 (míng fù qí shí de zhuānjiā) — "a genuine expert" — attributive use
- 可以说是名副其实 (kěyǐ shuō shì míng fù qí shí) — "one can say it truly lives up to its name"
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — the label does not correspond to the actual qualities; 这个称号名不副实; critical assertion of mismatch
- 名不副实的头衔 (míng bù fù shí de tóuxián) — "an undeserved title"
- 名实 (míngshí) — "name and reality" — the classical analytical pairing; usage in classical philosophical discourse (School of Names, 名家) is more specialized than the other items in this group
- 名实相符 (míng shí xiāngfú) — "name and reality correspond"
- 相符 (xiāngfú) — "consistent with, matching" (relational); requires two terms being compared
- 与事实相符 (yǔ shìshí xiāngfú) — "consistent with the facts"
- 本质 (běnzhì) — the intrinsic or defining nature of something; 本质上, 揭示本质, 抓住本质, 看清本质, 从本质上说; tends to appear in philosophical and moral discourse about what something inherently is
- 实质 (shízhì) — the actual content or underlying reality, often in contrast to formal or nominal character; 实质上, 实质性, 触及实质, 流于形式缺乏实质; tends to appear in legal and administrative discourse about what something amounts to in practical or legal terms
- 本质上 (běnzhìshàng) — adverbial: essentially, fundamentally
- 实质上 (shízhìshàng) — adverbial: in substance, in reality
- 实质性 (shízhìxìng) — attributive: substantive; 实质性进展, 实质性问题; attested primarily as an attributive adjective in fixed collocations rather than as a full nominalization
- 揭示本质 (jiēshì běnzhì) — "to reveal the essence"
- 认清本质 (rènqīng běnzhì) — "to clearly recognize the true nature"
- 抓住本质 (zhuāzhù běnzhì) — "to grasp the essence"
- 触及实质 (chùjí shízhì) — "to get to the substance, to address the real issue"
- 看清本质 (kànqīng běnzhì) — "to see clearly the true nature"
- 从本质上说 (cóng běnzhì shàng shuō) — "essentially speaking, fundamentally"
- 真才实学 (zhēn cái shí xué) — genuine talent and solid learning, as opposed to mere credentials or reputation
- 言过其实 (yán guò qí shí) — a statement that exceeds what the facts warrant; exaggeration; the only item in this group that evaluates a statement rather than a person or thing, but the statement in question is specifically one that overclaims about qualities
- 实力 (shílì) — genuine, demonstrated capacity as opposed to nominal rank or reputation; primarily about power and competence rather than correspondence between label and quality

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 名副其实的专家
- 名不副实的头衔
- 揭示本质 / 认清本质 / 抓住本质
- 触及实质
- 真正意义上的

**Notes:** 本质 and 实质 are closely related and sometimes interchangeable, but usage patterns distinguish them: 本质 tends to appear in philosophical and moral discourse about what something inherently is; 实质 tends to appear in legal and administrative discourse about what something amounts to in practical or legal terms. Both generate productive adverbial forms (本质上, 实质上) that function as discourse markers. 真正 overlaps with Group 1 (genuineness of objects) and Group 6 (genuine feelings or commitments) but is included here because it frequently appears in contexts evaluating whether something deserves its designation. 货真价实 overlaps with this group but is more strongly associated with commercial and object-authenticity contexts (Group 1). 名实 is included as the closest fit but its usage in classical philosophical discourse is more specialized than the other items in this group.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate whether a statement, report, or account corresponds to the facts

**Label:** Factual accuracy of statements and representations

**Definition:** These terms are used when evaluating whether a verbal or written representation — a news report, testimony, claim, or description — accurately reflects what actually happened or what is actually the case. They appear in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts, and they are typically applied to propositions, claims, and representations rather than to physical objects. Several items in this group are specifically associated with the institutional demand for accurate reporting. The key contrast is between a representation and the reality it purports to describe.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真实 (zhēnshí) — used to evaluate accounts, depictions, and testimony as faithfully reflecting reality; collocates with 反映, 还原, 追求; spans both the propositional and the representational/aesthetic dimensions of truth
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — the nominalized property, used when the degree or presence of factual fidelity is itself the object of evaluation; 新闻报道的真实性
- 真实报道 (zhēnshí bàodào) — "truthful reporting" — journalistic context
- 还原真实 (huányuán zhēnshí) — "to restore the truth, to recover the true picture"
- 贴近真实 (tiējìn zhēnshí) — "to be close to reality, to approach the truth"
- 反映真实情况 (fǎnyìng zhēnshí qíngkuàng) — "to reflect the true situation"
- 基于真实事件 (jīyú zhēnshí shìjiàn) — "based on true events"
- 如实 (rúshí) — adverbial; specifies that a communicative act (陈述, 反映, 回答) is performed without distortion; strongly associated with legal testimony; does not function as a predicate adjective
- 如实陈述 (rúshí chénshù) — "to state truthfully"
- 如实反映 (rúshí fǎnyìng) — "to accurately reflect"
- 如实回答 (rúshí huídá) — "to answer truthfully"
- 如实汇报 (rúshí huìbào) — "to report truthfully"
- 如实记录 (rúshí jìlù) — "to record faithfully"
- 失实 (shīshí) — evaluates a report or account as failing to correspond to the facts, without necessarily implying intent; 报道失实, 内容失实, 严重失实
- 报道失实 (bàodào shīshí) — "the report is inaccurate"
- 严重失实 (yánzhòng shīshí) — "seriously inaccurate"
- 失真 (shīzhēn) — evaluates a representation as having deviated from its original; overlaps with 失实 in media criticism contexts but extends into technical signal-distortion usage; used both in technical contexts (audio/visual distortion) and in evaluations of historical or journalistic accuracy; emphasizes departure from an original rather than from an external fact
- 失真度 (shīzhēndù) — technical nominalization measuring the degree of deviation from an original signal or representation
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — evaluates claims and representations as without factual basis; stronger negative connotation than 失实; 虚假信息, 虚假广告; carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception or fraud than 失实
- 虚假性 (xūjiǎxìng) — nominalized form; 陈述的虚假性
- 虚假宣传 (xūjiǎ xuānchuán) — "false advertising"
- 发布虚假信息 (fābù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to disseminate false information"
- 散布虚假消息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xiāoxi) — "to spread false news"
- 散布虚假信息 (sànbù xūjiǎ xìnxī) — "to spread false information"
- 发布虚假广告 (fābù xūjiǎ guǎnggào) — "to publish false advertising"
- 制造虚假繁荣 (zhìzào xūjiǎ fánróng) — "to manufacture a false prosperity"
- 属实 (shǔshí) — formal/legal confirmation that a reported matter corresponds to the facts; 情况属实, 经查属实; functions both as a description of a statement's truth-value and as the formal outcome of a verification procedure
- 经查属实 (jīng chá shǔshí) — "verified upon investigation"
- 情况属实 (qíngkuàng shǔshí) — "the situation is confirmed as factual"
- 不实 (bùshí) — "untrue, inaccurate" (formal)
- 据实 (jùshí) — "based on the facts, in accordance with the actual situation"
- 相符 (xiāngfú) — "consistent with, matching" (relational); requires two terms being compared
- 不相符 (bùxiāngfú) — "inconsistent with, not matching"
- 与事实相符 (yǔ shìshí xiāngfú) — "consistent with the facts"
- 与真实不符 (yǔ zhēnshí bù fú) — "inconsistent with reality"
- 事实 (shìshí) — denotes an established datum or actual state of affairs; 尊重事实, 歪曲事实, 以事实为依据, 铁的事实, 既成事实; tends to appear when facts are being cited as the basis for a judgment or as evidence
- 事实上 (shìshíshàng) — discourse marker introducing a correction or contrast between appearance and reality
- 尊重事实 (zūnzhòng shìshí) — "to respect the facts"
- 歪曲事实 (wāiqū shìshí) — "to distort the facts"
- 捏造事实 (niēzào shìshí) — "to fabricate facts"
- 以事实为依据 (yǐ shìshí wéi yījù) — "to take facts as the basis"
- 铁的事实 (tiě de shìshí) — fixed expression: incontrovertible fact
- 既成事实 (jìchéng shìshí) — "fait accompli, established fact"
- 有目共睹的事实 (yǒu mù gòng dǔ de shìshí) — "a fact visible to all"
- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — refers to the actual facts of a matter, especially when concealed or contested; 揭露真相, 查明真相, 掩盖真相, 真相大白; tends to appear when facts are hidden or disputed and need to be uncovered; distinct from 事实 (which simply denotes factual content) and 真实 (which evaluates accuracy of representation)
- 实情 (shíqíng) — the actual circumstances of a matter; 了解实情; near-synonym of 真相 in some contexts but less common and less associated with investigative exposure
- 真话 (zhēnhuà) — "truthful words, honest speech"; carries a slightly stronger moral valence of speaking from genuine conviction
- 实话 (shíhuà) — "honest words, straight talk"; slightly more associated with candid, unvarnished communication of facts
- 说实话 (shuō shíhuà) — "to tell the truth"
- 实话实说 (shíhuà shíshuō) — "to tell it like it is"
- 真有其事 (zhēn yǒu qí shì) — "it really happened, it's a true story"
- 这是真的 (zhè shì zhēn de) — "this is true/real"
- 不是真的 (bù shì zhēn de) — "it's not true/real"
- 千真万确 (qiān zhēn wàn què) — "absolutely true, beyond all doubt"
- 半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ) — describes a mixed state rather than evaluating something as clearly genuine or false; included here as the closest fit but noted as ambiguous
- 真真假假 (zhēnzhēn jiǎjiǎ) — "a mixture of truth and falsehood"; describes a dynamic or ambiguous situation
- 逼真 (bīzhēn) — evaluates a representation as close to the genuine article; primary domain is aesthetic and artistic (a painting that looks real, an imitation that is convincing); does not fit cleanly into the commercial/legal authenticity cluster of Group 1 or the factual accuracy cluster here; noted as a borderline item
- 逼真传神 (bīzhēn chuánshén) — "realistic and spirit-capturing"
- 模仿得逼真 (mófǎng de bīzhēn) — "imitated so realistically"
- 真实感 (zhēnshígǎn) — "sense of realism, feeling of authenticity"; evaluates representations (paintings, films, performances) for how closely they approach reality; aesthetic evaluation
- 写实 (xiěshí) — "realistic (in art or literature)"
- 写实主义 (xiěshí zhǔyì) — "realism as an artistic doctrine"
- 写实性 (xiěshíxìng) — "the quality of being realistic"
- 实录 (shílù) — "faithful record, veritable records" — historiographical fidelity
- 揭穿虚假 (jiēchuān xūjiǎ) — to expose the false

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 还原真实 / 还原事件真相
- 反映真实情况
- 如实陈述 / 如实反映 / 如实回答
- 报道失实 / 内容失实
- 发布虚假信息
- 揭穿虚假
- 尊重事实 / 歪曲事实 / 以事实为依据
- 铁的事实 / 既成事实 / 有目共睹的事实
- 经查属实

**Notes:** 真实 and 失实 are near-antonyms in journalistic and legal usage. 失真 overlaps with 失实 in media contexts but has a distinct technical register. 虚假 implies a stronger evaluative judgment (often deliberate deception) than 失实 (which may be inadvertent). 真相 tends to appear when facts are hidden or disputed and need to be uncovered; 事实 tends to appear when facts are being cited as the basis for a judgment or as evidence. 实情 is a near-synonym of 真相 in some contexts but is less common and less associated with investigative exposure. 逼真 and 真实感 are primarily used in aesthetic and technical contexts and could be considered a sub-cluster within this group. 真话 and 实话 are used interchangeably in many contexts but 实话 is slightly more associated with candid communication of facts, while 真话 carries a slightly stronger moral valence; the distinction is not consistent enough to assign them to separate groups. 正确 and 准确 overlap with this group but are treated primarily in Group 4, where the evaluative standard is conformity to a rule or target rather than correspondence to reality.

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate precision and correctness against a standard

**Label:** Correctness and precision relative to a standard

**Definition:** These terms evaluate whether something — an answer, a method, a measurement, a translation — conforms to a relevant standard or is free from error. They appear in educational, technical, and scientific contexts. The contrast is between conforming to a standard and deviating from it, rather than between genuine and fake, or between a representation and reality.

**Representative expressions:**

- 正确 (zhèngquè) — evaluates answers, methods, conduct as conforming to a standard; contrasts with 错误; 正确的道路, 保证正确; used more broadly for conformity to a standard (including moral and procedural standards)
- 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) — the nominalized property; 判断的正确性, 验证其正确性
- 不正确 (bùzhèngquè) — the standard negation
- 完全正确 (wánquán zhèngquè) — "completely correct"
- 基本正确 (jīběn zhèngquè) — "basically correct"
- 判断是否正确 (pànduàn shìfǒu zhèngquè) — "to judge whether something is correct"
- 错误 (cuòwù) — deviation from what is correct; functions as both noun and adjective; 犯错误, 纠正错误, 承认错误, 严重的错误; covers both factual mistakes and moral or political errors; broader in application than 不准确
- 错误性 (cuòwùxìng) — "erroneousness"
- 严重错误 (yánzhòng cuòwù) — "a serious error"
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — precision and exactness, especially in measurement, translation, and description; implies a tighter fit than 正确; 准确无误, 准确判断, 准确表达; used more specifically for precision of fit to a target (measurements, translations, predictions)
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — the nominalized property; 测量的准确性, 翻译的准确性
- 不准确 (bùzhǔnquè) — the standard negation
- 准确无误 (zhǔnquè wúwù) — "accurate and without error"
- 表述准确 (biǎoshù zhǔnquè) — "the formulation is accurate"
- 准确把握 (zhǔnquè bǎwò) — "to accurately grasp"
- 准确判断 (zhǔnquè pànduàn) — "accurate judgment"
- 准确预测 (zhǔnquè yùcè) — "accurate prediction"
- 准确描述 (zhǔnquè miáoshù) — "accurate description"
- 无法准确判断 (wúfǎ zhǔnquè pànduàn) — "unable to accurately judge"

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 犯错误 / 纠正错误 / 承认错误
- 验证其正确性
- 准确无误
- 准确判断 / 准确预测 / 准确描述

**Notes:** 正确 and 准确 are distinguished in usage: 正确 is used more broadly for conformity to a standard (including moral and procedural standards), while 准确 is used more specifically for precision of fit to a target (measurements, translations, predictions). 错误 is the primary antonym of 正确 in this group. This group overlaps with Group 3 (factual accuracy of statements): 准确 can describe both the precision of a measurement (Group 4) and the accuracy of a report (Group 3). The distinction is that Group 4 items foreground conformity to a standard or norm, while Group 3 items foreground correspondence to external reality. This group does not overlap substantially with Group 1 (factual accuracy of statements), though there is some overlap when 正确 is used to evaluate factual claims.

## Group 5: Terms used in the detection, verification, and establishment of facts

**Label:** Establishing and verifying what is actually the case

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the task is to determine, confirm, or establish what the facts of a matter are — particularly when facts are contested, hidden, or uncertain. They are associated with investigative, legal, and evidentiary processes. The shared feature is the procedural or epistemic act of verification rather than the content of what is verified.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — refers to the actual facts of a matter, especially when concealed or contested; the primary noun in this group as the object of investigative activity; 揭露真相, 查明真相, 掩盖真相, 真相大白
- 揭露真相 (jiēlù zhēnxiàng) — "to expose the truth"
- 查明真相 (chámíng zhēnxiàng) — "to investigate and establish the truth"
- 掩盖真相 (yǎngài zhēnxiàng) — "to cover up the truth"
- 了解真相 (liǎojiě zhēnxiàng) — "to learn the truth"
