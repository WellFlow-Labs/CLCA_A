# F3 — SEMANTIC GROUPS TABLE

| Group Label | Definition | Representative Expressions | Overlapping / Ambiguous Items | Boundary Notes |
|------------|------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| GROUP 1: Truth-Telling and Lying as Speech Acts | This group contains expressions that mark the act of speaking truthfully or falsely. These terms appear in constructions with verbs of saying (esan, mintzatu) and focus on the communicative act itself rather than the content's properties. They are used when evaluating whether someone is speaking honestly or dishonestly. | egia esan (to tell the truth), gezurra esan (to tell a lie), egia (truth - as object of speech verbs), gezurra (lie - as object of speech verbs), gezurti/gezurtia (liar - agent of lying speech), zintzo/zintzoa (honest - characterizing truthful speakers), zintzoki (honestly - manner of speaking) | None specifically noted | Discourse markers: "egia esan" introducing candid statements; Moral evaluation of speakers: "gezurtia da" (he/she is a liar); Manner adverbials: "zintzoki mintzatu" (to speak honestly) |
| GROUP 2: Verification and Authentication Processes | This group contains expressions focused on establishing, confirming, or proving truth through investigation or formal procedures. These terms appear in administrative, legal, and investigative contexts where truth must be actively established rather than simply stated. They involve processes and agents of verification. | egiazkotu (to verify, authenticate), egiaztatu (to verify, prove), egiaztatze (verification - process), egiaztapen (verification - result), egiaztatzailea (verifier - agent), ziurtatu (to make certain, ensure), frogatu (to prove, test), froga egin (to make proof), egiaztagiri (certificate - verification document), ziurtagiri (certificate) | None specifically noted | Administrative procedures: document authentication; Legal proceedings: establishing facts; Scientific contexts: testing and demonstration; Quality control: verification processes |
| GROUP 3: Genuine vs. Counterfeit Distinction | This group contains expressions that distinguish authentic objects, documents, or products from false or counterfeit ones. These terms appear primarily in commercial, legal, and administrative contexts where the authenticity of physical objects or official documents is at issue, rather than the truth of statements. | egiazko dokumentua (authentic document), gezurrezko dokumentua (false document), egiazko produktua (genuine product), gezurrezko produktua (counterfeit product), egiazko sinadura (authentic signature), gezurrezko sinadura (forged signature), faltsua (false, counterfeit), faltsutu (to falsify, forge), iruzurra (fraud), gezurrezko dirua (counterfeit money) | egiazko (belongs to multiple groups with physical objects), gezurrezko (belongs to multiple groups with objects) | Commercial transactions: distinguishing genuine from fake goods; Legal proceedings: forgery and fraud cases; Document authentication: verifying official papers; Consumer protection: identifying counterfeits |
| GROUP 4: Correctness and Error Evaluation | This group contains expressions used to evaluate whether statements, answers, actions, or procedures are correct or incorrect. These terms appear in educational, technical, and procedural contexts where accuracy and proper execution are assessed. They focus on conformity to standards or rules rather than moral truth. | zuzena (correct, right), okerra (wrong, incorrect), zuzendu (to correct), zuzenketa (correction), zuzentzailea (corrector), zuzen egin (to do correctly), zuzen erantzun (correct answer), okertu (to go wrong), zuzen ulertu (to understand correctly), zuzen aplikatu (to apply correctly) | zuzen/zuzena (belongs to GROUP 4 in epistemic/procedural contexts, GROUP 15 in physical/spatial contexts, GROUP 8 when extended to "zuzeneko") | Educational settings: grading answers; Technical procedures: following instructions; Editorial work: correcting texts; Problem-solving: identifying and fixing errors |
| GROUP 5: Appearance vs. Reality Distinction | This group contains expressions that mark the contrast between how things appear or seem and how they actually are. These terms are used when surface appearances may be misleading or when distinguishing superficial from underlying truth. They often function as epistemic hedges or qualifications. | itxura (appearance), itxurazko (apparent, seeming), itxuraz (apparently, seemingly), benetako (real, genuine), benetakoa (the real one), benetan (really, truly), egiazko izaera (true nature), egiazko aurpegia (true face), gezurrezko itxura (false appearance), itxura eman (to give appearance) | benetan/benetako (belongs to GROUP 5 as reality marker and GROUP 11 as intensifier), ageria (overlaps partially with GROUP 5 but focuses on evidential clarity), erreala/errealitatea (overlaps with GROUP 5 but functions more as philosophical/ontological category) | Epistemic qualification: "itxuraz" marking claims based on appearance; Revealing hidden reality: "egiazko izaera" (true nature); Distinguishing surface from substance; Unmasking deception: revealing "egiazko aurpegia" |
| GROUP 6: Certainty and Doubt Expressions | This group contains expressions marking the speaker's epistemic stance regarding certainty or uncertainty. These terms indicate confidence levels in knowledge or belief rather than evaluating truth itself. They appear in contexts where speakers express or question their degree of certainty. | ziurra (certain, sure), ziur egon (to be certain), ziurtasuna (certainty), ziurki (certainly), zalantza (doubt), zalantza izan (to have doubt), zalantzagarria (doubtful, questionable), zalantzan jarri (to put in doubt), zalantzarik gabe (without doubt), ziur asko (very sure) | None specifically noted | Expressing confidence: "ziur nago" (I am certain); Expressing uncertainty: "zalantza izan" (to have doubts); Questioning reliability: "zalantzagarria da" (it is questionable); Emphatic assertions: "zalantzarik gabe" (undoubtedly) |
| GROUP 7: Reliability and Trustworthiness Evaluation | This group contains expressions evaluating whether persons, sources, or information are worthy of trust or belief. These terms assess credibility and dependability rather than truth per se. They appear in contexts where the reliability of sources or the plausibility of claims is being evaluated. | fidagarria (reliable, trustworthy), sinesgarria (believable, credible), fede (faith, trust), fede izan (to have faith), fede eman (to give credence), sinestu (to believe), fidatu (to trust) | fede (belongs to GROUP 7 in trust contexts and GROUP 12 in religious contexts) | Evaluating sources: "iturburu fidagarria" (reliable source); Assessing credibility: "sinesgarria da" (it is believable); Trust relationships: "fede izan" (to have faith); Plausibility judgments: determining what merits belief |
| GROUP 8: Direct vs. Mediated Connection | This group contains expressions marking whether connections, relationships, or transmissions are direct and unmediated or involve intermediaries. These terms appear in technical, media, political, and commercial contexts where the presence or absence of intermediation is significant. The root "zuzen" extends from physical straightness to lack of mediation. | zuzenean (directly, live), zuzeneko (direct, live), zuzeneko emisioa (live broadcast), zuzeneko konexioa (direct connection), zuzeneko demokrazia (direct democracy), zuzeneko salmentak (direct sales), zuzeneko harremana (direct relationship), zuzeneko eragina (direct influence), zuzen-zuzenean (very directly) | zuzen/zuzena (belongs to GROUP 4 in epistemic/procedural contexts, GROUP 15 in physical/spatial contexts, GROUP 8 when extended to "zuzeneko") | Broadcasting: live vs. recorded transmission; Political organization: direct vs. representative democracy; Commerce: direct vs. intermediated sales; Causal analysis: direct vs. indirect effects; Telecommunications: unmediated connections |
| GROUP 9: Moral Honesty and Deception | This group contains expressions evaluating the moral character of persons or actions regarding honesty and deception. These terms focus on sincerity, integrity, and moral uprightness rather than factual accuracy. They appear in contexts of character evaluation and moral judgment. | zintzo/zintzoa (honest, sincere), zintzotasuna (honesty, integrity), zintzoki (honestly), zintzo ibili (to behave honestly), iruzurra (fraud, deceit), iruzurtia (fraudulent person), engainua (deception), engainatu (to deceive), gezurrezko modestia (false modesty), gezurrezko adiskidea (false friend) | gezurrezko (belongs to GROUP 3 with objects, GROUP 9 with character, GROUP 10 in judicial contexts, GROUP 14 with information) | Character evaluation: "zintzo da" (he/she is honest); Moral condemnation: "iruzurtia da" (he/she is a fraudster); Evaluating sincerity: distinguishing genuine from feigned behavior; Interpersonal trust: identifying false friends |
| GROUP 10: Legal and Juridical Truth | This group contains expressions specific to legal and judicial contexts where truth is established through formal procedures, testimony, and evidence. These terms appear in courtroom settings, legal documents, and discussions of justice and law. They often involve specialized legal meanings. | gezurrezko lekukotza (false testimony, perjury), gezurrezko salaketa (false accusation), gezurrezko aitorpena (false confession), egiazko lekukoa (true witness), zuzenbide (law, jurisprudence), zuzenbidea (the law), zuzenbide zibila (civil law), zuzenbide penala (criminal law), zuzentasun juridikoa (legal correctness), fede eman (to certify - legal sense) | gezurrezko (belongs to GROUP 3 with objects, GROUP 9 with character, GROUP 10 in judicial contexts, GROUP 14 with information), zuzentasuna (overlaps with GROUP 4 in epistemic sense, GROUP 10 in juridical sense, extends to moral rectitude beyond both) | Courtroom proceedings: testimony and evidence; Legal charges: accusations and confessions; Legal systems: branches and principles of law; Judicial evaluation: determining legal truth |
| GROUP 11: Emphatic Truth Markers and Intensifiers | This group contains expressions that intensify or emphasize the truth, genuineness, or completeness of something. These terms function as intensifiers or emphatic markers rather than making basic truth claims. They appear when speakers want to stress the absolute or unqualified nature of truth or falsehood. | egia hutsa (pure truth, nothing but truth), gezur hutsa (pure lie, complete lie), egia gordina (raw truth, harsh truth), egia garbiak (plain truths), egia sakona (deep truth), gezur-gezurra (complete lie - reduplicated), zuzen-zuzen (very straight, exactly), benetan (really, truly - emphatic), egiatan (in truth - emphatic), ziur asko (very sure) | benetan/benetako (belongs to GROUP 5 as reality marker and GROUP 11 as intensifier) | Emphatic assertions: stressing absolute truth; Dismissive statements: "gezur hutsa!" (complete lie!); Philosophical discourse: "egia sakona" (deep truth); Intensifying correctness: "zuzen-zuzen" (exactly right) |
| GROUP 12: Authentic vs. False Identity and Nature | This group contains expressions distinguishing the true or essential nature, identity, or character of persons and things from false or assumed ones. These terms appear when discussing what something or someone fundamentally is, as opposed to what they appear to be or claim to be. They focus on essential identity rather than factual statements. | egiazko izaera (true nature), egiazko identitatea (true identity), gezurrezko identitatea (false identity), egiazko aurpegia (true face), egiazko jatorria (true origin), benetako (real, genuine - identity sense), egiazko artista (true artist), gezurrezko profeta (false prophet), egiazko fedea (true faith) | egiazko (belongs to GROUP 3 with physical objects, GROUP 12 with persons and character, GROUP 13 with motivations, GROUP 14 with stories), fede (belongs to GROUP 7 in trust contexts and GROUP 12 in religious contexts) | Revealing character: "egiazko izaera" (true nature); Identity verification: true vs. false identity; Authenticity judgments: genuine vs. pretender artists; Religious discourse: true vs. false faith/prophets |
| GROUP 13: Actual vs. Stated Reasons and Intentions | This group contains expressions distinguishing actual motivations, reasons, and purposes from stated or apparent ones. These terms appear in explanatory and analytical contexts where speakers identify hidden or underlying causes that differ from surface explanations. They focus on uncovering real motivations. | egiazko arrazoia (true reason, real reason), egiazko asmoa (true intention), egiazko helburua (true goal), gezurrezko aitzakia (false excuse), gezurrezko promesa (false promise) | egiazko (belongs to GROUP 3 with physical objects, GROUP 12 with persons and character, GROUP 13 with motivations, GROUP 14 with stories) | Explanatory discourse: identifying actual causes; Strategic analysis: uncovering real objectives; Moral evaluation: distinguishing genuine from stated intentions; Political criticism: exposing false promises |
| GROUP 14: Factual vs. Fictional Narratives | This group contains expressions marking whether stories, accounts, or events are factual or fictional. These terms appear in narrative, journalistic, and historical contexts where the factual status of accounts must be indicated. They distinguish what actually happened from what is imagined or fabricated. | egiazko historia (true story), egiazko gertaera (real event), gezurrezko albistea (false news), gezurrezko informazioa (false information), egiazko istorioa (true story) | egiazko (belongs to GROUP 3 with physical objects, GROUP 12 with persons and character, GROUP 13 with motivations, GROUP 14 with stories), gezurrezko (belongs to GROUP 3 with objects, GROUP 9 with character, GROUP 10 in judicial contexts, GROUP 14 with information) | Narrative framing: marking stories as factual; Journalism: distinguishing news from misinformation; Historical discourse: actual vs. legendary events; Media criticism: identifying fake news |
| GROUP 15: Straightness and Physical Alignment | This group contains expressions describing physical straightness, alignment, or posture. These terms use the root "zuzen" in its literal spatial sense rather than its extended epistemic or moral senses. They appear in contexts of physical description, instruction, and adjustment. | zuzen jarri (to straighten physically), zuzen egon (to stand/sit straight), lerro zuzena (straight line), zuzen-lerro (straight line), zuzen-angelua (right angle), zuzen-bidea (straight path - literal sense) | zuzen/zuzena (belongs to GROUP 4 in epistemic/procedural contexts, GROUP 15 in physical/spatial contexts, GROUP 8 when extended to "zuzeneko") | Posture instructions: "zuzen egon" (sit up straight); Geometric descriptions: straight lines and right angles; Physical adjustments: straightening objects; Spatial navigation: direct routes |
| (Ungrouped Items) | Items that do not fit cleanly into any identified group | antzekoa (similar, resembling) - indicates resemblance rather than truth or falsity, functions in comparative rather than evaluative contexts; egokia (appropriate, suitable) - evaluates fitness or appropriateness rather than truth, functions in normative evaluation of suitability, distinct from truth-evaluation proper; ageria (evident, obvious) - marks visibility or manifestness of truth, overlaps partially with GROUP 5 but focuses on evidential clarity rather than appearance/reality contrast, may represent a distinct evidential category; erreala/errealitatea (real/reality) - borrowed term with limited integration, overlaps with GROUP 5 but functions more as philosophical/ontological category, less integrated into native semantic patterns | zuzentasuna (abstract nominal spanning multiple domains - overlaps with GROUP 4 in epistemic sense, GROUP 10 in juridical sense, extends to moral rectitude beyond both; the polysemy makes single group assignment problematic) | antzekoa may form its own micro-domain of similarity/resemblance; ageria may represent a distinct evidential category; erreala/errealitatea functions more as philosophical/ontological category; zuzentasuna's polysemy makes single group assignment problematic |
