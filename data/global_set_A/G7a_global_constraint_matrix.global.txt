## 1. Inferred Semantic Dimensions

Based on recurring patterns in F3 semantic grouping tables and supporting F1-F2 evidence across eight languages:

### D1: PROPOSITIONAL CORRESPONDENCE
Evaluating whether statements, claims, or assertions match actual states of affairs. Distinguished from object authenticity and character evaluation. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 1, Yoruba GROUP 1, Finnish GROUP 1, Basque "Truth-marking in statements," Tamil GROUP 1, Indonesian GROUP 1, Turkish GROUP 1, Korean GROUP 1).

### D2: MATERIAL AUTHENTICITY
Distinguishing genuine physical objects, documents, or materials from counterfeits, forgeries, or imitations. **Identified in 7/8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 2, Finnish GROUP 2, Basque "Genuineness and authenticity of objects," Tamil GROUP 11, Indonesian GROUP 2, Turkish GROUP 2, Korean GROUP 2; weak in Yoruba).

### D3: DISPOSITIONAL HONESTY
Evaluating persons' habitual truthfulness, integrity, or deceptiveness as stable character traits. Distinguished from individual speech acts. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian noted, Yoruba GROUP 4, Finnish GROUP 5, Basque GROUP 3, Tamil GROUP 3, Indonesian GROUP 3, Turkish GROUP 5, Korean GROUP 3).

### D4: PROCEDURAL CORRECTNESS
Evaluating whether actions, answers, or methods conform to established standards, rules, or proper procedures. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 1, Yoruba GROUP 2, Finnish GROUP 3, Basque GROUP 10, Tamil GROUP 2, Indonesian GROUP 2, Turkish GROUP 3, Korean GROUP 4).

### D5: MEASUREMENT EXACTNESS
Evaluating degree of precision in measurements, descriptions, timing, or targeting. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian noted, Yoruba GROUP 2, Finnish GROUP 6, Tamil noted, Indonesian GROUP 6, Turkish GROUP 6, Korean GROUP 4).

### D6: SOURCE RELIABILITY
Evaluating whether sources, persons, or information can be consistently depended upon over time. **Identified in 7/8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 5, Finnish GROUP 4, Basque GROUP 4, Tamil noted, Indonesian GROUP 4, Turkish GROUP 4, Korean GROUP 7; weak in Yoruba).

### D7: EPISTEMIC CERTAINTY
Expressing degree of confidence, sureness, or definiteness about knowledge or decisions. **Identified in 6/8 languages' F3 tables** (Basque GROUP 10, Indonesian noted, Turkish GROUP 9, Korean GROUP 6; weak in Tamil, Yoruba).

### D8: NORMATIVE LEGITIMACY
Evaluating whether claims, actions, or rights have proper moral, legal, or logical grounds. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 4, Yoruba GROUP 4, Finnish GROUP 13, Tamil noted, Indonesian GROUP 8, Turkish GROUP 13, Korean GROUP 8).

### D9: INTENTIONAL DECEPTION
Distinguishing deliberate falsehood with intent to mislead from unintentional error. **Identified in 5/8 languages' F3 tables** (Finnish GROUP 4, Tamil GROUP 1 vs. GROUP 2, Indonesian GROUP 3 vs. GROUP 2, Turkish noted, Korean GROUP 3).

### D10: EVIDENTIAL ESTABLISHMENT
Establishing truth through evidence, testimony, proof, or formal verification. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 6, Yoruba GROUP 6, Finnish GROUP 6, Basque GROUP 2, Tamil GROUP 5, Indonesian GROUP 6, Turkish GROUP 6, Korean GROUP 6 and GROUP 12).

### D11: TRUTH VISIBILITY
Truth emerging from hiddenness, being revealed, or being deliberately concealed. **Identified in all 8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian GROUP 6, Yoruba noted, Finnish noted, Basque noted, Tamil GROUP 6, Indonesian noted, Turkish GROUP 14, Korean GROUP 14).

### D12: EXPRESSION SINCERITY
Evaluating whether emotions, intentions, or expressions are genuine versus feigned or insincere. **Identified in 6/8 languages' F3 tables** (Georgian noted, Finnish GROUP 5, Basque GROUP 3, Indonesian noted, Turkish noted, Korean GROUP 2; weak in Yoruba, Tamil).

## 2. Inferred Constructional Functions

Based on recurring patterns in F4 construction tables and F6 naturalness judgments:

### C1: BASIC PREDICATION
Attributing truth-value to subjects (X is true/false/correct).

### C2: NEGATION
Denying truth-value or asserting opposite.

### C3: ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION
Modifying nouns with truth-domain predicates.

### C4: INCHOATIVE
Expressing change to truth-status (become true, turn out false).

### C5: CAUSATIVE
Agent causing truth-status change (verify, falsify, prove).

### C6: REVELATION
Truth emerging from hiddenness or being discovered.

### C7: CONCEALMENT
Truth being hidden or kept from view.

### C8: ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION
Creating abstract quality nouns (truthfulness, honesty).

### C9: AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION
Creating person nouns characterized by truth-behavior.

### C10: MANNER ADVERBIALIZATION
Creating manner adverbs (truly, honestly, accurately).

## 5. Narrative Analysis

### Strongly Recurrent Constraints

**CONSTRAINT 1: Inchoative Blocking with Abstract Propositional Truth (D1 × C4)**

**Pattern**: Six of eight languages block or mark simple inchoative constructions when the subject is an abstract propositional truth term (진실 "truth," totuus "truth," egia "truth," სიმართლე "truth").

**Evidence**:
- Korean F6: 진실이 되다 [MARGINAL] — "semantically odd"
- Georgian F6: სიმართლე გახდა [MARGINAL] — "requires specific contexts"
- Basque F6: egia bihurtu [MARGINAL] — "marked construction"
- Finnish F6: käydä toteen [CORE] but "restricted to predictions"
- Tamil F6: உண்மையாகு [CORE] but "requires context"
- Yoruba F6: (stative verb tọ́ resists inchoative)

**Exceptions**: Indonesian (menjadi benar [CORE] "fully natural"), Turkish (gerçekleşmek [CORE] "lexicalized and productive")

**Systematic substitution**: All six blocking languages substitute revelation/discovery verbs (all rated [CORE]):
- Korean: 드러나다 "come to light"
- Georgian: გამოვლინდა "was revealed"
- Basque: egia atera "truth came out"
- Finnish: paljastua "be revealed"
- Tamil: வெளிப்படு "come out"
- Yoruba: purọ́ jáde "lie emerged"

**Interpretation**: Abstract propositional truth resists conceptualization as "coming into being" (inchoative semantics). Languages systematically substitute constructions emphasizing "coming into view" (revelation semantics). This represents a **semantic incompatibility** between inchoative operators and abstract truth subjects, resolved through obligatory constructional substitution.

**Confidence**: **HIGH** — Six languages with explicit F6 [MARGINAL] ratings and documented [CORE] substitution strategies.

**CONSTRAINT 2: Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry for Dispositional Honesty (D3 × C9)**

**Pattern**: All eight languages lexicalize "liar" but seven lack dedicated "truth-teller" agentive nouns.

**Evidence**:
- **"Liar" lexicalized** (all F5 tables document):
  - Georgian: ტყუარა (t'q'uara)
  - Yoruba: apurọ́
  - Finnish: valehtelija
  - Basque: gezurtia
  - Tamil: பொய்யன் (poyyan)
  - Indonesian: pembohong
  - Turkish: yalancı
  - Korean: 거짓말쟁이 (geojinmaljaengi)

- **"Truth-teller" absent** (all F5 tables note):
  - Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean: No dedicated agentive
  - All use periphrastic constructions: "person who tells truth," "honest person"

- **Partial exception**: Tamil உண்மைவாதி "truth-advocate" exists but F1 notes "less common than liar terms"

**Systematic substitution**: All languages use productive periphrastic constructions documented in F4 tables.

**Interpretation**: This represents a **universal pragmatic asymmetry**: negative agent roles (marked behavior requiring characterization) are lexicalized; positive agent roles (unmarked/expected behavior) are expressed periphrastically. The asymmetry is **morphologically productive** (agentive affixes function normally) but **lexically blocked** (the specific positive combination is systematically avoided).

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Eight languages with consistent F5 documentation of liar lexicalization and truth-teller absence. Strongest universal asymmetry in entire dataset.

**CONSTRAINT 3: Dispositional Causation Blocking (D3 × C5)**

**Pattern**: No language shows productive constructions for causing someone to become a truth-teller or liar as a character transformation (dispositional causation).

**Evidence**:
- All F4 tables lack constructions like "make someone into a liar" or "cause someone to become honest" as character change
- Only constructions for causing specific acts documented: "cause to lie" (specific act), "cause to tell truth" (specific act)
- No language shows productive dispositional causation in F4 or F6 tables

**Interpretation**: Languages can express causing **specific truth-related acts** but not causing **dispositional character change**. This represents a **universal constructional gap** — dispositional traits resist causative derivation.

**Confidence**: **HIGH** — Documented through systematic absence across all F4 tables. No counterexamples found.

**CONSTRAINT 4: Revelation Construction Universality (D11 × C6)**

**Pattern**: All eight languages show highly productive revelation/discovery constructions, universally rated [CORE] in F6 tables.

**Evidence**: All F6 tables rate revelation constructions [CORE]:
- Georgian: გამოვლინდა "was revealed"
- Yoruba: purọ́ jáde "lie emerged"
- Finnish: paljastua "be revealed"
- Basque: egia atera "truth came out"
- Tamil: வெளிப்படு "come out"
- Indonesian: terungkap "be revealed"
- Turkish: ortaya çıkmak "come to light"
- Korean: 드러나다 "come to light"

**Interpretation**: Truth-revelation is a **universally productive constructional function**. All languages grammaticalize or lexicalize constructions for truth emerging from hiddenness, typically using intransitive or passive verb forms.

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Eight languages with consistent [CORE] ratings in F6 and extensive documentation in F4 tables.

**CONSTRAINT 5: Abstract Nominalization Universality (All Dimensions × C8)**

**Pattern**: All eight languages show highly productive abstract nominalization across all truth-related dimensions.

**Evidence**: All F4 tables document productive abstract nominalization rated [CORE]:
- Georgian: -ობა suffix (15+ examples in F2a)
- Yoruba: ì-/ò- prefixes (10+ examples in F2a)
- Finnish: -uus/-yys suffix (10+ examples in F2a)
- Basque: -tasun suffix (multiple examples in F2a)
- Tamil: -தன்மை suffix (multiple examples in F2a)
- Indonesian: ke-...-an circumfix (15+ examples in F2a)
- Turkish: -lık/-lik suffix (20+ examples in F2a)
- Korean: -성 suffix (15+ examples in F2a)

**Interpretation**: Abstract quality nominalization is a **universal productive pattern** in truth-domain, regardless of whether language prefers prefixing, suffixing, or circumfixing.

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Eight languages with extensive F2a documentation and [CORE] ratings in F4.

**CONSTRAINT 6: Light Verb Speech Act Universality (D1 × C11)**

**Pattern**: All eight languages express truth-telling through light verb constructions (truth-term + "say") rather than dedicated monomorphemic verbs.

**Evidence**: All F4 tables document truth-term + speaking verb constructions rated [CORE]:
- Georgian: სიმართლე თქმა "truth saying"
- Yoruba: sọ òtítọ́ "speak truth"
- Finnish: puhua totuutta "speak truth"
- Basque: egia esan "truth say"
- Tamil: உண்மை சொல் "truth say"
- Indonesian: mengatakan kebenaran "say truth"
- Turkish: doğru söylemek "true speak"
- Korean: 진실을 말하다 "truth-ACC say"

**Interpretation**: Truth-telling is **universally expressed through light verb constructions** rather than simple verbs. No language has a monomorphemic verb meaning "to truth."

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Eight languages with consistent F4 documentation and [CORE] F6 ratings.

### Likely Prohibitions or Avoidance Patterns

**PROHIBITION 1: Causative Truth Creation (D1 × C5 creation sense)**

**Pattern**: Most languages block causatives implying literal creation of truth while allowing verification causatives.

**Evidence**:
- **Verification sense [CORE]** (all languages): Georgian გამართლება, Finnish todistaa, Basque egiazkotu, Tamil உண்மைப்படுத்து, Indonesian membenarkan, Turkish doğrulamak, Korean 확인하다
- **Creation sense [MARGINAL]**: Korean F4 "implies manipulation," Tamil F4 similar restrictions, Georgian F4 "requires specific contexts," Basque F4 semantic markedness

**Interpretation**: Languages distinguish **verification causatives** (establishing pre-existing truth) from **creation causatives** (bringing truth into existence). Creation sense blocked because truth is discovered, not manufactured.

**Confidence**: **MODERATE** — Explicitly documented in 4 languages' F4 tables; inferred from verification productivity in all 8 languages.

**PROHIBITION 2: Degree Modification of Truth Nominals (D1 × C12)**

**Pattern**: Core truth nominals resist degree modification while adjectival derivatives accept it.

**Evidence**:
- **Blocked**: Korean F6 *매우 진실 "very truth" ungrammatical, Finnish F6 totuus resists degree modification
- **Allowed with adjectives**: Korean F6 매우 정확하다 "very accurate" [CORE], Indonesian F6 sangat benar "very true" [CORE]

**Interpretation**: Represents **category-based restriction** — nouns resist degree modification that adjectives accept.

**Confidence**: **MODERATE** — Explicitly documented in Korean and Finnish F6; similar patterns noted in Tamil.

**AVOIDANCE 1: Positive Agentive Roles**

**Pattern**: Languages systematically avoid lexicalizing positive agentive roles (truth-teller, honest person) while freely lexicalizing negative roles (liar, deceiver).

**Evidence**: See CONSTRAINT 2 above.

**Interpretation**: Reflects **pragmatic avoidance principle** — unmarked/expected behaviors don't require dedicated lexicalization.

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Universal across all 8 languages.

**AVOIDANCE 2: Simple Inchoatives for Abstract Truth**

**Pattern**: Languages systematically avoid simple inchoative constructions for abstract truth, substituting revelation constructions.

**Evidence**: See CONSTRAINT 1 above.

**Interpretation**: Reflects **conceptual avoidance** — truth is not conceptualized as coming into being.

**Confidence**: **HIGH** — Six languages with explicit avoidance and substitution.

### Important Divergences, Substitutions, or Asymmetries

**DIVERGENCE 1: Inchoative Productivity Split**

**Split pattern**:
- **Productive**: Indonesian (menjadi benar [CORE]), Turkish (gerçekleşmek [CORE])
- **Blocked/Restricted**: Korean ([MARGINAL]), Georgian ([MARGINAL]), Basque ([MARGINAL]), Finnish (restricted), Tamil (restricted), Yoruba (stative verb resists)

**Evidence**: F6 naturalness tables explicitly rate constructions for all languages.

**Interpretation**: Languages divide on whether abstract truth can "become" (Indonesian, Turkish allow) or only "be revealed" (others require). Represents **fundamental conceptual divergence** in truth ontology.

**DIVERGENCE 2: Passive Voice Preference**

**Split pattern**:
- **Passive preferred**: Finnish, Korean, Indonesian (F4 tables note)
- **Active/Intransitive preferred**: Yoruba, Basque, Tamil (F4 tables note)
- **Both common**: Georgian, Turkish (F4 tables note)

**Evidence**: F4 construction tables explicitly document voice preferences.

**Interpretation**: Correlates with **general passive productivity** in each language. Truth-domain follows typological patterns rather than showing domain-specific restriction.

**DIVERGENCE 3: Register Stratification Depth**

**Split pattern**:
- **Extensive**: Korean (4 parallel systems), Tamil (3 systems), Turkish (multiple systems) — F1 tables document
- **Moderate**: Indonesian, Finnish — F1 tables document
- **Minimal**: Yoruba, Georgian, Basque — F1 tables document

**Evidence**: F1 vocabulary tables explicitly mark register distinctions.

**Interpretation**: Reflects **sociolinguistic contact history** rather than structural constraint. Languages with extensive borrowing develop parallel systems.

**SUBSTITUTION 1: Revelation for Inchoative**

**Pattern**: Languages blocking simple inchoatives (D1 × C4 = BLOCKED) systematically substitute revelation constructions (D11 × C6 = ALLOWED).

**Evidence**: F6 tables show revelation verbs rated [CORE] in all languages where inchoatives are [MARGINAL].

**Interpretation**: **Functional substitution** — revelation constructions replace inchoatives when semantic incompatibility blocks expected pattern.

**Confidence**: **HIGH** — Documented in 6 languages with explicit F6 ratings.

**SUBSTITUTION 2: Periphrasis for Agentive**

**Pattern**: Languages blocking agentive "truth-teller" (D3 × C9 = BLOCKED) universally substitute periphrastic constructions.

**Evidence**: All F4 tables document periphrastic alternatives: "person who tells truth," "honest person."

**Interpretation**: **Syntactic substitution** — productive syntax compensates for blocked morphology.

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Universal across all 8 languages.

**ASYMMETRY 1: Liar vs. Truth-Teller (Universal)**

**Pattern**: All languages lexicalize "liar" (D3 × C9 = ALLOWED for negative pole) but block "truth-teller" (D3 × C9 = BLOCKED for positive pole).

**Evidence**: All F5 tables document this asymmetry.

**Interpretation**: **Pragmatic asymmetry** — marked behaviors require naming; unmarked behaviors don't.

**Confidence**: **VERY HIGH** — Strongest universal asymmetry in dataset.

**ASYMMETRY 2: Verification vs. Creation Causatives**

**Pattern**: Verification causatives productive (D10 × C5 = ALLOWED); creation causatives blocked (D1 × C5 creation sense = BLOCKED).

**Evidence**: All F2a tables document verification verbs. F4 tables note creation sense restrictions.

**Interpretation**: **Semantic asymmetry** — truth can be established but not created.

**Confidence**: **MODERATE** — Verification productivity documented in all 8 languages; creation blocking explicitly noted in 4 languages.

**ASYMMETRY 3: Revelation vs. Concealment Elaboration**

**Pattern**: Languages elaborate revelation vocabulary more than concealment vocabulary.

**Evidence**:
- Korean F1: Multiple revelation verbs (드러나다, 밝혀지다, 탄로나다)
- Georgian F1: Multiple revelation verbs (გამოვლინდა, გამოჩნდა)
- Turkish F1: Multiple revelation constructions (ortaya çıkmak, açığa çıkmak)
- Concealment: Typically one general "hide" verb per language (F1 tables)

**Interpretation**: **Cultural/pragmatic asymmetry** — truth-discovery more elaborated than truth-hiding.

**Confidence**: **MODERATE** — Clear in 3 languages; less pronounced in others.

## Summary

The matrix reveals **six strong universal constraints**:

1. **Inchoative blocking with abstract propositional truth** (6/8 languages) with systematic revelation substitution
2. **Agentive truth-teller blocking** (7/8 languages) with periphrastic substitution
3. **Dispositional causation blocking** (8/8 languages) — no productive constructions
4. **Revelation construction universality** (8/8 languages) — all [CORE]
5. **Abstract nominalization universality** (8/8 languages) — all dimensions
6. **Light verb speech act universality** (8/8 languages) — truth-telling/lying

**Universal productive patterns**:
- Basic predication (all dimensions × C1)
- Negation (all dimensions × C2)
- Adnominal modification (all dimensions × C3)
- Abstract nominalization (all dimensions × C8)
- Manner adverbialization (all dimensions × C10)
- Revelation constructions (D11 × C6)

**Systematic gaps**:
- Dispositional causation (D3 × C5)
- Truth-teller agentives (D3 × C9 positive pole)
- Truth creation causatives (D1 × C5 creation sense)
- Degree modification of truth nominals (D1 × C12 for nouns)

**Variable patterns** (language-specific or construction-dependent):
- Inchoative productivity (D1 × C4): 2 languages allow, 6 block/restrict
- Passive voice preferences (D11 × C6): Typologically conditioned
- Register stratification: Affects all dimensions in some languages

Evidence is **strongest** for basic predication, negation, nominalization, revelation, and light verb patterns (8/8 languages with consistent F4/F6 documentation). Evidence is **moderate** for inchoative restrictions and causative distinctions (6/8 languages with explicit documentation). Evidence is **weakest** for dispositional causation and some agentive patterns (documented through systematic absence rather than explicit blocking tests).
