| Direct truth predication / Predicate assertions of truth and falsehood | These expressions are used as complete or near-complete utterances to assert that a statement, claim, or situation is true or false. Their primary function is to commit the speaker to a position on the truth-value of something. | Evaluation of speech as truthful or deceptive / Enjoinment and description of truthful communication / Terms associated with the truthfulness or falsity of speech acts | These expressions are used specifically to evaluate a speech act or a speaker's words as honest, accurate, deceptive, or false. The target of evaluation is the speech act itself or the words produced, not a state of affairs in the world. They are also used in contexts where truthful speech is being required, described, or characterized as an action, and in imperative constructions requesting truthful speech. | Positive character and reliability evaluations | These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as genuinely what it claims or appears to be — honest, authentic, trustworthy, or accurate. They appear in moral instruction, legal testimony, and character assessments. They are applied both to persons (evaluating their integrity) and to statements or objects (evaluating their authenticity or accuracy). | *cheqa*; *cheqaq*; *cheqa runa*; *cheqa simi*; *cheqa sonqo*; *cheqa sonqowan*; *iñinapaq*; *mana llulla*; *mana pantasqa*; *cheqachisqa*; *sut'ichasqa*; *allinchasqa*; *riqsisqa*; *allintan riqsini*; *iñinapaqmi kay* | *Cheqachisqa* appears in both this group and Group 3 (verification and confirmation acts). | This group is defined by the evaluative direction (positive) and the target of evaluation (persons, statements, objects). The expressions *cheqa sonqo* and *cheqa sonqowan* are notable for locating the evaluation in the inner self (*sonqo*, heart), distinguishing inner sincerity from outward performance. *Riqsisqa* and *allintan riqsini* are included because in the attested contexts they function as reliability evaluations (confident, verified recognition) rather than merely descriptive identifications. |
| Negative character and reliability evaluations | These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as dishonest, deceptive, inauthentic, or unreliable. They appear in moral condemnation, legal discourse, and character assessments. Like Group 4, they apply to both persons and statements, but with the opposite evaluative direction. | *llulla*; *llullaq*; *llullakuq*; *llulla runa*; *llulla simi*; *llulla sonqo*; *mana iñinapaq*; *pantasqa*; *pantaq*; *llullasqa*; *llullakusqa*; *mana iñinapaqmi kay*; *llullam, manan cheqachu* | *Pantasqa* (mistaken, in error) is included here because in the attested contexts it functions as a negative reliability evaluation, though it is distinct from *llulla* in that error does not necessarily imply intent to deceive — this distinction is noted under the overlapping items section. | This group is the evaluative mirror of Group 4. The distinction between *llullaq* (one who lies, agentive) and *llullakuq* (habitual liar, reflexive-agentive) is attested within this group, with the latter emphasizing habitual or self-directed deception. |
| Causing or suffering error through another's action | These expressions describe situations where one person's action leads another into false belief or mistaken conduct. They appear in legal and moral contexts where the source of an error is attributed to an external agent, and where responsibility for the error is distributed between the one who misled and the one who was misled. They are distinct from expressions of simple error (Group 7) in that they involve an agent who acts on another. | *pantachiy*; *pantachiq*; *pantachisqa*; *pantachiwanku*; *mana pantachinaykichu*; *llullachiy*; *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani*; *llullakuyta munay*; *llullachikuy*; *pantachiy* | *Llullakuyta munay* is placed here because it describes intentional deception directed at another, but it could also be grouped with expressions of deliberate lying (Group 7). | *Pantachiy* and *llullachiy* are the core members. The construction *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani* is notable for linking deception by another (*llullakuy*) to one's own resulting error (*pantay*), making the causal relationship between Groups 6 and 7 explicit. *Pantachiy* (to mislead, to cause to err) describes the act of causing another to be in error, which functions alongside *llullachiy* (to deceive) in legal and moral discourse about harmful dishonesty. |
| Acts of lying and erring / Terms describing mistakes, errors, and going astray / Characterization of deceptive communication and conduct | These expressions describe the act of lying, deceiving, or being mistaken, with the agent as the one who performs or undergoes the act. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts where the conduct of a person is evaluated as dishonest or erroneous. They are distinct from Group 6 (moral character evaluation) in that they focus on specific acts rather than stable dispositions, and from Group 6 in that no external agent causes the error. They are distinguished from the falsehood terms in Group 1 in that they do not necessarily imply deliberate deception — they describe deviation from correctness without implying intent. | *llullakuy*; *llullakusqa*; *mana llullakuychu*; *llullakuyta munay*; *pantay*; *pantarqani*; *mana pantaychu*; *pantakuy*; *pantasqayta yachani*; *pantasqa*; *pantasqam*; *pantanim*; *mana pantanichu*; *pantasqaykiм chay*; *pantasqam chay yuyay*; *llulla rimay*; *llulla simi*; *llulla willay*; *llullachiy*; *llullachikuy*; *lulyayta yachay*; *llullayta yachay*; *llullayta saqiy*; *llulla nispa rimay*; *llulla tukuy*; *llulla hina rimay*; *mana yachaspa rimay* | *Pantasqa* and *pantasqam* overlap with Group 1 (direct truth predication) when used predicatively. *Llullakuyta munay* also appears in Group 6. *Llulla tukuy* also appears in narrative contexts describing theatrical or ritual performance where the pretending is not morally condemned; in those contexts it does not function as a negative evaluation. | The contrast between *pantay* (error, mistake) and *llulla* (lie, deception) is supported by their distinct usage contexts: *pantay* appears in contexts of acknowledgment and attribution of error without the moral condemnation associated with *llulla*, and no prohibitive construction (*ama pantaychu*) is attested in available documentation, unlike *ama llullakuychu*. *Mana yachaspa rimay* (to speak without knowing) is included because it appears in the same discourse contexts as characterizations of unreliable or misleading testimony, though it does not necessarily imply intent to deceive. |
