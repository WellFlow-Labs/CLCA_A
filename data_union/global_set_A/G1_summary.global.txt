# G1 — Cross-Language Summary of Observed Semantic Organization

---

## 1. Language Inventory

- **ka (Georgian)** — A South Caucasian language with rich agglutinative morphology; the data shows an exceptionally large and productive derivational family centered on the root *marṭl-* (right/correct/just), alongside a distinct root *č̣ešmarit-* (true/genuine) and a borrowed cluster around *q̇alb-* (false/counterfeit).
- **yo (Yoruba)** — A Niger-Congo tonal language; the data shows a core opposition between *òtítọ́* (truth) and *irọ́* (lie), a productive root *tọ́* (straight/correct) underlying both propositional and moral evaluation, and a character-evaluation cluster centered on *ìwà* (character/nature).
- **fi (Finnish)** — A Finno-Ugric agglutinative language; the data shows a large derivational family from *tosi/tode-* (true/real), a productive compound-adjective pattern using *mukainen* (conforming to) and *vastainen* (contrary to), and a register-differentiated pair *luotettava* (reliable) vs. *uskottava* (credible/believable).
- **eu (Basque)** — A language isolate with agglutinative morphology; the data shows a core opposition between *egia* (truth) and *gezurra* (lie), a productive verification cluster built on *egiazta-*, and a validity cluster around *balio* (value/validity) and *zilegi* (lawful/permissible).
- **ta (Tamil)** — A Dravidian language; the data shows a core term *uṇmai* (truth/reality) with extensive nominal and adverbial derivation, a colloquial genuineness pair *acal* (original) vs. *nakal/pōli* (copy/fake), and a character-evaluation cluster around *nērmai* (honesty/integrity).
- **id (Indonesian)** — An Austronesian language with a circumfix nominalization system; the data shows a central root *benar* (correct/true) with an unusually large derivational family, a legal validity cluster around *sah/absah* (valid), and a genuineness-versus-counterfeit cluster around *asli* (original) vs. *palsu* (fake).
- **tr (Turkish)** — A Turkic agglutinative language; the data shows a central root *doğru* (straight/correct/true) with the largest documented derivational family in the dataset, a parallel falsification cluster around *yanlış* (wrong/false), and a legal legitimacy cluster around *meşru* (legitimate) and *sah* (valid, shared with Arabic).
- **ko (Korean)** — A language isolate (or Koreanic) with agglutinative morphology and a large Sino-Korean vocabulary layer; the data shows a core opposition between *jinsil* (truth) and *geojit* (falsehood), a genuineness pair *jinjja* (real) vs. *gajja* (fake), and a character-evaluation cluster around *jeongjik* (honesty) and *jinjeongseong* (authenticity/genuineness of inner state).

## 2. Per-Language Semantic Sketch

### 2.1 Georgian (ka)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The data documents two primary truth nominals: *simarṭle* (truth, more colloquial and concrete, also covering moral rightness and justice) and *č̣ešmariteba* (truth, more philosophical and theological). These are not interchangeable: *simarṭle* dominates everyday speech and legal fact-finding, while *č̣ešmariteba* appears in religious, philosophical, and elevated literary registers. A third cluster around *namdvili* (genuine, real) handles authenticity of objects and persons, contrasting with *q̇albi* (false, counterfeit, forged). The *q̇alb-* root is borrowed (from Persian/Arabic via Turkish) and is concentrated in legal and commercial forgery contexts, while *namdvili* covers the positive authenticity pole. A separate cluster around *sarcmuno/sando* (credible/reliable) handles source evaluation. The *marṭl-* root is exceptionally productive, generating terms for orthography (*marṭlc̣era*), Orthodox Christianity (*marṭlmadidebeli*), the justice system (*marṭlmsajuleba*), proportionality (*marṭlzomiereba*), and justification/acquittal (*gamarṭleba*).

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *marṭl-* root is the most morphologically productive in the dataset for this language, generating compounds across legal, religious, linguistic, and moral domains that have no parallel in the other languages. The *si-...-e* circumfix nominalization pattern produces the primary abstract truth nominals (*simarṭle*, *siq̇albe*, *sicrue*, *sizuste*). The *-eba* suffix produces a second set of nominals with slightly more elevated register (*č̣ešmariteba*). The *-oba* suffix produces a third set naming properties of specific entities (*namdviloba*, *avtentiuroba*). These three patterns are documented as non-interchangeable.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The enclitic copula *-a* is the default for affirmative predication; negation requires the full copula *aris* (*ar aris*). The *osoittautua*-equivalent is *gamodga* (turned out to be) and *aġmočnda* (was found to be), both used in inchoative truth-evaluation contexts. The discourse-framing construction *simarṭle rom vtq̇vat* (to tell the truth, frankly) is specific to *simarṭle* and has no parallel with *č̣ešmariteba*. The concessive *martalia, magram* (it is true, but) is highly lexicalized and specific to *martali*.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The primary lexical oppositions are *simarṭle/siq̇albe*, *simarṭle/sicrue*, *namdvili/q̇albi*, *sc̣ori/mcdari*, and *zusti/uzusto*. A notable distinction is drawn between *q̇albi* (implying deliberate deception) and *mcdari* (implying honest error), and between *piqtiuri* (fictitious, sham — for transactions and entities) and *q̇albi* (forged — for physical documents and objects). The *sarcmuno/sando* near-synonym pair is distinguished by register and by whether epistemic credibility or practical reliability is foregrounded.

### 2.2 Yoruba (yo)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The core opposition is *òtítọ́* (truth) vs. *irọ́* (lie/falsehood), which are the most consistently paired terms in the dataset and appear in parallel constructions, parallel proverbs, and parallel agentive nominals. The root *tọ́* (straight/correct) underlies both *òtítọ́* and a large cluster of correctness-evaluation terms (*ó tọ́*, *kò tọ́*, *àtúntọ́*, *ẹ̀tọ́*). A separate cluster around *ìwà* (character/nature) handles moral character evaluation, with the central opposition *ìwà rere* (good character) vs. *ìwà burúkú/búburú* (bad character). Evidence and testimony evaluation is handled by a cluster around *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, related to *rí*, to see) and *ìjẹ́rìísí* (attestation/certification). The term *gidi* (genuine/real) handles colloquial authenticity evaluation, contrasting with *kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* (it is not the real thing).

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *ì-* nominalizing prefix is the most productive in the truth domain, generating *ìmọ̀* (knowledge), *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief/faith), *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (trust/reliance), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (attestation), *ìtànjẹ́* (deception), *ìdánwò* (testing), *ìdájọ́* (judgment), and others. The *a-* prefix generates morally evaluative agent nouns (*aṣírọ́*, liar; *adájọ́*, judge), while *olù-* generates functional role agents (*olùgbàgbọ́*, believer; *olùdánwò*, examiner). No single-word agentive for "truth-teller" is attested; the concept requires periphrastic expression. The *àì-* negation prefix is productive for nominals (*àìmọ̀*, ignorance; *àìgbàgbọ́*, unbelief).

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The focus construction with *ni* (predicate focus) and *ló* (subject focus, fused *ni* + *ó*) is highly productive in truth-domain assertions and proverbs. The existential negative *kò sí* (there is not) is the standard construction for denying the presence of truth, evidence, or justice. The *pé* complementizer is the standard frame for propositional complements after epistemic and evidential verbs. The *di* + nominal/adjectival inchoative is the general change-of-state construction. No dedicated inchoative morpheme exists; change-of-state is expressed periphrastically.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The primary lexical opposition is *òtítọ́/irọ́*, with parallel verbal constructions (*sọ òtítọ́/sọ irọ́*) and parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú/irọ́ kò ní dúró*). The *ìwà rere/ìwà burúkú* opposition is the central character-evaluation pair. The *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* (eyewitness) vs. *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness) compound pair is notable for combining a term from the evidence domain with a term from the truth/falsehood domain.

### 2.3 Finnish (fi)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The *tosi/tode-* root generates the largest documented derivational family in Finnish, covering truth (*totuus*), reality (*todellisuus*, *todellinen*), verification (*todentaa*, *todistaa*), probability (*todennäköinen*), and realization (*toteutua*, *toteuttaa*). A separate cluster around *aito* (genuine/authentic) handles object and personal authenticity, contrasting with *väärennös* (forgery) and *jäljitelmä* (imitation). The distinction between *luotettava* (reliable/dependable) and *uskottava* (credible/believable) is explicitly documented: *luotettava* evaluates a source's intrinsic dependability, while *uskottava* evaluates the believability of a specific claim or account. The *oikea/väärä* pair handles correctness/incorrectness across factual, normative, and identity-evaluation contexts. The compound adjective pattern using *mukainen* (conforming to) and *vastainen* (contrary to) on a genitive base is highly productive: *totuudenmukainen* (truthful), *totuudenvastainen* (contrary to truth), *oikeaoppinen* (orthodox), *vääräoppinen* (heterodox).

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *-uus/-yys* suffix is the primary abstract nominalizer for truth-domain adjectives (*totuus*, *luotettavuus*, *uskottavuus*, *aitous*, *rehellisyys*). The *epä-* prefix is the productive negation prefix for adjectives (*epäluotettava*, *epäaito*, *epärehellinen*). The *väärentää* (to forge) vs. *vääristää* (to distort) distinction is morphologically encoded: both derive from *väärä* but differ in whether they produce a fraudulent object or distort existing content. The *tarkistaa* (to check, everyday) vs. *tarkastaa* (to inspect, formal/official) distinction is register-based and semantically significant.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The *osoittautua* + translative construction is the primary inchoative for truth-domain adjectives (*väite osoittautui epätodeksi*, the claim turned out to be false). The *pitää* + essive construction (*pidän selitystä uskottavana*) expresses ongoing evaluative stance. The *todistaa* + NP + translative construction (*asiantuntija todisti maalauksen aidoksi*) is the standard certification construction. The *ei pidä paikkaansa* (it does not hold) is the most colloquial construction for propositional falsity, preferred over *ei ole tosi* in spoken Finnish. The *vastata* + partitive correspondence construction (*kuvaus vastaa totuutta*) explicitly encodes a two-place correspondence relation.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *totuudenmukainen/totuudenvastainen* pair is a productive morphological opposition using contrasting postpositional elements on the same genitive base. The *aito/epäaito* pair is morphological; the *aito/väärennös* pair is lexical. The *kopio* (neutral copy) vs. *jäljitelmä* (imitation with lesser status) vs. *väärennös* (fraudulent forgery) three-way distinction is explicitly documented. The *tarkistaa/tarkastaa* register distinction is noted as semantically significant rather than purely stylistic.

### 2.4 Basque (eu)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The core opposition is *egia* (truth) vs. *gezurra* (lie/falsehood), with parallel derivational families (*egiazkoa/gezurrezkoa*, *egia esan/gezurra esan*, *egia da/gezurra da*). A verification cluster built on *egiazta-* generates *egiaztatu* (to verify), *egiaztapen* (attestation), *egiaztagarria* (verifiable), *egiaztaezina* (unverifiable), and *egiaztagaitza* (difficult to verify). A validity cluster around *balio* (value/validity) generates *baliozkoa* (valid), *baliogabea* (invalid), *baliozkotu* (to validate), and *baliogabetu* (to invalidate). A separate cluster around *benetakoa* (genuine/real) handles authenticity with an emphasis on sincerity and non-artificiality, while *egiazkoa* handles factual correspondence. The *ziur* (certain) cluster generates *ziurtatu* (to certify), *ziurtasun* (certainty), and *ziurtagiri* (certificate). The *itxura* (appearance/semblance) cluster handles the appearance-versus-reality contrast.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *-tasun* suffix is the primary abstract nominalizer (*egiazkotasuna*, *benetakotasuna*, *fidagarritasuna*, *zuzentasuna*). The *-garri* suffix generates potential/worthy-of adjectives (*fidagarria*, *sinesgarria*, *egiaztagarria*, *frogagarria*), with *-gaitz* as the preferred negative counterpart (*fidagaitza*, *sineskaitza*) rather than *-gabe* (which expresses absence rather than impossibility). The *-gabe* privative suffix expresses absence (*baliogabea*, *egiaztatu gabe*), while *ezin* expresses impossibility (*egiaztaezina*, *frogaezina*); these are documented as non-interchangeable. The *-keria* pejorative suffix generates *gezurkeria* (habitual lying), *itxurakeria* (pretense/hypocrisy), *ustelkeria* (corruption).

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The *bihurtu* periphrastic inchoative is the most productive change-of-state construction for truth-domain nominals and adjectives (*egia bihurtu da*, it has become true; *baliogabea bihurtu da*, it has become invalid). Morphological *-tu* inchoatives are attested for some bases (*okertu*, *zintzotu*, *usteldu*) but not for *egia* or *gezurra* directly. The *-tzat jo/hartu/onartu* translative construction (*egiazkotzat jo*, to consider as genuine) is characteristic of legal and administrative registers. The *egia da ala gezurra?* disjunctive interrogative is a fixed binary framing construction.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *egiaztagarria/egiaztaezina* and *egiaztagarria/egiaztagaitza* pairs are morphological oppositions on the same verbal stem using contrasting suffixes (*-garri* vs. *-ezin* vs. *-gaitz*). The *baliozkoa/baliogabea* pair is morphological (*-zko* vs. *-gabe* on the same root). The *baliozkotu/baliogabetu* causative pair is documented as the only attested causative pair in the domain built on the same base with contrasting morphology. The *egiaztatu/egiaztatu gabeko* opposition is morphological (participial vs. participial + privative *gabe*).

### 2.5 Tamil (ta)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The core term *uṇmai* (truth/reality) is the most semantically broad in the dataset, covering propositional truth, factual reality, genuineness of objects, and sincerity of persons. It contrasts with *poy* (lie/falsehood) as its primary antonym. A separate cluster around *nērmai* (honesty/integrity, from *nēr*, straight/direct) handles character evaluation, contrasting with *vañcaṉai* (deceit) and *kaḷḷam* (guile). The genuineness-versus-counterfeit domain is handled by *acal* (original/genuine) vs. *nakal* (copy) vs. *pōli* (fake/counterfeit), with *nakal* documented as neutral (a certified copy is a *nakal* without negative implication) and *pōli* as always negative. The *cari* (correct/right) vs. *tavaṟu* (mistake/error/wrong) pair handles factual and moral correctness in everyday speech. The *niyāyam* (justice/fairness/reasonableness) cluster handles normative evaluation.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *-āna* adjectival copular suffix is the primary attributive derivation strategy (*uṇmaiyāna*, *poyyāna*, *cariyāna*, *tavaṟāna*, *nērmaiyāna*). The *-āka* adverbial copular suffix generates manner adverbials (*uṇmaiyāka*, *nērmaiyāka*, *cariyāka*). The *-il* locative suffix generates the discourse-framing adverbial *uṇmaiyil* (in truth/actually). The *-aṟṟa* privative participial suffix generates negative attributive adjectives (*uṇmaiyaṟṟa*, *nērmaiyaṟṟa*, *ātāramaṟṟa*), while *illāta* (lacking/without) generates a parallel set (*nērmaiyillāta*, *ātāramillāta*); both are productive but *illāta* is slightly more common in spoken registers. No single-word agentive for "truth-teller" is attested; the concept requires periphrastic expression. Agentive forms for negative roles (*poyyaṉ*, liar; *vañcakaṉ*, deceiver) are more lexicalized than positive counterparts.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The zero-copula predication with *uṇmai* (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*, that is indeed true) is the default. The *veḷippaṭu* (to come to light) intransitive construction treats truth as an entity moving from concealment to visibility. The *-āki viṭu* completive inchoative (*uṇmaiyākiviṭṭatu*, it has become true) marks completed change of state. The *uṇmaiyil* sentence-initial adverbial is a grammaticalized correction/contrast marker. The *tāṉ vellum* proverbial pattern (*uṇmai tāṉ vellum*, truth alone will prevail) is lexically restricted to a small set of moral-domain nominals.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *uṇmaiyāna/poyyāna* pair is morphological (parallel *-āna* derivations on contrasting bases). The *acal/nakal/pōli* three-way distinction is explicitly documented, with *nakal* occupying a neutral intermediate position. The *nampattakka/nampattakāta* pair is a morphological minimal pair (same base, contrasting participial suffixes *takka* vs. *takāta*). The *niyāyam/aniyāyam* and *niyāyam/anyāyam* pairs are lexical oppositions with Sanskrit-origin privative prefixation.

### 2.6 Indonesian (id)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The root *benar* (correct/true) generates the most productive derivational family in Indonesian, covering truth (*kebenaran*), confirmation (*membenarkan*), justification (*pembenaran*), and the discourse adverb *sebenarnya* (actually/in truth). The *sah/absah* cluster handles legal validity, with *sah* as the general-purpose term and *absah* as a more formal variant; the primary abstract nominal *keabsahan* is built on *absah* rather than *sah*, creating a morphological asymmetry. The *asli* (genuine/original) vs. *palsu* (fake/counterfeit) pair handles object authenticity, with *tiruan* (imitation) occupying a neutral intermediate position (parallel to Tamil *nakal*). The *jujur* (honest) cluster handles character evaluation. The *sahih* cluster is restricted to Islamic scholarly discourse. The *benar/salah* pair covers both propositional truth/falsity and moral right/wrong.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *ke-...-an* circumfix is the primary abstract nominalizer (*kebenaran*, *kesalahan*, *keaslian*, *kejujuran*, *keabsahan*). The *meN-...-kan* active transitive and *di-...-kan* passive are the primary verbal derivation strategies. The *pe(N)-* agentive suffix generates agent nouns for negative roles (*pembohong*, liar; *pemalsu*, forger; *peniru*, imitator) but no parallel lexicalized agent noun for "truth-teller" is attested. The *-itas* suffix applies only to loanword bases (*validitas*, *kredibilitas*, *otentisitas*) and does not extend to native roots. The *salah* root generates a large set of misaction compounds (*salah paham*, misunderstanding; *salah kaprah*, widespread misconception; *salah sasaran*, off-target; *salah tafsir*, misinterpretation) that have no parallel in other languages.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The zero-copula predication (*kabar itu benar*, that news is true) is the default. The *menjadi kenyataan* (to become reality) is a fixed inchoative collocation. The *terungkap* (to be revealed) and *terbukti* (to be proven) stative passives are the primary inchoative constructions for truth-revelation. The *sebenarnya* sentence-initial discourse marker is a grammaticalized correction/disclosure frame. The *ada benarnya* (there is some truth to it) partial-concession construction is highly conventionalized. The *benar atau tidak* (true or not) suspension-of-judgment construction is documented as a fixed pragmatic frame.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *benar/salah* pair is the primary lexical opposition. The *asli/palsu* pair is the primary authenticity opposition, with *tiruan* as a neutral intermediate. The *sah/tidak sah* pair is morphosyntactic (negation with *tidak*). The *sah/absah* near-synonym pair is distinguished by register rather than meaning. The *kebenaran formal/kebenaran materiil* pair is a legal-technical opposition between procedural and substantive truth.

### 2.7 Turkish (tr)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The root *doğru* (straight/correct/true) generates the largest documented derivational family in the dataset, covering truth (*doğruluk*), verification (*doğrulamak*, *doğrulama*), direction (*doğrultu*, *doğrudan*), linearity (*doğrusal*), and numerous compounds (*doğru yol*, *doğru bilgi*, *doğruluk değeri*, *doğruluk tablosu*). The parallel *yanlış* (wrong/false) root generates a matching falsification cluster (*yanlışlamak*, *yanlışlama*, *yanlışlanabilir*, *yanlışlanabilirlik*). The *gerçek* (real/genuine) cluster handles ontological genuineness, contrasting with *sahte* (fake/counterfeit). The *haklı/haksız* pair handles justification and rightfulness. The *meşru* (legitimate) vs. *yasal* (legal) distinction is explicitly documented: *yasal* evaluates conformity to written law, while *meşru* can extend to moral or political legitimacy beyond strict legality. The *sah* root (from Arabic, shared with Indonesian and Basque *sahih*) appears in Turkish as *sahih* (restricted to Islamic scholarly discourse) and in the compound *sahte* (fake).

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *-lık/-lik* suffix is the primary abstract nominalizer (*doğruluk*, *yanlışlık*, *güvenilirlik*, *özgünlük*, *dürüstlük*). The *-la-* factitive verbalizer is the primary verbalization strategy (*doğrulamak*, *yanlışlamak*). The *doğrulamak/yanlışlamak* parallel pair is the most explicitly documented morphological opposition in the dataset, encoding the logical positivist/Popperian distinction in Turkish academic discourse. The *-laş-* inchoative and *-laştır-* causative-inchoative chain is productive for some bases (*meşrulaşmak*, *meşrulaştırmak*) but not others. The *gayri meşru* (illegitimate) uses an Arabic-pattern negation rather than the Turkish privative *-sız*, creating a morphological asymmetry. The *doğrusal* (linear) technical term uses the *-sal* suffix restricted to scientific/technical vocabulary.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The null-copula predication (*bu haber doğru*, this news is correct) is the default; the *-dır* variant is restricted to formal, assertive, or written registers. The *değil* negation particle is the sole negation strategy for adjectival predicates. The *osoittautua*-equivalent is *çıkmak* (to come out/turn out: *tahmini doğru çıktı*, his prediction turned out to be correct). The *sebenarnya*-equivalent is *aslında* (actually/in fact), a grammaticalized correction marker. The *doğrusu* possessive form functions as a sincerity hedge (*doğrusu, bilmiyorum*, honestly, I don't know). The *gerçeğe aykırı/gerçeğe uygun* postpositional pair is a productive legal construction.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *doğru/yanlış* pair is the primary lexical opposition. The *doğrulamak/yanlışlamak* and *doğrulanabilirlik/yanlışlanabilirlik* pairs are the most explicitly parallel morphological oppositions in the dataset. The *gerçeğe uygun/gerçeğe aykırı* pair is a constructional opposition using contrasting postpositional elements on the same dative base. The *meşru/gayri meşru* pair uses Arabic-pattern negation. The *yasal/meşru* distinction is a documented lexical contrast between legality and legitimacy.

### 2.8 Korean (ko)

**Main lexical distinctions and overlaps (F1–F3):**
The data documents a large Sino-Korean vocabulary layer alongside native Korean roots. The core opposition is *jinsil* (眞實, truth/truthfulness) vs. *geojit* (falsehood), with *geojitmal* (lie, literally "false word") as the everyday spoken form. The *jinjja/gajja* pair handles colloquial genuineness-versus-counterfeit evaluation. The *jinjeongseong* (眞正性, authenticity/genuineness of inner state) cluster is prominent in contemporary public discourse about sincerity of apologies, political commitments, and artistic works. The *jeongjik* (正直, honesty/integrity) cluster handles character evaluation. The *sasil* (事實, fact/actuality) cluster handles factual correspondence. The *jinwi* (眞僞, truth or falsity) compound frames the binary question of authenticity. The *jinsang* (眞相, true state of affairs) cluster handles investigative truth-finding. The *tadasng* (妥當, valid/reasonable) cluster handles logical and normative soundness.

**Derivational/morphological asymmetries:**
The *-seong* (性) suffix is the primary abstract nominalizer for Sino-Korean bases (*jinsilseong*, *jinjeongseong*, *sinloeseong*, *sinbyeongseong*, *jeonghwakseong*, *tadangseong*). The *-hada* suffix is the primary predicative verbalization strategy for Sino-Korean bases (*jinsil-hada*, *jeongjik-hada*, *wijo-hada*). The *-doeda* suffix generates stative or resultant predicates (*jinsil-doeda*, *mosun-doeda*); the *-hada/-doeda* distinction is documented as semantically conditioned rather than freely variable. No single-word agentive for "truth-teller" is attested; the concept requires periphrastic expression. Agentive forms for negative roles (*geojitmal-jaengi*, liar; *wijobom*, forger) are more lexicalized than positive counterparts. The *-화하다* causative is productive for Sino-Korean bases (*muhyohwa-hada*, to invalidate; *hapbeophwa-hada*, to legalize) but not for native Korean bases.

**Constructional patterns (F4):**
The *[noun] + ida/i anida* copular predication is the default. The *있다/없다* existential frame is the primary predication strategy for abstract quality nouns (*jinjeongseong-i itda/eopda*, *sinbyeongseong-i itda/eopda*); these nouns do not take *-hada* as a predicative suffix. The *[noun]-(으)로 밝혀지다/드러나다/판명되다* inchoative constructions are the primary truth-revelation constructions. The *높다/낮다* scalar frame is used for degree-of-quality assessment (*jeonghwakdo-ga nopda*, accuracy is high). The *옳고 그름* (right and wrong) fixed coordinate pair is documented as a lexicalized collocation.

**Oppositional structures (F5):**
The *jinjja/gajja* pair is the primary colloquial genuineness opposition. The *jinsil/geojit* pair is the primary truth/falsehood opposition. The *matda/teullida* pair handles everyday factual correctness. The *olta/geureuda* pair handles moral and philosophical rightness/wrongness, with *teullida* having largely displaced *geureuda* in everyday factual contexts. The *유효/무효* (valid/invalid) pair is a Sino-Korean morphological opposition. The *합법/불법* (legal/illegal) pair handles lawfulness evaluation.

## 3. Cross-Language Summary

### Recurring Convergences (patterns observed across many languages)

- **A spatial/directional metaphor underlies truth and correctness terms in multiple languages.** Georgian *marṭl-* (straight/right), Yoruba *tọ́* (straight/correct), Finnish *oikea* (right, also directional), Basque *zuzen* (straight/correct), Tamil *nēr* (straight/direct), Indonesian *benar* (correct, possibly related to straightness), Turkish *doğru* (straight/correct/true), and Korean *olbareuda* (upright/correct) all show a root or base that combines spatial straightness with epistemic or moral correctness. This convergence is present in all eight languages.

- **All eight languages distinguish between propositional truth/falsity (is this claim accurate?) and object/identity genuineness (is this thing the real article?).** The two domains are handled by different lexical items in every language: Georgian *simarṭle/č̣ešmariteba* (propositional truth) vs. *namdvili/q̇albi* (genuine/fake); Yoruba *òtítọ́/irọ́* vs. *gidi/kì í ṣe gidi*; Finnish *totuus/epätotuus* vs. *aito/väärennös*; Basque *egia/gezurra* vs. *benetakoa/faltsua*; Tamil *uṇmai/poy* vs. *acal/pōli*; Indonesian *kebenaran/kebohongan* vs. *asli/palsu*; Turkish *doğru/yanlış* vs. *gerçek/sahte*; Korean *jinsil/geojit* vs. *jinjja/gajja*. In all cases the genuineness pair is more colloquial and object-oriented than the propositional truth pair.

- **All eight languages distinguish between deliberate deception and honest error.** Georgian *q̇albi* (deliberate forgery) vs. *mcdari* (honest mistake); Yoruba *irọ́* (deliberate lie) vs. *àṣìṣe* (error/mistake); Finnish *valhe/bohong* (deliberate lie) vs. *virhe* (error); Basque *gezurra* (lie) vs. *okerra* (mistake/error); Tamil *poy* (deliberate lie) vs. *tavaṟu* (mistake/error); Indonesian *bohong/dusta* (deliberate lie) vs. *keliru* (honest error); Turkish *yalan* (deliberate lie) vs. *yanlış* (error/mistake); Korean *geojitmal* (deliberate lie) vs. *olyyu* (error/fallacy). The distinction is lexically encoded in all eight languages, though the degree of morphological marking varies.

- **All eight languages have a cluster of terms for source/testimony credibility that is distinct from the cluster for propositional truth.** Georgian *sarcmuno/sando* (credible/reliable); Yoruba *fidagarri/sinesgarri* (trustworthy/believable); Finnish *luotettava/uskottava* (reliable/credible); Basque *fidagarria/sinesgarria* (trustworthy/believable); Tamil *nampakamāna/nampattakka* (reliable/credible); Indonesian *terpercaya/kredibel* (trusted/credible); Turkish *güvenilir/inanılır* (reliable/believable); Korean *sinloeseong/sinbyeongseong* (reliability/credibility). In all cases the credibility cluster is distinct from the truth-value cluster, and in several languages (Finnish, Basque, Yoruba) a further distinction is drawn between source reliability and content believability.

- **All eight languages show a three-way distinction in the genuineness-versus-counterfeit domain: original/genuine, neutral copy/imitation, and fraudulent fake.** Georgian *namdvili* (genuine) / *asli* (original) / *q̇albi* (forged); Finnish *aito/alkuperäinen* (genuine/original) / *kopio* (neutral copy) / *jäljitelmä* (imitation) / *väärennös* (forgery); Basque *jatorrizkoa* (original) / *kopiakoa* (copy) / *faltsua* (fake); Tamil *acal* (original) / *nakal* (neutral copy) / *pōli* (fake); Indonesian *asli* (genuine/original) / *tiruan* (imitation) / *palsu* (fake); Turkish *orijinal/özgün* (original/authentic) / *taklit* (imitation) / *sahte* (fake); Korean *jinpum* (genuine article) / *mojopum* (imitation goods) / *wijopum* (forged item). The neutral intermediate position (copy/imitation without fraudulent intent) is explicitly documented in at least six of the eight languages.

- **All eight languages show an asymmetry in agentive nominalization: lexicalized agent nouns for liars and forgers are more common than lexicalized agent nouns for truth-tellers.** Georgian has *mt'q̇uari* (liar) and *gamaq̇albebeli* (forger) but no single-word truth-teller agentive. Yoruba has *aṣírọ́* (liar) but no single-word truth-teller. Finnish has *valehtelija* (liar) and *väärentäjä* (forger) but no single-word truth-teller. Basque has *gezurti* (liar) but no single-word truth-teller. Tamil has *poyyaṉ* (liar) and *vañcakaṉ* (deceiver) but no single-word truth-teller. Indonesian has *pembohong* (liar) and *pemalsu* (forger) but no single-word truth-teller. Turkish has *yalancı* (liar) and *sahteci* (forger) but no single-word truth-teller. Korean has *geojitmal-jaengi* (liar) and *wijobom* (forger) but no single-word truth-teller. In all eight languages the concept of "truth-teller" requires periphrastic expression.

- **All eight languages show a proverbial or formulaic construction asserting the ultimate triumph or indestructibility of truth.** Georgian *simarṭle gamarjvebs* (truth prevails); Yoruba *òtítọ́ kò ní kú* (truth will not die); Finnish *totuus tulee ilmi* (the truth comes out); Basque *egia beti ateratzen da azalera* (the truth always comes to the surface); Tamil *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail); Indonesian *kebenaran akan terungkap pada akhirnya* (the truth will come out in the end); Turkish *doğruluk her zaman ödüllendirilmez* (truthfulness is not always rewarded — a more pessimistic variant, but the proverbial frame is present); Korean *jinsil-eun hanada* (there is only one truth). The specific framing varies (triumph, indestructibility, singularity), but the proverbial register is present in all eight languages.

### Major Divergences (patterns observed in only a few languages or showing strong cross-language differences)

- **The degree of morphological productivity of the primary truth root varies enormously across languages.** Turkish *doğru* and Georgian *marṭl-* generate the largest derivational families, extending into technical domains (Turkish: *doğrusal*, linear; *doğrultu*, direction; *doğrulanabilirlik*, verifiability), religious domains (Georgian: *marṭlmadidebeli*, Orthodox Christian; *marṭlsaidumlo*, true sacrament), and institutional domains (Georgian: *marṭlmsajuleba*, justice system; *marṭlc̣era*, orthography). By contrast, Yoruba *tọ́* and Tamil *uṇmai* generate smaller derivational families, with the Tamil family concentrated in adjectival, adverbial, and verbal noun forms rather than compound institutional terms.

- **The legality/legitimacy distinction is explicitly documented in Turkish, Georgian, and Korean but is less prominent in the other languages.** Turkish explicitly contrasts *yasal* (conforming to written law) with *meşru* (legitimate, potentially beyond strict legality). Georgian explicitly contrasts *kanonieri* (lawful, tied to positive law) with *legitimuri* (legitimate, with possible normative dimension beyond legality). Korean explicitly contrasts *hapbeop* (lawful) with *jeongdang* (legitimate/justified). In Basque, *zilegi* (lawful/permissible) and *baliozko* (valid) are documented but the legality/legitimacy distinction is less explicitly drawn. In Finnish, Indonesian, and Yoruba, the distinction is present but less prominently documented.

- **The appearance-versus-reality contrast is lexically encoded with dedicated terms in Georgian, Basque, and Finnish, but is handled more periphrastically in other languages.** Georgian *močvenebiti* (apparent/seeming/ostensible) and *piqtiuri* (fictitious/sham) are dedicated terms for the appearance-without-reality contrast. Basque *itxurazko* (apparent/seeming) and *itxurakeria* (pretense/hypocrisy) form a dedicated cluster. Finnish *näennäinen* (apparent/seeming/ostensible) is a dedicated term. In Yoruba, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean, the contrast is expressed through constructions (*itxuraz*, *sebenarnya*, *aslında*, *uṇmaiyil*) rather than dedicated adjectives.

- **The inner-state authenticity concept (sincerity/genuineness of feeling or intention, as opposed to performance) is most prominently developed in Korean and Georgian.** Korean *jinjeongseong* (authenticity/genuineness of inner state) is documented as a prominent contemporary public discourse term with its own evaluative construction (*jinjeongseong itda/eopda*). Georgian *gulc̣rpeli* (sincere/straight-hearted) and *tvaltmaqcoba* (eye-deceiving/hypocrisy) form a dedicated sincerity/hypocrisy pair. In other languages, sincerity is covered by more general terms: Yoruba *ìwà rere* (good character), Finnish *rehellinen* (honest), Basque *benetakoa* (genuine/sincere), Tamil *nērmai* (honesty/integrity), Indonesian *tulus/ikhlas* (sincere/selfless), Turkish *sahici* (genuine/real in an affective sense).

- **The philosophical/theological register for truth is most prominently developed in Georgian, Turkish, and Indonesian.** Georgian *č̣ešmariteba* is the dedicated philosophical and theological truth term, contrasting with the more everyday *simarṭle*. Turkish *hakikat* (from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*) is the elevated philosophical/religious truth term, contrasting with the more everyday *gerçek* and *doğru*. Indonesian *hakikat/hakiki* (from the same Arabic root) occupies a similar elevated register. In Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, and Korean, the philosophical register is handled by the same terms as everyday truth discourse, without a dedicated elevated register term.

- **The verification/authentication cluster is most elaborately developed in Basque and Finnish.** Basque has a dedicated verbal stem *egiazta-* generating *egiaztatu* (to verify), *egiaztapen* (attestation), *egiaztagarria* (verifiable), *egiaztaezina* (unverifiable), *egiaztagaitza* (difficult to verify), and *egiaztatze-agiri* (verification document). Finnish has *todentaa* (to verify/authenticate), *todistaa* (to prove/testify), *tarkistaa* (to check), and *tarkastaa* (to inspect/audit), with explicit register distinctions between them. In other languages, verification is handled by more general terms or light-verb constructions without a dedicated verification stem.

- **The Islamic scholarly register for authenticity (*sahih*, from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*) appears in Turkish, Indonesian, and Basque but not in the other languages.** Turkish *sahih* (sound/authentic, restricted to Islamic hadith scholarship), Indonesian *sahih* (sound/correct, primarily in Islamic and academic methodology contexts), and Basque *sahih* (not documented — but the Arabic root appears in *sah/absah* in Indonesian and Turkish) share an Arabic-origin authenticity term that is absent from Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Tamil, and Korean. This reflects the historical influence of Islamic scholarship on these languages.

### Asymmetries, Gaps, and Irregularities

- **The *-hada/-doeda* distinction in Korean (active/agentive vs. stative/resultant predication) has no direct parallel in the other languages.** Korean documents a systematic semantic distinction between *jinsil-hada* (to be truthful, active/performed quality) and *jinsil-doeda* (to be truthful, inherent/resultant quality), and between *wijo-hada* (to forge, agentive) and *wijo-doeda* (to be forged, passive/resultant). This morphological distinction between performed and inherent quality is not documented in the other seven languages.

- **The *doğrulamak/yanlışlamak* parallel morphological pair in Turkish, encoding the logical positivist/Popperian verification/falsification distinction, is unique in the dataset.** No other language documents a morphologically parallel pair of verbs specifically encoding the philosophical distinction between verifying and falsifying a hypothesis. Finnish has *todistaa* (to prove) and *osoittaa epätodeksi* (to show to be false), but these are not morphologically parallel. Basque has *frogatu* (to prove) and *gezurtatu* (to give the lie to), but these are not a parallel pair encoding the same philosophical distinction.

- **The *salah* + [verb/noun] misaction compound pattern in Indonesian is unique in the dataset.** Indonesian documents a highly productive pattern in which *salah* (wrong/mistaken) combines with verbs and nouns to generate misaction compounds: *salah paham* (misunderstanding), *salah kaprah* (widespread misconception), *salah sasaran* (off-target), *salah tafsir* (misinterpretation), *salah langkah* (misstep), *salah alamat* (wrong address/barking up the wrong tree). No other language in the dataset shows this degree of productivity in misaction compounding from a single error root.

- **The *sarwmunoeba* (credibility/faith) semantic bifurcation in Georgian — where the same form means "credibility" in secular contexts and "faith/religion" in theological contexts — is unique in the dataset.** No other language documents a single form that covers both epistemic credibility and religious faith as its primary meanings in different registers.

- **The *ìwà* (character/nature) cluster in Yoruba, which treats character evaluation as a distinct semantic domain organized around the concept of "the manner of being," has no direct structural parallel in the other languages.** While all languages have terms for honesty and dishonesty, Yoruba uniquely organizes character evaluation around a single abstract nominal (*ìwà*) that generates a large cluster of compounds and collocations (*ìwà rere*, *ìwà burúkú*, *ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́*, *ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin*, *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn*). The proverb *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (character is what makes a person) is the clearest statement of this organizing principle.

- **The *kebenaran formal/kebenaran materiil* (formal truth/material truth) legal distinction in Indonesian is unique in the dataset.** This distinction between truth established through proper legal procedure and truth as the actual facts of a case is documented as a technical term in Indonesian criminal procedure. No other language in the dataset documents an equivalent formal/material truth distinction as a technical legal term, though the underlying conceptual distinction (procedural correctness vs. substantive accuracy) is present in the legal vocabulary of several languages.

- **The *ziurrenik* (probably/most likely) semantic shift in Basque — where a form derived from *ziur* (certain) functions in practice as a probability hedge rather than an assertion of full certainty — is unique in the dataset.** No other language documents a comparable semantic shift from certainty to probability within the same derivational family. The data notes this as a fully established shift rather than a marginal usage.
