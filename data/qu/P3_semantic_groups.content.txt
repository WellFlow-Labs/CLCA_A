# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## Natural Semantic Groups

### GROUP 1: Truth-Asserting Terms

**Label:** Terms marking correspondence to reality

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to assert that statements, objects, or situations correspond to actual reality rather than being false, deceptive, or merely apparent. These terms are used when affirming accuracy, genuineness, or authenticity, and frequently appear in contexts where truth is being established, verified, or contrasted with falsehood.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqan (#1) — true, real, genuine
- chiqap (#2) — truly, really (adverbial)
- chiqamanta (#26) — from truth, truly
- chiqan kay (P1b) — to be true, truth, reality
- chiqan simi (P1b) — true word, truthful speech
- chiqan runa (P1b) — truthful person, honest person
- chiqanta rimay (P2b) — to speak the truth
- chiqanta willakuy (P2b) — to tell the truth
- chiqanpi kay (P2b) — to be in truth, to be in reality
- cheqan (#10) — straight, direct, correct
- suti (#13) — clear, evident, obvious
- sutimi kay (P2b) — it is clear, it is evident

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- chanin (#7) — valuable, worthy, legitimate (overlaps with legitimacy/validation terms)
- chiqan kaq (P1b/P2b) — that which is true (overlaps with substantivizing constructions)

---

### GROUP 2: Falsehood and Deception Terms

**Label:** Terms marking non-correspondence to reality and deception

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to characterize statements, objects, or persons as false, deceptive, or not corresponding to reality. These terms appear in contexts of moral judgment about dishonesty, identification of false claims, and warnings against deception. They contrast directly with truth-asserting terms.

**Representative expressions:**
- llulla (#3) — false, lie, deceptive
- llullakuy (#4) — to lie, to deceive
- llullakuq (P2a) — liar, one who lies
- llulla simi (P1b) — lying words, false speech
- llulla runa (P1b) — lying person, deceiver
- llullata rimay (P2b) — to speak lies
- llullata willakuy (P2b) — to tell lies
- llullawan kay (P2b) — to be with lies
- llulla kay (P1b) — to be false, falsehood
- llulla kaq (P1b/P2b) — that which is false, the fake
- ama llullakychu (P2b) — don't lie

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ch'usaq (#20) — empty, hollow, vain (overlaps with emptiness/lack terms)
- ch'usaq simi (P1b) — empty words, vain talk

---

### GROUP 3: Error and Mistake Terms

**Label:** Terms marking unintentional incorrectness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to characterize mistakes, errors, and being wrong without necessarily implying intentional deception. These terms appear in contexts of correction, learning, and acknowledging incorrect judgments or actions. They differ from deception terms in not implying deliberate dishonesty.

**Representative expressions:**
- pantay (#11) — to err, to make a mistake
- pantasqa (#12) — mistaken, erroneous
- pantayta ruray (P2b) — to make a mistake
- ama pantaychu (P2b) — don't make a mistake
- pantasqa kay (P2b) — to be mistaken

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- llulla yachay (P1b) — false knowledge, mistaken belief (could be error or deception)

---

### GROUP 4: Positive Evaluation Terms

**Label:** Terms marking general goodness and correctness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used for broad positive evaluation across moral, practical, and epistemic domains. These terms indicate that something meets standards of correctness, quality, or appropriateness, and are used in everyday contexts for approval, proper conduct, and functional adequacy. They are not restricted to truth-evaluation but include it.

**Representative expressions:**
- allin (#5) — good, well, correct
- allin kay (P1b) — to be good, goodness
- allin runa (P1b) — good person
- allin simi (P1b) — good words, proper speech
- allinta ruray (P2b) — to do good, to do well
- allinmi kay (P2b) — it is good
- allinchay (P2a) — to make good, to improve
- allinyay (P2a) — to become good, to improve
- allin kawsay (P1b) — good life, proper living

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- allin yachay (P1b) — good knowledge (overlaps with knowledge terms)
- allin ñan (P1b) — good path, right way (overlaps with directness/correctness)

---

### GROUP 5: Negative Evaluation Terms

**Label:** Terms marking general badness and incorrectness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions formed through negation of positive evaluation terms, used to indicate that something fails to meet standards or is improper. These are the primary means of expressing negative evaluation in everyday contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- mana allin (#6) — not good, bad, wrong
- mana allin kay (P2b) — to not be good, to be bad
- mana allin rikch'akuy (P2b) — to seem bad

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 6: Knowledge and Epistemic Terms

**Label:** Terms marking knowing versus thinking

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that distinguish between established knowledge (what is known with certainty) and subjective mental processes (thinking, believing, remembering). These terms are used in contexts where epistemic status is being evaluated or where the basis of claims is being established.

**Representative expressions:**
- yachay (#8) — to know, knowledge
- yachanta yachay (P2b) — to know well
- chiqanta yachay (P2b) — to know the truth
- mana yachay (P2b) — to not know, ignorance
- yuyay (#9) — to think, to remember, thought
- yuyaypi kay (P2b) — to be in thought, to be in mind
- yuyayman hamuy (P2b) — to come to mind

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- yachanayay (#24) — to be knowable (marginal status)
- musyay (#27) — to perceive, to sense, to realize (overlaps with perception terms)
- riqsiy (#28) — to know by acquaintance, to recognize (different type of knowing)

---

### GROUP 7: Perception and Appearance Terms

**Label:** Terms marking how things appear or seem

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to describe how things appear, seem, or are perceived, often with implications about whether appearance corresponds to reality. These terms are used when evaluating whether something is what it appears to be or when describing resemblance.

**Representative expressions:**
- rikch'ay (#14) — to resemble, to appear, to seem
- rikch'akuy (#15) — to seem, to appear
- chiqanman rikch'ay (P2b) — to resemble truth, to appear true
- llullaman rikch'ay (P2b) — to resemble lies, to appear false
- allin rikch'akuy (P2b) — to seem good
- musyay (#27) — to perceive, to sense, to realize
- riqsiy (#28) — to recognize, to identify

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- suti (#13) — clear, evident (overlaps with truth-asserting terms)
- sutita rikuy (P2b) — to see clearly

---

### GROUP 8: Verification and Authentication Terms

**Label:** Terms marking processes of establishing truth

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used when actively testing, verifying, or establishing the truth or authenticity of something. These terms appear in investigative, legal, and administrative contexts where evidence or proof is being sought or established.

**Representative expressions:**
- takyay (#23) — to verify, to test, to prove
- chiqanchay (P2a) — to verify, to authenticate
- chiqanchakuy (P2a) — to verify for oneself
- chiqanta takyay (P2b) — to verify the truth
- chanin (#7) — valuable, worthy, legitimate, valid
- chaninmi kay (P2b) — it is valid, it is legitimate
- chaninta quy (P2b) — to give value, to validate

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 9: Completeness and Wholeness Terms

**Label:** Terms marking fullness versus emptiness

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that evaluate whether something is complete, whole, and genuine versus incomplete, empty, or lacking substance. These terms can apply to physical objects, statements, or abstract qualities, and are used to assess whether something has all its essential elements.

**Representative expressions:**
- hunt'a (#17) — full, complete, whole
- hunt'a kay (P2b) — to be complete, to be whole
- mana hunt'a kay (P2b) — to be incomplete
- ch'ulla (#16) — single, odd, incomplete
- ch'usaq (#20) — empty, hollow, vain
- ch'usaq simi (P1b) — empty words, vain talk
- ch'usaq kay (P2b) — to be empty, to be hollow

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 10: Existence and Being Terms

**Label:** Terms marking what exists versus what doesn't

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that mark whether something actually exists or is real versus being non-existent or unreal. These terms are used to distinguish what has actual being from what is merely imagined, claimed, or false.

**Representative expressions:**
- kaq (#21) — being, existing, that which is
- chiqan kaq (P1b/P2b) — that which is true, the real one
- llulla kaq (P1b/P2b) — that which is false, the fake
- allin kaq (P1b/P2b) — that which is good
- mana kaq (#22) — not being, non-existent
- mana kaq ima (P2b) — non-existent thing

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 11: Directness and Straightness Terms

**Label:** Terms marking straight/direct versus crooked/indirect

**Definition:** This group includes expressions that describe physical straightness or directional correctness, which extend metaphorically to moral uprightness and proper conduct. These terms are used both for literal paths and for metaphorical evaluation of behavior and methods.

**Representative expressions:**
- cheqan (#10) — straight, direct, correct
- chiqan ñan (P1b) — true path, right way
- llulla ñan (P1b) — false path, wrong way
- allin ñan (P1b) — good path, right way
- cheqan ñanpi purin (P2b) — to walk on the straight path

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 12: Emphatic and Intensifying Particles

**Label:** Terms used to strengthen truth claims

**Definition:** This group includes particles and adverbial forms used to emphasize or intensify the truth or certainty of statements. These appear in discourse to strengthen assertions and confirm the speaker's commitment to what is being said.

**Representative expressions:**
- k'ari (#18) — truly, really, indeed (emphatic)
- k'arimi (P2b) — truly indeed, it is certainly so
- chiqap k'ari (P2b) — truly indeed, really and truly
- chiqapmi (P2b) — it is truly so (with direct evidential)
- chiqapsi (P2b) — it is truly so (with reportative evidential)
- chiqapcha (P2b) — it seems to be truly so (with conjectural evidential)

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- chiqanlla (P1b) — only the truth, purely true (limitative rather than emphatic)

---

### GROUP 13: Speech and Communication Terms

**Label:** Constructions involving truthful or false speech

**Definition:** This group includes multi-word constructions that combine truth/falsity terms with verbs of speaking, telling, or communicating. These are used when the focus is on the act of communication and its truthfulness rather than on abstract truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqanta rimay (P2b) — to speak the truth
- chiqanta willakuy (P2b) — to tell the truth
- chiqanta niy (P2b) — to say the truth
- chiqan rimay (P1b) — to speak truthfully
- chiqan willakuy (P1b) — truthful account
- llullata rimay (P2b) — to speak lies
- llullata willakuy (P2b) — to tell lies
- llulla rimay (P1b) — to speak falsely
- chiqan simi (P1b) — true word, truthful speech
- llulla simi (P1b) — lying words, false speech

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

---

### GROUP 14: Character and Disposition Terms

**Label:** Terms characterizing persons as truthful or deceptive

**Definition:** This group includes expressions used to evaluate people's character regarding truthfulness, honesty, and sincerity. These terms describe habitual dispositions rather than individual acts, and are used in moral evaluation and social judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- chiqan runa (P1b) — truthful person, honest person
- llulla runa (P1b) — lying person, deceiver
- llullakuq (P2a) — liar, one who lies
- allin runa (P1b) — good person
- chiqan sunqu (P1b) — true heart, sincere heart
- llulla sunqu (P1b) — false heart, insincere heart
- allin sunqu (P1b) — good heart, kind heart

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- None clearly identified

## Items Not Clearly Grouped

### Marginal or Context-Dependent Items:

1. **qhapaq (#19)** — rich, noble, genuine, powerful
   - Historically significant but usage in truth/falsity domain unclear from data
   - May relate to authenticity but primary meanings are social/economic

2. **imayna (#25)** — how, what kind, what manner
   - Interrogative term used in evaluating nature of things
   - Functions as a question word rather than assertion or evaluation
   - Constructions like imaynataq kay (P2b) show use in truth-seeking but not truth-asserting

3. **yachanayay (#24)** — to be knowable, to be ascertainable
   - Marked as [MARGINAL] in original data
   - Potential derivation indicating possibility of knowledge
   - Limited attestation in materials

### Items with Multiple Group Membership:

1. **chanin (#7)** — valuable, worthy, legitimate, valid
   - Belongs to both truth-asserting contexts (Group 1) and verification contexts (Group 8)
   - Primary function appears to be legitimacy/validation rather than simple truth

2. **musyay (#27)** — to perceive, to sense, to realize
   - Overlaps between knowledge terms (Group 6) and perception terms (Group 7)
   - Involves both perceptual and cognitive dimensions

3. **riqsiy (#28)** — to know by acquaintance, to recognize
   - Overlaps between knowledge terms (Group 6) and perception terms (Group 7)
   - Distinct type of knowing from yachay

4. **ch'usaq (#20)** and **ch'usaq simi** — empty, hollow, vain / empty words
   - Belongs to both falsehood terms (Group 2) and completeness terms (Group 9)
   - Metaphorical extension from physical emptiness to lack of truth/substance

## Notes on Grouping Patterns

1. **Asymmetry in group sizes:** Groups vary considerably in size, with speech constructions (Group 13) and positive evaluation terms (Group 4) being particularly large, while error terms (Group 3) and negative evaluation terms (Group 5) are smaller.

2. **Productive construction patterns:** Several groups (especially Groups 1, 2, 13, 14) are heavily populated by compositional constructions from P1b and P2b rather than simplex forms, suggesting these semantic areas are elaborated through composition.

3. **Negation as grouping principle:** Group 5 consists entirely of negated forms of Group 4 terms, showing that negation is a primary strategy for creating semantic opposites.

4. **Evidential integration:** Group 12 shows how evidential suffixes integrate with truth-asserting terms, but this is a grammatical pattern rather than a semantic grouping per se.

5. **Metaphorical extensions:** Group 11 shows clear metaphorical extension from spatial/physical domain (straight paths) to moral domain (correct conduct), but the grouping is based on shared usage patterns rather than assumed metaphor theory.

6. **No clear antonym pairs:** While some groups contrast (Groups 1 vs 2, Groups 4 vs 5), the language does not show systematic binary opposition across all semantic areas. Many terms lack clear opposites.
