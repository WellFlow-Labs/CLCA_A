# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

**1.**
**Form:** doğru
**Transliteration:** doğru
**Literal meaning:** straight, direct (from Proto-Turkic root)
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Used across everyday, moral, and logical contexts to assert that a statement or action conforms to fact or norm. Participates in productive derivation: *doğruluk* (truth, correctness), *doğrulamak* (to verify, to confirm), *doğrultmak* (to straighten, to correct).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** gerçek
**Transliteration:** gerçek
**Literal meaning:** real, genuine (of uncertain but old Turkic origin)
**Typical English translations:** real, true, genuine, actual
**Usage:** Refers to what is real or factually the case, often contrasted with appearance or fiction. Used in philosophical, journalistic, and everyday registers; also functions as a noun meaning "the truth" or "reality." Participates in *gerçeklik* (reality), *gerçekleşmek* (to come true, to materialize).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** hakikat
**Transliteration:** hakikat
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqīqa* — the essential nature of a thing, truth
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the true nature of something
**Usage:** Carries a more formal, philosophical, or literary weight than *gerçek*; often used in religious, ethical, and metaphysical discourse. Appears in expressions like *hakikati söylemek* (to tell the truth) and *hakikate ulaşmak* (to reach the truth).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** hak
**Transliteration:** hak
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqq* — right, due, truth, God (in Islamic usage)
**Typical English translations:** right, truth, justice, entitlement
**Usage:** Polysemous term covering both "what is true" and "what is rightfully owed"; used in legal, moral, and religious contexts. Appears in *haklı* (in the right, justified), *haksız* (unjust, wrong), *hak vermek* (to concede that someone is right).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** haklı
**Transliteration:** haklı
**Literal meaning:** *hak* + adjectival suffix *-lı* — "having right/truth"
**Typical English translations:** right, justified, in the right
**Usage:** Predicates that a person's claim, position, or action is correct or warranted. Commonly used in interpersonal and legal evaluation: *Haklısın* ("You are right/justified"). Contrasts with *haksız* (unjust, wrong, unwarranted).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** yanlış
**Transliteration:** yanlış
**Literal meaning:** from *yan* (side) + *-lış*; "going to the side," hence erroneous
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false
**Usage:** The primary term for incorrectness or error in statements, actions, or judgments. Used in everyday, educational, and moral contexts. Participates in *yanlışlık* (error, mistake), *yanlışlamak* (to falsify, to show to be wrong).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** sahte
**Transliteration:** sahte
**Literal meaning:** from Persian *sāḵte* — made, fabricated, hence counterfeit
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged
**Usage:** Applied to objects, documents, identities, or claims that are fabricated or not what they purport to be. Common in legal and commercial contexts: *sahte para* (counterfeit money), *sahte kimlik* (false identity). Does not typically apply to abstract propositions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** sahici
**Transliteration:** sahici
**Literal meaning:** from *sahih* (Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ*, sound, authentic) + *-ci*; colloquially "the real thing"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real
**Usage:** Informal and colloquial term for authenticity or genuineness, often used to distinguish something real from an imitation. Frequently used in everyday speech and informal writing; less common in formal or legal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** özgün
**Transliteration:** özgün
**Literal meaning:** *öz* (essence, self) + *-gün*; "of one's own essence," original
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic, unique
**Usage:** Used primarily in aesthetic and intellectual contexts to describe works, ideas, or styles that are not derivative. Appears in *özgünlük* (originality, authenticity) and is common in literary criticism and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** orijinal
**Transliteration:** orijinal
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from French/English *original*
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic (of an object)
**Usage:** Used especially in commercial and material contexts to distinguish an authentic item from a copy or imitation: *orijinal ürün* (original/genuine product). Overlaps with *özgün* but tends toward the concrete rather than the creative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** gerçekçi
**Transliteration:** gerçekçi
**Literal meaning:** *gerçek* (real) + *-çi* (agentive/adjectival suffix) — "one who deals in the real"
**Typical English translations:** realistic, realist
**Usage:** Describes persons, attitudes, or representations that are grounded in reality rather than idealization or fantasy. Used in aesthetic, political, and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in *gerçekçilik* (realism).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** doğrulamak
**Transliteration:** doğrulamak
**Literal meaning:** *doğru* (correct/true) + causative/factitive *-la-* + infinitive *-mak* — "to make true/correct"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to validate, to authenticate
**Usage:** Used in contexts of checking, confirming, or establishing the truth or correctness of a statement, document, or identity. Common in administrative, journalistic, and scientific registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** yanlışlamak
**Transliteration:** yanlışlamak
**Literal meaning:** *yanlış* (wrong/false) + *-la-* + *-mak* — "to make wrong," to falsify
**Typical English translations:** to falsify, to refute, to disprove
**Usage:** Used in logical, scientific, and argumentative contexts to describe the act of showing something to be false or incorrect. The term is used in Turkish translations of philosophy of science (notably in discussions of Popper's falsificationism).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** kanıtlamak
**Transliteration:** kanıtlamak
**Literal meaning:** *kanıt* (proof, evidence) + *-la-* + *-mak* — "to make into proof"
**Typical English translations:** to prove, to demonstrate, to establish
**Usage:** Used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts for the act of providing proof or demonstration. Participates in *kanıt* (evidence, proof) and *kanıtlanmış* (proven, established).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ispat
**Transliteration:** ispat
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ithbāt* — establishment, affirmation, proof
**Typical English translations:** proof, demonstration, verification
**Usage:** Primarily used in legal and formal contexts for the act or result of proving something. *İspat yükü* (burden of proof) is a standard legal expression. Somewhat more formal than *kanıt*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** güvenilir
**Transliteration:** güvenilir
**Literal meaning:** *güven* (trust) + passive *-il-* + potential *-ir* — "that can be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, or information to indicate that they can be depended upon. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. Contrasts with *güvenilmez* (unreliable, untrustworthy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** güvenilmez
**Transliteration:** güvenilmez
**Literal meaning:** *güven* + passive *-il-* + negative potential *-mez* — "that cannot be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
**Usage:** The direct negative counterpart of *güvenilir*; used to evaluate sources, witnesses, persons, or claims as lacking credibility or dependability. Appears in legal testimony evaluation and journalistic source assessment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** inanılır
**Transliteration:** inanılır
**Literal meaning:** *inan-* (to believe) + passive *-ıl-* + potential *-ır* — "that can be believed"
**Typical English translations:** believable, credible, plausible
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether a claim, story, or person is credible or plausible. Somewhat less formal than *güvenilir* and more focused on the believability of a specific claim or account.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** meşru
**Transliteration:** meşru
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *mašrūʿ* — lawful, sanctioned, legitimate
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, valid
**Usage:** Used in legal, political, and moral contexts to evaluate whether an action, authority, or claim is sanctioned by law, norm, or recognized principle. Participates in *meşruiyet* (legitimacy) and *meşrulaştırmak* (to legitimize).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** geçerli
**Transliteration:** geçerli
**Literal meaning:** *geçer* (passes, is accepted) + *-li* — "having currency/acceptance"
**Typical English translations:** valid, current, accepted, in force
**Usage:** Used to evaluate whether something (a document, argument, rule, or claim) is currently accepted, in force, or logically sound. *Geçerli bir argüman* (a valid argument); *geçerli pasaport* (valid passport). Participates in *geçerlilik* (validity).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** geçersiz
**Transliteration:** geçersiz
**Literal meaning:** *geçer* + *-siz* (privative suffix) — "without currency/acceptance"
**Typical English translations:** invalid, void, null, not accepted
**Usage:** The direct antonym of *geçerli*; used in legal, administrative, and logical contexts to declare something null, void, or not accepted. *Geçersiz oy* (invalid ballot); *geçersiz sözleşme* (void contract).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** özgürlük
**Transliteration:** özgürlük
**Literal meaning:** *özgür* (free) + *-lük* (abstract noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** freedom, liberty
**Usage:** Included here as a borderline case: in philosophical and political discourse, *özgürlük* is evaluated against criteria of authenticity and genuine agency, making it adjacent to authenticity vocabulary. However, its primary meaning is freedom rather than genuineness; marked accordingly.
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

**23.**
**Form:** asıl
**Transliteration:** asıl
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *aṣl* — root, origin, foundation, original
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, fundamental, main
**Usage:** Used to indicate the original or fundamental version of something, as opposed to a copy or derivative: *asıl belge* (original document), *asıl mesele* (the real/fundamental issue). Also used predicatively to assert what something truly is.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**24.**
**Form:** hakiki
**Transliteration:** hakiki
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ḥaqīqī* — of or pertaining to *ḥaqīqa* (truth, essence)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, true, authentic
**Usage:** A formal or literary adjective meaning genuine or truly what it claims to be. Used in contexts of authentication and philosophical assertion: *hakiki deri* (genuine leather), *hakiki dostluk* (true friendship). Somewhat more formal than *gerçek*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**25.**
**Form:** sahih
**Transliteration:** sahih
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *ṣaḥīḥ* — sound, correct, authentic
**Typical English translations:** authentic, sound, valid, correct
**Usage:** Used primarily in Islamic scholarly and religious contexts to evaluate the authenticity or soundness of hadith and religious texts. In general Turkish usage it is rare and domain-specific, though it survives in some formal and archaic registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**26.**
**Form:** taklitçi
**Transliteration:** taklitçi
**Literal meaning:** *taklit* (imitation, from Arabic *taqlīd*) + *-çi* (agentive) — "one who imitates"
**Typical English translations:** imitator, counterfeiter, fake
**Usage:** Applied to persons who copy or imitate, often with a negative connotation of inauthenticity or deception. Used in aesthetic, commercial, and moral evaluations. The base noun *taklit* (imitation, counterfeit) is itself widely used.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** taklit
**Transliteration:** taklit
**Literal meaning:** from Arabic *taqlīd* — following, imitation
**Typical English translations:** imitation, counterfeit, copy, fake
**Usage:** Used to describe objects, performances, or behaviors that copy or simulate something else, often implying inauthenticity. *Taklit ürün* (counterfeit/imitation product); *taklit etmek* (to imitate, to counterfeit). Widely used in commercial, legal, and aesthetic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**28.**
**Form:** yanıltıcı
**Transliteration:** yanıltıcı
**Literal meaning:** *yanılt-* (causative of *yanıl-*, to err) + *-ıcı* (agentive/adjectival) — "causing to err"
**Typical English translations:** misleading, deceptive, fallacious
**Usage:** Applied to statements, representations, or actions that lead others into error or false belief, whether intentionally or not. Common in legal (consumer protection, advertising law) and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** aldatıcı
**Transliteration:** aldatıcı
**Literal meaning:** *aldat-* (causative of *alda-*, to deceive) + *-ıcı* — "causing deception"
**Typical English translations:** deceptive, fraudulent, misleading
**Usage:** Stronger than *yanıltıcı* in implying intentional deception; used in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. *Aldatıcı reklam* (deceptive advertising); *aldatıcı davranış* (deceptive conduct).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**30.**
**Form:** özü sözü bir
**Transliteration:** özü sözü bir
**Literal meaning:** *öz* (essence/inner self) + *söz* (word/speech) + *bir* (one) — "whose inner self and word are one"
**Typical English translations:** sincere, authentic, a person of integrity, one who means what they say
**Usage:** A fixed idiomatic expression used to evaluate a person as genuine and consistent between their inner convictions and outward speech or conduct. Used in moral and character evaluation; functions as a predicate or attributive phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
