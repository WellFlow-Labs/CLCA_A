# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where the data support overlap between groups, this is noted explicitly. Where items do not fit cleanly into any group, they are listed separately at the end. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data as documented, not a desire for symmetry or completeness.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that something is the case, with direct speaker commitment

**Label:** Direct assertive expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used by a speaker to affirm a proposition as true, correct, or as-described, with the speaker's own direct evidential commitment attached. They appear in discourse as affirmations, confirmations, and validations of prior statements or of the speaker's own claims. They are characteristically marked with the direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi*, which in Quechua signals that the speaker has first-hand knowledge of what is asserted.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqam / cheqami* — "it is true (I assert this with direct knowledge)"
- *cheqapuni* — "absolutely true, certainly real" (with emphatic *-puni*)
- *cheqataq* — "and indeed true, but truly" (contrastive/confirmatory)
- *hinam / hinami* — "it is so, that is how it is"
- *allinmi* — "it is good, it is correct"
- *sut'im / sut'imi* — "it is clear, it is evident"
- *chiqapmi* — "it is truly so, it is exactly right" [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqam, arím* (Construction 12) — paired double affirmation
- *allinmi, cheqam* (Construction 40) — paired correctness-and-truth affirmation
- *iñiymi, cheqam* (Construction 35) — paired belief-and-truth affirmation
- *yachanim / yachaninim* (Construction 28) — "I know (it), I know for certain"

**Notes:** The defining feature of this group is the presence of the direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi*, which is not merely an intensifier but a grammatical marker of the speaker's epistemic commitment. The paired constructions (Constructions 12, 35, 40) represent an attested rhetorical pattern of reinforcing an assertion by combining two evidentially marked affirmatives. *Chiqapmi* is included with the uncertainty noted in P1.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to deny that something is the case, or to contradict a prior claim

**Label:** Direct denial and negation expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to deny the truth, correctness, or accuracy of a proposition, typically in response to a prior claim or in contexts where a false impression needs to be corrected. They characteristically use the Quechua negative construction *mana...chu* and frequently carry the direct evidential *-n* on *mana*, marking the speaker's direct assertion of the denial.

**Representative expressions:**

- *manan cheqachu* (Construction 5) — "it is not true, that is not correct"
- *manan llullachu* (Construction 17) — "it is not false, it is not a lie"
- *mana hinam / manahinam* (Derived Form 19.2) — "it is not so, that is not how it is"
- *mana allin* (P1 item 18) — "not good, not correct, wrong"
- *mana cheqa* (P1b item 15) — "untrue, incorrect, false, not genuine"
- *mana cheqaq* (P1b item 16) — "not genuine, inauthentic, false"
- *mana sut'i* (P1b item 11 / Derived Form 26.7) — "unclear, ambiguous, not plain"
- *manan pantanichu* (Construction 45) — "I am not mistaken, I have not erred"
- *manan yachanichu* (Construction 29) — "I do not know, I have no knowledge of it"
- *manan iñinichu* (Construction 34) — "I do not believe it, I do not trust this"
- *manan riqsinichunim* (Construction 26) — "I do not know/recognize (this person/thing)"
- *manan llullakuyta atinichu* (Construction 23) — "I cannot bring myself to lie"

**Notes:** This group is structurally defined by the *mana...chu* negation pattern. Several items in this group function as the direct negated counterparts of items in Group 1 (e.g., *manan cheqachu* vs. *cheqam*). Some items here (e.g., *manan yachanichu*, *manan riqsinichunim*) are conventionalized testimonial formulas for denying knowledge or recognition rather than denying the truth of a proposition per se; they are included here because they function in the same discourse slot as direct denials.

## Group 3: Expressions used to characterize a person or statement as honest, genuine, or reliable

**Label:** Positive character and reliability evaluations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as genuinely what it claims or appears to be — honest, authentic, trustworthy, or accurate. They appear in moral instruction, legal testimony, and character assessments. They are applied both to persons (evaluating their integrity) and to statements or objects (evaluating their authenticity or accuracy).

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa* — "true, real, correct, genuine, right" (base adjective)
- *cheqaq* — "true, real, genuine, authentic" (participial adjective)
- *cheqa runa* — "an upright person, a person of integrity"
- *cheqa simi* — "true speech, honest word"
- *cheqa sonqo* — "sincere, honest at heart, genuine in feeling"
- *cheqa sonqowan* — "sincerely, wholeheartedly, with genuine feeling"
- *iñinapaq* — "trustworthy, believable, credible, reliable"
- *mana llulla* (P1b item 19) — "not false, not deceptive, honest, genuine"
- *mana pantasqa* (Derived Form 24.6) — "not mistaken, not in error, correct, accurate"
- *cheqachisqa* (Derived Form 4.1) — "verified, confirmed, proven"
- *sut'ichasqa* (Derived Form 26.6) — "clarified, made clear, specified"
- *allinchasqa* (Derived Form 17.1) — "corrected, fixed, made right"
- *riqsisqa* (Derived Form 11.1) — "known, recognized, identified"
- *allintan riqsini* (Construction 27) — "I know him/her/it well, I recognize clearly"
- *iñinapaqmi kay* (Construction 36) — "to be trustworthy, to be worthy of belief"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the evaluative direction (positive) and the target of evaluation (persons, statements, objects). The expressions *cheqa sonqo* and *cheqa sonqowan* are notable for locating the evaluation in the inner self (*sonqo*, heart), distinguishing inner sincerity from outward performance. *Riqsisqa* and *allintan riqsini* are included because in the attested contexts they function as reliability evaluations (confident, verified recognition) rather than merely descriptive identifications.

---

## Group 4: Expressions used to characterize a person or statement as dishonest, false, or unreliable

**Label:** Negative character and reliability evaluations

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person, statement, or object as dishonest, deceptive, inauthentic, or unreliable. They appear in moral condemnation, legal discourse, and character assessments. Like Group 3, they apply to both persons and statements, but with the opposite evaluative direction.

**Representative expressions:**

- *llulla* — "false, lying, deceptive, fake, untrue" (base adjective)
- *llullaq* — "liar, deceiver, false person" (agentive)
- *llullakuq* — "habitual liar, chronic deceiver"
- *llulla runa* — "a dishonest person, a liar"
- *llulla simi* — "false speech, lying words"
- *llulla sonqo* — "false-hearted, deceitful, insincere"
- *mana iñinapaq* — "untrustworthy, unbelievable, unreliable"
- *pantasqa* — "mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error"
- *pantaq* (Derived Form 24.2) — "one who errs, one who makes mistakes"
- *llullasqa* (Derived Form 7.5) — "deceived, having been lied to"
- *llullakusqa* (Derived Form 7.7) — "having lied, having deceived"
- *mana iñinapaqmi kay* (Construction 37) — "to be untrustworthy, to be unworthy of belief"
- *llullam, manan cheqachu* (Construction 18) — paired assertion of falsehood and denial of truth

**Notes:** This group is the evaluative mirror of Group 3. The distinction between *llullaq* (one who lies, agentive) and *llullakuq* (habitual liar, reflexive-agentive) is attested within this group, with the latter emphasizing habitual or self-directed deception. *Pantasqa* (mistaken, in error) is included here because in the attested contexts it functions as a negative reliability evaluation, though it is distinct from *llulla* in that error does not necessarily imply intent to deceive — this distinction is noted under the overlapping items section below.

---

## Group 5: Expressions used in the act of verifying, confirming, or establishing accuracy

**Label:** Verification and confirmation expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker or agent is engaged in the act of establishing whether something is accurate, confirming that it is correct, or correcting an error. They appear in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts where the accuracy of a claim, document, or statement is actively being assessed or established. They are typically verbal or action-oriented rather than purely evaluative.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqachiy* — "to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct"
- *cheqachikuy* — "to verify for oneself, to confirm, to prove oneself genuine"
- *cheqachiq* (Derived Form 4.2) — "verifier, one who confirms"
- *cheqachisqa* (Derived Form 4.1) — "verified, confirmed, proven" *(also in Group 3)*
- *cheqachiy atiy* (Construction 13) — "to be able to verify, to be capable of confirming"
- *manan cheqachiyta atinichu* (Construction 14) — "I cannot verify it, I am unable to confirm it"
- *allinchay* — "to correct, to fix, to repair, to make right"
- *allinchakuy* (Derived Form 16.6) — "to correct oneself, to improve oneself, to reform"
- *allinchaq* (Derived Form 17.2) — "one who corrects, a fixer, a repairer"
- *sut'ichay* — "to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify"
- *sut'ichakuy* (Derived Form 26.5) — "to become clear, to clarify for oneself"
- *sut'ichay atiy* (Construction 49) — "to be able to clarify, to be capable of explaining"
- *riqsichiy* (Derived Form 11.3) — "to introduce, to make known, to identify (to someone)"
- *cheqa kasqanta yachay* (Construction 7) — "to know what is truly the case"
- *llulla kasqanta riqsiy* (Construction 21) — "to recognize that it is false, to identify something as a lie"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the active, procedural character of the expressions — they describe doing something to establish or restore accuracy, rather than simply evaluating a state. The expressions *cheqachiy* and *allinchay* are the most central; *sut'ichay* is included because in the attested contexts clarification functions as a form of accuracy-establishment. *Cheqachisqa* appears in both this group and Group 3 because the past participle form describes the result of a verification process (Group 5) and simultaneously functions as a positive reliability evaluation (Group 3).

## Group 6: Expressions used to enjoin or describe truthful speech

**Label:** Enjoinment and description of truthful communication

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truthful speech is being required, described, or characterized as an action. They are used in legal testimony, moral instruction, and everyday discourse to specify that speech should be or has been honest and accurate. They are typically verbal constructions combining truth-evaluation vocabulary with speech verbs.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqanta rimay* — "to tell the truth, to speak truthfully" (conventionalized collocation)
- *cheqata rimay* (Construction 1) — "to speak the truth" (compositional variant)
- *cheqa rimay* (P1b item 9) — "to speak the truth, truthful speech"
- *cheqa simi* (P1b item 8) — "true speech, honest word"
- *cheqa willay* (P1b item 28) — "to report truthfully, a truthful account"
- *sut'ita rimay* (Construction 47 / P1b item 5) — "to speak clearly, to speak plainly"
- *sut'i simi* (P1b item 6) — "clear speech, plain language"
- *sut'i willay* (P1b item 10) — "clear report, to inform clearly"
- *allintam rimay* (Construction 38) — "to speak well/correctly, to speak properly"
- *cheqanta rimayta munay* (Construction 15) — "to want to tell the truth"
- *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan* (Construction 16) — "you must tell the truth" (deontic)
- *sut'itatam rimanayki tiyan* (Construction 50) — "you must speak clearly" (deontic)
- *cheqa kasqanta willay* (Construction 2) — "to report what is truly the case"
- *cheqa kasqanman hina rimay* (Construction 3) — "to speak in accordance with what is actually the case"
- *yachaq kaspa rimay* (Construction 30) — "to speak knowing what one is talking about"
- *cheqanta rimay* vs. *mana yachaspa rimay* (Construction 31) — the latter characterizes speech made without adequate knowledge, functioning as the negative counterpart in this context

**Notes:** This group is defined by the combination of truth-evaluation vocabulary with speech and reporting verbs (*rimay*, *willay*). The deontic constructions (Constructions 16, 50) are included because they appear in the same discourse contexts (legal testimony, moral instruction) as the descriptive expressions, and they specify the same action (truthful speech) as obligatory. *Cheqa willay* and *sut'i willay* extend the pattern from speech to reporting more broadly.

---

## Group 7: Expressions used to describe deceptive or misleading speech and action

**Label:** Characterization of deceptive communication and conduct

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe speech, reporting, or conduct that is dishonest, misleading, or deceptive. They appear in moral condemnation, legal discourse, and narrative contexts. They characterize both the act of deceiving and the manner of deceptive communication.

**Representative expressions:**

- *llullakuy* — "to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods"
- *llulla rimay* (P1b item 7) — "to speak falsely, to lie in speech, false talk"
- *llulla simi* (P1b item 6) — "false speech, lying words"
- *llulla willay* (P1b item 8) — "false report, misinformation, to report falsely"
- *llullachiy* (Derived Form 7.3) — "to deceive someone, to cause to lie, to mislead"
- *llullachikuy* (Derived Form 7.4) — "to deceive oneself, to let oneself be deceived"
- *llullayta yachay* (Construction 20 / P1b item 18) — "to know how to lie, to be skilled at deceiving"
- *llullayta saqiy* (Construction 22) — "to stop lying, to abandon falsehood"
- *llulla nispa rimay* (Construction 19) — "to speak calling it false, to speak saying it is a lie"
- *llulla tukuy* (P1b item 15) — "to pretend, to feign, to act falsely"
- *llulla hina rimay* (Construction 42) — "to speak as if lying, to speak in the manner of a liar"
- *pantachiy* (Derived Form 24.3) — "to mislead, to cause to err, to lead astray"
- *mana yachaspa rimay* (Construction 31) — "to speak without knowing, to speak in ignorance"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the characterization of deceptive or misleading communication as an action or manner. *Pantachiy* (to mislead, to cause to err) is included because in the attested contexts it describes the act of causing another to be in error, which functions alongside *llullachiy* (to deceive) in legal and moral discourse about harmful dishonesty. *Mana yachaspa rimay* (to speak without knowing) is included because it appears in the same discourse contexts as characterizations of unreliable or misleading testimony, though it does not necessarily imply intent to deceive — this is noted under overlapping items below.

---

## Group 8: Expressions used in contexts of knowing, recognizing, and believing

**Label:** Epistemic state expressions

**Definition:** These expressions describe or assert states of knowing, recognizing, or believing — the epistemic conditions that are relevant to evaluating whether a claim is reliable or whether a person is in a position to make accurate assertions. They appear in testimonial contexts (establishing whether a witness knows or recognizes something), in philosophical and religious discourse (distinguishing knowledge from belief), and in everyday discourse about the grounds for assertion.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yachay* — "to know, to learn; knowledge"
- *yachaq* (Derived Form 12.1) — "one who knows, a wise person, a learned person"
- *yachasqa* (Derived Form 12.4) — "known, learned, that which is known"
- *yachakuy* (Derived Form 12.5) — "to learn, to study, to practice"
- *mana yachay* (Derived Form 12.6) — "not knowing, ignorance"
- *mana yachaq* (Derived Form 12.7) — "one who does not know, an uninformed person"
- *yachanim / yachaninim* (Construction 28) — "I know (it), I know for certain"
- *manan yachanichu* (Construction 29) — "I do not know, I have no knowledge of it"
- *riqsiy* — "to know by acquaintance, to recognize, to identify"
- *riqsiq* (Derived Form 11.2) — "one who knows/recognizes, an acquaintance"
- *riqsisqa* (Derived Form 11.1) — "known, recognized, identified"
- *riqsikuy* (Derived Form 11.4) — "to know oneself, to introduce oneself, to identify oneself"
- *mana riqsisqa* (Derived Form 11.5) — "unknown, unrecognized, unidentified"
- *manan riqsinichunim* (Construction 26) — "I do not know/recognize this person/thing"
- *iñiy* — "to believe, to trust, to have faith in"
- *iñiq* (Derived Form 13.1) — "believer, one who trusts"
- *iñisqa* (Derived Form 13.4) — "believed, trusted, that which is believed"
- *iñikuy* (Derived Form 13.3) — "to believe for oneself, to commit to belief"
- *mana iñiy* (Derived Form 13.7) — "not to believe, disbelief, distrust"
- *manan iñinichu* (Construction 34) — "I do not believe it, I do not trust this"
- *cheqa yachay* (P1b item 12) — "true knowledge, correct understanding"
- *cheqa iñiy* (P1b item 13) — "true faith, genuine belief, sincere trust"
- *cheqa kasqanta yachay* (Construction 7) — "to know what is truly the case"
- *cheqa kasqanpi iñiy* (Construction 8) — "to believe in what is truly the case"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the description of epistemic states (knowing, recognizing, believing) rather than by evaluation of truth or falsehood per se. These expressions are relevant to the truth-evaluation domain because in the attested contexts they appear as conditions for or grounds of reliable assertion — a person who *yachaq* (knows) is in a different epistemic position from one who merely *iñiq* (believes), and this distinction is drawn in testimonial and philosophical discourse. The contrast between *yachay* (propositional knowledge) and *riqsiy* (knowledge by acquaintance/recognition) is attested in the sources as a meaningful distinction within this group.

## Group 9: Expressions used in moral evaluation of persons

**Label:** Moral character evaluations of persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate persons — rather than statements or objects — as morally honest or dishonest, upright or deceptive. They appear in moral instruction, character assessments, and social evaluations. They characterize a person's fundamental character rather than a single act or statement.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa runa* — "an upright person, a person of integrity"
- *cheqa sonqo* — "sincere, honest at heart, genuine in feeling"
- *llulla runa* — "a dishonest person, a liar"
- *llulla sonqo* — "false-hearted, deceitful, insincere"
- *llullaq* — "liar, deceiver, false person" (as a characterization of a person)
- *llullakuq* — "habitual liar, chronic deceiver"
- *cheqa runam kay* (Construction 10) — "to be a person of integrity"
- *llullaq runam* (Construction 24) — "he/she is a lying person, that person is a liar"
- *iñinapaq* — "trustworthy, believable, credible" (applied to persons)
- *mana iñinapaq* — "untrustworthy, unreliable" (applied to persons)
- *mana llulla* (P1b item 19) — "not false, not deceptive, honest" (applied to persons)

**Notes:** This group overlaps substantially with Groups 3 and 4, which also include person-directed evaluations. The distinction drawn here is that the expressions in this group are specifically and characteristically used of persons and their character, rather than of statements or objects. The *sonqo* (heart/inner self) collocations (*cheqa sonqo*, *llulla sonqo*) are particularly characteristic of this group, as they locate the evaluation in the person's inner motivations rather than their outward conduct. *Iñinapaq* and *mana iñinapaq* appear in both this group and Group 3/4 because they are applied to both persons and statements in the attested record.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used in contexts of clarity and explicitness

**Label:** Clarity and explicitness evaluations

**Definition:** These expressions evaluate statements, explanations, or communications as clear, unambiguous, and explicit — or as failing to meet this standard. They appear in legal, administrative, and instructional contexts where precision of expression is required. While related to truth-evaluation, their primary concern is the quality of communication rather than its truth-value per se.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sut'i* — "clear, plain, explicit, evident, distinct, unambiguous"
- *sut'iq* (Derived Form 26.3) — "clear, explicit, evident" (participial)
- *sut'ita* (Derived Form 26.4) — "clearly, plainly, explicitly" (adverbial)
- *sut'ichay* — "to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify"
- *sut'ichakuy* (Derived Form 26.5) — "to become clear, to clarify for oneself"
- *sut'ichasqa* (Derived Form 26.6) — "clarified, made clear, specified"
- *sut'im / sut'imi* (Derived Form 26.2) — "it is clear, it is evident"
- *mana sut'i* (Derived Form 26.7) — "unclear, ambiguous, not plain"
- *manan sut'ichu* (Construction 48) — "it is not clear, it is not plain, that is ambiguous"
- *sut'ita rimay* (Construction 47) — "to speak clearly, to speak plainly"
- *sut'i simi* (P1b item 6) — "clear speech, plain language"
- *sut'i sutin* (P1b item 7) — "its proper name, its true name, its clear designation"
- *sut'i willay* (P1b item 10) — "clear report, explicit information"
- *sut'i yachay* (P1b item 9) — "clear knowledge, explicit understanding"
- *sut'itatam rimanayki tiyan* (Construction 50) — "you must speak clearly" (deontic)

**Notes:** This group is defined by the *sut'i* root and its derivatives. The expressions in this group are related to but distinct from the *cheqa* group: *sut'i* evaluates the quality of communication (clarity, explicitness, unambiguity), while *cheqa* evaluates its truth-value (correspondence to reality, genuineness). In the attested contexts, the two qualities are associated — clear speech is expected to be truthful speech — but they are not identical, and the two roots are used in distinct evaluative contexts. *Sut'i sutin* (its proper/true name) is notable as a point of overlap between clarity and authenticity.

## Group 11: Expressions used in narrative and discourse to confirm that events or descriptions match reality

**Label:** Discourse confirmation expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used in narrative and conversational discourse to confirm that a description, characterization, or account matches how things actually are or were. They function as discourse-level validators rather than as evaluations of specific propositions. They are characteristically used in response to or in continuation of a prior description.

**Representative expressions:**

- *hinam / hinami* — "it is so, that is how it is, indeed"
- *hinam kasqa* (Construction 43) — "so it was, that is how it turned out to be"
- *hinaspa* (Derived Form 19.3) — "and so, having been thus, thereupon" (converb)
- *cheqam* used as a discourse particle (Construction 11) — validating a following proposition
- *cheqam, arím* (Construction 12) — double affirmation in conversational interaction
- *cheqataq* — "and indeed, but truly, and in fact" (contrastive/confirmatory)
- *cheqa hina* (P1b item 21) — "as if real, like the genuine article, seemingly true"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the discourse function of the expressions rather than by their lexical content. *Hinam* and *cheqam* both appear in this group, but their primary membership is in Group 1 (direct assertive expressions); they are included here because in the attested record they also function as discourse-level confirmation particles. *Hinaspa* is included because it is the converb form of *hina* and functions as a clause-linking device that carries the confirmatory meaning of *hina* into narrative structure. *Cheqa hina* is included with the note that it occupies an ambiguous position between confirmation and appearance (see overlapping items below).

## Overlapping and ambiguous items

The following items appear in multiple groups or occupy positions that do not fit cleanly into a single group. They are listed here with notes on the nature of the overlap.

---

**cheqachisqa** (Derived Form 4.1) — "verified, confirmed, proven"
- Appears in Group 3 (positive reliability evaluation) and Group 5 (verification expressions)
- The past participle form describes both the result of a verification process (Group 5) and a positive evaluative state (Group 3). In the attested contexts, both readings are present and the form moves between the two functions depending on whether the focus is on the process or the resulting state.

---

**pantasqa** (P1 item 25) — "mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error"
- Appears in Group 4 (negative reliability evaluations)
- However, *pantasqa* differs from *llulla* and its derivatives in that error does not imply intent to deceive in the attested usage. A person who is *pantasqa* has made a mistake; a person who is *llullaq* has deliberately lied. This distinction is present in the data but the two are used in overlapping evaluative contexts (both are negative reliability evaluations). *Pantasqa* does not fit cleanly into Group 4 alongside *llulla* derivatives, but no separate group is warranted by the data for this single item.

---

**mana yachaspa rimay** (Construction 31) — "to speak without knowing, to speak in ignorance"
- Appears in Group 7 (deceptive communication) and Group 8 (epistemic state expressions)
- In the attested contexts this construction appears alongside characterizations of unreliable testimony (Group 7), but it does not imply intent to deceive — it describes an epistemic failure (Group 8) that results in unreliable speech. The construction bridges the two groups.

---

**cheqa hina** (P1b item 21) — "as if real, like the genuine article, seemingly true"
- Appears in Group 11 (discourse confirmation)
- However, the attested usage is ambiguous between two readings: (1) something that is as good as the real thing (positive, confirmatory), and (2) something that merely appears to be genuine without being so (appearance vs. reality). The second reading would place this item closer to Group 7 (deceptive communication) or in a separate category of appearance/reality expressions. The data do not clearly resolve this ambiguity.

---

**llulla tukuy** (P1b item 15) — "to pretend, to feign, to act falsely"
- Appears in Group 7 (deceptive communication)
- However, as noted in P1b, this expression also appears in narrative contexts describing theatrical or ritual performance where the pretending is not morally condemned. In those contexts it does not function as a negative evaluation. The boundary between condemned deception and recognized performance is not lexically marked in the expression itself.

---

**cheqa tukuy** (P1b item 25) — "to become real, to be realized; also: to pretend to be genuine"
- This item has two attested senses that would place it in different groups: the actualization sense (Group 5, verification/realization) and the pretense sense (Group 7, deceptive communication). The two senses are present in the same form and the data do not support assigning it to one group over the other.

---

**cheqakaychiy** (Derived Form 1.6) — "to realize, to actualize, to make real, to bring about"
- This form appears in administrative and political contexts (fulfilling plans, promises) and in religious contexts (fulfilling vows). Its connection to the truth-evaluation domain is through the root *cheqa*, but its primary function in the attested contexts is actualization rather than truth-evaluation per se. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**cheqa kawsay** (P1b item 23) — "true life, genuine living, authentic existence"
**llulla kawsay** (P1b item 13) — "false life, inauthentic living, a life of deception"
**cheqa ñan** (P1b item 24) — "the right path, the true way"
**llulla ñan** (P1b item 14) — "the wrong path, the false way"
- These expressions use *cheqa* and *llulla* in combination with *kawsay* (life) and *ñan* (path/road) in moral and religious discourse. They extend the truth/falsehood opposition into domains of life-conduct and moral direction. They are related to Group 9 (moral character evaluations) but apply to ways of living rather than to persons or statements. They do not fit cleanly into any single group and are noted here as a cluster of related expressions that extend the *cheqa/llulla* opposition into a broader moral-existential domain.

---

**cheqa Dios / llulla Dios** (P1b items 14, 12) — "the true God / false god"
- These expressions are attested specifically in Christian Quechua religious discourse. They use the *cheqa/llulla* opposition in a domain (religious identity of deities) that is distinct from the truth-evaluation contexts that define the other groups. They are noted here as domain-specific extensions of the *cheqa/llulla* opposition that do not fit into the groups defined above.

## Items that do not fit into any group

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit into any of the groups identified above, based on the usage patterns documented in the sources consulted.

---

**qunqoriy** — "to kneel, to submit"
- Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P1. The connection to truth-evaluation contexts is indirect and contextual (kneeling as a posture in sworn testimony). No multi-word construction combining this item with truth-evaluation vocabulary is attested. No grouping is warranted.

---

**pura** — "pure, only, among (members of a group), itself"
- Marked [MARGINAL] in P1. The authenticity/purity sense is noted as a possible connection to the domain, but no attested constructions combining *pura* with truth-evaluation vocabulary are documented. No grouping is warranted.

---

**qispiy / qispiq / qispichiy** — "to be free, to be saved, to be clear/pure; one who is free/innocent; to free, to save"
- Marked [MARGINAL] in P1 and P2a. The connection to the truth-evaluation domain is through legal and moral contexts of innocence and blamelessness, but the primary attested function is in religious discourse (salvation). The expressions do not participate in the usage patterns that define any of the groups above.

---

**qillqasqa / mana qillqasqa** — "written, document; unwritten"
- Marked [MARGINAL] in P1. These expressions are relevant to legal and administrative contexts where written records are evaluated for accuracy, but they do not themselves encode evaluation. They function as the object of evaluation rather than as evaluative expressions. No grouping is warranted.

---

**kallpachay / kallpachasqa** — "to strengthen, to confirm, to reinforce; strengthened, confirmed"
- Marked [UNCERTAIN] in P1 and P2a. The connection to truth-evaluation is indirect (confirming or giving authority to a statement or decision). The primary meaning is strengthening or reinforcing rather than truth-evaluation. No grouping is warranted.

---

**runa** — "person, human being"
- Marked [MARGINAL] in P1, included only for its participation in *cheqa runa* and *llulla runa*. As a standalone item it does not encode evaluation and does not belong to any group. Its participation in the collocations is captured in Groups 3, 4, and 9.
