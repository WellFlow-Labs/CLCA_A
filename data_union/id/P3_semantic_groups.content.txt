# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Indonesian (id)
# Layer: P3_semantic_groups

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns documented in P1 and P2: collocational partners, register distributions, contrastive pairs, derivational productivity, and the contexts in which terms appear together or are distinguished from one another. No external framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns do not support a clean grouping, this is stated explicitly. Items may appear in more than one group where their usage genuinely spans multiple patterns.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to evaluate statements and claims as true or false

**Label:** Factual correctness evaluation

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a proposition, report, or claim corresponds to what is actually the case. They appear in contexts of verification, denial, correction, and acknowledgment — in journalism, legal proceedings, everyday conversation, and formal discourse. The central contrast in this group is between terms that affirm correspondence with reality and terms that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *benar* — the unmarked positive evaluator; used to affirm that a statement or claim is correct; appears in confirmations, corrections, and denials (*tidak benar*, *benar adanya*, *memang benar demikian*); participates in constructions such as *memang benar bahwa...* (it is indeed true that...) and *sudah terbukti benar* (already proven true)
- *tidak benar* — standard negation; used in official denials and corrections (*informasi itu tidak benar*); also functions to characterize a claim as factually incorrect rather than deliberately false
- *salah* — primary antonym of *benar*; evaluates a statement as incorrect; the primary negative counterpart to *benar* in factual contexts (*tidak benar* and *salah* are both used, but *salah* more strongly implies the claim is wrong rather than merely unconfirmed)
- *keliru* — evaluates a belief or statement as based on error or misunderstanding, without necessarily implying deliberate deception; slightly more formal than *salah* in some registers; used specifically for mistaken belief or judgment, often without implying moral fault; appears in hedging constructions (*kalau tidak keliru*)
- *bohong* — evaluates a statement as deliberately untrue; functions as both noun and predicate
- *dusta* — near-synonym of *bohong*, more formal and literary in register; common in religious and moral discourse
- *kebenaran* — abstract noun for the property of being true; appears in *mencari kebenaran*, *menegakkan kebenaran*, *mengungkap kebenaran*, *menyembunyikan kebenaran*; used to refer to truth as a property or object of pursuit; in philosophical and religious collocations (*kebenaran hakiki*, *kebenaran sejati*, *kebenaran mutlak*) shades into Group 7 (essential or fundamental nature)
- *kebohongan* — abstract noun for a lie or the quality of being false; *mengungkap kebohongan* (to expose a lie), *kebohongan publik* (public deception)
- *kekeliruan* — abstract noun for error or mistaken belief; *mengakui kekeliruan* (to acknowledge one's error)
- *sebenarnya* — discourse adverb introducing a correction or revelation of what is actually the case, often contrasting with a prior assumption (*sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju*); also used as a conversational softener (*sebenarnya, saya mau tanya...*) in which the correction-of-false-belief meaning is not present
- *sebenar-benarnya* — emphatic form: "the absolute truth, truly and completely"; used in formal declarations and oaths
- *memang benar bahwa* — fixed concessive-affirmative frame acknowledging a fact before qualification
- *ada benarnya* — partial concession: "there is some truth to it"; acknowledges that a claim has some validity without full endorsement
- *benar, tapi* — standard concessive construction: "true, but"
- *tidak terbukti* — "not proven"; used to suspend or deny a truth claim for lack of evidence
- *informasi yang keliru* — "erroneous information"; used without implying deliberate deception
- *berita palsu* — "fake news"; used in contexts of deliberate misinformation
- *benar adanya* — a fixed construction affirming that something is genuinely and factually the case, often with a tone of solemn acknowledgment
- *kebenaran ilmiah*, *kebenaran formal*, *kebenaran materiil* — domain-specific collocations distinguishing types of factual correctness in scientific and legal registers
- *akurat* — used in journalistic and technical registers to evaluate data or reporting as free from error (*data yang akurat*, *informasi yang akurat dan terpercaya*); more restricted to formal and technical registers than *benar*
- *tepat* — used to evaluate whether a description, measurement, or statement fits correctly (*tidak tepat*, *kurang tepat*); extends to timing (*tepat pada waktunya*), word choice (*kata yang tepat*), and situational fit (*pilihan yang tepat untuk situasi ini*)
- *nyata* — used to assert that something is factually the case and perceptible, not merely theoretical (*bukti nyata*, *kenyataan di lapangan*); shades into Group 3 when it emphasizes perceptibility rather than factual correctness
- *kenyataan* — used when reality is referred to as something to be faced, acknowledged, or contrasted with appearance or expectation (*menghadapi kenyataan*, *menjadi kenyataan*)
- *diakui kebenarannya* — "its truth is acknowledged, recognized as true"; used in formal contexts where truth has been officially recognized

**Notes on internal distinctions:** Within this group, *bohong* and *dusta* specifically imply deliberate deception, while *keliru* and *salah* do not necessarily carry this implication. *Benar* and *tidak benar* are the most neutral and widely distributed; *dusta* is restricted to formal, literary, and religious registers where *bohong* would be used in everyday speech. *benar* and *keliru* are frequently contrasted in educational and analytical contexts. *akurat* and *tepat* overlap substantially but *akurat* is more restricted to formal and technical registers. *nyata* shades into Group 3 (genuineness) when it emphasizes perceptibility rather than factual correctness.

## Group 2: Terms used to characterize factual error or mistaken belief

**Label:** Characterizing error and mistaken belief

**Definition:** These terms are used to indicate that a belief, judgment, statement, or action is incorrect — typically without implying deliberate deception. They appear in contexts of correction, diagnosis of misunderstanding, and acknowledgment of fault. They contrast with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 1 (factual correctness).

**Representative expressions:**

- *salah* — the primary term for wrongness or error; used across factual, moral, and evaluative contexts; participates in a large family of compounds
- *keliru* — used specifically for mistaken belief or judgment, often without implying moral fault; *kekeliruan* (error, misconception); *mengelirukan* (to mislead, to cause to err)
- *kesalahan* — the nominalization of *salah*; covers error, mistake, and fault; *mengakui kesalahan* (to admit one's mistake), *kesalahan fatal* (fatal error), *kesalahan prosedur* (procedural error)
- *salah paham* — misunderstanding; a very common compound for incorrect interpretation of a message or intention
- *salah kaprah* — a widespread misconception that has become accepted as correct; used in journalistic and educational commentary; describes a state where something *salah* (wrong) has become *kaprah* (common/accepted), which is a specific social-epistemic phenomenon
- *salah tafsir* — misinterpretation, particularly of texts, laws, or religious passages
- *salah sangka* — mistaken assumption or false suspicion
- *kekeliruan* — error or misconception; *mengakui kekeliruan* (to acknowledge one's error)
- *tidak benar* — also functions in this group when used to characterize a claim as factually incorrect rather than deliberately false
- *salah baca*, *salah dengar*, *salah tulis*, *salah ucap*, *salah cetak* — a productive series of compounds characterizing specific types of perceptual or production error

**Notes:** The distinction between *salah* and *keliru* in practice is one of register and implied agency: *keliru* more often describes innocent error or misunderstanding, while *salah* covers a wider range including moral fault. *salah kaprah* is distinctive in that it describes error that has become socially normalized. The *salah* + [verb/noun] compounding pattern is highly productive and generates new forms as needed.

## Group 3: Terms used to characterize deliberate untruth

**Label:** Characterizing deliberate falsehood and lying

**Definition:** These terms are used to describe statements or persons where the untruth is understood to be intentional — where the speaker knows the statement is false and makes it anyway. They appear in moral, interpersonal, legal, and journalistic contexts. They contrast with Group 2 (innocent error) and are often paired with terms from Group 6 (deception and manipulation).

**Representative expressions:**

- *bohong* — the primary everyday term for lying; functions as noun, adjective, and base for verbal derivations; *berbohong* (to lie), *pembohong* (liar), *kebohongan* (falsehood, lie); *membohongi* (to lie to someone)
- *dusta* — a near-synonym of *bohong* in more formal, literary, and religious registers; *berdusta* (to lie), *pendusta* (liar), *kedustaan* (falsehood as a quality); commonly paired with *tipu daya* in Islamic moral discourse (*jauh dari dusta dan tipu daya*)
- *kebohongan* — the nominalization of *bohong*; refers to a lie or the state of deception; *mengungkap kebohongan* (to expose a lie), *kebohongan publik* (public deception)
- *tidak benar* — in some contexts functions as a euphemistic or formal way of characterizing a deliberate lie without using *bohong* directly
- *bohong demi kebaikan* — a fixed construction framing a lie as morally motivated (a white lie); used in ethical discussions
- *sekali bohong, selamanya tidak dipercaya* — a near-proverbial formula about the consequences of lying
- *ketahuan bohong* — a fixed collocation for the discovery of a lie (*caught lying*)
- *sengaja menyesatkan* — deliberately misleading; the addition of *sengaja* (intentionally) to *menyesatkan* marks the deception as intentional, relevant in legal contexts

**Notes:** *bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms distinguished primarily by register; *bohong* is the everyday term, *dusta* is formal and literary. Both contrast with *keliru* (Group 2) in that they imply intent. *sengaja menyesatkan* bridges this group and Group 6 (deception and manipulation). *palsu* (Group 4) can describe false statements but is more typically applied to objects and documents.

## Group 4: Terms used in legal and administrative validity judgments

**Label:** Formal and institutional validity

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where an act, document, decision, or status is evaluated as meeting or failing to meet formally required conditions — in court decisions, contracts, official documents, administrative procedures, and legislative processes. The central question in this group is not whether something is factually true but whether it is recognized as binding, legitimate, or procedurally correct by an authoritative institution.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sah* — the primary term; used in legal, religious, and administrative contexts to assert that something meets required conditions; appears in fixed formulas (*sah dan mengikat*, *dinyatakan sah*, *pernikahan yang sah*, *tidak sah*, *belum sah*)
- *tidak sah* — standard negation; used to declare an act, document, or status void or invalid; *tidak sah demi hukum* (null and void under the law)
- *absah* — more formal variant of *sah*; used in legal writing and academic discourse; generates *keabsahan* but does not independently generate the verbal and process nominal forms that *sah* does; *mengabsahkan* (to legitimize)
- *mengesahkan* — "to ratify, to validate, to legalize"; used for official approval of laws, documents, decisions (*mengesahkan undang-undang*, *mengesahkan dokumen*)
- *disahkan* — passive: "to be ratified"; standard in official announcements and legal reporting (*undang-undang disahkan*)
- *pengesahan* — "ratification, validation"; used in *pengesahan konstitusi*, *pengesahan dokumen*
- *keabsahan* — abstract noun: "validity, legitimacy"; used in *keabsahan kontrak*, *keabsahan keputusan*, *keabsahan pemilu*, *keabsahan pemerintahan*; *mempertanyakan keabsahan* (to question legitimacy)
- *sah dan mengikat* — fixed legal formula: "valid and binding"
- *sah secara hukum* — "legally valid"; specifies the domain of validity as statutory law; *benar secara hukum* (legally correct)
- *sah menurut agama* — "valid according to religion"; used where religious and civil law intersect, particularly for marriage
- *sah menurut agama tetapi tidak menurut negara* — a contrastive construction common in Indonesian discourse about the dual religious-civil legal system
- *dinyatakan sah* — "declared valid"; standard in court decisions and official rulings; *secara hukum dianggap sah* (considered legally valid)
- *alasan yang sah* — "valid/legitimate reason"; used in administrative law and official correspondence
- *tidak absah menurut hukum* — formal legal construction: "not legitimate under applicable law"
- *terbukti secara sah dan meyakinkan* — standard formula in Indonesian criminal verdicts, equivalent in function to "proven beyond reasonable doubt"
- *sah sebagai* — "recognized as, valid as"; used in legal recognition of status or capacity
- *anak sah* / *anak tidak sah* — fixed legal terms for legitimate and illegitimate children
- *sah secara agama* — constructions distinguishing legal from religious validity, reflecting the dual civil-religious structure of Indonesian family law
- *asli dan sah* — a fixed formula combining genuineness with legal validity, used in notarial language
- *valid* — "valid"; used in academic, scientific, and administrative contexts for instruments, arguments, or procedures meeting required standards; *data yang valid*, *validitas instrumen*
- *validitas* — "validity"; restricted to academic and technical registers
- *data yang valid* — "valid data"; frequent in research methodology
- *dinyatakan valid* — "declared valid"; standard in research reporting
- *memvalidasi* — to validate; *validasi* (validation); *divalidasi* (validated)
- *sahih* — used primarily in Islamic scholarly contexts and academic methodology to indicate that something meets the required standards of soundness (*hadis sahih*, *uji kesahihan*); see also Group 5
- *kesahihan* — the nominal form of *sahih*, used in academic research methodology (*uji kesahihan*)
- *kebenaran formal* / *kebenaran materiil* — formal truth vs. material truth; a distinction in Indonesian criminal procedure
- *diakui kebenarannya* — whose truth is officially acknowledged

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Sah* is the everyday-formal term; *absah* is more elevated and appears primarily in written legal and academic registers. *Sahih* (see Group 5) shares the same Arabic root but operates in a distinct domain (Islamic scholarly discourse) and is not interchangeable with *sah* in legal-administrative contexts. The formula *sah menurut agama* reflects the Indonesian legal context in which religious validity and civil validity are treated as distinct but related requirements. *sah*, *sahih*, and *valid* occupy distinct register positions: *sah* is the general-purpose term across legal, administrative, and religious contexts; *sahih* is restricted to Islamic scholarship and academic methodology; *valid* is restricted to academic and scientific registers. The distinction between *sah* (legal validity) and *asli* (genuineness) is maintained in practice: a document can be *asli* (genuine) but *tidak sah* (invalid) if it lacks proper authorization, and vice versa.

## Group 5: Terms used in Islamic scholarly evaluation of religious sources

**Label:** Terms with primary or strong Islamic religious usage

**Definition:** These terms appear specifically in Islamic religious and scholarly discourse to evaluate hadith (prophetic traditions) and religious rulings as meeting or failing to meet criteria of authenticity, chain-of-transmission reliability, and doctrinal soundness. Their distribution is largely restricted to this domain in Indonesian usage. Several also appear in secular philosophical or formal literary contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahih* — "sound, authentic, correct"; the primary evaluative term for hadith that meet the highest criteria of authentication; the collections *Sahih Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim* are referenced by this term
- *hadis sahih* — "authentic hadith"; the most frequent collocation of *sahih* in Indonesian
- *kesahihan* — abstract noun: "soundness, authenticity"; used in *kesahihan hadis*
- *hadis sahih riwayat [narrator]* — standard citation formula in Islamic scholarly discourse
- *mendustakan ayat-ayat Allah* — "to deny/reject the signs of God"; collocation of *mendustakan* in religious discourse
- *para nabi didustakan oleh kaumnya* — "the prophets were rejected/called liars by their people"; standard formulation in Islamic religious texts
- *berdusta* — "to lie, to speak falsely"; more elevated in register than *berbohong*; common in Islamic moral discourse
- *pendusta* — "liar"; more elevated than *pembohong*; common in Islamic moral discourse
- *dilarang berdusta* — "lying is forbidden"; common moral formulation in Islamic discourse
- *bukti sahih* — "sound/authentic evidence"; bridges the *bukti* and *sahih* families in Islamic scholarly and formal legal contexts
- *sah* — used for the validity of religious acts (prayer, marriage, fasting) according to Islamic jurisprudence (*sah secara agama*, *pernikahan yang sah*)
- *ikhlas* — from Arabic *ikhlāṣ*; in Islamic discourse specifically denotes performing acts solely for God without expectation of worldly reward; the connotation of selfless religious sincerity is stronger than in secular usage
- *dusta* — from Arabic; more common than *bohong* in Islamic moral and religious texts; appears in hadith and Quranic commentary
- *hakiki* — from Arabic *ḥaqīqī*; used in Islamic philosophical and Sufi discourse to refer to the essential or divine reality underlying appearances (*kebenaran hakiki*)
- *hakikat* — from Arabic *ḥaqīqa*; used in Islamic philosophy and Sufism to refer to the essential truth or inner reality of things (*hakikat manusia*, *pada hakikatnya*, *mencari hakikat*)
- *niat yang benar* — the collocation evaluating whether an action is motivated by correct religious intention (*niat* from Arabic *niyya*)
- *menegakkan kebenaran* — used in Islamic moral discourse for upholding truth against falsehood
- *tulus ikhlas* — the paired formula common in Islamic expressions of gratitude and moral evaluation
- *jauh dari dusta dan tipu daya* — collocation pairing *dusta* with *tipu daya* in Islamic moral discourse

**Notes:** *Sahih* shares its Arabic root (*ṣaḥīḥ*) with *sah* and *absah* but is not interchangeable with them in Indonesian usage. *Sah* and *absah* operate in legal-administrative contexts; *sahih* operates in Islamic scholarly contexts. The overlap is etymological rather than functional. *Dusta*, *berdusta*, and *pendusta* appear in both this group and Group 1/Group 3, but their frequency and characteristic collocations in Islamic religious discourse are sufficiently distinct to note here. This group is defined by context of use rather than by etymology alone.

## Group 6: Terms used to evaluate objects, documents, and persons as genuine or not genuine

**Label:** Genuineness and originality

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is whether an object, document, credential, or person is what it claims or appears to be — as opposed to being a copy, forgery, imitation, or assumed identity. They are used in commercial, legal, artistic, forensic, and identity contexts. The central contrast is between the original or genuine and the copy or fake.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asli* — the primary positive term: "genuine, original, native"; used for objects, documents, persons, and cultural items; contrasts with *palsu* and *tiruan*; *barang asli*, *naskah asli*, *foto asli*, *ijazah asli*, *dijamin asli*, *asli atau palsu?*, *tanda tangan asli*, *karya asli*
- *palsu* — the primary negative term: "fake, counterfeit, forged"; implies deliberate deception; *uang palsu*, *berita palsu*, *akun palsu*, *tanda tangan palsu*, *ijazah palsu*, *dokumen palsu*, *identitas palsu*, *obat palsu*, *produk palsu*
- *tiruan* — "imitation, copy"; less strongly negative than *palsu*; may be openly acknowledged as such; does not necessarily imply fraudulent intent; *barang tiruan*, *kulit tiruan*
- *otentik* — "authentic"; more formal and restricted in distribution than *asli*; used in legal, academic, and journalistic registers; *dokumen otentik*; does not generate the productive collocation network that *asli* does
- *otentisitas* — authenticity as an abstract property; used in academic registers
- *otentikasi* — authentication; *mengotentikasi* (to authenticate)
- *keaslian* — abstract noun: "authenticity, genuineness"; used in *mempertanyakan keaslian dokumen*, *dijamin keasliannya* (authenticity guaranteed), *diragukan keasliannya* (authenticity questioned), *sertifikat keaslian* (certificate of authenticity), *verifikasi keaslian* (authenticity verification), *uji keaslian*
- *pemalsuan* — "falsification, forgery, counterfeiting"; used in legal contexts for the act or crime
- *pemalsu* — "forger, counterfeiter"; agentive noun used in legal and journalistic contexts
- *memalsukan* — "to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit"
- *dipalsukan* — to have been forged or falsified
- *kepalsuan* — fakeness; in collocations such as *kepalsuan hidup modern* (the falseness of modern life) or *kepalsuan dalam hubungan manusia* (falseness in human relationships) extends beyond counterfeit objects into a broader evaluation of inauthenticity in social existence
- *meniru* — to imitate; *peniru* (imitator); *peniruan* (imitation)
- *asli atau palsu* — the standard binary opposition framing the question of authenticity; used in commercial and forensic contexts
- *dijamin asli* — "guaranteed genuine"; common commercial formula
- *barang tiruan* — "imitation goods"; used in commercial and legal contexts
- *barang palsu* — "fake/counterfeit goods"; implies fraud
- *uang palsu* — "counterfeit money"; one of the most frequent collocations of *palsu*
- *identitas palsu* — "false identity"; used in crime reporting and legal proceedings
- *nama palsu* — "false name, alias"
- *ijazah palsu* — "forged diploma/certificate"
- *versi aslinya* — "the original version of it"; used to distinguish originals from later versions or copies
- *sertifikat asli* — "original certificate"; administrative construction distinguishing original from copies
- *naskah asli* — original manuscript or text
- *foto asli* — original, unedited photograph
- *karya asli* — original creative work
- *terbukti palsu* — proven false/forged (in legal proceedings)
- *mengaku asli padahal palsu* — claiming to be genuine when in fact fake; a deception-characterization construction
- *bukan asli* — not genuine
- *asli dan sah* — a fixed formula combining genuineness with legal validity, used in notarial language
- *sulit dibedakan dari yang asli* — difficult to distinguish from the genuine article; a construction used in consumer protection and forensic contexts about high-quality counterfeits

**Notes on internal distinctions:** The three-way contrast *asli* / *tiruan* / *palsu* is well established in Indonesian commercial and legal discourse: *asli* is the genuine original, *tiruan* is an imitation that may be openly labeled as such, and *palsu* is a fraudulent substitute. *Palsu* implies deliberate deception and is used in legal contexts for fraud and forgery; *tiruan* describes a copy that may be openly acknowledged as such and does not necessarily imply fraud. *Asli* and *otentik* overlap in meaning but differ in register: *asli* is the everyday term, *otentik* is restricted to formal and written registers. *Sejati* (see Group 7) overlaps with *asli* in some uses but operates primarily in moral and relational contexts rather than object-authenticity contexts. *berita palsu* (fake news) is a notable extension of the *[noun] + palsu* pattern into the domain of information rather than physical objects, which creates overlap with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 9 (distortion of information). The *[noun] + palsu* compounding pattern is fully open and productive.

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate persons, qualities, and relationships as genuinely what they are claimed to be

**Label:** Honesty and sincerity in personal conduct; genuine qualities of persons

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is not whether an object is a forgery but whether a person, quality, or relationship is deep, complete, and genuine rather than superficial, performed, or partial, or whether a person's statements, actions, or motivations are free from deception, concealment, or hidden agenda. They are used in moral, literary, philosophical, and interpersonal discourse. The evaluative concern is with depth and completeness of a quality, or with freedom from deception in conduct, rather than with the distinction between original and copy.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sejati* — "true, genuine, real"; used in *teman sejati* (true friend), *cinta sejati* (true love), *kebenaran sejati* (genuine truth), *pahlawan sejati* (true hero), *kesejatian diri* (the trueness of the self), *mencari jati diri yang sejati* (seeking one's true self); carries a sense of depth and completeness; primarily attributive rather than used as a standalone noun
- *jujur* — "honest, truthful, sincere"; evaluates a person's conduct or statements as free from deception or concealment; *orang yang jujur*, *bicara jujur*, *jujur saja*, *tidak jujur*, *terus terang dan jujur*; *harus jujur pada diri sendiri* (must be honest with oneself); *tidak jujur dalam melaporkan keuangan* (dishonest in financial reporting); *mengaku jujur di hadapan hukum* (to make an honest admission in legal proceedings)
- *kejujuran* — abstract noun: "honesty, truthfulness, sincerity"; *kejujuran adalah nilai utama*
- *ketidakjujuran* — dishonesty
- *jujur dan amanah* — stable collocation: "honest and trustworthy"; used in character evaluation, particularly in political and religious contexts
- *jujur saja* — discourse marker: "honestly, to be honest"; introduces a candid statement, often one anticipated to be unwelcome
- *untuk jujur* — "to be honest"; slightly more formal variant of *jujur saja*
- *berkata jujur* — "to speak honestly, to tell the truth"
- *berkata benar* — to speak the truth; evaluates the truthfulness of a person's statements
- *bersikap jujur* — "to conduct oneself honestly"; used in institutional and moral contexts
- *terpercaya* — "trustworthy, reliable, credible"; evaluates a person, source, or institution as worthy of trust; *sumber terpercaya* (trusted source), *sumber yang terpercaya dan terverifikasi* (trusted and verified source), *tidak lagi terpercaya di mata publik* (no longer trusted in the eyes of the public)
- *tidak terpercaya* — "untrustworthy"
- *kepercayaan* — trust, belief, confidence; *kepercayaan publik* (public trust), *kepercayaan diri* (self-confidence)
- *mempercayai* — to trust or believe in someone or something
- *tulus* — used to evaluate inner motivation as free from hidden agenda or pretense; *dari hati yang tulus*, *dengan tulus*, *niat yang tulus*, *ketulusan*
- *ikhlas* — used to evaluate actions as performed without expectation of reward or recognition, particularly in religious contexts; carries a stronger connotation of selfless acceptance than *tulus*; *dengan ikhlas*, *ikhlas menerima*, *tulus ikhlas*, *keikhlasan*, *mengikhlaskan*
- *sungguh* — used to assert the sincerity or genuineness of a claim or commitment; *sungguh-sungguh*, *bersungguh-sungguh*, *kesungguhan*, *dengan sungguh-sungguh*, *sungguh-sungguh dari hati*
- *senyum palsu* — "false smile"; extends *palsu* from object-authenticity into emotional expression, evaluating a display as performed rather than genuine
- *harapan palsu* — "false hope"; applies *palsu* to an abstract psychological state
- *tampil apa adanya* — the fixed periphrastic construction meaning "to present oneself as one truly is"; the standard expression for personal authenticity in this sense

**Notes on internal distinctions:** *Sejati* and *asli* overlap in the sense of "genuine" but *sejati* is used for persons, qualities, and relationships where depth and completeness are at issue, while *asli* is used for objects, documents, and identity in the sense of "not a copy." *Jujur* specifically concerns freedom from deception in communication and conduct; it is not used for objects. *Tulus* and *ikhlas* are frequently paired (*tulus ikhlas*) and overlap substantially, but *ikhlas* carries a specifically religious connotation of selfless acceptance that *tulus* does not. *Sungguh* is primarily an intensifier of sincerity and commitment rather than a term for honesty per se; it shades into Group 1 when used to assert the reality of a claim rather than the sincerity of a person. *Senyum palsu* and *harapan palsu* represent extensions of *palsu* from Group 6 into this domain, where the concern is with performed or unfounded states rather than forged objects. *Terpercaya* and *kredibel* (see Group 8) are near-synonyms in journalistic and institutional contexts; *terpercaya* is the native form and *kredibel* is the loanword.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate the trustworthiness of persons, sources, or institutions

**Label:** Evaluating trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Definition:** These terms are used to assess whether a person, source, or institution can be relied upon to provide accurate information or to act with integrity. They appear in journalistic, political, and institutional contexts. They differ from Group 1 (factual correctness of statements) in that they evaluate the reliability of the producer of information rather than the content of any particular statement.

**Representative expressions:**

- *terpercaya* — trusted, reliable; *sumber terpercaya* (trusted source), *sumber yang terpercaya dan terverifikasi* (trusted and verified source), *tidak lagi terpercaya di mata publik* (no longer trusted in the eyes of the public); see also Group 7
- *kepercayaan* — trust, belief, confidence; *kepercayaan publik* (public trust)
- *kredibel*
