# Language: Korean (ko)

## OPPOSITION 1

**Term A:** 진실 (jinsil)  
**Term B:** 거짓 (geojit)  
**Transliteration:** jinsil vs. geojit  
**Translation:** truth vs. falsehood/lie  
**Description:** Core lexical opposition between truth and falsehood. Both terms appear extensively in the data with parallel constructional patterns (진실을 말하다 "tell the truth" vs. 거짓말을 하다 "tell a lie") and are used across all registers to mark the fundamental distinction between true and false statements.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 2

**Term A:** 사실 (sasil)  
**Term B:** 거짓 (geojit)  
**Transliteration:** sasil vs. geojit  
**Description:** Lexical opposition between fact and falsehood. 사실 emphasizes objective factuality while 거짓 marks deliberate falsity. Common in constructions like 사실이 아니다 (sasiri anida) "is not a fact" and appears in the fixed expression 사실무근 (sasilmugeun) "groundless, without factual basis."  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 3

**Term A:** 진짜 (jinjja)  
**Term B:** 가짜 (gajja)  
**Transliteration:** jinjja vs. gajja  
**Translation:** real/genuine vs. fake/counterfeit  
**Description:** Core colloquial opposition for authenticity of objects and identities. Appears in parallel constructions (진짜인지 가짜인지 "whether real or fake") and is the primary everyday opposition for distinguishing authentic from counterfeit items.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 4

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 5

**Term A:** 맞다 (matda)  
**Term B:** 틀리다 (teullida)  
**Transliteration:** matda vs. teullida  
**Translation:** to be correct vs. to be wrong/incorrect  
**Description:** Fundamental native Korean opposition for correctness evaluation, particularly in educational contexts. Appears in parallel constructions (맞는 답 "correct answer" vs. 틀린 답 "wrong answer") and coordinate expressions (맞고 틀리고 "right and wrong," 맞는지 틀리는지 "whether right or wrong").  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 6

**Term A:** 옳다 (olda)  
**Term B:** 그르다 (geureuda)  
**Transliteration:** olda vs. geureuda  
**Translation:** to be right/correct vs. to be wrong  
**Description:** Native Korean opposition for moral and logical correctness. Appears primarily in the fixed expression 옳고 그름 (olgo geureum) "right and wrong." 그르다 is archaic in contemporary usage, with 옳지 않다 (olji anta) "not right" being more common for negation.  
**Contrast type:** lexical  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE] for 옳다, [MARGINAL] for 그르다

## OPPOSITION 12

**Term A:** NP + 이/가 + TRUTH-TERM + 이다  
**Term B:** NP + 이/가 + TRUTH-TERM + 이/가 아니다  
**Transliteration:** (copular affirmation vs. copular negation)  
**Translation:** "X is [truth-term]" vs. "X is not [truth-term]"  
**Description:** Constructional opposition between affirmative and negative attribution using copula 이다 and its negation 아니다. Universal pattern applying to all truth-domain nominal predicates (이것은 사실이다 "This is a fact" vs. 이것은 사실이 아니다 "This is not a fact").  
**Contrast type:** constructional  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## OPPOSITION 16

**Term A:** 실제 (silje) "reality, actuality"

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

**Total oppositions identified:** 28 (including asymmetric and uncertain cases)

**Clear lexical oppositions:** 10 (진실/거짓, 사실/거짓, 진짜/가짜, 진본/모조, 맞다/틀리다, 옳다/그르다, 참/거짓, 진/위, 새빨간 거짓말/하얀 거짓말, 정직성/부정직)

**Morphological oppositions:** 6 (정직/부정직, 정확/부정확, 확실/불확실, 정당/부정당, 타당/부타당, plus derived forms)

**Constructional oppositions:** 7 (copular affirmation/negation, adjectival affirmation/negation, 신빙성 있다/없다, 진실 말하다/거짓말 하다, 진실 밝히다/숨기다, 진실성 의심되다/인정받다, plus 실제 contrasts)

**Asymmetric or uncertain cases:** 5 (진실 드러나다, 옳은 일, 명백하다, 그럴듯하다, 거짓말쟁이, 사실상)

**Notable patterns:**
- Sino-Korean terms show productive morphological negation with 부-/불- prefixes
- Native Korean terms rely more on lexical oppositions or periphrastic negation
- Negative terms (lies, falsehood) show richer agentive and intensification patterns than positive terms
- Several truth-domain concepts lack clear lexical opposites, relying on constructional or contextual contrast
