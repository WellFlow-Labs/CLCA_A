# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

**1.**
**Form:** tosi
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
**Literal meaning:** true, real
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, really

Used to assert that a statement corresponds to fact or that something is genuine. Functions both as an adjective (*tosi asia* — a true/real matter) and as an intensifying adverb in colloquial speech. Participates in derivation: *totuus* (truth), *todistaa* (to prove/witness), *toteuttaa* (to realize/implement).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** totuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** truth (derived from *tosi* + *-uus*, abstract noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, the truth

Refers to the property of being true or to a true state of affairs. Used in philosophical, moral, legal, and everyday contexts. Appears in compounds such as *totuudenmukaisuus* (truthfulness, conformity to truth).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** aito
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** genuine, authentic, original
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, original

Applied to objects, persons, emotions, and documents to assert that they are not counterfeit, imitated, or performed. Contrasts with *väärä* (false, wrong, counterfeit). Derives *aitous* (authenticity, genuineness).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** aitous
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** genuineness, authenticity (*aito* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness

Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in aesthetic, philosophical, and legal contexts when evaluating whether something is what it claims to be. Closely related to *alkuperäisyys* (originality, provenance).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** oikea
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** right, correct, proper, true
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper, genuine, real

Covers a wide range of evaluative senses: correctness of an answer, moral rightness of an action, and genuineness of an object or person. *Oikea vastaus* (the correct answer); *oikea ihminen* (the right person). Derives *oikeellisuus* (correctness), *oikeus* (right, justice, court).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** oikeellisuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** correctness (*oikea* + *-llinen* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, propriety

Used to evaluate whether a form, procedure, statement, or action conforms to a standard or norm. Common in administrative, linguistic, and legal registers. Distinct from *totuus* in that it emphasizes conformity to a rule rather than correspondence to fact.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** tarkka
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** precise, exact, accurate, careful
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, careful

Describes measurements, descriptions, reproductions, or persons as conforming closely to a standard or to reality. *Tarkka kuvaus* (an accurate description); *tarkka mittaus* (a precise measurement). Derives *tarkkuus* (accuracy, precision).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** tarkkuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** accuracy, precision (*tarkka* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness

Abstract noun used in scientific, technical, and evaluative contexts to denote the degree to which something corresponds exactly to a standard or to reality. Frequently used in measurement, translation, and reporting contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** luotettava
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (from *luottaa*, to trust, + *-ttava*, participial suffix)
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible

Applied to persons, sources, instruments, and institutions to assert that they can be trusted to perform consistently or to report accurately. *Luotettava lähde* (a reliable source). Derives *luotettavuus* (reliability, trustworthiness).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** luotettavuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** reliability, trustworthiness (*luotettava* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility

Abstract noun used in evaluative contexts — scientific, journalistic, legal — to assess whether a source, person, or instrument can be depended upon. Closely related to *uskottavuus* (credibility, plausibility).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** uskottava
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** believable, credible (from *uskoa*, to believe, + *-ttava*)
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable, plausible, convincing

Evaluates whether a claim, person, or representation is such that it can reasonably be believed. *Uskottava selitys* (a credible explanation). Derives *uskottavuus* (credibility). Distinct from *luotettava* in that it concerns believability rather than dependability.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** uskottavuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** credibility, plausibility (*uskottava* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** credibility, plausibility, believability

Used to assess whether a claim, narrative, or person commands belief. Appears in legal, journalistic, and everyday evaluative contexts. A witness's *uskottavuus* is a standard consideration in Finnish legal proceedings.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** väärä
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** wrong, false, incorrect, counterfeit
**Typical English translations:** wrong, false, incorrect, fake, counterfeit

Covers both epistemic falsity (a wrong answer) and inauthenticity (a counterfeit object). *Väärä vastaus* (wrong answer); *väärä seteli* (counterfeit banknote); *väärä nimi* (false name). Derives *vääryys* (wrongness, injustice), *väärentää* (to falsify, to forge).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** väärentää
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit (from *väärä* + *-entää*, causative suffix)
**Typical English translations:** to forge, to falsify, to counterfeit, to adulterate

Denotes the act of making something false or counterfeit — documents, signatures, currency, evidence. A standard legal term in Finnish criminal law. Derives *väärentäminen* (forgery, falsification) and *väärentäjä* (forger).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** väärentäminen
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** forgery, falsification (verbal noun of *väärentää*)
**Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting

The nominal form used in legal and administrative contexts to name the act or offense of forging or falsifying. Appears in compound legal terms such as *asiakirjan väärentäminen* (document forgery).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** todistaa
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** to prove, to testify, to witness (from *tosi* via *todistus*)
**Typical English translations:** to prove, to demonstrate, to testify, to witness, to certify

Used in legal contexts for giving testimony and in general contexts for demonstrating or proving a claim. *Todistaa syyllisyys* (to prove guilt); *todistaa oikeudessa* (to testify in court). Derives *todistus* (proof, certificate, testimony), *todistaja* (witness).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** todistus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** proof, testimony, certificate (from *todistaa*)
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, testimony, certificate, attestation

Covers both the abstract sense of proof or evidence and the concrete sense of a certificate or credential. *Syntymätodistus* (birth certificate); *todistus syyllisyydestä* (proof of guilt). Widely used across legal, administrative, and everyday contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** näennäinen
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** apparent, seeming, ostensible (from *näkyä*, to appear/be visible, + *-nnäinen*)
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible, superficial

Describes something that appears to be a certain way but may not genuinely be so. *Näennäinen yhdenmukaisuus* (apparent conformity). Used in philosophical, critical, and analytical contexts to flag a gap between appearance and reality.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** alkuperäinen
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** original, primary (from *alkuperä*, origin/source, + *-inen*)
**Typical English translations:** original, primary, initial, authentic

Evaluates whether something is the original rather than a copy, derivative, or later version. *Alkuperäinen asiakirja* (the original document); *alkuperäinen teos* (the original work). Derives *alkuperäisyys* (originality, authenticity of provenance).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** jäljitelmä
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** imitation, copy, replica (from *jäljitellä*, to imitate, + *-mä*, nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, copy, fake, counterfeit

Denotes an object or representation made to resemble another, often with an implication that it lacks the value or status of the original. Used in aesthetic, commercial, and legal contexts. Contrasts with *alkuperäinen* and *aito*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** kopio
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** copy (loanword from Swedish/Latin *kopia*)
**Typical English translations:** copy, duplicate, reproduction

Neutral term for a reproduction of a document, image, or object. Does not inherently imply deception, unlike *jäljitelmä* or *väärennös*. Used in administrative, artistic, and everyday contexts. *Virallinen kopio* (official copy).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** väärennös
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** forgery, fake, counterfeit (from *väärentää* + *-ös*, result nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** forgery, fake, counterfeit, falsification

The product of forgery — a forged document, counterfeit object, or fabricated evidence. Distinct from *kopio* (neutral copy) and *jäljitelmä* (imitation) in that it implies fraudulent intent. Standard term in Finnish legal language.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** rehellinen
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** honest, straightforward (etymology debated; possibly from *rehellisuus* as a loanword adaptation)
**Typical English translations:** honest, straightforward, upright, sincere

Evaluates persons, statements, or conduct as characterized by honesty and absence of deception. *Rehellinen vastaus* (an honest answer); *rehellinen ihminen* (an honest person). Derives *rehellisyys* (honesty).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** rehellisyys
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** honesty (*rehellinen* + *-yys*)
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, straightforwardness

Abstract noun denoting the quality of being honest in conduct, speech, or representation. Used in moral, professional, and everyday evaluative contexts. Frequently appears in discussions of journalistic, scientific, and personal integrity.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** vilpillinen
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** dishonest, fraudulent, deceitful (from *vilppi*, deceit/fraud, + *-llinen*)
**Typical English translations:** dishonest, fraudulent, deceitful, in bad faith

Describes conduct, persons, or representations characterized by deception or bad faith. *Vilpillinen menettely* (fraudulent conduct) is a standard phrase in Finnish legal and administrative language. Contrasts directly with *rehellinen*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** vilppi
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** deceit, fraud, bad faith
**Typical English translations:** deceit, fraud, dishonesty, bad faith, cheating

Denotes deliberate deception or fraudulent conduct, particularly in academic, commercial, and legal contexts. *Akateeminen vilppi* (academic fraud/misconduct) is a fixed expression in Finnish higher education. Derives *vilpillinen* (fraudulent, deceitful).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** totuudenmukaisuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** truthfulness, conformity to truth (*totuuden* [genitive of *totuus*] + *mukainen* [conforming to] + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, veracity, accuracy, conformity to truth

Evaluates whether a statement, account, or representation conforms to the truth or to facts. Used in legal contexts (e.g., evaluating witness statements) and in journalistic and academic contexts. More specific than *totuus* in emphasizing conformity rather than the property of truth itself.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** oikeudenmukaisuus
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** justice, fairness (*oikeuden* [genitive of *oikeus*, right/justice] + *mukainen* + *-uus*)
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, righteousness, equity

Evaluates conduct, decisions, or systems as conforming to principles of right and fairness. A central term in moral and legal discourse. Distinct from *oikeellisuus* (correctness to a rule) in that it concerns substantive fairness. Derives from the same root as *oikea* and *oikeus*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** epäaito
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** non-genuine, inauthentic (*epä-*, negating prefix, + *aito*)
**Typical English translations:** inauthentic, fake, spurious, not genuine

Directly negates *aito*; used to assert that something presented as genuine is not. Applied to objects, emotions, and conduct. Less common than *väärä* or *väärennös* in legal contexts but used in aesthetic and philosophical evaluation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** perusteeton
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning:** unfounded, groundless, without basis (*peruste*, basis/ground, + *-ton*, privative suffix)
**Typical English translations:** unfounded, groundless, unsubstantiated, baseless

Evaluates claims, accusations, or decisions as lacking adequate grounds or justification. *Perusteeton syytös* (an unfounded accusation). Standard in legal and administrative language. Contrasts with *perusteltu* (justified, well-founded).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
