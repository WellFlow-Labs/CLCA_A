# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / marṭ-
**Transliteration:** marṭ-
**P1 participation:** 3 items — martali (4), simartle (3), and the shared root underlying both
**Core meaning contribution:** Correctness, rightness, straightness — both in the factual sense (a correct statement) and the moral/legal sense (a just or fair outcome). This root is the primary building block for the truth-as-correctness cluster in Georgian.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ყალბ- / q̇alb-
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 participation:** 2 items — q̇albi (6), siq̇albe (5)
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, inauthenticity, forgery — particularly in the sense of deliberate fabrication or counterfeiting. This root anchors the falsity-as-deception cluster and is highly productive across legal, moral, and everyday registers.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ნამდვილ- / namdvil-
**Transliteration:** namdvil-
**P1 participation:** 2 items — namdvili (7), namdviloba (8)
**Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, authenticity, reality — the quality of being what one claims or appears to be. This root is the primary anchor for the authenticity cluster and is among the most productive in the domain.

---

*Note on selection rationale:* The root č̣ešmar- (items 1–2) is also central but participates in only 2 P1 items and has a more restricted derivational range than the three roots selected above. It is noted here and its attested extensions are included under a supplementary note at the end of Step 2.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: marṭ- (მართ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭ- + adjectival suffix -al- + -i
**Natural English translation:** right, correct, true, just, honest
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 4); included here as the base form for the expansion. Applied to persons, statements, and situations across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Morpheme breakdown:** si- (nominal prefix) + marṭ- + -al- + -e (nominal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, rightness, justice
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 3); included here as the primary nominal form of the root. Used across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** martla
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭ- + -al- + adverbial suffix -a
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, indeed, in truth
**Context of use:** Common adverb used in everyday speech to affirm the truth of a statement or to express genuine surprise. Functions as a discourse particle meaning "really?" or "is that so?" in conversational Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** martlac
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭ- + -al- + -a (adverbial) + -c (emphatic particle)
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, really (emphatic)
**Context of use:** Emphatic adverb used to confirm or reinforce a statement as genuinely true. More emphatic than martla; common in both spoken and written Georgian across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** მართლაც რომ
**Transliteration:** martlac rom
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlac + rom (subordinating conjunction)
**Natural English translation:** it is truly the case that, indeed it is so that
**Context of use:** Fixed collocation used to introduce a concessive or confirmatory clause. Common in spoken Georgian and informal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** მართლი
**Transliteration:** martli
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭ- + -al- + -i (variant adjectival form)
**Natural English translation:** just, righteous, upright
**Context of use:** An older or more literary/ecclesiastical variant of martali, used especially in religious texts and elevated prose to characterize a person of moral rectitude. Less common in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებელი
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebeli
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭl- + ma- + did- (great, to glorify) + -ebel- + -i; "one who glorifies rightly/truly"
**Natural English translation:** Orthodox (Christian), Eastern Orthodox
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for Eastern Orthodox Christianity and its adherents. Used in religious, historical, and everyday contexts. The compound encodes the idea of correct or true glorification of God.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებლობა
**Transliteration:** martlmadidebloба
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlmadidebel- + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Natural English translation:** Orthodoxy, Eastern Orthodox Christianity (as a faith or institution)
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the Orthodox Christian faith or the Orthodox Church as an institution. Used in theological, historical, and political discourse in Georgia.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულება
**Transliteration:** martlmsajuleba
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭl- + msajul- (judge, one who judges) + -eba (nominal suffix); "right/true judging"
**Natural English translation:** justice, administration of justice, judiciary
**Context of use:** The standard term for justice in the legal and institutional sense — the administration of law and the judicial system. Used in legal texts, constitutional documents, and political discourse. Equivalent to "justice" as a systemic concept.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულების სისტემა
**Transliteration:** martlmsajulebis sistema
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlmsajuleba (genitive -is) + sistema (loanword)
**Natural English translation:** justice system, judicial system
**Context of use:** Standard collocation used in legal, political, and journalistic discourse to refer to the institutional apparatus of courts, prosecutors, and law enforcement.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** მართლწერა
**Transliteration:** martlc̣era
**Morpheme breakdown:** marṭl- + c̣era (writing, from c̣er- "to write"); "correct writing"
**Natural English translation:** orthography, correct spelling
**Context of use:** The standard term for orthography or correct spelling in Georgian. Used in educational, editorial, and linguistic contexts. Equivalent to "spelling" as a normative concept.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** მართლწერის წესები
**Transliteration:** martlc̣eris c̣esebi
**Morpheme breakdown:** martlc̣era (genitive -is) + c̣esebi (rules, plural)
**Natural English translation:** spelling rules, orthographic rules
**Context of use:** Standard collocation used in grammar books, style guides, and educational materials to refer to the normative rules of Georgian spelling.

**13.**
**Form:** მართლმეტყველება
**Transliteration:** martlmetq̇veleba

**Natural English translation:** correct speech, orthoepy, proper diction
**Context of use:** Used in linguistic and educational contexts to refer to correct or standard pronunciation and speech. Less common than martlc̣era but attested in linguistic literature and language pedagogy.

**14.**
**Form:** მართებული

**Context of use:** Applied to arguments, decisions, conclusions, and actions to indicate that they are well-founded or appropriate. Common in formal written Georgian, legal reasoning, and academic discourse. Slightly more formal than martali.

**15.**

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three main roots in contexts where the contribution of the root appears to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From marṭ-:**

- **მართლმადიდებელი / martlmadidebeli** — the root marṭl- here participates in the name of a religious institution (Eastern Orthodox Christianity), where the "correctness" component is fused with a concept of glorification or worship.

- **მართლწერა / martlc̣era** — the root contributes to a term for orthographic correctness (spelling), where "right" applies to the formal conventions of a writing system rather than to propositional truth or moral rectitude.

- **მართლმეტყველება / martlmetq̇veleba** — the root contributes to a term for correct pronunciation or standard speech, where "right" applies to phonological and prosodic norms.

- **სასამართლო / sasamartlo** — the root is embedded in the standard word for a court of law, where the institutional and procedural sense has become primary and the root's contribution to the meaning of "truth" or "correctness" is backgrounded.

- **მართლზომიერება / martlzomiereba** — the root combines with a term for measure or proportion, producing a concept of lawful proportionality used in constitutional and administrative law, where the emphasis is on procedural legitimacy rather than factual truth.

- **გამართლება / gamartleba** — in the sense of "making excuses," the root participates in a context where the goal is not to establish truth but to provide a rationale for conduct, whether or not that rationale is itself truthful.

- **მართლი გზა / martli gza** — the root combines with a spatial metaphor (path, road) to produce a moral and religious concept of the correct way of living, where "right" is normative rather than descriptive.

---

**From q̇alb-:**

- **ყალბი ღიმილი / q̇albi ɣimili** — the root applies to a facial expression (a smile), where "false" characterizes an emotional performance rather than a propositional claim or a forged artifact.

- **ყალბი მეგობარი / q̇albi megobari** — the root applies to a relational role (friend), where "false" characterizes a person's social and moral conduct rather than a document or statement.

- **ყალბი ფასეულობა / q̇albi paseuloba** — the root applies to abstract values or standards, where "false" characterizes the normative or axiological status of a value system rather than the truth of a proposition or the authenticity of an object.

- **ყალბი ოქრო / q̇albi oq̇ro** — the root applies to a material substance (gold), where "false" characterizes physical composition rather than propositional content or intentional deception.

---

**From namdvil-:**

- **ნამდვილი ქართველი / namdvili q̇arteli** — the root participates in identity discourse where "genuine" characterizes ethnic or cultural belonging, a domain that may involve normative and exclusionary judgments rather than factual verification.

- **ნამდვილი ბედნიერება / namdvili bednierebа** — the root applies to a psychological state (happiness), where "genuine" characterizes the quality or depth of an inner experience rather than the truth of a proposition or the authenticity of an artifact.

- **ნამდვილი ცხოვრება / namdvili cxovrebа** — the root applies to the concept of a life as a whole, where "genuine" or "real" carries evaluative and existential weight beyond simple authenticity verification.

- **ნამდვილი ნიჭი / namdvili nič̣i** — the root applies to talent or ability, where "genuine" characterizes an innate quality rather than the truth of a claim or the authenticity of an object.

- **ნამდვილად / namdvilad** — in everyday speech, this adverb frequently functions as an intensifier or discourse particle ("really," "actually") in contexts where no explicit truth claim is being made, suggesting a degree/intensity function alongside its core authenticity function.
