# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## IDENTIFIED GROUPS

### GROUP 1: Propositional Affirmation and Denial

**Definition:** Expressions used to affirm or deny the truth-value of statements or propositions in discourse. These forms appear primarily in responses, confirmations, corrections, and evaluative contexts where speakers assess whether something said or claimed corresponds to reality. They function as direct judgments on propositional content.

**Representative expressions:**
- kweli (#1) - "true, truth"
- ukweli (#2) - "truth, reality, fact"
- ni kweli (P2b) - "it's true"
- si kweli (P2b) - "it's not true"
- kwa kweli (P1b, P2b) - "truly, really"
- ni kweli kwamba (P2b) - "it's true that..."
- si kweli kwamba (P2b) - "it's not true that..."
- kweli kweli (P1b, P2b) - "really truly"
- kweli kabisa (P2b) - "absolutely true"
- uongo (#4) - "lie, falsehood"
- ni uongo (P2b) - "it's a lie"
- kusema kweli (P1b, P2b) - "to tell the truth"
- kusema uongo (P2b) - "to tell lies"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- haki (#6) - overlaps with this group when used in "haki ni kwamba..." (the truth is that...) but primarily belongs to rights/justice domain
- batili (#12) - overlaps but more specialized for formal/legal invalidity

---

### GROUP 2: Correctness and Accuracy Evaluation

**Definition:** Expressions used to evaluate the correctness, accuracy, or precision of answers, information, procedures, calculations, and documents. These forms appear predominantly in educational, administrative, technical, and verification contexts where conformity to standards or factual accuracy is being assessed. They mark whether something meets established criteria for correctness.

**Representative expressions:**
- sahihi (#3) - "correct, accurate"
- usahihi (#24, P2a) - "correctness, accuracy"
- ni sahihi (P2b) - "it's correct"
- si sahihi (P2b) - "it's not correct"
- jibu sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct answer"
- taarifa sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "accurate information"
- hesabu sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct calculation"
- kwa usahihi (P1b, P2b) - "accurately, correctly"
- sawa (#7) - "correct, right, okay"
- ni sawa (P2b) - "it's correct/okay"
- kamili (#9) - "complete, perfect, accurate"
- ukamili (P2a) - "completeness, perfection"
- taarifa kamili (P2b) - "complete information"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- sahihi when used for document validation (overlaps with authentication group)
- kamili when emphasizing completeness vs. accuracy

---

### GROUP 3: Authenticity and Genuineness

**Definition:** Expressions used to distinguish genuine, authentic, or real entities from fake, counterfeit, or superficial ones. These forms appear in commercial contexts (verifying products), social contexts (evaluating relationships and character), and general discourse about the essential nature of things. They mark whether something is what it purports to be.

**Representative expressions:**
- halisi (#8) - "genuine, authentic, real"
- uhalisi (P2a) - "genuineness, authenticity"
- ni halisi (P2b) - "it's genuine"
- dhahabu halisi (P2b) - "genuine gold"
- -a kweli (#5) - "true, genuine, real" (in associative constructions)
- rafiki wa kweli (P1b, P2b) - "true friend"
- upendo wa kweli (P1b, P2b) - "true love"
- dhahabu ya kweli (P1b) - "real gold"
- jina la kweli (P1b, P2b) - "real name"
- ukweli halisi (P1b) - "genuine truth"
- -a dhati (#26) - "essential, inherent, genuine"
- -tupu (#27) - "empty, mere, hollow" (marking lack of substance)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- kweli when used for authenticity vs. propositional truth
- halisi when emphasizing purity vs. authenticity

---

### GROUP 4: Rights, Justice, and Entitlement

**Definition:** Expressions used to discuss legal rights, moral entitlements, justice, and what is due to persons. These forms appear in legal discourse, human rights contexts, moral arguments, and situations involving claims, disputes, and the distribution of what is owed. They mark normative claims about what people deserve or are entitled to.

**Representative expressions:**
- haki (#6) - "right, justice, entitlement"
- -a haki (#25) - "rightful, just, legitimate"
- -enye haki (#10) - "having right/justice, being in the right"
- haki yake (P1b) - "his/her right"
- haki za binadamu (P1b, P2b) - "human rights"
- haki za wanawake (P1b, P2b) - "women's rights"
- haki za watoto (P1b, P2b) - "children's rights"
- haki ya kura (P1b, P2b) - "right to vote"
- haki ya kumiliki (P1b, P2b) - "property right"
- dai haki (P1b, P2b) - "to claim a right"
- toa haki (P1b, P2b) - "to render justice"
- pata haki (P1b, P2b) - "to get justice"
- linda haki (P1b, P2b) - "to protect rights"
- vunja haki (P1b, P2b) - "to violate rights"
- kwa haki (P1b, P2b) - "justly, by rights"
- bila haki (P2b) - "unjustly, without justification"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- haki when used as "truth" (haki ni kwamba...) overlaps with propositional affirmation group
- uadilifu (#18) overlaps between justice and moral character

---

### GROUP 5: Moral Character and Uprightness

**Definition:** Expressions used to evaluate the moral character of persons, particularly regarding honesty, integrity, fairness, and uprightness. These forms appear in character descriptions, moral judgments, testimonials, and ethical discourse. They mark whether someone's conduct and character align with moral standards of truthfulness and justice.

**Representative expressions:**
- -adilifu (#17) - "just, fair, honest, upright"
- uadilifu (#18) - "justice, fairness, honesty, integrity"
- -nyofu (#20) - "straight, honest, upright"
- unyofu (P2a) - "straightness, uprightness, honesty"
- mtu adilifu (P2b) - "honest/just person"
- mtu wa kweli (P1b, P2b) - "truthful person"
- mwenye kweli (P1b, P2b) - "truthful person"
- -danganyifu (#23) - "deceptive, deceitful"
- udanganyifu (P2a) - "deceptiveness, deceitfulness"
- mdanganyifu (P2a) - "deceiver, liar"
- -bovu (#19) - "corrupt, rotten"
- ubovu (P2a) - "rottenness, corruption"
- kwa uadilifu (P2b) - "honestly, with integrity"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- -adilifu overlaps with justice domain
- mwenye kweli overlaps with propositional truth when describing truthful speech vs. character

---

### GROUP 6: Evidence, Proof, and Verification

**Definition:** Expressions used in contexts of establishing, confirming, or providing evidence for claims. These forms appear in legal proceedings, investigations, research, and situations requiring validation or proof. They mark the processes and materials used to establish what is true or valid.

**Representative expressions:**
- ushahidi (#15) - "evidence, testimony, witness"
- shahidi (P2a) - "witness (person)"
- shuhudia (P2a) - "to witness, to testify"
- hoja (#14) - "argument, reason, proof"
- thibitisha (#21) - "to confirm, verify, validate"
- uthibitisho (P2a) - "confirmation, verification"
- mthibitisho (P2a) - "proof, certificate"
- hakikisha (P2a) - "to ensure, to verify"
- hakikisha usahihi (P2b) - "ensure accuracy"
- thibitisha usahihi (P2b) - "confirm validity"
- tafuta ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to seek truth"
- gundua ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to discover truth"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- hoja overlaps with argumentation/reasoning
- thibitisha overlaps with authentication processes

---

### GROUP 7: Obviousness and Manifestation

**Definition:** Expressions used to mark what is evident, clear, obvious, or manifestly apparent. These forms appear when pointing out what should be visible or recognizable to observers, often in arguments or explanations. They indicate that truth or facts are openly displayed rather than hidden.

**Representative expressions:**
- dhahiri (#11) - "clear, evident, obvious"
- udhahiri (P2a) - "evidence, manifestation, obviousness"
- ni dhahiri (P2b) - "it's evident"
- ni dhahiri kwamba (P2b) - "it's evident that..."
- dhihirisha (P2a) - "to show, to demonstrate, to reveal"
- ukweli ulio wazi (P1b, P2b) - "obvious truth, clear truth"

---

### GROUP 8: Certainty and Confidence

**Definition:** Expressions used to mark the speaker's degree of certainty, confidence, or assurance about claims. These forms appear when emphasizing definiteness, removing doubt, or expressing strong conviction. They indicate epistemic stance regarding the reliability of knowledge or claims.

**Representative expressions:**
- hakika (#16) - "certain, sure, definite"
- uhakika (P2a) - "certainty, sureness"
- kwa hakika (P1b, P2b) - "certainly, surely"
- ni hakika kwamba (P2b) - "it's certain that..."
- kwa hakika kabisa (P2b) - "most certainly"
- ukweli usio na shaka (P1b, P2b) - "undoubted truth"
- bila shaka (P2b) - "without doubt"

---

### GROUP 9: Falsity and Invalidity

**Definition:** Expressions used to mark statements, claims, or documents as false, invalid, null, or without validity. These forms appear particularly in legal and formal contexts where official invalidation is required, as well as in general discourse about falsehood. They mark negation of truth or validity.

**Representative expressions:**
- batili (#12) - "false, invalid, null"
- ubatili (P2a) - "falsity, invalidity, nullity"
- ni batili (P2b) - "it's false/invalid"
- batilisha (P2a) - "to invalidate, to nullify"
- -a uongo (#13) - "false, fake, counterfeit"
- habari za uongo (P2b) - "false news"
- ushahidi wa uongo (P2b) - "false testimony"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- uongo (#4) overlaps with propositional denial but emphasizes deliberate deception
- batili more specialized for formal/legal contexts

---

### GROUP 10: Document Authentication and Validation

**Definition:** Expressions used specifically in administrative and legal contexts for validating, authenticating, or certifying documents, signatures, stamps, and copies. These forms appear in bureaucratic procedures requiring official verification. They mark whether documents meet formal requirements for acceptance.

**Representative expressions:**
- saini sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "valid signature"
- hati sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "valid document"
- nakala sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "certified copy"
- chapa sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "valid stamp"
- uthibitisho sahihi (P2b) - "valid verification"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- sahihi overlaps with general correctness evaluation
- thibitisha overlaps with general verification

---

### GROUP 11: Epistemic Actions and Processes

**Definition:** Expressions describing cognitive and communicative actions related to truth, including seeking, discovering, knowing, understanding, recognizing, accepting, confessing, hiding, and opposing truth. These forms appear in narratives about knowledge acquisition, disclosure, and resistance. They mark different ways agents interact with truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- jua ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to know the truth"
- elewa ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to understand the truth"
- tambua ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to recognize the truth"
- kubali ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to accept the truth"
- kiri ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to confess the truth"
- ficha ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to hide the truth"
- pinga ukweli (P1b, P2b) - "to oppose the truth"
- kuongea kweli (P1b, P2b) - "to speak truthfully"
- kuambia kweli (P1b, P2b) - "to tell the truth (to someone)"

---

### GROUP 12: Reasonableness and Sound Judgment

**Definition:** Expressions used to evaluate whether decisions, judgments, or persons exhibit reason, wisdom, or sound thinking. These forms appear in evaluative contexts assessing the rationality or sensibleness of actions and choices. They mark alignment with reasonable standards of judgment.

**Representative expressions:**
- -a busara (#28) - "reasonable, sensible, wise"
- mtu wa busara (P2b) - "reasonable person"
- uamuzi wa busara (P2b) - "reasonable decision"
- mwenye busara (P2b) - "wise person"

---

### GROUP 13: Procedural and Methodological Correctness

**Definition:** Expressions used to evaluate whether procedures, methods, ways of doing things, or timing are correct or appropriate. These forms appear in instructional and procedural contexts where proper execution matters. They mark conformity to proper methods rather than factual accuracy.

**Representative expressions:**
- njia sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct way, right method"
- namna sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct manner"
- wakati sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct time, right time"
- mahali sahihi (P1b, P2b) - "correct place"

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- These overlap with general correctness evaluation but emphasize procedure over fact

---

## ITEMS WITH UNCLEAR OR MULTIPLE GROUP MEMBERSHIP

**Items that span multiple groups:**
- **haki (#6)**: Functions in both propositional affirmation (haki ni kwamba...) and rights/justice domains
- **sahihi (#3)**: Spans correctness evaluation, document authentication, and procedural correctness
- **kweli (#1)**: Spans propositional affirmation and authenticity/genuineness
- **uadilifu (#18)**: Overlaps between justice domain and moral character
- **thibitisha (#21)**: Spans evidence/verification and document authentication

**Items with specialized or limited distribution:**
- **-tupu (#27)**: Appears primarily in negative evaluations marking emptiness/hollowness but limited attestation in truth domain
- **ukweli wa Mungu (P1b)**: Specialized religious usage
- **ukweli wa historia (P1b)**: Specialized historical/academic usage

**Items that resist clear grouping:**
- **sawa (#7)**: Functions across correctness, acceptability, and agreement contexts without clear boundaries
- **kamili (#9)**: Ambiguous between completeness and accuracy/perfection

---

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

1. **Overlap between propositional truth and authenticity**: The root -kweli participates in both evaluating truth of statements and marking genuineness of entities, showing these are not clearly separated in usage.

2. **Justice-truth connection**: The term haki bridges truth-evaluation and rights/justice domains, suggesting these concepts are linked in Swahili usage patterns.

3. **Correctness spans multiple domains**: Sahihi appears in factual accuracy, procedural correctness, and document validation, showing a broad semantic range.

4. **Moral character as distinct domain**: Expressions for moral uprightness (-adilifu, -nyofu) form a coherent group separate from propositional truth, though connected through honesty/truthfulness.

5. **Evidence and verification processes**: Forms related to proof and verification constitute a distinct procedural domain separate from truth-value judgments.

6. **Gradations not prominent**: Unlike some languages, Swahili truth vocabulary does not show extensive scalar or degree-based groupings (though intensifiers like kabisa exist).

7. **Legal/administrative specialization**: Document authentication forms a recognizable sub-domain with specialized vocabulary.
