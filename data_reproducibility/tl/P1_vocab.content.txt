# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

1. **totoo**
   - Literal meaning: true, real
   - Typical English translations: true, real, genuine, actual
   - Usage: Used to affirm the truth or reality of statements, events, or objects. Commonly appears in everyday speech to confirm accuracy or authenticity ("Totoo ba yan?" = "Is that true?").
   - [CORE]

2. **tunay**
   - Literal meaning: genuine, authentic
   - Typical English translations: true, genuine, authentic, real
   - Usage: Emphasizes authenticity or genuineness, often with emotional or qualitative weight. Used to describe sincere feelings, genuine articles, or true nature ("tunay na pagmamahal" = "true love").
   - [CORE]

3. **tama**
   - Literal meaning: correct, right
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, proper, accurate
   - Usage: Indicates correctness of statements, actions, or judgments. Widely used in evaluating answers, decisions, or conduct ("Tama ka" = "You're right").
   - [CORE]

4. **mali**
   - Literal meaning: wrong, incorrect
   - Typical English translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false
   - Usage: Denotes incorrectness, error, or wrongness in statements, actions, or moral conduct. Direct opposite of "tama" in most contexts.
   - [CORE]

5. **katotohanan**
   - Literal meaning: truth (derived from "totoo" + nominalizing affixes)
   - Typical English translations: truth, reality, fact
   - Usage: Abstract noun referring to truth as a concept or body of true statements. Used in formal and philosophical contexts ("Ano ang katotohanan?" = "What is the truth?").
   - [CORE]

6. **kasinungalingan**
   - Literal meaning: lie, falsehood (derived from "sinungaling" = liar)
   - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
   - Usage: Refers to false statements made with intent to deceive. Common in everyday and moral discourse about dishonesty.
   - [CORE]

7. **sinungaling**
   - Literal meaning: liar (one who lies)
   - Typical English translations: liar, dishonest person
   - Usage: Describes a person who habitually or characteristically tells lies. Strong negative moral judgment when applied to someone.
   - [CORE]

8. **peke**
   - Literal meaning: fake, counterfeit (borrowed from Spanish "feca")
   - Typical English translations: fake, counterfeit, phony, imitation
   - Usage: Describes objects or representations that are not genuine or authentic. Commonly used for counterfeit goods or insincere behavior.
   - [CORE]

9. **huwad**
   - Literal meaning: false, counterfeit, spurious
   - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit, spurious
   - Usage: More formal or literary term for something false or counterfeit. Often used in legal or formal contexts to describe fraudulent documents or false claims.
   - [CORE]

10. **wasto**
    - Literal meaning: correct, proper, accurate
    - Typical English translations: correct, accurate, proper, right
    - Usage: Indicates correctness with emphasis on precision or propriety. Often used in formal or technical contexts for accurate measurements or proper procedures.
    - [CORE]

11. **hustó**
    - Literal meaning: exact, just right, fitting (from Spanish "justo")
    - Typical English translations: exact, just right, fitting, appropriate
    - Usage: Describes something that is exactly right or perfectly fitting. Used for measurements, amounts, or appropriateness of actions.
    - [CORE]

12. **walang-katotohanan**
    - Literal meaning: without truth (walang = without + katotohanan = truth)
    - Typical English translations: untrue, false, baseless
    - Usage: Describes statements or claims that lack truth or factual basis. Used to dismiss false accusations or unfounded rumors.
    - [CORE]

13. **totóo nga**
    - Literal meaning: truly, indeed (totoo = true + nga = particle for emphasis)
    - Typical English translations: truly, indeed, really
    - Usage: Fixed expression used to emphasize or confirm the truth of a statement. Common in conversational affirmation.
    - [CORE]

14. **bulaán**
    - Literal meaning: false, untrue, fabricated
    - Typical English translations: false, untrue, fabricated, made-up
    - Usage: Describes statements or accounts that are false or fabricated. Can refer to lies or fictional narratives presented as truth.
    - [CORE]

15. **kathâ**
    - Literal meaning: fiction, invention, fabrication
    - Typical English translations: fiction, fabrication, invention, made-up story
    - Usage: Refers to something invented or fabricated, often creative fiction but also false stories. Used in literary contexts and to describe fabrications.
    - [CORE]

16. **di-totoo**
    - Literal meaning: not true (di = not + totoo = true)
    - Typical English translations: untrue, false, not real
    - Usage: Simple negation indicating something is not true or real. Common in everyday speech for denying truth claims.
    - [CORE]

17. **mapagkakatiwalaan**
    - Literal meaning: trustworthy, reliable (from "tiwala" = trust + affixes)
    - Typical English translations: trustworthy, reliable, dependable
    - Usage: Describes persons or things that can be trusted or relied upon. Used in evaluating character and reliability of sources.
    - [CORE]

18. **tapat**
    - Literal meaning: honest, faithful, loyal
    - Typical English translations: honest, faithful, sincere, loyal
    - Usage: Describes honesty in speech and faithfulness in conduct. Highly valued moral quality in personal and professional relationships.
    - [CORE]

19. **matapat**
    - Literal meaning: honest, sincere (ma- prefix + tapat)
    - Typical English translations: honest, sincere, truthful
    - Usage: Adjectival form emphasizing the quality of being honest or sincere. Used to describe honest persons or sincere statements.
    - [CORE]

20. **kawangis**
    - Literal meaning: resemblance, likeness, similarity
    - Typical English translations: resemblance, likeness, similar to
    - Usage: Indicates resemblance or similarity between things. Used in comparisons and descriptions of likeness.
    - [CORE]

21. **katulad**
    - Literal meaning: similar, like, same as (ka- + tulad = likeness)
    - Typical English translations: similar, like, the same as
    - Usage: Indicates similarity or sameness. Very common in comparative statements ("katulad mo" = "like you").
    - [CORE]

22. **kamukha**
    - Literal meaning: resembles in appearance (ka- + mukha = face)
    - Typical English translations: looks like, resembles, similar in appearance
    - Usage: Specifically refers to physical resemblance or appearance. Commonly used when comparing how people or things look.
    - [CORE]

23. **diwa**
    - Literal meaning: essence, spirit, true meaning
    - Typical English translations: essence, spirit, true meaning, substance
    - Usage: Refers to the essential nature or true meaning of something. Used in philosophical and interpretive contexts to discuss what something fundamentally is.
    - [CORE]

24. **tunay na kalikasan**
    - Literal meaning: true nature (tunay = true + na = linker + kalikasan = nature)
    - Typical English translations: true nature, real character, essence
    - Usage: Fixed expression referring to the fundamental or true nature of something. Used when revealing or discussing what something really is.
    - [CORE]

25. **wastô ang**
    - Literal meaning: correct is/are (wasto = correct + ang = marker)
    - Typical English translations: it is correct that, the correct one is
    - Usage: Construction used to affirm correctness or identify the correct option. Common in evaluative and instructional contexts.
    - [CORE]

26. **sa totoo lang**
    - Literal meaning: in truth only (sa = in + totoo = truth + lang = only)
    - Typical English translations: to be honest, truthfully, actually
    - Usage: Fixed expression used to introduce honest or frank statements. Very common conversational phrase for emphasizing sincerity.
    - [CORE]

27. **talaga**
    - Literal meaning: really, truly, indeed
    - Typical English translations: really, truly, indeed, actually
    - Usage: Emphasizes truth or reality of statements. Extremely common in everyday speech for confirmation and emphasis ("Talaga?" = "Really?").
    - [CORE]

28. **sigurado**
    - Literal meaning: sure, certain (from Spanish "seguro")
    - Typical English translations: sure, certain, definite
    - Usage: Indicates certainty or assurance about truth or occurrence. Common in expressing confidence about statements or predictions.
    - [CORE]
