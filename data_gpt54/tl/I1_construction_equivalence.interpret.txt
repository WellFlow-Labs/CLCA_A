## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Tagalog shows clear direct constructions for **ATTR**, **ADNOM**, **BECOME**, **VERIFICATION**, **NEGATION**, and **abstract NOMINALIZATION**.

For **REVELATION**, no construction is provided in P4a/P4b. There is therefore no attested basis here for saying that revelation substitutes for becoming. The available inchoative/resultative meaning is expressed directly by **magkatotoo / nagkatotoo**, so **BECOME is not replaced by REVELATION in the provided material**.

For **CAUSATION (factual)**, P4a/P4b explicitly state **no common expression identified**. The note mentions *wastuhin* only as a nearby corrective verb and explicitly says it is **not** established as a general causative truth-construction. Therefore this category is best treated as **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**, not as filled by a productive attested construction.

For **CAUSATION (character)**, no relevant construction is attested. P4b also notes that the source favors property terms such as *tapat, matapat, mapagkakatiwalaan*, but these are not presented as a causative construction. So this operator is simply absent in the provided material.

For **DISCONFIRMATION**, there is no separate dedicated construction labeled as such. Instead, the language material uses:
- ordinary analytic negation: **Hindi + predicate**
- support/basis denial: **walang saligan + ang NP**

So in this dataset, **DISCONFIRMATION is functionally expressed through NEGATION and BASIS/SUPPORT denial**, not through a distinct operator. This is an asymmetry: positive truth evaluation has simple predicate forms like **Totoo iyan**, while negative unsupportedness is often expressed with the separate predicative expression **Walang saligan ang paratang**, rather than just a negated positive adjective.

**NEGATION is analytic, not morphological**, in the attested core pattern: **Hindi + predicate + ang/si NP**. The fixed expression **walang saligan** is also analytic/predicative rather than inflectional.

**VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction**. Verification uses the borrowed procedural family:
- **beripikahin + object**
- **beripikadong + noun**

Disconfirmation, by contrast, is expressed through:
- **Hindi + predicate**
- **walang saligan + ang NP**

So the two functions are constructionally distinct in the provided material.

Multi-word expressions normalize as follows:
- **walang saligan**: predicative expression functioning like a predicate of unsupportedness/baselessness.
- **sa totoo lang**, **sa katotohanan**: clause-initial discourse-marking frames, not core operators in the canonical list.
- **beripikahin**: verb carrying the verification operator directly.
- **beripikadong**: adnominal modifier.
- **magkatotoo / nagkatotoo**: verbal inchoative/resultative forms carrying the become operator directly.
- No light-verb causative carrier is attested in the provided material.

### Lexical Gaps

- **BECOME** is attested specifically with the *totoo* root; broader lexical coverage is not established in P4a/P4b.

### Conceptual Gaps
- **CAUSATION (factual)** is not established as a productive truth-domain operation: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**
- **DISCONFIRMATION** is not represented by a distinct operator; it is **SUBSTITUTED BY NEGATION / BASIS-SUPPORT denial**

### Asymmetric Gaps
- Positive attribution is straightforward: **Totoo iyan.**, **Tumpak ang sinabi mo.**
- Negative unsupportedness often uses a different predicative operator: **Walang saligan ang paratang.**
- Thus there is an asymmetry between:
  - simple positive truth/correctness predicates, and
  - negative lack-of-basis expressions built from **saligan**
- Verification is directly attested, but corresponding factual causation is not; this is another asymmetry:
  - **VERIFICATION** = available
  - **CAUSATION (factual)** = no common expression identified
