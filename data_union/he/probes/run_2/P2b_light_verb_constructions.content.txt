# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 base items. Constructions already documented in P1b root expansion or P2a derivational system are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full, except where a construction warrants additional documentation of its syntactic or pragmatic properties. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven multi-word expressions that are not derivational forms.

Constructions are organized by P1 base item. Absence is recorded explicitly. No constructions are generated to fill expected semantic roles.

## Item 1 — אמת (*ʾemet*, "truth")

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לדבר אמת
- **Transliteration:** ledaber ʾemet
- **English translation:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
- **Usage notes:** The standard periphrastic construction for truth-telling, combining the verb *ledaber* ("to speak") with *ʾemet* as a direct object. Used across all registers in moral evaluation, legal testimony contexts, and everyday speech, e.g., *tsarikh ledaber ʾemet* ("one must tell the truth").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לומר את האמת
- **Transliteration:** lomar et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to tell the truth, to say the truth
- **Usage notes:** A near-synonym of *ledaber ʾemet* using the verb *lomar* ("to say") with the definite direct object *ha-ʾemet*. The definite article marks the truth as a specific, known truth rather than truth in the abstract. Common in everyday and legal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לגלות את האמת
- **Transliteration:** legallot et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to reveal the truth, to uncover the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *legallot* ("to reveal, to uncover") with *ha-ʾemet* as object, framing truth as something hidden that must be brought to light. Common in journalistic, investigative, and legal discourse, e.g., *ha-ḥaqiraʾh biqqeshah legallot et ha-ʾemet* ("the investigation sought to reveal the truth").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לחשוף את האמת
- **Transliteration:** laḥsof et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to expose the truth, to lay bare the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *laḥsof* ("to expose, to lay bare") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Carries a stronger connotation of exposing something deliberately concealed than *legallot*. Common in investigative journalism and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לברר את האמת
- **Transliteration:** levarer et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to ascertain the truth, to clarify the truth, to find out the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *levarer* ("to clarify, to ascertain") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Emphasizes the investigative process of determining what is true, e.g., *tsarikh levarer et ha-ʾemet* ("one must ascertain the truth"). Common in legal and investigative contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** להסתיר את האמת
- **Transliteration:** lehastir et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to hide the truth, to conceal the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *lehastir* ("to hide, to conceal") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Frames truth-concealment as an active, deliberate act. Common in moral condemnation and legal discourse, e.g., *hu histir et ha-ʾemet me-ha-shefeṭ* ("he concealed the truth from the judge").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לעוות את האמת
- **Transliteration:** leʿavvet et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to distort the truth, to twist the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *leʿavvet* ("to distort, to twist") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Describes the act of misrepresenting facts while maintaining a surface connection to them, as distinct from outright lying. Common in political and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לסלף את האמת
- **Transliteration:** lesalef et ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to falsify the truth, to distort the truth, to misrepresent the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the verb *lesalef* ("to distort, to falsify, to misrepresent") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Slightly more formal than *leʿavvet*; common in legal and journalistic registers to describe deliberate misrepresentation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** האמת היא ש...
- **Transliteration:** ha-ʾemet hi she-...
- **English translation:** the truth is that..., the fact is that...
- **Usage notes:** A fixed sentence-initial frame used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement, signaling that what follows is the genuine state of affairs as opposed to what may have been assumed. Extremely common in everyday speech and written discourse across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** אמת ויציב
- **Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-yatsiv
- **English translation:** true and established, truth and stability
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 5). A fixed liturgical phrase from the morning prayer service affirming the truth and permanence of the preceding declaration. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** אמת ושלום
- **Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-shalom
- **English translation:** truth and peace
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 6). A biblical and rabbinic paired expression used in ethical discourse. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לאמיתו של דבר
- **Transliteration:** le-ʾamito shel daʾvar
- **English translation:** in truth, in actual fact, to tell the truth about the matter
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 2) and P2a (Item 1). A fixed phrase signaling that what follows is the genuine underlying reality. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** אמת לאמיתה
- **Transliteration:** ʾemet le-ʾamitaʾh
- **English translation:** the absolute truth, truth in its truest sense
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 3). A fixed intensifying phrase emphasizing that something is unambiguously and completely true. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** רגע האמת
- **Transliteration:** regaʿ ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** the moment of truth
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 16). A fixed phrase marking a decisive or revelatory moment. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** חצי אמת
- **Transliteration:** ḥatsi ʾemet
- **English translation:** half-truth
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 14). A collocation describing a technically accurate but misleading statement. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לא כל האמת
- **Transliteration:** lo kol ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** not the whole truth, not the full truth
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used to characterize a statement as incomplete or selectively true, e.g., *hu ʾamar lo kol ha-ʾemet* ("he did not tell the whole truth"). Common in legal and everyday discourse as a characterization of partial disclosure.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** כל האמת ורק האמת
- **Transliteration:** kol ha-ʾemet ve-raq ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase modeled on the courtroom oath formula, used in legal testimony and in everyday speech to emphasize complete and exclusive truthfulness. Appears in Israeli legal procedure and in popular discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** לחיות עם האמת
- **Transliteration:** liḥyot ʿim ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to live with the truth, to face the truth
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used in psychological and everyday discourse to describe accepting and coming to terms with a difficult reality, e.g., *tsarikh liḥyot ʿim ha-ʾemet* ("one must face the truth"). Common in therapeutic and literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** להתמודד עם האמת
- **Transliteration:** lehitmodded ʿim ha-ʾemet
- **English translation:** to confront the truth, to deal with the truth
- **Usage notes:** Uses the reflexive verb *lehitmodded* ("to cope with, to confront") with *ha-ʾemet* as object. Emphasizes the psychological challenge of accepting a difficult truth. Common in therapeutic, journalistic, and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20:**
- **Base Form:** אמת
- **Example:** אמת מר / אמת כואבת
- **Transliteration:** ʾemet mar / ʾemet koʾevet
- **English translation:** bitter truth / painful truth
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 17 and 15). Collocations describing truths that are difficult to accept. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 2 — אמיתי (*ʾamiti*, "true, genuine, real, authentic")

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** חבר אמיתי
- **Transliteration:** ḥaver ʾamiti
- **English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
- **Usage notes:** A highly frequent attributive collocation used to distinguish a genuinely loyal and supportive friend from a superficial acquaintance. Common across all registers and age groups in Israeli Hebrew.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** אהבה אמיתית
- **Transliteration:** ʾahavaʾh ʾamitit
- **English translation:** true love, genuine love
- **Usage notes:** A frequent attributive collocation used in everyday, literary, and popular cultural discourse to describe love that is genuine rather than superficial or performative. Very common across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** בעיה אמיתית
- **Transliteration:** beʿayaʾh ʾamitit
- **English translation:** a real problem, a genuine problem
- **Usage notes:** An attributive collocation used to characterize a problem as genuinely serious rather than trivial or imagined, e.g., *ze beʿayaʾh ʾamitit* ("this is a real problem"). Common in everyday and professional discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** סיבה אמיתית
- **Transliteration:** sibaʾh ʾamitit
- **English translation:** the real reason, the genuine reason
- **Usage notes:** An attributive collocation used to distinguish the actual underlying reason for something from stated or apparent reasons, e.g., *maʾh ha-sibaʾh ha-ʾamitit?* ("what is the real reason?"). Common in investigative, analytical, and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** זה לא אמיתי
- **Transliteration:** ze lo ʾamiti
- **English translation:** this is not real, this is not genuine, this can't be real
- **Usage notes:** A predicative construction used both to deny the genuineness of something and, in colloquial usage, as an exclamation of disbelief or astonishment, e.g., *ze lo ʾamiti, hu ʾamar kaʾzot?* ("this can't be real, he said that?"). The exclamatory use is common in informal speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** רגש אמיתי
- **Transliteration:** regeʃ ʾamiti
- **English translation:** a genuine feeling, a real emotion
- **Usage notes:** An attributive collocation used in psychological, literary, and everyday discourse to describe an emotion as genuinely felt rather than performed or simulated, e.g., *hu hibit ʾalav be-regeʃ ʾamiti* ("he looked at him with genuine feeling").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** שמו האמיתי
- **Transliteration:** shmo ha-ʾamiti
- **English translation:** his real name, his true name
- **Usage notes:** An attributive collocation used to distinguish a person's actual name from a pseudonym, stage name, or alias, e.g., *shmo ha-ʾamiti hu Yosef, lo Yossi* ("his real name is Yosef, not Yossi"). Common in journalistic and everyday contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Base Form:** אמיתי
- **Example:** פנים אמיתיות
- **Transliteration:** panim ʾamitiyot
- **English translation:** one's true face, one's real self
- **Usage notes:** A collocation used figuratively to describe a person's genuine character or intentions as opposed to the persona they present publicly, e.g., *hiʿru et ha-panim ha-ʾamitiyot shelo* ("they revealed his true face"). Common in political and social commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 3 — נכון (*nakhon*, "correct, right, accurate, true")

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** זה נכון
- **Transliteration:** ze nakhon
- **English translation:** that is correct, that is right, that is true
- **Usage notes:** The most basic predicative construction with *nakhon*, used to affirm the accuracy or correctness of a statement. Extremely common across all registers as a response to a claim or assertion.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** זה לא נכון
- **Transliteration:** ze lo nakhon
- **English translation:** that is not correct, that is not right, that is wrong
- **Usage notes:** The negated predicative construction, used to deny the accuracy or correctness of a statement. Also used in moral evaluation to mean "that is not right/proper," e.g., *ze lo nakhon laaʿsot kaʾzot* ("it is not right to do such a thing"). Very common across all registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** נכון לומר ש...
- **Transliteration:** nakhon lomar she-...
- **English translation:** it is correct to say that..., it is accurate to say that...
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used to introduce a qualified or carefully framed assertion, signaling that the speaker considers the following statement to be accurate, e.g., *nakhon lomar she-ha-matsav hishtaper* ("it is correct to say that the situation has improved"). Common in formal and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** נכון לעכשיו
- **Transliteration:** nakhon le-ʿakhshav
- **English translation:** correct as of now, accurate at this time, as of now
- **Usage notes:** A fixed temporal qualification phrase used in journalistic, administrative, and everyday discourse to indicate that a statement is accurate at the present moment but may change, e.g., *ha-netunim nekhonim le-ʿakhshav* ("the data are accurate as of now"). Very common in news reporting and official communications.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** נכון לתאריך...
- **Transliteration:** nakhon le-taʾarikh...
- **English translation:** correct as of [date], accurate as of [date]
- **Usage notes:** A fixed administrative and journalistic phrase used to specify the date as of which information is accurate, e.g., *ha-netunim nekhonim le-taʾarikh 1 be-yanuar* ("the data are accurate as of January 1"). Standard in official documents, reports, and databases.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** לא נכון לומר ש...
- **Transliteration:** lo nakhon lomar she-...
- **English translation:** it is not correct to say that..., it would be inaccurate to say that...
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used to correct or qualify a claim, signaling that the following formulation is inaccurate or misleading, e.g., *lo nakhon lomar she-ha-mediniyut nakhshelah* ("it is not correct to say that the policy has failed"). Common in academic, journalistic, and political discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** נכון שאמרת ש...?
- **Transliteration:** nakhon she-ʾamarta she-...?
- **English translation:** is it correct that you said that...?, is it true that you said...?
- **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction used in legal cross-examination and everyday discourse to confirm or challenge a prior statement, e.g., *nakhon she-ʾamarta she-lo hayita sham?* ("is it correct that you said you were not there?"). Common in legal and confrontational discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Base Form:** נכון
- **Example:** הדבר הנכון לעשות
- **Transliteration:** ha-daʾvar ha-nakhon laʿasot
- **English translation:** the right thing to do, the correct course of action
- **Usage notes:** A fixed phrase used in moral and practical deliberation to identify the appropriate or correct action, e.g., *ze ha-daʾvar ha-nakhon laʿasot* ("this is the right thing to do"). Common in ethical, political, and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 4 — שקר (*sheker*, "lie, falsehood, deception, untruth")

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** לדבר שקר
- **Transliteration:** ledaber sheker
- **English translation:** to speak falsehood, to tell lies
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction parallel to *ledaber ʾemet*, using *sheker* as a direct object of *ledaber*. Used in moral and legal condemnation of lying, e.g., *asur ledaber sheker* ("it is forbidden to speak falsehood"). Common in religious and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** עד שקר
- **Transliteration:** ʿed sheker
- **English translation:** false witness, perjurer
- **Usage notes:** A fixed biblical and legal collocation for a witness who gives false testimony, derived from the ninth commandment (*lo taʿaneh be-reʿakha ʿed shaqer*, "you shall not bear false witness against your neighbor"). Used in both religious law and modern legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** עדות שקר
- **Transliteration:** ʿedut sheker
- **English translation:** false testimony, perjury
- **Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Item 4). A fixed legal and biblical collocation for deliberately false testimony. Cross-referenced here for completeness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** מדבר שקרים
- **Transliteration:** medaber sheqarim
- **English translation:** one who speaks lies, a liar
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using the plural *sheqarim* ("lies") as the object of *medaber* ("speaker"), used in biblical and literary registers to characterize a habitual liar, e.g., *peh medaber sheqarim* ("a mouth that speaks lies," from Psalms). More literary than *shaqran*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** שקר לבן
- **Transliteration:** sheker lavan
- **English translation:** white lie
- **Usage notes:** A calque from English "white lie," used in everyday discourse to describe a harmless or socially motivated falsehood told to avoid hurting someone's feelings, e.g., *ze rak sheker lavan* ("it's just a white lie"). Common in everyday speech; the calque is well established in modern Israeli Hebrew.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** שקר מוחלט
- **Transliteration:** sheker muḥlat
- **English translation:** an outright lie, an absolute falsehood
- **Usage notes:** An attributive collocation used to characterize a statement as completely and unambiguously false, e.g., *ze sheker muḥlat* ("that is an outright lie"). Common in political and everyday discourse as a strong denial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** מרחק מן השקר
- **Transliteration:** merḥaq min ha-sheker
- **English translation:** distance from falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A phrase derived from the biblical injunction *midvar sheker tirḥaq* ("distance yourself from falsehood," Exodus 23:7), used in ethical and religious discourse to describe the obligation to avoid any association with deception. Appears in rabbinic and modern ethical writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** מידבר שקר תרחק
- **Transliteration:** midvar sheker tirḥaq
- **English translation:** distance yourself from falsehood, keep far from falsehood
- **Usage notes:** The biblical injunction from Exodus 23:7, cited as a fixed phrase in religious, ethical, and legal discourse. Used to establish the normative obligation to avoid not only lying but any proximity to deception. A foundational phrase in Jewish legal and ethical literature.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** לבנות על שקר
- **Transliteration:** livnot ʿal sheker
- **English translation:** to build on a lie, to be founded on falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used figuratively to describe a relationship, institution, or claim whose foundation is false, e.g., *ha-yedidut ha-zot benuyyah ʿal sheker* ("this friendship is built on a lie"). Common in everyday and literary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10:**
- **Base Form:** שקר
- **Example:** לחיות בשקר
- **Transliteration:** liḥyot be-sheker
- **English translation:** to live a lie, to live in falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used in psychological and literary discourse to describe a person whose life or self-presentation is fundamentally false, e.g., *hu ḥay be-sheker* ("he is living a lie"). Common in therapeutic and literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 5 — מזויף (*mezuyaf*, "forged, counterfeit, fake")

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** תעודת זהות מזויפת
- **Transliteration:** teʿudat zehut mezuyefet
- **English translation:** a forged identity document, a fake ID
- **Usage notes:** The most common attributive collocation with *mezuyaf*, used in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts to describe a fraudulently produced identity document. A standard phrase in Israeli criminal law and security discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** מסמך מזויף
- **Transliteration:** mismaḵh mezuyaf
- **English translation:** a forged document, a falsified document
- **Usage notes:** A standard legal and administrative collocation for any document that has been fraudulently produced or altered, e.g., *הגיש מסמך מזויף לבית המשפט* ("he submitted a forged document to the court"). Very common in legal and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** חתימה מזויפת
- **Transliteration:** ḥatimaʾh mezuyefet
- **English translation:** a forged signature
- **Usage notes:** A standard legal collocation for a signature that has been fraudulently reproduced or imitated, e.g., *ha-ḥatimaʾh ʿal ha-sheq hayetah mezuyefet* ("the signature on the check was forged"). Common in criminal law and banking contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** שטר מזויף
- **Transliteration:** shtar mezuyaf
- **English translation:** a counterfeit banknote, a forged bill
- **Usage notes:** A standard legal and financial collocation for counterfeit currency, e.g., *nimtsaʾu shtarot mezuyafim be-veit ha-ʿesek* ("counterfeit banknotes were found at the business"). Common in criminal law and banking discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** ציור מזויף
- **Transliteration:** tsiyur mezuyaf
- **English translation:** a forged painting, a fake artwork
- **Usage notes:** A collocation used in art authentication and legal contexts to describe a work of art that has been fraudulently attributed to a different artist or period, e.g., *ha-tsiyur hitgalaʾh ke-mezuyaf* ("the painting turned out to be a forgery"). Common in art market and legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** תוצאות מזויפות
- **Transliteration:** totsaʾot mezuyafot
- **English translation:** falsified results, rigged results
- **Usage notes:** A collocation used in electoral, scientific, and sports contexts to describe results that have been fraudulently manipulated, e.g., *totsaʾot ha-beḥirot hayu mezuyafot* ("the election results were falsified"). Common in political and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** מזויף
- **Example:** מוצר מזויף
- **Transliteration:** mutsaʾr mezuyaf
- **English translation:** a counterfeit product, a fake product
- **Usage notes:** A collocation used in consumer protection and commercial law to describe goods that fraudulently imitate genuine branded products, e.g., *mutsarim mezuyafim ba-shuk* ("counterfeit products in the market"). Common in commercial and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 6 — מקורי (*meqori*, "original, authentic, genuine")

---

- **Base Form:** צדק
- **Example:** עיוות צדק
- **Transliteration:** ʿivut tsedeq
- **English translation:** miscarriage of justice, perversion of justice
- **Usage notes:** A fixed legal collocation for a serious failure of the justice system to produce a just outcome, e.g., *ha-harshʿaʾh hayetah ʿivut tsedeq* ("the conviction was a miscarriage of justice"). Standard in Israeli legal discourse and journalism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
