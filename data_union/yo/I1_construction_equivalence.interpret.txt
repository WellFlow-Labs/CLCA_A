## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION as a sub-type of BECOME, not a replacement

P4a documents *farahàn pé* + propositional complement (*ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* — "it became clear that it is the truth") as a distinct lexical item within the BECOME construction set. The verb *farahàn* ("to become visible/clear") is specifically used for epistemic revelation — the emergence of clarity about a proposition — while *di* + nominal covers general change-of-state and the motion verbs (*padà sí*, *jáde kúrò nínú*, *yí padà*) cover directional or orientational change. REVELATION does not replace BECOME in Yoruba; rather, it is one of four periphrastic sub-patterns that together cover the BECOME operator. The *farahàn* sub-pattern is the only one that takes a propositional (*pé*-clause) complement; the others take nominal or adjectival complements. This is a functional specialization within a shared construction type, not a substitution relationship.

### 2.2 CAUSATION (character) substituted by CAUSATION (factual)

No structurally distinct construction separates character-causation from factual causation in the attested data. Both are expressed through the *fi*-instrumental serial verb pattern. The example P4a provides for character-causation (*Ìdánwò ló fi ìwà ẹni hàn* — "it is testing that causes a person's character to be revealed") uses the same *fi* + [NOUN] + *hàn* structure as the factual-causation examples (*ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* — "he/she presented evidence before the court"). The focus construction (*ló*) adds identificational emphasis in the character-causation example but is not structurally obligatory for that reading. CAUSATION (character) is therefore **substituted by** the CAUSATION (factual) construction; the two are not formally distinguished.

### 2.3 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction types

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are expressed through the same set of construction types, with affirmative and negative forms respectively. The existential pair *wà* / *kò sí*, the copular pair *jẹ́* / *kò jẹ́* (and *kì í ṣe*), and the stative pair *tọ́* / *kò tọ́* are all exact structural parallels. P4a explicitly notes this parallelism for the negative attribution constructions (§2 of P4a: "fully parallel to the affirmative construction"). Neither VERIFICATION nor DISCONFIRMATION has a dedicated construction; both are distributed across the same three construction types. The two operators are therefore not formally distinguished from each other or from general ATTR and EXISTENTIAL ASSERTION.

### 2.4 NEGATION: analytic vs. morphological asymmetry

Negation in Yoruba is primarily analytic at the syntactic level: *kò* precedes stative and verbal predicates, *kì í ṣe* is the negative copula for nominal predicates, and *kò sí* is the negative existential. These three forms are not interchangeable; their distribution is conditioned by predicate type (stative verbal, nominal, existential). One morphological negation sub-pattern exists — the *àì-* prefix on nominalized forms (*àìmọ̀*, *àìgbàgbọ́*, *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*) — but this is restricted to nominalized forms and does not apply to verbal or copular predicates. There is therefore an asymmetry: morphological negation is available only within the NOMINALIZATION domain, while syntactic negation covers all other predicate types.

### 2.5 Positive–negative asymmetry in agentive nominalization

P4a documents negative agentive forms (*aláìgbàgbọ́* — "unbeliever", *aláìmọ̀* — "ignorant person") using the *aláì-* prefix. The corresponding affirmative agentive forms use *a-*, *olù-*, or the periphrastic *ènìyàn* + [noun] pattern. There is no single affix that pairs symmetrically with *aláì-* across all roots; the affirmative agentive system uses multiple prefix classes while the negative agentive system uses a single prefix (*aláì-*). This is an asymmetry in the morphological paradigm: the negative agentive is more formally unified than the affirmative agentive.

### 2.6 DISCOVERY not attested as a distinct operator

P4a does not document a construction labeled DISCOVERY (in the sense of coming to know something for the first time through investigation). The closest attested patterns are the propositional complement construction (*mọ̀ pé* — "know that") and the epistemic revelation construction (*farahàn pé* — "it became clear that"). Neither is specifically restricted to first-time discovery as opposed to ongoing knowledge or testimony. No substitution relationship is therefore reportable; DISCOVERY as a distinct operator is simply not documented in P4a.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps (missing constructions)

- **Morphological causative:** No causative morpheme is attested. All causative-adjacent meaning is expressed periphrastically through the *fi*-instrumental serial verb construction or the permissive-causative *jẹ́ kí*. P4a explicitly states this across three separate notes in §5. This is a morphosyntactic gap, not a lexical one: the meaning is expressible, but only through multi-word constructions.
- **Morphological inchoative:** No inchoative morpheme is attested. All change-of-state meaning is expressed periphrastically through *di* + nominal, motion verbs, or the serial verb *yí padà*. P4a explicitly states this in §4. Same status as the causative gap.
- **Single unified VERIFICATION construction:** No single construction is dedicated to the VERIFICATION operator. The function is distributed across existential assertion (*wà*), testimonial propositional complement (*jẹ́rìí pé*), and copular attribution (*jẹ́* + evaluative nominal). This is a structural gap in the sense that no single operator maps to VERIFICATION; it is not a lexical gap, since the relevant vocabulary and constructions exist.
- **Single unified DISCONFIRMATION construction:** Same status as VERIFICATION. The function is distributed across *kò sí*, *kò jẹ́* / *kì í ṣe*, and *kò* + stative. No dedicated DISCONFIRMATION construction exists.
- **Formally distinct CAUSATION (character) construction:** No construction is structurally distinct from CAUSATION (factual) for the character-causation reading. The *fi*-instrumental pattern covers both.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **No single word for "to verify" or "to authenticate" as a verb:** P4a documents the nominal *ìfọwọ́sí* ("authentication") and the agentive *ẹlẹ́rìí* ("witness"), but no single verbal root meaning "to verify" is documented as a primary item. The function is expressed through *jẹ́rìí pé* (testify that), *fi ẹ̀rí hàn* (present evidence), or copular attribution. This is a lexical gap in the verbal paradigm for VERIFICATION.
- **No single word for "to discover" (first-time epistemic access):** As noted in §2.6, no dedicated discovery verb is attested. *mọ̀* ("to know") and *farahàn* ("to become clear") cover adjacent meanings but are not restricted to first-time discovery.
- **Productive *ẹlẹ́-* contraction limited to one attested base:** P4a explicitly states that the *ẹlẹ́-* contraction is lexicalized rather than fully productive, attested specifically for *ẹ̀rí* → *ẹlẹ́rìí* and the extension *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú*. Extension to other truth-domain nouns is not documented. This is a lexical gap in the agentive nominalization paradigm.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from P4a/P4b. All ten CLCA operators are expressible in Yoruba through some combination of attested constructions, even where no single dedicated construction exists. The absence of morphological causative and inchoative forms does not block the relevant meanings; it only requires periphrastic expression.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (only one polarity attested or productive)

- **Negative agentive more formally unified than affirmative agentive:** The *aláì-* prefix provides a single unified negative agentive strategy, while the affirmative agentive system distributes across *a-*, *olù-*, *ẹlẹ́-*, and periphrastic *ènìyàn* + [noun]. The negative side is morphologically more regular than the positive side within this sub-domain.
- **Morphological negation restricted to nominalized forms:** The *àì-* prefix applies only to nominalized bases (*àìmọ̀*, *àìgbàgbọ́*). No corresponding morphological affirmative prefix exists that pairs with *àì-* across the same bases; the affirmative nominals (*ìmọ̀*, *ìgbàgbọ́*) use the *ì-* prefix, which is not the direct positive counterpart of *àì-* in a paradigmatic sense. The negative morphological strategy is therefore present where the affirmative morphological strategy uses a different prefix class.
- **Negative existential (*kò sí*) more lexically specific than affirmative existential (*wà*):** *kò sí* is a fused negative existential form, while the affirmative *wà* is a general existential verb. The negative form is more grammaticalized (fused) than the affirmative form. This is a minor formal asymmetry with no functional consequence for the CLCA mapping.
- **No asymmetry in ATTR positive vs. negative:** The affirmative and negative attribution constructions are fully parallel in P4a; no gap exists on either side.
