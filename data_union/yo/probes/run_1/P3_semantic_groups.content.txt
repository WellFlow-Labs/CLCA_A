# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed across the P1 and P2 data. Where the data show that terms are used together, opposed to each other, or restricted to the same discourse contexts, this is treated as evidence of grouping. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups reflects what the data support; no attempt has been made to produce a balanced or exhaustive taxonomy.

---

## Group 1: Terms Used in the Act of Speaking Truly or Falsely

**Label:** Terms used in the act of speaking truly or falsely

**Definition:** These terms appear primarily as objects of the verb *sọ* (to say/speak) or in constructions that evaluate an utterance as true or false. They are the terms most directly associated with the speech act of asserting something, and they are consistently opposed to each other in the same constructions and proverbs. The core contrast is between *sọ òtítọ́* (speak the truth) and *sọ irọ́* (tell a lie), which function as a matched pair across moral, legal, and everyday discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *òtítọ́* — truth, truthfulness (P1 item 1)
- *irọ́* — lie, falsehood (P1 item 2)
- *sọ òtítọ́* — speak the truth (P2b construction 1)
- *sọ irọ́* — tell a lie (P2b construction 12)
- *má sọ irọ́* — do not tell a lie (P2b construction 15)
- *òtítọ́ ni* — it is the truth, emphatic (P2b construction 5)
- *irọ́ pátápátá* — complete/total lie (P2a item 8)
- *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀* — speak with truth, speak truthfully (P2b construction 8)
- *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* — correct/truthful utterance (P1b Root 1, item 19)

**Notes:** *òtítọ́* and *irọ́* are the most consistently paired terms in the entire dataset. They appear in parallel constructions (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), in parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú* / *irọ́ kò ní dúró*), and in the compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness), which is formed by negating the truth-function of the witness role. *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* (correct speech, P1b Root 1 item 13) and *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* also belong here as they evaluate utterances for accuracy, though they shade toward Group 2 as well.

---

## Group 2: Terms Used to Evaluate Whether Something Meets a Standard of Correctness

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate whether something meets a standard of correctness

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where an action, measurement, answer, procedure, or statement is assessed against a criterion of correctness or accuracy. They are not restricted to speech acts and apply broadly to objects, measurements, conduct, and decisions. The predicative construction *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* (it is correct / it is not correct) is the most basic instantiation of this group, and the root *tọ́* is the morphological core.

**Representative expressions:**
- *òtọ́* — correct, right, accurate (P1 item 13)
- *ó tọ́* — it is correct (P2b construction 56)
- *kò tọ́* — it is not correct (P2b construction 57)
- *ó tọ́ pé* — it is right that (P2b construction 58)
- *kò tọ́ pé* — it is not right that (P2b construction 59)
- *ìdáhùn tọ́* — correct answer (P1b Root 1, item 17)
- *ìwọ̀n tọ́* — accurate measurement (P1b Root 1, item 18)
- *ìlànà tọ́* — correct procedure (P1b Root 1, item 16)
- *ìgbésẹ̀ tọ́* — correct action/step (P1b Root 1, item 20)
- *àṣìṣe* — error, mistake (P1 item 15)
- *àìtọ́* — incorrectness (P2a item 2)
- *àtúntọ́* / *ìtúntọ́* — correction, rectification (P2a items 5, 10 in P1b)
- *ìtọ́ni* — guidance, direction, correction (P1 item 14)

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 by its breadth of application: *ó tọ́* can evaluate a measurement, an answer, a procedure, or a moral action, not only a speech act. *àṣìṣe* belongs here as the primary term for error or deviation from a standard, and it contrasts with *òtọ́* in the same way that *irọ́* contrasts with *òtítọ́* in Group 1 — though the contrast in this group is less tightly paired. *ìtọ́ni* and *àtúntọ́* appear here because they describe the process of bringing something back into conformity with a standard.

## Group 3: Terms Used in Legal and Quasi-Legal Proceedings to Establish What Happened

**Label:** Terms used in legal and quasi-legal proceedings to establish what happened

**Definition:** These terms appear specifically in contexts where the truth of a past event or claim is being formally established through testimony, evidence, questioning, or judgment. They are associated with the roles of witness, judge, and party in a proceeding, and they participate in constructions about producing, evaluating, accepting, or rejecting proof. This group is defined by its restriction to adversarial or evaluative proceedings rather than by any single semantic feature.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ẹ̀rí* — evidence, proof, testimony (P1 item 8)
- *ẹ̀rí ojú* — eyewitness testimony (P1 item 9)
- *ẹ̀rí etí* — hearsay evidence (P1b Root 3, item 2)
- *ẹlẹ́rìí* — witness (P2a item 21)
- *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* — eyewitness (P2a item 22)
- *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* — false witness, perjurer (P2a item 23)
- *ìjẹ́rìísí* — testimony, attestation, certification (P1 item 10)
- *jẹ́rìí* — to testify, to bear witness (P1 item 11)
- *jẹ́rìí pé* — to testify that (P1b Root 3, item 22)
- *jẹ́rìí fún* — to testify for (P1b Root 3, item 9)
- *mú ẹ̀rí wá* — bring evidence, produce proof (P2b construction 41)
- *ẹ̀rí rẹ kò tó* — your evidence is insufficient (P2b construction 42)
- *ẹ̀rí kò sí* — there is no evidence (P2b construction 44)
- *ẹ̀rí tó péye* — sufficient evidence (P1b Root 3, item 7)
- *ẹ̀rí tó gbára* — reliable evidence (P1b Root 3, item 8)
- *ẹ̀rí ìwé* — documentary evidence (P1b Root 3, item 4)
- *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu* — interrogation, formal questioning (P1 item 27)
- *fọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu ẹni* — interrogate a person (P2b construction 95)
- *ìdánilójú* — assurance, verification, confirmation (P1 item 25)
- *ìdájọ́* — judgment, verdict, ruling (P1 item 28)
- *dájọ́ lórí* — pass judgment on (P2b construction 96)
- *adájọ́* — judge (P2a item 51)
- *ilé ìdájọ́* — court of law (P2a item 52)

**Notes:** This group is the most internally differentiated in the dataset. Within it, there is a sub-pattern around the hierarchy of evidence types: *ẹ̀rí ojú* (eyewitness) is consistently marked as more reliable than *ẹ̀rí etí* (hearsay), and *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* is the most credible witness category. The compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness) is notable because it combines a term from this group (*ẹlẹ́rìí*) with a term from Group 1 (*irọ́*), marking the violation of the witness role's truth-function. *ìdánwò* (test, examination) overlaps with this group but is treated separately below.

---

## Group 4: Terms Used to Evaluate Whether a Person Has the Epistemic Basis for a Claim

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate whether a person has the epistemic basis for a claim

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is not whether a statement is true or false, but whether the person making it actually knows what they are talking about. They evaluate the epistemic standing of a speaker or agent — whether they have genuine knowledge, or whether their claim is undermined by ignorance. The contrast between *ìmọ̀* and *àìmọ̀* is the core opposition in this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìmọ̀* — knowledge, understanding, awareness (P1 item 4)
- *àìmọ̀* — ignorance, lack of knowledge (P1 item 5)
- *mọ̀ dájú* — know for certain (P1b Root 2, item 10)
- *ìmọ̀ dájú* — certain knowledge (P1b Root 2, item 11)
- *ẹni tó mọ̀* — one who knows (P1b Root 2, item 12)
- *ẹni tí kò mọ̀* — one who does not know (P1b Root 2, item 13)
- *mọ̀ pé* — know that (propositional knowledge) (P1b Root 2, item 25)
- *nítorí àìmọ̀* — because of ignorance (P2b construction 30)
- *jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀* — come out of ignorance (P2b construction 32)
- *ìmọ̀ jinlẹ̀* — deep knowledge (P1b Root 2, item 2)
- *ìmọ̀ gígbòòrò* — broad knowledge (P1b Root 2, item 3)
- *ìmọ̀ kò ní pé* — knowledge is never complete (P2b construction 28)
- *aláìmọ̀* — ignorant person (P2a item 13)
- *amọ̀na* — one who knows the way, guide (P2a item 11)

**Notes:** The use of *nítorí àìmọ̀* (because of ignorance) as a mitigating factor in moral and legal assessments is a distinctive usage pattern: ignorance functions here as an explanation that reduces culpability, which is a different function from the terms in Group 1 (which evaluate the content of a statement) or Group 3 (which evaluate evidence in a proceeding). *mọ̀ dájú* and *ìmọ̀ dájú* are notable because they combine the knowledge root with a certainty marker, placing them at the intersection of this group and Group 3.

## Group 5: Terms Used to Evaluate Whether a Person or Thing Is Authentic Rather Than Imitation or Pretense

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate whether a person or thing is authentic rather than imitation or pretense

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the question is whether something or someone is what it appears to be — whether the outward presentation corresponds to an inner or underlying reality. They are used in commercial, interpersonal, and moral contexts to distinguish genuine items, relationships, or conduct from counterfeits, superficial performances, or deceptions. They do not primarily evaluate propositions or speech acts.

**Representative expressions:**
- *gidi* — genuine, real, authentic (P1 item 12)
- *gidi gidi* — very genuine, intensified (P2a item 28)
- *ojúlówó* — genuine, authentic, legitimate (P1 item 20)
- *ojúlówó ni* — it is the genuine one (P2b construction 78)
- *kò jẹ́ ojúlówó* — it is not genuine (P2b construction 79)
- *ọrẹ gidi* — a genuine friend (P2b construction 52)
- *ìfẹ́ gidi* — genuine love (P2b construction 53)
- *èyí ni gidi* — this is the real one (P2b construction 54)
- *kò jẹ́ gidi* — it is not genuine (P2b construction 55)
- *àgàbàgebè* — pretense, hypocrisy, sham (P1 item 22)
- *ṣe àgàbàgebè* — engage in pretense (P2b construction 83)
- *àgàbàgebè ló ń ṣe* — what they are doing is pretense (P2b construction 84)
- *aṣàgàbàgebè* — hypocrite, pretender (P2a item 42)
- *ìfarahàn* — genuine manifestation, self-disclosure (P1 item 23)

**Notes:** *gidi* and *ojúlówó* are near-synonymous in many contexts but *ojúlówó* appears more in commercial and legal assessments of legitimacy, while *gidi* is more productive in interpersonal and colloquial contexts (e.g., *ọrẹ gidi*, *ìfẹ́ gidi*). *àgàbàgebè* is the primary term for the opposite of authenticity in this group — it specifically marks the gap between outward appearance and inner reality, which is the defining concern of the group. *ìfarahàn* belongs here because it describes genuine self-disclosure as opposed to concealment, though it also appears in religious contexts (see Group 7 below).

---

## Group 6: Terms Used in Moral Evaluation of a Person's Character and Conduct

**Label:** Terms used in moral evaluation of a person's character and conduct

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the object of evaluation is a person's fundamental nature or habitual conduct rather than a specific statement, action, or object. They are used to assess whether a person is, at a deep level, good or bad, righteous or corrupt. The compound *ìwà rere* / *ìwà burúkú* is the core opposition, and *ìwà* itself is the most general term in this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìwà* — character, conduct, nature (P1 item 17)
- *ìwà rere* — good character, virtue (P1 item 18)
- *ìwà burúkú* — bad character, vice (P1 item 19)
- *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* — character is what makes a person (P2b construction 71)
- *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* — good character saves a person (P2b construction 72)
- *ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn* — bad character destroys a person (P2b construction 73)
- *ìwà rere ju ẹwà lọ* — good character surpasses beauty (P2b construction 76)
- *yẹra fún ìwà burúkú* — avoid bad character (P2b construction 77)
- *ní ìwà rere* — have good character (P2b construction 75)
- *ìwà tọ́* — upright/correct character (P1b Root 1, item 14)
- *òdodo* — justice, righteousness, fairness (P1 item 3)
- *ṣe òdodo* — do justice, act righteously (P2b construction 19)
- *alòdodo* — just/righteous person (P2a item 9)
- *àìdodo* — injustice, unrighteousness (P2a item 10)
- *olòtítọ́* — truthful person (P2a item 1)
- *aláìrọ́* — one who does not lie (P2a item 6)
- *aṣírọ́* — liar (P2a item 7)

**Notes:** This group is defined by the evaluation of persons rather than propositions, objects, or events. The proverb *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (character is what makes a person) is the clearest statement of the group's central concern. *òdodo* belongs here because it evaluates persons and conduct as righteous or just, though it also appears in legal contexts (Group 3) in the compound *ìdájọ́ òdodo*. The agentive forms *olòtítọ́*, *aláìrọ́*, and *aṣírọ́* belong here because they evaluate persons by their habitual truth-telling or lying behavior, even though the root terms (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*) belong primarily to Group 1.

## Group 7: Terms Used in Religious and Moral Contexts to Describe Acceptance of Something as True or Reliable

**Label:** Terms used in religious and moral contexts to describe acceptance of something as true or reliable

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where a person's stance toward a claim, entity, or person is evaluated — specifically, whether they accept something as true, reliable, or worthy of trust. They are most prominent in religious discourse (Christian and Islamic Yoruba) but also appear in interpersonal and moral contexts. The key distinction from Group 4 is that these terms evaluate the stance of the believer or truster, not the epistemic basis of a claim.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìgbàgbọ́* — belief, faith, trust, conviction (P1 item 6)
- *ní ìgbàgbọ́* — have faith (P2b construction 33)
- *dúró nínú ìgbàgbọ́* — stand firm in faith (P2b construction 34)
- *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ ló gbani là* — your faith is what saves you (P2b construction 35)
- *padà sí ìgbàgbọ́* — return to faith (P2b construction 36)
- *ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú* — faith without works is dead (P2b construction 37)
- *aláìgbàgbọ́* — unbeliever (P2a item 16)
- *olùgbàgbọ́* — believer (P2a item 17)
- *ìgbàgbọ́ jinlẹ̀* — deep faith (P2a item 18)
- *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — trust, reliance, dependability (P1 item 7)
- *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé ẹni* — rely on a person (P2b construction 38)
- *ẹni tí a lè gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — a person who can be relied upon (P2b construction 39)
- *aláìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — untrustworthy person (P2a item 19)
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbàgbọ́* — attestation/declaration of faith (P1b Root 3, item 13)
- *ìmọ̀ àti ìgbàgbọ́* — knowledge and belief (P1b Root 2, item 24)

**Notes:** *ìgbàgbọ́* and *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* are distinct within this group: *ìgbàgbọ́* evaluates acceptance of a proposition or entity as true (belief, faith), while *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* evaluates reliance on a person or thing as dependable (trust, reliability). However, both appear in the same discourse contexts — religious and interpersonal — and both evaluate a person's stance rather than the content of a claim. The construction *ìmọ̀ àti ìgbàgbọ́* (knowledge and belief) is notable because it explicitly contrasts this group with Group 4, treating knowing and believing as distinct but related stances.

## Group 8: Terms Used to Evaluate Conduct as Involving Intentional Misrepresentation

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate conduct as involving intentional misrepresentation

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the evaluation is not merely that something is false, but that a person has deliberately acted to create a false impression in another person. They imply intentionality and are used in legal and moral condemnation of deceptive conduct. They are distinguished from *irọ́* (Group 1) by their focus on the act of deceiving rather than the content of a false statement.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìtànjẹ́* — deception, fraud, deceit (P1 item 21)
- *tànjẹ́ ẹni* — deceive a person (P2b construction 81)
- *ṣe ìtànjẹ́* — commit deception (P2b construction 80)
- *ìtànjẹ́ kò ní gbádùn* — deception will not prosper (P2b construction 82)
- *aṣètànjẹ́* — deceiver, fraudster (P2a item 40)
- *ìtànjẹ́ ńlá* — major fraud (P2a item 41)
- *àgàbàgebè* — pretense, hypocrisy, sham (P1 item 22)
- *aṣàgàbàgebè* — hypocrite, pretender (P2a item 42)

**Notes:** *ìtànjẹ́* and *àgàbàgebè* are both in this group but differ in emphasis: *ìtànjẹ́* is used primarily in legal and commercial contexts of fraud and deliberate deception, while *àgàbàgebè* is used in moral and social contexts of hypocrisy and performative insincerity. Both are distinguished from *irọ́* (a false statement) by their focus on the deceptive act and the deceiver's intention. *àgàbàgebè* also belongs to Group 5 (authenticity vs. pretense), illustrating overlap between groups.

## Group 9: Terms Used in Contexts of Testing or Verifying Something Before Accepting It

**Label:** Terms used in contexts of testing or verifying something before accepting it

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where a claim, person, or object is subjected to a process of examination or testing in order to establish its truth, quality, or reliability. They describe the procedural dimension of truth-establishment — the process of finding out rather than the state of knowing. They appear in educational, scientific, legal, and religious contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìdánwò* — test, trial, examination (P1 item 26)
- *dánwò rẹ̀* — test it, examine it (P2b construction 93)
- *ṣe ìdánwò* — conduct a test (P2b construction 90)
- *kọjá ìdánwò* — pass a test (P2b construction 91)
- *kùnà ìdánwò* — fail a test (P2b construction 92)
- *olùdánwò* — examiner (P2a item 47)
- *ìdánwò ńlá* — major examination (P2a item 48)
- *ìdánilójú* — assurance, verification, confirmation (P1 item 25)
- *fún ní ìdánilójú* — give assurance/verification (P2b construction 87)
- *ìdánilójú wà* — there is assurance/confirmation (P2b construction 88)
- *ìdánilójú kò sí* — there is no assurance (P2b construction 89)
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* — evidence from testing (P1b Root 3, item 21)

**Notes:** *ìdánwò* and *ìdánilójú* are related but distinct: *ìdánwò* describes the process of testing or examining, while *ìdánilójú* describes the outcome of establishing certainty in someone's mind. Both appear in legal and educational contexts, and both are oriented toward the process of establishing truth rather than asserting it. *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* (evidence from testing) links this group to Group 3, illustrating how test results can become evidence in a proceeding.

## Group 10: Terms Used in Contexts of Moral Transgression and Its Consequences

**Label:** Terms used in contexts of moral transgression and its consequences

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where conduct is evaluated as a violation of a moral or religious norm, and where consequences — guilt, punishment, forgiveness — are at issue. They are used in religious discourse (Christian and Islamic Yoruba) and in moral instruction. The primary distinction within this group is between *àṣìṣe* (error, which may be unintentional) and *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (sin/guilt, which implies moral culpability).

**Representative expressions:**
- *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — sin, guilt, offense (P1 item 16)
- *ṣe ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — commit sin (P2b construction 66)
- *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — confess sin (P2b construction 67)
- *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì* — forgive sin (P2b construction 68)
- *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀* — sin will not go unpunished (P2b construction 69)
- *wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — be in a state of sin (P2b construction 70)
- *alẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — sinner (P2a item 33)
- *àìṣẹ̀* — sinlessness, innocence (P2a item 34)
- *àṣìṣe* — error, mistake, fault (P1 item 15)
- *ṣe àṣìṣe* — make an error (P2b construction 62)
- *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* — confess/admit an error (P2b construction 63)
- *darí àṣìṣe jì* — forgive an error (P2b construction 65)
- *àṣìṣe kò ní ṣẹlẹ̀ mọ́* — the error will not happen again (P2b construction 64)
- *àṣìṣe ńlá* — serious wrongdoing (P2a item 32)
- *aṣìṣe* — one who errs (P2a item 31)

**Notes:** The parallel constructions *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* / *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* and *darí àṣìṣe jì* / *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì* show that these two terms participate in the same constructional frames, which is why they are grouped together. However, the data consistently show that *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* is more strongly associated with religious discourse and moral culpability, while *àṣìṣe* can describe factual errors without implying moral guilt. The construction *ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (whoever tells a lie will bear guilt, P2b construction 18) links Group 1 to this group by treating lying as a sin.

## Ambiguous or Overlapping Items

The following items do not fit cleanly into a single group or show significant overlap across groups:

**òdodo** (justice, righteousness, P1 item 3): Appears in Group 6 (moral evaluation of persons) and in Group 3 (legal proceedings) via *ìdájọ́ òdodo* (just judgment). The term evaluates both persons and institutional decisions.

**ìfarahàn** (manifestation, revelation, P1 item 23): Appears in Group 5 (authenticity vs. pretense) and in Group 7 (religious acceptance). In religious contexts it describes divine self-disclosure; in moral contexts it describes genuine self-presentation as opposed to concealment.

**àgàbàgebè** (pretense, hypocrisy, P1 item 22): Appears in Group 5 (authenticity) and Group 8 (intentional misrepresentation). The term covers both the inauthenticity of a person's presentation and the deliberate deceptive intent behind it.

**ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́** (false witness, P2a item 23): Combines a term from Group 3 (*ẹlẹ́rìí*, witness) with a term from Group 1 (*irọ́*, lie). It belongs most naturally to Group 3 as a legal category but is formed by importing the core opposition of Group 1.

**ìjẹ́rìísí ìwé** (written certification, P2a item 24) and **ìjẹ́rìísí ìmọ̀** (academic certification, P2a item 25): These belong to Group 3 in their legal function but appear in administrative and educational contexts that are not strictly legal proceedings.

**ìtọ́jú** (care, maintenance, P1b Root 1 item 7): Flagged in P1b as a peripheral extension of the *tọ́* root. It does not fit into any of the groups above, as its primary meaning is careful maintenance rather than truth-evaluation, correctness-assessment, or moral evaluation. It is left ungrouped.

**ìmúrasílẹ̀** (preparation, readiness, P1 item 24): Flagged [UNCERTAIN] in P1 and no common construction was identified in P2b. It does not fit into any of the groups above and is left ungrouped.

**dọ̀gba** (equal, equivalent, P1 item 29) and **ìjọra** (resemblance, similarity, P1 item 30): These terms appear in evaluative contexts (fairness, identification) but do not cluster with any of the groups above. *dọ̀gba* is used in assessments of proportionality and fairness, and *ìjọra* in assessments of likeness. They share a concern with correspondence between two things, but this concern is not strongly represented elsewhere in the dataset as a distinct usage pattern. They are noted as potentially forming a small cluster around correspondence and comparison, but the data do not support a full group.
