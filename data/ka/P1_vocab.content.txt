# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

## Lexical Items

### 1
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი  
**Transliteration:** č'ešmarit'i  
**Literal meaning:** From root related to "genuine/authentic"  
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic

Used to describe statements that correspond to reality or facts, and also to characterize persons or things as genuine or authentic. Commonly appears in both everyday speech and formal discourse to affirm the accuracy or genuineness of something.

**[CORE]**

### 2
**Form:** სიმართლე  
**Transliteration:** simart'le  
**Literal meaning:** Nominalized form from root mart'l- (right, correct)  
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness

A fundamental term denoting truth as a concept, correctness of statements, or moral rightness. Used across contexts from factual accuracy ("tell the truth") to moral evaluation ("justice and truth").

**[CORE]**

### 3
**Form:** მართალი  
**Transliteration:** mart'ali  
**Literal meaning:** From root mart'l- (right, correct)  
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, just, righteous

Describes statements, actions, or persons as correct, right, or morally upright. Frequently used in both epistemic contexts (correct answer) and moral/legal contexts (righteous person, just cause).

**[CORE]**

### 4
**Form:** ტყუილი  
**Transliteration:** ṭq'uili  
**Literal meaning:** Basic root for falsehood  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

The primary term for a false statement or lie. Used both as a noun denoting falsehood and in various derived forms to describe deceptive speech or false representations.

**[CORE]**

### 5
**Form:** ყალბი  
**Transliteration:** q'albi  
**Literal meaning:** Basic root for counterfeit/fake  
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged

Primarily used to describe objects or representations that are counterfeit, forged, or inauthentic. Also extends to false statements or deceptive appearances, particularly when deliberate fabrication is implied.

**[CORE]**

### 6
**Form:** ნამდვილი  
**Transliteration:** namdvili  
**Literal meaning:** Related to root meaning "real/actual"  
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, actual, true

Emphasizes authenticity and reality, distinguishing genuine items from imitations or false representations. Used for both concrete objects (genuine article) and abstract evaluations (real truth).

**[CORE]**

### 7
**Form:** სწორი  
**Transliteration:** sc'ori  
**Literal meaning:** Root meaning "straight, correct"  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, accurate

Denotes correctness, accuracy, or propriety in statements, actions, or arrangements. Commonly used for factual correctness (correct answer) and proper conduct or arrangement.

### 8
**Form:** უტყუარი  
**Transliteration:** uṭq'uari  
**Literal meaning:** u- (without) + ṭq'u- (lie) + -ari (adjectival suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** truthful, honest, infallible

Describes persons or sources as truthful, not given to lying, or statements/methods as infallible. Used to characterize reliability and trustworthiness.

### 10

**Literal meaning:** From root mcdar- (err, mistake)

### 11
