## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### BECOME and REVELATION share a construction type
P4a and P4b document both *osoittautua* ("to turn out to be") and *paljastua* ("to be revealed as") as inchoative constructions using the translative case complement. The structural pattern is identical: matrix verb + NP + translative of adjective. The semantic distinction between BECOME (a property coming to obtain) and REVELATION (a hidden property being exposed) is carried entirely by the choice of matrix verb, not by any structural difference. *paljastua* is described as specifically restricted to the revelation of hidden truth, while *osoittautua* is the general inchoative. Because both use the same translative complement construction, REVELATION does not constitute a structurally independent operator in Finnish as documented; it is a lexical variant within the BECOME construction slot. No full-sentence example for *paljastua* is provided in P4a/P4b, which limits the confidence of this characterization.

### VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction
The translative predication construction (P4a §8) serves both VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION. The matrix verbs (*todistaa*, *osoittaa*, *julistaa*, *katsoa*, *todeta*) are the same; the direction of the evaluation is determined solely by the translative complement: *aidoksi* (as genuine) for verification, *vääräksi* (as false) or *pätemättömäksi* (as void) for disconfirmation. This is a systematic asymmetry in the sense that Finnish does not grammaticalize the verification/disconfirmation distinction structurally; it is entirely lexical. The same pattern holds for the inchoative construction: *osoittautua aidoksi* (turn out to be genuine) vs. *osoittautua vääräksi* (turn out to be false).

### NEGATION: both morphological and analytic strategies are available
Finnish provides two fully grammatical negation strategies for truth-domain adjectives. The morphological strategy (*epä-* prefixation) produces lexicalized antonyms (*epäluotettava*, *epätosi*, *epärehellinen*, *epäaito*) and is preferred in formal and written registers. The analytic strategy (*ei ole* + adjective) is available for all adjectives without restriction and is more frequent in spoken registers. There is no documented case where one strategy is obligatory and the other blocked; the choice is register-conditioned rather than grammatically forced. This means Finnish NEGATION is simultaneously morphological and analytic, with register as the conditioning factor.

### Asymmetry in the ATTR operator: propositional vs. personal epistemic attribution
The ATTR operator in Finnish is realized by two structurally distinct patterns. Propositional truth attribution uses the standard copular pattern (*olla* + predicative nominative adjective: *Se on tosi*, *Väite on epätosi*). Personal epistemic attribution — attributing correctness or incorrectness to a person's belief or claim — uses the lexicalized inessive construction (*olla oikeassa* / *olla väärässä*). The inessive construction does not extend productively to other truth-domain adjectives; it is restricted to *oikea* and *väärä* in this fixed form. This means that for most truth-domain adjectives, only propositional attribution is available in the standard copular pattern, while the personal epistemic reading requires the fixed inessive forms.

### CAUSATION (character) is not attested
P4a and P4b document morphological causatives for factual truth operations (*todentaa* for verification, *väärentää* for forgery, *vääristää* for distortion). No causative construction targeting a character-level truth property — such as causing someone to become honest, reliable, or sincere — is documented in either step. The character-level adjectives (*rehellinen*, *luotettava*, *vilpitön*, *vilpillinen*) appear in attributive, predicative, and nominalization patterns, but no causative verb derived from them is listed. This is the only CLCA operator for which no construction of any kind is attested in the P4a/P4b data.

### No agentive nominalization for "truth-teller"
P4a §7 explicitly notes that no dedicated agentive form for "truth-teller" is derived from *tosi* or *totuus*. The concept of "liar" is expressed by *valehtelija*, derived from *valehdella* (to lie), which is outside the documented P1 vocabulary. *Totuudenetsijä* (truth-seeker) is noted as a compound agentive rather than a direct derivation. This represents a lexical gap within the agentive nominalization pattern: the *-ja/-jä* suffix is productive, but the relevant base forms for "truth-teller" are not attested as yielding an agentive in P4a/P4b.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction for causing a character-level truth property (e.g., causing someone to be honest, reliable, or sincere) is attested in P4a or P4b. The morphological causative pattern is documented only for factual truth operations (*todentaa*, *väärentää*, *vääristää*). This is a structural gap in the sense that the causative morphology is not applied to character-domain truth adjectives in the documented data.

### Lexical Gaps
- **Agentive nominalization for "truth-teller"**: No *-ja/-jä* agentive derived directly from *tosi* or *totuus* is attested. The productive suffix exists; the base form does not yield the expected agentive in documented usage.
- **Agentive nominalization for "liar"**: *valehtelija* is noted as the relevant form but is explicitly placed outside P1 vocabulary. It is therefore a lexical gap relative to the documented truth-domain vocabulary, though not a gap in Finnish as a whole.
- **Periphrastic causative in the truth domain**: The *saada* + passive participle causative is noted as existing in Finnish but is described as not dominant in the truth domain and has no attested example in P4a/P4b. Its status in this domain is therefore **NO COMMON EXPRESSION — circumlocution required** based on available data.

### Conceptual Gaps
- None are identifiable from the P4a/P4b data alone. All documented CLCA operators that are attested have constructions that are described as natural and non-marginal (with the exception of *todentua*, which is [MARGINAL] due to subject restrictions, not conceptual unavailability).

### Asymmetric Gaps
- **BECOME / *todentua***: The inchoative verb *todentua* is subject-restricted (predictions, expectations, hypotheses only) and rated [MARGINAL] in P4b. The general inchoative *osoittautua* + translative is available for both positive and negative truth-domain properties without documented asymmetry. No asymmetry between positive and negative forms is documented for the translative complement construction.
- **INESSIVE FIXED PREDICATION (§10)**: The construction *olla oikeassa* / *olla väärässä* is documented for both *oikea* (correct) and *väärä* (wrong/incorrect). No asymmetry between the positive and negative forms is documented. However, this construction does not extend to other truth-domain adjectives, which means that personal epistemic attribution for properties such as *luotettava*, *rehellinen*, or *vilpitön* is not available through this pattern — only through the general copular construction (§1).
- **REVELATION**: No full-sentence example for *paljastua* is provided in P4a/P4b. The construction is attested as existing but is documented only at the level of description, not exemplification. This is an asymmetric documentation gap rather than a confirmed structural gap.
