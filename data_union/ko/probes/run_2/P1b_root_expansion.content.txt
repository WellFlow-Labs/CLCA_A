# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Korean (ko)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** 진 (眞)
**Transliteration:** jin
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 진짜 (jinjja), 진정 (jinjeong), 진정성 (jinjeongseong), 진위 (jinwi) — **5 items**
**Core meaning contribution:** Genuine, true, real — the positive pole of the truth/authenticity axis. Contributes the sense that something corresponds to or embodies what it actually is, as opposed to being false, imitated, or performed.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** 실 (實)
**Transliteration:** sil
**P1 items participating:** 진실 (jinsil), 사실 (sasil), 실체 (silche) — **3 items**
**Core meaning contribution:** Real, actual, substantive — the sense that something has concrete existence or factual grounding, as opposed to being empty, nominal, or fictitious.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** 정 (正)
**Transliteration:** jeong
**P1 items participating:** 정확 (jeonghwak), 올바르다 (olbareuda, contains 바르다 which shares the upright/correct semantic field), 정직 (jeongjik), 공정 (gongjeon), 타당 (tadang, contains 당 當 which overlaps) — **4 items directly; 정 appears as a morpheme in 정확, 정직, 공정**
**Core meaning contribution:** Correct, upright, proper — the normative sense that something conforms to a standard, whether factual, moral, or procedural.

> **Note on scope:** 진 (眞) is the most productive root in the truth-evaluation domain and generates the widest range of compounds. 실 (實) and 정 (正) are both highly productive but their compounds extend substantially into domains beyond truth evaluation (reality, substance, correctness, justice). All three are expanded below. Because all three roots are highly productive (each exceeding 20 common compounds), the expansion below focuses on the 20–30 most common and representative forms for each, with a note that additional forms exist.

---

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Root 1: 진 (眞) — jin

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 실 (實, real)
**English:** truth, truthfulness
**Context:** Used in moral, legal, and everyday discourse to refer to what is actually the case. Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 진실성
**Transliteration:** jinsilseong
**Breakdown:** 진실 (jinsil, truth) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**English:** truthfulness, veracity, truth-value
**Context:** Used in legal testimony evaluation and academic discourse to assess whether a statement or person possesses the quality of being truthful. Appears in constructions like 진실성을 검증하다 (to verify truthfulness).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 진실되다
**Transliteration:** jinsiltoeda
**Breakdown:** 진실 (jinsil, truth) + 되다 (toeda, to be/become)
**English:** to be truthful, to be genuine
**Context:** Predicative form used to evaluate a person or statement as genuinely truthful in character. Common in moral and interpersonal evaluation.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 진실하다
**Transliteration:** jinsilhada
**Breakdown:** 진실 (jinsil, truth) + 하다 (hada, to do/be)
**English:** to be truthful, honest, sincere
**Context:** Used to describe a person as honest and sincere in their dealings. Slightly more active in connotation than 진실되다.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 진실규명
**Transliteration:** jinsil gyumyeong
**Breakdown:** 진실 (jinsil, truth) + 규명 (gyumyeong, 糾明, investigation/clarification)
**English:** truth investigation, clarification of the truth
**Context:** Used in political, legal, and historical contexts, particularly in reference to official investigations into past events such as human rights violations or historical incidents. Appears frequently in the names of government commissions.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 진실화해위원회
**Transliteration:** jinsil hwahae wiwonhoe
**Breakdown:** 진실 (jinsil, truth) + 화해 (hwahae, reconciliation) + 위원회 (wiwonhoe, committee/commission)
**English:** Truth and Reconciliation Commission
**Context:** The name of the official South Korean government body established to investigate historical injustices. A fixed institutional term.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, genuine) + 짜 (colloquial suffix)
**English:** real, genuine; (adverb) really, truly
**Context:** Already listed in P1. Extremely common in everyday speech.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 진짜배기
**Transliteration:** jinjjabaegi
**Breakdown:** 진짜 (jinjja, genuine) + 배기 (baegi, colloquial intensifying suffix denoting the genuine article)
**English:** the real thing, the genuine article, the real deal
**Context:** Colloquial and emphatic form of 진짜, used to stress that something is unambiguously authentic. Common in everyday speech and advertising.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 진정
**Transliteration:** jinjeong
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 정 (正/情, correct/sincere)
**English:** genuine, true, sincere; (adverb) truly, really
**Context:** Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeongseong
**Breakdown:** 진정 (jinjeong, genuine) + 성 (性, -ness)
**English:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity
**Context:** Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 진정하다
**Transliteration:** jinjeonghada
**Breakdown:** 진정 (jinjeong, genuine/true) + 하다 (hada, to be)
**English:** to be genuine, sincere, true
**Context:** Predicative form used to evaluate whether a person's feelings, intentions, or expressions are authentic. Common in interpersonal and public discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 진정한
**Transliteration:** jinjeonghan
**Breakdown:** 진정 (jinjeong, genuine) + 한 (han, adjectival ending)
**English:** true, genuine, real (attributive)
**Context:** Extremely common attributive adjective. Appears in expressions like 진정한 사랑 (true love), 진정한 친구 (true friend), 진정한 의미 (true meaning).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 위 (僞, false)
**English:** authenticity vs. falsity; truth or falsehood
**Context:** Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 진위 여부
**Transliteration:** jinwi yeobu
**Breakdown:** 진위 (jinwi, truth or falsehood) + 여부 (yeobu, whether or not)
**English:** whether something is true or false, the question of authenticity
**Context:** A fixed collocation used in legal, journalistic, and investigative contexts to frame the question of whether a claim, document, or object is genuine. Appears in constructions like 진위 여부를 확인하다 (to verify whether something is genuine).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 진위를 가리다
**Transliteration:** jinwireul garida
**Breakdown:** 진위 (jinwi) + 를 (reul, object marker) + 가리다 (garida, to distinguish/determine)
**English:** to determine the truth or falsity of something, to establish authenticity
**Context:** A fixed verbal collocation used in legal, investigative, and journalistic contexts. Standard phrasing for the act of adjudicating whether something is genuine.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 진품
**Transliteration:** jinpum
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, genuine) + 품 (品, goods/article/item)
**English:** genuine article, authentic item, original
**Context:** Used in art, antiques, consumer goods, and legal contexts to designate an item as the authentic original rather than a copy or counterfeit. Contrasts with 가품 (gapum, fake goods) and 모조품 (mojopum, imitation goods).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 진품명품
**Transliteration:** jinpum myeongpum
**Breakdown:** 진품 (jinpum, genuine article) + 명품 (myeongpum, 名品, famous/prestigious goods)
**English:** genuine masterpiece; authentic prestigious item
**Context:** Also the title of a long-running Korean television program in which experts authenticate antiques and artworks. The phrase combines authenticity with quality/prestige.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 진상
**Transliteration:** jinsang
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 상 (相/狀, appearance/state/situation)
**English:** the true state of affairs, the real situation, the facts of the matter
**Context:** Used in investigative, journalistic, and legal contexts to refer to what actually happened, as opposed to what was reported or alleged. Appears in 진상을 밝히다 (to reveal the true facts), 진상조사 (jinsang josa, fact-finding investigation).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 진상조사
**Transliteration:** jinsang josa
**Breakdown:** 진상 (jinsang, true state of affairs) + 조사 (josa, 調査, investigation/inquiry)
**English:** fact-finding investigation, inquiry into the true facts
**Context:** Used in political, legal, and institutional contexts to describe an official or formal investigation into what actually occurred. Common in news reporting and government documents.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 진의
**Transliteration:** jinui
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 의 (意, intention/meaning)
**English:** true intention, real meaning, genuine purpose
**Context:** Used in legal, diplomatic, and interpersonal contexts to probe what someone actually meant or intended, as opposed to what they said on the surface. Appears in 진의를 파악하다 (to grasp the true intention).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 진면목
**Transliteration:** jinmyeonmok
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 면목 (myeonmok, 面目, face/appearance/true character)
**English:** true nature, real character, genuine face
**Context:** Used to describe the authentic character or quality of a person or thing when it is fully revealed. Appears in expressions like 진면목을 드러내다 (to reveal one's true nature).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 진수
**Transliteration:** jinsu
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/essential) + 수 (髓, marrow/essence)
**English:** essence, quintessence, the very best
**Context:** Used to describe the most essential or finest quality of something — the core of what makes it what it is. Appears in expressions like 한국 문화의 진수 (the essence of Korean culture).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 진리
**Transliteration:** jilli
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 리 (理, principle/reason/truth)
**English:** truth (in a philosophical or universal sense), verity, principle
**Context:** Used in philosophical, religious, and academic contexts to refer to fundamental or universal truth, as opposed to contingent factual accuracy. Appears in 진리를 추구하다 (to pursue truth), 영원한 진리 (eternal truth).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 진리값
**Transliteration:** jilligap
**Breakdown:** 진리 (jilli, truth) + 값 (gap, value)
**English:** truth value
**Context:** A technical term in logic and philosophy of language referring to the truth or falsity of a proposition. Used in academic and formal logical discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 진술
**Transliteration:** jinsul
**Breakdown:** 진 (陳, to state/set forth — note: different character from 眞) + 술 (述, to narrate/state)
**English:** statement, testimony, account
**Context:** Used in legal and formal contexts for a formal statement or testimony. Note: the 진 here is 陳 (to state), not 眞 (true), though the compound is semantically adjacent to truth-evaluation contexts and frequently co-occurs with truth-evaluation vocabulary.
**Status:** [UNCERTAIN] — the 진 morpheme here is a different character (陳) from the 진 (眞) under expansion; included because of frequent co-occurrence in truth-evaluation contexts and potential for morphological confusion.

---

**26.**
**Form:** 진심
**Transliteration:** jinsim
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 심 (心, heart/mind)
**English:** sincerity, genuine feeling, heartfelt sentiment
**Context:** Used in interpersonal and moral contexts to evaluate whether a person's expressed feelings or intentions are genuine. Appears in 진심으로 (jinshimeuro, sincerely/from the heart), 진심 어린 (jinsim eorin, heartfelt).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 진심으로
**Transliteration:** jinsimeuro
**Breakdown:** 진심 (jinsim, sincere heart) + 으로 (euro, instrumental/manner particle)
**English:** sincerely, genuinely, from the heart
**Context:** A fixed adverbial expression used in apologies, expressions of gratitude, and declarations of feeling to assert that the sentiment is genuine. Extremely common in everyday speech and formal correspondence.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 진가
**Transliteration:** jinga
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 가 (價, value/worth)
**English:** true value, real worth
**Context:** Used to describe the genuine worth or quality of a person or thing, often in contexts where it has been previously unrecognized. Appears in 진가를 발휘하다 (to demonstrate one's true worth), 진가를 알아보다 (to recognize the true value of something).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 진경
**Transliteration:** jingyeong
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/real) + 경 (景, scenery/view)
**English:** true scenery, real landscape; (in art history) realistic landscape painting
**Context:** Used in art-historical contexts to refer to the genre of Korean landscape painting (진경산수화, jingyeong sansuhwa) that depicts actual Korean landscapes rather than idealized Chinese models. Associated with the 18th-century painter Jeong Seon.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 진화
**Transliteration:** jinhwa
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 화 (畵/畫, painting/picture) — note: also exists as 진화 (進化, evolution), a homophone with different characters
**English:** (in art) authentic painting, genuine artwork
**Context:** Used in art authentication contexts to refer to a painting verified as genuine. Distinct from the more common 진화 (進化, evolution). Less frequent than other compounds on this list.
**Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

> **Note:** Additional compounds with 진 (眞) exist, particularly in Buddhist and philosophical terminology (e.g., 진여 眞如, jinyeo, "suchness/tathātā"; 진언 眞言, jineon, "mantra/true word"), but these are domain-specific and less central to the everyday truth-evaluation lexicon.

### Root 2: 실 (實) — sil

---

**1.**
**Form:** 사실
**Transliteration:** sasil
**Breakdown:** 사 (事, matter/affair) + 실 (實, real)
**English:** fact, actuality; (adverb) in fact, actually
**Context:** Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 사실상
**Transliteration:** sasilsang
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, fact) + 상 (上, on/in terms of)
**English:** in fact, in practice, effectively, de facto
**Context:** Used as an adverbial expression to indicate that something is the case in practice even if not formally or officially so. Extremely common in legal, journalistic, and everyday discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 사실관계
**Transliteration:** sasil gwangye
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, fact) + 관계 (gwangye, 關係, relationship/connection)
**English:** the facts of the matter, factual circumstances
**Context:** Used in legal and journalistic contexts to refer to the factual circumstances of a case or situation, as distinct from legal interpretation or opinion.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 사실확인
**Transliteration:** sasil hwagin
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, fact) + 확인 (hwagin, 確認, confirmation/verification)
**English:** fact-checking, verification of facts
**Context:** Used in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts to describe the process of verifying whether a claim corresponds to the facts.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 사실주의
**Transliteration:** sasil juui
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, fact/reality) + 주의 (juui, 主義, -ism/doctrine)
**English:** realism (in art, literature, or philosophy)
**Context:** Used in literary, artistic, and philosophical contexts to refer to the movement or approach that aims to depict reality as it actually is. Equivalent to "realism" as an aesthetic or philosophical doctrine.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 사실적
**Transliteration:** sasiljeok
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, fact/reality) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix)
**English:** realistic, factual, true-to-life
**Context:** Used in aesthetic and descriptive contexts to evaluate whether a representation, depiction, or account corresponds closely to reality. Common in literary and art criticism.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 사실혼
**Transliteration:** sasilhon
**Breakdown:** 사실 (sasil, de facto/actual) + 혼 (婚, marriage)
**English:** common-law marriage, de facto marriage
**Context:** A legal term referring to a cohabiting relationship that functions as a marriage in practice but is not formally registered. Used in family law and administrative contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Breakdown:** 진 (眞, true) + 실 (實, real)
**English:** truth
**Context:** Already listed in P1 and under Root 1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 실체
**Transliteration:** silche
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 체 (體, body/substance)
**English:** substance, true nature, real entity
**Context:** Already listed in P1.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 실체적
**Transliteration:** silchejeok
**Breakdown:** 실체 (silche, substance/true nature) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix)
**English:** substantive, real, pertaining to the true nature
**Context:** Used in legal and philosophical contexts to distinguish substantive matters from procedural or formal ones. Appears in 실체적 진실 (silchejeok jinsil, substantive truth) in legal discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 실체적 진실
**Transliteration:** silchejeok jinsil
**Breakdown:** 실체적 (silchejeok, substantive) + 진실 (jinsil, truth)
**English:** substantive truth, material truth
**Context:** A legal term distinguishing the actual truth of what occurred from procedural or formal determinations. Used in criminal procedure discourse to describe the goal of finding what actually happened.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 실제
**Transliteration:** silje
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 제 (際, occasion/boundary/actual circumstance)
**English:** reality, actuality; (adverb) actually, in reality
**Context:** Used to contrast what is actually the case with what is supposed, claimed, or imagined. Extremely common in everyday speech. Appears in 실제로 (siljero, actually/in reality), 실제 상황 (silje sanghwang, actual situation).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 실제로
**Transliteration:** siljero
**Breakdown:** 실제 (silje, reality) + 로 (ro, manner particle)
**English:** actually, in reality, in practice
**Context:** One of the most common adverbial expressions in Korean, used to assert that something is the case in actual fact. Appears across all registers.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 실상
**Transliteration:** silsang
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real) + 상 (相/狀, appearance/state)
**English:** the real state of affairs, the actual situation, the truth of the matter
**Context:** Used to contrast the actual situation with appearances or claims. Similar to 진상 (jinsang) but somewhat more neutral in register. Appears in 실상을 파악하다 (to grasp the real situation).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 실질
**Transliteration:** siljil
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 질 (質, substance/nature/quality)
**English:** substance, actual content, real quality
**Context:** Used to distinguish the substantive reality of something from its formal or nominal appearance. Appears in legal and policy contexts: 실질적 (siljiljeok, substantive/real), 실질적 의미 (siljiljeok uimi, substantive meaning).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 실질적
**Transliteration:** siljiljeok
**Breakdown:** 실질 (siljil, substance) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix)
**English:** substantive, real, actual, de facto
**Context:** Used in legal, policy, and analytical contexts to distinguish what is substantively the case from what is merely formal or nominal. Extremely common in legal and administrative language.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 실증
**Transliteration:** siljeung
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 증 (證, proof/evidence)
**English:** empirical verification, positive proof, demonstration
**Context:** Used in academic and scientific contexts to refer to empirical demonstration or verification. Appears in 실증주의 (siljeung juui, positivism), 실증적 (siljeungjeok, empirical/positivist).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 실증주의
**Transliteration:** siljeung juui
**Breakdown:** 실증 (siljeung, empirical verification) + 주의 (juui, -ism)
**English:** positivism (philosophical/scientific)
**Context:** Used in philosophical and social-scientific contexts to refer to the doctrine that knowledge must be grounded in empirically verifiable facts. Standard academic terminology.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 실화
**Transliteration:** silhwa
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 화 (話, story/account)
**English:** true story, account based on real events
**Context:** Used in literary, cinematic, and journalistic contexts to designate a narrative as based on actual events. Appears in 실화를 바탕으로 한 (based on a true story).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 실명
**Transliteration:** silmyeong
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 명 (名, name)
**English:** real name, actual name (as opposed to a pseudonym or alias)
**Context:** Used in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts to refer to a person's actual registered name. Appears in 실명제 (silmyeongje, real-name system), a policy requiring use of real names in financial or online contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 실명제
**Transliteration:** silmyeongje
**Breakdown:** 실명 (silmyeong, real name) + 제 (制, system/institution)
**English:** real-name system, real-name verification system
**Context:** Refers to policies requiring individuals to use their actual registered names in financial transactions or online platforms. A significant term in Korean administrative and internet governance discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 실태
**Transliteration:** siltae
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 태 (態, state/condition)
**English:** actual state, real condition, true situation
**Context:** Used in investigative, journalistic, and policy contexts to describe the actual condition of something, often implying a gap between official claims and reality. Appears in 실태조사 (siltae josa, survey of actual conditions).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 실태조사
**Transliteration:** siltae josa
**Breakdown:** 실태 (siltae, actual state) + 조사 (josa, investigation/survey)
**English:** survey of actual conditions, fact-finding survey
**Context:** Used in government, academic, and journalistic contexts to describe systematic investigation of the real state of affairs in a given area (e.g., housing conditions, labor practices).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 실존
**Transliteration:** siljon
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 존 (存, to exist)
**English:** actual existence, real existence; (philosophical) existence
**Context:** Used in philosophical contexts (existentialism: 실존주의, siljon juui) and in everyday contexts to assert that something actually exists. Appears in 실존 인물 (siljon inmul, real/historical person).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 실존주의
**Transliteration:** siljon juui
**Breakdown:** 실존 (siljon, existence) + 주의 (juui, -ism)
**English:** existentialism
**Context:** The standard Korean term for the philosophical movement. Used in academic and intellectual discourse.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 실력
**Transliteration:** sillyeok
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 력 (力, power/ability)
**English:** actual ability, real skill, true competence
**Context:** Used to evaluate a person's genuine capability as opposed to their credentials, reputation, or claimed ability. Extremely common in educational, professional, and everyday contexts.
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 실적
**Transliteration:** siljeok
**Breakdown:** 실 (實, real/actual) + 적 (績, achievement/result)
**English:** actual results, real performance, track record
**Context:** Used in business, academic, and administrative contexts to evaluate concrete outcomes as opposed to plans or promises. Appears in 실적을 평가하다 (to evaluate actual performance).
**Status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 실용
**Transliteration:** silyong

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots (진 眞, 실 實, 정 正) in contexts where their contribution appears to shift away from the core truth-evaluation domain. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**진 (眞) — peripheral extensions:**

- **진수** (jinsu, 眞髓): appears in contexts of aesthetic excellence and cultural quintessence, not only in contexts of truth or authenticity.
- **진경** (jingyeong, 眞景): appears in art-historical discourse as a genre label for a specific style of landscape painting, where the primary contrast is with idealized/conventional depiction rather than with falsehood.
- **진심** (jinsim, 眞心): appears in contexts of emotional expression and interpersonal warmth, where the primary function is intensity or sincerity of feeling rather than factual accuracy.
- **진가** (jinga, 眞價): appears in contexts of evaluating worth, quality, or talent, where the primary contrast is with underestimation or unrecognized merit rather than with falsity.
- **진리** (jilli, 眞理): appears in religious and philosophical contexts where "truth" functions as an object of pursuit or devotion rather than as a property of propositions.
- **진언** (jineon, 眞言): appears in Buddhist ritual contexts as a term for mantra, where 진 (眞) contributes a sense of sacred or ultimate reality rather than propositional truth.

---

**실 (實) — peripheral extensions:**

- **실력** (sillyeok, 實力): appears in contexts of competitive evaluation, educational assessment, and professional ranking, where the primary contrast is with credentials or reputation rather than with falsehood.
- **실용** (silyong, 實用) and **실용주의** (silyong juui): appear in political and policy discourse where the primary contrast is with ideological rigidity rather than with falsity.
- **실존주의** (siljon juui): appears in philosophical discourse where 실 (實) contributes a sense of concrete individual existence rather than factual accuracy.
- **실현** (silhyeon): appears in motivational and aspirational contexts (realizing dreams, achieving goals) where the primary contrast is with unrealized potential rather than with falsehood.
- **실적** (siljeok): appears in business and administrative contexts where the primary concern is performance measurement rather than truth evaluation.
- **실망** (silmang, 失望 — note: different character 失, not 實): flagged here only to note that the phonological similarity may cause confusion; this is a different morpheme.

---

**정 (正) — peripheral extensions:**

- **정상** (jeongsang) and **정상화** (jeongsanghwa): appear in medical, diplomatic, and administrative contexts where the primary contrast is with abnormality or dysfunction rather than with incorrectness or dishonesty.
- **정의** (jeongui, 正義): appears in political and social movement contexts where the primary concern is distributive or procedural justice rather than truth or accuracy.
- **정당방위** (jeongdang bangwi): appears in criminal law contexts where the primary concern is legal justification of force rather than epistemic correctness.
- **정론** (jeongron): appears in journalistic identity discourse where 정 (正) functions as a marker of institutional commitment or prestige rather than as an epistemic evaluation.
- **정오표** (jeongopyo): appears in publishing contexts where the primary function is administrative correction of textual errors rather than moral or epistemic evaluation.
- **바로잡다** (barojapda): appears in political and historical discourse where the primary concern is rectification of injustice or distortion, spanning both epistemic and moral domains simultaneously.
