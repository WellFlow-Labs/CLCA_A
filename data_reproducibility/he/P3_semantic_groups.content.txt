# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Identified Groups

### GROUP 1: Truth-Falsehood Opposition
**Definition:** This group contains the primary antonymic pair used across all registers to characterize statements, representations, or testimony as either true or false. These terms carry moral weight and are used in legal, religious, and everyday evaluative contexts. They form the basic binary opposition for truth evaluation in Hebrew.

**Representative expressions:**
- אמת (emet) "truth"
- שקר (sheker) "falsehood, lie"
- באמת (be-emet) "truly, really"
- לומר את האמת (lomar et ha-emet) "to tell the truth"
- לומר שקר (lomar sheker) "to tell a lie"
- דבר שקר (davar sheker) "false statement"
- עד שקר (ed sheker) "false witness"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- אמיתי (amiti) "true, genuine" — overlaps with this group but extends into authenticity evaluation beyond simple truth-falsehood
- שקרי (shikri) "false, lying" — adjectival form that bridges this group and descriptive characterizations

---

### GROUP 2: Correctness Assessment
**Definition:** This group contains terms used primarily to affirm or deny the correctness or accuracy of statements, answers, or assessments, particularly in educational, technical, and conversational contexts. These terms focus on factual accuracy rather than moral truth, and are commonly used in corrections, confirmations, and evaluations of precision.

**Representative expressions:**
- נכון (nakhon) "correct, right"
- לא נכון (lo nakhon) "incorrect, wrong"
- מדויק (meduyak) "precise, exact"
- תשובה נכונה (tshuvah nkhonah) "correct answer"
- זה נכון (ze nakhon) "that's correct"
- בדיוק (be-diyuk) "exactly, precisely"
- דיוק (diyuk) "precision, accuracy"
- לא מדויק (lo meduyak) "not precise, inaccurate"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- צודק (tsodek) "right, correct" — overlaps but carries additional moral/justice connotations
- טעות (ta'ut) "error, mistake" — related as negation of correctness but focuses on the error itself rather than evaluation

---

### GROUP 3: Reliability and Trustworthiness
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to evaluate persons, sources, or information as reliable, trustworthy, or credible. These terms are particularly common in legal, academic, and journalistic contexts for assessing the dependability of testimony, sources, or data. They focus on whether something or someone can be trusted rather than on truth per se.

**Representative expressions:**
- אמין (amin) "reliable, trustworthy"
- מהימן (meheyман) "trustworthy, credible"
- מקור אמין (makor amin) "reliable source"
- עד אמין (ed amin) "reliable witness"
- אמינות (aminut) "reliability, credibility"
- מהימנות (meheymanut) "reliability, trustworthiness"
- בלתי אמין (bilti amin) "unreliable"
- מפוקפק (mefukpak) "doubtful, dubious"
- מקור מפוקפק (makor mefukpak) "dubious source"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- נאמן (ne'eman) "faithful, loyal, trustworthy" — overlaps but extends more into loyalty and faithfulness
- אמונה (emunah) "faith, belief, trust" — related but functions more as abstract noun for the state of trusting

---

### GROUP 4: Genuineness and Authenticity
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to characterize objects, persons, experiences, or representations as genuine, authentic, or real as opposed to fake, forged, or counterfeit. These terms are common in commercial, aesthetic, and cultural contexts, and often involve evaluation of whether something is what it purports to be.

**Representative expressions:**
- אמיתי (amiti) "true, genuine, authentic"
- אותנטי (otenti) "authentic, genuine"
- מזויף (mezuyaf) "forged, fake, falsified"
- זיוף (ziyuf) "forgery, falsification"
- סיפור אמיתי (sipur amiti) "true story"
- אוכל אותנטי (okhel otenti) "authentic food"
- מסמך מזויף (mismakh mezuyaf) "forged document"
- כסף מזויף (kesef mezuyaf) "counterfeit money"
- אמיתות (amitiyut) "authenticity, genuineness"
- אותנטיות (otentiyut) "authenticity"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- אמת (emet) "truth" — the root concept but functions differently in this domain
- מציאותי (metsi'uti) "real, realistic" — overlaps in the "real" sense but extends to feasibility

---

### GROUP 5: Rightness and Justice
**Definition:** This group contains terms that combine epistemic correctness with moral rightness or justice. These terms are used to affirm that someone is right in their position or that an action is just, and they appear frequently in legal, moral, and argumentative discourse where correctness and justice intersect.

**Representative expressions:**
- צודק (tsodek) "right, correct, just"
- צדק (tsedek) "justice, righteousness"
- מוצדק (mutsдak) "justified, warranted"
- אתה צודק (atah tsodek) "you're right"
- בצדק (be-tsedek) "justly, rightly"
- משפט צדק (mishpat tsedek) "just judgment"
- הצדקה (hatsdakah) "justification"
- ביקורת מוצדקת (bikoret mutsдeket) "justified criticism"
- לא מוצדק (lo mutsдak) "unjustified"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- צדיק (tsadik) "righteous person" — related but functions as character description
- צדקה (tsedakah) "charity" — etymologically related but semantically distant in modern usage

---

### GROUP 6: Honesty and Sincerity
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to evaluate whether persons or their expressions are honest, sincere, or forthright in their communication and conduct. These terms focus on the speaker's or actor's genuineness and lack of deception, and are common in interpersonal and moral evaluation contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- יושר (yosher) "honesty, uprightness"
- כנות (khenut) "honesty, sincerity"
- כנה (khen) "honest, sincere"
- יושר לב (yosher lev) "honesty of heart"
- לדבר בכנות (ledaber be-khenut) "to speak honestly"
- תשובה כנה (tshuvah khenah) "honest answer"
- ביושר (be-yosher) "honestly, uprightly"
- בכנות (be-khenut) "honestly, sincerely"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- אמת (emet) "truth" — related but focuses on content rather than manner
- שקר (sheker) "falsehood" — the opposite but focuses on the statement rather than the speaker's character

---

### GROUP 7: Certainty and Doubt
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to express or evaluate degrees of certainty, confidence, or doubt about propositions or situations. These terms function as epistemic modifiers indicating the speaker's or subject's level of confidence, and are extremely common in conversational hedging and emphatic assertion.

**Representative expressions:**
- ודאי (vadai) "certain, sure"
- בטוח (batuakh) "sure, certain"
- ספק (safek) "doubt, uncertainty"
- אני בטוח (ani batuakh) "I'm sure"
- אתה בטוח? (atah batuakh?) "are you sure?"
- אין ספק (ein safek) "no doubt"
- בלי ספק (bli safek) "without doubt"
- בוודאי (be-vadai) "certainly, surely"
- ספקן (safkan) "skeptic, doubter"
- ספקנות (safkanut) "skepticism"
- יש ספק (yesh safek) "there is doubt"
- ספק אם (safek im) "doubtful whether"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- ביטחון (bitakhon) "security, confidence" — overlaps but also means physical security
- ודאות (vada'ut) "certainty" — abstract nominalization within this group

---

### GROUP 8: Error and Mistake
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to characterize statements, judgments, or actions as mistaken or erroneous, typically without implying deliberate deception. These terms focus on unintentional incorrectness and are common in corrections, apologies, and evaluations that distinguish error from falsehood.

**Representative expressions:**
- טעות (ta'ut) "error, mistake"
- מוטעה (mut'eh) "mistaken, erroneous"
- טועה (to'eh) "mistaken, erring"
- לעשות טעות (la'asot ta'ut) "to make a mistake"
- בטעות (be-ta'ut) "by mistake, mistakenly"
- אתה מוטעה (atah mut'eh) "you're mistaken"
- רעיון מוטעה (ra'ayon mut'eh) "mistaken idea"
- הנחה מוטעית (hanakhah mut'et) "mistaken assumption"
- לתקן טעות (letaken ta'ut) "to correct a mistake"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- לא נכון (lo nakhon) "incorrect" — overlaps but is more neutral evaluation
- מטעה (mat'eh) "misleading" — related but implies causing error in others

---

### GROUP 9: Reality and Actuality
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to distinguish what is real or actual from what is imaginary, idealistic, or merely apparent. These terms are common in philosophical discourse and everyday evaluation of whether something exists in reality or whether plans and expectations are realistic or feasible.

**Representative expressions:**
- מציאות (metsi'ut) "reality, actuality"
- מציאותי (metsi'uti) "real, realistic"
- עובדה (uvdah) "fact"
- זאת עובדה (zot uvdah) "that's a fact"
- עובדה היא ש... (uvdah hi she...) "the fact is that..."
- לחזור למציאות (lakhzor la-metsi'ut) "to return to reality"
- בדיקת מציאות (bdikat metsi'ut) "reality check"
- עובדתי (uvdati) "factual"
- לא מציאותי (lo metsi'uti) "unrealistic"
- סיכוי מציאותי (sikuy metsi'uti) "realistic chance"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- אמיתי (amiti) "true, real" — overlaps in the "real" sense but also covers genuineness
- מהות (mahut) "essence, nature" — related but more philosophical/abstract

---

### GROUP 10: Affirmation and Negation Particles
**Definition:** This group contains basic particles and short expressions used for affirming or denying propositions in conversation and discourse. These are among the most frequent expressions in the language and serve fundamental communicative functions of agreement, disagreement, and confirmation.

**Representative expressions:**
- כן (ken) "yes, right"
- לא (lo) "no, not"
- נכון (nakhon) "correct, right" (when used as interjection)
- באמת? (be-emet?) "really?"
- אם כן (im ken) "if so, therefore"
- לא כן? (lo khen?) "isn't it? right?"
- כן, אבל... (ken, aval...) "yes, but..."

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- זה נכון (ze nakhon) "that's correct" — fuller construction but serves similar function
- בוודאי (be-vadai) "certainly" — affirmative but with epistemic qualification

---

### GROUP 11: Resemblance and Appearance
**Definition:** This group contains terms used to express similarity, resemblance, or appearance between entities or to indicate that something seems or appears to be the case. These terms are common in comparative descriptions and epistemic hedging where the speaker indicates appearance rather than certainty.

**Representative expressions:**
- דומה (domeh) "similar, resembling; seems"
- דומה ל... (domeh le-...) "similar to..., resembles..."
- דומה ש... (domeh she...) "it seems that..."
- דומה לי ש... (domeh li she...) "it seems to me that..."
- דמיון (dimyon) "similarity, resemblance"
- כנראה (ken-nir'eh) "apparently, seemingly"

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**
- דמיון (dimyon) "imagination" — same form but different sense
- לא דומה (lo domeh) "not similar" — negation within this group

---

## Items Not Clearly Grouped

### Standalone or Peripheral Items:

**מהות (mahut) "essence, nature"**
- Philosophical term that doesn't fit cleanly into usage-based groups
- Used in abstract inquiry into essential nature of things
- Related to truth evaluation but operates at different level of abstraction

**שקרן (shakran) "liar"**
- Character description rather than truth evaluation
- Derived from sheker but functions differently

**צדיק (tsadik) "righteous person"**
- Character description in religious/moral domain
- Related to tsedek but not primarily evaluative of truth

**צדקה (tsedakah) "charity"**
- Semantically distant from truth evaluation despite etymological connection
- Functions in social welfare domain

**אומן (oman) "craftsman"**
- Etymologically related to '-m-n root but semantically peripheral
- Not used in truth evaluation

**אומנות (omanut) "art, craft"**
- Related to oman, peripheral to truth domain

**תיקון (tikun) "repair, correction"**
- Action-oriented rather than evaluative
- Related to correctness but functions differently

**נאמנות (ne'emanut) "faithfulness, loyalty"**
- Extends beyond reliability into loyalty domain
- Overlaps with multiple groups but doesn't fit cleanly

**אמונה (emunah) "faith, belief"**
- Abstract state rather than evaluation
- Related to trust but functions in religious/epistemic belief domain

**ביטחון (bitakhon) "security, confidence"**
- Dual meaning (physical security and epistemic confidence) makes grouping difficult
- Overlaps with certainty group but extends beyond it

## Notes on Grouping Patterns

### Observed Asymmetries:

1. **Group size variation**: Groups range from very small (Group 11: Resemblance) to quite large (Group 7: Certainty and Doubt), reflecting actual usage patterns rather than imposed balance.

2. **Overlap zones**: Several items (particularly amiti, tsodek, domeh) function across multiple groups, reflecting genuine polysemy or breadth of usage rather than analytical confusion.

3. **Register differentiation**: Some groups (e.g., Reliability and Trustworthiness) show strong association with formal/legal registers, while others (e.g., Affirmation and Negation) are register-neutral.

4. **Root-based clustering**: The three major roots ('-m-t, ts-d-k, k-w-n) each contribute to multiple groups, but their derivatives don't form unified semantic groups — they disperse across different usage domains.

5. **Moral vs. epistemic**: Some groups (Truth-Falsehood, Rightness and Justice, Honesty and Sincerity) carry moral weight, while others (Correctness Assessment, Certainty and Doubt) are more purely epistemic.

6. **Action vs. evaluation**: Most groups involve evaluation, but some items focus on actions (telling truth, making mistakes, correcting errors) rather than states or qualities.

### Items Resisting Grouping:

Several items remain ungrouped because they:
- Operate at different levels of abstraction (mahut)
- Describe persons rather than evaluate statements (shakran, tsadik)
- Function in semantically distant domains despite etymological connections (tsedakah, omanut)
- Show dual meanings that span multiple domains (bitakhon, dimyon)
- Represent abstract states rather than evaluations (emunah, ne'emanut)

This distribution reflects actual usage patterns in Hebrew rather than an attempt to create comprehensive or balanced categories.
