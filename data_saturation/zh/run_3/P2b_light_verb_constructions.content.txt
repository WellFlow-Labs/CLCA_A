# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. The boundary between a "construction" and a "compound" is not always sharp in Chinese; the criterion applied here is whether the item involves a syntactically productive or semi-productive pattern (e.g., a verb-complement structure, a topic-comment frame, a fixed predicate frame) rather than a lexicalized compound already documented in P1 or P1b.

Items already fully documented as compounds in P1 or P1b (e.g., 真相, 事实, 假设) are not repeated here unless they participate in a distinct constructional pattern worth documenting. Derivational forms from P2a are excluded.

Where no common construction is attested for a given P1 item or pattern, this is stated explicitly.

Constructions are organized by the P1 base item they most directly involve.

## CONSTRUCTIONS

---

### Base: 真 (zhēn)

---

**1.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 这是真的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** This is true / This is real / This is genuine
**Usage Notes:** The canonical predicate frame for asserting truth or genuineness, using the copula 是 and the nominalizing/adjectivalizing particle 的. Extremely common across all registers; functions as the default construction for asserting that something is not fake or not false.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 是真是假
**Transliteration:** shì zhēn shì jiǎ
**English Translation:** Whether it is true or false; is it real or fake?
**Usage Notes:** A fixed interrogative or evaluative frame using parallel 是…是… structure to pose the question of authenticity. Used in everyday speech and in contexts of evaluating claims, objects, or situations ("说不清是真是假" — can't tell whether it's true or false).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 来真的
**Transliteration:** lái zhēn de
**English Translation:** To get serious about it; to do it for real; to mean business
**Usage Notes:** A colloquial construction using 来 (to come/do) + 真的, meaning to engage with something genuinely rather than playfully or nominally. Used in everyday speech ("他是来真的" — he's serious about this / he means it for real).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 说真的
**Transliteration:** shuō zhēn de
**English Translation:** To be honest; honestly speaking; speaking truthfully
**Usage Notes:** A discourse-framing construction used to signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine opinion or the actual facts, as opposed to polite or evasive speech. Extremely common in everyday conversation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 真有其事
**Transliteration:** zhēn yǒu qí shì
**English Translation:** It really happened; the matter is real; it's a true story
**Usage Notes:** A fixed predicate construction asserting that a reported event or matter actually occurred. Used in journalistic and everyday contexts to confirm the factual basis of a report or rumor ("此事真有其事" — this matter really did happen).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Base Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**Example:** 不是真的
**Transliteration:** bù shì zhēn de
**English Translation:** It's not true; it's not real; it's not genuine
**Usage Notes:** The negated form of construction 1 above; the standard predicate frame for denying truth, reality, or genuineness. Included here because the negated construction has distinct pragmatic uses (denial, correction, reassurance) that differ from the affirmative.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

### Base: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**7.**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 这是假的
**Transliteration:** zhè shì jiǎ de
**English Translation:** This is fake / This is false / This is not genuine
**Usage Notes:** The canonical predicate frame for asserting that something is counterfeit, false, or not genuine. The direct negative counterpart to construction 1. Used across commercial, legal, and everyday registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 装假
**Transliteration:** zhuāng jiǎ
**English Translation:** To put on a false front; to pretend; to fake it
**Usage Notes:** A verb-object construction meaning to deliberately perform or simulate something that is not genuine. Slightly more colloquial than 假装 (P1b item 9 under 假); 装假 foregrounds the performative act of putting on a false appearance.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 打假
**Transliteration:** dǎ jiǎ
**English Translation:** To crack down on counterfeits; to combat fakes; anti-counterfeiting
**Usage Notes:** A verb-object construction using 打 (to strike/combat) + 假, meaning to take action against counterfeit goods or fraudulent practices. Extremely common in commercial, regulatory, and journalistic contexts ("打假行动" — anti-counterfeiting campaign; "打假力度" — intensity of anti-fake enforcement).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Base Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**Example:** 辨真假
**Transliteration:** biàn zhēnjiǎ
**English Translation:** To distinguish the genuine from the fake; to tell real from false
**Usage Notes:** A verb-object construction using 辨 (to distinguish) + 真假, meaning to evaluate and determine whether something is authentic or counterfeit. Related to the P1 item 辨别真伪 (item 14) but more colloquial and shorter.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**19.**
**Base Form:** 正确 (zhèngquè)
**Example:** 答对了 / 答错了
**Transliteration:** dá duì le / dá cuò le
**English Translation:** Got it right / Got it wrong (in answering a question)
**Usage Notes:** These are the standard constructions for asserting correctness or incorrectness of an answer, using the resultative complements 对 (correct) and 错 (wrong) rather than 正确 or 错误 directly. Included here because they represent the primary spoken/everyday construction for the correctness evaluation that 正确/错误 express in more formal registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Summary Note on Constructional Patterns

The most productive constructional patterns attested across this vocabulary set are:

1. **Predicate frame (Subject + Adjective/Stative Verb):** Used with 可靠, 准确, 失实, 属实, 确凿, 失真, and others to make direct evaluative assertions about subjects.

2. **Verb-object frame with 说, 做, 发布, 散布, 捏造, 歪曲:** Used with 真话/实话/假话, 虚假信息, 事实, and related items to describe speech acts and their truth-value.

3. **经 + Verb construction:** Used with 核实, 证实, 查实, and 调查 to assert that verification has been performed through a formal process.

4. **Fixed adverbial frames (从…上说, 实质上, 事实上, 从实际出发):** Used to introduce claims about the fundamental or actual nature of something, contrasting with nominal or surface appearances.

5. **Prohibition frames (严禁, 不得):** Used with 弄虚作假, 欺骗, and related items in regulatory and legal contexts.

Asymmetries are preserved: several P1 items (particularly technical terms, fixed 成语, and items whose primary function is adverbial) do not yield distinct multi-word constructions beyond their standard syntactic use.
