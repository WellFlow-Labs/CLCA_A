## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *false*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** These are the primary single-word antonyms in the propositional truth-evaluation domain. *True* asserts correspondence with fact; *false* asserts non-correspondence or deception. The opposition is documented explicitly in P1 (items 1 and 10), in P3 (Groups 1 and 6), and in P4b where *ring true* and *ring false* are identified as a contrasting pair.
**Contrast type:** Lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *untrue*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** *Untrue* is the morphological negation of *true* via the prefix *un-*, documented in P1b (1.4) and P2a (1.4). Unlike the lexical opposition *true* / *false*, this pair is formed within a single root family; *untrue* carries an additional sense of unfaithfulness or disloyalty not present in *false*.

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** *valid*
**Term B:** *sound*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** A constructional contrast documented in P1 (items 6 and 25) and P3 (Group 8). In formal logic, *valid* describes an argument whose conclusion follows necessarily from its premises regardless of whether the premises are true; *sound* describes an argument that is both valid and has true premises. *Sound* is therefore a stronger condition than *valid* within the logical register.
**Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 42

**Term A:** Subject + BE + truth-domain adjective (simple attribution)
**Term B:** Subject + SEEM/APPEAR + truth-domain adjective (hedged attribution)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A

The copula *be* asserts the truth-domain property directly; *seem* and *appear* introduce epistemic hedging, indicating that the property is apparent rather than confirmed. The data notes this as an attested alternate to the core copular construction.

## Opposition 43

**Term A:** Prefixed negative adjective (*inaccurate*, *invalid*, *illegitimate*)
**Term B:** *not* + adjective (*not accurate*, *not valid*, *not legitimate*)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** A constructional contrast documented in P4a (Construction 2) and P4b. The data notes that prefixed forms tend toward formal and written registers while *not* + adjective is register-neutral. Additionally, some prefixed forms (*disingenuous*, *spurious*) carry connotations beyond simple negation that the free negator construction does not replicate.
**Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 44

**Term A:** Agent noun via verb suffixation: *faker*, *falsifier*, *counterfeiter* (negative pole)
**Term B:** Compound agent noun: *truth-teller*, *truth-seeker* (positive pole)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** A structural asymmetry documented in P4a (Construction 7) and P4b. The data explicitly identifies this as an asymmetry: negative-pole agents are formed by suffixation on the corresponding verb (*fake* → *faker*; *falsify* → *falsifier*), while positive-pole agents require compounding (*truth* + *tell* → *truth-teller*) because direct suffixation on the adjective (*\*truer*, *\*truther*) is not standard.
**Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 45

**Term A:** *it is true that … but* (concessive construction)
**Term B:** Simple assertion: *it is true that …* (without contrastive connector)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** N/A
**Description of contrast:** A constructional contrast documented in P4a (Construction 9) and P4b. The data notes that the contrastive connector (*but*, *however*, *nevertheless*) is obligatory for the concessive reading; without it, the utterance functions as simple assertion. The presence or absence of the connector determines whether *true* functions as a discourse operator (concessive) or as a predicate (assertion).
**Contrast type:** Constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
