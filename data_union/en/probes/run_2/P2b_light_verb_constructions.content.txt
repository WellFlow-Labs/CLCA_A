# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: English (en)
# Input: P1 items #1–#30; P2a derivational data

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

This document records attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 base items. The inventory covers periphrastic expressions, idiomatic phrases, fixed collocations, and syntactically-driven constructions. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Compounds and collocations already documented in P1b root expansion (e.g., *truth table*, *false alarm*, *ground truth*) are cross-referenced but not re-analyzed in full; they are noted where relevant to avoid gaps.

**Scope decisions:**
- Constructions are recorded at the level of the P1 base item, not at the level of derived forms. Where a derived form (e.g., *truthfully*) participates in a construction, it is noted under the base item (*truthful*) with a cross-reference.
- Fixed phrases already documented in P1b (e.g., *truth be told*, *to tell the truth*, *false pretenses*) are listed here with a brief cross-reference notation rather than full re-analysis, to preserve completeness without duplication.
- Constructions involving *pseudo-* (Item 28) are treated as prefixation products documented in P2a; no additional multi-word constructions are identified for the prefix itself.
- Register variation is noted where it affects distribution.

## Item 1 — true

---

**1.1**
- Base Form: true
- Example: *come true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: (of a hope, prediction, or dream) to be realized or fulfilled
- Usage notes: Used in general speech to describe the realization of a desired or predicted state. "Her dream finally came true." Noted in P1b Step 3 as a peripheral extension where the truth-domain sense recedes.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.2**
- Base Form: true
- Example: *ring true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to seem genuine, accurate, or believable
- Usage notes: Used in general speech to evaluate the plausibility or authenticity of a statement or account. "His explanation didn't quite ring true." The metaphor derives from testing coins by their sound.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.3**
- Base Form: true
- Example: *hold true*
- Transliteration: N/A
- English translation: to remain valid or applicable; to continue to be the case
- Usage notes: Used in general and formal registers to assert the continued applicability of a principle or statement. "This observation holds true across all the cases examined."
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**1.4**

- Transliteration: N/A

- Usage notes: Used in general registers to describe a prediction or claim that is subsequently confirmed. "His fears proved true when the results were announced."

**1.5**

- Transliteration: N/A
