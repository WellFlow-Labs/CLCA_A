# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** doğru
**Term B:** yanlış
**Transliteration:** doğru / yanlış
**English translation:** correct, true / wrong, incorrect, mistaken
**Description of contrast:** The primary binary opposition in the propositional correctness domain; *doğru* asserts that a statement or action conforms to fact or norm, while *yanlış* asserts that it deviates from fact or norm. The pair is explicitly coordinated in the fixed construction *doğru yanlış sorusu* (true/false question) and in *doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek* (to distinguish the true from the false).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** doğru
**Term B:** yanlış
**Transliteration:** doğru / yanlış
**English translation:** correct / wrong
**Description of contrast:** Beyond propositional correctness, the same pair operates in moral evaluation of actions and conduct; *doğru yol* (the right path) contrasts with *doğru yoldan sapmak* (to deviate from the right path), where *yanlış* is the implicit or explicit alternative. The moral and propositional uses of the pair are not distinguished by form.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** doğrulamak
**Term B:** yanlışlamak
**Transliteration:** doğrulamak / yanlışlamak
**English translation:** to verify, to confirm / to falsify, to refute
**Description of contrast:** Parallel factitive verbalizations of *doğru* and *yanlış* respectively; *doğrulamak* is the act of establishing that something is correct or true, while *yanlışlamak* is the act of showing that something is false or incorrect. Both are formed by the same *-la-* verbalizer suffix on their respective base adjectives.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** doğrulanabilir / doğrulanabilirlik
**Term B:** yanlışlanabilir / yanlışlanabilirlik
**Transliteration:** doğrulanabilir / doğrulanabilirlik — yanlışlanabilir / yanlışlanabilirlik
**English translation:** verifiable / verifiability — falsifiable / falsifiability
**Description of contrast:** Parallel technical terms in philosophy of science, formed by identical morphological stacking (*-la-* + *-n-* + *-abil-* + *-ir/-lik*) on *doğru* and *yanlış* respectively; the opposition reflects the logical positivist versus Popperian debate about the criterion of scientific meaningfulness, as documented in the Turkish philosophical literature cited in P2a.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** haklı
**Term B:** haksız
**Transliteration:** haklı / haksız
**English translation:** right, justified, in the right / unjust, wrong, unwarranted, in the wrong
**Description of contrast:** A morphological opposition formed by the adjectival suffix *-lı* (having) versus the privative suffix *-sız* (without) on the base *hak*; *haklı* predicates that a person's claim or position is warranted, while *haksız* predicates that it is not. The pair is explicitly coordinated in the formulaic question *hak mı haksız mı?* (right or wrong? justified or unjust?).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** haklılık
**Term B:** haksızlık
**Transliteration:** haklılık / haksızlık
**English translation:** justification, rightness / injustice, wrong, unfairness
**Description of contrast:** Abstract noun forms derived from *haklı* and *haksız* respectively by the *-lık* suffix; *haklılık* names the property of being justified or in the right, while *haksızlık* names the condition or act of being unjust or wrongful. The opposition is fully parallel at the morphological level.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** güvenilir
**Term B:** güvenilmez
**Transliteration:** güvenilir / güvenilmez
**English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, credible / unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
**Description of contrast:** A morphological opposition formed by the positive potential suffix *-ir* versus the negative potential suffix *-mez* on the passive stem *güvenil-*; both are formed from the same base *güven* (trust) and the same passive *-il-*, differing only in the polarity of the potential suffix. The contrast is explicitly noted in P1 as a direct antonym pair.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** güvenilir
**Term B:** güvenilmez
**Transliteration:** güvenilir / güvenilmez
**English translation:** reliable / unreliable
**Description of contrast:** Beyond the morphological structure, the pair functions as a lexical opposition in source evaluation contexts (journalistic, legal, everyday); the two forms are used as direct evaluative contrasts when assessing witnesses, sources, or institutions, as documented in P1 items 16 and 17.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term B:** inanılmaz

**English translation:** believable, credible, plausible / unbelievable, incredible, implausible
**Description of contrast:** A morphological opposition parallel to *güvenilir / güvenilmez*, formed by positive potential *-ır* versus negative potential *-maz* on the passive stem *inanıl-*; *inanılır* evaluates a claim or account as capable of being believed, while *inanılmaz* evaluates it as incapable of being believed or as astonishing.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** geçerli
**Term B:** geçersiz
**Transliteration:** geçerli / geçersiz
**English translation:** valid, in force, accepted / invalid, void, null, not accepted
**Description of contrast:** A morphological opposition formed by the adjectival suffix *-li* (having currency/acceptance) versus the privative suffix *-siz* (without currency/acceptance) on the stem *geçer-*; *geçerli* asserts that something is currently accepted or in force, while *geçersiz* asserts that it is null or not accepted. The pair is explicitly noted as a direct antonym pair in P1 items 20 and 21.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Term B:** geçersizlik

**English translation:** validity / invalidity, nullity
**Description of contrast:** Abstract noun forms derived from *geçerli* and *geçersiz* respectively by the *-lik* suffix; the opposition is fully parallel at the morphological level and is used in legal and administrative contexts to name the contrasting conditions of being valid versus being void.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** gerçek
**Term B:** gerçek dışı
**Transliteration:** gerçek / gerçek dışı
**English translation:** real, actual / unreal, surreal, fictitious
**Description of contrast:** *Gerçek dışı* is formed by adding the postpositional element *dışı* (outside of, beyond) to *gerçek*; the construction asserts that something falls outside the domain of ordinary reality. The opposition is between what exists within reality and what lies beyond or outside it, documented in P1b Root 3 and P2a Item 2.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** gerçek
**Term B:** kurgu
**Transliteration:** gerçek / kurgu
**English translation:** reality / fiction
**Description of contrast:** A lexical opposition explicitly coordinated in the fixed phrase *gerçek ile kurgu* (reality and fiction) and *gerçek ile kurgu arasındaki sınır* (the boundary between reality and fiction); *gerçek* names what actually exists or occurred, while *kurgu* (from *kur-*, to construct) names what is invented or constructed. The pair is documented in P1b Root 3, item 24 and P2b Item 2.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** gerçek
**Term B:** sanal
**Transliteration:** gerçek / sanal
**English translation:** real / virtual
**Description of contrast:** The compound *sanal gerçeklik* (virtual reality) places *sanal* (virtual, from Arabic *ṣanʿa*, craft/artifice) in explicit contrast with *gerçek* (real); the compound names a technologically mediated simulation of reality, where *sanal* marks the departure from unmediated reality. The opposition is implicit in the compound rather than expressed as a coordinate pair.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** orijinal
**Term B:** taklit
**Transliteration:** orijinal / taklit
**English translation:** original, genuine / imitation, counterfeit, copy
**Description of contrast:** A lexical opposition in commercial and legal contexts between an authentic item and an imitation of it; *orijinal ürün* (genuine product) contrasts with *taklit ürün* (counterfeit/imitation product). The pair is documented in P1 items 10 and 27 and in P2b Item 7.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** sahte
**Term B:** gerçek / hakiki / orijinal / asıl
**Transliteration:** sahte / gerçek, hakiki, orijinal, asıl
**English translation:** fake, counterfeit, forged / real, genuine, original, authentic
**Description of contrast:** *Sahte* (fake, fabricated) stands in opposition to multiple terms that assert genuineness or authenticity; the data does not support a single fixed binary pair but rather a one-to-many opposition in which *sahte* is the negative pole and *gerçek*, *hakiki*, *orijinal*, and *asıl* each serve as positive poles in different contexts (ontological, formal/literary, commercial, and documentary respectively). The asymmetry is noted in P3 Group 4.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** sahte
**Term B:** sahici
**Transliteration:** sahte / sahici
**English translation:** fake, counterfeit / genuine, the real thing (colloquial)
**Description of contrast:** In informal registers, *sahici* (genuine, the real thing) functions as the colloquial positive counterpart to *sahte* (fake); the opposition is register-specific and is not a morphological pair. *Sahici* is documented in P1 item 8 as an informal term for authenticity, contrasting with the inauthenticity denoted by *sahte*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** özgün
**Term B:** taklit
**Transliteration:** özgün / taklit
**English translation:** original, not derivative / imitation, copy
**Description of contrast:** In aesthetic and intellectual contexts, *özgün* (original, of one's own essence) contrasts with *taklit* (imitation, copy); *özgün* asserts that a work or idea is not derived from another, while *taklit* asserts that it copies or imitates something else. The opposition is documented in P3 Group 9 and is distinct from the commercial *orijinal / taklit* opposition (Opposition 15) in operating in creative and intellectual rather than commercial domains.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** meşru
**Term B:** gayri meşru
**Transliteration:** meşru / gayri meşru
**English translation:** legitimate, lawful / illegitimate, unlawful, illicit
**Description of contrast:** *Gayri meşru* is formed by the Arabic-origin prefix *gayrı* (other than, non-) prefixed to *meşru*; this is the standard negation strategy for *meşru* in formal registers, as documented in P2a Item 19. The Turkish privative suffix *-sız* is not used to negate *meşru* in standard usage; the Arabic-pattern negation dominates.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** meşruiyet
**Term B:** meşruiyet krizi
**Transliteration:** meşruiyet / meşruiyet krizi
**English translation:** legitimacy / legitimacy crisis
**Description of contrast:** Not a binary lexical opposition but a constructional contrast documented in P2a Item 19; *meşruiyet* names the condition of being legitimate, while *meşruiyet krizi* names the condition of that legitimacy being challenged or absent. The opposition is between the presence and the breakdown of the property, expressed constructionally rather than through a single antonym.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**English translation:** evidenced, proven / without evidence, unsubstantiated

The opposition is between claims or positions that are supported by evidence and those that are not.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Term B:** asıl / asıllı

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** yanıltıcı
**Term B:** aldatıcı
**Transliteration:** yanıltıcı / aldatıcı
**English translation:** misleading, deceptive (not necessarily intentional) / deceptive, fraudulent (intentional)
**Description of contrast:** Both terms denote deception but differ in the degree of intentionality attributed; *yanıltıcı* (from *yanılt-*, causative of *yanıl-*, to err) can apply to unintentional misleading, while *aldatıcı* (from *aldat-*, causative of *alda-*, to deceive) implies intentional deception. The distinction is explicitly noted in P1 items 28 and 29 and in P3 Group 6.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

Documented in P3 Group 6.

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** özü sözü bir
**Term B:** (no single lexicalized antonym identified)
**Transliteration:** özü sözü bir
**English translation:** sincere, a person of integrity, whose inner self and word are one
**Description of contrast:** *Özü sözü bir* (literally "whose essence and word are one") asserts consistency between inner conviction and outward speech; its implicit contrast is with a person whose inner convictions and outward speech diverge, but no single lexicalized antonym for this specific meaning is documented in the data. The opposition is constructional and implicit rather than expressed by a fixed contrasting form.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 27

**Term A:** gerçeğe uygun beyan
**Term B:** gerçeğe aykırı beyan
**Transliteration:** gerçeğe uygun beyan / gerçeğe aykırı beyan
**English translation:** statement conforming to fact / statement contrary to fact
**Description of contrast:** A constructional opposition in legal registers formed by substituting *uygun* (conforming to) and *aykırı* (contrary to) in the same postpositional frame *gerçeğe ___ beyan*; the two constructions are explicitly documented as a contrasting pair in P1b Root 3 items 17–18 and P2b Item 2. The opposition is entirely constructional: the same frame with two antonymous postpositions produces the legal contrast between truthful and false declarations.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 28

**Term A:** doğrulamak (to verify/confirm)
**Term B:** yanlışlamak (to falsify/refute)
**Transliteration:** doğrulamak / yanlışlamak
**English translation:** to confirm as true / to show as false
**Description of contrast:** At the constructional level, these two verbs occupy the same syntactic slot (transitive verb taking a propositional object) but produce opposite epistemic outcomes; *doğrulamak* establishes that a claim is correct, while *yanlışlamak* establishes that it is incorrect. The parallel is explicitly noted in P2a Items 1 and 6 and in P3 Group 3.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** Bu haber doğru. (null-copula attribution)
**Term B:** Bu haber doğru değil. (negated attribution with *değil*)
**Transliteration:** bu haber doğru / bu haber doğru değil
**English translation:** "This news is correct/true." / "This news is not correct/true."
**Description of contrast:** The constructional opposition between affirmative null-copula predication and negation with *değil*; *değil* is the sole negation strategy for adjectival predicates in Turkish, as documented in P4a Construction 2 and P4b. The opposition is fully productive across all truth-domain adjectives and is not restricted to any single lexical item.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** haklı çıkmak (to be proven right)
**Term B:** haksız çıkmak (to be proven wrong/unjustified)
**Transliteration:** haklı çıkmak / haksız çıkmak
**English translation:** to be vindicated, to turn out to be correct / to turn out to be unjustified, to be proven wrong
**Description of contrast:** A constructional opposition formed by substituting *haklı* and *haksız* in the same light-verb construction with *çıkmak* (to come out, to emerge); *haklı çıkmak* is explicitly documented in P2a Item 5 and P2b Item 5 as a fixed collocation for vindication. *Haksız çıkmak* is the parallel negative construction. Both are productive and attested.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** doğruluk (truth/correctness as a property of propositions)
**Term B:** doğruluk (integrity as a property of persons)
**Transliteration:** doğruluk / doğruluk
**English translation:** truth, correctness (propositional) / integrity, honesty (personal)
**Description of contrast:** This is not an opposition between two different forms but a documented internal semantic contrast within a single form; *doğruluk* covers both the logical property of propositions (*doğruluk değeri*, truth value) and the moral property of persons (*doğruluğa bağlılık*, commitment to integrity). The contrast is explicitly noted in P3 under "Ambiguous or Overlapping Items" as a case where the same form covers two distinct evaluative domains.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 32

**Term B:** hayalci (idealistic, dreamy)
**Transliteration:** gerçekçi / hayalci

**Description of contrast:** *Gerçekçi* (from *gerçek* + *-çi*) is documented in P3 Group 9 as contrasting with *hayalci* (from *hayal*, dream/illusion + *-ci*) in evaluative contexts concerning whether an approach or attitude is grounded in reality or in idealization. *Hayalci* is not a P1 item but is cited in P2a Item 11 as the term used to express the concept "unrealistic" where a privative derivative of *gerçekçi* is unavailable.

## Opposition 33

**Term A:** sanal gerçeklik (virtual reality)
**Term B:** artırılmış gerçeklik (augmented reality)
**Transliteration:** sanal gerçeklik / artırılmış gerçeklik
**English translation:** virtual reality / augmented reality
**Description of contrast:** Both compounds use *gerçeklik* (reality) as their head noun but differ in the modifier: *sanal* (virtual, simulated) versus *artırılmış* (augmented, enhanced); the opposition is between a fully simulated reality and a reality that is supplemented by digital overlay. Both are documented in P1b Root 3 items 13–14 and P2b Item 2. This is a constructional contrast within a technical domain rather than a truth-domain opposition, but it is supported by the data.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** hakikat (truth, reality — elevated register)
**Term B:** hakikat sonrası (post-truth — contemporary journalistic register)
**Transliteration:** hakikat / hakikat sonrası
**English translation:** truth / post-truth
**Description of contrast:** *Hakikat sonrası* (post-truth) is formed by adding the postpositional element *sonrası* (after, post-) to *hakikat*; the construction names an epistemic condition defined by the diminished influence of *hakikat* itself. The opposition is between the concept and its negation-by-supersession rather than a simple antonym pair. Documented in P2b Item 3.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 35

**Term A:** ispat yükü (burden of proof — on the claimant)
**Term B:** ispat yükünü tersine çevirmek (reversal of the burden of proof)
**Transliteration:** ispat yükü / ispat yükünü tersine çevirmek
**English translation:** burden of proof / to reverse the burden of proof
**Description of contrast:** A constructional opposition in legal registers between the default allocation of the obligation to prove (*ispat yükü* resting on the claimant) and the procedural act of shifting that obligation to the other party (*ispat yükünü tersine çevirmek*); both are documented in P2b Item 15. The opposition is between a default legal condition and its deliberate inversion.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 36

**Term A:** hakkını teslim etmek (to give someone their due)
**Term B:** hak yemek (to deprive someone of their due)
**Transliteration:** hakkını teslim etmek / hak yemek
**English translation:** to give someone what they are rightfully owed / to take what is not one's due, to deprive someone of their rights
**Description of contrast:** Two idiomatic constructions built on *hak* that express opposite actions with respect to rightful entitlement; *hakkını teslim etmek* (to deliver what is owed) and *hak yemek* (to consume/take what is not owed) are documented in P1b Root 2 items 7 and 22 and in P2b Item 4. The opposition is constructional and idiomatic rather than morphological.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
