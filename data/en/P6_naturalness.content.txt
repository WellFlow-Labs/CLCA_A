# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 NATURALNESS: CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: English (en)

---

## SUBSECTION 1: INCHOATIVE CHANGE (Becoming True/False)

### 1.1 BECOME + truth-domain adjective

**Pattern:** Subject + BECOME + truth-domain adjective

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| The story became true. | /ðə ˈstɔːri bɪˈkeɪm truː/ | "The story was realized/came to pass." | [MARGINAL] | Rare; "came true" strongly preferred for realization. "Became true" acceptable only for propositions previously false that are now verified. |
| His fears became real. | /hɪz fɪəz bɪˈkeɪm rɪəl/ | "His fears were actualized." | [MARGINAL] | "Became real" less common than "became reality" or "came true." Acceptable but marked. |
| The claim became false. | /ðə kleɪm bɪˈkeɪm fɔːls/ | "The claim ceased to be true." | [MARGINAL] | Very rare; implies change in factual status. "Proved false" or "turned out to be false" strongly preferred. |

**General observation:** Simple "become + truth-adjective" is marginal across the domain. English strongly prefers specific verbs (prove, turn out, come) or other constructions for inchoative meaning.

---

### 1.2 COME + truth-domain adjective

**Pattern:** Subject + COME + truth-domain adjective

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| His predictions came true. | /hɪz prɪˈdɪkʃənz keɪm truː/ | "His predictions were realized." | [CORE] | Fixed expression for realization of predictions, hopes, dreams. Extremely common. Not productive with other adjectives. |
| *The statement came false. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. "Come" does not combine with "false" or other negative truth-adjectives. |
| *The document came authentic. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. "Come" restricted to "true" in this domain. |

**General observation:** "Come true" is a fixed, non-productive expression. No other truth-domain adjectives combine with "come" for inchoative meaning.

### 1.3 PROVE + truth-domain adjective

**Pattern:** Subject + PROVE + truth-domain adjective

**Attested examples:**

|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| The rumor proved false. | /ðə ˈruːmər pruːvd fɔːls/ | "The rumor was revealed to be untrue." | [CORE] | Common construction emphasizing evidential discovery. Formal/written register. |
| His theory proved correct. | /hɪz ˈθɪəri pruːvd kəˈrɛkt/ | "His theory was shown to be accurate." | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common in academic and analytical contexts. |

**General observation:** "Prove + adjective" is highly productive with truth-domain adjectives, emphasizing evidential basis for discovering the truth-status. Works with both positive and negative terms.

## SUBSECTION 2: CAUSATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS (Making True/False)

### 2.1 MAKE + object + truth-domain adjective

**Pattern:** Agent + MAKE + object + truth-domain adjective

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| *He made the statement true. | — | — | [MARGINAL] | Highly marked. Implies changing reality to match statement, which is conceptually odd. "Made the statement come true" slightly better but still awkward. |
| *They made the claim false. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. Cannot make a claim false through action (can only prove it false). |
| *She made the document authentic. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. Authenticity is inherent, not caused. "Authenticated" required instead. |

**General observation:** Analytic causative "make + object + adjective" is highly restricted or ungrammatical with truth-domain adjectives. English uses dedicated causative verbs instead.

### 2.3 Resultative causatives

**Pattern:** Agent + verb + object + truth-domain adjective (resultative)

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| They proved him wrong. | /ðeɪ pruːvd hɪm rɒŋ/ | "They demonstrated that he was incorrect." | [CORE] | Productive pattern with "prove." Common in argumentative contexts. |
| The evidence proved her right. | /ði ˈɛvɪdəns pruːvd hər raɪt/ | "The evidence demonstrated she was correct." | [CORE] | Same pattern. Common across contexts. |
| They proved the claim false. | /ðeɪ pruːvd ðə kleɪm fɔːls/ | "They demonstrated the claim was untrue." | [CORE] | Productive with "prove + false/true/correct/wrong." |
| *They made him honest. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical as resultative. Character traits cannot be caused this way. |
| *She painted it true. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. General resultatives do not work with truth-adjectives. |

**General observation:** Resultative constructions are restricted to "prove + object + adjective" pattern. General resultatives (*make/paint/hammer + object + truth-adjective) are ungrammatical.

**Pattern:** Agent + verb + object + as + truth-domain adjective/noun

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |

| She exposed it as fake. | /ʃiː ɪkˈspəʊzd ɪt əz feɪk/ | "She revealed its counterfeit nature." | [CORE] | Common with "expose/reveal" for unmasking deception. |
| He revealed it as a lie. | /hiː rɪˈviːld ɪt əz ə laɪ/ | "He showed it was false." | [CORE] | Common in investigative contexts. |
| They identified it as counterfeit. | /ðeɪ aɪˈdɛntɪfaɪd ɪt əz ˈkaʊntərfɪt/ | "They determined it was fake." | [CORE] | Common in verification contexts. |

**General observation:** "Verb + object + as + adjective/noun" is productive for revealing or confirming truth-status, especially with verbs of revelation (expose, reveal) and verification (identify, confirm).

## SUBSECTION 4: LOSS OF TRUTH-STATUS (Becoming Untrue/Invalid)

### 4.1 CEASE to be + truth-domain adjective

**Pattern:** Subject + CEASE to be + truth-domain adjective

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| The claim ceased to be true. | /ðə kleɪm siːst tə bi truː/ | "The claim stopped being factual." | [MARGINAL] | Formal/archaic. "Became false" or "was proven false" preferred. |
| The contract ceased to be valid. | /ðə ˈkɒntrækt siːst tə bi ˈvælɪd/ | "The contract stopped being in force." | [CORE] | Common in legal contexts for expiration. |
| *He ceased to be honest. | — | — | [MARGINAL] | Awkward for character traits. Implies sudden change, which is odd for honesty. |

**General observation:** "Cease to be + adjective" is formal/archaic for most truth-domain terms, but acceptable for legal validity (expiration).

---

### 4.2 LOSE + nominalized truth-property

**Pattern:** Subject + LOSE + nominalized truth-property

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| The claim lost its truth. | /ðə kleɪm lɒst ɪts truːθ/ | "The claim ceased to be true." | [MARGINAL] | Awkward. "Was proven false" strongly preferred. |
| The contract lost its validity. | /ðə ˈkɒntrækt lɒst ɪts vəˈlɪdəti/ | "The contract became invalid." | [CORE] | Common in legal contexts for expiration. |
| The document lost its authenticity. | /ðə ˈdɒkjumənt lɒst ɪts ˌɔːθɛnˈtɪsəti/ | "The document ceased to be genuine." | [MARGINAL] | Odd phrasing. Authenticity is inherent, not lost. "Was revealed as fake" preferred. |
| He lost his honesty. | /hiː lɒst hɪz ˈɒnɪsti/ | "He ceased to be honest." | [MARGINAL] | Awkward. "Became dishonest" or "lost his integrity" preferred. |

**General observation:** "Lose + nominalization" works best for legal/institutional properties (validity) but is awkward for inherent properties (authenticity) or character traits (honesty).

---

### 4.3 Expiration and invalidation

**Pattern:** Subject + EXPIRE / BE invalidated / BE revoked

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| The contract expired. | /ðə ˈkɒntrækt ɪkˈspaɪərd/ | "The contract ceased to be valid." | [CORE] | Standard for time-limited validity. Very common. |
| The license was revoked. | /ðə ˈlaɪsəns wəz rɪˈvəʊkt/ | "The license was made invalid." | [CORE] | Standard for authority-based invalidation. Common in legal contexts. |
| The results were invalidated. | /ðə rɪˈzʌlts wər ɪnˈvælɪdeɪtɪd/ | "The results were made invalid." | [CORE] | Common in scientific and testing contexts. |
| *The truth expired. | — | — | [UNATTESTED] | Ungrammatical. Truth does not expire. |

**General observation:** Specialized verbs (expire, revoke, invalidate) express loss of validity in institutional/legal contexts. Not applicable to propositional truth or inherent properties.

## SUBSECTION 5: DISCOVERY AND REVELATION (Epistemic Change)

**Pattern:** Agent + DISCOVER/FIND OUT + that + clause

**Attested examples:**

| Expression | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Notes |

**General observation:** Discovery verbs with that-clauses or nominalized truth-terms are standard for expressing epistemic change (coming to know truth-status).

## SUBSECTION 10: SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

### 10.1 Highly productive patterns

**CORE patterns attested across multiple truth-domain terms:**

1. **Subject + PROVE + truth-adjective** — evidential discovery (prove true/false/correct/authentic/reliable)
2. **Subject + TURN OUT to be + truth-adjective** — discovery/revelation (turn out to be true/fake/honest/sound)
3. **Agent + causative-verb + object** — lexicalized causatives (verify, falsify, authenticate, validate, correct, realize, counterfeit, fake)
4. **Agent + PROVE + object + truth-adjective** — resultative (prove him wrong/right, prove it false)
5. **Agent + verb + object + as + truth-adjective** — revelation/identification (expose as fake, confirm as genuine)
6. **Subject + REMAIN + truth-adjective** — stability (remain true/valid/honest/authentic)
7. **Agent + DISCOVER/REALIZE/LEARN + that-clause** — epistemic change (discover it was true, realize it was fake)
8. **Truth-nominalization + BE + revealed/exposed** — passive revelation (truth was revealed, lie was exposed)

### 10.2 Restricted or fixed patterns

**Patterns with limited productivity:**

1. **Subject + COME + true** — FIXED expression, not productive with other adjectives
2. **Subject + HOLD + true** — FIXED expression, not productive with other adjectives
3. **Subject + BECOME + truth-adjective** — MARGINAL, specific verbs strongly preferred
4. **Agent + MAKE + object + truth-adjective** — UNGRAMMATICAL, lexicalized causatives required

### 10.3 Unattested or ungrammatical patterns

**Patterns that do not work in this domain:**

1. **Subject + GET + truth-adjective** — ungrammatical for inchoative change
2. **Subject + KEEP + truth-adjective** — ungrammatical for stability
3. **Agent + MAKE + object + truth-adjective** — ungrammatical, dedicated verbs required
4. **General resultatives** (*paint it true, *hammer it correct) — ungrammatical
5. **Progressive with stative predicates** (*is being true) — ungrammatical

### 10.4 Domain-specific observations

**Semantic constraints on change:**

1. **Propositional truth** (true/false) resists simple causation — cannot "make a statement true" by action, only verify or prove it
2. **Inherent properties** (authentic/genuine) resist loss — authenticity doesn't expire or get lost, only gets revealed as absent
3. **Character traits** (honest/trustworthy) resist sudden change — "became dishonest" awkward, implies gradual process
4. **Legal/institutional validity** shows specialized patterns — expire, revoke, invalidate for loss of validity
5. **Epistemic change** (discovery, realization) requires different constructions from ontological change

**Aspectual constraints:**

1. **Stative predicates** (be true, be authentic) resist progressive aspect
2. **Dynamic verbs** (come true, prove false, verify) accept progressive freely
3. **Perfect aspect** requires temporal context with statives but works freely with dynamics

**Causative strategies:**

1. **Lexicalized causatives dominate** — verify, falsify, authenticate, validate, correct
2. **Analytic causatives fail** — *make it true ungrammatical
3. **Resultatives restricted** — only "prove + object + adjective" productive
4. **Nominalization required** for some causatives — maintain the validity, preserve the authenticity

This analysis documents only attested patterns from the provided context, preserving asymmetries and gaps in the data.
