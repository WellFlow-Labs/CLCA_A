# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

**Native script:** א-מ-נ
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-n
**P1 items participating:** 4 — *meheymen* (8), *ʾemunaʾh* (9), *neʾeman* (10), *ʾaminut* (27)
**Core meaning contribution:** Firmness, reliability, trust, and faithfulness. This root generates the largest cluster of terms in the truth-evaluation domain, covering trustworthiness of persons, credibility of testimony, and the quality of belief or faith. It underlies both epistemic evaluation (is this source credible?) and moral-relational evaluation (is this person faithful?).

---

### Root 2: א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t)

**Native script:** א-מ-ת
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-t
**P1 items participating:** 2 — *ʾemet* (1), *ʾamitʾi* (2)
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as a stable, grounded reality. This root provides the primary lexical anchor for the concept of truth itself in Hebrew, generating both the noun and its adjectival derivative. It is etymologically related to א-מ-נ (sharing the ʾ-m sequence) and contributes the sense of something that holds firm and corresponds to reality.

---

### Root 3: י-ש-ר (y-sh-r)

**Native script:** י-ש-ר
**Transliteration:** y-sh-r
**P1 items participating:** 2 — *yashar* (15), *yosher* (16)
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, uprightness, and moral directness. This root contributes the spatial-moral metaphor of a straight path or line as the basis for evaluating honesty and integrity. It generates terms used in both moral character evaluation and procedural/legal contexts where conduct is assessed as correct or deviant.

> **Note on root selection:** Roots א-מ-נ and א-מ-ת are closely related and may share a proto-Semitic base. They are treated here as distinct roots because Hebrew lexicography and morphological analysis consistently distinguishes them, and they generate non-overlapping derivational families. Root י-ש-ר is selected over ד-י-ק (d-y-q) and צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q) because it participates in a broader range of moral, legal, and everyday evaluative expressions and is more productive in modern Hebrew.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n) — Full Expansion

> This root is highly productive. The 25 most common expressions are listed below; additional technical and liturgical forms exist beyond this set.

---

**1.**
**Form:** אמן
**Transliteration:** ʾamen
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-נ; frozen liturgical form, "so be it, it is firm/true"
**Natural English translation:** Amen; so be it
**Context of use:** Used at the conclusion of prayers, blessings, and oaths across Jewish religious practice to affirm the truth or validity of what has been said. Also used colloquially in Israeli Hebrew to express agreement or affirmation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** מאמין
**Transliteration:** maʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-נ; qal active participle, "one who believes/trusts"
**Natural English translation:** believer; one who believes; trusting
**Context of use:** Used in religious discourse for a person who holds faith, and in everyday speech for someone who believes a claim or trusts a person, e.g., *ʾani maʾamin she-hu tsodeq* ("I believe he is right").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** להאמין
**Transliteration:** lehaʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-נ; hifʿil infinitive, "to cause to trust" → "to believe, to trust"
**Natural English translation:** to believe; to trust
**Context of use:** The standard verb for believing a claim or trusting a person in everyday, legal, and religious contexts, e.g., *qashe li lehaʾamin* ("it is hard for me to believe").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** אמון
**Transliteration:** ʾimun
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-נ; piʿel verbal noun pattern, "training, trust-building"
**Natural English translation:** trust; confidence; training
**Context of use:** Used in two distinct senses: (a) interpersonal or institutional trust, e.g., *ʾimun ha-tsibur* ("public trust/confidence"), and (b) professional or physical training, e.g., *ʾimun tsvaʾi* ("military training"). Context disambiguates.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** אמון ציבורי
**Transliteration:** ʾimun tsiburi
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾimun (root א-מ-נ, "trust") + tsiburi ("public")
**Natural English translation:** public trust; public confidence
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in political, journalistic, and institutional discourse to evaluate whether citizens trust government bodies, public figures, or institutions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אמון בית משפט
**Transliteration:** ʾimun beyt mishpat
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾimun (root א-מ-נ, "trust") + beyt mishpat ("court of law")
**Natural English translation:** the court's trust; judicial confidence
**Context of use:** Used in legal discourse to describe the degree to which a court places confidence in a witness, expert, or piece of evidence, e.g., *ha-ʿed zakaʾh be-ʾimun beyt ha-mishpat* ("the witness earned the court's trust").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** נאמנות
**Transliteration:** neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-נ; abstract noun from *neʾeman*, "faithfulness, loyalty"
**Natural English translation:** loyalty; faithfulness; fidelity; allegiance
**Context of use:** Used in moral, legal, and political contexts to describe the quality of being faithful to a person, institution, or obligation, e.g., *shevaʿat neʾemanut* ("oath of allegiance/loyalty").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** שבועת נאמנות
**Transliteration:** shevaʿat neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** shevaʿah ("oath") + neʾemanut (root א-מ-נ, "faithfulness")
**Natural English translation:** oath of allegiance; loyalty oath
**Context of use:** A fixed legal and civic expression for the formal oath sworn by public officials, new citizens, or military personnel affirming their loyalty to the state or institution.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** נאמן בית
**Transliteration:** neʾeman bayit
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman (root א-מ-נ, "trusted one") + bayit ("house")
**Natural English translation:** trusted household steward; majordomo; trusted aide
**Context of use:** A biblical and literary expression for a trusted household manager or personal aide; also used in modern Hebrew for a trusted personal assistant or steward in institutional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**

**Natural English translation:** competent to testify; admissible as a witness
**Context of use:** A legal term in both halakhic and Israeli civil law indicating that a person meets the criteria to serve as a valid witness, e.g., in matters of contract, marriage, or criminal proceedings.

**11.**

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** אמינות עד
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut ʿed
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾaminut (root א-מ-נ, "credibility") + ʿed ("witness")

**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew word for art in all its forms — visual art, performing arts, literary arts — and also for skilled craftsmanship. Used in aesthetic, cultural, and educational discourse.

**16.**

**17.**

**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to describe a circus performer or acrobat; also used figuratively to describe someone performing elaborate or implausible maneuvers, e.g., in political or rhetorical contexts.

---

**20.**

### Root א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t) — Full Expansion

> This root is moderately productive. All common expressions are listed below.

---

**1.**
**Form:** אמת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root א-מ-ת; base noun, "truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth; the truth
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 1); included here as the base form for the expansion. The primary noun of the root, used across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** לאמיתו של דבר
**Transliteration:** le-ʾamito shel daʾvar
**Morpheme breakdown:** le- ("to, in") + ʾamito ("its truth," root א-מ-ת) + shel daʾvar ("of the matter")
**Natural English translation:** in truth; in actual fact; to tell the truth about the matter
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in legal, journalistic, and everyday discourse to signal that what follows is the genuine or underlying reality of a situation, cutting through appearances or prior misrepresentations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** אמת לאמיתה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet le-ʾamitaʾh
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth") + le-ʾamitaʾh ("to its truth," root א-מ-ת)
**Natural English translation:** the absolute truth; truth in its truest sense; the genuine truth
**Context of use:** A fixed intensifying phrase used in legal testimony, philosophical discourse, and everyday speech to emphasize that something is unambiguously and completely true, without qualification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** דובר אמת
**Transliteration:** dover ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** dover ("speaker," from root ד-ב-ר) + ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth")
**Natural English translation:** truth-teller; one who speaks the truth
**Context of use:** Used in moral evaluation of persons as someone who consistently tells the truth, in both everyday and legal contexts. Also appears in biblical and literary registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** אמת ויציב
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-yatsiv
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth") + ve- ("and") + yatsiv ("stable, established")
**Natural English translation:** true and established; truth and stability
**Context of use:** A fixed liturgical phrase from the morning prayer service (*Shaḥarit*), used to affirm the truth and permanence of the preceding declaration of faith. Occasionally cited in literary and philosophical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אמת ושלום
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-shalom
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth") + ve- ("and") + shalom ("peace")
**Natural English translation:** truth and peace
**Context of use:** A biblical and rabbinic paired expression (from Zechariah 8:19 and rabbinic literature) used in ethical and homiletical discourse to describe the dual values of honesty and harmony. Appears in discussions of when truth may be modified for the sake of peace.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** מפני דרכי שלום
**Transliteration:** mipney darkhe shalom
**Morpheme breakdown:** mipney ("because of, for the sake of") + darkhe ("ways of") + shalom ("peace")
**Natural English translation:** for the sake of peace; in the interest of peaceful relations
**Context of use:** A halakhic and ethical phrase used in discussions of when truth-telling may be modified or omitted to preserve social harmony. Directly relevant to the *ʾemet ve-shalom* tension in Jewish ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

> *Note: This expression does not contain the root א-מ-ת directly but is included because it is the standard counterpart phrase in the fixed discourse around ʾemet ve-shalom.*

---

**8.**
**Form:** חותמו של הקב"ה אמת
**Transliteration:** ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh huʾ ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** ḥotamo ("his seal") + shel ha-qadosh barukh huʾ ("of the Holy One, blessed be He") + ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth")
**Natural English translation:** The seal of the Holy One, blessed be He, is truth
**Context of use:** A fixed rabbinic dictum (Talmud Bavli, Shabbat 55a) asserting that truth is the defining attribute of the divine. Cited in theological, ethical, and homiletical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** אמת המידה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ha-midaʾh
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾemet (root א-מ-ת, "truth") + ha-midaʾh ("the measure, the standard")
**Natural English translation:** the true measure; the standard; the benchmark; the criterion
**Context of use:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse — aesthetic, journalistic, legal, and scientific — to refer to the genuine standard against which something is measured or judged, e.g., *ʾemet ha-midaʾh le-hatslaḥaʾh* ("the true measure of success").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**10.**

**Natural English translation:** truth in his mouth; he speaks truthfully
**Context of use:** A biblical and literary expression (cf. 1 Kings 17:24) used to affirm that a person's words are true. Appears in religious and literary registers.

**Natural English translation:** relative truth
