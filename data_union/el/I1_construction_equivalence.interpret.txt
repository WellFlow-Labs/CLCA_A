## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for BECOME as the primary idiomatic inchoative

The canonical BECOME operator (bare inchoative: γίνομαι + adjective) is grammatical in Greek but is noted in both P4a and P4b as less natural for truth-value transitions specifically. In practice, the BECOME function in the truth domain is carried primarily by passive or middle forms of αποδεικνύω ("to prove/demonstrate") — yielding αποδείχτηκε / αποδείχθηκε — and secondarily by αποκαλύπτεται / αποκαλύφθηκε ("is/was revealed"). Both of these verbs simultaneously encode the process of evaluation or disclosure and its result, making them semantically richer than a bare inchoative.

The distinction between the two substituting verbs is preserved in the data: αποδεικνύεται implies that evidence or argument has established a new truth-value (epistemic force), while αποκαλύφθηκε implies that an agent or process has disclosed a previously hidden state (disclosure force). The REVELATION construction (*αποκαλύφθηκε ως πλαστή*) is therefore both a substitute for BECOME and a distinct operator in its own right, with a disclosure component absent from αποδεικνύεται.

The bare γίνομαι + adjective construction is listed in the Alternate Constructions table as grammatical but non-idiomatic in this domain; no corpus example with γίνομαι and a truth-domain adjective is provided in the data.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction

VERIFICATION (επαληθεύω, διαπιστώνω, τεκμηριώνω, αποδεικνύω) and DISCONFIRMATION (διαψεύδω, αποδεικνύω ότι … ψευδής) are expressed by lexically distinct verbs. The two operators are directionally opposite — VERIFICATION causes something to be established as true; DISCONFIRMATION causes something to be established as false — and Greek maintains this distinction through separate dedicated verbs rather than through a single construction with a polarity parameter. There is no single verb that covers both directions.

There is, however, a partial overlap: αποδεικνύω can serve both VERIFICATION (when the complement is a true proposition) and DISCONFIRMATION (when the complement is a false proposition, as in *Απέδειξε ότι η κατάθεση ήταν ψευδής*). In this latter use, αποδεικνύω functions as a DISCONFIRMATION operator by embedding a negative truth-value in its complement clause, rather than by any change in the verb's own semantics. The dedicated DISCONFIRMATION verb διαψεύδω does not require an embedded complement specifying falsity; it encodes falsity lexically.

### 2.3 NEGATION: analytic and morphological strategies coexist with register differentiation

Two negation strategies are attested and both are productive. Analytic negation (δεν + είναι + positive adjective) is universal, register-neutral, and applies to all adjectives in the domain. Morphological negation via privative prefix (αν-/α-: αναξιόπιστος, αναληθής, ανακριβής, άκυρος) is preferred in formal, journalistic, and legal registers. The two strategies are not always interchangeable: άκυρος is the standard legal term for "invalid/null" and is not freely substitutable by δεν είναι έγκυρος, even though the latter is grammatical. This indicates that for some items, the morphological form has acquired a specialized or conventionalized meaning that the analytic form does not fully replicate.

Not all positive adjectives have a lexicalized privative counterpart, which limits the productivity of the morphological strategy. The analytic strategy has no such limitation.

### 2.4 Asymmetry between positive and negative agentive nominalizations

The data attests a clear asymmetry: ψεύτης / ψεύτρα ("liar") is a fully lexicalized, register-neutral agentive noun for the agent of falsehood. No parallel agentive noun for "truth-teller" is attested in common use. The concept of a truth-teller requires periphrastic expression (αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια, "one who tells the truth"). This asymmetry is explicitly noted across both P4a and P4b and is treated here as a lexical gap rather than a conceptual or morphosyntactic one: the -τής suffix is productive (αξιολογητής is attested), but the specific formation *αληθευτής is not in common use.

### 2.5 CAUSATION (factual) is expressed through sub-domain-restricted lexical verbs

Greek has no morphological causative and no productive periphrastic causative that is idiomatic in the truth domain. The general periphrastic causative κάνω + NP + να + subjunctive is grammatical but is explicitly noted as non-idiomatic here. Instead, CAUSATION of truth-states is distributed across a set of dedicated lexical verbs, each restricted to its own sub-domain: αποδεικνύω (cause to be proven true), επαληθεύω (cause to be verified), διαψεύδω (cause to be shown false), νοθεύω (cause to be adulterated/falsified), πλαστογραφώ (cause a document to be forged), ακυρώνω (cause to become invalid). These verbs are not interchangeable; substituting one for another would change the sub-domain or the specific causal relationship encoded.

### 2.6 DISCOURSE-FRAMING constructions are pragmatically distinct from ATTR

The discourse-framing constructions (η αλήθεια είναι ότι…, για να λέμε την αλήθεια…) are structurally similar to ATTR in that they contain the copula and the noun αλήθεια, but they function as speaker-oriented framing devices rather than propositional truth predications. They do not evaluate an external claim's truth-value; they signal the speaker's stance (frankness, candor, precision) toward the following proposition. Both P4a and P4b explicitly distinguish these from propositional attribution.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **No morphological causative construction exists** in the truth domain or in Greek generally. All causative expression is periphrastic or lexical. This is a morphosyntactic gap relative to languages with productive causative morphology, but it is not a functional gap: the domain is fully covered by dedicated lexical verbs.
- **No bare inchoative construction (γίνομαι + truth-domain adjective) is idiomatic** for truth-value transitions. The BECOME function is structurally displaced onto αποδείχτηκε and αποκαλύφθηκε. The bare inchoative is grammatical but not attested as natural in this domain.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **No agentive noun for "truth-teller"** is attested in common use. *αληθευτής is not in common use; the concept requires periphrastic expression (αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια). This is a lexical gap: the morphological resources for forming such a noun exist (the -τής suffix is productive), but the specific item is absent from common usage.
- **Not all positive truth-domain adjectives have a lexicalized privative counterpart.** The morphological negation strategy (αν-/α- prefix) is therefore not fully productive across the domain. The analytic strategy (δεν + είναι) fills this gap universally.
- **No dedicated construction for CAUSATION (character)** — causing a person to become a liar or truth-teller as a character state — is attested. The agentive noun ψεύτης labels the character state but no construction for inducing it is present in the data.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data. All major truth-domain operations (attribution, modification, becoming, revelation, causation, verification, disconfirmation, negation, nominalization) are expressible in Greek, though some require substitution or circumlocution.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps (only negative or only positive forms missing)

- **Agentive nominalization asymmetry:** The negative pole (agent of falsehood: ψεύτης / ψεύτρα) is fully lexicalized and register-neutral. The positive pole (agent of truth-telling) has no common lexicalized form and requires periphrasis. This is a one-directional lexical gap.
- **Privative adjective asymmetry:** Morphological negation (privative prefix) is available for most but not all positive adjectives in the domain. The gap is on the negative side: some positive adjectives lack a privative counterpart, but no privative adjective lacks a corresponding positive form.
- **Inchoative asymmetry:** The data does not attest a dedicated inchoative construction for the transition to a true state that is structurally parallel to the transition to a false state. Both directions use αποδείχτηκε, which is not directionally asymmetric in form, but the examples in P4a and P4b include more instances of the false-state transition (*αποδείχτηκε ψευδής*, *αποδείχτηκε αναξιόπιστος*) than the true-state transition. This is an attestation asymmetry in the data rather than a structural gap.
