# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: *totoo***

- **Root form:** totoo
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*totoo*, *katotohanan*, *makatotohanan*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Correspondence between a statement or representation and reality; the basic predicate of truth-assertion in Tagalog. This root anchors the propositional and abstract dimensions of truth.

---

**Root 2: *tunay***

- **Root form:** tunay
- **P1 items participating:** 4 (*tunay*, *katunayan*, *pagpapatunay*, *awtentiko* [partial — *awtentiko* is a borrowing that semantically overlaps but does not morphologically derive from *tunay*])
- **Correction:** Morphologically direct: 3 (*tunay*, *katunayan*, *pagpapatunay*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Genuineness, authenticity, and the evidentiary establishment of something as real. This root anchors the authenticity and verification dimensions of truth-evaluation.

---

**Root 3: *sinungaling* / *mali***

Both are candidates for a third root. *Mali* participates in 3 items (*mali*, *pagkakamali*, and the derivational base for *kamalian*) and anchors the error/incorrectness pole. *Sinungaling* participates in 3 items (*sinungaling*, *kasinungalingan*, *pagsisinungaling*) and anchors the deception/lying pole.

**Selected: *mali*** — chosen because its morphological productivity is broader and its semantic range (factual error, moral wrong, procedural incorrectness) is wider than *sinungaling*, which is more narrowly confined to intentional deception.

- **Root form:** mali
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*mali*, *pagkakamali*, and the base of *kamalian*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Incorrectness, error, and wrongness across factual, moral, and procedural domains; the primary antonym of *tama* and the root of the error-evaluation cluster.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

### ROOT 1: *totoo*

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root *totoo*; possibly reduplicated base
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
**Context of use:** Used predicatively to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality (*Totoo ba iyan?* — "Is that true?"). The foundational predicate of truth-assertion in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Transliteration:** katotohanan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-nan* (circumfix forming abstract noun); "the state of being *totoo*"
**Natural English translation:** truth, the truth, reality
**Context of use:** The standard abstract noun for truth in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse. Used in formal declarations, moral exhortations, and Bible translations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** makatotohanan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *maka-* + *totohanan*; "in accordance with or aligned with truth/reality"
**Natural English translation:** realistic, true-to-life, truthful, verisimilar
**Context of use:** Applied to literary, cinematic, and artistic works to evaluate whether their depictions correspond faithfully to reality. Less common in purely propositional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** totoong-totoo
**Transliteration:** totoong-totoo
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo* (full reduplication for intensification)
**Natural English translation:** very real, absolutely true, genuinely genuine
**Context of use:** An intensified form used in emphatic speech to stress that something is unambiguously real or true. Common in emotional or emphatic registers of everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** sa katotohanan
**Transliteration:** sa katotohanan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *sa* (locative/oblique marker) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** in truth, in reality, truthfully speaking
**Context of use:** A discourse connector used to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement, equivalent to "in truth" or "actually." Common in both spoken and written Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** ang katotohanan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *ang* (topic/nominative marker) + *katotohanan*
**Natural English translation:** the truth
**Context of use:** The standard nominal phrase used when referring to truth as a specific entity or fact in discourse (*Sabihin ang katotohanan* — "Tell the truth"). Appears in legal oaths, moral exhortations, and religious texts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** totoo naman
**Transliteration:** totoo naman
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* + *naman* (discourse particle indicating concession, turn-taking, or mild emphasis)
**Natural English translation:** it's true though, that's actually true, well, it is real
**Context of use:** Used in conversational Tagalog to concede a point or affirm something while managing the flow of dialogue. The particle *naman* softens or contextualizes the assertion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** hindi totoo
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *hindi* (negation) + *totoo*
**Natural English translation:** not true, untrue, false
**Context of use:** The standard negation of *totoo*, used to deny the truth of a statement or the reality of a situation. Common in everyday speech and in formal denials.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** totoo ang lahat
**Transliteration:** totoo ang lahat
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* + *ang* (topic marker) + *lahat* (all, everything)
**Natural English translation:** everything is true, it's all true
**Context of use:** Used in confessional, testimonial, or emphatic contexts to assert the complete truth of a set of statements. Common in legal testimony and personal declarations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** katotohanang-katotohanan
**Transliteration:** katotohanang-katotohanan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *katotohanan* + *-ng* (linker) + *katotohanan* (full reduplication for intensification)
**Natural English translation:** absolute truth, the whole truth, pure truth
**Context of use:** An intensified or emphatic form of *katotohanan*, used in rhetorical, religious, or philosophical contexts to stress the completeness or absoluteness of a truth. Less common than the base form; found in literary and religious registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**11.**
**Form:** pagtatago ng katotohanan
**Transliteration:** pagtatago ng katotohanan

**Context of use:** Used in legal, moral, and journalistic discourse to characterize the act of withholding or suppressing true information. Appears in investigative reporting and legal argumentation.

**12.**

**Transliteration:** pagsasabi ng katotohanan

### ROOT 2: *tunay*

---

**1.**
**Form:** tunay
**Transliteration:** tunay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root *tunay*
**Natural English translation:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Context of use:** Used to evaluate persons, objects, relationships, and claims as authentic rather than imitation or false. Applied across everyday, legal, and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katunayan
**Transliteration:** katunayan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "the state of being *tunay*; proof"
**Natural English translation:** proof, evidence, genuineness, verification
**Context of use:** Used as an abstract noun meaning "genuineness" and as a discourse connector (*sa katunayan* — "in fact"). In legal and formal contexts it carries the sense of evidence or proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** pagpapatunay
**Transliteration:** pagpapatunay
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *pag-* + *pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an*; "the act of causing something to be verified/proven"
**Natural English translation:** verification, authentication, confirmation, proof
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts for the act of verifying or authenticating documents, claims, or identities.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** sa katunayan
**Transliteration:** sa katunayan
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *sa* + *katunayan*
**Natural English translation:** in fact, as a matter of fact, in reality
**Context of use:** A very common discourse connector used to introduce a factual clarification or to emphasize the reality of a situation. Used across spoken and written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** patunayan
**Transliteration:** patunayan

**Natural English translation:** to prove, to demonstrate, to verify
**Context of use:** The standard verb for the act of proving or demonstrating something to be true or genuine. Used in legal argumentation, scientific discourse, and everyday persuasion (*Patunayan mo* — "Prove it").

**6.**

**Context of use:** Used to express the possibility or achievement of proving something. Common in legal and argumentative discourse (*Hindi mapatunayan* — "cannot be proven").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 3: *mali*

---

**1.**
**Form:** mali
**Transliteration:** mali
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root *mali*
**Natural English translation:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
**Context of use:** The primary predicate for evaluating statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong. Used across factual, moral, and procedural domains.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** kamalian
**Transliteration:** kamalian
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *ka-* + *mali* + *-an*; "the state or instance of being *mali*"
**Natural English translation:** error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing
**Context of use:** The standard abstract noun for an error or mistake. Used in moral, factual, and procedural evaluations (*Aminin ang kamalian* — "Admit the error/mistake").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** pagkakamali
**Transliteration:** pagkakamali
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *pag-* + *ka-* + *mali* + *-an*; verbal noun of *magkamali*
**Natural English translation:** mistake, error, fault
**Context of use:** The standard nominal form for a specific instance of making a mistake. Used across factual, moral, and procedural domains. More specific to a single act of error than *kamalian*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** magkamali
**Transliteration:** magkamali
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *mag-* (actor-focus verb prefix) + *ka-* + *mali*; "to make a mistake, to err"
**Natural English translation:** to make a mistake, to err, to be wrong
**Context of use:** The standard verb for committing an error. Used across all registers and domains (*Nagkamali ako* — "I made a mistake").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** nagkamali
**Transliteration:** nagkamali
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *nag-* (perfective of *mag-*) + *ka-* + *mali*; "made a mistake"
**Natural English translation:** made a mistake, erred, was wrong
**Context of use:** The completed-aspect form of *magkamali*, used to state that an error was committed. Extremely common in everyday speech and formal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** maling impormasyon
**Transliteration:** maling impormasyon

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the three roots operating in contexts where their contribution appears to shift away from straightforward truth/falsity or error evaluation. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From root *totoo*:**

- *totoo sa sarili* — the root *totoo* here applies to a person's relationship with their own identity and values rather than to a proposition's correspondence with external reality.
- *totoong-totoo* — the full reduplication functions as an intensifier of degree or emphasis, a role that extends beyond simple truth-predication.
- *totoo na* — the combination with the particle *na* marks a change of state or the onset of a new situation, giving *totoo* a temporal-aspectual function.
- *makatotohanan* — the root contributes to aesthetic evaluation of representations (literature, film) rather than to propositional truth-claims.
- *katotohanang pansarili* — the compound frames truth as a subjective or personal possession rather than an objective correspondence.

---

**From root *tunay*:**

- *tunay na kaibigan*, *tunay na pagmamahal* — the root evaluates the depth and authenticity of relational bonds rather than the truth of propositions.
- *tunay na Pilipino* — the root contributes to identity evaluation and nationalist discourse, where "genuineness" concerns cultural or ethnic belonging.
- *tunay na mukha* — the root is applied metaphorically to a person's hidden character or nature, functioning as a metaphor for revelation of concealed identity.
- *tunay na halaga* — the root evaluates worth or value, a domain that overlaps with economic and moral assessment rather than truth-evaluation.
- *tunay na bayani* — the root contributes to the evaluation of whether a person merits a social or honorific designation, a function closer to normative classification than truth-predication.
- *tunay na kahulugan* — the root contributes to interpretive evaluation of meaning, where "genuineness" concerns hermeneutic correctness rather than factual correspondence.

---

**From root *mali*:**

- *maling landas*, *maling daan* — the root is applied to life direction and moral conduct through spatial metaphor, where "wrongness" concerns moral or existential orientation rather than factual incorrectness.
- *maling oras* — the root evaluates temporal appropriateness rather than factual or moral correctness.
- *maling tao* — the root evaluates identity-matching in practical contexts (wrong person identified) rather than the truth-value of a proposition.
- *maling desisyon* — the root evaluates practical judgment and its consequences rather than factual accuracy.
- *maling paggamit* — the root evaluates procedural or normative propriety of use, a function that overlaps with the domain of *wasto* (proper/appropriate) rather than purely factual correctness.
