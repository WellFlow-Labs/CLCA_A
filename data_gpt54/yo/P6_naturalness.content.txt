## 1. BECOMING TRUE / COMING TO BE ESTABLISHED AS TRUE
### Pattern
**[Clause/claim] + jẹ́ òtítọ́**; sometimes strengthened by **ó dájú** or supported by **fi ẹ̀rí hàn**

### Examples
- **Ó jẹ́ òtítọ́.** → *ó jẹ́ òtítọ́* → “It is true / It turns out to be true.”
- **Ó dájú.** → *ó dájú* → “It is certain / It is definitely so.”

### Notes
The supplied material supports predicative expressions for a truth state, especially **jẹ́ òtítọ́** and **òtítọ́ ni**. These can describe a claim as true, including cases where something is established as true in discourse.  
However, the context does **not** clearly establish a dedicated inchoative construction specifically meaning “become true.” So the safest statement is that Yoruba here uses ordinary predication of truth, sometimes with evidential support, rather than a special “become true” form.

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / COMING TO BE REJECTED AS FALSE
### Pattern
**[Clause/claim] + kì í ṣe òtítọ́**; **[Clause/claim] + irọ́ ni**

### Examples
- **Kì í ṣe òtítọ́.** → *kì í ṣe òtítọ́* → “It is not true.”
- **Irọ́ ni.** → *irọ́ ni* → “It is a lie / It is false.”

### Notes
These expressions encode the resulting evaluation that a claim is false or not true.  
Again, the context does **not** clearly provide a special inchoative expression meaning “become false.” What is clearly attested is the state attribution or reclassification of a claim as false: either softer **kì í ṣe òtítọ́** or stronger **irọ́ ni**.

## 3. BECOMING RIGHT / CORRECT
### Pattern
**[X] + tọ́ / ó tọ́**

### Examples
- **Ó tọ́.** → *ó tọ́* → “It is right / correct.”
- **L’òtítọ́, ó tọ́.** → *l’òtítọ́, ó tọ́* → “In fact, it is right.”

### Notes
The context supports **tọ́** as a predicate of correctness, propriety, or rightness. This can describe the state an action, judgment, or statement has.  
But there is **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED** in the supplied material for a dedicated “become right/correct” construction. The attested pattern is simple predication, not a special change-of-state form.

---

## 4. BECOMING WRONG / INCORRECT
### Pattern
**[X] + kò tọ́**

### Examples
- **Kò tọ́.** → *kò tọ́* → “It is not right / It is wrong.”
- **L’òtítọ́, kò tọ́.** → *l’òtítọ́, kò tọ́* → “In fact, it is wrong.”

### Notes
This is the standard negative counterpart to **tọ́** in the supplied material. It covers moral wrongness, impropriety, and factual/practical incorrectness.  
As above, the context does **not** clearly establish a dedicated “become wrong” expression; it supports only the resulting evaluative state.

## 5. CAUSING TRUTH TO BECOME MANIFEST
### Pattern
**fi òtítọ́ hàn**

### Examples
- **Fi òtítọ́ hàn.** → *fi òtítọ́ hàn* → “Show / reveal the truth.”
- **Ẹ̀rí fi òtítọ́ hàn.** → *ẹ̀rí fi òtítọ́ hàn* → “Evidence shows the truth.”

### Notes
This is the clearest dynamic causative-type expression in the truth domain from the supplied material. It does not mean “make something true,” but rather “cause the truth to become visible/manifest/known.”  
So the causation here is epistemic and evidential, not ontological.

---

## 6. CAUSING EVIDENCE TO BECOME AVAILABLE OR VISIBLE
### Pattern
**fi ẹ̀rí hàn**

### Examples
- **Fi ẹ̀rí hàn.** → *fi ẹ̀rí hàn* → “Present evidence / show proof.”
- **Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn.** → *ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn* → “He/she presented evidence.”

### Notes
This is another strong analytic causative-like construction. It means causing evidence to be displayed or made available in discourse, argument, investigation, or court-like settings.  
It often functions as the practical route by which a claim comes to be treated as **òtítọ́** or **ó dájú**.

---

## 7. CAUSING CERTAINTY / MAKING SURE
### Pattern
**rí i dájú**; also **rí i pé**

### Examples
- **Rí i dájú.** → *rí i dájú* → “Make sure / ascertain.”
- **Rí i pé ó tọ́.** → *rí i pé ó tọ́* → “See to it that it is right / correct.”

### Notes
These are important dynamic expressions in the broader truth-related domain.  
**Rí i dájú** means to make something certain, secure, or checked.  
**Rí i pé** means to ensure that something is the case.  
These do not directly mean “make true,” but they encode active verification, confirmation, and procedural establishment.

## 8. SPEAKING, KNOWING, OR VALUING TRUTH AS DYNAMIC ACTIVITY
### Pattern
**sọ òtítọ́** / **mọ̀ òtítọ́** / **fẹ́ òtítọ́** / **máa sọ òtítọ́**

### Examples
- **Sọ òtítọ́.** → *sọ òtítọ́* → “Tell the truth.”
- **Mọ̀ òtítọ́.** → *mọ̀ òtítọ́* → “Know the truth.”
- **Máa sọ òtítọ́.** → *máa sọ òtítọ́* → “Tell the truth habitually.”

### Notes
These are dynamic in the sense of actions and dispositions involving truth:
- **sọ òtítọ́** = produce truthful speech
- **mọ̀ òtítọ́** = come to possess truth as knowledge/state
- **fẹ́ òtítọ́** = value truth
- **máa sọ òtítọ́** = habitual truth-speaking

They do not encode change-of-state morphology, but they are central truth-related event expressions.

## 9. EVIDENTIAL SUPPORT AS A DYNAMIC STATE
### Pattern
**ní ẹ̀rí** / **kò sí ẹ̀rí** / **jẹ́ ẹlẹ́rìí** / **pe ẹlẹ́rìí**

### Examples
- **Mo ní ẹ̀rí.** → *mo ní ẹ̀rí* → “I have evidence.”
- **Kò sí ẹ̀rí.** → *kò sí ẹ̀rí* → “There is no evidence.”
- **Ó jẹ́ ẹlẹ́rìí.** → *ó jẹ́ ẹlẹ́rìí* → “He/she is a witness.”
- **Pe ẹlẹ́rìí.** → *pe ẹlẹ́rìí* → “Call a witness.”

### Notes
These expressions encode the dynamics of support and verification:
- evidence can be possessed or absent,
- a person can function as witness,
- a witness can be called.

This is not “truth becoming true,” but it is a central pathway by which truth claims are stabilized, challenged, or established.

## 10. AUTHENTICITY AND VERIFICATION
### Pattern
**ojúlówó**; **ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀**; **bá mu**; **rí i dájú**

### Examples
- **Ó jẹ́ ojúlówó.** → *ó jẹ́ ojúlówó* → “It is genuine / authentic.”
- **Ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀.** → *ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀* → “proof of identity”
- **Ó bá mu.** → *ó bá mu* → “It matches / corresponds.”
- **Rí i dájú.** → *rí i dájú* → “Make sure / verify.”

### Notes
The context supports authenticity and matching as a practical truth-related domain.  
But there is **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED** in the supplied material for a dedicated causative like “make it authentic” or an inchoative like “become authentic.”  
What is clearly supported is:
- predicating authenticity (**ojúlówó**),
- checking identity/documentation (**ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀**),
- matching/correspondence (**bá mu**),
- verification (**rí i dájú**).

## 11. MORAL RIGHTNESS AS ACTION, NOT JUST STATE
### Pattern
**ṣe òdodo** / **dúró fún òdodo** / **fẹ́ òdodo** / **tẹ̀lé òdodo**

### Examples
- **Ṣe òdodo.** → *ṣe òdodo* → “Do what is right / act justly.”
- **Dúró fún òdodo.** → *dúró fún òdodo* → “Stand for justice.”
- **Fẹ́ òdodo.** → *fẹ́ òdodo* → “Love justice.”
- **Tẹ̀lé òdodo.** → *tẹ̀lé òdodo* → “Follow righteousness / pursue justice.”

### Notes
In the **òdodo** domain, the dynamic expressions are mainly actional and dispositional rather than change-of-state. Yoruba here encodes righteousness/justice through doing, standing for, loving, or following it.  
There is **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED** in the supplied material for a dedicated “become righteous/just” construction.

## 12. STABILITY VS ABSENCE OF SUPPORT
### Pattern
**ó dájú** / **ní ẹ̀rí** / **kò sí ẹ̀rí**

### Examples
- **Ó dájú.** → *ó dájú* → “It is certain.”
- **Mo ní ẹ̀rí.** → *mo ní ẹ̀rí* → “I have evidence.”
- **Kò sí ẹ̀rí.** → *kò sí ẹ̀rí* → “There is no evidence.”

### Notes
A stable truth-related state in the supplied material is often encoded not by a special verb meaning “stabilize,” but by:
- certainty (**ó dájú**),
- possession of evidence (**ní ẹ̀rí**),
- or denial of support (**kò sí ẹ̀rí**).

So stability is expressed through settled evaluation and evidential grounding, not through a distinct grammatical category of stabilization.

## 13. SUMMARY OF WHAT IS AND IS NOT CLEARLY ATTESTED
### Clearly attested dynamic patterns
- **fi òtítọ́ hàn** — reveal/show the truth
- **fi ẹ̀rí hàn** — present/show evidence
- **rí i dájú** — make sure, ascertain
- **rí i pé** — ensure that
- **sọ òtítọ́** — tell the truth
- **mọ̀ òtítọ́** — know the truth
- **ṣe òdodo** — act justly
- **dúró fún òdodo** — stand for justice
- **pe ẹlẹ́rìí** — call a witness

### Not clearly attested as dedicated constructions
- “become true” as a special inchoative form
- “become false” as a special inchoative form
- “make something true” as a dedicated causative
- “become authentic” as a dedicated inchoative
- “make something authentic” as a dedicated causative
- “become righteous” as a dedicated inchoative

### Overall note
The supplied Yoruba material encodes truth dynamics mainly through:
1. **predication of states** — *jẹ́ òtítọ́, kì í ṣe òtítọ́, ó tọ́, kò tọ́, ó dájú*  
2. **analytic showing/verification constructions** — *fi òtítọ́ hàn, fi ẹ̀rí hàn, rí i dájú, rí i pé*  
3. **action expressions involving truth or justice** — *sọ òtítọ́, mọ̀ òtítọ́, ṣe òdodo*
