# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

**Note on transliteration:** Finnish uses the Latin alphabet with two additional letters (ä, ö). No transliteration is required; native script forms are given directly.

---

**1. tosi**
- **Form:** tosi
- **Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
- **Literal meaning:** true, real (adjective/adverb; cognate with Estonian *tõsi*)
- **Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, really
- **Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and to intensify claims about reality. Appears in both propositional contexts (*se on tosi* — "it is true") and as an adverbial intensifier (*tosi hyvä* — "really good"). Functions both as an adjective (*tosi asia* — a true/real matter) and as an intensifying adverb in colloquial speech. Participates in productive derivation: *totuus* (truth), *todistaa* (to prove/witness), *toteuttaa* (to realize/implement).
- **[CORE]**

---

**2. totuus**
- **Form:** totuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** truth (derived from *tosi* + nominalizing suffix *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** truth, the truth, truthfulness
- **Usage:** The standard nominal form for truth as an abstract property or as a specific true state of affairs. Used in philosophical, legal, moral, and everyday discourse (*kertoa totuus* — "to tell the truth"). Appears in compounds such as *totuudenmukaisuus* (truthfulness, accuracy) and serves as a productive base for compounds such as *totuudenmukainen*.
- **[CORE]**

---

**3. aito**
- **Form:** aito
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** genuine, authentic (adjective; etymology debated, possibly from older Germanic borrowing)
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, original
- **Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, emotions, documents, and cultural products to assert that they are not imitations or counterfeits (*aito nahka* — "genuine leather"; *aito suomalainen* — "a true/authentic Finn"; *aito allekirjoitus* — "genuine signature"; *aito tunne* — "genuine feeling"). Contrasts with *väärennös* (forgery) and *jäljitelmä* (imitation). Productive in derivation: *aitous* (authenticity), *aitoutua* (to become authentic).
- **[CORE]**

---

**4. aitous**
- **Form:** aitous
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** genuineness, authenticity (from *aito* + *-uus* / *-us*)
- **Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness
- **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in aesthetic, philosophical, and legal contexts when evaluating whether something is what it claims to be. Frequently appears in discussions of art authentication and personal identity. Closely related to *alkuperäisyys* (originality, provenance).
- **[CORE]**

---

**5. oikea**
- **Form:** oikea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** right, correct (adjective; also means "right" as in direction)
- **Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper, true, genuine, real
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate correctness of answers, actions, and identities (*oikea vastaus* — "the correct answer"; *oikea henkilö* — "the right person"; *oikea ihminen* — "the right person"). Also used to mean "genuine" or "real" in contrast to imitation (*oikea nahka* — "real leather"). Overlaps semantically with *tosi* in some contexts but emphasizes normative or identificatory rightness rather than propositional truth. Derives *oikeellisuus* (correctness), *oikeus* (right, justice, court).
- **[CORE]**

---

**6. oikeellisuus**
- **Form:** oikeellisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** correctness, accuracy (derived from *oikea* + *-llinen* + *-uus* / *-llisuus*)
- **Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, propriety
- **Usage:** Used in formal, administrative, linguistic, and legal contexts to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard or norm (*kielellinen oikeellisuus* — "linguistic correctness"). More formal and abstract than *oikea* itself. Distinct from *totuus* in that it emphasizes conformity to a rule rather than correspondence to fact. Overlaps with *pätevyys* in formal evaluation but emphasizes applicability and qualification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**7. todellinen**
- **Form:** todellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** real, actual (derived from *tosi/tode-* + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** real, actual, genuine, true
- **Usage:** Asserts that something exists or is the case in reality, as opposed to being apparent, imagined, or nominal (*todellinen syy* — "the real reason"; *todellinen elämä* — "real life"). Contrasts with *näennäinen* (apparent, seeming) and *kuvitteellinen* (imaginary). Derives *todellisuus* (reality).
- **[CORE]**

---

**8. todellisuus**
- **Form:** todellisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** reality (from *todellinen* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** reality, actuality
- **Usage:** The standard term for reality as a domain against which claims or representations are evaluated. Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts (*todellisuudessa* — "in reality"). Appears in compounds and collocations evaluating correspondence between representation and fact.
- **[CORE]**

---

**9. luotettava**
- **Form:** luotettava
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (passive present participle of *luottaa* — "to trust"; from *luottaa* + *-ttava*, participial suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible
- **Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, instruments, and institutions to assert that they can be trusted or depended upon to perform consistently or to report accurately (*luotettava lähde* — "a reliable source"; *luotettava henkilö* — "a trustworthy person"). Derives *luotettavuus* (reliability, trustworthiness). Contrasts with *epäluotettava* (unreliable).
- **[CORE]**

---

**10. luotettavuus**
- **Form:** luotettavuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** reliability, trustworthiness (from *luotettava* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
- **Usage:** The nominal form used in formal evaluations of sources, methods, witnesses, and systems. Frequently appears in academic, journalistic, and legal discourse (*tutkimuksen luotettavuus* — "the reliability of the research"). Closely related to but distinct from *uskottavuus* (credibility, plausibility).
- **[CORE]**

---

**11. epäluotettava**
- **Form:** epäluotettava
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** unreliable, untrustworthy (from *epä-* + *luotettava*)
- **Typical English translations:** unreliable, untrustworthy, undependable
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *luotettava*, used to characterize sources, persons, or data that cannot be trusted. Appears in evaluative contexts across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers.
- **[CORE]**

---

**12. uskottava**
- **Form:** uskottava
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** believable, credible (passive present participle of *uskoa* — "to believe"; from *uskoa* + *-ttava*)
- **Typical English translations:** credible, believable, plausible, convincing
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether a claim, person, or representation is such that it can or should be believed (*uskottava selitys* — "a credible explanation"; *uskottava todistaja* — "a credible witness"). Derives *uskottavuus* (credibility). Distinct from *luotettava* in that it concerns believability rather than dependability.
- **[CORE]**

---

**13. uskottavuus**
- **Form:** uskottavuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** credibility, plausibility (from *uskottava* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** credibility, plausibility, believability
- **Usage:** Used to assess whether a claim, narrative, or person commands belief. Appears in legal, journalistic, and everyday evaluative contexts. A witness's *uskottavuus* is a standard consideration in Finnish legal proceedings.
- **[CORE]**

---

**14. pätevä**
- **Form:** pätevä
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** valid, competent, qualified (present participle of *päteä* — "to be valid, to apply, to be competent")
- **Typical English translations:** valid, competent, qualified, sound
- **Usage:** Applied to arguments, credentials, persons, and legal instruments to assert that they meet required standards or hold in the relevant domain (*pätevä peruste* — "a valid reason/ground"; *pätevä opettaja* — "a qualified teacher"; *pätevä todiste* — "valid evidence"; *pätevä hakija* — "qualified applicant"). Derives *pätevyys* (validity, competence). Used in both logical and professional evaluation contexts.
- **[CORE]**

---

**15. pätevyys**
- **Form:** pätevyys
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** validity, competence (from *pätevä* + *-yys*)
- **Typical English translations:** validity, competence, qualification, soundness
- **Usage:** The nominal form used to evaluate whether a person holds the required qualifications or whether an argument or instrument meets formal standards (*todistuksen pätevyys* — "the validity of a certificate"). Appears in legal, academic, and professional contexts. Overlaps with *oikeellisuus* in formal evaluation but emphasizes applicability and qualification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**16. pätemätön**
- **Form:** pätemätön
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** invalid, incompetent (from *päteä* + negative *-mätön*)
- **Typical English translations:** invalid, incompetent, unqualified, void
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *pätevä*; used in legal contexts to declare instruments or arguments void or invalid, and of persons to indicate lack of qualification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**17. väärä**
- **Form:** väärä
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** wrong, false, crooked (adjective; also has spatial sense of "crooked, bent")
- **Typical English translations:** wrong, false, incorrect, fake, counterfeit
- **Usage:** The primary antonym of both *oikea* and *tosi* in evaluative contexts. Covers both epistemic falsity (a wrong answer) and inauthenticity (a counterfeit object) (*väärä vastaus* — "a wrong answer"; *väärä henkilöllisyys* — "a false identity"; *väärä seteli* — "counterfeit banknote"; *väärä nimi* — "false name"; *väärä väite* — "false claim"; *väärä teko* — "wrong act"). Used across moral, logical, and factual evaluation. Derives *vääryys* (wrongness, injustice), *väärentää* (to falsify, to forge), *väärennös* (forgery).
- **[CORE]**

---

**18. epätosi**
- **Form:** epätosi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** untrue, false (negation prefix *epä-* + *tosi*)
- **Typical English translations:** untrue, false
- **Usage:** The direct negation of *tosi*, used to assert that a statement does not correspond to reality. More explicitly propositional in use than *väärä*, and more formal in register. Appears in logical and philosophical discourse; less common in casual speech than *väärä* or *ei totta*. More explicitly logical in register than *väärä*, though the two overlap.
- **[CORE]**

---

**19. väärentää**
- **Form:** väärentää
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to falsify, to forge (derived from *väärä* + causative suffix *-ntää* / *-entää*)
- **Typical English translations:** to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit, to adulterate
- **Usage:** Denotes the deliberate act of making something false, counterfeit, or fraudulent — documents, signatures, currency, evidence (*väärentää asiakirja* — "to forge a document"; *väärentää historiaa* — "to falsify history"). A standard legal term in Finnish criminal law. Used in legal and moral contexts. Derives *väärennös* (forgery, counterfeit), *väärentäjä* (forger), and *väärentäminen* (forgery, falsification).
- **[CORE]**

---

**20. väärentäminen**
- **Form:** väärentäminen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** forgery, falsification (verbal noun of *väärentää*)
- **Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting
- **Usage:** The nominal form used in legal and administrative contexts to name the act or offense of forging or falsifying. Appears in compound legal terms such as *asiakirjan väärentäminen* (document forgery).
- **[CORE]**

---

**21. väärennös**
- **Form:** väärennös
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** forgery, counterfeit (from *väärentää* + result nominalization *-ös*)
- **Typical English translations:** forgery, fake, counterfeit, falsification
- **Usage:** Refers to the product of deliberate falsification — a forged document, counterfeit object, or fabricated representation (*taulun väärennös* — "a forgery of a painting"). Distinct from *kopio* (neutral copy) and *jäljitelmä* (imitation) in that it implies fraudulent intent. Standard term in Finnish legal language. Used in legal, art-authentication, and documentary contexts. Contrasts with *aito* (genuine) and *alkuperäinen* (original).
- **[CORE]**

---

**22. alkuperäinen**
- **Form:** alkuperäinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** original (from *alkuperä* — "origin, source" + *-inen*)
- **Typical English translations:** original, authentic, primary, initial, source
- **Usage:** Asserts that something is the first or source version rather than a copy, translation, or derivative (*alkuperäinen teos* — "the original work"; *alkuperäinen sopimus* — "the original contract"; *alkuperäinen asiakirja* — "the original document"). Used in legal, artistic, and documentary evaluation. Overlaps with *aito* but emphasizes temporal and genealogical priority rather than non-counterfeit status. Derives *alkuperäisyys* (originality, authenticity of provenance).
- **[CORE]**

---

**23. tarkka**
- **Form:** tarkka
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** precise, accurate, exact (adjective)
- **Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, careful
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether a measurement, description, translation, or observation corresponds closely to its object or standard (*tarkka mittaus* — "an accurate measurement"; *tarkka kuvaus* — "an accurate description"). Derives *tarkkuus* (accuracy, precision) and *tarkistaa* (to check, to verify). Emphasizes closeness of correspondence rather than binary truth.
- **[CORE]**

---

**24. tarkkuus**
- **Form:** tarkkuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** accuracy, precision (from *tarkka* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness
- **Usage:** The nominal form used to evaluate the degree of correspondence between a representation, measurement, or description and its referent (*mittauksen tarkkuus* — "the accuracy of the measurement"). Used in scientific, technical, and evaluative discourse. Distinct from *oikeellisuus* in that it admits of degrees. Frequently used in measurement, translation, and reporting contexts.
- **[CORE]**

---

**25. tarkistaa**
- **Form:** tarkistaa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to check, to verify, to inspect (from *tarkka* + causative/factitive suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** to check, to verify, to inspect, to review
- **Usage:** Denotes the act of examining something to determine whether it is accurate, correct, or in order (*tarkistaa tiedot* — "to verify the information"; *tarkistaa passi* — "to check the passport"). Used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts. Derives *tarkistus* (check, verification, review).
- **[CORE]**

---

**26. todistaa**
- **Form:** todistaa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to prove, to testify, to witness (from *tosi* via *todistus* / *tosi* + *-staa*)
- **Typical English translations:** to prove, to demonstrate, to testify, to witness, to certify, to attest
- **Usage:** Used in legal contexts for giving testimony and in logical/epistemic contexts for proving a claim (*todistaa oikeudessa* — "to testify in court"; *todistaa väite* — "to prove a claim"; *todistaa syyllisyys* — "to prove guilt"; *todistaa asiakirja* — "to certify a document"). Covers both the act of proving a claim and the act of giving testimony or certifying a document. Central to both legal and epistemic evaluation. Derives *todistus* (proof, certificate, testimony), *todistaja* (witness), *todiste* (piece of evidence).
- **[CORE]**

---

**27. todiste**
- **Form:** todiste
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** piece of evidence (from *todistaa*, result nominalization)
- **Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, piece of evidence, exhibit
- **Usage:** Refers to a specific item of evidence used to establish the truth or falsity of a claim, particularly in legal contexts (*todiste rikoksesta* — "evidence of a crime"; *esittää todiste* — "to present evidence"). Distinct from *todistus* (which can mean a certificate or testimony as a whole) in referring to individual evidentiary items. Used in legal and investigative discourse.
- **[CORE]**

---

**28. todistus**
- **Form:** todistus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** proof, testimony, certificate (verbal noun from *todistaa*)
- **Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, testimony, certificate, attestation
- **Usage:** Covers both the abstract sense of proof or evidence and the concrete sense of a certificate or credential (*syntymätodistus* — "birth certificate"; *todistus syyllisyydestä* — "proof of guilt"). Widely used across legal, administrative, and everyday contexts.
- **[CORE]**

---

**29. näennäinen**
- **Form:** näennäinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** apparent, seeming (from *näkyä* — "to appear, to be visible" + *-nnäinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible, superficial
- **Usage:** Describes something that appears to be the case but may not be, or that has the surface form of a quality without its substance (*näennäinen yksimielisyys* — "apparent consensus"; *näennäinen rehellisyys* — "ostensible honesty"; *näennäinen yhdenmukaisuus* — "apparent conformity"). Used in philosophical, critical, and analytical contexts to signal a gap between appearance and reality. Contrasts with *todellinen* (real, actual).
- **[CORE]**

---

**30. rehellinen**
- **Form:** rehellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** honest (from *rehellis-*; etymology uncertain, possibly from Swedish *redlig*)
- **Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, straightforward, upright, sincere
- **Usage:** Evaluates persons, statements, and conduct as characterized by honesty and non-deception (*rehellinen vastaus* — "an honest answer"; *rehellinen ihminen* — "an honest person"). Used across everyday, moral, and legal contexts. Derives *rehellisyys* (honesty). Contrasts with *epärehellinen* (dishonest) and *vilpillinen* (fraudulent, deceitful).
- **[CORE]**

---

**31. rehellisyys**
- **Form:** rehellisyys
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** honesty (from *rehellinen* + *-yys*)
- **Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, truthfulness, straightforwardness
- **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality or virtue of being honest in conduct, speech, or representation. Used in moral, professional, and legal evaluations of character and conduct. Frequently appears in discussions of journalistic, scientific, and personal integrity.
- **[CORE]**

---

**32. epärehellinen**
- **Form:** epärehellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** dishonest (from *epä-* + *rehellinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** dishonest, untruthful
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *rehellinen*; used to characterize persons or conduct as dishonest. Common in moral and everyday evaluative contexts.
- **[CORE]**

---

**33. valheellinen**
- **Form:** valheellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** mendacious, false (from *valhe* — "lie" + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** false, mendacious, lying, deceitful
- **Usage:** Evaluates statements or persons as characterized by lying or deliberate falsehood (*valheellinen todistus* — "false testimony"; *valheellinen väite* — "a mendacious claim"). More formal and explicitly moral in register than *väärä*. Used in legal and ethical contexts. Derives from *valhe* (lie) and *valehdella* (to lie).
- **[CORE]**

---

**34. vilppi**
- **Form:** vilppi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** deceit, fraud, bad faith
- **Typical English translations:** deceit, fraud, dishonesty, bad faith, cheating
- **Usage:** Denotes deliberate deception or fraudulent conduct, particularly in academic, commercial, and legal contexts. *Akateeminen vilppi* (academic fraud/misconduct) is a fixed expression in Finnish higher education. Derives *vilpillinen* (fraudulent, deceitful).
- **[CORE]**

---

**35. vilpillinen**
- **Form:** vilpillinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** dishonest, fraudulent, deceitful (from *vilppi*, deceit/fraud, + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** dishonest, fraudulent, deceitful, in bad faith
- **Usage:** Describes conduct, intent, or persons characterized by deception or bad faith (*vilpillinen menettely* — "fraudulent conduct"). A standard phrase in Finnish legal and administrative language. Contrasts directly with *rehellinen* and with *vilpitön* (sincere, in good faith).
- **[CORE]**

---

**36. vilpitön**
- **Form:** vilpitön
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** sincere, in good faith, guileless (from *vilppi* + negative *-tön*)
- **Typical English translations:** sincere, honest, in good faith, guileless
- **Usage:** Describes persons or conduct free from deception or ulterior motive (*vilpitön tarkoitus* — "sincere intention"; *vilpitön mieli* — "good faith"). Important in both moral and legal evaluations of intent.
- **[CORE]**

---

**37. oikeudenmukaisuus**
- **Form:** oikeudenmukaisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** justice, fairness (from *oikeuden* — genitive of *oikeus* "justice/right" + *mukainen* — "in accordance with" + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, righteousness, equity
- **Usage:** Evaluates conduct, decisions, or systems as conforming to principles of justice or fairness (*oikeudenmukainen tuomio* — "a just verdict"; *oikeudenmukainen kohtelu* — "fair treatment"). A central term in moral, legal, and political discourse. Distinct from *oikeellisuus* (correctness to a rule) in that it concerns substantive fairness. Derives from the same root as *oikea* and *oikeus*.
- **[CORE]**

---

**38. oikeudenmukainen**
- **Form:** oikeudenmukainen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** just, fair (from *oikeuden* — genitive of *oikeus* "justice/right" + *mukainen* — "in accordance with")
- **Typical English translations:** just, fair, righteous
- **Usage:** Evaluates persons, decisions, and systems as conforming to principles of justice or fairness (*oikeudenmukainen tuomio* — "a just verdict"; *oikeudenmukainen kohtelu* — "fair treatment"). Used in moral, legal, and political discourse. Derives *oikeudenmukaisuus* (justice, fairness). Distinct from *oikea* in emphasizing procedural and distributive justice rather than simple correctness.
- **[CORE]**

---

**39. asianmukainen**
- **Form:** asianmukainen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** proper, appropriate (from *asian* — genitive of *asia* "matter, thing" + *mukainen* — "in accordance with")
- **Typical English translations:** proper, appropriate, adequate, correct
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether something is done or constituted in the manner appropriate to the relevant matter or standard (*asianmukainen menettely* — "proper procedure"; *asianmukainen dokumentaatio* — "proper documentation"). Used heavily in administrative, legal, and professional contexts. Emphasizes procedural and normative correctness.
- **[CORE]**

**40. perusteeton**
- **Form:** perusteeton
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** unfounded, groundless (from *peruste* — "ground, basis, reason" + *-ton* privative suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** unfounded, groundless, unsubstantiated, baseless
- **Usage:** Evaluates claims, accusations, or decisions as lacking adequate grounds or justification (*perusteeton syytös* — "an unfounded accusation"; *perusteeton väite* — "an unsubstantiated claim"). Used in legal, academic, and argumentative contexts. Contrasts with *perusteltu* (justified, well-founded).
- **[CORE]**

---

**41. perusteltu**
- **Form:** perusteltu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** justified, well-founded (past passive participle of *perustella* — "to justify, to give reasons for")
- **Typical English translations:** justified, well-founded, substantiated, reasoned
- **Usage:** Evaluates claims, decisions, or beliefs as supported by adequate grounds or reasoning (*perusteltu päätös* — "a justified decision"; *perusteltu epäilys* — "a well-founded suspicion"). Used in legal, academic, and deliberative contexts. Contrasts with *perusteeton* (unfounded). Derives from *peruste* (ground, basis).
- **[CORE]**

---

**42. epäaito**
- **Form:** epäaito
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** inauthentic, not genuine (negation prefix *epä-* + *aito*)
- **Typical English translations:** inauthentic, fake, spurious, not genuine
- **Usage:** The direct negation of *aito*, used to assert that something presented as genuine is not. Applied to objects, emotions, and conduct (*epäaito tunne* — "an inauthentic feeling"; *epäaito tuote* — "a non-genuine product"). Less common than *aito* itself but attested in both everyday and philosophical discourse. Less common than *väärä* or *väärennös* in legal contexts but used in aesthetic and philosophical evaluation. Derives *epäaitous* (inauthenticity).
- **[CORE]**

---

**43. totuudenmukaisuus**
- **Form:** totuudenmukaisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** truthfulness, conformity to truth (from *totuuden* — genitive of *totuus* + *mukainen* — "conforming to" + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** truthfulness, veracity, accuracy, conformity to truth
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether a statement, account, or representation conforms to the truth or to facts. Used in legal contexts (e.g., evaluating witness statements) and in journalistic and academic contexts. More specific than *totuus* in emphasizing conformity rather than the property of truth itself.
- **[CORE]**

---

**44. jäljitelmä**
- **Form:** jäljitelmä
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** imitation, copy, replica (from *jäljitellä*, to imitate, + *-mä*, nominal suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, copy, fake, counterfeit
- **Usage:** Denotes an object or representation made to resemble another, often with an implication that it lacks the value or status of the original. Does not necessarily imply fraudulent intent. Used in aesthetic, commercial, and legal contexts. Contrasts with *alkuperäinen* and *aito*.
- **[CORE]**

---

**45. kopio**
- **Form:** kopio
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** copy (l
