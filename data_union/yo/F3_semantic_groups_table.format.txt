| Expressions used in asserting or denying that a statement is true | These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker is affirming or rejecting the factual accuracy of a claim, account, or statement, or making, supporting, or challenging a claim about whether something said or reported corresponds to what actually happened or is the case. They are contrasted with each other directly in usage (e.g., *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* vs. *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*) and appear in the same syntactic frames with *sọ* (to say/speak), *ni* (focus/copula), and *kò sí...nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* (there is no...in his/her words). They are used in legal testimony, everyday dispute, and moral instruction. The core contrast is between *sọ òtítọ́* (speak the truth) and *sọ irọ́* (tell a lie), which function as a matched pair across moral, legal, and everyday discourse. | *òtítọ́*; *irọ́*; *sọ òtítọ́*; *sọ irọ́*; *pa irọ́*; *má sọ irọ́*; *òtítọ́ ni*; *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ*; *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*; *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*; *Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*; *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀*; *ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́*; *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́*; *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́*; *irọ́ pátápátá*; *òtítọ́ pátápátá*; *irọ́ tótóbi*; *irọ́ funfun*; *olòtítọ́*; *olóirọ́*; *Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni*; *A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni*; *Òtítọ́ kò ní kú*; *dájúdájú*; *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́*; *ìwádìí òtítọ́* | *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* and *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* shade toward Group 2; *irọ́ funfun* is marginal within this group in that it introduces a gradation not present in the core contrast; *dájúdájú* functions specifically as an intensifier within this group — it does not name truth or falsehood but marks the speaker's certainty about a claim; *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* and *ìwádìí òtítọ́* appear at the procedural end of truth-assertion contexts rather than in simple first-person assertion | *òtítọ́* and *irọ́* are the most consistently paired terms in the entire dataset. They appear in parallel constructions (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), in parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú* / *irọ́ kò ní dúró*), and in the compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness), which is formed by negating the truth-function of the witness role. *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* and *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* also belong here as they evaluate utterances for accuracy, though they shade toward Group 2 as well. *irọ́ funfun* (white lie) is marginal within this group in that it introduces a gradation not present in the core contrast. *dájúdájú* functions specifically as an intensifier within this group — it does not name truth or falsehood but marks the speaker's certainty about a claim. *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* and *ìwádìí òtítọ́* are compound expressions that presuppose the existence of truth as something that can be admitted or sought; they appear at the procedural end of truth-assertion contexts rather than in simple first-person assertion. |
| Expressions used to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard of correctness or propriety | These expressions appear in contexts where an action, measurement, answer, procedure, or statement is assessed against a criterion of correctness or accuracy, or where the question is not primarily whether a statement is true, but whether an action, decision, procedure, object, or person meets a recognized standard of what is right, proper, or correct. They are not restricted to speech acts and apply broadly to objects, measurements, conduct, and decisions. The predicative construction *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* (it is correct / it is not correct) is the most basic instantiation of this group, and the root *tọ́* is the morphological core. The evaluative predicate *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* is the anchor of this group, and it appears across factual, moral, procedural, and social domains. | *tọ́*; *òtọ́*; *ó tọ́*; *kò tọ́*; *ó tọ́ pé*; *kò tọ́ pé*; *ìtọ́*; *àìtọ́*; *ìtọ́sí*; *tọ́ sí*; *àtúntọ́* / *ìtúntọ́*; *ìtọ́ni*; *ọ̀nà tọ́*; *ìdáhùn tọ́*; *ìpinnu tọ́*; *àkọsílẹ̀ tọ́*; *ìsọ tọ́*; *ìwà tọ́*; *ìwọ̀n tọ́*; *ìlànà tọ́*; *ìgbésẹ̀ tọ́*; *àṣìṣe*; *Ó tọ́ pé kí a sọ òtítọ́*; *Kò tọ́ pé kí o sọ irọ́*; *Ó tọ́ gẹ́gẹ́ bí ó ti yẹ*; *Ẹ tọ́ ọ sí ọ̀nà tó tọ́* | *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* and *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* shade toward Group 1; *ìwà tọ́* overlaps with Group 4; *àṣìṣe* also appears in Group 4 | This group is the largest and most diffuse. The root *tọ́* appears in factual contexts (Group 1), moral contexts (Group 4), legal contexts (Group 3), and procedural/administrative contexts. The expressions here are those where the evaluative function of *tọ́* is most clearly foregrounded without being reducible to any single domain. *ó tọ́* functions as a discourse marker of affirmation across all these contexts. *ìwà tọ́* overlaps with Group 4 (the character evaluation domain), but in usage it appears specifically in moral evaluation of persons rather than in procedural or factual correctness contexts. |
| Expressions used in legal and formal proceedings to establish what happened | These expressions appear specifically in contexts where the truth of a past event or claim is being formally established through testimony, evidence, questioning, or judgment, or where a party is required to produce, evaluate, or formally accept evidence about events or facts. They are associated with the roles of witness, judge, and party in a proceeding, and they participate in constructions about producing, evaluating, accepting, or rejecting proof. They cluster around the root *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, witness, related to *rí*, to see) and involve distinctions between types of evidence (direct, circumstantial, documentary, hearsay, expert) and between valid and invalid evidence. This group is defined by its restriction to adversarial or evaluative proceedings rather than by any single semantic feature. | *ẹ̀rí*; *ẹ̀rí ojú*; *ẹ̀rí etí*; *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́*; *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn*; *ẹ̀rí tààrà*; *ẹ̀rí àyíká*; *ẹ̀rí ìwé*; *ẹ̀rí àkọsílẹ̀*; *ẹ̀rí àfihàn*; *ẹ̀rí ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀*; *ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀*; *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò*; *ẹ̀rí tó péye*; *ẹ̀rí tó gbára*; *àìní ẹ̀rí*; *ẹlẹ́rìí*; *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú*; *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́*; *ìjẹ́rìí*; *ìjẹ́rìísí*; *jẹ́rìí*; *jẹ́rìísí*; *jẹ́rìí pé*; *jẹ́rìí fún*; *jẹ́rìí sí*; *olùjẹ́rìí*; *mú ẹ̀rí wá*; *ẹ̀rí rẹ kò tó*; *ẹ̀rí kò sí*; *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi*; *Ẹ̀rí tó wà ń tọ́ka sí i*; *Ó gba ẹ̀rí jọ*; *Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀*; *Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ kò tó ohunkóhun*; *Ẹ̀rí tó gbéṣẹ̀ ni a nílò*; *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó lòdì sí i*; *Ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí*; *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu*; *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò*; *fọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu ẹni*; *ìdánilójú*; *ìdájọ́*; *dájọ́ lórí*; *adájọ́*; *ilé ìdájọ́*; *ìfọwọ́sí*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbéyàwó*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkẹ́kọ̀ọ́*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìwà*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkọ́lé*; *Ó gba ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ wò*; *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo*; *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́*; *Kò ní ìjẹ́rìísí tó yẹ*; *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbàgbọ́* | *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* combines a term from this group (*ẹlẹ́rìí*) with a term from Group 1 (*irọ́*); the contrast *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* vs. *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* shows that Group 1 vocabulary is applied to evaluate items in this sub-context; *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* also appears in Group 10 | This group is the most internally differentiated in the dataset. Within it, there is a sub-pattern around the hierarchy of evidence types: *ẹ̀rí ojú* (eyewitness) is consistently marked as more reliable than *ẹ̀rí etí* / *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́* (hearsay), and *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* is the most credible witness category. The compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness) is notable because it combines a term from this group (*ẹlẹ́rìí*, witness) with a term from Group 1 (*irọ́*, lie), marking the violation of the witness role's truth-function. *ẹ̀rí* and *ìjẹ́rìísí* overlap considerably in this group but are distinguishable: *ẹ̀rí* refers to any evidence or sign (including physical traces), while *ìjẹ́rìísí* refers specifically to personal attestation or formal certification. *ìdánwò* and *ìdájọ́* represent sequential stages in a formal process — testing precedes judgment — and both appear in the same institutional contexts. *ìfọwọ́sí* marks the endpoint of formal confirmation and is more administrative than evidential. The certification compounds (*ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí*, etc.) appear in administrative and credentialing contexts where the question is whether a document or credential is valid; the contrast *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* vs. *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* shows that Group 1 vocabulary (truth/falsehood) is applied to evaluate items in this sub-context. *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* also appears in Group 10 because its morphological description frames it as examining what a person says in response to questioning — the act of eliciting and evaluating received speech. |
| Expressions used to evaluate the fundamental moral character of a person | These expressions appear in contexts where the object of evaluation is a person's fundamental nature or habitual conduct rather than a specific statement, action, or object. They are used to assess whether a person is, at a deep level, good or bad, righteous or corrupt. The central concept is *ìwà* (character, the manner of being), and the primary contrast is between *ìwà rere* (good character) and *ìwà burúkú* / *ìwà búburú* (bad character). These expressions appear in proverbs, moral instruction, and everyday moral evaluation, and they assess persons rather than propositions or actions in isolation. | *ìwà*; *ìwà rere*; *ìwà burúkú*; *ìwà búburú*; *ìwà tọ́*; *ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́*; *ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin*; *ìwà ìbàjẹ́*; *ìwà àìtọ́*; *ìwà àgàbàgebè*; *ìwà ọmọlúwàbí*; *ọmọlúwàbí*; *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn*; *Ìwà rere ló ń gbé ènìyàn ga*; *Ìwà rere ju ọrọ̀ lọ*; *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́*; *ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn*; *ìwà rere ju ẹwà lọ*; *yẹra fún ìwà burúkú*; *ní ìwà rere*; *Ó ní ìwà rere*; *Kò ní ìwà rere*; *Ìwà rẹ̀ dára*; *Ìwà rẹ̀ kò dára*; *Ìwà rẹ̀ hàn gbangba*; *Ó ń ṣe àgàbàgebè pẹ̀lú ìwà rẹ̀*; *Ìwà búburú rẹ̀ ló pa á*; *òdodo*; *ṣe òdodo*; *alòdodo*; *àìdodo*; *olòtítọ́*; *aláìrọ́*; *aṣírọ́*; *àṣìṣe*; *ṣe àṣìṣe*; *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe*; *darí àṣìṣe jì*; *àṣìṣe kò ní ṣẹlẹ̀ mọ́*; *àṣìṣe ńlá*; *aṣìṣe*; *àbùkù*; *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *ṣe ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì*; *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀*; *wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *alẹ̀ṣẹ̀*; *àìṣẹ̀* | *ìwà tọ́* overlaps with Group 2; *òdodo* also appears in Group 3 in the compound *ìdájọ́ òdodo*; *olòtítọ́*, *aláìrọ́*, and *aṣírọ́* have root terms (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*) that belong primarily to Group 1; *àṣìṣe* also appears in Group 2; the construction *ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* links Group 1 to this group; *àṣìṣe ìmọ̀* ("error of knowledge") places *àṣìṣe* in the epistemic domain aligning with Group 1 | This group is defined by the evaluation of persons rather than propositions, objects, or events. The proverb *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (character is what makes a person) is the clearest statement of the group's central concern. *ìwà* is the organizing term of this group — nearly all other expressions in it are either compounds of *ìwà* or terms that appear in the same evaluative contexts. *òdodo* belongs here because it evaluates persons and conduct as righteous or just, though it also appears in legal contexts (Group 3) in the compound *ìdájọ́ òdodo*. The agentive forms *olòtítọ́*, *aláìrọ́*, and *aṣírọ́* belong here because they evaluate persons by their habitual truth-telling or lying behavior, even though the root terms (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*) belong primarily to Group 1. The parallel constructions *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* / *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* and *darí àṣìṣe jì* / *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì* show that *àṣìṣe* and *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* participate in the same constructional frames. The data consistently show that *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* is more strongly associated with religious discourse and moral culpability, while *àṣìṣe* can describe factual errors without implying moral guilt. The construction *ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (whoever tells a lie will bear guilt) links Group 1 to this group by treating lying as a sin. *àṣìṣe* is also used in epistemic contexts (incorrect knowledge, factual error) that align with Group 1; the construction *àṣìṣe ìmọ̀* ("error of knowledge") explicitly places it in the epistemic domain. *ìwà àìtọ́* explicitly combines the character domain (*ìwà*) with the correctness domain (*tọ*), suggesting that in Yoruba usage these two domains are not fully separate. *àbùkù* is placed here because its usage centers on a person or thing falling short of what it should be. |
