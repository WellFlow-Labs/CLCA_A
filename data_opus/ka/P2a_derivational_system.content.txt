# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational Morphology
## Language: Georgian (ka)
## Transliteration: ISO 9984 (1996), consistent with P1/P1b

This document records **attested derivational morphology** built on the P1 base items. Only derivations that are genuinely used in Georgian are included. Inflectional adverbs, fixed collocations, and semantically shifted compounds documented in P1b are noted as such where relevant, but the focus here is on derivational morphology proper. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved.

## P1 #1 — ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti) "true, genuine"

**Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
- **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmarit- + -eba (nominalizing suffix)
- **English:** truth, verity
- **Usage:** Abstract noun for truth in philosophical, moral, and religious registers. (Also listed independently as P1 #2.)
- **Status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form:** ჭეშმარიტად
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmaritad
- **Literal Breakdown:** č̣ešmarit- + -ad (adverbial)
- **English:** truly, verily
- **Usage:** Elevated/liturgical adverb ("verily"), common in religious and rhetorical discourse.
- **Status:** [CORE]

## P1 #4 — მართალი (martali) "right, true, righteous"

**Derived Form:** მართლა
- **Transliteration:** martla
- **Literal Breakdown:** martl- + -a (adverbial)
- **English:** really, truly, indeed
- **Usage:** Extremely common everyday confirmatory/interrogative adverb ("really?").
- **Status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form:** მართლაც
- **Transliteration:** martlac
- **Literal Breakdown:** martl- + -a + -c (additive clitic)
- **English:** indeed, in fact, sure enough
- **Usage:** Confirms that something turned out true/as expected; everyday and written.
- **Status:** [CORE]

> Note: Many further martl-/mart- forms exist (e.g., samartali, samartliani), but these are built on the root rather than on the adjective martali specifically; they are documented under root expansion in P1b.

## Summary observations (supported by the data above)

- **Two derivational patterns recur across many items:** the abstract-noun suffixes **-eba/-oba** (e.g., sandooba, sizusṭe, gulc̣rpeloba, p̣aṭiosneba) and the adverbial suffix **-ad** (e.g., zusṭad, namdvilad, gulc̣rpelad, ǰerovnad). The adverbial -ad derivation is the most broadly available across adjectival bases.
- **The privative/negative prefix ara-** is attested for several trust/honesty adjectives (arasando, arasaimedo, arap̣aṭiosani) but is **not uniformly available** across the field.
- **Several P1 items are themselves derivatives** (č̣ešmariteba, simartle, namdviloba, sicrue, msgavseba, igiveoba, arseba, martebuloba) and show little or no further derivation; these are marked "(no common derivation identified)."
- **Asymmetries preserved:** namdv- is low-derivation (per P1b); uṭq̇uari, igiveoba, and martebuloba show no productive onward derivation; naɣdi and ǰerovani are register-restricted with minimal derivation.
- Verbal/compound forms built on the broader roots (e.g., the extensive martl- family, moṭq̇ueba, gaq̇albeba) are included only where they attach to a specific P1 base; the fuller root inventories remain in P1b and are not duplicated or completed here.
