## Preliminary Note on Method

Each opposition below is extracted directly from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is posited unless it is explicitly supported by the data in those steps — through direct contrast statements, antonym citations, derivational negation pairs, or documented constructional alternations. Where the data supports asymmetry, asymmetry is preserved. Where the data does not support a clean opposition, this is noted.

**English translation:** true / false
**Description of contrast:** P1 establishes these as the primary positive and negative poles of propositional truth-evaluation in English; P3 Group 1 treats them as the most general and syntactically flexible members of the statement-evaluation group. ## Opposition 23

**Term A:** valid (#7)
**Term B:** sound (#8)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** valid / sound
**Description of contrast:** P1 and P3 Group 2 document these as technically distinct in formal logic: *valid* predicates that a conclusion follows from premises regardless of whether the premises are true; *sound* predicates that an argument is both valid and has true premises. *Sound* is thus a stronger condition than *valid* in the logical sense. P3 notes that both are used with looser meanings in general registers.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 40

**Term A:** to be honest (P2b, Item 18.1) — discourse marker
**Term B:** predicative use: *he is honest* — attributive/predicative construction
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to be honest [discourse marker] / he is honest [predication]
