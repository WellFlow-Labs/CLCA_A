# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: English (en)
# Source: P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, P4b, P5

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

This step documents how English encodes dynamic truth-related meanings: change of state (becoming true/false/valid/etc.), causation of truth-related states, and stability or persistence of such states. All vocabulary is drawn from the CONTEXT (P1–P5 and P1b). Auxiliary verbs, aspect markers, copulas, and function words are added as needed for grammatical well-formedness.

The analysis treats each truth-domain term separately where behavior differs. Where no common expression exists for a given term or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Patterns are reported from attested data in the CONTEXT; no constructions are invented beyond what the CONTEXT supports.

Three main subsection types are addressed:
1. **Becoming** — change into a truth-related state
2. **Causing** — bringing about a truth-related state in something else
3. **Stability / Persistence** — remaining in or losing a truth-related state

A fourth subsection addresses **dynamic expressions not fitting the above types** that are attested in the CONTEXT.

## 1. BECOMING — Change into a Truth-Related State

### 1.1 General Pattern

English has no dedicated morphological inchoative for the truth domain. Change of state is expressed periphrastically. The CONTEXT (P4a §4) identifies four main frames:

- **PROVE + adjective** (evidential inchoative: retrospective confirmation)
- **TURN OUT TO BE + adjective** (discovery inchoative: often implies surprise)
- **BECOME + adjective** (general inchoative: grammatical but marginal for most items)
- **COME TRUE** (fixed idiom: restricted to wishes, predictions, fears)

These frames are not equally available across all truth-domain adjectives. Availability is documented item by item below.

The only fully lexicalized inchoative in the domain is *come true*, which is restricted to *true* with subjects denoting wishes, predictions, or fears. All other items rely on periphrastic constructions (*prove*, *turn out to be*, *become*). This gap is noted explicitly in P4a (Construction 4) and P5 (Opposition 32). The asymmetry between *come true* (lexicalized) and *\*come false* (not attested) is a structural feature of the domain.

### 1.3 Items for Which No Inchoative Construction Is Attested

The following items from P1 have no attested inchoative construction in the CONTEXT: *veridical*, *bona fide* (adjective), *ersatz*, *pseudo-*, *specious*. For *veridical* and *veracious*, this reflects their restricted register (philosophical and literary respectively) and the absence of dedicated verbal forms. For *bona fide* and *ersatz*, this reflects their status as fixed expressions or loanwords with limited morphological integration. For *pseudo-*, this reflects its status as a bound prefix. For *specious*, no inchoative construction is documented in the CONTEXT.

## 2. CAUSING — Bringing About a Truth-Related State

### 2.1 General Pattern

As documented in P4a Section 5, English has no productive morphological causative for this domain. Causative meaning is expressed through dedicated causative verbs derived from the domain (*verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*, *falsify*, *legitimize*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*, *canonize*, *correct*) or periphrastically through *make* + NP + ADJ. The dedicated verbs are the primary strategy; *make* + NP + ADJ is grammatically available but less domain-specific.

The P1–P5 data shows a structural asymmetry in the causative domain. Dedicated causative verbs exist for both directions of change:

- **Toward truth/genuineness/validity:** *verify*, *validate*, *authenticate*, *legitimize*, *correct*
- **Away from truth/genuineness/validity:** *falsify*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*, *fabricate*, *counterfeit*

However, the negative-direction verbs (*falsify*, *fabricate*, *counterfeit*) are more strongly associated with deliberate agency and intent than the positive-direction verbs (*verify*, *validate*), which can describe both agentive acts and impersonal processes (*the evidence verified the claim* vs. *the evidence falsified the claim* — both grammatical, but *falsify* more strongly implies human agency in the P1–P5 data).
