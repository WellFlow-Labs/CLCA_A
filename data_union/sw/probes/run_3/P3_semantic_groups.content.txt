# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1 and P2 outputs. Where items appear together in fixed constructions, are explicitly contrasted, or recur in the same discourse contexts, this is treated as evidence for grouping. No external semantic framework has been applied. Items that do not show clear grouping patterns are listed separately at the end.

---

## GROUP 1: Expressions used in asserting or denying that a statement is true

**Label:** Propositional truth assertion and denial

**Definition:** These expressions are used to affirm or reject the truth-value of a specific claim or statement. They appear characteristically in response to claims, in argument, and in contexts where a speaker is committing to or challenging the factual status of a proposition. They are frequently paired or contrasted with each other.

**Representative expressions:**

- *ni kweli* — "it is true"
- *si kweli* — "it is not true"
- *kweli kabisa* — "absolutely true"
- *ni kweli kabisa kwamba...* — "it is absolutely true that..."
- *kweli au uongo* — "true or false"
- *Je, ni kweli?* — "Is it true? / Really?"
- *ukweli ni kwamba...* — "the truth is that..."
- *ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...* — "the truth of the matter is that..."
- *ni sahihi* — "it is correct"
- *si sahihi* — "it is not correct / wrong"
- *sahihi kabisa* — "absolutely correct"
- *haki kabisa!* — "absolutely right!"
- *kweli kweli* — "really, truly"
- *kwa kweli* — "truly, in truth"
- *kwa kweli kweli* — "really and truly"

**Notes:** The pairs *ni kweli / si kweli* and *ni sahihi / si sahihi* function as direct opposites within this group. *Haki kabisa* is used as an affirmative response particle in spoken Swahili and overlaps with Group 3 (rights/justice expressions), but its use as a truth-affirmation response is well attested. *Kweli kweli* and *kwa kweli* appear both as propositional affirmations and as discourse markers of sincerity (see Group 2).

---

## GROUP 2: Expressions used as sincerity or honesty markers in speech

**Label:** Sincerity framing in discourse

**Definition:** These expressions are used at the beginning of utterances, or parenthetically, to signal that the speaker is being honest or genuine — not to assert the truth of a specific proposition, but to frame the entire speech act as sincere. They function as discourse markers rather than as predicates.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kwa kweli* — "honestly, truly" (sentence-initial)
- *kwa kweli, sijui* — "honestly, I don't know"
- *kusema ukweli* — "to tell the truth" (parenthetical frame)
- *kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo* — "to tell the truth, I didn't like that"
- *kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu* — "truly, this is amazing"
- *kwa kweli kweli* — "really and truly" (emphatic sincerity marker)

**Notes:** Several of these expressions also appear in Group 1 (*kwa kweli*, *kweli kweli*). The distinction observed in usage is that in Group 1 they modify a specific propositional claim, while here they frame the speaker's overall stance. *Kusema ukweli* as a parenthetical is particularly characteristic of this group and does not appear in Group 1 usage.

---

## GROUP 3: Expressions used in evaluating whether objects, documents, or persons are genuine rather than counterfeit or imitative

**Label:** Authenticity and genuineness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, person, or cultural product is what it purports to be — as opposed to being fake, counterfeit, or imitative. They appear characteristically in commercial, legal, and consumer contexts, and are frequently contrasted with *bandia* (fake/counterfeit).

**Representative expressions:**

- *halisi* — "genuine, authentic"
- *dhahabu halisi* — "genuine gold"
- *bidhaa halisi* — "genuine goods"
- *nakala halisi* — "authentic copy"
- *picha halisi* — "genuine photograph"
- *mtu halisi* — "a genuine person"
- *hisia halisi* — "genuine feelings"
- *halisi na ya kweli* — "genuine and true"
- *halisi kwa asilimia mia* — "one hundred percent genuine"
- *hii ni halisi, si bandia* — "this is genuine, not fake"
- *halisi au bandia — jinsi ya kutofautisha* — "genuine or fake — how to tell the difference"
- *kuthibitisha kuwa halisi* — "to verify that (something) is genuine"
- *bandia* — "fake, counterfeit"
- *pesa bandia* — "counterfeit money"
- *bidhaa bandia* — "counterfeit goods"
- *dawa bandia zinaweza kuua* — "counterfeit medicines can kill"
- *hii ni bandia — usiinunue* — "this is fake — don't buy it"
- *jinsi ya kutambua bidhaa bandia* — "how to identify counterfeit goods"

**Notes:** *Halisi* and *bandia* are the core contrasting pair in this group. *Mtu halisi* and *hisia halisi* extend the authenticity evaluation to persons and emotional states, which overlaps with Group 7 (character evaluation). *Nakala halisi* and *picha halisi* overlap with Group 5 (document and verification contexts).

---

## GROUP 4: Expressions used in evaluating accuracy, correctness, or conformity to a standard

**Label:** Accuracy and correctness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something — a statement, calculation, document, pronunciation, translation, or procedure — conforms to a standard of correctness or accuracy. They appear characteristically in educational, administrative, scientific, and technical contexts, and are frequently applied to specific domains by the pattern *X sahihi* or *usahihi wa X*.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahihi* — "correct, accurate"
- *usahihi* — "accuracy, correctness"
- *jibu sahihi* — "correct answer"
- *taarifa sahihi* — "accurate report"
- *takwimu sahihi* — "accurate statistics"
- *tarehe sahihi* — "correct date"
- *matamshi sahihi* — "correct pronunciation"
- *sarufi sahihi* — "correct grammar"
- *tafsiri sahihi* — "accurate translation"
- *hesabu sahihi* — "correct calculation"
- *maelezo sahihi* — "accurate description"
- *lugha sahihi* — "correct language"
- *utambuzi sahihi* — "correct identification"
- *usahihi wa taarifa* — "accuracy of a report"
- *usahihi wa kipimo* — "accuracy of measurement"
- *kwa usahihi* — "accurately, precisely"
- *kwa usahihi zaidi* — "more precisely, to be more exact"
- *kwa usahihi zaidi, idadi ni elfu tatu, si mbili* — precision-correction frame
- *sahihi na kamili* — "accurate and complete"
- *thibitisha usahihi* — "verify the accuracy"
- *kuhakikisha usahihi* — "to ensure accuracy"
- *jibu lako si sahihi — jaribu tena* — "your answer is not correct — try again"
- *taarifa hii imethibitishwa kuwa sahihi* — "this report has been verified to be accurate"
- *hakikisha kuwa taarifa ni sahihi kabla ya kuisambaza* — accuracy-before-sharing frame
- *sahihisha makosa* — "correct the errors"
- *kusahihisha* — "to correct, to proofread"

**Notes:** This group is the most internally differentiated, with *sahihi* applying across a very wide range of domains. The pattern *kwa usahihi zaidi* as a precision-correction frame is particularly characteristic. *Sahihi* in its "signature" meaning (see Group 5) is a distinct usage that does not belong here.

---

## GROUP 5: Expressions used in formal verification, authentication, and document contexts

**Label:** Verification and document authentication

**Definition:** These expressions are used in administrative, legal, and institutional contexts where the formal confirmation or authentication of documents, identities, or facts is at issue. They appear characteristically in bureaucratic procedures, legal proceedings, and official communications.

**Representative expressions:**

- *thibitisha* — "to confirm, to verify, to authenticate"
- *uthibitisho* — "confirmation, verification, proof"
- *kuthibitisha* — "to confirm, to verify"
- *kuthibitishwa* — "to be confirmed, to be authenticated"
- *uthibitisho wa hati* — "authentication of a document"
- *thibitisha utambulisho wako* — "verify your identity"
- *hati sahihi* — "valid document"
- *nakala halisi* — "authentic/certified copy"
- *sahihi* (signature meaning) — "signature"
- *sahihi ya mkono* — "handwritten signature"
- *weka sahihi* — "sign (a document)"
- *weka sahihi hapa* — "sign here"
- *sahihi ya kidijitali* — "digital signature"
- *sahihi yake haikubaliani na ile ya pasipoti* — signature mismatch construction
- *kuhakikisha sahihi* — "to verify the signature"
- *taarifa hii imethibitishwa kuwa sahihi* — "this report has been verified to be accurate"
- *kuthibitisha kuwa halisi* — "to verify that (something) is genuine"

**Notes:** This group overlaps substantially with Group 4 (*sahihi* as "correct") and Group 3 (*halisi* as "genuine"). The "signature" meaning of *sahihi* is specific to this group and does not appear in Group 4. *Nakala halisi* appears in both Group 3 and here. *Uthibitisho* is the most characteristic item of this group.

---

## GROUP 6: Expressions used in characterizing acts or patterns of deception

**Label:** Deception and dishonesty characterization

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize acts, persons, or patterns of conduct as involving deliberate deception, misleading, or fraud. They appear in moral condemnation, legal proceedings, and narrative contexts where the intentional production of false impressions is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uongo* — "lie, falsehood"
- *uongo mtupu* — "pure falsehood, outright lie"
- *kudanganya* — "to deceive, to mislead"
- *udanganyifu* — "deception, deceitfulness"
- *mdanganyifu* — "a deceiver"
- *wadanganyifu* — "deceivers"
- *kudanganyika* — "to be deceived, to fall for it"
- *kudanganywa* — "to be deceived"
- *ulaghai* — "fraud, swindling"
- *mlaghai* — "a fraudster"
- *kulaghai* — "to defraud, to swindle"
- *kesi ya ulaghai* — "a fraud case"
- *alishtakiwa kwa ulaghai* — "he/she was charged with fraud"
- *alikudanganya kwa makusudi* — "he/she deceived you deliberately"
- *kudanganya kwa maneno na matendo* — "to deceive by words and deeds"
- *alidanganywa na macho yake mwenyewe* — "he/she was deceived by his/her own eyes"
- *udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani* — "his/her deception was exposed publicly"
- *uongo wake ulidhihirika* — "his/her lie was exposed"
- *kueneza uongo* — "to spread lies"
- *amejaa uongo* — "he/she is full of lies"
- *kusema uongo ni dhambi* — "telling lies is a sin"
- *kujikinga na udanganyifu* — "to protect oneself from deception"

**Notes:** *Ulaghai* is more specific than *udanganyifu*, carrying connotations of material fraud and appearing more in legal contexts. *Uongo* is the broadest term and appears across all registers. The distinction between *uongo* (falsehood as a state or product) and *kudanganya* (the act of deceiving) is consistently maintained in usage. *Mwongo* (liar) belongs here but was documented in P2a rather than P1; it is included as it is a direct derivation of *uongo*.

---

## GROUP 7: Expressions used in evaluating a person's character as honest, trustworthy, or their opposite

**Label:** Character evaluation for honesty and trustworthiness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons — rather than statements or objects — as honest, trustworthy, faithful, or their opposites. They appear in moral discourse, testimonials, religious contexts, and interpersonal evaluation, and typically apply to the person's habitual conduct or essential character rather than a single act.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uaminifu* — "faithfulness, trustworthiness"
- *amini* — "to trust; trustworthy"
- *mwaminifu* — "a faithful/trustworthy person"
- *waaminifu* — "faithful people"
- *kuamini* — "to believe, to trust"
- *kuaminika* — "to be trustworthy, to be credible"
- *kuaminiwa* — "to be trusted"
- *imani* — "faith, belief, trust"
- *mtu wa kweli* — "a truthful/genuine person"
- *mtu halisi* — "a genuine person"
- *mtu sahihi* — "the right/correct person"
- *uaminifu ni msingi wa uhusiano wowote* — "faithfulness is the foundation of any relationship"
- *kuonyesha uaminifu katika kazi* — "to demonstrate faithfulness in work"
- *mtu asiye na uaminifu hawezi kuaminiwa* — "a person without faithfulness cannot be trusted"
- *siwezi kumwamini tena* — "I can no longer trust him/her"
- *mtu wa kweli hasemi uongo* — "a truthful person does not tell lies"
- *mwongo* — "liar" (from P2a)
- *mdanganyifu* — "a deceiver"

**Notes:** *Mtu wa kweli*, *mtu halisi*, and *mtu sahihi* all evaluate persons but with different emphases: *mtu wa kweli* emphasizes honesty in speech and conduct; *mtu halisi* emphasizes authenticity of character; *mtu sahihi* emphasizes being the correct or appropriate person (and overlaps with Group 4). *Imani* is included here because of its close semantic and etymological relationship to *amini* and its use in trust/belief contexts, though it has a stronger religious dimension. *Mdanganyifu* and *mwongo* appear in both this group and Group 6; the distinction is that in Group 6 they describe acts, while here they describe persons.

---

## GROUP 8: Expressions used in proverbial or moral assertions about truth and falsehood

**Label:** Proverbial and moral assertions about truth and falsehood

**Definition:** These expressions occur as fixed or semi-fixed statements that assert general moral or epistemic principles about truth, falsehood, and their consequences. They are used in moral instruction, sermons, and everyday discourse to invoke shared values rather than to evaluate a specific claim or person.

**Representative expressions:**

- *ukweli unauma* — "the truth hurts"
- *ukweli unauma, lakini ni dawa* — "the truth hurts, but it is medicine"
- *kweli inaumiza* — "truth is painful"
- *kweli ni mchungu* — "truth is bitter"
- *kweli haiozi* — "truth does not rot"
- *kweli hushinda uongo* — "truth defeats falsehood"
- *ukweli daima hushinda* — "truth always prevails"
- *ukweli haina budi kujulikana* — "truth must inevitably become known"
- *ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele* — "a hidden truth cannot be hidden forever"
- *ukweli unaweza kufichwa kwa muda, lakini si milele* — "truth can be hidden for a time, but not forever"
- *uongo una miguu mifupi* — "a lie has short legs"
- *uongo ni adui wa amani* — "falsehood is an enemy of peace"
- *panda uongo, vuna aibu* — "plant lies, harvest shame"
- *kusema uongo ni dhambi* — "telling lies is a sin"
- *sema kweli, hata kama inakudhuru* — "tell the truth, even if it harms you"
- *hakuna kitu kisichokuwa na kasoro* — "nothing is without a flaw"
- *bora kidogo chenye ubora kuliko kingi kisichofaa* — "better a little of quality than much that is worthless"
- *haki ni haki, bila kujali mtu ni nani* — "right is right, regardless of who the person is"
- *kukiri makosa ni nguvu, si udhaifu* — "admitting mistakes is strength, not weakness"

**Notes:** This group is defined by discourse function (proverbial/moral assertion) rather than by a single lexical item. Items from several other groups appear here in their proverbial uses. The habitual marker *hu-* (*hushinda*, *haiozi*) is a grammatical feature characteristic of proverbial constructions in Swahili and is observed across multiple items in this group.

---

## GROUP 9: Expressions used in describing the concealment, revelation, or pursuit of truth

**Label:** Truth as something hidden, revealed, or sought

**Definition:** These expressions treat truth as an entity that can be hidden, suppressed, discovered, or actively pursued. They appear in investigative journalism, legal discourse, political commentary, and narrative contexts where the accessibility or inaccessibility of truth is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**

- *ukweli uliofichwa* — "hidden truth"
- *ukweli uliofunuliwa* — "revealed truth"
- *tafuta ukweli* — "seek the truth"
- *ukweli unajulikana* — "the truth is known / comes out"
- *ukweli ulidhihirika baada ya uchunguzi* — "the truth became evident after the investigation"
- *kudhihirisha ukweli mbele ya wote* — "to reveal the truth publicly"
- *udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani* — "his/her deception was exposed publicly"
- *uongo wake ulidhihirika* — "his/her lie was exposed"
- *ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele* — "a hidden truth cannot be hidden forever"
- *ukweli unaweza kufichwa kwa muda, lakini si milele* — "truth can be hidden for a time, but not forever"
- *kukabili uhalisi* — "to face reality"
- *mbali na uhalisi* — "far from reality, out of touch with reality"

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 8 (proverbial assertions) for items like *ukweli uliofichwa haufichiki milele*. The distinction is that Group 8 items function primarily as moral maxims, while Group 9 items appear in investigative and narrative contexts where the concealment or discovery of specific facts is described. *Kukabili uhalisi* and *mbali na uhalisi* are included here because they describe engagement with or avoidance of actual conditions, though they could also be placed in Group 10.

---

## GROUP 10: Expressions using *uhalisi* in reference to actual conditions or constructed environments

**Label:** Reference to actual or constructed reality

**Definition:** These expressions use *uhalisi* (and related forms) to refer to actual conditions of a situation, as opposed to how things are represented, imagined, or idealized. A subset of these expressions applies *uhalisi* to technologically constructed environments, where the term names a simulated or augmented perceptual experience.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uhalisi* — "reality, authenticity"
- *uhalisi wa hali* — "the reality of the situation"
- *uhalisi wa maisha* — "the reality of life"
- *uhalisi mgumu* — "harsh reality"
- *kukabili uhalisi* — "to face reality"
- *mbali na uhalisi* — "far from reality"
- *uhalisi dhidi ya uongo* — "reality versus falsehood"
- *uhalisi pepe* — "virtual reality"
- *uhalisi wa kidijitali* — "digital reality"
- *uhalisi ulioboreshwa* — "augmented reality"

**Notes:** The technology-domain uses (*uhalisi pepe*, *uhalisi ulioboreshwa*) are internally consistent in that they use *uhalisi* to name a constructed perceptual environment, but they contrast with the other items in this group, which use *uhalisi* to refer to unmediated actual conditions. These two uses are not contrasted with each other in the data; they appear in entirely separate discourse contexts.

---

## GROUP 11: Expressions using *uhalisia* in literary and aesthetic criticism

**Label:** Realism as an aesthetic or literary quality

**Definition:** These expressions use *uhalisia* to evaluate or name the quality of faithfully representing reality in artistic or literary works. They appear in literary criticism, arts journalism, and academic discourse, and are applied to aesthetic movements, genres, or individual works.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uhalisia* — "realism (aesthetic/philosophical)"
- *uhalisia wa kisanaa* — "artistic realism"
- *uhalisia wa kijamii* — "social realism"
- *uhalisia wa kichawi* — "magical realism"
- *uhalisi wa kisanaa* — "artistic authenticity / the reality of art"
- *uhalisia wa kijadi* — "traditional realism"

**Notes:** This group is relatively domain-specific and small. *Uhalisia wa kichawi* (magical realism) is notable because it applies the realism term to a genre that deliberately incorporates non-realistic elements; this tension is observed in the data but not resolved. *Uhalisi wa kisanaa* (using *uhalisi* rather than *uhalisia*) appears in a related but slightly different context — evaluating authenticity of artistic expression rather than naming a movement.

---

## GROUP 12: Expressions used in asserting or evaluating rights and justice

**Label:** Rights, justice, and entitlement assertions

**Definition:** These expressions use *haki* to assert entitlements, evaluate conduct as fair or just, or characterize justice as a value. They appear in legal, political, and moral discourse and are distinct from the truth-assertion uses of *haki* (Group 1), though the same word is involved.

**Representative expressions:**

- *haki* — "right, justice, entitlement"
- *haki za binadamu* — "human rights"
- *haki yake ni kupata jibu* — "his/her right is to receive an answer"
- *kudai haki* — "to claim one's rights"
- *kupigana kwa haki* — "to fight for justice"
- *haki za binadamu zinakiukwa* — "human rights are being violated"
- *kwa haki, yeye anastahili tuzo* — "in all fairness, he/she deserves a reward"
- *haki ni haki, bila kujali mtu ni nani* — "right is right, regardless of who the person is"
- *ukweli ni haki* — "truth is justice / truth is a right"

**Notes:** *Haki* is the item with the widest distribution across groups in this analysis: it appears in Group 1 (truth affirmation: *haki kabisa*), Group 8 (moral assertion: *haki ni haki*), and here. *Ukweli ni haki* is included here because it explicitly equates truth with justice/rights, but it could also be placed in Group 1 or Group 8. The rights-and-justice uses are distinguished by the presence of entitlement framing (*haki yake*, *kudai haki*, *zinakiukwa*) rather than simple affirmation.

---

## GROUP 13: Expressions used in comparing or distinguishing things

**Label:** Comparison, resemblance, and distinction

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate the degree of similarity or difference between two things. They appear in analytical, legal, scientific, and everyday contexts where the relationship between two entities — whether they match, resemble, or differ — is being assessed.

**Representative expressions:**

- *fanana* — "to resemble, to be alike"
- *kufanana* — "to resemble"
- *mfanano* — "resemblance, similarity"
- *kufanana na* — "to resemble, to be similar to"
- *kufananisha* — "to compare, to liken"
- *vinafanana sana* — "they resemble each other closely"
- *vinafanana kama ndugu* — "they resemble each other like siblings"
- *haifanani hata kidogo* — "it does not resemble it at all"
- *tofauti* — "difference, different"
- *utofauti* — "difference, diversity"
- *kutofautiana* — "to differ from each other"
- *kutofautisha* — "to differentiate, to distinguish"
- *kuna tofauti kubwa kati ya kweli na uongo* — "there is a great difference between truth and falsehood"
- *tofauti yao ni ndogo sana* — "their difference is very small"
- *kutofautisha kati ya ukweli na maoni* — "to distinguish between truth and opinion"
- *kufananisha hali mbili tofauti si haki* — "comparing two different situations is not fair"
- *mfano* — "example, likeness, model"
- *kwa mfano* — "for example"

**Notes:** *Mfano* is included here because its core meaning involves likeness and resemblance, and it appears in comparative constructions. However, *kwa mfano* ("for example") functions primarily as a discourse marker in instructional contexts and may not belong in this group. *Kutofautisha kati ya ukweli na maoni* links this group to Group 1 (truth assertion) through the specific contrast of truth versus opinion.

---

## GROUP 14: Expressions used in evaluating quality, defect, and error

**Label:** Quality, defect, and error evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something meets a standard of quality, whether it contains a flaw or defect, or whether an action constitutes an error or wrong. They appear in commercial, educational, moral, and legal contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *bora* — "best, excellent"
- *ubora* — "quality, excellence"
- *ubora wa bidhaa* — "quality of goods"
- *udhibiti wa ubora* — "quality control"
- *bora zaidi* — "better, even better"
- *kasoro* — "defect, flaw"
- *bidhaa yenye kasoro* — "a defective product"
- *kasoro hii inaweza kusahihishwa* — "this defect can be corrected"
- *hakuna kitu kisichokuwa na kasoro* — "nothing is without a flaw"
- *kukosoa* — "to criticize, to find fault"
- *ukosoaji* — "criticism, critique"
- *mkosoaji* — "a critic"
- *kosa* — "error, mistake, fault"
- *makosa* — "errors, mistakes"
- *kukosa* — "to make a mistake; to lack"
- *kukosea* — "to wrong someone"
- *mkosaji* — "an offender, a wrongdoer"
- *kukiri makosa ni nguvu, si udhaifu* — "admitting mistakes is strength, not weakness"
- *kosa kubwa* — "a serious mistake"
- *makosa ya kisheria* — "legal offenses"
- *kufanya makosa mara moja ni kawaida; kurudia ni makosa* — first-error vs. repetition construction

**Notes:** *Kasoro* (defect in an object) and *kosa* (error in an action or statement) are used in overlapping but distinguishable contexts: *kasoro* more often applies to physical objects and products, while *kosa* applies more broadly to actions, statements, and moral conduct. Both appear in the same evaluative domain and are grouped together here on that basis.

---

## GROUP 15: Expressions used in religious or theological contexts involving truth and belief

**Label:** Truth and belief in religious discourse

**Definition:** These expressions appear characteristically in religious contexts — sermons, theological writing, religious education — where truth is grounded in divine authority, scripture, or faith rather than in empirical or legal standards.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kweli ya Mungu* — "God's truth"
- *ukweli wa Injili* — "the truth of the Gospel"
- *imani* — "faith, belief, trust"
- *kuamini Mungu* — "to believe in God"
- *mwaminifu* (in religious sense) — "a believer, the faithful"
- *waaminifu* (in religious sense) — "the faithful" (plural)
- *udhihirisho wa Mungu* — "a manifestation of God"

**Notes:** This group is defined by discourse context rather than by a distinct set of lexical items. Most items here also appear in other groups (*imani* in Group 7, *mwaminifu* in Group 7, *udhihirisho* in Group 9). The religious-context uses are distinguished by the specific referents (*Mungu*, *Injili*) and by the framing of truth as divinely grounded.

---

## GROUP 16: Expressions used in domain-specific truth or accuracy qualification

**Label:** Domain-qualified truth and accuracy

**Definition:** These expressions qualify *ukweli* or *usahihi* with a domain modifier (using the *ki-* adjectival prefix) to specify the type of truth or accuracy at issue. They appear in formal, academic, and professional discourse where different standards of truth apply in different fields.

**Representative expressions:**

- *ukweli wa kisayansi* — "scientific truth"
- *ukweli wa kihistoria* — "historical truth"
- *ukweli wa kisheria* — "legal truth"
- *usahihi wa taarifa* — "accuracy of a report"
- *usahihi wa kipimo* — "accuracy of measurement"
- *makosa ya kisheria* — "legal offenses"
- *uhalisia wa kisanaa* — "artistic realism"
- *uhalisia wa kijamii* — "social realism"

**Notes:** The *ki-* domain-qualifier pattern is productive across multiple base forms (*ukweli*, *usahihi*, *uhalisia*). This group captures the pattern rather than a single lexical item. *Makosa ya kisheria* is included because it applies the error/wrong concept to the legal domain specifically. *Uhalisia wa kisanaa* and *uhalisia wa kijamii* also appear in Group 11.

## GROUP 17: Expressions involving *asili* in reference to origin, essential nature, or nativeness

**Label:** Origin, essential nature, and nativeness

**Definition:** These expressions use *asili* to refer to the fundamental origin, essential character, or native status of a person, language, or thing. They appear in cultural, linguistic, and philosophical discourse where the question of what something fundamentally is or where it comes from is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asili* — "origin, nature, essence"
- *uasili* — "originality, nativeness"
- *kiasili* — "originally, natively, in the original manner"
- *lugha ya asili* — "native/original language"
- *asili yake ni nzuri* — "his/her nature is good"
- *kwa asili yake, yeye ni mtu mwaminifu* — "by his/her nature, he/she is a trustworthy person"
- *lugha ya asili yake ni Kiswahili* — "his/her native language is Swahili"
- *kurudi asili* — "to return to one's origins"
- *asili ya tatizo ni nini?* — "what is the root/origin of the problem?"

**Notes:** *Asili* overlaps with *halisi* in some contexts (both can evaluate authenticity), but *asili* characteristically refers to origin and essential nature rather than to the absence of counterfeiting. *Kiswahili halisi* (Group 3) and *lugha ya asili* (this group) both evaluate language authenticity but from different angles: *halisi* evaluates purity/genuineness, while *asili* evaluates nativeness/origin.

## Ambiguous or Multi-Group Items

The following items show clear membership in more than one group and cannot be cleanly assigned:

- **haki**: Appears in Group 1 (truth affirmation: *haki kabisa*), Group 8 (moral proverb: *haki ni haki*), and Group 12 (rights/justice). The same word is used across these contexts without a clear boundary.

- **mtu wa kweli / mtu halisi / mtu sahihi**: All evaluate persons, but *mtu wa kweli* emphasizes honesty (Group 7), *mtu halisi* emphasizes authenticity of character (Groups 3 and 7), and *mtu sahihi* emphasizes being the correct or appropriate person (Groups 4 and 7).

- **ukweli ni haki**: Belongs to Group 1 (truth assertion), Group 8 (moral assertion), and Group 12 (rights/justice) simultaneously.

- **nusu kweli**: Belongs to Group 1 (propositional truth evaluation) but also to Group 8 (moral discourse about partial honesty). It treats truth as scalar, which is not characteristic of most Group 1 items.

- **imani**: Belongs to Group 7 (character/trustworthiness) and Group 15 (religious discourse). The religious and interpersonal uses are not always distinguishable.

- **udhahiri / kudhihirisha / kudhihirika / udhihirisho**: These forms appear in Group 9 (revelation of truth) and Group 1 (evidential assertion: *ni dhahiri kwamba*). The evidential use (*ni dhahiri kwamba*) is closer to Group 1, while the revelation uses (*ukweli ulidhihirika*) are closer to Group 9.

- **kufananisha hali mbili tofauti si haki**: Belongs to Group 13 (comparison) and Group 12 (rights/justice) simultaneously.

- **uhalisi mgumu**: Belongs to Group 10 (reference to actual conditions) but also has an evaluative-affective dimension not shared by other Group 10 items.

## Items Without Clear Group Membership

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups identified above, or their usage patterns are insufficiently distinct to warrant grouping:

- **ukweli ni silaha** ("truth is a weapon"): A political/rhetorical metaphor that does not fit the patterns of any group above. It characterizes truth by its instrumental power rather than by its epistemic or moral properties.

- **udanganyifu ni silaha ya wanyonge** ("deception is the weapon of the weak"): Similarly, a political characterization that does not fit the deception-characterization group (Group 6) cleanly, as it evaluates deception sociologically rather than morally or legally.

- **ukweli ni mwanga katika giza** ("truth is a light in the darkness"): A metaphorical construction that appears in religious and moral discourse but does not fit the proverbial group (Group 8) or the religious group (Group 15) cleanly.

- **halisisha** (causative verb: "to make genuine, to authenticate"): Documented in P2a as uncertain; insufficient usage evidence to place in any group.

- **ubandia** (abstract noun: "fakeness"): Documented in P2a as uncertain; insufficient usage evidence to place in any group.

- **uhaki** (abstract noun: "justice, righteousness"): Documented in P2a as uncertain; insufficient usage evidence to place in any group.

- **udanganyaji** (alternative nominalization of *danganya*): Documented in P2a as marginal; insufficient usage evidence to distinguish from *udanganyifu* for grouping purposes.

- **kweli kwa kweli** ("in all truth"): Documented in P1b as marginal; overlaps with Group 2 (sincerity framing) but is insufficiently attested to place confidently.

- **amini usiku, usimwamini mchana** (proverbial caution about trust): Documented in P2b as marginal; the exact meaning is contextually variable and the construction does not fit Group 8 (moral proverbs about truth/falsehood) or Group 7 (character evaluation) cleanly.
