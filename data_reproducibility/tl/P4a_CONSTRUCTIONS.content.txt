# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X ay totoo/tama/tunay** — Topic marker **ang/si** + subject, predicate marker **ay**, truth-domain predicate. Alternative: Predicate-initial with **ang** marking subject.

### Examples
- Ang balita ay totoo.
  - ang balita ay totoo
  - "The news is true."

- Tama ang sagot mo.
  - tama ang sagot mo
  - "Your answer is correct."

### Notes
Tagalog allows both topic-initial (ang X ay PRED) and predicate-initial (PRED ang X) word orders. The predicate-initial pattern is more common in everyday speech for truth attributions.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**Hindi/walang + truth-domain term** — Negator **hindi** precedes predicates; **walang** (without) combines with abstract nominalizations.

### Examples
- Hindi totoo ang sinabi niya.
  - hindi totoo ang sinabi niya
  - "What he/she said is not true."

- Walang katotohanan ang balita.
  - walang katotohanan ang balita
  - "The news has no truth / is baseless."

### Notes
**Hindi** is the standard predicate negator. **Walang** (without) + nominalized form creates stronger negation implying complete absence of the quality. Contracted **di-** forms exist but are less emphatic.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**Truth-domain term + linker -ng/-na + noun** — Attributive adjectives follow the noun they modify, connected by linker **-ng** (after vowels/n) or **na** (after consonants).

### Examples
- tunay na pagmamahal
  - tunay na pagmamahal
  - "true love / genuine love"

- tamang sagot
  - tamang sagot
  - "correct answer"

### Notes
The linker **-ng/-na** is obligatory between modifier and head noun. The modifier always follows the noun in Tagalog, unlike English. Some combinations are highly lexicalized (e.g., **tunay na pagmamahal**, **tamang panahon**).

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**Mag- + ka- + root** — Verbal prefix combination **mag-ka-** marks inchoative/achievement meaning "to become X" or "to come to be X."

### Examples
- Magkatotoo ang pangarap ko.
  - magkatotoo ang pangarap ko
  - "My dream came true / was realized."

- Nagkamali ako.
  - nagkamali ako
  - "I made a mistake / I became wrong."

### Notes
The **mag-ka-** combination specifically marks achievement or inchoative aspect, indicating transition into a state. Past/completed aspect uses **nag-ka-**, future uses **mag-ka-**. This pattern is highly productive with truth-domain roots.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Pa- + root + -an** (object-focus) or **i- + root** (directional) — Causative affixes mark "cause to be X" or "make X."

### Examples
- Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo.
  - patunayan mo ang sinabi mo
  - "Prove what you said / Make it verified."

- Itama mo ang pagkakamali.
  - itama mo ang pagkakamali
  - "Correct the mistake / Make it right."

### Notes
**Pa-...-an** is the primary causative for truth-domain terms, focusing on the object being made true/correct. **I-** causative (directional) is used with **tama** and **wasto**. The causative is highly productive across truth-domain vocabulary.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Ka- + root + -an** (circumfix) or **pagka- + root** — Creates abstract nouns meaning "the quality/state of being X."

### Examples
- katotohanan
  - katotohanan
  - "truth, reality, fact"

- katapatan
  - katapatan
  - "honesty, sincerity, loyalty"

### Notes
**Ka-...-an** is the most productive abstract nominalizer for truth-domain terms. **Pagka-** nominalizer emphasizes state or condition. Both patterns are highly productive. The **ka-...-an** forms often serve as the primary abstract noun for the concept.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Root with agentive morphology** — Agent nouns are formed through various patterns, some lexicalized.

### Examples
- sinungaling
  - sinungaling
  - "liar (one who lies)"

- (no productive agentive pattern for truth-tellers)

### Notes
Tagalog has limited productive agentive nominalization for truth-domain terms. **Sinungaling** (liar) appears to be derived but the base is not independently attested. There is no common single-word agentive for "truth-teller" — this would be expressed periphrastically as **taong nagsasabi ng totoo** (person who tells the truth).

---

## 8. RECIPROCAL / MUTUAL ACTION

### Pattern
**Mag- + root + -an** — Reciprocal/mutual action marker indicating "do X to each other."

### Examples
- Magtapatan na tayo.
  - magtapatan na tayo
  - "Let's be honest with each other."

- Magbulaan sila.
  - magbulaan sila
  - "They lie to each other."

### Notes
The **mag-...-an** pattern marks reciprocal or mutual action. Common with honesty/dishonesty terms (**tapat**, **bulaán**) but not productive with factual truth terms (**totoo**). The pattern emphasizes mutual or reciprocal relationship between participants.

---

## 9. INTENSIFICATION / DEGREE

### Pattern
**Napaka- + root** (very/extremely) or **reduplication** — Intensifiers mark high degree of quality.

### Examples
- napakatotoo
  - napakatotoo
  - "very true, extremely real"

- tamang-tama
  - tamang-tama
  - "exactly right, just perfect"

### Notes
**Napaka-** prefix marks superlative/extreme degree. Reduplication with linker (**root-ng-root**) also intensifies meaning. Both patterns are productive with truth-domain adjectives. **Paka-** prefix marks "as X as possible."

---

## 10. QUESTION FORMATION

### Pattern
**Truth-domain term + ba** (yes/no question) or **interrogative + truth-domain term** — Particle **ba** marks polar questions.

### Examples
- Totoo ba yan?
  - totoo ba yan
  - "Is that true?"

- Tama ba ang sagot ko?
  - tama ba ang sagot ko
  - "Is my answer correct?"

### Notes
The question particle **ba** immediately follows the predicate in polar questions. This is the standard pattern for seeking confirmation of truth claims. Extremely productive and common in conversational contexts.

---

## 11. CONDITIONAL

### Pattern
**Kung + truth-domain term** — Conditional marker **kung** (if) introduces hypothetical truth conditions.

### Examples
- Kung totoo ang sinabi mo...
  - kung totoo ang sinabi mo
  - "If what you said is true..."

- Kung tama ako...
  - kung tama ako
  - "If I'm correct..."

### Notes
**Kung** marks protasis (if-clause) in conditional constructions. Very common with truth-domain terms for expressing hypothetical truth conditions or hedging claims. The pattern is fully productive.

---

## 12. EMPHATIC CONFIRMATION

### Pattern
**Truth-domain term + nga/naman/talaga** — Emphatic particles strengthen affirmation or add nuance.

### Examples
- Totoo nga!
  - totoo nga
  - "It really is true! / Indeed!"

- Tama naman.
  - tama naman
  - "It is correct though / You're right but..."

### Notes
Particles **nga** (emphatic), **naman** (concessive), **talaga** (really), **pa** (still/even), **rin/din** (also) modify illocutionary force. These are extremely common in conversational contexts. **Nga** provides strong confirmation; **naman** acknowledges a point before contrasting.

---

## 13. CESSATION / SUFFICIENCY MARKER

### Pattern
**Tama/hustó + na** — Particle **na** (already/now) with correctness terms signals sufficiency or cessation.

### Examples
- Tama na!
  - tama na
  - "That's enough! / Stop now!"

- Hustó na yan.
  - hustó na yan
  - "That's enough of that / That's sufficient."

### Notes
This represents semantic shift from correctness evaluation to sufficiency/cessation marking. **Tama na** is extremely common as discourse marker for stopping actions. The pattern is specific to **tama** and **hustó** and does not extend to other truth-domain terms.

---

## 14. PROGRESSIVE ASPECT

### Pattern
**Infix -in- or -um- + root** (with aspect marking) — Progressive/imperfective aspect in verbal forms.

### Examples
- Itinatama ko ang mali.
  - itinatama ko ang mali
  - "I am correcting the error."

- Pinapatunayan niya ang katotohanan.
  - pinapatunayan niya ang katotohanan
  - "He/she is proving the truth."

### Notes
Progressive aspect uses **-in-** infix (object focus) or reduplication of first syllable. The pattern applies to causative and active verbal forms. Common in describing ongoing processes of verification, correction, or proof.

---

## 15. COMPARISON

### Pattern
**Katulad/kamukha + ng/possessive** — Comparison markers indicating similarity or resemblance.

### Examples
- Katulad mo siya.
  - katulad mo siya
  - "He/she is like you / similar to you."

- Kamukha niya ang tatay niya.
  - kamukha niya ang tatay niya
  - "He/she looks like his/her father."

### Notes
**Katulad** marks general similarity; **kamukha** specifically marks physical resemblance. Both take genitive complements with **ng** or possessive pronouns. These forms appear to be lexicalized from **ka-** prefix + root, but are not productively derived.

---

## 16. LACK / ABSENCE

### Pattern
**Walang + nominalized form** — **Walang** (without/no) + abstract noun marks complete absence.

### Examples
- Walang katotohanan ang akusasyon.
  - walang katotohanan ang akusasyon
  - "The accusation has no truth / is baseless."

- Walang katapatan ang taong iyon.
  - walang katapatan ang taong iyon
  - "That person has no honesty / is dishonest."

### Notes
**Walang** (contraction of **wala** + **ng**) marks existential negation or absence. Stronger than simple **hindi** negation, implying complete lack of the quality. Very productive with **ka-...-an** abstract nominalizations.

---

## 17. IMPERATIVE / DIRECTIVE

### Pattern
**Verb stem (+ mo/ka)** — Bare verb stem or with pronoun enclitic forms imperative.

### Examples
- Magtapat ka!
  - magtapat ka
  - "Be honest! / Confess!"

- Itama mo yan!
  - itama mo yan
  - "Correct that! / Fix that!"

### Notes
Imperative uses bare verb stem. Second-person pronouns **ka** (subject) or **mo** (object) are enclitic. Negative imperative uses **huwag** + verb. Very common in directives involving honesty, correction, or verification.

---

## 18. FIXED DISCOURSE MARKERS

### Pattern
**Sa totoo lang** and similar fixed expressions — Grammaticalized phrases functioning as discourse markers.

### Examples
- Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.
  - sa totoo lang hindi ko alam
  - "To be honest, I don't know."

- Ang totoo...
  - ang totoo
  - "The truth is... / Actually..."

### Notes
Several truth-domain expressions have grammaticalized into discourse markers: **sa totoo lang** (to be honest), **ang totoo** (the truth is), **kung totoo** (if true). These function to frame or qualify statements rather than make truth claims themselves.

---

## 19. POSSESSION OF QUALITY

### Pattern
**May + nominalized form** — Existential **may** (has/there is) + abstract noun indicates possession of quality.

### Examples
- May katotohanan ang sinabi niya.
  - may katotohanan ang sinabi niya
  - "What he/she said has truth / is truthful."

- May tama ka.
  - may tama ka
  - "You have a point / You're partially correct."

### Notes
**May** marks existential possession or presence. Common pattern for acknowledging partial truth or validity. Contrasts with **walang** (without) for absence. The construction allows nuanced acknowledgment of partial correctness or truth.

---

## 20. EVIDENCE / PROOF CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**Katunayan/patunayan** — Specialized vocabulary for proof and verification.

### Examples
- Ano ang katunayan mo?
  - ano ang katunayan mo
  - "What is your proof / evidence?"

- Patunayan mo!
  - patunayan mo
  - "Prove it!"

### Notes
**Katunayan** (proof, evidence) is the **ka-...-an** nominalization of **tunay**. **Patunayan** is the causative verb "to prove." This semantic domain is specifically associated with **tunay** (genuine/authentic) rather than **totoo** (factually true), showing lexical specialization.
