# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST

---

## Preliminary Note

Oppositions are extracted only where the context explicitly documents a contrastive relationship — through direct statement, paired usage, or constructional contrast. Where the context notes a relationship without specifying it as oppositional, this is recorded but not treated as an opposition. Overlapping oppositions are preserved without consolidation.

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** असत्य / *asatya*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *asatya*
4. **English translation:** truth, truthfulness / untruth, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** *Asatya* is the direct prefixal negation of *satya*, formed by *a-* + *satya*. The pair is attested across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as a moral pair, with *asatya* denoting a false statement or the quality of being untrue. The pair is explicitly identified as a contrastive pair used to frame philosophical positions. It appears in witness evaluation, lists of vices, and discussions of testimony as a direct positive/negative opposition. Both are attested in prescriptive formulas (*satyaṃ brūyāt* / *asatyaṃ na vadet*) and in characterizations of persons (*satyavādin* / *asatyavādin*).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** ऋत / *ṛta*
2. **Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
3. **Transliteration:** *ṛta* / *anṛta*
4. **English translation:** cosmic order, right / falsehood, violation of right order
5. **Description of contrast:** *Ṛta* designates the ordered functioning of the cosmos and the moral-ritual rightness aligned with it; *anṛta* is its negation, formed by *an-* + *ṛta*, designating falsehood and moral wrongness in Vedic and early classical literature. The pair is the foundational Vedic opposition in the truth domain, used in Vedic, legal, and moral contexts to contrast proper cosmic and ritual ordering with its violation. *Anṛta* is the standard antonym of *ṛta* in Vedic and later literature, carrying the sense of "contrary to cosmic order." The pair is the older Vedic counterpart to the *satya/asatya* opposition and carries stronger cosmological connotations.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *anṛta*
4. **English translation:** truth / falsehood, disorder
5. **Description of contrast:** *Anṛta* is contrasted with *satya* in many texts. The construction *anṛtaṃ na vadet* ("one should not speak falsehood") is parallel in structure to *satyaṃ vada* ("speak the truth"). The contrast is attested in Vedic and early *dharmaśāstra* literature; *anṛta* is the Vedic-register counterpart to *satya* as its negation.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** सत् / *sat*
2. **Term B:** असत् / *asat*
3. **Transliteration:** *sat* / *asat*
4. **English translation:** being, the real, what genuinely exists / non-being, the unreal, what lacks genuine existence
5. **Description of contrast:** *Sat* and *asat* are the primary ontological binary in Sanskrit philosophical literature. *Asat* is the ontological and evaluative antonym of *sat*, formed by *a-* + *sat*. They are explicitly paired in fixed formulas such as *nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ* / *nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ* (Bhagavadgītā 2.16) and in the causation debate (*satkāryavāda* vs. *asatkāryavāda*). The dvandva compound *sadasat* ("being and non-being") is attested as a paired form. The construction *sataḥ na asataḥ jāyate* ("the world arises from being, not from non-being") is documented as a philosophical formula exploiting this contrast. The pair is foundational in Vedāntic and Upaniṣadic literature; the prayer *asato mā sad gamaya* ("lead me from the unreal to the real") treats the two as the poles of an ontological movement. The contrast covers both ontological status and evaluative quality (genuine vs. spurious).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** प्रमा / *pramā*
2. **Term B:** अप्रमा / *apramā*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramā* / *apramā*
4. **English translation:** valid cognition / non-valid cognition, erroneous cognition
5. **Description of contrast:** *Pramā* designates a cognition that correctly apprehends its object as it is; *apramā* is the technical negation of *pramā*, formed by *a-* + *pramā*, designating a cognition that fails to do so. The two terms are explicitly contrasted as the positive and negative poles of the same epistemological evaluative dimension in Nyāya and related literature. The pair is explicitly listed as a contrastive pair structuring philosophical debate. *Apramā* includes *bhrama* (*bhrānti*), *saṃśaya*, and related states as species. *Pramā* is the result of a successful *pramāṇa*; *apramā* is the result of a failed or absent *pramāṇa*.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*
2. **Term B:** अप्रमाण / *apramāṇa*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramāṇa* / *apramāṇa*
4. **English translation:** valid means of knowledge / invalid means of knowledge, non-authority
5. **Description of contrast:** *Apramāṇa* is the morphological negation of *pramāṇa*, formed by *a-* + *pramāṇa*, characterizing a source or means of cognition that fails to yield valid knowledge or meet the criteria for validity. The constructional contrast *pramāṇam asti* / *na tatra pramāṇam asti* is documented as a standard formula pair in philosophical argumentation. The pair is operative in epistemological literature wherever the question of whether a source qualifies as a genuine *pramāṇa* is at issue.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*
2. **Term B:** प्रमाणाभास / *pramāṇābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramāṇa* / *pramāṇābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** valid means of knowledge / semblance of a valid means of knowledge, pseudo-valid means of knowledge
5. **Description of contrast:** *Pramāṇa* designates a genuinely reliable epistemic source; *pramāṇābhāsa* designates something that merely appears to be such a source but fails to meet the conditions for genuine validity. The contrast is between the genuine instance and its spurious appearance. The *-ābhāsa* suffix systematically marks the pseudo-instance as distinct from the genuine instance. The parallel formula *hetvābhāso na hetuḥ* ("a fallacious reason is not a reason") is documented as the constructional expression of this contrast pattern.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological (via *ābhāsa* suffix)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** प्रामाण्य / *prāmāṇya*
2. **Term B:** अप्रामाण्य / *aprāmāṇya*
3. **Transliteration:** *prāmāṇya* / *aprāmāṇya*
4. **English translation:** validity (the property of being a genuine means of knowledge) / invalidity
5. **Description of contrast:** *Prāmāṇya* names the abstract property that makes a cognition or epistemic source valid; *aprāmāṇya* is its direct morphological negation, naming the property of failing to be valid. Both terms appear in epistemological debates about how validity is established and recognized.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **Description of contrast:** *Aprāmāṇika* is formed as *a-* + *prāmāṇika* and is explicitly described as contrasted with *prāmāṇika*. Both are used in philosophical, legal, and textual-critical contexts to evaluate sources and documents.

## Opposition 10

1. **Term A:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥprāmāṇya*
2. **Term B:** परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
3. **Transliteration:** *svataḥprāmāṇya* / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
4. **English translation:** intrinsic validity (validity established by the cognition itself) / extrinsic validity (validity established by something external)
5. **Description of contrast:** These two terms name opposing positions in a specific intra-school debate: *svataḥprāmāṇya* is the Mīmāṃsā doctrine that the validity of a cognition is established by the cognition itself (self-certifying); *parataḥprāmāṇya* is the Nyāya doctrine that validity must be established by an external means such as successful action. The two terms are explicitly paired as contrasting positions in epistemological literature and are explicitly listed as a contrastive pair structuring philosophical debate.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical (opposing adverbial prefixes *svataḥ* vs. *parataḥ* within the same compound frame)
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** हेतु / *hetu*
2. **Term B:** हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *hetu* / *hetvābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** genuine logical reason / semblance of a logical reason, fallacious reason, pseudo-reason
5. **Description of contrast:** *Hetu* is a probative logical ground that meets the conditions for valid inference; *hetvābhāsa* is the primary technical application of the *ābhāsa* pattern in the logical domain, designating a reason that appears valid but fails to be genuinely probative. The contrast is stated explicitly in the fixed formula *hetvābhāsaḥ na hetuḥ* ("a pseudo-reason is not a genuine reason"), used in logical literature to mark the distinction between valid and invalid inferential grounds.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological (via *ābhāsa* suffix); also characterized as lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** धर्म / *dharma*
2. **Term B:** अधर्म / *adharma*
3. **Transliteration:** *dharma* / *adharma*
4. **English translation:** right conduct, duty, the sustaining order / wrong conduct, violation of duty, unrighteousness
5. **Description of contrast:** *Dharma* and *adharma* are the primary pair for evaluating conduct as conforming to or violating right order in Sanskrit ethical, legal, and epic literature. *Adharma* is the direct negation of *dharma*, formed by *a-* + *dharma*, and is a fundamental evaluative category in Sanskrit ethical literature. They are regularly contrasted in prescriptive and narrative contexts, and in Vaiśeṣika philosophy they name opposing categories of unseen moral force. The pair appears throughout *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

1. **Term A:** साधु / *sādhu* (in grammatical usage)
2. **Term B:** अपशब्द / *apaśabda*
3. **Transliteration:** *sādhu* / *apaśabda*
4. **English translation:** correct, well-formed word / deviant, incorrect word, solecism, corrupt form
5. **Description of contrast:** In Sanskrit grammatical literature, *sādhu* designates a word or form sanctioned by Pāṇinian grammar and the usage of the learned, while *apaśabda* designates a form that deviates from that standard. *Apaśabda* is the opposite of *sādhu* in the grammatical sense. *Sādhu* and *apaśabda* are the core terms of the grammatical correctness evaluator group, explicitly contrasted with each other in grammatical literature.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** साधु / *sādhu*
2. **Term B:** असाधु / *asādhu*
3. **Transliteration:** *sādhu* / *asādhu*
4. **English translation:** correct (linguistic form), virtuous, good / incorrect (linguistic form), bad, improper
5. **Description of contrast:** *Asādhu* is the negation of *sādhu*, formed by *a-* + *sādhu*. In grammatical literature it characterizes an incorrect or ill-formed linguistic expression; in ethics it marks a person or act as bad or improper. The pair is explicitly listed as a contrastive pair. The constructional contrast *sādhu iti prayujyate* / *sādhu na bhavati* is documented as a standard formula pair. The formulaic pair *sādhuḥ śabdaḥ* / *asādhuḥ śabdaḥ* is documented as the standard grammatical evaluation formula.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

1. **Term A:** मुख्य / *mukhya*
2. **Term B:** गौण / *gauṇa*
3. **Transliteration:** *mukhya* / *gauṇa*
4. **English translation:** primary, literal, direct (use of a term) / secondary, figurative, based on a shared quality
5. **Description of contrast:** In Mīmāṃsā and philosophical grammar, *mukhya* designates the primary or literal sense of a term, while *gauṇa* designates a secondary or figurative use licensed by a quality (*guṇa*) shared between the primary and secondary referents. The two terms are the standard contrast pair for evaluating whether a predication is literal or figurative.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** मुख्य / *mukhya*
2. **Term B:** औपचारिक / *aupacārika*
3. **Transliteration:** *mukhya* / *aupacārika*
4. **English translation:** primary, literal / figurative, secondary, conventional
5. **Description of contrast:** *Mukhya* and *aupacārika* are contrasted in Mīmāṃsā and philosophical grammar as the primary/literal versus the secondary/conventional mode of linguistic reference. *Aupacārika* emphasizes the conventional or courtesy dimension of secondary use, distinguishing it from *gauṇa* which emphasizes the quality-based dimension, though both contrast with *mukhya*.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** वस्तु / *vastu*
2. **Term B:** आभास / *ābhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *vastu* / *ābhāsa*
4. **English translation:** real thing, genuine entity / mere semblance, appearance without genuine reality
5. **Description of contrast:** *Vastu* designates a genuinely existing thing that cognition must correspond to; *ābhāsa* designates something that merely appears to be what it is not. The contrast is stated explicitly in the formula *ābhāsamātram na vastu* ("a mere semblance is not a real thing"), attested in epistemological and Vedāntic literature as a standard ontological denial directly contrasting *ābhāsa* (something that resembles the genuine article without being it) with *vastu* (the genuine article itself).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** वस्तु / *vastu*
2. **Term B:** प्रतिभास / *pratibhāsa*
3. **Transliteration:** *vastu* / *pratibhāsa*
4. **English translation:** real thing, actual entity / mere appearance, semblance
5. **Description of contrast:** *Vastu* designates what something genuinely is as a real entity; *pratibhāsa* designates how something appears, particularly when the appearance may not correspond to reality. The construction *pratibhāse sati api vastu na tathā* ("even though there is an appearance, the real thing is not like that") is documented as a formulaic expression of this contrast.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** सत् / *sat*
2. **Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
3. **Transliteration:** *sat* / *mithyā*
4. **English translation:** genuine being, the absolutely real / neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal, illusory
5. **Description of contrast:** In Advaita Vedānta, *mithyā* is explicitly defined as neither *sat* nor *asat* (*mithyā na sat na asat*), making it a third ontological category that stands in contrast to *sat* (absolute reality) without being its simple negation. The *sat* / *mithyā* contrast is therefore asymmetric: *mithyā* does not simply negate *sat* but occupies an intermediate position. The formula *mithyeti niścitam* is documented as the standard way of assigning this status. This contrast is specific to the Advaita framework and is not the same as the *sat/asat* binary.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** मिथ्या / *mithyā*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *mithyā*
4. **English translation:** truth, the real / false, illusory, of indeterminate ontological status
5. **Description of contrast:** *Mithyā* is used in contrast to *satya* in both ethical speech contexts (false speech versus true speech) and Advaita Vedāntic ontology (where *mithyā* designates what is neither absolutely *sat* nor absolutely *asat*). The contrast is lexical rather than morphological; *mithyā* is not derived from *satya*. The dual usage of *mithyā* spans both the ethical and ontological domains.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 33

1. **Term A:** सत्कार्यवाद / *satkāryavāda*
2. **Term B:** असत्कार्यवाद / *asatkāryavāda*
3. **Transliteration:** *satkāryavāda* / *asatkāryavāda*
4. **English translation:** the doctrine that the effect pre-exists (as real) in the cause / the doctrine that the effect does not pre-exist in the cause
5. **Description of contrast:** These two terms name directly opposing positions in the Sanskrit philosophical debate about causation: the Sāṃkhya-Vedānta position that the effect is genuinely real in the cause prior to production (*satkāryavāda*), versus the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika and Buddhist position that the effect is a genuinely new entity not pre-existing in the cause (*asatkāryavāda*). They are explicitly paired as contrasting doctrinal positions and are defined explicitly in contrast to each other.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

1. **Term A:** परमार्थ / *paramārtha* (and पारमार्थिक / *pāramārthika*)
2. **Term B:** व्यावहारिक / *vyāvahārika*
3. **Transliteration:** *paramārtha* / *vyāvahārika*
4. **English translation:** ultimate truth, highest reality / conventional, practical, empirical
5. **Description of contrast:** *Vyāvahārika* denotes the level of truth or validity that holds within conventional or practical usage, as opposed to the ultimate (*pāramārthika*) level. The paired construction *paramārthasatyam* / *saṃvṛtisatyam* (or *vyāvahārikasatyam*) constitutes the two-truths doctrine
