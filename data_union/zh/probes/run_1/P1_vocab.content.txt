# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (single morpheme; functions as root in many compounds)
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that something corresponds to fact or is not counterfeit. Appears as a free morpheme ("这是真的") and as a productive bound root in compounds. Contrasts with 假 (jiǎ).
**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** false, fake, borrowed (single morpheme)
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
**Usage:** Directly contrasts with 真; used to describe forgeries, lies, and inauthentic objects or claims. Productive in compounds such as 假冒 and 假话. Common in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts.
**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal meaning:** true + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** truthful, authentic, real, factual
**Usage:** Describes statements, accounts, or depictions that accurately reflect reality. Frequently used in journalism, legal testimony, and literary criticism to evaluate fidelity to fact. More explicitly evaluative than 真 alone.
**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** 虚假
**Transliteration:** xūjiǎ
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false
**Typical English translations:** false, fictitious, fraudulent, deceptive
**Usage:** Typically applied to claims, advertising, or representations that are deliberately misleading or without factual basis. Common in legal and regulatory language (e.g., 虚假广告, false advertising). Carries a stronger negative connotation than 假 alone.
**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** 正确
**Transliteration:** zhèngquè
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Evaluates statements, answers, methods, or conduct as conforming to a standard or being free from error. Widely used in educational, technical, and moral contexts. Contrasts with 错误 (cuòwù).
**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** 错误
**Transliteration:** cuòwù
**Literal meaning:** wrong/mistaken + error/mistake
**Typical English translations:** wrong, erroneous, mistaken, error
**Usage:** Applied to statements, beliefs, actions, or judgments that deviate from what is correct or true. Functions as both noun and adjective. Productive in compounds and common across all registers.
**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** 准确
**Transliteration:** zhǔnquè
**Literal meaning:** standard/level + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Emphasizes precision and exactness, often in technical, scientific, or measurement contexts. Also used to evaluate the accuracy of translations, descriptions, or predictions. Implies a tighter fit to a target than 正确.
**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** 可靠
**Transliteration:** kěkào
**Literal meaning:** can + lean on/depend on
**Typical English translations:** reliable, dependable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates sources, persons, equipment, or information as worthy of trust or consistent in performance. Common in everyday speech and technical contexts alike. Often used to assess whether something can be safely acted upon.
**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal meaning:** true + appearance/face/reality
**Typical English translations:** the truth, the true state of affairs, the facts
**Usage:** Refers to the actual facts of a matter, especially when they are hidden or contested. Frequently appears in investigative, legal, and journalistic contexts (e.g., 揭露真相, to expose the truth). Functions as a noun referring to a state of affairs rather than a property of a statement.
**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** 事实
**Transliteration:** shìshí
**Literal meaning:** matter/affair + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** fact, the facts, reality
**Usage:** Denotes an actual state of affairs or an established datum, as opposed to opinion or conjecture. Central to legal argumentation (事实认定, finding of fact) and everyday discourse. Often used in the phrase 事实上 (in fact, as a matter of fact).
**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** true + false/spurious
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness vs. forgery, true or false
**Usage:** A compound that frames the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit, typically applied to documents, artworks, or claims. Common in legal, archival, and connoisseurship contexts. Often appears in the phrase 辨别真伪 (to distinguish the genuine from the fake).
**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** 伪造
**Transliteration:** wěizào
**Literal meaning:** false/spurious + to make/fabricate
**Typical English translations:** to forge, to fabricate, forgery, counterfeit
**Usage:** Refers specifically to the act of making a false copy or fraudulent document, signature, or object. A key term in criminal law (伪造文书, document forgery). Implies deliberate deception rather than mere error.
**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** 冒充
**Transliteration:** màochōng
**Literal meaning:** to risk/pretend + to fill/act as
**Typical English translations:** to impersonate, to pass off as, to pose as
**Usage:** Describes the act of falsely presenting oneself or an object as something it is not. Used in legal and everyday contexts (e.g., 冒充警察, impersonating a police officer). Focuses on the act of misrepresentation of identity or nature.
**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** 名副其实
**Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
**Literal meaning:** name + matches/corresponds to + its + reality
**Typical English translations:** living up to one's name, worthy of the title, genuine
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression (成语) used to affirm that a label, title, or reputation accurately corresponds to the actual qualities of the person or thing. Evaluates the fit between representation and reality. Its negation 名不副实 is equally common.
**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** 名不副实
**Transliteration:** míng bù fù shí
**Literal meaning:** name + not + matches + reality
**Typical English translations:** not living up to one's name, undeserving of the title, in name only
**Usage:** The negative counterpart of 名副其实; asserts that a label or reputation does not correspond to actual qualities. Used in evaluative criticism of persons, institutions, or products. Common in both formal writing and colloquial speech.
**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** 如实
**Transliteration:** rúshí
**Literal meaning:** as + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** truthfully, as things actually are, accurately
**Usage:** An adverbial expression meaning to report or describe something exactly as it is, without distortion. Frequently appears in legal contexts (如实陈述, to testify truthfully) and journalistic ones. Implies both accuracy and completeness of representation.
**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** 失真
**Transliteration:** shīzhēn
**Literal meaning:** lose + true/genuine
**Typical English translations:** distortion, loss of fidelity, inaccurate representation
**Usage:** Originally a technical term for signal distortion in audio or image reproduction, now also used metaphorically for accounts or representations that deviate from reality. Evaluates the degree to which a copy or report fails to match its original. Common in both technical and media-criticism contexts.
**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** 客观
**Transliteration:** kèguān
**Literal meaning:** guest/external + observe/view
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial, unbiased
**Usage:** Evaluates a judgment, account, or stance as based on external facts rather than personal perspective or interest. Widely used in academic, journalistic, and everyday evaluative discourse. Contrasts with 主观 (subjective).
**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** 主观
**Transliteration:** zhǔguān
**Literal meaning:** master/internal + observe/view
**Typical English translations:** subjective, personal, biased
**Usage:** Describes judgments or perceptions shaped by personal perspective, feeling, or interest rather than external fact. Often used to qualify or criticize an evaluation as insufficiently grounded in evidence. Contrasts with 客观.
**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** 本质
**Transliteration:** běnzhì
**Literal meaning:** root/origin + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, fundamental character
**Usage:** Refers to the intrinsic or defining nature of something, as opposed to its surface appearance. Used in philosophical, political, and everyday discourse to assert what something fundamentally is. Appears in constructions like 本质上 (essentially, in essence).
**[CORE]**

---

**21.**
**Form:** 实质
**Transliteration:** shízhì
**Literal meaning:** solid/real + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** substance, essence, real nature
**Usage:** Emphasizes the actual content or underlying reality of something, often in contrast to its formal or nominal character. Common in legal language (实质性问题, substantive issue) and philosophical discourse. Overlaps with 本质 but tends to foreground practical or legal substance.
**[CORE]**

**22.**
**Form:** 表里如一
**Transliteration:** biǎo lǐ rú yī
**Literal meaning:** outside + inside + like + one
**Typical English translations:** consistent inside and out, what one appears to be, genuine in character
**Usage:** A fixed expression evaluating a person or thing as having no discrepancy between outward appearance and inner reality. Used as a moral compliment, implying integrity and authenticity. Its implied opposite is 表里不一 (inconsistent, two-faced).
**[CORE]**

**23.**
**Form:** 弄虚作假
**Transliteration:** nòng xū zuò jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** to manipulate + emptiness + to make + false
**Typical English translations:** to engage in fraud, to falsify, to practice deception
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression describing deliberate falsification or fraudulent conduct, especially in official, academic, or commercial contexts. Commonly used in anti-corruption and regulatory discourse. Implies systematic rather than incidental deception.
**[CORE]**

**24.**
**Form:** 货真价实
**Transliteration:** huò zhēn jià shí
**Literal meaning:** goods + genuine + price + real/solid
**Typical English translations:** genuine goods at fair prices, authentic, the real thing
**Usage:** Originally a commercial expression affirming that goods are authentic and prices honest; now used more broadly to mean genuinely what it claims to be. Evaluates the correspondence between representation and reality in commercial and extended contexts. Carries a positive, affirming tone.
**[CORE]**

**25.**
**Form:** 鉴定
**Transliteration:** jiàndìng
**Literal meaning:** to examine/appraise + to determine/fix
**Typical English translations:** to authenticate, to appraise, to identify, authentication, appraisal
**Usage:** Refers to the expert evaluation of an object, document, or substance to determine its authenticity, nature, or quality. Central to legal (forensic identification), art-market, and archival contexts. Implies authoritative judgment by a qualified party.
**[CORE]**

---

**26.**
**Form:** 辨别
**Transliteration:** biànbié
**Literal meaning:** to distinguish + to differentiate
**Typical English translations:** to distinguish, to discern, to tell apart
**Usage:** Describes the cognitive or perceptual act of differentiating between things, including genuine from fake, right from wrong, or one kind from another. Used across moral, perceptual, and analytical contexts. Often collocates with 真假, 是非, or 好坏.
**[CORE]**

**27.**
**Form:** 是非
**Transliteration:** shìfēi
**Literal meaning:** is/right + is not/wrong
**Typical English translations:** right and wrong, truth and falsehood, merits of a case
**Usage:** A compound denoting the distinction between what is correct or right and what is not, used in moral, legal, and factual evaluations. Appears in expressions like 辨别是非 (to distinguish right from wrong) and 是非曲直 (rights and wrongs of a matter). Functions both as a noun and in compounds.
**[CORE]**

**28.**
**Form:** 原真性
**Transliteration:** yuánzhēnxìng
**Literal meaning:** original + true/genuine + -ness (nominalizing suffix)
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, originality, integrity (of heritage or artifacts)
**Usage:** A more specialized term used primarily in heritage conservation, museum studies, and cultural policy discourse, often as a translation of the technical concept "authenticity" in international frameworks (e.g., UNESCO documents). Less common in everyday speech. Reflects a domain-specific borrowing pattern.

**29.**
**Form:** 失实
**Transliteration:** shīshí
**Literal meaning:** lose + fact/reality
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, contrary to fact, misrepresenting the facts
**Usage:** Used to evaluate a statement, report, or account as failing to correspond to the facts, without necessarily implying deliberate deception. Common in journalistic and legal contexts (报道失实, inaccurate reporting). Implies a gap between representation and reality.
**[CORE]**

**30.**
**Form:** 道地
**Transliteration:** dàodì
**Literal meaning:** road/way + ground/place (etymology debated; possibly dialectal)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, of the proper kind, real (especially of products or skills)
**Usage:** Used colloquially to affirm that something — typically a product, skill, or cultural practice — is of the authentic, proper variety rather than an imitation. Common in contexts evaluating food, dialect, craftsmanship, or cultural authenticity. More colloquial and informal than 正宗.
