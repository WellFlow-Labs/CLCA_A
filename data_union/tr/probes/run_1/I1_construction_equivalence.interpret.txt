## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by BECOME

No construction in P4a or P4b is documented as encoding REVELATION as a distinct operator — that is, a construction specifically encoding the moment at which something hidden comes to light, or at which a truth is discovered by a subject. The BECOME construction (P4a #4) encodes state change (*meşrulaşmak*, *geçersiz hale gelmek*) but does not encode the perspectival or epistemic dimension of a truth being revealed to someone. The discourse marker construction (P4a #10) encodes corrective or sincerity framing (*aslında*, *doğrusu*) but this is a pragmatic hedge, not a predicate construction for revelation. On the basis of the P4a and P4b data, REVELATION as a canonical CLCA operator has no dedicated construction and is not systematically substituted by any single documented operator; the closest available form is the BECOME construction for state change, but the epistemic discovery dimension is not covered.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction

Both VERIFICATION (*doğrulama*) and DISCONFIRMATION (*yalanlama*, *yanlışlama*) are expressed through the same process nominalization construction (P4a #8: verb stem + *-ma/-me*). The distinction between them is entirely lexical: the base verb encodes whether the process confirms or refutes. There is no asymmetry in the construction itself. The ability/potential nominalization (P4a #9) also produces a parallel pair: *doğrulanabilirlik* (verifiability) and *yanlışlanabilirlik* (falsifiability), again using the same construction with different bases. This parallel is noted in P4a as directly encoding a philosophical distinction (logical positivist / Popperian), but the construction is the same for both.

### 2.3 NEGATION is analytic for adjectival predicates

For truth-domain adjectival predicates, negation is analytic: the invariant particle *değil* is inserted after the adjective and before any copular suffixes. This is the obligatory and only strategy for this construction type; no morphological negation on the adjective itself is available. For verbal predicates (e.g., the process nominalizations used as verb phrases), a separate morphological strategy (*-me/-ma* on the verb stem) applies, but this is a different construction domain. Within the ATTR and NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION constructions, the analytic *değil* strategy is the sole form.

P4b notes that double negation (*asılsız değil*, "not unfounded") is grammatical and used for rhetorical attenuation. This is structurally regular but pragmatically marked.

### 2.4 Asymmetry between morphological and periphrastic strategies in BECOME and CAUSATION

Both BECOME (P4a #4) and CAUSATION (P4a #5) show a consistent asymmetry: the morphological strategies (*-laş-* for inchoative, *-laştır-* for causative) are lexically restricted to a subset of P1 bases, while the periphrastic strategies (*hale gelmek* for inchoative, *kılmak* for causative) are universally available for all adjectives. This means that for items where the morphological strategy is unavailable or marginal (e.g., *doğrulaşmak* is noted as marginal; *geçersizleşmek* is noted as rare), the periphrastic form is the default. There is no documented case where the morphological strategy is available but the periphrastic is not; the asymmetry runs in one direction only.

### 2.5 CAUSATION (factual) and CAUSATION (character) are not documented as unified predicate constructions

Neither CAUSATION (factual) nor CAUSATION (character) appears in P4a or P4b as a labeled construction type with a dedicated predicate pattern. What is attested is:

- For factual causation: the causative construction (P4a #5, *-laştır-* / *kılmak*) encodes rendering something into a state, and the process nominalization (P4a #8) encodes the act of establishing or refuting a fact. These are related but not identical to a CAUSATION (factual) operator as typically understood (e.g., "X caused it to be true that P").
- For character causation: the agentive nominalization (P4a #7) encodes habitual or dispositional character roles (*yalancı*, *yanıltıcı*), but these are nominal/adjectival forms, not predicate constructions encoding that someone caused a character state.

No substitution pattern is documented for these operators; they are partially covered by adjacent constructions.

### 2.6 Discourse markers as a distinct functional class

P4a #10 documents a set of truth-domain items that have grammaticalized as sentence-initial discourse markers (*doğrusu*, *aslında*, *gerçek şu ki*, *hakikaten*). These do not map to any of the canonical CLCA operators as predicate constructions; they function as sincerity hedges or corrective frames at the discourse level. P4b confirms that *doğrusu* and *aslında* are fully grammaticalized and that their etymological compositionality is not synchronically active. These are noted here as a functional class present in the data that does not correspond to a canonical CLCA operator.

### 2.7 Approximation construction as a distinct epistemic pattern

P4a #11 documents a spatial metaphor construction (*doğruya en yakın*, *gerçekten uzak*) that encodes degrees of correspondence to truth. This construction does not map directly to any canonical CLCA operator; it is a degree or approximation modifier rather than a predicate or nominalization. It is noted here as an attested pattern without a canonical CLCA equivalent.

### 2.8 Contrastive distinction construction

P4a #12 documents a concessive construction (*ama* / *olmakla birlikte* + *değil*) used to mark conceptual distinctions between paired truth-domain terms (*yasal ama meşru değil*). This is a syntactic pattern rather than a lexical or morphological operator. It does not map to a canonical CLCA operator but is noted as a productive pattern in the data for marking near-synonym distinctions.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **REVELATION** | No dedicated construction encoding the moment of discovery or coming-to-light of a truth is attested in P4a or P4b. The BECOME construction covers state change but not the epistemic/perspectival dimension of revelation. **MISSING (structural gap)** |
| **CAUSATION (factual) as a unified predicate construction** | No single construction type is documented as encoding "X caused it to be the case that P is true/false." The causative construction (P4a #5) and process nominalization (P4a #8) cover adjacent meanings but not this operator as a unified predicate. **MISSING (structural gap)** |
| **CAUSATION (character) as a predicate construction** | No predicate construction encoding "X caused Y to become a liar / deceiver" is documented. Character attribution is expressed through agentive nominalization (nominal/adjectival forms), not through a dedicated predicate construction. **MISSING (structural gap)** |

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **Morphological inchoative for some P1 bases** | *Doğrulaşmak* is noted as marginal; *geçersizleşmek* is noted as rare. These are lexical gaps within the *-laş-* strategy, not gaps in the overall BECOME operator (covered by periphrastic alternative). |
| **Morphological causative for some P1 bases** | The *-laştır-* strategy is lexically restricted; not all P1 bases accept it. Covered by periphrastic *kılmak*. |
| **Arabic *-iyet* nominalization** | Restricted to Arabic-origin bases (*meşruiyet*, *hakkaniyet*); not available for native Turkish bases. This is a lexical restriction, not a gap in the NOMINALIZATION operator overall. |

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps — that is, cases where the meaning is structurally blocked or unrepresentable — are documented in P4a or P4b. All noted absences are either structural gaps (no dedicated construction) or lexical restrictions (some bases excluded from a strategy that has an available alternative).

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

| Asymmetry | Description |
|---|---|
| **Positive vs. negative BECOME morphology** | The *-laş-* inchoative is noted as marginal for *doğrulaşmak* (becoming true/correct) but productive for *meşrulaşmak* (becoming legitimate). No parallel asymmetry is documented for the negative/falsity direction specifically; the periphrastic alternative covers both. |
| **Potential nominalization: positive and negative forms both attested but register-restricted** | Both *doğrulanabilirlik* (verifiability) and *yanlışlanabilirlik* (falsifiability) are attested but both are marked [MARGINAL] and restricted to philosophical/academic registers. The asymmetry here is register-based, not positive/negative. |
| **Agentive nominalization: adjectival use more frequent than nominal use for *-ıcı* forms** | P4b notes that *yanıltıcı* and similar *-ıcı* forms are more commonly used adjectivally than as pure agent nouns. This is a frequency asymmetry within the agentive nominalization construction, not a gap. |
