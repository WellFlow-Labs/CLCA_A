# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested derivational morphology for the 30 P1 base items. The P1b root expansion has already documented derivations from the three central roots (tosi/tode-, oikea/oikeu-, väärä/vääre-) in considerable detail. This step therefore focuses on the remaining P1 items not covered as base forms in P1b, while cross-referencing P1b material where a P1 item is itself a derived form that generates further derivations. Forms already fully documented in P1b are noted with a cross-reference rather than repeated in full.

---

## Item 1: tosi

**Base Form:** tosi
**Note:** Derivations from this base are documented in P1b Root 1. Cross-reference: P1b Root 1, items 1–30 and peripheral extensions. The most directly derived forms are: *totuus* (truth), *todellinen* (real), *todellisuus* (reality), *todistaa* (to prove/testify), *todentaa* (to verify), *todennäköinen* (probable). No additional derivations from *tosi* as a base are identified beyond those documented in P1b.

## Item 3: aito

**Base Form:** aito

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitous
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -us (nominalizing suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** authenticity; genuineness
6. **Usage:** The standard nominal form for authenticity as an abstract property, used in philosophical, cultural, and commercial discourse (*tuotteen aitous* — "the authenticity of the product"; *tunteiden aitous* — "the genuineness of the emotions"). Appears in both evaluative and philosophical contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitoutua
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -utua (reflexive/inchoative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to become authentic; to become genuine
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical and psychological discourse to describe a process by which something or someone comes to be or becomes authentic (*ihminen voi aitoutua kokemusten kautta* — "a person can become authentic through experiences"). Less common than *aitous* but attested in existentialist and humanistic contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aitouttaa
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -uttaa (causative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to authenticate; to make authentic; to certify as genuine
6. **Usage:** Used in administrative and commercial contexts to refer to the act of certifying or confirming that something is genuine (*aitouttaa asiakirja* — "to authenticate a document"). Less common than *todentaa* in administrative usage but attested.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** aito
2. **Derived Form:** aidosti
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** aito + -sti (adverbial suffix)

6. **Usage:** Used as a sentence adverb or modifier to assert that something is done or experienced in a genuine manner (*aidosti kiinnostunut* — "genuinely interested"; *aidosti suomalainen* — "authentically Finnish"). Common in everyday and journalistic discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form 5**

3. **Transliteration:** N/A

5. **Natural English translation:** inauthentic; not genuine; fake; spurious
6. **Usage:** The direct negation of *aito*, documented as a P1 item (item 30). Generates its own nominal *epäaitous* (inauthenticity).
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Derived Form 6**

**Derived Form 3**

3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** epä- (negation prefix) + todellinen
5. **Natural English translation:** unreal; not real; surreal; illusory
6. **Usage:** Used to characterize something as lacking the quality of being real, either in a philosophical sense or in everyday discourse to describe something that feels dreamlike or impossible (*tilanne tuntui epätodelliselta* — "the situation felt unreal"). Attested in literary, psychological, and everyday contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** todellinen
2. **Derived Form:** epätodellisuus
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **Literal Breakdown:** epä- + todellinen + -uus
5. **Natural English translation:** unreality; the unreal
6. **Usage:** The nominal form of *epätodellinen*, used in philosophical and literary discourse to refer to the quality or domain of the unreal (*unen epätodellisuus* — "the unreality of the dream"). Less common than *todellisuus* but attested.

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on luotettava:** *Luotettava* is itself a passive present participle of *luottaa* (to trust). The verb *luottaa* generates a broader cluster including *luottamus* (trust, confidence), *luottamuksellinen* (confidential, trustful), *luottamuksellisuus* (confidentiality), *luottamushenkilö* (elected representative, person of trust), and *epäluottamus* (distrust, no-confidence). These are derivations of *luottaa* rather than of *luotettava* directly, and are noted here for completeness without full documentation, as *luottaa* is not itself a P1 item.
