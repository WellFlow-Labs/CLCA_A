## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### REVELATION substitutes for BECOME
Greek strongly prefers REVELATION constructions (βγαίνω/αποκαλύπτω + η αλήθεια) over BECOME constructions (γίνομαι + TRUTH-ADJ) when expressing truth emergence. The P4b data marks BECOME as [MARGINAL] with the note: "More natural with correctness/clarity terms (σωστός, σαφής) than with core truth terms (αλήθεια). Truth is typically conceptualized as static rather than emergent."

This represents a conceptual preference: truth is not something that *becomes* but rather something that *is revealed* or *comes to light*. The BECOME construction is available for correctness and clarity (γίνεται σωστό "it becomes correct," γίνεται σαφής "it becomes clear") but resisted for core truth vocabulary.

### VERIFICATION as middle/passive voice
Greek expresses VERIFICATION through the middle/passive voice construction αληθεύομαι rather than through a separate causative or transitive verb. This construction (αληθεύτηκε η πρόβλεψη "the prediction proved true") is marked [MARGINAL] in P4b, indicating it is less common than active forms but productive for expressing confirmation of predictions or claims.

### DISCONFIRMATION through morphological causative
Greek uses the morphological causative ακυρώνω "invalidate" (from άκυρος "invalid") rather than analytic causation for DISCONFIRMATION. This is marked [CORE] and "highly productive with institutional objects (documents, decisions, contracts)." The pattern is restricted to formal/institutional contexts.

### Analytic vs. Lexical NEGATION
Greek employs both strategies equally:
- **Analytic**: δεν + positive term (δεν είναι αλήθεια "is not true")
- **Lexical**: inherently negative terms (είναι ψέμα "is a lie," είναι λάθος "is wrong")

P4b notes: "Both analytic negation (δεν + positive) and lexical negation (ψέμα, λάθος) are equally productive. Choice depends on emphasis and register."

### Asymmetry in agentive nominalization
Greek shows strong productivity for **negative agents** (ψεύτης "liar," ψεύτρα "female liar," ψευταράς "big liar," απατεώνας "swindler") but lacks common positive equivalents. P4a notes: "No common agentive noun for 'truth-teller' in everyday use (αληθολόγος exists but is marginal/literary)."

This represents an asymmetric gap where the morphological pattern is available but culturally/lexically restricted to negative characterizations.

### Light verb constructions as obligatory patterns
Greek requires light verbs for two core operations:
- **Truth-telling**: λέω + την αλήθεια/ψέματα (obligatory; no single-word verb in colloquial Greek)
- **Error-making**: κάνω + λάθος (obligatory; λάθος does not function as verb)

P4a explicitly notes: "The verb λέω is obligatory; no single-word verb equivalent to English 'lie' exists (*ψεμίζω is not standard)." The formal alternative ψεύδομαι exists but is marked as "more formal" in P4b.

### Morphological vs. Analytic CAUSATION
Greek shows a split:
- **Analytic causation** (κάνω + adjective) is marked [MARGINAL] for truth domain
- **Morphological causatives** (ακυρώνω "invalidate," βεβαιώνω "certify") are [CORE] in formal/institutional contexts

P4a notes: "Greek has both analytic causatives (κάνω + adjective) and morphological causatives (ακυρώνω 'invalidate,' βεβαιώνω 'certify'). The morphological pattern is productive for legal/administrative terms but not for core truth vocabulary."

Critically, P4a identifies a lexical gap: "While ακυρώνω 'invalidate' exists, there is no parallel *αληθώνω 'make true' or *σωστώνω 'make correct.' Causation is expressed analytically (κάνω σωστό)."

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps

1. **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction attested for causing someone to become a truth-teller or liar as a character trait. Greek can express causing someone to lie on a specific occasion (through general causative constructions) but lacks a specialized construction for character causation in the truth domain.

2. **Degree modification of core truth terms**: P4a explicitly notes: "Greek does not commonly use degree modifiers (*'very true,' *'somewhat false') with core truth vocabulary. Instead, it uses different lexical items (απόλυτη αλήθεια 'absolute truth') or emphatic constructions."

3. **Comparative truth constructions**: P4a notes: "No standard construction for comparing truth-values (*'more true than'). Greek uses alternative strategies (πιο σωστό 'more correct' works for correctness but not for truth per se)."

### Lexical Gaps

1. **Single-word causative for "make true"**: P4a explicitly identifies this gap: "While ακυρώνω 'invalidate' exists, there is no parallel *αληθώνω 'make true' or *σωστώνω 'make correct.'"

2. **Common positive agentive nominalization**: P4a notes: "No common agentive noun for 'truth-teller' in everyday use (αληθολόγος exists but is marginal/literary)."

3. **Single-word verb for "lie" in colloquial Greek**: P4a notes: "The verb λέω is obligatory; no single-word verb equivalent to English 'lie' exists (*ψεμίζω is not standard). The verb ψεύδομαι exists but is more formal."

### Conceptual Gaps

1. **BECOME with core truth terms**: Not a complete gap but a semantic restriction. P4b marks this [MARGINAL] with the explanation: "Truth is typically conceptualized as static rather than emergent." The construction exists but is conceptually blocked for core truth vocabulary, preferring REVELATION instead.

2. **Evidential marking on truth terms**: P4a notes: "Greek does not grammatically mark evidentiality on truth vocabulary. Source of knowledge is expressed through separate constructions, not integrated into truth-marking."

3. **Habitual truth-telling constructions**: P4a notes: "No specialized aspectual construction for habitual truth-telling distinct from general habitual aspect marking."

### Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization**: Highly productive for negative agents (ψεύτης, απατεώνας) but no common positive equivalent in everyday use.

2. **Morphological causatives**: ακυρώνω "invalidate" exists but no parallel *αληθώνω "make true."

3. **Color modification**: Productive with ψέμα (άσπρο ψέμα "white lie," μαύρο ψέμα "black lie") but P4b notes this is "specific to ψέμα (not productive with other truth terms)."

4. **Living construction**: P4b notes ζω στο ψέμα "live a lie" is "specific to ψέμα (not used with λάθος)."

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available

- **ATTR**: X είναι TRUTH-TERM (standard copular construction)
- **ADNOM**: TRUTH-ADJ + NOUN (standard attributive modification)
- **REVELATION**: βγαίνω/αποκαλύπτω + η αλήθεια (intransitive and transitive forms)
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: ακυρώνω + OBJECT (morphological causative for invalidation)
- **NEGATION**: Both analytic (δεν + positive) and lexical (inherently negative terms)
- **NOMINALIZATION (abstract)**: TRUTH-ROOT + -ότητα/-εια/-ία (highly productive)

### Available with Substitution

- **BECOME**: Available as γίνομαι + TRUTH-ADJ but marginal with core truth terms; **REVELATION substitutes** for truth emergence
- **VERIFICATION**: Available as αληθεύομαι (middle/passive voice) but marginal frequency; more commonly expressed through active revelation constructions

### Partially Available

- **CAUSATION (factual)**: 
  - Analytic form (κάνω + adjective) marked [MARGINAL]
  - Morphological causatives available for specific operations (ακυρώνω "invalidate," βεβαιώνω "certify") but **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** for general "make true"
  - Restricted to correctness domain (κάνω σωστό) rather than core truth domain

- **NOMINALIZATION (agentive)**: 
  - Fully productive for negative agents (ψεύτης, απατεώνας)
  - **Asymmetric gap**: no common positive agentive in everyday use

### Missing Entirely

- **CAUSATION (character)**: No specialized construction for causing someone to become a truth-teller or liar as a character trait
- **Degree modification**: No productive pattern for degree modification of core truth terms (*"very true")
- **Comparative constructions**: No standard construction for comparing truth-values (*"more true than")
- **Evidential marking**: No grammatical integration of evidentiality into truth-marking

## 5. Additional Observations

### Multi-word expressions functioning as operators

Several multi-word expressions function as complete operators:

1. **λέω + την αλήθεια/ψέματα**: Functions as verbal operator for truth-telling/lying (light verb construction)
2. **κάνω + λάθος**: Functions as verbal operator for error-making (light verb construction)
3. **πιάνω + X + στο ψέμα/λάθος**: Functions as verbal operator for catching in falsehood
4. **ζω + στο ψέμα**: Functions as verbal operator for living in falsehood

These are not circumlocutions but grammaticalized multi-word operators marked [CORE] in P4b.

### Prefix productivity

Two prefixes show exceptional productivity:

1. **ψευδο-** (pseudo-): "Exceptionally productive in Greek, creating compounds across multiple domains (scientific, legal, religious). Documented extensively in P1b. Pattern remains productive for new coinages."

2. **ορθο-** (ortho-): "Highly productive, particularly in technical, religious, and educational domains. Creates compounds indicating both physical straightness and abstract correctness."

These function as productive morphological operators for creating new truth-domain vocabulary.

### Register stratification

Several constructions show register restrictions:

- **ψεύδομαι** (formal) vs. **λέω ψέματα** (colloquial) for "lie"
- **αληθεύομαι** (formal) vs. active alternatives for verification
- **Morphological causatives** (formal/institutional) vs. analytic causatives (general)

This represents functional equivalence across registers rather than true gaps.
