# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Note on script and transliteration:** Tagalog is written in the Latin alphabet in standard modern use; native Baybayin script is not in general contemporary use for these lexical items. Transliteration column is therefore not applicable for most entries. Where Spanish-derived orthography differs from pronunciation, pronunciation notes are added.

---

## Lexical Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** totoo
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *totoo*; possibly a reduplication-derived form; cognate with related Austronesian forms meaning "real, actual"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Usage description:** *Totoo* is the primary everyday term for asserting that a statement or situation corresponds to reality. It is used both predicatively (*Totoo iyan* — "That is true") and as a discourse particle affirming sincerity. It participates in productive derivation: *katotohanan* (truth, the abstract noun), *totoong-totoo* (intensified: very real/true).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-an* (nominalizing circumfix forming abstract nouns); "the state or quality of being *totoo*"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, the truth of the matter
**Usage description:** *Katotohanan* is the standard abstract noun for truth, used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse. It frequently appears in formal declarations and moral exhortations (*Sabihin ang katotohanan* — "Tell the truth"). It is the expected translation equivalent of "truth" in Bible translations and legal oaths.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** tunay
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *tunay*; etymology not fully established; possibly from Proto-Philippine stock
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Usage description:** *Tunay* emphasizes authenticity or genuineness, often applied to persons, objects, or relationships rather than propositions. *Tunay na kaibigan* means "a true/genuine friend," and *tunay na ginto* means "real gold." It contrasts with *huwad* (fake) and *peke* (counterfeit) and participates in the abstract nominal *katunayan* (proof, evidence, genuineness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** katunayan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *ka-* + *tunay* + *-an*; "the state of being *tunay*"; also functions as "proof" or "evidence"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, genuineness, verification
**Usage description:** *Katunayan* is used both as an abstract noun meaning "genuineness" and, very commonly, as a discourse connector meaning "in fact" or "as a matter of fact" (*Sa katunayan* — "In fact / As a matter of fact"). In legal and formal contexts it carries the sense of evidence or proof that establishes something as real.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** tama
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *tama*; from Proto-Philippine; core meaning relates to correctness, hitting a mark
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, proper
**Usage description:** *Tama* is the standard term for correctness of answers, judgments, actions, or measurements. It is used in evaluating factual claims (*Tama ang sagot* — "The answer is correct"), moral conduct, and procedural propriety. It contrasts with *mali* (wrong, incorrect) and participates in derivations such as *katamaan* and the adverb *nang tama* (correctly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** mali
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *mali*; Proto-Philippine origin; core meaning is error, wrongness
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous
**Usage description:** *Mali* is the primary antonym of *tama*, used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong. It applies across factual (*Mali ang datos* — "The data is wrong"), moral (*Mali ang ginawa mo* — "What you did was wrong"), and procedural domains. The abstract noun *kamalian* means "error" or "mistake."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** huwad
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *huwad*; indigenous Tagalog term
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, imitation
**Usage description:** *Huwad* denotes something that falsely presents itself as genuine — a counterfeit document, a false identity, or an imitation object. It is used in legal contexts (*huwad na dokumento* — "forged/counterfeit document") and in moral evaluation of deceptive representations. It is somewhat more formal or literary than *peke* in contemporary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** peke
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *fake* via English, or directly from English *fake*; fully nativized in colloquial Tagalog
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, phony
**Usage description:** *Peke* is the colloquial, widely used term for something counterfeit or inauthentic — fake goods, false credentials, or insincere behavior. It is used more informally than *huwad* and is extremely common in everyday speech. *Peke* can also be applied to persons acting inauthentically (*Peke ka* — "You're being fake/phony").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** lehitimo
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *legítimo*; "legitimate"
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, genuine, rightful
**Usage description:** *Lehitimo* is used in legal, political, and formal contexts to evaluate whether a claim, authority, or status is rightfully held and properly established. *Lehitimong anak* means "legitimate child" (in the legal sense), and *lehitimong pamahalaan* means "legitimate government." It is a borrowing that has stabilized in formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** wasto
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *vasto* or possibly *justo*; the exact etymological path is debated; fully integrated into Tagalog
**Typical English translations:** correct, proper, right, accurate
**Usage description:** *Wasto* overlaps with *tama* in meaning "correct" but tends to emphasize propriety, appropriateness, or conformity to a standard. It is commonly used in evaluating grammar (*wastong gamit* — "correct/proper usage"), procedure, and moral conduct. *Kawastuhan* is the corresponding abstract noun meaning "correctness" or "propriety."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** tapat
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *tapat*; core spatial meaning is "directly facing, opposite"; extended to mean "straightforward, honest"
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, straightforward, loyal, faithful
**Usage description:** *Tapat* evaluates persons or statements as honest and without concealment. *Maging tapat* means "to be honest/straightforward," and *tapatan* means to speak frankly with someone. The abstract noun *katapatan* means "honesty," "sincerity," or "loyalty/faithfulness," covering both epistemic and moral dimensions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** katapatan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *ka-* + *tapat* + *-an*; "the quality of being *tapat*"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, faithfulness, loyalty
**Usage description:** *Katapatan* is used as an abstract noun covering both the epistemic sense (honesty in speech) and the relational-moral sense (faithfulness, loyalty). It appears in formal declarations, vows, and moral discourse. The semantic range is broader than English "honesty" alone, encompassing fidelity to persons and commitments.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** mapagkakatiwalaan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *ma-* (potential/quality prefix) + *pagkakatiwalaan* (verbal noun from *tiwala*, "trust"); "able to be trusted"
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage description:** *Mapagkakatiwalaan* is the standard adjectival form for evaluating persons, sources, or institutions as worthy of trust. It is used in contexts of evaluating witnesses, information sources, and persons in positions of responsibility. The root *tiwala* (trust, confidence) is highly productive in Tagalog derivation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** tiwala
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *tiwala*; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is trust, confidence, reliance
**Typical English translations:** trust, confidence, reliance, faith (in a person or institution)
**Usage description:** *Tiwala* is used to evaluate the reliability and trustworthiness of persons, institutions, and claims. *May tiwala ako sa kanya* means "I trust him/her." It is foundational to evaluations of credibility and is highly productive morphologically, generating *mapagkakatiwalaan*, *pagtitiwalaan*, *tiwalahin*, and others.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** kredible
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From English *credible*, via borrowing; used in journalistic and legal registers
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable
**Usage description:** *Kredible* is used in formal, journalistic, and legal contexts to evaluate whether a source, witness, or claim merits belief. It is a relatively recent borrowing and is more common in written and broadcast media than in everyday speech. It coexists with native alternatives such as *mapagkakatiwalaan* but occupies a more specifically epistemic niche.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**16.**
**Form:** akma
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *akma*; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is fit, suited, matching
**Typical English translations:** fitting, appropriate, accurate (of a match or fit), suitable
**Usage description:** *Akma* evaluates whether something corresponds correctly to a standard, requirement, or other item — a translation that is accurate, a size that fits, or a description that matches reality. *Akma ang paglalarawan* means "the description is accurate/fitting." It is used in aesthetic, practical, and evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**17.**
**Form:** angkop
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *angkop*; indigenous Tagalog; meaning centers on suitability and appropriateness
**Typical English translations:** appropriate, suitable, fitting, proper
**Usage description:** *Angkop* evaluates whether something is appropriate or suitable to a context, standard, or purpose. It is used in moral, aesthetic, and practical evaluations (*angkop na pag-uugali* — "appropriate behavior"). It overlaps with *akma* but tends more toward normative appropriateness than physical or descriptive fit.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**18.**
**Form:** tumpak
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *tumpak*; indigenous Tagalog; core meaning is precise, exact, hitting the mark
**Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate, spot-on
**Usage description:** *Tumpak* emphasizes precision and exactness, used to evaluate statements, translations, measurements, or descriptions that hit the mark exactly. *Tumpak ang sinabi mo* means "What you said is exactly right/precise." It is stronger than *tama* in implying exactness rather than mere correctness.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**19.**
**Form:** sigurado
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *seguro*; "sure, certain, secure"
**Typical English translations:** sure, certain, definite, reliable
**Usage description:** *Sigurado* is used to evaluate the certainty of a claim or the reliability of a person or thing. *Sigurado ka ba?* means "Are you sure?" It is used both as a predicate adjective and as a discourse marker of epistemic certainty. It is fully integrated into colloquial and formal Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**20.**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *maka-* (prefix indicating alignment with or capacity for) + *totohanan* (from *totoo*); "in accordance with truth, realistic"
**Typical English translations:** realistic, truthful, true-to-life, verisimilar
**Usage description:** *Makatotohanan* is used in aesthetic and narrative contexts to evaluate whether a representation, portrayal, or depiction corresponds faithfully to reality. It is commonly applied to literature, film, and art (*makatotohanang pagkukuwento* — "realistic/truthful storytelling"). It is less common in purely propositional contexts than *totoo*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**21.**
**Form:** orihinal
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *original*; "original"
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic (as opposed to copy or imitation)
**Usage description:** *Orihinal* evaluates whether something is the genuine source item rather than a copy, reproduction, or imitation. It is used in legal, commercial, and aesthetic contexts (*orihinal na kopya* — "original copy [of a document]"; *orihinal na obra* — "original work"). It coexists with *tunay* but specifically foregrounds the original-versus-copy distinction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**22.**
**Form:** awtentiko
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *auténtico* or English *authentic*; "authentic"
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage description:** *Awtentiko* is used in formal, legal, and commercial contexts to evaluate whether an item or document is genuinely what it purports to be. It is more formal and less colloquial than *tunay* or *orihinal* and appears frequently in legal certification language. Its distribution is somewhat narrower than *tunay*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**23.**
**Form:** lehitimasyon
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish/English *legitimación/legitimation*; the process of establishing legitimacy
**Typical English translations:** legitimation, legitimization
**Usage description:** *Lehitimasyon* appears in legal and political discourse to refer to the process by which a claim, status, or authority is formally established as legitimate. It is a domain-specific term found primarily in legal documents and academic writing rather than everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**24.**
**Form:** pagpapatunay
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *pag-* (verbal noun prefix) + *pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an* (locative/applicative); "the act of causing something to be verified/proven"
**Typical English translations:** verification, authentication, confirmation, proof
**Usage description:** *Pagpapatunay* is the verbal noun for the act of verifying, authenticating, or proving something. It is used in legal, administrative, and formal contexts (*pagpapatunay ng dokumento* — "authentication/verification of a document"). It is morphologically transparent and productive within the *tunay* family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**25.**
**Form:** pagsisinungaling
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *pag-* + *si-* + *sinungaling* (liar, one who lies); verbal noun meaning "the act of lying"
**Typical English translations:** lying, telling a lie, falsehood (as an act)
**Usage description:** *Pagsisinungaling* is the verbal noun for the act of lying or deceiving through false statements. It is used in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts to characterize conduct as dishonest. The root *sinungaling* (liar) is itself a common and productive form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** sinungaling
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root *sinungaling*; etymology debated; functions as both noun (liar) and adjective (lying, false)
**Typical English translations:** liar, lying, false (of a person)
**Usage description:** *Sinungaling* is the standard term for a person who lies or for characterizing someone as dishonest. It is used as a direct predicate (*Sinungaling ka* — "You are a liar") and in nominal constructions. It is one of the most common terms in the semantic field of deception and dishonesty in Tagalog.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *ka-* + *sinungaling* + *-an*; abstract noun meaning "the quality or instance of lying; a lie; falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage description:** *Kasinungalingan* is the abstract noun for a lie or falsehood, used to evaluate statements as false and deceptive. It contrasts directly with *katotohanan* (truth) and appears in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. *Puno ng kasinungalingan* means "full of lies/falsehood."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**28.**
**Form:** pruweba
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From Spanish *prueba*; "proof, evidence, test"
**Typical English translations:** proof, evidence, test
**Usage description:** *Pruweba* is used in legal and everyday contexts to refer to evidence or proof that establishes the truth or falsity of a claim. *Walang pruweba* means "there is no proof/evidence." It is fully integrated into colloquial and formal Tagalog and coexists with *katunayan* in the sense of evidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** ebidensya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** From English *evidence* (via Spanish *evidencia*); fully nativized in legal and journalistic registers
**Typical English translations:** evidence
**Usage description:** *Ebidensya* is the standard term in legal and journalistic contexts for evidence used to evaluate the truth of claims or the guilt or innocence of persons. It is more specifically legal-procedural in connotation than *pruweba* and is the expected term in courtroom and investigative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**30.**
**Form:** pagkakamali
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** *pag-* + *ka-* + *mali* + *-an* (verbal noun of *magkamali*, "to make a mistake"); "the act or instance of being wrong/making an error"
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, fault
**Usage description:** *Pagkakamali* is the standard nominal form for a mistake or error, used to evaluate actions, statements, or judgments as having gone wrong. *Aminin ang pagkakamali* means "to admit one's mistake." It is used across factual, moral, and procedural domains and is morphologically transparent from the root *mali*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
