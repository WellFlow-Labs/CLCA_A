Below are attested derivational morphology items associated with the P1 lexical items. I include only forms that are genuinely used or strongly supported by the P1/P1b material and common Modern Greek usage. Where no common derivation is clearly supported, I state so explicitly.

---

### 1. Base Form: **αλήθεια**
1. **Derived Form:** αληθινός  
   **Transliteration:** alithinós  
   **Literal Breakdown:** from *αλήθεια* + adjectival formation  
   **Natural English translation:** true; real; genuine  
   **Usage description:** Common adjective for something genuine or real rather than fake, superficial, or merely apparent. It is widely used for people, feelings, stories, and objects.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **Derived Form:** αληθής  
   **Transliteration:** alithís  
   **Literal Breakdown:** learned adjectival formation related to *αλήθεια*  
   **Natural English translation:** true; correct  
   **Usage description:** More formal than *αληθινός*, especially for statements, claims, declarations, and logical truth values. Frequent in legal, academic, and formal prose.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **Derived Form:** αληθεύω  
   **Transliteration:** alithevo  
   **Literal Breakdown:** verbal formation from αληθ-  
   **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth; to be true  
   **Usage description:** Used both for speaking truthfully and for a claim or report proving true. It is established, though often somewhat more formal than everyday *λέω την αλήθεια*.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **Derived Form:** αληθοφανής  
   **Transliteration:** alithofanís  
   **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + -οφανής “appearing”  
   **Natural English translation:** plausible; truth-like; lifelike  
   **Usage description:** Refers to something that appears true or convincing without necessarily being true. Common in literary criticism, rhetoric, and everyday evaluation of explanations or narratives.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **Derived Form:** αληθοφάνεια  
   **Transliteration:** alithofáneia  
   **Literal Breakdown:** from *αληθοφανής*  
   **Natural English translation:** plausibility; verisimilitude  
   **Usage description:** Used for the quality of seeming true or believable, especially in literature, theater, film, and rhetoric. It concerns appearance of truth rather than truth itself.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **Derived Form:** αληθώς  
   **Transliteration:** alithós  
   **Literal Breakdown:** learned adverb from αληθ-  
   **Natural English translation:** truly; indeed  
   **Usage description:** Elevated or literary adverb, also found in religious or rhetorical language. It is not common in ordinary colloquial speech.  
   **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
