# G7b — Empirical vs. AOML Constraint Comparison

---

## Preliminary Notes

The empirical matrix (G7a) was derived inductively from F-phase data across 8 languages. The AOML matrix is a theoretical model derived from a separate 16-language CLCA protocol. The comparison below maps between them without forcing alignment. Where structures are incommensurable, this is noted explicitly.

The empirical matrix uses 13 semantic dimensions (D1–D13) and 20 constructional functions (CF1–CF20). The AOML matrix uses 7 primary axes (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, VERIF, LIFE), 2 meta-axes (DEG, TEMP), 7 core operators (ATTR, CAUSE, BECOME, RESULT, ABSTR, STRAT, CONTR), 4 levels, and 6 structural rules (R1–R6). The comparison proceeds dimension-by-dimension and then by operator/rule.

## Summary

### Confirmed Patterns

**1. ATTR operator universally licensed across all axes (CF1, CF2, CF3).**
Predicative attribution, negation, and adnominal modification are ALLOWED for all empirical dimensions. This matches AOML's prediction that ATTR is universally licensed across all primary axes. The match is clean and consistent across all 8 languages and all 13 empirical dimensions.

**2. AOML R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry) is strongly supported.**
The empirical finding that positive causation of authenticity is blocked (cannot manufacture genuine goods) while negative causation is licensed (can forge, destroy authenticity) directly matches R3. This is one of the clearest AOML-empirical matches in the dataset. The asymmetry is documented across all 8 languages and is consistent with AOML's target-type analysis (ontological properties cannot be produced de novo externally).

**3. AOML R4 (Positive Emergence Bias) is strongly supported.**
The empirical finding that positive inchoative constructions (CF4) are consistently more productive than negative inchoative constructions (CF5) for propositional truth (D1) matches R4's prediction that negative emergence is marked or dispreferred. The asymmetry is documented across all 8 languages, with Finnish explicitly lacking a common expression for "becoming untrue" and Yoruba lacking constructions for both directions.

**4. ABSTR operator licensed for VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, VERIF (CF8).**
Abstract nominalization is ALLOWED for all empirical dimensions where AOML predicts it is licensed. The match is consistent across all 8 languages.

**5. CONTR operator universally licensed (CF18, CF19, CF20).**
Contrastive/corrective frames, concessive constructions, and compound formation with [FALSE+N] are ALLOWED across all languages. This matches AOML's prediction that CONTR is universally licensed across all primary axes.

**6. VER-VERIF structural separation is empirically supported.**
The empirical finding that verification verbs substitute for direct causatives of truth adjectives (CF6 for D1) is consistent with AOML's structural separation of VER (factual correspondence) and VERIF (evidential establishment). The substitution pattern — where "prove" and "confirm" replace "make true" — reflects the AOML prediction that VERIF runs claim→world while VER is a state to be discovered, not produced.

**7. RESULT operator licensed for VER and AUTH (CF15, CF16).**
Discovery/revelation constructions are ALLOWED across all languages. This matches AOML's prediction that RESULT × VER and RESULT × AUTH are licensed. The cross-language preference for revelation constructions over inchoative constructions for truth emergence is consistent with AOML's architecture.

**8. ABSTR × LIFE = blocked is partially supported.**
AOML predicts that abstract nominalization of LIFE is universally blocked. The empirical data does not directly test this for the LIFE axis proper (representational fidelity), but the absence of abstract nominalizations for lifelikeness/verisimilitude as a distinct category in the empirical data is consistent with this prediction. The conflict noted below (similarity nominalizations) is conditional on whether similarity belongs to LIFE.

### Partial or Conditional Matches

**1. CAUSE × VER = restricted (CF6 for D1).**
AOML predicts restricted; empirical data shows VARIABLE with verification verbs substituting for direct causatives. The match is directional: both predict non-free causation of propositional truth. The substitution pattern is consistent with AOML's architecture but not directly predicted by it.

**2. BECOME × VER = restricted (CF4 for D1).**
AOML predicts restricted; empirical data shows VARIABLE (5 allow, 2 marginal, 1 absent). The match is directional. The variation across languages (yo and ko most restricted; ka, fi, tr most permissive) is consistent with AOML's "restricted" classification but the cross-language variation is not explained by AOML.

**3. DEG[VER] and R5 (CF11 for D1).**
R5 prohibits DEG[VER] from substituting for VER in inference. Empirical data shows that some languages resist degree modification of truth predicates entirely (treating truth as binary). The empirical finding is stronger than R5 predicts (grammatical restriction vs. inferential restriction). The empirical finding may be a grammatical reflex of R5's logical constraint, but the two are not identical.

**4. SINC axis and D5 (Honesty/Moral Character).**
AOML's SINC axis covers honesty of intent and character. Empirical D5 covers honesty and moral character. The mapping is close but not perfect: AOML's SINC includes reliability as a subregion, while empirical data treats reliability as a distinct dimension (D7). The AOML merger may lose empirical distinctions.

**5. NORM axis and D6/D10.**
AOML's NORM axis covers moral, legal, and procedural rightness. Empirical data distinguishes D6 (Moral Rightness and Justice) from D10 (Validity and Legitimacy). Both map to NORM, but the empirical finding that D10 × CF6 is ALLOWED while D6 × CF6 is VARIABLE suggests that institutional validity (D10) and moral rightness (D6) behave differently under causation. AOML's single NORM axis does not predict this difference.

**6. Provisional REVEAL operator and CF15/CF16.**
The provisional REVEAL operator is licensed for VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, VERIF and blocked for LIFE. Empirical data shows strong cross-language preference for revelation/discovery constructions over inchoative constructions for truth emergence. This is consistent with REVEAL being licensed for VER. However, the empirical data does not clearly distinguish REVEAL from RESULT + STRAT in all cases, which is the key diagnostic AOML uses to justify REVEAL as a separate primitive.

### Conflicts

**1. D5 × CF6 (Causative of honesty) vs. AOML R1.**
AOML R1 predicts universal blocking of CAUSE × SINC (p < 1e-9, 16 of 16 languages). Empirical G7a rates D5 × CF6 as VARIABLE rather than BLOCKED. The conflict may be terminological: empirical "make someone honest" includes enabling/facilitating constructions (encourage, teach, create conditions for) that AOML may classify differently from direct causation. If AOML's CAUSE is restricted to direct causation and excludes enabling/facilitating, R1 may still hold. The empirical data does not clearly distinguish these. This conflict requires clarification of AOML's CAUSE operator scope before it can be resolved.

**2. D7 × CF4/CF5 (Reliability inchoatives) vs. AOML BECOME × SINC.**
If reliability is a SINC subregion (as AOML Layer 2 specifies), AOML predicts BECOME × SINC = restricted (marginal). Empirical data shows D7 × CF4 and D7 × CF5 are both ALLOWED (gaining and losing credibility are equally natural and productive). This is a genuine conflict if reliability belongs to SINC. The conflict suggests either (a) reliability should not be placed within SINC, or (b) AOML's BECOME × SINC restriction is too strong for the reliability subregion.

**3. D10 × CF6/CF7 (Validity causatives) vs. AOML CAUSE × NORM.**
AOML predicts CAUSE × NORM = restricted. Empirical data shows D10 × CF6 and D10 × CF7 are both ALLOWED (conferring and revoking validity are natural across languages). This is a genuine conflict if validity belongs to NORM. The conflict suggests that institutional/procedural validity (D10) may require different operator licensing than moral rightness (D6) within the NORM axis. AOML's single NORM axis may conflate two empirically distinct sub-domains.

**4. D11 × CF8 (Similarity nominalization) vs. AOML ABSTR × LIFE = blocked.**
AOML predicts that abstract nominalization of LIFE is universally blocked. If similarity/resemblance is a LIFE subregion (as AOML Layer 2 specifies), then abstract nominalizations of similarity should be blocked. Empirical data shows that similarity nominalizations are natural across languages (yo ìjọra "mutual resemblance," id kemiripan "similarity," eu antzetasuna "similarity," ko 유사성 "similarity"). This is a genuine conflict if similarity belongs to LIFE. The conflict suggests either (a) similarity should not be placed within LIFE, or (b) AOML's ABSTR × LIFE = blocked prediction is too strong.

**5. D11 × CF4 (Similarity inchoative) vs. AOML BECOME × LIFE.**
AOML predicts BECOME × LIFE = restricted. Empirical data shows D11 × CF4 is ALLOWED (becoming more similar is natural). If similarity is a LIFE subregion, this is a conflict. The conflict reinforces the suggestion that similarity may not belong within LIFE.

### Unmodeled Empirical Findings

**1. Agentive nominalization asymmetry (CF9 vs. CF10).**
The most consistent cross-language finding in the empirical data — that "liar" agentives are universally lexicalized while "truth-teller" agentives are universally absent or periphrastic — has no counterpart in AOML. AOML does not model agentive nominalization as a constructional operator. This asymmetry is documented across all 8 languages and is one of the strongest empirical findings in the dataset. It is not captured by any AOML axis, operator, or structural rule.

**2. Discourse marker grammaticalization (CF13).**
The universal grammaticalization of truth-domain forms into discourse markers (ka მართლაც, yo nitootọ́, fi totta kai, eu benetan, ta uṇmaiyil, id sebenarnya, tr gerçekten, ko 사실은) is not modeled in AOML. This is a pragmatic/diachronic phenomenon that AOML's synchronic semantic architecture does not address.

**3. Metalinguistic truth predicate asymmetry (CF14).**
The asymmetry between truth-telling (verb + object construction) and lying (dedicated verb or compound verb) across multiple languages is not modeled in AOML. This is a lexicalization pattern about speech-act constructions that AOML does not address.

**4. Compound formation [FALSE+N] as a productive morphological strategy (CF20).**
The universal productivity of [deception modifier + domain noun] compounds (false testimony, counterfeit money, fake news) is not modeled as a distinct constructional operator in AOML. AOML's CONTR operator covers the semantic content but not the morphological/lexical compounding strategy.

**5. Register stratification within the truth domain.**
Tamil's three-way register stratification (uṇmai general, mey literary/classical, nijam colloquial) and Korean's Sino-Korean vs. native Korean stratum are not modeled in AOML. AOML does not have a register dimension.

**6. Semantic extension of "lie/false" to "futile/in vain" (Georgian ტყუილი).**
This single-language finding has no AOML counterpart. AOML does not model semantic extension patterns.

**7. Practical reality vs. theory as a distinct dimension (D12).**
The extensive Korean 실- cluster and Finnish tosielämä cluster suggest that the theory/practice distinction is a more elaborated empirical domain than AOML models. AOML's AUTH axis includes "actualization" as a subregion, but this does not fully capture the breadth of D12.

**8. Cross-language variation in inchoative and causative productivity for D1.**
The empirical finding that Yoruba and Korean show the strongest resistance to inchoative and causative constructions for propositional truth, while Georgian, Turkish, and Indonesian show more productive patterns, is not explained by AOML. AOML predicts restricted for BECOME × VER and CAUSE × VER but does not predict cross-language variation in the degree of restriction.

### Unattested AOML Structures

**1. AOML ESS axis (Essence).**
The empirical matrix does not have a dimension that clearly corresponds to AOML's ESS axis (inherent nature, definitional properties). D9 (Appearance vs. Underlying Reality) maps partially to ESS, but the empirical data does not directly test CAUSE × ESS = blocked (R2) or BECOME × ESS = blocked (R2). The ESS axis is not clearly attested in the empirical data.

**2. AOML LIFE axis (Lifelikeness/Representational Fidelity).**
The empirical matrix does not have a dimension that clearly corresponds to AOML's LIFE axis (constructed resemblance to reality, verisimilitude). D11 (Similarity and Correspondence) maps partially to LIFE, but the empirical data treats similarity as a natural property of objects rather than a constructed representational property. The LIFE axis as defined in AOML (representational fidelity of constructed things) is not clearly attested as a distinct empirical dimension.

**3. AOML TEMP meta-axis (provisional).**
The empirical matrix does not include temporal constructions as a distinct constructional function. AOML's provisional TEMP meta-axis (temporal position modifier) is not directly testable from the G7a data.

**4. AOML Level system (EMPIRICAL, CONVENTIONAL, FORMAL, ULTIMATE).**
The empirical matrix does not include a level dimension. AOML's four-level stratification (and UFA Type 4 violations from level-crossing) is not directly tested by the empirical data.

**5. AOML UFA Type 3 (Verification Habitat Mismatch).**
The empirical matrix does not test validation method appropriateness. AOML's specification of admissible and inadmissible validation methods for each axis (e.g., CONSENSUS is inadmissible for VER) is not directly tested by the empirical data.

## Theoretical Discussion

### Where AOML Appears Strongly Supported

**The VER-VERIF structural separation is the strongest theoretical contribution confirmed by the empirical data.** The empirical finding that verification verbs substitute for direct causatives of truth adjectives across all 8 languages — that "prove" and "confirm" are natural while "make true" is constrained — is exactly what AOML's separation of VER (factual correspondence) from VERIF (evidential establishment) predicts. Languages do not treat truth as something that can be directly produced; they treat it as something that can be established through evidence. This is a non-trivial theoretical prediction that the empirical data supports.

**R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry) is strongly confirmed.** The finding that positive causation of authenticity is blocked while negative causation is licensed is one of the cleanest AOML-empirical matches. The asymmetry is documented across all 8 languages and is consistent with AOML's target-type analysis. This is the strongest single structural rule confirmation in the comparison.

**R4 (Positive Emergence Bias) is strongly confirmed.** The cross-language asymmetry between positive and negative inchoative constructions for propositional truth is one of the most consistent empirical findings and directly matches R4's prediction.

**The CONTR operator's universal licensing is confirmed.** Contrastive/corrective frames, concessive constructions, and compound formation are ALLOWED across all languages and all dimensions. This is consistent with AOML's prediction that CONTR is universally licensed.

### Where AOML May Require Refinement

**1. The NORM axis conflates two empirically distinct sub-domains.**
Empirical data distinguishes D6 (Moral Rightness and Justice) from D10 (Validity and Legitimacy). Both map to AOML's NORM axis, but they behave differently under causation: D10 × CF6 is ALLOWED (conferring validity is natural) while D6 × CF6 is VARIABLE (causing moral rightness is constrained). AOML's single NORM axis does not predict this difference. A refinement might distinguish NORM-moral (moral rightness, justice) from NORM-institutional (validity, legitimacy, procedural correctness), with different operator licensing for each.

**2. The SINC axis may be too broad.**
AOML places reliability as a subregion of SINC. Empirical data treats reliability (D7) as a distinct dimension from honesty/moral character (D5), and the two dimensions show different constructional behavior: D7 × CF4/CF5 are ALLOWED (reliability changes in both directions are natural) while D5 × CF4 is ALLOWED but D5 × CF5 is VARIABLE. If reliability belongs to SINC, AOML underpredicts the productivity of inchoative constructions for reliability. A refinement might separate SINC-dispositional (honesty, integrity as stable character traits) from SINC-relational (reliability, credibility as assessable properties of sources).

**3. The LIFE axis placement of similarity is problematic.**
AOML places resemblance within LIFE (Layer 2). Empirical data shows that similarity/resemblance (D11) has different constructional behavior from representational fidelity: similarity nominalizations are natural (conflicting with ABSTR × LIFE = blocked), similarity inchoatives are ALLOWED (conflicting with BECOME × LIFE = restricted), and similarity is freely gradable. A refinement might restrict LIFE to constructed representational fidelity (verisimilitude of artworks, simulations, representations) and treat natural similarity between objects as a distinct dimension not within LIFE.

**4. The scope of CAUSE needs clarification relative to R1.**
The conflict between R1 (CAUSE × SINC = universally blocked) and the empirical finding that D5 × CF6 is VARIABLE (not BLOCKED) may reflect ambiguity in the scope of CAUSE. If CAUSE includes enabling, facilitating, and creating conditions for (as well as direct causation), R1 may be too strong. If CAUSE is restricted to direct causation, R1 may still hold but the empirical data does not clearly test this distinction. AOML would benefit from a more explicit specification of what counts as CAUSE vs. ENABLE/FACILITATE.

**5. The agentive nominalization asymmetry requires a new structural rule or operator.**
The most consistent cross-language finding — that "liar" agentives are universally lexicalized while "truth-teller" agentives are universally absent — is not captured by any AOML structure. This asymmetry is as robust as R1 (documented in all 8 languages, with the same directionality) and deserves a structural rule. A candidate formulation: "Agentive nominalization of the negative pole of D2 (deliberate deception) is universally licensed; agentive nominalization of the positive pole is universally blocked or periphrastic." This would be a new structural rule (R7 candidate) not currently in AOML.

### Where Empirical Findings Suggest New Dimensions, Constraints, or Revised Strength

**New dimension candidate: Institutional Validity (D10 split from D6).**
The empirical data suggests that institutional/procedural validity (D10) should be treated as a distinct dimension from moral rightness (D6), with different operator licensing. Specifically, CAUSE × D10 is ALLOWED while CAUSE × D6 is VARIABLE. This suggests a new axis or sub-axis for institutional validity with more permissive causation licensing than NORM-moral.

**New dimension candidate: Source Reliability (D7 split from D5).**
The empirical data suggests that source reliability (D7) should be treated as a distinct dimension from personal honesty (D5), with different inchoative behavior. Specifically, D7 × CF4/CF5 are both ALLOWED while D5 × CF4 is ALLOWED and D5 × CF5 is VARIABLE. This suggests a new axis or sub-axis for source reliability with more permissive inchoative licensing than SINC-dispositional.

**New structural rule candidate: Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry.**
The universal asymmetry between "liar" (lexicalized) and "truth-teller" (absent/periphrastic) agentives is as robust as any AOML structural rule and deserves formal status. This finding is not captured by any current AOML structure.

**Revised constraint strength for BECOME × SINC.**
AOML predicts BECOME × SINC = restricted (marginal). Empirical data shows D5 × CF4 (character change toward honesty) is ALLOWED across all languages with F6 data. The constraint strength should be revised upward for the positive direction: BECOME(+) × SINC may be more productive than AOML predicts, even if BECOME(−) × SINC remains restricted.

**Revised constraint strength for CAUSE × NORM (institutional subtype).**
AOML predicts CAUSE × NORM = restricted. Empirical data shows D10 × CF6/CF7 are ALLOWED. If institutional validity is separated from moral rightness within NORM, the constraint strength for CAUSE × NORM-institutional should be revised to licensed (or at least more permissive than restricted).

**The discourse marker grammaticalization pattern (CF13) is a cross-linguistically robust finding without AOML counterpart.** The universal grammaticalization of truth-domain forms into discourse markers is documented across all 8 languages and all language families. This is a pragmatic/diachronic phenomenon that AOML's synchronic architecture does not address. Whether this requires a new AOML component or is outside AOML's scope is a theoretical question, but the finding is robust enough to warrant acknowledgment.

**The metalinguistic truth predicate asymmetry (CF14) suggests a lexicalization constraint not in AOML.** The asymmetry between truth-telling (verb + object) and lying (dedicated verb) across multiple languages suggests a lexicalization tendency: the negative speech act (lying) is more morphologically condensed than the positive speech act (truth-telling). This parallels the agentive asymmetry and may reflect the same underlying tendency. Neither is currently modeled in AOML.
