Below is a data-based synthesis of recurring semantic distinctions that appear across the languages in the supplied F1–F3 materials.

---

## Emergent cross-language semantic dimensions

### 1. Factual truth of statements, reports, and claims
This dimension covers whether a proposition, report, statement, or allegation is presented as matching what is actually the case. Across languages, this is one of the most central groupings, often expressed by basic truth nouns/adjectives and by constructions meaning “it is true,” “tell the truth,” or “the truth came out.”

Representative terms:
- **ka**: სიმართლე, მართალი, სიმართლის თქმა
- **yo**: òtítọ́, òtítọ́ ni, jẹ́ òtítọ́
- **fi**: totta, tosi, totuus, pitää paikkansa
- **eu**: egia, egia da
- **ta**: உண்மை, உண்மையைச் சொல்
- **id**: benar, kebenaran, benar bahwa ...
- **tr**: doğru, gerçek, hakikat, doğruyu söylemek
- **ko**: 진실, 사실, 사실이다

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in all languages
- Often also **morphologically derived** into abstract nouns/adverbs
- In some languages **merged with correctness/rightness** (especially Yoruba; partly Georgian, Turkish, Korean)
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 2. Falsehood, lying, and factual non-truth
This dimension covers the negative side of truth evaluation: lies, false statements, false reports, and claims judged not to match facts. In many languages this includes both everyday lying vocabulary and more formal terms for false allegations, false reporting, or fabricated information.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: ყალბი, სიყალბე, სიმართლეს არ შეესაბამება
- **yo**: irọ́, irọ́ ni, kì í ṣe òtítọ́, èké
- **fi**: epätosi, väärä, virheellinen
- **eu**: gezur, faltsu, gezurrezko
- **ta**: பொய், பொய்யான, உண்மையில்லை
- **id**: tidak benar, palsu
- **tr**: yanlış, sahte, uydurma, asılsız
- **ko**: 거짓, 거짓말, 허위, 사실이 아니다

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often supported by **morphological negation** or dedicated falsehood lexemes
- Sometimes split between **ordinary lie** and **formal falsity/fraud** (e.g. Korean 거짓 vs 허위; Turkish sahte vs asılsız; Finnish epätosi vs väärä/virheellinen)
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 3. Genuineness, authenticity, and “the real thing”
This dimension concerns whether an object, person, feeling, identity, or relation is genuine rather than fake, imitated, nominal, or merely apparent. It is distinct from proposition truth in many languages, though some terms overlap with “real/true.”

Representative terms:
- **ka**: ნამდვილი, ნამდვილობა
- **yo**: ojúlówó, òtọ́
- **fi**: aito, autenttinen, aitous, alkuperäinen
- **eu**: benetako, egiazko, autentiko
- **ta**: உண்மையான, நிஜமான, அசல்
- **id**: asli, keaslian, sejati
- **tr**: hakiki, sahici, gerçek, özgün
- **ko**: 진짜, 진정한, 정품, 진품/진본

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often **morphologically derived** into abstract nouns
- In some languages partly **merged with truth/reality** (Basque egiazko/benetako; Tamil உண்மை-based forms; Turkish gerçek; Yoruba òtọ́)
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 4. Fake, forged, counterfeit, fabricated
This dimension covers non-genuine objects, forged documents, counterfeit goods, fabricated identities, and similar cases where something is presented as real but is not. In several languages this is a strong practical domain, especially for documents, signatures, products, and media content.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: ყალბი, სიყალბე
- **yo**: èké, irọ́
- **fi**: väärennös, jäljitelmä, epäaito
- **eu**: gezurrezko, faltsu
- **ta**: போலி, பொய்யான
- **id**: palsu, pemalsuan
- **tr**: sahte, taklit, uydurma
- **ko**: 가짜, 위작

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often linked to **morphological derivation** for “forgery/falsification”
- Sometimes overlaps with general falsehood rather than object-authenticity only
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 5. Correctness, rightness, and properness
This dimension covers whether an answer, action, method, judgment, or interpretation is correct or right. In many languages it extends beyond factual correctness into practical or moral rightness.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: სწორი, სისწორე, მართებული
- **yo**: tọ́, títọ́, kò tọ́
- **fi**: oikea, oikein
- **eu**: zuzen, egoki, zuzentasun
- **ta**: சரி, சரியான
- **id**: benar, betul, tepat
- **tr**: doğru, yerinde, isabetli
- **ko**: 올바르다, 옳다

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often **merged with truth** (Yoruba strongly; also Georgian მართალი, Turkish doğru, Indonesian benar, Korean 옳다 in some uses)
- Often also **merged with moral evaluation**
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 6. Error, wrongness, and incorrectness
This dimension is the negative counterpart to correctness: wrong answers, mistaken judgments, faulty interpretations, improper actions, or errors. In some languages the same forms cover both factual error and moral fault.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: სწორი არ არის, მართებული არ არის
- **yo**: kò tọ́, àṣìṣe
- **fi**: väärä, virheellinen
- **eu**: oker
- **ta**: தவறு, தவறான, சரியில்லை
- **id**: tidak benar, tidak tepat, kurang tepat
- **tr**: yanlış, isabetsiz
- **ko**: 그름, 정오, 사실과 다르다

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often also expressed by **negated positive predicates**
- In some languages **merged with moral fault** (Tamil தவறு; Yoruba kò tọ́)
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 7. Precision, exactness, and accuracy of detail
This dimension concerns closeness to exact fact, detail, measurement, timing, or wording. It is narrower than general truth/correctness and often appears in technical, scientific, educational, or administrative contexts.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: ზუსტი, სიზუსტე
- **yo**: péye
- **fi**: paikkansapitävä, precise uses of exactness terms; also exactness under tosi/tarkkuus-like domain is represented here mainly by accurate/precise constructions in the supplied set
- **ta**: சரியான in some contexts, plus verification/correction expressions; less sharply lexicalized as a separate core than in some others
- **id**: tepat, ketepatan, akurat, keakuratan
- **tr**: doğru in some uses, isabetli, doğruluk, doğruluk oranı
- **ko**: 정확하다, 정확성, 정확도

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in many languages
- Sometimes **split from general correctness** (Georgian, Indonesian, Korean, Finnish)
- Sometimes **merged with correctness** rather than sharply separated (Yoruba, Tamil, Turkish)
- **Strongly to moderately attested**

---

### 8. Conformity, correspondence, and matching to a standard/source/fact
This dimension covers whether one thing matches another: a statement with facts, a document with rules, a version with an original, or data with evidence. It often appears in formal evaluation, auditing, comparison, and verification.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: სიმართლეს შეესაბამება, მსგავსი/მსგავსება
- **yo**: bá mu, ẹ̀rí-based support, rí i dájú in procedural checking
- **fi**: vastata todellisuutta, perustua todellisuuteen, paikkansapitävä
- **eu**: egiaztatu family; antzeko, berdin, identiko; also truth-assessment via egia
- **ta**: ஒத்த, ஒற்றுமை, மூலத்துடன் ஒப்பிட்டு பார்
- **id**: sesuai, kesesuaian, cocok, kecocokan, mencocokkan
- **tr**: gerçeğe uygun, aslına uygun, aslına sadık
- **ko**: 사실과 다르다, 정품 여부를 확인하다, 닮다/흡사하다 in resemblance-related matching

Status:
- Often **clearly lexicalized**
- Frequently **morphologically derived**
- In some languages split into subareas: **formal conformity** vs **everyday fit** vs **resemblance**
- **Strongly attested**, though internally heterogeneous

---

### 9. Reliability, trustworthiness, and dependability
This dimension concerns whether a source, person, system, witness, or body of information can be relied on. It is distinct from truth itself: something may be treated as reliable or unreliable before a specific claim is fully established.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: სარწმუნო, უტყუარი
- **yo**: dájú in some uses; evidential support terms partly overlap, though the lexical split is weaker
- **fi**: luotettava, luotettavuus
- **eu**: fidagarri
- **ta**: நம்பகமான, நம்பகத்தன்மை, உறுதியான
- **id**: andal, keandalan
- **tr**: güvenilir, güvenilirlik
- **ko**: 신뢰하다, 신뢰성, 믿을 만하다

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in many languages
- Sometimes **weakly separated** from certainty/evidence rather than reliability proper (Yoruba)
- Often distinct from credibility/plausibility
- **Strongly attested cross-linguistically, though weaker in Yoruba**

---

### 10. Credibility, believability, and persuasive plausibility
This dimension concerns whether something seems believable, convincing, or plausible, without necessarily being established as true. It is especially common for explanations, testimony, performances, narratives, and public claims.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: დამაჯერებელი, სარწმუნო (partly), უტყუარი as stronger contrast point
- **yo**: dájú partly overlaps, but a separate lexicalized plausibility set is weaker
- **fi**: uskottava, uskottavuus
- **eu**: sinesgarri
- **ta**: நம்பகமான partly overlaps; a sharply separate plausibility lexicon is less developed in the supplied set
- **id**: not as sharply central in the supplied core set, though evaluative passives and fit terms partly cover it
- **tr**: inandırıcı, inandırıcılık
- **ko**: 믿을 만하다, 신뢰성 partly; also appearance-based seeming constructions

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in some languages (Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Turkish)
- **Merged with reliability or trust** in others (Tamil, Korean, partly Indonesian, Yoruba)
- **Tentative as a universal dimension**, but strong where attested

---

### 11. Formal validity, legal/procedural standing, and institutional recognition
This dimension concerns whether something counts as valid, operative, recognized, or in force under legal, administrative, procedural, or technical standards. It is distinct from ordinary truth and often from moral justification.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: მართებული, მართებულობა, მართლზომიერი, მართლმსაჯულება
- **fi**: laillinen, lainmukainen, oikeudellinen, valid-related technical terms
- **eu**: egiaztatu family partly procedural, but a large separate validity subsystem is less prominent
- **ta**: உறுதி/உறுதிப்படுத்து partly procedural; legal-validity lexicon less sharply centralized in the supplied truth-domain set
- **id**: sah, absah, valid, validitas, keabsahan
- **tr**: geçerli, geçerlilik, meşru, meşruiyet
- **ko**: 유효하다, 무효, 유효성, 무효화

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in several languages
- Often **morphologically derived**
- In some languages split from **justification/rightness**; in others partly merged
- **Strongly attested**, especially in Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, Georgian

---

### 12. Justification, legitimacy, fairness, and being in the right
This dimension concerns whether an action, claim, authority, reaction, or decision is justified, fair, rightful, or legitimate. It is not primarily about factual truth, though some languages use overlapping roots.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: მართებული, გამართლება, მართლმსაჯულება
- **yo**: òdodo, ṣe òdodo
- **fi**: oikeus, oikeudenmukainen, oikeutettu
- **ta**: நீதி, நியாயம், தர்மம்
- **id**: patut, also some uses of benar/membenarkan and sah/absah in institutional contexts
- **tr**: haklı, meşru, meşruiyet
- **ko**: 정당하다, 정당성, 타당하다 (partly)

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often **separate from factual truth**
- In some languages **merged with correctness/rightness** (Yoruba, Georgian, Turkish, Korean)
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 13. Essence, underlying nature, and what something really is
This dimension concerns the core nature, essence, or underlying substance of a thing, often contrasted with appearance, surface description, or secondary detail. It is common in philosophical, reflective, critical, and explanatory discourse.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: არსი, არსებითი
- **fi**: olemus
- **eu**: funtsean
- **ta**: மெய்ப்பொருள், தத்துவம், சாரம்
- **id**: hakikat, jati diri
- **tr**: öz, asıl, hakikat
- **ko**: 본질, 실체

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often partly **merged with reality/truth** in elevated or philosophical usage
- Usually distinct from everyday proposition truth
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 14. Appearance vs reality / seeming vs revealed status
This dimension concerns the contrast between how something looks or is presented and what it turns out really to be. It often appears in expressions meaning “looks real,” “seems fake,” “the truth came out,” or “the real nature was revealed.”

Representative terms:
- **ka**: ნამდვილი სახე, სიმართლე გაირკვა
- **fi**: todellisuus vs appearance; paljastua väärennökseksi
- **eu**: itxurazko vs benetako/egiazko
- **ta**: உண்மை வெளிவரு, உண்மை தெரியவரு
- **id**: diduga palsu / terbukti palsu / dipastikan asli
- **tr**: gerçek yüzü, gerçeği çarpıtmak, hakikatten uzak
- **ko**: 진짜처럼 보이다, 가짜처럼 보이다, 실체가 드러나다

Status:
- Often **constructionally strong**
- Sometimes lexicalized through explicit appearance terms (Basque itxurazko)
- Often realized through **revelation/disclosure constructions** rather than a single lexical opposition
- **Strongly attested**

---

### 15. Resemblance, similarity, and likeness
This dimension concerns whether one thing resembles another in appearance, form, pattern, or style. It is adjacent to authenticity and representation, but does not itself mean truth or genuineness.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: მსგავსი, მსგავსება
- **yo**: bá mu partly, though broader than resemblance
- **fi**: identtinen, near-identity/sameness terms
- **eu**: antzeko, berdin, identiko
- **ta**: ஒத்த, ஒற்றுமை
- **id**: mirip, kemiripan
- **tr**: aslına uygun, sadık, plus resemblance-related descriptive uses
- **ko**: 닮다, 흡사하다, 닮은꼴

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized**
- Often separate from truth proper
- In some languages overlaps with **matching/correspondence** or **faithfulness to original**
- **Moderately to strongly attested**

---

### 16. Faithfulness to an original, source, or model
This dimension concerns whether a copy, translation, restoration, representation, or adaptation remains faithful to an original source or form. It is related to authenticity and resemblance, but specifically organized around source-dependence.

Representative terms:
- **ka**: მსგავსი/მსგავსება partly relevant, but less sharply source-faithful as a separate lexical cluster
- **fi**: alkuperäinen, identtinen, authentic-source expressions
- **ta**: மூலம், மூலத்துடன் ஒப்பிட்டு பார்
- **id**: kesesuaian, mencocokkan, asli in source-checking contexts
- **tr**: aslına uygun, aslına sadık, sadık
- **ko**: 모사하다, 실물과 흡사하다, 정품 여부 확인 in source-authenticity contexts

Status:
- **Clearly lexicalized** in some languages (especially Turkish)
- Elsewhere often **merged with resemblance/authenticity/conformity**
- **Moderately attested**, not equally strong in all languages

## Final assessment

### Strongly supported cross-linguistically
The following dimensions are strongly supported across the dataset:

- **Factual truth of statements**
- **Falsehood / non-truth**
- **Genuineness / authenticity**
- **Fake / forged / counterfeit**
- **Correctness / rightness / properness**
- **Error / wrongness**
- **Reliability / trustworthiness**
- **Formal validity / institutional validity**
- **Justification / legitimacy / fairness**
- **Essence / underlying nature**
- **Appearance vs reality**

These recur in multiple languages with clear lexical or constructional evidence.

### Tentative or weakly attested
The following are present, but less evenly supported across all languages:

- **Credibility / plausibility** as distinct from reliability
- **Faithfulness to an original/source** as a separate dimension
- **Precision / exactness** as fully separate from correctness in every language
- **Resemblance / similarity** as part of the truth-related domain rather than a neighboring comparison domain

These are strong in some languages, but merged or less central in others.

### Dimensions that split, merge, or vary by language
Several dimensions vary substantially:

- **Truth vs correctness**: strongly merged in **Yoruba**; partly merged in **Georgian, Turkish, Indonesian, Korean**
- **Truth vs authenticity**: partly merged in **Basque, Tamil, Turkish, Yoruba**
- **Correctness vs moral rightness**: often merged in **Yoruba, Tamil, Turkish, Korean**
- **Reliability vs credibility**: clearly split in **Finnish, Georgian, Turkish, Basque**; less sharply split in **Tamil, Indonesian, Korean, Yoruba**
- **Validity vs justification**: clearly split in **Indonesian, Turkish, Korean, Finnish**; less sharply separated elsewhere
- **Essence vs truth**: often adjacent but distinct; especially elaborated in **Tamil, Turkish, Indonesian, Korean**
- **Dynamic truth expressions**: many languages prefer **revelation/verification** over a simple “become true” pattern, especially **Georgian, Yoruba, Basque, Tamil, Turkish, Korean**

Overall, the strongest recurring cross-language structure is not a single truth/not-truth opposition alone, but a broader field in which languages repeatedly distinguish:
- what is **factually true**,
- what is **genuine/authentic**,
- what is **correct/proper**,
- what is **valid/recognized**,
- what is **trustworthy/believable**,
- and what is **fundamentally real or essential**.
