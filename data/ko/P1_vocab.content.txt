# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Korean (ko)

## Lexical Items

### 1. 진실 (jinsil)
**Literal meaning:** true + reality  
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity

Used to refer to factual accuracy or the state of being true, often in contexts of testimony, statements, or moral character. Commonly appears in both everyday discourse and formal contexts such as legal proceedings or philosophical discussion.

**[CORE]**

### 2. 거짓 (geojit)
**Literal meaning:** false, lie  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

Refers to statements or representations that are false or deceptive. Used broadly in moral, everyday, and legal contexts to denote intentional or unintentional falsity.

**[CORE]**

### 3. 사실 (sasil)
**Literal meaning:** matter + reality  
**Typical English translations:** fact, reality, truth

Denotes objective facts or actual states of affairs, distinguished from opinions or interpretations. Frequently used in journalism, academic writing, and everyday conversation to establish factual basis.

**[CORE]**

### 4. 참 (cham)
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine

Native Korean word for truth or genuineness, often used as a modifier or in compounds. Can express both factual truth and authenticity of character or objects.

**[CORE]**

### 5. 정직 (jeongjik)
**Literal meaning:** upright + straight  
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness

Refers to moral character involving truthfulness and straightforwardness in conduct. Primarily used in evaluating personal character and ethical behavior.

**[CORE]**

### 6. 진짜 (jinjja)
**Literal meaning:** true + (colloquial marker)  
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic

Colloquial term for authenticity or genuineness, used to distinguish real from fake objects, statements, or situations. Very common in everyday speech.

**[CORE]**

### 7. 가짜 (gajja)
**Literal meaning:** false + (colloquial marker)  
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false

Colloquial counterpart to 진짜, denoting inauthenticity or falseness. Used for counterfeit objects, false statements, or pretense.

**[CORE]**

### 8. 정확 (jeonghwak)
**Literal meaning:** correct + exact  
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness

Refers to correctness and precision in statements, measurements, or representations. Common in technical, academic, and everyday contexts requiring precision.

**[CORE]**

### 9. 올바르다 (olbareuda)
**Literal meaning:** correct + to be  
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper

Evaluates correctness of actions, judgments, or states of affairs, often with moral or normative implications. Used in ethical, practical, and factual evaluations.

**[CORE]**

### 10. 맞다 (matda)
**Literal meaning:** to fit, to be correct  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate

Versatile verb indicating correctness, accuracy, or appropriateness. Used in confirming factual statements, answers, or fit between things.

**[CORE]**

### 11. 틀리다 (teullida)
**Literal meaning:** to be wrong, to err  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken

Indicates incorrectness or error in statements, answers, or judgments. Common in educational, everyday, and evaluative contexts.

**[CORE]**

### 12. 진위 (jinwi)
**Literal meaning:** true + false  
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, truth or falsehood, genuineness

Formal term used to discuss the determination of truth versus falsity, especially in legal or investigative contexts. Often appears in phrases like "진위를 가리다" (to determine authenticity).

**[CORE]**

### 13. 허위 (heowi)
**Literal meaning:** empty + false  
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, untruth

Formal term for falsity, especially in legal contexts (e.g., 허위진술 "false testimony"). Carries connotations of deliberate deception or legal violation.

**[CORE]**

### 14. 진정 (jinjeong)
**Literal meaning:** true + genuine  
**Typical English translations:** genuine, sincere, authentic

Refers to genuineness or sincerity, particularly of emotions, intentions, or character. Used in evaluating authenticity of feelings or motivations.

**[CORE]**

### 15. 실제 (silje)
**Literal meaning:** reality + substance  
**Typical English translations:** actual, real, reality

Denotes actual reality as opposed to appearance, theory, or expectation. Common in contrasting what is real versus what is claimed or imagined.

**[CORE]**

### 16. 본질 (bonjil)
**Literal meaning:** root + substance  
**Typical English translations:** essence, nature, substance

Philosophical and analytical term for the fundamental nature or essence of something. Used in abstract discussions about what things fundamentally are.

**[CORE]**

### 17. 정당하다 (jeongdanghada)
**Literal meaning:** correct + appropriate + to be  
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, justified, rightful

Evaluates legitimacy or justification of actions, claims, or rights. Common in legal, political, and moral discourse.

**[CORE]**

### 18. 타당하다 (tadanghada)
**Literal meaning:** appropriate + suitable + to be  
**Typical English translations:** valid, reasonable, sound

Assesses validity or reasonableness of arguments, claims, or reasoning. Frequently used in academic and logical contexts.

**[CORE]**

### 19. 신뢰 (sinroe)
**Literal meaning:** trust + rely  
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, credibility

Refers to trustworthiness or reliability of persons, institutions, or information. Central to evaluations of credibility and dependability.

**[CORE]**

### 20. 믿음 (mideum)
**Literal meaning:** believing (nominalized)  
**Typical English translations:** belief, faith, trust

Native Korean term for belief or trust, used in both religious and secular contexts. Denotes confidence in truth or reliability of something.

**[CORE]**

### 21. 확실 (hwaksil)
**Literal meaning:** certain + real  
**Typical English translations:** certain, sure, definite

Indicates certainty or definiteness of facts or situations. Used to express high confidence in truth or occurrence of something.

**[CORE]**

### 22. 명백하다 (myeongbaekhada)
**Literal meaning:** bright + clear + to be  
**Typical English translations:** obvious, clear, evident

Describes clarity or obviousness of truth or facts. Used when something is manifestly true or evident.

**[CORE]**

### 23. 거짓말 (geojinmal)
**Literal meaning:** false + speech  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

Specific term for false statements or lies. Distinguished from general falsity by referring specifically to verbal deception.

**[CORE]**

### 24. 속이다 (sogida)
**Literal meaning:** to deceive  
**Typical English translations:** deceive, cheat, trick

Verb denoting intentional deception or misleading. Used in contexts of fraud, dishonesty, or trickery.

**[CORE]**

### 25. 진본 (jinbon)
**Literal meaning:** true + original  
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic copy, genuine article

Refers to authentic or original versions of documents, artworks, or objects. Common in art, legal, and archival contexts.

**[CORE]**

### 26. 모조 (mojo)
**Literal meaning:** imitate + make  
**Typical English translations:** imitation, counterfeit, replica

Denotes imitations or counterfeits, especially of valuable objects. Used in contexts distinguishing authentic from copied items.

**[CORE]**

### 27. 위조 (wijo)
**Literal meaning:** false + make  
**Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeit, fabrication

Refers to fraudulent fabrication, especially of documents, currency, or signatures. Carries legal implications of criminal deception.

**[CORE]**

### 28. 진실성 (jinsilseong)
**Literal meaning:** truth + nature/quality  
**Typical English translations:** truthfulness, veracity, sincerity

Abstract noun denoting the quality of being truthful or sincere. Used in evaluating character or the nature of statements.

**[CORE]**
