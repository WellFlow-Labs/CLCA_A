# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note on Yoruba derivational morphology:**
Yoruba derivational morphology is primarily prefixal nominalization. The most productive derivational process is the attachment of nominalizing prefixes (*ì-*, *ò-*, *ẹ̀-*, *à-*, *ọ̀-*) to verbal bases. Adjectival and adverbial modification is largely achieved through juxtaposition rather than affixation. Reduplication functions as an intensifying or iterative process in some forms. The inventory below reports only derivations that are directly supported by the P1 data and by well-attested usage; no paradigm completion has been performed.

---

## 1. Base Form: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

---

**Derived Form 1:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** aláròtítọ́ / ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ènìyàn òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ènìyàn* (person) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** a truthful person, a person of truth
**Usage:** Attributive nominal construction used in character evaluation to describe someone consistently honest in speech and conduct. Appears in social and moral assessment contexts across registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** sọ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *sọ* (to say, to speak) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth
**Usage:** The standard verbal collocation for truth-telling; used in legal oaths, moral instruction, everyday assertions, and religious discourse across all registers.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** òtítọ́ ni
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni
**Literal Breakdown:** *òtítọ́* (truth) + *ni* (copula/focus marker)
**Natural English Translation:** it is the truth; that is true
**Usage:** Fixed assertive expression used to affirm the truth of a statement; appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and religious affirmation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìjẹ́ òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìjẹ́* (acknowledgment, admission; from *jẹ*, to accept/consume) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** acknowledgment of the truth, admission of truth
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts for the act of formally admitting or acknowledging that something is true; appears in court proceedings and religious confession contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5:**
**Base Form:** òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Transliteration:** ìwádìí òtítọ́
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìwádìí* (investigation, inquiry) + *òtítọ́* (truth)
**Natural English Translation:** investigation of the truth, truth inquiry, fact-finding
**Usage:** Used in legal, journalistic, and administrative contexts for the process of formally investigating what is true; appears in news media and official inquiry language.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on derivational productivity:** *òtítọ́* participates in productive genitive/attributive constructions of the pattern [NOUN + *òtítọ́*] and [VERB + *òtítọ́*], but these are syntactic collocations rather than morphological derivations in the strict sense. No affixal derivation from *òtítọ́* as a base (i.e., *òtítọ́* + affix → new form) is attested in the P1 data.

**Natural English Translation:** scientific knowledge, science
**Usage:** Used in educational and journalistic discourse as the standard Yoruba expression for scientific knowledge or science as a field; appears in school materials and public communication.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3:**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *ìmọ̀* (knowledge) + *ìjìnlẹ̀* (depth, profundity; from *jìn* = deep + *ilẹ̀* = ground)
**Natural English Translation:** deep knowledge, expert knowledge, profound understanding
**Usage:** Used in academic, legal, and evaluative contexts to refer to specialized or expert knowledge; appears in discourse about expertise and scholarly authority.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4:**
**Base Form:** ìmọ̀
**Derived Form:** àìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** àìmọ̀
**Literal Breakdown:** *à-* (nominalizer, here with negative force) + *ì-* (nominalizer) + *mọ̀* (to know); or *àì-* (negative prefix) + *mọ̀* (to know)
**Natural English Translation:** ignorance, lack of knowledge, not knowing
**Usage:** The standard negated nominal form of *ìmọ̀*; used across registers to describe the state of not knowing or being unaware; appears in moral instruction, legal contexts, and everyday speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on the *àì-* prefix:** The prefix *àì-* (negation of nominalized verbal forms) is attested as a productive derivational element in Yoruba. *àìmọ̀* is the most directly relevant instance in the P1 domain. This prefix is noted here because it generates a semantically significant antonym within the truth-evaluation domain.

## Summary of Derivational Patterns Observed

The following patterns are attested across the P1 items. They are reported descriptively without imposing a typological framework:

**1. Nominalization via *ì-* prefix on verbal base:**
Attested in: *ìmọ̀* (from *mọ̀*), *ìgbàgbọ́* (from *gbàgbọ́*), *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (from *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé*), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (from *jẹ́rìísí*), *ìtànjẹ́* (from *tànjẹ́*), *ìfarahàn* (from *farahan*), *ìdánwò* (from *dán wò*), *ìdájọ́* (from *dájọ́*), *ìfọwọ́sí* (from *fọwọ́ sí*), *ìrírí* (from *rírí*), *ìwà* (from *wà*), *ìpìlẹ̀* (from *pilẹ̀*). This is the most productive derivational process attested in the P1 data.

**2. Nominalization via *ẹ̀-* prefix on verbal base:**
Attested in: *ẹ̀rí* (from *rí*), *ẹ̀tọ́* (from *tọ́*). Less productive than *ì-* nominalization in this domain but clearly attested.

**3. Nominalization via *ò-* prefix:**
Attested in: *òtítọ́* (from *tọ́* with intervening element), *òdodo*. The *ò-* prefix nominalizes but is less productive than *ì-* in this domain.

**4. Nominalization via *à-* prefix (often with negative or resultant-state meaning):**
Attested in: *àṣìṣe* (from *ṣìṣe*), *àṣà*. The *à-* prefix here generates nominals with a resultant or negative-state meaning.

**5. Negation via *àì-* prefix on nominalized or verbal forms:**
Attested in: *àìmọ̀* (from *ìmọ̀*/*mọ̀*), *àìgbàgbọ́* (from *ìgbàgbọ́*/*gbàgbọ́*), *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (from *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*/*gbẹ́kẹ̀lé*), *ìwà àìtọ́* (from *tọ́*). This prefix generates antonyms within the epistemic and moral domains.

**6. Reduplication as intensification:**
Attested in: *dájúdájú* (from *dájú*). This is the only clear instance of intensifying reduplication in the P1 truth-evaluation domain.

**7. Agentive/participant nominal via *ẹni* + noun contraction:**
Attested in: *ẹlẹ́rìí* (from *ẹni* + *ẹ̀rí*). This produces a nominal meaning "person associated with [noun]."

**8. Productive [NOUN + evaluative modifier] constructions:**
Attested across multiple bases: *ìwà rere*, *ìwà burúkú*, *ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́*, *ìgbàgbọ́ rere*, *ọ̀rẹ́ gidi*, *ènìyàn ojúlówó*, etc. These are syntactic-compositional rather than affixal but are sufficiently regular to constitute a productive pattern.

**9. Productive [*ẹ̀tọ́* + domain noun] and [*ìjẹ́rìísí* + domain noun] patterns:**
Attested in multiple forms for rights categories and certificate types respectively. These are syntactic-compositional patterns generating semantically transparent compounds.
