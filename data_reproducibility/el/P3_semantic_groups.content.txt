# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## GROUP 1: PROPOSITIONAL CORRESPONDENCE

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, beliefs, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs. These expressions are invoked when assessing factual accuracy of claims, distinguishing what is the case from what is claimed to be the case. Commonly used in contexts of verification, correction, and factual dispute.

**Representative expressions:**
- αλήθεια (alítheia) "truth" — used for correspondence to facts
- αληθινός (alithinós) "true, genuine" — describes factually accurate statements
- αληθής (alithís) "true, truthful" — formal term for factual truth
- ψέμα (pséma) "lie" — deliberate false statement
- ψευδής (psevdís) "false, untrue" — describes factually inaccurate statements
- αναληθής (analithís) "untrue, false" — formal negation of truth
- πραγματικός (pragmatikós) "real, actual" — contrasts reality with false claims
- σωστός (sostós) "correct, right" — affirms factual accuracy
- λάθος (láthos) "mistake, error" — unintentional inaccuracy
- ακριβής (akrivís) "accurate, precise" — high degree of correspondence

**Typical constructions:**
- λέω την αλήθεια (tell the truth)
- λέω ψέματα (tell lies)
- είναι σωστό (it's correct)
- κάνω λάθος (to make a mistake)
- πραγματική ιστορία (true story)

---

## GROUP 2: AUTHENTICITY AND GENUINENESS

**Definition:** Terms distinguishing original, legitimate items from copies, counterfeits, or imitations. These expressions focus on the provenance and material authenticity of objects, documents, and artifacts rather than propositional truth. Commonly used in commercial, legal, and art contexts where establishing originality matters.

**Representative expressions:**
- γνήσιος (gnísios) "genuine, authentic" — legitimate origin
- αυθεντικός (afthentikós) "authentic, original" — not copied or fake
- πλαστός (plastós) "fake, counterfeit, forged" — deliberately falsified
- κίβδηλος (kívdilos) "counterfeit, adulterated" — debased quality
- νόθος (nóthos) "illegitimate, spurious" — questionable authenticity
- ψευδώνυμο (psevdónymo) "pseudonym" — false name vs. real name

**Typical constructions:**
- γνήσιο προϊόν (genuine product)
- γνήσιο ανταλλακτικό (genuine part)
- αυθεντικό έργο (authentic work)
- πλαστά χρήματα (counterfeit money)
- πλαστό έγγραφο (forged document)
- αληθινό όνομα (real name)

**Ambiguous items:**
- αληθινός (alithinós) — can indicate both propositional truth AND material authenticity
- αυθεντικό αντίγραφο (certified copy) — authentic copy, paradoxical usage

---

## GROUP 3: PROCEDURAL AND NORMATIVE CORRECTNESS

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, procedures, or conduct conform to established standards, rules, or norms. These expressions assess correctness relative to conventions, regulations, or proper methods rather than factual truth. Commonly used in educational, technical, religious, and administrative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ορθός (orthós) "correct, right, proper" — conformity to standards
- ορθότητα (orthótita) "correctness, rightness" — quality of conforming
- σωστός (sostós) "correct, right, proper" — appropriate procedure
- ορθόδοξος (orthódoxos) "orthodox" — conformity to doctrine
- ορθοδοξία (orthodoxía) "orthodoxy" — correct belief system
- ορθογραφία (orthografía) "orthography" — correct spelling
- ορθογραφικός (orthografikós) "orthographic" — spelling-related

**Typical constructions:**
- κάνω σωστά (to do correctly)
- σωστή απάντηση (correct answer)
- σωστή λύση (correct solution)
- ορθή γωνία (right angle)
- ορθή λογική (correct logic)

**Ambiguous items:**
- σωστός (sostós) — overlaps with factual correctness (Group 1) and procedural correctness
- ακριβής (akrivís) — can indicate both factual accuracy and procedural precision

---

## GROUP 4: LEGAL AND ADMINISTRATIVE VALIDITY

**Definition:** Terms used to assess whether documents, statements, or legal instruments have official force and recognition within institutional systems. These expressions concern formal legitimacy and legal standing rather than factual truth or material authenticity. Predominantly used in legal, bureaucratic, and administrative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- έγκυρος (énkyros) "valid, legitimate" — has legal force
- άκυρος (ákyros) "invalid, void" — lacks legal force
- εγκυρότητα (enkyrótita) "validity, legitimacy"
- ακυρότητα (akyrótita) "invalidity, nullity"
- ακυρώνω (akyróno) "to invalidate, to cancel"
- ακύρωση (akýrosi) "cancellation, annulment"
- ψευδομαρτυρία (psevdomartyría) "false testimony, perjury"
- ψευδομάρτυρας (psevdomártyras) "false witness, perjurer"
- ψευδορκία (psevdorkía) "perjury, false oath"
- ψευδόρκος (psevdórkos) "perjurer, oath-breaker"

**Typical constructions:**
- έγκυρο έγγραφο (valid document)
- άκυρο έγγραφο (invalid document)
- έγκυρη άδεια (valid license)
- κηρύσσω άκυρο (to declare invalid)
- ψευδής κατάθεση (false testimony)
- ψευδής δήλωση (false statement)

---

## GROUP 5: SINCERITY AND INTENTIONAL DECEPTION

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether speakers are expressing their genuine beliefs and intentions or deliberately misleading others. These expressions focus on the speaker's honesty and good faith rather than the factual accuracy of what is said. Commonly used in moral evaluation of character and communication.

**Representative expressions:**
- ειλικρινής (ilikrinís) "sincere, genuine, honest" — without hidden motives
- ειλικρίνεια (ilikríneia) "sincerity, genuineness"
- ψεύτης (pséftis) "liar" — person who deliberately deceives
- ψεύτρα (psévtra) "female liar"
- ψευταράς (pseftarás) "big liar, habitual liar"
- ψεύδομαι (psévdome) "to lie, to tell lies" — deliberate falsehood
- ψευδολογία (psevdología) "lying, mendacity"
- ψευδολόγος (psevdológos) "liar, mendacious person"
- απατηλός (apatilós) "deceptive, misleading"
- απάτη (apáti) "deception, fraud"
- απατεώνας (apateónas) "swindler, fraud"
- απατώ (apató) "to deceive, to cheat"

**Typical constructions:**
- ειλικρινά λυπάμαι (I'm sincerely sorry)
- ειλικρινής γνώμη (honest opinion)
- είναι ψεύτης (he/she is a liar)
- πιάνω στο ψέμα (to catch in a lie)

**Ambiguous items:**
- ψέμα (pséma) — can indicate both deliberate lie (Group 5) and factual falsehood (Group 1)
- αληθεύω (alithévo) "to tell the truth" — overlaps with both propositional truth and sincerity

---

## GROUP 6: RELIABILITY AND TRUSTWORTHINESS

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether sources, persons, or information can be depended upon or believed. These expressions assess credibility and dependability based on track record and consistency rather than immediate truth-value. Commonly used when evaluating testimony, sources, and character references.

**Representative expressions:**
- αξιόπιστος (axiópistos) "reliable, trustworthy, credible"
- αξιοπιστία (axiopistía) "reliability, trustworthiness"
- βέβαιος (véveos) "certain, sure, reliable"
- βεβαιότητα (veveótita) "certainty, assurance"
- βεβαιώνω (veveóno) "to certify, to confirm"
- βεβαίωση (veveósi) "certification, confirmation"
- αμφίβολος (amfívolos) "doubtful, dubious, questionable"
- αμφιβολία (amfivolía) "doubt, uncertainty"

**Typical constructions:**
- αξιόπιστη πηγή (reliable source)
- αξιόπιστο πρόσωπο (trustworthy person)
- αξιόπιστα στοιχεία (reliable data)
- έγκυρη πηγή (valid source)
- είμαι βέβαιος (I'm certain)
- βέβαιη πληροφορία (certain information)
- αμφίβολη προέλευση (doubtful origin)

**Ambiguous items:**
- έγκυρη πηγή — overlaps with legal validity (Group 4) and reliability assessment

---

## GROUP 7: FABRICATION AND CONSTRUCTION

**Definition:** Terms specifically indicating that something has been artificially created, invented, or manufactured rather than naturally occurring or genuinely reported. These expressions emphasize the constructed nature of false claims, often implying deliberate creation of false evidence or narratives. Commonly used in legal accusations and investigative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- κατασκευασμένος (kataskevazménos) "fabricated, made-up, concocted"
- κατασκευάζω (kataskevázo) "to construct, to fabricate"
- κατασκευή (kataskevί) "construction, fabrication"
- πλαστός (plastós) "fake, false, counterfeit, forged"
- πλαστότητα (plastótita) "fakeness, falseness"
- νοθεία (nothía) "adulteration, falsification"
- νοθεύω (nothévo) "to adulterate, to falsify"

**Typical constructions:**
- κατασκευασμένη ιστορία (fabricated story)
- κατασκευασμένα στοιχεία (fabricated evidence)
- κατασκευασμένη κατηγορία (fabricated charge)
- πλαστή ταυτότητα (fake identity)

**Note:** This group emphasizes the active process of creating falsehoods rather than simple incorrectness or error.

---

## GROUP 8: JUSTICE AND FAIRNESS

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether actions, judgments, or distributions conform to principles of fairness and moral rightness. These expressions assess ethical correctness in treatment and judgment rather than factual accuracy. Predominantly used in moral, legal, and social contexts where fairness is at stake.

**Representative expressions:**
- δίκαιος (díkaios) "just, fair, righteous"
- δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosýni) "justice, fairness"
- δικαιότητα (dikaiótita) "justness, fairness"
- άδικος (ádikos) "unjust, unfair, wrong"
- αδικία (adikía) "injustice, unfairness"

**Typical constructions:**
- δίκαιη κρίση (fair judgment)
- δίκαιη τιμωρία (just punishment)
- δίκαιη δίκη (fair trial)
- άδικη μεταχείριση (unfair treatment)
- άδικη κατηγορία (unfair accusation)

**Note:** This group is relatively distinct from other truth-related terms, focusing on moral/ethical evaluation rather than epistemic or factual assessment.

---

## GROUP 9: APPEARANCE AND REALITY DISTINCTIONS

**Definition:** Terms specifically marking the contrast between how things appear and how they actually are. These expressions are used when discussing misleading appearances, illusions, or situations where surface presentation differs from underlying reality. Common in philosophical discourse and discussions of perception.

**Representative expressions:**
- αληθοφανής (alithofanís) "apparently true, plausible, verisimilar"
- αληθοφάνεια (alithofáneia) "verisimilitude, appearance of truth"
- ψευδαίσθηση (psevdésthisi) "illusion, false perception"
- απατηλός (apatilós) "deceptive, misleading"
- απατηλή εμφάνιση (deceptive appearance)

**Typical constructions:**
- απατηλή διαφήμιση (misleading advertising)

**Note:** This group specifically concerns the phenomenology of truth and falsity rather than truth-value itself.

---

## GROUP 10: SCIENTIFIC AND TECHNICAL PSEUDO-TERMS

**Definition:** Compound terms using ψευδ- prefix in scientific and technical contexts to indicate resemblance, superficial similarity, or false classification rather than deliberate deception. These terms function as taxonomic or descriptive labels in specialized domains. Predominantly used in scientific, medical, and technical discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- ψευδοεπιστήμη (psevdoepistími) "pseudoscience"
- ψευδοπόδιο (psevdopódio) "pseudopod"
- ψευδομεταμόρφωση (psevdometamórfosi) "pseudomorphosis"
- ψευδοκυοφορία (psevdokyoforía) "false pregnancy"
- ψευδοκύηση (psevdokýisi) "false pregnancy"
- ψευδάργυρος (psevdárgyros) "zinc" (literally "false silver")
- ψευδοδιάγνωση (psevdodiágnosi) "false diagnosis"

**Note:** These terms use the ψευδ- root but with specialized technical meanings distinct from moral or epistemic falsity.

---

## GROUP 11: RATIONALITY AND LOGICAL CORRECTNESS

**Definition:** Terms evaluating whether thinking, reasoning, or arguments follow proper logical principles and rational standards. These expressions assess cognitive correctness and sound reasoning rather than factual truth or procedural correctness. Commonly used in intellectual, philosophical, and argumentative contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ορθολογισμός (orthologismós) "rationalism, rational thinking"
- ορθολογικός (orthologikós) "rational, reasonable, logical"
- ορθολογιστής (orthologistís) "rationalist"
- ορθή λογική (correct logic)

**Note:** This group is relatively small but distinct, focusing on cognitive/logical evaluation rather than other forms of correctness.

---

## GROUP 12: PHYSICAL STRAIGHTNESS AND UPRIGHTNESS

**Definition:** Terms describing literal physical orientation, posture, or geometric properties. These expressions use the ορθ- root in its spatial rather than correctness sense, though metaphorical extensions to moral uprightness occur. Predominantly used in medical, anatomical, and geometric contexts.

**Representative expressions:**
- ορθοστασία (orthostasía) "upright posture, standing position"
- ορθόπνοια (orthópnia) "orthopnea" (breathing difficulty except when upright)
- ορθογώνιος (orthogónios) "right-angled, rectangular"
- ορθογώνιο (orthogónio) "rectangle"
- ορθοποδώ (orthopodó) "to walk straight, to walk uprightly"
- ορθοπεδικός (orthopedikós) "orthopedic"
- ορθοπεδική (orthopedikí) "orthopedics"

**Typical constructions:**
- ορθή γωνία (right angle)
- ορθή στάση (upright posture)

**Note:** This group shows the polysemy of ορθ-, which can indicate both physical straightness and abstract correctness.

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with multiple group memberships:

**σωστός (sostós) "correct, right"**
- Participates in Group 1 (propositional correspondence) when affirming factual accuracy
- Participates in Group 3 (procedural correctness) when evaluating proper methods
- Usage context determines which sense is active

**ακριβής (akrivís) "accurate, precise"**
- Can indicate factual accuracy (Group 1)
- Can indicate procedural precision (Group 3)
- Often emphasizes degree of exactness rather than simple correctness

**αληθινός (alithinós) "true, genuine"**
- Participates in Group 1 (propositional truth) for factual claims
- Participates in Group 2 (authenticity) for material objects
- Common in both domains with distinct usage patterns

**πραγματικός (pragmatikós) "real, actual"**
- Primarily Group 1 (propositional correspondence)
- Also contrasts with imaginary/fictional (Group 9 appearance/reality)
- Usage: στην πραγματικότητα functions as discourse marker

**απατηλός (apatilós) "deceptive, misleading"**
- Participates in Group 5 (intentional deception)
- Participates in Group 9 (appearance vs. reality)
- Can indicate both deliberate deception and unintentional misleading

### Items with unclear or marginal status:

**ψευδώνυμο (psevdónymo) "pseudonym"**
- Related to authenticity (Group 2) but functions primarily as standalone term
- Does not participate in productive patterns beyond simple reference
- Not clearly integrated into broader semantic groupings

**ψευδοϊστορία (psevdoïstoría) "false history, pseudohistory"**
- Could belong to Group 1 (propositional falsity)
- Could belong to Group 10 (technical pseudo-terms)
- Usage context determines classification

**ψευδοεπώνυμο (psevdoepónymo) "false surname"**
- Related to Group 2 (authenticity) and legal contexts
- Limited usage patterns, primarily administrative
- Marginal item with restricted domain

**ψευδοκλασικός (psevdoklasikós) "pseudo-classical"**
- Aesthetic judgment term, not clearly fitting existing groups
- Could suggest need for aesthetic/stylistic evaluation group
- Insufficient related terms to establish distinct group

### Exclamatory and discourse-marking uses:

**αλήθεια; (interrogative)**
**αλήθεια μου (exclamation)**
**ψέματα! (exclamation)**
**βέβαια (affirmative response)**
**ακριβώς (affirmative response)**

These items function primarily as discourse markers and interjections rather than semantic evaluations. They use truth-vocabulary but serve pragmatic rather than truth-evaluative functions. They do not fit cleanly into semantic groupings based on truth-assessment.

### Color-modified lies:

**άσπρο ψέμα (white lie)**
**μαύρο ψέμα (black lie)**
**κόκκινο ψέμα (red lie)**

These form a distinct scalar pattern based on severity/harm rather than truth-value. They could constitute a separate micro-group for "lie severity gradation" but this would be a very small group with limited membership.

### Diminutive forms:

**ψεματάκι (little lie)**
**λαθάκι (small mistake)**

These function primarily to minimize severity rather than to categorize types of falsity. They represent a morphological pattern (diminutive formation) rather than a semantic grouping.

## NOTES ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Observed cross-cutting dimensions:

1. **Intentionality**: Some groups distinguish deliberate falsification (Group 5, Group 7) from unintentional error (λάθος in Group 1)

2. **Domain specificity**: Some groups are domain-restricted (Group 4 legal, Group 10 scientific, Group 12 physical/geometric)

3. **Object of evaluation**: Groups differ in what they evaluate:
   - Statements/beliefs (Group 1)
   - Objects/documents (Group 2)
   - Procedures/conduct (Group 3)
   - Legal instruments (Group 4)
   - Speaker intentions (Group 5)
   - Sources/persons (Group 6)
   - Actions/judgments (Group 8)

4. **Polysemy of roots**: The ορθ- root shows clear polysemy between physical straightness (Group 12) and various forms of correctness (Groups 3, 11). The ψευδ- root shows polysemy between moral/epistemic falsity and technical resemblance (Group 10).

### Patterns NOT forming distinct groups:

**Adverbial forms**: While many bases have adverbial derivatives (αληθώς, ορθώς, σωστά, etc.), these do not form a semantic group—they simply represent grammatical derivation from their base adjectives.

**Abstract quality nouns**: Similarly, -ότητα derivatives (ορθότητα, σωστότητα, etc.) represent grammatical nominalization rather than semantic grouping.

**Negation patterns**: While some terms are morphologically negative (αναληθής, άδικος, άκυρος), they do not form a coherent semantic group—they simply negate their positive counterparts within their respective domains.

### Absence of expected groupings:

No clear grouping emerged for:
- **Temporal aspects** of truth (eternal vs. contingent truth)
- **Degrees of certainty** (though βέβαιος and αμφίβολος suggest this dimension)
- **Subjective vs. objective** truth (not a salient distinction in usage patterns)
- **Correspondence vs. coherence** theories (philosophical distinction not reflected in usage)

These absences suggest that Greek truth-vocabulary is organized primarily around practical contexts of use (legal, commercial, moral, scientific) rather than abstract philosophical distinctions.
