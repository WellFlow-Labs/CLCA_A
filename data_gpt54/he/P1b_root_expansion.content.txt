## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **א־מ־נ / א־מ־ת** — *ʾ-m-n / ʾ-m-t*
**Participates in:** 6 P1 items  
**P1 items:** אמת, אמיתי, מהימן, מהימנות, אמין, אמינות  
**Core meaning contribution:** truth, trustworthiness, reliability, firmness, credibility; this cluster contributes the core vocabulary for truth as a property and for trust/reliability as an evaluative status.

### 2. **צ־ד־ק** — *ts-d-q*
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items  
**P1 items:** צודק, צדק  
**Core meaning contribution:** rightness, justification, justice; this root contributes evaluation of being right, justified, or just rather than merely factually true.

### 3. **ד־י־ק** — *d-y-q*
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items  
**P1 items:** מדויק, דיוק  
**Core meaning contribution:** precision, exactness, accuracy; this root contributes the sense of exact correspondence, careful specification, and non-approximation.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

## Root: **א־מ־נ / א־מ־ת** — *ʾ-m-n / ʾ-m-t*

This root-family is highly productive. Below are the most common and well-attested forms; additional forms exist.

---

### **אמת**
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** root noun from א־מ־ת  
**Natural English translation:** truth  
**Context of use:** Basic noun for truth in everyday, philosophical, religious, legal, and public discourse. Used for the truth of statements, situations, and principles.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **אמיתי**
**Transliteration:** *ʾamiti*  
**Literal breakdown:** אמת + adjectival formation  
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine  
**Context of use:** Used for true statements and also for genuine people, feelings, objects, or relationships. Common in both colloquial and formal usage.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **באמת**
**Transliteration:** *beʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** ב־ “in/really” + אמת  
**Natural English translation:** really, truly, in truth  
**Context of use:** Very common adverb in speech and writing. It can mark sincerity, emphasis, surprise, or actual truth.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **למען האמת**
**Transliteration:** *lemaʿan ha-ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “for the sake of the truth”  
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth, honestly  
**Context of use:** Common discourse marker introducing a candid admission or correction. Used in conversation, journalism, and essays.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **האמת**
**Transliteration:** *ha-ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** definite form of אמת  
**Natural English translation:** the truth  
**Context of use:** Used both referentially and as a discourse marker, especially in colloquial speech: *האמת ש־...* “the truth is / actually...”.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **האמת היא ש־**
**Transliteration:** *ha-ʾemet hi she-*  
**Literal breakdown:** “the truth is that—”  
**Natural English translation:** the truth is that, actually  
**Context of use:** Fixed phrase for introducing what the speaker presents as the real situation. Common in speech, interviews, and argumentative prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### **אמת לאמיתה**
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet la-ʾamitah*  
**Literal breakdown:** “truth to its truth”  
**Natural English translation:** the full truth, truth in the deepest sense  
**Context of use:** Literary, rabbinic, and elevated usage. Used when emphasizing complete or essential truth rather than partial correctness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **דבר אמת**
**Transliteration:** *davar ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “word/thing of truth”  
**Natural English translation:** true statement, truthful thing  
**Context of use:** Formal, literary, and religious usage for a truthful utterance or valid claim. Less common in casual speech.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **אנשי אמת**
**Transliteration:** *anshei ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “people of truth”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful people, people of integrity  
**Context of use:** Biblical, religious, and elevated moral discourse. Used for persons marked by honesty and integrity.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **תורת אמת**
**Transliteration:** *torat ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “teaching/law of truth”  
**Natural English translation:** true teaching, Torah of truth  
**Context of use:** Religious and liturgical usage. Refers to divine teaching or doctrine understood as true.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **חותמו של הקדוש ברוך הוא אמת**
**Transliteration:** *ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh hu ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “the seal of the Holy One, blessed be He, is truth”  
**Natural English translation:** “God’s seal is truth”  
**Context of use:** Rabbinic and religious discourse. A fixed traditional saying about divine truthfulness.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **אמת ויציב**
**Transliteration:** *ʾemet ve-yatsiv*  
**Literal breakdown:** “true and firm”  
**Natural English translation:** true and certain / true and established  
**Context of use:** Liturgical and religious phrase, also recognizable culturally outside prayer contexts. It can be cited as a formula of affirmation.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **אמת ושלום**

**Natural English translation:** truth and peace  
**Context of use:** Biblical, religious, and public-moral discourse. Used in value language rather than narrow factual evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### **איש אמת**
**Transliteration:** *ʾish ʾemet*  
**Literal breakdown:** “man of truth”  
**Natural English translation:** truthful man, man of integrity  
**Context of use:** Literary, moral, and religious usage for a person characterized by honesty. Not especially colloquial.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Root: **צ־ד־ק** — *ts-d-q*
