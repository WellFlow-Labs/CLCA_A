# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns support a grouping, it is described; where they do not, items are left ungrouped or flagged as ambiguous. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data, not a desire for symmetry.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or affirm that something is the case

**Label:** Direct affirmation of truth or correctness

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker asserts, with their own epistemic commitment, that a proposition is true, that a thing is genuine, or that a state of affairs is as described. They appear in contexts of testimony, confirmation, and correction of prior claims. The direct evidential suffix *-mi/-m* is characteristically present, marking the speaker's first-hand grounding.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaqmi* — "it is indeed true (I know directly)"
- *cheqam kay* — "this is true"
- *hinam* — "it is indeed so"
- *allinmi* — "it is indeed good/right"
- *kikinmi* — "it is indeed the very one"
- *cheqaqtam rimani* — "I am speaking the truth"
- *cheqaqtam willakuni* — "I am reporting truthfully"
- *cheqatam willasqayki* — "I have told you the truth"
- *cheqaqpuni* — "absolutely true, definitely genuine"
- *cheqa cheqa* — "truly true, absolutely genuine" (reduplicated emphatic)
- *kikinmi paymi* — "it is indeed he/she himself/herself"
- *qelqasqam* — "it is written / it is documented"

**Notes:** The presence of *-mi/-m* is not optional in these contexts; it is a grammatical requirement for assertive predicates in Southern Quechua. The grouping therefore reflects a grammatically enforced pattern of epistemic commitment, not merely a stylistic tendency. *Cheqaqpuni* and *cheqa cheqa* represent intensified forms within this group, used when doubt has been raised or when emphasis is required.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to deny or negate truth, correctness, or genuineness

**Label:** Negation of truth or correctness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to deny that a proposition is true, that a thing is genuine, or that a state of affairs is as described. They follow the grammatical pattern *mana* [predicate] *-chu* and appear in contexts of contradiction, correction, and refusal of a prior claim. They are the standard forms for rejecting a truth assertion in Southern Quechua discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *mana cheqachu* — "it is not true"
- *mana cheqa* — "untrue, not genuine" (attributive)
- *mana cheqaqmi* — "it is definitely not true (I know directly)"
- *mana allinchu* — "it is not good/right"
- *mana kikinchu* — "it is not the same / not genuine"
- *mana hinachu* — "it is not so / that is not how it is"
- *mana llullachu* — "it is not a lie"
- *mana pantasqa* — "not mistaken, without error"
- *mana pantaq* — "unerring, one who does not make mistakes"
- *mana yachaq* — "one who does not know, ignorant"
- *mana riqsinichu* — "I do not recognize it / I am not acquainted with it"
- *mana yachanichu* / *mana yacha-ni-chu* — "I do not know"

**Notes:** This group is defined by a shared grammatical construction (*mana* + *-chu*) applied across multiple base items. The group is internally heterogeneous in what is being negated — truth of a proposition (*mana cheqachu*), identity (*mana kikinchu*), knowledge (*mana yachanichu*) — but the usage pattern of negation-in-context is consistent. *Mana llullachu* is notable as a negation of falsity rather than an affirmation of truth, and could also be placed in Group 1; it is listed here because its primary function is denial.

## Group 3: Expressions used to mark the source of a truth claim

**Label:** Marking the epistemic source of a claim

**Definition:** These expressions encode, as part of their grammatical form, information about how the speaker came to know or believe what they are asserting — whether through direct experience, through hearsay, or with uncertainty. They appear obligatorily in assertive contexts in Southern Quechua and are not optional stylistic additions. The contrast among these forms is systematic and grammaticalized.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaqmi* — "it is indeed true" (direct evidential: speaker knows first-hand)
- *cheqaqsi* — "reportedly true" (reportative evidential: speaker heard it from someone else)
- *cheqaqchus* — "is it really true? / perhaps true" (dubitative: speaker is uncertain)
- *hinam* — "it is indeed so" (direct evidential)
- *hinachus* — "is it really so? / perhaps it is like that" (dubitative)
- *allinmi* — "it is indeed good/right" (direct evidential)
- *allinchus* — "is it good/right?" (dubitative/interrogative)
- *cheqaqsi* — "reportedly true" (reportative)

**Notes:** This group reflects the grammaticalized evidential system of Southern Quechua, in which the source of a speaker's knowledge is obligatorily marked on predicates. The grouping is not based on a shared lexical root but on a shared grammatical function: all these expressions encode epistemic source as part of their form. The three-way contrast (direct / reportative / dubitative) is systematic across predicates. This group overlaps substantially with Group 1 (the direct evidential forms also appear there), but the grouping here foregrounds the contrast *among* source-marking forms rather than the affirmative function of any single form.

---

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate speech or testimony as truthful or false

**Label:** Evaluation of speech and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in contexts where the truthfulness or falsity of a spoken or reported account is at issue. They combine truth-evaluation roots with words for speech, mouth, or narration (*simi*, *rimay*, *willakuy*), and appear in legal testimony, moral exhortation, and the evaluation of reported accounts. The focus is on the act of speaking or reporting, not on the truth of a state of affairs independently of its being communicated.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaq simi* — "true word / truthful speech / honest testimony"
- *cheqa simi* — "true word / truthful speech"
- *llulla simi* — "false word / lying speech / dishonest testimony"
- *cheqa rimay* — "to speak truthfully / truthful speaking"
- *llulla rimay* — "to speak falsely / false speaking"
- *cheqaqta rimay* — "to speak the truth"
- *cheqaqta willakuy* — "to report truly / to give a true account"
- *llulla willakuy* — "false account / lying narration"
- *cheqa willakuy* — "true account / accurate narration"
- *cheqaqtam rimani* — "I am speaking the truth"
- *cheqaqtam willakuni* — "I am reporting truthfully"
- *cheqa nispa rimay* — "speak saying it is true / speak truthfully"
- *llulla nispa rimay* — "to speak saying it is false / to speak falsely"
- *llullam rimawanki* — "you are lying to me"
- *llullam nisqayki* — "what you said is a lie"
- *cheqatam willasqayki* — "I have told you the truth"
- *cheqaqtam yachani* — "I know what is true"
- *riqsispa rimay* — "speak from acquaintance / speak having recognized it"
- *yachaspa rimay* — "speak from knowledge / speak having learned it"

**Notes:** The last two expressions (*riqsispa rimay*, *yachaspa rimay*) belong here because they impose a condition on speech — that it be grounded in genuine knowledge or acquaintance — rather than simply describing speech as truthful. They are also relevant to Group 5 (knowledge and acquaintance). The *-ta* accusative/adverbial construction (*cheqaqta rimay*, *cheqaqta willakuy*) is a consistent pattern in this group, marking the truth-evaluation term as an adverbial modifier of the speech verb.

---

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate whether someone has genuine knowledge or acquaintance

**Label:** Genuine knowledge and acquaintance

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a person genuinely knows something — either propositionally (knowing that something is the case) or by acquaintance (knowing or recognizing a person or thing). They appear in evaluations of expertise, in challenges to claims of knowledge, and in contexts of establishing whether someone is qualified to make a judgment. The contrast between *yachay* (propositional knowledge/skill) and *riqsiy* (knowledge by acquaintance/recognition) is preserved in usage.

**Representative expressions:**
- *yachay* — "to know, knowledge, skill"
- *yachaq* — "one who knows, knower, expert"
- *yachasqa* — "learned, known, skilled"
- *yachakuy* — "to learn, to study, to acquire knowledge"
- *yachachiy* — "to teach, to cause to know"
- *yachachiq* — "teacher, one who causes to know"
- *mana yachaq* — "one who does not know, ignorant"
- *yachanim* — "I know"
- *yachanichu* / *mana yachanichu* — "I don't know"
- *yachasqaykichu?* — "did you know it? / do you have knowledge of it?"
- *riqsiy* — "to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with"
- *riqsiq* — "one who knows, one who recognizes"
- *riqsisqa* — "recognized, known, identified"
- *riqsichiy* — "to introduce, to make known"
- *riqsikuy* — "to introduce oneself, to know oneself"
- *riqsinim* — "I know him/her/it"
- *mana riqsinichu* — "I do not recognize it"
- *riqsisqaykichu?* — "do you know him/her/it?"
- *cheqa yuyay* — "correct understanding, right opinion, true belief"

**Notes:** *Cheqa yuyay* ("correct thought / true opinion") belongs here because its usage concerns whether a belief or judgment is well-founded, which is closely related to whether the person holding it has genuine knowledge. It could also be placed in Group 6 (evaluation of persons). The distinction between *yachay* and *riqsiy* is preserved as a usage-based distinction within this group rather than collapsed.

---

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate a person's character as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Evaluation of a person's honesty or character

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize a person as fundamentally honest, genuine, or trustworthy, or conversely as fundamentally dishonest, deceptive, or false. They appear in moral evaluation of persons and in expressions of trust or distrust. The focus is on the person's character or inner disposition, not on a single act or statement.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqa runa* — "honest person, genuine person, person of integrity"
- *llulla runa* — "dishonest person, deceiver, person of false character"
- *cheqaq* (applied to persons) — "truthful, genuine" (as a character evaluation)
- *llullaq* — "liar, deceiver, one who is false"
- *llullakuq* — "habitual liar, one who lies repeatedly"
- *cheqa sunqu* — "sincere, honest at heart, genuine in spirit"
- *llulla sunqu* — "deceitful at heart, false-hearted, insincere in spirit"
- *cheqaq runam* — "he/she is a truthful person"
- *llullam kay runa* — "this person is a liar"
- *cheqa munayniyuq* — "one of genuine intention, truly willing, sincere in purpose"
- *pantaq* — "one who errs" (when applied to persons as a character evaluation)
- *mana pantaq* — "unerring, one who does not make mistakes" (applied to persons or sources)

**Notes:** *Pantaq* and *mana pantaq* are included here because in some attested uses they characterize a person or source (including divine authority) as reliable or unreliable, which overlaps with character evaluation. However, *pantaq* more typically describes a person who makes errors rather than one who is dishonest, and it belongs more centrally to Group 7 (error and mistake). *Mana pantaq* in religious contexts (applied to divine authority as infallible) is a usage that sits between this group and Group 7.

---

## Group 7: Expressions used to evaluate a statement, action, or judgment as mistaken or in error

**Label:** Error and mistake (as distinct from deliberate deception)

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a statement, action, or judgment as incorrect or mistaken, without necessarily implying deliberate deception. They appear in practical and moral contexts of identifying and correcting errors, and they contrast with expressions of deliberate falsehood (*llulla*) in that the error may be inadvertent. The root *panta-* is the primary base for this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *pantay* — "to err, to make a mistake, error"
- *pantasqa* — "mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error"
- *pantaq* — "one who errs, one who is wrong"
- *pantakuy* — "to err (intransitive), to go wrong"
- *pantachiy* — "to mislead, to cause an error, to lead astray"
- *pantachikuy* — "to mislead oneself, to cause oneself to err"
- *pantarisqa* — "having erred at a specific moment"
- *pantasqayuq* — "one who is in error, one who has made a mistake"
- *pantay pantay* — "full of errors, very wrong, repeatedly mistaken"
- *mana pantasqa* — "not mistaken, without error, correct"
- *mana pantaq* — "unerring, one who does not make mistakes"
- *pantarqankim* — "you were wrong / you made a mistake"
- *pantaychu* (negative imperative) — "do not err"
- *pantasqaykichu?* — "were you wrong? / did you make a mistake?"
- *pantasqayta yachani* — "I know that I was wrong / I know my mistake"
- *pantay tiyan* — "there is an error"
- *pantachiwankim* — "you are misleading me"
- *cheqachus pantasqachu?* — "is it true or is it mistaken?"

**Notes:** The causative form *pantachiy* ("to cause to err / to mislead") introduces an element of agency and potential deliberateness, which brings it closer to the *llulla* domain. It is placed here because the primary meaning concerns causing error rather than causing deception, but it is flagged as an overlapping item (see Ambiguous Items below).

---

## Group 8: Expressions used in correcting or replacing an inaccurate account

**Label:** Correction and replacement of inaccurate accounts

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in discourse contexts where a prior claim, appearance, or account is being replaced with a more accurate one. They signal a shift from what was said or appeared to be the case to what is actually the case. They appear at the discourse level, functioning as pragmatic markers of correction rather than simply as truth predicates.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaqa* — "the truth is..., in truth, actually, in reality" (topic-marked corrective)
- *cheqaqam, mana hinachu* — "the truth is, it is not like that"
- *cheqaqam, kay hinam* — "the truth is, it is like this"
- *cheqanchay* — "to verify, to confirm, to correct, to establish as true"
- *cheqanchasqa* — "verified, confirmed, corrected, established as true"
- *cheqanchakuy* — "to verify for oneself, to make sure"
- *cheqanchasqam* — "it has been verified / it is confirmed"
- *allinchay* — "to make good, to correct, to fix, to put right"
- *allinchasqa* — "corrected, fixed, made right"
- *mana cheqachu* (in corrective contexts) — "it is not true" (as a correction of a prior claim)

**Notes:** *Cheqaqa* is the most distinctively discourse-level item in this group; its function is specifically to introduce a corrective statement, not merely to assert truth. *Cheqanchay* and *allinchay* are more action-oriented (the act of correcting or verifying), while *cheqaqa* is a discourse marker. The group is held together by the shared function of replacing an inaccurate account, but the members differ in whether they are discourse markers, verbal actions, or resultative states.

---

## Group 9: Expressions used to assert identity or sameness of a thing or person

**Label:** Assertion of identity and sameness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something is the very thing in question, that two things are identical, or that a person is the genuine individual referred to. They appear in contexts of authentication, identification, and confirmation of identity, and they contrast with expressions that evaluate truth of propositions rather than identity of objects or persons.

**Representative expressions:**
- *kikin* — "same, identical, oneself, the very one, exact"
- *kikinmi* — "it is indeed the very one / it is exactly that"
- *kikinlla* — "exactly the same, precisely identical"
- *kikinmi paymi* — "it is indeed he/she himself/herself"
- *mana kikinchu* — "it is not the same / not genuine"
- *cheqa* (in authenticity contexts) — "genuine, real, authentic" (applied to objects)
- *cheqa hina* — "like the truth / appearing genuine / seemingly real"

**Notes:** *Cheqa* in authenticity contexts (e.g., evaluating whether an object is the genuine article rather than an imitation) overlaps with this group, but its primary usage is broader. *Cheqa hina* is included here because it specifically concerns the appearance of genuineness in an object or representation, which is closely related to identity evaluation. This group is smaller than others and may overlap with Group 1 in cases where asserting identity is also asserting truth.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used in legal, administrative, or testimonial contexts

**Label:** Legal, administrative, and testimonial usage

**Definition:** These expressions appear with particular frequency or with specialized meaning in contexts of legal testimony, administrative verification, and the evaluation of documents. They are not exclusively legal in usage, but their attestation in legal and administrative contexts is notable and distinguishes them from expressions used only in everyday moral or conversational discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *cheqaq simi* — "true word / truthful speech / honest testimony"
- *llulla simi* — "false word / lying speech / dishonest testimony"
- *cheqa qelqa* — "authentic document, correct text, genuine writing"
- *llulla qelqa* — "false document, forged writing, fraudulent record"
- *qelqasqa* — "written, recorded, documented"
- *qelqasqam* — "it is written / it is documented"
- *qelqasqa hinam* — "it is as written / it is as documented"
- *cheqanchasqa* — "verified, confirmed" (in official/administrative sense)
- *cheqanchasqam* — "it has been officially verified"
- *cheqanchay tiyan* — "it needs to be verified"
- *llullachiy* — "to cause someone to lie / to induce false testimony"
- *pantachiy* — "to mislead / to cause someone to err" (in legal contexts)
- *pantachiwankim* — "you are misleading me"
- *riqsispa rimay* — "speak from acquaintance / speak having recognized it"
- *yachaspa rimay* — "speak from knowledge / speak having learned it"
- *cheqaqtam willakuni* — "I am reporting truthfully"
- *cheqachus llullachus?* — "is it true or is it false?" (as a legal determination)

**Notes:** This group is defined by context of use rather than by a shared semantic feature. Several items in this group also appear in other groups (e.g., *cheqaq simi* in Group 4, *cheqanchasqa* in Group 8). The grouping reflects the observation that certain expressions cluster in legal and administrative attestation, not that they are exclusively legal terms.

---

## Group 11: Expressions used in moral instruction or exhortation

**Label:** Moral instruction and exhortation

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts of enjoining honest conduct, condemning dishonesty, or instructing someone to act correctly. They are used in moral teaching, religious instruction, and everyday moral evaluation, and they often take imperative or prohibitive forms. They are distinguished from purely descriptive truth-evaluation expressions by their directive or evaluative force toward conduct.

**Representative expressions:**
- *mana llullakuychu* — "do not lie / do not deceive" (negative imperative)
- *mana pantaychu* — "do not err / do not make a mistake" (negative imperative)
- *cheqa nispa rimay* — "speak truthfully / speak saying it is true" (imperative)
- *cheqa ñan* — "the right path / the correct way / the true road"
- *cheqa runa* — "honest person, person of integrity" (as a moral ideal)
- *llulla runa* — "dishonest person, deceiver" (as a moral condemnation)
- *cheqa sunqu* — "sincere, honest at heart" (as a moral quality)
- *llulla sunqu* — "deceitful at heart" (as a moral failing)
- *cheqan* — "what is right, what is correct, justice"
- *cheqanmi kay* — "this is what is right"
- *mana cheqanchu* — "that is not right / that is not just"
- *cheqa yupaychay* — "true worship, genuine reverence" (in religious instruction)
- *cheqa Dios* — "the true God" (in religious instruction)
- *allintam ruwarqanki* — "you did the right thing / you acted correctly"

**Notes:** *Cheqa ñan* ("the right path") is particularly associated with moral and religious instruction contexts, where it refers to a correct course of conduct. *Cheqa Dios* and *cheqa yupaychay* are specifically attested in Quechua Christian religious instruction and represent a specialized sub-context within this group. The group overlaps with Group 6 (character evaluation) in items like *cheqa runa* and *cheqa sunqu*, which function both as descriptions of character and as moral ideals to be pursued.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items do not fit cleanly into a single group, or their usage patterns support placement in multiple groups. They are listed here with notes on the relevant overlaps.

---

**cheqa** (base form)
- Belongs to: Groups 1, 6, 8, 9, 11
- Notes: As the primary root, *cheqa* participates in virtually all groups. Its base form is used predicatively (Group 1), attributively in character evaluation (Group 6), in corrective discourse (Group 8), in authenticity/identity contexts (Group 9), and in moral instruction (Group 11). It cannot be assigned to a single group.

---

**cheqan** ("what is right, justice")
- Belongs to: Groups 1, 11
- Notes: *Cheqan* is used both to assert what is correct in a given situation (Group 1) and in moral and legal evaluation of whether a course of action is just (Group 11). Its usage in legal contexts also connects it to Group 10.

---

**pantachiy** ("to mislead, to cause to err")
- Belongs to: Groups 7, 10
- Notes: *Pantachiy* primarily describes causing inadvertent error (Group 7), but in legal contexts it describes deliberate misleading of a witness or party, which brings it close to the *llulla* domain (deliberate deception). Its causative structure also connects it to Group 10 (legal contexts).

---

**llullachiy** ("to cause someone to lie, to induce false testimony")
- Belongs to: Groups 6, 10
- Notes: *Llullachiy* evaluates the character of the person who induces lying (Group 6) and appears specifically in legal contexts of evaluating induced false testimony (Group 10). It is less clearly a member of Group 7 (error) because the deception is deliberate.

---

**cheqa hina** ("like the truth, appearing genuine")
- Belongs to: Groups 3, 9
- Notes: *Cheqa hina* expresses uncertainty about whether something is genuinely true or merely appears so (Group 3, epistemic source), and it evaluates the apparent identity or genuineness of an object (Group 9). It does not fit cleanly into either group.

---

**allin** and its derivatives (*allinta*, *allinchay*, *allinchasqa*, *allinpuni*, *mana allin*)
- Belongs to: Groups 1, 2, 8, 11
- Notes: *Allin* covers a broader evaluative domain than truth specifically (goodness, correctness, propriety, health). Its participation in the truth-evaluation domain is real but secondary to its broader evaluative function. It appears in Groups 1 and 2 (affirmation and negation), Group 8 (correction: *allinchay*), and Group 11 (moral instruction). It is not a central member of any single group.

---

**mana pantaq** ("unerring, one who does not make mistakes")
- Belongs to: Groups 6, 7
- Notes: When applied to persons in everyday contexts, *mana pantaq* evaluates character (Group 6). When applied to divine authority in religious contexts, it functions as a claim of infallibility that is closer to Group 11 (moral/religious instruction). It is also a negated form within Group 7.

---

**cheqa yuyay** ("correct understanding, right opinion, true belief")
- Belongs to: Groups 5, 6, 11
- Notes: *Cheqa yuyay* concerns whether a belief or judgment is well-founded (Group 5, knowledge), whether a person's views reflect good character (Group 6), and whether someone is thinking correctly in a moral sense (Group 11). Its placement depends on context.

---

**riqsispa rimay** and **yachaspa rimay** ("speak from acquaintance / speak from knowledge")
- Belongs to: Groups 4, 5, 10
- Notes: These converb constructions impose a condition on speech (Group 4, evaluation of speech), require genuine knowledge or acquaintance (Group 5), and appear in legal and testimonial contexts (Group 10). They are genuinely multi-group items.

---

**cheqachus llullachus?** ("is it true or is it false?")
- Belongs to: Groups 3, 10
- Notes: This disjunctive interrogative marks epistemic uncertainty (Group 3) and appears in legal contexts as a demand for a determination (Group 10). It also involves the truth/falsity contrast that runs through Groups 1 and 2.

---

## Items that do not fit clearly into any group

The following items from P1 and P2 do not show sufficient usage-pattern evidence to place them in any of the groups above. They are listed here without forced assignment.

---

**tiqsi** ("foundation, basis, origin, fundamental nature")
- Notes: *Tiqsi* appears in philosophical and cosmological discourse and in nominal compounds (*tiqsi muyu*, *tiqsi pacha*). Its connection to truth evaluation is through the concept of fundamental nature, but no usage patterns in P1 or P2 show it functioning in the same contexts as the other items. It remains ungrouped.

---

**rura** ("real, genuine, actual")
- Notes: *Rura* was assigned [UNCERTAIN] status in P1 due to limited and variable attestation. No usage patterns in P1 or P2 are sufficient to place it in any group. It remains ungrouped.

---

**kallpachakuy** ("to confirm, to validate, to verify, to strengthen a claim")
- Notes: *Kallpachakuy* was assigned [UNCERTAIN] status in P1. While its morphological structure is transparent, no usage patterns in P1 or P2 are sufficient to place it in any group with confidence. It remains ungrouped.

---

**ima kay** ("what it is, its nature, its essence")
- Notes: *Ima kay* was assigned [UNCERTAIN] status in P1. It appears in philosophical and reflective contexts but no usage patterns in P1 or P2 are sufficient to place it in any group. It remains ungrouped.

---

**cheqa kay** ("truth, reality, genuineness, what is truly the case")
- Notes: *Cheqa kay* was assigned [MARGINAL] status in P1 and appears in formal or reflective registers. It is related to Group 1 (affirmation of truth) and Group 11 (moral/religious instruction) but its usage is too restricted and register-specific to place it confidently in either. It is flagged as potentially belonging to a register-specific sub-group that is not sufficiently documented to describe here.
