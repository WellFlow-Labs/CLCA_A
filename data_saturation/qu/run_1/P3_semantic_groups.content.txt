# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1 and P2. No external conceptual framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns support multiple groupings for a single item, this is noted. Where no clear grouping is supported, items are listed separately at the end. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data as found.

---

## GROUP 1: Expressions used to assert or deny the truth of a claim

**Label:** Direct truth predication

**Definition:** These expressions are used as complete or near-complete utterances to assert that a statement, claim, or situation is true or false. They appear in contexts of testimony, dispute, and everyday affirmation, and they typically take the direct evidential enclitic *-m/-mi* or the negative polar *-chu*. Their primary function is to commit the speaker to a position on the truth-value of something.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqam* — 'it is true' (with direct evidential)
- *cheqam kay* — 'it is true / that is the truth'
- *mana cheqachu* — 'it is not true / it is false'
- *llullam* — 'it is a lie / that is false'
- *mana llullachu* — 'it is not a lie'
- *cheqachus?* — 'is it true?'
- *cheqachus, manachus?* — 'is it true or not?'
- *llullachu?* — 'is it a lie?'
- *pantasqam* — 'it is mistaken / that is wrong'
- *sut'im* — 'it is clear / that is plain'
- *mana sut'ichu* — 'it is not clear'
- *chiqapmi* — 'truly / indeed' (Ayacucho-Chanka)
- *chiqap cheqam* — 'it is truly true' (emphatic, Ayacucho-Chanka)
- *cheqallam* — 'it is purely the truth'
- *kikinmi* — 'it is the very same / it is the genuine one'
- *mana kikinchu* — 'it is not the same / it is not genuine'

**Notes:** This group is the largest and most internally consistent. The direct evidential *-m/-mi* is a near-obligatory feature of the assertoric members. The interrogative members (*-chu*) form a natural sub-cluster. *Pantasqam* and *sut'im* participate in this group but also appear in other groups (see below).

## GROUP 2: Expressions used to characterize a person's speech as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Evaluation of speech as truthful or deceptive

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate a speech act or a speaker's words as honest, accurate, deceptive, or false. They appear in legal testimony, moral instruction, and everyday contexts where the reliability of what someone says is at issue. The target of evaluation is the speech act itself or the words produced, not a state of affairs in the world.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqata rimay* — 'tell the truth / speak truthfully'
- *cheqata rimani* — 'I am telling the truth'
- *cheqa simi* — 'true word / truthful speech'
- *cheqa rimay* — 'truthful speaking / to speak truthfully'
- *llulla simi* — 'false word / lying speech'
- *llulla rimay* — 'to speak falsely / lying speech'
- *llulla nispa rimay* — 'to speak saying it is a lie / to speak falsely'
- *cheqa nispa rimay* — 'to assert truthfully'
- *mana cheqam nispa rimay* — 'to say that it is not true'
- *sut'ita rimay* — 'speak clearly / say it plainly'
- *sut'ita rimani* — 'I am speaking clearly'
- *allin rimay* — 'to speak well / proper speaking'
- *allin simi* — 'good word / proper speech'
- *kikin simiwan rimay* — 'to speak with one's own words / in one's own voice'
- *cheqam, Diospa sutimpi* — 'it is true, in God's name' (sworn testimony formula)

**Notes:** *Cheqata rimay* and *sut'ita rimay* are structurally parallel (adjective in accusative + *rimay*) and appear in the same instructional and testimonial contexts. *Cheqa simi* and *llulla simi* are direct contrasts within this group. *Allin rimay* and *allin simi* overlap with Group 5 (evaluation of conduct and character) but appear here specifically in speech-evaluation contexts.

## GROUP 3: Expressions used in the process of verifying or confirming something

**Label:** Verification and confirmation acts

**Definition:** These expressions describe or name the act of checking a claim, document, or situation against reality and establishing its accuracy. They appear in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts where an agent actively investigates or certifies the truth of something. They are distinct from simple truth predication (Group 1) in that they describe a process rather than a result.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqanchay* — 'to verify / to confirm / to establish as true'
- *cheqanchakuy* — 'to verify for oneself / to confirm on one's own behalf'
- *cheqanchasqa* — 'verified / officially confirmed / certified'
- *cheqanchasqam* — 'it has been verified / it is confirmed'
- *cheqanchaq* — 'one who verifies / a verifier'
- *qelqata cheqanchay* — 'to verify a document'
- *cheqanchay atinichu* — 'I cannot verify it'
- *riqsispa cheqanchay* — 'to verify by recognizing / to confirm through identification' [UNCERTAIN]
- *sut'inchay* — 'to clarify / to make explicit / to explain'
- *sut'inchakuy* — 'to clarify for oneself'
- *sut'inchasqa* — 'clarified / made explicit'
- *sut'inchaykuway* — 'please clarify it for me'
- *sut'inchay atinichu* — 'I cannot clarify it'
- *allinchay* — 'to correct / to fix / to make right'
- *allinchakuy* — 'to correct oneself / to improve oneself'
- *allinchachiy* — 'to have something fixed / to cause someone to correct'
- *pantasqata allinchay* — 'to correct what is mistaken'
- *qelqata allinchay* — 'to correct a document'
- *allinchay atinichu* — 'I cannot fix it'

**Notes:** *Cheqanchay*, *sut'inchay*, and *allinchay* form a natural cluster here: all three are *-ncha-* causative derivatives of evaluative adjectives, all describe an agent acting on something to bring it into a correct or clear state, and all appear in administrative and pedagogical contexts. *Allinchay* also overlaps with Group 5 (moral correction of conduct). *Cheqanchasqa* and *sut'inchasqa* appear here as resultative forms describing the outcome of verification/clarification acts, but they also participate in Group 1 as predicative assertions.

## GROUP 4: Expressions used to describe being misled or causing error in another

**Label:** Causing or suffering error through another's action

**Definition:** These expressions describe situations where one person's action leads another into false belief or mistaken conduct. They appear in legal and moral contexts where the source of an error is attributed to an external agent, and where responsibility for the error is distributed between the one who misled and the one who was misled. They are distinct from expressions of simple error (Group 6) in that they involve an agent who acts on another.

**Representative expressions:**

- *pantachiy* — 'to mislead / to cause to err / to lead astray'
- *pantachiq* — 'one who misleads / a deceiver'
- *pantachisqa* — 'misled / caused to err / led astray'
- *pantachiwanku* — 'they misled me'
- *mana pantachinaykichu* — 'you should not mislead anyone'
- *llullachiy* — 'to cause to lie / to induce falsehood'
- *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani* — 'I was mistaken because of his/her lying'
- *llullakuyta munay* — 'to want to lie / to intend to deceive' [overlaps with Group 6]

**Notes:** *Pantachiy* and *llullachiy* are the core members. The construction *llullakusqanmanta pantarqani* is notable for linking deception by another (*llullakuy*) to one's own resulting error (*pantay*), making the causal relationship between Groups 4 and 6 explicit. *Llullakuyta munay* is placed here because it describes intentional deception directed at another, but it could also be grouped with expressions of deliberate lying (Group 6).

---

## GROUP 5: Expressions used to evaluate a person's character or inner disposition

**Label:** Moral characterization of persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe a person as genuinely good, honest, bad, or deceptive in their character or inner disposition, rather than evaluating a specific act or statement. They appear in everyday moral discourse, narrative characterization, and religious contexts. Many involve the word *sonqo* ('heart, inner self') or the word *runa* ('person'), marking the evaluation as applying to the person as a whole rather than to a single action.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa sonqo* — 'sincere / honest-hearted / genuine in spirit'
- *cheqa sonqoyoq* — 'one who has a true heart / a person of integrity'
- *cheqaq runa* — 'a truthful person / a genuine person'
- *cheqaq runam* — 'he/she is a truthful person'
- *allin sonqo* — 'good-hearted / kind / well-intentioned'
- *allin sonqoyoq* — 'one who has a good heart / a kind-hearted person'
- *allin runa* — 'a good person / a person of good character'
- *llulla sonqo* — 'false-hearted / deceitful in spirit / insincere'
- *llulla runa* — 'a lying person / a deceitful person'
- *llullaq* — 'liar / one who lies / deceiver'
- *llullakuq* — 'one who habitually lies / a chronic liar'
- *mana allin runa* — 'a bad person / a person of bad character'
- *allinchakuy* — 'to correct oneself / to improve oneself' [overlaps with Group 3]

**Notes:** The *sonqo* compounds (*cheqa sonqo*, *allin sonqo*, *llulla sonqo*) form a tight sub-cluster: they all evaluate the inner disposition of a person rather than their outward acts. The *runa* compounds (*allin runa*, *cheqaq runa*, *llulla runa*, *mana allin runa*) form a parallel sub-cluster evaluating persons as social and moral agents. *Allinchakuy* appears here because it describes self-directed moral improvement, but it also belongs to Group 3 as a verification/correction act.

## GROUP 6: Expressions used to describe the act of lying or being in error

**Label:** Acts of lying and erring

**Definition:** These expressions describe the act of lying, deceiving, or being mistaken, with the agent as the one who performs or undergoes the act. They appear in moral, legal, and everyday contexts where the conduct of a person is evaluated as dishonest or erroneous. They are distinct from Group 5 (character evaluation) in that they focus on specific acts rather than stable dispositions, and from Group 4 in that no external agent causes the error.

**Representative expressions:**

- *llullakuy* — 'to lie / to deceive / to tell falsehoods'
- *llullakusqa* — 'having lied / the act of lying (completed)'
- *mana llullakuychu* — 'do not lie'
- *llullakuyta munay* — 'to want to lie / to intend to deceive'
- *pantay* — 'to err / to be mistaken / to make an error'
- *pantarqani* — 'I was wrong / I made a mistake'
- *mana pantaychu* — 'do not err'
- *pantakuy* — 'to err on one's own account / to go astray'
- *pantasqayta yachani* — 'I know that I was wrong'
- *pantasqa* — 'mistaken / erroneous / wrong'
- *pantasqam* — 'it is mistaken / that is wrong'

**Notes:** *Pantay* and *llullakuy* are the core verbal members. *Pantasqa* and *pantasqam* overlap with Group 1 (direct truth predication) when used predicatively, but they are grouped here because their primary derivational and contextual home is the description of erring. *Mana llullakuychu* and *mana pantaychu* are negative imperatives prohibiting these acts, and they appear in the same moral instruction contexts as the positive forms.

---

## GROUP 7: Expressions used to assert the identity or sameness of something

**Label:** Identity and sameness assertion

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something is the very same thing, the identical original, or the genuine article rather than a substitute or copy. They appear in contexts of authentication, identification, and the distinction of originals from imitations. They differ from simple truth predication (Group 1) in that the evaluative target is identity or sameness rather than the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kikin* — 'same / identical / self / the very one / genuine'
- *kikinmi* — 'it is the very same / it is the genuine one'
- *kikinlla* — 'the very same / exactly identical / none other than'
- *kikinllam* — 'it is none other than (I know this directly)'
- *mana kikinchu* — 'it is not the same / it is not the genuine one'
- *kikin kasqanta riqsiy* — 'to recognize it as the genuine one'
- *cheqa kikin* — 'the very genuine thing itself / truly identical'
- *cheqa hina rikukun* — 'it appears to be genuine / it looks like the real thing'
- *llulla hina rikukun* — 'it appears to be false / it looks like a lie'
- *allin hina rikukun* — 'it appears to be good / it looks fine'
- *cheqa hina* — 'like the real thing / as if genuine'
- *llulla hina* — 'like a lie / as if false'
- *mana hina* — 'not like that / unlike it'

**Notes:** The *kikin* expressions form the core of this group. The *hina* similative constructions (*cheqa hina*, *llulla hina*, *allin hina rikukun*) are included here because their primary function is to evaluate resemblance or likeness — the degree to which something approximates the genuine article — rather than to assert truth or goodness directly. *Cheqa hina* and *llulla hina* are direct contrasts within this sub-cluster. *Cheqa kikin* combines both the truth-evaluation and identity-assertion functions and overlaps with Group 1.

---

## GROUP 8: Expressions used in the evidential marking of claims

**Label:** Marking the source and certainty of a claim

**Definition:** These expressions encode the speaker's relationship to the evidence for a claim — whether the speaker knows something directly, has heard it from another, or is only guessing. They appear across all registers and attach to predicates of all kinds, including truth-evaluation predicates. Their function is not to evaluate the content of a claim but to signal how the speaker came to hold it and how confident they are.

**Representative expressions:**

- *-m / -mi* — direct evidential ('I know this directly / I assert this')
- *-s / -si* — reportative evidential ('reportedly / I heard that')
- *-chá* — conjectural marker ('probably / perhaps / I suppose')
- *cheqam* (vs. *cheqas*) — 'it is true (I know directly)' vs. 'it is true (I was told)'
- *cheqachá* — 'it is probably true / perhaps it is genuine'
- *llullachá* — 'it is probably a lie / I suspect it is false'
- *pantasqachá* — 'it is probably mistaken / I think it may be wrong'
- *cheqas* — 'it is true, reportedly'
- *llullas nispa rimay* — 'to report that it is false'
- *cheqam nispa rimay* — 'to say that it is true (asserting direct knowledge)'

**Notes:** This group is structurally distinct from all others in that its members are enclitics rather than roots or compounds. They do not form a semantic field in the usual sense but rather a grammatical system that cuts across all other groups. Every truth-evaluation expression in Groups 1–7 can in principle take any of these three markers, and the choice among them constitutes a separate layer of meaning. The contrast *cheqam* vs. *cheqas* is included here as the clearest illustration of the system's function.

---

## GROUP 9: Expressions used in the evaluation of written documents

**Label:** Assessment of written records

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in contexts where a written document, text, or record is evaluated for its accuracy, authenticity, or proper form. They appear in administrative and legal registers and involve the combination of truth-evaluation roots with the word *qelqa* ('writing, document'). They are a contextually specialized sub-cluster rather than a semantically independent group.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa qelqa* — 'authentic document / genuine text / true record'
- *cheqa qelqam* — 'it is an authentic document'
- *llulla qelqa* — 'false document / forged text / fraudulent record'
- *llulla qelqam* — 'it is a false document'
- *allin qelqa* — 'a good document / a well-written text / a correct record'
- *qelqata cheqanchay* — 'to verify a document'
- *qelqata allinchay* — 'to correct a document'
- *qelqasqa* — 'written / recorded in writing'
- *qelqapi qelqasqa* — 'written in a document / recorded in writing'
- *qelqachiy* — 'to cause to write / to have something written / to dictate'
- *cheqanchasqam* — 'it has been verified / it is confirmed' (used of documents)

**Notes:** This group is defined by context of use (written documents) rather than by a shared root or morphological pattern. *Cheqa qelqa* and *llulla qelqa* are direct contrasts. The group overlaps substantially with Groups 1 and 3: *cheqa qelqam* is also a direct truth predication, and *qelqata cheqanchay* is also a verification act. The grouping is justified by the consistent co-occurrence of *qelqa* with truth-evaluation vocabulary in administrative and legal registers.

---

## GROUP 10: Expressions used to describe knowledge and recognition of persons or things

**Label:** Knowing and recognizing

**Definition:** These expressions describe the states and acts of knowing a fact, knowing a person or thing, or recognizing something as what it is. They appear in epistemic evaluation contexts where the question is whether a person correctly knows or identifies something. The contrast between *yachay* (knowing facts/skills) and *riqsiy* (knowing/recognizing persons and things) is maintained within this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- *yachay* — 'to know (a fact or skill) / to learn'
- *yachani* — 'I know'
- *yachanichu* — 'I do not know'
- *yachay atinichu* — 'I cannot know / it is impossible to know'
- *cheqa kasqanta yachani* — 'I know that it is true'
- *mana cheqa kasqanta yachay* — 'to know that it is not true'
- *llulla kasqanta yachay* — 'to know that it is a lie'
- *mana allin kasqanta yachay* — 'to know that it is not good'
- *sut'i kasqanta yachay* — 'to know that it is clear'
- *pantasqa kasqanta mana yachanichu* — 'I did not know that it was wrong'
- *pantasqayta yachani* — 'I know that I was wrong'
- *mana yachanichu imachus kasqanta* — 'I do not know what it is'
- *mana yachanichu imayna kasqanta* — 'I do not know what it is like'
- *riqsiy* — 'to know / to recognize / to be acquainted with'
- *riqsinim* — 'I know him/her/it'
- *mana riqsinichu* — 'I do not recognize it'
- *riqsisqa* — 'known / recognized'
- *riqsichiy* — 'to introduce / to make known'
- *riqsikuy* — 'to get to know each other / to become acquainted'
- *kikin kasqanta riqsiy* — 'to recognize it as the genuine one'
- *cheqa riqsiy* — 'true recognition / correct identification' [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqa yachay* — 'true knowledge / genuine understanding'

**Notes:** The *X kasqanta yachay* construction (knowing that X is the case) is the most productive pattern in this group and links it directly to Groups 1 and 6: the object of *yachay* is typically a truth-evaluation predicate. *Riqsiy* forms a sub-cluster focused on recognition and acquaintance rather than propositional knowledge. *Cheqa yachay* and *cheqa riqsiy* are compounds that combine the truth-evaluation root with knowledge vocabulary and could also be placed in Group 1.

## GROUP 11: Expressions used in descriptions of authentic or inauthentic ways of living

**Label:** Authentic and inauthentic existence

**Definition:** These expressions apply truth-evaluation and goodness-evaluation roots to entire ways of living or modes of existence rather than to specific statements, acts, or persons. They appear in philosophical, religious, and contemporary political discourse about what constitutes a genuine or proper life. They are distinguished from character evaluation (Group 5) by their focus on a mode of living rather than a person's disposition.

**Representative expressions:**

- *cheqa kawsay* — 'true life / real life / genuine existence / authentic living'
- *allin kawsay* — 'good life / well-being / living well / the good life'
- *llulla kawsay* — 'false life / inauthentic existence / a life of deception' [UNCERTAIN]
- *sumaq kawsayta maskay* — 'to seek a good life / to look for excellent living'

**Notes:** This is a small group with limited membership. *Cheqa kawsay* and *allin kawsay* are the most firmly attested. *Llulla kawsay* is flagged [UNCERTAIN] in P1b and is included here with that caveat. The group is notable because it represents an extension of truth-evaluation and goodness-evaluation vocabulary to a domain (modes of living) that is distinct from the propositional and behavioral domains that dominate the other groups. *Sumaq kawsayta maskay* is included because it appears in the same philosophical and political discourse contexts as *allin kawsay*.

---

## GROUP 12: Expressions used in social greeting and well-being inquiry

**Label:** Formulaic well-being exchange

**Definition:** These expressions are used in conventionalized social exchanges about a person's well-being. They involve the root *allin* but their evaluative content is largely reduced to a phatic social function: the inquiry and its expected response form a fixed exchange. They are distinguished from other *allin* expressions by their formulaic character and their restriction to social greeting contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *imaynallan?* — 'how are you? / how is it going?'
- *allinllan / allinllam* — 'just fine / quite well' (response to greeting)
- *allinllachu?* — 'are you all right? / is everything okay?'
- *allin p'unchaykim* — 'good day (to you)'
- *allin p'unchay* — 'good day / a good day'
- *allin tuta* — 'good night'
- *allin kachun* — 'may it be good / may all be well' (benediction)
- *allinmi kanqa* — 'it will be all right / everything will be fine'
- *mana allinkuchu* — 'he/she is not well / it is not going well'

**Notes:** This group is defined by register and function rather than by semantic content. The expressions are not used to evaluate the truth of a claim or the quality of an act; they are used to open, close, or maintain social interaction. *Allin kachun* is included here because it appears in farewell and ritual speech contexts alongside the greeting formulas, though its optative structure distinguishes it from the purely phatic members. *Mana allinkuchu* is included because it is the negative counterpart used in the same social contexts to report that someone is unwell.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

The following items show clear usage patterns but do not fit cleanly into a single group, or their placement is uncertain for reasons noted.

---

**allin** (root)
Participates in Groups 3, 5, 6, 12, and potentially others. As a root it is too broadly distributed to assign to a single group. Its specific collocations and derivatives are assigned to groups above.

---

**cheqa** (root)
Participates in Groups 1, 2, 7, 9, 10, and 11. As a root it is the most broadly distributed item in the dataset. Its specific collocations and derivatives are assigned to groups above.

---

**sumaq** — 'beautiful / good / fine / excellent'
*Sumaq* appears in aesthetic evaluation contexts (*sumaqmi* 'it is beautiful') and in the philosophical *sumaq kawsay* construction. It participates in the same *-ncha-* derivational pathway as *cheqa* and *allin* (*sumaqchay* 'to beautify'). Its usage overlaps with *allin* in positive evaluation but is more strongly associated with aesthetic contexts. It does not fit cleanly into any single group above and is not forced into one.

---

**cheqa ñan** — 'the right path / the true way'
**allin ñan** — 'the right path / the good way'
These expressions apply truth-evaluation and goodness-evaluation roots to a course of conduct or belief. They appear in moral and religious instruction contexts. They overlap with Group 5 (moral characterization) and Group 11 (authentic existence) but are not identical to either. They are noted here as a pair without assignment to a single group.

---

**cheqa Dios** — 'the true God'
**llulla Dios** — 'a false god'
These expressions apply truth-evaluation roots to the ontological status of a divine being. They appear exclusively in religious discourse (colonial and contemporary Christian Quechua texts). They do not fit the patterns of Groups 1, 5, or 11 cleanly and are noted here without assignment.

---

**cheqa kaynin** — 'its true nature / its genuine being / what it truly is'
**cheqa kay** — 'truth / reality / genuineness / the state of being true'
These nominalized expressions refer to the abstract concept of truth or the essential nature of something. They appear in philosophical and religious discourse. They overlap with Group 1 (truth predication) and Group 11 (authentic existence) but function more as abstract nominals than as predicative or evaluative expressions. They are noted here without assignment to a single group.

---

**cheqa unanchay** — 'correct interpretation / true reading (of a sign or symbol)' [UNCERTAIN]
This expression applies the truth-evaluation root to the interpretation of signs and omens. It appears in traditional and religious discourse. It overlaps with Group 10 (knowing and recognizing) but involves a specialized hermeneutic context not well represented elsewhere in the dataset. It is flagged [UNCERTAIN] and noted here without assignment.

---

**cheqa kasqanman hina** — 'as if it were true / in the manner of its being genuine' [UNCERTAIN]
This complex similative construction overlaps with Group 7 (identity and sameness) and Group 1 (truth predication) but involves a conditional or hypothetical frame not shared by other members of either group. It is flagged [UNCERTAIN] and noted here without assignment.

---

**pantachikuy** — 'to cause oneself to err / to mislead oneself' [UNCERTAIN]
This expression overlaps with Group 4 (causing error in another) and Group 6 (acts of erring) but involves a reflexive self-directed causation that does not fit either group cleanly. It is flagged [UNCERTAIN] and noted here without assignment.

---

**qispiy** and its derivatives (*qispichiy*, *qispisqa*, *qispiq*, *qispisqam*, *qispiyta munay*)
The root *qispiy* ('to be free / to be saved / to be clear / to be pure') was flagged [MARGINAL] in P1. Its derivatives appear primarily in religious discourse (salvation, liberation from fault). They do not participate in the same usage patterns as the other groups in this analysis. No grouping is assigned.

---

**k'achi** — 'beautiful / fine / elegant / well-made' [UNCERTAIN]
This root was flagged [UNCERTAIN] in P1 and showed limited derivational productivity in P2a. Its usage in aesthetic evaluation overlaps with *sumaq* but is less well-attested. No grouping is assigned.

---

**rura** — 'deed / act / thing done' [UNCERTAIN]
The root *rura* as a standalone nominal was flagged [UNCERTAIN] in P1. Its participial form *rurasqa* ('made, done') appears in constructions like *allin rurasqam* ('it is well made'), which overlap with Group 3 (verification and correction) and Group 9 (written documents). No single grouping is assigned to the root itself.

---

**imayna** — 'how / in what way / what kind'
This interrogative form participates in Group 10 (*mana yachanichu imayna kasqanta* 'I do not know what it is like') and in Group 12 (*imaynallan?* 'how are you?'). Its distribution across these two groups reflects its general interrogative function rather than a specific semantic domain. No single grouping is assigned.
