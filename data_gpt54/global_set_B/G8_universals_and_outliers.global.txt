## 1. Candidate Universals / Strong Tendencies

1. **Simple predicative evaluation is broadly available in the truth-related domain.**  
   - **Statement:** All or nearly all attested languages have a basic way to say that something is true, false, correct, reliable, fake, valid, or similar, using ordinary predication.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 这是真的, 这个消息很可靠  
     - **he**: זה נכון, המקור מהימן  
     - **sa**: सत्यम् एतत्, युक्तं भवति  
     - **el**: Αυτό είναι αληθές, Η πηγή είναι έγκυρη  
     - **en**: The statement is true, The source is reliable  
     - **tl**: Totoo iyan, Tumpak ang sinabi mo  
     - **sw**: Ni kweli, Hii ni sahihi  
     - **qu**: allinmi, pantasqa  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** statement truth, correctness, credibility, authenticity, validity; simple predicative evaluation.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** The exact morphosyntax differs greatly: copular in some languages, zero-copula or predicate-initial in others. Quechua evidence is thinner and more lexically narrow than in the larger systems.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

2. **Negative predicative evaluation is also broadly available, usually by analytic negation and/or lexical negative forms.**  
   - **Statement:** The languages recurrently support direct rejection patterns such as “not true,” “incorrect,” “baseless,” “invalid,” or “fake.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 不可信, 不实, 失实  
     - **he**: לא נכון, לא מדויק, אינו תואם למציאות  
     - **sa**: न युक्तम्, प्रमाणं नास्ति, असत्य  
     - **el**: δεν είναι σωστό, ψευδής, αποδεικνύεται ψευδής  
     - **en**: not true, untrue, incorrect, invalid, fake  
     - **tl**: Hindi tama, Hindi pa tiyak, Walang saligan  
     - **sw**: si kweli, si sahihi, ni uongo, ni batili  
     - **qu**: mana allin, possibly mana chiqaq  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** negative predication across truth, correctness, credibility, support, validity, authenticity.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** The balance between analytic negation and lexical negative items varies. Quechua support is strongest for **mana allin** and weaker elsewhere.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

3. **Noun modification/classification is a strong recurrent strategy.**  
   - **Statement:** The languages broadly allow truth-related meanings to classify nouns: “true story,” “correct answer,” “fake document,” “reliable source,” “authentic product,” etc.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 虚假的信息, 名副其实的专家  
     - **he**: סיפור אמיתי, מסמך מזויף  
     - **sa**: तथ्यं वचनम्, प्रमाणिकः ग्रन्थः  
     - **el**: αληθινή ιστορία, πλαστή υπογραφή  
     - **en**: true story, authentic document  
     - **tl**: totoong balita, pekeng dokumento, wastong gamit  
     - **sw**: taarifa sahihi, dawa bandia, bidhaa ya asili  
     - **qu**: llulla runa, llulla simi, chiqaq simi  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** truth/falsity, correctness, authenticity, fakery, credibility.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** The morphosyntactic means differ substantially: compounds, agreement, linker forms, genitive constructions, or adjective+noun patterns.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

4. **A distinction between statement truth/falsity and correctness/right answer is strongly recurrent.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages lexicalize or at least recurrently separate “true/false” from “correct/incorrect/right/wrong.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 真/假 vs 正确/错误  
     - **he**: אמת/שקר or נכון/לא נכון vs צודק/נכון distinctions  
     - **sa**: सत्य/असत्य vs सम्यक्/युक्त/उचित  
     - **el**: αλήθεια/ψέμα vs σωστός/ορθός  
     - **en**: true/false vs correct/right  
     - **tl**: totoo vs tama/wasto  
     - **sw**: kweli/uongo vs sahihi  
     - **qu**: chiqaq/llulla vs allin/mana allin, pantasqa  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** statement truth vs correctness.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** Some broad items blur the distinction, e.g. **he נכון**, **tl tama**, **qu allin**, **zh 真/真实** in some contexts.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

5. **A distinction between broad correctness and narrower precision/exactness is also a strong tendency.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages distinguish being broadly right from being exact, precise, or finely accurate.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 正确 vs 准确  
     - **he**: נכון vs מדויק  
     - **el**: σωστός/ορθός vs ακρίβεια/ακριβής  
     - **en**: correct/right vs accurate/exact/precise  
     - **tl**: tama/wasto vs tumpak/eksakto  
     - **sw**: sahihi/usahihi covers much of this area, though the split is weaker  
     - **sa**: यथार्थ / तथ्य partly support a precision-like zone distinct from broader propriety  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** correctness vs precision.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** The split is weaker or less clearly lexicalized in **qu** and less sharply partitioned in **sw**. Sanskrit supports the distinction, but often through scholastic rather than everyday vocabulary.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

6. **Credibility/trustworthiness is recurrently distinguished from verified truth.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages have a lexical or constructional zone for “believable / trustworthy / reliable” that is not the same as “true.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 可信, 可靠 vs 属实  
     - **he**: אמין, מהימן vs אמת / נכון  
     - **el**: έγκυρος, credible-like uses vs αληθής  
     - **en**: credible, reliable, trustworthy vs true  
     - **tl**: mapagkakatiwalaan, maaasahan vs totoo  
     - **sw**: kuaminika, aminika, aminifu vs kweli  
     - **sa**: प्रमाणिक / प्रामाण्य partly overlap with validity and trust  
     - **qu**: iñiy / iñisqa suggest a weaker credibility/belief zone  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** credibility, trustworthiness, reliability, verification.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** In **sa** and **qu**, the distinction is less elaborated and may merge with belief, proof, or trust.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

7. **Honesty/sincerity is commonly expressed separately from bare truth-value.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages distinguish “being true” from “speaking honestly / sincerely / candidly.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 如实, 说真的, 真话  
     - **he**: לומר את האמת, למען האמת, אנשי אמת  
     - **sa**: सत्यवादिन्, सत्यभाषिन्, सत्यवचन  
     - **el**: ειλικρινής, ειλικρίνεια  
     - **en**: truthful, truthfulness, tell the truth  
     - **tl**: tapat, matapat, sa totoo lang  
     - **sw**: mkweli, aminifu, uaminifu  
     - **qu**: speech-specific contrasts like chiqaq simi / llulla simi, plus liar terms  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** honesty, sincerity, truthful speech, person-labeling, discourse framing.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** The boundary between honesty and trustworthiness is often fuzzy. In some languages, the same forms also cover loyalty or faithfulness.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

8. **Authenticity/genuineness vs fake/counterfeit is a strong recurrent lexical contrast.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages have a distinct lexical field for genuine/authentic objects, identities, or relations versus fake/counterfeit/forged ones.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 真品, 假货, 冒牌, 伪造  
     - **he**: אותנטי, מקורי, מזויף, זיוף  
     - **el**: αυθεντικός, γνήσιος, πλαστός, παραποιημένος  
     - **en**: genuine, authentic, real vs fake, counterfeit, forgery  
     - **tl**: tunay, totoong vs peke, huwad  
     - **sw**: halisi, ya asili vs bandia, feki  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** authenticity, fake/counterfeit, noun modification, verification/authentication.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** This domain is less clearly elaborated as a separate object-centered field in **sa** and **qu**.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

9. **Languages often distinguish simple error/mistake from lying/deception.**  
   - **Statement:** Wrongness due to mistake and wrongness due to deception are recurrently separated.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 错误 / 失实 vs 虚假 / 伪造 / 弄虚作假  
     - **he**: לא מדויק / לא נכון vs שקר / כוזב / שקרי / זיוף  
     - **sa**: panta-/mistake family vs मिथ्या / मृषा / असत्य / अनृत  
     - **el**: incorrectness/precision terms vs ψέμα / ψευδής / πλαστός / παραποιημένος  
     - **en**: incorrect/inaccurate vs lie/fake/forgery  
     - **tl**: tama/wasto/tumpak vs peke/huwad and lying-related sincerity terms  
     - **sw**: sahihi vs uongo / bandia / feki  
     - **qu**: pantay/pantasqa vs llulla/llullasqa  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** correctness, precision, falsehood, deception, counterfeit.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** Some languages blur the line in broad negative terms, but the distinction is still recurrently available.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

10. **Speech-action constructions are a strong recurrent packaging strategy.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages prefer constructions like “tell the truth,” “lie,” “answer truthfully,” or “false statement” rather than relying only on static truth predicates.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 如实回答, 说实话  
     - **he**: לומר את האמת, לספר שקר  
     - **sa**: सत्यं वद, मिथ्या वदति, असत्यं वदति  
     - **el**: λέω την αλήθεια, λέω ψέματα  
     - **en**: tell the truth  
     - **sw**: sema ukweli, alisema uongo  
     - **qu**: llullakuy, llulla simi / chiqaq simi  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** speech/reporting action, honesty, truth vs falsehood.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** In **tl**, discourse framing is more prominent in the construction tables than a central “tell the truth” light-verb pattern, though the broader domain still supports truthful-speech meanings.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

11. **Verification/proof/result constructions recurrently substitute for broad “become true” constructions.**  
   - **Statement:** Instead of a general inchoative “become true,” many languages use constructions meaning “be verified,” “prove true,” “be shown false,” “come out,” or “be authenticated.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 属实, 经查属实, 查证属实  
     - **he**: לגלות את האמת, להוציא את האמת לאור, מבחן האמת  
     - **sa**: प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्, प्रमाणसिद्ध  
     - **el**: αποδεικνύεται ψευδής, αποδείχθηκε πλαστή  
     - **en**: prove true, authenticate, validate  
     - **tl**: beripikahin, beripikado  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** verification, truth, authenticity, validity, dynamic/resultative constructions.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** **sw** and **qu** show this less clearly as a dedicated domain. Swahili has adjacent credibility/evidence constructions; Quechua evidence is sparse.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

12. **A single general inchoative “become true/false/correct/authentic” system is broadly weak or absent.**  
   - **Statement:** The languages generally do not show one unified, productive change-of-state pattern covering the whole truth-related domain.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Explicitly weak/absent in **he, sw, qu**  
     - Limited and domain-specific in **sa**  
     - Result/discovery-based in **el**  
     - Distributed across different constructions in **en**  
     - Concentrated in one family in **tl** (**magkatotoo / nagkatotoo**)  
     - Productive but lexically restricted in **zh**  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** inchoative/change-of-state across truth, correctness, authenticity, validity, credibility.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** English and Tagalog do have strong local patterns; Chinese also has productive change-of-state in some subdomains. So this is not “absence of inchoatives,” but rather “absence of a single broad inchoative system.”  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

13. **A single general causative “make X true/correct/authentic” system is also broadly weak.**  
   - **Statement:** Languages tend not to use one general causative construction across the truth-related domain; instead they use specialized verbs such as ‘verify’, ‘authenticate’, ‘validate’, ‘correct’, ‘falsify’, ‘mislead’.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 伪造, 发布虚假信息, 纠正错误  
     - **he**: להצדיק, להוכיח, להוציא את האמת לאור  
     - **sa**: प्रमाणीकरणम्, प्रमाणीकृतम्, proof-based causation  
     - **el**: παραποιώ, πλαστογραφώ  
     - **en**: authenticate, validate, invalidate, correct  
     - **tl**: beripikahin, wastuhin  
     - **qu**: pantachiy  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** causative/induced result, verification, falsification, correction, error-causation.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** Some languages allow limited causative-like patterns in subdomains, but the broad generalization still holds.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

14. **Authenticity/genuineness resists ordinary inchoatives more than falsification does.**  
   - **Statement:** “Become authentic/genuine” is often weak or absent, while “prove fake/forged,” “falsify,” or “forge” is much better supported.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - Weak/absent ordinary inchoatives for authenticity in **he, el, en, tl, sw, qu**, and partly **zh**  
     - Strong lexicalization for fake/forged/falsified in **zh, he, el, en, tl, sw**  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** authenticity, fake/counterfeit, inchoative, causative.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** This is strongest as a tendency in the current sample; it is not equally well evidenced in **sa** and **qu** because the authenticity domain itself is less elaborated there.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

15. **Negative-side lexicalization is often stronger than positive-side lexicalization.**  
   - **Statement:** Terms for liar, fake, forged, falsified, baseless, etc. are often more compact or more entrenched than positive counterparts like truth-teller or become-genuine.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **en**: liar strong, truth-teller marginal  
     - **el**: ψεύτης strong, no equally common positive counterpart  
     - **he**: no strong everyday single-word positive truth-teller in F4  
     - **qu**: llullaq / llulla runa strong; positive counterpart weaker  
     - **zh**: 说真话的人 is paraphrastic; negative domains often more compact  
     - Similar asymmetry in fake/forged vs become-authentic across several languages  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** person-labeling, authenticity, falsification, lexical asymmetry.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** Some languages do have positive person terms (e.g. **sa सत्यवादिन्**, **sw mkweli**), so the asymmetry is not universal in a strict sense.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

16. **Truth-related discourse framing is recurrent across languages.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages have fixed expressions meaning roughly “to tell the truth,” “actually,” “in reality,” or “in essence,” used to frame stance rather than simply assert truth.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 说真的, 说实话, 从本质上看  
     - **he**: למען האמת, האמת היא ש־, ליתר דיוק  
     - **sa**: यथार्थतः, तत्त्वतः  
     - **el**: Η αλήθεια είναι ότι..., Στην πραγματικότητα..., Στην ουσία...  
     - **en**: truth be told, in truth  
     - **tl**: sa totoo lang, sa katotohanan  
     - **sw**: kwa kweli, kweli ni kwamba  
     - **qu**: chiqap, possibly chiqaqta  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** discourse framing, stance, truth/reality/essence.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** These are not always equally grammaticalized, and their exact semantics range from candor to emphasis to correction.  
   - **Confidence:** **high**

17. **A basis/support dimension is recurrent, but less uniformly lexicalized than truth or correctness.**  
   - **Statement:** Many languages have ways to say that a claim has grounds or lacks them, but this dimension is less evenly developed than truth/falsity.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **tl**: may saligan / walang saligan, salig sa ebidensiya  
     - **sa**: प्रमाण, प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्, प्रमाणं नास्ति  
     - **zh**: 属实-related verification and reliable-basis expressions  
     - **he**: מהימנות, תואם את העובדות, doubt-casting constructions  
     - **en**: valid, sound basis  
     - **sw**: some overlap via evidence/credibility, but less sharply lexicalized  
     - **qu**: weakly supported  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** basis/support, evidential grounding, validity, verification.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** This dimension is strong in some languages and weak in others; it may merge with validity, credibility, or verification.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

18. **Resemblance/fidelity is often kept distinct from statement truth.**  
   - **Statement:** Several languages distinguish “being true” from “matching an original,” “being faithful to a source,” or “seeming lifelike.”  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 仿真 / 逼真 vs 真 / 真实  
     - **he**: דומה, זהה, תואם  
     - **el**: πιστός, πιστότητα, απαράλλακτος, κατά γράμμα  
     - **en**: faithful to the original, true to life, exact copy  
     - **sw**: -fanana, mfano  
     - **qu**: kikin, rikch’akuy, chiqan  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** resemblance, correspondence, fidelity, appearance-of-truth.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** This distinction is less elaborated in **tl** and less clearly constructional in some languages.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

19. **Appearance-of-truth / plausibility is recurrent but not universal as a clearly separate lexical zone.**  
   - **Statement:** Some languages have dedicated expressions for seeming true, plausible, or lifelike without full truth commitment.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 仿真, 逼真, 以假乱真  
     - **he**: דומה לאמת, דומה למציאות  
     - **el**: αληθοφανής, αληθοφάνεια  
     - **en**: ring true, verisimilitude, semblance  
     - weaker or merged evidence in **sw** and **qu**  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** plausibility, seeming-real, appearance-of-truth, fidelity.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** Weak or absent as a clearly separate lexical field in **tl**, **sa**, and much of **qu**.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

20. **Essence/substance is a recurrent but non-universal neighboring dimension.**  
   - **Statement:** Some languages strongly lexicalize “essence/substance/underlying nature” as distinct from surface form, but this is not equally developed across the sample.  
   - **Evidence summary:**  
     - **zh**: 本质, 实质  
     - **he**: מהות  
     - **sa**: स्वरूप, तत्त्व  
     - **el**: ουσία, κατ’ ουσίαν, στην ουσία  
     - weaker or more diffuse in **en, tl, sw, qu**  
   - **Relevant dimensions / constructions / contrasts:** essence, substance, reality, discourse framing.  
   - **Caveats / exceptions:** This is strong only in part of the sample and should not be treated as a secure universal of the truth domain itself.  
   - **Confidence:** **medium**

## 2. Major Outliers

### A. Language-level outliers

1. **Chinese (zh): unusually fine-grained partitioning**
   - **What diverges:** Chinese has an especially dense lexical split among everyday truth, official verification, deception/fraud, correctness, precision, credibility, commercial authenticity, realism, original-style authenticity, essence/substance, and legitimacy.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely a real structural richness in the sampled domain, not just sparse evidence elsewhere.
   - **Notes:** Especially notable are **属实 / 不实 / 失实**, **虚假 / 伪造**, **可信 / 可靠**, **仿真 / 逼真**, **本质 / 实质**.

2. **Sanskrit (sa): unusually elaborate proof/validity/cognition system**
   - **What diverges:** Sanskrit has a much richer technical-philosophical lexicon around valid cognition, proof, validity, and factual correspondence than most other languages in the sample.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad within scholastic/philosophical subdomain**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely a real structural difference in the documented register.
   - **Notes:** The **प्रमा / प्रमाण / प्रामाण्य** family is especially distinctive.

3. **Hebrew (he): especially strong absence of a general inchoative truth-domain pattern**
   - **What diverges:** The data explicitly reports no common general “become true/correct/authentic” construction.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad constructional**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Probably real in the sampled domain, though more corpus evidence could refine it.
   - **Notes:** Dynamic meanings are rerouted through revelation, vindication, testing, and precision-improvement.

4. **Quechua (qu): smaller and less elaborated system in several modern subdomains**
   - **What diverges:** Compared with zh/he/el/en/tl/sw, Quechua shows less lexical elaboration for authenticity, verification, credibility, and dynamic truth-state change.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately to strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could reflect both a real structural profile and limited evidence in the current dataset.
   - **Notes:** The system is still clear in truth vs lying vs mistake vs rightness, but thinner elsewhere.

5. **Tagalog (tl): dynamic truth concentrated in one lexical family**
   - **What diverges:** Tagalog has a notably strong dynamic pattern in the **totoo** family (**magkatotoo / nagkatotoo**) while many neighboring domains remain stative.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **narrow-to-mid**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real in the sampled lexicon.
   - **Notes:** This concentration is sharper than in most other languages.

6. **Swahili (sw): strong reliance on agreement/genitive/relative constructions**
   - **What diverges:** Swahili expresses many truth-related contrasts through noun-class agreement, genitive quality constructions, and relative-clause modifiers rather than through a large inventory of simple adjectives.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad morphosyntactic profile**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.
   - **Notes:** Especially visible in **wa/ya kweli**, **habari za uongo**, **chanzo kinachoaminika**.

### B. Construction-level outliers

1. **Chinese 属实 as a verification adjective that resists ordinary inchoatives**
   - **What diverges:** **属实** does not behave like a normal adjective in change-of-state constructions; *变得属实* is explicitly rejected.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **narrow but very clear**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely a real lexical-constructional restriction.
   - **Notes:** Dynamics are expressed through **经查属实 / 查证属实 / 是否属实** instead.

2. **Hebrew lack of a general inchoative construction**
   - **What diverges:** No common general “become true/correct/authentic” pattern is identified.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad constructional**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Probably real in the current evidence.
   - **Notes:** This is more explicit than in most other languages.

3. **Quechua repeated absence of common “become true/false/wrong” expressions**
   - **What diverges:** F6 repeatedly reports no common expression for these transitions.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **broad constructional**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could partly reflect limited evidence, but the repeated pattern is notable.
   - **Notes:** Dynamicity is clearer in **pantachiy** and **llullakuy** than in truth-state change.

4. **Tagalog interrogative checking as an explicitly foregrounded construction type**
   - **What diverges:** Tagalog F4 explicitly foregrounds interrogative checking (**Totoo ba iyan?**, **Sigurado ka ba?**) more than the other languages’ F4 tables do.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **narrow**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Probably partly an artifact of documentation emphasis, not necessarily a deep structural difference.
   - **Notes:** Other languages certainly have such questions, but they were not isolated as F4 types.

5. **Swahili dynamicity concentrated in credibility rather than truth proper**
   - **What diverges:** The clearest dynamic/state-shift-compatible pattern is **kuaminika / haaminiki**, not a general truth inchoative.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **mid-level**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real in the sampled material.
   - **Notes:** This makes credibility more dynamic than truth proper in the available evidence.

### C. Dimension-specific outliers

1. **Authenticity/counterfeit as a weakly elaborated domain in Sanskrit and Quechua**
   - **What diverges:** Unlike zh/he/el/en/tl/sw, these languages do not show a comparably rich object-centered authenticity vs counterfeit lexicon in the supplied material.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could be partly evidential, especially for Quechua; for Sanskrit it may reflect register and lexical selection.

2. **Precision as a weakly separated dimension in Quechua**
   - **What diverges:** The sample does not show a robust, clearly separate precision/exactness field comparable to zh/he/el/en/tl.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely partly due to limited evidence; current support is weak.

3. **Essence/substance as a strongly elaborated dimension only in part of the sample**
   - **What diverges:** zh/he/sa/el strongly lexicalize this dimension; en/tl/sw/qu do so much less clearly in the supplied data.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could reflect both real lexical differences and dataset scope.

4. **Appearance-of-truth/plausibility as strongly lexicalized mainly in Greek, English, Chinese**
   - **What diverges:** This dimension is much clearer in **el/en/zh** than in **tl/sa/qu**, and only moderate in **he/sw**.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely both.

5. **Verification as a highly specialized lexical domain in Chinese**
   - **What diverges:** Chinese has a particularly specialized verification lexicon and formulaic system.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **strongly**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific but rich**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Likely real.

6. **Support/basis as especially strong in Tagalog and Sanskrit**
   - **What diverges:** These languages show especially clear support/grounding constructions relative to some others.
   - **How strongly it diverges:** **moderately**
   - **Narrow or broad:** **dimension-specific**
   - **Structural difference or limited evidence?:** Could partly reflect lexical selection in the dataset.

## 3. Open Questions

1. **Is correctness a cross-linguistically independent major dimension, rather than just a subcase of truth or normativity?**  
   The evidence strongly suggests yes, but the current synthesis still treats it partly as overlapping with truth and propriety.

2. **Is precision/exactness likewise an independent major dimension across languages?**  
   It is robust in many languages, but weaker in some. More evidence is needed from languages like Quechua and Swahili.

3. **Should credibility be treated as distinct from both sincerity and verification?**  
   The current evidence suggests that source credibility, personal trustworthiness, and checked validity do not always behave alike.

4. **How strong is the asymmetry between negative-side lexicalization and positive-side lexicalization?**  
   The pattern looks recurrent, but it is not yet secure enough to state more strongly than a tendency.

5. **How general is the resistance of authenticity to ordinary inchoatives?**  
   This looks strong in the current sample, but more languages and more colloquial evidence would help confirm it.

6. **How general is the preference for verification/result constructions over broad “become true” constructions?**  
   This is one of the strongest patterns in the current data, but it would benefit from broader sampling.

7. **Is the support/basis dimension universally adjacent to verification, or does it sometimes form a separate lexical field?**  
   Sanskrit and Tagalog suggest a stronger independent basis/support zone than some other languages.

8. **How much of the weak evidence in Quechua reflects true structural sparsity versus limited documentation in the current dataset?**  
   This is especially important for precision, authenticity, and verification.

9. **How much of the Hebrew “no general inchoative” result is a true structural property versus a consequence of the lexical sample?**  
   The evidence is strong, but broader corpus-style confirmation would be useful.

10. **Are discourse-framing expressions (“to tell the truth,” “actually,” “in reality,” “in essence”) best treated as part of the truth domain proper or as a neighboring pragmatic layer?**  
    They are recurrent and important, but their status remains analytically open.

11. **How sharply should resemblance/fidelity be separated from appearance-of-truth/plausibility?**  
    Greek and English suggest a distinction, but the two areas overlap in several languages.

12. **Do some languages systematically route dynamic truth meanings through speech-action, verification, or evidential constructions rather than state-change constructions?**  
    The current evidence strongly suggests this, but a broader typological sample would help determine how general it is.
