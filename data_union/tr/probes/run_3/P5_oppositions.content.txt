# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4a/P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit co-occurrence, contrastive framing, or antonym notation in the prior steps. No opposition is inferred from general linguistic knowledge not present in the context. Where a term has multiple contrasts, each is listed separately. Where no clear opposition is supported, this is stated explicitly.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** doğru
**Term B:** yanlış
**Transliteration:** doğru / yanlış
**English translation:** correct, true / wrong, incorrect, mistaken
**Description of contrast:** These are the primary antonym pair for evaluating statements, answers, and actions as conforming to or deviating from what is factually or normatively correct. They are explicitly identified as contrasting in P1 (items 1 and 6), participate in parallel constructions (*doğru çıkmak* / *yanlış çıkmak*), and are paired in the binomial *yalan yanlış* (P2b, Item 8, Construction 5).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** doğru
**Term B:** eğri
**Transliteration:** doğru / eğri
**English translation:** straight, correct, honest / crooked, bent, dishonest
**Description of contrast:** *Eğri* is explicitly identified in P1b (ROOT 1, item 30) as the primary antonym of *doğru* in both physical (a crooked line) and moral (a dishonest person or action) senses. The proverbial expression *doğruya doğru, eğriye eğri demek* (to call a spade a spade) pairs them directly as the two poles of honest evaluation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** doğru
**Term B:** yalan
**Transliteration:** doğru / yalan
**English translation:** true, correct / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** P1 (items 1 and 8) explicitly notes that *yalan* contrasts with *doğru*; the parallel constructions *doğruyu söylemek* (to tell the truth) and *yalan söylemek* (to tell a lie) are documented as direct contrasts in P2b (Items 1 and 8). The contrast is between a statement conforming to fact and a deliberately false statement.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** gerçek
**Term B:** sahte
**Transliteration:** gerçek / sahte
**English translation:** real, genuine / fake, counterfeit, false
**Description of contrast:** P1 (item 2) explicitly identifies *sahte* as a contrast term for *gerçek*. Both participate in the attributive pattern ([adjective] + noun) and in the predicate outcome construction (*gerçek çıkmak* / *sahte çıkmak*).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term B:** teyit etmek
**Transliteration:** tasdik etmek / teyit etmek
**English translation:** to certify, to attest (official/notarial) / to confirm, to corroborate (journalistic/informational)

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** hakikaten
**Term B:** gerçekten
**Transliteration:** hakikaten / gerçekten
**English translation:** truly, in truth (formal/solemn) / really, truly (across all registers)
**Description of contrast:** P1b (ROOT 3, item 8) and P2a (Item 3, Derived Form 1) document *hakikaten* as "more formal than *gerçekten*" and "more solemn." Both are adverbs asserting that something is genuinely the case, but they differ in register: *hakikaten* is restricted to formal, literary, and philosophical contexts, while *gerçekten* is used across all registers including colloquial speech. This is a register contrast rather than a semantic opposition.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Items for which no clear opposition is identified

- **hakikatperest** — no contrast term documented
- **sahihlik** — no contrast term documented in P1/P2 beyond the general *sahih* / non-*sahih* distinction implicit in its domain-specific use
