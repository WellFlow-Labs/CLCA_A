# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

### Root 1: **totoo**

**Root form:** totoo
**P1 participation:** 5 items — *totoo* (1), *katotohanan* (2), *makatotohanan* (19), *parang totoo* (28), *totoong-totoo* (mentioned within item 1)
**Core meaning contribution:** Factual truth, reality, the correspondence of a proposition or state of affairs to what actually is the case. This root anchors the domain of truth as facticity and reality.

---

### Root 2: **tunay**

**Root form:** tunay
**P1 participation:** 4 items — *tunay* (3), *katunayan* (4), *pagpapatunay* (29), and participates in the morphological base of *mapagkakatiwalaan* indirectly via the trust/verification cluster
**Core meaning contribution:** Authenticity, genuineness, the quality of being what something claims or appears to be. This root anchors the domain of truth as authenticity and verification.

---

### Root 3: **tapat**

**Root form:** tapat
**P1 participation:** 2 items — *tapat* (20), *katapatan* (21)
**Core meaning contribution:** Honesty, sincerity, directness, fidelity. This root anchors the interpersonal and moral dimension of truth-telling — the alignment between inner state and outward expression.

---

**Note on selection:** *Tiwala* (trust) was considered as a fourth candidate but participates in only 2 P1 items directly (*tiwala*, *mapagkakatiwalaan*, *di-mapagkakatiwalaan*). It is highly productive morphologically but its core domain is trust/confidence rather than truth-evaluation per se. It is noted here but not selected as a primary root for expansion. The three roots selected — *totoo*, *tunay*, *tapat* — together cover factual truth, authenticity, and honest expression, constituting the primary structural triad of the truth-evaluation domain in Tagalog.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT A: **totoo**

---

**A-01**
**Form:** totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo
**Morpheme breakdown:** root (no transparent decomposition in modern usage)
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine, actually
**Context of use:** Base predicate used to affirm the truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs in everyday speech. Used across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-02**
**Form:** katotohanan
**Transliteration:** katotohanan
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ka-* + *totoo* + *-an* (nominalizing circumfix)
**Natural English translation:** truth, the truth, reality, the true state of affairs
**Context of use:** Abstract nominal used in moral, legal, philosophical, and religious discourse to refer to truth as a quality or to a specific true state of affairs. *Sabihin ang katotohanan* ("tell the truth") is a standard fixed phrase.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-03**
**Form:** totoong-totoo
**Transliteration:** totoong-totoo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* + *-ng* (linker) + *totoo* (full reduplication with linker)
**Natural English translation:** very real, absolutely true, genuinely genuine
**Context of use:** Intensified form used in everyday speech to emphasize that something is emphatically real or true, often with an emotional or emphatic register. *Totoong-totoo ang nangyari* ("what happened was absolutely real").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-04**
**Form:** makatotohanan
**Transliteration:** makatotohanan
**Morpheme breakdown:** *maka-* + *totoo* + *-an* ("in accordance with truth/reality")
**Natural English translation:** realistic, true-to-life, truthful, verisimilar
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic, narrative, and moral evaluation to assess whether a representation or depiction faithfully corresponds to reality. Common in film, literary, and journalistic criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-05**
**Form:** parang totoo
**Transliteration:** parang totoo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *parang* (like, as if, seemingly) + *totoo* (true/real)
**Natural English translation:** seems real, looks genuine, as if true
**Context of use:** Used in everyday evaluative discourse to describe something that has the appearance of being real without asserting that it is. Common in assessments of performances, stories, and objects.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-06**
**Form:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** hindi totoo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *hindi* (negation particle) + *totoo* (true/real)
**Natural English translation:** not true, untrue, not real, false
**Context of use:** Standard negation of *totoo*, used across all registers to deny the truth or reality of a proposition. *Hindi totoo iyan* ("that is not true").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-07**
**Form:** totoo ba?
**Transliteration:** totoo ba?
**Morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* (true/real) + *ba* (question particle)
**Natural English translation:** Is that true? Really? Is it real?
**Context of use:** Extremely common interrogative expression used in everyday conversation to request confirmation of a claim or to express surprise. Functions as a discourse-level truth-check.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-08**
**Form:** totoo naman
**Transliteration:** totoo naman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* (true/real) + *naman* (particle: on the other hand, for its part, indeed)
**Natural English translation:** it really is true, it is indeed real, truly so
**Context of use:** Used in conversational contexts to affirm truth while acknowledging a preceding doubt or contrast. *Totoo naman ang sinabi ko* ("what I said is indeed true").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-09**
**Form:** totoo nga
**Transliteration:** totoo nga
**Morpheme breakdown:** *totoo* (true/real) + *nga* (particle: indeed, as expected, affirmative emphasis)
**Natural English translation:** indeed true, yes it really is, as expected it's real
**Context of use:** Used to confirm a truth that the speaker or interlocutor has come to accept, often after some deliberation or evidence. *Totoo nga pala* ("it really is true after all").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**A-10**

**Transliteration:** totoo pala

**Form:** tunay
**Transliteration:** tunay
**Morpheme breakdown:** root (no transparent decomposition)
**Natural English translation:** genuine, real, authentic, true
**Context of use:** Base predicate used to assert authenticity or genuineness of objects, persons, emotions, or relationships. *Tunay na kaibigan* ("a true/genuine friend").

**Transliteration:** katunayan

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots in contexts where the contribution of the root appears to shift away from its core truth-evaluation function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

### Extensions of **totoo**

- **Totoong-totoo** is used in everyday speech not only to assert factual truth but also to intensify emotional states: *totoong-totoo ang sakit ng puso ko* ("my heartache is absolutely real"). The root here marks degree of felt intensity rather than propositional truth.

- **Makatotohanan** in aesthetic contexts evaluates the quality of a representation's correspondence to life — a function that involves criteria of artistic judgment (verisimilitude) rather than simple factual accuracy.

- **Totoo** appears in expressions like *totoo ang buhay* ("life is real/serious") where it functions to assert the seriousness or gravity of a situation rather than the truth of a proposition.

- **Katotohanan** appears in near-proverbial expressions such as *ang katotohanan ay mapait* ("truth is bitter") where it functions as a subject of predication about the nature of truth itself, shifting from an epistemic to a quasi-philosophical or emotional register.

---

### Extensions of **tunay**

- **Tunay na Diyos** and **tunay na relihiyon** involve *tunay* in theological assertions about ontological status (what genuinely exists or is divine) rather than empirical authenticity.

- **Tunay na bayani** and **tunay na Pilipino** involve *tunay* in identity and political discourse where authenticity is defined by cultural, moral, or ideological criteria rather than material or factual ones.

- **Tunay na sining** involves *tunay* in aesthetic judgment, where "genuine art" is evaluated by criteria of artistic merit or cultural value rather than factual correspondence.

- **Tunay na halaga** extends *tunay* into economic and philosophical discourse about intrinsic versus assigned value — a domain involving normative rather than purely factual assessment.

- **Katunayan** functions as a discourse marker meaning "in fact" or "as a matter of fact," where it operates as a pragmatic particle marking evidential or emphatic stance rather than referring to a specific piece of evidence.

---

### Extensions of **tapat**

- **Tapat** in its spatial sense ("facing, directly opposite") — as in *sa tapat ng bahay* ("across from the house") — is a fully distinct usage that coexists with the honesty sense. The relationship between the spatial and moral senses is present in the lexical item but the spatial use is entirely outside the truth-evaluation domain.

- **Katapatan sa bansa** and **katapatan sa Diyos** involve *katapatan* in the sense of loyalty and devotion rather than epistemic honesty — a shift from truth-telling to commitment and allegiance.

- **Tapat na loob** and **katapatan ng loob** involve *tapat* in combination with *loob* (inner self), a culturally specific Tagalog concept. Here *tapat* functions within a moral-psychological framework that concerns the alignment of inner disposition with outward behavior, which extends beyond simple truth-telling into character and relational ethics.

- **Pagtatapat** in religious contexts (confession of sin) involves *tapat* in a ritual-institutional function where honest disclosure is embedded in a sacramental or devotional practice, not merely an epistemic act.

- **Tapat na paglilingkod** involves *tapat* in the sense of dedicated, committed service — where the primary evaluative dimension is reliability and commitment rather than truthfulness of statement.
