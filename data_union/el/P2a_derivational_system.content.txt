# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Greek (el)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This step documents attested derivational morphology for the P1 base forms. Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-documented in full, to avoid redundancy. The focus here is on derivations not already captured in P1 or P1b, plus systematic documentation of the derivational profile of each P1 item.

Where a P1 item is itself a derivation of another P1 item (e.g., αλήθεια → αληθινός), the relationship is noted but not re-expanded unless additional derived forms exist.

Derivational productivity is markedly uneven across the P1 inventory. The αληθ- and ψευδ- root families are highly productive, generating verbal, nominal, adjectival, and adverbial forms as well as open-class compounding (particularly ψευδο-). Items such as κίβδηλος, δήθεν, and τάχα are derivationally inert in contemporary use. This asymmetry is preserved in the documentation below and reflects actual patterns in the language rather than gaps in analysis.

## 1. Base Form: αληθής (*alithís*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** alíthia
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εια (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being unconcealed"
- **Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
- **Usage:** The primary abstract noun derived from αληθής. Already documented as a P1 core item and expanded in P1b. The derivational relationship is transparent and productive. Cross-reference: P1 #2, P1b Root 2 #1.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθινός
- **Transliteration:** alithinós
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ινός (adjectival suffix indicating material or characteristic nature); "of or belonging to truth"
- **Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
- **Usage:** Already documented as a P1 core item and expanded in P1b. The suffix -ινός shifts the adjective from a formal/philosophical register (αληθής) toward a more colloquial and applied register, with a stronger connotation of genuineness versus imitation. Cross-reference: P1 #3, P1b Root 2 #12–16.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθεύω
- **Transliteration:** alithévo
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix); "to be truthful, to speak truth"
- **Natural English translation:** to speak the truth; to be true (of a proposition)
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical and formal written registers; less common in everyday speech. Documented in P1b Root 2 #2. Cross-reference: P1b Root 2 #2.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθώς
- **Transliteration:** alithós
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ώς (adverbial suffix); "in a true manner"
- **Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, verily
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of αληθής. Used primarily in formal, liturgical, and philosophical registers. Appears in the Easter liturgical exchange "Χριστός Ανέστη / Αληθώς Ανέστη" (Christ is risen / Truly He is risen). Less common in everyday speech than αληθινά. Documented in P1b Root 1 #19.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αναληθής
- **Transliteration:** analithís
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative prefix) + αληθής (true)
- **Natural English translation:** untrue, false
- **Usage:** The privative negation of αληθής; used in formal philosophical and logical contexts to describe a proposition that does not correspond to reality. Documented in P1b Root 1 #7. Cross-reference: P1 #20, P1b Root 1 #7.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base:** αληθής
- **Derived:** αληθοφανής
- **Transliteration:** alithofanís
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ο- (linking vowel) + φαν- (from φαίνομαι, to appear) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** plausible, verisimilar, truth-seeming, lifelike
- **Usage:** Applied to narratives or accounts that have the appearance of truth. Used in rhetorical, literary, and philosophical contexts to describe something that has the appearance of truth. Documented in P1b Root 1 #10.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The αληθ- root is moderately productive in derivation. The primary derivational paths are: (a) the abstract noun αλήθεια, (b) the colloquial adjective αληθινός with its adverb αληθινά, (c) the formal adverb αληθώς, (d) the verb αληθεύω, and (e) the compound stem αληθοφαν- for the appearance-of-truth cluster. The privative form αναληθής exists but is marginal. The adverb αληθινά is derived from αληθινός rather than directly from αληθής and is documented under item 3 below. Additional philosophical compounds (αληθοφάνεια, αληθοφανής) are documented in P1b Root 2 #3–4.

## 2. Base Form: αλήθεια (*alíthia*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αλήθεια
- **Derived:** αναλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** analíthia
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αλήθεια (truth)
- **Natural English translation:** untruth, falsehood
- **Usage:** The privative abstract noun corresponding to αναληθής; used in philosophical and formal written registers as a more elevated alternative to ψέμα. Documented in P1b Root 1 #8.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αλήθεια
- **Derived:** αληθοφάνεια
- **Transliteration:** alithofánia
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθ- + φαίν- (from φαίνομαι, to appear) + -εια (abstract nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, appearance of truth, plausibility
- **Usage:** Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophy to describe the quality of appearing true without necessarily being true. Documented in P1b Root 1 #9.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αλήθεια
- **Derived:** μετα-αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** meta-alíthia
- **Literal Breakdown:** μετα- (beyond, after) + αλήθεια
- **Natural English translation:** post-truth
- **Usage:** Used in journalistic, political, and academic registers to describe an epistemic condition in which emotional appeals outweigh objective facts. A calque of English "post-truth." Documented in P1b Root 1 #30.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** αλήθεια is itself a derived form of αληθής (see above). It functions primarily as a base noun and derivational source rather than itself being a derived form with further productive suffixation. Its main derivational output is the privative αναλήθεια and the compound αληθοφάνεια. The adverbial and phrasal uses documented in P1b (στ' αλήθεια, αλήθεια ή ψέμα, etc.) are syntactic rather than morphological derivations and are not repeated here.

## 3. Base Form: αληθινός (*alithinós*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αληθινός
- **Derived:** αληθινά
- **Transliteration:** alithiná
- **Literal Breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + -α (adverbial suffix, neuter plural used adverbially); "truly, genuinely"
- **Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely
- **Usage:** The standard adverbial form of αληθινός; the most common adverbial form in everyday speech for expressing genuine truth or sincerity. Used as an intensifier and truth-marker. Documented in P1b Root 2 #12. Cross-reference: P1b Root 2 #12.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** αληθινός does not appear to generate further nominal or verbal derivations beyond the adverb αληθινά. No abstract noun *αληθινότητα is in common use; the function is covered by αλήθεια and γνησιότητα. No verb *αληθινεύω is attested. The adjective itself is a derivation from αληθ- and does not generate a distinct nominal or verbal paradigm of its own.

## 4. Base Form: ψευδής (*psevdís*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** ψευδής
- **Derived:** ψεύδος
- **Transliteration:** psévdos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- + -ος (neuter noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** lie, falsehood
- **Usage:** The primary noun for falsehood; shares the root with ψευδής. Fully documented in P1 (item 5). The relationship between ψευδής and ψεύδος is one of co-derivation from the shared root ψευδ- rather than direct derivation of one from the other.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** ψευδής
- **Derived:** ψευδώς
- **Transliteration:** psevdós
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + -ώς (adverbial suffix); "in a false manner"
- **Natural English translation:** falsely, untruthfully
- **Usage:** The formal adverbial form of ψευδής. Used in legal and formal contexts to describe actions or statements made falsely: "ψευδώς κατηγορείται" (he/she is falsely accused); "ψευδώς ισχυρίζεται" (falsely claims). Less common in everyday speech than ψεύτικα. Documented in P1b Root 1 #5.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** ψευδής
- **Derived:** ψευδεπίγραφος
- **Transliteration:** psevdhepígrafos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- (false) + επίγραφος (inscribed, attributed)
- **Natural English translation:** pseudepigraphical, falsely attributed
- **Usage:** A technical term in philology and biblical studies. Documented in P1b Root 1 #11.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The colloquial adjective ψεύτικος and the agent noun ψεύτης are derived from the colloquial stem ψεύτ- (itself a reduction of ψευδ-) rather than directly from ψευδής, and are documented under ψεύδος below, where the shared root is treated as the base. No abstract noun *ψευδότητα is in common use; ψεύδος itself serves this function.

## 5. Base Form: ψεύδος (*psévdos*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψεύτης
- **Transliteration:** psévtis
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- (with regular consonant cluster simplification to ψευτ-) + -ης (agent noun suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** liar
- **Usage:** The primary everyday noun for a person who lies; used across all registers and age groups. Documented in P1b Root 2 #3.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψεύτικος
- **Transliteration:** psévtikos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψεύτ- (colloquial stem) + -ικος (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** fake, false, phony, counterfeit
- **Usage:** The most common colloquial adjective for "fake" or "false" in everyday Modern Greek; more informal than ψευδής. Documented in P1b Root 2 #4.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψεύδομαι
- **Transliteration:** psévdome
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδ- (falsehood) + -ομαι (middle/passive verbal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell a falsehood
- **Usage:** The primary verb for lying; slightly more formal than the colloquial λέω ψέματα. Documented in P1b Root 2 #5.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψέμα
- **Transliteration:** pséma
- **Literal Breakdown:** colloquial reduction of ψεύδος; ψευδ- + -μα (nominal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** lie, fib
- **Usage:** The most common colloquial noun for a lie in everyday Modern Greek; plural ψέματα is extremely frequent. Documented in P1b Root 2 #7.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψευδαίσθηση
- **Transliteration:** psevdésthisi
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδο- (false) + αίσθηση (sensation, perception)
- **Natural English translation:** illusion, hallucination, false impression
- **Usage:** Used in psychological, medical, and everyday contexts for a false perception or mistaken impression of reality. Documented in P1b Root 2 #18.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base:** ψεύδος
- **Derived:** ψευδώνυμο
- **Transliteration:** psevdónymo
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευδο- (false) + ὄνομα/ώνυμο (name)
- **Natural English translation:** pseudonym, pen name, alias
- **Usage:** Used in literary, legal, and everyday contexts for an assumed or false name. Documented in P1b Root 2 #12.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The ψευδο- prefix is highly productive and generates a large open class of compounds (ψευδοεπιστήμη, ψευδοδιλήμμα, ψευδοπροφήτης, etc.). A representative selection is documented in P1b Root 2 #10–30. Productivity of this prefix is not bounded by the P1 item list.

## 6. Base Form: ψεύτικος (*psévtikos*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** ψεύτικος
- **Derived:** ψεύτης
- **Transliteration:** psévtis
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψευτ- (falsehood) + -ης (agentive suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** liar
- **Usage:** The base noun underlying ψεύτικος; the agentive noun for a person who lies. The derivational relationship runs: ψεύτης → ψεύτικος (adjectival extension). Documented in P1b.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** ψεύτικος
- **Derived:** ψεύτικα
- **Transliteration:** psévtika
- **Literal Breakdown:** ψεύτικος (false, fake) + -α (adverbial suffix); "in a false manner"
- **Natural English translation:** falsely, fakely, in a fake manner
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of ψεύτικος. Used in everyday speech to describe behavior or expression that is performed or feigned: "κλαίει ψεύτικα" (he/she is crying fakely/crocodile tears). Common in colloquial and informal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** ψεύτικος is itself derived from ψεύτης. No abstract noun *ψεύτικότητα is in common use. The function is covered by ψεύδος and related forms.

## 7. Base Form: γνήσιος (*gníssios*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησιότητα
- **Transliteration:** gnissiótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνήσιος (genuine) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix indicating quality); "the quality of being genuine"
- **Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, legitimacy
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in legal, commercial, cultural, and everyday contexts: "η γνησιότητα ενός έργου τέχνης" (the authenticity of a work of art); "έλεγχος γνησιότητας" (authenticity check); "πιστοποίηση γνησιότητας" (certification of authenticity). Common in administrative and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησίως
- **Transliteration:** gnissíos
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνήσιος (genuine) + -ως (adverbial suffix); "in a genuine manner"
- **Natural English translation:** genuinely, legitimately, authentically
- **Usage:** The formal adverbial form of γνήσιος. Used in legal and official documents to indicate that something is done or presented in a genuine or legitimate manner. Less common in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησιοποιώ
- **Transliteration:** gnisiopoió
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνήσιος + ποιώ (I make) + verbal suffix; "to make genuine"
- **Natural English translation:** to authenticate, to certify as genuine
- **Usage:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to refer to the act of officially certifying a document or signature as genuine. "Γνησιοποίηση υπογραφής" (certification of signature) is a standard administrative procedure in Greece.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** γνήσιος
- **Derived:** γνησιοποίηση
- **Transliteration:** gnisiopoíisi
- **Literal Breakdown:** γνησιοποιώ + nominal suffix -ση; "the act of making genuine"
- **Natural English translation:** authentication, certification of genuineness
- **Usage:** The nominal form corresponding to γνησιοποιώ. Used in administrative and legal contexts. "Γνησιοποίηση υπογραφής" is the standard term for the official certification of a signature at a public authority (KEP, notary, etc.).
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** γνήσιος does not appear to generate a common verbal derivation beyond γνησιοποιώ. No verb *γνησιεύω or similar is identified as attested. The derivational family is: γνήσιος (adjective) → γνησιότητα (abstract noun), γνησίως (adverb), γνησιοποιώ (verb) → γνησιοποίηση (verbal noun). νόθος (P1 item 15) functions as the lexical antonym of γνήσιος in contexts of legitimacy of birth and textual attribution, but is etymologically unrelated; it is not a derivation of γνήσιος.

## 8. Base Form: αυθεντικός (*afthentikós*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντικότητα
- **Transliteration:** afthentikótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** αυθεντικός (authentic) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix); "the quality of being authentic"
- **Natural English translation:** authenticity, genuineness, originality
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for authenticity. Used in aesthetic, philosophical, psychological, legal, and commercial contexts: "η αυθεντικότητα ενός έργου τέχνης" (the authenticity of a work of art); "αυθεντικότητα χαρακτήρα" (authenticity of character); "η αυθεντικότητα της υπογραφής" (the authenticity of the signature); "η αυθεντικότητα του εαυτού" (the authenticity of the self). Highly productive in contemporary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντικά
- **Transliteration:** afthentika
- **Literal Breakdown:** αυθεντικός (authentic) + -α (adverbial suffix); "in an authentic manner"
- **Natural English translation:** authentically, genuinely
- **Usage:** The adverbial form of αυθεντικός. Used to describe behavior or expression that is genuinely consistent with one's character: "συμπεριφέρεται αυθεντικά" (he/she behaves authentically); "εκφράζεται αυθεντικά" (he/she expresses himself/herself authentically). Common in psychological, self-help, and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αυθεντικός
- **Derived:** αυθεντία
- **Transliteration:** afthentía
- **Literal Breakdown:** αὐθέντης (one who acts on their own authority) + -ία (abstract nominal suffix); "authority, authoritativeness"
- **Natural English translation:** authority, authenticity (archaic/formal); also: authoritarianism (in some contexts)
- **Usage:** An older or more formal nominal form related to the same root as αυθεντικός. Derived from the same base noun αυθέντης as αυθεντικός; the relationship is one of shared etymological base rather than direct suffixation. In modern Greek, αυθεντία primarily means "authority" in the sense of recognized expertise or power, rather than "authenticity" in the aesthetic sense. In some modern contexts it carries a negative connotation of authoritarian rule. The semantic split between αυθεντία (authority) and αυθεντικότητα (authenticity) is well established in contemporary usage. Retained in formal and historical registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The base αυθέντης (the underlying noun) is archaic in modern Greek; αυθεντικός and its derivatives are the productive modern forms. αυθεντικός does not appear to generate a productive verbal derivation in common Modern Greek use.

## 9. Base Form: αξιόπιστος (*aksiópistos*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** αξιόπιστος
- **Derived:** αξιοπιστία
- **Transliteration:** aksiopistía
- **Literal Breakdown:** αξιόπιστος (trustworthy) + -ία (abstract nominal suffix); "the quality of being worthy of trust"
- **Natural English translation:** credibility, reliability, trustworthiness
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for credibility or reliability. Used in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts: "η αξιοπιστία της πηγής" (the credibility of the source). Documented in P1b Root 3 #8. Cross-reference: P1b Root 3 #8.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** αξιόπιστος
- **Derived:** αναξιόπιστος
- **Transliteration:** anaksiópistos
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αξιόπιστος (reliable)
- **Natural English translation:** unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
- **Usage:** The privative negation of αξιόπιστος; used in the same registers as the base form to indicate that a source, person, or institution cannot be trusted. Documented in P1b Root 3 #9.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** αξιόπιστος
- **Derived:** αναξιοπιστία
- **Transliteration:** anaksiopistía
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀν- (privative) + αξιοπιστία (credibility)
- **Natural English translation:** unreliability, lack of credibility
- **Usage:** The abstract noun for lack of credibility; used in formal, journalistic, and legal registers to describe the quality of being untrustworthy or not credible. Documented in P1b Root 3 #9.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** αξιόπιστος is itself a compound (ἄξιος + πίστις). Its derivational productivity beyond the abstract noun and privative pair is limited. No common adverb *αξιόπιστα is in widespread use; the adverbial function is typically expressed periphrastically. No common verbal derivation from αξιόπιστος is identified.

## 10. Base Form: έγκυρος (*éngiros*)

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** εγκυρότητα
- **Transliteration:** engirotita
- **Literal Breakdown:** έγκυρ- (valid, authoritative) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix); "the quality of having authority/validity"
- **Natural English translation:** validity, authority, credibility, legitimacy
- **Usage:** The standard abstract noun for validity or authority. Used in legal, administrative, and epistemic contexts: "η εγκυρότητα ενός εγγράφου" (the validity of a document); "η εγκυρότητα μιας πηγής" (the credibility of a source); "αμφισβητώ την εγκυρότητα" (I contest the validity). Common in legal and journalistic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** εγκύρως
- **Transliteration:** engíros
- **Literal Breakdown:** έγκυρος + adverbial suffix -ως; "in a valid manner"
- **Natural English translation:** validly, with authority, legitimately
- **Usage:** Formal adverbial form used in legal and administrative registers to indicate that something is done with proper authority or validity. Less common than the adjective form.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** άκυρος
- **Transliteration:** ákiros
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἀ- (privative) + κῦρος (authority, validity); "without authority/validity"
- **Natural English translation:** invalid, void, null, cancelled
- **Usage:** The privative counterpart of έγκυρος, formed directly from the shared root κῦρος rather than from έγκυρος itself; noted here as a co-derivative from the same base. Used in legal and administrative contexts to describe a document, contract, or decision that has no legal force: "η σύμβαση είναι άκυρη" (the contract is void); "άκυρο εισιτήριο" (invalid ticket); "άκυρη ψήφος" (spoiled/invalid ballot). Extremely common in legal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** ακυρότητα
- **Transliteration:** akirótita
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἄκυρος (invalid) + -ότητα (abstract nominal suffix); "the quality of being invalid"
- **Natural English translation:** invalidity, nullity, voidness
- **Usage:** The abstract noun corresponding to άκυρος. Used in legal contexts to refer to the state of being legally void or invalid: "η ακυρότητα της σύμβασης" (the nullity of the contract). Common in legal documents and commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base:** έγκυρος
- **Derived:** ακυρώνω
- **Transliteration:** akiróno
- **Literal Breakdown:** ἄκυρος (invalid) + -ώνω (verbal suffix); "to make invalid"
- **Natural English translation:** to cancel, to annul, to invalidate, to void
- **Usage:** The verb for canceling or annulling something. Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts: "ακύρωσε την κράτηση" (he/she cancelled the reservation); "ακυρώνω μια σύμβαση" (to annul a contract); "ακυρώνω μια απόφαση" (to annul a decision); "ακυρώνω ένα ταξίδι" (to cancel a trip
