# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
## Language: English (en)

All vocabulary below is drawn strictly from CONTEXT (P1–P5). Auxiliaries, aspect markers, and function words are added as permitted. Each truth-related term is treated separately where its behavior differs.

---

## A. INCHOATIVE — "X becomes [truth-related state]"

### A1. come true (actualization sense)
**Pattern:** SUBJECT (wish/prediction) + *come* + *true*
**Example:** A dream comes true. / Her prediction came true.
**Translation:** a wish/prediction becomes realized
**Notes:** Fixed inchoative, the single most established "become" construction in the domain (P2b, P4a #4). Crucially, the *true* here carries the **realization/actualization** sense, NOT statement-truth — flagged peripheral in P1b and P3. It does not generalize to "*the statement came true" meaning "became factually correct."
**Status:** [CORE]

### A2. get real (informal inchoative imperative)
**Pattern:** *get* + *real*
**Example:** Get real!
**Translation:** become realistic; stop deceiving yourself
**Notes:** Fixed informal imperative (P2b, P4a #4). Restricted to imperative/exhortative use; "*she got real" in the inchoative "became realistic" sense is not the established reading.
**Status:** [CORE]

### A3. ring true / ring false (emergence of apparent state)
**Pattern:** SUBJECT (statement) + *ring* + *true* / *false*
**Example:** Her story rings true. / His excuse rang false.
**Translation:** comes to seem genuine / comes to seem insincere
**Notes:** Perception-copula construction (P2a, P2b, P4a #1, P5 Opp. 25). Marks **apparent** rather than asserted truth; closer to "presents as" than to a true change of state, but it is the nearest dynamic/emergent expression for seeming-truth.
**Status:** [CORE]

### A4. General copular inchoative — "become true / become false / become valid"
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *become* + truth-domain ADJ
**Example:** The claim became false (once circumstances changed).
**Translation:** X enters the state of being true/false/valid
**Notes:** P4a/P4b explicitly rate general *become true/false* as **compositional but uncommon** [MARGINAL]. It is grammatical and uses only CONTEXT vocabulary, but no fixed idiom supports it. Latinate items (*become valid*, *become legitimate*) likewise occur only as ordinary compositional combinations.
**Status:** [MARGINAL]

### A5. Other native core items — "become right"
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *come* / *put* + *right*
**Example:** Things came right in the end. / put right.
**Notes:** *come right* (matters resolving favorably) and *put/set right* (P2b) exist, but these draw on the **good-order/remedial** sense of *right*, not factual correctness. No common inchoative exists for *right* in the "become factually correct" sense beyond compositional *become right*.
**Status:** [MARGINAL] (good-order sense [CORE] but peripheral to domain)

### A6. NO COMMON EXPRESSION — most adjectives
For **accurate, genuine, authentic, sincere, honest, faithful, proper, reliable, credible, plausible, legitimate, exact, trustworthy**, no fixed or idiomatic inchoative ("become accurate," "become honest") exists. Only compositional *become* + ADJ is available. **No common dedicated expression exists.**

---

## B. INCHOATIVE TOWARD OPPOSED STATE — "X becomes [non-truth / opposed state]"

### B1. ring false / strike a false note
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *ring* + *false* / *strike a false note*
**Example:** His apology rang false.
**Translation:** comes to seem insincere/inauthentic
**Notes:** The negative-pole counterpart of A3 (P2b, P5 Opp. 25). The only established **emergent-toward-opposed-state** idiom in the domain.
**Status:** [CORE]

### B2. show one's true colors (revelation of concealed real state)
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *show* + *true colors*
**Example:** He showed his true colors.
**Translation:** reveals one's real (often worse) character
**Notes:** Idiom (P1b, P2b, P3 Group 5). Encodes **revelation** of a pre-existing genuine state rather than a change into it — a dynamic of disclosure, not transformation. Uses the **loyalty/character** sense of *true*.
**Status:** [CORE]

### B3. General compositional — "become false / become untrue / become fake"
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *become* / *turn* + opposed ADJ
**Example:** The statement became untrue once the facts changed.
**Notes:** Compositional only; no fixed idiom. Suppletive *false/fake* (P4a/P4b, P5 Opp. 27) is preferred over prefixed *untrue/unreal* in such predication, but the inchoative itself remains uncommon.
**Status:** [MARGINAL]

### B4. NO COMMON EXPRESSION — opposed-state inchoatives
For becoming *dishonest, insincere, inaccurate, invalid, illegitimate, improper, unreliable, implausible, unfaithful*, **no common dedicated inchoative expression exists** — only compositional *become* + negated ADJ.

## C. CAUSATIVE — "Y causes X to be [truth-related state]"

This is the **richest dynamic field** in the domain, via the suffixal causative layer (P2a, P4a #5, P4b #5).

### C1. Suffixal causatives toward a truth-state
**Pattern:** Y + CAUSATIVE-VERB + X
| Verb | Base | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| verify | *ver-* (true) | cause X to be confirmed true | [CORE] |
| validate | *valid* | cause X to be (recognized as) valid | [CORE] |
| authenticate | *authentic* | cause X to be confirmed genuine | [CORE] |
| legitimize / legitimise | *legitimate* | cause X to be legitimate | [CORE] |
| rectify | *rect-/correct* | cause X to be put right | [CORE] |
| justify | *just* | cause X to be shown right/warranted | [CORE] |
| correct (v.) | *correct* | cause X to be free from error | [CORE] |

**Example:** The lab verified the result. / The notary authenticated the document. / She rectified the error.
**Notes:** This productive layer is the **default and most natural** way English encodes "causing a truth-state." Each verb is lexically tied to a specific Latinate base; there is no single general causative.
**Status:** [CORE]

### C2. Suffixal causatives toward an opposed state
**Pattern:** Y + CAUSATIVE-VERB + X
| Verb | Base | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| falsify | *false* | cause X to be false / tamper with | [CORE] |
| invalidate | *invalid* | cause X to cease to be valid | [CORE] |
| forge | *forged* | cause a fraudulent (fake) X to exist | [CORE] |
| counterfeit (v.) | *counterfeit* | cause a fraudulent copy of X | [CORE] |
| fake (v.) | *fake* | cause a feigned X | [CORE] |

**Example:** He falsified the records. / The new evidence invalidated her claim. / They forged the signature.
**Notes:** Robust negative-pole causative set. Note **asymmetry**: *falsify* means both "make false" and "tamper with," while there is no single verb "*truify" — the positive side relies on *verify/validate/authenticate* (different bases). Causation toward fakeness uses fabrication verbs (*forge, counterfeit, fake*) which **create a fake object** rather than convert an existing true one.
**Status:** [CORE]

### C3. Periphrastic causative — "make / render X [ADJ]"
**Pattern:** Y + *make* / *render* + X + truth-domain ADJ
**Example:** This makes the argument valid. / New facts rendered the claim false.
**Translation:** Y causes X to be in the given state
**Notes:** P4a/P4b #5: native core adjectives (*true, real, right*) **lack a simple causative verb** and must use periphrasis. "*Make true*" / "*make real*" / "*make right*" are the established causatives for these high-frequency native items. *make right* additionally carries the remedial/good-order sense.
**Status:** [CORE] (the obligatory strategy for native core; productive generally)

### C4. Particle causative — "true up"
**Pattern:** Y + *true up* + X
**Example:** true up the wheel
**Translation:** cause X to be brought into (physical) alignment
**Notes:** P1b, P2b, P4a #5. Causative of the **alignment/straightness** sense of *true*, peripheral to the truth domain but the only single-word causative built on native *true*.
**Status:** [CORE] (peripheral sense)

### C5. NO SIMPLE CAUSATIVE — native core truth adjectives
There is **no simple lexical causative verb** for *true* ("*to true a statement" = make it true), *real* ("*to real"), or *right* in the factual sense. **No common single-word expression exists**; periphrastic *make* + ADJ (C3) is obligatory.

## D. RESULTATIVE / DEVERBAL OUTCOME OF CAUSATION

### D1. Nominalized results of causative acts
**Pattern:** CAUSATIVE-VERB → result/process NOUN
| Noun | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| verification | the act/result of confirming truth | [CORE] |
| validation | the act/result of validating | [CORE] |
| authentication | the act/result of confirming genuineness | [CORE] |
| falsification | the act/result of making false / tampering | [CORE] |
| correction | the act/result of correcting | [CORE] |
| justification | the act/result/grounds of justifying | [CORE] |
| forgery | the fraudulent result of forging | [CORE] |
| legitimation | the act of legitimizing | [MARGINAL] |

**Notes:** These nominalize the dynamic event (P2a). They denote either the **process** (verification underway) or the **result** (a forgery as object). Strong Latinate productivity; the native core (*true, real, right*) yields no parallel causation-result noun.
**Status:** [CORE] (Latinate); native gap.

## E. STABILITY / PERSISTENCE EXPRESSIONS

### E1. tried and true / tried and tested
**Pattern:** fixed binomial
**Example:** a tried and true method
**Translation:** proven reliable through repeated use (stable over time)
**Notes:** Fixed binomial (P1b, P2b) encoding **stability/persistence of reliability**. The closest the domain comes to expressing endured, time-tested constancy.
**Status:** [CORE]

### E2. true to form / true to type / true to one's word
**Pattern:** *true to* + NOUN
**Example:** True to form, he was late.
**Translation:** consistent with the expected/established nature
**Notes:** Productive *true to* frame (P1b, P2b, P3 Group 5). Encodes **constancy/consistency** with a prior pattern — a stability notion, drawing on the fidelity sense of *true*.
**Status:** [CORE]

### E3. ring true (persistence of credibility) / stand
**Pattern:** SUBJECT + *stand* (e.g., "the claim stands")
**Notes:** From CONTEXT, *stand corrected* (P2b) exists but means "acknowledge error," not persistence. No CONTEXT-supported general "the claim stands / remains true" idiom is documented beyond compositional *remain true/valid*. Compositional *remain true* / *stay true* / *hold true* are natural but not separately attested as fixed units in CONTEXT.
**Status:** [MARGINAL] (compositional only within CONTEXT)

### E4. keep it real / stay true (persistence of authenticity)
**Pattern:** *keep* + *it* + *real* / *stay* + *true*
**Example:** Keep it real.
**Translation:** remain authentic/honest over time
**Notes:** *keep it real* is an attested fixed imperative (P2b) encoding **maintenance** of an authentic state. *stay true* draws on the same *true to* fidelity sense.
**Status:** [CORE] (*keep it real*); [MARGINAL] (*stay true* as fixed unit)

## F. REVELATION / DISCLOSURE DYNAMICS (truth emerging into view)

### F1. show / reveal one's true colors
Covered at B2 — disclosure of a pre-existing genuine state. [CORE]

### F2. the truth comes out / the moment of truth
**Pattern:** *the truth* + *come out*; *moment of truth*
**Example:** The truth came out. / This is the moment of truth.
**Notes:** *the truth comes out* is compositional but highly conventional; CONTEXT attests *moment of truth* (P1b, P2b, P3) as a fixed idiom for a **decisive testing point** — a dynamic crisis sense rather than a change-of-truth-value. *come out* in the "be revealed" sense is not separately documented as a fixed unit in CONTEXT, so treat as compositional.
**Status:** *moment of truth* [CORE]; *truth comes out* [MARGINAL] (compositional within CONTEXT)

## KEY FINDINGS (data-supported)

1. **Causation is the richest dynamic field**, encoded primarily by the **Latinate suffixal layer** (*-ify, -ate, -ize*: verify, falsify, validate, authenticate, legitimize, rectify, justify, invalidate). This is the default and most natural way English causativizes truth-states.

2. **Native/Latinate split persists from earlier steps.** Native core adjectives (*true, real, right*) have **NO simple causative verb** and require periphrastic *make* + ADJ; the only single-word native causative (*true up*) belongs to the peripheral alignment sense. Latinate items, by contrast, carry dedicated causative verbs but **no idioms**.

3. **Causative asymmetry:** there is no positive single-word counterpart "*truify*" matching *falsify*; the positive side is split across different bases (*verify/validate/authenticate*). Causation toward fakeness uses **fabrication verbs** (*forge, counterfeit, fake*) that **create a new fake object** rather than convert an existing genuine one.

4. **Inchoatives are weak and idiom-bound.** The only [CORE] "become" expressions (*come true*, *get real*, *ring true/false*) exploit **peripheral senses** (actualization, realism, apparent-seeming) rather than core statement-truth. General *become true/false* is compositional and uncommon. **No dedicated inchoative exists for the great majority of adjectives** (accurate, honest, valid, etc.).

5. **Stability is expressed only through the native *true* fidelity cluster** (*tried and true*, *true to form/type/word*, *keep it real*) — all drawing on the loyalty/consistency sense, not factual truth.

6. **Revelation dynamics** (*show one's true colors*, *moment of truth*) encode the **disclosure of a pre-existing genuine state** or a **decisive test**, not a change in truth-value — a conceptually distinct dynamic the domain handles idiomatically.

7. **Overall asymmetry:** English encodes *causing* truth-states far more richly (dedicated verbs + nominalized results) than *spontaneous entry into* truth-states (few, idiom-bound, peripheral-sense inchoatives), and persistence/stability least systematically of all.
