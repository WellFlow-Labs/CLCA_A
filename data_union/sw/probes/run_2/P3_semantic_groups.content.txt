# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Method Note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed across P1 and P1b/P2 documentation. Where items appear together in the same constructions, are explicitly contrasted with each other, or are restricted to the same discourse contexts, this is taken as evidence of grouping. No external framework is imposed. Items that do not show clear grouping patterns are listed separately at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality

**Label:** Reality-asserting expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to affirm that a claim, statement, or state of affairs is accurate or genuine. They appear in contexts where the speaker is asserting correspondence between a representation and what is actually the case. They contrast with expressions in Group 2 (falsehood/deception) and are frequently used together with those expressions in binary frames (*kweli au uongo*, *bidhaa halisi, si bandia*).

**Representative expressions:**

- *kweli* — "truth; true; truly" (P1 Item 1)
- *ukweli* — "truth; truthfulness; veracity" (P1 Item 2)
- *kweli kweli* — "truly; really; genuinely" (P1 Item 12)
- *kwa kweli* — "in truth; honestly" (P1b Root 1, Item 4)
- *kwa kweli kweli* — "truly and sincerely; in all seriousness" (P1b Root 1, Item 5)
- *ni kweli* — "it is true" (P1b Root 1, Item 28)
- *kweli kabisa* — "absolutely true" (P1b Root 1, Item 27)
- *ukweli mtupu* — "the plain truth; nothing but the truth" (P1b Root 1, Item 6)
- *ukweli wote* — "the whole truth" (P1b Root 1, Item 7)
- *sahihi* — "correct; accurate; valid" (P1 Item 7)
- *sahihi sahihi* — "exactly right; precisely correct" (P2a Item 7, Derived Form 4)
- *sawa* — "correct; right; equal" (P1 Item 26)
- *sawa sawa* — "exactly; precisely; correctly" (P1 Item 27)
- *dhahiri* — "evident; manifest; clear" (P1 Item 13)
- *ni dhahiri kwamba…* — "it is evident that…" (P2b Item 13, Construction 1)
- *kwa dhahiri* — "evidently; clearly" (P2b Item 13, Construction 2)
- *hali halisi* — "the actual situation; the real state of affairs" (P1 Item 24)
- *halisi* — "genuine; authentic; real" (P1 Item 4)
- *haki* (in truth-asserting uses) — "right; truth" (P1 Item 10)
- *haki ya Mungu* — oath formula asserting veracity (P1 Item 11)
- *ukweli wa Mungu* — "God's truth" (P1b Root 1, Item 26)

**Notes:** Within this group, there is a further distinction in usage between expressions that assert truth of a *statement* (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *sawa*) and expressions that assert the genuineness of an *object or situation* (*halisi*, *hali halisi*). These two sub-patterns overlap but are not identical: *sahihi* is used of answers, documents, and calculations; *halisi* is used of gold, goods, and persons. *Dhahiri* is distinctive in that it asserts self-evidence rather than simple accuracy — it is used when the speaker treats the truth as requiring no further argument. *Kweli* and its reduplicated and prepositional forms are the most broadly distributed across all these sub-contexts.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to characterize statements or objects as false, deceptive, or inauthentic

**Label:** Falsehood and inauthenticity expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a statement, object, or person as not corresponding to reality, as deliberately misleading, or as not genuine. They appear in moral condemnation, legal proceedings, and commercial fraud contexts. They are frequently contrasted with Group 1 expressions in the same discourse.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uongo* — "lie; falsehood; deception" (P1 Item 3)
- *kusema uongo* — "to tell lies" (P2b Item 3, Construction 1)
- *uongo mtupu* — "pure lies; nothing but lies" (P2b Item 3, Construction 5)
- *uongo wa wazi* — "a blatant lie" (P2b Item 3, Construction 6)
- *uongo una miguu mifupi* — "a lie has short legs" (P2b Item 3, Construction 3)
- *bandia* — "fake; counterfeit; artificial" (P1 Item 6)
- *pesa bandia* — "counterfeit money" (P2b Item 6, Construction 1)
- *hati bandia* — "a forged document" (P2b Item 6, Construction 2)
- *dawa bandia* — "fake medicine" (P2b Item 6, Construction 3)
- *tabasamu la bandia* — "a fake smile" (P2b Item 6, Construction 6)
- *udanganyifu* — "deception; deceitfulness; fraudulence" (P1 Item 21)
- *kudanganya* — "to deceive; to mislead; to lie" (P1 Item 22)
- *kudanganywa* — "to be deceived" (P2a Item 22, Derived Form 1)
- *mdanganyifu* — "a deceiver; a fraudulent person" (P2a Item 21, Derived Form 2)
- *muongo* — "a liar" (P2a Item 3, Derived Form 1)
- *waongo* — "liars" (P2a Item 3, Derived Form 2)
- *shahidi wa uongo* — "a false witness; a perjurer" (P2b Item 17, Construction 2)
- *si kweli* — "it is not true; that's false" (P1b Root 1, Item 29)
- *imani potofu* — "false belief; misguided faith" (P1b Root 3, Item 28)

**Notes:** Within this group, *uongo* and *bandia* are used in partially distinct contexts. *Uongo* is primarily used of statements and verbal acts; *bandia* is primarily used of objects, documents, and physical goods. *Udanganyifu* and *kudanganya* emphasize the deliberate, intentional character of the deception, whereas *uongo* can describe a false statement without necessarily implying intent. *Muongo* and *mdanganyifu* are agentive forms that characterize persons rather than acts or objects. The proverbial construction *uongo una miguu mifupi* is distinctive in that it asserts the inevitable exposure of falsehood rather than simply characterizing something as false.

## Group 3: Expressions used in the verification and confirmation of claims

**Label:** Verification and proof expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where a claim, document, or piece of information is being checked, confirmed, or substantiated. They appear in legal proceedings, journalistic practice, scientific discourse, and administrative contexts. They are oriented toward the process or result of establishing that something is true, rather than simply asserting that it is.

**Representative expressions:**

- *thibitisha* — "to confirm; to verify; to prove" (P1 Item 14)
- *uthibitisho* — "confirmation; verification; proof; attestation" (P1 Item 15)
- *kuthibitishwa* — "to be confirmed; to be verified" (P2a Item 14, Derived Form 2)
- *uthibitisho wa maandishi* — "written confirmation; documentary proof" (P2b Item 15, Construction 1)
- *uthibitisho wa kisayansi* — "scientific confirmation; empirical proof" (P2b Item 15, Construction 2)
- *kutoa uthibitisho* — "to provide proof; to furnish confirmation" (P2b Item 15, Construction 3)
- *kuthibitisha utambulisho* — "to verify identity" (P2b Item 14, Construction 3)
- *kuthibitisha madai* — "to verify claims; to substantiate allegations" (P2b Item 14, Construction 4)
- *ushahidi* — "evidence; testimony; proof" (P1 Item 16)
- *ushahidi wa maandishi* — "documentary evidence" (P2b Item 16, Construction 1)
- *ushahidi wa kutosha* — "sufficient evidence" (P2b Item 16, Construction 3)
- *ushahidi madhubuti* — "solid evidence; compelling testimony" (P2b Item 16, Construction 4)
- *kukusanya ushahidi* — "to gather evidence" (P2b Item 16, Construction 5)
- *kutoa ushahidi* — "to give evidence; to testify" (P2b Item 16, Construction 2)
- *kupinga ushahidi* — "to challenge evidence; to contest testimony" (P2b Item 16, Construction 6)
- *shahidi* — "witness; testifier" (P1 Item 17)
- *shahidi wa jicho* — "an eyewitness" (P2b Item 17, Construction 1)
- *kushuhudia* — "to witness; to testify; to attest" (P2a Item 16, Derived Form 3)
- *kudhihirisha* — "to demonstrate; to show clearly; to make evident" (P2a Item 13, Derived Form 1)
- *kudhihirika* — "to become evident; to be revealed" (P2a Item 13, Derived Form 2)
- *thibitisha habari kabla ya kuisambaza* — "verify the news before spreading it" (P2b Item 14, Construction 1)
- *habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi* — "the news has been confirmed by multiple sources" (P2b Item 14, Construction 2)

**Notes:** *Thibitisha* and *ushahidi* are the central items in this group. *Thibitisha* is oriented toward the act of verifying or confirming; *ushahidi* is oriented toward the material or testimony used as the basis for verification. *Shahidi* is the person who provides *ushahidi*. *Kudhihirisha* and *kudhihirika* overlap with this group in argumentative contexts where evidence is presented as making something self-evident, but they also appear in Group 1 contexts (asserting that something is clear). The construction *habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi* is characteristic of journalistic verification discourse and shows the integration of *thibitisha* with source-evaluation language.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate the legitimacy or lawfulness of acts, claims, and entitlements

**Label:** Legitimacy and lawfulness expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether an act, claim, document, or entitlement is legally or morally valid and recognized. They appear in legal, religious, and political discourse. They are oriented toward the question of whether something is rightfully permitted or recognized, rather than simply whether it is factually accurate.

**Representative expressions:**

- *halali* — "lawful; legitimate; permissible; rightful" (P1 Item 30)
- *uhalali* — "legality; legitimacy; lawfulness" (P1 Item 29)
- *uhalali wa kisheria* — "legal legitimacy; lawfulness" (P1b Root 2, Item 14)
- *uhalali wa kikatiba* — "constitutional legitimacy" (P1b Root 2, Item 15)
- *uhalali wa uchaguzi* — "the legitimacy of an election" (P1b Root 2, Item 16)
- *kuhalalisha* — "to legitimize; to legalize; to validate" (P2a Item 30, Derived Form 3)
- *kuhalalishwa* — "to be legitimized; to be made lawful" (P2a Item 30, Derived Form 4)
- *kuthibitisha uhalali* — "to verify legitimacy; to validate" (P1b Root 2, Item 17)
- *kupinga uhalali* — "to challenge legitimacy; to contest validity" (P1b Root 2, Item 18)
- *halali ya kisheria* — "legally permissible" (P1b Root 2, Item 19)
- *halali ya kidini* — "religiously permissible" (P1b Root 2, Item 20)
- *chakula halali* — "halal food; lawfully permissible food" (P1b Root 2, Item 21)
- *mali halali* — "lawfully acquired property; legitimate wealth" (P1b Root 2, Item 22)
- *mrithi halali* — "rightful heir; legitimate inheritor" (P1b Root 2, Item 23)
- *serikali halali* — "legitimate government" (P1b Root 2, Item 24)
- *haki halali* — "a legitimate right; a lawful entitlement" (P1b Root 2, Item 25)
- *haki* (in entitlement uses) — "right; entitlement; due" (P1 Item 10)
- *haki za binadamu* — "human rights" (P2a Item 10, Derived Form 1)
- *kudai haki* — "to claim a right; to assert one's rights" (P2b Item 10, Construction 2)
- *uhalali* — "legality; legitimacy" (P1 Item 29)
- *hati halisi* — "a genuine document; a valid deed" (P1b Root 2, Item 28)

**Notes:** *Halali* and *haki* are the two primary items in this group, but they are used in partially distinct ways. *Halali* evaluates whether something is permitted — it is used of food, property, acts, and governments. *Haki* evaluates whether something is owed or rightfully due — it is used of entitlements, rights, and claims. The two overlap in constructions like *haki halali* (a legitimate right), where both dimensions are present. *Uhalali* is the abstract form used when the property of legitimacy itself is being discussed, particularly in political and legal discourse about elections and constitutional validity. The religious sense of *halali* (Islamic dietary law) and the legal/political sense (electoral legitimacy) are both attested and both treated here as instances of the same usage pattern, since the underlying evaluative question — is this permitted/recognized? — is the same in both contexts.

## Group 5: Expressions used to evaluate persons or sources as trustworthy or untrustworthy

**Label:** Trustworthiness evaluation expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assess whether a person, institution, or source of information can be relied upon to be honest and accurate. They appear in moral character evaluation, journalistic source assessment, and relational contexts. They are oriented toward the reliability of an agent rather than the accuracy of a specific claim.

**Representative expressions:**

- *amini* — "to believe; to trust; trustworthy" (P1 Item 19)
- *uaminifu* — "faithfulness; trustworthiness; reliability; honesty" (P1 Item 18)
- *mwaminifu* — "a faithful person; a trustworthy person" (P1 Item 20)
- *waaminifu* — "faithful people; trustworthy persons" (P1b Root 3, Item 4)
- *kuamini* — "to believe; to trust; to have faith in" (P1b Root 3, Item 5)
- *kuaminiwa* — "to be trusted; to be found trustworthy" (P1b Root 3, Item 6)
- *kuaminiwa na watu* — "to be trusted by people; to have public credibility" (P1b Root 3, Item 7)
- *mtu wa kuaminiwa* — "a trustworthy person; a person to be trusted" (P1b Root 3, Item 22)
- *si wa kuaminiwa* — "not to be trusted; untrustworthy" (P1b Root 3, Item 23)
- *uaminifu wa habari* — "reliability of information; journalistic trustworthiness" (P1b Root 3, Item 17)
- *uaminifu wa chanzo* — "reliability of the source" (P1b Root 3, Item 18)
- *uaminifu wa mfumo* — "reliability of the system; system integrity" (P1b Root 3, Item 19)
- *uaminifu katika ndoa* — "faithfulness in marriage; marital fidelity" (P1b Root 3, Item 20)
- *uaminifu wa mtumishi* — "faithfulness of a servant/employee" (P1b Root 3, Item 21)
- *amana* — "trust; something held in trust; integrity" (P1b Root 3, Item 24)
- *kukiuka amana* — "to breach a trust; to betray a confidence" (P1b Root 3, Item 25)
- *kushika amana* — "to keep a trust; to honor a trust" (P1b Root 3, Item 26)
- *mtumishi mwaminifu* — "a faithful servant; a trustworthy employee" (P2b Item 20, Construction 1)
- *rafiki mwaminifu* — "a faithful friend; a trustworthy friend" (P2b Item 20, Construction 2)
- *muongo wa hali ya juu* — "a consummate liar" (P2b Item 3, Construction 2) [negative pole]
- *siamini macho yangu* — "I can't believe my eyes" (P2b Item 19, Construction 5)

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 (truth-asserting expressions) by its orientation toward agents rather than propositions or objects. *Uaminifu* and *amana* are the abstract nouns most characteristic of this group; *mwaminifu* and *mtu wa kuaminiwa* are the person-evaluating forms. The group extends across several domains: personal character (*rafiki mwaminifu*), institutional reliability (*uaminifu wa mfumo*), journalistic source evaluation (*uaminifu wa chanzo*), and marital fidelity (*uaminifu katika ndoa*). *Amana* is distinctive in that it refers both to the quality of trustworthiness and to the thing entrusted — it bridges the evaluative and the transactional. The negative pole of this group (*si wa kuaminiwa*, *muongo*) overlaps with Group 2.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate the originality or natural state of something

**Label:** Origin and naturalness expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something is in its original, natural, or unmodified state, or whether a person or practice belongs to a particular place or tradition by origin. They appear in cultural, linguistic, environmental, and philosophical discourse. They are oriented toward the question of where something comes from or what it is in its unaltered form.

**Representative expressions:**

- *asili* — "origin; nature; original; authentic" (P1 Item 5)
- *asili ya binadamu* — "the nature of humanity; human nature" (P2b Item 5, Construction 3)
- *wa asili* — "native; indigenous; of natural origin" (P2a Item 5, Derived Form 1)
- *kiasili* — "originally; naturally; in the original manner" (P2a Item 5, Derived Form 2)
- *uasili* — "originality; nativeness; the quality of being original" (P2a Item 5, Derived Form 3)
- *dawa ya asili* — "traditional medicine; natural medicine" (P2b Item 5, Construction 1)
- *mtu wa asili* — "a native person; an indigenous person" (P2b Item 5, Construction 2)
- *lugha ya asili* — "a native language; a mother tongue" (P2b Item 5, Construction 6)
- *kurudi asili* — "to return to one's origins; to go back to nature" (P2b Item 5, Construction 4)
- *kwa asili yake* — "by its very nature; inherently" (P2b Item 5, Construction 5)

**Notes:** This group is smaller and more internally consistent than the others. *Asili* is the central item, and its usage is consistently oriented toward origin and natural state rather than toward accuracy or legitimacy. The group overlaps with Group 1 (authenticity) in that *asili* can be used to evaluate whether something is genuine, but the emphasis is on origin and natural condition rather than on correspondence to reality. *Halisi* (Group 1) and *asili* are both used in authenticity evaluation but in different ways: *halisi* evaluates against a standard of genuineness (not counterfeit), while *asili* evaluates against a standard of origin (not modified or imported). The construction *kwa asili yake* (by its very nature) is distinctive in that it asserts inherent rather than acquired properties.

## Group 7: Expressions used in the evaluation of errors and wrongdoing

**Label:** Error and fault expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate acts, statements, or judgments as incorrect, faulty, or morally wrong. They appear in moral instruction, legal proceedings, and educational contexts. They are oriented toward identifying and characterizing what has gone wrong, and in some cases toward the response to error (admission, correction, repetition).

**Representative expressions:**

- *kosa* — "error; mistake; fault; wrong; sin" (P1 Item 9)
- *makosa* — "errors; mistakes; faults; wrongs" (P1 Item 8)
- *kukosa* — "to err; to make a mistake; to be wrong; to lack" (P2a Item 9, Derived Form 2)
- *kukosea* — "to wrong someone; to err against someone" (P2a Item 9, Derived Form 3)
- *kosa la kimakusudi* — "a deliberate error; an intentional wrong" (P2b Item 9, Construction 1)
- *kosa la bahati mbaya* — "an unfortunate mistake; an accidental wrong" (P2b Item 9, Construction 2)
- *kukiri kosa* — "to confess a fault; to admit a mistake" (P2b Item 9, Construction 3)
- *si kosa langu* — "it is not my fault" (P2b Item 9, Construction 4)
- *kukubali makosa* — "to admit mistakes; to acknowledge errors" (P2b Item 8, Construction 2)
- *kurudia makosa* — "to repeat mistakes" (P2b Item 8, Construction 3)
- *bila makosa* — "without errors; faultlessly" (P2b Item 8, Construction 1)
- *makosa ya kibinadamu* — "human error" (P2b Item 8, Construction 4)
- *kujifunza kutoka kwa makosa* — "to learn from mistakes" (P2b Item 8, Construction 5)
- *makosa ya kiakili* — "cognitive errors; errors of reasoning" (P2a Item 9, Derived Form 5)
- *kusahihisha* — "to correct; to rectify; to validate" (P2a Item 7, Derived Form 1)
- *kusahihishwa* — "to be corrected; to be validated" (P2a Item 7, Derived Form 3)
- *hii si sahihi* — "this is not correct; this is wrong" (P2b Item 7, Construction 6)

**Notes:** This group is oriented toward the identification and handling of error. The constructions *kosa la kimakusudi* versus *kosa la bahati mbaya* show that Swahili usage distinguishes intentional from accidental error within this domain. The constructions *kukiri kosa*, *kukubali makosa*, and *kurudia makosa* show that the group extends to the response to error (admission, repetition, learning). *Kusahihisha* (to correct) belongs here as the remedial response to error, though it also appears in Group 3 (verification) when used of document validation. *Kukosa* in the sense of "to lack" (as in *kukosa pesa*, to lack money) is noted in P1b as a semantic extension outside the error domain and is not included here.

## Group 8: Expressions used in religious and moral belief contexts

**Label:** Belief and faith expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe or evaluate the holding of religious or moral convictions, and the quality of that holding. They appear in Islamic and Christian devotional discourse, theological instruction, and moral evaluation of persons. They are oriented toward the relationship between a person and what they hold to be true or sacred, rather than toward the accuracy of a specific claim.

**Representative expressions:**

- *imani* — "faith; belief; trust; conviction" (P1b Root 3, Item 9)
- *imani ya kweli* — "true faith; genuine belief; sincere conviction" (P1b Root 3, Item 10)
- *imani ya kidini* — "religious faith; religious belief" (P1b Root 3, Item 11)
- *imani kwa Mungu* — "faith in God; belief in God" (P1b Root 3, Item 12)
- *imani thabiti* — "firm faith; steadfast belief; unshakeable conviction" (P1b Root 3, Item 27)
- *imani potofu* — "false belief; misguided faith; erroneous conviction" (P1b Root 3, Item 28)
- *kukosa imani* — "to lack faith; to be faithless" (P1b Root 3, Item 13)
- *kupoteza imani* — "to lose faith; to become disillusioned" (P1b Root 3, Item 14)
- *mwamini* — "a believer; one who believes" (P1b Root 3, Item 15)
- *waamini* — "believers; the faithful" (P1b Root 3, Item 16)
- *kuamini kwa moyo wote* — "to believe with one's whole heart; to trust wholeheartedly" (P1b Root 3, Item 8)
- *kuamini bila shaka* — "to believe without doubt; to trust without reservation" (P1b Root 3, Item 29)
- *ukweli wa kidini* — "religious truth; doctrinal truth" (P1b Root 1, Item 14)
- *ukweli wa Mungu* — "God's truth; divine truth" (P1b Root 1, Item 26)

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 5 (trustworthiness) through the shared root *amini*, but the expressions here are oriented toward the content and quality of belief rather than toward the reliability of a person. *Imani thabiti* (firm faith) and *imani potofu* (false belief) show that within this group there is an internal evaluative dimension: not all belief is equally valued, and the quality of holding a belief (firmness, sincerity, correctness) is assessed. *Mwamini* and *waamini* are person-referring forms that belong here rather than in Group 5 because they identify persons by their belief rather than by their reliability. The constructions *kuamini kwa moyo wote* and *kuamini bila shaka* are characteristic of devotional discourse and emphasize the wholeness and unconditionality of belief.

## Group 9: Expressions used in the evaluation of copies, reproductions, and representations

**Label:** Copy and reproduction evaluation expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a document, object, or representation is an original, a legitimate copy, or a fraudulent reproduction. They appear in legal, administrative, and commercial contexts where the status of a document or object relative to an original is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**

- *nakala* — "copy; duplicate; transcript; reproduction" (P1 Item 23)
- *kunakili* — "to copy; to transcribe; to reproduce" (P2a Item 23, Derived Form 1)
- *nakala halisi* — "a certified true copy; an authenticated copy" (P1b Root 2, Item 27)
- *hati halisi* — "a genuine document; an authentic certificate" (P1b Root 2, Item 28)
- *hati bandia* — "a forged document; a fake certificate" (P2b Item 6, Construction 2)
- *ushahidi wa maandishi* — "documentary evidence; written testimony" (P2b Item 16, Construction 1)
- *uthibitisho wa maandishi* — "written confirmation; documentary proof" (P2b Item 15, Construction 1)

**Notes:** This group is smaller than the others and is defined by a specific contextual domain rather than by a broad semantic pattern. The central tension in this group is between *nakala halisi* (a certified true copy, which is authentic despite being a copy) and *hati bandia* (a forged document, which is inauthentic despite appearing to be genuine). This tension is noted in P1b as a distinctive feature of the *nakala halisi* construction. The group overlaps with Group 1 (authenticity) and Group 2 (inauthenticity) but is distinguished by its specific orientation toward the document/reproduction domain.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items appear in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single group:

---

**haki**

*Haki* appears in Group 1 (truth-asserting, as in oath formulas and *haki ni haki*), Group 4 (legitimacy and entitlement, as in *haki za binadamu*, *kudai haki*), and in constructions that blend both senses (*haki halali*). The P1 documentation notes that *haki* covers both "what is correct or true" and "a legitimate entitlement or right." These two uses are attested in different constructions and contexts, but the item itself does not split cleanly into two separate lexical entries in usage.

---

**dhahiri / kudhihirisha / kudhihirika**

*Dhahiri* appears in Group 1 (asserting that something is clearly true) and in Group 3 (demonstrating or making evident through argument or evidence). The distinction is between *ni dhahiri* (it is evident — asserting self-evidence) and *kudhihirisha* (to demonstrate — an active process of making evident). In practice these shade into each other in argumentative discourse.

---

**sahihi / kusahihisha**

*Sahihi* appears in Group 1 (asserting correctness) and Group 7 (error evaluation, as the standard against which errors are measured and the tool by which they are corrected). *Kusahihisha* (to correct) belongs primarily to Group 7 but also appears in Group 3 when used of document validation.

---

**ukweli nusu**

*Ukweli nusu* (a half-truth) is used in moral and journalistic discourse to describe a statement that is technically accurate but misleading through omission. It belongs to Group 1 in that it involves a truth claim, but it is evaluatively negative in the same way as Group 2 expressions. The construction *ukweli nusu ni uongo* (a half-truth is a lie) explicitly bridges the two groups.

---

**akili bandia**

*Akili bandia* (artificial intelligence) belongs to Group 2 in form (using *bandia*, the inauthenticity marker) but is used in technology discourse without the evaluative negativity characteristic of Group 2. The P1b documentation notes that *bandia* here carries the "artificial/not natural" sense rather than the "counterfeit/fraudulent" sense. This item does not fit cleanly into any group as defined by usage patterns in the truth-evaluation domain.

---

**uhalisi pepe / uhalisi ulioboreshwa**

*Uhalisi pepe* (virtual reality) and *uhalisi ulioboreshwa* (augmented reality) use the *uhalisi* (reality/genuineness) root in technology discourse where "reality" refers to a simulated or enhanced perceptual environment. These are noted in P1b as peripheral extensions. They do not fit into any of the groups above, which are all oriented toward evaluation of truth, authenticity, or legitimacy in non-simulated contexts.

---

**heshima / kuheshimu / mheshimiwa**

*Heshima* (respect; dignity; honor) and its derivations appear in P1 and P2a in the context of moral evaluation of conduct. The P1 documentation notes that *heshima* is relevant to assessments of rightness of behavior in social and ethical contexts. However, the usage patterns documented do not show *heshima* being used in the same constructions or contrasts as the other items in this inventory. It does not group clearly with any of the groups above. It is noted here as an item that participates in moral evaluation but in a domain (social respect and dignity) that is distinct from the truth, authenticity, legitimacy, and trustworthiness domains that define the groups above.

---

## Items with No Clear Grouping

The following items from P1 are attested and documented but do not show clear grouping patterns with other items based on the usage evidence in P1 and P2:

- *usawa* (equality; balance; equilibrium) — derived from *sawa* but used primarily in equality and balance contexts rather than truth-evaluation contexts
