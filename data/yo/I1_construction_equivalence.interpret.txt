## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### BECOME vs. REVELATION
The verb **di** 'become' serves dual functions:
1. **Change-of-state**: A proposition becomes true (ontological change)
2. **Epistemic realization**: Something turns out to be true (discovery/verification)

The language does NOT substitute REVELATION for BECOME. Instead, REVELATION is expressed through:
- Serial verb construction **sọ...jáde** 'tell...come.out' (literally 'speak-exit')
- Causative-permissive **jẹ́ kí...hàn** 'let...show'

These are distinct from **di**, which focuses on the state change itself rather than the act of making something known.

### VERIFICATION Patterns
VERIFICATION is not expressed through a dedicated construction but through:
- **Polar questions** (§10): **Ṣé ó tọ́?** 'Is it true?'
- **Content questions** (§11): **Kí ni òtítọ́?** 'What is truth?'
- **Conditional constructions** (§22): **Bí ó bá tọ́...** 'If it is true...'

The language treats verification as an interrogative or conditional operation rather than as a distinct semantic operator.

### DISCONFIRMATION = NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION
DISCONFIRMATION is fully expressed through negative attribution constructions:
- **Kì í ṣe** (emphatic negative copula)
- **Kọ́** (negative focus marker)
- **Kò** (standard verbal negation)

There is NO separate construction for disconfirmation; it uses the same structures as general negation applied to truth predicates.

### CAUSATION Asymmetry
**CAUSATION (factual)** is robustly expressed through **mú X V** 'make X VERB':
- **Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́** 'He/She made me believe'
- **Ó mú mi mọ̀** 'He/She made me know'

**CAUSATION (character)** has NO dedicated construction. To express 'make someone truthful/honest', the language requires circumlocution:
- **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** - Would require periphrastic constructions like **mú X di olótítọ́** 'make X become truthful.person' or **kọ́ X ní ìwà òtítọ́** 'teach X in character truthful'

This represents a **structural gap**: the language readily causativizes epistemic states (belief, knowledge) but not character traits.

### NEGATION: Analytic Strategies
Yoruba employs **analytic negation** with distinct markers by construction type:
- **Kò** for verbal predicates: **Kò tọ́** 'It is not correct'
- **Kì í ṣe** for emphatic copular negation: **Kì í ṣe òtítọ́** 'It is not truth'
- **Kọ́** for negative focus: **Irọ́ kọ́** 'It's not a lie'
- **Má** for prohibitives: **Má purọ́** 'Don't lie'
- **Kò sí** for negative existentials: **Kò sí irọ́** 'There is no lie'

No morphological (bound) negation exists in the truth domain.

### Truth vs. Falsity Asymmetry
The language shows lexical richness for both truth and falsity:
- **Truth**: **òtítọ́** (noun), **tọ́** (verb), **olótítọ́** (agent)
- **Falsity**: **irọ́** (noun), **purọ́** (verb), **apurọ́** (agent)

However, there is an **asymmetry in moral evaluation**:
- Lying has a dedicated verb: **purọ́** 'to lie'
- Truth-telling requires a phrase: **sọ òtítọ́** 'tell truth' (no single verb *'to truth')

This suggests that deception is conceptualized as a unitary action, while truth-telling is compositional.

### Focus Construction Preference
The **ni** focus construction (§21) is heavily preferred for:
- Emphatic assertions: **Òtítọ́ ni** 'It IS truth'
- Contrastive denials: **Irọ́ kọ́** 'It's NOT a lie'
- Character attribution: **Olótítọ́ ni baba mi** 'My father IS a truthful person'

This suggests that truth-domain discourse in Yoruba is inherently **contrastive and emphatic**, favoring focus constructions over neutral predication.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
1. **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction for 'make X truthful/honest'
   - The language can causativize epistemic states (**mú X gbàgbọ́** 'make X believe') but not character traits
   - Requires circumlocution with **di** 'become' or teaching/training verbs

### Lexical Gaps
1. **Single-word verb for 'tell the truth'**: 
   - Must use phrase **sọ òtítọ́** 'tell truth'
   - Contrast with **purọ́** 'lie' (single verb)

### Conceptual Gaps
**NONE IDENTIFIED**. All truth-domain concepts appear expressible, though some require multi-word constructions.

### Asymmetric Gaps
1. **Truth-telling vs. Lying**:
   - Lying: **purọ́** (single verb)
   - Truth-telling: **sọ òtítọ́** (verb + object phrase)
   
2. **Causation of states vs. traits**:
   - Epistemic causation: **mú X gbàgbọ́** 'make X believe' ✓
   - Character causation: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** ✗

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available
- **ATTR**: Two constructions (**jẹ́** neutral, **ni** emphatic)
- **ADNOM**: Direct N+ADJ and relative clauses with **tí**
- **BECOME**: **di** for change-of-state and epistemic realization
- **REVELATION**: Serial verb **sọ...jáde** and causative **jẹ́ kí...hàn**
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: **mú X V** for causing epistemic states
- **NEGATION**: Multiple analytic strategies (**kò**, **kì í ṣe**, **kọ́**, **má**)
- **NOMINALIZATION**: Multiple productive strategies (abstract **ì-**, gerundive reduplication, agentive **oní-**/**a-**)

### Available with Substitution
- **VERIFICATION**: Expressed through interrogative constructions (§10, §11)
  - No dedicated verification operator; uses polar/content questions
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Expressed through negative attribution (§2)
  - No separate construction; uses general negation on truth predicates

### Partially Available
**NONE**

### Missing Entirely
- **CAUSATION (character)**: No construction for 'make X truthful/honest'
  - Structural gap requiring circumlocution
  - Cannot directly causativize character traits in truth domain

---

## Summary Statement

Yoruba provides robust constructional support for truth-domain operations with the following characteristics:

1. **Dual copular system**: Neutral **jẹ́** vs. emphatic **ni** for attribution
2. **Analytic negation**: Multiple context-sensitive negation markers
3. **Productive nominalization**: Four distinct strategies for creating truth-domain nominals
4. **Verification through interrogation**: No dedicated verification operator; uses questions
5. **Revelation as serial verb**: Conceptualized as 'speak-exit' or 'let-show'
6. **Causation asymmetry**: Can causativize epistemic states but not character traits
7. **Lexical asymmetry**: Single verb for lying (**purọ́**) but phrasal expression for truth-telling (**sọ òtítọ́**)

The primary **structural gap** is the inability to directly causativize character traits (making someone truthful/honest), requiring periphrastic constructions with 'become' or 'teach'.
