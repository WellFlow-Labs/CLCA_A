# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## Lexical Items

1. **doğru**
   - Literal meaning: straight, direct
   - Typical English translations: true, correct, right, accurate
   - Usage: Used to evaluate statements as factually accurate or morally right. Also functions as a postposition meaning "toward" and as an adverb meaning "correctly." Common in both everyday and formal discourse.
   - [CORE]

2. **yanlış**
   - Literal meaning: from root *yan* (side) + *-lış* (nominalizer), suggesting "deviation"
   - Typical English translations: wrong, false, incorrect, mistaken
   - Usage: Primary term for evaluating statements, actions, or beliefs as incorrect or morally wrong. Used across all registers from casual conversation to academic writing.
   - [CORE]

3. **gerçek**
   - Literal meaning: from Persian *gercek*, related to "real, actual"
   - Typical English translations: real, true, genuine, actual
   - Usage: Emphasizes authenticity and reality rather than mere correctness. Used to distinguish genuine from fake, or actual from apparent. Common in philosophical and everyday contexts.
   - [CORE]

4. **sahte**
   - Literal meaning: from Arabic *saḫt* (hard, firm), semantic shift to "counterfeit"
   - Typical English translations: fake, counterfeit, false, phony
   - Usage: Specifically denotes inauthenticity or deliberate deception in objects, documents, or persons. Carries stronger connotation of fraudulence than simple incorrectness.
   - [CORE]

5. **hakikat**
   - Literal meaning: from Arabic *ḥaqīqa* (truth, reality)
   - Typical English translations: truth, reality, the real truth
   - Usage: Formal or elevated term for fundamental truth, often used in philosophical, religious, or literary contexts. Implies deeper or ultimate truth rather than simple factual accuracy.
   - [CORE]

6. **güvenilir**
   - Literal meaning: *güven* (trust) + *-ilir* (passive possibility suffix)
   - Typical English translations: reliable, trustworthy, dependable
   - Usage: Evaluates persons, sources, or objects as worthy of trust or confidence. Common in assessments of information sources, character references, and product reviews.
   - [CORE]

7. **dürüst**
   - Literal meaning: from Persian *durust* (correct, proper)
   - Typical English translations: honest, truthful, sincere
   - Usage: Evaluates persons or conduct as morally upright and truthful. Emphasizes character and integrity rather than mere factual accuracy.
   - [CORE]

8. **hak**
   - Literal meaning: from Arabic *ḥaqq* (right, truth, justice)
   - Typical English translations: right, just, true, God
   - Usage: Multifaceted term used for rights, justice, truth, and in religious contexts for God. In evaluative contexts, indicates what is rightfully due or morally correct.
   - [CORE]

9. **adil**
   - Literal meaning: from Arabic *ʿādil* (just, fair)
   - Typical English translations: just, fair, equitable
   - Usage: Primarily used in legal and moral contexts to evaluate actions, decisions, or persons as fair and impartial. More formal than everyday speech.
   - [CORE]

10. **yanıltıcı**
    - Literal meaning: *yanılt-* (to mislead) + *-ıcı* (agentive suffix)
    - Typical English translations: misleading, deceptive, deceiving
    - Usage: Evaluates statements, appearances, or representations as likely to cause false beliefs. Common in critical analysis and warnings about misinformation.
    - [CORE]

11. **kesin**
    - Literal meaning: from Arabic *qaṭʿ* (cutting, decisive)
    - Typical English translations: certain, definite, absolute, precise
    - Usage: Indicates high degree of certainty or precision in statements or measurements. Used to emphasize definitiveness and lack of ambiguity.
    - [CORE]

12. **şüpheli**
    - Literal meaning: *şüphe* (doubt, from Arabic *šubha*) + *-li* (possessive suffix)
    - Typical English translations: doubtful, suspicious, dubious, suspect
    - Usage: Evaluates persons, claims, or situations as warranting doubt or suspicion. Common in both epistemic contexts (uncertain truth) and moral contexts (suspicious character).
    - [CORE]

13. **asıl**
    - Literal meaning: from Arabic *aṣl* (origin, root, essence)
    - Typical English translations: original, genuine, essential, main
    - Usage: Distinguishes the authentic or fundamental from derivative or superficial. Used to identify what something truly or originally is.
    - [CORE]

14. **özgün**
    - Literal meaning: *öz* (essence, self) + *-gün* (archaic suffix)
    - Typical English translations: original, authentic, genuine
    - Usage: Emphasizes originality and authenticity, particularly in creative or intellectual work. Contrasts with copied or derivative material.
    - [CORE]

15. **taklit**
    - Literal meaning: from Arabic *taqlīd* (imitation)
    - Typical English translations: imitation, copy, fake, counterfeit
    - Usage: Denotes something made to resemble something else, ranging from neutral copying to fraudulent counterfeiting. Context determines whether pejorative.
    - [CORE]

16. **isabetli**
    - Literal meaning: *isabet* (hitting the mark, from Arabic *iṣāba*) + *-li*
    - Typical English translations: accurate, apt, on target, well-aimed
    - Usage: Evaluates statements, judgments, or actions as hitting the mark or being appropriate. Implies both accuracy and appropriateness.
    - [CORE]

17. **tutarlı**
    - Literal meaning: *tut-* (to hold) + *-ar* + *-lı* (consistent, coherent)
    - Typical English translations: consistent, coherent, logical
    - Usage: Evaluates arguments, behaviors, or systems as internally consistent and free from contradiction. Important in logical and moral evaluation.
    - [CORE]

18. **geçerli**
    - Literal meaning: *geç-* (to pass) + *-er* + *-li* (valid, current)
    - Typical English translations: valid, current, applicable
    - Usage: Indicates that something meets required standards or conditions, particularly in legal, logical, or temporal contexts. Used for validity of arguments, documents, or tickets.
    - [CORE]

19. **meşru**
    - Literal meaning: from Arabic *mašrūʿ* (lawful, legitimate)
    - Typical English translations: legitimate, lawful, rightful
    - Usage: Evaluates actions, claims, or authority as conforming to law or accepted norms. Common in legal and political discourse.
    - [CORE]

20. **haksız**
    - Literal meaning: *hak* (right) + *-sız* (without)
    - Typical English translations: unjust, unfair, wrong, unrightful
    - Usage: Evaluates actions or situations as lacking justice or rightfulness. Used in moral and legal contexts to indicate violation of rights or fairness.
    - [CORE]

21. **yalan**
    - Literal meaning: native Turkish word for lie
    - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
    - Usage: Denotes deliberately false statements. Distinguished from mere error by implication of intentional deception. Very common in everyday speech.
    - [CORE]

22. **inandırıcı**
    - Literal meaning: *inan-* (to believe) + *-dır* (causative) + *-ıcı* (agentive)
    - Typical English translations: convincing, credible, believable, persuasive
    - Usage: Evaluates statements, arguments, or performances as capable of inducing belief. Focuses on persuasive power rather than actual truth.
    - [CORE]

23. **sağlam**
    - Literal meaning: native Turkish, originally "healthy, sound"
    - Typical English translations: solid, sound, reliable, robust, well-founded
    - Usage: Evaluates physical objects, arguments, or evidence as structurally sound and dependable. Metaphorically extended from physical solidity to epistemic reliability.
    - [CORE]

24. **kuşkulu**
    - Literal meaning: *kuşku* (doubt) + *-lu* (possessive suffix)
    - Typical English translations: doubtful, questionable, dubious
    - Usage: Similar to *şüpheli* but slightly less formal. Indicates presence of doubt or uncertainty about truth or reliability.
    - [CORE]

25. **haklı**
    - Literal meaning: *hak* (right) + *-lı* (possessive)
    - Typical English translations: right, justified, in the right
    - Usage: Evaluates persons or positions as having justice or correctness on their side. Common in disputes and moral evaluations.
    - [CORE]

26. **hatalı**
    - Literal meaning: *hata* (error, from Arabic *ḫaṭaʾ*) + *-lı*
    - Typical English translations: erroneous, faulty, mistaken, defective
    - Usage: Indicates presence of errors or faults, whether in statements, products, or actions. Less morally charged than *yanlış* in some contexts.
    - [CORE]

27. **doğal**
    - Literal meaning: *doğa* (nature) + *-l* (adjectival)
    - Typical English translations: natural, genuine, authentic
    - Usage: Evaluates things as occurring in nature or being free from artificiality. Used to distinguish authentic from artificial or forced.
    - [CORE]

28. **yapay**
    - Literal meaning: *yap-* (to make) + *-ay* (adjectival)
    - Typical English translations: artificial, synthetic, man-made
    - Usage: Denotes human construction or manufacture, contrasting with natural. Can be neutral or pejorative depending on context.
    - [CORE]
