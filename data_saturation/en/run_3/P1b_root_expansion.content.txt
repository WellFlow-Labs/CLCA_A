# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
**Language: English (en)**

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: *ver-* (Latin *verus* "true")**

- Root form: *ver-* (no native script distinction from English orthography; Latin origin)
- P1 items participating: **veracious**, **veracity**, **verisimilitude** — plus indirect participation through the semantic field anchored by *verus* that underlies the entire domain
- Core meaning contribution: Encodes the property of correspondence to fact or reality as an inherent quality; the root that most directly names "truth" as a property of propositions and persons in the Latinate stratum of English

---

**Root 2: *tru-* / *trēow-* (Old English *trēowe* "faithful, trustworthy")**

- Root form: *tru-* (Modern English reflex of OE *trēow-*)
- P1 items participating: **true**, **truth**, **truthful**, **trustworthy** (implied in derivational family), **truly** (derivation of *true*)
- Core meaning contribution: The primary Germanic root encoding both factual correspondence and personal fidelity/reliability; the most productive single root in the domain for everyday English

---

**Root 3: *fid-* / *fide-* (Latin *fides* "faith, trust")**

- Root form: *fid-* / *fide-*
- P1 items participating: **faithful**, **bona fide**, **credible** (via *credere*, semantically adjacent), **fraudulent** (as negative counterpart in the trust domain)
- Core meaning contribution: Encodes trust, good faith, and fidelity to commitment or original; links the truth domain to interpersonal reliability and legal good-faith concepts

---

> **Note on selection rationale:** The *ver-* root is the most semantically concentrated in the truth-evaluation domain. The *tru-* root is the most productive in everyday English. The *fid-* root is selected over alternatives (e.g., *leg-* "law," *cred-* "believe") because it participates in the widest range of cross-register expressions — legal, moral, aesthetic, and interpersonal — that the protocol requires expanding.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

### ROOT 1: *ver-* (Latin *verus* "true")

---

**1.**
- Form: verify
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *ver-* "true" + *-ify* (Latin *-ficare* "to make")
- Natural English translation: to establish or confirm the truth or accuracy of something
- Context of use: Used across administrative, legal, scientific, and everyday contexts; one verifies a claim, an identity, a measurement, or a fact by checking it against evidence or a standard.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**2.**
- Form: verification
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *ver-* + *-ific-* + *-ation* (nominalization)
- Natural English translation: the process or result of verifying; confirmation of truth or accuracy
- Context of use: Used in legal, scientific, administrative, and technical contexts; *verification of identity*, *verification of a hypothesis*, *independent verification*.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**3.**
- Form: verifiable
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *ver-* + *-ifi-* + *-able* "capable of being made true/confirmed"
- Natural English translation: capable of being verified or confirmed by evidence
- Context of use: Used in scientific, philosophical, and journalistic discourse; a *verifiable claim* is one that can in principle be checked against evidence. Central to logical positivism's verificationism.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**4.**
- Form: unverifiable
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *un-* + *verifiable*
- Natural English translation: not capable of being verified
- Context of use: Used in philosophy of science, journalism, and legal contexts to characterize claims that cannot be checked against available evidence.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**5.**
- Form: verifiability
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *verifiable* + *-ity*
- Natural English translation: the property of being verifiable
- Context of use: Central term in philosophy of science and epistemology; the *verifiability criterion* of meaning was a key tenet of logical positivism.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**6.**
- Form: verdict
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: Latin *vere* "truly" + *dictum* "said, spoken"
- Natural English translation: a formal decision or judgment, especially by a jury
- Context of use: Primarily legal; the jury's *verdict* is the formal declaration of guilt or innocence. Extended to any authoritative judgment or conclusion.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**7.**
- Form: verity
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: Latin *veritas* "truth" via Old French *verité*; *ver-* + *-ity*
- Natural English translation: a true principle or belief; a fundamental truth
- Context of use: Formal and literary; used in philosophical and religious discourse — *eternal verities*, *the verities of human experience*. Somewhat archaic in everyday use.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**8.**
- Form: verism
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *ver-* + *-ism* "doctrine of truth/realism"
- Natural English translation: a style or doctrine emphasizing faithful, unidealized depiction of reality
- Context of use: Used in art history and literary criticism to describe movements (especially in Italian opera and visual art) committed to realistic, unembellished representation of life.
- Epistemic status: **[MARGINAL]**

---

**9.**
- Form: verist
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *ver-* + *-ist*
- Natural English translation: an adherent of verism; one committed to realistic depiction
- Context of use: Used in art criticism and literary history; a *verist* painter or composer works in the verismo tradition.
- Epistemic status: **[MARGINAL]**

---

**10.**
- Form: verismo
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: Italian *ver-* + *-ismo* (borrowed into English)
- Natural English translation: a late 19th-century Italian operatic and literary movement emphasizing realistic, everyday subject matter
- Context of use: Used in musicology and literary criticism; *verismo opera* (Puccini, Mascagni) is characterized by contemporary, lower-class settings and naturalistic drama.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]** (within its domain)

---

**11.**
- Form: veridical
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: Latin *veridicus*: *ver-* "true" + *dicere* "to say" + *-al*
- Natural English translation: truthful; corresponding to facts; (of a perception or dream) accurately representing reality
- Context of use: Used in philosophy, psychology, and epistemology; a *veridical perception* is one that accurately represents the external world, contrasted with hallucination or illusion.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]** (within its domain)

---

**12.**
- Form: veridicality
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *veridical* + *-ity*
- Natural English translation: the property of being veridical; accuracy of representation
- Context of use: Technical term in philosophy of perception and psychology; used to assess whether a perceptual experience accurately reflects its object.

---

**13.**
- Form: veritas

- Natural English translation: truth (used as a motto or in formal/philosophical contexts)
- Context of use: Appears in institutional mottoes (*Veritas* — Harvard; *Lux et Veritas* — Yale); used in philosophical and rhetorical contexts as a direct invocation of truth as a value.

---

**14.**

- Natural English translation: in wine there is truth; people speak honestly when drunk

**15.**

**16.**

---

**17.**

- Literal morpheme breakdown: Old French *averer*, from Latin *ad-* + *verus*
- Natural English translation: to assert or affirm with confidence; to declare as a fact
- Context of use: Used in legal and formal written contexts; a witness or party *avers* a fact, meaning they assert it positively and are prepared to prove it.

**21.**

- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *verification* + *principle*
- Natural English translation: the philosophical criterion that a statement is meaningful only if it is empirically verifiable

---

**22.**
- Form: verificationism
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *verification* + *-ism*
- Natural English translation: the philosophical doctrine that the meaning of a statement consists in its method of verification

---

**23.**
- Form: verificationist
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *verificationism* + *-ist*
- Natural English translation: an adherent of verificationism; of or relating to verificationism

---

**24.**
- Form: veracious account
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *veracious* + *account* (collocation)
- Natural English translation: a truthful, accurate narrative or report
- Context of use: Used in legal, historical, and journalistic contexts to characterize testimony or reporting as reliably truthful.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

**25.**
- Form: vouch for
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: Old French *voucher* "to call as witness," from Latin *vocare*; semantically adjacent to *ver-* family through the truth-attestation function
- Natural English translation: to give a personal guarantee of the truth, quality, or reliability of someone or something

> **Note:** *vouch* does not derive from *ver-* etymologically; it is included here because it functions as a near-synonym of *aver* in the truth-attestation cluster and is frequently collocated with *ver-* family items. It is flagged for transparency.

- Context of use: Used in everyday, legal, and professional contexts; *I can vouch for his honesty*; *vouch for the accuracy of a document*.
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

> **Productivity note:** The *ver-* root is moderately productive in English. The forms above represent the main attested compounds and derivatives. Additional technical coinages exist in specialized philosophical literature (e.g., *truthlikeness* as a translation of *verisimilitude* in Popperian philosophy of science) but these are paraphrases rather than *ver-* derivatives.

### ROOT 2: *tru-* (Old English *trēow-* "faithful, trustworthy")

> **Note on scope:** The Modern English root *tru-* is the reflex of OE *trēow-* and is the most productive single root in the everyday English truth domain. The related root *trust* (from Old Norse *traust*) is treated as a distinct but closely related stem and is included where it participates in the same semantic field. Derivations from *true* and *trust* are listed together because they are synchronically perceived as a single root cluster by English speakers, though they have distinct etymological origins.

---

- Form: truly
- Transliteration: N/A
- Literal morpheme breakdown: *true* + *-ly*
- Natural English translation: in a true manner; genuinely; really
- Context of use: Used as an adverb of manner (*truly believe*), as an intensifier (*truly remarkable*), and in epistolary closings (*yours truly*).
- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

- Epistemic status: **[CORE]**

---

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions show the root's contribution shifting in meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From *ver-*:**

- **verify** appears in computing contexts (*verify a disk*, *verify a checksum*) where the operation is procedural/technical rather than epistemic
- **verdict** is used in aesthetic and consumer contexts (*the verdict on the new restaurant*) where it functions as a judgment of quality rather than a determination of truth
- **verismo** / **verism** operate in aesthetic evaluation where "truth" means stylistic realism rather than propositional accuracy
- **verisimilitude** in philosophy of science (Popper's *Wahrheitsnähe*) involves a quantitative or comparative notion of approximation to truth, distinct from binary truth/falsity
- **veridical** in psychology is applied to dreams and hallucinations, where the question is whether an internal experience corresponds to an external state

---

**From *tru-*:**

- **true north** in figurative use (leadership, personal values) applies the root to moral orientation and guiding principles rather than factual correspondence
- **true to type** in biology applies the root to genetic/phenotypic consistency across generations, a reproductive rather than epistemic concept
- **true** in carpentry and engineering (*true up a surface*, *out of true*) refers to geometric alignment and precision, not propositional truth
- **true-blue** applies the root to political loyalty and personal constancy, where the relevant standard is commitment rather than factual accuracy
- **truism** carries a dismissive or pejorative connotation — a statement that is *too* obviously true to be worth asserting — inverting the normally positive valence of the root
- **trust** in financial and legal contexts (*trust fund*, *trustee*, *trust law*) applies the root to fiduciary obligation and asset management
- **post-truth** applies the root in a negating or superseding construction that frames truth itself as a historical or contingent phenomenon

---

**From *fid-*:**

- **fidelity** in audio technology (*high fidelity*, *hi-fi*) applies the root to the accuracy of signal reproduction, a technical rather than interpersonal or propositional domain
- **confidence** in statistics (*confidence interval*, *confidence level*) applies the root to a quantitative measure of probability, not personal trust
- **confidence trick** / **con man** apply the root to the mechanism of deception — trust is the instrument of fraud rather than a positive value
- **diffidence** and **diffident** apply the root to self-assessment and social behavior (shyness, hesitancy) rather than to truth or reliability of information
- **infidel** applies the root to religious identity and group membership rather than to the truth-value of propositions
- **bad faith** in Sartrean existentialism applies the root to a structure of self-deception about freedom and responsibility, a philosophical use distinct from legal or interpersonal trust
- **Fido** as a proper name applies the root to animal loyalty and human-animal bonding
- **semper fidelis** applies the root to institutional loyalty and military identity
