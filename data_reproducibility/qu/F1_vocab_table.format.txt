# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — F1 Vocabulary Table

| chiqaq | chiqaq | true, truthful (from root *chiqay* "to be true") | true, real, genuine, authentic | This term is used to affirm the accuracy or genuineness of statements, objects, or persons. It appears in both everyday discourse to validate claims and in more formal contexts to distinguish authentic from false representations. | [CORE] |
| cheqaq | cheqaq | variant of *chiqaq* (dialectal variation) | true, real, correct | This is a dialectal variant of *chiqaq* used in some Quechua varieties. It functions identically to affirm truth or accuracy of statements and situations. | [CORE] |
| llulla | llulla | lie, false | false, lie, untrue, deceptive | Used to characterize statements or representations as false or deceptive. This term can function as both noun (a lie) and adjective (false), and is commonly used in moral evaluations of dishonest speech or behavior. | [CORE] |
| allin | allin | good, well | good, right, correct, proper, well | This is a general evaluative term used for positive assessment of conduct, objects, states, and actions. | mana allin | mana allin | not good | bad, wrong, improper, incorrect | A fixed negative expression used to evaluate conduct, states, or objects as deficient or improper. This is the standard way to express negative moral or practical evaluation in many Quechua varieties. | [CORE] |

| sumaq | sumaq | beautiful, good, pleasant | beautiful, good, pleasant, excellent | An aesthetic and moral evaluative term indicating beauty, goodness, or excellence. It is used for positive evaluation of objects, persons, conduct, and situations across multiple domains. | [CORE] |
| millay | millay | ugly, disgusting, abominable | ugly, disgusting, abominable, hateful | A strong negative evaluative term used for aesthetic, moral, and general disapproval. It characterizes objects, conduct, or persons as repugnant or morally reprehensible. | [CORE] |
| yupaychay | yupaychay | to honor, to respect, to value | to honor, to respect, to revere, to value | Used in contexts evaluating the worthiness of persons or things for respect and honor. It appears in moral and social contexts regarding proper recognition of value or status. | [CORE] |
