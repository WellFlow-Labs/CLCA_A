# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgia (2002), as used in Georgian passports and official romanization, supplemented by standard academic convention for Georgian studies. Applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine quality) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements, beliefs, and propositions, and also to characterize persons or things as genuine or authentic. Appears in philosophical, theological, and everyday evaluative contexts. Participates in productive derivation: č̣ešmariteba (truth, as abstract noun), č̣ešmaritad (truly, adverb).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity, genuineness
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in Georgian, used across philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday registers. Commonly appears as the subject or object of predications about what is the case. Central to Georgian theological and philosophical writing. Contrasts with mtkuaroba (falsehood) and t'yupiloba (deception).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominalizing circumfix, front part) + martal- (right, correct, just) + -e (back part of circumfix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, justice
**Usage:** One of the two primary words for truth in Georgian, often more colloquial or concrete than č̣ešmariteba. Used for factual correctness of statements and for moral rightness of conduct. Also carries connotations of fairness and justice. The phrase simartle tkva ("told the truth") is highly frequent in everyday speech. Also used in the sense of what is factually accurate in a dispute.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** root martal- (right, correct, just) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, true, just, honest, innocent
**Usage:** Applied to persons (a truthful, just, or honest person), statements (a correct or true claim), and situations or verdicts (a rightful state of affairs, a just outcome). Covers both epistemic correctness and moral uprightness, making it semantically broad. In legal contexts can carry the sense of innocent or vindicated. Closely related to simartle through shared root. Commonly used in everyday speech and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -e
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, falseness, forgery, inauthenticity, fraud
**Usage:** Denotes the abstract quality of being false or counterfeit, applicable to documents, currency, statements, and persons. Used in both epistemic contexts (the falsity of a statement) and moral-legal contexts (fraudulence of conduct). Frequently appears in legal contexts involving forgery or fraud. Antonym of simartle and č̣ešmariteba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** borrowed root (cf. Persian/Arabic qalb, "heart; counterfeit," via Turkish); adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged, spurious
**Usage:** The standard adjective for counterfeit or forged items (q̇albi dokumenti, "forged document"; q̇albi fuli, "counterfeit money"). Also used for insincere or false behavior and colloquially for inauthentic persons. Strongly associated with deliberate deception or forgery. Appears frequently in legal and commercial contexts. Productive in derivation: siq̇albe, q̇alboba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ყალბობა
**Transliteration:** q̇alboba
**Literal meaning:** q̇albi + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** falseness, forgery, fraud, falsification
**Usage:** The nominal form of q̇albi, referring to the act or state of being counterfeit or fraudulent. Used in legal discourse to describe forgery or falsification of documents and evidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**8.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** na- (perfective/resultative prefix) + mdvil- (root related to being placed, settled, established) + -i; roughly "that which has been established/settled"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** The primary adjective for authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, and states of affairs. Does not typically predicate truth of propositions directly. Namdvili oqro ("real gold"), namdvili megobari ("true friend"). Widely used across registers and participates in productive derivation (namdviloba, "genuineness, authenticity").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, realness, reality
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from namdvili, used in legal, philosophical, administrative, and archival contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in discussions of document authentication, identity verification, and philosophical discourse about reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**10.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (root associated with lying/falsehood) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The standard noun for a lie or falsehood in everyday and moral discourse. Refers specifically to a lie or deliberate untruth, with a stronger connotation of intentional deception than siq̇albe. Contrasts directly with simartle. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in legal contexts involving perjury or deception. Closely related to mtkuari ("liar").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**11.**
**Form:** ტყუილი
**Transliteration:** ẗq̇uili
**Literal meaning:** ẗq̇uil- (lie, falsehood) + nominal suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The most common everyday word for a lie, used in direct moral evaluation of statements and persons. Slightly more colloquial than sicrue. The verb ẗq̇uili tkva is a standard fixed expression meaning "told a lie." Also used adverbially in the sense of "in vain, for nothing."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**12.**
**Form:** მცდარი
**Transliteration:** mcdari
**Literal meaning:** m- (subject preverb) + cdar- (to err, to be mistaken) + -i
**Typical English translations:** erroneous, mistaken, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to beliefs, judgments, and statements that are factually incorrect or logically flawed. More neutral in tone than sicrue (it implies error rather than deliberate deception). Common in academic, scientific, and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**13.**
**Form:** მტყუარი
**Transliteration:** mt'q̇uari
**Literal meaning:** mt'q̇u- (root of lying) + agentive suffix -ari
**Typical English translations:** liar, false (person), untruthful
**Usage:** An agentive noun or adjective characterizing a person who lies or a statement that is untrue. Used in moral evaluation of persons and in characterizing false testimony. Participates in the derivational family including mtkuaroba.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**14.**
**Form:** სწორი
**Transliteration:** sc̣ori
**Literal meaning:** root sc̣or- (straight, correct) + -i
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, straight, proper
**Usage:** Used for factual correctness of answers, calculations, statements, decisions, and conduct. sc̣ori pasukhi ("correct answer") is a highly frequent collocation. Overlaps with martali in the sense of "correct" but is more commonly used for factual correctness and less for moral uprightness. Also used geometrically (straight line) and morally (upright person). Very frequent in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**15.**
**Form:** უზუსტო
**Transliteration:** uzusto
**Literal meaning:** u- (privative prefix) + zusto (exact, precise) + privative construction
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, imprecise, inexact
**Usage:** Used to describe statements, measurements, or representations that lack precision or accuracy. Appears frequently in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**16.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** borrowed root (cf. Persian/Turkish zust, "exact, precise"); adjectival form + -i
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** The standard adjective for precision and exactness in measurements, translations, statements, and reproductions. Commonly used to evaluate whether a representation faithfully corresponds to what it represents. zusti targmani ("accurate translation"), zusti monacemebi ("precise data"). Very common across technical, scientific, and everyday registers. Participates in derivation: sizuste, zustad ("precisely, exactly" as adverb).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**17.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- (exact, precise) + -e
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness, correctness
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from zusti, used to describe the property of being accurate or precise in measurements, translations, reproductions, and statements. Common in scientific, journalistic, technical, and legal discourse when the degree of accuracy of a representation is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**18.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarc̣muno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (circumfix front, indicating fitness/capacity, "worthy of") + rc̣mun- (root of belief, trust, faith) + -o (circumfix back)
**Typical English translations:** credible, reliable, trustworthy, believable
**Usage:** Applied to sources, witnesses, evidence, and persons to indicate that they are worthy of belief or trust. Frequently used in legal and journalistic contexts (sarcmuno mowme, "credible witness"). Participates in the productive sa-...-o pattern for "worthy of X" or "capable of X." Participates in derivation: sarwmunoba ("credibility, reliability"). Contrasts with usarcmuno ("incredible, unreliable").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**19.**
**Form:** სარწმუნოება
**Transliteration:** sarwmunoeba
**Literal meaning:** sarwmuno + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** credibility, reliability, trustworthiness; also faith, religion (in theological register)
**Usage:** In secular contexts, denotes the property of being credible or trustworthy. In theological and ecclesiastical contexts, the dominant meaning shifts to faith or religion. The semantic range is broad and context-dependent.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**20.**
**Form:** სანდო
**Transliteration:** sando
**Literal meaning:** sa- + nd- (root related to trust, confidence; cf. ndoba "trust") + -o
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, dependable
**Usage:** Applied primarily to persons, sources, and institutions to indicate reliability and trustworthiness. Somewhat more colloquial than sarcmuno and tends to emphasize practical reliability over epistemic credibility. Closely synonymous with sarwmuno but may carry a slightly stronger interpersonal connotation of personal trust. Frequently used in journalism and everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**21.**
**Form:** დამაჯერებელი
**Transliteration:** damajerebeliˈ
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + majer- (causative of "believe," to convince, to make believe) + -ebeli (agentive/adjectival suffix, "capable of causing") + -i
**Typical English translations:** convincing, persuasive, plausible, credible
**Usage:** Describes arguments, evidence, persons, or performances that are convincing or persuasive. Distinct from sarc̣muno in emphasizing the effect of persuasion on a hearer rather than an intrinsic property of trustworthiness. Commonly used in rhetorical, legal, and critical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**22.**
**Form:** გულწრფელი
**Transliteration:** gulc̣rpeli
**Literal meaning:** gul- (heart) + c̣rpel- (sincere, straight) + -i; literally "straight-hearted"
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt, candid, frank
**Usage:** Applied to persons, expressions, and intentions to indicate sincerity and absence of pretense or deception. Used in moral evaluation of character and in assessing the authenticity of emotional or verbal expression. gulc̣rpeli bodiši ("sincere apology"), gulc̣rpeli grznoba ("genuine feeling"). Contrasts with terms implying hypocrisy or pretense.

**23.**
**Form:** თვალთმაქცობა
**Transliteration:** tvaltmaqcoba
**Literal meaning:** tval- (eye) + t- (connective) + maqc- (to turn, to deceive the eye; deceiver, juggler) + -oba (nominalizing suffix); literally "eye-turning, eye-deceiving"
**Typical English translations:** deception, trickery, hypocrisy, pretense, illusion, charlatanism
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception through appearance or performance, including hypocrisy and charlatanism. Used in moral condemnation of persons who present a false appearance or engage in hypocritical or deceptive conduct. The morphological structure explicitly encodes visual deception. Also used for visual illusions or sleight of hand in more literal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**24.**
**Form:** მოჩვენებითი
**Transliteration:** močvenebiti
**Literal meaning:** mo- (preverb) + čven- (root of showing/appearing) + -ebit- + -i; roughly "of the nature of mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, illusory, ostensible, spurious
**Usage:** Describes something that merely appears to be the case without actually being so. Applied to arguments, qualities, and identities. Carries a critical or skeptical evaluative force. močvenebiti თანხმობა ("ostensible agreement"), močvenebiti safrtkhe ("apparent danger"). Used in philosophical, legal, and analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**25.**
**Form:** ფიქტიური
**Transliteration:** piqtiuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin/Russian fictivnyj; adjectival form
**Typical English translations:** fictitious, fictional, fabricated, sham
**Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for fictitious entities, sham transactions, or fabricated claims. piqtiuri kompania ("fictitious company"), piqtiuri qidva-gayidva ("sham transaction"). A loanword with a narrower, more formal register than native alternatives.

**26.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avtentiuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek/Latin authenticus via European languages; adjectival form + -uri
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** A loanword used primarily in formal, legal, archival, and academic contexts to describe documents, texts, or artifacts of verified origin and integrity. Less common in everyday speech than namdvili, which covers similar ground in informal registers. Increasingly common in contemporary Georgian. Coexists with namdvili but tends to appear in more technical or international-facing discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
