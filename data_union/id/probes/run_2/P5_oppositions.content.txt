# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit attestation in the context. No opposition has been inferred from general linguistic knowledge or imposed from outside the data. Where the context documents an asymmetry between two terms (e.g., different register, different implication of intent), this is reported in the description rather than suppressed.

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** *benar*
2. **Term B:** *salah*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** correct / wrong
5. **Description of contrast:** *Benar* affirms that a statement or claim corresponds to reality; *salah* denies this. The pair is explicitly documented as a fixed binary formula (*benar atau salah*, "true or false / right or wrong") used in educational, logical, and moral contexts, and appears in test formats (*soal benar atau salah*). Both terms also extend into moral evaluation, where *benar* means "right" and *salah* means "wrong" or "at fault."
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** *benar*
2. **Term B:** *tidak benar*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** correct / not correct
5. **Description of contrast:** *Tidak benar* is the standard morphological negation of *benar*, formed by inserting the negator *tidak* before the predicate adjective. The context documents this as the standard formula in official denials, corrections, and rebuttals, and notes it as distinct from *salah* in that it denies confirmation rather than asserting wrongness.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** *benar*
2. **Term B:** *kurang benar*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** correct / not entirely correct
5. **Description of contrast:** *Kurang benar* is a softened negation using *kurang* ("less, insufficient") rather than *tidak*, indicating that something is not fully correct without constituting a direct denial. The context documents this as a politeness strategy common in conversational and journalistic registers, functionally distinct from the categorical negation *tidak benar*.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** *asli*
2. **Term B:** *palsu*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** genuine / fake
5. **Description of contrast:** *Asli* indicates that something is the original and not a copy, forgery, or imitation; *palsu* indicates that something is not genuine and typically involves deliberate deception. The context documents the fixed binary interrogative *asli atau palsu?* ("genuine or fake?") and notes that *palsu* carries a stronger connotation of deliberate deception than *salah*. The pair is attested across commercial, legal, and journalistic registers.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** *asli*
2. **Term B:** *tiruan*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** genuine / imitation
5. **Description of contrast:** *Asli* indicates the original; *tiruan* indicates a replica or imitation that does not necessarily involve deceptive intent. The context explicitly documents the three-way contrast *asli* / *tiruan* / *palsu* and states that *tiruan* is an imitation that may be openly labeled as such, unlike *palsu* which implies fraud. The pair *barang asli* vs. *barang tiruan* is attested in commercial and legal discourse.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** *bohong*
2. **Term B:** *dusta*
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** lie (everyday) / lie (formal/religious)
5. **Description of contrast:** Both terms mean "lie" or "falsehood," but the context explicitly documents them as register-differentiated near-synonyms: *bohong* is the everyday term, while *dusta* is more formal, literary, and common in religious (especially Islamic) discourse. The context notes that the multi-word constructions available to *dusta* are a subset of those available to *bohong*, and that constructions common with *bohong* (e.g., *ketahuan bohong*, *jangan bohong*) do not have equally common *dusta* equivalents.

## Opposition 32

1. **Term A:** *pembohong* / *pemalsu* / *pendusta* / *peniru*
2. **Term B:** *orang yang jujur* (periphrastic)
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** liar / forger / (formal) liar / imitator — vs. — an honest person
5. **Description of contrast:** The context explicitly documents an asymmetry in agentive nominalization: the *pe(N)-* prefix productively forms agentive nominals for negative roles in the truth domain (*pembohong*, *pemalsu*, *pendusta*, *peniru*), but the corresponding positive agentive nominal for honesty (*\*penjujur*) is unattested. The positive role is expressed periphrastically as *orang yang jujur*. The context states: "agentive nominals are established for negative roles but not positive ones in this domain."
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

1. **Term A:** *ke-...-an* nominalization (abstract quality)
2. **Term B:** *pe(N)-...-an* nominalization (process/event)
3. **Transliteration:** N/A
4. **English translation:** (abstract quality) / (act or process)
5. **Description of contrast:** The context explicitly documents these two circumfixes as producing semantically distinct nominals from the same roots: *ke-...-an* produces abstract quality nouns (*kebenaran* "truth as quality", *kesalahan* "error as quality"), while *pe(N)-...-an* produces process or event nouns (*pembenaran* "act of justifying", *pemalsuan* "act of forging"). The context states these are "not interchangeable" and provides the contrast *kebenaran* vs. *pembenaran* as an example.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
