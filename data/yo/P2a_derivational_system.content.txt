## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS IDENTIFIED

### BASE: **òtítọ́** (truth, truthfulness)

#### 1. olótítọ́
**Derived Form:** olótítọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** oní-òtítọ́ (possessor-truth)  
**Translation:** truthful person, honest person, one who possesses truth  
**Usage:** Used to characterize persons who habitually speak truthfully or who are known for their honesty. Common in character assessment and moral discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. aláìlótítọ́
**Derived Form:** aláìlótítọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** oní-àì-ní-òtítọ́ (possessor-NEG-have-truth)  
**Translation:** untruthful person, dishonest person  
**Usage:** Used to characterize persons who lack truthfulness or who are habitually dishonest. Common in negative moral evaluation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. ní òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** ní òtítọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ní (have/with) + òtítọ́ (truth)  
**Translation:** in truth, truthfully, honestly  
**Usage:** Adverbial expression used to emphasize that something is being said truthfully or to introduce a truthful statement. Very common in discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. pẹ̀lú òtítọ́
**Derived Form:** pẹ̀lú òtítọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** pẹ̀lú (with) + òtítọ́ (truth)  
**Translation:** with truth, truthfully, honestly  
**Usage:** Prepositional phrase indicating that something is done or said with truthfulness. Common in formal and emphatic speech.  
**[CORE]**

### BASE: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

#### 1. oníròrọ́
**Derived Form:** oníròrọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** oní-irọ́-irọ́ (possessor-lie-lie, reduplicated)  
**Translation:** habitual liar, chronic liar  
**Usage:** Used to characterize persons who lie frequently or habitually. The reduplication emphasizes the persistent nature of the lying. Common in strong moral condemnation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. ẹlẹ́rọ́
**Derived Form:** ẹlẹ́rọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ẹni-tí-ó-ní-irọ́ (person-REL-3SG-have-lie) → oní-irọ́  
**Translation:** liar, one who has lies  
**Usage:** Standard term for a person who tells lies. Less emphatic than oníròrọ́ but still carries negative moral judgment. Very common in everyday discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. ní irọ́
**Derived Form:** ní irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ní (have/with) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** falsely, with falsehood, in a lying manner  
**Usage:** Adverbial expression indicating that something is done or said falsely. Common in describing deceptive actions or speech.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. pẹ̀lú irọ́
**Derived Form:** pẹ̀lú irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** pẹ̀lú (with) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** with lies, with falsehood  
**Usage:** Prepositional phrase indicating accompaniment by falsehood. Used to characterize speech or actions as involving deception.  
**[CORE]**

## GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

### Productive Patterns Identified:

1. **Possessive/Agentive with oní- (→ olí-, ẹlẹ́-):**
   - Very productive for creating agent nouns from nominal bases
   - Examples: olótítọ́, ẹlẹ́rọ́, olódodo, ẹlẹ́tàn, ẹlẹ́rìí
   - Consistently marks persons characterized by possession of the quality

2. **Negative Possessive with oní-àì-:**
   - Productive for creating negative characterizations
   - Examples: aláìlótítọ́, aláìgbàgbọ́, aláìníwà, aláìṣe òdodo
   - Marks persons characterized by lack of the quality

3. **Agentive with a-:**
   - Productive for verbal bases
   - Examples: apurọ́, agbàgbọ́, aṣe, aṣòótọ́, abàjẹ́
   - Creates agent nouns from verbs

4. **Nominalizer ì-:**
   - Very productive for creating abstract nouns from verbs
   - Examples: ìgbàgbọ́, ìpurọ́, ìdájú, ìṣe, ìtọ́, ìjọra, ìbámu
   - Creates state/result nominals

5. **Gerundive (reduplicating initial CV):**
   - Productive for emphasizing process/action
   - Examples: gbígbàgbọ́, pípa irọ́, ṣíṣe, jíjẹ́, yíyẹ, títàn, bíbàjẹ́, jíjẹ́rìí
   - Emphasizes ongoing or repeated action

6. **Compound Nominals:**
   - Productive for complex concepts
   - Examples: ìtọ́sọ́nà, ìtọ́ka, ìtọ́kasí, ìdánilójú, àrídájú
   - Often involve multiple morphemes in fixed combinations

### Gaps and Asymmetries:

- Not all nominal bases take oní- derivation (e.g., pàtàkì, gidi)
- Some bases show only nominal derivation (ì-) without agentive (a-)
- Ideophonic forms (jẹ́jẹ́) resist further derivation
- Already complex derived forms (ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀) do not undergo further derivation
- Some verbal bases show rich derivational possibilities (ṣe, gbàgbọ́) while others are more limited

### Notes on Productivity:

The most productive patterns involve:
1. Agent/possessor marking (oní-/a-)
2. Abstract nominalization (ì-)
3. Gerundive formation (CV reduplication)

These patterns apply consistently across the truth-evaluation domain, though individual lexical items may show idiosyncratic gaps or specialized forms.
