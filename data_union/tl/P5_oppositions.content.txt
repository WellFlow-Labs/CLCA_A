# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

The oppositions below are extracted exclusively from patterns, contrasts, and explicit statements documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is introduced from external sources. Where the data documents an asymmetry (e.g., one term has a fuller paradigm than its contrast), this is reported without normalization. Where the data does not support a clear opposition, this is stated explicitly.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** hindi totoo
**Transliteration:** totoo / hindi totoo
**English translation:** true/real / not true/not real, false
**Description of contrast:** *Hindi totoo* is the standard morphosyntactic negation of *totoo*, formed by the general negation particle *hindi* preceding the adjectival predicate. The contrast is between asserting and denying the factual truth or reality of a proposition or state of affairs, as in *Hindi totoo ang balita* ("The news is not true"). *Hindi totoo* is described in P2b as the standard formula for denying the truth of a proposition or the reality of a situation.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** totoo / kasinungalingan
**English translation:** true/real / lie, falsehood, untruth
**Description of contrast:** Documented explicitly in P1b A-28 and P2b A-17 as a conventionalized oppositional framing (*katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan* — "truth versus falsehood"). *Totoo* and its nominal *katotohanan* assert factual truth; *kasinungalingan* refers to a deliberate false statement. P1 states that *kasinungalingan* "contrasts directly with *katotohanan* (truth)"; by extension, *totoo* (the root of *katotohanan*) and *kasinungalingan* occupy opposite poles of the truth/falsehood axis. The contrast is between truth and intentional untruth.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** katotohanan
**Term B:** kasinungalingan
**Transliteration:** katotohanan / kasinungalingan
**English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood, untruth
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *kasinungalingan* "contrasts directly with *katotohanan*." Documented in P1 item 2 as the primary nominal contrast for *katotohanan*, and in P1b A-28 as a near-formulaic oppositional pair. Both are abstract nouns formed by the *ka-…-an* circumfix; *katotohanan* derives from *totoo* (true/real) and *kasinungalingan* derives from *sinungaling* (liar). The contrast is between truth as an abstract quality and falsehood as an abstract quality.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** totoo
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** totoo / peke
**English translation:** real / fake
**Description of contrast:** These two terms are used in direct contrast within the same construction, as attested in *Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala* ("The product looked real but turned out to be fake"). *Totoo* asserts factual reality or genuineness; *peke* asserts deliberate inauthenticity of an object or performance.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** tama
**Term B:** mali
**Transliteration:** tama / mali
**English translation:** correct/right / wrong, incorrect, mistaken
**Description of contrast:** P1 states that *mali* "is the primary antonym of *tama*." Documented in P1 items 5 and 6 as the primary binary contrast for correctness evaluation, and in P1 item 8 as the conventionalized paired expression *tama o mali* ("right or wrong"). Both are root-level adjectives used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments across factual, moral, and procedural domains; *tama* asserts correctness and *mali* asserts incorrectness or wrongness, as in *Tama ang sagot mo* ("Your answer is correct") and *Mali ang datos* ("The data is wrong").
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Term B:** mali ka

**Description of contrast:** These are the standard minimal predicate constructions for affirming and denying a person's correctness respectively, documented in P2b as the most frequent utterances for agreement and disagreement in Tagalog conversation.

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** huwad
**Transliteration:** tunay / huwad
**English translation:** genuine, real, authentic / fake, counterfeit, false
**Description of contrast:** P1 states that *tunay* "contrasts with *huwad* (fake)." Documented across P1, P1b, and P3 (Group 3) as the primary contrast between authenticity and counterfeiting in formal and legal registers. *Tunay* evaluates something as authentically what it is; *huwad* denotes something that falsely presents itself as genuine. Both are used in legal and evaluative contexts, though *huwad* is described as more formal or literary. The P1 data contrasts *orihinal* and *awtentiko* with *huwad* and *peke*, and the same contrast applies to *tunay* as the native authenticity term.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** tunay
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** tunay / peke
**English translation:** genuine, real, authentic / fake, counterfeit, phony
**Description of contrast:** P1 states that *tunay* "contrasts with… *peke* (counterfeit)." Documented in P1 items 3 and 10 and in P3 Group 3 as a contrast between authenticity and inauthenticity, with *peke* occupying a more colloquial register than *huwad*. Both *huwad* and *peke* contrast with *tunay*, but they are not synonymous with each other in register. The contrast between *tunay* and *peke* is the everyday colloquial version of the *tunay*/*huwad* opposition. P1 notes *peke* is "more informal" than *huwad*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** huwad
**Term B:** peke
**Transliteration:** huwad / peke
**English translation:** counterfeit/forged, fake (formal/literary) / fake/phony (colloquial)
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1 items 9 and 10 and in P3 Group 3 as two items covering the same semantic territory (inauthenticity, counterfeiting) but differing in register rather than in core meaning. P1 describes *huwad* as "somewhat more formal or literary than *peke* in contemporary usage." P3 explicitly notes: "*huwad* is formal/literary, *peke* is colloquial." *Huwad* is used in legal and formal contexts; *peke* is colloquial and extremely common in everyday speech. The P1 data consistently distinguishes them by register and domain: *huwad* is more formal and legal, applied to documents and currency; *peke* is colloquial and extends more readily to emotional performances and persons. They occupy overlapping rather than identical semantic space; this entry documents a register and domain distinction between near-synonyms rather than a truth-domain opposition.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** may katotohanan
**Term B:** walang katotohanan
**Transliteration:** may katotohanan / walang katotohanan
**English translation:** there is truth in it, partly true / without truth, having no truth, baseless
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b A-21 and A-20, P2b A-12 and A-11, and P3 Group 1 as a constructional contrast using the existential *may* (there is) versus the existential negation *wala* + *-ng* (there is none). P2b documents both constructions as fixed evaluative formulas: *may katotohanan* is used "to concede partial truth in a claim" and *walang katotohanan* is used "to characterize a claim, rumor, or statement as entirely without factual basis," as in *Walang katotohanan ang alegasyon* ("The allegation has no truth to it"). The contrast is between acknowledging and denying the presence of truth in a claim; this is a constructional opposition at the level of the existential frame rather than a lexical opposition between two root forms.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** may katunayan
**Term B:** walang katunayan
**Transliteration:** may katunayan / walang katunayan
**English translation:** with proof, supported by evidence / without proof, unproven, lacking evidence
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b B-21 and B-20, P2b B-07, and P3 Group 2 as a constructional contrast parallel to *may katotohanan* / *walang katotohanan* but operating in the proof-and-verification domain rather than the factual-truth domain. P2b documents both as fixed formulas in legal and argumentative discourse: *may katunayan* asserts evidentiary support and *walang katunayan* asserts the absence of evidentiary support, as in *Walang katunayan ang akusasyon* ("The accusation has no proof"). The structural contrast is the same *may*/*wala* opposition applied to *katunayan* (proof/evidence).
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

**Term A:** napatunayan
**Term B:** hindi mapatunayan
**Transliteration:** napatunayan / hindi mapatunayan
**English translation:** has been proven, was verified / cannot be proven, is unprovable
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b B-06 and B-05 and P2a D-B-05 and D-B-04.
