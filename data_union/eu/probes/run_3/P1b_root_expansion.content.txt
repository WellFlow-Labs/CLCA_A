# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: *egia-***

- **Root form:** *egia* (Latin script; no separate script)
- **P1 participation:** 4 items directly (*egia*, *egiazkoa*, *egiaztatua*, and the verbal base *egiaztatu* underlying *egiaztatua*); the root also underlies the derivational chain *egiazta-* in item 25 and is the etymological base of item 26 (*frogatu*) by functional parallel. Direct morphological participation: **4 items** (*egia*, *egiazkoa*, *egiaztatua*, and the adverbial/verbal stem *egiazta-*).
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary nominal and derivational root for truth as a property of propositions, states of affairs, and certified facts. Provides the base for adjectival, verbal, and nominal derivatives across epistemic, legal, and moral registers.

---

**Root 2: *itxura-***

- **Root form:** *itxura* (Latin script)
- **P1 participation:** 3 items (*itxurakoa*, *itxuragilea*, *itxuraz*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The root for appearance, semblance, and outward form as potentially distinct from inner reality. Generates derivatives marking the gap between how things seem and how they are, functioning as the primary appearance-vs.-reality axis in the domain.

---

**Root 3: *zuzen-***

- **Root form:** *zuzen* (Latin script)
- **P1 participation:** 2 items directly (*zuzena*, and *okerra* as its defined antonym structuring the same semantic field). *Zuzen* itself participates in a large derivational family. Direct morphological participation: **2 items** (*zuzena*; *okerra* defines the field by opposition).
- **Core meaning contribution:** The root for straightness, correctness, rightness, and justice. Covers epistemic correctness, moral rightness, and procedural/legal justice in a single form, making it the most semantically broad root in the domain.

---

**Note on selection:** *Gezurr-* (root of *gezurra*, *gezurrezkoa*) was considered for inclusion as a third root but yields a smaller and less structurally central derivational family than *zuzen-*. It is addressed in the expansion of *egia-* where the two roots interact. *Uste-* (root of *ustez*) is productive but its derivational family is primarily epistemic-modal rather than truth-evaluative in the strict sense; it is noted in Step 3.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: *egia-*

---

**1.1**
- **Form:** egia esan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egia* (truth) + *esan* (to say)
- **Natural English translation:** to tell the truth
- **Context of use:** The most basic and frequent collocation of *egia* in everyday speech. Used in direct assertions, moral exhortations, and legal contexts (e.g., witness testimony). Appears in both imperative (*egia esazu*, "tell the truth") and declarative constructions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
- **Form:** egia da
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egia* (truth) + *da* (is, 3sg present of *izan*)
- **Natural English translation:** it is true; that is true
- **Context of use:** The standard predicative construction for asserting the truth of a proposition. Ubiquitous in everyday speech, philosophical discourse, and formal argumentation. Functions as the baseline truth-assertion formula in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
- **Form:** egia esatea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egia* (truth) + *esan* (to say) + *-tea* (nominalization suffix, verbal noun)
- **Natural English translation:** truth-telling; the act of telling the truth
- **Context of use:** Nominalized form of *egia esan*, used in ethical and philosophical discussions about the duty or practice of truth-telling. Appears in moral philosophy texts and religious discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
- **Form:** egiazko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egia* (truth) + *-zko* (adjectival/relational suffix, indefinite form)
- **Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine (attributive, indefinite)
- **Context of use:** The indefinite (non-article-bearing) form of *egiazkoa*, used in attributive position before a noun (*egiazko informazioa*, "true/genuine information"). Common in written and formal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
- **Form:** egiazkotasuna
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egia* (truth) + *-zko* (adjectival suffix) + *-tasun* (abstract noun suffix) + *-a* (definite article)
- **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, genuineness, authenticity
- **Context of use:** Abstract nominal form denoting the property of being truthful or genuine. Used in philosophical, ethical, and literary critical discourse when discussing the quality of truthfulness as an abstract property.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.6**
- **Form:** egiaztatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egiazta-* (stem derived from *egia* via *egiazkoa*) + *-tu* (perfective/infinitive verbal suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** to verify, to certify, to confirm, to attest
- **Context of use:** The primary verb for the act of verification or certification. Used in legal, administrative, scientific, and journalistic contexts. Appears in constructions such as *datuak egiaztatu* ("to verify the data") and *sinadura egiaztatu* ("to certify a signature").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.7**
- **Form:** egiaztatze
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *egiazta-* (verification stem) + *-tze* (process nominalization suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** verification, the process of verifying
- **Context of use:** Verbal noun denoting the process of verification. Used in administrative, legal, and technical contexts when referring to verification procedures (*egiaztatze-prozesua*, "the verification process").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.8**

- **Context of use:** Nominal form denoting the result of verification — a certificate, attestation, or confirmed finding. Common in legal and administrative documents. Slightly more formal and result-oriented than *egiaztatze*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### ROOT 2: *itxura-*

---

**2.1**
- **Form:** itxura
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *itxura* (appearance, semblance, form; base noun)
- **Natural English translation:** appearance, look, semblance, form
- **Context of use:** The base nominal form, used in everyday speech to refer to the outward appearance or look of a person or thing. Appears in constructions such as *itxura ona du* ("it/he/she looks good") and *itxura txarra* ("bad appearance/look").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.2**
- **Form:** itxuraz
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *itxura* (appearance) + *-z* (instrumental/adverbial suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** apparently, seemingly, ostensibly
- **Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (item 27). Included here for completeness of the root family. The primary adverbial hedge marking appearance vs. reality.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.3**
- **Form:** itxurazko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Morpheme breakdown:** *itxura* (appearance) + *-z* (instrumental) + *-ko* (adjectival suffix)
- **Natural English translation:** apparent, seeming, ostensible (attributive)
- **Context of use:** Attributive adjectival form meaning "apparent" or "seeming," used before nouns (*itxurazko adostasuna*, "apparent agreement"). Common in political and analytical discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.4**

- **Context of use:** A fixed verbal collocation meaning to pretend or feign. Used in everyday speech and literary contexts to describe deliberate performance of a false appearance. One of the most common expressions for pretense in Basque.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.5**

- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three central roots but appear in contexts where the root's contribution shifts away from its core truth-evaluation function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From *egia-*:**

- *egiazko maitasuna* ("true love") — *egiazko* applied to an emotional state rather than a proposition or object.
- *egiazki* used as an intensifier in exclamatory contexts (*egiazki harrigarria da*, "it is truly remarkable") — functions as a degree marker.
- *egiaztatua* applied to identity documents (*nortasun-agiria egiaztatua*, "certified identity document") — the verification is of identity rather than propositional truth.
- *gezurtia* applied to memory or perception (*oroimen gezurtia*, "false/unreliable memory") — the lie-root applied to a cognitive faculty rather than an agent.

---

**From *itxura-*:**

- *itxura fisikoa* — the appearance-root applied to physical description with no implication of deception or appearance-reality contrast.
- *itxura berria* in commercial/design contexts — appearance as aesthetic presentation rather than epistemic semblance.
- *itxura aldatu* in contexts of physical transformation (e.g., renovation, makeover) — change of appearance without any deceptive or epistemic implication.
- *itxuragabea* applied to shapeless or formless objects — the absence of appearance as an aesthetic or physical property rather than an epistemic one.

---

**From *zuzen-*:**

- *zuzendaria* (director, manager) — the straight/direct root applied to institutional authority and leadership.
- *zuzenbidea* (law, jurisprudence) — the correctness/rightness root applied to an entire institutional and disciplinary domain.
- *zuzenean* in broadcast contexts (*zuzenean emititu*, "to broadcast live") — the directness root applied to temporal immediacy and media transmission.
- *zuzendu* in the sense of "to address" (*gutuna zuzendu*, "to address a letter") — directness applied to spatial/communicative orientation.
- *zuzen-zuzen* as a spatial adverb (*zuzen-zuzen aurrera*, "straight ahead") — the correctness root functioning as a purely spatial directional.
- *okerreko bidea* in physical navigation ("the wrong road/path") — the wrongness root applied to spatial orientation rather than epistemic or moral error.
- *okertu* applied to physical objects (*egurra okertu da*, "the wood has warped/bent") — the wrongness root applied to physical deformation.
