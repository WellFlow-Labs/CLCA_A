# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-n  
**P1 items:** 5 items (אמת emet, אמיתי amiti, אמין amin, נאמן ne'eman, מהימן meheyman)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Firmness, stability, reliability, trustworthiness. This root conveys the notion of something being established, dependable, and worthy of trust. It encompasses both factual truth (correspondence to reality) and relational trust (reliability of persons or sources).

### Root 2: צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q)
**Transliteration:** ts-d-q  
**P1 items:** 2 items (צודק tsodek, מוצדק mutsдak)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, correctness, justice. This root combines moral righteousness with factual correctness, indicating that something is as it should be, both ethically and factually.

### Root 3: ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r)
**Transliteration:** sh-q-r  
**P1 items:** 1 item (שקר sheker)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Falsehood, deception, lying. The primary root for expressing untruth and deliberate deception in Hebrew.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

1. **אמונה**
   - Transliteration: emuna
   - Literal breakdown: ʾ-m-n + feminine noun pattern
   - Translation: faith, belief, trust
   - Context: Used in religious contexts for faith in God, and in secular contexts for trust or confidence in persons, institutions, or ideas. Central to theological discourse but also common in everyday expressions of trust.
   - [CORE]

2. **להאמין**
   - Transliteration: leha'amin
   - Literal breakdown: causative verb form of ʾ-m-n
   - Translation: to believe, to trust, to have faith
   - Context: The standard verb for believing something to be true or trusting someone. Used across all registers from casual conversation to formal discourse.
   - [CORE]

3. **מאמין**
   - Transliteration: ma'amin
   - Literal breakdown: active participle of ha'amin
   - Translation: believer, one who believes
   - Context: Describes someone who believes or has faith, particularly in religious contexts but also used for anyone who accepts a claim or ideology.
   - [CORE]

4. **אמינות**
   - Transliteration: aminut
   - Literal breakdown: ʾ-m-n + abstract noun suffix -ut
   - Translation: reliability, credibility, trustworthiness
   - Context: Abstract noun used in formal and technical contexts to discuss the reliability of sources, data, measurements, or persons. Common in academic and professional discourse.
   - [CORE]

5. **אמנם**
   - Transliteration: omnam
   - Literal breakdown: ʾ-m-n + adverbial suffix
   - Translation: indeed, it is true that, although
   - Context: Discourse marker used to acknowledge a truth or concede a point, often before introducing a contrasting statement. Common in argumentative and expository writing.
   - [CORE]

6. **אמן**
   - Transliteration: amen
   - Literal breakdown: ʾ-m-n (fixed liturgical form)
   - Translation: amen, so be it, truly
   - Context: Liturgical response affirming the truth or desirability of what has been said. Used in religious services and adopted into secular contexts for emphatic agreement.
   - [CORE]

7. **נאמנות**
   - Transliteration: ne'emanut
   - Literal breakdown: ne'eman + abstract noun suffix -ut
   - Translation: faithfulness, loyalty, fidelity
   - Context: Describes the quality of being faithful or loyal, used in personal relationships, professional ethics, and discussions of textual or historical accuracy.
   - [CORE]

8. **אמת לאמיתה**
   - Transliteration: emet la'amita
   - Literal breakdown: truth + to-its-truth (emphatic construction)
   - Translation: the absolute truth, the real truth
   - Context: Emphatic expression used to stress that something is genuinely true, often after preliminary or partial statements. Common in conversational Hebrew.
   - [CORE]

9. **באמונה**
   - Transliteration: be'emuna
   - Literal breakdown: in/with + faith/trust
   - Translation: faithfully, in good faith, trustingly
   - Context: Adverbial expression indicating that something is done with trust, sincerity, or good faith. Used in legal contexts and descriptions of sincere conduct.
   - [CORE]

10. **מהימנות**
    - Transliteration: meheymanut
    - Literal breakdown: meheyman + abstract noun suffix -ut
    - Translation: reliability, credibility, authenticity
    - Context: Formal term for the quality of being reliable or credible, particularly of sources, documents, or testimony. Common in academic and legal writing.
    - [CORE]

11. **אמת ויציב**
    - Transliteration: emet veyatsiv
    - Literal breakdown: truth + and-firm/stable
    - Translation: true and established, absolutely true
    - Context: Fixed liturgical phrase from Jewish prayer, used to affirm absolute truth. Sometimes used in secular contexts for emphatic affirmation.
    - [CORE]

12. **אמיתות**
    - Transliteration: amitiyut
    - Literal breakdown: amiti + abstract noun suffix -ut
    - Translation: authenticity, genuineness, truthfulness
    - Context: Abstract noun describing the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in discussions of art, identity, and factual accuracy.
    - [CORE]

13. **לא להאמין**
    - Transliteration: lo leha'amin
    - Literal breakdown: not + to-believe
    - Translation: unbelievable, incredible
    - Context: Common expression of disbelief or amazement, used when something seems too extraordinary to be true (can be positive or negative).
    - [CORE]

14. **מעולם לא האמנתי**
    - Transliteration: me'olam lo he'emanti
    - Literal breakdown: from-world not + I-believed
    - Translation: I never believed
    - Context: Expression used to state that one has never believed something, emphasizing long-standing disbelief or skepticism.
    - [CORE]

15. **אמונת חכמים**
    - Transliteration: emunat ḥakhamim
    - Literal breakdown: faith-of + sages/scholars
    - Translation: trust in the sages, scholarly authority

    - [MARGINAL]

    - Context: Legal and ethical expression indicating sincere, honest intention without deception. Common in contracts and ethical discourse.
    - [CORE]

17. **אמת מוחלטת**
    - Transliteration: emet muḥletet
    - Literal breakdown: truth + absolute
    - Translation: absolute truth
    - Context: Philosophical and everyday term for truth that is unconditional and universal. Used in epistemological discussions and emphatic assertions.
    - [CORE]

18. **אמת היסטורית**
    - Transliteration: emet historit
    - Literal breakdown: truth + historical
    - Translation: historical truth
    - Context: Academic term for factual accuracy regarding past events. Used in historiography and discussions of historical accuracy versus interpretation.
    - [CORE]

19. **אמת מדעית**
    - Transliteration: emet mada'it

    - Translation: scientific truth
    - Context: Term for empirically verified or scientifically established facts. Used in discussions of scientific knowledge and methodology.

    - Transliteration: emet nitsḥit

    - Transliteration: emet subiyektivit
    - Literal breakdown: truth + subjective
    - Translation: subjective truth
    - Context: Philosophical term for truth relative to individual perspective or experience. Used in epistemological and psychological discussions.
    - [CORE]

    - Transliteration: leha'id edut emet
    - Literal breakdown: to-testify + testimony-of + truth
    - Translation: to give true testimony, to testify truthfully
    - Context: Legal expression for providing truthful testimony in court or formal proceedings. Part of oath formulas.

24. **נאמן לעצמו**

    - Transliteration: ne'eman lidvarav
    - Literal breakdown: faithful + to-his-words

    - Transliteration: emuna temima

### ROOT 2: צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q)

1. **צדק**
   - Transliteration: tsedek
   - Literal breakdown: ts-d-q (noun form)
   - Translation: justice, righteousness, rightness
   - Context: Central term in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. Denotes both moral righteousness and procedural justice. Used in discussions of ethics, law, and social justice.
   - [CORE]

2. **צדיק**
   - Transliteration: tsadik
   - Literal breakdown: ts-d-q + agent noun pattern
   - Translation: righteous person, just person
   - Context: Describes someone who is morally upright and just. In religious contexts, refers to especially righteous individuals. Used in moral evaluation of character.
   - [CORE]

3. **צדקה**
   - Transliteration: tsedaka
   - Literal breakdown: ts-d-q + feminine noun pattern
   - Translation: charity, righteousness, justice
   - Context: Primarily means charitable giving in modern Hebrew, but retains connotations of justice and righteousness. Central to Jewish ethical practice.
   - [CORE]

4. **להצדיק**
   - Transliteration: lehatsדik
   - Literal breakdown: causative verb form of ts-d-q
   - Translation: to justify, to vindicate, to declare right
   - Context: Verb meaning to provide justification for actions or claims, or to declare someone right. Used in legal, moral, and argumentative contexts.
   - [CORE]

5. **הצדקה**
   - Transliteration: hatsдaka
   - Literal breakdown: causative noun form of ts-d-q
   - Translation: justification, vindication

6. **צודק בטענתו**

7. **צדקת הדין**

8. **צדק חברתי**

   - [CORE]

9. **צדק מוחלט**

## Step 3 — Note Peripheral Extensions

### Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **אמן** (oman) - "craftsman, artist" - This form shares the consonantal root but derives from a different semantic development, relating to skill and mastery rather than truth or trust. The connection is through the notion of reliability/expertise in craft.

2. **אימון** (imun) - "training, practice" - Modern Hebrew term for training or practice, derived from the causative form. The semantic extension moves from "making firm/reliable" to "developing skill through repetition."

3. **אמנות** (omanut) - "art" - Abstract noun from oman (craftsman), referring to artistic practice. The root's contribution here relates to skilled craft rather than truth-evaluation.

4. **אמונה תמימה** - When used in contexts of religious faith, the root extends beyond epistemic evaluation into devotional and relational domains, where "truth" becomes less about factual correspondence and more about commitment and trust.

5. **נאמן** in phrases like **נאמן לסגנון** (ne'eman lesignon) - "faithful to a style" - extends the root into aesthetic consistency rather than truth per se.

### Root צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **צדקה** (tsedaka) - While maintaining connection to justice/righteousness, in modern usage this primarily means "charity" - a shift from abstract justice to concrete charitable action.

2. **צדק** in botanical/zoological contexts - The root appears in some technical nomenclature where it may indicate "proper" or "typical" specimens rather than moral or factual correctness.

3. **צודק** in colloquial usage can function as a simple agreement marker ("you're right") with weakened moral or justice connotations, becoming more about conversational acknowledgment.

### Root ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r)

**Peripheral extensions noted:**

1. **שקר** in idiomatic expressions like **לא שקר** (lo sheker) - "not bad, pretty good" - where the negation of falsehood becomes an understated positive evaluation, moving away from truth-evaluation into quality assessment.

2. **שקרי** in aesthetic contexts - Can describe artificial or imitation materials (e.g., **עור שקרי** "fake leather") where the focus is on authenticity of materials rather than propositional truth.

3. The root in compound forms describing deceptive appearances in nature or technology, where "false" indicates mimicry or simulation rather than intentional deception.
