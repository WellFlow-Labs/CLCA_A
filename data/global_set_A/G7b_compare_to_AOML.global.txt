## 3. Summary

### Confirmed Patterns

**1. Dispositional Causation Block (STRONGEST CONFIRMATION)**

**Empirical**: D3 (Dispositional Honesty) × C5 (Causative) = BLOCKED across all 8 languages. No productive constructions for causing dispositional character change ("make someone honest/dishonest").

**AOML**: Rule R1 (Dispositional Causation Block): CAUSE × DISPOSITIONAL → universally blocked. Claims "16/16 languages, p < 1e-9."

**Alignment**: **STRONG MATCH**. Empirical data from 8 languages directly confirms AOML's strongest empirical claim. This is the **most robust empirical-theoretical alignment** in the entire comparison.

**2. Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry**

**Empirical**: D2 (Material Authenticity) × C5 (Causative) shows polarity asymmetry: forgery/falsification allowed (negative causation), manufacturing authenticity blocked (positive causation).

**AOML**: Rule R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry): CAUSE(+) × AUTH → blocked; CAUSE(−) × AUTH → licensed.

**Alignment**: **STRONG MATCH**. Empirical data confirms AOML's prediction that authenticity cannot be externally created but can be destroyed or forged. Supports AOML's ontological target-type class distinction.

**3. Revelation Construction Universality**

**Empirical**: D11 (Truth Visibility) × C6 (Revelation) = ALLOWED across all 8 languages, universally rated [CORE] in F6 tables.

**AOML**: REVEAL provisional operator noted as "emerged unprompted across 4+ language families" with "15/16 languages show preference for revelatory constructions."

**Alignment**: **STRONG MATCH**. Empirical data from 8/8 languages strongly supports AOML's provisional REVEAL operator. AOML's description ("disclosure of latent truth," "presupposes pre-existing state") matches empirical characterization ("emerging from hiddenness").

**Recommendation**: Empirical data supports promoting REVEAL from provisional to confirmed operator status.

**4. Abstract Nominalization Universality**

**Empirical**: All dimensions × C8 (Abstract Nominalization) = ALLOWED across all 8 languages with high productivity.

**AOML**: ABSTR operator = universally_licensed (with exceptions for LIFE and marginal for ESS).

**Alignment**: **MATCH**. Empirical data confirms universal productivity of abstract nominalization in truth-domain.

**Note**: AOML's exception for LIFE cannot be tested (LIFE not identified as empirical dimension). ESS exception also cannot be tested.

**5. Core Axis Structure**

**Empirical**: Four dimensions clearly identified (D1, D2, D3, D8) corresponding to four AOML axes (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM).

**AOML**: Seven primary axes predicted.

**Alignment**: **PARTIAL MATCH**. Four of seven AOML axes have clear empirical counterparts. Three axes (ESS, LIFE, VERIF as axis) not clearly identified as separate dimensions in empirical data.

### Partial or Conditional Matches

**1. Inchoative Restriction Strength**

**Empirical**: D1 × C4 shows strong blocking (6/8 languages rate [MARGINAL]) with systematic revelation-verb substitution.

**AOML**: BECOME × VER = restricted (polarity-conditioned).

**Partial match**: Both recognize restriction, but empirical data suggests **stronger blocking** than AOML's "restricted" category implies. AOML's "restricted" suggests conditional licensing; empirical shows near-universal blocking with obligatory substitution.

**Refinement needed**: AOML's "restricted" category may need subdivision:
- **Conditionally licensed** (allowed under specific conditions)
- **Strongly restricted** (blocked in most contexts, substitution required)
- **Universally blocked** (no licensing conditions)

Empirical D1 × C4 appears to be "strongly restricted" rather than "conditionally licensed."

**2. Procedural Conformity Classification**

**Issue**: Empirical D4 (Procedural Correctness) shows unrestricted inchoative and causative licensing, conflicting with AOML's prediction that BECOME × NORM and CAUSE × NORM are restricted.

**Possible resolution**: Empirical D4 may not map to AOML NORM but to:
- **VER's "correctness" subregion** (Layer 2)
- **NORM's institutional/procedural subtype** (which AOML notes is licensed for causation)
- **Separate dimension** not modeled in AOML

**Alignment**: **CONDITIONAL MATCH** — depends on whether empirical D4 is classified as NORM or VER subregion.

**Recommendation**: AOML should clarify whether procedural/technical correctness belongs to VER or NORM.

**3. Verification Process vs. Verification Axis**

**Issue**: Empirical D10 (Evidential Establishment) focuses on verification actions (prove, confirm, authenticate). AOML VERIF is described as both "process of proving" and distinct axis.

**Empirical**: D10 × C5 (verification causatives) = ALLOWED (8/8 languages, universally productive).

**AOML**: CAUSE × VERIF = restricted.

**Partial match**: Both recognize verification as distinct from simple truth, but empirical causatives are **agentive actions** (perform verification) not **stative causation** (cause verification state). AOML's restriction may apply to stative causation only.

**Refinement needed**: AOML could distinguish:
- **Agentive causatives**: Perform action in domain (verify, prove) — empirically productive
- **Stative causatives**: Cause state change (make verified) — may be restricted

**4. Sincerity Scope**

**Issue**: Empirical D3 (Dispositional Honesty) is broader than AOML SINC (inner alignment).

**Empirical**: D3 includes habitual truthfulness, general integrity, character evaluation.

**AOML**: SINC focuses on "inner alignment between stated and actual belief or intention."

**Partial match**: Overlap exists but empirical dimension encompasses:
- AOML SINC (inner alignment)
- AOML SINC subregions (reliability, faithfulness in Layer 2)
- Aspects of habitual factual accuracy (may involve VER)

**Refinement needed**: AOML could clarify whether habitual truthfulness belongs to SINC or represents SINC + VER interaction.

**5. Positive Emergence Bias Scope**

**Empirical**: Inchoative polarity asymmetry exists but is less pronounced than AOML claims. Indonesian and Turkish freely allow both positive and negative inchoatives.

**AOML**: Rule R4 claims "14/16 languages" show positive emergence bias.

**Partial match**: Bias exists in some languages (Korean, Georgian, Basque show preference for positive emergence) but clear exceptions exist (Indonesian, Turkish show no bias).

**Refinement needed**: AOML Rule R4 should be clarified as:
- **Statistical tendency** (most languages show bias)
- **Not universal** (clear exceptions exist)
- **Strength varies** by language and construction type

### Conflicts

**1. Procedural Correctness Causation**

**Empirical**: D4 × C5 = ALLOWED (8/8 languages freely allow "make correct," "correct errors").

**AOML**: CAUSE × NORM = restricted.

**Conflict**: If empirical D4 maps to AOML NORM, this is direct conflict.

**Resolution options**:
- **Option A**: Empirical D4 is VER subregion (correctness), not NORM
- **Option B**: AOML NORM restriction applies only to moral normativity, not procedural correctness
- **Option C**: AOML restriction is too strong; should be "variable"

**Recommendation**: Clarify NORM scope in AOML framework.

**2. Procedural Correctness Inchoatives**

**Empirical**: D4 × C4 = ALLOWED (8/8 languages freely allow "become correct," "become accurate").

**AOML**: BECOME × NORM = restricted.

**Conflict**: Same as above. If D4 = NORM, this conflicts with AOML.

**Resolution**: Same options as above.

**3. Degree Modification Category Restrictions**

**Empirical**: Core truth nominals resist degree modification (Korean *매우 진실 ungrammatical) while adjectival forms accept it (매우 정확하다 grammatical).

**AOML**: DEG meta-axis modulates all primary axes without category restrictions.

**Conflict**: Empirical shows **category-based restriction** (nouns vs. adjectives) not predicted by AOML.

**Refinement needed**: AOML's DEG meta-axis may need syntactic category parameter:
- DEG licensing conditioned by syntactic category (nouns vs. adjectives)
- Nouns resist degree modification
- Adjectives accept degree modification

### Unmodeled Empirical Findings

**1. Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry**

**Empirical finding**: Universal blocking of "truth-teller" agentive (7/8 languages) while "liar" freely lexicalized (8/8 languages).

**AOML status**: Not modeled. No agentive nominalization operator.

**Significance**: Robust cross-linguistic universal documented in all F5 tables, confirmed in G2, G4, G6. Represents **pragmatic constraint**: marked behaviors (lying) require naming; unmarked behaviors (truth-telling) don't.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add agentive nominalization operator with polarity-conditioned licensing (negative pole licensed, positive pole blocked for dispositional axes).

**2. Concealment Constructions**

**Empirical finding**: Universal concealment vocabulary (8/8 languages) with productive constructions for hiding/covering up truth.

**AOML status**: Not modeled. AOML has REVEAL but no concealment operator.

**Significance**: Concealment is functional opposite of revelation, yet AOML only models one pole. Empirical data shows both poles equally productive.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add CONCEAL operator as complement to REVEAL, or model concealment as REVEAL(−) (negative polarity).

**3. Manner Adverbialization**

**Empirical finding**: Universal productivity of manner adverbialization (8/8 languages) creating adverbs from truth-domain adjectives (truly, honestly, accurately).

**AOML status**: Not modeled. No manner adverbialization operator.

**Significance**: Universal productive pattern across all dimensions, not captured by AOML's operator inventory.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add manner adverbialization operator or treat it as syntactic realization of ATTR.

**4. Light Verb Speech Act Constructions**

**Empirical finding**: Universal pattern of truth-term + "say/speak" constructions (8/8 languages, all [CORE]) rather than dedicated monomorphemic verbs.

**AOML status**: Not modeled. Doesn't predict or explain why truth-telling is expressed through light verb constructions.

**Significance**: Universal constructional pattern suggesting truth-telling conceptualized as object-transfer (give truth through speech) rather than simple action.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add light verb construction pattern or explain through ATTR + speech act domain interaction.

**5. Practice vs. Theory Dimension**

**Empirical finding**: D19 (Practice vs. Theory) identified in Korean with extensive lexicalization (실- root family), present but less elaborated in other languages.

**AOML status**: Not modeled. No practice/theory distinction.

**Significance**: Empirical dimension not captured by AOML's seven primary axes. Important for policy evaluation and practical reasoning.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add as:
- Eighth primary axis
- Subregion of VER (factual reality vs. theoretical claims)
- Meta-axis (actual vs. potential)

**6. Revelation-Inchoative Substitution Pattern**

**Empirical finding**: Languages blocking simple inchoatives (D1 × C4) systematically substitute revelation constructions (D11 × C6). This is obligatory functional substitution.

**AOML status**: AOML has REVEAL and BECOME restrictions but doesn't predict systematic substitution.

**Significance**: Explains why revelation vocabulary is universally productive even in languages blocking inchoatives. Represents **constructional substitution rule** not captured by AOML.

**Recommendation**: AOML could add **Substitution Rule S1**: When BECOME × VER is blocked for abstract truth terms, languages obligatorily substitute REVEAL constructions.

### Unattested AOML Structures

**1. ESS (ESSENCE) as Separate Primary Axis**

**AOML prediction**: ESS is one of seven primary axes, representing "inherent nature, fundamental properties."

**Empirical status**: Not clearly identified as separate dimension in G3 analysis.

**Evidence gap**: F3 semantic grouping tables don't identify "essence" as separate group in most languages. Where essence terms appear (Turkish hakikat, Korean 본질), they're grouped with philosophical/elevated truth (register variation) rather than distinct semantic dimension.

**Interpretation**: AOML's ESS may be:
- **Theoretically motivated** but not empirically grounded in cross-linguistic lexical distinctions
- **Subsumed** under empirical D1 (factual truth) or D2 (genuineness)
- **Present but not lexicalized** as separate dimension

**Recommendation**: Requires targeted investigation: Do languages systematically distinguish essential properties from factual properties in truth-domain vocabulary and constructions?

**2. LIFE (LIFELIKENESS) as Separate Primary Axis**

**AOML prediction**: LIFE is one of seven primary axes, representing "representational fidelity, verisimilitude."

**Empirical status**: Not identified as separate dimension in G3 analysis.

**Evidence gap**: F3 tables don't identify "lifelikeness" or "verisimilitude" as separate group. Realism terms (Korean 사실적, Turkish gerçekçi) grouped with factual accuracy rather than as distinct dimension.

**AOML's key diagnostic**: "LIFE blocks REVEAL — constructed things cannot be revealed, only discovered."

**Empirical test**: Cannot be performed. F6 naturalness tables don't systematically test revelation constructions with realism/fidelity terms.

**Interpretation**: AOML's LIFE may be:
- **Theoretically motivated** but not empirically grounded
- **Subsumed** under empirical D2 (authenticity) or D21 (plausibility)
- **Present in specialized domains** (art criticism) but not core truth-domain

**Recommendation**: Requires targeted investigation: Do languages systematically distinguish representational fidelity from factual truth and object authenticity? Do revelation constructions behave differently with fidelity terms?

**3. TEMP Meta-Axis**

**AOML prediction**: TEMP is provisional meta-axis representing "position, direction, and persistence of claim across time."

**AOML's own assessment**: "PROVISIONAL. Emerged as D21 in CLCA-Revision (6/8 Set A). Did not surface in CLCA-1.1 global synthesis. Cross-architectural confirmation pending."

**Empirical status**: Not identified as separate meta-axis in current G3 analysis. Temporal aspects appear in specific constructions (revelation over time, persistence) but not as cross-cutting meta-axial dimension.

**Interpretation**: AOML's provisional status is appropriate. Current empirical data doesn't clearly support TEMP as meta-axis.

**Recommendation**: Maintain provisional status pending targeted investigation.

## 4. Theoretical Discussion

### Where AOML Appears Strongly Supported

**1. Core Operator Inventory**

AOML's core operators align well with empirical constructional functions:
- **ATTR ↔ C1** (Predication): Strong match
- **CAUSE ↔ C5** (Causative): Strong match with refinements needed
- **BECOME ↔ C4** (Inchoative): Strong match with restriction strength needing clarification
- **RESULT ↔ C6** (Revelation): Strong match, supports provisional REVEAL
- **ABSTR ↔ C8** (Abstract Nominalization): Strong match
- **CONTR ↔ C2** (Negation): Partial match

**Assessment**: AOML's operator inventory captures major constructional functions. Some empirical constructions (agentive, manner adverbial, light verb, concealment) not modeled.

**2. Dispositional Causation Block (Rule R1)**

**Strongest empirical-theoretical alignment**: Empirical data from 8 languages directly confirms AOML's claim that dispositional causation is universally blocked.

**Assessment**: **Robust confirmation**. Rule R1 is AOML's "strongest single empirical finding" and empirical data fully supports it.

**3. Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry (Rule R3)**

Empirical data confirms AOML's prediction that authenticity cannot be manufactured (CAUSE+ blocked) but can be destroyed or forged (CAUSE− licensed).

**Assessment**: **Strong confirmation**. Rule R3 supported by empirical evidence. Validates AOML's ontological target-type class distinction.

**4. Revelation Operator**

AOML's provisional REVEAL operator is strongly supported by empirical data showing universal revelation constructions (8/8 languages, all [CORE]) and systematic preference for revelation over simple inchoatives.

**Assessment**: **Strong support for promoting REVEAL to confirmed operator status**. Empirical data from 8 languages confirms AOML's observation from 4+ families.

**5. Primary Axis Distinctions**

AOML's distinction between VER (factual correspondence), AUTH (object genuineness), SINC (character honesty), and NORM (moral/legal rightness) is supported by empirical identification of corresponding dimensions (D1, D2, D3, D8).

**Assessment**: **Core axis structure is empirically grounded**. Four of seven AOML primary axes have clear empirical counterparts.

### Where AOML May Require Refinement

**1. Restriction Strength Gradation**

**Issue**: AOML uses three-way "licensed/restricted/blocked" categories. Empirical data suggests need for finer gradation.

**Proposed refinement**:
- **Universally licensed** (no restrictions observed)
- **Conditionally licensed** (allowed under specific conditions)
- **Strongly restricted** (blocked in most contexts, substitution required)
- **Universally blocked** (no licensing conditions, p < 1e-9)

**Evidence**: Empirical D1 × C4 (inchoative blocking) is "strongly restricted" not merely "restricted." AOML's current "restricted" conflates conditional licensing with near-universal blocking.

**2. Agentive vs. Stative Causative Distinction**

**Issue**: AOML's CAUSE operator doesn't distinguish:
- **Agentive causatives**: Performing an action (verify, prove, justify)
- **Stative causatives**: Causing a state change (make true, make honest)

**Evidence**: Empirical shows verification causatives (agentive) universally productive while truth-creation causatives (stative) blocked. AOML predicts both as "restricted" without distinguishing.

**Proposed refinement**: Split CAUSE into:
- **CAUSE-AGENTIVE**: Perform action in domain (verify, prove, authenticate)
- **CAUSE-STATIVE**: Cause state change (make true, make honest)

Different licensing conditions apply to each subtype.

**3. Procedural vs. Moral Normativity**

**Issue**: AOML's NORM axis may conflate:
- **Procedural/technical correctness** (empirical D4)
- **Moral/legal normativity** (empirical D8)

**Evidence**: Empirical D4 shows unrestricted causation/inchoation; D8 shows restrictions. If both map to NORM, this creates conflict.

**Proposed refinement**: Distinguish:
- **NORM-PROCEDURAL**: Technical/procedural correctness (less restricted)
- **NORM-MORAL**: Moral/legal ought-claims (more restricted)

Or reclassify procedural correctness as VER subregion.

**4. Syntactic Category Constraints**

**Issue**: AOML doesn't model syntactic category restrictions (nouns vs. adjectives).

**Evidence**: Empirical shows degree modification blocked for nouns (Korean *매우 진실) but allowed for adjectives (매우 정확하다). AOML's DEG meta-axis doesn't predict this.

**Proposed refinement**: Add syntactic category parameter:
- DEG licensing conditioned by syntactic category of host term
- Nouns resist degree modification
- Adjectives accept degree modification

**5. Polarity Asymmetry Generalization**

**Issue**: AOML documents polarity asymmetries for specific axes (Rule R3 for AUTH, Rule R4 for BECOME) but doesn't generalize.

**Evidence**: Empirical shows polarity asymmetries across multiple dimensions:
- AUTH: Can destroy but not create (Rule R3)
- BECOME: Positive emergence natural, negative marked (Rule R4)
- Agentive: Negative agents lexicalized, positive blocked (unmodeled)

**Proposed refinement**: Add general principle:
- **Negative Pole Elaboration Bias**: Across truth-domain, negative poles (falsehood, fakeness, lying, error) are more readily lexicalized and morphologically elaborated than positive poles.

This would unify Rules R3, R4, and agentive asymmetry under single principle.

### Where Empirical Findings Suggest New Dimensions

**1. Practice vs. Theory (Empirical D19)**

**Empirical finding**: Korean shows extensive lexicalization of practical/actual reality (실- root family: 실제, 실질, 실효성, 실력, 실적) distinct from theoretical/nominal claims.

**AOML status**: Not modeled as separate axis.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate. Clearly lexicalized in Korean (F3 GROUP 5); present but less elaborated in other languages.

**Recommendation**: Consider adding as:
- **Eighth primary axis**: PRAC (PRACTICAL) — actual implementation vs. theoretical/nominal
- **Subregion of VER**: Practical reality as subtype of factual truth
- **Meta-axis**: ACTUAL (actual vs. potential/theoretical)

**2. Appearance vs. Reality (Empirical D10)**

**Empirical finding**: Some languages (Basque, Turkish) lexicalize appearance/seeming as distinct from reality (itxurazko vs. egiazkoa).

**AOML status**: May be captured by STRAT operator (surface vs. deep) but not as separate axis.

**Evidence strength**: Weak. Only 2/8 languages show clear lexicalization.

**Recommendation**: Clarify whether appearance/reality is:
- STRAT applied to VER/AUTH
- Separate dimension
- Constructional pattern only

### Where Empirical Findings Suggest New Constructional Constraints

**1. Revelation-Inchoative Substitution Rule**

**Empirical finding**: Languages blocking simple inchoatives (D1 × C4) systematically substitute revelation constructions (D11 × C6). Functional substitution driven by semantic incompatibility.

**AOML status**: Not explicitly modeled. Has REVEAL and BECOME restrictions but doesn't predict systematic substitution.

**Proposed addition**: **Substitution Rule S1**: When BECOME × VER is blocked for abstract truth terms, languages obligatorily substitute REVEAL constructions.

**Evidence strength**: Strong. Documented in 6/8 languages with F6 [MARGINAL] for inchoatives, [CORE] for revelation verbs.

**2. Agentive Polarity Asymmetry Rule**

**Empirical finding**: Agentive nominalization productive for negative agents (liar, deceiver) but blocked for positive agents (truth-teller) across all 8 languages.

**AOML status**: Not modeled. No agentive nominalization operator.

**Proposed addition**: **Agentive Asymmetry Rule A1**: Agentive nominalization in truth-domain is polarity-conditioned:
- Negative pole: Licensed (liar, deceiver, fraud)
- Positive pole: Blocked (truth-teller, honest person)

**Evidence strength**: Strong. Universal across all 8 languages (7/8 complete blocking, 1/8 marginal).

**3. Category-Based Degree Modification Rule**

**Empirical finding**: Degree modification blocked for core truth nominals but allowed for adjectival derivatives.

**AOML status**: Not modeled. DEG meta-axis doesn't predict category restrictions.

**Proposed addition**: **Category Restriction Rule C1**: DEG meta-axis licensing conditioned by syntactic category:
- Adjectives: DEG freely licensed
- Nouns: DEG blocked or restricted
- Verbs: DEG variable

**Evidence strength**: Moderate. Documented in Korean and Finnish; similar patterns in other languages but less systematically tested.

### Where Empirical Findings Suggest Revised Constraint Strength

**1. Inchoative Blocking with Abstract Truth**

**Current AOML**: BECOME × VER = restricted (polarity-conditioned)

**Empirical finding**: BECOME × VER (abstract truth terms) = strongly restricted or blocked (6/8 languages)

**Proposed revision**: Strengthen restriction for abstract truth terms:
- **BECOME × VER (concrete/gradable)**: Conditionally licensed
- **BECOME × VER (abstract truth)**: Strongly restricted (substitution required)

**Evidence**: F6 tables show [MARGINAL] ratings in 6/8 languages.

**2. Positive Emergence Bias Scope**

**Current AOML**: Rule R4 claims "14/16 languages" show positive emergence bias.

**Empirical finding**: Bias exists but less pronounced. Indonesian and Turkish freely allow both positive and negative inchoatives.

**Proposed revision**: Clarify Rule R4 as:
- **Statistical tendency** (most languages show bias)
- **Not universal** (clear exceptions exist)
- **Strength varies** by language and construction type

**Evidence**: F6 shows Indonesian menjadi benar [CORE], Turkish gerçekleşmek [CORE] without polarity restrictions.

**3. Normative Causation Licensing**

**Current AOML**: CAUSE × NORM = restricted

**Empirical finding**: If empirical D8 (Normative Legitimacy) maps to NORM, causatives are universally productive (justify, legitimize all [CORE]).

**Proposed revision**: Clarify that NORM causation is:
- **Blocked for direct moral causation** (make something morally right)
- **Licensed for institutional causation** (justify, legitimize as institutional actions)

AOML notes institutional licensing but doesn't make distinction explicit.

**Evidence**: All F2a tables document productive justification/legitimization verbs.

## Conclusion

### Strong Empirical Support for AOML

**Confirmed structures**:
1. **Dispositional Causation Block (Rule R1)** — Strongest confirmation (8/8 languages)
2. **Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry (Rule R3)** — Strong confirmation
3. **Revelation Construction Universality (REVEAL operator)** — Strong support for promotion to confirmed status
4. **Core operator inventory** (ATTR, CAUSE, BECOME, RESULT, ABSTR, CONTR) — Well-supported
5. **Primary axis distinctions** (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM) — Four of seven axes clearly supported

### Areas Requiring Refinement

**Refinement needed**:
1. **Restriction strength gradation** — Need finer categories beyond licensed/restricted/blocked
2. **Agentive vs. stative causative distinction** — CAUSE operator needs subdivision
3. **Procedural vs. moral normativity** — NORM axis needs clarification or subdivision
4. **Syntactic category constraints** — DEG meta-axis needs category-based licensing conditions
5. **Polarity asymmetry generalization** — Unify Rules R3, R4, and agentive asymmetry under general principle

### Unmodeled Empirical Patterns

**Major findings not captured by AOML**:
1. **Agentive nominalization asymmetry** (universal: liar lexicalized, truth-teller blocked)
2. **Concealment constructions** (universal productivity, no AOML operator)
3. **Manner adverbialization** (universal productivity, no AOML operator)
4. **Light verb speech act constructions** (universal pattern, not predicted)
5. **Revelation-inchoative substitution** (systematic constructional substitution rule)
6. **Practice vs. theory dimension** (robust in Korean, present in others)

### Unattested AOML Structures

**AOML structures not clearly supported**:
1. **ESS as separate primary axis** — May be subsumed under VER or AUTH
2. **LIFE as separate primary axis** — May be subsumed under AUTH or plausibility
3. **TEMP meta-axis** — AOML's provisional status appropriate; not identified in current data
4. **Three-way target-type ontology** — Dispositional class confirmed; factual/epistemic distinction less clear

### Overall Assessment

**AOML's core architecture is empirically well-grounded**:
- Four of seven primary axes clearly supported (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM)
- Core operators align with major constructional functions
- Strongest empirical finding (dispositional causation block) is AOML's strongest claim
- Revelation operator strongly supported

**AOML requires refinement in**:
- Restriction strength classification (need finer gradation)
- Causative operator subdivision (agentive vs. stative)
- Syntactic category constraints (nouns vs. adjectives for DEG)
- Coverage of universal empirical patterns (agentive, concealment, manner adverbial, light verb)

**AOML's theoretical commitments** (ESS, LIFE as separate axes; TEMP meta-axis) are **not clearly supported** by current empirical data but also **not clearly contradicted**. They remain theoretical hypotheses requiring targeted investigation with:
- Explicit elicitation testing essence vs. factual properties
- Testing LIFE diagnostic (do revelation constructions block with fidelity terms?)
- Broader language sample
- Cross-architectural validation

**Recommendation for AOML v2.2**:
1. Promote REVEAL to confirmed operator (strong empirical support)
2. Add agentive nominalization operator with polarity asymmetry
3. Add concealment operator (complement to REVEAL)
4. Refine restriction strength categories (4-way instead of 3-way)
5. Clarify procedural vs. moral normativity distinction
6. Add syntactic category parameter to DEG licensing
7. Maintain ESS, LIFE, TEMP as provisional pending targeted investigation
8. Add Substitution Rule S1 (revelation for inchoative)
9. Add Agentive Asymmetry Rule A1 (negative pole licensed, positive blocked)
10. Generalize polarity asymmetry across Rules R3, R4, and agentive patterns
