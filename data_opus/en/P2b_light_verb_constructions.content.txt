# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
## Language: English (en)

Since English uses Latin script natively, transliteration is identical to native form throughout. The following documents **attested multi-word constructions** (collocations, idioms, fixed phrases, periphrastic patterns) involving P1 items. Single-word derivations (P2a) and bound-morpheme forms are excluded. Constructions already listed under P1b root expansion are included here where they are genuine multi-word units.

## Summary Notes on Distribution (data-supported)

- **Richest construction fields:** *true/truth* and *right* and *real* dominate, each supplying numerous idioms, fixed binomials, discourse markers, and frames. These are the native Germanic core items of the domain.
- **Discourse-marker cluster:** several P1 items yield clause-level candor hedges — *truth be told*, *to tell the truth*, *to be honest*, *to be exact*, *correct me if I'm wrong*. These cluster around honesty/precision and are conversational.
- **Authentication idioms cluster:** *the genuine article*, *the real thing*, *the real deal*, *the real McCoy* form a near-synonymous set of fixed NPs asserting genuineness — a notable redundancy concentrated on *real/genuine*.
- **Epistolary fixed forms:** *Yours sincerely* / *Yours faithfully* form a complementary pair governed by addressee naming convention.
- **Peripheral-sense constructions:** many *true*/*right* idioms exploit non-factual senses — alignment (*out of true*, *true up*), desert (*serve someone right*), good order (*right as rain*), realization (*come true*).
- **Notable absence:** the more learned/Latinate items (*accurate, authentic, reliable, valid, legitimate, counterfeit, spurious, mimetic, veracious, verisimilitude, truthful, forged, trustworthy*) yield **no fixed multi-word constructions** — only ordinary, compositional collocations. Construction-formation in this domain is strongly skewed toward the native, high-frequency, monosyllabic core (*true, false, real, right, fake, honest*).
- **Asymmetry preserved:** *false* generates fewer and less idiomatic constructions than its antonym *true*, and several positive items (*accurate, valid, legitimate*) have no idiomatic counterpart constructions at all.
