# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## 1. אמת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet
**Literal meaning:** truth; from root א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t), related to firmness, reliability
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** One of the most fundamental terms in Hebrew, used across everyday, legal, religious, and philosophical discourse to assert that a statement or situation corresponds to reality. Appears in fixed expressions such as *le-ʾemet* ("truly") and *ʾemet ve-shalom* ("truth and peace"). Participates in extensive derivational patterns.
**[CORE]**

---

## 2. אמיתי
**Transliteration:** ʾamitʾi
**Literal meaning:** adjectival derivation from א-מ-ת; "of truth"
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** Used attributively to describe persons, objects, or states as genuinely what they claim or appear to be, as opposed to false or simulated. Common in both colloquial and formal registers, e.g., *ʾohev ʾamitʾi* ("a true/genuine friend"). Contrasts with *medumaʾ* ("imagined") or *meumat* ("fabricated").
**[CORE]**

---

## 3. נכון
**Transliteration:** nakhon
**Literal meaning:** from root כ-ו-נ (k-w-n), "established, prepared, correct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, true
**Usage:** Widely used to evaluate statements, answers, or facts as accurate or correct, particularly in everyday and educational contexts. Also carries a sense of moral or practical rightness in some collocations, e.g., *ze lo nakhon* ("that's not right/correct"). Participates in derivations such as *nekhonah* (f.), *le-nakhen* ("to correct").
**[CORE]**

---

## 4. שקר
**Transliteration:** sheker
**Literal meaning:** from root ש-ק-ר (sh-q-r), falsehood
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, untruth
**Usage:** The primary antonym of *ʾemet*, used to characterize statements, persons, or representations as false or deceptive. Appears in legal, moral, and everyday contexts, including the biblical prohibition *midvar sheker tirḥaq* ("distance yourself from falsehood"). Participates in derivations such as *shakran* ("liar") and *shaqri* ("false, spurious").
**[CORE]**

---

## 5. מזויף
**Transliteration:** mezuyaf
**Literal meaning:** passive participle from root ז-י-פ (z-y-f), "to forge, to falsify"
**Typical English translations:** forged, counterfeit, fake, falsified
**Usage:** Applied to documents, currency, signatures, and other objects or representations that have been deliberately made to appear genuine when they are not. Common in legal and administrative contexts, e.g., *teʿudat zehut mezuyefet* ("a forged identity document"). The root also yields *ziyyuf* ("forgery, falsification").
**[CORE]**

---

## 6. מקורי
**Transliteration:** meqori
**Literal meaning:** from *meqor* ("source, origin"); "of the source/origin"
**Typical English translations:** original, authentic, genuine
**Usage:** Used to describe objects, texts, or works as being the original rather than a copy or imitation, and also to describe persons or ideas as genuinely creative or distinctive. Appears in aesthetic, legal, and commercial contexts, e.g., *yetsirah meqorit* ("an original work"). Contrasts with *ha-ʿataqah* ("the copy") or *ziyyuf* ("forgery").
**[CORE]**

---

## 7. אותנטי
**Transliteration:** ʾotenti
**Literal meaning:** loanword from Greek/English *authentic*
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine
**Usage:** A borrowing used in philosophical, psychological, and cultural discourse to describe persons, experiences, or objects as genuinely what they are, without pretense or imitation. More common in educated or academic registers than in colloquial speech. Often used in discussions of personal identity or cultural heritage.
**[CORE]**

---

## 8. מהימן
**Transliteration:** meheymen
**Literal meaning:** from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n), "to trust, to be faithful"; causative/intensive pattern
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, witnesses, or information to indicate that they can be trusted or depended upon. Common in legal testimony contexts (*ʿed meheymen*, "a credible witness") and in everyday evaluation of sources. Shares a root with *ʾemet* and *ʾemunaʾh*.
**[CORE]**

---

## 9. אמונה
**Transliteration:** ʾemunaʾh
**Literal meaning:** from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n); "faithfulness, trust, belief"
**Typical English translations:** faith, trust, faithfulness, reliability
**Usage:** Denotes both the quality of being trustworthy or faithful and the act of believing or trusting. Used in religious, moral, and interpersonal contexts; *ʾemunaʾh* in a person implies they are reliable and honest. The root connects it to *neʾeman* ("faithful, reliable") and *maʾamin* ("believer").
**[CORE]**

---

## 10. נאמן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman
**Literal meaning:** from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n); passive/stative pattern, "one who is trusted"
**Typical English translations:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy, reliable
**Usage:** Describes persons who are dependable and honest in fulfilling obligations or representing facts accurately. Used in legal contexts (*neʾeman le-ʿedut*, "competent/reliable as a witness") and in everyday moral evaluation. Also used as a title, e.g., *neʾeman bayit* ("a trusted household steward").
**[CORE]**

---

## 11. מדויק
**Transliteration:** meduyaq
**Literal meaning:** passive participle from root ד-י-ק (d-y-q), "to be precise, exact"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, descriptions, translations, or statements to indicate that they correspond closely to the facts or standard. Common in scientific, technical, and journalistic registers, e.g., *tiʾur meduyaq* ("an accurate description"). The root also yields *diyuq* ("precision, exactness") and *ledayeq* ("to be precise").
**[CORE]**

---

## 12. כנות
**Transliteration:** kanut
**Literal meaning:** abstract noun from *kʾen* ("sincere, honest"); "sincerity, honesty"
**Typical English translations:** sincerity, honesty, candor
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being genuine and straightforward in one's statements or intentions, without concealment or pretense. Used in moral and interpersonal evaluation, e.g., *medaber be-kanut* ("speaking with sincerity"). Contrasts with *tsevʿiʿut* ("hypocrisy") and *remiyyaʾh* ("deceit").
**[CORE]**

---

## 13. כן
**Transliteration:** kʾen
**Literal meaning:** from root כ-נ-נ or כ-ו-נ; "upright, sincere, honest"
**Typical English translations:** sincere, honest, candid, genuine
**Usage:** Describes a person or statement as straightforward and free from deception. Used in moral evaluation of persons and their speech, e.g., *adam kʾen* ("an honest/sincere person"). Participates in derivations including *kanut* ("sincerity") and *le-hiyot kʾen* ("to be honest").
**[CORE]**

---

## 14. צדק
**Transliteration:** tsedeq
**Literal meaning:** from root צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q), "rightness, justice"
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, rightness
**Usage:** A central term in Hebrew moral and legal discourse, denoting conformity to a standard of right conduct or just outcome. Used in legal contexts (*mishpat u-tsedeq*, "justice and righteousness") and in evaluating whether an action or judgment is correct or fair. Participates in a large derivational family including *tsaddiq* ("righteous person") and *letsdiq* ("to justify, to vindicate").
**[CORE]**

---

## 15. ישר
**Transliteration:** yashar
**Literal meaning:** from root י-ש-ר (y-sh-r), "straight, upright"
**Typical English translations:** upright, honest, straightforward, just
**Usage:** Describes persons, conduct, or paths as morally straight and honest, without deviation or deception. Appears in biblical and modern usage, e.g., *ha-yashar be-ʿeynav* ("what is right in his eyes"). Yields derivations such as *yosher* ("uprightness, integrity") and *le-yashʾer* ("to straighten, to approve").
**[CORE]**

---

## 16. יושר
**Transliteration:** yosher
**Literal meaning:** abstract noun from root י-ש-ר; "straightness, uprightness"
**Typical English translations:** integrity, uprightness, honesty, fairness
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of moral straightforwardness and integrity in a person's conduct or character. Used in ethical and legal discourse, e.g., *ʾish yosher* ("a man of integrity"). Closely related to *yashar* but functions as an abstract quality noun rather than an attributive adjective.
**[CORE]**

---

## 17. רמאות
**Transliteration:** ramaʾut
**Literal meaning:** abstract noun from root ר-מ-א (r-m-ʾ), "to deceive, to cheat"
**Typical English translations:** deceit, fraud, cheating, trickery
**Usage:** Denotes the practice of deliberately misleading others, used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate conduct as dishonest or fraudulent. Appears in legal discourse concerning commercial fraud and in everyday moral condemnation. The root yields *rammʾai* ("deceiver, cheat") and *leramot* ("to deceive").
**[CORE]**

---

## 18. זיוף
**Transliteration:** ziyyuf
**Literal meaning:** verbal noun from root ז-י-פ (z-y-f), "to forge, to falsify"
**Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting
**Usage:** Refers to the act or product of deliberately falsifying documents, signatures, currency, or other representations. A standard legal and administrative term, e.g., *ziyyuf mismaḵh* ("document forgery"). Closely related to *mezuyaf* ("forged") and *mezayef* ("forger").
**[CORE]**

---

## 19. הונאה
**Transliteration:** honaʾaʾh
**Literal meaning:** verbal noun from root א-נ-ה in hifʿil pattern, "to deceive, to defraud"
**Typical English translations:** deception, fraud, swindling
**Usage:** Used in legal and moral contexts to describe deliberate misleading of another person, particularly for material gain. Appears in contract law and consumer protection discourse, e.g., *honaʾaʾh be-miqaḥ u-memkar* ("fraud in buying and selling," a classical legal category). Yields *meʾoneh* ("deceiver") and *lehaʾot* ("to deceive").
**[CORE]**

---

## 20. מהות
**Transliteration:** mahut
**Literal meaning:** from *maʾh* ("what") + abstract suffix; "whatness, essence"
**Typical English translations:** essence, nature, substance, what something fundamentally is
**Usage:** A philosophical and general-purpose term for the fundamental nature or defining character of something, used in contexts where one evaluates what something truly or essentially is. Appears in philosophical writing and in educated everyday speech, e.g., *mahut ha-daʾvar* ("the essence of the matter"). Participates in the compound *mahutʾi* ("essential, substantive").
**[CORE]**

---

## 21. עצם
**Transliteration:** ʿetsem
**Literal meaning:** from root ע-צ-מ (ʿ-ts-m), "bone; self; very thing, essence"
**Typical English translations:** essence, the very thing itself; (in compounds) the actual, the real
**Usage:** Used in philosophical and legal discourse to refer to the essential or actual nature of something, often in the construction *be-ʿetsem* ("in essence, actually") or *ʿetsem ha-daʾvar* ("the very matter itself"). Also functions as a reflexive intensifier. Its use as an essence-term is more contextually determined than *mahut*.
**[CORE]**

---

## 22. דמיון
**Transliteration:** dimyon
**Literal meaning:** from root ד-מ-ה (d-m-h), "to resemble, to be like"; "resemblance, likeness"
**Typical English translations:** resemblance, similarity, likeness; (also) imagination
**Usage:** In evaluative contexts, used to assess the degree to which one thing resembles another, relevant to questions of authenticity, imitation, and representation. The same word also means "imagination," which can create ambiguity; context disambiguates. Appears in aesthetic and legal discussions of imitation and copying.
**[CORE]**

---

## 23. דיוק
**Transliteration:** diyuq
**Literal meaning:** verbal noun from root ד-י-ק (d-y-q), "precision, exactness"
**Typical English translations:** precision, accuracy, exactness
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being exact or precise in measurement, description, or statement. Common in scientific, scholarly, and journalistic registers, e.g., *be-diyuq* ("precisely, exactly") and *ḥoser diyuq* ("lack of precision, inaccuracy"). Closely related to *meduyaq* ("accurate, precise").
**[CORE]**

---

## 24. פסול
**Transliteration:** pasul
**Literal meaning:** from root פ-ס-ל (p-s-l), "to disqualify, to invalidate"; passive participle
**Typical English translations:** invalid, disqualified, unfit, inadmissible
**Usage:** Used in legal and religious law (halakhah) to declare a person, object, document, or testimony as failing to meet the required standard and therefore not valid or admissible. Contrasts with *kasher* ("valid, fit"). Common in rabbinic legal discourse and in modern Israeli legal usage.
**[CORE]**

---

## 25. כשר
**Transliteration:** kasher
**Literal meaning:** from root כ-ש-ר (k-sh-r), "fit, proper, valid"
**Typical English translations:** fit, valid, proper, legitimate; (in dietary law) kosher
**Usage:** Beyond its well-known dietary application, *kasher* is used broadly in legal and evaluative contexts to mean that something meets the required standard and is therefore valid or legitimate, e.g., *ʿed kasher* ("a valid/competent witness"), *get kasher* ("a valid bill of divorce"). Contrasts with *pasul* ("invalid, disqualified").
**[CORE]**

---

## 26. צביעות
**Transliteration:** tsevʿiʿut
**Literal meaning:** from *tsavoaʿ* ("dyed, colored"); abstract noun; "being two-colored, hypocrisy"
**Typical English translations:** hypocrisy, insincerity, two-facedness
**Usage:** Denotes the quality of presenting a false appearance of virtue or sincerity while concealing contrary intentions or conduct. Used in moral evaluation of persons and public figures, e.g., *ze tsevʿiʿut mehuḥatsefet* ("that is blatant hypocrisy"). Contrasts with *kanut* ("sincerity") and *yosher* ("integrity").
**[CORE]**

---

## 27. אמינות
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut
**Literal meaning:** abstract noun from root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n); "trustworthiness, credibility"
**Typical English translations:** credibility, trustworthiness, reliability
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being believable and dependable, applied to persons, sources, institutions, or testimony. A key term in legal evaluation of witnesses (*ʾaminut ha-ʿed*, "the credibility of the witness") and in journalistic and academic assessment of sources. Closely related to *meheymen* and *neʾeman*.
**[CORE]**

---

## 28. ספק
**Transliteration:** safek
**Literal meaning:** from root ס-פ-ק (s-p-q), "doubt, uncertainty"
**Typical English translations:** doubt, uncertainty, question
**Usage:** Used in legal, religious, and everyday contexts to indicate that the truth, validity, or identity of something is uncertain or in question. In halakhic discourse, *safek* triggers specific rules for resolving uncertainty. In everyday usage, *be-li safek* ("without doubt") and *yesh safek* ("there is doubt") are common evaluative phrases.
**[CORE]**

---

## 29. חיקוי
**Transliteration:** ḥiqquy
**Literal meaning:** verbal noun from root ח-ק-ה (ḥ-q-h), "to imitate, to copy"
**Typical English translations:** imitation, mimicry, copy
**Usage:** Refers to the act or product of imitating something, used in aesthetic, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something is original or merely a copy. Relevant to questions of authenticity and resemblance, e.g., *ḥiqquy zol* ("a cheap imitation"). Contrasts with *meqori* ("original") and relates to *mezuyaf* when the imitation is deceptive.
**[CORE]**

---

## 30. עדות
**Transliteration:** ʿedut
**Literal meaning:** from root ע-ד-ה/ע-ו-ד (ʿ-d-h/ʿ-w-d), "testimony, witness"
**Typical English translations:** testimony, evidence, witness
**Usage:** The standard term for testimony given by a witness in legal or quasi-legal contexts, where its truthfulness and reliability are subject to evaluation. Central to both biblical law and modern Israeli legal procedure, e.g., *ʿedut sheqer* ("false testimony") and *ʿedut neʾemenet* ("reliable testimony"). Participates in derivations including *ʿed* ("witness") and *lehaʿid* ("to testify").
**[CORE]**
