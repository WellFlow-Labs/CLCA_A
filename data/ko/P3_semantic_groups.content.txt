# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Korean (ko)

---

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis identifies natural groupings based solely on observed usage patterns in Korean, as documented in P1 and P2. Groupings reflect how terms are actually used, contrasted, and distributed across contexts in Korean discourse, not imposed conceptual categories.

---

## GROUP 1: Factual Correspondence Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, representations, or beliefs correspond to actual states of affairs. These items appear in contexts of verification, fact-checking, and distinguishing accurate from inaccurate information. They are commonly used with verbs of speaking, knowing, and confirming.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진실 (jinsil) — truth
- 사실 (sasil) — fact
- 참 (cham) — true
- 정확하다 (jeonghwakhada) — to be accurate
- 맞다 (matda) — to be correct
- 틀리다 (teullida) — to be wrong
- 올바르다 (olbareuda) — to be right/correct

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Combine with 말하다 (speak): 진실을 말하다, 사실을 말하다
- Combine with 알다 (know): 진실을 알다, 사실을 알다
- Combine with 확인하다 (verify): 사실을 확인하다
- Used in questions about correctness: 맞아? (Is it correct?), 틀렸어? (Is it wrong?)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 진실 vs. 사실: Both translate as "truth/fact" but 진실 emphasizes truthfulness as quality while 사실 emphasizes factual status. They often appear together in contrastive constructions.
- 맞다 vs. 올바르다: Both mean "correct" but 맞다 is more general while 올바르다 carries normative/moral connotations.

---

## GROUP 2: Authenticity and Genuineness Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish genuine from fake objects, persons, or qualities. These items appear primarily in contexts of authentication, verification of identity or origin, and evaluation of sincerity. They commonly modify concrete nouns (objects, people) and abstract nouns (feelings, intentions).

**Representative expressions:**
- 진짜 (jinjja) — real, genuine
- 가짜 (gajja) — fake, counterfeit
- 진정하다 (jinjeonghada) — to be genuine
- 진품 (jinpum) — genuine article
- 진본 (jinbon) — original, authentic copy
- 모조 (mojo) — imitation
- 위조 (wijo) — forgery
- 진짜배기 (jinjjabbaegi) — the real deal

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Used in questions: 진짜야? (Really? Is it real?)
- Contrastive pairing: 진짜인지 가짜인지 (whether real or fake)
- Modify concrete nouns: 가짜돈 (counterfeit money), 가짜명품 (fake luxury goods)
- Modify interpersonal terms: 진짜 친구 (real friend), 가짜 친구 (fake friend)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 진짜 vs. 진정하다: 진짜 is colloquial and versatile; 진정하다 is more formal and emphasizes sincerity of feelings/intentions
- 모조 vs. 위조: Both refer to non-genuine items, but 위조 implies criminal intent while 모조 can be neutral (legitimate replicas)

---

## GROUP 3: Deception and Lying Terms

**Definition:** Terms specifically denoting false statements, acts of deception, or lying behavior. These items appear in moral discourse, legal contexts, and everyday discussions of dishonesty. They commonly combine with verbs of speaking and doing, and often appear with agentive or habitual markers.

**Representative expressions:**
- 거짓 (geojit) — falsehood, lie
- 거짓말 (geojinmal) — lie (spoken)
- 거짓말하다 (geojinmalhada) — to lie
- 거짓말쟁이 (geojinmaljaengi) — liar
- 속이다 (sogida) — to deceive
- 거짓증언 (geojitjeungeon) — false testimony
- 거짓증거 (geojitjeungeo) — false evidence
- 허위 (heowi) — falsehood (formal/legal)

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Verbal constructions: 거짓말을 하다 (to tell a lie), 거짓말을 꾸미다 (to fabricate a lie)
- Agentive forms: 거짓말쟁이 (habitual liar)
- Legal compounds: 거짓증언, 거짓증거, 허위진술
- Intensified forms: 새빨간 거짓말 (blatant lie)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 거짓 vs. 거짓말: 거짓 is more general (false/falsehood); 거짓말 specifically refers to spoken lies
- 거짓 vs. 허위: Both mean falsehood, but 허위 is formal/legal register while 거짓 is general

---

## GROUP 4: Honesty and Character Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating moral character related to truthfulness, integrity, and straightforwardness in conduct. These items appear in character assessments, moral discourse, and interpersonal evaluations. They describe persons rather than statements, emphasizing habitual disposition rather than individual acts.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정직하다 (jeongjikada) — to be honest
- 정직 (jeongjik) — honesty
- 정직성 (jeongjikseong) — honesty (as quality)
- 솔직하다 (soljikada) — to be frank, candid
- 진심 (jinsim) — sincerity, true heart
- 진정성 (jinjeonseong) — authenticity, genuineness

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Describe persons: 정직한 사람 (honest person)
- Describe manner of speaking: 솔직히 말하면 (frankly speaking), 정직하게 말하다 (to speak honestly)
- Evaluate character: 정직성이 있다 (to have integrity)
- Express sincerity: 진심으로 (sincerely, from the heart)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 정직하다 vs. 솔직하다: 정직하다 emphasizes moral integrity; 솔직하다 emphasizes openness/directness in communication
- 진심 vs. 진정성: 진심 refers to sincere feelings; 진정성 is abstract quality of being genuine

---

## GROUP 5: Certainty and Confidence Terms

**Definition:** Terms expressing degrees of certainty, confidence, or doubt about truth or facts. These items appear in epistemic contexts where speakers evaluate their confidence in knowledge or beliefs. They commonly combine with verbs of knowing and appear in hedging or assertive constructions.

**Representative expressions:**
- 확실하다 (hwaksilhada) — to be certain
- 확실성 (hwaksilseong) — certainty
- 틀림없다 (teullimeopda) — without doubt, certainly
- 의심하다 (uisimhada) — to doubt
- 의심스럽다 (uisimseureopda) — doubtful, suspicious
- 명백하다 (myeongbaekhada) — obvious, clear

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Epistemic constructions: 확실히 알다 (to know for certain), 확실하지 않다 (not certain)
- Assertive constructions: 틀림없이 (certainly, surely)
- Doubt expressions: 의심의 여지가 없다 (no room for doubt)
- Hedging: 확실한지 모르다 (not sure whether it's certain)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 확실하다 vs. 틀림없다: Both express certainty, but 틀림없다 is more emphatic and idiomatic
- 의심하다 vs. 의심스럽다: 의심하다 is active doubting; 의심스럽다 describes something as appearing doubtful

---

## GROUP 6: Trust and Reliability Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating trustworthiness, reliability, or credibility of persons, institutions, or information. These items appear in contexts of interpersonal relationships, institutional evaluation, and source assessment. They emphasize ongoing relationships and reputational qualities rather than single acts.

**Representative expressions:**
- 신뢰하다 (silloehada) — to trust
- 신뢰 (sinroe) — trust
- 신뢰성 (sinroeseong) — reliability, trustworthiness
- 믿다 (mitda) — to believe, trust
- 믿음 (mideum) — belief, trust
- 신빙성 (sinbingseong) — credibility

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Ability constructions: 믿을 수 있다 (can be trusted), 신뢰할 수 있다 (is trustworthy)
- Relational constructions: 믿음을 주다 (to give trust), 신뢰를 잃다 (to lose trust)
- Evaluative constructions: 신뢰성이 있다 (has reliability), 신빙성이 높다 (has high credibility)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 신뢰하다 vs. 믿다: 신뢰하다 is more formal and emphasizes rational trust; 믿다 is general and can include faith
- 신뢰성 vs. 신빙성: Both refer to trustworthiness, but 신빙성 specifically relates to believability of information/testimony

---

## GROUP 7: Validity and Reasonableness Terms

**Definition:** Terms evaluating logical validity, reasonableness, or appropriateness of arguments, claims, or reasoning. These items appear in analytical, academic, and argumentative contexts. They assess whether reasoning is sound or conclusions are justified, rather than whether statements correspond to facts.

**Representative expressions:**
- 타당하다 (tadanghada) — to be valid, reasonable
- 타당성 (tadangseong) — validity, reasonableness
- 정당하다 (jeongdanghada) — to be legitimate, justified
- 정당성 (jeongdangseong) — legitimacy, justification
- 그럴듯하다 (geureoldeuthada) — to be plausible

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Evaluate arguments: 타당한 이유 (valid reason), 타당한 근거 (reasonable grounds)
- Evaluate legitimacy: 정당한 권리 (legitimate right), 정당성이 있다 (has legitimacy)
- Evaluate plausibility: 그럴듯한 이야기 (plausible story), 그럴듯하게 들리다 (sounds plausible)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 타당하다 vs. 정당하다: 타당하다 emphasizes logical validity; 정당하다 emphasizes moral/legal legitimacy
- 그럴듯하다: Can overlap with truth-evaluation but specifically marks surface plausibility, often with implicit skepticism

---

## GROUP 8: Reality and Actuality Terms

**Definition:** Terms contrasting actual reality with appearance, theory, or expectation. These items appear in contexts distinguishing what actually exists or happens from what is claimed, imagined, or theorized. They commonly function as discourse markers or adverbial modifiers.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실제 (silje) — actual, reality
- 실제로 (siljero) — actually, in reality
- 실상 (silsang) — actual situation
- 실질적 (siljiljeok) — substantial, practical
- 실체 (silche) — substance, entity
- 본질 (bonjil) — essence, nature

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Contrastive discourse markers: 실제로는 (in reality), 사실상 (de facto)
- Contrast with appearance: 실제와 다르다 (differs from reality)
- Emphasize substance: 실질적인 변화 (substantial change)
- Philosophical contexts: 본질을 파악하다 (to grasp the essence)

**Ambiguous/overlapping items:**
- 실제 vs. 사실: Both can mean "actual/real" but 실제 emphasizes concrete reality while 사실 emphasizes factual status
- 실질적 vs. 실제적: Both mean "actual/practical" with subtle differences in emphasis

---

## GROUP 9: Practice and Implementation Terms

**Definition:** Terms emphasizing putting ideas into practice, actualizing plans, or demonstrating abilities through action. These items appear in contexts contrasting theory with practice, plans with execution, or claims with demonstrated competence. They commonly combine with action verbs.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실천 (silcheon) — practice, implementation
- 실천하다 (silcheonhada) — to practice, put into action
- 실행 (silhaeng) — execution, implementation
- 실현 (silhyeon) — realization, actualization
- 실현하다 (silhyeonhada) — to realize, actualize
- 실력 (sillyeok) — actual ability, competence
- 실적 (siljeok) — actual results, performance

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Contrast theory and practice: 이론과 실천 (theory and practice)
- Describe implementation: 계획을 실행하다 (to execute a plan)
- Evaluate competence: 실력을 보여주다 (to demonstrate ability)
- Measure results: 실적을 평가하다 (to evaluate performance)

**Note:** This group shows the extension of 실 (實) from "reality/actuality" into the domain of practical action and demonstrated competence.

---

## GROUP 10: Formal Investigation and Verification Terms

**Definition:** Terms used in formal, legal, or investigative contexts for determining truth, authenticity, or facts. These items appear in official proceedings, journalistic investigation, and formal inquiry. They often combine with legal or procedural vocabulary.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진위 (jinwi) — authenticity, truth or falsehood
- 진위를 가리다 (jinwireul garida) — to determine authenticity
- 진상 (jinsang) — truth of the matter, real situation
- 진상을 밝히다 (jinsangeul balkhida) — to reveal the truth
- 진술 (jinsul) — statement, testimony
- 진단 (jindan) — diagnosis, assessment

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Legal/investigative contexts: 진위를 밝히다 (to clarify authenticity)
- Formal testimony: 진술하다 (to testify)
- Official inquiry: 진상규명 (truth-finding, fact-finding)
- Professional assessment: 진단하다 (to diagnose)

**Note:** These terms are marked by formal register and institutional contexts, distinguishing them from everyday truth-evaluation terms.

---

## GROUP 11: Information and Media Terms

**Definition:** Terms specifically about information, news, or data in contemporary media and information contexts. These items appear in discussions of media reliability, information quality, and contemporary concerns about misinformation. They commonly combine with modern loanwords.

**Representative expressions:**
- 거짓정보 (geojitjeongbo) — false information, misinformation
- 거짓뉴스 (geojitnuseu) — fake news
- 가짜뉴스 (gajjanuseu) — fake news
- 허위사실 (heowisasil) — false facts

**Typical usage patterns:**
- Media criticism: 가짜뉴스를 퍼뜨리다 (to spread fake news)
- Information evaluation: 거짓정보를 구별하다 (to distinguish false information)
- Contemporary discourse about media and information quality

**Note:** This is a relatively recent grouping reflecting contemporary concerns about information reliability in digital media contexts.

## ITEMS WITH AMBIGUOUS OR MULTIPLE GROUP MEMBERSHIP

### 사실 (sasil) — fact
- **Primary group:** Group 1 (Factual Correspondence Terms)
- **Secondary patterns:** Functions uniquely as discourse marker (사실은 "in fact"), overlaps with Group 8 (Reality and Actuality) in some uses
- **Note:** Shows broader distribution than other Group 1 items, functioning both as truth-evaluation term and as discourse marker for introducing factual information

### 진실 (jinsil) — truth
- **Primary group:** Group 1 (Factual Correspondence Terms)
- **Secondary patterns:** Overlaps with Group 4 (Honesty and Character) when used to evaluate character (진실한 사람 "truthful person")
- **Note:** More abstract and philosophical than 사실, can refer both to factual truth and to truthfulness as character trait

### 진짜 (jinjja) — real, genuine
- **Primary group:** Group 2 (Authenticity and Genuineness Terms)
- **Secondary patterns:** Functions as intensifier/emphasis marker in colloquial speech beyond truth-evaluation
- **Note:** Highly versatile colloquial term that extends beyond truth-domain into general emphasis

### 믿다 (mitda) — to believe, trust
- **Spans:** Group 1 (epistemic belief in truth) and Group 6 (interpersonal trust)
- **Note:** Ambiguous between epistemic belief (believing something is true) and interpersonal trust (trusting someone)

### 정확하다 (jeonghwakhada) — to be accurate
- **Primary group:** Group 1 (Factual Correspondence Terms)
- **Secondary patterns:** Emphasizes precision rather than simple truth/falsity, creating partial overlap with technical/measurement contexts
- **Note:** More specific than general truth-terms, focusing on degree of precision

---

## ITEMS THAT DO NOT FIT CLEANLY INTO GROUPS

### 참 (cham) — true, real
**Reason for ambiguity:** Functions both as truth-evaluation term (Group 1) and as general intensifier in colloquial speech. In modern Korean, its use as truth-term is somewhat formal/literary, while its use as intensifier ("really, very") is more common. Does not show the same productivity patterns as other Group 1 items.

### 올바르다 (olbareuda) — to be right, correct
**Reason for ambiguity:** Spans factual correctness (Group 1) and moral/normative rightness. The moral dimension creates overlap with Group 4 (Honesty and Character), but it's not specifically about character traits. Occupies intermediate position between factual and normative evaluation.

### 진정하다 (jinjeonghada) — to be genuine, sincere
**Reason for ambiguity:** Could belong to Group 2 (Authenticity) or Group 4 (Honesty and Character) depending on context. When modifying feelings/intentions, it's about sincerity (Group 4); when modifying objects/situations, it's about genuineness (Group 2).

### 명백하다 (myeongbaekhada) — obvious, clear, evident
**Reason for ambiguity:** Partially overlaps with Group 5 (Certainty) but emphasizes obviousness/clarity rather than subjective confidence. Could be seen as evaluating epistemic accessibility rather than truth per se.

### 본질 (bonjil) — essence, nature
**Reason for ambiguity:** Included in Group 8 (Reality and Actuality) but functions somewhat differently, being more philosophical and less about practical reality. Represents a more abstract, metaphysical concept than other Group 8 items.

## OBSERVATIONS ON GROUPING PATTERNS

### Register-based clustering:
- **Formal/legal register:** Groups 3 (legal deception terms), 10 (investigation terms)
- **Colloquial register:** Group 2 (진짜/가짜 and related terms)
- **Academic/analytical register:** Group 7 (validity terms), Group 8 (reality terms)

### Semantic domain clustering:
- **Interpersonal domain:** Groups 4 (character), 6 (trust)
- **Epistemic domain:** Groups 1 (factual correspondence), 5 (certainty)
- **Practical/action domain:** Group 9 (practice and implementation)

### Functional clustering:
- **Evaluative terms:** Groups 1, 2, 4, 7
- **Relational terms:** Group 6
- **Action/process terms:** Group 9
- **Institutional terms:** Group 10

### Asymmetries noted:
1. **Truth vs. falsehood asymmetry:** Truth-related terms (Group 1) show different distribution patterns than deception terms (Group 3). Truth terms combine more freely with epistemic verbs (know, confirm), while deception terms combine more with action verbs (do, fabricate).

2. **Abstract vs. concrete asymmetry:** Authenticity terms (Group 2) show high productivity with concrete nouns (objects, goods), while factual correspondence terms (Group 1) combine primarily with abstract nouns (statements, information).

3. **Native vs. Sino-Korean asymmetry:** Native Korean roots (거짓, 참) show different derivational and distributional patterns than Sino-Korean roots (진, 실), with Sino-Korean roots showing greater productivity in formal compounds.

4. **Character vs. act asymmetry:** Terms in Group 4 (character) describe habitual dispositions, while terms in Group 3 (deception) describe specific acts, reflecting different aspectual profiles.

---

## NOTES ON METHODOLOGY

This grouping is based solely on observed usage patterns in Korean as documented in P1 and P2. Groups reflect:
- Distributional patterns (which verbs, nouns, contexts items appear with)
- Contrastive patterns (which items are explicitly contrasted in usage)
- Register patterns (formal vs. colloquial contexts)
- Functional patterns (evaluative, relational, action-oriented uses)

The analysis does NOT impose:
- Universal semantic categories
- Predetermined conceptual frameworks
- Symmetrical or balanced groupings
- Complete coverage of all items

Some items remain ambiguous or ungrouped because Korean usage does not provide clear evidence for their classification. This reflects the actual complexity and flexibility of the Korean truth-evaluation lexicon.
