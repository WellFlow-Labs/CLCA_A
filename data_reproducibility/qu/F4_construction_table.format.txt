# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — F4 Construction Table
# Language: Quechua (qu)

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Usage Notes | Epistemic Status |
|------------------|---------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|------------------|
| ATTRIBUTION | kayqa chiqaqmi | kayqa chiqaq-mi | this is true | Extremely common in everyday discourse; copula *kay* regularly omitted in present tense with validator suffixes carrying predicative function. Used for both permanent and temporary states. | [CORE] |
| ATTRIBUTION | payqa llulla runam | pay-qa llulla runa-m | he/she is a liar | Standard pattern for character attribution; nominal predicates with -mi validator are unmarked constructions for identity/classification statements. | [CORE] |
| NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION | kayqa mana chiqaqchu | kayqa mana chiqaq-chu | this is not true | Standard negation pattern; *mana* + predicate + -chu is the unmarked negative construction. The -chu suffix is obligatory in emphatic/categorical negation. | [CORE] |
| NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION | payqa mana allin runachu | pay-qa mana allin runa-chu | he/she is not a good person | Common in evaluative statements about persons; same structural pattern as above, highly productive across all predicate types. | [CORE] |
| ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION | chiqaq simi | chiqaq simi | true word, truthful speech | Unmarked and highly productive pattern for all modification; no agreement marking required. Used freely in all registers and contexts. | [CORE] |
| ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION | llulla runa | llulla runa | lying person, liar | Standard pattern for character description; prenominal position is fixed and obligatory for attributive modification. | [CORE] |
| BECOME/INCHOATIVE | allinyay | allin-ya-y | to become good, to improve | Highly productive with stative bases; common in discourse about change, improvement, and development. Used across all semantic domains. | [CORE] |
| BECOME/INCHOATIVE | chiqaqyay | chiqaq-ya-y | to become true, to be verified | Standard pattern for expressing verification processes; frequently used in epistemic contexts where truth-status changes. | [CORE] |
| CAUSATIVE | chiqanchay | chiqaq-ncha-y | to verify, to authenticate | Common in formal/institutional contexts; the -ncha- variant (with epenthetic -n-) appears with certain roots, possibly phonologically conditioned. | [CORE] |
| CAUSATIVE | allinyachiy | allin-ya-chi-y | to make good, to improve, to fix | Extremely common in practical contexts; stacking of -ya- + -chi- is productive and creates "cause to become" semantics systematically. | [CORE] |
| ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION | yachay | yacha-y | knowledge, knowing | Infinitive-as-noun is unmarked pattern; extremely common in abstract discourse. No formal distinction between infinitive and nominal use. | [CORE] |
| ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION | pantana | panta-na | place/means of error | Less frequent than infinitive nominalization; -na creates more specialized instrumental/locative meanings, restricted to certain semantic contexts. | [MARGINAL] |
| AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION | llullaq | llulla-q | liar, one who lies | Highly productive for agent/characteristic description; used freely for both habitual agents and adjectival modification. | [CORE] |
| AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION | chiqanchaq | chiqaq-ncha-q | verifier, one who authenticates | Common in institutional/formal contexts; -q freely combines with all verbal derivations including causatives. | [CORE] |
| MANNER ADVERBIAL | chiqaqta rimay | chiqaq-ta rima-y | to speak truthfully | Standard and obligatory pattern for manner modification; -ta on evaluative terms is the unmarked adverbial strategy. | [CORE] |
| MANNER ADVERBIAL | allinta ruway | allin-ta ruwa-y | to do well, to do properly | Extremely common in all contexts; this specific collocation (*allinta ruway*) is particularly frequent as a general directive. | [CORE] |
| EVIDENTIAL/VALIDATOR | chiqaqmi | chiqaq-mi | it is true (I assert) | Evidential marking is obligatory in main clauses; -mi marks direct knowledge/personal assertion, used in all assertive contexts. | [CORE] |
| EVIDENTIAL/VALIDATOR | chiqaqsi | chiqaq-si | it is true (I heard) | Reportative -si obligatory for hearsay information; crucial for distinguishing information source in truth-domain discourse. | [CORE] |
| EPISTEMIC SOURCE | rikuywan yachay | riku-y-wan yacha-y | to know by seeing | Common pattern for explicit evidential marking; contrasts with grammatical evidentials by making source lexically explicit. | [CORE] |
| EPISTEMIC SOURCE | uyariywan yachay | uyari-y-wan yacha-y | to know by hearing | Parallel to visual knowledge construction; used when evidential basis needs emphasis or clarification beyond grammatical marking. | [CORE] |
| REFLEXIVE/MIDDLE | llullakuy | llulla-ku-y | to lie (intransitive) | Common detransitivizing pattern; -ku- creates intransitive versions of evaluative verbs, focusing on subject's action/state. | [CORE] |
| REFLEXIVE/MIDDLE | pantakuy | panta-ku-y | to err, to make a mistake | Very frequent in error discourse; -ku- emphasizes that error affects/belongs to subject, standard form for "make a mistake." | [CORE] |
| ABUNDANCE/INTENSITY | chiqaqsapa | chiqaq-sapa | very truthful, full of truth | Less frequent than base forms; used primarily for character assessment and intensification, more common in evaluative/moral discourse. | [MARGINAL] |
| ABUNDANCE/INTENSITY | llullasapa | llulla-sapa | very deceitful, full of lies | Similar distribution to above; -sapa forms tend to appear in character descriptions and moral evaluations rather than everyday usage. | [MARGINAL] |
| POSSESSOR | yachayniyuq | yacha-y-ni-yuq | having knowledge, knowledgeable | Common pattern for expressing possession of abstract qualities; -niyuq variant appears with certain abstract nouns. | [CORE] |
| POSSESSOR | chaninyuq | chanin-yuq | having value, legitimate | Frequent in evaluative contexts; -yuq alone (without -ni-) appears with some roots, distribution may be lexically conditioned. | [CORE] |
| COMPARATIVE/EQUATIVE | payhina chiqaq | pay-hina chiqaq | as true as him/her | Standard comparison strategy; *hina/shina* is the unmarked particle for all equative/similative constructions. | [CORE] |
| COMPARATIVE/EQUATIVE | kayhina allin | kay-hina allin | as good as this | Very common in evaluative comparisons; no morphological comparative exists, *hina* construction is obligatory for comparison. | [CORE] |
| MAGNITUDE MODIFICATION | huch'uy pantay | huch'uy panta-y | small error, minor mistake | Common pattern for grading errors/faults; size terms productively modify evaluative nouns to indicate seriousness/magnitude. | [CORE] |
| MAGNITUDE MODIFICATION | hatun huch'a | hatun huch'a | great sin, serious fault | Frequent in moral/religious discourse; *hatun* "big" regularly intensifies negative evaluations, particularly moral faults. | [CORE] |
| LIMITATIVE | chiqaqlla | chiqaq-lla | only true, just the true one | Common restrictive/minimizing particle; -lla appears freely with all word classes, used for both exclusivity and minimization. | [CORE] |
| LIMITATIVE | pantalla | panta-lla | just an error, only a mistake | Frequent in contexts minimizing significance; -lla on negative evaluations often functions to downplay seriousness. | [CORE] |
| EMPHATIC | chiqapuni | chiqaq-puni | truly indeed, really | Common emphatic particle; -puni strengthens assertions and appears frequently in contexts requiring strong affirmation or certainty. | [CORE] |
| EMPHATIC | allinpuni | allin-puni | very good indeed, certainly good | Very frequent in positive evaluations; -puni adds speaker confidence and is common in reassurances and strong endorsements. | [CORE] |
| PAST PARTICIPLE | pantasqa | panta-sqa | mistaken, having erred | Highly productive resultative/passive; -sqa creates both stative ("is mistaken") and passive ("was mistaken") readings depending on context. | [CORE] |
| PAST PARTICIPLE | yachasqa | yacha-sqa | known, learned | Extremely common with epistemic verbs; creates both resultative states and nominal uses ("what is known, knowledge"). | [CORE] |
| CULTURAL CONCEPT | allin kawsay | allin kawsay | good life, well-being | Culturally central concept; semi-lexicalized but structurally transparent, used extensively in social/philosophical discourse. | [CORE] |
| CULTURAL CONCEPT | sumaq kawsay | sumaq kawsay | beautiful life, harmonious living | Key Andean philosophical term; parallel to *allin kawsay*, represents central ethical ideal, widely used in cultural/political contexts. | [CORE] |
| PLURAL ON ABSTRACTS | yachaykuna | yacha-y-kuna | knowledges, sciences | Common pattern for multiple domains/types; particularly productive with *yachay* to mean "sciences, areas of knowledge." | [CORE] |
| PLURAL ON ABSTRACTS | pantaykuna | panta-y-kuna | errors, mistakes (plural) | Standard pluralization of abstract nouns; creates count readings of mass concepts, used when enumerating multiple instances. | [CORE] |
| DISCONFIRMATION | (no common expression identified) | — | — | No construction exists for disconfirmation as a positive operation (i.e., "to prove false," "to falsify"). The language would require circumlocution, likely combining negation with causative or demonstrative constructions (e.g., showing that something is not true). This represents a genuine structural gap where the negative counterpart to verification lacks a dedicated or even common expression. | [GAP] |
