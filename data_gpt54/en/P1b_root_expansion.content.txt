## Step 1 — Identify central roots

For English, the most recurrent building blocks in the P1 set are primarily **lexical stems** rather than highly productive native bound morphemes. The clearest central roots are:

1. **true** — Transliteration: N/A  
   - **Participates in:** 6 P1 items  
     (*truth, true, trustworthy, true to life, ring true,* and historically also the *true* element underlying older fidelity-related usage)
   - **Core meaning contribution:** correctness, genuineness, accordance with reality, or faithfulness.

2. **right** — Transliteration: N/A  
   - **Participates in:** 4 P1 items  
     (*right, correct* [semantic overlap but not same root], *proper, just* are not morphologically from *right*, so strictly the root itself appears mainly in *right* and in many ordinary expansions outside P1)
   - **Core meaning contribution:** correctness, propriety, justification, or normative acceptability.

3. **real** — Transliteration: N/A  
   - **Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
     (*real, the real thing,* and semantically related authenticity/genuineness expressions built on *real*)
   - **Core meaning contribution:** actuality, non-fakeness, existence, genuineness.

Because English truth-evaluation vocabulary is etymologically mixed, no single root dominates the whole domain. Other important stems such as **valid**, **authentic**, **accur-**, **faith-**, and **genuin-** are also significant, but they occur less repeatedly as shared building blocks in the P1 list.
