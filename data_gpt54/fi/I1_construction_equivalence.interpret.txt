## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Finnish shows direct support for core **ATTR**, **ADNOM**, **BECOME**, **NEGATION**, and **NOMINALIZATION** patterns in the truth domain.

### Revelation vs. Become
**REVELATION** is clearly attested through **osoittautua epätodeksi** and **paljastua väärennökseksi**. It does not replace **BECOME** across the board, because **BECOME** is directly attested in **tulla todeksi**. However, in falsity/discovery contexts, revelation-type verbs appear to carry functions that are close to inchoative outcome marking. Descriptively:  
- positive realization: **tulla todeksi**  
- discovered falsity / exposed status: **osoittautua epätodeksi**, **paljastua väärennökseksi**

So REVELATION does not substitute for BECOME generally, but it does cover part of the same result-domain space, especially retrospective evaluation.

### Discovery / verification vs. causation
The notes explicitly state that there is **no single general causative verb** for the whole semantic domain. Instead, Finnish uses lexical verbs such as **todistaa** and **oikeuttaa**, or the result construction **tehdä jokin uskottavaksi**. This means factual causation is not expressed by one uniform construction.

Because of this, **VERIFICATION** often carries functions that might elsewhere be associated with causative truth-making:
- **todistaa jokin** ‘prove something’
- **pitää paikkansa**
- **On totta, että ...**

So verification does not fully replace causation, but causation is lexically restricted and verification/proof constructions are more clearly established.

### Negation type
**NEGATION** is analytic, not morphological, in the constructions provided:
- **ei ole**
- **ei pidä paikkaansa**

No morphological negation pattern is described for the construction itself.

### Verification and disconfirmation
**VERIFICATION** and **DISCONFIRMATION** partly share the same constructional base:
- positive: **pitää paikkansa**, **On totta, että ...**
- negative: **ei pidä paikkaansa**, **ei ole tosi**

This is a clear asymmetry with shared operator material in the **pitää paikkansa** pattern, where disconfirmation is formed analytically by negating the same expression.

### Truth vs. falsity asymmetry
There is a descriptive asymmetry in the result domain:
- truth realization is expressed by **tulla todeksi**
- falsity after checking/discovery is expressed by **osoittautua epätodeksi**

So positive and negative outcomes are not shown with one single symmetric construction in the data.

### Character vs. factual domains
The material distinguishes factual correctness from credibility/reliability:
- factual: **totta**, **epätosi**, **pitää paikkansa**, **todistaa**
- character/evaluation: **uskottava**, **luotettava**, **tehdä ... uskottavaksi**, **pitää ... luotettavana**

This means character-related causation is less broadly supported than factual attribution/evaluation.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- No clear structural gap for **ATTR**, **ADNOM**, **BECOME**, **REVELATION**, **NEGATION**, or **NOMINALIZATION**.
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: no single general causative construction across the whole truth domain; only lexically distributed patterns.
- **CAUSATION (character)**: no broad general construction established; only limited circumlocutory/resultative expressions.

### Lexical Gaps
- No simple core lexicalized single-word agent noun for ‘truth-teller’ is supported in the provided material. The notes explicitly say Finnish often uses periphrastic **puhua totta** instead.
- For causation, there is a lexical gap in the sense that no single general verb means “make true/authentic” across the whole domain.

### Conceptual Gaps
- No conceptual gap is directly established for truth attribution, negation, verification, or nominalization.
- The main limitation is operational distribution: causation is not generalized as one operator across the domain.

### Asymmetric Gaps
- Positive and negative result expressions are asymmetric:
  - positive becoming: **tulla todeksi**
  - negative discovered falsity: **osoittautua epätodeksi**
- Verification and disconfirmation are more robustly expressed than generalized causation.
- Character-domain causation is weaker than factual-domain attribution and verification.
