# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a DERIVATIONAL SYSTEM
# Language: Finnish (fi)

## Methodology Note

This analysis documents **attested derivational morphology** based on the P1 vocabulary and P1b root expansion data provided. All forms listed have been identified in the source material as actually used in Finnish. No paradigms have been completed speculatively, and asymmetries in derivational patterns are preserved as found.

---

## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS BY BASE FORM

### 1. BASE: tosi

#### 1.1 Nominal derivatives

**totuus**
- Form: totuus
- Breakdown: tosi + -uus (abstract noun suffix)
- Translation: truth
- Usage: Abstract noun denoting the quality or state of being true. Used across all registers for truth as a concept.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosiasia**
- Form: tosiasia
- Breakdown: tosi + asia (matter, thing)
- Translation: fact
- Usage: Compound noun for established fact or factual matter. Standard term in argumentative and analytical discourse.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosikko**
- Form: tosikko
- Breakdown: tosi + -kko (person noun suffix, often pejorative)
- Translation: overly serious person
- Usage: Colloquial, mildly pejorative term for someone who takes things too seriously or literally.
- Epistemic status: [MARGINAL]

**tosipoika**
- Form: tosipoika
- Breakdown: tosi + poika (boy)
- Translation: real boy, genuine boy
- Usage: Colloquial compound emphasizing authentic boyish qualities or masculinity.
- Epistemic status: [MARGINAL]

**tositarina**
- Form: tositarina
- Breakdown: tosi + tarina (story)
- Translation: true story
- Usage: Narrative based on actual events rather than fiction. Common in media contexts.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tositapahtumat**
- Form: tositapahtumat
- Breakdown: tosi + tapahtumat (events, plural)
- Translation: real events, actual events
- Usage: Events that actually occurred, used especially in media descriptions.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosielämä**
- Form: tosielämä
- Breakdown: tosi + elämä (life)
- Translation: real life
- Usage: Actual life as opposed to fiction, simulation, or virtual contexts.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosi-tv**
- Form: tosi-tv
- Breakdown: tosi + TV (television)
- Translation: reality TV
- Usage: Genre term for reality television programming.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosirakkautta**
- Form: tosirakkautta (partitive case)
- Breakdown: tosi + rakkaus (love) + -ta (partitive)
- Translation: true love
- Usage: Genuine romantic love as opposed to infatuation or false affection.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

#### 1.2 Adjectival derivatives

**tosiasiallinen**
- Form: tosiasiallinen
- Breakdown: tosiasia + -llinen (adjectival suffix)
- Translation: factual, actual, de facto
- Usage: Describes what is actually the case in practice, often contrasted with nominal or theoretical status.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosipohjainen**
- Form: tosipohjainen
- Breakdown: tosi + pohja (base, foundation) + -inen (adjectival suffix)
- Translation: based on fact, fact-based
- Usage: Describes narratives or claims grounded in actual events or facts.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**totinen**
- Form: totinen
- Breakdown: toti- (emphatic/archaic form of tosi) + -nen (adjectival suffix)
- Translation: serious, earnest, solemn
- Usage: Describes serious or solemn demeanor. Somewhat archaic or formal register.
- Epistemic status: [MARGINAL]

#### 1.3 Adverbial derivatives

**tosiasiassa**
- Form: tosiasiassa
- Breakdown: tosiasia + -ssa (inessive case)
- Translation: in fact, actually
- Usage: Adverbial expression emphasizing actual reality versus appearance or expectation.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**tosissaan**
- Form: tosissaan
- Breakdown: tosi + -ssa (inessive) + -an (possessive suffix)
- Translation: seriously, in earnest
- Usage: Idiomatic expression meaning seriously or not joking.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

**totisesti**
- Form: totisesti
- Breakdown: toti- + -sesti (adverb suffix)
- Translation: truly, verily, indeed
- Usage: Archaic or formal emphatic adverb. Used in religious, literary, or very formal contexts.
- Epistemic status: [MARGINAL]

**tosimielessä**
- Form: tosimielessä
- Breakdown: tosi + mieli (mind) + -ssä (inessive)
- Translation: seriously, in earnest
- Usage: Adverbial expression emphasizing sincerity or seriousness of intent.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

#### 1.4 Abstract noun derivatives

**tosiasiallisuus**
- Form: tosiasiallisuus
- Breakdown: tosiasiallinen + -uus (abstract noun suffix)
- Translation: factuality, actuality
- Usage: Abstract noun for the quality of being factual or actual. Formal register.
- Epistemic status: [CORE]

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Productivity Patterns

**Highly productive roots:**
- **tosi/tode-**: Shows extensive derivation across multiple categories (nominal, adjectival, adverbial, verbal). Particularly productive in forming compounds and abstract nouns.
- **väärä/väär-**: Highly productive, especially with the väärin- prefix forming numerous compounds denoting various types of errors and wrongdoing.
- **oikea/oike-**: Productive across multiple domains, with extensive legal/institutional terminology.

**Moderately productive roots:**
- **väittää**: Shows regular derivational patterns for debate/argumentation terminology.
- **virhe**: Limited but regular derivation for error-related concepts.
- **rehellinen**: Standard derivation for honesty-related concepts.

**Limited productivity:**
- **aito**: Shows minimal derivation beyond basic abstract noun.
- **valhe**: Primary derivative is the verb *valehdella* and the productive prefix *vale-*.

### Derivational Asymmetries

1. The tosi/tode- root shows extensive development in both truth-evaluation and realization/actualization domains (toteutua, toteuttaa family).

2. The oikea root shows polysemy-driven derivation, with separate families for correctness, legal rights, and directional meaning.

3. The väärä root shows parallel but not identical derivational patterns to oikea, with particular productivity in error and wrongdoing terminology.

4. Negative derivatives (epä-, -tön) are selectively applied and not uniformly productive across all bases.

5. Abstract noun formation (-uus, -yys, -suus) is highly regular and productive across most adjectival bases.

6. Adverbial formation (-sti) is regular for adjectives but shows some lexical gaps.

### Gaps and Constraints

- Not all bases show equal derivational potential
- Some semantic domains (e.g., legal terminology under oikea) show concentrated derivation
- Directional meanings of oikea show limited derivation compared to correctness meanings
- The vale- prefix is productive but individual compounds are not exhaustively documented here
