# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Observations

The groupings below emerge from patterns visible in the P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b data: which items co-occur, which are contrasted with each other, which appear in the same syntactic frames, and which are restricted to particular contexts. The number of groups is not predetermined. Items that do not fit cleanly are noted at the end.

---

## GROUP 1

**Label:** Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement corresponds to what is the case

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker is affirming or rejecting the factual accuracy of a claim, account, or statement. They are contrasted with each other directly in usage (e.g., *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* vs. *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*) and appear in the same syntactic frames with *sọ* (to say/speak), *ni* (focus/copula), and *kò sí...nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* (there is no...in his/her words). The core contrast in this group is between what a statement is and what it is not.

**Representative expressions:**
- *òtítọ́* — truth, factual correctness
- *irọ́* — lie, falsehood
- *ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́* — true statement, words of truth
- *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* — correct/true word or matter
- *sọ òtítọ́* — to tell the truth
- *sọ irọ́* / *pa irọ́* — to tell a lie
- *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* — it is the truth that he/she spoke (cleft)
- *Irọ́ ni ó sọ* — it is a lie that he/she spoke (cleft)
- *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* — there is no truth in his/her words
- *Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* — there is no lie in his/her words
- *òtítọ́ pátápátá* — the whole truth
- *irọ́ tótóbi* — a great/serious lie
- *irọ́ funfun* — a white/minor lie
- *olòtítọ́* — a truthful person
- *olóirọ́* — a liar
- *Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* — it became clear that it is the truth
- *A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni* — we know that it is a lie
- *Òtítọ́ kò ní kú* — truth will not die (proverbial)

**Notes:** *irọ́ funfun* (white lie) is marginal within this group in that it introduces a gradation not present in the core contrast. *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* overlaps with Group 2 (see below), since *tọ́* also evaluates correctness in non-propositional contexts.

---

## GROUP 2

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard of correctness or propriety

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is not primarily whether a statement is true, but whether an action, decision, procedure, object, or person meets a recognized standard of what is right, proper, or correct. The evaluative predicate *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* is the anchor of this group, and it appears across factual, moral, procedural, and social domains. The root *tọ́* (straight, correct) is shared across all members.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tọ́* — to be correct, right, proper, straight
- *ó tọ́* — it is correct/right/proper
- *kò tọ́* — it is not correct/right/proper
- *ìtọ́* — correctness, propriety, rightness (abstract nominal)
- *àìtọ́* — incorrectness, impropriety, wrongness
- *ìtọ́sí* — correction, rectification
- *tọ́ sí* — to correct, to set right
- *ọ̀nà tọ́* — correct method, right way
- *ìdáhùn tọ́* — correct answer
- *ìpinnu tọ́* — correct decision
- *àkọsílẹ̀ tọ́* — accurate record
- *ìsọ tọ́* — speaking correctly, truthful speech
- *ìwà tọ́* — upright character, proper conduct
- *Ó tọ́ pé kí a sọ òtítọ́* — it is right that we speak the truth
- *Kò tọ́ pé kí o sọ irọ́* — it is not right that you tell a lie
- *Ó tọ́ gẹ́gẹ́ bí ó ti yẹ* — it is correct as it should be
- *Ẹ tọ́ ọ sí ọ̀nà tó tọ́* — direct him/her to the correct path

**Notes:** This group is the largest and most diffuse. The root *tọ́* appears in factual contexts (Group 1), moral contexts (Group 4), legal contexts (Group 5), and procedural/administrative contexts. The expressions here are those where the evaluative function of *tọ́* is most clearly foregrounded without being reducible to any single domain. *ó tọ́* functions as a discourse marker of affirmation across all these contexts.

---

## GROUP 3

**Label:** Expressions used in the production, presentation, and evaluation of evidence in formal settings

**Definition:** These expressions appear primarily in legal and quasi-legal contexts where the question is what can be shown, demonstrated, or attested to support or undermine a claim. They cluster around the root *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, witness, related to *rí*, to see) and involve distinctions between types of evidence (direct, circumstantial, documentary, hearsay, expert) and between valid and invalid evidence. The person who bears evidence (*ẹlẹ́rìí*) and the act of bearing it (*jẹ́rìí*) are central.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ẹ̀rí* — evidence, proof, testimony, witness
- *ẹ̀rí ojú* — eyewitness evidence, direct evidence
- *ẹ̀rí tààrà* — direct evidence
- *ẹ̀rí àyíká* — circumstantial evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìwé* — documentary evidence
- *ẹ̀rí àfihàn* — demonstrative/physical evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn* — hearsay evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀* — expert/scientific evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀* — identification evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* — test/trial evidence
- *àìní ẹ̀rí* — absence of evidence
- *ẹlẹ́rìí* — witness, one who bears evidence
- *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* — eyewitness
- *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* — false witness, perjurer
- *jẹ́rìí* — to testify, to bear witness
- *ìjẹ́rìí* — testimony, the act of witnessing
- *jẹ́rìí fún* — to testify for/on behalf of
- *jẹ́rìí sí* — to testify against/to
- *olùjẹ́rìí* — testifier, one who bears testimony
- *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi* — there is no sufficient evidence to convict
- *Ẹ̀rí tó wà ń tọ́ka sí i* — the available evidence points to him/her
- *Ó gba ẹ̀rí jọ* — he/she gathered evidence
- *Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* — he/she presented evidence before the court
- *Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ kò tó ohunkóhun* — his/her evidence amounts to nothing
- *Ẹ̀rí tó gbéṣẹ̀ ni a nílò* — it is valid evidence that we need
- *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó lòdì sí i* — there is no evidence against him/her
- *Ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí* — he/she refused to testify
- *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* — interrogation, cross-examination

**Notes:** *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness) connects this group to Group 1 (the falsehood domain). *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* belongs here because its morphological description explicitly frames it as examining what comes from a person's mouth in order to test the accuracy of their account.

---

## GROUP 4

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate the fundamental moral character of a person

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the evaluation is not of a single act or statement but of what a person fundamentally is. The central concept is *ìwà* (character, the manner of being), and the primary contrast is between *ìwà rere* (good character) and *ìwà búburú* (bad character). These expressions appear in proverbs, moral instruction, and everyday moral evaluation, and they assess persons rather than propositions or actions in isolation.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìwà* — character, nature, the manner of being
- *ìwà rere* — good character, virtue, moral excellence
- *ìwà búburú* — bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
- *ìwà tọ́* — upright/proper character
- *ìwà ìbàjẹ́* — corrupt character/conduct
- *ìwà ọmọlúwàbí* — the character of a well-bred/noble person
- *Ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* — it is character that makes a person (proverb)
- *Ìwà rere ló ń gbé ènìyàn ga* — good character is what elevates a person (proverb)
- *Ìwà rere ju ọrọ̀ lọ* — good character surpasses wealth (proverb)
- *Ó ní ìwà rere* — he/she has good character
- *Kò ní ìwà rere* — he/she does not have good character
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ dára* — his/her character is good
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ kò dára* — his/her character is not good
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ hàn gbangba* — his/her true character is clearly visible
- *Ó ń ṣe àgàbàgebè pẹ̀lú ìwà rẹ̀* — he/she is pretending to have good character
- *Ìwà búburú rẹ̀ ló pa á* — his/her bad character was his/her undoing (proverb)
- *àbùkù* — blemish, defect, disgrace, fault (a person or thing with *àbùkù* fails to be what it should be)

**Notes:** *ìwà tọ́* overlaps with Group 2 (the *tọ́* correctness domain), but in usage it appears specifically in moral evaluation of persons rather than in procedural or factual correctness contexts. *àbùkù* is placed here because its usage in the P1 data centers on a person or thing falling short of what it should genuinely be, which aligns with the character-evaluation function of this group, though it also appears in assessments of objects and is not exclusively about persons.

---

## GROUP 5

**Label:** Expressions used in formal certification and official authentication

**Definition:** These expressions appear in administrative, legal, and institutional contexts where the question is whether something has been officially verified, authenticated, or endorsed by a recognized authority. The anchor is *ìjẹ́rìísí* (certification, attestation) and its compounds, along with *ìfọwọ́sí* (endorsement, ratification by hand/signature). The function is not to assert truth in the propositional sense but to establish that something has been formally confirmed as genuine or valid.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìjẹ́rìísí* — certification, attestation, verification, confirmation
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí* — birth certificate
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbéyàwó* — marriage certificate
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkẹ́kọ̀ọ́* — academic certificate, diploma
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìwà* — character reference, attestation of good character
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkọ́lé* — building certification
- *ìfọwọ́sí* — endorsement, ratification, authentication by signature
- *Ó gba ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ wò* — he/she examined/checked his/her certificate
- *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* — his/her certificate is genuine/valid
- *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* — his/her certificate is false/fraudulent
- *Kò ní ìjẹ́rìísí tó yẹ* — he/she does not have the appropriate certification

**Notes:** This group is connected to Group 3 (evidence and testimony) through the shared root *jẹ́rìí*, but the usage context is distinct: Group 3 expressions appear in proceedings where evidence is presented and contested, while Group 5 expressions appear in administrative and credentialing contexts where the question is whether a document or credential is valid. The contrast *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* vs. *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* shows that Group 1 vocabulary (truth/falsehood) is applied to evaluate items in this group.

---

## GROUP 6

**Label:** Expressions used to describe the cognitive act of accepting a claim or person as reliable

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is whether a person has taken something as true or trustworthy — whether they have received and accepted a claim, testimony, or person. The core verbs are *gbàgbọ́* (to believe, to accept as heard/true) and *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (to lean on, rely on), and the group includes their nominalizations and negations. A distinction visible in usage is between *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief, faith, propositional or religious commitment) and *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (practical reliance, dependability).

**Representative expressions:**
- *gbàgbọ́* — to believe, to trust, to have faith in
- *ìgbàgbọ́* — belief, faith, trust, conviction
- *àìgbàgbọ́* — unbelief, disbelief, lack of faith
- *olùgbàgbọ́* — believer, one who has faith
- *aláìgbàgbọ́* — unbeliever, one who lacks faith
- *gbàgbọ́ pé* — to believe that (propositional construction)
- *kò gbàgbọ́* — to disbelieve, to not believe
- *gbàgbọ́ nínú rẹ̀* — to believe in him/her/it
- *Ó gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ láìbéèrè* — he/she believed his/her words without questioning
- *Ó ṣòro láti gbàgbọ́ pé...* — it is difficult to believe that...
- *Ẹ gbàgbọ́ mi* — believe me
- *ìgbàgbọ́ ìsìn* — religious faith, creed
- *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀* — his/her belief has no foundation
- *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára* — his/her faith is strong
- *Ó dúró ṣinṣin nínú ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀* — he/she stood firm in his/her faith
- *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà* — his/her belief changed
- *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — trust, reliance, dependability, confidence in
- *gbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — to rely on, to depend on
- *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* — distrust, lack of confidence in
- *Ó tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀* — it is right/proper to rely on him/her
- *Kò tọ́ láti gbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀* — it is not right to rely on him/her
- *Ó fi ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ sí i* — he/she placed his/her trust in him/her/it
- *Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́* — he/she accepted the truth (serial verb construction)

**Notes:** The distinction between *ìgbàgbọ́* and *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* is visible in usage: *ìgbàgbọ́* takes propositional complements (*gbàgbọ́ pé...*) and appears in religious discourse about faith, while *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* takes personal or institutional objects and appears in assessments of practical reliability. Both are negated with *àì-* and both appear in evaluations of whether trust is warranted (*ó tọ́ láti...*), which connects this group to Group 2.

## GROUP 7

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether a person has the knowledge required to speak reliably

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the credibility or culpability of a person depends on whether they knew or could have known something. The anchor is *ìmọ̀* (knowledge, awareness) and its verbal base *mọ̀* (to know). The contrast between *ìmọ̀* (having knowledge) and *àìmọ̀* (ignorance, not knowing) is used to assess whether a person's testimony is reliable, whether they acted with awareness, or whether their belief is well-founded.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìmọ̀* — knowledge, understanding, awareness
- *mọ̀* — to know, to be aware of, to recognize
- *àìmọ̀* — ignorance, lack of knowledge, unawareness
- *ìmọ̀ jinlẹ̀* / *ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀* — deep/expert/scientific knowledge
- *olùmọ̀* — knowledgeable person, one who knows, expert
- *aláìmọ̀* — ignorant person, one who lacks knowledge
- *mọ̀ pé* — to know that (propositional construction)
- *Ó ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀* — he/she has knowledge of it
- *Kò ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀* — he/she has no knowledge of it
- *Ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ nípa ọ̀rọ̀ yìí kò tó* — his/her knowledge of this matter is insufficient
- *Ó fi ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ hàn* — he/she demonstrated his/her knowledge
- *Ìmọ̀ ni agbára* — knowledge is power (proverbial)
- *Ó wà ní ìmọ̀ pé...* — he/she is aware that...
- *Ó gbà ìmọ̀ lọ́wọ́ olùkọ́ rẹ̀* — he/she received knowledge from his/her teacher
- *ìmọ̀ ara ẹni* — self-knowledge, self-awareness
- *ìgbàgbọ́ àìmọ̀* — belief based on ignorance, uninformed belief

**Notes:** *ìgbàgbọ́ àìmọ̀* (uninformed belief) connects this group to Group 6, appearing in critical discourse about whether a belief is epistemically grounded. *ẹ̀rí ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀* (expert evidence) connects this group to Group 3, where the knowledge of an expert witness is the basis for a particular type of evidence.

---

## GROUP 8

**Label:** Expressions used to assert that something is genuine or authentic rather than fake or imitated

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is whether an object, person, or claim is the real version of what it purports to be, as opposed to a counterfeit, imitation, or pretense. The anchor is *gidi* (genuine, real, authentic). Unlike Group 1 (which concerns whether statements are true) and Group 5 (which concerns formal certification), this group concerns the ontological status of things and persons — whether they are what they appear to be.

**Representative expressions:**
- *gidi* — genuine, real, authentic
- *nǹkan gidi* — the real thing, a genuine thing
- *gidi gan* / *gidi gan-an* — truly genuine, absolutely real
- *àìgidi* — inauthenticity, fakeness
- *Èyí ni nǹkan gidi* — this is the real thing
- *Kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* — it is not the real thing
- *Ó jẹ́ gidi gan-an* — it is truly genuine
- *Ẹ ṣàyẹ̀wò bóyá ó jẹ́ gidi* — examine it to see whether it is genuine

**Notes:** This group is small. *gidi* does not participate in the standard Yoruba nominalization patterns as productively as the verbal roots, and it functions primarily as a modifier. The group connects to Group 5 through the construction *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* (his/her certificate is genuine), where *òdodo* (righteousness/justice) is used to assert authenticity — suggesting that in some contexts *òdodo* and *gidi* overlap in the authenticity domain, though their primary usages are distinct.

---

## GROUP 9

**Label:** Expressions used in moral and religious discourse about the correct path or righteous conduct

**Definition:** These expressions appear in moral, religious, and ethical contexts where the evaluation is of a person's overall orientation or course of conduct against a standard of what is right or divinely sanctioned. They use spatial metaphors (path, direction) and appear in exhortation, condemnation, and description of moral states. The contrast is between being on the right path and departing from it.

**Representative expressions:**
- *òdodo* — justice, righteousness, fairness, rectitude
- *tọ̀nà* — right path, correct way
- *ìtọ́sọ̀nà* — guidance, correct direction on a path
- *ìtọ́nà* — correct path (nominalized)
- *Ó wà lórí tọ̀nà* — he/she is on the right path
- *Ó kúrò lórí tọ̀nà* — he/she departed from the right path
- *Ẹ padà sí tọ̀nà* — return to the right path
- *Òdodo yóò ṣẹ* — justice will prevail
- *Kò sí òdodo nínú ìdájọ́ náà* — there is no justice in that judgment
- *Ẹ ṣe àdájọ́ pẹ̀lú òdodo* — judge with justice
- *ìdájọ́ òdodo* — just/righteous judgment
- *ìṣe òdodo* — righteous action, doing what is right
- *Ó ṣe ohun tó tọ́ àti òdodo* — he/she did what is right and just

**Notes:** *òdodo* appears in both legal contexts (just judgment, fair ruling) and moral/religious contexts (righteousness, righteous conduct). Its placement here reflects the pattern in P1 that it emphasizes moral rightness rather than factual accuracy, and its co-occurrence with *tọ́* in constructions like *ohun tó tọ́ àti òdodo* suggests it occupies a distinct but adjacent position to the *tọ́* correctness domain (Group 2). *ìdájọ́* (judgment, verdict) appears here as a collocate of *òdodo* but also belongs to Group 3/5 contexts; it is not listed as a standalone member of this group.

---

## GROUP 10

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether a person has properly received and acted on information or instruction

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is not just whether a person heard something but whether they genuinely received, understood, and acted on it. The root *gbọ́* (to hear, understand, obey) underlies this group, and the contrast between *ìgbọ́ràn* (obedience, heeding) and *àìgbọ́ràn* (disobedience, failure to heed) shows that hearing and acting are treated as connected in usage. These expressions appear in parenting, religious, governance, and educational discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *gbọ́* — to hear, to listen, to understand, to obey
- *ìgbọ́* — hearing, understanding, comprehension
- *ìgbọ́ràn* — obedience, compliance, heeding
- *àìgbọ́ràn* — disobedience, non-compliance, failure to heed
- *gbọ́ tán* — to have fully heard/understood
- *gbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀* — to heed advice, to listen to counsel
- *gbọ́ àlàyé* — to receive and understand an explanation
- *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* — interrogation (examining what comes from a person's mouth)

**Notes:** This group is distinct from Group 6 (believing/trusting) in that the emphasis here is on reception and compliance rather than on propositional acceptance. The connection between hearing and obeying visible in *ìgbọ́ràn* is a usage pattern specific to this cluster. *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* appears here as well as in Group 3 because its morphological description frames it as examining what a person says in response to questioning — the act of eliciting and evaluating received speech.

---

## GROUP 11

**Label:** Expressions used in testing, examining, and verifying claims or qualities

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where something is subjected to systematic examination to determine whether it meets a standard or is what it claims to be. The anchor is *ìdánwò* (test, examination, trial) and *ìdánilójú* (convincing proof, assurance). These expressions appear in academic, scientific, legal, and commercial contexts. The function is procedural: establishing truth or quality through a process of examination rather than through assertion or testimony.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìdánwò* — test, examination, trial, verification
- *dánwò* — to test, to examine, to try, to verify
- *ìdánwò ìmọ̀* — knowledge test, examination of understanding
- *ìdánwò ìlera* — medical test, health examination
- *àbájáde ìdánwò* — test result, examination outcome
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* — test/trial evidence
- *ìdánilójú* — assurance, convincing proof, establishing in someone's eyes
- *dánilójú* — to convince, to assure, to provide proof to
- *àìdánilójú* — failure to convince, insufficiency of evidence
- *Ẹ ṣàyẹ̀wò bóyá ó jẹ́ gidi* — examine it to see whether it is genuine

**Notes:** *ìdánilójú* is oriented toward the effect on a recipient (convincing someone), while *ìdánwò* is oriented toward the process of examination itself. Both appear in contexts where the adequacy of evidence or quality is being assessed. *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* connects this group to Group 3.

## AMBIGUOUS AND OVERLAPPING ITEMS

The following items from P1 and P2 show usage patterns that place them in more than one group, or that do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**ẹ̀tọ́ / ẹ̀tọ́ ènìyàn / ẹ̀tọ́ ìdìbò**

*ẹ̀tọ́* (right, entitlement, what is due) is derived from *tọ́* and belongs morphologically to Group 2, but its primary usage in the P1b and P2a data is in a distinct domain: legal and political entitlement (human rights, right to vote, right to information). The constructions *àìní ẹ̀tọ́* (lack of rights) and *ìdáàbò ẹ̀tọ́* (protection of rights) do not appear in the same contexts as *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* evaluations of correctness. *ẹ̀tọ́* could be placed in Group 2 on morphological grounds, but its usage pattern is sufficiently distinct that it does not fit cleanly there. It is noted here as an item that straddles the correctness domain and a separate entitlement/rights domain not fully represented in the groups above.

---

**àṣà / àṣà ìbílẹ̀**

*àṣà* (custom, tradition, established practice) appears in P1 as a standard against which conduct is evaluated for propriety or correctness. In usage it appears with *tẹ̀lé* (to follow), *lòdì sí* (to go against), and *kò tọ́* (is not right). It functions as a reference standard for evaluation rather than as an evaluative expression itself. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above: it is not about propositional truth (Group 1), not about a person's character (Group 4), not about evidence (Group 3), and not about the *tọ́* correctness predicate in the same way as Group 2 items. It is noted here as an item that provides a normative standard used by other groups without belonging to any of them.

---

**ìtẹ́lọrùn / àìtẹ́lọrùn**

*ìtẹ́lọrùn* (satisfaction, sufficiency, adequacy) and its negation *àìtẹ́lọrùn* appear in evaluative contexts where the question is whether something meets a required standard. In P1 it is marked [UNCERTAIN] for direct epistemic relevance. In usage it appears in assessments of whether evidence, conduct, or an object is adequate. It overlaps with Group 11 (testing and verifying) and Group 2 (conformity to a standard) but does not fit cleanly into either. It is noted here as a marginal item.

---

**ìbáradọ̀**

*ìbáradọ̀* (consistency, conformity, correspondence, agreement) appears in P1 as relevant to logical and legal assessments of coherence. It is marked [UNCERTAIN]. No clear usage cluster emerges from the P2 data for this item, and it does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. It is noted here as an item without a clear group placement based on available data.

---

**ìtọ́sọ̀nà / tọ̀nà / ìtọ́nà**

These items appear in both Group 2 (correctness/propriety, via the *tọ́* root) and Group 9 (right path, moral/religious orientation). Their placement in Group 9 reflects the observation that in usage they appear specifically in moral and religious discourse about direction and conduct, not in the procedural or factual correctness contexts where other Group 2 items appear. They are listed in both groups with this note.

---

**ìdájọ́**

*ìdájọ́* (judgment, verdict, ruling, adjudication) appears as a collocate of *òdodo* (Group 9: *ìdájọ́ òdodo*, righteous judgment) and in legal contexts adjacent to Group 3 (evidence and testimony) and Group 5 (formal certification). It does not appear as a standalone member of any group in the P1 data, and its usage spans multiple contexts. It is noted here as an item that functions as a site where several groups intersect rather than belonging to any one of them.

---

**ìfọwọ́sí**

*ìfọwọ́sí* (endorsement, ratification, authentication by signature) is placed in Group 5 (formal certification) in the P1 data. Its morphological description (*confirming by hand*) and usage in legal and administrative authentication contexts support this placement. However, it is a less productive item in the data than *ìjẹ́rìísí* and its compounds, and its group membership is noted as tentative.
