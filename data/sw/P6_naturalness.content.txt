# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / ACQUIRING TRUTH

### Pattern
**Kuwa + kweli/ukweli** (become true/truth) or **-thibitika** (become confirmed/established)

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Habari hiyo imekuwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** habari hiyo imekuwa kweli
- **Translation:** "That news has become true / turned out to be true"
- **Usage:** Common when information previously uncertain is confirmed. The periphrastic construction with kuwa (become) + kweli is the standard expression.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Maneno yake yamekuwa ukweli
- **Transliteration:** maneno yake yamekuwa ukweli
- **Translation:** "His/her words have become truth / proven true"
- **Usage:** Used when predictions or claims are validated by events. Ukweli (abstract noun) emphasizes the state of being true.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Jambo hili limethibitika
- **Transliteration:** jambo hili limethibitika
- **Translation:** "This matter has been confirmed / established as true"
- **Usage:** Stative verb form (thibitika < thibitisha) indicating that something has achieved confirmed status. Common in formal/administrative contexts.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

### Notes
The periphrastic strategy (kuwa + predicate) is more productive than dedicated stative verbs. Thibitika is one of the few attested stative forms specifically for truth-confirmation. No simple verb *"kwelika" exists.

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / ACQUIRING FALSEHOOD

### Pattern
**Kuwa + uongo** (become a lie) or **-potoka** (go astray, become erroneous)

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Habari hiyo imekuwa uongo
- **Transliteration:** habari hiyo imekuwa uongo
- **Translation:** "That news has become a lie / turned out to be false"
- **Usage:** Used when information thought to be true is revealed as false. Parallel construction to "kuwa kweli."
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Amepotoka
- **Transliteration:** amepotoka
- **Translation:** "He/she has gone astray / erred / become mistaken"
- **Usage:** Indicates deviation from truth or correctness. Can refer to both factual error and moral transgression. Does not specifically mean "become false" but implies departure from truth.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Taarifa zake zimekuwa si sahihi
- **Transliteration:** taarifa zake zimekuwa si sahihi
- **Translation:** "His/her information has become incorrect / turned out to be inaccurate"
- **Usage:** Uses negative copular construction (si sahihi) rather than positive falsehood term. Common strategy for expressing loss of correctness.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

### Notes
There is no dedicated stative verb meaning "become false" (no *"ongoka" or similar). The periphrastic kuwa + uongo or kuwa + negative predicate is standard. -Potoka indicates deviation but not specifically falsification.

## 3. BECOMING GENUINE / ACQUIRING AUTHENTICITY

### Pattern
**Kuwa + halisi** (become genuine) — **LIMITED ATTESTATION**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Mapenzi yao yamekuwa halisi
- **Transliteration:** mapenzi yao yamekuwa halisi
- **Translation:** "Their love has become genuine / grown authentic"
- **Usage:** Describes development of authenticity in relationships or emotions. Less common than static attribution "ni halisi."
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Bidhaa hii imekuwa halisi baada ya ukaguzi
- **Transliteration:** bidhaa hii imekuwa halisi baada ya ukaguzi
- **Translation:** "This product has become genuine after inspection / been verified as genuine"
- **Usage:** Awkward construction; more natural to say "imegunduliwa kuwa halisi" (has been discovered to be genuine). The change is epistemic (our knowledge) rather than ontological (the object itself).
- **Naturalness:** [UNCERTAIN]

### Notes
"Becoming genuine" is conceptually problematic—objects either are or are not genuine. The construction kuwa + halisi is rare and typically describes epistemic change (discovery of authenticity) or development of sincerity in relationships, not material transformation.

## 4. BECOMING FAKE / ACQUIRING FALSENESS

### Pattern
**Kuwa + bandia** (become fake) — **VERY RARE / UNNATURAL**

### Examples

**No common expression identified.**

### Notes
Objects do not "become fake"—they are manufactured as genuine or counterfeit. The concept of transformation into fakeness is not conventionally expressed. One might say "imegunduliwa kuwa bandia" (has been discovered to be fake), but this is epistemic discovery, not ontological change. The construction *"imekuwa bandia" is semantically anomalous.

## 5. BECOMING COMPLETE / ACHIEVING COMPLETENESS

### Pattern
**-kamilika** (become complete, stative) or **kuwa kamili** (become complete, periphrastic)

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Kazi imekamilika
- **Transliteration:** kazi imekamilika
- **Translation:** "The work has become complete / been completed"
- **Usage:** Standard stative verb for completion. Very common in administrative and project contexts. The -ik- extension creates intransitive stative meaning.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Taarifa zimekuwa kamili
- **Transliteration:** taarifa zimekuwa kamili
- **Translation:** "The information has become complete / is now complete"
- **Usage:** Periphrastic alternative to kamilika. Emphasizes current state of completeness.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Ukweli umekamilika
- **Transliteration:** ukweli umekamilika
- **Translation:** "The truth has become complete / the full truth has emerged"
- **Usage:** Used when additional information completes the picture of truth. Less common than kamilika with concrete objects.
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

### Notes
-Kamilika is one of the most productive stative verbs in the truth domain. It applies readily to tasks, information, and processes. The periphrastic kuwa kamili is also common and interchangeable in many contexts.

## 6. BECOMING CERTAIN / ACQUIRING CERTAINTY

### Pattern
**Kuwa na hakika** (become with certainty, have certainty) or **-hakikika** (become certain, stative) — **LATTER RARE**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Sasa nina hakika
- **Transliteration:** sasa nina hakika
- **Translation:** "Now I am certain / I have certainty now"
- **Usage:** Possessive construction (nina = I have) with hakika. Describes acquisition of certainty. Very common for expressing epistemic change.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Baada ya uchunguzi, tumekuwa na hakika
- **Transliteration:** baada ya uchunguzi tumekuwa na hakika
- **Translation:** "After investigation, we have become certain / acquired certainty"
- **Usage:** Periphrastic construction with kuwa na (become with/have). Standard expression for epistemic transition to certainty.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Jambo limehakikika
- **Transliteration:** jambo limehakikika
- **Translation:** "The matter has become certain / been confirmed"
- **Usage:** Stative verb form (hakikika < hakikisha). Less common than possessive construction but attested in formal contexts.
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

### Notes
The possessive construction (kuwa na hakika, nina hakika) is far more common than the stative verb hakikika. Certainty is typically expressed as something one "has" rather than a state something "becomes."

## 7. BECOMING UNCERTAIN / LOSING CERTAINTY

### Pattern
**Kuwa bila hakika** (become without certainty) or **-poteza hakika** (lose certainty)

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Sasa sina hakika
- **Transliteration:** sasa sina hakika
- **Translation:** "Now I am not certain / I lack certainty now"
- **Usage:** Negative possessive (sina = I don't have). Describes loss or absence of certainty. Very common.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Tumepoteza hakika yetu
- **Transliteration:** tumepoteza hakika yetu
- **Translation:** "We have lost our certainty"
- **Usage:** Uses verb poteza (lose) with hakika as object. Emphasizes active loss of previously held certainty.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Baada ya habari hizo, amekuwa bila hakika
- **Transliteration:** baada ya habari hizo amekuwa bila hakika
- **Translation:** "After that news, he/she has become without certainty / uncertain"
- **Usage:** Periphrastic construction with kuwa bila (become without). Describes transition to uncertainty.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

### Notes
Multiple strategies exist: negative possessive (sina hakika), loss verb (poteza hakika), and privative construction (kuwa bila hakika). All are natural and common.

## 9. BECOMING OBSCURE / LOSING CLARITY

### Pattern
**Kuwa si dhahiri** (become not clear) or **-potea** (disappear, be lost) — **LATTER METAPHORICAL**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Ukweli umekuwa si dhahiri
- **Transliteration:** ukweli umekuwa si dhahiri
- **Translation:** "The truth has become unclear / is no longer evident"
- **Usage:** Negative copular construction. Describes loss of clarity or evidence.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Ukweli umepotea
- **Transliteration:** ukweli umepotea
- **Translation:** "The truth has disappeared / been lost / become obscured"
- **Usage:** Uses verb potea (disappear/be lost) metaphorically. Common for describing obscuration or concealment of truth.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Mambo yamekuwa si wazi
- **Transliteration:** mambo yamekuwa si wazi
- **Translation:** "Matters have become unclear / not clear"
- **Usage:** Uses wazi (clear/open) in negative construction. Common alternative to dhahiri.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

### Notes
No dedicated stative verb for "becoming obscure." Strategies include negative constructions (si dhahiri, si wazi) and metaphorical use of potea (disappear/be lost).

## 10. CAUSING TO BE TRUE / MAKING TRUE

### Pattern
**-thibitisha** (confirm, verify, establish as true) or **-fanya kuwa kweli** (make be true) — **LATTER RARE**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Ushahidi umethibitisha ukweli
- **Transliteration:** ushahidi umethibitisha ukweli
- **Translation:** "The evidence has confirmed the truth / established the truth"
- **Usage:** Causative verb thibitisha (< thabit- root + -ish-). Standard expression for verification and confirmation. Very common.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Uchunguzi umethibitisha habari hizo
- **Transliteration:** uchunguzi umethibitisha habari hizo
- **Translation:** "The investigation has confirmed that news / verified those reports"
- **Usage:** Same causative pattern with different subject. Common in investigative and journalistic contexts.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Matukio yamefanya maneno yake kuwa kweli
- **Transliteration:** matukio yamefanya maneno yake kuwa kweli
- **Translation:** "Events have made his/her words (become) true / proven his/her words true"
- **Usage:** Periphrastic causative with fanya (make/do) + kuwa kweli. Less common than thibitisha but attested.
- **Naturalness:** [MARGINAL]

### Notes
Thibitisha is the dedicated causative for "making true" in the sense of confirming or verifying. The periphrastic fanya kuwa kweli is possible but rare, as it suggests ontological change rather than epistemic confirmation.

## 11. CAUSING TO BE FALSE / MAKING FALSE

### Pattern
**-potoa/potosha** (lead astray, cause to err) or **-fanya kuwa uongo** (make be false) — **LATTER VERY RARE**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Amempotosha
- **Transliteration:** amempotosha
- **Translation:** "He/she has led him/her astray / caused him/her to err / misled him/her"
- **Usage:** Causative verb potosha (< -potoka + -sh-). Indicates causing someone to deviate from truth or correctness. Common in moral discourse.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Propaganda imewapotoa watu
- **Transliteration:** propaganda imewapotoa watu
- **Translation:** "Propaganda has led people astray / misled people"
- **Usage:** Alternative causative form potoa (< -potoka + -a). Similar meaning to potosha. Common in political discourse.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 3:**
- **Swahili:** Amedanganya na kufanya habari kuwa uongo
- **Transliteration:** amedanganya na kufanya habari kuwa uongo
- **Translation:** "He/she has deceived and made the news (become) false / falsified the news"
- **Usage:** Periphrastic construction with fanya kuwa uongo. Very rare; more natural to use specific verbs like -danganya (deceive) or -potoa/potosha (mislead).
- **Naturalness:** [UNCERTAIN]

### Notes
There is no simple causative meaning "make false" or "falsify." The concepts are expressed through verbs of deception (-danganya), misleading (-potoa/-potosha), or causing error. The periphrastic fanya kuwa uongo is semantically odd.

## 16. CAUSING TO DECEIVE / MAKING SOMEONE LIE

### Pattern
**-danganya** (deceive, lie to, trick) — **NO CAUSATIVE OF CAUSATIVE**

### Examples

**Example 1:**
- **Swahili:** Amewadanganya watu
- **Transliteration:** amewadanganya watu
- **Translation:** "He/she has deceived people / lied to people / tricked people"
- **Usage:** Standard verb for deception. Already causative in meaning (causing people to believe falsehoods).
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**Example 2:**
- **Swahili:** Usimdanganye
- **Transliteration:** usimdanganye
- **Translation:** "Don't deceive him/her / don't lie to him/her"
- **Usage:** Negative imperative. Very common in moral instruction.
- **Naturalness:** [CORE]

**No causative of -danganya exists** (no *"danganyisha" meaning "cause someone to deceive others"). The concept would be expressed periphrastically if needed.

### Notes
-Danganya already encodes causation (causing someone to believe falsehoods). There is no productive way to create a second-order causative meaning "cause someone to deceive."

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### PRODUCTIVE PATTERNS IDENTIFIED:

1. **Periphrastic inchoatives with kuwa (become):** Highly productive for truth-state changes (kuwa kweli, kuwa uongo, kuwa halisi, kuwa kamili, etc.). This is the default strategy for "becoming X."

2. **Stative verbs with -ik- extension:** Productive for some roots (kamilika, thibitika, dhihirika, hakikika, danganyika) but not universal. Creates intransitive "become X" meaning.

3. **Causative verbs with -ish-/-sh- extension:** Extremely productive for "making X" (sahihisha, hakikisha, kamilisha, thibitisha, dhihirisha, kosesha, potosha). This is the primary causative strategy.

4. **Possessive constructions for epistemic states:** Productive with kuwa na/nina/sina for certainty (kuwa na hakika, nina hakika, sina hakika). Treats epistemic states as possessions.

5. **Action verbs with truth-domain objects:** Highly productive for dynamic relations to truth (tafuta ukweli, funua ukweli, hifadhi ukweli, kubali ukweli, kataa ukweli, etc.).

6. **Persistence with -baki (remain):** Standard verb for expressing stability of truth-states (ukweli unabaki, uongo unabaki).

7. **Cessation with -acha (stop):** Productive for ending truth-related behaviors (acha kusema uongo, acha kudanganya).

8. **Inception with -anza (begin):** Productive for beginning truth-related behaviors (anza kusema ukweli).

### GAPS AND LIMITATIONS:

1. **No "becoming genuine/fake":** Objects don't ontologically transform between genuine and counterfeit. Only epistemic discovery is expressed (imegunduliwa kuwa halisi/bandia).

2. **No gradual truth-transitions:** Swahili strongly prefers binary truth-states over gradual transitions. Expressions like "becoming more true" are awkward and rare.

3. **No causative of causative:** Verbs like -danganya (already causative: cause to believe falsehoods) don't take further causative derivation.

4. **Limited stative verb productivity:** While -ik- extension exists, it's not productive for all truth-domain roots. Many rely on periphrastic kuwa + predicate instead.

5. **Asymmetry in revelation vs. concealment:** Multiple verbs for revealing truth (funua, gundua, dhihirisha) but fewer for creating falsehood (mainly -danganya and -potoa/potosha).

### NATURALNESS DISTRIBUTION:

- **[CORE] constructions:** 27 patterns (vast majority)
- **[MARGINAL] constructions:** 5 patterns (becoming genuine, becoming more true/false, some stative forms)
- **[UNCERTAIN] constructions:** 2 patterns (making false with fanya kuwa uongo, becoming fake)

The domain shows high productivity in causative and periphrastic constructions, with strong preference for discrete state changes over gradual transitions.
