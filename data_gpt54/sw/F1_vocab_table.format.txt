| Form | Transliteration | Literal Meaning | Common Translations | Usage Description | Epistemic Status |

| kweli | kweli | basic lexical item; ‘truth, true’ | true, truth, real, indeed | Used very commonly to affirm that a statement is accurate or that something is genuinely the case. It also appears in expressions such as *ni kweli* “it is true” and can mark emphatic confirmation in everyday speech. | [CORE] |
| uongo | uongo | noun; ‘falsehood, lie’ | lie, falsehood, untruth | This is the ordinary word for a lie or something false. It is used for false statements, deception, and sometimes broader accusations that a claim or report is not true. | [CORE] |
| -kweli | -kweli | adjectival stem related to *kweli*; ‘true, genuine’ | true, genuine, real | As an adjectival stem, it occurs with agreement, for example *wa kweli*, *ya kweli*, *cha kweli*. It is used for genuine persons or things, not only for true propositions, as in contrasts between authentic and fake or superficial cases. | [CORE] |
| -ongo | -ongo | adjectival stem from *uongo*; ‘false, lying’ | false, untrue, deceitful | This stem is used in agreeing forms such as *si ya/wa kweli bali ya/wa uongo* and in expressions referring to false statements or deceptive behavior. It is less independent than *uongo* but is clearly part of ordinary evaluative usage around falsity. | [CORE] |
| sahihi | sahihi | borrowing from Arabic; ‘correct, exact, valid’ | correct, accurate, right, valid | This is widely used for correctness in answers, forms, spelling, calculations, and official details. It also extends to precision and exactness, especially in educational, bureaucratic, and informational contexts. | [CORE] |
| usahihi | usahihi | *u-* abstract noun from *sahihi*; ‘correctness, accuracy’ | correctness, accuracy, precision | This noun is used when discussing the quality of being correct or accurate, for example in writing, reporting, or measurement. It is common in formal and semi-formal registers, especially where evaluation of exactness is explicit. | [CORE] |
| -sahihi | -sahihi | adjectival use of *sahihi*; ‘correct, proper’ | correct, proper, accurate | In agreement with noun classes, this stem describes answers, procedures, forms, or identifications as correct. It is especially common in school, administrative, and instructional language. | [CORE] |
| halisi | halisi | lexical item; ‘real, actual, genuine’ | genuine, authentic, real, actual | This word is used for things considered genuine rather than fake, artificial, or merely apparent. It can describe material objects, identity, value, or the actual nature of a situation. | [CORE] |
| uhalisia | uhalisia | *u-* abstract noun from *halisi*; ‘reality, realism, genuineness’ | reality, realism, authenticity, genuineness | This noun appears in philosophical, literary, and general evaluative contexts concerning what is real or realistic. It can refer either to reality itself or to realism/authenticity in representation, depending on context. | [CORE] |
| bandia | bandia | lexical item; ‘fake, imitation, counterfeit’ | fake, counterfeit, imitation, bogus | This is used for objects, documents, medicines, money, or credentials judged not to be genuine. It is common in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts where authenticity is in question. | [CORE] |
| feki | feki | borrowing from English “fake” | fake, bogus, counterfeit | This form is common in colloquial and media usage, especially in urban speech, for fake goods, fake accounts, or fake information. It overlaps with *bandia* but often sounds more informal and contemporary. | [CORE] |
| aminifu | aminifu | from root related to trust/faith; ‘trustworthy, faithful, honest’ | trustworthy, faithful, honest, reliable | This adjective is used for persons whose conduct or statements can be relied on. It often carries a moral dimension, evaluating character as dependable and sincere rather than merely factually correct. | [CORE] |
| uaminifu | uaminifu | *u-* abstract noun from *aminifu*; ‘trustworthiness, honesty, faithfulness’ | honesty, trustworthiness, integrity, faithfulness | This noun is common in moral, religious, and social evaluation of character. It refers to the quality of being reliable and honest in speech, conduct, and obligations. | [CORE] |
| kuaminika | kuaminika | verb with stative/passive-like extension from *-amini*; ‘to be believable/trustworthy’ | be credible, be trustworthy, be reliable | This verb is used of people, reports, institutions, and evidence that can be trusted or believed. It is especially useful when evaluating whether a source or claim deserves confidence. | [CORE] |
| aminika | aminika | adjective/participle from *kuaminika*; ‘credible, trustworthy’ | credible, trustworthy, reliable | This form commonly describes testimony, sources, witnesses, and institutions as believable or dependable. It is frequent in journalism, public discourse, and formal evaluation of reliability. | [CORE] |
| -fanana | -fanana | verbal root; ‘resemble each other, be alike’ | resemble, be similar, look alike | This verb is used when judging whether one thing matches or resembles another in appearance or character. It is relevant in evaluating likeness, comparison, and representational similarity. | [CORE] |
| mfano | mfano | noun; ‘example, model, likeness’ | example, model, likeness, image | Besides its very common sense “example,” this word also appears in contexts of likeness, pattern, or representation. It can be used when something is judged as a model or resemblance of something else. | [CORE] |
| asili | asili | lexical item; ‘origin, nature, essence, original state’ | nature, essence, origin, original, authentic | This word is used for what something fundamentally is, where it comes from, or its original condition. It is common in discussions of identity, authenticity, and essential character, including philosophical and cultural contexts. | [CORE] |
| ya asili | ya asili | ‘of origin/of the original kind’ | original, natural, authentic, genuine | This fixed expression is often used for products, materials, or conditions considered natural or original rather than artificial or adulterated. It can also mark authenticity in contrast with imitation. | [CORE] |
| haki | haki | lexical item; ‘right, justice, due claim’ | right, justice, rightful claim, fairness | This is a central term in moral, legal, and social evaluation of what is right or just. It can refer both to abstract justice and to a person’s right or entitlement. | [CORE] |
| -enye haki | -enye haki | possessive construction ‘having justice/right’ | just, fair, rightful, legitimate | This expression is used to describe actions, decisions, or persons as fair or just. It is especially natural where Swahili uses a descriptive construction rather than a simple inherited adjective. | [CORE] |
| batili | batili | borrowing of Arabic origin; ‘void, null, invalid, false’ | invalid, null, void, false | This term is common in legal, religious, and formal evaluative language for something lacking validity or standing. It can apply to arguments, contracts, claims, or acts judged not to hold as right or valid. | [CORE] |
| halali | halali | borrowing from Arabic; ‘lawful, permitted, legitimate’ | lawful, legitimate, permissible, valid | This word is used in legal, religious, and everyday contexts for what is permitted or legitimate. It evaluates conduct, status, or possession in terms of rightness and acceptability under a norm. | [CORE] |
| haramu | haramu | borrowing from Arabic; ‘forbidden, illicit, illegitimate’ | forbidden, illegal, illicit, illegitimate | This is the common opposite of *halali* in many contexts. It is used for conduct, goods, earnings, or relations judged wrong, unlawful, or religiously prohibited. | [CORE] |
| dhahiri | dhahiri | borrowing from Arabic; ‘apparent, evident, manifest’ | evident, obvious, apparent, manifest | This word is used when the truth or character of something is considered plainly visible or unmistakable. It often appears in formal or elevated style when evaluating whether something is clearly the case. | [CORE] |
