## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **egi(a)** — Transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 4 P1 items  
(*egia, egiazko, egiaz, egiazki*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the core notion of “truth,” “what is true,” or “in truth,” and serves as the main native lexical base for truth and truth-derived evaluation.

---

### 2. **zuzen-** — Transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 3 P1 items  
(*zuzen, zuzena, zuzentasun*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the notion of “right/correct/straight,” covering correctness, rectitude, and procedural or formal rightness.

---

### 3. **gezur-** — Transliteration: —
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items  
(*gezur, gezurrezko*)

**Core meaning contribution:**  
Contributes the notion of “lie, falsehood, falsity,” especially in relation to deception, untruth, and fabricated or bogus things.

---

## Step 2 — Root expansion

## Root: **egi(a)**

Note: this root has a moderate family in common use, but not a very large one.

### 1. **egia**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** simplex noun  
**Natural English translation:** truth  
**Context of use:** Basic noun for “truth,” used in everyday, moral, philosophical, and religious contexts. It can refer to the truth of a statement or truth in a broader abstract sense.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. **egiaz**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* + adverbial/instrumental ending  
**Natural English translation:** truly; really; in truth  
**Context of use:** Common adverb in speech and writing for confirming sincerity or factuality. Often used emphatically in discourse.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. **egiazko**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* + *-zko*  
**Natural English translation:** true; genuine; real  
**Context of use:** Used for things regarded as true or genuine, including statements, feelings, relationships, and objects. Common in both ordinary and evaluative language.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. **egiazki**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* + *-zki*  
**Natural English translation:** truly; genuinely  
**Context of use:** More literary or formal adverb than *egiaz*. Found especially in elevated, older, or stylistically marked prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 5. **egiatan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* + locative/adverbial ending  
**Natural English translation:** in truth; actually  
**Context of use:** A recognized adverbial form, but much less common than *egiaz* or *benetan*. It appears mainly in formal, literary, or somewhat archaizing usage.  
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 6. **egiantzekoa / egiantz**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** historically related truth-form  
**Natural English translation:** truth; true doctrine / truth-like form  
**Context of use:** These are older or literary forms associated with “truth,” especially in historical or religious texts. They are not part of ordinary present-day everyday usage.  
**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

### 7. **egia esan**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + *esan* “say”  
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth; truth be told  
**Context of use:** Very common fixed expression in conversation and writing. It introduces frankness, correction, or candid personal evaluation.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. **egia esateko**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + *esateko* “to say / for saying”  
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth; honestly  
**Context of use:** Common discourse marker used much like English “to tell the truth.” It often introduces a candid admission or a softened disagreement.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9. **egia da**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + copula  
**Natural English translation:** it is true  
**Context of use:** Extremely common clause for conceding or affirming that something is true. Since this is a fixed evaluative expression rather than a mere inflectional variant of a P1 item, it is regularly used in argumentation and discussion.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10. **egia ote den**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + interrogative/evidential structure  
**Natural English translation:** whether it is true  
**Context of use:** Common embedded expression in reported doubt, inquiry, and evidential evaluation. Used in both everyday and formal prose.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11. **egia bilatu**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + *bilatu* “seek”  
**Natural English translation:** seek the truth  
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, journalistic, legal, and moral contexts. It can refer to inquiry, investigation, or existential searching.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12. **egia jakin**
**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *egia* “truth” + *jakin* “know”  
**Natural English translation:** know the truth; learn the truth  
**Context of use:** Common in narratives, testimony, and interpersonal situations where concealed facts are revealed. Also used in legal and journalistic contexts.

**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** definite form of *zuzen*

## Root: **gezur-**

Note: this root has a solid but smaller family in common use.

**Transliteration:** —

**Transliteration:** —  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** *gezur* + *-zko*

**Literal morpheme breakdown:** definite form of *gezur*
