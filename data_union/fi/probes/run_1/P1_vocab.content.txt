# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

**1. tosi**
- **Form:** tosi
- **Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
- **Literal meaning:** true, real (adjective/adverb; cognate with Estonian *tõsi*)
- **Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, really
- **Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and to intensify claims about reality. Appears in both propositional contexts (*se on tosi* — "it is true") and as an adverbial intensifier (*tosi hyvä* — "really good"). Participates in productive derivation: *totuus* (truth), *todistaa* (to prove/witness), *toteuttaa* (to realize/implement).
- **[CORE]**

---

**2. totuus**
- **Form:** totuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** truth (derived from *tosi* + nominalizing suffix *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** truth, the truth
- **Usage:** The standard nominal form for truth as an abstract property or as a specific true state of affairs. Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday discourse (*kertoa totuus* — "to tell the truth"). Appears in compounds such as *totuudenmukaisuus* (truthfulness, accuracy).
- **[CORE]**

---

**3. aito**
- **Form:** aito
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** genuine, authentic (adjective; etymology debated, possibly from older Germanic borrowing)
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, original
- **Usage:** Applied to objects, persons, emotions, and cultural products to assert that they are not imitations or counterfeits (*aito nahka* — "genuine leather"; *aito suomalainen* — "a true/authentic Finn"). Contrasts with *väärennös* (forgery) and *jäljitelmä* (imitation). Productive in derivation: *aitous* (authenticity), *aitoutua* (to become authentic).
- **[CORE]**

---

**4. oikea**
- **Form:** oikea
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** right, correct (adjective; also means "right" as in direction)
- **Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper, true, the real
- **Usage:** Used to evaluate correctness of answers, actions, and identities (*oikea vastaus* — "the correct answer"; *oikea henkilö* — "the right person"). Overlaps semantically with *tosi* in some contexts but emphasizes normative or identificatory rightness rather than propositional truth. Derives *oikeellisuus* (correctness), *oikeus* (right, justice, court).
- **[CORE]**

---

**5. oikeellisuus**
- **Form:** oikeellisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** correctness, accuracy (derived from *oikea* + *-llinen* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, propriety
- **Usage:** Used in formal, administrative, and linguistic contexts to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard or norm (*kielellinen oikeellisuus* — "linguistic correctness"). More formal and abstract than *oikea* itself. Common in legal and bureaucratic language.
- **[CORE]**

---

**6. todellinen**
- **Form:** todellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** real, actual (derived from *tosi/tode-* + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** real, actual, genuine, true
- **Usage:** Asserts that something exists or is the case in reality, as opposed to being apparent, imagined, or nominal (*todellinen syy* — "the real reason"; *todellinen elämä* — "real life"). Contrasts with *näennäinen* (apparent, seeming) and *kuvitteellinen* (imaginary). Derives *todellisuus* (reality).
- **[CORE]**

---

**7. todellisuus**
- **Form:** todellisuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** reality (from *todellinen* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** reality, actuality
- **Usage:** The standard term for reality as a domain against which claims or representations are evaluated. Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts (*todellisuudessa* — "in reality"). Appears in compounds and collocations evaluating correspondence between representation and fact.
- **[CORE]**

---

**8. luotettava**
- **Form:** luotettava
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (passive present participle of *luottaa* — "to trust")
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible
- **Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, instruments, and institutions to assert that they can be trusted or depended upon (*luotettava lähde* — "a reliable source"; *luotettava henkilö* — "a trustworthy person"). Derives *luotettavuus* (reliability, trustworthiness). Contrasts with *epäluotettava* (unreliable).
- **[CORE]**

---

**9. luotettavuus**
- **Form:** luotettavuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** reliability, trustworthiness (from *luotettava* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
- **Usage:** The nominal form used in formal evaluations of sources, methods, witnesses, and systems. Frequently appears in academic, journalistic, and legal discourse (*tutkimuksen luotettavuus* — "the reliability of the research"). Closely related to but distinct from *uskottavuus* (credibility, plausibility).
- **[CORE]**

---

**10. uskottava**
- **Form:** uskottava
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** believable, credible (passive present participle of *uskoa* — "to believe")
- **Typical English translations:** credible, believable, plausible, convincing
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether a claim, person, or representation is such that it can or should be believed (*uskottava selitys* — "a credible explanation"; *uskottava todistaja* — "a credible witness"). Derives *uskottavuus* (credibility). Distinct from *luotettava* in that it concerns believability rather than dependability.
- **[CORE]**

---

**11. pätevä**
- **Form:** pätevä
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** valid, competent, qualified (present participle of *päteä* — "to be valid, to apply, to be competent")
- **Typical English translations:** valid, competent, qualified, sound
- **Usage:** Applied to arguments, credentials, persons, and legal instruments to assert that they meet required standards or hold in the relevant domain (*pätevä peruste* — "a valid reason/ground"; *pätevä opettaja* — "a qualified teacher"). Derives *pätevyys* (validity, competence). Used in both logical and professional evaluation contexts.
- **[CORE]**

---

**12. pätevyys**
- **Form:** pätevyys
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** validity, competence (from *pätevä* + *-yys*)
- **Typical English translations:** validity, competence, qualification, soundness
- **Usage:** The nominal form used to evaluate whether a person holds the required qualifications or whether an argument or instrument meets formal standards (*todistuksen pätevyys* — "the validity of a certificate"). Appears in legal, academic, and professional contexts. Overlaps with *oikeellisuus* in formal evaluation but emphasizes applicability and qualification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**13. väärä**
- **Form:** väärä
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** wrong, false, crooked (adjective; also has spatial sense of "crooked, bent")
- **Typical English translations:** wrong, false, incorrect, fake
- **Usage:** The primary antonym of both *oikea* and *tosi* in evaluative contexts (*väärä vastaus* — "a wrong answer"; *väärä henkilöllisyys* — "a false identity"). Used across moral, logical, and factual evaluation. Derives *vääryys* (wrongness, injustice), *väärentää* (to falsify, to forge), *väärennös* (forgery).
- **[CORE]**

---

**14. epätosi**
- **Form:** epätosi
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** untrue, false (negation prefix *epä-* + *tosi*)
- **Typical English translations:** untrue, false
- **Usage:** The direct negation of *tosi*, used to assert that a statement does not correspond to reality. More explicitly propositional in use than *väärä*, and more formal in register. Appears in logical and philosophical discourse; less common in casual speech than *väärä* or *ei totta*.
- **[CORE]**

---

**15. väärentää**
- **Form:** väärentää
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to falsify, to forge (derived from *väärä* + causative suffix *-ntää*)
- **Typical English translations:** to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit, to adulterate
- **Usage:** Denotes the deliberate act of making something false, counterfeit, or fraudulent (*väärentää asiakirja* — "to forge a document"; *väärentää historiaa* — "to falsify history"). Used in legal and moral contexts. Derives *väärennös* (forgery, counterfeit) and *väärentäjä* (forger).
- **[CORE]**

---

**16. väärennös**
- **Form:** väärennös
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** forgery, counterfeit (from *väärentää* + result nominalization *-ös*)
- **Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeit, fake, falsification
- **Usage:** Refers to the product of deliberate falsification — a forged document, counterfeit object, or fabricated representation (*taulun väärennös* — "a forgery of a painting"). Used in legal, art-authentication, and documentary contexts. Contrasts with *aito* (genuine) and *alkuperäinen* (original).
- **[CORE]**

---

**17. alkuperäinen**
- **Form:** alkuperäinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** original (from *alkuperä* — "origin, source" + *-inen*)
- **Typical English translations:** original, authentic, primary, source
- **Usage:** Asserts that something is the first or source version rather than a copy, translation, or derivative (*alkuperäinen teos* — "the original work"; *alkuperäinen sopimus* — "the original contract"). Used in legal, artistic, and documentary evaluation. Overlaps with *aito* but emphasizes temporal and genealogical priority rather than non-counterfeit status.
- **[CORE]**

---

**18. tarkka**
- **Form:** tarkka
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** precise, accurate, exact (adjective)
- **Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, careful
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether a measurement, description, translation, or observation corresponds closely to its object or standard (*tarkka mittaus* — "an accurate measurement"; *tarkka kuvaus* — "an accurate description"). Derives *tarkkuus* (accuracy, precision) and *tarkistaa* (to check, to verify). Emphasizes closeness of correspondence rather than binary truth.
- **[CORE]**

---

**19. tarkkuus**
- **Form:** tarkkuus
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** accuracy, precision (from *tarkka* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness
- **Usage:** The nominal form used to evaluate the degree of correspondence between a representation, measurement, or description and its referent (*mittauksen tarkkuus* — "the accuracy of the measurement"). Used in scientific, technical, and evaluative discourse. Distinct from *oikeellisuus* in that it admits of degrees.
- **[CORE]**

---

**20. tarkistaa**
- **Form:** tarkistaa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to check, to verify, to inspect (from *tarkka* + causative/factitive suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** to check, to verify, to inspect, to review
- **Usage:** Denotes the act of examining something to determine whether it is accurate, correct, or in order (*tarkistaa tiedot* — "to verify the information"; *tarkistaa passi* — "to check the passport"). Used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts. Derives *tarkistus* (check, verification, review).
- **[CORE]**

---

**21. todistaa**
- **Form:** todistaa
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** to prove, to testify, to witness (from *tosi/tode-* + *-staa*)
- **Typical English translations:** to prove, to demonstrate, to testify, to witness, to attest
- **Usage:** Used in legal contexts for giving testimony (*todistaa oikeudessa* — "to testify in court") and in logical/epistemic contexts for proving a claim (*todistaa väite* — "to prove a claim"). Derives *todistus* (proof, certificate, testimony), *todistaja* (witness), *todiste* (piece of evidence). Central to both legal and epistemic evaluation.
- **[CORE]**

---

**22. todiste**
- **Form:** todiste
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** piece of evidence (from *todistaa*, result nominalization)
- **Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, piece of evidence
- **Usage:** Refers to a specific item of evidence used to establish the truth or falsity of a claim, particularly in legal contexts (*todiste rikoksesta* — "evidence of a crime"). Distinct from *todistus* (which can mean a certificate or testimony as a whole) in referring to individual evidentiary items. Used in legal and investigative discourse.
- **[CORE]**

---

**23. näennäinen**
- **Form:** näennäinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** apparent, seeming (from *näkyä* — "to appear, to be visible" + *-nnäinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible, superficial
- **Usage:** Describes something that appears to be the case but may not be, or that has the surface form of a quality without its substance (*näennäinen yksimielisyys* — "apparent consensus"; *näennäinen rehellisyys* — "ostensible honesty"). Used to signal a gap between appearance and reality. Contrasts with *todellinen* (real, actual).
- **[CORE]**

---

**24. rehellinen**
- **Form:** rehellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** honest (from *rehellis-*; etymology uncertain, possibly from Swedish *redlig*)
- **Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, straightforward
- **Usage:** Evaluates persons and conduct as characterized by honesty and non-deception (*rehellinen vastaus* — "an honest answer"; *rehellinen ihminen* — "an honest person"). Derives *rehellisyys* (honesty). Used in moral evaluation of persons and their statements or actions. Contrasts with *epärehellinen* (dishonest) and *valheellinen* (mendacious).
- **[CORE]**

---

**25. valheellinen**
- **Form:** valheellinen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** mendacious, false (from *valhe* — "lie" + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** false, mendacious, lying, deceitful
- **Usage:** Evaluates statements or persons as characterized by lying or deliberate falsehood (*valheellinen todistus* — "false testimony"; *valheellinen väite* — "a mendacious claim"). More formal and explicitly moral in register than *väärä*. Used in legal and ethical contexts. Derives from *valhe* (lie) and *valehdella* (to lie).
- **[CORE]**

---

**26. oikeudenmukainen**
- **Form:** oikeudenmukainen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** just, fair (from *oikeuden* — genitive of *oikeus* "justice/right" + *mukainen* — "in accordance with")
- **Typical English translations:** just, fair, righteous
- **Usage:** Evaluates persons, decisions, and systems as conforming to principles of justice or fairness (*oikeudenmukainen tuomio* — "a just verdict"; *oikeudenmukainen kohtelu* — "fair treatment"). Used in moral, legal, and political discourse. Derives *oikeudenmukaisuus* (justice, fairness). Distinct from *oikea* in emphasizing procedural and distributive justice rather than simple correctness.
- **[CORE]**

---

**27. asianmukainen**
- **Form:** asianmukainen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** proper, appropriate (from *asian* — genitive of *asia* "matter, thing" + *mukainen* — "in accordance with")
- **Typical English translations:** proper, appropriate, adequate, correct
- **Usage:** Evaluates whether something is done or constituted in the manner appropriate to the relevant matter or standard (*asianmukainen menettely* — "proper procedure"; *asianmukainen dokumentaatio* — "proper documentation"). Used heavily in administrative, legal, and professional contexts. Emphasizes procedural and normative correctness.
- **[CORE]**

---

**28. perusteeton**
- **Form:** perusteeton
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** unfounded, groundless (from *peruste* — "ground, basis, reason" + *-ton* privative suffix)
- **Typical English translations:** unfounded, groundless, unsubstantiated, baseless
- **Usage:** Evaluates claims, accusations, or decisions as lacking adequate grounds or justification (*perusteeton syytös* — "an unfounded accusation"; *perusteeton väite* — "an unsubstantiated claim"). Used in legal, academic, and argumentative contexts. Contrasts with *perusteltu* (justified, well-founded).
- **[CORE]**

---

**29. perusteltu**
- **Form:** perusteltu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** justified, well-founded (past passive participle of *perustella* — "to justify, to give reasons for")
- **Typical English translations:** justified, well-founded, substantiated, reasoned
- **Usage:** Evaluates claims, decisions, or beliefs as supported by adequate grounds or reasoning (*perusteltu päätös* — "a justified decision"; *perusteltu epäilys* — "a well-founded suspicion"). Used in legal, academic, and deliberative contexts. Contrasts with *perusteeton* (unfounded). Derives from *peruste* (ground, basis).
- **[CORE]**

---

**30. epäaito**
- **Form:** epäaito
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** inauthentic, not genuine (negation prefix *epä-* + *aito*)
- **Typical English translations:** inauthentic, not genuine, fake, spurious
- **Usage:** The direct negation of *aito*, used to assert that something lacks genuine character or is a counterfeit or imitation (*epäaito tunne* — "an inauthentic feeling"; *epäaito tuote* — "a non-genuine product"). Less common than *aito* itself but attested in both everyday and philosophical discourse. Derives *epäaitous* (inauthenticity).
- **[CORE]**
