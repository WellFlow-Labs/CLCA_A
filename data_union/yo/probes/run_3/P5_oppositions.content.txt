# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Oppositions below are extracted strictly from patterns documented in P1 through P4b. Each opposition is supported by explicit attestation in the context. Where the context signals uncertainty about a form or its usage, this is reflected in the epistemic status. No opposition has been inferred beyond what the data directly supports. The number of oppositions reflects the data; no symmetry has been imposed.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** òtítọ́
**Term B:** irọ́
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ / irọ́
**English translation:** truth, truthfulness / lie, falsehood, untruth
**Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly identifies *irọ́* as "the standard antonym of *òtítọ́*"; the two terms appear together in proverbs (*irọ́ àti òtítọ́ kò lè papọ̀ gbé*, "lie and truth cannot coexist"), in parallel verbal collocations (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), and in legal and moral discourse as the primary binary of honest versus false speech. The contrast is between a statement that corresponds to what is the case and one that does not.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** sọ òtítọ́
**Term B:** sọ irọ́
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́ / sọ irọ́
**English translation:** to speak the truth, to tell the truth / to tell a lie, to lie
**Description of contrast:** Both are verbal collocations built on the same verb *sọ* ("to say, to speak") with contrasting nominal objects; P2b documents them as the standard antonymic pair for truth-telling versus lying across all registers. The constructional parallelism is exact: same verb, contrasting object.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** òtítọ́ ni
**Term B:** kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni / kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**English translation:** it is the truth; that is true / there is no truth in his/her words
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as fixed assertive constructions used in legal testimony and everyday dispute; the first affirms the truth of a statement, the second denies it. The contrast is between existential affirmation and existential denial of truth within a specific speech act or account.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** ìmọ̀
**Term B:** àìmọ̀
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ / àìmọ̀
**English translation:** knowledge, understanding, awareness / ignorance, lack of knowledge, not knowing
**Description of contrast:** P2a documents *àìmọ̀* as "the standard negated nominal form of *ìmọ̀*," derived by the productive *àì-* prefix; P3 assigns both to Group 6 (expressions used in describing the basis for knowing something) as antonyms. The contrast is between the state of knowing and the state of not knowing.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Term B:** àìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ / àìgbàgbọ́
**English translation:** belief, faith, trust, conviction / unbelief, lack of faith, disbelief
**Description of contrast:** P2a documents *àìgbàgbọ́* as derived from *ìgbàgbọ́* via the *àì-* negative prefix and describes it as "the antonym of *ìgbàgbọ́* in religious and everyday discourse." The contrast is between the state of believing or having faith and the state of not believing.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Term B:** àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**Transliteration:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé / àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé
**English translation:** trust, reliability, dependability / unreliability, untrustworthiness, lack of dependability
**Description of contrast:** P2a documents *àìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* as derived from *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* via the *àì-* prefix and used "in social and moral evaluation to describe the quality of being unreliable or untrustworthy." The contrast is between being a person or institution that can be relied upon and one that cannot.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** ìwà rere
**Term B:** ìwà burúkú
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / ìwà burúkú
**English translation:** good character, virtue, moral goodness / bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
**Description of contrast:** P1 identifies *ìwà burúkú* as "the standard antonym of *ìwà rere*"; both are fixed attributive expressions built on the same head noun *ìwà* with contrasting evaluative modifiers (*rere* "good" / *burúkú* "bad, evil"). P3 places them at opposite poles of Group 4. The contrast is between a person's fundamental nature being judged morally good versus morally deficient.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** ojúlówó
**Term B:** àgàbàgebè
**Transliteration:** ojúlówó / àgàbàgebè
**English translation:** genuine, authentic, real, legitimate / pretense, sham, fake behavior, hypocrisy
**Description of contrast:** P3 identifies these as occupying opposite sides of Group 3 (expressions used to evaluate whether a person or thing is what it claims or appears to be); *ojúlówó* describes something or someone that is genuinely what it presents itself as, while *àgàbàgebè* describes conduct or presentation that is false or performative. The contrast is between authentic self-presentation and sham self-presentation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** ojúlówó
**Term B:** àdàwọ̀
**Transliteration:** ojúlówó / àdàwọ̀
**English translation:** genuine, authentic, real / counterfeit, imitation
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents the construction *èyí ni ohun ojúlówó, kì í ṣe àdàwọ̀* ("this is the genuine article, it is not a counterfeit/imitation"), which explicitly pairs *ojúlówó* and *àdàwọ̀* as contrasting terms in commercial and evaluative discourse. The contrast is between an object that is what it claims to be and one that imitates or falsely represents itself as such.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** ìfarahàn
**Term B:** ìwà
**Transliteration:** ìfarahàn / ìwà
**English translation:** appearance, manifestation, how something presents itself / character, nature, being, essence
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents the construction *ìfarahàn rẹ̀ kò dára, ṣùgbọ́n ìwà rẹ̀ dára* ("his/her appearance is not good, but his/her character is good"), which explicitly opposes surface presentation (*ìfarahàn*) to underlying essential nature (*ìwà*). P3 notes that "the gap between *ìfarahàn* and underlying reality is a recurrent theme in Yoruba proverbial thought." The contrast is between how something or someone presents itself and what it fundamentally is.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** ẹ̀rí ojú
**Term B:** ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ẹ̀rí ojú / ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́
**English translation:** eyewitness evidence, direct visual evidence / hearsay evidence, evidence based on hearing
**Description of contrast:** P1b and P2a document both as specific types of *ẹ̀rí* distinguished in legal contexts; P3 notes they are used "to distinguish evidence based on what was heard (as opposed to directly seen)." The contrast is between evidence grounded in direct visual observation and evidence grounded in reported speech or hearing.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** ó kọjá ìdánwò náà
**Term B:** kò kọjá ìdánwò náà
**Transliteration:** ó kọjá ìdánwò náà / kò kọjá ìdánwò náà
**English translation:** he/she passed the test/examination / he/she did not pass the test; he/she failed the examination
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both constructions as a contrasting pair using the same verb *kọjá* ("to pass") with and without the negator *kò*; both are described as "widely attested in educational and evaluative discourse." The contrast is between successful and unsuccessful completion of a verification or examination process.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** ìdájọ́ náà tọ́
**Term B:** ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́
**Transliteration:** ìdájọ́ náà tọ́ / ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́
**English translation:** the judgment is correct/just / the judgment is not correct/just; the verdict is wrong
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as a contrasting pair using the stative predicate *tọ́* ("to be correct, right") with and without *kò*; the second is described as used "to challenge the justice or correctness of a verdict." The contrast is between affirming and denying the correctness of a formal judgment.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́
**Term B:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó
**Transliteration:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́ / ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó
**English translation:** trust in him/her has broken / his/her faith/belief has collapsed
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as structurally parallel constructions describing the destruction of trust or belief, using different verbs of collapse (*fọ́* "to break/shatter" for *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*; *wó* "to collapse/fall" for *ìgbàgbọ́*) and notes they "appear in similar contexts of betrayal or disillusionment." This is not a semantic opposition between the two constructions but a structural parallel that distinguishes the two concepts (*ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* versus *ìgbàgbọ́*) through different collapse metaphors. The contrast is between the breaking of interpersonal reliance and the collapse of internal conviction, expressed through non-identical but parallel constructions.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó
**Term B:** ẹ̀rí wà tó fi hàn pé...
**Transliteration:** kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó / ẹ̀rí wà tó fi hàn pé...
**English translation:** there is no sufficient evidence / there is evidence that shows that...
**Description of contrast:** P4a and P2b document both as the standard means of asserting or denying the existence of evidence in legal and evaluative discourse, using the existential *wà* versus the negative existential *kò sí*. The contrast is between affirming and denying the existence of adequate evidentiary support for a claim.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** ìtànjẹ́
**Term B:** irọ́
**Transliteration:** ìtànjẹ́ / irọ́
**English translation:** deception, fraud, trickery, misrepresentation / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** P1 distinguishes these as "closely related in usage" but notes that *ìtànjẹ́* "carries a stronger connotation of intentional manipulation" and is specifically associated with instrumental deception for material ends (as in *wọ́n fi ìtànjẹ́ gbà owó rẹ̀*, "they used deception to take his/her money"), a construction not attested with *irọ́*. P3 places them in the same Group 8 but marks *ìtànjẹ́* as more specific. The contrast is between simple false statement and deliberate, instrumentalized deception with harmful intent.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** ìtànjẹ́
**Term B:** àgàbàgebè
**Transliteration:** ìtànjẹ́ / àgàbàgebè
**English translation:** deception, fraud, trickery / pretense, sham, fake behavior, hypocrisy
**Description of contrast:** P3 distinguishes these within Group 8: "*ìtànjẹ́* typically involves material harm or gain (fraud, swindling), while *àgàbàgebè* describes social performance — presenting oneself as virtuous or sincere when one is not." The contrast is between deception oriented toward material ends and deception oriented toward social self-presentation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** gidi
**Term B:** ojúlówó
**Transliteration:** gidi / ojúlówó
**English translation:** real, genuine, actual (colloquial/informal) / genuine, authentic, real, legitimate (across registers)
**Description of contrast:** P3 identifies these as "near-synonyms" in Group 3 but distinguishes them by register: "*gidi* is more colloquial and informal, while *ojúlówó* appears across formal and informal registers." This is not a semantic opposition but a register-based distributional contrast between two terms that share the same evaluative meaning.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** ìmọ̀
**Term B:** ìgbàgbọ́
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ / ìgbàgbọ́
**English translation:** knowledge, understanding, awareness / belief, faith, trust, conviction
**Description of contrast:** P3 distinguishes these within Group 6: "*ìmọ̀* is used for knowledge that is or could be verified, while *ìgbàgbọ́* is used for conviction that rests on acceptance or faith, including religious faith." The contrast is between a state grounded in verifiable information and a state grounded in acceptance or trust.
**Contrast type:** lexical

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** ìwà àìtọ́
**Term B:** ìwà rere / òdodo
**Transliteration:** ìwà àìtọ́ / ìwà rere; òdodo
**English translation:** incorrect conduct, improper character, wrongful behavior / good character, virtue; justice, righteousness
**Description of contrast:** P2a documents *ìwà àìtọ́* as used "in moral and legal contexts to describe conduct that deviates from what is correct or proper" and notes it as "a compound that brings together the *ìwà* and *tọ* roots." P3 notes it appears at the boundary between the character domain and the correctness domain. The contrast is between conduct that conforms to what is correct and right (*ìwà rere*, *òdodo*) and conduct that deviates from it (*ìwà àìtọ́*). The opposition is partial: *ìwà àìtọ́* contrasts with *ìwà rere* on the character axis and with *òdodo* on the correctness-of-conduct axis, but these are not fully equivalent contrasts.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** kò jẹ́ + [NOMINAL PREDICATE]
**Term B:** kò + [STATIVE VERBAL PREDICATE]
**Transliteration:** Kò jẹ́ ohun ojúlówó. / Ìdájọ́ náà kò tọ́.
**English translation:** It is not a genuine thing. / The judgment is not correct.
**Description of contrast:** P4a and P4b document these as two structurally distinct sub-patterns of negative attribution that are "not interchangeable": when the predicate is nominal, the copula *jẹ́* is obligatory (*kò jẹ́* + nominal); when the predicate is a stative verb, *kò* precedes the verb directly with no copula. The contrast is between two constructional strategies for negation that are conditioned by the grammatical category of the predicate.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** ẹlẹ́rìí (contracted agentive)
**Term B:** ènìyàn irọ́ / ènìyàn òtítọ́ (periphrastic agentive)
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí / ènìyàn irọ́; ènìyàn òtítọ́
**English translation:** witness (person of evidence) / a liar; a truthful person
**Description of contrast:** P4a and P4b document two distinct agentive nominalization strategies in the truth domain: a lexicalized contracted form (*ẹlẹ́rìí*, from *ẹni* + *ẹ̀rí*) attested only for the witness concept, and a productive periphrastic pattern (*ènìyàn* + evaluative noun) used for liar, truthful person, and deceiver. P4b explicitly states the contracted form "is lexicalized rather than fully productive" and that "no single productive agentive suffix exists across the domain." The contrast is between a lexicalized agentive form restricted to one item and a productive periphrastic strategy used across the domain.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

**Term A:** dájúdájú
**Term B:** dájú
**Transliteration:** dájúdájú / dájú
**English translation:** certainly, definitely, surely, without doubt (emphatic) / to be certain, to be clear, to be definite (base form)
**Description of contrast:** P1 and P2a document *dájúdájú* as derived from *dájú* by reduplication, with the reduplicated form described as "more emphatic than the simple *dájú*." The contrast is between a base epistemic predicate/modifier and its reduplicated intensified form; the reduplication adds degree of certainty rather than changing the semantic category.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

**Term A:** ìwà ẹni ló ń ṣe é
**Term B:** ìfarahàn rẹ̀ kò dára, ṣùgbọ́n ìwà rẹ̀ dára
**Transliteration:** ìwà ẹni ló ń ṣe é / ìfarahàn rẹ̀ kò dára, ṣùgbọ́n ìwà rẹ̀ dára
**English translation:** it is a person's character that defines them / his/her appearance is not good, but his/her character is good
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both constructions as attested in moral and philosophical discourse; the first asserts the primacy of *ìwà* (character) as the defining feature of a person, while the second explicitly subordinates *ìfarahàn* (appearance) to *ìwà* using the adversative *ṣùgbọ́n* ("but"). Together they document a constructional pattern in which surface appearance is explicitly contrasted with and ranked below essential character.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́
**Term B:** ìwà burúkú ló pa á
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́ / ìwà burúkú ló pa á
**English translation:** it is good character that saves one / it is bad character that destroyed him/her
**Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as focus constructions (*ló* = focus marker) attested in moral and proverbial discourse; they are structurally parallel (same focus construction, same verb slot pattern) but with contrasting subjects (*ìwà rere* / *ìwà burúkú*) and contrasting outcomes (salvation / destruction). The contrast is between the protective consequence of good character and the destructive consequence of bad character, expressed through parallel focus constructions.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
