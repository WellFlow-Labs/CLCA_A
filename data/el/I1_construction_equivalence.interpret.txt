## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### REVELATION substitutes for BECOME

Greek shows a clear preference for REVELATION constructions over BECOME constructions when expressing truth-domain inchoative meanings. While the BECOME pattern exists (γίνομαι + adjective), it is marked as **[MARGINAL]** in P4b for truth-domain vocabulary. Instead, Greek systematically uses:

1. **Metaphorical revelation constructions**: βγαίνει η αλήθεια στη φόρα (literally "the truth comes out to the surface")
2. **Explicit revelation verbs**: αποκαλύπτω/αποκαλύπτεται "reveal/is revealed"
3. **Light metaphors**: φέρνω/έρχεται στο φως "bring/comes to light"

This substitution reflects a conceptual preference: truth is not conceived as something that "becomes" true, but rather as something hidden that emerges or is brought to visibility. The BECOME construction is available but dispreferred; speakers choose REVELATION patterns instead.

### DETECTION substitutes for simple DISCONFIRMATION

For lies specifically, Greek employs specialized DETECTION constructions (πιάνω κάποιον στο ψέμα "catch someone in a lie") alongside general DISCONFIRMATION verbs (ξεσκεπάζω "expose," διαψεύδω "refute"). The DETECTION pattern emphasizes the catching/trapping aspect of discovering lies, using the metaphor of lies as coverings or traps. This is more specific than simple disconfirmation and carries additional pragmatic force.

### VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION use parallel but distinct constructions

Greek maintains separate verb sets for these operations:
- **VERIFICATION**: βεβαιώνω "confirm," επιβεβαιώνω "verify," πιστοποιώ "certify"
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: ακυρώνω "invalidate," διαψεύδω "refute," ξεσκεπάζω "expose"

These are not the same construction with different polarity; they are distinct lexical verbs with specialized administrative/legal uses. Both can take nominal complements (βεβαίωση "confirmation" vs. ακύρωση "cancellation").

### CAUSATION shows no factual/character distinction

Greek uses the same construction ([AGENT] + κάνω + [NP-ACC] + [ADJ-ACC]) for both factual causation ("This makes me certain") and character causation ("This makes me truthful"). There is no morphosyntactic distinction between causing someone to be in a truth-state versus causing someone to have a truth-related character trait. The construction is semantically underspecified for this distinction.

### NEGATION is purely analytic

Greek has no morphological negation for truth-domain predicates. All negation uses the preverbal particle δεν:
- Δεν είναι αλήθεια (not: *ψέμα as morphological negative of αλήθεια)
- Δεν είναι σωστό (not: *λάθος as morphological negative of σωστό)

While antonyms exist (αλήθεια/ψέμα, σωστό/λάθος), they are separate lexical items, not morphological negations. The language requires syntactic negation with δεν.

### Asymmetry in agentive nominalization

Greek shows productive agentive nominalization for **negative** truth-domain behaviors:
- ψεύτης/ψεύτρα "liar"
- υποκριτής "hypocrite"
- απατεώνας "swindler"

But lacks productive agentive nominalization for **positive** truth-domain behaviors:
- **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** for "truth-teller" (*αληθευτής not standard)
- Circumlocution required: αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια "the one who tells the truth"

This asymmetry reflects cultural/linguistic marking: deviation from truth-telling norms receives lexical encoding, while truth-telling itself is unmarked and requires periphrasis.

### Compound formation asymmetry

The ψευδ- prefix is highly productive for creating critical terms marking false claims:
- ψευδοεπιστήμη "pseudoscience"
- ψευδοδημοκρατία "pseudo-democracy"
- ψευδοπληροφορία "misinformation"

No equivalent productive prefix exists for marking genuine/authentic claims. The ορθ- prefix exists but serves a different function (correctness/straightness) and is less productive in contemporary Greek. This creates an asymmetry: easy lexical creation for falsity-marking, but no parallel mechanism for authenticity-marking.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps

**None identified.** All canonical CLCA operators have corresponding constructions in Greek, though some show substitution patterns (REVELATION for BECOME) or lack morphosyntactic distinctions (factual vs. character CAUSATION).

### Lexical Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization for truth-tellers**: No single-word equivalent for "truth-teller" exists. The pattern is productive for negative agents (ψεύτης "liar") but blocked for positive agents.
   - **Status**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**
   - **Circumlocution**: αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια "the one who tells the truth"

2. **Productive prefix for authenticity-marking**: No productive prefix equivalent to ψευδ- exists for marking genuine/authentic claims.
   - **Status**: Lexical gap in derivational morphology
   - **Available alternative**: ορθ- serves partially but with different semantics (correctness rather than authenticity)

### Conceptual Gaps

**None identified.** All truth-domain conceptual operations appear expressible in Greek, though some require specific constructions or circumlocutions.

### Asymmetric Gaps

1. **Agentive nominalization**: Productive for negative behaviors only
   - Present: ψεύτης "liar," απατεώνας "swindler," υποκριτής "hypocrite"
   - Absent: *αληθευτής "truth-teller" (not standard)

2. **Compound formation**: Productive for falsity-marking only
   - Present: ψευδ- prefix (ψευδοεπιστήμη, ψευδοδημοκρατία)
   - Limited: ορθ- prefix exists but serves different function and less productive

3. **Number marking in light verb constructions**:
   - λέω ψέματα "tell lies" — obligatorily plural
   - λέω την αλήθεια "tell the truth" — obligatorily singular
   - Asymmetry in number requirements between truth and lie constructions

### Morphosyntactic Gaps

**None identified.** Greek does not show missing construction types; all operators are expressible through available morphosyntactic patterns.

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available

1. **ATTR** — Predicative attribution with copula είμαι
   - Pattern: [NP] + είμαι + [TRUTH-ADJ/NOUN]
   - Status: Core construction, extremely high-frequency

2. **ADNOM** — Attributive modification
   - Pattern: [TRUTH-ADJ] + [NOUN]
   - Status: Core construction with full agreement morphology

3. **NEGATION** — Analytic negation
   - Pattern: δεν + [COP/VERB]
   - Status: Core construction, purely syntactic (no morphological negation)

4. **NOMINALIZATION (abstract)** — Productive suffixation
   - Patterns: -ότητα, -εια, -ία, -ση
   - Status: Highly productive across multiple suffix types

5. **CAUSATION (factual)** — Analytic and morphological causatives
   - Patterns: κάνω + [NP-ACC] + [ADJ-ACC]; -ώνω suffix verbs
   - Status: Core construction with multiple realizations

6. **VERIFICATION** — Specialized verification verbs
   - Verbs: βεβαιώνω, επιβεβαιώνω, πιστοποιώ
   - Status: Core construction in administrative/legal registers

7. **DISCONFIRMATION** — Specialized disconfirmation verbs
   - Verbs: ακυρώνω, διαψεύδω, ξεσκεπάζω
   - Status: Core construction in administrative/legal registers

8. **REVELATION** — Metaphorical emergence constructions
   - Patterns: βγαίνω στη φόρα, φέρνω στο φως, αποκαλύπτω
   - Status: Core construction, culturally salient

### Available with Substitution

9. **BECOME** — Inchoative with γίνομαι
   - Pattern: [NP] + γίνομαι + [ADJ]
   - Status: **SUBSTITUTED BY REVELATION** (preferred pattern)
   - Note: Construction exists but marked as [MARGINAL] for truth-domain; speakers prefer REVELATION constructions

10. **CAUSATION (character)** — Same as factual causation
    - Pattern: κάνω + [NP-ACC] + [ADJ-ACC]
    - Status: No morphosyntactic distinction from factual causation
    - Note: Construction available but semantically underspecified for factual/character distinction

### Partially Available

11. **NOMINALIZATION (agentive)** — Productive for negative agents only
    - Pattern: -της/-τρια/-ας suffixes
    - Status: **ASYMMETRIC** — productive for ψεύτης "liar," απατεώνας "swindler," but blocked for positive agents
    - Gap: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION** for "truth-teller"

### Missing Entirely

**None.** All canonical CLCA operators have some form of expression in Greek, though asymmetries and substitution patterns exist.

## Additional Specialized Constructions (Not in Canonical Set)

Greek shows several highly productive constructions not represented in the canonical CLCA operator set:

1. **LIGHT VERB (truth/lie)** — λέω + [TRUTH/LIE-ACC]
   - Extremely high-frequency construction for speech acts
   - Obligatory number asymmetry (singular αλήθεια vs. plural ψέματα)

2. **LIGHT VERB (error)** — κάνω + λάθος
   - Unmarked construction for error-making
   - Extremely high-frequency across all registers

3. **DETECTION** — πιάνω + [OBJECT] + στο ψέμα
   - Specialized construction for catching lies
   - Overlaps functionally with DISCONFIRMATION but adds catching/trapping semantics

4. **CORRECTION** — διορθώνω/σωστεύω + [ERROR-ACC]
   - Specialized construction for error correction
   - Overlaps functionally with CAUSATION but lexically specialized

5. **ADMISSION** — παραδέχομαι/ομολογώ + [TRUTH/ERROR-ACC]
   - Specialized construction for acknowledging truth or error
   - Common in apology and conflict resolution contexts

6. **COMPOUND FORMATION** — ψευδ- and ορθ- prefixes
   - Highly productive derivational patterns
   - ψευδ- asymmetrically productive for falsity-marking

7. **DISCOURSE MARKERS** — Grammaticalized truth-domain expressions
   - αλήθεια; "really?", πραγματικά "really", ακριβώς "exactly"
   - Extremely high-frequency in spoken Greek

These constructions represent language-specific elaborations of the truth-domain beyond the canonical operator set, reflecting Greek's particular grammatical and cultural encoding of truth-related meanings.
