# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope:** Quechua is a dialect continuum with significant lexical variation across varieties (Cusco-Collao, Ayacucho, Ecuadorian, etc.). Items below are drawn primarily from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka), which has the most extensive documentation. Variety-specific distribution is noted where known. No transliteration system is needed beyond the standard academic orthography used in Quechua linguistics (IPA-based practical orthography as standardized by Peruvian and Bolivian academies); this orthography is used consistently below.

---

## Items

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** N/A (standard orthography)
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; no further decomposition established
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, correct, right

**Usage description:** *Cheqa* is the primary term for truth and correctness across Southern Quechua varieties, used in propositional contexts ("that is true"), in assessments of persons ("a genuine/honest person"), and in evaluations of objects or situations as being what they appear to be. It participates in extensive derivation (see items below). It is among the most frequently cited and documented lexical items in Quechua semantic studies.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (nominalizing/adjectivizing suffix); "one who is true / that which is true"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real, honest

**Usage description:** *Cheqaq* functions as an adjectival and nominal form derived from *cheqa*, used to describe persons as truthful or honest, and statements or things as genuine or accurate. It is frequently used in moral evaluation of persons and in assertions about the truth of propositions. Widely attested in both Cusco and Ayacucho varieties.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaqta
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** cheqaq + *-ta* (accusative/adverbial case); "truly, in truth, genuinely"
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, honestly, in truth

**Usage description:** *Cheqaqta* is the adverbial form used to assert that something is genuinely the case or to emphasize the sincerity or accuracy of a statement. It commonly appears in discourse as an evidential-like reinforcer of a speaker's commitment to the truth of what they are saying. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**4.**

**Usage description:** *Cheqaqchay* is used in contexts of verification or confirmation — establishing that something is indeed the case or that a claim is accurate. It appears in legal and testimonial contexts as well as in everyday speech when a speaker confirms a prior assertion. Attested in Southern Quechua documentation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**14.**

**Usage description:** *Allin* is the primary term for goodness and rightness in Southern Quechua, used in moral evaluation of conduct and persons, and in assessments of whether something is done correctly or is in proper order. **15.**
**Form:** mana allin
**Transliteration:** N/A

**Typical English translations:** bad, wrong, improper, not right

**Usage description:** *Mana allin* is the standard negated form used to evaluate conduct, persons, or states as morally or practically wrong or improper. It is a conventionalized fixed expression in Southern Quechua and functions as the primary antonym of *allin* in evaluative contexts. Widely attested.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**16.**
**Form:** recto / riqtu
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Borrowing from Spanish *recto* ("straight, upright, correct"); adapted into Quechua phonology in some varieties as *riqtu*
**Typical English translations:** upright, correct, straight, honest

**Usage description:** This Spanish loanword has been integrated into some Quechua varieties, particularly in contexts of moral and legal evaluation of persons as upright or correct in conduct. Its distribution is uneven across varieties and it is more common in urban or contact varieties. Included as an attested item in documented Quechua speech but marked accordingly.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**17.**
**Form:** pura
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal meaning / morphological breakdown:** Root; "pure, among, between, only"
**Typical English translations:** pure, genuine, only, among (one's own kind)

**Usage description:** *Pura* carries a sense of purity or genuineness in some evaluative contexts, particularly in descriptions of something unmixed or unadulterated. Its primary meaning is relational ("among"), but in certain contexts it functions to assert the genuine or pure nature of something. Its use in evaluative contexts of genuineness is less central than its relational use and should be treated with caution.

**Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]
