# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST (International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** सत्य
**Transliteration:** satya
**Literal meaning:** from *sat* (being, existing, real) + adjectival suffix *-ya*
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality, the real
**Usage:** Used across Vedic, epic, and philosophical literature to describe statements, persons, and states of affairs that accord with what is real or actual. Applied to speech acts (truthful utterance), to persons (one who speaks truly), and to metaphysical states (ultimate reality). Participates in extensive derivation: *satyatā*, *satyavādin*, *satyavrata*, etc.
**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** ऋत
**Transliteration:** ṛta
**Literal meaning:** from root *ṛ-* (to go, to fit, to be ordered); "that which is properly ordered/fitted"
**Typical English translations:** cosmic order, truth, right, the fitting
**Usage:** Primarily Vedic; designates the cosmic and ritual order that underlies both natural regularity and moral rightness. Contrasted with *anṛta* (disorder, falsehood). Closely related to but distinct from *satya*, with stronger cosmological and ritual connotations.
**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** अनृत
**Transliteration:** anṛta
**Literal meaning:** *an-* (negation) + *ṛta* (cosmic order, truth)
**Typical English translations:** untruth, falsehood, disorder, the unreal
**Usage:** The standard antonym of *ṛta* in Vedic and later literature; applied to false speech, deceptive conduct, and states contrary to proper order. Used in legal and moral contexts to characterize lying or fraudulent representation.
**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** असत्य
**Transliteration:** asatya
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *satya* (truth, the real)
**Typical English translations:** untruth, falsehood, the false
**Usage:** The standard negation of *satya*; used in ethical, legal, and philosophical contexts to characterize false statements, deceptive persons, or illusory states. Appears in lists of vices and in discussions of testimony (*pramāṇa*) as a disqualifying property of a witness.
**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** प्रमाण
**Transliteration:** pramāṇa
**Literal meaning:** *pra-* (forth, correctly) + *māṇa* (measuring, from root *mā-*, to measure)
**Typical English translations:** valid means of knowledge, proof, authority, standard, measure
**Usage:** Central technical term in Indian epistemology (*nyāya*, *mīmāṃsā*, *Buddhist* logic, etc.) designating a reliable or valid source or means of cognition. Applied to persons, texts, and instruments regarded as authoritative standards. Participates in derivation: *pramāṇatā* (the property of being a valid means), *apramāṇa* (invalid means).
**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** प्रमा
**Transliteration:** pramā
**Literal meaning:** *pra-* + *mā-* (to measure); "correct measuring/cognition"
**Typical English translations:** valid cognition, true knowledge, correct apprehension
**Usage:** In epistemological literature, designates the result of a successful *pramāṇa*: a cognition that accurately represents its object. Distinguished from *apramā* (erroneous cognition) and from mere belief. Used in *Nyāyasūtra* and commentarial traditions.
**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** यथार्थ
**Transliteration:** yathārtha
**Literal meaning:** *yathā* (as, according to) + *artha* (object, meaning, thing); "as the object/thing is"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, true to the object, correct, literal, real
**Usage:** Used to characterize cognitions, statements, or representations that correspond to their objects as those objects actually are. Applied in epistemology to distinguish accurate from inaccurate cognition, and in aesthetics and grammar to characterize apt or fitting expression.
**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** अयथार्थ
**Transliteration:** ayathārtha
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *yathārtha* (accurate, as the object is)
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, not corresponding to the object, false
**Usage:** The negation of *yathārtha*; used in epistemological contexts to characterize cognitions or statements that fail to represent their objects correctly. Less frequent than *yathārtha* but attested in commentarial literature.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** धर्म
**Transliteration:** dharma
**Literal meaning:** from root *dhṛ-* (to hold, to sustain); "that which holds/sustains"
**Typical English translations:** duty, right conduct, law, righteousness, moral order, property (in logic)
**Usage:** Extremely broad term covering moral rightness, legal norm, social duty, and (in logic/ontology) a property or characteristic of a thing. In ethical and legal contexts (*Dharmaśāstra*), used to evaluate conduct as right or fitting. In Buddhist and Jain usage, also designates the teaching or ultimate constituents of experience.
**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** अधर्म
**Transliteration:** adharma
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *dharma* (right conduct, law, order)
**Typical English translations:** unrighteousness, wrongdoing, violation of duty, moral disorder
**Usage:** The standard antonym of *dharma* in ethical and legal literature; applied to actions, persons, and states that violate moral or legal norms. Used in *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature to evaluate conduct negatively.
**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** सत्
**Transliteration:** sat
**Literal meaning:** present participle of root *as-* (to be); "being, existing, real"
**Typical English translations:** being, real, existent, good, true, genuine
**Usage:** Used both ontologically (that which truly exists) and evaluatively (that which is genuine or good). In compounds such as *sat-puruṣa* (a genuine/good person) and *sat-karman* (right action), the evaluative sense is prominent. Foundational to the *satya* family of terms.
**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** असत्
**Transliteration:** asat
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *sat* (being, real, genuine)
**Typical English translations:** non-being, unreal, false, bad, spurious
**Usage:** Ontological and evaluative antonym of *sat*; used to characterize what is unreal, illusory, or morally bad. In Vedāntic literature, central to discussions of appearance versus reality. In ethical contexts, characterizes spurious or bad persons and actions.
**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** वास्तव
**Transliteration:** vāstava
**Literal meaning:** from *vastu* (thing, real object, substance) + adjectival suffix *-va*
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, genuine, pertaining to the real object
**Usage:** Used to characterize what pertains to or corresponds with actual things or states of affairs, as opposed to what is imagined, conventional, or merely apparent. Appears in philosophical and commentarial literature to distinguish real properties from attributed or conventional ones.
**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** वस्तु
**Transliteration:** vastu
**Literal meaning:** from root *vas-* (to dwell, to be); "thing, real entity, substance"
**Typical English translations:** real thing, object, substance, actual state of affairs
**Usage:** Designates a real or actual entity as opposed to a mere name, concept, or appearance. In epistemological and ontological contexts, used to ask what something *really* is (*vastu-svarūpa*). In legal contexts, can refer to the actual subject matter of a dispute.
**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** स्वरूप
**Transliteration:** svarūpa
**Literal meaning:** *sva* (own, self) + *rūpa* (form, appearance); "own form, intrinsic nature"
**Typical English translations:** own nature, intrinsic character, true form, real nature
**Usage:** Used to ask or assert what something fundamentally or essentially is, as opposed to how it appears or is conventionally described. Appears in philosophical, grammatical, and legal contexts. In *Dharmaśāstra*, used to determine the true character of an act or object for purposes of evaluation.
**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** तत्त्व
**Transliteration:** tattva
**Literal meaning:** *tat* (that) + *tva* (abstract suffix); "that-ness, the being of that"
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, true nature, principle, essence
**Usage:** Used to designate the real or essential nature of something, often in the phrase *tattvatas* ("in truth, in reality, according to the real nature"). In philosophical literature, refers to fundamental ontological categories. In evaluative contexts, used to distinguish genuine understanding from superficial or mistaken apprehension.
**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** औचित्य
**Transliteration:** aucitya
**Literal meaning:** from *ucita* (fitting, appropriate) + abstract suffix *-ya*; "fittingness, appropriateness"
**Typical English translations:** propriety, fittingness, appropriateness, decorum
**Usage:** Central term in Sanskrit aesthetics (*alaṃkāraśāstra*), particularly in Kṣemendra's *Aucityavicāracarcā*, where it designates the quality of being fitting or appropriate in literary composition. Also used in ethical contexts to evaluate conduct as suitable to circumstances, role, or context.
**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** न्याय
**Transliteration:** nyāya
**Literal meaning:** from *ni-* + root *i-* (to go); "going into, method, right procedure, rule"
**Typical English translations:** right method, logical reasoning, rule, justice, the Nyāya philosophical school
**Usage:** Used to designate correct logical procedure, a rule or maxim, and (in legal contexts) justice or right adjudication. In the name of the *Nyāya* philosophical school, refers to the discipline of correct reasoning and valid inference. Evaluates arguments and procedures as methodologically sound.
**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** साधु
**Transliteration:** sādhu
**Literal meaning:** from root *sādh-* (to accomplish, to be effective); "effective, straight, good"
**Typical English translations:** correct, good, virtuous, proper; (in grammar) a grammatically correct form
**Usage:** In Pāṇinian grammatical tradition, *sādhu* designates a linguistically correct or well-formed expression, as opposed to *asādhu* (incorrect form). In ethical contexts, characterizes a person or action as genuinely good or virtuous. The grammatical and moral senses both involve evaluation against a standard of correctness.
**[CORE]**

---

**20.**
**Form:** असाधु
**Transliteration:** asādhu
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *sādhu* (correct, good)
**Typical English translations:** incorrect, bad, improper; (in grammar) an ill-formed expression
**Usage:** Antonym of *sādhu* in both grammatical and ethical registers. In grammar, marks a form as violating Pāṇinian rules. In ethical literature, characterizes a person or act as bad or improper. The dual usage illustrates the overlap between correctness and goodness in Sanskrit evaluative vocabulary.
**[CORE]**

---

**21.**
**Form:** प्रतिभास
**Transliteration:** pratibhāsa
**Literal meaning:** *prati-* (against, back, in return) + *bhāsa* (shining, appearance, from root *bhās-*); "counter-appearance, mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** appearance, semblance, illusory appearance, seeming
**Usage:** Used in epistemological and Vedāntic literature to designate how something appears, particularly when the appearance may not correspond to reality. Contrasted with *tattva* or *vastu* to mark the distinction between how things seem and what they are. Central in discussions of perceptual error (*bhrānti*).
**[CORE]**

---

**22.**
**Form:** भ्रान्ति
**Transliteration:** bhrānti
**Literal meaning:** from root *bhram-* (to wander, to be confused); "wandering, confusion, error"
**Typical English translations:** error, illusion, mistaken cognition, confusion
**Usage:** Standard term for cognitive error or illusion in epistemological literature; the classic example is mistaking a rope for a snake (*rajju-sarpa-bhrānti*). Used to evaluate a cognition as failing to accurately represent its object. Participates in derivation: *bhrānta* (erroneous, confused).
**[CORE]**

---

**23.**
**Form:** विपर्यय
**Transliteration:** viparyaya
**Literal meaning:** *vi-* (apart, wrongly) + *paryaya* (going around, reversal, from *pari-* + *i-*); "reversal, inversion"
**Typical English translations:** erroneous cognition, misapprehension, inversion, wrong knowledge
**Usage:** In *Sāṃkhya* and *Yoga* literature, one of the five types of mental modification (*vṛtti*), designating false or inverted cognition. In epistemological contexts more broadly, used to characterize cognitions that represent their objects in a reversed or incorrect manner. Distinguished from *bhrānti* in some technical usages.
**[CORE]**

---

**24.**
**Form:** आभास
**Transliteration:** ābhāsa
**Literal meaning:** *ā-* (toward, resembling) + *bhāsa* (shining, appearance); "semblance, mere appearance"
**Typical English translations:** semblance, fallacy, apparent (but not genuine) instance, pseudo-
**Usage:** Used in logic to designate a *fallacy* or *pseudo-*instance: *hetvābhāsa* (fallacious reason, pseudo-*hetu*), *pramāṇābhāsa* (pseudo-valid means of knowledge). The term marks something as resembling the genuine article without being it. Also used in Vedāntic contexts for the appearance of consciousness in a limited medium.
**[CORE]**

---

**25.**
**Form:** शुद्ध
**Transliteration:** śuddha
**Literal meaning:** past passive participle of root *śudh-* (to be pure, to be cleansed); "purified, clean"
**Typical English translations:** pure, correct, unadulterated, genuine, clean
**Usage:** Used in ritual, grammatical, and evaluative contexts to characterize something as free from contamination, error, or admixture. In grammar, *śuddha* can mark a form as correct or uncontaminated. In philosophical contexts, used to characterize pure or unmixed states (e.g., *śuddha-caitanya*, pure consciousness).
**[CORE]**

---

**26.**
**Form:** मिथ्या
**Transliteration:** mithyā
**Literal meaning:** from *mithā* (alternately, wrongly, falsely); "falsely, wrongly, in vain"
**Typical English translations:** false, wrong, illusory, in vain, erroneously
**Usage:** Used as an adverb and adjective to characterize statements, cognitions, or entities as false or illusory. In Advaita Vedānta, *mithyā* has a specific technical sense: neither absolutely real (*sat*) nor absolutely unreal (*asat*), but of indeterminate ontological status. Also used in everyday ethical contexts for false speech or vain effort.
**[CORE]**

---

**27.**
**Form:** कृत्रिम
**Transliteration:** kṛtrima
**Literal meaning:** from *kṛta* (made, done, from root *kṛ-*) + suffix *-ima*; "made, manufactured, artificial"
**Typical English translations:** artificial, manufactured, not genuine, counterfeit, feigned
**Usage:** Used to characterize objects, substances, or behaviors as produced or fabricated rather than natural or genuine. In medical (*Āyurvedic*) and legal contexts, used to distinguish artificial or adulterated substances from genuine ones. In aesthetic and rhetorical contexts, can characterize affected or unnatural style.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**28.**
**Form:** अकृत्रिम
**Transliteration:** akṛtrima
**Literal meaning:** *a-* (negation) + *kṛtrima* (artificial, manufactured)
**Typical English translations:** natural, genuine, not artificial, unfeigned
**Usage:** The negation of *kṛtrima*; used to characterize what is natural, unmanufactured, or genuine as opposed to artificial or counterfeit. Appears in philosophical and aesthetic contexts to mark authenticity or naturalness. Less frequently attested than *kṛtrima*.
**[MARGINAL]**

---

**29.**
**Form:** साक्षात्
**Transliteration:** sākṣāt
**Literal meaning:** *sa-* (with) + *akṣa* (eye, sense organ) + instrumental suffix; "with one's own eyes, directly"
**Typical English translations:** directly, in person, face to face, actually, really
**Usage:** Used to assert that something is directly perceived or genuinely present, as opposed to inferred, reported, or represented. In legal contexts, *sākṣī* (witness, from the same root) designates one who has direct knowledge. The term evaluates a claim or presence as immediate and unmediated.
**[CORE]**

---

**30.**
**Form:** यथावत्
**Transliteration:** yathāvat
**Literal meaning:** *yathā* (as, in which manner) + *vat* (having, like); "as it is, in the proper manner"
**Typical English translations:** accurately, correctly, as it really is, properly, exactly
**Usage:** Used adverbially to characterize actions, descriptions, or cognitions as corresponding exactly to how things are or as performed in the proper manner. Appears in contexts of instruction, testimony, and description where accuracy or fidelity to the actual state of affairs is being asserted or required.
**[CORE]**
