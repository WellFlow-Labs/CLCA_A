# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: English (en)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **TRUE** (Old English *trēowe*)
- **Participates in**: 2 P1 items directly (true, truthful), plus related forms (trustworthy, trust-)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Correspondence to reality; faithfulness; reliability; that which can be depended upon

### Root 2: **FALSE** (Latin *falsus*)
- **Participates in**: 2 P1 items directly (false, fallacious), plus related forms (counterfeit contains *fait* "made")
- **Core meaning contribution**: Deviation from reality; deception; incorrectness; that which misleads

### Root 3: **REAL** (Latin *realis*)
- **Participates in**: 2 P1 items directly (real, reliable contains *re-* prefix)
- **Core meaning contribution**: Actual existence; genuineness; substantive rather than apparent

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: TRUE / TRUTH

**1. truth**
- Morpheme breakdown: true + -th (noun suffix)
- Translation: truth
- Context: Abstract noun denoting the quality of being true, the body of true statements, or reality itself. Used in philosophical discourse ("the truth about X"), everyday speech ("tell the truth"), and fixed expressions ("the truth of the matter").
- [CORE]

**2. truly**
- Morpheme breakdown: true + -ly (adverb suffix)
- Translation: truly
- Context: Adverbial form used to affirm sincerity ("I truly believe"), emphasize genuineness ("truly remarkable"), or intensify statements. Common in both formal and informal registers.
- [CORE]

**3. untrue**
- Morpheme breakdown: un- + true
- Translation: untrue
- Context: Negated form marking statements or representations as not corresponding to facts. Used in formal and informal contexts as a less harsh alternative to "false" or "lying."

**4. untruth**
- Morpheme breakdown: un- + truth

## Step 3 — Note Peripheral Extensions

### TRUE/TRUST root extensions:

**Intensity/degree marking**: "truly" functions as an intensifier ("truly awful") beyond its core truth-marking function.

**Mechanical/physical alignment**: "true" appears in carpentry and engineering contexts ("true up," "out of true") denoting physical straightness or proper alignment, extending spatial metaphor.

**Loyalty/fidelity domain**: "true" in "true love," "true blue," "true to one's word" emphasizes constancy and loyalty rather than correspondence to facts.

**Essential nature**: "true" in "true self," "true nature," "true calling" marks authentic essence rather than factual accuracy.

**Financial/legal trust**: The trust/trustee family extends into property law and financial instruments where the connection to truth-telling becomes attenuated.

### FALSE root extensions:

**Musical register**: "falsetto" uses the root to denote artificial vocal production, extending the deception metaphor to sound production.

**Structural concealment**: "false bottom," "false wall," "false ceiling" denote hidden architectural features, extending beyond representation to physical structure.

**Botanical/anatomical**: "false ribs" (anatomy), "false acacia" (botany) use false to mark resemblance without identity in taxonomic contexts.

### REAL root extensions:

**Emphasis/intensification**: "really" and "real" function as general intensifiers ("real good," "really fast") with minimal semantic content about reality.

**Property/legal domain**: "real estate," "real property" use real to distinguish immovable property from personal property, a technical legal distinction.

**Mathematical domain**: "real numbers" (vs. imaginary numbers) extends the reality concept to mathematical ontology.

**Temporal immediacy**: "real time" marks simultaneity rather than reality per se.

**Practical vs. theoretical**: "real world" contrasts practical application with theory, shifting from ontological to pragmatic distinction.
