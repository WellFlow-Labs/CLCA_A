# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## IDENTIFIED SEMANTIC GROUPS

### GROUP 1: Factual Correspondence Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to affirm or deny that statements, beliefs, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs or reality. These expressions are invoked when verifying information, checking facts, or distinguishing what actually exists or occurred from what does not. Commonly appear in contexts of confirmation, denial, investigation, and reporting.

**Representative Expressions:**
- totoo (true, real)
- katotohanan (truth, reality)
- hindi totoo (not true, untrue)
- di-totoo (untrue, false)
- totoo ba? (is it true?)
- may katotohanan (has truth, there is truth to it)
- walang katotohanan (without truth, baseless)
- batay sa katotohanan (based on truth, factual)
- ayon sa katotohanan (according to truth, actually)
- makatotohanan (realistic, plausible, true-to-life)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- sa katunayan (in fact, actually) - overlaps with evidential/proof terms
- totoo nga (it's really true) - overlaps with emphatic affirmation patterns
- nagtotoo/tumutuoo/magkatotoo (coming true, being realized) - extends to realization/manifestation rather than simple factual correspondence

---

### GROUP 2: Authenticity and Genuineness Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to distinguish what is genuine, authentic, or essential from what is imitation, fake, or superficial. These expressions emphasize the true nature or identity of persons, objects, relationships, or emotions, often contrasting inner reality with outer appearance. Frequently used to evaluate sincerity, identify counterfeits, or affirm essential character.

**Representative Expressions:**
- tunay (genuine, authentic, true)
- tunay na (truly, genuinely)
- tunay na tunay (very genuine, absolutely true)
- tunay na pag-ibig (true love)
- tunay na kaibigan (true friend)
- tunay na kulay (true colors, real character)
- tunay na pangalan (real name)
- tunay na ama/ina (real father/mother, biological parent)
- pagkatunay (genuineness, authenticity)
- peke (fake, counterfeit)
- peke-peke (very fake, obviously fake)
- huwad (false, counterfeit)
- huwad na huwad (completely false)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- tunay na dahilan/layunin (true reason/purpose) - overlaps with hidden vs. stated motives
- hindi tunay (not genuine) - simple negation, less specific than dedicated falsity terms
- gawâ-gawâ (made-up, fabricated) - overlaps with fabrication/invention terms

---

### GROUP 3: Correctness and Error Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether statements, answers, actions, or judgments are correct or incorrect according to standards, facts, or norms. These expressions appear in contexts of verification, testing, problem-solving, and moral evaluation. They mark alignment or misalignment with what is proper, accurate, or right.

**Representative Expressions:**
- tama (correct, right, proper)
- tama ba? (is it correct?)
- tama ka/ako/siya (you're/I'm/he's right)
- hindi tama (not correct, wrong)
- tama nga (indeed correct, that's right)
- mali (wrong, incorrect)
- mali ka/ako/siya (you're/I'm/he's wrong)
- pagkakamali (mistake, error)
- magkamali (to make a mistake)
- nagkakamali (is making mistakes)
- kamali-mali (very wrong, full of errors)
- wasto (correct, proper)
- wastuhan (to correct, to rectify)
- tumpak (accurate, precise, exact)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- tama na (enough, stop) - semantic shift to cessation/sufficiency rather than correctness
- hustó (exact, just right) - overlaps with precision and appropriateness
- tumama (to be correct / to hit) - polysemy with physical contact sense
- katamaan (correctness / being hit) - polysemy issue

---

### GROUP 4: Deception and Lying Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to characterize dishonest communication, deliberate falsehoods, or acts of deceiving others. These expressions identify lies, liars, and lying behavior, often appearing in accusations, denials, or moral judgments about truthfulness. They focus on intentional misrepresentation in speech or behavior.

**Representative Expressions:**
- sinungaling (liar, deceitful)
- sinungaling ka/siya (you're/he's a liar)
- kasinungalingan (lie, falsehood)
- magsinungaling (to lie, to tell lies)
- nagsisinungaling (is lying)
- sinungaling na sinungaling (complete liar, habitual liar)
- bulaán (false, deceitful, lying)
- bulaan (to lie to, to deceive)
- magbulaan (to lie to each other)
- binubulaan (is being lied to)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- puro kasinungalingan (all lies) - emphatic construction
- walang kasinungalingan (no lies) - negation pattern

---

### GROUP 5: Cheating and Fraud Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to identify dishonest practices in transactions, games, competitions, or relationships involving trickery, fraud, or unfair advantage. These expressions emphasize deceptive actions rather than just false statements, often appearing in contexts of fairness, justice, and rule-following.

**Representative Expressions:**
- dáyà (cheat, fraud, deceit)
- mandaya (to cheat, to deceive)
- dayain (to cheat on, to defraud)
- pagdadaya (cheating, deception, fraud)
- nandadaya (is cheating)
- madaya (deceitful, fraudulent, unfair)
- dáyà yan (that's cheating)
- walang dáyà (no cheating, fair)

---

### GROUP 6: Illusion and Deceptive Appearance Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to describe misleading appearances, visual deception, or situations where things are not as they seem. These expressions focus on perceptual or representational deception rather than verbal lies, often appearing in contexts of revelation, unmasking, or distinguishing appearance from reality.

**Representative Expressions:**
- linlang (deception, illusion, delusion)
- manlinlang (to deceive, to trick)
- linlangin (to deceive, to fool)
- paglilinlang (deception, trickery)
- nanlilinlang (is deceiving)
- malinlang (to be deceived, to be fooled)
- palabás (pretense, show, facade)
- magpalabas (to pretend, to put on a show)
- palabas-labas (all show, pure pretense)
- kunwari (pretend, feign, as if)
- magkunwari (to pretend, to feign)
- nagkukunwari (is pretending)
- kunwari lang (just pretending, only joking)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- gawâ-gawâ (made-up, fabricated) - overlaps with fabrication and inauthenticity
- palabás lang (just for show) - emphatic construction

---

### GROUP 7: Fabrication and Invention Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to characterize things that are created, invented, or made up rather than naturally occurring or factually based. These expressions distinguish creative or dishonest fabrication from reality, appearing in contexts of fiction, imagination, and false claims.

**Representative Expressions:**
- kathâ (fiction, invention, fabrication)
- kumatha (to create fiction, to invent)
- kathain (to invent, to fabricate)
- pagkakatha (invention, creation, fabrication)
- kathang-isip (imaginary, fictional, figment of imagination)
- kathang-isip lang (just imaginary, merely fictional)
- gawâ-gawâ (made-up, fabricated, artificial)
- gawâ-gawâ lang (just made-up, merely fabricated)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- kathâ lamang (merely fiction) - emphatic construction
- kathang-isip lang yan (that's just imaginary) - demonstrative construction

---

### GROUP 8: Evidence and Proof Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to refer to evidence, proof, or demonstration that establishes truth or genuineness. These expressions appear in legal, investigative, and argumentative contexts where claims must be supported or verified through evidence. They focus on the means of establishing truth rather than truth itself.

**Representative Expressions:**
- katunayan (proof, evidence, fact)
- patunayan (to prove, to verify)
- pagpapatunay (proving, verification)
- mapatunayan (can be proven, provable)
- napatunayan (was proven, has been verified)
- pinatunayan (proved, verified)
- katunayan nito (proof of this)
- bilang katunayan (as proof, as evidence)
- walang katunayan (without proof, unproven)
- may katunayan (has proof, there is evidence)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- sa katunayan (in fact, actually) - functions as discourse marker rather than referring to evidence per se

---

### GROUP 9: Concealment and Revelation Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to describe what is hidden or secret versus what is exposed or evident. These expressions mark the distinction between concealed and revealed information, appearing in contexts of disclosure, privacy, transparency, and discovery.

**Representative Expressions:**
- lihim (secret, hidden, concealed)
- ilihim (to hide, to keep secret)
- paglilihim (hiding, concealment, secrecy)
- inililihim (is being hidden, is being kept secret)
- lihiman (secretly, in secret)
- sa lihim (in secret, secretly)
- walang lihim (no secrets, nothing hidden)
- may lihim (has a secret)
- lantád (exposed, open, evident)
- ilantad (to expose, to reveal)
- paglalantad (exposure, revelation)
- inilalantad (is being exposed)
- lantaran (openly, publicly)
- lantád na (already exposed, now evident)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- lihim na pag-ibig (secret love) - collocation pattern
- lantád sa lahat (exposed to all) - emphatic construction

---

### GROUP 10: Honesty and Sincerity Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to characterize persons or communications as honest, sincere, and truthful versus dishonest or insincere. These expressions evaluate character and communicative intent, often appearing in contexts of trust, integrity, and moral evaluation. They focus on the disposition to tell truth rather than truth-value of specific statements.

**Representative Expressions:**
- tapat (sincere, honest, truthful, faithful)
- katapatan (honesty, sincerity, loyalty)
- magtapat (to be honest, to confess)
- nagtapat (was honest, confessed)
- tapat na tapat (very honest, completely sincere)
- tapat na tao (honest person)
- tapat sa (faithful to, loyal to)
- hindi tapat (not honest, unfaithful)
- walang katapatan (without honesty, disloyal)
- may katapatan (has honesty, has loyalty)
- sinsero (sincere, honest)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- pagtapat (honesty, confession) - nominalized form
- tapat sa salita (true to one's word) - collocation extending to reliability

---

### GROUP 11: Appropriateness and Fit Terms

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate whether something is fitting, suitable, or appropriate for a particular context, purpose, or person. These expressions assess contextual fit rather than absolute correctness, appearing in judgments of suitability, propriety, and matching.

**Representative Expressions:**
- akma (fitting, appropriate, suitable)
- umakma (to fit, to be appropriate)
- akmaín (to fit, to adjust)
- pag-akma (fitting, adjustment)
- akma sa (fitting for, appropriate to)
- hindi akma (not fitting, inappropriate)
- dapat (should, ought, must, proper)
- dapat lang (as it should be, only proper)
- dapat-dapat (very proper, highly appropriate)
- kadapat-dapat (most appropriate, highly deserving)
- hindi dapat (should not, improper)
- hustó (exact, just right, fitting)
- hustó lang (just right, just enough)
- hustó na hustó (exactly right, perfectly fitting)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- tama lang (just right, appropriate) - overlaps with correctness terms but emphasizes sufficiency
- dapat na (should already) - temporal aspect

---

### GROUP 12: Discourse Management and Hedging Expressions

**Definition:** Expressions that function primarily to manage conversational flow, introduce statements, or modify illocutionary force rather than directly asserting truth-values. These appear as discourse markers, hedges, or pragmatic particles that frame how utterances should be interpreted.

**Representative Expressions:**
- totoo lang (honestly, to be honest)
- sa totoo lang (to be honest, truthfully speaking)
- totóhanán (seriously, in earnest, for real)
- totoo naman (it is true though, admittedly)
- sa katunayan (in fact, actually, as a matter of fact)
- kung totoo (if true)
- totoo man (even if true, though it be true)
- kunwari (pretend, as if, supposedly)
- para kunwari (as if, as though)

**Ambiguous/Overlapping Items:**
- totoo ba? (is it true?) - interrogative function but also discourse marker expressing surprise
- totoo nga (it's really true) - emphatic affirmation overlapping with factual assertion

---

### GROUP 13: Emphatic and Intensifying Constructions

**Definition:** Reduplicated or particle-modified forms that intensify or emphasize the degree, certainty, or completeness of the base meaning. These constructions add emphasis rather than changing core semantic content, appearing across multiple semantic domains.

**Representative Expressions:**
- totoong-totoo (very true, absolutely real)
- tunay na tunay (very genuine, absolutely true)
- tapat na tapat (very honest, completely sincere)
- sinungaling na sinungaling (complete liar, habitual liar)
- huwad na huwad (completely false, thoroughly counterfeit)
- peke-peke (very fake, obviously fake)
- kamali-mali (very wrong, full of errors)
- palabas-labas (all show, pure pretense)
- tama-tama (just right, exactly right, coincidentally)
- hustó na hustó (exactly right, perfectly fitting)
- totoo nga (indeed true, really true)
- tama nga (indeed correct, that's right)
- tunay nga (indeed genuine, really true)
- sobrang totoo (very true, extremely real)
- sobrang mali (very wrong, extremely incorrect)
- sobrang tama (very correct, exactly right)

---

### GROUP 14: Comparative and Superlative Constructions

**Definition:** Constructions that express relative degrees of truth, correctness, genuineness, or other qualities within the domain. These appear in contexts of comparison, ranking, or identifying extremes.

**Representative Expressions:**
- mas totoo (more true, more real)
- mas tunay (more genuine, more authentic)
- mas tama (more correct)
- mas mali (more wrong)
- pinakatotoo (most true, truest)
- pinakatunay (most genuine, most authentic)
- pinakatama (most correct)
- pinakamali (most wrong)

---

### GROUP 15: Cessation and Sufficiency Expressions

**Definition:** Expressions derived from correctness terms but semantically shifted to indicate sufficiency, completion, or requests to stop. These appear primarily in directive contexts requesting cessation of actions or indicating that a threshold has been reached.

**Representative Expressions:**
- tama na (enough, that's enough, stop now)
- tama na yan (that's enough, stop that)
- tama na po (that's enough [polite], please stop)

**Note:** This represents a notable semantic extension from the correctness domain into quantity/cessation domains, specific to "tama."

---

## UNGROUPED OR AMBIGUOUS ITEMS

### Items with Multiple Group Membership:

1. **gawâ-gawâ** (made-up, fabricated)
   - Belongs to both GROUP 2 (Authenticity - as opposite of genuine) and GROUP 7 (Fabrication - as creative invention)
   - Usage context determines which sense is primary

2. **sa katunayan** (in fact, actually)
   - Functions as discourse marker (GROUP 12) but etymologically related to evidence terms (GROUP 8)
   - Pragmatic function has diverged from literal meaning

3. **makatotohanan** (realistic, plausible)
   - Overlaps GROUP 1 (Factual Correspondence) but emphasizes verisimilitude rather than actual truth
   - Evaluates representations rather than facts themselves

4. **tunay na dahilan/layunin** (true reason/purpose)
   - Could belong to GROUP 2 (Authenticity) or relate to concealment themes (GROUP 9)
   - Emphasizes hidden vs. stated motives

### Items with Unclear Grouping:

1. **Temporal/aspectual forms of totoo:**
   - nagtotoo, tumutuoo, magkatotoo (coming true, being realized)
   - These extend beyond simple factual correspondence into realization/manifestation
   - May warrant separate grouping if more examples emerge

2. **Polysemous forms of tama:**
   - tumama, tamaan (to be correct / to hit)
   - Physical contact sense may be separate root
   - Correctness sense clearly belongs to GROUP 3

3. **Particle combinations:**
   - Many constructions with ba, nga, lang, naman, etc.
   - These are productive patterns rather than semantic groups
   - Distributed across multiple semantic domains

### Notes on Grouping Decisions:

- Groups reflect **usage patterns** observed in the data, not imposed theoretical categories
- Overlap between groups is natural and reflects actual semantic relationships
- Some groups are larger (e.g., GROUP 1, GROUP 2) because these domains show richer lexicalization in Tagalog
- Smaller groups (e.g., GROUP 5, GROUP 15) represent more specialized semantic niches
- Discourse functions (GROUP 12) are distinguished from semantic content because usage patterns differ markedly
- Emphatic constructions (GROUP 13) are grouped by formal pattern rather than semantic content since they apply across domains
