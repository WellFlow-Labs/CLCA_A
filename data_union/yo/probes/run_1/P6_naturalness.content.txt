# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

This step documents how Yoruba encodes dynamic meanings — becoming, causing, and maintaining or losing truth-related states — using vocabulary established in P1 through P5. The analysis draws exclusively on attested constructions and vocabulary from the CONTEXT. Where a dynamic expression is not attested for a given term, this is stated explicitly. Where the same construction type applies across multiple terms, this is noted rather than repeated in full for each.

The CONTEXT establishes the following resources for encoding dynamics:

- **Motion-based inchoative:** *padà sí* (return to), *jáde kúrò nínú* (come out from), *di* (become) — documented in P4a Construction 4
- **Permissive-causative:** *jẹ́ kí* (let/cause) — P4a Construction 5
- **Instrumental serial verb:** *fi* (use/employ as instrument) — P4a Construction 5
- **Negative future:** *kò ní* + predicate — P2b constructions 6, 13, 16, 21, 31, 69, 82
- **Existential pair:** *wà* / *kò sí* — P4a Construction 10
- **Directional verb:** *dúró sí* (stand by/remain committed to), *dúró nínú* (stand firm in) — P2b constructions 3, 34
- **Habitual marker:** *ń* — P2b constructions 26, 71, 72, 73

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1a. Becoming truthful / coming into truth

**Pattern:** Motion-directional construction — *padà sí* + truth-domain noun

**Attested example:**
> *Padà sí ìgbàgbọ́*
> "Return to faith / come back to belief"
> (P2b construction 36)

**Notes:** This is the only fully attested inchoative construction in the CONTEXT involving a truth-domain noun as the goal state. The verb *padà* (return) implies a prior state of faith that was lost and is being recovered; it encodes re-entry into a state rather than first entry. No parallel construction *padà sí òtítọ́* (return to truth) is attested in the CONTEXT, though the structural pattern would permit it.

---

**Pattern:** Motion-directional construction — *jáde kúrò nínú* + opposed state

**Attested example:**
> *Jáde kúrò nínú àìmọ̀*
> "Come out of ignorance / leave ignorance behind"
> (P2b construction 32)

**Notes:** This construction encodes transition into knowledge by describing departure from its opposed state (*àìmọ̀*, ignorance). The goal state (knowledge, *ìmọ̀*) is implied rather than named. This is the only attested construction of this type in the CONTEXT. No parallel *jáde kúrò nínú irọ́* (come out of falsehood) or *jáde kúrò nínú ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (come out of sin) is attested as a fixed expression in the CONTEXT, though *wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (be in a state of sin, P2b construction 70) implies the logical possibility of such a departure.

---

**Pattern:** *di* + nominal predicate (become)

**Attested in P4a** as an alternate inchoative construction (Construction 4, alternate row). No example sentence is provided in the CONTEXT for this pattern with any truth-domain noun. P4a notes it as an alternate to the motion-based pattern and rates it [UNCERTAIN] for frequency relative to the motion construction.

**Conclusion:** No attested example of *di* + truth-domain nominal is available in the CONTEXT. This pattern is noted as structurally possible but not documented with truth-domain vocabulary.

---

### 1b. Becoming a non-truth / opposed state

**Pattern:** *wà ní* + opposed state (be in / enter a state)

**Attested example:**
> *Wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀*
> "Be in sin / exist in a state of guilt / live in transgression"
> (P2b construction 70)

**Notes:** *Wà ní* encodes being in a state rather than transition into it; it is a stative-locative construction rather than a strict inchoative. However, P2b documents it in contexts of moral discourse where the state of sin is described as something a person can be in, implying entry and exit are possible. No construction encoding the moment of entering sin (*di alẹ̀ṣẹ̀*, become a sinner) is attested in the CONTEXT.

---

**Pattern:** Proverbial constructions asserting the fate of falsehood and deception

**Attested examples:**
> *Irọ́ kò ní dúró* — "A lie will not stand" (P2b construction 16)
> *Irọ́ ò ní gbádùn* — "Falsehood will not prosper" (P2b construction 13)
> *Ìtànjẹ́ kò ní gbádùn* — "Deception will not prosper" (P2b construction 82)

**Notes:** These constructions encode the dynamic fate of falsehood and deception — their inability to persist or succeed — using the negative future *kò ní* / *ò ní*. They do not describe a moment of becoming but assert that the opposed state (standing, prospering) will not be achieved. They are stability-denying rather than strictly inchoative.

## 2. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 2a. Causing someone to be in a truth-related state

**Pattern:** *jẹ́ kí* + NP + *jẹ́* + nominal predicate (permissive-causative)

**Attested example:**
> *Jẹ́ kí òtítọ́ jẹ́ olùdarí rẹ*
> "Let truth be your guide / cause truth to lead you"
> (P2b construction 11)

**Notes:** This construction encodes a causative-permissive relationship in which the speaker urges the addressee to allow truth to occupy the role of guide. The subject of the embedded clause is *òtítọ́* (truth) rather than a person, so the construction causes truth to function as an agent rather than causing a person to become truthful. This is the only attested *jẹ́ kí* construction with a truth-domain noun in the CONTEXT.

---

**Pattern:** *dánilójú* — causing certainty in a person's mind

**Attested example:**
> *Fún ní ìdánilójú*
> "Give assurance to / provide confirmation to / convince"
> (P2b construction 87)

**Notes:** The verbal base *dánilójú* (P2a item 44) is documented as meaning "to make one certain, to convince" — its morphological analysis is *dá* (make/establish) + *ni* (person/one) + *lójú* (in the eye/certain). This is the most direct causative construction in the truth domain: it encodes the act of establishing certainty in another person. The ditransitive *fún ní ìdánilójú* is the attested constructional frame.

---

**Pattern:** *fi* + truth-domain noun + verb (instrumental causation of manner)

**Attested example:**
> *Fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀*
> "Speak with truth / speak truthfully"
> (P2b construction 8)

**Notes:** The *fi* instrumental construction encodes truth as the instrument or manner of speaking rather than as a caused state. P4a notes that this construction "encodes manner rather than strict causation." It is included here because it describes a person deploying truth as a means, which is a weak causative relationship between the speaker's choice and the truth-character of their speech.

---

### 2b. Causing someone to know / causing knowledge

**Pattern:** *fún ní ìtọ́ni* (give guidance to)

**Attested example:**
> *Fún ní ìtọ́ni*
> "Give guidance to / provide direction to / instruct"
> (P2b construction 60)

**Notes:** This construction encodes the act of causing another person to be directed toward what is correct. The causal agent provides *ìtọ́ni* (guidance/correction) to a recipient. It is a causative of correctness-orientation rather than of propositional knowledge, but it belongs to the same domain.

---

**Pattern:** *pín ìmọ̀* (share knowledge)

**Attested example:**
> *Pín ìmọ̀*
> "Share knowledge / distribute understanding"
> (P2b construction 25)

**Notes:** This construction encodes the transmission of knowledge from one person to others. It is a weak causative in that the sharer causes others to have knowledge, but the construction does not encode the recipient's resulting state explicitly.

---

### 2c. Causing a false or opposed state

**Pattern:** *tànjẹ́ ẹni* (deceive a person)

**Attested example:**
> *Tànjẹ́ ẹni*
> "Deceive a person / defraud someone / trick one"
> (P2b construction 81)

**Notes:** This is the most direct causative construction for producing a false epistemic state in another person: the deceiver causes the deceived person to hold a false belief. The verbal base *tànjẹ́* takes a person as its direct object, encoding the person as the target of the caused state.

---

**Pattern:** *gbọ́ irọ́* (believe a lie / be taken in by a lie)

**Attested example:**
> *Gbọ́ irọ́*
> "Believe a lie / accept a falsehood / be taken in by a lie"
> (P2b construction 17)

**Notes:** This construction encodes the result state of successful deception from the perspective of the deceived person. It is not strictly causative in form but describes the state that deception causes: the person has come to accept a false statement as true. P2b notes it "appears in moral warnings and in assessments of gullibility or deception."

## 3. STABILITY AND PERSISTENCE OF TRUTH-RELATED STATES

### 3a. Stability of truth

**Pattern:** *dúró sí* + truth-domain noun (stand by / remain committed to)

**Attested example:**
> *Dúró sí òtítọ́*
> "Stand by the truth / hold to the truth / remain committed to the truth"
> (P2b construction 3)

**Notes:** This construction encodes the maintenance of a commitment to truth under conditions that might otherwise cause departure from it. The verb *dúró* (to stand, to remain) encodes stability and resistance to change. P2b notes it is "used in moral and legal contexts to describe steadfast commitment to truthfulness under pressure."

---

**Pattern:** *dúró nínú* + truth-domain noun (stand firm in)

**Attested example:**
> *Dúró nínú ìgbàgbọ́*
> "Stand firm in faith / remain in belief / hold fast to conviction"
> (P2b construction 34)

**Notes:** Structurally parallel to *dúró sí òtítọ́* but with the locative *nínú* (in/within) rather than the directional *sí* (toward/by). Both encode persistence in a truth-related state. P2b notes this is "used in religious discourse to urge steadfastness in faith."

---

**Pattern:** Negative future *kò ní kú* (will not die) — permanence of truth

**Attested example:**
> *Òtítọ́ kò ní kú*
> "Truth will not die / truth is imperishable"
> (P2b construction 6)

**Notes:** This proverbial construction asserts the absolute stability of truth using the negative future *kò ní* + *kú* (die). It encodes truth as a state that cannot be destroyed or ended. P2b notes it "appears in moral discourse and in legal contexts to assert that the truth will eventually emerge."

---

### 3b. Instability and impermanence of falsehood and deception

**Pattern:** Negative future *kò ní* + persistence verb — falsehood cannot endure

**Attested examples:**
> *Irọ́ kò ní dúró* — "A lie will not stand" (P2b construction 16)
> *Irọ́ ò ní gbádùn* — "Falsehood will not prosper" (P2b construction 13)
> *Ìtànjẹ́ kò ní gbádùn* — "Deception will not prosper" (P2b construction 82)
> *Òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú* — "Justice must not die" (P2b construction 21, normative rather than descriptive)

**Notes:** The negative future construction *kò ní* is the primary grammatical resource for asserting the instability of falsehood and deception. It is used with *dúró* (stand/endure) and *gbádùn* (prosper/succeed) to deny that these states can persist. The construction with *òdodo* (P2b construction 21) is normative (*kò gbọdọ̀*, must not) rather than predictive (*kò ní*, will not), encoding a different modal force — obligation rather than prediction.

---

### 3c. Stability of sin and its consequences

**Pattern:** *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀* (sin will not escape / go unpunished)

**Attested example:**
> *Ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀*
> "Sin will not go unpunished / guilt will not escape / offense will not be overlooked"
> (P2b construction 69)

**Notes:** This construction uses *kò ní* + *yọ̀* (escape/slip away) to assert that the consequences of sin are stable and inescapable. It encodes the persistence of moral consequence rather than the persistence of the sin-state itself.

---

### 3d. Stability of error and commitment to non-recurrence

**Pattern:** *àṣìṣe kò ní ṣẹlẹ̀ mọ́* (the error will not happen again)

**Attested example:**
> *Àṣìṣe kò ní ṣẹlẹ̀ mọ́*
> "The error will not happen again / the mistake will not recur"
> (P2b construction 64)

**Notes:** This construction uses *kò ní* + *ṣẹlẹ̀* (happen/occur) + *mọ́* (again/anymore) to assert the non-recurrence of a past error. It encodes a commitment to change — the error-state will not be re-entered. P2b notes it "appears in legal and interpersonal discourse" in contexts of apology and reform.

| Being in a state (stative-locative) | *wà ní* + noun | *wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* | *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* |

| Instrumental manner | *fi* + noun + verb | *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀* | *òtítọ́* |

| Inescapability of consequence | *kò ní yọ̀* | *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀* | *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* |

## 5. TERMS FOR WHICH NO DYNAMIC EXPRESSION IS ATTESTED

The following truth-domain terms from P1 do not appear in any attested dynamic construction in the CONTEXT:

- *òdodo* (justice/righteousness): The construction *òdodo kò gbọdọ̀ kú* (P2b construction 21) is normative rather than dynamic; no inchoative or causative construction is attested.
- *ẹ̀rí* and its compounds: No construction encoding becoming evidence, causing evidence to exist, or evidence persisting is attested beyond the existential pair *ẹ̀rí tó péye wà* / *ẹ̀rí kò sí*.
- *gidi* and *ojúlówó*: No dynamic construction is attested; both appear only in attributive and identificational constructions.
- *ìjọra* and *dọ̀gba*: No dynamic construction is attested in the CONTEXT.
- *ìmúrasílẹ̀*: Flagged [UNCERTAIN] in P1; no dynamic construction is attested.
