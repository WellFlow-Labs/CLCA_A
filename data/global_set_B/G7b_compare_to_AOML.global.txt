## 2. Summary Analysis

### 2.1 Confirmed Patterns (MATCH Alignments)

**STRONG EMPIRICAL SUPPORT FOR AOML CORE STRUCTURE**

1. **Basic Attribution (ATTR) Universally Licensed**
   - Empirical: F1 ALLOWED across all dimensions in all 8 languages
   - AOML: ATTR licensed for all primary axes
   - **Evidence**: Perfect match. Both systems treat basic predication as universally available.

2. **Dispositional Causation Block (Rule R1)**
   - Empirical: D5 (Honesty/Sincerity) × F5 (Causative) shows VARIABLE/restricted status
   - AOML: SINC × CAUSE = universally_blocked
   - **CONFLICT NOTED**: Empirical shows variable rather than universal blocking. See §2.3 below.

3. **Positive Emergence Bias (Rule R4)**
   - Empirical: F4 asymmetry — positive emergence natural, negative emergence blocked (7/8 languages)
   - AOML: BECOME(+) >> BECOME(−)
   - **Evidence**: Strong match. G6 documents: "become good" productive, "become false" blocked/marginal.

4. **Abstract Nominalization Productivity**
   - Empirical: F10 ALLOWED across all dimensions (8/8 languages)
   - AOML: ABSTR licensed for all axes except LIFE (blocked), ESS (restricted)
   - **Evidence**: Strong match with noted exceptions. AOML LIFE blocking confirmed by empirical gap in lifelikeness nominalization.

5. **Inchoative Restrictions for Truth**
   - Empirical: D1 × F4 shows VARIABLE (restricted in 7/8 languages); preference for revelation constructions
   - AOML: VER × BECOME = restricted; REVEAL operator provisional
   - **Evidence**: Strong match. Empirical documents preference for "truth emerged/was revealed" over "became true."

6. **Essence Stability (Rule R2)**
   - Empirical: D-ESS not explicitly tested, but inference from data suggests essential properties resist causation/change
   - AOML: ESS × CAUSE = blocked; ESS × BECOME = blocked
   - **Evidence**: Indirect support. No language shows "make essentially X" or "become essentially X" as productive.

7. **Contrast/Opposition Universally Licensed**
   - Empirical: All dimensions show productive opposition structures (F5 opposition tables)
   - AOML: CONTR licensed for all axes
   - **Evidence**: Perfect match. All languages show binary oppositions (true/false, genuine/fake, etc.).

### 2.2 Partial or Conditional Matches

**AREAS OF STRUCTURAL OVERLAP WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

1. **Causative Verification (F5) and VER × CAUSE**
   - Empirical: ALLOWED in 6/8 languages with productive causative morphology
   - AOML: VER × CAUSE = restricted
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Both agree verification is possible but conditioned. Empirical shows high productivity in languages with causative morphology; AOML predicts general restriction without specifying morphological conditioning.
   - **Gap**: AOML doesn't model the morphological typology variation (affixal vs. resultative compounds vs. separate roots).

2. **Authenticity Causation and Rule R3**
   - Empirical: D2 × F5 shows ALLOWED for verification; D2 × F6 shows VARIABLE for falsification
   - AOML: AUTH × CAUSE(+) = blocked; AUTH × CAUSE(−) = licensed
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. AOML predicts asymmetry (can't manufacture authenticity, can forge/destroy it). Empirical shows verification productive but doesn't clearly separate positive creation from verification. Empirical "verify authenticity" may not equal "manufacture authenticity."
   - **Interpretation**: AOML Rule R3 may be correct but empirical data conflates verification (establishing pre-existing authenticity) with causation (creating authenticity de novo).

3. **Precision/Accuracy (D4) and DEG Meta-Axis**
   - Empirical: D4 treated as separate dimension in 5/8 languages
   - AOML: Models precision as DEG[VER] — degree modification of veridical axis
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Structural difference: empirical treats precision as dimension; AOML treats as meta-axis. Both capture the gradability and distinction from binary correctness.
   - **Theoretical issue**: Is precision a separate axis or degree modification of truth? Empirical lexicalization in 5/8 languages suggests possible independent axis, but AOML meta-axis model is also defensible.

4. **Validity/Legitimacy (D9) and NORM Axis**
   - Empirical: D9 lexicalized in 4/8 languages (legal/institutional contexts)
   - AOML: Maps to NORM axis (institutional rightness)
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Empirical D9 is more restricted (legal/administrative registers); AOML NORM is broader (moral, legal, procedural). Both capture institutional validation but with different scope.

5. **Reliability/Dependability (D10) and SINC or VER**
   - Empirical: D10 lexicalized in 4/8 languages (track record, consistency)
   - AOML: Could map to SINC (character reliability) or VER (factual consistency)
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Empirical dimension doesn't map cleanly to single AOML axis. Reliability spans character (SINC) and factual consistency (VER). AOML doesn't model this as separate axis.

6. **Relational/Emotional Authenticity (D14) and SINC or AUTH**
   - Empirical: D14 attested in 7/8 languages (genuine feelings, true friendship)
   - AOML: Could map to SINC (sincerity of emotion) or AUTH (genuineness of relationship)
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Empirical dimension spans both AOML axes. "True love" involves both authentic emotion (AUTH?) and sincere feeling (SINC?). AOML doesn't clearly model relational authenticity.

7. **Comparative Constructions (F13) and DEG Meta-Axis**
   - Empirical: F13 ALLOWED universally; all languages use analytic comparison only
   - AOML: DEG meta-axis models degree modification
   - **Alignment**: PARTIAL. Both capture gradability, but empirical documents universal blocking of synthetic forms (*truer, *falser). AOML doesn't predict this morphological restriction.

### 2.3 Conflicts (Empirical Contradicts AOML)

**AREAS WHERE EMPIRICAL DATA CHALLENGES AOML PREDICTIONS**

1. **MAJOR CONFLICT: Dispositional Causation (Rule R1)**
   - **AOML Prediction**: SINC × CAUSE = universally_blocked (p < 1e-9, 16 of 16 languages)
   - **Empirical Finding**: D5 × F5 shows VARIABLE/restricted, not universally blocked
   - **Evidence**: 
     - G4 notes: "Languages show varying strategies for causative verification/correction"
     - F6 data shows some languages allow constructions like "make honest" or "improve character"
     - Chinese 使...诚实, Greek κάνω κάποιον ειλικρινή show marginal but attested causative constructions
   - **Interpretation**: AOML Rule R1 may be too strong. Empirical data suggests dispositional causation is **restricted** rather than **universally blocked**. The restriction may be:
     - Indirect causation allowed (create conditions for honesty)
     - Long-term cultivation allowed (educate toward honesty)
     - Direct instant causation blocked (can't make someone honest immediately)
   - **Recommendation**: Rule R1 should be revised from "universally blocked" to "severely restricted" with specification of allowed indirect pathways.

2. **CONFLICT: Error Emergence (Rule R4 Application)**
   - **AOML Prediction**: BECOME(−) blocked or marked across truth-domain (Rule R4)
   - **Empirical Finding**: D7 × F4 shows "make mistake" ALLOWED and productive (7/8 languages)
   - **Evidence**:
     - F6 shows "make mistake" constructions [CORE] across languages
     - Light verb constructions (F12) for error-making universal
     - No blocking observed for negative emergence in error domain
   - **Interpretation**: Rule R4 (positive emergence bias) does not apply uniformly to error domain. "Making mistakes" is conceptualized as action, not state emergence. AOML may need to distinguish:
     - State emergence (becoming false) — blocked
     - Action performance (making mistakes) — allowed
   - **Recommendation**: Rule R4 should specify it applies to state emergence, not action performance.

3. **CONFLICT: Causative Falsification Strength**
   - **AOML Prediction**: VER × CAUSE(−) = restricted (not blocked)
   - **Empirical Finding**: F6 shows BLOCKED (8/8 languages) or uses completely different strategies
   - **Evidence**:
     - G6 documents: "No language shows productive causative 'make false' from false/lie roots"
     - All languages use deception verbs (deceive, mislead) instead
     - Only Greek shows marginal διαψεύδω; English falsify uses different root
   - **Interpretation**: Empirical blocking is stronger than AOML restriction. Falsification is not just restricted but appears structurally blocked as direct causation. Languages reconceptualize as:
     - Deception (causing false belief)
     - Corruption (destroying truth)
     - Forgery (creating false documents)
   - **Recommendation**: AOML should strengthen VER × CAUSE(−) from "restricted" to "blocked" with note that falsification is achieved through alternative conceptualizations.

### 2.4 Unmodeled Empirical Findings

**STRONG EMPIRICAL PATTERNS NOT CAPTURED BY AOML**

1. **Agentive Nominalization Asymmetry (F8 vs F9)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Negative agentives (liar, deceiver) productive in 8/8 languages; positive agentives (truth-teller) blocked in 6/8 languages
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled. AOML operator set doesn't include agentive derivation.
   - **Evidence Strength**: VERY STRONG. G6 documents as "strongest universal blocking pattern in the data."
   - **Theoretical Significance**: Represents systematic cross-linguistic asymmetry in derivational morphology. Suggests universal cognitive bias toward marking negative moral agents.
   - **Recommendation**: AOML should add AGENT operator with polarity-conditioned licensing: AGENT(−) universally licensed; AGENT(+) blocked for most axes.

2. **Light Verb Constructions (F12)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: "Speak truth/lies" and "make mistake" constructions universally conventionalized (8/8 languages)
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as distinct constructional function
   - **Evidence Strength**: VERY STRONG. Universal across all languages despite structural variation.
   - **Theoretical Significance**: Represents universal pragmatic importance of truth-telling and error-making speech acts.
   - **Recommendation**: AOML could add SPEECH-ACT operator or note that certain axis × verb combinations are universally conventionalized.

3. **Discourse Marker Grammaticalization (F17)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Truth-domain terms grammaticalize into discourse markers in 7/8 languages (not Sanskrit)
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled
   - **Evidence Strength**: STRONG. G5 documents as "universal pragmatic pathway active in living languages."
   - **Theoretical Significance**: Represents diachronic pathway: truth-assertion → speaker stance marker → discourse management particle.
   - **Recommendation**: AOML could note this as pragmatic extension beyond core semantic geometry, or add DISCOURSE-MANAGEMENT function.

4. **Adverbial Derivation (F11)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Manner adverbs (truly, honestly, accurately) universally productive (8/8 languages)
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as distinct operator
   - **Evidence Strength**: VERY STRONG. Universal across all languages with varying formal strategies.
   - **Recommendation**: AOML could add MANNER operator or note that adverbial derivation is universally licensed.

5. **Substantiality/Concreteness Dimension (D8)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Strong lexicalization in Chinese (实/虚 shí/xū) and Quechua (hunt'a/ch'usaq); moderate in Greek
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as primary axis
   - **Evidence Strength**: MODERATE. Strong in 2/8 languages; weak in others.
   - **Theoretical Significance**: Represents evaluation of whether something has genuine substance versus being empty/hollow. Applies to both physical objects and abstract qualities (arguments, promises).
   - **Recommendation**: Insufficient evidence for universal axis, but could be added as language-specific elaboration or cultural dimension.

6. **Completeness/Wholeness Dimension (D11)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Lexicalized in 5/8 languages; applied to truth in constructions like "whole truth" vs. "half-truth"
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as primary axis
   - **Evidence Strength**: MODERATE. Present in multiple languages but not universal.
   - **Recommendation**: Could be modeled as subregion within VER (completeness of truth-telling) or as separate dimension if more evidence emerges.

7. **Clarity/Obviousness Dimension (D12)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Lexicalized in 5/8 languages (Hebrew ברור, Greek dhahiri, Swahili dhahiri, Tagalog lantád, Quechua suti)
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as primary axis; possibly DEG[VER] (degree of epistemic accessibility)
   - **Evidence Strength**: MODERATE. Present in multiple languages.
   - **Recommendation**: Could be modeled as DEG[VER] (degree of clarity) or as epistemic accessibility subregion within VER.

8. **Certainty/Confidence Dimension (D13)**
   - **Empirical Finding**: Lexicalized in 4/8 languages; Quechua grammaticalizes through evidentials
   - **AOML Status**: Not modeled as primary axis
   - **Evidence Strength**: MODERATE. Present in multiple languages but variable.
   - **Recommendation**: This is epistemic stance (speaker confidence) rather than truth-value. AOML correctly doesn't model as axis. Could be noted as pragmatic/evidential extension.

   - **Recommendation**: Requires targeted empirical investigation. Current data insufficient to confirm or disconfirm.

   - **Empirical Status**: Partially supported

   - **Recommendation**: DISPOSITIONAL target type strongly supported; FACTUAL/EPISTEMIC distinction requires clarification or additional evidence.

## 3. Theoretical Discussion

### 3.1 Where AOML Appears Strongly Supported

**CORE STRUCTURAL PREDICTIONS CONFIRMED**

1. **Seven-Axis Minimal Basis**
   - AOML predicts seven primary axes are necessary and sufficient
   - Empirical data identifies 14 semantic dimensions (D1-D14)
   - **Alignment**: AOML's seven axes can account for most empirical dimensions:
     - VER covers D1 (propositional), D3 (correctness), D7 (error)
     - AUTH covers D2 (material authenticity), D14 (relational authenticity)
     - SINC covers D5 (honesty/sincerity), D10 (reliability - partially)
     - NORM covers D9 (validity/legitimacy), D3 (normative correctness)
     - ESS, VERIF, LIFE cover remaining specialized dimensions
   - **Unaccounted**: D8 (substantiality), D11 (completeness), D12 (clarity), D13 (certainty)
   - **Interpretation**: AOML's seven axes provide good coverage but some empirical dimensions (D8, D11, D12, D13) don't map cleanly. These may be:
     - Subregions within AOML axes (Layer 2)
     - Meta-axis modifications (DEG, TEMP)
     - Language-specific elaborations
     - Genuine gaps in AOML

2. **Operator Licensing Asymmetries**
   - AOML predicts systematic asymmetries in operator licensing
   - Empirical data confirms:
     - **Rule R1** (dispositional causation block): Supported with qualification (see §2.3 conflict)
     - **Rule R3** (authenticity polarity asymmetry): Partially supported
     - **Rule R4** (positive emergence bias): Strongly supported
   - **Interpretation**: AOML's operator licensing framework captures real cross-linguistic asymmetries. The asymmetries are not arbitrary but reflect structural constraints on how truth-domain meanings can be expressed.

3. **Meta-Axis Substitution Prohibition (Rule R5)**
   - AOML predicts DEG[A] cannot substitute for A in inference
   - Empirical data doesn't directly test this (no fallacy analysis in current data)
   - **Indirect support**: G3 notes precision (D4) is distinct from simple correctness (D3) in 5/8 languages, suggesting degree modification doesn't equal base claim

### 3.2 Where AOML May Require Refinement

   - **Issue**: AOML models VERIF as primary axis; empirical data treats verification as constructional function (F5)
   - **Theoretical Question**: Is verification a truth-kind (axis) or a process (operator)?
   - **Evidence**:
     - Empirical G3 Dimension 15: "Verification Process vs. Truth State" — universal distinction (8/8 languages)
     - But empirical treats verification as **function** not **dimension**
     - AOML treats VERIF as axis with its own validation methods
   - **Interpretation**: Both models capture the distinction but structure it differently:
     - AOML: Verification is a claim-type (axis) — "this is verified" is different kind of claim than "this is true"
     - Empirical: Verification is a process (function) — "verify X" is operation on truth-claims
   - **Recommendation**: Clarify whether VERIF is:
     - **Axis**: "X is verified" as distinct truth-kind
     - **Operator**: "Verify X" as operation on other axes
     - **Both**: Verification has both state (verified status) and process (verifying action) aspects

3. **Precision/Accuracy (D4) as Dimension vs. Meta-Axis**
   - **Issue**: Empirical treats precision as separate dimension (D4); AOML models as DEG[VER]
   - **Evidence**:
     - 5/8 languages lexically distinguish precision from simple correctness
     - Chinese 准确 vs. 对, Hebrew מדויק vs. נכון, Greek ακριβής vs. σωστός, English accurate vs. correct
   - **Theoretical Question**: Is precision degree modification of truth or separate dimension?
   - **Arguments for Meta-Axis (AOML)**:
     - Precision is gradable (more/less precise)

     - Precision is "how correct" not "whether correct"
   - **Arguments for Separate Dimension (Empirical)**:
     - Lexicalized separately in 5/8 languages
     - Different verification procedures (precision requires measurement; correctness requires binary check)
     - Can be correct without being precise (right answer, wrong decimal places)
   - **Recommendation**: This is genuine theoretical ambiguity. Both models are defensible. AOML meta-axis model is more parsimonious; empirical separate dimension model reflects lexicalization patterns. Could be resolved by:
     - Testing whether precision claims require independent validation methods
     - Analyzing whether precision violations constitute distinct fallacy types
     - Examining whether precision can be predicated independently of correctness

4. **Completeness/Wholeness (D11) as Dimension**
   - **Issue**: Empirical identifies completeness as dimension (D11) in 5/8 languages; AOML doesn't model it
   - **Evidence**: Applied to truth in "whole truth" vs. "half-truth"; also to objects and processes
   - **Theoretical Question**: Is completeness:
     - Subregion within VER (completeness of truth-telling)?
     - Meta-axis modification (degree of completeness)?
     - Separate dimension?
   - **Recommendation**: Likely subregion within VER or DEG modification rather than separate axis. Empirical data shows it applies across domains (not just truth), suggesting it's not truth-domain-specific axis.
