Below is a construction-focused inventory for the **P1 base forms**, restricted to **attested multi-word constructions** in Modern Greek and avoiding mere derivational listings already handled in P2a. I include fixed phrases, common predicate patterns, and recurrent syntactic frames. Where I do not identify a common construction centered on the P1 item, I state so explicitly.

---

## 1. Base Form: **αλήθεια**

### 1. Example: **λέω την αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** léo tin alítheia
- **English translation:** tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** Very common everyday predicate for speaking truthfully. Used both literally and in interpersonal/moral contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2. Example: **την αλήθεια να λέμε**
- **Transliteration:** tin alítheia na léme
- **English translation:** to tell the truth; truth be told
- **Usage notes:** Common discourse formula introducing a candid admission or correction. Often slightly conversational or rhetorical.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3. Example: **η αλήθεια είναι ότι...**
- **Transliteration:** i alítheia eínai óti...
- **English translation:** the truth is that...
- **Usage notes:** Extremely common clause-introducing construction in speech and writing. Often used to present the speaker’s real assessment, sometimes close to “actually.”
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. Example: **στην αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** stin alítheia
- **English translation:** in truth
- **Usage notes:** Attested but much less common than alternatives like *στην πραγματικότητα*. It belongs more to elevated or rhetorical usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

### 5. Example: **μακριά από την αλήθεια**
- **Transliteration:** makriá apó tin alítheia
- **English translation:** far from the truth
- **Usage notes:** Common evaluative phrase for claims, impressions, or depictions that do not match reality. Used in journalism, argumentation, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. Example: **κόκκος αλήθειας**
- **Transliteration:** kókkos alítheias
- **English translation:** grain of truth
- **Usage notes:** Established idiomatic expression for a small element of truth within an otherwise doubtful or exaggerated claim. Common in public and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
