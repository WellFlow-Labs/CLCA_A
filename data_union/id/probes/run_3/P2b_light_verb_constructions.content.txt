# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the 30 P1 base forms. "Multi-word construction" is understood here as a syntactically-driven or periphrastic expression that is not reducible to simple affixal derivation (handled in P2a) and not a bare noun-modifier compound (handled in P1b). The focus is on:

- Periphrastic verbal constructions (*sudah terbukti benar*, *tidak dapat dibenarkan*)
- Idiomatic fixed expressions (*benar-benar dari hati*, *jujur saja*)
- Discourse-level formulae (*sebenarnya begini*, *pada kenyataannya*)
- Evaluative and modal constructions (*terbukti palsu*, *dinyatakan tidak sah*)
- Collocational patterns that are syntactically productive but semantically constrained

Constructions already fully documented as derivational forms in P2a (e.g., *kebenaran*, *memalsukan*) are not repeated here. Noun-modifier compounds documented in P1b (e.g., *berita palsu*, *dokumen palsu*) are not repeated here unless they participate in a larger periphrastic construction.

Where no common construction exists for a base form or a particular construction type, this is stated explicitly.

## 1. Base Form: **benar**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Itu benar adanya.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That is genuinely the case. / That is truly so.
- **Usage Notes:** A fixed predicative construction used to affirm that something is factually and genuinely the case, often with a tone of solemn acknowledgment or resignation. The phrase *benar adanya* functions as a fixed unit; *adanya* (its existence, as it is) reinforces the sense of factual givenness. Common in formal speech and writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Ada benarnya juga.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** There is some truth to it as well. / It has a point.
- **Usage Notes:** A partial-concession construction used to acknowledge that a claim or argument has some validity without fully endorsing it. *Ada benarnya* is a fixed unit; *juga* (also, as well) is optional and adds a concessive nuance. Common in conversational and argumentative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Memang benar bahwa harga terus naik.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** It is indeed true that prices keep rising.
- **Usage Notes:** A confirmation construction using *memang* (indeed, as expected) + *benar* + *bahwa* (that) to introduce a factual claim that is being affirmed, often in response to a prior assertion or in the context of acknowledging an uncomfortable truth. *Memang benar* functions as a fixed affirmative unit.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Tidak dapat dibenarkan dengan alasan apa pun.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** It cannot be justified by any reason whatsoever.
- **Usage Notes:** A modal negation construction used in legal, moral, and journalistic discourse to declare that an action or claim is wholly without justification. *Tidak dapat dibenarkan* (cannot be justified/confirmed) is a fixed evaluative unit; the addition of *dengan alasan apa pun* (by any reason whatsoever) intensifies the rejection.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Sudah terbukti benar.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** It has already been proven true. / It has been verified as correct.
- **Usage Notes:** A resultative construction combining *sudah* (already) + *terbukti* (proven, verified) + *benar* (true) to indicate that the truth of something has been established through evidence. Common in journalistic, legal, and scientific discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Kalau benar demikian, maka...
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** If that is indeed the case, then...
- **Usage Notes:** A conditional construction used in argumentation and formal discourse to introduce a consequence that follows if a prior claim is accepted as true. *Kalau benar demikian* (if truly so) is a fixed conditional opener. Common in legal reasoning, journalistic commentary, and formal debate.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Benar atau tidak, hal itu perlu diselidiki.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** True or not, the matter needs to be investigated.
- **Usage Notes:** A suspension-of-judgment construction using *benar atau tidak* (true or not) to bracket the truth value of a claim while asserting that it warrants attention regardless. Common in journalistic and investigative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8:**
- **Base Form:** benar
- **Example:** Saya rasa ada benarnya pendapat itu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** I think there is some truth to that opinion.
- **Usage Notes:** A hedged partial-endorsement construction combining *saya rasa* (I think/feel) + *ada benarnya* (there is some truth to it) + the evaluated item. The hedge *saya rasa* marks the speaker's epistemic tentativeness. Common in conversational and opinion discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 2. Base Form: **salah**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Itu bukan salah saya.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That is not my fault. / That is not my mistake.
- **Usage Notes:** A denial-of-responsibility construction using *bukan salah* + possessive to reject attribution of fault. *Bukan salah saya* is a fixed unit in interpersonal and legal discourse. The construction *bukan salah X* (not X's fault) is fully productive with any possessive.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Tidak ada salahnya mencoba.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** There is no harm in trying. / It is not wrong to try.
- **Usage Notes:** A permission-granting construction using *tidak ada salahnya* + infinitive to suggest that a proposed action is unobjectionable. *Tidak ada salahnya* is a fixed unit; the following verb phrase is open. Common in motivational and advisory discourse. Documented in P1b as a peripheral extension of *salah*.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Siapa yang salah dalam kasus ini?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Who is at fault in this case? / Who is wrong in this case?
- **Usage Notes:** An interrogative fault-attribution construction using *siapa yang salah* (who is wrong/at fault) to open an inquiry into responsibility. *Yang salah* (the one who is wrong/at fault) is a productive relative-clause construction that can be nominalized: *yang salah harus bertanggung jawab* (the one who is wrong must take responsibility).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Jangan salahkan diri sendiri.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Don't blame yourself.
- **Usage Notes:** An imperative construction using *jangan* (don't) + *salahkan* (blame) + reflexive *diri sendiri* (oneself). A common expression in interpersonal and psychological discourse. The construction *jangan salahkan* + object is fully productive.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Kalau salah, harus diakui.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** If wrong, it must be acknowledged. / If mistaken, one must admit it.
- **Usage Notes:** A conditional moral construction used in ethical and interpersonal discourse to assert the obligation to acknowledge error. *Kalau salah* (if wrong) is a fixed conditional opener in moral argumentation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Itu salah besar.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That is a serious mistake. / That is gravely wrong.
- **Usage Notes:** An intensified error-evaluation construction using *salah* + *besar* (big, great) to characterize an error or moral wrong as serious. *Salah besar* is a fixed evaluative unit used in moral, legal, and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** salah
- **Example:** Bukan berarti dia salah.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That does not mean he is wrong. / It does not follow that he is at fault.
- **Usage Notes:** A logical-denial construction using *bukan berarti* (does not mean) + *salah* to block an inference from a prior claim to a conclusion of fault or error. Common in argumentative and legal discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: **asli**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Dijamin keasliannya.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Its authenticity is guaranteed. / Authenticity guaranteed.
- **Usage Notes:** A commercial and legal guarantee construction using *dijamin* (guaranteed) + *keasliannya* (its authenticity). Commonly used in product marketing, art sales, and document certification. Functions as a fixed assurance formula.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Apakah ini benar-benar asli?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Is this really genuine? / Is this truly authentic?
- **Usage Notes:** An authenticity-inquiry construction combining *benar-benar* (truly, really) + *asli* (genuine) to intensify the question about authenticity. Common in commercial, forensic, and everyday contexts when evaluating whether an item is genuine.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Terbukti asli oleh para ahli.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Proven genuine by experts. / Verified as authentic by specialists.
- **Usage Notes:** A resultative authentication construction using *terbukti* (proven, verified) + *asli* (genuine) + *oleh* (by) + agent. Common in art authentication, forensic document analysis, and legal proceedings.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Ini asli, bukan tiruan.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** This is genuine, not an imitation.
- **Usage Notes:** A contrastive authenticity assertion using *asli* (genuine) + *bukan* (not) + *tiruan* (imitation) to explicitly distinguish an original from a copy. The *asli ... bukan tiruan* frame is a common commercial and legal formula.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Diragukan keasliannya.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Its authenticity is in doubt. / The authenticity is questioned.
- **Usage Notes:** A doubt-expression construction using *diragukan* (doubted, questioned) + *keasliannya* (its authenticity). Common in legal, journalistic, and forensic contexts when the genuineness of a document, artwork, or claim is under scrutiny.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** asli
- **Example:** Mengaku asli padahal palsu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Claiming to be genuine when in fact fake.
- **Usage Notes:** A deception-characterization construction using *mengaku* (claiming to be) + *asli* + *padahal* (when in fact, whereas) + *palsu* (fake). The *mengaku X padahal Y* frame is a productive construction for characterizing false claims of identity or authenticity. Common in consumer protection and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 4. Base Form: **palsu**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** palsu
- **Example:** Terbukti palsu di pengadilan.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Proven false in court. / Established as fraudulent in court proceedings.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal resultative construction using *terbukti* (proven) + *palsu* (false, forged) + *di pengadilan* (in court). Common in legal journalism and court reporting to describe the judicial determination that a document, claim, or identity is fraudulent.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** palsu
- **Example:** Ternyata itu semua palsu belaka.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** It turns out that was all entirely fake. / As it turned out, it was nothing but a fabrication.
- **Usage Notes:** A revelation-of-falsity construction using *ternyata* (it turns out, as it turned out) + *itu semua* (all of that) + *palsu belaka* (entirely fake). *Ternyata* marks the discovery or revelation of a previously unknown truth; the construction is common in journalistic exposés and personal narratives.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** palsu
- **Example:** Diduga menggunakan dokumen palsu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Suspected of using forged documents.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal-journalistic suspicion construction using *diduga* (suspected, alleged) + *menggunakan* (using) + *dokumen palsu* (forged documents). *Diduga* is a standard journalistic hedge marking unverified allegations. The construction is fully productive with any *[noun] palsu* compound.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** palsu
- **Example:** Tidak ada yang palsu dari pernyataan itu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** There is nothing false about that statement.
- **Usage Notes:** A negated-falsity construction using *tidak ada yang palsu* (there is nothing false) + *dari* (about, from) + the evaluated item. Used in defensive or exculpatory discourse to deny that a statement or document contains any falsification.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** palsu
- **Example:** Berpura-pura asli padahal palsu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Pretending to be genuine when in fact fake.
- **Usage Notes:** A pretense-characterization construction using *berpura-pura* (to pretend, to feign) + *asli* (genuine) + *padahal* (when in fact) + *palsu* (fake). The *berpura-pura X padahal Y* frame characterizes deliberate misrepresentation. Common in consumer protection and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 5. Base Form: **nyata**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Pada kenyataannya, hal itu tidak terjadi.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** In reality, that did not happen. / The fact of the matter is, that did not occur.
- **Usage Notes:** A reality-framing construction using *pada kenyataannya* (in reality, in fact) as a fixed adverbial phrase to introduce a claim about what is actually the case, often contrasting with appearances, expectations, or prior assertions. One of the most common discourse-level constructions in this domain.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Bukti nyata yang tidak bisa dibantah.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Concrete evidence that cannot be refuted. / Tangible proof that is irrefutable.
- **Usage Notes:** An evidential construction using *bukti nyata* (concrete/tangible evidence) + *yang tidak bisa dibantah* (that cannot be refuted). The relative clause *yang tidak bisa dibantah* is a common intensifying extension in legal and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Hal ini nyata-nyata melanggar hukum.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** This clearly/manifestly violates the law.
- **Usage Notes:** An intensified violation-assertion construction using *nyata-nyata* (clearly, manifestly) + predicate. *Nyata-nyata* functions as a pre-predicate intensifier emphasizing that the violation is unmistakable and undeniable. Common in legal and journalistic discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Menjadi kenyataan yang pahit.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** To become a bitter reality.
- **Usage Notes:** A reality-realization construction using *menjadi* (to become) + *kenyataan* (reality) + evaluative modifier. *Kenyataan yang pahit* (bitter reality) is a common collocation; the *menjadi kenyataan* (to become reality) construction is productive with various modifiers and is used in literary, journalistic, and everyday registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Menghadapi kenyataan dengan lapang dada.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** To face reality with an open heart. / To accept reality graciously.
- **Usage Notes:** A reality-acceptance construction using *menghadapi kenyataan* (to face reality) + *dengan lapang dada* (with an open heart, graciously). *Menghadapi kenyataan* is a fixed collocation; *dengan lapang dada* is a common idiomatic extension. Used in motivational, moral, and literary discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Jauh dari kenyataan.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Far from reality. / Divorced from reality.
- **Usage Notes:** A fixed evaluative construction using *jauh dari kenyataan* (far from reality) to characterize a claim, plan, or expectation as unrealistic or disconnected from actual conditions. Common in political commentary, journalism, and everyday evaluative discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7:**
- **Base Form:** nyata
- **Example:** Dinyatakan bersalah oleh hakim.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Declared guilty by the judge.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal declaration construction using *dinyatakan* (declared, pronounced) + *bersalah* (guilty) + *oleh* (by) + agent. *Dinyatakan bersalah* is a fixed legal formula for the judicial verdict of guilt. The *dinyatakan* + [status/condition] construction is productive across legal and administrative contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 6. Base Form: **sungguh**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** sungguh
- **Example:** Sungguh, saya tidak bermaksud demikian.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Truly, I did not mean it that way. / I genuinely did not intend that.
- **Usage Notes:** A sincerity-assertion construction using *sungguh* as a sentence-initial intensifier to affirm the genuineness of a denial or declaration. The sentence-initial *sungguh* functions as a discourse particle marking earnest assertion, distinct from the adjectival or adverbial uses of the base form.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** sungguh
- **Example:** Apakah kamu sungguh-sungguh serius dengan ini?
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Are you genuinely serious about this? / Do you really mean this?
- **Usage Notes:** An earnestness-inquiry construction using *sungguh-sungguh serius* (genuinely serious) to question whether a person's commitment or statement is sincere. The combination *sungguh-sungguh* + adjective is a productive intensification pattern.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** sungguh
- **Example:** Kalau sungguh-sungguh, pasti bisa.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** If you are truly earnest about it, you can certainly do it.
- **Usage Notes:** A conditional-motivational construction using *kalau sungguh-sungguh* (if earnest/serious) to assert that genuine effort leads to success. A common formula in motivational, educational, and parental discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** sungguh
- **Example:** Sungguh-sungguh dari hati.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Truly from the heart. / Genuinely heartfelt.
- **Usage Notes:** A sincerity-intensification construction using *sungguh-sungguh* + *dari hati* (from the heart) to emphasize the depth and genuineness of a feeling or commitment. Common in personal, religious, and literary discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## 7. Base Form: **sejati**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** sejati
- **Example:** Cinta sejati tidak mengenal batas.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** True love knows no bounds.
- **Usage Notes:** A fixed-compound predication construction using *cinta sejati* (true love) as subject + predicate. *Cinta sejati* is the most common fixed compound of *sejati* and functions as a near-lexicalized unit in literary, popular, and religious discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** sejati
- **Example:** Itulah makna persahabatan yang sejati.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That is the meaning of true friendship.
- **Usage Notes:** A definitional construction using *itulah* (that is) + *makna* (meaning) + *[noun] yang sejati* (true [noun]) to identify the essential or authentic meaning of a concept. The *yang sejati* relative-clause construction is productive with abstract nouns in philosophical and literary discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** sejati
- **Example:** Mencari jati diri yang sejati.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Seeking one's true self. / In search of one's authentic identity.
- **Usage Notes:** A philosophical self-inquiry construction using *mencari* (to seek) + *jati diri* (identity, selfhood) + *yang sejati* (that is true/authentic). *Jati diri* is itself a compound related to *sejati*; the full construction is common in philosophical, psychological, and religious discourse about authentic selfhood.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note on sejati:** *Sejati* participates primarily in fixed attributive compounds (*cinta sejati*, *pahlawan sejati*, *teman sejati*) rather than in a wide range of periphrastic constructions. The constructions documented above represent the main patterns; the base form does not generate a large inventory of distinct multi-word constructions beyond the *[noun] sejati* and *[noun] yang sejati* patterns.

## 8. Base Form: **sah**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** sah
- **Example:** Dinyatakan sah oleh pengadilan.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Declared valid by the court. / Pronounced lawful by the court.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal declaration construction using *dinyatakan* (declared) + *sah* (valid) + *oleh* (by) + agent. A standard formula in Indonesian legal discourse for the judicial or official confirmation of validity. The *dinyatakan sah* unit is fixed; the agent phrase is variable.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** sah
- **Example:** Secara hukum dianggap sah.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Considered legally valid. / Regarded as lawful under the law.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal-evaluative construction using *secara hukum* (legally, under the law) + *dianggap* (considered, regarded as) + *sah* (valid). Common in legal commentary and administrative discourse to evaluate the legal status of an act or document.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3:**
- **Base Form:** sah
- **Example:** Belum dinyatakan sah secara resmi.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Not yet officially declared valid. / Has not yet been formally ratified.
- **Usage Notes:** A temporal-modal construction using *belum* (not yet) + *dinyatakan sah* (declared valid) + *secara resmi* (officially). Common in administrative and electoral discourse to describe the status of a decision or result that is pending official confirmation.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4:**
- **Base Form:** sah
- **Example:** Pernikahan itu sah menurut agama tetapi tidak menurut negara.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** That marriage is valid according to religion but not according to the state.
- **Usage Notes:** A contrastive validity construction using *sah menurut* (valid according to) + [authority] + *tetapi tidak menurut* (but not according to) + [authority]. This construction is common in Indonesian discourse about the dual religious-civil legal system governing marriage and other personal status matters.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5:**
- **Base Form:** sah
- **Example:** Tidak sah demi hukum.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Null and void under the law. / Invalid by operation of law.
- **Usage Notes:** A legal nullity construction using *tidak sah* (invalid) + *demi hukum* (by operation of law, under the law). *Demi hukum* is a fixed legal phrase of Dutch-influenced origin (*van rechtswege*); the full construction *tidak sah demi hukum* is a standard formula in Indonesian legal documents and court decisions.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## 9. Base Form: **absah**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** absah
- **Example:** Secara formal dianggap absah.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Formally considered legitimate. / Regarded as valid in formal terms.
- **Usage Notes:** A formal-validity construction using *secara formal* (formally) + *dianggap* (considered) + *absah* (legitimate, valid). *Absah* in this construction is interchangeable with *sah* but carries a slightly more formal register. Common in legal and administrative writing.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** absah
- **Example:** Mempertanyakan keabsahan hasil pemilu.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** To question the legitimacy of the election results.
- **Usage Notes:** A legitimacy-challenge construction using *mempertanyakan* (to question, to challenge) + *keabsahan* (legitimacy, validity) + *hasil* (results) + [domain]. *Mempertanyakan keabsahan* is a fixed collocational unit in political and legal discourse, particularly in electoral contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

> **Note on absah:** The inventory of distinct multi-word constructions specific to *absah* (as opposed to *sah*) is limited. Most constructions involving the concept of legal validity use *sah* rather than *absah*; *absah* appears primarily in the nominalization *keabsahan* and in formal written registers. No additional constructions specific to *absah* beyond those documented above are identified.

## 10. Base Form: **tepat**

---

**Construction 1:**
- **Base Form:** tepat
- **Example:** Tepat pada waktunya.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** Exactly on time. / Right on schedule.
- **Usage Notes:** A temporal precision construction using *tepat* (exactly) + *pada waktunya* (at its time, on time). *Tepat pada waktunya* is a fixed adverbial phrase used to indicate punctuality or timely occurrence. Common across administrative, everyday, and literary registers.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2:**
- **Base Form:** tepat
- **Example:** Pilihan yang tepat untuk situasi ini.
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **English Translation:** The right choice for this situation. / An appropriate selection for this context.
- **Usage Notes:** A situational-appropriateness construction using *pilihan yang tepat* (the right/appropriate choice) + *untuk* (for) + [situation]. The *yang tepat untuk* frame is productive across domains for evaluating the fitness of a choice, word, action, or tool for a given context.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---
