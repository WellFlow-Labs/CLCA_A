# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

**Methodological note:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of usage, contrast, collocation, register, and discourse function as documented in P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data supports. Items that appear in multiple groups are listed under each relevant group with a note. Items that do not fit clearly into any group are collected at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a proposition or state of affairs corresponds to what is actually the case

**Label:** Factual truth assertion and denial

**Definition:** These expressions are used in everyday and formal discourse to affirm or deny that something is true or real as a matter of fact — that it actually happened, actually exists, or actually is the case. They appear in contexts of confirming reports, correcting misconceptions, and responding to doubt. The core items in this group participate in interrogative truth-checks (*totoo ba?*), discourse-initial framing (*ang totoo*, *sa katotohanan*), and particle-marked epistemic stance constructions (*totoo nga pala*, *totoo naman*).

**Representative expressions:**

- *totoo* (P1-1) — base predicate for asserting factual truth or reality
- *katotohanan* (P1-2) — nominal form; "the truth," "the true state of affairs"
- *totoong-totoo* (P1-1, P1b A-03) — intensified: "absolutely true/real"
- *hindi totoo* (P1b A-06) — standard negation: "not true, not real"
- *totoo ba?* (P1b A-07, P2b A-01) — conventionalized interrogative truth-check
- *totoo naman* (P1b A-08, P2b A-04) — responsive affirmation against doubt
- *totoo nga* (P1b A-09) — confirmatory: "indeed true"
- *totoo pala* (P1b A-10) — realization: "it turns out to be true"
- *totoo nga pala* (P2b A-02) — confirmed realization after update
- *totoo pa rin* (P2b A-07) — persistent truth: "still true"
- *hindi naman totoo* (P2b A-08) — softened denial
- *totoo man o hindi* (P1b A-30, P2b A-06) — concessive frame: "whether true or not"
- *ang totoo* (P1b A-11, P2b A-03) — discourse marker: "the truth is, to tell the truth"
- *ang totoo nito* (P1b A-12) — "the truth of this matter"
- *sa katotohanan* (P1b A-13, P2b A-15) — discourse marker: "in truth, in reality"
- *ang katotohanan ay* (P1b A-14) — formal sentence-initial frame: "the truth is that..."
- *walang katotohanan* (P1b A-20, P2b A-11) — "there is no truth in it, groundless"
- *may katotohanan* (P1b A-21, P2b A-12) — "there is some truth in it"
- *totoo ang lahat* (P1b A-27) — "everything is true"
- *totoo sa buhay* (P1b A-24) — "true to life, realistic"
- *aktuwal* (P1-18) — formal/journalistic: "actual, as opposed to hypothetical"
- *aktuwal na nangyari* (P2b K-02) — "what actually happened"
- *makatotohanan* (P1-19, P1b A-04) — "realistic, true-to-life, truthful"
- *makatotohanang pelikula* (P2b K-01) — "realistic film"

**Notes:** *Totoo* is the dominant item in this group and is the only one that participates in the full range of particle-marked epistemic stance constructions documented in P2b. *Aktuwal* and *makatotohanan* participate in this group but are more restricted in register (formal/journalistic and aesthetic respectively). *Sa katotohanan* overlaps with Group 2 (see below) in that it can introduce evidentially grounded statements, but its primary function here is as a factual-truth framing device.

## Group 2: Expressions used in contexts of establishing, demanding, or denying proof and evidence

**Label:** Proof and verification

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the question is not merely whether something is true but whether it can be demonstrated, authenticated, or substantiated through evidence or a formal process. They are concentrated in legal, administrative, investigative, and formal argumentative discourse. The cluster centers on the root *tunay* and its derivations, particularly the *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* verbal paradigm.

**Representative expressions:**

- *katunayan* (P1-4, P1b B-02) — "proof, evidence; in fact" (both nominal and discourse marker)
- *patunay* (P1b B-22, P2b B-08) — "a piece of proof, evidence, attestation"
- *patunayan* (P1b B-04, P2b B-09) — verbal: "to prove, to verify, to demonstrate"
- *mapatunayan* (P1b B-05, P2b B-10) — potential: "to be able to prove, provable"
- *napatunayan* (P1b B-06) — completed: "has been proven, was verified"
- *pinatunayan* (P1b B-07) — completed actor-focus: "proved, verified"
- *pagpapatunay* (P1-29, P1b B-03) — nominal: "the act/process of verification, authentication"
- *pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan* (P1b B-28, P2b B-11) — "identity verification"
- *patunay ng pagkakakilanlan* (P1b B-23) — "proof of identity"
- *walang katunayan* (P1b B-20, P2b B-07) — "without proof, unsubstantiated"
- *may katunayan* (P1b B-21) — "there is evidence, it is substantiated"
- *sa katunayan* (P1b B-18, P2b B-05) — discourse marker: "in fact, as a matter of fact"
- *bilang katunayan* (P1b B-19, P2b B-06) — "as proof, as evidence"
- *patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi* (P2b B-09) — "prove what you said"
- *hindi mapatunayan* (P2b B-10) — "cannot be proven"
- *hanapin ang katotohanan* (P1b A-15) — "to seek the truth" (overlaps with Group 1)
- *paghahanap ng katotohanan* (P1b A-25) — "the search for truth" (overlaps with Group 1)

**Notes:** *Sa katunayan* functions as a discourse marker meaning "in fact" and overlaps with Group 1 (*sa katotohanan*), but its derivation from *tunay* (authenticity, verification) and its use in evidentially grounded statements places it more naturally here. The *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* verbal paradigm is the most fully developed verbal paradigm in the entire P1/P2 dataset and is specific to this group; no parallel verbal paradigm exists for *totoo* or *tapat* in the truth-evaluation domain.

## Group 3: Expressions used to evaluate whether something is what it claims or appears to be

**Label:** Authenticity and genuineness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate objects, persons, emotions, relationships, or representations as either genuinely what they appear to be or as imitations, counterfeits, or pretenses. They appear in commercial, material, relational, aesthetic, and identity contexts. The core items center on *tunay* (genuine, authentic) and its contrasts with *huwad* (counterfeit) and *peke* (fake). Unlike Group 1, the question here is not whether a proposition is factually true but whether a thing is the real instance of its kind.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tunay* (P1-3, P1b B-01) — "genuine, real, authentic"
- *tunay na tunay* (P1b B-08, P2b B-01) — intensified: "absolutely genuine"
- *tunay na ginto* (P1b B-13, P2b K-03 via *dalisay*) — "genuine gold"
- *tunay na pagmamahal* (P1b B-09) — "genuine/real love"
- *tunay na kaibigan* (P1b B-10) — "a true/genuine friend"
- *tunay na halaga* (P1b B-14) — "true/real value"
- *tunay na kahulugan* (P1b B-15) — "true/real meaning"
- *tunay na layunin* (P1b B-16, P2b B-03) — "real intention, true purpose"
- *tunay na nangyari* (P1b B-17, P2b B-02) — "what really happened"
- *tunay na kalagayan* (P1b B-30) — "the true/real state of affairs"
- *tunay na pagkatao* (P1b B-24) — "true/authentic character"
- *hindi tunay* (P1b B-29, P2b B-04) — "not genuine, inauthentic"
- *huwad* (P1-9) — "fake, counterfeit, spurious"
- *huwad na pera* (P2b F-02) — "counterfeit money"
- *huwad na dokumento* (P1-9) — "fake document"
- *huwad na* (P1-27, P2b F-01) — "has been found to be fake"
- *huwad na huwad* (P2a D-P1-19) — intensified: "completely fake" [marginal]
- *paghuhuwad* (P2a D-P1-20) — "counterfeiting, forgery"
- *humuwad* (P2a D-P1-21) — verbal: "to counterfeit, to forge"
- *peke* (P1-10) — colloquial: "fake, counterfeit, phony"
- *pekeng produkto* (P2b F-03) — "fake product"
- *peke lang* (P2b F-04) — dismissive: "it's just a fake"
- *peke o hindi* (P2b F-05) — binary frame: "fake or not"
- *orihinal* (P1-17) — "original, genuine, not a copy"
- *orihinal na kopya* (P2b I-05) — "original copy"
- *dalisay* (P1-22) — "pure, unadulterated, genuine"
- *dalisay na ginto* (P2b K-03) — "pure gold"
- *dalisay na puso* (P2b K-04) — "pure heart"
- *kadalisayan* (P2a D-U-03) — "purity" [marginal]
- *lehitimo* (P1-16) — "legitimate, lawful, genuine"
- *lehitimong negosyo* (P2b I-03) — "legitimate business"
- *lehitimidad* (P2a D-P1-28) — "legitimacy"
- *hindi lehitimo* (P2a D-P1-29, P2b I-04) — "illegitimate"

**Notes:** *Tunay* is the central native item in this group. *Huwad* and *peke* are its primary contrasts; *huwad* is more formal and legal, *peke* is colloquial. *Orihinal*, *dalisay*, and *lehitimo* participate in this group but each has a more restricted domain: *orihinal* is used primarily for documents and products, *dalisay* for substances and moral purity, *lehitimo* for legal standing. *Tunay na nangyari* and *tunay na kalagayan* also overlap with Group 1 (factual truth) and Group 2 (verification), since asserting what "really happened" involves both authenticity and factual accuracy.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate correctness, accuracy, or propriety of answers, actions, and methods

**Label:** Correctness and accuracy evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether an answer, action, measurement, or method meets a standard of correctness or accuracy. They appear prominently in educational, procedural, and moral evaluation contexts. The primary contrast is between *tama* (correct, right) and *mali* (wrong, incorrect), with *wasto* and *eksakto* occupying more formal and more precise registers respectively.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tama* (P1-5) — "correct, right, accurate, proper"
- *tamang-tama* (P2a D-P1-25) — intensified: "exactly right, just right"
- *tama ka* (P2b D-01) — "you are right/correct"
- *tama ka naman* (P2b D-02) — concessive affirmation: "you are right, actually"
- *tama ba ang sagot ko?* (P2b D-03) — correctness-check question
- *tama o mali* (P1-8, P2b D-04) — binary evaluative frame: "right or wrong"
- *mali* (P1-6) — "wrong, incorrect, mistaken, erroneous"
- *maling-mali* (P2a D-P1-03) — intensified: "completely wrong"
- *mali ka* (P2b D-05) — "you are wrong"
- *mali ang aking pagkakaunawa* (P2b D-06) — "my understanding was wrong"
- *nagkamali ako* (P2b D-07) — "I made a mistake"
- *pasensya na, nagkamali ako* (P2b D-08) — apology-plus-admission formula
- *pagkakamali* (P2a D-P1-01) — "mistake, error"
- *magkamali* (P2a D-P1-02) — verbal: "to make a mistake, to err"
- *kamali-mali* (P2a D-P1-04) — "prone to error" [marginal]
- *wasto* (P1-7) — formal: "correct, proper, accurate"
- *wastong paraan* (P2b E-01) — "the correct/proper method"
- *wastong pagbigkas* (P2b E-02) — "correct pronunciation"
- *hindi wasto* (P2b E-03) — "not proper, incorrect"
- *kawastuhan* (P2a D-P1-22) — "correctness, propriety"
- *iwasto* (P2a D-P1-23) — verbal: "to correct, to rectify"
- *iniwasto* (P2a D-P1-24) — completed: "corrected, rectified"
- *eksakto* (P1-25) — "exact, precise, accurate"
- *eksaktong-eksakto* (P1-24, P2a D-P1-30) — intensified: "exactly right, spot-on"
- *eksakto ang oras* (P2b K-06) — "the time is exact"
- *perpekto* (P1-23) — "perfect, flawless, exact"
- *perpektong kopya* (P2b K-05) — "perfect copy"
- *katamaan* (P2a D-P1-26) — "correctness, rightness" [marginal]

**Notes:** *Tama* and *mali* are the dominant everyday items and form the primary binary contrast in this group. *Wasto* occupies a more formal register and carries a stronger sense of procedural or moral propriety. *Eksakto* and *perpekto* are used when precision or flawlessness is specifically at issue. *Tama o mali* as a binary frame overlaps with Group 5 (moral evaluation) since the same construction is used for both factual correctness and moral rightness.

## Group 5: Expressions used in moral evaluation of persons and actions as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Honesty, sincerity, and moral truthfulness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons, statements, and inner dispositions as honest, sincere, and without concealment, or as dishonest and deceptive. They appear in interpersonal, moral, religious, and legal contexts. The core items center on *tapat* (honest, sincere, straightforward) and its derivations, and on *sinungaling* (liar) and *kasinungalingan* (lie, falsehood). A distinctive feature of this group is the emphasis on the alignment between inner state and outward expression, including the culturally specific *loob* (inner self) constructions.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tapat* (P1-20, P1b C-01) — "honest, sincere, straightforward, frank"
- *katapatan* (P1-21, P1b C-02) — "honesty, sincerity, fidelity, loyalty"
- *maging tapat* (P1b C-03) — "to be honest, to be sincere"
- *maging tapat sa iyong sarili* (P1b C-19, P2b C-01) — "be honest with yourself"
- *maging tapat ka sa akin* (P2b C-02) — "be honest with me"
- *tapatang sagot* (P1b C-04) — "honest answer"
- *tapatang salita* (P1b C-05) — "honest words, frank speech"
- *tapat na tao* (P1b C-06) — "an honest person"
- *tapat na puso* (P1b C-07) — "sincere heart"
- *tapat na loob* (P1b C-25) — "sincere inner self, honest disposition"
- *katapatan ng loob* (P1b C-26) — "sincerity of inner disposition"
- *hindi tapat* (P1b C-17, P2b C-03) — "not honest, insincere"
- *walang katapatan* (P1b C-16, P2b C-08) — "without honesty, faithless"
- *magtapat* (P1b C-22, P2a D-C-02) — verbal: "to be honest, to confess, to come clean"
- *magtapat ka na* (P2b C-04) — "come clean already"
- *nagtapat siya sa kanyang asawa* (P2b C-05) — "he came clean to his spouse"
- *nagtatapat* (P1b C-23, P2a D-C-03) — progressive: "is confessing, is coming clean"
- *nagtapat* (P1b C-24, P2a D-C-04) — completed: "confessed, came clean"
- *pagtatapat* (P1b C-21, P2a D-C-05) — nominal: "confession, honest disclosure"
- *pagtatapat ng kasalanan* (P2b C-10) — "confession of sin"
- *tapat na pagtatapat* (P1b C-20) — "honest/sincere confession"
- *pagiging tapat* (P1b C-18, P2a D-C-06) — "being honest, honesty as a state"
- *taos-pusong katapatan* (P1b C-15, P2b C-07) — "wholehearted sincerity"
- *sinungaling* (P1-12) — "liar, lying, deceitful"
- *sinungaling ka* (P2b G-02) — "you are a liar"
- *kasinungalingan* (P1-11, P2a D-P1-05) — "lie, falsehood, untruth"
- *huwag magsabi ng kasinungalingan* (P2b G-01) — "do not tell a lie"
- *huwag kang magsinungaling* (P2b G-03) — "don't lie"
- *magsinungaling* (P2a D-P1-06) — verbal: "to lie, to tell a lie"
- *nagsinungaling* (P2a D-P1-07) — completed: "lied"
- *puno ng kasinungalingan* (P2b G-04) — "full of lies"
- *katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan* (P1b A-28, P2b A-17) — "truth versus falsehood"

**Notes:** *Tapat* and its derivations are the core of this group. The *loob* constructions (*tapat na loob*, *katapatan ng loob*) are specific to this group and do not appear in Groups 1–4; they reflect a culturally specific moral-psychological framework in Tagalog. *Pagtatapat* in religious contexts (confession of sin) involves an institutional dimension not present in the other groups. *Kasinungalingan* and *sinungaling* are the primary contrasts to *tapat* in this group. *Tapat na pagmamahal* (P1b C-27) and *tapat na asawa* (P1b C-10) overlap with Group 6 (loyalty and fidelity) since *tapat* in these collocations foregrounds faithfulness rather than honesty of statement.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate commitment, loyalty, and fidelity in ongoing relationships or roles

**Label:** Fidelity and loyal commitment

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person maintains genuine commitment, loyalty, or dedication to a person, institution, cause, or role over time. They appear in contexts of marriage, friendship, employment, civic duty, and religious devotion. The core items are drawn from *katapatan* (fidelity, loyalty) in combination with *sa* + the object of commitment, and from *tiwala* (trust) as the relational basis for ongoing commitment.

**Representative expressions:**

- *katapatan sa pag-ibig* (P1b C-11, P2b C-06) — "faithfulness in love, fidelity in a relationship"
- *katapatan sa trabaho* (P1b C-12) — "dedication to work, commitment to one's job"
- *katapatan sa bansa* (P1b C-13) — "loyalty to the nation"
- *katapatan sa Diyos* (P1b C-14) — "faithfulness to God"
- *tapat na asawa* (P1b C-10) — "a faithful spouse"
- *tapat na kaibigan* (P1b C-09) — "a loyal/faithful friend"
- *tapat na paglilingkod* (P1b C-08) — "faithful/dedicated service"
- *tapat na pangako* (P1b C-28) — "a sincere/genuine promise"
- *taos-pusong katapatan* (P1b C-15, P2b C-07) — "wholehearted fidelity"
- *walang katapatan* (P1b C-16, P2b C-08) — "faithless, without loyalty"
- *tiwala* (P1-14) — "trust, confidence, reliance"
- *may tiwala ako sa kanya* (P2b H-01) — "I trust him/her"
- *wala na akong tiwala sa kanya* (P2b H-02) — "I no longer trust him/her"
- *magtiwala* (P2a D-P1-10) — verbal: "to trust, to rely on"
- *nagtiwala ako sa kanya at niloko niya ako* (P2b H-04) — "I trusted him and he deceived me"
- *mapagkakatiwalaan* (P1-13) — "trustworthy, reliable, dependable"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* (P1-30) — "untrustworthy, unreliable"
- *tiwala sa sarili* (P2a D-P1-14, P2b H-03) — "self-confidence"

**Notes:** This group overlaps substantially with Group 5 (honesty and moral truthfulness) because *katapatan* covers both honesty of statement and loyalty of commitment, and the same word is used in both domains. The distinction between the two groups is one of emphasis: Group 5 items foreground the alignment of speech with inner state (not lying, confessing), while Group 6 items foreground sustained commitment to a person, role, or cause over time. *Tiwala* and its derivations are placed here because trust is the relational basis for the fidelity constructions, but *tiwala* also participates in Group 7 (credibility assessment) through *mapagkakatiwalaan*.

## Group 7: Expressions used to assess the credibility or reliability of persons and sources

**Label:** Credibility and reliability assessment

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether a person, witness, source, or institution can be relied upon to provide accurate information or to perform their role faithfully. They appear prominently in legal, journalistic, and institutional discourse. The cluster involves *mapagkakatiwalaan* (trustworthy, reliable), *kredible* (credible), and related constructions.

**Representative expressions:**

- *mapagkakatiwalaan* (P1-13) — "trustworthy, reliable, dependable"
- *mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon* (P2b H-05) — "reliable source of information"
- *mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* (P2b H-06) — "reliable/trustworthy witness"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaan* (P1-30, P2a D-P1-09) — "untrustworthy, unreliable"
- *di-mapagkakatiwalaang saksi* (P2b H-07) — "unreliable witness"
- *kredible* (P1-15) — "credible, believable"
- *kredible na saksi* (P2b I-01) — "credible witness"
- *hindi kredible* (P2b I-02) — "not credible"
- *kredibilidad* (P2a D-P1-27) — "credibility"
- *katapatan ng isang saksi* (P1b C-30) — "the honesty/sincerity of a witness"
- *tapat na ulat* (P1b C-29) — "honest/accurate report"

**Notes:** This group is defined by its institutional and evaluative context (assessing sources and witnesses) rather than by a single root. *Mapagkakatiwalaan* derives from *tiwala* (Group 6) but functions in this group through its application to information sources and witnesses rather than to personal relationships. *Kredible* is a borrowed term used specifically in formal and journalistic registers. *Katapatan ng isang saksi* and *tapat na ulat* draw from the *tapat* root (Groups 5 and 6) but appear in the credibility-assessment context of this group.

## Group 8: Expressions used to describe deliberate misleading or deception

**Label:** Deception and misleading

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate acts, persons, or representations as deliberately misleading or deceptive — not merely incorrect but intended to create a false impression. They appear in moral, legal, consumer protection, and journalistic discourse. The cluster involves *mapanlinlang* (deceptive, misleading), *linlang* and its verbal forms, and the *sinungaling*/*kasinungalingan* cluster from Group 5.

**Representative expressions:**

- *mapanlinlang* (P1-26) — "deceptive, misleading, fraudulent"
- *mapanlinlang na advertisement* (P2b J-01) — "deceptive advertisement"
- *mapanlinlang na impormasyon* (P2b J-02) — "misleading information"
- *linlangin* (P2a D-P1-16) — verbal: "to deceive someone, to mislead"
- *huwag mong linlangin ang mga tao* (P2b J-03) — "do not deceive the people"
- *nililinlang* (P2a D-P1-17) — progressive: "is being deceived"
- *nililinlang ang mga mamimili* (P2b J-04) — "consumers are being deceived"
- *manlinlang* (P2a D-P1-18) — agentive: "a deceiver, one who misleads"
- *sinungaling* (P1-12) — "liar, lying" (overlaps with Group 5)
- *kasinungalingan* (P1-11) — "lie, falsehood" (overlaps with Group 5)
- *nagsinungaling* (P2a D-P1-07) — "lied" (overlaps with Group 5)

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 5 (honesty and moral truthfulness) in that *sinungaling* and *kasinungalingan* appear in both. The distinction is that Group 5 items are used primarily in interpersonal moral evaluation (is this person honest? did they tell the truth?), while Group 8 items are used in contexts where the deliberate, strategic nature of the deception is foregrounded — particularly in consumer, political, and institutional contexts. *Mapanlinlang* is the item most specific to this group and does not appear in Group 5 contexts.

## Group 9: Expressions used in discourse about partial, incomplete, or qualified truth

**Label:** Partial and qualified truth

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a claim is evaluated as neither fully true nor simply false — it contains some truth, omits relevant information, or is true only in a limited sense. They appear in argumentative, journalistic, and moral discourse where the adequacy of a truth claim is at issue.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kalahating katotohanan* (P1b A-18, P2b A-13) — "half-truth, partial truth"
- *kalahating katotohanan lang* (P2b A-13) — "only a half-truth"
- *buong katotohanan* (P1b A-19, P2b A-14) — "the whole truth, the full truth"
- *may katotohanan* (P1b A-21, P2b A-12) — "there is some truth in it"
- *walang katotohanan* (P1b A-20, P2b A-11) — "there is no truth in it" (contrast)
- *parang totoo* (P1-28, P1b A-05, P2b A-05) — "seems true/real, as if true"
- *katotohanang mabigat* (P1b A-22) — "a hard/weighty truth"
- *katotohanang masakit* (P1b A-23) — "a painful/hurtful truth"

**Notes:** This group is smaller and less tightly defined than the others. *Kalahating katotohanan* and *buong katotohanan* form a natural pair (partial vs. complete truth). *Parang totoo* belongs here because it marks the appearance of truth without asserting truth — a qualified epistemic stance. *Katotohanang mabigat* and *katotohanang masakit* are included because they evaluate truth claims in terms of their emotional or moral weight rather than their factual status, which is a form of qualification. These items could also be considered ungrouped (see below).

## Group 10: Expressions used in formal or institutional contexts to assert near-proverbial or general claims about truth

**Label:** General assertions about the nature of truth

**Definition:** These expressions function as near-proverbial or formulaic statements about truth as an abstract quality — its permanence, its bitterness, its opposition to falsehood. They appear in religious, editorial, literary, and homiletic discourse and function as moral or philosophical assertions rather than descriptions of specific states of affairs.

**Representative expressions:**

- *ang katotohanan ay mapait* (P2b A-10) — "the truth is bitter"
- *ang katotohanan ay mananatili* (P1b A-29, P2b A-16) — "the truth will endure"
- *katotohanan laban sa kasinungalingan* (P1b A-28, P2b A-17) — "truth versus falsehood"
- *tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan* (P1b A-26) — "defender of truth"
- *sabihin ang katotohanan* (P1b A-16, P2b A-09) — "tell the truth" (as a moral injunction)
- *hanapin ang katotohanan* (P1b A-15) — "seek the truth"
- *paghahanap ng katotohanan* (P1b A-25) — "the search for truth"
- *itago ang katotohanan* (P1b A-17) — "to hide the truth"

**Notes:** This group is defined by discourse function (near-proverbial, formulaic, moral-philosophical) rather than by a shared root or semantic field. Several items here also appear in other groups: *sabihin ang katotohanan* overlaps with Group 5 (honest disclosure), *hanapin ang katotohanan* overlaps with Group 2 (verification). Their placement here reflects their additional function as general moral assertions about truth.

## Ambiguous and overlapping items

The following items are noted as belonging to multiple groups without a clear primary placement:

**tunay na Diyos / tunay na relihiyon / tunay na bayani / tunay na Pilipino** (P1b B-27, B-26, B-12, B-11): These collocations use *tunay* in identity, theological, and political discourse where "genuine" is defined by ideological, cultural, or theological criteria rather than material or factual ones. They participate in Group 3 (authenticity evaluation) but the criteria for authenticity are different from those applied to *tunay na ginto* or *tunay na dokumento*. They are noted here as overlapping items that sit at the edge of Group 3.

**tapat na pagmamahal / tapat na kaibigan** (P1b C-27, C-09): These collocations use *tapat* in the sense of sincere, faithful love or friendship. They overlap between Group 5 (honesty and moral truthfulness) and Group 6 (fidelity and loyal commitment) depending on whether the emphasis is on the absence of pretense or on sustained loyalty.

**katapatan** (P1-21): As documented, this single word covers honesty of statement (Group 5), loyal commitment (Group 6), and credibility of witness (Group 7). It is the most cross-group item in the dataset.

**sa katotohanan / sa katunayan** (P1b A-13, B-18): Both function as discourse markers meaning "in truth/in fact." *Sa katotohanan* is placed in Group 1 (factual truth assertion) and *sa katunayan* in Group 2 (proof and verification), but in actual usage they are near-synonymous discourse markers and the distinction is one of register and emphasis rather than clear semantic difference.

**tunay na nangyari / aktuwal na nangyari** (P1b B-17, P2b K-02): Both mean "what really happened" and appear in investigative and narrative contexts. *Tunay na nangyari* is placed in Group 3 (authenticity) because it uses the *tunay* root, but it also functions as a factual truth assertion (Group 1). *Aktuwal na nangyari* is placed in Group 1 but also participates in Group 3.

## Items that do not fit clearly into any group

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups above. They are listed without forced placement.

**nagkatotoo** (P2a D-U-01): "came true, was realized." This form describes the realization of a prediction or wish rather than the assertion of factual truth or the evaluation of authenticity. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

**katapat** (P2a D-U-02): In its spatial sense ("counterpart, the one facing"), this form is entirely outside the truth-evaluation domain. In its relational sense ("counterpart in negotiation"), it is also outside the groups documented here.

**tiwala sa sarili** (P2a D-P1-14, P2b H-03): "Self-confidence." While derived from *tiwala* (trust), this collocation refers to confidence in oneself rather than to trust in another person or to the reliability of a source. It participates in Group 6 only loosely and does not fit Groups 1–5 or 7–10.

**perpektong kopya** (P2b K-05): "Perfect copy." This construction evaluates a reproduction as flawless, which is a correctness/accuracy evaluation (Group 4) but also an authenticity evaluation (Group 3, since a perfect copy may be a counterfeit). The item sits between the two groups without a clear primary placement.

**peke-peke** (P2a D-U-04): The marginal intensified colloquial form. Its irregular reduplication (without linker) and marginal status mean it does not add to any group beyond what *peke* already covers.

**kamali-mali** (P2a D-P1-04): The marginal form meaning "prone to error." It participates in Group 4 (correctness evaluation) but its marginal status and limited attestation mean it is noted here rather than placed firmly in that group.

**katamaan** (P2a D-P1-26): The marginal nominal "correctness, rightness." Participates in Group 4 but is less common than *kawastuhan* or the predicative use of *tama*, and its marginal status is noted.
