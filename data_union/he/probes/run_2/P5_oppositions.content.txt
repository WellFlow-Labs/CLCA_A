# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** אמת (*ʾemet*)
**Term B:** שקר (*sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet / sheker
**English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** These are the primary antonyms in the Hebrew truth-evaluation domain, explicitly identified as such in P1 (item 4: "The primary antonym of *ʾemet*"). They appear in direct opposition in fixed expressions such as *ʿedut ʾemet* vs. *ʿedut sheker* and in the biblical injunction *midvar sheker tirḥaq*, which presupposes *ʾemet* as the positive pole.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** אמת (*ʾemet*)
**Term B:** שקר לבן (*sheker lavan*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet / sheker lavan
**English translation:** truth / white lie
**Description of contrast:** *Sheker lavan* is documented in P2b as a sub-type of *sheker* that is socially motivated and harmless, implicitly contrasted with *ʾemet* as the full positive pole. The collocation *ze rak sheker lavan* ("it's just a white lie") presupposes a scale on which *ʾemet* and *sheker muḥlat* occupy the endpoints and *sheker lavan* occupies an intermediate position.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** אמת מוחלטת (*ʾemet muḥletet*)
**Term B:** אמת יחסית (*ʾemet yaḥasit*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet muḥletet / ʾemet yaḥasit
**English translation:** absolute truth / relative truth
**Description of contrast:** These two collocations are documented together in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 12 and 13) as the opposing poles in philosophical and epistemological discourse about the conditionality of truth. *ʾEmet muḥletet* denotes truth that is unconditional and not perspective-dependent; *ʾemet yaḥasit* denotes truth conditioned by perspective or framework.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** אמת (*ʾemet*)
**Term B:** חצי אמת (*ḥatsi ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet / ḥatsi ʾemet
**English translation:** truth / half-truth
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 14) and P2b (Item 1, Construction 15), *ḥatsi ʾemet* is explicitly defined as a statement that is technically accurate but misleading through omission. The construction *ze rak ḥatsi ʾemet* ("that's only a half-truth") presupposes *ʾemet* (the whole truth) as the contrasting positive standard.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** כל האמת ורק האמת (*kol ha-ʾemet ve-raq ha-ʾemet*)
**Term B:** לא כל האמת (*lo kol ha-ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** kol ha-ʾemet ve-raq ha-ʾemet / lo kol ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** the whole truth and nothing but the truth / not the whole truth
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b (Item 1, Constructions 16 and 17) as legal and quasi-legal formulas. The first asserts complete and exclusive truthfulness; the second characterizes a statement as incomplete or selectively true. They are structurally parallel negation-based contrasts within the same discourse frame.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Term B:** מזויף (*mezuyaf*)
**Transliteration:** meqori / mezuyaf
**English translation:** original, genuine / forged, counterfeit, fake
**Description of contrast:** Explicitly contrasted in P1 (item 6: "*meqori* contrasts with *ha-ʿataqah* ('the copy') or *ziyyuf* ('forgery')") and in P3 (Group 3), where *meqori* and *mezuyaf* are the positive and negative poles of the genuineness-of-objects dimension. The contrast is operative in legal, commercial, and art authentication contexts.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** מקורי (*meqori*)
**Term B:** חיקוי (*ḥiqquy*)
**Transliteration:** meqori / ḥiqquy
**English translation:** original / imitation, copy
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1 (item 29: "*ḥiqquy* contrasts with *meqori* ('original')") and in P3 (Group 3). Unlike the *meqori* / *mezuyaf* opposition, this contrast does not necessarily imply deliberate fraud; *ḥiqquy* describes imitation that may or may not be deceptive, while *mezuyaf* implies intentional deception.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**Term A:** כשר (*kasher*)
**Term B:** פסול (*pasul*)
**Transliteration:** kasher / pasul
**English translation:** valid, fit, admissible / invalid, disqualified, inadmissible
**Description of contrast:** Explicitly identified as antonyms in P1 (item 24: "*pasul* contrasts with *kasher* ('valid, fit')"; item 25: "*kasher* contrasts with *pasul* ('invalid, disqualified')"). These are the primary procedural-evaluative antonyms in legal and halakhic discourse, used to declare whether a witness, document, or object meets or fails to meet the required standard.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** כנות (*kanut*)
**Term B:** צביעות (*tsevʿiʿut*)
**Transliteration:** kanut / tsevʿiʿut
**English translation:** sincerity, honesty / hypocrisy, two-facedness
**Description of contrast:** Explicitly documented as antonyms in P1 (item 26: "*tsevʿiʿut* contrasts with *kanut* ('sincerity') and *yosher* ('integrity')"). *Kanut* denotes genuine straightforwardness without concealment; *tsevʿiʿut* denotes the presentation of a false appearance of virtue while concealing contrary intentions.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** יושר (*yosher*)
**Term B:** צביעות (*tsevʿiʿut*)
**Transliteration:** yosher / tsevʿiʿut
**English translation:** integrity, uprightness / hypocrisy, two-facedness
**Description of contrast:** Also explicitly documented as antonyms in P1 (item 26). *Yosher* denotes moral straightforwardness and integrity of conduct; *tsevʿiʿut* denotes the concealment of contrary conduct behind a false moral appearance. The contrast is between genuine moral character and its performed simulation.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term B:** רמייה (*remiyyaʾh*)

The contrast is between genuine straightforwardness and deliberate misleading.

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** אמיתי (*ʾamiti*)
**Term B:** מדומה (*medumeh*)
**Transliteration:** ʾamiti / medumeh
**English translation:** genuine, real / imagined, supposed, simulated
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P2a (Item 22) where *medumeh* is explicitly noted as contrasting with *ʾamiti* ("genuine"): e.g., *ʾohev medumeh* ("a supposed/imagined friend") vs. *ʾohev ʾamiti* ("a genuine friend"). *Medumeh* describes things as merely supposed or imagined rather than actually what they appear to be.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** אמיתי (*ʾamiti*)
**Term B:** מזויף (*mezuyaf*)
**Transliteration:** ʾamiti / mezuyaf
**English translation:** genuine, real / forged, fake
**Description of contrast:** Implied by the P3 Group 3 documentation, where *ʾamiti* and *mezuyaf* occupy opposing positions in the evaluation of whether objects are genuine. While not explicitly paired as antonyms in a single P1 entry, the contrast is operative in collocations such as *mutsaʾr ʾamiti* vs. *mutsaʾr mezuyaf* ("a genuine product" vs. "a counterfeit product").
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

**Term A:** נאמן (*neʾeman*)
**Term B:** לא נאמן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman / lo neʾeman
**English translation:** faithful, trustworthy / not faithful, untrustworthy
**Description of contrast:** Hebrew has no single-word antonym for *neʾeman* documented in the P1–P4 data. Negation is expressed syntactically with *lo*, as documented in P4b for the parallel case of *meheymen* (*lo meheymen*, "not reliable"). The absence of a lexical antonym for *neʾeman* is a gap in the data; the opposition exists only at the constructional level.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** מהימן (*meheymen*)
**Term B:** לא מהימן (*lo meheymen*)
**Transliteration:** meheymen / lo meheymen
**English translation:** reliable, credible / not reliable, not credible
**Description of contrast:** Documented explicitly in P2b (Item 8, Construction 5): "*lo meheymen* — the negated predicative construction used in legal and everyday discourse to characterize a source, witness, or claim as lacking credibility." Hebrew has no single-word antonym for *meheymen*; negation is the only available strategy.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** אמון (*ʾimun*)
**Term B:** אי-אמון (*ʾi-ʾimun*)
**Transliteration:** ʾimun / ʾi-ʾimun
**English translation:** trust, confidence / distrust, no-confidence
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b (Root א-מ-נ, item 23) and P3 (Groups 5 and 8). The prefix *ʾi-* is a productive negation prefix in Hebrew that forms the antonym of *ʾimun* directly. The opposition is institutionalized in the parliamentary term *hatsaʿat ʾi-ʾimun* ("motion of no confidence"), which presupposes *ʾimun* as the positive pole.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

**Term A:** לדבר אמת (*ledaber ʾemet*)
**Term B:** לדבר שקר (*ledaber sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ledaber ʾemet / ledaber sheker
**English translation:** to tell the truth / to speak falsehood
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b (Item 1, Construction 1; Item 4, Construction 1) as parallel periphrastic constructions using the same verb *ledaber* with *ʾemet* and *sheker* respectively as direct objects. They are structurally parallel and semantically opposed, forming the primary verbal opposition for truth-telling vs. lying.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** לגלות את האמת (*legallot et ha-ʾemet*)
**Term B:** להסתיר את האמת (*lehastir et ha-ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** legallot et ha-ʾemet / lehastir et ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to reveal the truth / to hide the truth
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b (Item 1, Constructions 3 and 6) as members of the set of periphrastic constructions with *ha-ʾemet* as object. They are structurally parallel and semantically opposed along the dimension of disclosure vs. concealment of truth.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** לחשוף את האמת (*laḥsof et ha-ʾemet*)
**Term B:** להסתיר את האמת (*lehastir et ha-ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** laḥsof et ha-ʾemet / lehastir et ha-ʾemet
**English translation:** to expose the truth / to hide the truth
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b (Item 1, Constructions 4 and 6). *Laḥsof* and *legallot* are near-synonyms on the disclosure side, but P2b notes that *laḥsof* "carries a stronger connotation of exposing something deliberately concealed." The opposition with *lehastir* is the same in kind as Opposition 18 but with a stronger implication of deliberate prior concealment on both sides.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** שקרן (*shaqran*)
**Term B:** דובר אמת (*dover ʾemet*)
**Transliteration:** shaqran / dover ʾemet
**English translation:** liar / truth-teller
**Description of contrast:** Both are agentive expressions for persons characterized by their habitual relationship to truth-telling, documented in P2a (Item 4) and P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 4) respectively. *Shaqran* is a morphological agentive (*-an* suffix); *dover ʾemet* is a periphrastic agentive (active participle + noun object). They occupy opposing positions in moral evaluation of persons as habitual liars vs. habitual truth-tellers.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

**Term A:** נכון לעכשיו (*nakhon le-ʿakhshav*)
**Term B:** לא נכון לומר ש... (*lo nakhon lomar she-...*)
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-ʿakhshav / lo nakhon lomar she-...
**English translation:** correct as of now / it is not correct to say that...
**Description of contrast:** Both constructions are documented in P2b (Item 3, Constructions 4 and 6). *Nakhon le-ʿakhshav* asserts temporally qualified accuracy; *lo nakhon lomar she-* denies the accuracy of a formulation. They are not direct antonyms but represent the positive and negative poles of the *nakhon* predicate frame applied to propositional content.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

**Term A:** אמת פנימית (*ʾemet pnimit*)
**Term B:** אמת מדעית (*ʾemet madaʿit*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet pnimit / ʾemet madaʿit
**English translation:** inner truth, personal truth / scientific truth
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations are documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 18 and 20). P1b notes that *ʾemet pnimit* refers to "a subjective or personal sense of what is true for an individual, as distinct from objective or external truth," while *ʾemet madaʿit* refers to "claims established through scientific method and evidence, as distinct from opinion or belief." They represent the subjective/personal vs. objective/evidential poles of a dimension within the *ʾemet* domain.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

**Term A:** אמת היסטורית (*ʾemet historit*)
**Term B:** אמת פנימית (*ʾemet pnimit*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet historit / ʾemet pnimit
**English translation:** historical truth / inner truth, personal truth
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations are documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 19 and 18). *ʾEmet historit* refers to an accurate account of what actually occurred, used in historiographical and legal contexts; *ʾemet pnimit* refers to subjective personal experience of truth. They represent the external/factual vs. internal/experiential poles within the *ʾemet* domain, distinct from the absolute/relative opposition in Opposition 3.

## Opposition 27

**Term A:** חותמו של הקב"ה אמת (*ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh huʾ ʾemet*)
**Term B:** שקר (*sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh barukh huʾ ʾemet / sheker
**English translation:** the seal of the Holy One, blessed be He, is truth / falsehood
**Description of contrast:** The rabbinic dictum documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, item 8) asserts truth as the defining attribute of the divine. In the rabbinic discourse where this dictum appears, *sheker* is the implicit antonym representing the demonic or human-failing pole. The opposition is not a direct lexical pair but is structurally present in the theological discourse documented in P1b.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 28

**Term A:** עד כשר (*ʿed kasher*)
**Term B:** עד שקר (*ʿed sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ʿed kasher / ʿed sheker
**English translation:** a valid/competent witness / a false witness
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations are documented in P1 (items 25 and 30 respectively) and in P2b (Item 4, Construction 2). They represent the two poles of witness evaluation in legal and halakhic discourse: *ʿed kasher* meets the procedural standard for valid testimony; *ʿed sheker* gives deliberately false testimony. The contrast combines the procedural dimension (*kasher* / *pasul*) with the moral dimension (*ʾemet* / *sheker*).
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** עדות נאמנת (*ʿedut neʾemenet*)
**Term B:** עדות שקר (*ʿedut sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ʿedut neʾemenet / ʿedut sheker
**English translation:** reliable testimony / false testimony
**Description of contrast:** Both collocations are documented in P1 (item 30). They represent the positive and negative poles of testimony evaluation: *ʿedut neʾemenet* is testimony that is credible and dependable; *ʿedut sheker* is testimony that is deliberately false. The contrast combines the epistemic dimension (*neʾeman* / *sheker*) within the same head noun *ʿedut*.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 30

**Term A:** אמת ושלום (*ʾemet ve-shalom*)
**Term B:** מפני דרכי שלום (*mipney darkhe shalom*)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-shalom / mipney darkhe shalom
**English translation:** truth and peace / for the sake of peace (implying modification of truth)
**Description of contrast:** Both expressions are documented in P1b (Root א-מ-ת, items 6 and 7). P1b explicitly notes that *mipney darkhe shalom* is "the standard counterpart phrase in the fixed discourse around *ʾemet ve-shalom*," used in discussions of when truth-telling may be modified or omitted to preserve social harmony. The opposition is not between two terms but between two principles within a fixed ethical discourse: the obligation to truth vs. the permission to modify truth for peace.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

The contrast is between genuine moral character and its performative simulation, parallel to the *kanut* / *tsevʿiʿut* opposition but within a single root family.

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** שקרן (*shaqran*)
**Term B:** משקר (*meshaqer*)
**Transliteration:** shaqran / meshaqer
**English translation:** liar (habitual character) / one who is lying (in the act)
**Description of contrast:** Both are documented in P2a (Item 4) as agentive derivations of root ש-ק-ר. P2a explicitly notes: "*shaqran* implies character, *meshaqer* implies act." The contrast is not between truth and falsehood but between two grammatical perspectives on the same negative agent: characterological (*-an* suffix) vs. act-based (active participle).
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 33

**Term A:** רמאות (*ramaʾut*)
**Term B:** שקר (*sheker*)
**Transliteration:** ramaʾut / sheker
**English translation:** deceit, fraud, cheating / lie, falsehood
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P3 (Group 2 observations): "*sheker* and *ramaʾut* are both antonyms of honesty but differ: *sheker* concerns false statements, while *ramaʾut* concerns deceptive conduct more broadly." Both are antonyms of *ʾemet* and *kanut* but are not antonyms of each other; rather, they represent two distinct dimensions of dishonesty — propositional falsity vs. behavioral deception.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 34

**Term A:** כן (*kʾen*)
**Term B:** צבוע (*tsavuaʿ*)
**Transliteration:** kʾen / tsavuaʿ
**English translation:** sincere, honest / hypocritical, two-faced
**Description of contrast:** *Tsavuaʿ* is the adjectival base from which *tsevʿiʿut* ("hypocrisy") is derived, documented in P1 (item 26: "from *tsavoaʿ* ('dyed, colored')"). While *tsavuaʿ* itself is not a P1 base item, the opposition between *kʾen* and the *tsevʿiʿut* family is documented in P1 and P3. The contrast is between a person who is genuinely what they appear to be (*kʾen*) and one who presents a false appearance (*tsavuaʿ* / *tsevʿiʿut*).
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 36

**Term A:** נכונות (*nekhonot*) [sense: correctness]
**Term B:** נכונות (*nekhonot*) [sense: readiness, willingness]
**Transliteration:** nekhonot / nekhonot
**English translation:** correctness, accuracy / readiness, willingness
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P2a (Item 3, Derived Form 3) as a case of polysemy within a single form: "An abstract noun with two distinct senses: (a) the quality of being correct or accurate; and (b) readiness or willingness." This is not an opposition between two terms but an internal contrast within a single form. The two senses are not antonyms of each other; they represent divergent semantic developments from the same root כ-ו-נ.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 37

**Term A:** אמינות (*ʾaminut*)
**Term B:** ספק (*safek*)
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut / safek
**English translation:** credibility, trustworthiness / doubt, uncertainty

In halakhic discourse, *safek* triggers specific resolution rules that presuppose the absence of *ʾaminut*.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 40

**Term A:** אמת (*ʾemet*) [propositional sense]
**Term B:** אמת (*ʾemet*) [essential-nature sense]
**Transliteration:** ʾemet / ʾemet
**English translation:** truth (of a statement) / truth (of a thing's nature)
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P3 (Ambiguous and Overlapping Items): "*ʾemet* appears across Groups 1, 2, and 6. In *ledaber ʾemet* ('to tell the truth'), it belongs to Group 2. In *ze nakhon / ze ʾemet* ('that is correct/true'), it belongs to Group 1. In *le-ʾamito shel daʾvar* ('in actual fact'), it belongs to Group 6." This is not an opposition between two terms but an internal semantic contrast within a single word across different constructional contexts.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
