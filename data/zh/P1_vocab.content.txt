# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

## Lexical Items

1. **真 (zhēn)**
   - Literal meaning: true, real, genuine
   - Typical English translations: true, real, genuine, authentic
   - Usage: Used to evaluate the authenticity or veracity of statements, objects, or persons. Commonly appears in compounds and can function as an adverb meaning "really" or as an adjective meaning "genuine/true."
   - [CORE]

2. **假 (jiǎ)**
   - Literal meaning: false, fake, artificial
   - Typical English translations: false, fake, counterfeit, artificial
   - Usage: Evaluates something as not genuine, counterfeit, or untrue. Used for both physical objects (fake goods) and statements (false claims).
   - [CORE]

3. **对 (duì)**
   - Literal meaning: correct, right, facing/toward
   - Typical English translations: correct, right, yes
   - Usage: Indicates correctness of statements, answers, or actions. Commonly used in everyday speech to affirm accuracy or appropriateness of something.
   - [CORE]

4. **错 (cuò)**
   - Literal meaning: wrong, mistaken, error
   - Typical English translations: wrong, incorrect, mistaken, error
   - Usage: Evaluates statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or mistaken. Used both as adjective and verb meaning "to be wrong" or "to make an error."
   - [CORE]

5. **实 (shí)**
   - Literal meaning: solid, real, actual, fruit/seed
   - Typical English translations: real, actual, solid, substantial
   - Usage: Emphasizes reality, actuality, or substantiality of something. Often contrasted with 虚 (xū, empty/false) in philosophical and everyday contexts.
   - [CORE]

6. **虚 (xū)**
   - Literal meaning: empty, void, false, weak
   - Typical English translations: false, empty, hollow, unreal
   - Usage: Indicates lack of substance, falseness, or unreality. Used in philosophical contexts and everyday speech to describe insubstantial or deceptive things.
   - [CORE]

7. **诚 (chéng)**
   - Literal meaning: sincere, honest, true
   - Typical English translations: sincere, honest, truthful
   - Usage: Evaluates the sincerity or honesty of persons, statements, or conduct. Central to Confucian moral philosophy and everyday moral evaluation.
   - [CORE]

8. **实际 (shíjì)**
   - Literal meaning: solid + border/limit = actual, practical
   - Typical English translations: actual, practical, realistic
   - Usage: Refers to what is actually the case as opposed to theoretical or imagined. Used to evaluate whether something corresponds to reality or is practically feasible.
   - [CORE]

9. **准确 (zhǔnquè)**
   - Literal meaning: standard + definite = accurate, precise
   - Typical English translations: accurate, precise, exact
   - Usage: Evaluates the precision or accuracy of measurements, statements, or representations. Commonly used in technical, scientific, and everyday contexts requiring exactness.
   - [CORE]

10. **正确 (zhèngquè)**
    - Literal meaning: upright/correct + definite = correct
    - Typical English translations: correct, right, proper
    - Usage: Indicates correctness of statements, decisions, or actions, often with moral or normative implications. Widely used in educational, political, and everyday contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **真实 (zhēnshí)**
    - Literal meaning: true + real = true, real, authentic
    - Typical English translations: true, real, authentic, genuine
    - Usage: Emphasizes the authenticity and reality of something, particularly statements, representations, or situations. Stronger than 真 alone in asserting correspondence to reality.
    - [CORE]

12. **虚假 (xūjiǎ)**
    - Literal meaning: empty + false = false, fake
    - Typical English translations: false, fake, phony, fraudulent
    - Usage: Evaluates something as deliberately false or deceptive. Often used in legal and formal contexts to describe fraudulent claims or counterfeit goods.
    - [CORE]

13. **真相 (zhēnxiàng)**
    - Literal meaning: true + appearance/aspect = truth, facts
    - Typical English translations: truth, the real facts, the actual situation
    - Usage: Refers to the true state of affairs or facts of a matter, especially when previously hidden or misrepresented. Commonly used in investigative and journalistic contexts.
    - [CORE]

14. **事实 (shìshí)**
    - Literal meaning: matter/affair + real = fact
    - Typical English translations: fact, reality, truth
    - Usage: Denotes objective facts or what actually happened. Used to distinguish factual claims from opinions or interpretations in various contexts.
    - [CORE]

15. **诚实 (chéngshí)**
    - Literal meaning: sincere + real = honest, truthful
    - Typical English translations: honest, truthful, sincere
    - Usage: Evaluates persons or conduct as honest and truthful. Central moral virtue term used in everyday moral evaluation and character assessment.
    - [CORE]

16. **可靠 (kěkào)**
    - Literal meaning: can + lean on = reliable, dependable
    - Typical English translations: reliable, dependable, trustworthy
    - Usage: Evaluates the reliability or trustworthiness of persons, information, or objects. Commonly used to assess whether something can be depended upon.
    - [CORE]

17. **确实 (quèshí)**
    - Literal meaning: definite + real = indeed, really, true
    - Typical English translations: indeed, really, true, authentic
    - Usage: Affirms the truth or reality of a statement or situation. Functions both as adverb (indeed) and adjective (authentic/true).
    - [CORE]

18. **符合 (fúhé)**
    - Literal meaning: tally/符 + join = conform to, accord with
    - Typical English translations: conform to, accord with, correspond to, match
    - Usage: Indicates correspondence or conformity between something and a standard, rule, or reality. Used to evaluate whether something matches or fits with something else.
    - [CORE]

19. **本质 (běnzhì)**
    - Literal meaning: root/origin + quality = essence, nature
    - Typical English translations: essence, nature, essential quality
    - Usage: Refers to the fundamental nature or essence of something. Used in philosophical and analytical contexts to distinguish essential from superficial characteristics.
    - [CORE]

20. **真正 (zhēnzhèng)**
    - Literal meaning: true + upright/correct = genuine, real, true
    - Typical English translations: genuine, real, true, authentic
    - Usage: Emphasizes genuineness or authenticity, often contrasting with superficial or false appearances. Used to identify what something really or fundamentally is.
    - [CORE]

21. **欺骗 (qīpiàn)**
    - Literal meaning: deceive + cheat = deceive, cheat
    - Typical English translations: deceive, cheat, defraud
    - Usage: Describes deliberate deception or cheating. Used to evaluate conduct as dishonest or fraudulent, with negative moral connotations.
    - [CORE]

22. **谎言 (huǎngyán)**
    - Literal meaning: lie + speech = lie, falsehood
    - Typical English translations: lie, falsehood, untruth
    - Usage: Refers to deliberately false statements. Used in moral and everyday contexts to identify and condemn dishonest speech.
    - [CORE]

23. **真理 (zhēnlǐ)**
    - Literal meaning: true + principle/reason = truth
    - Typical English translations: truth, verity
    - Usage: Refers to fundamental truths or principles, especially in philosophical, religious, or ideological contexts. More abstract and elevated than 真相 (concrete facts).
    - [CORE]

24. **正当 (zhèngdàng)**
    - Literal meaning: upright/correct + appropriate = legitimate, proper, justified
    - Typical English translations: legitimate, proper, justified, rightful
    - Usage: Evaluates actions, claims, or rights as morally or legally justified. Used in legal, moral, and political contexts to assess legitimacy.
    - [CORE]

25. **合理 (hélǐ)**
    - Literal meaning: join/accord + principle/reason = reasonable, rational
    - Typical English translations: reasonable, rational, logical
    - Usage: Evaluates whether something accords with reason or logic. Used to assess the rationality of arguments, decisions, or arrangements.
    - [CORE]

26. **名副其实 (míng fù qí shí)**
    - Literal meaning: name + match + its + reality = worthy of the name
    - Typical English translations: worthy of the name, live up to one's reputation
    - Usage: Fixed expression evaluating whether something truly corresponds to its name or reputation. Used to assess whether reality matches appearance or claim.
    - [CORE]

27. **货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí)**
    - Literal meaning: goods + true + price + real = genuine goods at fair prices
    - Typical English translations: genuine goods at fair prices, authentic and reasonably priced
    - Usage: Fixed expression originally from commerce, now used more broadly to indicate something is genuine and of good value. Evaluates both authenticity and worth.
    - [CORE]

28. **名不副实 (míng bù fù shí)**
    - Literal meaning: name + not + match + reality = unworthy of the name
    - Typical English translations: unworthy of the name, not live up to one's reputation
    - Usage: Fixed expression indicating that something does not correspond to its name or reputation. Used to criticize discrepancy between appearance and reality.
    - [CORE]

29. **言行一致 (yán xíng yīzhì)**
    - Literal meaning: speech + conduct + one + consistent = words match deeds
    - Typical English translations: words match deeds, practice what one preaches
    - Usage: Fixed expression evaluating consistency between what someone says and does. Important moral evaluation criterion in Chinese culture.
    - [CORE]

30. **表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī)**
    - Literal meaning: outside + inside + like + one = same inside and out
    - Typical English translations: same inside and out, consistent in appearance and reality
    - Usage: Fixed expression praising consistency between external appearance and internal reality. Used to evaluate sincerity and authenticity of persons or things.
    - [CORE]
