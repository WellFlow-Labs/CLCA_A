# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Methodological note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions already documented in P1b root expansion are noted as cross-references rather than re-documented in full. Absence is recorded explicitly. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and naturalness; [UNCERTAIN] is assigned where a construction is plausible but attestation is not confirmed with high confidence.

## P1 Item #1 — ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)

**Base Form:** č̣ešmariti

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი გზა
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti gza
- **English translation:** the true path, the right way
- **Usage notes:** Used in theological and moral contexts to refer to the correct spiritual or ethical course of action. Appears in religious texts and moral instruction; overlaps with martali gza (P1b) but carries stronger theological weight.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti mc̣me
- **English translation:** a true witness, a truthful witness
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal and theological contexts to characterize a witness as genuinely truthful. In theological contexts, echoes biblical usage; in legal contexts, contrasts with q̇albi mc̣me.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი მნიშვნელობა
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti mnišvneloba
- **English translation:** the true meaning, the genuine significance
- **Usage notes:** Used in hermeneutic, literary, and philosophical contexts to refer to the actual or intended meaning of a text or statement as opposed to a surface or distorted reading.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Cross-reference:** Additional č̣ešmariti + noun constructions are documented in P1b (č̣ešmar- root expansion, items 1–21). Those forms are not re-listed here.

---

**Note on periphrastic and idiomatic constructions:** No idiomatic multi-word constructions involving č̣ešmariti that are not attributive noun phrases are identified with confidence beyond those already documented in P1b.

## P1 Item #2 — ჭეშმარიტება (č̣ešmariteba)

**Base Form:** č̣ešmariteba

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება ითქვა
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba itqva
- **English translation:** the truth was spoken, the truth has been told
- **Usage notes:** A resultative construction used in formal and literary registers to assert that a true statement has been made. Appears in rhetorical and narrative contexts marking the disclosure of truth.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება გამოვლინდა
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba gamovlinda
- **English translation:** the truth came to light, the truth was revealed
- **Usage notes:** Used in journalistic, literary, and theological contexts to assert that what was hidden or unknown has been disclosed. The verb gamovlinda is the perfective of gamovlindeba ("to be revealed, to come to light").
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება ერთია
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba ertia
- **English translation:** truth is one, there is one truth
- **Usage notes:** A formulaic assertion used in theological and philosophical contexts to assert the unity or uniqueness of truth. Appears in apologetic and philosophical writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** ჭეშმარიტება მარადიულია
- **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba maradiulia
- **English translation:** truth is eternal
- **Usage notes:** Used in theological and philosophical registers to assert the timeless character of truth. Appears in sermons, philosophical texts, and formal rhetoric.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Cross-reference:** Genitive constructions with č̣ešmariteba (č̣ešmaritebis dzieba, č̣ešmaritebis gamovlena, č̣ešmaritebis k'rit'eriumi, č̣ešmaritebis t'eoria, č̣ešmaritebis moment'i) are documented in P1b (č̣ešmar- root expansion, items 9–13) and are not re-listed here.

## P1 Item #3 — მართალი (martali)

**Base Form:** martali

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** მართალი ხარ
- **Transliteration:** martali xar
- **English translation:** you are right, you are correct
- **Usage notes:** Extremely frequent in everyday spoken Georgian as a direct acknowledgment that the interlocutor's statement is correct. Functions as a standard agreement response.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** მართალი ვარ
- **Transliteration:** martali var
- **English translation:** I am right, I am correct
- **Usage notes:** Used in everyday speech to assert the correctness of one's own position or statement. Appears in argument and discussion contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** მართალი იყო
- **Transliteration:** martali iq̇o
- **English translation:** he/she was right, it was true
- **Usage notes:** Past tense construction used to retrospectively confirm the correctness of a person's statement or position. Very common in narrative and evaluative speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** მართალი ბრძანდებით
- **Transliteration:** martali brdzandebit
- **English translation:** you are right (honorific/formal)
- **Usage notes:** Polite or honorific form of the agreement construction, using the honorific copula brdzandebit. Used in formal speech and when addressing elders or persons of higher status.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** მართალი გითხრა
- **Transliteration:** martali gitxra
- **English translation:** to tell you the truth, honestly speaking
- **Usage notes:** A formulaic expression used to introduce a candid or honest statement, often one that the speaker expects may be unwelcome. Functions as a discourse marker of sincerity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Example:** მართალი სიტყვა
- **Transliteration:** martali sit'q̇va
- **English translation:** a true word, an honest word
- **Usage notes:** Cross-reference: documented in P1b (mart- root expansion, item 29). Not re-documented here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Example:** მართალი კაცი
- **Transliteration:** martali k'aci
- **English translation:** an honest man, a righteous person
- **Usage notes:** Cross-reference: documented in P1b (mart- root expansion, item 24). Not re-documented here.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Example:** მართალია, მაგრამ...
- **Transliteration:** martalia, magram...
- **English translation:** it is true that..., but... / granted, but...
- **Usage notes:** A concessive construction used in argumentation to acknowledge the truth of a prior claim before introducing a qualification or counterargument. Very common in spoken and written Georgian.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item #4 — სიმართლე (simartle)

**Base Form:** simartle

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** სიმართლე თქვი
- **Transliteration:** simartle tqvi
- **English translation:** tell the truth, you told the truth
- **Usage notes:** The imperative or past form of the collocation simartle tqma. Extremely common in everyday speech as a command or assertion about truth-telling. The imperative form (simartle tqvi as command) is among the most frequent truth-related expressions in colloquial Georgian.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** სიმართლე გითხრა
- **Transliteration:** simartle gitxra
- **English translation:** to tell you the truth, honestly
- **Usage notes:** A formulaic discourse marker used to introduce a candid statement. Parallel to martali gitxra; both are common in spoken Georgian as sincerity markers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** სიმართლეს ვამბობ
- **Transliteration:** simartles vambob
- **English translation:** I am telling the truth, I speak the truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in everyday and formal speech to assert the truthfulness of one's own statement. Appears in argument, testimony, and everyday assertion contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** სიმართლეს ვერ ამბობს
- **Transliteration:** simartles ver ambobs
- **English translation:** he/she cannot tell the truth, he/she is unable to speak truthfully
- **Usage notes:** Used in moral evaluation to characterize a person as constitutionally or habitually unable to be truthful. Appears in everyday and literary registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** სიმართლე მოითხოვს
- **Transliteration:** simartle moitxovs

**Construction 6**

---

**Construction 7**

- **Transliteration:** simartle mc̣area
- **English translation:** truth is bitter, the truth hurts
- **Usage notes:** Cross-reference: documented in P1b (mart- root expansion, item 28). Not re-documented here.

---

- **English translation:** it is close to the truth, it approximates the truth

- **Transliteration:** simartleshi vart
- **English translation:** we are in the right, we are on the side of truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in moral and political discourse to assert that one's position or cause is the truthful or just one. Appears in political rhetoric and moral argumentation.

---

**Construction 10**
- **Example:** სიმართლის ნახევარი
- **Transliteration:** simartlis naxevari
- **English translation:** a half-truth, half of the truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in critical and evaluative contexts to characterize a statement that contains some truth but omits or distorts other relevant facts. Appears in journalistic and analytical writing.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Example:** სიმართლის მარცვალი
- **Transliteration:** simartlis marcvali
- **English translation:** a grain of truth
- **Usage notes:** Used in evaluative contexts to acknowledge that a claim contains a small element of truth despite being largely false or exaggerated. Functions as a partial concession in argumentation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item #5 — ნამდვილი (namdvili)

**Base Form:** namdvili

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი ოქრო
- **Transliteration:** namdvili oqro
- **English translation:** real gold, genuine gold
- **Usage notes:** The canonical example of namdvili in commercial and everyday use, contrasting genuine material with imitation. Appears in commercial contexts and as a standard illustrative example in linguistic description of the word.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი სახელი
- **Transliteration:** namdvili saxeli
- **English translation:** real name, true name, actual name
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts to refer to a person's actual name as opposed to a pseudonym, alias, or assumed name. Contrasts with q̇albi saxeli.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი მიზეზი
- **Transliteration:** namdvili mizezi
- **English translation:** the real reason, the actual cause
- **Usage notes:** Used in analytical and everyday contexts to distinguish the actual cause or motive from a stated or apparent one. Overlaps with č̣ešmariti mizezi but is more colloquial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი ამბავი
- **Transliteration:** namdvili ambavi
- **English translation:** the real story, what actually happened
- **Usage notes:** Used in journalistic and everyday contexts to assert that an account reflects what actually occurred, as opposed to a distorted or fabricated version. Common in colloquial speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი პრობლემა
- **Transliteration:** namdvili p'roblema
- **English translation:** the real problem, the actual issue
- **Usage notes:** Used in analytical and everyday contexts to identify the genuine underlying problem as opposed to a surface or apparent one. Very common in spoken and written Georgian.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი ბუნება
- **Transliteration:** namdvili buneba
- **English translation:** true nature, real nature, actual character
- **Usage notes:** Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts to refer to the genuine character of a person or thing. Overlaps with č̣ešmariti buneba but is more colloquial.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Example:** ნამდვილი ცხოვრება
- **Transliteration:** namdvili cxovreba
- **English translation:** real life, actual life
- **Usage notes:** Used in literary, philosophical, and everyday contexts to contrast genuine lived experience with idealized, fictional, or superficial existence. Appears in literary criticism and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on idiomatic constructions:** No idiomatic multi-word constructions involving namdvili beyond attributive noun phrases are identified with confidence.

## P1 Item #7 — ყალბი (q̇albi)

**Base Form:** q̇albi

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** ყალბი დოკუმენტის გაყალბება
- **Transliteration:** q̇albi dok'ument'is gaq̇albeba
- **English translation:** the forgery of a forged document, document falsification
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal contexts when specifying the act of falsification with reference to the document type. The construction combines the attributive q̇albi with the verbal noun gaq̇albeba to specify both the object and the act.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** ყალბი ამოიცნო
- **Transliteration:** q̇albi amoicno
- **English translation:** identified the fake, recognized the forgery
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of identifying something as counterfeit or fraudulent. The verb amoicnobs ("to recognize, to identify") combines with q̇albi as a nominal predicate.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Cross-reference:** Attributive constructions with q̇albi (q̇albi dok'ument'i, q̇albi p'uli, q̇albi xelmc̣era, q̇albi mc̣moba, q̇albi in'p'ormacia, q̇albi siaxle, q̇albi p'iradoba, q̇albi braldeba, q̇albi mc̣me, q̇albi čveneba, q̇albi p'ret'enzia, q̇albi saxeli, q̇albi oqro, q̇albi grdznobebi, q̇albi ɣimili, q̇albi p'at'osi, q̇albi vitareba, q̇albi angarishi, q̇albi vačroba, q̣albi eksp'ert'iza) are documented in P1b (q̇alb- root expansion, items 1–25) and are not re-listed here.

## P1 Item #9 — მტკიცება (mt'k'iceba)

**Base Form:** mt'k'iceba

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** მტკიცების ტვირთი
- **Transliteration:** mt'k'icebis t'virti
- **English translation:** burden of proof
- **Usage notes:** The standard legal phrase for the burden of proof, specifying which party bears the obligation to establish a claim. Appears extensively in legal proceedings, codes of procedure, and legal commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** მტკიცების სტანდარტი
- **Transliteration:** mt'k'icebis st'andart'i
- **English translation:** standard of proof
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal contexts to refer to the level of certainty required to establish a claim (e.g., beyond reasonable doubt, balance of probabilities). Appears in procedural law and legal commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** მტკიცების ვალდებულება
- **Transliteration:** mt'k'icebis valdebuleba
- **English translation:** obligation to prove, duty to establish
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal contexts to refer to the procedural obligation of a party to prove their claim. Closely related to mt'k'icebis t'virti but emphasizes the obligatory character of the duty.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** მტკიცებას ვერ ახერხებს
- **Transliteration:** mt'k'icebas ver axerxebs
- **English translation:** is unable to prove, fails to establish
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal and argumentative contexts to characterize a party's failure to meet their burden of proof. Appears in court decisions and legal commentary.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item #11 — სიყალბე (siq̇albe)

**Base Form:** siq̇albe

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** სიყალბეში დაიჭირეს
- **Transliteration:** siq̇albeshi daič̣ires
- **English translation:** was caught in a lie/fraud, was caught in the act of falsification
- **Usage notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of catching someone in a fraudulent or dishonest act. The locative construction with siq̇albe functions idiomatically to mean being caught in the act of deception.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** სიყალბე გამოაშკარავდა
- **Transliteration:** siq̇albe gamoašk'aravda
- **English translation:** the fraud/falsity was exposed, the deception came to light
- **Usage notes:** Used in journalistic and legal contexts to describe the exposure of something fraudulent or false. The verb gamoašk'aravda ("was exposed, was unmasked") is a standard collocation partner for siq̇albe.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Cross-reference:** Genitive constructions with siq̇albe (siq̇albis gamovlena, siq̇albeze c̣asvla, siq̇albis dadastureba, siq̇albis damadasturebeli) are documented in P1b (q̇alb- root expansion, items 21–22) and P1 (items #19, #30) and are not re-listed here.

## P1 Item #12 — სიცრუე (sicrue)

**Base Form:** sicrue

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** სიცრუეში დაიჭირეს
- **Transliteration:** sicrueši daič̣ires
- **English translation:** was caught in a lie, was caught lying
- **Usage notes:** Used in everyday and legal contexts to describe the act of catching someone in a lie. Parallel in structure to siq̇albeshi daič̣ires but applies specifically to verbal deception rather than document fraud.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** სიცრუეს ამბობს
- **Transliteration:** sicrues ambobs
- **English translation:** is telling a lie, is lying
- **Usage notes:** The standard verbal construction for the act of lying, using the verb ambobs ("says, tells"). Very common in everyday moral evaluation and in legal contexts involving false statements.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** სიცრუეა
- **Transliteration:** sicruea
- **English translation:** it is a lie, that is false
- **Usage notes:** A predicative construction used to characterize a statement as a lie. Extremely common in everyday speech as a direct rebuttal of a false claim.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** სიცრუეს ეფუძნება
- **Transliteration:** sicrues ep'uzdneba
- **English translation:** is based on a lie, rests on falsehood
- **Usage notes:** Used in analytical and moral contexts to characterize a claim, argument, or situation as founded on a false premise. Appears in political commentary and moral argumentation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
