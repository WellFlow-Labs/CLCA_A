# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 843 (transliteration of Modern Greek), applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (privative) + λήθη (concealment, forgetting); "unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, veridical
**Usage:** Used of statements, propositions, and claims that correspond to reality. Appears in both everyday and philosophical registers. Participates in a productive derivational family (αλήθεια, αληθεύω, αληθινός).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Literal meaning:** noun derived from αληθής; "the state of being unconcealed"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality
**Usage:** The primary noun for truth in Greek, used across everyday, legal, philosophical, and religious contexts. Frequently appears in fixed expressions such as λέω την αλήθεια ("I tell the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Literal meaning:** adjectival extension of αληθής; "of the nature of truth"
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, true, authentic
**Usage:** Often used to distinguish something genuine from a copy, imitation, or false version (e.g., αληθινός φίλος, "a true/genuine friend"). Slightly more colloquial in register than αληθής.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** ψευδής
**Transliteration:** psevdís
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύδος (falsehood, lie)
**Typical English translations:** false, untrue, lying, spurious
**Usage:** Applied to statements, claims, documents, or persons to indicate falsity or deceptiveness. Common in legal and formal contexts (e.g., ψευδής κατάθεση, "false testimony").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** ψεύδος
**Transliteration:** psévdos
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, lie; root shared with ψευδής
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The primary noun for a lie or falsehood. Used in both everyday speech and formal argumentation. Participates in compounds such as ψευδοεπιστήμη ("pseudoscience").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** γνήσιος
**Transliteration:** gníssios
**Literal meaning:** from γένος (birth, kind); "of legitimate birth," hence "genuine"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, legitimate, real
**Usage:** Used of objects, documents, persons, and relationships to assert authenticity or legitimacy of origin. Common in legal contexts (e.g., γνήσιο αντίγραφο, "certified/genuine copy") and in evaluating artworks or products.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** αυθεντικός
**Transliteration:** afthentikós
**Literal meaning:** from αυθέντης (one who acts on their own authority); "authoritative, original"
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine, original
**Usage:** Applied to documents, artworks, persons, and experiences to indicate that they are what they claim to be or that they originate from the stated source. Widely used in cultural, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** αξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** aksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + πίστις (trust, faith); "worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, credible, trustworthy
**Usage:** Used of persons, sources, institutions, and information to indicate that they can be trusted or believed. Common in journalism, legal proceedings, and everyday evaluation of sources.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** έγκυρος
**Transliteration:** éngiros
**Literal meaning:** from ἐν- + κῦρος (authority, validity); "having authority/force"
**Typical English translations:** valid, authoritative, legitimate
**Usage:** Applied to documents, arguments, elections, and contracts to indicate that they meet the required conditions for legal or logical force. Contrasts with άκυρος ("invalid, null").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** ακριβής
**Transliteration:** akrivís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (intensive) + κρίβανος or related to ἀκριβόω (to be exact); "exact, precise"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, exact, precise
**Usage:** Used of measurements, descriptions, translations, and accounts to indicate that they correspond closely to what they represent. Appears in both technical and everyday registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ορθός
**Transliteration:** orthós
**Literal meaning:** straight, upright, correct
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, orthodox
**Usage:** Applied to answers, procedures, beliefs, and conduct to indicate conformity with a standard or norm. Participates in many compounds: ορθογραφία ("correct writing"), ορθόδοξος ("of correct belief").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** σωστός
**Transliteration:** sostós
**Literal meaning:** from σώζω (to save, preserve); "sound, intact, correct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, sound
**Usage:** The most common colloquial term for "correct" in everyday Modern Greek. Used of answers, behavior, decisions, and persons. Often interchangeable with ορθός in informal contexts but more frequent in speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** λανθασμένος
**Transliteration:** lanthazmenos
**Literal meaning:** past participle of λανθάνομαι (to be mistaken, to err)
**Typical English translations:** wrong, mistaken, erroneous, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to beliefs, statements, calculations, and actions to indicate that they are in error. Common in everyday and educational contexts as the standard antonym of σωστός.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** εικονικός
**Transliteration:** eikonikós
**Literal meaning:** from εικόνα (image, icon); "of or like an image"
**Typical English translations:** simulated, virtual, nominal, figurative; also: iconic
**Usage:** Has developed a range of uses: in modern contexts it frequently means "virtual" or "simulated" (e.g., εικονική πραγματικότητα, "virtual reality"), and can also mean "nominal" or "token" in legal/financial contexts, indicating something that resembles but is not the real thing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** νόθος
**Transliteration:** nóthos
**Literal meaning:** of illegitimate birth; spurious, bastard
**Typical English translations:** spurious, counterfeit, illegitimate, adulterated
**Usage:** Used historically and in formal registers to describe texts, documents, or objects of doubtful or false attribution. Also used of persons born outside legal marriage. In textual criticism, applied to works falsely attributed to an author.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** πλαστός
**Transliteration:** plastós
**Literal meaning:** from πλάθω (to mold, fabricate); "molded, fabricated"
**Typical English translations:** forged, counterfeit, fabricated, fake
**Usage:** Primarily used in legal and everyday contexts for forged documents, counterfeit currency, or fabricated evidence (e.g., πλαστό διαβατήριο, "forged passport"). Implies deliberate fabrication.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** κίβδηλος
**Transliteration:** kívdilos
**Literal meaning:** of uncertain etymology; originally applied to debased or counterfeit coinage
**Typical English translations:** counterfeit, spurious, adulterated, base
**Usage:** Used in formal, literary, and philosophical registers to describe something that appears genuine but is not — coins, arguments, or persons. Less common in everyday Modern Greek; more frequent in written and elevated registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**18.**
**Form:** αμφίβολος
**Transliteration:** amfívolos
**Literal meaning:** ἀμφί (on both sides) + βάλλω (to throw); "thrown both ways, doubtful"
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, dubious, ambiguous, questionable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, persons, or situations whose truth, reliability, or quality is in question. Used in both everyday and formal registers to express uncertainty about the status of something.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** αμφισβητήσιμος
**Transliteration:** amfisvitiísimos
**Literal meaning:** from αμφισβητώ (to dispute, contest); "disputable"
**Typical English translations:** disputable, contestable, questionable, debatable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, rights, facts, and decisions to indicate that their correctness or legitimacy can be challenged. Common in legal and argumentative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** αναληθής
**Transliteration:** analithís
**Literal meaning:** ἀν- (privative) + αληθής; "not true"
**Typical English translations:** untrue, false
**Usage:** A formal or literary negation of αληθής, used in philosophical and written contexts. Less common in everyday speech than ψευδής; tends to appear in contexts where the negation of truth-value is being discussed explicitly.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**21.**
**Form:** εικαζόμενος
**Transliteration:** eikazómenos
**Literal meaning:** present participle passive of εικάζω (to conjecture, surmise); "conjectured, supposed"
**Typical English translations:** alleged, supposed, putative, conjectured
**Usage:** Used in legal and journalistic contexts to describe a person or fact whose status has not been established (e.g., ο εικαζόμενος δράστης, "the alleged perpetrator"). Marks epistemic reservation about a claim.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** φερόμενος ως
**Transliteration:** ferómenos os
**Literal meaning:** present participle of φέρω (to carry, bear) + ως (as); "being carried/presented as"
**Typical English translations:** alleged, purported, so-called, presented as
**Usage:** A fixed participial expression used in legal, journalistic, and formal contexts to indicate that something is described or presented as having a certain identity or status without that status being confirmed (e.g., φερόμενος ως ιδιοκτήτης, "the purported owner").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** δήθεν
**Transliteration:** díthен
**Literal meaning:** from δή (indeed, just) + θεν (suffix of origin/manner); "supposedly, allegedly"
**Typical English translations:** supposedly, allegedly, so-called, ostensibly
**Usage:** A particle or adverb expressing skepticism or irony about a claim — that something is presented as being the case but the speaker doubts it. Frequently used with scare-quote force in everyday speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** τάχα
**Transliteration:** tácha
**Literal meaning:** originally "quickly, perhaps"; in modern usage, "supposedly, as if"
**Typical English translations:** supposedly, allegedly, as if, ostensibly
**Usage:** Used colloquially to cast doubt on a claim or to indicate that something is presented as true but is not believed by the speaker. Often interchangeable with δήθεν in informal registers, though with slightly different pragmatic nuances.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ουσιαστικός
**Transliteration:** ussiastikós
**Literal meaning:** from ουσία (essence, substance); "of or pertaining to essence"
**Typical English translations:** substantial, essential, real, material
**Usage:** Used to distinguish what is genuinely significant or real from what is superficial or formal. In legal contexts, ουσιαστικό δίκαιο means "substantive law" as opposed to procedural law. Also used in everyday speech to mean "real" or "meaningful."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** ουσία
**Transliteration:** ussía
**Literal meaning:** from εἶναι (to be); "being, essence, substance"
**Typical English translations:** essence, substance, matter, gist
**Usage:** Used in philosophical contexts for the fundamental nature of a thing, and in everyday contexts for the essential point or substance of a matter (e.g., η ουσία του ζητήματος, "the substance/gist of the matter"). Also used in legal contexts for "substance" as opposed to form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** επαληθεύω
**Transliteration:** epalithévo
**Literal meaning:** ἐπί (upon, further) + αληθεύω (to be true, to verify); "to verify, to confirm as true"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to validate, to corroborate
**Usage:** Used of the process of checking or confirming that a claim, result, or document is accurate or genuine. Common in scientific, journalistic, and administrative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** διαψεύδω
**Transliteration:** diapsévdo
**Literal meaning:** διά (through, thoroughly) + ψεύδω (to deceive, to falsify); "to refute, to give the lie to"
**Typical English translations:** to refute, to deny, to disprove, to contradict
**Usage:** Used when a claim, rumor, or allegation is officially or demonstrably shown to be false. Common in journalistic and official contexts (e.g., η κυβέρνηση διέψευσε τις φήμες, "the government denied/refuted the rumors").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** αξιόλογος
**Transliteration:** aksiólogos
**Literal meaning:** ἄξιος (worthy) + λόγος (word, account, reason); "worthy of account"
**Typical English translations:** noteworthy, significant, considerable, of merit
**Usage:** Applied to persons, works, or achievements to indicate that they are genuinely worthy of attention or merit. Used in evaluative contexts across everyday, academic, and critical registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** εικαστικός
**Transliteration:** eikastikós
**Literal meaning:** from εικάζω (to represent by likeness, to conjecture); "of or pertaining to representation by likeness"
**Typical English translations:** visual (arts), representational, figurative
**Usage:** In Modern Greek, primarily used in the fixed expression εικαστικές τέχνες ("visual/plastic arts"), referring to arts that produce visible representations. The term carries the sense of arts concerned with likeness and representation, though its evaluative dimension (resemblance, fidelity of representation) is more prominent in older or technical usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
