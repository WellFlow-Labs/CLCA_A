## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### VERIFICATION → CAUSATION Merger
Tagalog does not distinguish between causative constructions for making something true and constructions for verifying/proving truth. The **pa-...-an** causative pattern serves both functions:
- *Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo.* can mean both "Make what you said verified" (causation) and "Prove what you said" (verification)
- The nominalized form *katunayan* "proof, evidence" derives from the same root but functions specifically in the verification domain
- This represents a systematic merger where verification is conceptualized as causation of truth-status

### DISCONFIRMATION → NEGATION Merger
There is no dedicated construction for disconfirmation distinct from simple negation. Disconfirming a claim uses the same structures as negating it:
- **Hindi** + truth-domain predicate: *Hindi totoo ang sinabi niya.* "What he/she said is not true."
- **Walang** + nominalization: *Walang katotohanan ang balita.* "The news has no truth."
- The language does not grammatically distinguish between "X is not true" (negation) and "X has been shown to be false" (disconfirmation)

### REVELATION Gap
Tagalog lacks a dedicated construction for revelation (coming to know/discover truth). This semantic domain is expressed through:
- BECOME constructions: *Magkatotoo ang pangarap ko.* "My dream came true" (achievement of truth-state)
- Periphrastic expressions with knowledge verbs: *Nalaman ko ang katotohanan.* "I learned/discovered the truth" (using knowledge verb + nominalization)
- The language does not grammaticalize the distinction between becoming true and being revealed as true

### Asymmetry in Agentive Nominalization
A striking asymmetry exists in agentive forms:
- **Negative agentive exists**: *sinungaling* "liar" (lexicalized)
- **Positive agentive missing**: No single-word equivalent for "truth-teller"; requires periphrasis (*taong nagsasabi ng totoo* "person who tells the truth")
- This represents a lexical gap rather than a structural gap, as the morphological pattern for agentive nominalization is not productive in the truth domain

### Reciprocal Construction Restrictions
The **mag-...-an** reciprocal pattern shows semantic restrictions:
- **Productive with character terms**: *Magtapatan na tayo.* "Let's be honest with each other." / *Magbulaan sila.* "They lie to each other."
- **Not used with factual truth terms**: *totoo* "true" does not occur in this pattern
- This suggests the reciprocal construction is specialized for interpersonal honesty/dishonesty rather than factual truth claims

### Discourse Marker Grammaticalization
Several truth-domain constructions have grammaticalized into discourse markers, shifting from truth-predication to epistemic/evidential functions:
- *Sa totoo lang* "to be honest" (epistemic framing)
- *Ang totoo* "the truth is / actually" (correction marker)
- *Tama na* "that's enough" (sufficiency marker, semantic shift from correctness)
- These represent functional extensions beyond the core truth-domain semantics

### Negation Strategies
Tagalog employs two distinct negation strategies with different pragmatic effects:
- **Hindi** (predicate negation): Standard, neutral negation
- **Walang** + nominalization (existential negation): Stronger, more categorical negation implying complete absence
- Both are fully productive, but *walang* constructions carry additional emphatic force

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
1. **REVELATION**: No dedicated construction for "come to be known as true" or "be revealed as true"
   - Meaning expressed through BECOME constructions or periphrastic knowledge verbs
   - The language does not grammaticalize the epistemic transition distinct from state transition

2. **VERIFICATION (as distinct from CAUSATION)**: No separate construction for verification/proof distinct from causative
   - VERIFICATION merged with CAUSATION (factual) through **pa-...-an** pattern
   - Lexical specialization exists (*katunayan* "proof") but no distinct grammatical construction

3. **DISCONFIRMATION (as distinct from NEGATION)**: No separate construction for showing something to be false
   - DISCONFIRMATION merged with NEGATION
   - Both use **hindi** or **walang** patterns without grammatical distinction

### Lexical Gaps
1. **Positive agentive nominalization**: No common single-word term for "truth-teller"
   - *Sinungaling* "liar" exists as lexicalized form
   - Positive equivalent requires periphrasis: *taong nagsasabi ng totoo* "person who tells the truth"
   - Represents asymmetry favoring negative characterization

### Conceptual Gaps
None identified. All truth-domain concepts can be expressed, though some require periphrasis or substitution by related operators.

### Asymmetric Gaps
1. **Agentive nominalization asymmetry**:
   - Negative form (liar): lexicalized single word
   - Positive form (truth-teller): no single word, circumlocution required
   - Pattern not productive for either polarity, but lexicalization favors negative

2. **Reciprocal construction restriction**:
   - Available for character/honesty terms (*tapat*, *bulaán*)
   - Not available for factual truth terms (*totoo*)
   - Represents semantic rather than structural restriction

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully Available
- **ATTR**: Both predicate-initial and topic-initial word orders; unrestricted productivity
- **ADNOM**: Linker construction fully productive across all truth-domain terms
- **BECOME**: **Mag-ka-** inchoative fully productive; regular aspect marking
- **CAUSATION (factual)**: **Pa-...-an** and **i-** causatives fully productive
- **NEGATION**: Both **hindi** (predicate negation) and **walang** (existential negation) fully productive
- **NOMINALIZATION (abstract)**: **Ka-...-an** circumfix fully productive

### Available with Substitution
- **VERIFICATION**: Expressed through CAUSATION (factual) constructions
  - No distinct grammatical marking
  - Lexical specialization in *katunayan* "proof" but uses same morphology as abstract nominalization
  
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: Expressed through NEGATION constructions
  - No distinct grammatical marking for evidential disconfirmation vs. simple negation
  
- **CAUSATION (character)**: Partially expressed through reciprocal **mag-...-an** construction
  - Restricted to interpersonal honesty contexts
  - Not available for factual truth domain

### Partially Available
- **NOMINALIZATION (agentive)**: 
  - Negative form lexicalized (*sinungaling* "liar")
  - Positive form requires periphrasis
  - Pattern not productive

### Missing Entirely
- **REVELATION**: No dedicated construction
  - Meaning expressed through BECOME or periphrastic knowledge verbs
  - No grammaticalization of epistemic discovery distinct from state change

---

## Summary Statement

Tagalog exhibits a relatively complete inventory of truth-domain constructions with three notable characteristics:

1. **Merger patterns**: VERIFICATION merges with CAUSATION, and DISCONFIRMATION merges with NEGATION, suggesting the language does not grammatically distinguish evidential operations from their corresponding state-change or negation operations.

2. **Structural gap**: REVELATION lacks a dedicated construction, with the semantic domain covered by BECOME constructions or lexical verbs of knowledge.

3. **Asymmetric lexicalization**: Agentive nominalization shows a negative bias, with a lexicalized term for "liar" but no equivalent single-word term for "truth-teller."

All other canonical operators are fully supported through productive morphological or syntactic patterns.
