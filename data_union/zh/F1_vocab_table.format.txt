| 较真 | jiàozhēn | compare/be particular about + true/real | to be a stickler; to take things too literally; to be pedantic about accuracy | Colloquial expression describing a tendency to insist on exactness or to take things too literally. Can be used approvingly (conscientious) or disapprovingly (pedantic). Common in everyday speech. | [CORE] |
| 真话 | zhēnhuà | true + speech/words | the truth, a true statement, honest words | Refers to a statement that accurately reflects reality or what the speaker actually believes or knows. Appears in the contrast 真话假话 (true words and false words) and in expressions like 说真话 (to tell the truth). Common in everyday and moral discourse. | [CORE] |
| 实际 | shíjì | solid/real + border/boundary/occasion | actual, practical, in practice, reality | Contrasts what is actually the case with what is theoretical, ideal, or claimed. Extremely common across all registers: 实际情况 (the actual situation), 脱离实际 (divorced from reality), 实际上 (in practice, actually). | [CORE] |
| 实际上 | shíjìshàng | solid/real + border/occasion + on/above | in fact, actually, in practice, in reality | An adverbial phrase used to introduce a correction or clarification, asserting what is actually the case as opposed to what appears or is claimed. One of the most common discourse markers in Chinese across all registers. | [CORE] |
| 现实 | xiànshí | present/manifest + solid/real | reality, the real world; realistic, pragmatic | Refers to the actual state of the world as it currently exists, often contrasted with ideals, wishes, or fiction. Also used as an adjective meaning pragmatic or realistic: 面对现实 (to face reality), 现实主义 (realism). | [CORE] |
| 实现 | shíxiàn | solid/real + to appear/manifest | to realize, to achieve, to bring about, to actualize | Describes the process of making something actual or concrete that was previously only potential or planned. Common in goal-oriented, political, and everyday discourse: 实现梦想 (to realize a dream), 实现目标 (to achieve a goal). | [CORE] |
| 实施 | shíshī | solid/real + to carry out/implement | to implement, to put into effect, to enforce | Used in legal, administrative, and policy contexts to describe the putting into practice of a law, plan, or measure: 实施法律 (to implement a law), 实施方案 (to carry out a plan). | [CORE] |
| 实践 | shíjiàn | solid/real + to tread/practice | practice, to practice, to put into practice | Refers to the application of knowledge or principles in action, as opposed to theory. Central to Marxist epistemology in Chinese political discourse (实践是检验真理的唯一标准, practice is the sole criterion for testing truth) and to everyday usage. | [CORE] |
| 实验 | shíyàn | solid/real + to test/verify | experiment, to experiment, experimental | The standard term for scientific experiment or experimental procedure. Also used more broadly for any trial or test of a hypothesis or method: 做实验 (to conduct an experiment), 实验室 (laboratory). | [CORE] |
| 实证 | shízhèng | solid/real + evidence/proof | empirical evidence, empirical proof; positivism (in philosophy) | Used in scientific and philosophical discourse to refer to evidence grounded in observation and fact. 实证主义 is the standard translation of positivism. Also used in legal contexts for concrete proof. Central to discussions of empiricism and evidence-based reasoning: 实证研究 (empirical research). | [CORE] |
| 实情 | shíqíng | solid/real + situation/circumstances | the true situation, the actual facts, the real state of affairs | Refers to the actual circumstances of a matter, especially when these may differ from what has been reported or claimed. Common in legal, investigative, and everyday contexts: 了解实情 (to understand the true situation). | [CORE] |
| 实话 | shíhuà | solid/real + speech/words | the truth, truthful words, honest statement, straight talk | Refers to a statement that accurately reflects what the speaker knows or believes, as opposed to polite evasions or lies. Appears in the common expression 实话实说 (to tell it like it is, to speak the truth plainly) and 说实话 (to tell the truth). | [CORE] |
| 实话实说 | shí huà shí shuō | solid/real + speech + solid/real + to speak | to tell it like it is, to speak plainly and honestly, to say it straight | A fixed expression used to introduce or request a frank, unvarnished account. Common in everyday conversation and also the name of a well-known Chinese television talk show, which extended its currency. | [CORE] |
| 实力 | shílì | solid/real + strength/power | actual strength, real capability, substantive power | Refers to the genuine, demonstrated capacity of a person, team, or state, as opposed to nominal rank or reputation. Common in sports, military, political, and business discourse: 实力派 (a person of genuine ability), 综合实力 (overall strength), 展示实力 (to demonstrate real strength). | [CORE] |
| 务实 | wùshí | to attend to/engage with + solid/real | pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth, results-oriented | Evaluates a person, policy, or approach as focused on concrete realities and achievable outcomes rather than ideology or abstraction. Common in political and administrative discourse: 务实的态度 (a pragmatic attitude), 务实外交 (pragmatic diplomacy). | [CORE] |
| 扎实 | zhāshi / zhāshí | to plant/root + solid/real | solid, thorough, well-grounded, substantial | Evaluates knowledge, skills, or work as genuinely thorough and well-founded rather than superficial. Common in educational and professional contexts: 基础扎实 (a solid foundation), 工作扎实 (thorough, solid work), 扎实推进 (to advance steadily and thoroughly). | [CORE] |
