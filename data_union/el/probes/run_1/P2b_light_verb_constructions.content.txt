# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The focus is on periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven constructions that are not reducible to morphological derivation (handled in P2a). Constructions already documented as phrasal examples in P1b are cross-referenced where relevant but are not repeated in full unless they warrant additional analysis. Where no common construction exists for a given base item, this is explicitly recorded. Epistemic status reflects confidence in attestation and currency.

A construction is included only where there is positive evidence of its currency as a unit — i.e., where the combination is not fully predictable from its parts, or where it functions as a recognized formulaic or semi-fixed expression. Fully compositional noun phrases (e.g., "αληθής πρόταση" = "true proposition") are not listed unless they have achieved some degree of formulaic status.

## 1. Base Form: αληθής (*alithís*)

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** Αληθώς ανέστη
- **Transliteration:** Alithós anésti
- **English translation:** Truly He is risen / He is risen indeed
- **Usage notes:** The liturgical response to "Χριστός ανέστη" (Christ is risen) in the Eastern Orthodox Easter service. A fixed formulaic exchange used annually at Pascha; the adverbial form αληθώς (from αληθής) is preserved here in a context where it would not appear in everyday speech. Functions as a complete ritual speech act.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** αληθής αξία
- **Transliteration:** alithís aksía
- **English translation:** true value, real value, intrinsic value
- **Usage notes:** A semi-fixed collocation used in financial, economic, and philosophical discourse to refer to the actual or intrinsic value of an asset or thing as opposed to its nominal or market value. Already noted in P1b; documented here as a recognized collocational unit in financial registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** αληθής αγάπη
- **Transliteration:** alithís agápi
- **English translation:** true love
- **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation used in literary, religious, and everyday contexts to describe love that is genuine and deep as opposed to superficial or feigned. Already noted in P1b; functions as a recognized formulaic phrase in both secular and Christian discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** αληθής λόγος
- **Transliteration:** alithís lógos
- **English translation:** true account, true word, truthful statement
- **Usage notes:** Used in philosophical and religious discourse to designate a statement or account that corresponds to reality. In Christian theological contexts, participates in discussions of the Logos. Less fixed than αληθής αγάπη; more compositional, but attested as a recognized collocation in formal registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

*Note: αληθής does not appear to participate in many idiomatic multi-word constructions beyond the above. Its primary role is as a predicate adjective or attributive modifier in compositional noun phrases. The more productive constructions involving the truth-evaluation domain are built on αλήθεια (see below).*

## 2. Base Form: αλήθεια (*alíthia*)

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** Αλήθεια;
- **Transliteration:** Alíthia?
- **English translation:** Really? / Is that true? / Seriously?
- **Usage notes:** Used as a discourse particle expressing surprise or requesting confirmation. Extremely frequent in spoken Greek across all age groups and registers. Functions as a complete utterance with rising intonation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** Αλήθεια, ήθελα να σε ρωτήσω…
- **Transliteration:** Alíthia, íthela na se rotíso…
- **English translation:** By the way, I wanted to ask you…
- **Usage notes:** Used as a topic-shift or topic-introduction particle at the start of a turn or clause. The truth-evaluation meaning is bleached; the function is purely discourse-organizational. Extremely common in spoken Greek.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** στ' αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** st' alíthia
- **English translation:** truly, honestly, in truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed adverbial phrase (contraction of εις την αλήθεια) used to introduce a sincere or frank statement. Already documented in P1b. Functions as a sentence adverb or discourse marker signaling sincerity.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** Η αλήθεια είναι ότι…
- **Transliteration:** I alíthia íne óti…
- **English translation:** The truth is that…
- **Usage notes:** A fixed clausal frame used to introduce a frank, corrective, or concessive statement. Already documented in P1b. Extremely common in both spoken and written Greek; functions as a discourse-framing device.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** λέω την αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** léo tin alíthia
- **English translation:** to tell the truth
- **Usage notes:** The standard colloquial expression for telling the truth. Already documented in P1b. Used in everyday speech, legal contexts, and moral discourse. The construction is fully transparent but functions as the canonical expression for this act.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Example:** αλήθεια ή ψέμα
- **Transliteration:** alíthia í pséma
- **English translation:** truth or lie; true or false
- **Usage notes:** A binary framing used in everyday speech and game contexts. Already documented in P1b. Also used rhetorically to demand a definitive answer.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Example:** αλήθεια ή τόλμη
- **Transliteration:** alíthia í tólmi
- **English translation:** truth or dare
- **Usage notes:** The name of the party game "truth or dare" as used in Greek. Already documented in P1b. A recognized fixed phrase in contemporary colloquial usage.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**
- **Example:** μισή αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** misí alíthia
- **English translation:** half-truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation used in everyday and journalistic discourse to describe a statement that is technically accurate but misleading by omission. Already documented in P1b. Carries negative evaluative force.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**
- **Example:** πικρή αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** pikrí alíthia
- **English translation:** bitter truth, hard truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation used to describe a truth that is unpleasant or painful to acknowledge. Already documented in P1b. Common in everyday speech and literary contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**
- **Example:** γυμνή αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** gimní alíthia
- **English translation:** naked truth, plain truth, bare truth
- **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation used to describe truth presented without embellishment or concealment. Already documented in P1b. Used in rhetorical and literary contexts to emphasize directness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**
- **Example:** αλήθεια του Θεού
- **Transliteration:** alíthia tu Theú
- **English translation:** God's truth; I swear to God it's true
- **Usage notes:** Used in religious discourse and as an emphatic oath in everyday speech. Already documented in P1b. "Αλήθεια του Θεού!" functions as a strong assertion of truthfulness, invoking divine witness.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**
- **Example:** αλήθεια και δικαιοσύνη
- **Transliteration:** alíthia ke dhikiosíni
- **English translation:** truth and justice
- **Usage notes:** A paired formula used in legal, political, and moral discourse. Already documented in P1b. Functions as a recognized value-pair in official and rhetorical contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 13**
- **Example:** αλήθεια και ψέμα
- **Transliteration:** alíthia ke pséma
- **English translation:** truth and lies; truth and falsehood
- **Usage notes:** A paired formula framing the binary opposition between truthfulness and deception. Already documented in P1b. Common in everyday speech, journalism, and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**
- **Example:** Για να λέμε την αλήθεια…
- **Transliteration:** Ya na léme tin alíthia…
- **English translation:** To tell the truth… / To be honest… / Truthfully speaking…
- **Usage notes:** A fixed discourse-framing phrase used to introduce a frank or candid statement, often one that the speaker might otherwise hesitate to make. Functions as a hedging or sincerity-marking device. Very common in spoken Greek.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 15**
- **Example:** Αν θέλεις την αλήθεια…
- **Transliteration:** An thélis tin alíthia…
- **English translation:** If you want the truth… / If you want my honest opinion…
- **Usage notes:** A fixed conditional frame used to introduce a frank personal assessment or opinion. Functions as a sincerity marker and hedging device. Common in spoken Greek across registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**
- **Example:** Πες μου την αλήθεια.
- **Transliteration:** Pes mu tin alíthia.
- **English translation:** Tell me the truth.
- **Usage notes:** A direct imperative construction used to demand truthful disclosure. Functions as a canonical speech act of demanding truth. Extremely common in everyday speech, interrogation contexts, and interpersonal confrontation.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**
- **Example:** Η αλήθεια πονάει.
- **Transliteration:** I alíthia ponái.
- **English translation:** The truth hurts.
- **Usage notes:** A fixed proverbial expression used to acknowledge that truthful statements can be painful. Functions as a semi-proverbial utterance in everyday speech. Corresponds to the English "the truth hurts."
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**
- **Example:** Η αλήθεια βγαίνει πάντα στο φως.
- **Transliteration:** I alíthia vgéni pánda sto fos.
- **English translation:** The truth always comes to light. / Truth will out.
- **Usage notes:** A proverbial expression asserting that concealed truths are eventually revealed. Functions as a fixed proverbial utterance. Common in everyday speech and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**
- **Example:** κατά βάθος η αλήθεια είναι ότι…
- **Transliteration:** katá vathos i alíthia íne óti…
- **English translation:** Deep down, the truth is that…
- **Usage notes:** A compound discourse frame combining the depth-metaphor (κατά βάθος = deep down) with the truth-framing construction. Used to introduce a frank assessment of an underlying reality. Common in spoken and written Greek.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 20**
- **Example:** με την αλήθεια στο στόμα
- **Transliteration:** me tin alíthia sto stóma
- **English translation:** with the truth on one's lips; speaking truthfully
- **Usage notes:** A fixed prepositional phrase used to describe someone who speaks frankly and truthfully. Used in everyday and literary registers to characterize a person's manner of speaking.
- **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 21**
- **Example:** Αυτή είναι η αλήθεια.
- **Transliteration:** Aftí íne i alíthia.
- **English translation:** That is the truth. / That's the truth of it.
- **Usage notes:** A fixed assertive construction used to close a statement with an emphatic truth-claim. Functions as a discourse-closing or emphatic-assertion device. Common in everyday speech and formal discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 22**
- **Example:** Δεν υπάρχει μία αλήθεια.
- **Transliteration:** Dhen ipárchi mía alíthia.
- **English translation:** There is no single truth. / Truth is not one.
- **Usage notes:** A fixed philosophical or relativist assertion used in academic, journalistic, and everyday discourse to challenge the idea of a single objective truth. Functions as a recognized argumentative formula in contemporary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**
- **Example:** η δική μου αλήθεια
- **Transliteration:** i dhikí mu alíthia
- **English translation:** my truth, my own truth
- **Usage notes:** A construction used in contemporary discourse — particularly in psychological, therapeutic, and social media registers — to assert a personal or subjective truth. Reflects a relativist framing of truth as individually constituted. Common in contemporary spoken and written Greek.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 24**
- **Example:** Η αλήθεια είναι σχετική.
- **Transliteration:** I alíthia íne schetikí.
- **English translation:** Truth is relative.
- **Usage notes:** A fixed philosophical assertion used in academic and everyday discourse to express relativism about truth. Functions as a recognized argumentative formula. Common in contemporary discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 3. Base Form: αληθινός (*alithinós*)

---

**Construction 1**
- **Example:** αληθινός φίλος
- **Transliteration:** alithinós fílos
- **English translation:** true friend, genuine friend
- **Usage notes:** A common collocation used to distinguish a genuine, loyal friend from a superficial acquaintance. Already documented in P1b. Functions as a recognized evaluative collocation in everyday speech and proverbial expressions.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**
- **Example:** αληθινή ιστορία
- **Transliteration:** alithiní istoría
- **English translation:** true story, real story
- **Usage notes:** Used to label narratives as based on actual events rather than fiction. Already documented in P1b. The phrase "βασισμένο σε αληθινή ιστορία" (based on a true story) is a recognized formula in film and journalism.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** αληθινός εαυτός
- **Transliteration:** alithinós eftós
- **English translation:** true self, authentic self
- **Usage notes:** Used in psychological, philosophical, and popular discourse to refer to a person's genuine identity. Already documented in P1b. Common in self-help and therapeutic registers.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** αληθινός Θεός
- **Transliteration:** alithinós Theós
- **English translation:** the true God
- **Usage notes:** Used in Christian theological and liturgical discourse to distinguish the God of Christian faith from false gods. Already documented in P1b. Appears in the Nicene Creed and other liturgical texts as a fixed formula.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** Είναι αληθινό αυτό;
- **Transliteration:** Íne alithinó aftó?
- **English translation:** Is this true? / Is this real?
- **Usage notes:** A common interrogative construction used in everyday speech to question the truth or reality of a claim or situation. Fully compositional but functions as a canonical truth-questioning formula.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**
- **Example:** αληθινή αγάπη
- **Transliteration:** alithiní agápi
- **English translation:** true love, real love
- **Usage notes:** A common collocation used in everyday, literary, and popular cultural contexts to describe genuine romantic love. Parallel to αληθής αγάπη but more colloquial. Common in song lyrics, film, and everyday speech.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**
- **Example:** Βασισμένο σε αληθινή ιστορία
- **Transliteration:** Vasizméno se alithiní istoría
- **English translation:** Based on a true story
- **Usage notes:** A fixed formula used in film, television, and publishing to indicate that a narrative is based on actual events. Functions as a recognized genre-marking phrase. Extremely common in contemporary media.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: αληθινός participates primarily in attributive collocations rather than idiomatic constructions. The constructions above are the most formulaically established. Additional attributive uses (αληθινός πόνος, αληθινή χαρά, etc.) are fully compositional and not listed.*

## 4. Base Form: γνήσιος (*gníssios*)

---

**Construction 1**

**Construction 2**

---

**Construction 3**
- **Example:** γνήσιο προϊόν
- **Transliteration:** gnísio proión
- **English translation:** genuine product, authentic product
- **Usage notes:** A common commercial collocation used to distinguish authentic goods from counterfeits or imitations. Used in advertising, consumer protection, and everyday commercial discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** γνήσιος Έλληνας
- **Transliteration:** gnísios Éllinas
- **English translation:** genuine Greek, true Greek
- **Usage notes:** Used in identity and cultural discourse to assert authentic ethnic or cultural identity. Can carry nationalist or exclusionary connotations depending on context. Attested in political and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**
- **Example:** πιστοποίηση γνησιότητας
- **Transliteration:** pistopiísi gnissiótitas
- **English translation:** authentication, certification of authenticity
- **Usage notes:** A fixed administrative and legal collocation referring to the formal process of certifying that an object, document, or work is genuine. Already documented in P1b. Common in art authentication, notarial, and archival contexts.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: γνήσιος participates primarily in attributive collocations. No strongly idiomatic multi-word constructions beyond the above are identified.*

- **Transliteration:** afthentikí ékdhosi

**Construction 3**

- **Transliteration:** afthentikí iyesía
- **English translation:** authentic leadership
- **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation in contemporary management and organizational discourse, corresponding to the English concept of "authentic leadership." Used in business, academic, and self-help registers. Reflects a borrowing of the English management concept.

---

**Construction 4**
- **Example:** Είναι αυθεντικός ως άνθρωπος.
- **Transliteration:** Íne afthentikós os ánthropos.
