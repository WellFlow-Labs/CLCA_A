# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Swahili (sw)
# Layer: P2a_derivational_system

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Derivations are documented only where the derived form is independently attested in use, not merely constructible by rule. Where a form appears in P1b root expansion, it is included here if it represents a genuine derivational relationship; its prior appearance is noted. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved without comment beyond factual observation. Forms already fully documented in P1 or P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-derived.

## BASE FORM 1: **kweli**

---

**Derived Form 1.1**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Derived Form:** ukweli
3. **Transliteration:** ukweli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract nominal prefix) + *kweli*
5. **Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; veracity
6. **Usage:** The standard abstract nominalization of *kweli*, used in philosophical, legal, moral, and everyday discourse to refer to the property or state of being true. Already documented in P1; cross-referenced here as the primary derivational product of this root.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.2**
1. **Base Form:** kweli
2. **Derived Form:** kweli kweli
3. **Transliteration:** kweli kweli
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *kweli* + *kweli* (full reduplication)
5. **Natural English translation:** truly; really; genuinely
6. **Usage:** Reduplicated intensifier used to emphasize the sincerity or accuracy of an assertion. Already documented in P1; cross-referenced here.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation from kweli:** No agentive nominal (*mweli*, *mkweli*) meaning "truth-teller" is in common use as a lexicalized form derived directly from *kweli*. The function of "one who tells the truth" is expressed periphrastically (*mtu anayesema kweli*). No verbal derivation from *kweli* itself (e.g., *kukweli*, *\*kwelisha*, *\*kwelika*) is attested as a productive form. The root does not appear to take verbal extensions productively. The primary derivational outputs are *ukweli* (nominalization) and *kweli kweli* (reduplication).

## BASE FORM 2: **ukweli**

*ukweli* is itself a derivation of *kweli* (see above). No further productive derivation from *ukweli* as a base is attested. The form participates in numerous collocations (e.g., *ukweli wa mambo*, *ukweli mtupu*, *ukweli mzima*, *ukweli uchi*) documented in P1b, but these are phrasal rather than morphological derivations.

**(no further derivation from ukweli as base identified)**

## BASE FORM 3: **uongo**

---

**Derived Form 3.1**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Derived Form:** mwongo
3. **Transliteration:** mwongo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (class 1 person prefix) + *-ongo* (falsehood/deception stem)
5. **Natural English translation:** liar; deceiver; a person who tells lies
6. **Usage:** The standard agentive nominal for a person who habitually tells lies. Used in moral condemnation and character evaluation across spoken and written registers (*Yeye ni mwongo*, "He/she is a liar"). Appears in everyday, literary, and religious registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.2**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Derived Form:** waongo
3. **Transliteration:** waongo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (class 2 plural prefix) + *-ongo* (the root, with *u-* class prefix dropped in plural)
5. **Natural English translation:** liars; deceivers; people who tell lies
6. **Usage:** The plural of *mwongo*, used in moral and social discourse to refer to a group of persons evaluated as dishonest (*waongo hawaamini*, "liars are not trusted").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3.3**
1. **Base Form:** uongo
2. **Derived Form:** kusema uongo
3. **Transliteration:** kusema uongo
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive) + *sema* (to say) + *uongo* (falsehood)
5. **Natural English translation:** to tell lies; to speak falsely
6. **Usage:** The standard verbal phrase for the act of lying. Not a morphological derivation from *uongo* itself but the primary verbal expression of the concept; included here as the closest functional verbal equivalent.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on verbal derivation from uongo:** A direct verbal derivation *kuuonga* or *kuonga* meaning "to lie" is not attested as a standard productive form. The verb *kuongea* (to speak, to talk) shares the *-ong-* stem but is not straightforwardly derived from *uongo* in the sense of "to lie"; its primary meaning is neutral speech. The verbal function is carried by *kudanganya* (see base form 16 below) and the periphrastic *kusema uongo*.

2. **Derived Form:** halisisha
3. **Transliteration:** halisisha

The causative function is also expressed by *kuthibitisha uhalisi* or similar periphrastic constructions. The derivational output of this root is primarily nominal.

## BASE FORM 7: **sahihi**

---

**Derived Form 7.1**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** usahihi
3. **Transliteration:** usahihi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract nominal prefix) + *sahihi*
5. **Natural English translation:** correctness; accuracy; precision; validity
6. **Usage:** The abstract nominalization of *sahihi*, referring to the quality of being correct or accurate. Used in academic, scientific, formal, and legal discourse to evaluate the degree of accuracy of a statement, measurement, or procedure (*usahihi wa takwimu*, "the accuracy of statistics").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.2**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** sahihisha
3. **Transliteration:** sahihisha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *sahihi* + *-sha* (causative verbal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to correct; to rectify; to make accurate
6. **Usage:** A causative verbal form meaning to make something correct or to correct an error. Attested in educational and editorial contexts (*sahihisha makosa*, "correct the errors"). Appears in language teaching and proofreading contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.3**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** kusahihisha
3. **Transliteration:** kusahihisha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *sahihi* + *-sha* (causative suffix, variant of *-isha*)
5. **Natural English translation:** to correct; to rectify; to verify; to validate; to mark (examinations); to proofread
6. **Usage:** The causative verbal derivation from *sahihi*; means to make something correct, to correct an error, or to verify/validate a document or claim. Used in educational, legal, and administrative contexts (*kusahihisha makosa*, "to correct errors"; *kusahihisha hati*, "to validate a document"; *kazi ya kusahihisha*, "the work of correcting/proofreading"). This is a productive and widely used form.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.4**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** kusahihishwa
3. **Transliteration:** kusahihishwa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* + *sahihi* + *-sh-* (causative) + *-wa* (passive suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be corrected; to be rectified; to be validated
6. **Usage:** The passive of *kusahihisha*; used to describe the state of something that has been corrected or validated by an external agent. Appears in administrative and educational contexts (*Hati imesahihishwa*, "The document has been validated/corrected").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.5**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** usahihishaji
3. **Transliteration:** usahihishaji
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract nominal prefix) + *sahihish-* + *-aji* (process/action nominalizing suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** correction; rectification; amendment; the process of marking or editing
6. **Usage:** Nominal form referring to the process of correcting errors or marking work. Used in educational and administrative discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.6**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** msahihishaji
3. **Transliteration:** msahihishaji
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (class 1 singular agentive prefix) + *sahihish-* + *-aji*
5. **Natural English translation:** corrector; examiner; marker; editor
6. **Usage:** Agentive nominal referring to a person who corrects or marks work, particularly in educational contexts. The plural *wasahihishaji* (class 2 prefix *wa-*) is equally attested.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.7**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** wasahihishaji
3. **Transliteration:** wasahihishaji
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (class 2 plural agentive prefix) + *sahihish-* + *-aji*
5. **Natural English translation:** correctors; examiners; markers; editors (plural)
6. **Usage:** Plural agentive nominal used in educational administration when referring to a team of examination markers or editors.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.8**
1. **Base Form:** sahihi
2. **Derived Form:** sahihi sahihi
3. **Transliteration:** sahihi sahihi
4. **Literal Breakdown:** full reduplication of *sahihi*
5. **Natural English translation:** exactly right; precisely correct; spot on
6. **Usage:** A reduplicated intensifier used to assert exact correctness or precise accuracy. Functions similarly to *sawa sawa*. Common in spoken Swahili as an emphatic confirmation.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on the signature use of sahihi:** The use of *sahihi* to mean "signature" (as in *sahihi ya mkono*, *weka sahihi*) represents a semantic extension rather than a morphological derivation. No additional morphological forms are built on this extended sense beyond the phrasal constructions documented in P1b.

## BASE FORM 9: **haki**

---

**Derived Form 9.1**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** uhaki
3. **Transliteration:** uhaki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract nominal prefix) + *haki*
5. **Natural English translation:** justice; righteousness; the quality of being right or just
6. **Usage:** The abstract nominalization of *haki*, referring to the quality of justice or rightness. Less common than *haki* itself in everyday use; appears in philosophical, moral, and legal discourse. Note that *haki* already functions as an abstract noun, so *uhaki* represents a further degree of abstraction.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 9.2**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** kuhaki
3. **Transliteration:** kuhaki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive) + *haki*
5. **Natural English translation:** to be right; to be just; to deserve
6. **Usage:** A verbal form attested in some registers meaning to be right or to deserve something. Less productive than other verbal derivations in this domain; appears in spoken and informal written Swahili.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Derived Form 9.3**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** kwa haki
3. **Transliteration:** kwa haki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *kwa* (by, with, in manner of) + *haki*
5. **Natural English translation:** justly; rightly; with justice; in truth
6. **Usage:** A prepositional adverbial phrase used to assert that something is done justly or rightly, or to affirm the truth of a statement. Functions similarly to *kwa kweli* in truth-assertion contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9.4**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** haki haki
3. **Transliteration:** haki haki
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *haki* + *haki* (full reduplication)
5. **Natural English translation:** truly; really; absolutely right; in all fairness
6. **Usage:** Reduplicated form of *haki* used as an intensifier, parallel to *kweli kweli*. Used in spoken Swahili to emphasize that something is genuinely right or true. Less frequent than *kweli kweli* but attested in spoken registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 9.5**
1. **Base Form:** haki
2. **Derived Form:** haki za binadamu
3. **Transliteration:** haki za binadamu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *haki* (rights) + *za* (of, class 10 plural linker) + *binadamu* (human beings)
5. **Natural English translation:** human rights
6. **Usage:** The standard Swahili term for human rights. A collocational phrase rather than a morphological derivation, but the primary domain-specific extension of *haki* in legal and political discourse. Appears in constitutional, advocacy, and journalistic contexts. The basis for a large family of related expressions in legal and political discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on further derivation from haki:** No causative verbal, agentive, or further nominal derivations built directly on *haki* as a morphological base are identified beyond those documented above. The extensive *haki ya X* and *haki za X* constructions documented in P1b are phrasal, not morphologically derived forms.

5. **Natural English translation:** to make sure; to ensure; to verify; to confirm; to ascertain
6. **Usage:** Causative verbal derivation, extremely common in everyday, administrative, and legal Swahili. Used to describe the act of making certain that something is the case or of verifying a fact or condition. This is among the most productive derivations in this domain.

1. **Base Form:** hakika (via *kuhakikisha*)
2. **Derived Form:** uhakikisho
3. **Transliteration:** uhakikisho

5. **Natural English translation:** assurance; guarantee; verification; confirmation
6. **Usage:** Nominal form used in administrative, legal, and commercial contexts to refer to a formal guarantee or confirmation. Common in insurance, contracts, and quality assurance contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on the morphological relationship between hakika and kuhakikisha:** The causative *-sha* suffix attaches to a stem *hakikish-* that shows internal modification relative to the base *hakika*. This is consistent with the behavior of Arabic-derived bases in Swahili verbal derivation and does not represent an irregular form.

## BASE FORM 12: **amini**

---

**Derived Form 12.1**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuamini
3. **Transliteration:** kuamini
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *amini*
5. **Natural English translation:** to believe; to trust; to have faith in
6. **Usage:** The infinitive verbal form. Used in religious, moral, and everyday discourse to describe the act of believing or trusting. Central to both Islamic and Christian Swahili discourse on faith and credibility (*kuamini Mungu*, "to believe in God"; *vigumu kuamini*, "hard to believe"). Extremely frequent across registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.2**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** aminifu
3. **Transliteration:** aminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival/qualitative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** trustworthy; faithful; reliable; honest
6. **Usage:** Adjectival form used to evaluate persons, institutions, or representations as reliably honest and faithful. The *-fu* suffix derives qualitative adjectives from nominal or verbal bases in Swahili.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.3**
1. **Base Form:** amini (via *aminifu*)
2. **Derived Form:** uaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** uaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *u-* (abstract nominal prefix) + *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival/nominal suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** faithfulness; trustworthiness; loyalty; integrity; reliability
6. **Usage:** The standard abstract nominalization of *amini*, referring to the quality of being trustworthy or faithful. Used in moral evaluation of character and in assessments of whether a source or account can be relied upon. Already listed in P1; cross-referenced here.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.4**
1. **Base Form:** amini (via *aminifu*)
2. **Derived Form:** mwaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** mwaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (class 1 singular prefix, before vowel) + *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** a faithful/trustworthy person; a believer; a loyal person
6. **Usage:** The agentive nominal for a person who is trustworthy or faithful. Used in moral, religious, and professional contexts (*mwaminifu katika kazi*, "faithful in work"). In religious contexts, *mwaminifu* can also mean "a believer/faithful one." Already documented in P1; cross-referenced here.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.5**
1. **Base Form:** amini (via *aminifu*)
2. **Derived Form:** waaminifu
3. **Transliteration:** waaminifu
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *wa-* (class 2 plural prefix) + *amini* + *-fu* (adjectival suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** faithful/trustworthy people; believers; loyal persons (plural)
6. **Usage:** The plural of *mwaminifu*, used in religious and moral discourse to refer to a group of faithful or trustworthy persons. In religious discourse, *waaminifu* commonly refers to the body of believers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.6**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** mwamini
3. **Transliteration:** mwamini
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *m-* (class 1 person prefix) + *amini*
5. **Natural English translation:** a believer; one who believes; the faithful
6. **Usage:** The agentive nominal for a person who believes, particularly in religious contexts. Distinct from *mwaminifu* (trustworthy person) in that *mwamini* emphasizes the act of believing rather than the quality of trustworthiness.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.7**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminika
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminika
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive) + *amini* + *-ka* (stative suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trustworthy; to be believable; to be credible
6. **Usage:** The stative form of *amini*, meaning to be in a state of being trustworthy or credible. Used in character evaluation and in assessing the credibility of claims (*habari hii haiaminiki*, "this news is not credible").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.8**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminiwa
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminiwa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-* + *amini* + *-wa* (passive suffix)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trusted; to be believed; to be found trustworthy; to be relied upon
6. **Usage:** The passive form of *kuamini*; used in contexts of evaluating whether a person or claim is trusted by others (*yeye haaminiwa*, "he/she is not trusted").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.9**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** kuaminiwa na
3. **Transliteration:** kuaminiwa na
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ku-aminiwa* (passive infinitive) + *na* (by, agentive marker)
5. **Natural English translation:** to be trusted by; to be believed by
6. **Usage:** The passive construction with agentive *na*, specifying who trusts or believes. Documented here as a productive syntactic pattern rather than a separate lexical item (*kuaminiwa na watu*, "to be trusted by people").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.10**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** imani
3. **Transliteration:** imani
4. **Literal Breakdown:** from Arabic *īmān* (faith, belief); shares Arabic root *'-m-n* with *amini*
5. **Natural English translation:** faith; belief; trust; conviction
6. **Usage:** The primary nominal form for religious faith or deep conviction. Related to *amini* through the shared Arabic root (*amana*/*īmān*) rather than through synchronic Swahili morphological derivation. Both entered Swahili from Arabic, but Swahili speakers treat them as related. Used extensively in religious, moral, and everyday discourse. Documented here because of its semantic and etymological proximity and its high frequency in the truth-evaluation domain.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12.11**
1. **Base Form:** amini
2. **Derived Form:** amana
3. **Transliteration:** amana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** from Arabic *amāna* (trust, deposit held in trust); shares Arabic root *'-m-n* with *amini*
5. **Natural English translation:** trust; a trust; something held in trust; integrity
6. **Usage:** Related to *amini* through the shared Arabic root. The relationship is etymological rather than synchronically productive in Swahili morphology. Documented here to record the root relationship.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on imani and amana:** The relationship between *amini* and *imani* / *amana* in Swahili is one of shared Arabic etymology (*أمن*, *أمين*, *إيمان*, *أمانة*) rather than synchronic Swahili morphological derivation. Both are borrowed forms. The connection is documented here because speakers and lexicographers consistently treat them as related, but the derivational relationship is historical rather than productive within Swahili.

**Note on derivational asymmetry:** The suffix *-fu* in *mwaminifu* and *uaminifu* is productive in this derivational family but does not appear to generate further forms from *amini* beyond those documented. No causative (*kuaminisha*, "to make someone trust") is attested as a standard productive form, though *kuamini* itself can take objects.
