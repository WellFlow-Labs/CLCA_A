### BASE: **egia** (truth)

#### Construction 1
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia esan
- **Transliteration:** egia esan
- **Translation:** to tell the truth
- **Usage:** Standard collocation for speaking truthfully. Used across all registers from everyday conversation to formal testimony.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 2
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Example:** egia bilatu
- **Transliteration:** egia bilatu
- **Translation:** to seek the truth
- **Usage:** Common in philosophical, investigative, and religious contexts to describe the pursuit of truth.
- **Status:** [CORE]

#### Construction 3
- **Base Form:** egia

- **Transliteration:** egia aurkitu

## SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

### High-frequency construction types identified:

1. **Verb + noun collocations** (e.g., egia esan, gezurra esan, iruzur egin, huts egin)
   - Highly productive pattern for expressing actions related to truth/falsehood concepts

2. **Genitive + noun constructions** (e.g., egiaren bila, egiaren alderdi, egiaren zati)
   - Common for expressing relationships and aspects of truth concepts

3. **Prepositional phrases** (e.g., egiaren kontra, egiaren alde, agerian jarri)
   - Used for expressing stance or position relative to truth concepts

4. **Reduplication for emphasis** (e.g., gezur-gezurra, zuzen-zuzen, berdin-berdin, ziur-ziur)
   - Productive pattern for emphatic intensification

5. **Locative constructions** (e.g., agerian egon, zalantzan jarri, hutsean egin)
   - Common for expressing states and positions

6. **Modal constructions** (e.g., zuzendu behar, frogatu behar)
   - Standard pattern for expressing necessity

### Asymmetries noted:

- **egia** and **gezurra** show rich constructional productivity with many verb collocations
- **zuzen/oker** show parallel constructions (zuzen ibili/oker ibili, zuzen egin/oker egin)
- Some bases (fidagarria, sinesgarria, benetakoa) show minimal multi-word construction patterns
- Abstract quality nouns (-tasuna forms) show limited constructional productivity beyond instrumental case marking
- Participial forms (egiazkotua) function primarily as modifiers without productive multi-word patterns
