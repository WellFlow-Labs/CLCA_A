# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1 and P2. The primary evidence for each grouping is: (a) which items appear together in the same constructions or discourse contexts, (b) which items are explicitly contrasted with each other in attested usage, (c) which items share derivational behavior or appear in parallel morphological slots, and (d) which contexts (legal, moral, testimonial, philosophical, everyday) draw on which items. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Items that do not fit cleanly into any grouping are listed separately at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that something is the case, based on the speaker's own direct access

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker commits to the truth or falsity of a proposition on the basis of their own direct experience, perception, or knowledge. They appear in testimonial, legal, and everyday assertive contexts where the speaker is staking a personal claim. The defining feature of this group is that the speaker's own epistemic position is foregrounded — not the quality of the thing being evaluated, but the speaker's relationship to it. The direct evidential clitic *-mi/-m* is the grammatical anchor of this group and appears on or alongside all other members when they are used in this way.

**Representative expressions:**

- **-mi / -m** (direct evidential clitic): The core grammatical marker of this group; attaches to the focused constituent to signal that the speaker asserts from direct evidence.
- **cheqam** (cheqa + -m): "It is true (I assert from direct knowledge)." Used in testimonial and legal contexts to commit personally to the truth of a proposition.
- **llullam** (llulla + -m): "It is false (I assert from direct knowledge)." Used in confrontational and testimonial contexts to personally deny the truth of something.
- **sut'im** (sut'i + -m): "It is clear (I assert from direct knowledge)." Used to personally affirm the unambiguous character of something.
- **hinam** (hina + -m): "It is indeed so." Used to affirm that a state of affairs is as described, from direct knowledge.
- **cheqaqpaqmi**: Used as a discourse-level emphatic affirmation of truth from direct knowledge.
- **Cheqam kay** ("This is true"): Minimal predicative construction anchored by direct evidential.
- **Llullam kay** ("This is false"): Direct evidential denial.
- **Cheqa kasqanta yachani** ("I know that it is true"): Nominalized complement construction asserting direct propositional knowledge.
- **Llulla kasqanta yachani** ("I know that it is false"): Parallel construction for direct knowledge of falsity.
- **Riqsinim chay runata** ("I know/recognize that person"): Direct evidential assertion of acquaintance.
- **Cheqaqmi kay runa** ("This person is genuinely truthful"): Direct evidential moral evaluation.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Cheqapuni* and *llullapuni* (with the emphatic suffix *-puni*) are closely related to this group in that they assert certainty, but they do not grammatically require the direct evidential and can appear in contexts where the basis of certainty is not specified. They are noted under Group 5 below.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to relay or characterize information whose source is someone other than the speaker

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker is reporting what they have heard, been told, or received from another source, rather than asserting from their own direct experience. They appear in narrative, testimonial, and conversational contexts where the speaker explicitly marks that they are not the original source of the information. The reportative evidential clitic *-si/-s* is the grammatical anchor of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **-si / -s** (reportative evidential clitic): The core grammatical marker; attaches to the focused constituent to signal that the speaker's information comes from another source.
- **cheqas** (cheqa + -s): "It is apparently true / it is said to be true." Used when relaying a truth claim from another source.
- **llullas** (llulla + -s): "It is reportedly false / they say it is a lie." Used when relaying a characterization of falsity from another source.
- **Cheqa kasqanta willaway** ("Tell me about its being true"): A construction requesting information about truth, relevant to contexts where information is being relayed or sought from another party.
- **Llullakuqtam riqsini** ("I know the liar"): While this uses the direct evidential, it appears in contexts where the speaker is characterizing someone whose deception has been reported or observed, and it is included here as a borderline case.

**Note on the contrast with Group 1:** The contrast between *cheqam* and *cheqas*, and between *llullam* and *llullas*, is explicitly grammaticalized in Quechua and is one of the most clearly attested usage-based distinctions in the data. The two groups are defined by this contrast. The cross-construction pattern documented in P2b (evidential contrast in truth-evaluation discourse) directly illustrates how speakers use these two groups in opposition.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Iñisqa* ("believed, trusted") describes something accepted as true on the basis of another's testimony or general credibility, placing it at the boundary between this group and Group 4 (expressions used to evaluate credibility). It is noted under Group 4.

## Group 3: Expressions used to mark that a claim is inferred, guessed, or uncertain

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker signals that they are not asserting from direct knowledge or report, but rather inferring, guessing, or conjecturing. They appear in contexts of uncertainty, tentative affirmation, or hedged assertion. The conjectural clitic *-chá* is the grammatical anchor of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **-chá** (conjectural/inferential evidential clitic): The core grammatical marker; attaches to the focused constituent to signal inference or conjecture.
- **cheqacha** (cheqa + -chá): "It is probably true / I suppose it is correct." Used when a speaker tentatively affirms the truth of something without direct knowledge.

**Note on size:** This group is small in terms of attested lexical items because the conjectural function is primarily carried by the grammatical clitic *-chá* rather than by a set of lexical expressions. The clitic can attach to any predicate, so the group's range is grammatically broad but lexically thin in the P1/P2 data.

**Relationship to Groups 1 and 2:** Groups 1, 2, and 3 together constitute the evidential system documented in P1 items 28–30. They are separated here because they represent distinct usage contexts rather than a single unified group, and because the lexical items that combine with each clitic (cheqam, cheqas, cheqacha; llullam, llullas) show different distributional patterns.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate the credibility or reliability of a person or statement

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize whether a person, statement, or account can be trusted or relied upon as a source of accurate information. They appear in testimonial, legal, and interpersonal contexts where the question is not simply whether something is true, but whether the source is trustworthy. The contrast between *iñiy* (to believe/trust) and *yachay* (to know) is central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **iñiy** ("to believe, to trust, to have faith in"): Used to describe the act of trusting a person or accepting a statement as credible, without necessarily having direct knowledge of its truth.
- **iñisqa** ("believed, trusted, accepted as true"): Used to characterize a person or statement as having been accepted as credible. Appears in evaluations of witness reliability.
- **iñiq** ("one who believes / believer"): Agentive nominalization characterizing a person by their disposition to trust or believe.
- **Iñisqam chay runa** ("That person is trusted"): Predicative construction evaluating a person's credibility.
- **Mana iñisqachu chay simi** ("That statement is not believed"): Negated construction denying credibility to a statement.
- **Iñinichu chay simita** ("I do not believe that statement"): First-person denial of trust in a statement.
- **Iñinim, mana yachanichu** ("I believe it, but I do not know it"): The contrast construction explicitly distinguishing belief from knowledge, which is the defining usage-based distinction of this group.
- **cheqa iñiy** ("true faith / genuine belief"): Used in religious and philosophical discourse to distinguish genuine from false credence.
- **llulla iñiy** ("false belief / false faith"): The counterpart characterizing mistaken or false credence.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Iñiy* overlaps with Group 1 in that both concern epistemic commitment, but *iñiy* is specifically used in contexts where the speaker or subject does not have direct access — it is the term used precisely when direct knowledge (*yachay*) is absent. The construction *Iñinim, mana yachanichu* makes this contrast explicit. *Iñisqa* also overlaps with Group 2 in that trusted information often comes from reported sources.

---

## Group 5: Expressions used to characterize a person, statement, or object as genuinely what it claims or appears to be

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether something or someone is authentic, genuine, or correctly identified — not merely whether a proposition is true, but whether an entity is what it is said or appears to be. They appear in contexts of authentication, identification, and moral characterization of persons. The root *cheqa* in its adjectival and compound uses is central to this group, as is *riqsiy* (to recognize/identify).

**Representative expressions:**

- **cheqa** ("true, real, genuine, correct"): Used attributively and predicatively to characterize entities as genuine or authentic, not only propositions as true.
- **cheqaq** ("truthful, genuine"): Used in moral and testimonial contexts to characterize a person as genuinely honest.
- **cheqa runa** ("genuine person / honest person"): Compound characterizing a person as authentically trustworthy.
- **cheqa sunqu** ("true heart / sincere"): Compound characterizing a person's inner disposition as genuine, not merely outward behavior.
- **cheqapuni** ("absolutely true / certainly genuine"): Emphatic form used to assert unquestionable genuineness.
- **cheqa cheqa** (reduplication: "truly true / very genuine"): Intensified form used in emphatic assertions of authenticity.
- **riqsiy** ("to know, to recognize, to identify"): Used in contexts of identifying whether something or someone is genuine or correctly identified.
- **reqsisqa** ("recognized, known, identified"): Participial form used to describe something authenticated through recognition.
- **Cheqa kasqanta riqsini** ("I recognize it as genuine"): Construction combining recognition with the truth/genuineness predicate.
- **llulla runa** ("lying/false person"): The counterpart compound characterizing a person as inauthentic or dishonest.
- **llulla sunqu** ("false heart / insincere"): Counterpart characterizing inner inauthenticity.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Cheqa* and *cheqaq* also appear in Group 1 (in evidential constructions) and in Group 6 (in moral instruction). The distinction is that in Group 5, the evaluative target is an entity (person, object, inner state) rather than a proposition or speech act. *Cheqapuni* and *cheqa cheqa* are placed here rather than in Group 1 because their primary function appears to be intensifying the genuineness predicate rather than marking the speaker's epistemic source.

## Group 6: Expressions used in directing or evaluating speech for accuracy and honesty

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in contexts where speech — testimony, statement, report — is being directed, evaluated, or characterized for its accuracy and honesty. They are concentrated in legal, testimonial, and moral instructional contexts. The collocations with *rimay* ("to speak") are the defining feature of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **cheqata rimay** ("speak truly / speak correctly"): Fixed collocation used in moral exhortation and legal instruction.
- **cheqanta rimay** ("speak the truth"): Variant with the nominalized *cheqan* as object.
- **cheqa rimay** ("truthful speech / to speak truthfully"): Used as both nominal and verbal collocation.
- **cheqa simi** ("true word / true statement"): Compound used to characterize truthful speech or testimony.
- **llulla simi** ("false word / false statement"): Counterpart compound characterizing false or deceptive speech.
- **llulla rimay** ("false speech / to speak falsely"): Counterpart collocation.
- **sut'ita rimay** ("speak clearly / speak explicitly"): Collocation directing clear and unambiguous speech.
- **sut'i simi** ("clear statement / explicit speech"): Compound characterizing unambiguous speech.
- **Cheqa simiwan rimay** ("Speak with true words"): Instrumental construction directing truthful speech.
- **Llulla simiwan ama rimaychu** ("Do not speak with false words"): Prohibitive construction against false testimony.
- **Mana pantaspa rimay** ("Speak without erring"): Converbal construction directing accurate speech.
- **Cheqa nispa rimay** ("Speak saying it is true"): Quotative converbal construction directing affirmation of truth in speech.
- **Sut'ita rimaway** ("Speak to me clearly"): Directive requesting explicit speech from an interlocutor.
- **Siminta cheqanchay** ("Verify his/her word"): Construction directing verification of testimony.
- **Cheqa siminta mañay** ("Ask for his/her true word"): Construction requesting honest testimony.

**Note on this group:** This group is distinguished from Group 5 (authenticity of persons/entities) and Group 1 (speaker's epistemic commitment) by its focus on the speech act itself as the object of evaluation. The expressions here are used to direct, request, or characterize speech as a performance, not to evaluate the speaker's inner character or the speaker's epistemic access.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Cheqa simi* and *llulla simi* also appear in Group 5 (as characterizations of a type of speech-entity) and could be placed there. They are included here because their most frequent attested use is in the context of directing or evaluating testimony.

## Group 7: Expressions used to describe the act of correcting or rectifying an error

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a mistake, error, or false state of affairs is being corrected, set right, or rectified. They appear in legal, administrative, moral, and everyday contexts where the focus is on the process of moving from an incorrect to a correct state. The root *cheqanchay* and the root *allin* in its causative forms are central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **cheqanchay** ("to verify, to confirm, to correct, to rectify"): The primary verb for establishing or confirming the truth of something, or correcting an error.
- **cheqanchasqa** ("verified, confirmed, corrected"): Participial form describing the result of correction or verification.
- **cheqanchariy** ("to begin to set right / to go about correcting"): Inceptive form describing the initiation of correction.
- **allinchay** ("to improve, to make good, to correct"): Causative form of *allin* used for making something correct or good.
- **allinchaykuy** ("to correct, to fix, to make right"): Reflexive-middle form used for correction, particularly of one's own errors.
- **Pantasqaykita cheqanchay** ("Correct your error"): Construction directing self-correction.
- **Rurasqanta cheqanchay** ("Verify what he/she did"): Construction directing verification of an action.
- **Pantasqayta yachani** ("I know that I was wrong"): Construction expressing acknowledgment of one's own error as a precondition for correction.
- **Cheqanchayta atinichu** ("I cannot verify it"): Construction expressing inability to correct or confirm.
- **Sut'ichay** ("to clarify, to make explicit"): Included here because it appears in contexts of correcting misunderstanding, though it is also central to Group 8.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Sut'ichay* belongs both here and in Group 8 (expressions for making something clear). *Cheqanchay* overlaps with Group 1 in that verification involves epistemic commitment, but its primary usage is procedural (the act of checking or correcting) rather than assertive (the act of claiming).

---

## Group 8: Expressions used to describe making something clear, explicit, or unambiguous

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the focus is on the quality of clarity, precision, or explicitness in a statement, representation, or identification — not on whether something is true in a correspondence sense, but on whether it is stated or presented without ambiguity. The root *sut'i* is the anchor of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **sut'i** ("clear, plain, explicit, exact, precise"): Base adjective evaluating the clarity and precision of statements or representations.
- **sut'ichay** ("to clarify, to make explicit, to specify"): Primary verbal derivation used in explanation, legal testimony, and correction of misunderstanding.
- **sut'ichasqa** ("clarified, made explicit, specified"): Participial form describing the result of clarification.
- **sut'ichakuy** ("to clarify for oneself"): Reflexive-middle form for self-clarification.
- **sut'ita** ("clearly, plainly, explicitly"): Adverbial form modifying verbs of speaking or showing.
- **sut'im** ("it is clear"): Direct evidential assertion of clarity.
- **sut'ipuni** ("absolutely clear"): Emphatic form asserting unambiguous clarity.
- **sut'i simi** ("clear statement / explicit speech"): Compound characterizing unambiguous speech.
- **sut'i sutiy** ("exact name / proper name"): Compound used in contexts requiring precise identification.
- **Sut'ichay munani** ("I want to clarify it"): Volitional construction expressing intention to clarify.
- **Mana sut'ichu** ("It is not clear"): Negated construction evaluating something as ambiguous or unclear.

**Note on the relationship between Group 8 and Group 6:** Group 6 concerns the honesty and accuracy of speech; Group 8 concerns the clarity and explicitness of speech. These overlap in legal and testimonial contexts, where both *cheqata rimay* and *sut'ita rimay* may be used, but they are distinct in that *sut'i* can be used in contexts where honesty is not at issue (e.g., *sut'i yupay*, "exact number"; *sut'i sutiy*, "exact name") while *cheqa* in speech contexts always carries an implication of truthfulness.

---

## Group 9: Expressions used to describe error, being wrong, or being misled

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize a person, statement, or action as incorrect, mistaken, or in error, and to describe the process of causing or falling into error. They appear in moral, legal, practical, and epistemic evaluative contexts. The root *pantay* is the anchor of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- **pantay** ("to err, to be mistaken, to be wrong"): Primary verb for being in error.
- **pantasqa** ("mistaken, wrong, in error"): Participial/adjectival form characterizing a person or statement as incorrect.
- **pantachiy** ("to mislead, to cause to err"): Causative form describing deliberate or inadvertent misleading.
- **pantakuy** ("to make a mistake, to err"): Reflexive-middle form for the act of making an error.
- **Pantanichu** ("I am not wrong"): Negated self-defense construction.
- **Pantasqam chay rimay** ("That statement is mistaken"): Predicative construction evaluating a statement as incorrect.
- **Mana pantasqachu** ("It is not mistaken"): Negated construction defending correctness.
- **Mana pantaspa rimay** ("Speak without erring"): Converbal construction directing accurate speech.
- **mana allin** ("not good / not right / wrong"): Negated *allin* used as a general evaluative expression for wrongness or impropriety.
- **Mana allim chay rurasqa** ("That is not done correctly"): Predicative construction evaluating an action as wrong.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Mana allin* is placed here because its primary evaluative function in the data is to characterize something as wrong or improper, but it is a general negation of *allin* and does not specifically encode error in the epistemic sense that *pantasqa* does. *Pantachiy* ("to mislead") overlaps with Group 10 (deception) in that deliberate misleading is a form of deception, but it is placed here because its primary usage is in the domain of causing error rather than intentional false representation.

---

## Group 10: Expressions used to describe deliberate false representation or deception

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the focus is on intentional or culpable misrepresentation — lying, deceiving, falsifying, or feigning. They appear in moral, legal, and narrative contexts where the agent's intent to deceive is foregrounded. The root *llulla* in its verbal and compound forms is the anchor of this group, distinguished from Group 9 (error) by the implication of intentionality.

**Representative expressions:**

- **llulla** ("false, lying, deceptive, fake"): Base adjective for falsehood and deception.
- **llullakuy** ("to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods"): Standard verb for the act of lying.
- **llullakuq** ("liar, deceiver"): Agentive nominalization characterizing a habitual deceiver.
- **llullakusqa** ("having lied, having deceived"): Participial form for completed deception.
- **llullachay** ("to falsify, to make false, to fake"): Causative form for deliberate falsification.
- **llullachakuy** ("to engage in deception"): Reflexive-middle form for the act of deceiving.
- **llulla tukuy** ("to pretend, to feign, to play false"): Compound with *tukuy* ("to become/pretend") describing the performance of false appearance.
- **cheqa tukuy** ("to pretend to be genuine"): Compound describing the performance of false genuineness — placed here because its meaning is deceptive performance, despite the *cheqa* root.
- **Llullakuyta saqiy** ("Stop lying"): Directive construction to cease deception.
- **Llullakuqtam riqsini** ("I know the liar"): Construction asserting direct knowledge of a deceiver.
- **Llulla kasqanta yachani** ("I know that it is false"): Construction asserting knowledge of deliberate falsity.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Llulla* as a base adjective also appears in Group 5 (in *llulla runa*, *llulla sunqu*) where the focus is on the character of a person rather than the act of deception. The distinction is between characterizing a person as false (Group 5) and describing the act of deceiving (Group 10). *Cheqa tukuy* is placed here despite its *cheqa* root because its meaning is specifically about feigning genuineness, which is a form of deception.

---

## Group 11: Expressions used in moral characterization of persons across multiple domains

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate a person's character as a whole — their honesty, integrity, sincerity, or their opposites — rather than to evaluate a specific statement, action, or epistemic state. They appear in moral discourse, narrative characterization, and religious texts. They typically combine a truth/falsity root with a term for a person or inner state.

**Representative expressions:**

- **cheqa runa** ("genuine/honest person"): Compound characterizing a person as authentically trustworthy.
- **cheqa sunqu** ("true heart / sincere"): Compound characterizing genuine inner disposition.
- **llulla runa** ("lying/false person"): Counterpart characterizing a dishonest person.
- **llulla sunqu** ("false heart / insincere"): Counterpart characterizing inner inauthenticity.
- **cheqa kawsay** ("true life / authentic existence"): Compound characterizing a genuine way of living.
- **llulla kawsay** ("false life / inauthentic existence"): Counterpart characterizing an inauthentic way of living.
- **cheqa yuyay** ("correct understanding / right thinking"): Compound characterizing sound judgment.
- **llulla yuyay** ("false thought / mistaken understanding"): Counterpart characterizing erroneous thinking.
- **cheqa ñan** ("the right path / the correct way"): Metaphorical compound characterizing the morally correct course.
- **cheqa iñiy** ("true faith / genuine belief"): Used in religious discourse to characterize authentic belief.
- **llulla iñiy** ("false belief / false faith"): Counterpart characterizing mistaken belief.

**Note on this group:** This group is distinguished from Group 5 (authenticity of entities) by its focus on the moral evaluation of persons across multiple life domains (living, thinking, believing, acting) rather than on the authentication of a specific entity or claim. The expressions here tend to appear in extended moral, religious, or philosophical discourse rather than in specific testimonial or legal contexts.

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:** *Cheqa sunqu* and *llulla sunqu* could be placed in Group 5 (authenticity of persons) since they characterize the inner character of a person. They are placed here because their usage in the data is specifically in moral and affective evaluation of character rather than in authentication or identification contexts. *Cheqa kawsay* and *cheqa ñan* are peripheral to this group and are noted below as items that do not fit cleanly.

## Items that do not fit cleanly into any group

**k'uychi** ("rainbow; by extension, deceptive appearance"): The figurative extension to deceptive appearance is of uncertain attestation, and no constructions involving *k'uychi* in the truth-evaluation domain are documented. It cannot be placed in any group on the basis of the available data.

**cheqa kawsay** and **llulla kawsay** ("true/false life"): These appear in philosophical and religious discourse and are included in Group 11, but their evaluative target (a way of living or mode of existence) is sufficiently distinct from the other members of Group 11 that they sit uneasily there. They are noted here as items whose grouping is uncertain.

**cheqa ñan** ("the right path"): This expression combines spatial/directional connotations with moral evaluation in a way that does not map cleanly onto any of the groups above. It appears in moral and religious discourse but its primary image is directional rather than epistemic or characterological.

**cheqa Dios** ("the true God"): This expression uses *cheqa* to distinguish among entities of the same category (deities) in a religious context. It appears in colonial and post-colonial religious literature and does not fit the usage patterns of any of the groups above, which are oriented toward truth-evaluation, authenticity, or moral characterization in more general domains.

**cheqa yachay** ("true knowledge"): This expression appears in philosophical and educational discourse and could be placed in Group 5 (authenticity) or Group 4 (credibility/knowledge), but its evaluative target — a type of knowledge rather than a person, statement, or epistemic state — does not fit cleanly into either.

**sut'i yupay** ("exact number") and **sut'i rikuy** ("to see clearly"): These expressions extend the *sut'i* root into numerical/quantitative and visual domains respectively. They are documented in P1b and P2a but do not fit cleanly into Group 8 (clarity of speech/statement) or any other group, as their contexts of use are sufficiently distinct.

**ruray** and its derivations (*ruraq*, *rurasqa*): These appear in the data primarily in constructions evaluating whether an action was genuinely performed or correctly performed (*cheqata ruray*, *rurasqanta cheqanchay*). They participate in Groups 6 and 7 as supporting elements but do not constitute a natural group of their own in the truth-evaluation domain on the basis of the available data.

**hina** ("like, similar to, as if"): This particle appears in constructions such as *cheqa hinam* and *mana hina kasqanmanta* and is relevant to evaluations of resemblance and correspondence, but its primary function is as a comparison/manner particle rather than a truth-evaluation term. It participates in Groups 1 and 3 as a host for evidential clitics but does not form a natural group of its own.

---

## Note on items belonging to multiple groups

The following items are explicitly noted as belonging to more than one group, based on documented usage patterns:

- **cheqa**: Groups 1, 5, 6, 7, 11 (depending on whether it is used in evidential constructions, authenticity evaluations, speech-direction, correction, or moral characterization)
- **llulla**: Groups 5, 6, 10 (depending on whether it characterizes a person, a speech act, or a deceptive action)
- **sut'ichay**: Groups 7 and 8 (correction of misunderstanding and clarification)
- **cheqa simi / llulla simi**: Groups 6 and 5 (speech evaluation and entity characterization)
- **iñisqa**: Groups 2 and 4 (reported acceptance and credibility evaluation)
- **pantachiy**: Groups 9 and 10 (error and deliberate misleading)
- **cheqa sunqu / llulla sunqu**: Groups 5 and 11 (authenticity of persons and moral characterization)
- **cheqapuni / llullapuni**: Groups 1 and 5 (emphatic assertion and genuineness evaluation)
