# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4b Construction Usage & Constraints
# Language: Indonesian (id)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-copula attribution | *Informasi itu benar.* | Informasi itu benar. | "That information is true/correct." | [CORE] | Default pattern in spoken and informal written registers; no register restriction on truth-domain adjectives. |
| *Adalah* attribution | *Pernyataan ini adalah kebohongan.* | Pernyataan ini adalah kebohongan. | "This statement is a lie." | [CORE] | Formal and written registers; used with nominal predicates; more common in official, journalistic, and academic prose. |
| *Merupakan* attribution | — | — | — | [CORE] | Slightly more formal than *adalah*; attested in P4a notes but no standalone example provided; same distributional context as *adalah*. |

**Alternate constructions:** Zero-copula and *adalah*/*merupakan* forms are in free variation by register; no truth-domain-specific restriction attested.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Tidak* + predicate adjective | *Laporan itu tidak benar.* | Laporan itu tidak benar. | "That report is not true/incorrect." | [CORE] | Standard formula in official denials and journalistic corrections; obligatory for adjective negation; *tidak benar* is a high-frequency fixed phrase. |
| *Bukan* + predicate noun/nominal | *Ini bukan dokumen asli.* | Ini bukan dokumen asli. | "This is not a genuine document." | [CORE] | Obligatory for nominal predicate negation; mixing *tidak* and *bukan* is ungrammatical in standard usage; no register restriction attested. |

**Alternate constructions:** None attested beyond the *tidak*/*bukan* split. The two forms are not interchangeable; distribution is grammatically determined by predicate category.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-nominal adjective without *yang* | *dokumen palsu* | dokumen palsu | "a forged/fake document" | [CORE] | Productive default for noun-modifier compounds; common across registers; preferred for single-adjective modification. |
| Post-nominal adjective with *yang* | *sumber yang terpercaya* | sumber yang terpercaya | "a trusted/reliable source" | [CORE] | Preferred when modifier is participial or the construction is more explicitly predicative; also common across registers. |

**Alternate constructions:** Both patterns are fully productive. The *yang* form is preferred for participial modifiers and longer phrases; the bare post-nominal form is preferred for compact noun-modifier compounds. No truth-domain-specific restriction attested beyond this general distributional pattern.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Menjadi* + truth-domain noun/adjective | *Hal itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan.* | Hal itu akhirnya menjadi kenyataan. | "That eventually became reality." | [CORE] | *Menjadi kenyataan* is a fixed collocation; *menjadi* + truth-domain noun/adjective is productive generally but this specific collocation is the most frequent in this domain. |
| *Terbukti* + truth-domain adjective | *Klaim tersebut terbukti palsu.* | Klaim tersebut terbukti palsu. | "That claim was proven false." | [CORE] | High-frequency in legal and journalistic discourse; expresses that a truth-domain evaluation has been established through evidence or official process; no overt agent required. |

**Alternate constructions:** *Terbukti* constructions overlap partially with the *ter-* stative passive (§8) but are documented separately in P4a on the basis of their resultative-evidential function. No additional alternate forms attested.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Me-...-kan* causative from truth-domain adjective | *Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik.* | Pernyataan itu mengelirukan publik. | "That statement misled/confused the public." | [CORE] | Productive pattern; attested forms include *membenarkan*, *mengelirukan*, *menyesatkan*, *memalsukan*, *mengesahkan*, *menyatakan*; common across registers. |
| *Me-...-kan* causative: confirm/justify | *Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu.* | Tidak ada yang bisa membenarkan tindakan itu. | "Nothing can justify/sanction that action." | [CORE] | *Membenarkan* carries both 'to confirm' and 'to justify/sanction' readings; context-dependent disambiguation; common in formal and legal registers. |

**Restrictions noted:** *Akurat*, *sahih*, *sejati*, and *kredibel* lack attested causative forms in standard usage; the pattern is productive but not universal across truth-domain bases.

**Alternate constructions:** None attested for the causative function beyond *me-...-kan*; periphrastic alternatives not documented in P4a data.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Ke-...-an* nominalization | *Kejujuran adalah nilai yang paling penting.* | Kejujuran adalah nilai yang paling penting. | "Honesty is the most important value." | [CORE] | Highly productive; all major truth-domain adjectives form *ke-...-an* nominalizations; common across registers including formal, written, and spoken. |
| *Ke-...-an* nominalization: validity | *Keabsahan dokumen itu dipertanyakan.* | Keabsahan dokumen itu dipertanyakan. | "The validity/legitimacy of that document was questioned." | [CORE] | Frequent in legal and administrative discourse; *keabsahan* and *kesahihan* are near-synonyms with partially overlapping distribution in this domain. |
| *-Itas* nominalization (loanword bases) | — | — | — | [CORE] | Attested in P4a notes (*validitas*, *kredibilitas*, *otentisitas*) but no standalone example provided; restricted to loanword bases; more common in academic and technical registers. |

**Alternate constructions:** *-Itas* suffix applies only to loanword bases and does not compete with *ke-...-an* on native bases. Both strategies are attested; distribution is determined by base origin.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Pe(N)-* agentive from truth-domain base | *Dia adalah pembohong yang tidak bisa dipercaya.* | Dia adalah pembohong yang tidak bisa dipercaya. | "He is a liar who cannot be trusted." | [CORE] | Productive for bases denoting deception and forgery; *pembohong*, *pendusta*, *pemalsu*, *peniru*, *penyesat* all attested; common across registers. |
| *Pe(N)-* agentive: forger/counterfeiter | *Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi.* | Pemalsu uang itu akhirnya ditangkap polisi. | "The currency counterfeiter was finally arrested by police." | [CORE] | Frequent in legal and journalistic discourse; *pemalsu* is the standard term for forger/counterfeiter in these registers. |
| Periphrastic truth-teller expression | — | — | — | [CORE] | No single derived agentive form for 'truth-teller' is attested; concept expressed periphrastically as *orang yang jujur* or *orang yang berkata benar*; asymmetry with deception-domain agentives is consistent across the data. |

**Restrictions noted:** The asymmetry between productive agentive forms for liars and forgers versus the absence of a lexicalized truth-teller agentive is documented in P4a and is consistent with the attested data. No alternate single-word form for 'truth-teller' is attested.

**Alternate constructions:** *Manipulator* is a loanword formation attested in P4a; it does not follow the *pe(N)-* pattern and is restricted to the specific meaning of manipulation.

## 8. PASSIVE / RESULTATIVE STATE

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Di-...-kan* passive | *Dokumen itu diduga dipalsukan oleh tersangka.* | Dokumen itu diduga dipalsukan oleh tersangka. | "That document is suspected of having been forged by the suspect." | [CORE] | Primary construction in legal and journalistic discourse for acts of forgery, validation, manipulation, and declaration; agent may be expressed with *oleh* or omitted. |
| *Di-...-kan* passive: ratification | *Undang-undang itu telah disahkan oleh DPR.* | Undang-undang itu telah disahkan oleh DPR. | "That law has been ratified by the parliament." | [CORE] | Standard formula in official and legislative discourse; *disahkan* is the fixed term for formal ratification; agent (*oleh* phrase) is typically retained in this register. |
| *Ter-* stative passive | — | — | — | [CORE] | Attested in P4a notes (*terbukti*, *tersesat*, *terpercaya*); expresses resultant state without necessarily implying a specific agent; distinct from *di-* passive in that no agentive *oleh* phrase is typical. |

**Alternate constructions:** *Di-* passive and *ter-* stative passive are documented as distinct patterns in P4a. The *ter-* stative overlaps with the inchoative/resultative construction in §4 (*terbukti*) but is treated as a separate pattern on distributional grounds. No additional alternate forms attested beyond those listed in P4a.

## 9. DISCOURSE-LEVEL FRAMING CONSTRUCTIONS

| Construction Type | Example | Transliteration | Translation | Naturalness | Usage Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Pada kenyataannya* frame | *Pada kenyataannya, hal itu tidak pernah terjadi.* | Pada kenyataannya, hal itu tidak pernah terjadi. | "In reality, that never happened." | [CORE] | Used to correct a false appearance or prior claim; sentence-initial or parenthetical; common across spoken and written registers. |
| *Sebenarnya* frame | *Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju dengan pendapat itu.* | Sebenarnya, saya tidak setuju dengan pendapat itu. | "Actually/In truth, I disagree with that opinion." | [CORE] | High-frequency across all registers; functions as correction of false appearance or introduction of a contrasting claim; the most frequent of the attested framing adverbials. |
| *Pada hakikatnya* frame | — | — | — | [CORE] | Attested in P4a notes; used for essential characterization; more common in formal and written registers than *sebenarnya*. |
| *Pada esensinya* frame | — | — | — | [MARGINAL] | Attested in P4a notes; near-synonym of *pada hakikatnya*; appears to be less frequent and more restricted to formal written registers; no standalone example provided in P4a data. |
| *Jujur saja* frame | — | — | — | [CORE] | Attested in P4a notes; introduces candid admission; common in spoken and informal written registers; distinct rhetorical function from correction-of-appearance frames. |

**Alternate constructions:** All five framing adverbials are attested in P4a. They are not interchangeable: *sebenarnya* and *pada kenyataannya* function primarily as correction frames; *pada hakikatnya* and *pada esensinya* function as essential-characterization frames; *jujur saja* functions as a candid-admission frame. No additional alternate forms attested beyond those listed in P4a.
