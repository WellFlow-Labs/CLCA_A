# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Tamil (ta)

## Lexical Items

### 1
**Form:** உண்மை  
**Transliteration:** uṇmai  
**Literal meaning:** that which exists/is  
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact, genuine

**Usage:** Used broadly for truth in factual, moral, and existential contexts. Can refer to truthfulness of statements ("uṇmaiyai col" = speak the truth) or genuineness of objects and persons. Common in everyday speech and formal discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2
**Form:** பொய்  
**Transliteration:** poy  
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, lie  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth, fake

**Usage:** Primary term for falsehood, lies, and deception. Used for false statements ("poy collu" = tell lies) and fake objects. Contrasts directly with uṇmai in moral and factual contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3
**Form:** மெய்  
**Transliteration:** mey  
**Literal meaning:** body; truth, reality  
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, genuine, real

**Usage:** Literary and formal term for truth and reality, often with philosophical or spiritual connotations. Used in classical Tamil literature and formal registers. Related to physical reality and authenticity.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4
**Form:** நிஜம்  
**Transliteration:** nijam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) reality, truth  
**Typical English translations:** truth, reality, fact

**Usage:** Commonly used in colloquial speech for truth and reality, particularly in questions like "nijamā?" (really? is it true?). Borrowed from Sanskrit but fully integrated into everyday Tamil usage.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5
**Form:** சரி  
**Transliteration:** cari  
**Literal meaning:** correct, right, proper  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, okay, proper

**Usage:** Extremely common term for correctness, rightness, and appropriateness. Used for factual accuracy ("cari illai" = not correct) and moral rightness. Also functions as agreement marker in conversation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6
**Form:** தப்பு  
**Transliteration:** tappu  
**Literal meaning:** mistake, error, wrong  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, mistake, error, fault

**Usage:** Primary term for wrongness, mistakes, and errors in both factual and moral contexts. Used in everyday speech for incorrect answers, moral wrongs, and faults. Direct opposite of cari.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7
**Form:** நேர்மை  
**Transliteration:** nērmai  
**Literal meaning:** straightness, uprightness  
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness

**Usage:** Refers to honesty and moral integrity of persons and conduct. Used to describe honest behavior and truthful character. Common in moral and ethical discourse about personal qualities.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8
**Form:** நியாயம்  
**Transliteration:** niyāyam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) justice, rightness  
**Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, rightness, legitimacy

**Usage:** Used for justice, fairness, and what is right or legitimate. Common in legal, moral, and everyday contexts to evaluate actions and situations. Often appears in phrases like "niyāyam illai" (not fair/just).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9
**Form:** பொருத்தம்  
**Transliteration:** poruttam  
**Literal meaning:** fitness, suitability, matching  
**Typical English translations:** suitability, appropriateness, compatibility, match

**Usage:** Refers to appropriateness, suitability, and proper matching between things. Used to evaluate whether something fits or is appropriate for a context. Common in assessments of compatibility and correctness of fit.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10
**Form:** தவறு  
**Transliteration:** tavaṟu  
**Literal meaning:** mistake, error, deviation  
**Typical English translations:** mistake, error, wrong, fault

**Usage:** Formal term for mistakes, errors, and wrongdoing. Used in both factual contexts (errors in work) and moral contexts (wrongful conduct). Slightly more formal than tappu.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11
**Form:** உண்மையான  
**Transliteration:** uṇmaiyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is true/real (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic

**Usage:** Adjectival form used to modify nouns, indicating truthfulness or genuineness. Common in phrases like "uṇmaiyāṉa viṣayam" (true matter/fact). Productive derivational form from uṇmai.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12
**Form:** போலி  
**Transliteration:** pōli  
**Literal meaning:** fake, counterfeit, imitation  
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, imitation

**Usage:** Refers to fake or counterfeit objects, false representations, and pretense. Used for inauthentic items and deceptive appearances. Common in contexts of fraud and imitation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13
**Form:** அசல்  
**Transliteration:** acal  
**Literal meaning:** (from Arabic/Urdu) original, genuine  
**Typical English translations:** original, genuine, authentic, real

**Usage:** Used to indicate originality and genuineness, particularly of objects and documents. Common in commercial and legal contexts. Often contrasted with pōli (fake).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14
**Form:** நம்பகம்  
**Transliteration:** nampakam  
**Literal meaning:** trustworthiness, reliability  
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility

**Usage:** Refers to reliability and trustworthiness of persons, sources, and information. Used to evaluate credibility in formal and informal contexts. Related to nampikkai (trust/belief).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 15
**Form:** சாத்தியம்  
**Transliteration:** cāttiyam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) possibility, feasibility  
**Typical English translations:** possible, feasible, potential

**Usage:** Used to evaluate possibility and feasibility of events and actions. Common in assessments of what can or cannot happen. Often appears in negative constructions to indicate impossibility.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 16
**Form:** சந்தேகம்  
**Transliteration:** cantēkam  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) doubt, suspicion  
**Typical English translations:** doubt, suspicion, uncertainty

**Usage:** Refers to doubt, suspicion, and uncertainty about truth or facts. Used when questioning reliability or accuracy of information. Common in everyday speech and formal inquiry.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17
**Form:** நம்பிக்கை  
**Transliteration:** nampikkai  
**Literal meaning:** trust, belief, faith  
**Typical English translations:** trust, belief, faith, confidence

**Usage:** Refers to trust, belief, and confidence in persons, statements, or systems. Used in religious, interpersonal, and epistemic contexts. Can indicate both faith and credence.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18
**Form:** ஒப்பு  
**Transliteration:** oppu  
**Literal meaning:** similarity, resemblance, comparison  
**Typical English translations:** similarity, resemblance, comparison, likeness

**Usage:** Used to indicate resemblance and similarity between things. Common in comparative evaluations and assessments of likeness. Often appears in compound forms.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 19
**Form:** பொய்யான  
**Transliteration:** poyyāṉa  
**Literal meaning:** that which is false (adjectival form)  
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, untrue, lying

**Usage:** Adjectival form modifying nouns to indicate falseness or deception. Used for false statements, fake objects, and deceptive appearances. Productive derivation from poy.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 20
**Form:** உறுதி  
**Transliteration:** uṟuti  
**Literal meaning:** firmness, certainty, confirmation  
**Typical English translations:** certainty, confirmation, assurance, firmness

**Usage:** Refers to certainty, confirmation, and assured truth of matters. Used when something is definitively established or confirmed. Common in formal and legal contexts for verification.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21
**Form:** தெளிவு  
**Transliteration:** teḷivu  
**Literal meaning:** clarity, clearness  
**Typical English translations:** clarity, clearness, lucidity, understanding

**Usage:** Refers to clarity and clear understanding of truth or facts. Used when something becomes clear or is clearly understood. Important in epistemic contexts about knowledge and comprehension.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 22
**Form:** குற்றம்  
**Transliteration:** kuṟṟam  
**Literal meaning:** fault, defect, crime  
**Typical English translations:** fault, defect, crime, guilt, blame

**Usage:** Used for faults, defects, crimes, and moral wrongs. Common in legal and moral contexts to indicate wrongdoing or culpability. Can refer to both minor faults and serious crimes.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 23
**Form:** அறம்  
**Transliteration:** aṟam  
**Literal meaning:** virtue, righteousness, dharma  
**Typical English translations:** virtue, righteousness, dharma, morality, duty

**Usage:** Classical Tamil term for virtue, righteousness, and moral duty. Central concept in Tamil ethics and literature. Used in formal and literary contexts for moral rightness and proper conduct.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24
**Form:** நீதி  
**Transliteration:** nīti  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) justice, righteousness  
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, morality, ethics

**Usage:** Refers to justice, moral rightness, and ethical principles. Common in legal, moral, and philosophical discourse. Used to evaluate fairness and righteousness of actions and systems.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 25
**Form:** இயல்பு  
**Transliteration:** iyalpu  
**Literal meaning:** nature, natural state, essence  
**Typical English translations:** nature, natural, essence, inherent quality

**Usage:** Refers to the natural state, essence, or inherent quality of things. Used to indicate what something fundamentally is or its true nature. Common in philosophical and descriptive contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 26
**Form:** மாயை  
**Transliteration:** māyai  
**Literal meaning:** illusion, delusion, magic  
**Typical English translations:** illusion, delusion, deception, maya

**Usage:** Refers to illusion, deception, and false appearances. Used in philosophical contexts (particularly Vedantic) and everyday speech for deceptive appearances. Indicates something that appears real but is not.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 27
**Form:** ஏமாற்று  
**Transliteration:** ēmāṟṟu  
**Literal meaning:** deception, cheating, fraud  
**Typical English translations:** deception, cheating, fraud, trick

**Usage:** Refers to active deception, cheating, and fraudulent behavior. Used when someone deliberately misleads or tricks others. Common in contexts of dishonesty and fraud.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 28
**Form:** சாட்சி  
**Transliteration:** cāṭci  
**Literal meaning:** (from Sanskrit) witness, testimony, evidence  
**Typical English translations:** witness, testimony, evidence, proof

**Usage:** Refers to witness, testimony, and evidence in legal and factual contexts. Used to indicate proof or attestation of truth. Common in legal discourse and verification contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
