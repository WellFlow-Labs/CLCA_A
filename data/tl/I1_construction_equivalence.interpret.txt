## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 VERIFICATION substituted by CAUSATION (factual)

The language does not have a distinct VERIFICATION construction. Instead, the causative construction with *pa-* (Construction 5) serves this function. The form *patunayan* (pa- + tunay + -an) means both "cause to be proven/genuine" and "verify/prove." This represents a systematic substitution where the causative operator extends to cover verification semantics.

**Evidence:**
- *Patunayan mo ang sinabi mo* functions as "Prove/Verify what you said"
- *Pinatotohanan niya ang kanyang salita* means "He/she is proving/verifying his/her word"

**Constraint:** This substitution is lexically restricted. Not all truth-domain roots productively form causatives. *Patunayan* is highly lexicalized and common, but the pattern does not extend freely across the semantic field.

### 2.2 REVELATION missing; no substitution pattern

The language lacks a dedicated construction for REVELATION ("turn out to be true/false," "be revealed as true/false"). This meaning would require circumlocution using:
- Existential constructions (Construction 8): *May katotohanan pala* "There is truth (to it) after all"
- Discourse markers with discovery particles: *Totoo nga* "Indeed true" (Construction 16)
- Separate lexical verbs for discovery/revelation not in the truth-domain construction inventory

**No systematic substitution:** BECOME (Construction 4) does not substitute for REVELATION. *Nagtotoo* means "coming true/being realized" (for predictions, dreams), not "turning out to be true" (for discovered facts).

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION missing; partial circumlocution through NEGATION + VERIFICATION

DISCONFIRMATION ("prove false," "disprove") lacks a dedicated construction. The language would express this through:
- Negation of verification: *Patunayan na hindi totoo* "Prove that (it) is not true"
- Separate lexical verbs outside the construction inventory

This represents a **circumlocution requirement** rather than a systematic substitution.

### 2.4 CAUSATION (character) entirely missing

The language has no construction for "make someone truthful/honest/a liar." The causative construction (Construction 5) applies to factual truth-values (*patunayan* "prove/verify") but does not extend to character attribution.

**Attempted forms are unattested:**
- *✗pa-totoo-in ang tao* (intended: "make the person truthful")
- *✗pa-sinungaling-in* (intended: "make someone a liar")

This represents a **conceptual gap** in the causative system.

### 2.5 NEGATION: Analytic pattern with register variation

NEGATION is consistently analytic, using the particle *hindi* before the predicate (Construction 2). An alternate form exists with the contracted prefix *di-*, which is more colloquial and less emphatic.

**Pattern:**
- Standard: *hindi* + PREDICATE (*hindi totoo*)
- Colloquial: *di-* + PREDICATE (*di-totoo*)

Both are fully productive, with no morphological negation on the truth-domain terms themselves.

### 2.6 Asymmetry in nominalization productivity

Abstract nominalization (Construction 6) is highly productive with the *ka-...-an* circumfix, generating forms freely across truth-domain adjectives.

Agentive nominalization (Construction 7) shows severe productivity restrictions:
- Most forms are lexicalized (*sinungaling* "liar")
- The *mang-* pattern exists but applies to limited roots (*mandaraya* "cheater")
- No general productive pattern for creating agent nouns in this domain

This represents an **asymmetric productivity gap** between abstract and agentive nominalization.

### 2.7 Specialized construction restricted to single lexeme

Construction 17 (Cessation/Sufficiency: *tama na*) represents a semantic extension specific to *tama* alone. This pattern does not extend to other truth-domain terms:
- *tama na* "enough/stop" ✓
- *✗totoo na* (does not mean "enough")
- *✗mali na* (does not mean "stop")

This is a **lexically idiosyncratic pattern** rather than a productive construction type.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

**REVELATION:** No construction exists for "turn out to be true/false" or "be revealed as X." The language lacks a dedicated operator for discovered truth-values. Circumlocution required using existential constructions, discourse markers, or separate lexical verbs.

**CAUSATION (character):** No construction exists for "make someone truthful/honest" or "make someone a liar." The causative *pa-* construction does not extend to character attribution, only to factual verification.

**DISCONFIRMATION:** No construction exists for "prove false" or "disprove." Must be expressed through negation + verification (*patunayan na hindi totoo*) or separate lexical items.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

None identified at the construction level. Individual lexical items may be missing, but all major construction types have lexical instantiations.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

**CAUSATION (character):** The causative system appears to block application to character/disposition. The language can express "prove something true" (*patunayan*) but not "make someone truthful." This suggests a conceptual restriction on causative operations in the truth domain.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Nominalization productivity:**
- Abstract nominalization: Fully productive (*ka-...-an*)
- Agentive nominalization: Severely restricted (mostly lexicalized forms)

**Causative productivity:**
- Factual causation/verification: Available but lexically restricted (*patunayan* common, other roots less productive)
- Character causation: Entirely missing

**Positive/Negative symmetry:**
- No gaps identified. Negation applies uniformly across all constructions.

### 3.5 Productivity Restrictions

**Construction 5 (Causative):** While the *pa-* + voice pattern is grammatically productive, its application to truth-domain terms is lexically restricted. *Patunayan* is highly lexicalized and common; other roots do not form causatives as freely.

**Construction 7 (Agentive Nominalization):** The *mang-* pattern exists but applies to very few truth-domain roots. Most agent nouns are lexicalized without transparent derivational morphology.

**Construction 17 (Cessation/Sufficiency):** Restricted to *tama* only; does not generalize.

## 5. Additional Observations

### 5.1 Discourse and Pragmatic Constructions

Several constructions (8, 9, 10, 13, 16) serve primarily discourse-organizing or pragmatic functions rather than core semantic operations:
- **Existential constructions** (8): Assert presence/absence of truth/evidence
- **Evidential/epistemic constructions** (9): Frame statements with evidential basis
- **Discourse markers** (10): Manage conversational flow (*sa totoo lang*, *totóhanán*)
- **Concessive constructions** (13): Acknowledge truth while introducing contrast
- **Emphatic affirmation** (16): Confirm previously doubted information

These are fully productive and core to Tagalog discourse structure but do not map directly to the canonical CLCA operators, which focus on semantic operations (attribution, modification, causation, etc.).

### 5.2 Modification and Intensification

Constructions 11 and 12 (Comparative and Intensification) represent degree modification rather than distinct semantic operators. They apply productively to truth-domain terms but function as general modification strategies rather than truth-domain-specific constructions.

### 5.3 Interrogative and Conditional

Constructions 14 and 15 (Conditional and Interrogative) are general syntactic frames that apply to truth-domain predicates without modification. They do not represent truth-domain-specific operators.

### 5.4 Specialized Constructions

Construction 17 (Cessation/Sufficiency with *tama na*) and Construction 18 (Pronominal Attribution) represent specialized patterns:
- **Construction 17** is lexically idiosyncratic (restricted to *tama*)
- **Construction 18** is a subtype of ATTR with pronominal topics, extremely common in conversational agreement/disagreement
