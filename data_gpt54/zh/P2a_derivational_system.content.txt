Below are attested derivationally related forms associated with the P1 lexical items. I include only forms that are common or strongly supported in actual usage. Where I do not identify a common derivation, I state so explicitly.

### 1. Base Form: 真
1. **Derived Form:** 真正  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “real” + 正 “proper; actual”  
   **Natural English translation:** genuine; real; truly  
   **Usage description:** Very common in speech and writing for distinguishing what is actual or genuine from what is only apparent or nominal. It can function adjectivally or adverbially, as in 真正的问题 “the real problem.”  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **Derived Form:** 真相  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 相 “appearance; aspect”  
   **Natural English translation:** truth; the real facts  
   **Usage description:** Common for the actual facts behind an event, rumor, or dispute. Frequent in journalism, legal discussion, and public commentary.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **Derived Form:** 真伪  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnwěi  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 伪 “false”  
   **Natural English translation:** authenticity; true or false  
   **Usage description:** Used especially in appraisal, law, and commerce for determining whether something is genuine. Often appears in phrases like 辨别真伪.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **Derived Form:** 真假  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 假 “false”  
   **Natural English translation:** true and false; authenticity  
   **Usage description:** Common in everyday speech for asking or judging whether something is real or fake. Slightly more colloquial than 真伪.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **Derived Form:** 真品  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnpǐn  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “genuine” + 品 “item; product”  
   **Natural English translation:** genuine article; authentic item  
   **Usage description:** Common in commerce and collecting for authentic goods, artworks, or branded items. It contrasts naturally with 假货 or 仿品.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **Derived Form:** 真迹  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnjì  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “authentic” + 迹 “trace; writing”  
   **Natural English translation:** authentic work; original handwriting  
   **Usage description:** Used especially for calligraphy, painting, and manuscripts to indicate an original work by the actual creator. Common in art history and appraisal.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **Derived Form:** 真话  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnhuà  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 话 “speech”  
   **Natural English translation:** truth; truthful words  
   **Usage description:** Very common in everyday speech for words that correspond to fact or honest belief. Often contrasted with 假话.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **Derived Form:** 真知  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnzhī  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 知 “knowledge”  
   **Natural English translation:** true knowledge  
   **Usage description:** Used in reflective, philosophical, and educational contexts for genuine understanding rather than superficial opinion. It is less colloquial but well established.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **Derived Form:** 真知灼见  
   **Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真知 “true knowledge” + 灼见 “penetrating view”  
   **Natural English translation:** true knowledge and penetrating insight  
   **Usage description:** Fixed formal expression used to praise insightful analysis or judgment. Common in essays, speeches, and evaluations of scholarship or policy.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **Derived Form:** 真名实姓  
    **Transliteration:** zhēn míng shí xìng  
    **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “real” + 名 “name” + 实 “actual” + 姓 “surname”  
    **Natural English translation:** real name and surname; true identity  
    **Usage description:** Used for actual personal identity as opposed to pseudonym or alias. Common in administration, online discourse, and policing contexts.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **Derived Form:** 真本事  
    **Transliteration:** zhēn běnshi  
    **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “real” + 本事 “ability”  
    **Natural English translation:** real ability  
    **Usage description:** Common colloquial expression for genuine competence rather than bluff or appearance. Often used in praise or challenge.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **Derived Form:** 真功夫  
    **Transliteration:** zhēn gōngfu  
    **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “real” + 功夫 “skill; effort”  
    **Natural English translation:** real skill; genuine mastery  
    **Usage description:** Used for actual expertise acquired through practice. Common in performance, craftsmanship, sports, and figurative evaluation.  
    **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

   **Usage description:** Used in legal, administrative, and policing contexts for fake credentials or false documentary proof. The exact referent depends on context.  
   **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **Derived Form:** 真假  
   **Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ  
   **Literal Breakdown:** 真 “true” + 假 “false”  
   **Natural English translation:** true and false; authenticity  
   **Usage description:** Common in everyday speech for distinguishing real from fake. It is a standard paired form involving 假.

## Brief note
Many Chinese items here derive most naturally through highly attested suffix-like formatives such as **-性**, **-度**, **-者**, **-器**, and through conventionalized adverbial **-地** or fixed collocational extensions. For several idiomatic expressions, no common further derivation is identifiable.
