# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

**Note on transliteration:** Finnish uses the Latin alphabet with two additional letters (ä, ö). No transliteration is required; native script forms are given directly.

---

**1. tosi**
- **Form:** tosi
- **Literal meaning:** true, real (adjective/adverb; cognate with Estonian *tõsi*)
- **Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, really
- **Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements ("se on tosi" — "that is true") and to intensify adjectives ("tosi hyvä" — "really good"). Participates in derivation: *totuus* (truth), *todistaa* (to prove/witness). [CORE]

---

**2. totuus**
- **Form:** totuus
- **Literal meaning:** truth (derived from *tosi* + nominalizing suffix *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being true or to a true state of affairs. Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts ("kertoa totuus" — "to tell the truth"). Productive base for compounds such as *totuudenmukainen*. [CORE]

---

**3. oikea**
- **Form:** oikea
- **Literal meaning:** right, correct (adjective; also "right" as in direction)
- **Typical English translations:** right, correct, proper, genuine, real
- **Usage:** Applied to statements, actions, objects, and persons to indicate correctness or appropriateness ("oikea vastaus" — "the correct answer"; "oikea henkilö" — "the right person"). Also used to mean "genuine" or "real" in contrast to imitation ("oikea nahka" — "real leather"). [CORE]

---

**4. oikeellisuus**
- **Form:** oikeellisuus
- **Literal meaning:** correctness (derived from *oikea* + *-llisuus*)
- **Typical English translations:** correctness, accuracy, propriety
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being correct or conforming to a standard, used in linguistic, legal, and administrative contexts ("kielellinen oikeellisuus" — "linguistic correctness"). More formal and abstract than *oikea* itself. [CORE]

---

**5. virheellinen**
- **Form:** virheellinen
- **Literal meaning:** erroneous, faulty (from *virhe* "error/mistake" + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** erroneous, incorrect, faulty, flawed
- **Usage:** Describes statements, data, procedures, or objects that contain errors or deviate from what is correct. Commonly used in legal, technical, and administrative language ("virheellinen tieto" — "incorrect information"). [CORE]

---

**6. aito**
- **Form:** aito
- **Literal meaning:** genuine, authentic (adjective)
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, real, original
- **Usage:** Used to distinguish genuine items or persons from imitations or fakes ("aito allekirjoitus" — "genuine signature"; "aito tunne" — "genuine feeling"). Participates in derivation: *aitous* (genuineness, authenticity). [CORE]

---

**7. aitous**
- **Form:** aitous
- **Literal meaning:** genuineness, authenticity (from *aito* + *-us*)
- **Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being genuine or authentic, used in aesthetic, philosophical, and legal contexts. Frequently appears in discussions of art authentication and personal identity. [CORE]

---

**8. väärä**
- **Form:** väärä
- **Literal meaning:** wrong, false, crooked (adjective)
- **Typical English translations:** wrong, false, incorrect, fake, counterfeit
- **Usage:** Applied to statements ("väärä väite" — "false claim"), objects ("väärä seteli" — "counterfeit banknote"), and actions ("väärä teko" — "wrong act"). Covers both epistemic falsity and moral wrongness. [CORE]

---

**9. väärentää**
- **Form:** väärentää
- **Literal meaning:** to falsify, to forge (verb; from *väärä* + causative *-ntää*)
- **Typical English translations:** to forge, to falsify, to counterfeit, to adulterate
- **Usage:** Refers to the act of producing a false or forged version of a document, signature, currency, or other item. Central to legal and criminal contexts. Derived noun: *väärentäminen* (forgery, falsification). [CORE]

---

**10. väärentäminen**
- **Form:** väärentäminen
- **Literal meaning:** forging, falsifying (verbal noun from *väärentää*)
- **Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting
- **Usage:** The nominalized form of *väärentää*, used in legal texts and criminal proceedings to name the act of forgery or falsification. Appears in compound legal terms. [CORE]

---

**11. epätosi**
- **Form:** epätosi
- **Literal meaning:** untrue, false (from negating prefix *epä-* + *tosi*)
- **Typical English translations:** untrue, false
- **Usage:** Directly negates *tosi*; used to characterize statements or propositions as not true. More explicitly logical in register than *väärä*, though the two overlap. [CORE]

---

**12. todenmukainen**
- **Form:** todenmukainen
- **Literal meaning:** truth-like, verisimilar (from genitive of *tosi* + *mukainen* "conforming to")
- **Typical English translations:** truthful, verisimilar, realistic, plausible, accurate
- **Usage:** Describes representations, accounts, or depictions that conform to or resemble reality ("todenmukainen kuvaus" — "a realistic/accurate description"). Used in aesthetic and journalistic contexts. [CORE]

---

**13. luotettava**
- **Form:** luotettava
- **Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (from *luottaa* "to trust" + passive participial *-ttava*)
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible
- **Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, information, and instruments to indicate that they can be trusted or depended upon ("luotettava lähde" — "a reliable source"). Participates in derivation: *luotettavuus* (reliability). [CORE]

---

**14. luotettavuus**
- **Form:** luotettavuus
- **Literal meaning:** reliability, trustworthiness (from *luotettava* + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of being reliable or trustworthy, used in evaluations of sources, witnesses, instruments, and institutions. Common in scientific, legal, and journalistic discourse. [CORE]

---

**15. epäluotettava**
- **Form:** epäluotettava
- **Literal meaning:** unreliable, untrustworthy (from *epä-* + *luotettava*)
- **Typical English translations:** unreliable, untrustworthy, undependable
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *luotettava*, used to characterize sources, persons, or data that cannot be trusted. Appears in evaluative contexts across legal, journalistic, and everyday registers. [CORE]

---

**16. pätevä**
- **Form:** pätevä
- **Literal meaning:** valid, competent, qualified (adjective; from *päteä* "to be valid/apply")
- **Typical English translations:** valid, competent, qualified, sound
- **Usage:** Used of arguments, legal instruments, and persons; an argument is *pätevä* if it is sound or valid, a person is *pätevä* if they are qualified or competent ("pätevä todiste" — "valid evidence"; "pätevä hakija" — "qualified applicant"). [CORE]

---

**17. pätemätön**
- **Form:** pätemätön
- **Literal meaning:** invalid, incompetent (from *päteä* + negative *-mätön*)
- **Typical English translations:** invalid, incompetent, unqualified, void
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *pätevä*; used in legal contexts to declare instruments or arguments void or invalid, and of persons to indicate lack of qualification. [CORE]

---

**18. todistaa**
- **Form:** todistaa
- **Literal meaning:** to prove, to testify, to witness (verb; from *tosi* + *-staa*)
- **Typical English translations:** to prove, to demonstrate, to testify, to certify, to witness
- **Usage:** Covers both the act of proving a claim and the act of giving testimony or certifying a document. Central to legal and epistemic discourse ("todistaa syyllisyys" — "to prove guilt"; "todistaa asiakirja" — "to certify a document"). [CORE]

---

**19. todiste**
- **Form:** todiste
- **Literal meaning:** proof, evidence (derived from *todistaa*)
- **Typical English translations:** evidence, proof, exhibit
- **Usage:** Refers to a piece of evidence or proof, particularly in legal proceedings ("esittää todiste" — "to present evidence"). Distinct from *todistus* (certificate, testimony) in that it refers to an evidentiary item rather than a document or act. [CORE]

---

**20. todistus**
- **Form:** todistus
- **Literal meaning:** certificate, testimony, proof (verbal noun from *todistaa*)
- **Typical English translations:** certificate, testimony, proof, attestation
- **Usage:** Refers to a formal document certifying something (e.g., a school certificate, medical certificate) or to an act of testimony. Widely used in administrative and legal contexts. [CORE]

---

**21. vilpillinen**
- **Form:** vilpillinen
- **Literal meaning:** fraudulent, dishonest, deceitful (from *vilppi* "fraud, deceit" + *-llinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** fraudulent, dishonest, deceitful, in bad faith
- **Usage:** Describes conduct, intent, or persons characterized by deception or fraud ("vilpillinen menettely" — "fraudulent conduct"). Used in legal and moral evaluations; contrasted with *vilpitön* (sincere, in good faith). [CORE]

---

**22. vilpitön**
- **Form:** vilpitön
- **Literal meaning:** sincere, in good faith, guileless (from *vilppi* + negative *-tön*)
- **Typical English translations:** sincere, honest, in good faith, guileless
- **Usage:** Describes persons or conduct free from deception or ulterior motive ("vilpitön tarkoitus" — "sincere intention"; "vilpitön mieli" — "good faith"). Important in both moral and legal evaluations of intent. [CORE]

---

**23. rehellinen**
- **Form:** rehellinen
- **Literal meaning:** honest (adjective; from *rehellis-*, etymology debated, possibly from Swedish *redlig*)
- **Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, straightforward
- **Usage:** Describes persons and conduct characterized by honesty and truthfulness. Used across everyday, moral, and legal contexts ("rehellinen vastaus" — "an honest answer"). Derivation: *rehellisyys* (honesty). [CORE]

---

**24. rehellisyys**
- **Form:** rehellisyys
- **Literal meaning:** honesty (from *rehellinen* + *-yys*)
- **Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, truthfulness
- **Usage:** Refers to the property or virtue of being honest. Used in moral, professional, and legal evaluations of character and conduct. [CORE]

---

**25. epärehellinen**
- **Form:** epärehellinen
- **Literal meaning:** dishonest (from *epä-* + *rehellinen*)
- **Typical English translations:** dishonest, untruthful
- **Usage:** The negated counterpart of *rehellinen*; used to characterize persons or conduct as dishonest. Common in moral and everyday evaluative contexts. [CORE]

---

**26. oikeudenmukaisuus**
- **Form:** oikeudenmukaisuus
- **Literal meaning:** justice, fairness (from genitive of *oikeus* "right/law" + *mukainen* "conforming to" + *-uus*)
- **Typical English translations:** justice, fairness, righteousness
- **Usage:** Refers to the property of conforming to principles of justice or fairness, used in moral, legal, and political discourse. Evaluates conduct, institutions, and outcomes. [CORE]

---

**27. totuudenmukainen**
- **Form:** totuudenmukainen
- **Literal meaning:** conforming to truth (from genitive of *totuus* + *mukainen*)
- **Typical English translations:** truthful, accurate, faithful to the truth
- **Usage:** Describes accounts, representations, or statements that conform to the truth ("totuudenmukainen kertomus" — "a truthful/accurate account"). Used in legal contexts for witness statements and in journalistic contexts for reporting. [CORE]

---

**28. jäljitelmä**
- **Form:** jäljitelmä
- **Literal meaning:** imitation, copy (from *jäljitellä* "to imitate" + *-mä*)
- **Typical English translations:** imitation, copy, replica, fake
- **Usage:** Refers to an object or representation that imitates or copies an original, without necessarily implying fraudulent intent. Used in aesthetic and commercial contexts to distinguish copies from originals. [CORE]

---

**29. vääristely**
- **Form:** vääristely
- **Literal meaning:** distortion, misrepresentation (from *vääristää* "to distort" + *-ely*)
- **Typical English translations:** distortion, misrepresentation, falsification, twisting
- **Usage:** Refers to the act or practice of distorting facts, statements, or representations ("tosiasioiden vääristely" — "distortion of facts"). Used in journalistic, legal, and political evaluative contexts. [CORE]

---

**30. harhaanjohtava**
- **Form:** harhaanjohtava
- **Literal meaning:** misleading (from *harhaan* "astray" + *johtava* "leading"; present participle of *johtaa harhaan*)
- **Typical English translations:** misleading, deceptive, fallacious
- **Usage:** Describes statements, representations, or conduct that lead others to false conclusions, even if not outright false ("harhaanjohtava mainos" — "a misleading advertisement"). Used in legal, commercial, and epistemic evaluations. [CORE]
