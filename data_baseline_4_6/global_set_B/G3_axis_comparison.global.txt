# G3 — Emergent Cross-Language Semantic Dimensions

---

## 1. Methodology

The dimensions below are inferred from patterns of lexical grouping, co-occurrence, and contrast visible in the F1–F3 tables and the G1–G2 summaries. Each dimension is defined by what the data actually shows: which terms cluster together, which terms are kept apart, and where languages differ in how they draw boundaries. No dimension is proposed unless it is supported by evidence from at least two languages.

---

## 2. Proposed Dimensions

---

### Dimension A: Propositional Correspondence

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether a statement, claim, or belief corresponds to actual states of affairs from terms used for other purposes. The primary contrast is between what is the case and what is asserted to be the case. Terms in this dimension are applied to propositions, reports, and assertions rather than to objects, persons, or actions.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 真实 (true/real of statements), 事实 (fact), 符合事实 (conform to facts), 真相 (the true state of affairs)
- he: אמת (truth, factual), נכון (correct, factual), שקר (falsehood), מוכח (proven)
- sa: satya (truth, factual), asatya (false), tathya (factual, corresponding to fact), yathārtha (accurate, corresponding to reality)
- el: αλήθεια (truth), ψέμα (lie), αληθινός when applied to statements, ψευδής (false)
- en: true, false, accurate, correct (of statements), erroneous
- tl: totoo (true, factual), kasinungalingan (lie, falsehood), hindi totoo (not true)
- sw: kweli (truth), uongo (lie/falsehood), sahihi (correct, accurate)
- qu: chiqan (true), llulla (false), chiqanta rimay (to speak the truth)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. This is the most consistently attested dimension in the corpus. In every language, there is a primary binary opposition (truth/falsehood) applied to propositional content. The terms in this dimension are the most productive sources of derivation and compounding in every language.

---

### Dimension B: Material Authenticity

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, artifact, or credential is genuine (original, not imitated or forged) from terms used for other purposes. The contrast is between authentic and counterfeit, original and copy, real and fake as applied to things rather than to propositions.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 真品 (genuine article), 真迹 (authentic work), 真丝 (real silk), 真皮 (genuine leather), 正版 (authorized edition), 正品 (genuine product), 假 (fake, of objects), 虚假 (fraudulent)
- he: אמיתי (genuine, authentic), אותנטי (authentic), מזויף (forged, fake), תעודה מזויפת (forged document), כסף מזויף (counterfeit money)
- sa: (no dedicated specialized terms; satya/asatya applied to objects, but no commercial authenticity vocabulary equivalent to zh 真品)
- el: γνήσιος (genuine, authentic), αυθεντικός (authentic), πλαστός (forged, counterfeit), κίβδηλος (adulterated, counterfeit), νόθος (spurious, illegitimate)
- en: genuine, authentic, fake, counterfeit, bogus, spurious, bona fide
- tl: tunay (genuine, authentic), peke (fake, counterfeit), huwad (false, counterfeit)
- sw: halisi (genuine, authentic), bandia (fake, artificial, counterfeit)
- qu: chiqan kaq (the real/genuine one), llulla kaq (the false/fake one); no dedicated specialized vocabulary

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in zh, he, el, en, tl, sw. Weakly lexicalized in sa and qu, where the primary truth/falsity terms are extended to cover this domain without dedicated specialized vocabulary. The degree of lexical separation from Dimension A varies: Tagalog has the clearest split (totoo for propositional truth, tunay for material authenticity); Chinese, Hebrew, Greek, English, and Swahili have specialized terms for material authenticity that are distinct from the primary truth terms; Sanskrit and Quechua use the primary truth terms for both domains.

---

### Dimension C: Procedural Correctness

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether an answer, calculation, procedure, or action conforms to a standard, rule, or expected norm from terms used to evaluate propositional truth or material authenticity. The contrast is between correct and incorrect as applied to performances, solutions, and procedures rather than to statements about the world.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 对/错 (correct/wrong, of answers), 正确 (correct, of answers and procedures), 改正/纠正 (to correct an error), 准确 (accurate, precise)
- he: נכון (correct, right), לא נכון (not correct, wrong), יותר נכון (more correct), תיקן (corrected)
- sa: samyak (right, correct, complete), sādhu (good, proper, correct), sādhu uktam (well said), sādhu kṛtam (well done)
- el: σωστός/λάθος (correct/wrong), ορθός (correct, straight), διορθώνω (to correct), ορθότητα (correctness)
- en: correct, incorrect, right (of answers), wrong (of answers), accurate, error, mistake
- tl: tama/mali (correct/wrong), wasto (correct, proper), tumpak (accurate, precise)
- sw: sahihi (correct, accurate), makosa (mistakes, errors), -potoka (to err, to deviate)
- qu: allin (good, correct), mana allin (not good, wrong), pantay (to err), pantasqa (mistaken)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in zh, he, el, en, tl, sw. In Chinese, the 对/错 pair is the primary vehicle for this dimension, clearly distinct from 真/假 (Dimension A). In Hebrew, נכון covers both Dimension A and Dimension C with some overlap. In Quechua, allin/mana allin covers Dimension C but also covers moral goodness (Dimension E), without a dedicated correctness/error pair. In Sanskrit, samyak and sādhu cover correctness but overlap substantially with moral rightness. The dimension is present in all languages but the degree of lexical separation from Dimensions A and E varies.

---

### Dimension D: Personal Honesty and Sincerity

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether a person habitually tells the truth, acts without deception, or represents themselves sincerely from terms used to evaluate the truth-value of specific statements or the authenticity of objects. The contrast is between a disposition or character trait (being honest, being a liar) and a property of propositions or objects.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 诚 (sincere), 诚实 (honest, truthful), 真诚 (genuine, sincere), 真心 (sincere, wholehearted), 老实 (honest, frank), 正直 (upright, honest), 欺骗 (to deceive), 谎言 (lie, falsehood)
- he: כנה (honest, sincere), יושר (honesty, integrity), שקרן (liar), שקרנות (mendacity), להיות כנה (to be honest)
- sa: satyavādin (truth-speaking), satyavrata (committed to truth), mithyāvādin (liar), mithyābhāṣaṇa (false speech), satyaṃ vada (speak truth, imperative)
- el: ειλικρινής (sincere, honest), ειλικρίνεια (sincerity), υποκριτής (hypocrite), υποκρισία (hypocrisy), ψεύτης (liar)
- en: honest, dishonest, truthful, untruthful, sincere, hypocrite, liar
- tl: tapat (honest, sincere, faithful), katapatan (honesty, loyalty), sinungaling (liar), magsinungaling (to lie)
- sw: mwongo (liar), udanganyifu (deception, deceitfulness), mtu wa kweli (genuine/truthful person), adili (just, fair)
- qu: chiqan runa (truthful person), llulla runa (lying person), llullakuq (liar), chiqan sunqu (true heart)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. In every language, there are terms that evaluate persons as honest or dishonest, truthful or lying, as a character trait rather than as a property of specific statements. The asymmetry noted in G1 and G2 — "liar" is lexicalized in all languages while "truth-teller" is not — is a consistent feature of this dimension. In Hebrew, the א-מ-נ root merges this dimension with Dimension F (reliability/trustworthiness) in a way not found in other languages.

---

### Dimension E: Moral Rightness

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether actions, conduct, or situations are morally right, just, or proper from terms used for factual truth, material authenticity, or procedural correctness. The contrast is between what is morally good or just and what is factually true or procedurally correct.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 正义 (justice, righteousness), 正当 (legitimate, proper, justified), 公正 (fair, impartial), 正气 (righteousness, moral courage), 合理 (reasonable, rational)
- he: צדק (justice, righteousness), צדיק (righteous person), צדקה (charity, righteousness), מוצדק (justified, warranted), לעשות צדק (to do justice)
- sa: dharma (duty, righteousness, proper conduct), adharma (unrighteousness, wrong conduct), sādhu (good, virtuous, proper), dhārmika (righteous, religious)
- el: δίκαιος (just, fair, righteous), δικαιοσύνη (justice, fairness), άδικος (unjust, unfair), αδικία (injustice)
- en: right (moral sense), wrong (moral sense), righteous, righteousness, just, unjust, legitimate, wrongful
- tl: dapat (should, ought, proper), dapat lang (as it should be), tama (correct, right, proper — overlapping with Dimension C)
- sw: haki (right, justice, truth, entitlement), adili (just, fair, equitable), uadilifu (justice, fairness), hukumu (judgment, verdict)
- qu: allin (good, correct — overlapping with Dimension C), allin kawsay (good life, proper living), cheqan ñan (right path, proper course)

**Distribution.** Present in all eight languages, but the degree of lexical separation from Dimension C (procedural correctness) and Dimension A (propositional correspondence) varies considerably. Hebrew has the most developed dedicated vocabulary for this dimension (the צ-ד-ק root family). Chinese has a substantial dedicated vocabulary (正义, 公正, 正当). Sanskrit has dharma as a central term. Greek has the δίκαιος family. English has right/wrong as polysemous terms covering both Dimensions C and E. Tagalog's tama covers both Dimensions C and E without a dedicated moral rightness term. Quechua's allin covers both Dimensions C and E. Swahili's haki covers both moral rightness and legal entitlement. The merger of Dimensions C and E under a single term is attested in en (right/wrong), tl (tama), qu (allin), and sw (haki), while zh, he, sa, and el maintain clearer lexical separation.

---

### Dimension F: Reliability and Trustworthiness

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether persons, sources, or information can be consistently depended upon from terms used for other purposes. The contrast is between what can be trusted or relied upon (based on track record, consistency, or character) and what is merely true on a given occasion.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 可靠 (reliable, dependable), 可靠性 (reliability), 确实 (indeed, reliable), 踏实 (steady, dependable)
- he: אמין (reliable, trustworthy), נאמן (faithful, loyal, reliable), מהימן (reliable, credible), אמינות (reliability, credibility), נאמנות (faithfulness, loyalty)
- sa: (no dedicated reliability grouping distinct from truthfulness; the closest is āptavākya "testimony of a reliable person" within the pramāṇa framework)
- el: βέβαιος (certain, sure, reliable), αμφίβολος (doubtful, dubious), βεβαιότητα (certainty), αμφιβολία (doubt)
- en: reliable, unreliable, trustworthy, untrustworthy, dependable, sound (of sources)
- tl: (no dedicated reliability grouping; katunayan covers evidence/proof; tapat covers honesty/faithfulness)
- sw: madhubuti (reliable, accurate, precise, firm), mwenye ukweli (truthful person), ushahidi madhubuti (reliable evidence)
- qu: (no dedicated reliability grouping; chiqan covers both truth and reliability without a dedicated split)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in zh, he, el, en. Weakly attested or merged with other dimensions in sa, tl, sw, qu. The most notable feature of this dimension is the Hebrew א-מ-נ root, which merges Dimension F (reliability/trustworthiness) with Dimension A (propositional truth) and Dimension D (personal honesty) and Dimension G (belief/faith) into a single root family — a merger not found in any other language. In Chinese, 可靠 is clearly distinct from 真 (truth) and 诚 (honesty). In Greek, βέβαιος covers both certainty (Dimension H) and reliability. In English, reliable/trustworthy are clearly distinct from true/honest.

---

### Dimension G: Belief and Epistemic Acceptance

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to describe the mental act of accepting something as true, or the disposition to accept claims, from terms used to evaluate whether those claims actually are true. The contrast is between believing (a mental state) and being true (a property of propositions).

**Representative terms:**
- zh: (no dedicated belief vocabulary in the truth domain; 相信 "to believe" exists but is not in the F1 tables)
- he: אמונה (faith, belief, trust), להאמין (to believe, to trust), מאמין (believer), אמונה עיוורת (blind faith), אמונה תמימה (simple faith)
- sa: śraddhā (faith — not in F1 tables but implied by contrast with pramāṇa), āgama/śabda (verbal testimony as epistemic source)
- el: (no dedicated belief vocabulary in the truth domain; πίστη exists but is not in the F1 tables)
- en: believe, belief, trust (partially), faith (partially)
- tl: paniniwala sa katotohanan (belief in truth — attested in F1 as a collocation)
- sw: (no dedicated belief vocabulary in the truth domain)
- qu: yuyay (to think, to remember, thought, opinion — distinguished from yachay "to know")

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized only in Hebrew (the א-מ-נ root family). Weakly attested or absent as a dedicated grouping in most other languages. The Quechua distinction between yachay (established knowledge) and yuyay (subjective thought/opinion) is the closest parallel to a belief/knowledge distinction in the corpus, but it is not as elaborated as the Hebrew system. Sanskrit's pramāṇa framework implicitly distinguishes valid knowledge from mere belief, but the belief side is not lexically developed within the truth domain. This dimension is the least consistently attested across languages in the corpus.

---

### Dimension H: Epistemic Certainty

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate the degree of confidence or certainty with which something is known or asserted from terms used to evaluate the truth-value of propositions. The contrast is between how certain one is (an epistemic stance) and whether something is true (a property of propositions).

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 确实 (indeed, really, true — certainty), 确凿 (conclusive, irrefutable), 无疑 (undoubted — implied by 确实)
- he: ודאי (certain, sure, definite), בוודאי (certainly, surely), לא ודאי (not certain, uncertain), ברור (clear, obvious)
- sa: (certainty is expressed through the pramāṇa framework rather than dedicated certainty terms; hakika is not Sanskrit)
- el: βέβαιος (certain, sure, reliable), βεβαιότητα (certainty), αμφίβολος (doubtful, dubious), αμφιβολία (doubt)
- en: certain, uncertain, sure, doubtful, definite, indefinite
- tl: (no dedicated certainty grouping; totoo nga and totoong-totoo function as emphatic affirmations)
- sw: hakika (certain, sure, definite), uhakika (certainty), kwa hakika (certainly), bila ya hakika (without certainty)
- qu: -mi (direct knowledge evidential), -si (reportative evidential), -cha (conjectural evidential) — grammatically obligatory

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in he, el, sw. Present but less developed in zh and en. Grammatically encoded (rather than lexically) in qu through the obligatory evidential system. Absent as a dedicated grouping in sa and tl. The Quechua evidential system is the most distinctive feature of this dimension: while other languages express certainty through optional lexical items, Quechua encodes the epistemic source of every main-clause assertion through obligatory suffixes. This is a structural difference, not merely a lexical one.

---

### Dimension I: Validity and Logical Soundness

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether arguments, reasoning, or formal procedures meet standards of logical or legal validity from terms used for factual truth, material authenticity, or moral rightness. The contrast is between what follows from premises correctly (logical validity) or what has legal force (legal validity) and what is factually true.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 合理 (reasonable, rational), 合理性 (rationality, reasonableness), 正当 (legitimate, justified — overlapping with Dimension E)
- he: תקף (valid, in force), טיעון תקף (valid argument), זה לא תקף (this is not valid), מוצדק (justified, warranted)
- sa: pramāṇa (valid means of knowledge), pramāṇatva (validity, status of being valid), pramāṇābhāsa (semblance of valid knowledge, pseudo-proof)
- el: έγκυρος (valid, legitimate), άκυρος (invalid, null, void), εγκυρότητα (validity), ακυρώνω (to invalidate)
- en: valid, invalid, sound, unsound, fallacious, fallacy, valid argument
- tl: (no dedicated validity grouping; may katunayan/walang katunayan covers evidence/proof)
- sw: (no dedicated validity grouping; hoja madhubuti covers reliable argument/evidence)
- qu: chanin (valuable, worthy, legitimate, valid), mana chanin (invalid, illegitimate)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in he, el, en. Present but less developed in zh, sa, qu. Absent as a dedicated grouping in tl and sw. Sanskrit has the most elaborated vocabulary for epistemic validity (the pramāṇa system), but this is organized around valid means of knowledge rather than logical validity of arguments. Hebrew תקף and Greek έγκυρος/άκυρος are the closest parallels to the English valid/invalid pair. The dimension is present in most languages but is most clearly lexicalized in the European languages in the corpus (he, el, en).

### Dimension J: Appearance vs. Reality

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether something is what it appears to be, or whether appearances correspond to underlying reality, from terms used for propositional truth or material authenticity. The contrast is between surface appearance and deeper reality, or between how things seem and how they are.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 表里如一 (same inside and out), 名副其实 (worthy of the name), 名不副实 (unworthy of the name), 华而不实 (flashy but lacking substance), 虚有其表 (impressive in appearance only)
- he: מציאותי (realistic, real), לא מציאותי (unrealistic), אמיתי (genuine, real — when contrasting with appearance)
- sa: mithyā (false, illusory — metaphysical), ābhāsa (appearance, semblance, reflection), vyavahāra (conventional reality), paramārtha (ultimate reality), mithyāropa (false superimposition)
- el: αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude, appearance of truth), αληθοφανής (truth-like, plausible), εικονικός (virtual, notional, apparent), πραγματικός (real, actual)
- en: real vs. apparent, real vs. virtual, surreal, verisimilitude (marginal), so-called
- tl: palabás (pretense, show, facade), kunwari (pretend, feign, as if), tunay na kulay (true colors, real character)
- sw: uhalisia (authenticity, genuineness, reality), kwa uhalisia (in reality, actually), uhalisia wa mambo (reality of things)
- qu: rikch'ay (to resemble, to appear, to seem), rikch'akuy (to seem, to appear), chiqanman rikch'ay (to resemble truth), llullaman rikch'ay (to appear false)

**Distribution.** Present in all eight languages but with very different degrees of elaboration. Sanskrit has the most developed vocabulary for this dimension, with dedicated philosophical terms (mithyā, ābhāsa, vyavahāra/paramārtha) and the unique two-truths framework. Chinese handles this dimension primarily through fixed expressions (chengyu) rather than dedicated single terms. Greek has αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude) as a dedicated term. English has the real/virtual and real/surreal pairs. Tagalog has palabás and kunwari as dedicated terms for pretense. Swahili handles this through uhalisia and related forms. Quechua handles it through appearance verbs (rikch'ay, rikch'akuy). The dimension is present everywhere but is most lexically elaborated in Sanskrit and least in Quechua.

---

### Dimension K: Completeness and Substantiality

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate whether something is complete, whole, or has genuine substance from terms used for propositional truth or material authenticity. The contrast is between what is full, complete, and substantial and what is empty, hollow, or lacking essential content.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 实 (solid, real, actual, substantial), 虚 (empty, void, false, weak), 充实 (substantial, rich, fulfilling), 扎实 (solid, sturdy, sound), 华而不实 (flashy but lacking substance), 实实在在 (genuinely, down-to-earth)
- he: (no dedicated substantiality grouping; ουσιαστικός is Greek, not Hebrew)
- sa: sattva (being, existence, essence, goodness), sattā (existence, being, reality), sat (being, existing, real, good)
- el: ουσιαστικός (substantial, essential, real), ουσία (substance, essence, matter), ουσιαστικά (substantially, essentially, basically)
- en: substantial, insubstantial, real (in the sense of "real help"), genuine (in the sense of "genuine effort")
- tl: (no dedicated substantiality grouping; hunt'a is Quechua, not Tagalog)
- sw: kamili (complete, perfect, whole, entire), ukamili (completeness, perfection), safi (clean, pure — extended to substantiality)
- qu: hunt'a (full, complete, whole), ch'usaq (empty, hollow, vain), ch'ulla (single, odd, incomplete)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in zh (the 实/虚 opposition is one of the most productive in the corpus), el (ουσιαστικός family), sw (kamili family), and qu (hunt'a/ch'usaq). Present but less developed in sa (sat/asat covers existence/non-existence but not substantiality specifically). Absent as a dedicated grouping in he and tl. The Chinese 实/虚 opposition is the most productive vehicle for this dimension in the corpus, generating numerous compounds and fixed expressions. The dimension overlaps with Dimension A (propositional correspondence) in some contexts — empty claims are also false claims — but the core contrast is between substance and emptiness rather than between truth and falsity.

---

### Dimension L: Deception and Intentional Falsification

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to describe deliberate acts of deceiving, misleading, or creating false impressions from terms used to describe mere error or factual falsity. The contrast is between intentional deception (an action with a deceiving agent) and unintentional error or simple falsity.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 欺骗 (to deceive, to cheat), 谎言 (lie, falsehood), 说谎 (to lie), 撒谎 (to lie, colloquial), 虚假 (false, fraudulent — often deliberate)
- he: לשקר (to lie), שקרן (liar), שקרנות (mendacity), להפיץ שקרים (to spread lies), עד שקר (false witness)
- sa: mithyābhāṣaṇa (false speech, lying), mithyāvāda (false doctrine, lying), mithyāvādin (liar), mithyāsākṣin (false witness)
- el: ψεύδομαι (to lie, to deceive), ψευδολογία (lying, mendacity), ψευδομαρτυρία (false testimony), απατηλός (deceptive, misleading), απάτη (deception, fraud)
- en: lie, liar, deceive, deception, fraud, falsify, forge, counterfeit (verb)
- tl: sinungaling (liar), magsinungaling (to lie), dáyà (cheat, fraud, deceit), linlang (deception, illusion), mandaya (to cheat)
- sw: -danganya (to deceive, to lie, to trick), mdanganyifu (deceiver), udanganyifu (deception, deceitfulness), mwongo (liar), sema uongo (to tell a lie)
- qu: llullakuy (to lie, to deceive), llullachiy (to cause to lie, to deceive), llullakuq (liar)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. This dimension is consistently distinguished from Dimension C (procedural error) in all languages: lying is treated as intentional, while making a mistake is not. The distinction between Dimension L (intentional deception) and Dimension A (propositional falsity) is also maintained in all languages, though the same terms sometimes cover both (zh 假 can mean both "false" and "fake/deceptive"; he שקר covers both "falsehood" and "lie"). Tagalog is notable for splitting this dimension most finely, with separate terms for lying in speech (sinungaling), cheating in transactions (dáyà), and deceptive appearances (linlang).

---

### Dimension M: Precision and Exactness

**Definition.** This dimension distinguishes terms used to evaluate the degree of exactness or precision in measurements, descriptions, or representations from terms used for simple truth/falsity or correctness. The contrast is between approximate and exact, or between roughly correct and precisely correct.

**Representative terms:**
- zh: 准确 (accurate, precise), 准确性 (accuracy, precision), 准确度 (degree of accuracy), 精确 (precise, exact)
- he: מדויק (precise, exact), יותר מדויק (more precise), להיות מדויק (to be precise)
- sa: (no dedicated precision grouping; the closest is the measurement vocabulary from √mā: māna, mātrā, parimāṇa)
- el: ακριβής (accurate, exact, precise), ακρίβεια (accuracy, precision, exactness), ακριβώς (exactly, precisely)
- en: accurate, inaccurate, accuracy, precise, precision, exact, exactly
- tl: tumpak (accurate, precise, exact), eksakto (exact, from Spanish), hustó (exact, just right)
- sw: madhubuti (reliable, accurate, precise, firm), usahihi (correctness, accuracy, authenticity)
- qu: (no dedicated precision grouping; chiqan covers both truth and correctness without a dedicated precision term)

**Distribution.** Clearly lexicalized in zh, he, el, en, tl. Present but less developed in sw (madhubuti covers both reliability and precision). Absent as a dedicated grouping in sa and qu. The dimension is consistently distinguished from Dimension C (procedural correctness) in the languages where it is lexicalized: being precise is a matter of degree, while being correct is binary. The Chinese 准确 family and the Greek ακριβής family are the most developed vehicles for this dimension.

## 4. Assessment of Dimensions

### Strongly Supported Cross-Linguistically

**Dimensions A, C, D, and L** are the most robustly attested across the corpus.

- **Dimension A (Propositional Correspondence)** is present and clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. It is the foundation of the truth-related domain in every language.
- **Dimension C (Procedural Correctness)** is present in all eight languages, though the degree of lexical separation from Dimension A varies.
- **Dimension D (Personal Honesty and Sincerity)** is present and clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, with the consistent asymmetry that "liar" is lexicalized while "truth-teller" is not.
- **Dimension L (Deception and Intentional Falsification)** is present and clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, consistently distinguished from unintentional error.

**Dimension B (Material Authenticity)** and **Dimension E (Moral Rightness)** are also strongly supported, present in all eight languages, though with varying degrees of lexical separation from adjacent dimensions.

### Moderately Supported

**Dimensions F, H, I, J, K, and M** are present in most languages but show more variation in how clearly they are lexicalized and how sharply they are distinguished from adjacent dimensions.

- **Dimension F (Reliability)** is clearly lexicalized in four languages (zh, he, el, en) and weakly attested in the others.
- **Dimension H (Epistemic Certainty)** is clearly lexicalized in three languages (he, el, sw) and grammatically encoded in one (qu); absent in the others.
- **Dimension I (Validity)** is clearly lexicalized in three languages (he, el, en) and weakly present in others.
- **Dimension J (Appearance vs. Reality)** is present in all languages but ranges from a fully elaborated philosophical vocabulary (sa) to a few fixed expressions (zh) to appearance verbs (qu).
- **Dimension K (Completeness and Substantiality)** is clearly lexicalized in four languages (zh, el, sw, qu) and absent in two (he, tl).
- **Dimension M (Precision and Exactness)** is clearly lexicalized in five languages (zh, he, el, en, tl) and absent in two (sa, qu).

### Tentative or Weakly Attested

**Dimension G (Belief and Epistemic Acceptance)** is the weakest dimension in the corpus. It is clearly lexicalized only in Hebrew (the א-מ-נ root family). The Quechua yachay/yuyay distinction provides a partial parallel, but the belief side is not elaborated. In most other languages, belief vocabulary exists (Chinese 相信, Greek πίστη, English believe) but falls outside the truth-domain vocabulary as documented in the F1 tables. This dimension may be present in all languages but is not consistently integrated into the truth-related lexical domain.

### Dimensions That Split, Merge, or Vary Across Languages

**Dimensions C and E (Procedural Correctness and Moral Rightness)** are merged under a single term in at least four languages: English right/wrong, Tagalog tama, Quechua allin/mana allin, and Swahili haki. They are clearly separated in Chinese (对/错 vs. 正义), Hebrew (נכון vs. צדק), Sanskrit (samyak vs. dharma), and Greek (σωστός vs. δίκαιος).

**Dimensions A and B (Propositional Correspondence and Material Authenticity)** are merged under the primary truth terms in Sanskrit and Quechua, clearly separated in Tagalog (totoo vs. tunay), and partially separated in Chinese, Hebrew, Greek, English, and Swahili.

**Dimensions D and F (Personal Honesty and Reliability)** are merged under the א-מ-נ root in Hebrew in a way not found in any other language. In all other languages, honesty (a moral character trait) and reliability (a track-record-based property) are covered by distinct terms.

**Dimension H (Epistemic Certainty)** is lexical in most languages but grammatical in Quechua, where it is encoded through obligatory evidential suffixes rather than optional lexical items. This is a structural difference, not merely a difference in degree of lexicalization.

**Dimension J (Appearance vs. Reality)** shows the greatest cross-linguistic variation in elaboration: from a fully developed philosophical vocabulary with dedicated adverbial forms in Sanskrit, to fixed expressions in Chinese, to appearance verbs in Quechua, to near-absence as a dedicated domain in Hebrew and Tagalog.
