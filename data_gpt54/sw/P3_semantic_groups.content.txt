## 1. Truth assertion and direct contradiction

**Definition:**  
This group covers expressions used to say that something is true, to insist on it, or to deny it directly. In usage, these forms are common in conversation, argument, rebuttal, and explanatory framing, especially around whether a statement matches the facts.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **kweli** — kweli  
- **ni kweli** — ni kweli  
- **kwa kweli** — kwa kweli  
- **kweli kabisa** — kweli kabisa  
- **kweli kweli** — kweli kweli  
- **kweli ni kwamba** — kweli ni kwamba  
- **ukweli** — ukweli  
- **ukweli ni kwamba** — ukweli ni kwamba  
- **ukweli wa mambo** — ukweli wa mambo  
- **ukweli mtupu** — ukweli mtupu  
- **si kweli** — si kweli  
- **kweli au si kweli** — kweli au si kweli  
- **kusema ukweli** — kusema ukweli  
- **sema ukweli** — sema ukweli  

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**  
- **kwa kweli** often works more as an intensifier or discourse marker than as a literal truth claim.  
- **kweli** and **-kweli** also extend into genuineness/authenticity, not only statement truth.

## 2. Lying, false reports, and accusation of falsity

**Definition:**  
This group includes expressions used to label speech, reports, or claims as lies or falsehoods, and to describe the act of lying. In practice, these forms are common in accusation, moral correction, media talk, and public dismissal of reports.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **uongo** — uongo  
- **-ongo** — -ongo  
- **mwongo** — mwongo  
- **kusema uongo** — kusema uongo  
- **sema uongo** — sema uongo  
- **ni uongo** — ni uongo  
- **uongo mtupu** — uongo mtupu  
- **habari za uongo** — habari za uongo  
- **taarifa za uongo** — taarifa za uongo  
- **wa uongo** — wa uongo  
- **ya uongo** — ya uongo  
- **si ya kweli bali ya uongo** — si ya kweli bali ya uongo  

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**  
- **-ongo** is less independent and often appears inside agreeing descriptive phrases.  
- **ni uongo** overlaps with direct contradiction, but is typically stronger and more accusatory than **si kweli**.

## 3. Genuineness, authenticity, and “real” identity or substance

**Definition:**  
This group gathers expressions used for things, persons, names, goods, or situations treated as genuine, real, original, or not merely apparent. In usage, these forms often contrast with imitation, superficiality, false identity, or counterfeit status.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **-kweli** — -kweli  
- **wa kweli** — wa kweli  
- **ya kweli** — ya kweli  
- **cha kweli** — cha kweli  
- **halisi** — halisi  
- **uhalisia** — uhalisia  
- **jina halisi** — jina halisi  
- **tukio halisi** — tukio halisi  
- **fedha halisi** — fedha halisi  
- **asili** — asili  
- **ya asili** — ya asili  
- **kwa asili** — kwa asili  
- **asili ya** — asili ya  
- **dawa ya asili** — dawa ya asili  
- **nywele za asili** — nywele za asili  
- **bidhaa ya asili** — bidhaa ya asili  

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**  
- **-kweli** belongs here when used for genuine persons or things, but also belongs with truth assertion.  
- **uhalisia** can refer either to reality itself or to realism/authenticity in representation.  
- **ya asili** may mean “natural” in some contexts and “authentic/original” in others.

## 4. Counterfeit, fake, and imitation items

**Definition:**  
This group includes expressions used for objects, documents, accounts, or goods judged not genuine. In practice, these are especially common in legal, commercial, health, and digital contexts where authenticity is under question.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **bandia** — bandia  
- **feki** — feki

- **bidhaa feki** — bidhaa feki

## 6. Proper method, placement, timing, and procedure

**Definition:**  
This group includes uses of **sahihi** where the issue is not mainly whether a statement is true, but whether something is done in the proper way, at the proper time, or in the proper place. These expressions are common in instruction, planning, medicine, administration, and practical advice.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **matumizi sahihi** — matumizi sahihi  
- **utaratibu sahihi** — utaratibu sahihi  
- **njia sahihi** — njia sahihi  
- **wakati sahihi** — wakati sahihi  
- **mahali sahihi** — mahali sahihi  

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**  
- These expressions sit close to the correctness group, but in usage they often concern appropriateness or norm-following rather than factual accuracy.  
- **njia sahihi** can also take on moral or strategic overtones depending on context.

## 7. Trustworthiness, credibility, and dependable sources

**Definition:**  
This group covers expressions used to evaluate whether a person, source, witness, institution, report, or evidence can be trusted or believed. In usage, these forms are especially common in journalism, law, public discussion, and institutional evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**

- **asiyeaminika** — asiyeaminika  
- **kutoaminika** — kutoaminika

This group includes expressions used when a speaker presents something as obvious, evident, or plainly supported. In usage, these forms are common in formal argument, commentary, and legal-style presentation.

## 13. Resemblance, likeness, and exemplification

**Definition:**  
This smaller grouping covers expressions used for resemblance, likeness, and giving examples or models. In usage, these items are not central to truth/falsity, but they recur where comparison, representation, or illustration is being made.

**Representative expressions:**  
- **-fanana** — -fanana  
- **kufanana na** — kufanana na  
- **wanafanana** — wanafanana  
- **mfano** — mfano  
- **kwa mfano** — kwa mfano  
- **mfano wa** — mfano wa  

**Ambiguous or overlapping items:**  
- **mfano** often simply means “example,” but in some contexts it also carries “model” or “likeness.”  
- These items do not cluster strongly with the evaluative truth terms, though they are relevant to comparison and representation.

## Items with especially broad or unstable grouping

These do not fit only one grouping cleanly:

- **kweli / -kweli** — truth assertion, emphasis, and genuineness  
- **kwa kweli** — truth-related but often just a discourse marker/intensifier  
- **mkweli** — truth-telling and moral character  
- **uhalisia** — reality, authenticity, or realism depending on context  
- **taarifa sahihi** — accuracy/correctness, but close to reliability  
- **taarifa inayoaminika** — credibility/reliability, but close to factual dependability  
- **batili** — invalidity, nullity, and sometimes falsity  
- **imani** — belief/faith/confidence, often outside narrow truth evaluation

## Items not strongly grouped beyond limited local patterns

A few items are better treated as relatively isolated or weakly connected outside their immediate constructions:

- **uongozi** — listed as historically related but synchronically lexicalized and not part of the main falsity pattern  
- **chanzo aminika** — possible but less fixed than **chanzo kinachoaminika**  
- **kweli ya mambo** — less fixed than **ukweli wa mambo**
