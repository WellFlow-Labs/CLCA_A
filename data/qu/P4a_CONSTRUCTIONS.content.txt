# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Truth/falsity predicates combine with the copula **kay** "to be" to form basic attributive constructions. The predicate appears in bare form (no case marking) before the copula.

### Examples
- **chiqan kay** → chiqan kay → "to be true, to be real"
- **llulla kay** → llulla kay → "to be false, to be a lie"

### Notes
The copula **kay** is obligatory for stative predication. Evidential suffixes attach to the copula or predicate to mark epistemic stance (e.g., **chiqanmi kay** "it is truly so" with direct evidential **-mi**).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negation is formed with the preverbal particle **mana** preceding the predicate. The copula **kay** follows the predicate as in affirmative constructions.

### Examples
- **mana allin kay** → mana allin kay → "to not be good, to be bad"
- **mana chanin kay** → mana chanin kay → "to be invalid, to be illegitimate"

### Notes
**Mana** is the standard negator for declarative sentences. The negative suffix **-chu** may optionally attach to the verb for emphasis or in interrogatives (e.g., **mana allinchu** "isn't it good?").

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms directly precede the noun they modify without intervening morphology. The modifier-noun order is fixed.

### Examples
- **chiqan simi** → chiqan simi → "true word, truthful speech"
- **llulla runa** → llulla runa → "lying person, deceiver"

### Notes
Quechua uses simple juxtaposition for adnominal modification. No agreement marking or linking morphology appears. The entire NP may take case suffixes (e.g., **chiqan simita** "the true word-ACC").

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Inchoative meaning is expressed through the suffix **-ya-** attached to quality terms, creating verbs meaning "to become X" or "to get X-er."

### Examples
- **allinyay** → allinyay → "to become good, to improve, to get better"
- **llullayay** → llullayay → "to lie repeatedly, to become habitually deceitful"

### Notes
The suffix **-ya-** has both inchoative ("become") and intensifying/continuative functions. Context determines which reading is primary. The resulting form takes standard verbal inflection.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Causative meaning is expressed through the suffix **-cha-** (or **-chi-** after certain stems), creating verbs meaning "to make X" or "to cause to be X."

### Examples
- **chiqanchay** → chiqanchay → "to verify, to authenticate, to make true"
- **allinchay** → allinchay → "to make good, to improve, to fix"

### Notes
The causative **-cha-** is highly productive with quality terms. It may combine with the reflexive **-ku-** to form **-cha-ku-** "to make oneself X" (e.g., **chiqanchakuy** "to verify for oneself").

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Abstract nominalizations are formed through the copula **kay** "to be/being" following the quality term, or through the agentive/substantive suffix **-q** → **kaq** "being, that which is."

### Examples
- **chiqan kay** → chiqan kay → "truth, the state of being true"
- **chiqan kaq** → chiqan kaq → "that which is true, the true one, the real thing"

### Notes
The **kay** construction creates process nominals or abstract states. The **kaq** construction creates substantivized forms identifying entities. Both patterns are productive across quality terms.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Agent nouns are formed through the suffix **-q** attached to verbal stems, creating forms meaning "one who X-s" or "X-er."

### Examples
- **llullakuq** → llullakuq → "liar, one who lies, deceiver"
- **chiqan rimaq** → chiqan rimaq → "truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully"

### Notes
The agentive **-q** attaches to verbal stems (including derived verbs like **llullakuy** "to lie"). For habitual characterization, the base verb typically includes reflexive **-ku-** before **-q**.

---

## 8. ACCUSATIVE + VERB CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms in accusative case (**-ta**) combine with verbs of speaking, telling, or doing to form "V the truth/lie" constructions.

### Examples
- **chiqanta rimay** → chiqanta rimay → "to speak the truth, to tell the truth"
- **llullata willakuy** → llullata willakuy → "to tell lies, to give false testimony"

### Notes
This is the most productive multi-word pattern. Common verbs include **rimay** "speak," **willakuy** "tell/narrate," **niy** "say," and **ruray** "do/make." The accusative marks the truth/falsity term as direct object.

---

## 9. LOCATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms with locative suffix **-pi** "in, at" combine with verbs of being or dwelling to indicate being "in" a state of truth/falsity.

### Examples
- **chiqanpi kay** → chiqanpi kay → "to be in truth, to be in reality, to actually be"
- **llullapi tiyay** → llullapi tiyay → "to dwell in lies, to live in falsehood"

### Notes
The locative **-pi** creates spatial metaphors for abstract states. With **kay** "to be," it emphasizes actual state; with **tiyay** "to dwell/sit," it suggests habitual condition.

---

## 10. DIRECTIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms with directional/allative suffix **-man** "to, toward" combine with verbs of motion or resemblance to indicate movement or orientation toward truth/falsity.

### Examples
- **chiqanman kutiy** → chiqanman kutiy → "to return to truth, to come back to reality"
- **llullaman rikch'ay** → llullaman rikch'ay → "to resemble lies, to appear false"

### Notes
The directional **-man** creates goal-oriented constructions. With motion verbs (**kutiy** "return," **hamuy** "come"), it indicates literal movement; with **rikch'ay** "resemble," it indicates resemblance or appearance.

---

## 11. ABLATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms with ablative suffix **-manta** "from, out of" indicate source or manner, often functioning adverbially.

### Examples
- **chiqamanta rimay** → chiqamanta rimay → "to speak from truth, to speak truly"
- **llullamanta** → llullamanta → "from lies, out of deception, falsely"

### Notes
The ablative **-manta** marks source or origin. When combined with speech verbs, it characterizes the basis or manner of speaking. It may function independently as an adverb.

---

## 12. PURPOSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/goodness terms with purposive/benefactive suffix **-paq** "for, for the sake of" indicate purpose or intended benefit.

### Examples
- **chiqanpaq rimay** → chiqanpaq rimay → "to speak for truth, to speak for the sake of truth"
- **allinpaq** → allinpaq → "for good, for the sake of good, beneficially"

### Notes
The purposive **-paq** marks intended goal or beneficiary. It justifies actions or describes beneficial purposes. May function independently as an adverb or combine with verbs.

---

## 13. EVIDENTIAL MARKING

### Pattern
Truth-asserting terms combine with evidential suffixes to mark epistemic stance: **-mi** (direct knowledge), **-si** (reportative), **-cha** (conjectural).

### Examples
- **chiqapmi** → chiqapmi → "it is truly so (I know directly)"
- **chiqapsi** → chiqapsi → "it is truly so (they say), it is reportedly true"

### Notes
Evidentials are obligatory in main clauses and attach to the focused element (often the predicate or adverb). They indicate the speaker's source of information and degree of certainty.

---

## 14. PROHIBITIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Prohibitions are formed with **ama** (negative imperative) + verb + **-chu** (negative suffix), used to forbid lying or error.

### Examples
- **ama llullakychu** → ama llullakychu → "don't lie, do not deceive"
- **ama pantaychu** → ama pantaychu → "don't make a mistake, don't err"

### Notes
The prohibitive requires both **ama** and **-chu**. This construction is common in moral instruction and parental commands. The verb appears in bare stem form.

---

## 15. SUBSTANTIVIZING WITH KAQ

### Pattern
Quality terms combine with the substantivizer **kaq** (< **ka-** "be" + **-q** agentive) to create "that which is X" constructions, identifying entities by their qualities.

### Examples
- **chiqan kaq** → chiqan kaq → "that which is true, the true one, the real thing"
- **llulla kaq** → llulla kaq → "that which is false, the false one, the fake"

### Notes
The **kaq** construction creates definite descriptions identifying specific entities. It contrasts authentic/genuine items with false/counterfeit ones. The entire phrase may take case suffixes.

---

## 16. DISJUNCTIVE QUESTIONS

### Pattern
Alternative questions are formed by repeating the predicate in affirmative and negative forms, each with interrogative **-chu**.

### Examples
- **allinchu mana allinchu** → allinchu mana allinchu → "good or bad, whether good or not"
- **chiqanchu llullachu** → chiqanchu llullachu → "true or false, whether true or false"

### Notes
The interrogative **-chu** attaches to each alternative. This construction is used in deliberations and evaluative questions. The order is typically affirmative-negative.

---

## 17. LIMITATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
The limitative suffix **-lla** "only, just" attaches to truth/quality terms to emphasize exclusivity or purity.

### Examples
- **chiqanlla** → chiqanlla → "only the truth, purely true, nothing but true"
- **allinlla** → allinlla → "just good, simply good, only good"

### Notes
The limitative **-lla** emphasizes that something is exclusively or purely X without admixture. It functions as an intensifier in emphatic assertions.

---

## 18. COMITATIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Truth/falsity terms with comitative/instrumental suffix **-wan** "with, by means of" indicate accompaniment or instrumentality.

### Examples
- **chiqanwan kay** → chiqanwan kay → "to be with truth, to have truth on one's side"
- **llullawan** → llullawan → "with lies, by means of lies, through deception"

### Notes
The comitative **-wan** marks accompaniment or instrument. It describes association with truth/falsity or methods involving truth/deception.

---

## 19. DOUBLE EMPHASIS

### Pattern
Multiple emphatic elements stack to create intensified assertions, combining adverbial forms with emphatic particles.

### Examples
- **chiqap k'ari** → chiqap k'ari → "truly indeed, really and truly"
- **k'arimi** → k'arimi → "truly indeed, it is certainly so" (emphatic + evidential)

### Notes
Quechua allows stacking of emphatic elements (**chiqap** "truly" + **k'ari** "indeed") and combination with evidentials (**k'ari** + **-mi**) for maximum emphasis.

---

## 20. PERCEPTION + TRUTH CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Verbs of perception or recognition take truth/falsity terms as accusative objects to form "perceive/recognize X" constructions.

### Examples
- **chiqanta musyay** → chiqanta musyay → "to perceive the truth, to realize what is true"
- **llullata riqsiy** → llullata riqsiy → "to recognize lies, to identify falsehood"

### Notes
Perception verbs (**musyay** "perceive," **riqsiy** "recognize," **rikuy** "see") combine with truth/falsity terms in accusative to describe epistemic access to truth or detection of falsehood.
