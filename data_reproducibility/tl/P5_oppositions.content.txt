# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## ATTESTED OPPOSITIONS

### OPPOSITION 1
- **Term A:** totoo
- **Term B:** hindi totoo / di-totoo
- **Transliteration:** totoo vs. hindi totoo / di-totoo
- **English Translation:** "true, real" vs. "not true, false, unreal"
- **Description of Contrast:** Direct morphological negation of factual truth using standard negator **hindi** or contracted **di-**. This is the primary opposition for factual correspondence to reality.
- **Contrast Type:** morphological
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Primary Opposition Types Identified:

1. **Morphological Negations (using hindi/di-):** The most productive pattern, applying to nearly all truth-domain terms (totoo, tama, tunay, tapat, wasto, sigurado, etc.). This is the default negation strategy.

2. **Morphological Negations (using walang + nominalization):** Creates stronger, more categorical negation implying complete absence rather than simple denial (walang-katotohanan, walang-katapatan).

3. **Lexical Oppositions:** Several dedicated lexical items exist in opposition:
   - totoo vs. kasinungalingan/bulaán/kathâ
   - tama vs. mali
   - tunay vs. peke/huwad
   - tapat/matapat vs. sinungaling
   - magtapat vs. magsinungaling

4. **Constructional Oppositions:** Many oppositions are realized through contrasting constructions using the same grammatical frame:
   - Question vs. negation (totoo ba vs. hindi totoo)
   - Affirmation vs. denial with same subject (tama ka vs. mali ka)
   - Possession vs. absence (may katotohanan vs. walang katotohanan)
   - Emphatic affirmation vs. negation (totoo nga vs. hindi totoo)

### Asymmetries:

1. **Positive terms show more lexical diversity than negative terms:** There are multiple positive terms for truth/correctness (totoo, tunay, tama, wasto, hustó) but fewer dedicated negative terms (mali, peke, huwad, bulaán). Most negation is morphological rather than lexical.

2. **Some terms lack clear opposites:** Terms like **patunayan** (to prove) and **itama** (to correct) have no attested dedicated lexical opposites, though they can be negated morphologically.

3. **The tama/mali opposition is the most symmetrical:** This pair functions as the clearest antonym pair, with **mali** serving as the direct opposite of **tama** across most contexts.

4. **Honesty domain shows both lexical and morphological oppositions:** The honesty/dishonesty domain has both dedicated lexical items (tapat vs. sinungaling) and productive morphological negation (hindi tapat, walang-katapatan).

5. **No universal "false" term:** Unlike English "false" which can oppose both "true" and "correct," Tagalog distributes this semantic space across multiple terms (hindi totoo, mali, peke, huwad, bulaán) with different usage contexts.
