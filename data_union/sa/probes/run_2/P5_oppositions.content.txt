# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Preliminary Note

Oppositions are extracted only where the CONTEXT explicitly documents a contrastive relationship — through direct statement, paired usage, or constructional contrast. Where the CONTEXT notes a relationship without specifying it as oppositional, this is recorded but not treated as an opposition. Overlapping oppositions are preserved without consolidation.

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** असत्य / *asatya*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *asatya*
4. **English translation:** truth, truthfulness / untruth, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** *Asatya* is the direct prefixal negation of *satya*, formed by *a-* + *satya*; the CONTEXT documents both as attested across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as a moral pair, with *asatya* denoting a false statement or the quality of being untrue. The CONTEXT explicitly identifies this pair in P3 Group 8 as a "contrastive pair used to frame philosophical positions."
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** सत्य / *satya*
2. **Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
3. **Transliteration:** *satya* / *anṛta*
4. **English translation:** truth / falsehood, disorder
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT notes in P1 (item 9) that *anṛta* is "contrasted with *satya* in many texts," and documents the construction *anṛtaṃ na vadet* ("one should not speak falsehood") as parallel in structure to *satyaṃ vada* ("speak the truth"). The contrast is attested in Vedic and early *dharmaśāstra* literature; *anṛta* is the Vedic-register counterpart to *satya* as its negation.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** ऋत / *ṛta*
2. **Term B:** अनृत / *anṛta*
3. **Transliteration:** *ṛta* / *anṛta*
4. **English translation:** cosmic order, right / falsehood, disorder
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents in P1 (item 2) that *ṛta* "denotes the cosmic and moral order" and is "contrasted with *anṛta* (disorder, falsehood)," and in P1 (item 9) that *anṛta* is formed as *an-* + *ṛta* meaning "contrary to cosmic order." This is the foundational Vedic opposition in the truth domain.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** सत् / *sat*
2. **Term B:** असत् / *asat*
3. **Transliteration:** *sat* / *asat*
4. **English translation:** real, existent, true, good / unreal, false, non-existent, bad
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents *asat* in P1 (item 7) as "the negation of *sat*," formed by *a-* + *sat*, and notes that both are used in metaphysical debates about ontological status and in moral contexts. The dvandva compound *sadasat* ("being and non-being") is documented in P2a (item 6) as a paired form. The pair is explicitly listed in P3 Group 8 as a contrastive pair structuring philosophical debate, and the construction *sataḥ na asataḥ jāyate* ("the world arises from being, not from non-being") is documented in P2b as a philosophical formula exploiting this contrast.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** परमार्थ / *paramārtha* (and पारमार्थिक / *pāramārthika*)
2. **Term B:** व्यावहारिक / *vyāvahārika*
3. **Transliteration:** *paramārtha* / *vyāvahārika*
4. **English translation:** ultimate truth, highest reality / conventional, practical, empirical
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents this pair throughout P1 (items 23 and 24), P2a, P2b, and P3, consistently describing *vyāvahārika* as denoting "the level of truth or validity that holds within conventional or practical usage, as opposed to the ultimate (*pāramārthika*) level." The paired construction *paramārthasatyam* / *saṃvṛtisatyam* (or *vyāvahārikasatyam*) is documented in P2b (item 23) as "the two-truths doctrine central to Madhyamaka Buddhism and Advaita Vedānta." The pair is explicitly listed in P3 Group 8.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥprāmāṇya*
2. **Term B:** परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
3. **Transliteration:** *svataḥprāmāṇya* / *parataḥprāmāṇya*
4. **English translation:** intrinsic validity, self-certifying authority / extrinsic validity, externally established authority
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both terms in P1b (root 2 expansion) and describes them as "opposing positions" in Sanskrit epistemological debate: *svataḥprāmāṇya* is "the Mīmāṃsā doctrine that the validity of a cognition is established by the cognition itself," while *parataḥprāmāṇya* is "the Nyāya doctrine that the validity of a cognition must be established by an external means." The pair is explicitly listed in P3 Group 8 as a contrastive pair structuring philosophical debate.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** सत्यं वद / *satyaṃ vada*
2. **Term B:** अनृतं न वदेत् / *anṛtaṃ na vadet*
3. **Transliteration:** *satyaṃ vada* / *anṛtaṃ na vadet*
4. **English translation:** "speak the truth" / "one should not speak falsehood"
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both constructions in P2b (items 1 and 9 respectively) and explicitly notes that *anṛtaṃ na vadet* is "parallel in structure to *satyaṃ vada* but negated." Both are prescriptive formulas in Vedic and *dharmaśāstra* literature; one is a positive injunction, the other a prohibition, covering the same normative domain from opposite directions.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** प्रमाणम् अस्ति / *pramāṇam asti*
2. **Term B:** न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति / *na tatra pramāṇam asti*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramāṇam asti* / *na tatra pramāṇam asti*
4. **English translation:** "there is evidence for it / a valid means of cognition exists" / "there is no valid means of cognition for that / there is no evidence for that"
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both constructions in P2b (item 4) as a standard formula pair in philosophical argumentation, with the negated form described as "used in philosophical argumentation to deny that a claim has epistemic support" and as "a standard formula for rejecting an opponent's position as lacking evidential grounding."
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

1. **Term A:** प्रमा उत्पद्यते / *pramā utpadyate*
2. **Term B:** भ्रमः जायते / *bhramaḥ jāyate*
3. **Transliteration:** *pramā utpadyate* / *bhramaḥ jāyate*
4. **English translation:** "valid cognition arises / is produced" / "error arises / mistaken cognition comes about"
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both constructions in P2b (items 5 and 11) and in P4a and P4b, where they are described as "structurally parallel" and "explicitly contrasted in epistemological texts." Both use the same inchoative construction pattern with the arising cognitive state as grammatical subject; the contrast is between the valid and erroneous outcomes of a cognitive process.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **Transliteration:** *hetvābhāsa* / *hetu*

## Opposition 30

1. **Term A:** सत्यं वद / *satyaṃ vada* (accusative imperative)
2. **Term B:** सत्येन शपामि / *satyena śapāmi* (instrumental oath)
3. **Transliteration:** *satyaṃ vada* / *satyena śapāmi*
4. **English translation:** "speak the truth" (injunction to another) / "I swear by truth" (first-person oath)
5. **Description of contrast:** The CONTEXT documents both constructions in P2b (item 1) and P4a (construction 9), noting that they "occupy different syntactic roles" — the accusative marks truth as "the content of the commanded speech act" while the instrumental marks truth as "the ground of the oath" — but both participate in the same performative pattern. The contrast is between two syntactic strategies within the same pragmatic domain.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
