## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for BECOME
Turkish does not have a dedicated REVELATION construction parallel to BECOME. Instead, the language uses **Construction 12 (Resultative/Outcome)** with the verb *çıkmak* "to emerge, come out":
- *doğru çıkmak* "to turn out to be true"
- *yalan çıkmak* "to turn out to be a lie"

This construction systematically expresses revelation semantics—the outcome of verification or the resolution of prior uncertainty. Unlike BECOME (*gerçekleşmek* "to come true"), which focuses on the process of change, the *çıkmak* construction emphasizes the epistemic outcome: something is revealed to have a particular truth status.

**Pattern**: The language avoids a dedicated inchoative for revelation and instead uses a resultative construction that implies prior uncertainty or claim that is now resolved.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share identical construction
Turkish uses **Construction 13 (Denominative Verbalization)** with the suffix *-la/-le* for both verification and disconfirmation:
- *doğrulamak* "to verify, to confirm" (from *doğru* "true/correct")
- *yalanlamak* "to deny, to refute" (from *yalan* "lie")

There is no structural distinction between these operations. Both are transitive denominative verbs formed from truth-domain nominals. The semantic difference (verification vs. disconfirmation) is lexically encoded in the base, not in the construction itself.

### 2.3 CAUSATION shows asymmetry
**CAUSATION (factual)** is fully productive via the causative suffix *-tır/-tir* on inchoative bases:
- *gerçekleştirmek* "to realize, to make real"
- *kesinleştirmek* "to finalize, to make definite"

However, **CAUSATION (character)** lacks a dedicated construction. To express "make someone truthful/honest" or "make someone a liar," Turkish requires circumlocution:
- Light verb constructions: *birini dürüst yapmak* "to make someone honest" (lit. "to make someone honest")
- Complex clauses: *birinin yalancı olmasına neden olmak* "to cause someone to become a liar"

These are not grammaticalized constructions but compositional phrases. The language shows a structural gap for character causation.

### 2.4 NEGATION shows dual strategy
Turkish employs both **analytic** and **morphological** negation:

**Analytic** (Construction 2): Particle *değil* for predicative negation
- *Bu yanlış değil* "This is not wrong"

**Morphological** (Construction 11): Privative suffix *-sız/-siz* and negative potential *-mez/-maz*
- *şüphesiz* "doubtless, without doubt"
- *güvenilmez* "unreliable"

The privative suffix creates lexicalized negative adjectives that function independently, while *değil* negates predicates syntactically. Some bases productively take privative derivation (*hatasız* "flawless"), while others use only analytic negation or have lexicalized negative forms (*güvenilmez* from *güvenilir* "reliable").

### 2.5 Light verb constructions as semantic carriers
**Construction 8 (Verbal Phrase Constructions)** uses light verbs extensively:
- *yalan söylemek* "to tell a lie" (lit. "lie say-INF")
- *haksızlık etmek* "to commit injustice" (lit. "injustice do-INF")

These function as single semantic units where the light verb (*söylemek* "say," *etmek* "do," *yapmak* "make") carries tense/aspect/agreement while the truth-domain nominal carries lexical meaning. This pattern is highly productive and preferred over simple denominative verbs in many contexts (e.g., *yalan söylemek* is more common than *yalanlamak* for "to lie" in the sense of telling falsehoods).

### 2.6 Manner adverbials show multiple strategies
**Construction 9 (Manner Adverbials)** exhibits three distinct morphological strategies:
- Suffix *-ca/-ce*: *dürüstçe* "honestly"
- Ablative case *-dan/-den* (lexicalized): *gerçekten* "really, truly"
- Instrumental case *-la/-le* (lexicalized): *kesinlikle* "certainly," *yanlışlıkla* "by mistake"

All three are productive to varying degrees, with ablative and instrumental forms showing more lexicalization. The language does not prefer one strategy uniformly; instead, different bases select different suffixes, sometimes with semantic distinctions.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
**CAUSATION (character)**: No grammaticalized construction for expressing "make X become truthful/honest/a liar." The language requires circumlocution with light verbs (*yapmak* "make," *etmek* "do") or complex causal clauses. This is a genuine structural gap—the productive causative suffix *-tır/-tir* applies to inchoative verbs (factual causation) but does not extend to character causation in the truth domain.

**REVELATION (dedicated construction)**: No inchoative or dedicated construction for revelation parallel to BECOME. The language substitutes with the resultative construction (*çıkmak* "to emerge"), which is productive but structurally distinct from inchoative derivation.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps
No systematic lexical gaps were identified. Turkish shows high productivity in deriving truth-domain vocabulary through:
- Inchoative *-leş/-laş*
- Causative *-tır/-tir*
- Abstract nominalization *-lık/-lik*
- Privative *-sız/-siz*
- Denominative *-la/-le*

Individual lexical items may lack certain derived forms (e.g., not all adjectives productively take privative suffixes), but these are item-specific rather than systematic gaps.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps
**Character causation** appears to be a conceptual gap. The language does not grammaticalize the notion of causing someone to acquire or lose truth-related character traits (honesty, dishonesty, trustworthiness). This may reflect a cultural or conceptual distinction between:
- Factual causation (making propositions/states become true/false) — grammaticalized
- Character causation (making persons become truthful/deceptive) — not grammaticalized

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps
**Positive vs. Negative forms**: No systematic asymmetry. Turkish productively derives both positive and negative forms:
- Positive: *doğru* "true," *güvenilir* "reliable"
- Negative: *yanlış* "wrong," *güvenilmez* "unreliable"

**Truth vs. Falsity**: No asymmetry. Both truth and falsity domains show equal productivity:
- Truth: *doğrulamak* "to verify," *doğruluk* "truth"
- Falsity: *yalanlamak* "to deny," *yalan* "lie"

**VERIFICATION vs. DISCONFIRMATION**: No asymmetry. Both use identical denominative construction (*-la/-le*).

## Summary

Turkish exhibits a highly productive morphological system for truth-domain constructions, with 10 of 12 canonical CLCA operators fully available through dedicated grammatical constructions. The language shows:

1. **Complete coverage** of attribution, adnominal modification, becoming, factual causation, verification, disconfirmation, negation (both analytic and morphological), and nominalization (both abstract and agentive).

2. **Functional substitution** for REVELATION via resultative construction (*çıkmak*), which serves the semantic function but is structurally distinct from inchoative BECOME.

3. **Structural gap** for CAUSATION (character), which lacks grammaticalization and requires circumlocution.

4. **No asymmetries** between positive/negative forms, truth/falsity domains, or verification/disconfirmation operations.

5. **High productivity** across all attested constructions, with minimal lexical restrictions and full integration across registers.
