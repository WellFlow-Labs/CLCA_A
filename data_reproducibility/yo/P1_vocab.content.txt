# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## Lexical Items

### 1. òtítọ́
**Form:** òtítọ́  
**Literal meaning:** Noun derived from "tọ́" (to be straight, correct)  
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, honesty

This is the primary noun for truth in Yoruba, used in both everyday and formal contexts. It appears in moral, legal, and religious discourse to refer to factual accuracy and moral honesty.

**[CORE]**

### 2. tọ́
**Form:** tọ́  
**Literal meaning:** to be straight, to be correct, to be right  
**Typical English translations:** to be true, to be correct, to be right

This verb is used to evaluate statements, actions, and situations for correctness or appropriateness. It can describe both factual accuracy and moral rightness.

**[CORE]**

### 3. irọ́
**Form:** irọ́  
**Literal meaning:** Noun derived from "purọ́" (to lie)  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth

This is the standard term for falsehood or lie, used as the direct opposite of òtítọ́. It appears in everyday speech and formal contexts when discussing deception or inaccuracy.

**[CORE]**

### 4. purọ́
**Form:** purọ́  
**Literal meaning:** to lie, to tell falsehood  
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to be false

This verb describes the act of lying or being false. It is commonly used to evaluate statements or claims as untrue.

**[CORE]**

### 5. òdodo
**Form:** òdodo  
**Literal meaning:** Noun related to straightness, uprightness  
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, fairness

This term is used in moral and legal contexts to describe justice and righteous conduct. It emphasizes moral correctness and fairness in dealings.

**[CORE]**

### 6. ṣe déédé
**Form:** ṣe déédé  
**Literal meaning:** "ṣe" (to do) + "déédé" (exactly, precisely)  
**Typical English translations:** to be exact, to be accurate, to be precise

This expression is used when emphasizing exactness or precision in representation or action. It appears in contexts requiring careful accuracy.

**[CORE]**

### 7. gidi
**Form:** gidi  
**Literal meaning:** genuine, real, authentic  
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true

This adjective evaluates objects, persons, or situations for genuineness and authenticity. It distinguishes the real from the fake or imitation.

**[CORE]**

### 8. nitootọ́
**Form:** nitootọ́ / ní tòótọ́  
**Literal meaning:** "ní" (in/with) + "tòótọ́" (truth)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, truthfully, in truth, honestly

This adverbial expression emphasizes the truthfulness of a statement. It is commonly used to assert sincerity or factual accuracy.

**[CORE]**

### 9. ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀
**Form:** ṣe déédé bẹ́ẹ̀  
**Literal meaning:** "ṣe déédé" (to be exact) + "bẹ́ẹ̀" (like that)  
**Typical English translations:** to be exactly so, to be just like that

This expression confirms that something corresponds exactly to a description or expectation. It emphasizes accurate resemblance or correspondence.

**[CORE]**

### 10. jọ
**Form:** jọ  
**Literal meaning:** to resemble, to be similar  
**Typical English translations:** to resemble, to be like, to be similar

This verb describes resemblance or similarity between things. It is used to evaluate how closely something matches another thing.

**[CORE]**

### 11. bá mu
**Form:** bá mu  
**Literal meaning:** "bá" (to meet/match) + "mu" (to hold/grasp)  
**Typical English translations:** to correspond, to match, to agree with

This expression indicates correspondence or agreement between things. It is used when evaluating whether something matches expectations or standards.

**[CORE]**

### 12. ìbámu
**Form:** ìbámu  
**Literal meaning:** Noun from "bá mu" (to correspond)  
**Typical English translations:** correspondence, agreement, conformity

This noun describes the state of correspondence or agreement. It appears in contexts evaluating whether things align or match properly.

**[CORE]**

### 13. ìwà rere
**Form:** ìwà rere  
**Literal meaning:** "ìwà" (character/conduct) + "rere" (good)  
**Typical English translations:** good character, good conduct, virtue

This fixed expression evaluates moral character and conduct. It is widely used in moral discourse to describe proper behavior.

**[CORE]**

### 14. ìwà búburú
**Form:** ìwà búburú  
**Literal meaning:** "ìwà" (character/conduct) + "búburú" (bad)  
**Typical English translations:** bad character, bad conduct, vice

This expression evaluates character or conduct negatively. It is the opposite of ìwà rere and appears in moral evaluations.

**[CORE]**

### 15. yẹ

### 19. dájú
