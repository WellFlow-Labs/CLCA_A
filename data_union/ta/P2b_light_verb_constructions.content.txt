# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This document records attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — involving lexical items identified in P1. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where no common construction is identified for a given item or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Asymmetry across entries is preserved. Register variation is noted where relevant.

The constructions are organized by P1 base form. Within each entry, construction types are distinguished where the distinction is supported by the data (e.g., verb-object collocations, predicative constructions, fixed idiomatic expressions, comparative constructions). No construction type is assumed to exist for every item.

---

## Item 1: உண்மை / *uṇmai*

*uṇmai* is the most constructionally productive item in this inventory. It participates in a wide range of periphrastic verbal phrases, fixed evaluative expressions, and idiomatic collocations across registers.

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai col
4. **English translation:** tell the truth, speak truthfully
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic and frequent verbal construction with *uṇmai*; used as an imperative, infinitive, or nominalized form across all registers. Appears in everyday speech, legal instruction, and moral exhortation. The verb *col* (to say/tell) is the default verbal complement of *uṇmai* in this construction. The finite forms *uṇmai collukiṟāṉ* (he is telling the truth), *uṇmai colluvēṉ* (I will tell the truth) are equally productive.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை சொல்லணும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai collāṇum
4. **English translation:** (One) must tell the truth / You have to tell the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A very common deontic construction combining *uṇmai col* (to tell the truth) with the obligative suffix *-āṇum*. Appears in moral instruction, parental speech, and legal contexts. Extremely frequent in spoken Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை பேசு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai pēcu
4. **English translation:** speak the truth, talk honestly
5. **Usage notes:** Parallel to *uṇmai col* but with *pēcu* (to speak), which carries a slightly more sustained or conversational sense. Used in moral instruction and everyday speech. The two constructions (*uṇmai col* and *uṇmai pēcu*) are largely interchangeable in most contexts, though *pēcu* may emphasize the ongoing act of speaking rather than a single utterance. Appears in proverbs and moral exhortation: *uṇmai pēcu, nīti vāḻu* (speak truth, live justly).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அவர் உண்மை பேசுகிறார்
3. **Transliteration:** avar uṇmai pēcukiṟār
4. **English translation:** He/She is speaking the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A simple predicative construction combining *uṇmai* as a direct object with the verb *pēcu* (to speak). Common in everyday speech and in evaluations of testimony. Contrasts with *poy pēcu* (to speak falsely).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை மறை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai maṟai
4. **English translation:** hide the truth, conceal the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction (*uṇmai* + *-ai*) with the verb *maṟai* (to hide, conceal); used in legal and moral condemnation of deliberate suppression of true information. The accusative marking on *uṇmai* is obligatory in this construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை மறைக்கிறார்கள்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai maṟaikkiṟārkaḷ
4. **English translation:** They are concealing the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A common accusatory construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative (*uṇmaiyai*) with the verb *maṟai* (to conceal, to hide). Appears in journalistic, legal, and political discourse when alleging suppression of facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை மறைக்கப்பட்டது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu
4. **English translation:** The truth was concealed / The truth was suppressed
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction using *maṟai* (to conceal) in the Tamil passive (*-kkap-paṭu*). Used in legal, journalistic, and moral discourse to characterize deliberate suppression of truth. The active form *uṇmai maṟaittāṉ* (he concealed the truth) is equally common.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை மூடி மறைத்தார்கள்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai mūṭi maṟaittārkaḷ
4. **English translation:** They covered up the truth / They concealed the truth (by covering it)
5. **Usage notes:** A compound verb construction using *mūṭu* (to cover) + *maṟai* (to conceal) in sequence, intensifying the sense of deliberate suppression. The double verb construction is a natural Tamil pattern for expressing thoroughness of an action. Used in political and journalistic discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை அறி
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai aṟi
4. **English translation:** know the truth, find out the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *aṟi* (to know, to come to know); used across registers to describe the epistemic act of arriving at knowledge of what is true. Common in investigative, legal, and philosophical contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.10**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை அறிய வேண்டும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai aṟiya vēṇṭum
4. **English translation:** One must know the truth / It is necessary to know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction using *aṟi* (to know) in the infinitive + *vēṇṭum* (must, it is necessary). Common in philosophical, investigative, and moral discourse expressing the obligation or necessity of attaining knowledge of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை உணர்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai uṇar
4. **English translation:** realize the truth, come to feel/know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *uṇar* (to feel, to realize, to become aware of); distinct from *uṇmaiyai aṟi* in that *uṇar* implies a more experiential or affective process of coming to know, rather than purely cognitive acquisition. Common in literary and reflective writing.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.12**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை தெரிஞ்சுக்கோ
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai teriñcukkō
4. **English translation:** find out the truth (for yourself), get to know the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Colloquial spoken Tamil construction with *teri* (to become known/visible) in the reflexive-benefactive form *teriñcukko*; used in everyday speech, often with an implication that the hearer should investigate or discover the truth themselves. More colloquial than *uṇmaiyai aṟi*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.13**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu
4. **English translation:** the truth came out, the truth emerged
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction in which *uṇmai* is the subject and *veḷiyē va* (to come outside, to emerge) is the predicate; used in narrative, journalistic, and everyday speech to describe the revelation of previously hidden truth. The construction treats truth as an entity that moves from concealment to visibility.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.14**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை வெளியே வரும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷiyē varum
4. **English translation:** The truth will come out
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed proverbial construction asserting the inevitable emergence of hidden facts. Extremely common in everyday speech, narrative, and moral discourse. Functions as a reassurance or warning depending on context.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.15**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu
4. **English translation:** The truth came to light / The truth was revealed
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction using the verb *veḷippaṭu* (to come out, become visible, be revealed). Common in journalistic and narrative Tamil to describe the emergence of a previously concealed truth. The verbal noun form *uṇmai veḷippaṭutal* is documented in P1b; this is the finite past form in active use.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.16**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai teriya vantatu
4. **English translation:** the truth became known, the truth came to light
5. **Usage notes:** Intransitive construction with *teriya va* (to come to be known/visible); closely related to *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* but with *teri* emphasizing the becoming-known aspect rather than the coming-out aspect. Common in colloquial and journalistic Tamil. Slightly more colloquial than *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.17**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டு
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭu
4. **English translation:** establish the truth, prove the truth definitively
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *nilaināṭṭu* (to establish firmly, to make stand); used in legal and formal argumentative contexts for the act of definitively proving that something is true. More emphatic than *meyppiкka* in its implication of permanence.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.18**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்று தெரியவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu teriyavillai
4. **English translation:** it is not known what the truth is, the truth is unclear
5. **Usage notes:** Embedded question construction (*uṇmai eṉṉa* = what the truth is) with the negative *teriyavillai* (is not known); used in everyday and journalistic Tamil to express epistemic uncertainty about what is actually true. A natural way to express agnosticism about the facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.19**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னன்னு தெரியலை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaṉṉu teriyalai
4. **English translation:** (I/We) don't know what the truth is
5. **Usage notes:** A common spoken construction combining *uṇmai* with the interrogative *eṉṉa* (what) and the negative experiential *teriyalai* (don't know / it's not known). Appears in everyday speech when expressing epistemic uncertainty about the facts of a matter.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.20**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்று கண்டுபிடிக்கணும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟu kaṇṭupiṭikkāṇum
4. **English translation:** (We) need to find out what the truth is
5. **Usage notes:** A common investigative construction combining *uṇmai* with the embedded question *eṉṉaveṉṟu* (what it is) and the verb *kaṇṭupiṭi* (to find out, to discover). Appears in journalistic, legal, and everyday investigative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.21**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** இது உண்மையா இல்லையா என்று தெரியவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** itu uṇmaiyā illaiyā eṉṟu teriyavillai
4. **English translation:** It is not known whether this is true or not
5. **Usage notes:** A common epistemic construction expressing uncertainty about the truth value of a proposition. The structure *X-ā illai-ā eṉṟu teriyavillai* is a productive pattern in Tamil for expressing uncertainty; *uṇmai* is one of the most frequent fillers. Appears across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.22**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையில் என்ன நடந்தது என்று யாருக்கும் தெரியாது
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyil eṉṉa naṭantatu eṉṟu yārukum teriyātu
4. **English translation:** nobody knows what actually happened, what really happened is unknown to anyone
5. **Usage notes:** Extended construction using *uṇmaiyil* as an adverbial frame for an embedded question; illustrates the use of *uṇmaiyil* to introduce a contrast between appearance and reality in complex sentence structures. Common in journalistic and narrative Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.23**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை நிலை என்ன?
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai nilai eṉṉa?
4. **English translation:** What is the actual state of affairs? / What is the true situation?
5. **Usage notes:** An interrogative construction using *uṇmai nilai* (true state/condition) as a nominal phrase. Common in journalistic and administrative Tamil when questioning the accuracy of official accounts or reports. The construction presupposes a gap between appearance and reality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.24**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்னவென்றால்...
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟāl...
4. **English translation:** The truth is that... / What the truth is, is...
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed discourse-framing construction used to introduce a clarification or correction of the actual state of affairs. Functions as a topic-introducing frame in spoken and written Tamil. Extremely common across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.25**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** இதில் உண்மை இல்லை
3. **Transliteration:** itil uṇmai illai
4. **English translation:** There is no truth in this / This has no truth to it
5. **Usage notes:** A predicative construction using the existential negative *illai* (there is not) to deny the truth of a claim or statement. Common in everyday speech and in formal rebuttals. The locative *itil* (in this) is the standard framing.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.26**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** கொஞ்சமாவது உண்மை இருக்கா?
3. **Transliteration:** koñcamāvatu uṇmai irukkā?
4. **English translation:** Is there even a little truth (in this)?
5. **Usage notes:** A rhetorical or genuine question construction combining *uṇmai* with the existential *irukku* (there is) and the scalar particle *koñcamāvatu* (at least a little, even a bit). Common in skeptical or accusatory everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.27**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை நேருக்கு நேர் சொல்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nērukku nēr col
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth face to face / Say it directly to (someone's) face
5. **Usage notes:** A construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative with the fixed adverbial *nērukku nēr* (face to face, directly) and the verb *col* (to say). Emphasizes directness and courage in truth-telling. Common in interpersonal and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.28**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்று நிரூபிக்கணும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṟu nirūpikkāṇum
4. **English translation:** (It) must be proved to be true / (One) must prove that it is true
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *uṇmai* with the quotative *eṉṟu* and the verb *nirūpikka* (to prove, to demonstrate). Common in legal and argumentative contexts where the burden of proof is at issue.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.29**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மைதான்
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaitāṉ
4. **English translation:** that is indeed true, that is the truth (emphatic)
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with *uṇmai* as predicate nominal and *-tāṉ* as emphatic particle; used in spoken Tamil to affirm a statement as true, often in response to a challenge or to emphasize agreement. Extremely common in everyday speech. The particle *tāṉ* adds a sense of acknowledgment or concession.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.30**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை தான் வெல்லும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai tāṉ vellum
4. **English translation:** Truth alone will prevail / It is truth that will win
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction asserting the ultimate triumph of truth. The particle *tāṉ* (alone, only, it is precisely) adds contrastive emphasis. Extremely common in moral and political discourse; appears in speeches, proverbs, and popular culture. Closely related to the Gandhian motto *satyamēva jayatē* in its Tamil cultural function.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.31**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை எப்போதும் வெல்லும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eppōtum vellum
4. **English translation:** truth always wins, truth always prevails
5. **Usage notes:** Proverbial construction with *vell* (to win, to prevail); a widely attested moral and motivational expression in Tamil, appearing in literature, political speech, and everyday moral discourse. Reflects a culturally salient belief in the ultimate triumph of truth.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.32**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** அது உண்மையா?
3. **Transliteration:** atu uṇmaiyā?
4. **English translation:** is that true? is that really so?
5. **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction with *uṇmai* as predicate nominal and the question particle *-ā*; the standard way to ask whether a statement is true in spoken Tamil. Extremely common across all registers. The affirmative response *ām, uṇmai* (yes, it is true) is equally frequent.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.33**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மை என்றாலும் சொல்ல வேண்டாம்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai eṉṟālum colla vēṇṭām
4. **English translation:** even if it is true, (you) need not say it; even true things need not be said
5. **Usage notes:** Concessive conditional construction (*eṉṟālum* = even if it is said/even though); used in pragmatic and moral instruction about the limits of truth-telling, expressing the idea that truth alone does not always justify utterance. Appears in proverb-like and advisory speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.34**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*

3. **Transliteration:** uṇmai kacakkum
4. **English translation:** the truth is bitter, truth tastes bitter
5. **Usage notes:** Idiomatic construction using *kacakku* (to be bitter, to taste bitter); a widely used expression in Tamil to convey that true statements are often unpleasant to hear. Functions as a proverbial observation and appears in everyday speech, literature, and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.35**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*

4. **English translation:** wait until the truth is known, wait until the truth comes out
5. **Usage notes:** Temporal subordinate clause construction (*teriyum varai* = until it becomes known) used in everyday and narrative Tamil to advise patience pending the revelation of facts. Common in spoken Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.36**

5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *oppukkoḷ* (to accept, to admit, to acknowledge); used in legal, interpersonal, and moral contexts for the act of admitting what is true, especially when previously denied. Common in legal discourse and everyday confrontational speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.37**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை ஒப்புக்கொள்ளணும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai oppukkoḷḷāṇum
4. **English translation:** (One) must acknowledge / admit the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction combining *uṇmai* in the accusative with the verb *oppukkoḷ* (to accept, to acknowledge, to admit) and the obligative suffix. Common in moral, legal, and interpersonal contexts where admission of facts is at issue.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.38**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*

4. **English translation:** He admitted the truth / He acknowledged the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *ottukkō* (to admit, acknowledge, concede) with *uṇmai* as accusative object. Common in legal, interpersonal, and narrative contexts to describe the act of admitting a true state of affairs. The construction implies prior concealment or denial.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.39**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை திரிக்காதே
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai tirikāte
4. **English translation:** don't distort the truth, don't twist the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Accusative construction with *tiri* (to twist, to distort) in the negative imperative; used in moral and legal condemnation of misrepresentation that falls short of outright lying but involves distortion of facts. Common in journalistic and political discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.40**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை முழுவதும் சொல்லவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai muḻuvatum collavillai
4. **English translation:** (he/she) did not tell the whole truth, the full truth was not told
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with *muḻuvatum* (completely, fully) as an adverbial modifier; used in legal and moral contexts to characterize partial disclosure as a form of deception. The construction implies that selective truth-telling is itself a form of concealment.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.41**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை நம்பவில்லை
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai nampavil̲l̲ai
4. **English translation:** (He/she/they) did not believe the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *nampu* (to believe/trust) with *uṇmai* as accusative object in the negative. Used in narrative and moral discourse to describe the failure or refusal to accept a true state of affairs. Highlights the distinction between truth and its acceptance.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 1.42**

1. **Base Form:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Example:** உண்மையை எதிர்கொள்ள வேண்டும்
3. **Transliteration:** uṇmaiyai etiṟkoḷḷa vēṇṭum
4. **English translation:** One must face the truth / It is necessary to confront the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A deontic construction using *etiṟkoḷ* (to face, confront) + *vēṇṭum*. Used in moral, psychological, and journalistic discourse to express the obligation to acknowledge difficult truths. Less common than *uṇmaiyai aṟiya vēṇṭum* but well-attested in written Tamil.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
