# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed across the P1 and P2 data. Where the data show that terms are used together, opposed to each other, or restricted to the same discourse contexts, this is treated as evidence of grouping. No external semantic framework has been imposed. The number of groups reflects what the data support; no attempt has been made to produce a balanced or exhaustive taxonomy.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used in asserting or denying that a statement is true

**Label:** Expressions used in asserting or denying that a statement is true

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker is affirming or rejecting the factual accuracy of a claim, account, or statement, or making, supporting, or challenging a claim about whether something said or reported corresponds to what actually happened or is the case. They are contrasted with each other directly in usage (e.g., *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* vs. *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*) and appear in the same syntactic frames with *sọ* (to say/speak), *ni* (focus/copula), and *kò sí...nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* (there is no...in his/her words). They are used in legal testimony, everyday dispute, and moral instruction. The core contrast is between *sọ òtítọ́* (speak the truth) and *sọ irọ́* (tell a lie), which function as a matched pair across moral, legal, and everyday discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- *òtítọ́* — truth, truthfulness; the primary term for what a statement is when it corresponds to reality; appears in *sọ òtítọ́* ("speak the truth"), *òtítọ́ ni* ("it is the truth"), *kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* ("there is no truth in his/her words")
- *irọ́* — lie, falsehood; the standard antonym of *òtítọ́*; appears in *sọ irọ́* ("tell a lie"), *pa irọ́* ("tell a lie"), *kò sí irọ́ nínú rẹ̀* ("there is no lie in it"), *irọ́ ní ẹsẹ̀ kúkúrú* ("a lie has short legs")
- *sọ òtítọ́* — speak the truth
- *sọ irọ́* — tell a lie
- *pa irọ́* — tell a lie
- *má sọ irọ́* — do not tell a lie
- *òtítọ́ ni* — it is the truth, emphatic
- *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* — it is the truth that he/she spoke (cleft)
- *Irọ́ ni ó sọ* — it is a lie that he/she spoke (cleft)
- *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* — there is no truth in his/her words
- *Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* — there is no lie in his/her words
- *fi òtítọ́ sọ̀rọ̀* — speak with truth, speak truthfully
- *ọ̀rọ̀ òtítọ́* — true statement, words of truth
- *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* — correct/true word or matter
- *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* — correct/truthful utterance
- *irọ́ pátápátá* — complete/total lie
- *òtítọ́ pátápátá* — the whole truth
- *irọ́ tótóbi* — a great/serious lie
- *irọ́ funfun* — a white/minor lie
- *olòtítọ́* — a truthful person
- *olóirọ́* — a liar
- *Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* — it became clear that it is the truth
- *A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni* — we know that it is a lie
- *Òtítọ́ kò ní kú* — truth will not die (proverbial)
- *dájúdájú* — certainly, definitely, without doubt; used as an epistemic intensifier to assert that a claim is definitely true; appears in *dájúdájú, òtítọ́ ni* ("certainly, it is the truth"), *mo mọ̀ dájúdájú pé...* ("I know for certain that...")
- *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* — acknowledgment of the truth, admission of truth; used when a party formally accepts that a statement is true, particularly in legal and confessional contexts
- *ìwádìí òtítọ́* — investigation of the truth, fact-finding; used in legal and journalistic contexts for the process of establishing what is true

**Notes:** *òtítọ́* and *irọ́* are the most consistently paired terms in the entire dataset. They appear in parallel constructions (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), in parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú* / *irọ́ kò ní dúró*), and in the compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness), which is formed by negating the truth-function of the witness role. *ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* and *ìsọ̀rọ̀ tọ́* also belong here as they evaluate utterances for accuracy, though they shade toward Group 2 as well. *irọ́ funfun* (white lie) is marginal within this group in that it introduces a gradation not present in the core contrast. *dájúdájú* functions specifically as an intensifier within this group — it does not name truth or falsehood but marks the speaker's certainty about a claim. *ìjẹ́ òtítọ́* and *ìwádìí òtítọ́* are compound expressions that presuppose the existence of truth as something that can be admitted or sought; they appear at the procedural end of truth-assertion contexts rather than in simple first-person assertion.

## Group 2: Expressions used to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard of correctness or propriety

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate whether something conforms to a standard of correctness or propriety

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where an action, measurement, answer, procedure, or statement is assessed against a criterion of correctness or accuracy, or where the question is not primarily whether a statement is true, but whether an action, decision, procedure, object, or person meets a recognized standard of what is right, proper, or correct. They are not restricted to speech acts and apply broadly to objects, measurements, conduct, and decisions. The predicative construction *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* (it is correct / it is not correct) is the most basic instantiation of this group, and the root *tọ́* is the morphological core. The evaluative predicate *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* is the anchor of this group, and it appears across factual, moral, procedural, and social domains.

**Representative expressions:**
- *tọ́* — to be correct, right, proper, straight
- *òtọ́* — correct, right, accurate
- *ó tọ́* — it is correct/right/proper
- *kò tọ́* — it is not correct/right/proper
- *ó tọ́ pé* — it is right that
- *kò tọ́ pé* — it is not right that
- *ìtọ́* — correctness, propriety, rightness (abstract nominal)
- *àìtọ́* — incorrectness, impropriety, wrongness
- *ìtọ́sí* — correction, rectification
- *tọ́ sí* — to correct, to set right
- *àtúntọ́* / *ìtúntọ́* — correction, rectification
- *ìtọ́ni* — guidance, direction, correction
- *ọ̀nà tọ́* — correct method, right way
- *ìdáhùn tọ́* — correct answer
- *ìpinnu tọ́* — correct decision
- *àkọsílẹ̀ tọ́* — accurate record
- *ìsọ tọ́* — speaking correctly, truthful speech
- *ìwà tọ́* — upright/correct character
- *ìwọ̀n tọ́* — accurate measurement
- *ìlànà tọ́* — correct procedure
- *ìgbésẹ̀ tọ́* — correct action/step
- *àṣìṣe* — error, mistake (P1 item 15)
- *Ó tọ́ pé kí a sọ òtítọ́* — it is right that we speak the truth
- *Kò tọ́ pé kí o sọ irọ́* — it is not right that you tell a lie
- *Ó tọ́ gẹ́gẹ́ bí ó ti yẹ* — it is correct as it should be
- *Ẹ tọ́ ọ sí ọ̀nà tó tọ́* — direct him/her to the correct path

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 by its breadth of application: *ó tọ́* can evaluate a measurement, an answer, a procedure, or a moral action, not only a speech act. *àṣìṣe* belongs here as the primary term for error or deviation from a standard, and it contrasts with *òtọ́* in the same way that *irọ́* contrasts with *òtítọ́* in Group 1 — though the contrast in this group is less tightly paired. *ìtọ́ni* and *àtúntọ́* appear here because they describe the process of bringing something back into conformity with a standard. This group is the largest and most diffuse. The root *tọ́* appears in factual contexts (Group 1), moral contexts (Group 4), legal contexts (Group 3), and procedural/administrative contexts. The expressions here are those where the evaluative function of *tọ́* is most clearly foregrounded without being reducible to any single domain. *ó tọ́* functions as a discourse marker of affirmation across all these contexts. *ìwà tọ́* overlaps with Group 4 (the character evaluation domain), but in usage it appears specifically in moral evaluation of persons rather than in procedural or factual correctness contexts.

## Group 3: Expressions used in legal and formal proceedings to establish what happened

**Label:** Expressions used in legal and formal proceedings to establish what happened

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in contexts where the truth of a past event or claim is being formally established through testimony, evidence, questioning, or judgment, or where a party is required to produce, evaluate, or formally accept evidence about events or facts. They are associated with the roles of witness, judge, and party in a proceeding, and they participate in constructions about producing, evaluating, accepting, or rejecting proof. They cluster around the root *ẹ̀rí* (evidence, witness, related to *rí*, to see) and involve distinctions between types of evidence (direct, circumstantial, documentary, hearsay, expert) and between valid and invalid evidence. This group is defined by its restriction to adversarial or evaluative proceedings rather than by any single semantic feature.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ẹ̀rí* — evidence, proof, testimony, witness; the primary term for anything that supports a claim in legal or evaluative contexts; appears in *mú ẹ̀rí wá* ("bring evidence"), *kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó* ("there is no sufficient evidence"), *ẹ̀rí wà tó fi hàn pé...* ("there is evidence that shows that...")
- *ẹ̀rí ojú* — eyewitness evidence, direct visual evidence; a specific type of evidence considered particularly strong; appears in *ẹ̀rí ojú ló pọ̀ jù* ("eyewitness evidence is the strongest")
- *ẹ̀rí etí* — hearsay evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́* — hearsay evidence, evidence based on hearing; distinguished from *ẹ̀rí ojú* in legal contexts
- *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́ràn* — hearsay evidence
- *ẹ̀rí tààrà* — direct evidence
- *ẹ̀rí àyíká* — circumstantial evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìwé* — documentary evidence
- *ẹ̀rí àkọsílẹ̀* — documentary evidence, written evidence
- *ẹ̀rí àfihàn* — demonstrative/physical evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìmọ̀ ìjìnlẹ̀* — expert/scientific evidence
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánimọ̀* — identification evidence; used in administrative and security contexts
- *ẹ̀rí ìdánwò* — evidence from testing, test/trial evidence
- *ẹ̀rí tó péye* — sufficient evidence
- *ẹ̀rí tó gbára* — reliable evidence
- *àìní ẹ̀rí* — absence of evidence
- *ẹlẹ́rìí* — witness, one who bears evidence
- *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* — eyewitness
- *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* — false witness, perjurer
- *ìjẹ́rìí* — testimony, the act of witnessing
- *ìjẹ́rìísí* — testimony, attestation, certification; used for formal attestation in court and religious contexts; appears in *wọ́n béèrè ìjẹ́rìísí lọ́wọ́ rẹ̀* ("they requested testimony from him/her"), *kò sí ìjẹ́rìísí tó tó* ("there is no sufficient testimony")
- *jẹ́rìí* — to testify, to bear witness
- *jẹ́rìísí* — verbal base: to testify, to attest; appears in *ó jẹ́rìísí pé ó wà níbẹ̀* ("he/she testified that he/she was there")
- *jẹ́rìí pé* — to testify that
- *jẹ́rìí fún* — to testify for/on behalf of
- *jẹ́rìí sí* — to testify against/to
- *olùjẹ́rìí* — testifier, one who bears testimony
- *mú ẹ̀rí wá* — bring evidence, produce proof
- *ẹ̀rí rẹ kò tó* — your evidence is insufficient
- *ẹ̀rí kò sí* — there is no evidence
- *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi* — there is no sufficient evidence to convict
- *Ẹ̀rí tó wà ń tọ́ka sí i* — the available evidence points to him/her
- *Ó gba ẹ̀rí jọ* — he/she gathered evidence
- *Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* — he/she presented evidence before the court
- *Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ kò tó ohunkóhun* — his/her evidence amounts to nothing
- *Ẹ̀rí tó gbéṣẹ̀ ni a nílò* — it is valid evidence that we need
- *Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó lòdì sí i* — there is no evidence against him/her
- *Ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí* — he/she refused to testify
- *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu* — interrogation, formal questioning
- *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* — interrogation, cross-examination; morphological description explicitly frames it as examining what comes from a person's mouth in order to test the accuracy of their account
- *fọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nu ẹni* — interrogate a person
- *ìdánilójú* — assurance, verification, confirmation
- *ìdájọ́* — judgment, verdict, ruling; the outcome of a formal evaluative process; appears in *ìdájọ́ náà tọ́* ("the judgment is correct"), *ó dúró dè ìdájọ́* ("he/she is awaiting judgment")
- *dájọ́ lórí* — pass judgment on
- *adájọ́* — judge
- *ilé ìdájọ́* — court of law
- *ìfọwọ́sí* — endorsement, confirmation, ratification, authentication; the formal act of officially confirming something; appears in *wọ́n fọwọ́ sí àdéhùn náà* ("they ratified the agreement"), *kò tíì gba ìfọwọ́sí* ("it has not yet received endorsement")
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí* — birth certificate
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbéyàwó* — marriage certificate
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkẹ́kọ̀ọ́* — academic certificate, diploma
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìwà* — character reference, attestation of good character
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìkọ́lé* — building certification
- *Ó gba ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ wò* — he/she examined/checked his/her certificate
- *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* — his/her certificate is genuine/valid
- *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* — his/her certificate is false/fraudulent
- *Kò ní ìjẹ́rìísí tó yẹ* — he/she does not have the appropriate certification
- *ìjẹ́rìísí ìgbàgbọ́* — attestation/declaration of faith

**Notes:** This group is the most internally differentiated in the dataset. Within it, there is a sub-pattern around the hierarchy of evidence types: *ẹ̀rí ojú* (eyewitness) is consistently marked as more reliable than *ẹ̀rí etí* / *ẹ̀rí ìgbọ́* (hearsay), and *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú* is the most credible witness category. The compound *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* (false witness) is notable because it combines a term from this group (*ẹlẹ́rìí*, witness) with a term from Group 1 (*irọ́*, lie), marking the violation of the witness role's truth-function. *ẹ̀rí* and *ìjẹ́rìísí* overlap considerably in this group but are distinguishable: *ẹ̀rí* refers to any evidence or sign (including physical traces), while *ìjẹ́rìísí* refers specifically to personal attestation or formal certification. *ìdánwò* and *ìdájọ́* represent sequential stages in a formal process — testing precedes judgment — and both appear in the same institutional contexts. *ìfọwọ́sí* marks the endpoint of formal confirmation and is more administrative than evidential. The certification compounds (*ìjẹ́rìísí ìbí*, etc.) appear in administrative and credentialing contexts where the question is whether a document or credential is valid; the contrast *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* vs. *Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ irọ́* shows that Group 1 vocabulary (truth/falsehood) is applied to evaluate items in this sub-context. *ìfọ̀rọ̀wánilẹ́nuwò* also appears in Group 10 because its morphological description frames it as examining what a person says in response to questioning — the act of eliciting and evaluating received speech.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate the fundamental moral character of a person

**Label:** Expressions used to evaluate the fundamental moral character of a person

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where the object of evaluation is a person's fundamental nature or habitual conduct rather than a specific statement, action, or object. They are used to assess whether a person is, at a deep level, good or bad, righteous or corrupt. The central concept is *ìwà* (character, the manner of being), and the primary contrast is between *ìwà rere* (good character) and *ìwà burúkú* / *ìwà búburú* (bad character). These expressions appear in proverbs, moral instruction, and everyday moral evaluation, and they assess persons rather than propositions or actions in isolation.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ìwà* — character, conduct, nature, the manner of being; the base term for a person's fundamental nature; appears in *ìwà rẹ̀ ti hàn* ("his/her character has been revealed"), *ìwà ẹni ló ń ṣe é* ("it is a person's character that defines them")
- *ìwà rere* — good character, virtue, moral excellence; appears in *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* ("it is good character that saves one")
- *ìwà burúkú* — bad character, vice; appears in *ìwà burúkú ló pa á* ("it is bad character that destroyed him/her")
- *ìwà búburú* — bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
- *ìwà tọ́* — upright/correct character
- *ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́* — gentle character, gentleness, mild and careful conduct; appears in *ìwà pẹ̀lẹ́ ló yẹ ọmọlúwàbí* ("gentle character befits a person of good breeding")
- *ìwà ìdúróṣinṣin* — steadfast character, integrity, firmness of conduct; used in praise of leaders and in religious discourse about faithfulness
- *ìwà ìbàjẹ́* — corrupt character/conduct; used in political and moral criticism
- *ìwà àìtọ́* — incorrect conduct, improper character, wrongful behavior; used in moral and legal evaluation; notable as a compound that brings together the *ìwà* and *tọ* roots
- *ìwà àgàbàgebè* — hypocritical conduct, pretentious character; used in social and moral criticism
- *ìwà ọmọlúwàbí* — the character of a well-bred/noble person
- *ọmọlúwàbí* — a person of good character and proper upbringing; the term for the ideal person who embodies good character; appears in *ìwà ọmọlúwàbí* ("the character of a well-bred, virtuous person")
- *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* — character is what makes a person (proverb)
- *Ìwà rere ló ń gbé ènìyàn ga* — good character is what elevates a person (proverb)
- *Ìwà rere ju ọrọ̀ lọ* — good character surpasses wealth (proverb)
- *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́* — good character saves a person
- *ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn* — bad character destroys a person
- *ìwà rere ju ẹwà lọ* — good character surpasses beauty
- *yẹra fún ìwà burúkú* — avoid bad character
- *ní ìwà rere* — have good character
- *Ó ní ìwà rere* — he/she has good character
- *Kò ní ìwà rere* — he/she does not have good character
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ dára* — his/her character is good
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ kò dára* — his/her character is not good
- *Ìwà rẹ̀ hàn gbangba* — his/her true character is clearly visible
- *Ó ń ṣe àgàbàgebè pẹ̀lú ìwà rẹ̀* — he/she is pretending to have good character
- *Ìwà búburú rẹ̀ ló pa á* — his/her bad character was his/her undoing (proverb)
- *òdodo* — justice, righteousness, fairness; used in moral, legal, and religious contexts to describe conduct or outcomes that conform to what is right; appears in *ṣe òdodo fún gbogbo ènìyàn* ("do justice to all people"), *ó ń wá òdodo* ("he/she is seeking justice")
- *ṣe òdodo* — do justice, act righteously
- *alòdodo* — just/righteous person
- *àìdodo* — injustice, unrighteousness
- *olòtítọ́* — truthful person
- *aláìrọ́* — one who does not lie
- *aṣírọ́* — liar
- *àṣìṣe* — error, mistake, fault, wrongdoing; appears in *ṣe àṣìṣe* ("to make a mistake"), *jẹ́wọ́ àṣìṣe rẹ* ("admit your mistake"), *kò ṣe àṣìṣe kankan* ("he/she made no mistake at all")
- *ṣe àṣìṣe* — make an error
- *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* — confess/admit an error
- *darí àṣìṣe jì* — forgive an error
- *àṣìṣe kò ní ṣẹlẹ̀ mọ́* — the error will not happen again
- *àṣìṣe ńlá* — serious wrongdoing
- *aṣìṣe* — one who errs
- *àbùkù* — blemish, defect, disgrace, fault; a person or thing with *àbùkù* fails to be what it should be
- *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — sin, guilt, offense
- *ṣe ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — commit sin
- *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — confess sin
- *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì* — forgive sin
- *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ kò ní yọ̀* — sin will not go unpunished
- *wà ní ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — be in a state of sin
- *alẹ̀ṣẹ̀* — sinner
- *àìṣẹ̀* — sinlessness, innocence

**Notes:** This group is defined by the evaluation of persons rather than propositions, objects, or events. The proverb *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (character is what makes a person) is the clearest statement of the group's central concern. *ìwà* is the organizing term of this group — nearly all other expressions in it are either compounds of *ìwà* or terms that appear in the same evaluative contexts. *òdodo* belongs here because it evaluates persons and conduct as righteous or just, though it also appears in legal contexts (Group 3) in the compound *ìdájọ́ òdodo*. The agentive forms *olòtítọ́*, *aláìrọ́*, and *aṣírọ́* belong here because they evaluate persons by their habitual truth-telling or lying behavior, even though the root terms (*òtítọ́*, *irọ́*) belong primarily to Group 1. The parallel constructions *jẹ̀wọ́ àṣìṣe* / *jẹ̀wọ́ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* and *darí àṣìṣe jì* / *darí ẹ̀ṣẹ̀ jì* show that *àṣìṣe* and *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* participate in the same constructional frames. The data consistently show that *ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* is more strongly associated with religious discourse and moral culpability, while *àṣìṣe* can describe factual errors without implying moral guilt. The construction *ẹni tó bá sọ irọ́ á jẹ ẹ̀ṣẹ̀* (whoever tells a lie will bear guilt) links Group 1 to this group by treating lying as a sin. *àṣìṣe* is also used in epistemic contexts (incorrect knowledge, factual error) that align with Group 1; the construction *àṣìṣe ìmọ̀* ("error of knowledge") explicitly places it in the epistemic domain. *ìwà àìtọ́* explicitly combines the character domain (*ìwà*) with the correctness domain (*tọ*), suggesting that in Yoruba usage these two domains are not fully separate. *àbùkù* is placed here because its usage centers on a person or thing falling short of what it should
