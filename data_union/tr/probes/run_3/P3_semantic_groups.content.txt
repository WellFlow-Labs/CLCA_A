# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which items co-occur, which are contrasted with each other, which share typical contexts (legal, journalistic, moral, aesthetic, etc.), and which participate in similar constructional patterns. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

## Group 1 — Expressions used to evaluate statements and claims as correct or incorrect

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize a statement, answer, piece of information, or claim as either conforming to or deviating from what is factually the case. They appear in contexts where the question at issue is whether something said or believed is accurate. They contrast directly with each other and participate in similar constructions (e.g., *doğru çıkmak* / *yanlış çıkmak*, *doğruyu söylemek* / *yalan söylemek*).

**Representative expressions:**
- *doğru* — used to evaluate a statement, answer, or direction as correct or accurate
- *yanlış* — used to evaluate a statement, action, or belief as deviating from what is correct
- *yalan* — used to characterize a statement as deliberately false
- *doğruluk* — names the property of being accurate or truthful
- *yanlışlık* — names the property or instance of being wrong or incorrect
- *doğruyu söylemek / doğruyu söylemek* — to tell the truth
- *yalan söylemek* — to tell a lie
- *doğru çıkmak* — to turn out to be correct
- *yalan çıkmak* — to turn out to be a lie
- *yanlış çıkmak* (implied by pattern; see *doğru çıkmak* / *sahte çıkmak*)
- *doğruluk payı olmak* — there is a grain of truth in something
- *yalan yanlış* — thoroughly inaccurate, full of errors and falsehoods

**Notes:** *Doğru* and *yanlış* are the primary antonym pair in this group. *Yalan* overlaps with this group but carries an additional implication of intentionality (see Group 3). *Hakikat* and *gerçek* also appear in statement-evaluation contexts but are documented separately because their primary usage patterns differ (see Groups 2 and 5).

---

## Group 2 — Expressions used to assert that something has genuine existence or is not illusory

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that a person, object, event, or state is genuinely what it appears or is claimed to be — that it is not fabricated, simulated, or illusory. They appear in contexts where the contrast is between something real and something fake, imagined, or constructed. They participate in constructions like *gerçek anlamda*, *gerçek hayatta*, *gerçeği kabul etmek*, and *gerçekle yüzleşmek*.

**Representative expressions:**
- *gerçek* — used to assert that something is not illusory, fabricated, or simulated
- *gerçeklik* — names the property of being real or the domain of reality
- *gerçekten* — adverb asserting that something is genuinely the case
- *gerçek anlamda / gerçek anlamıyla* — in the true sense of the word
- *gerçek hayatta* — in real life (contrasted with fictional or digital contexts)
- *gerçeği kabul etmek* — to accept the truth, to face reality
- *gerçekle yüzleşmek* — to confront the truth, to face reality
- *gerçeği yansıtmak* — to reflect reality
- *gerçeği çarpıtmak* — to distort reality
- *gerçeklikten kopuk* — detached from reality
- *gerçeklik algısı* — perception of reality
- *gerçek dışı* — unreal, outside the bounds of reality
- *asıl* — the real, original, or fundamental version of something
- *özü* / *özünde* — at its core, in its essential nature

**Notes:** *Gerçek* and *hakikat* both translate as "truth" in many contexts, but their usage patterns differ: *gerçek* is used across all registers and focuses on the contrast between real and not-real, while *hakikat* is used in more formal and philosophical registers and focuses on deep or essential truth (see Group 5). *Asıl* and *özü* overlap with this group in that they identify the genuine or fundamental version of something, but they also participate in other patterns (see notes on ungrouped items).

---

## Group 3 — Expressions used to evaluate persons, objects, or representations as intentionally deceptive or fake

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the evaluation involves not merely error or inaccuracy but the deliberate presentation of something false as genuine, or the intentional creation of a false impression. They appear in legal, commercial, moral, and interpersonal contexts where intent to deceive is at issue. They contrast with expressions in Group 6 (authenticity) and Group 1 (simple correctness).

**Representative expressions:**
- *yalan* — a deliberately false statement; contrasts with *doğru* but adds intentionality
- *yalancı* — a person who tells lies
- *yalancılık* — the practice or habit of lying
- *sahte* — presented as genuine but not; applied to objects, documents, identities
- *sahteci* — a person who produces fakes or forgeries
- *sahtecilik* — the practice of forgery, counterfeiting, or fraud
- *aldatıcı* — intentionally creating false impressions; stronger than *yanıltıcı*
- *aldatmak* — to deceive, to cheat, to defraud
- *aldatmaca* — a deceptive act, trick, or sham
- *aldatma* — the act of deceiving
- *kılık değiştirmiş* — having changed its guise, presenting itself under a false appearance
- *sahte para* — counterfeit money
- *sahte kimlik* — false identity
- *sahte belge* — forged document
- *sahte çıkmak* — to turn out to be fake
- *yalan dünya* — the deceitful/illusory world (fixed phrase)
- *yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar* — proverb: lies are eventually exposed

**Notes:** *Yanıltıcı* (misleading) is placed in a separate group (Group 4) because its usage pattern does not require intentionality in the same way *aldatıcı* does. *Sahte* overlaps with Group 3 and Group 4 depending on context: *sahte gülümseme* (fake smile) implies performance rather than legal forgery, but the core pattern of "presented as genuine but not" is consistent across uses.

---

## Group 4 — Expressions used to evaluate representations as misleading without necessarily implying deliberate intent

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize statements, appearances, or representations as causing false impressions or errors, without necessarily implying that the speaker or producer intended to deceive. They appear in epistemic, advertising, and analytical contexts where the focus is on the effect of a representation rather than the intent behind it.

**Representative expressions:**
- *yanıltıcı* — misleading, causing false impressions; does not require intentional deception
- *yanıltıcılık* — the property of being misleading
- *yanıltmak* — to mislead, to cause to err
- *yanılmak* — to be mistaken, to err
- *yanılgı* — an error, misconception, or fallacy
- *yanlış anlamak* — to misunderstand, to understand incorrectly
- *yanlış anlaşılmak* — to be misunderstood
- *yanlış bilgi vermek* — to give incorrect information, to misinform

**Notes:** The boundary between Group 3 and Group 4 is not always sharp in usage. *Yanıltıcı* and *aldatıcı* are sometimes used interchangeably, but the documented pattern shows *aldatıcı* more consistently implying intent. *Yanlış* items in this group shade into Group 1 (incorrect statements) depending on context; they are placed here when the focus is on the effect on the receiver rather than the truth-value of the statement itself.

---

## Group 5 — Expressions used in formal, philosophical, or religious registers to invoke deep or essential truth

**Definition:** These expressions appear in philosophical, religious, literary, and formal registers to refer to truth understood as fundamental, essential, or weighty — not merely factual accuracy but something closer to the true nature of things or a truth that demands acknowledgment. They contrast in register with the more colloquial *gerçek* and *doğru*, and they participate in constructions like *hakikat arayışı*, *hakikati söylemek*, and *hakikat karşısında*.

**Representative expressions:**
- *hakikat* — deep or fundamental truth, the true nature of something; formal and philosophical
- *hakikaten* — truly, in truth; more solemn than *gerçekten*
- *hakikatperest* — one devoted to truth; literary
- *hakikat arayışı* — the search for truth
- *hakikati söylemek* — to tell the truth (in a formal or weighty sense)
- *hakikat karşısında* — in the face of truth
- *hakikat ortaya çıkmak* — the truth comes out
- *acı hakikat* — bitter truth (fixed phrase)
- *hakikat acı olabilir* — the truth can be bitter
- *hakiki* — genuine, real, authentic; formal or literary register
- *hakiki anlamda* — in the true sense; more elevated than *gerçek anlamda*
- *sahih* — sound, valid, authentic; used in Islamic and scholarly contexts
- *sahihlik* — soundness, validity; used in textual and religious scholarship
- *sahih hadis* — sound hadith; domain-specific to Islamic studies

**Notes:** *Hakikat* and *gerçek* overlap in some contexts (both can translate as "truth"), but their register and constructional patterns differ consistently enough to warrant separate grouping. *Sahih* is the most domain-restricted item in this group, appearing almost exclusively in Islamic studies and textual scholarship. *Hakiki* overlaps with Group 6 (authenticity expressions) in some uses (e.g., *hakiki deri* — genuine leather), but its formal register and participation in *hakikat*-cluster constructions places it primarily here.

---

## Group 6 — Expressions used to evaluate persons, works, or things as genuine rather than imitative or performed

**Definition:** These expressions are used to assert that something is genuinely what it is — not a copy, imitation, performance, or pretense. They appear in aesthetic, interpersonal, and commercial contexts. Unlike Group 2 (real vs. illusory), the contrast here is specifically between the genuine and the imitative or performed, and unlike Group 3 (deceptive), the focus is on the positive quality of authenticity rather than the negative quality of fakeness.

**Representative expressions:**
- *özgün* — original, not derivative or imitative; used in aesthetic and intellectual contexts
- *özgünlük* — originality, authenticity; the property of being original
- *özgün olmak* — to be original
- *özgünlük iddiasında bulunmak* — to claim originality
- *özgünlükten yoksun* — lacking originality
- *sahici* — genuine, real, authentic; colloquial and affectively warm
- *sahicilik* — genuineness, authenticity; colloquial register
- *sahici olmak* — to be genuine
- *sahicilikten uzak* — far from genuine
- *hakiki deri* — genuine leather (commercial context)
- *hakiki dost* — a true friend (interpersonal context)
- *özü sözü bir* — a person whose inner self and words are one; sincere and consistent
- *dürüst* — honest, upright, straightforward
- *dürüstlük* — honesty, uprightness, integrity
- *doğru dürüst* — properly, decently; (of a person) genuinely decent and honest

**Notes:** *Özgün* and *sahici* are the clearest members of this group, but they differ in register and typical domain: *özgün* is used primarily in aesthetic and intellectual contexts, while *sahici* is colloquial and interpersonal. *Hakiki* appears here in commercial and interpersonal uses (*hakiki deri*, *hakiki dost*) but belongs primarily to Group 5 by register. *Özü sözü bir* is placed here because it evaluates a person as consistent between inner character and outward expression — a form of personal authenticity — though it has no close morphological relatives in the P1/P2 data. *Taklit* (imitation) functions as the primary contrast term for this group.

---

## Group 7 — Expressions used to evaluate imitation, copying, or derivativeness

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize objects, works, performances, or behaviors as imitative rather than original. They appear in aesthetic, commercial, and moral contexts. They function as the primary contrast set for Group 6 (authenticity expressions) and overlap with Group 3 (deceptive expressions) in commercial contexts (counterfeit goods).

**Representative expressions:**
- *taklit* — imitation, copy, counterfeit; applied in aesthetic and commercial contexts
- *taklit etmek* — to imitate, to copy, to mimic
- *taklitçi* — an imitator, mimic, or counterfeiter
- *taklitçilik* — the practice of imitating or copying
- *taklit ürün* — counterfeit product
- *taklidi bırakmak* — to give up imitation
- *taklitçilik özgünlüğün karşıtıdır* — imitation is the opposite of originality (attested usage)

**Notes:** *Taklit* overlaps with Group 3 (deceptive/fake) in commercial contexts where counterfeit goods are at issue, but in aesthetic contexts it describes imitation without necessarily implying intent to deceive. The overlap is genuine and reflects actual usage ambiguity.

---

## Group 8 — Expressions used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate documents, claims, or authority as valid or invalid

**Definition:** These expressions are used in legal, administrative, and institutional contexts to evaluate whether a document, argument, rule, or claim meets required standards, is currently in force, or has been officially certified. They participate in constructions like *geçerli olmak*, *geçersiz kılmak*, *tasdik etmek*, and *teyit etmek*.

**Representative expressions:**
- *geçerli* — valid, in force, currently accepted
- *geçerlilik* — validity, the property of being in force
- *geçerliliği sona ermek* — for validity to expire
- *geçerli neden / geçerli gerekçe* — a valid reason / justification
- *geçersiz* — invalid, void, null
- *geçersizlik* — invalidity, nullity
- *geçersiz kılmak* — to invalidate, to render void
- *geçersiz saymak* — to count as invalid
- *tasdik* — official certification, attestation
- *tasdik etmek / tasdik ettirmek* — to certify, to have certified
- *tasdikli kopya* — certified copy
- *teyit* — confirmation, corroboration
- *teyit etmek* — to confirm, to corroborate
- *teyit edilmek* — to be confirmed
- *teyitli haber* — confirmed news
- *teyit almak* — to receive confirmation
- *doğrulama* — verification, validation
- *doğrulamak* — to verify, to confirm, to validate
- *kimlik doğrulama* — identity verification
- *iki adımlı doğrulama* — two-step verification
- *meşru* — legitimate, lawful
- *meşruiyet* — legitimacy
- *meşruiyet krizi* — legitimacy crisis
- *meşruiyetini yitirmek* — to lose legitimacy
- *gayri meşru* — illegitimate, unlawful
- *meşrulaştırmak* — to legitimize
- *asılsız* — unfounded, baseless, groundless
- *asılsızlık* — groundlessness, baselessness
- *sahihlik* — soundness, validity (in scholarly/religious contexts)

**Notes:** This group is large because Turkish has several partially overlapping terms for validity and certification that are differentiated by register and institutional context. *Tasdik* is more formal and notarial; *teyit* is more journalistic and informational; *doğrulama* is used across technical and journalistic contexts; *geçerli/geçersiz* are the primary legal-administrative pair. *Meşru/meşruiyet* focuses specifically on legitimacy of authority and action rather than document validity. *Asılsız* (unfounded) belongs here because it evaluates claims as lacking the factual basis required for validity, though it also participates in Group 1 patterns.

## Group 9 — Expressions used to evaluate persons or positions as justified or unjustified in their claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a person's claim, judgment, complaint, or action is warranted — whether they are "in the right." They are applied to persons and positions rather than to statements or documents, and they participate in constructions like *haklı çıkmak*, *haklı olmak*, *hak vermek*, and *haklı olarak*. They contrast with expressions in Group 1 (which evaluate statements) and Group 8 (which evaluate documents and authority).

**Representative expressions:**
- *haklı* — in the right, justified; applied to persons or positions
- *haksız* — unjustified, in the wrong
- *haklılık* — the state of being in the right, justifiedness
- *haksızlık* — injustice, unfairness, the state of being unjust
- *haklı çıkmak* — to be proven right
- *haklı olmak* — to be right, to be in the right
- *haklı olarak* — rightly, justifiably
- *haklı şikâyet / haklı eleştiri / haklı itiraz* — a justified complaint / criticism / objection
- *hak vermek* — to concede that someone is right
- *haksız yere* — unjustly, wrongfully

**Notes:** *Haklı* and *doğru* both translate as "right" in English, but their usage patterns differ: *doğru* evaluates statements and facts, while *haklı* evaluates persons and positions as warranted. The distinction is consistent across the P1 and P2 data. *Hak* (right, entitlement) participates in both this group and Group 10 (rights and entitlements); the overlap is genuine.

---

## Group 10 — Expressions used in contexts of rights, entitlements, and what is due to a person

**Definition:** These expressions are used in legal, moral, and political contexts to refer to what a person is entitled to, what they deserve, or what is owed to them. They participate in constructions like *hakkını vermek*, *hakkını aramak*, *hakkını yemek*, *hak kazanmak*, and *insan hakları*. The focus is on entitlement and desert rather than on truth-evaluation.

**Representative expressions:**
- *hak* — right, entitlement, due
- *hak etmek* — to deserve, to merit
- *hak kazanmak* — to earn the right
- *hakkını vermek* — to give something its due, to do justice to something
- *hakkını aramak* — to seek one's rights
- *hakkını yemek* — to deprive someone of their due
- *hak tanımamak* — to deny rights
- *hak gözetmek* — to uphold rights
- *hak ihlali* — rights violation
- *hak sahibi* — rights holder
- *insan hakları* — human rights
- *temel haklar* — fundamental rights
- *telif hakkı* — copyright
- *fikri mülkiyet hakkı* — intellectual property right
- *hak ve adalet* — right and justice
- *hak-hukuk* — one's rights and legal entitlements
- *hakkaniyet* — equity, fairness
- *hakkaniyetli* — equitable, fair
- *hakkaniyetsiz* — inequitable, unfair
- *meşru müdafaa* — self-defense (legitimate defense)

**Notes:** *Hak* participates in both Group 9 (justified/unjustified persons) and Group 10 (entitlements), and this overlap is genuine — the same word covers both domains in Turkish. *Hakkaniyet* is placed here rather than in Group 9 because its usage focuses on distributive fairness rather than on whether a specific person is in the right. *Meşru müdafaa* is placed here as a legal entitlement construction, though *meşru* belongs primarily to Group 8.

---

## Group 11 — Expressions used to describe the act of verifying or confirming information

**Definition:** These expressions are used to describe the process of checking, establishing, or confirming the truth or validity of a claim, piece of information, or document. They appear in journalistic, legal, technical, and administrative contexts. Unlike Group 8 (which focuses on the status of documents and authority), this group focuses on the epistemic act of verification itself.

**Representative expressions:**
- *doğrulamak* — to verify, to confirm, to validate
- *doğrulanmak* — to be verified, to be confirmed
- *doğrulama* — verification, confirmation, validation
- *doğrulayıcı* — verifying, confirmatory; a verifier
- *doğrulayamamak* — to be unable to verify
- *kimlik doğrulama* — identity verification
- *iki adımlı doğrulama* — two-step verification
- *teyit etmek* — to confirm, to corroborate
- *teyit edilmek* — to be confirmed
- *teyit almak* — to receive confirmation
- *teyit beklemek* — to await confirmation
- *tasdik etmek* — to certify, to attest
- *doğruluk testi* — truth test, fact-check
- *yalanlamak* — to deny, to refute, to give the lie to
- *yalanlanmak* — to be denied, to be refuted
- *yalanlama* — denial, refutation

**Notes:** *Yalanlamak* and *yalanlama* are included here because in journalistic and official usage they function as the negative counterpart of *teyit etmek* — the act of officially denying or contradicting a claim. This is a usage-based observation: in news language, *teyit* (confirmation) and *yalanlama* (denial/refutation) appear as contrasting outcomes of the verification process. *Yanlışlamak* (to falsify, to show as wrong) also belongs here in philosophical and scientific contexts, though it is documented primarily under *yanlış* derivations.

---

## Group 12 — Expressions used to evaluate conduct or character as honest and consistent

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons as genuinely honest, consistent between their inner character and outward behavior, or morally upright. They appear in moral and interpersonal contexts. Unlike Group 6 (authenticity of works and things), the focus here is specifically on personal honesty and moral integrity.

**Representative expressions:**
- *dürüst* — honest, upright, straightforward
- *dürüstlük* — honesty, uprightness, integrity
- *özü sözü bir* — a person whose inner self and words are one; sincere and consistent
- *doğru dürüst* — properly, decently; (of a person) genuinely decent and honest
- *doğruluk* — truthfulness, accuracy, integrity (in the moral sense)
- *doğruya doğru, eğriye eğri demek* — to call a spade a spade; to call things as they are

**Notes:** This group is small and overlaps with Group 6 (authenticity) and Group 1 (correct statements). *Dürüst* and *özü sözü bir* are the clearest members. *Doğruluk* appears in both Group 1 (accuracy of statements) and here (honesty of character), reflecting genuine polysemy in usage. *Doğruya doğru, eğriye eğri demek* is placed here because it prescribes honest evaluation of persons and situations rather than simply asserting factual correctness.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 participate in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single group. Their placement is noted without forcing resolution.

---

**asıl**
Participates in Group 2 (the real, original version of something) and Group 8 (the original document as opposed to a copy, in legal contexts). Also participates in Group 6 in some uses (the genuine article as opposed to an imitation). The usage pattern is consistent — *asıl* identifies the foundational or original instance — but the relevant contrast varies by context.

---

**özü / öz**
Participates in Group 2 (the essential nature of something, contrasted with surface appearance) and Group 12 (the inner self of a person, as in *özü sözü bir*). Also appears in Group 6 via *özgün* (whose etymology involves *öz*). The item is not easily assigned to a single group.

---

**hakiki**
Participates in Group 5 (formal/philosophical register, *hakikat* cluster) and Group 6 (authenticity of objects and persons: *hakiki deri*, *hakiki dost*). The register difference is consistent but the semantic overlap is genuine.

---

**doğruluk**
Participates in Group 1 (accuracy of statements: *doğruluk payı*, *doğruluk testi*) and Group 12 (honesty of character: moral *doğruluk*). This reflects attested polysemy in usage rather than a classification problem.

---

**yanlışlamak / yanlışlama**
Participates in Group 1 (showing a statement to be wrong) and Group 11 (the act of falsification as a verification procedure in scientific and philosophical contexts). The Popperian usage (*hipotezi yanlışlamak*) is distinct enough from everyday *yanlış* usage to create genuine ambiguity.

---

**meşru / meşruiyet**
Participates in Group 8 (legal validity and legitimacy of authority) and Group 10 (rights and entitlements: *meşru müdafaa*). The core usage is in Group 8, but the entitlement dimension is present in several constructions.

---

**gerçekleşmek / gerçekleştirmek**
These forms derive from *gerçek* but their usage is primarily about actualization of plans and events rather than truth-evaluation or reality-assertion. They do not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. P1b already flagged these as peripheral extensions of the *gerçek-* root. No group assignment is made.

---

**gerçekçi / gerçekçilik**
These forms are used in aesthetic contexts (realism as an artistic movement) and in everyday evaluation of attitudes (a realistic person). They derive from *gerçek* but their primary usage is not about truth-evaluation or reality-assertion in the senses covered by Groups 1 and 2. No group assignment is made; they represent a domain-specific extension of the *gerçek-* root into aesthetic and attitudinal evaluation.

---

**sanal gerçeklik / artırılmış gerçeklik / gerçeküstü / gerçeküstücülük**
These are technical and aesthetic compounds that use *gerçeklik* or *gerçek* as a component but whose primary usage is domain-specific (computing, digital media, art history). They do not participate in the truth-evaluation or authenticity patterns that define the groups above. No group assignment is made.

---

**doğrusal / doğrusal olmayan / doğru açı / doğru orantılı / doğrultu**
These forms derive from *doğru* but their usage is in mathematical, geometric, and spatial contexts where the root contributes "straight" or "linear" rather than "correct" or "true." P1b already flagged these as peripheral extensions. No group assignment is made.

---

**yalan dünya**
This fixed phrase participates in Group 3 (deceptive/fake) in that it characterizes the world as deceptive or illusory, but its usage is primarily philosophical and resigned rather than evaluative of a specific deceptive act. It is noted as a boundary case.

---

**kılık değiştirmiş**
This participial phrase (having changed its guise) participates in Group 3 (deceptive presentation) but is a marginal item in P1 with limited constructional range. It is noted without full group assignment.
