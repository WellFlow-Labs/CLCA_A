# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Adjective (or noun in construct) follows or precedes the noun it modifies. Hebrew attributive adjectives are postnominal and agree in gender, number, and definiteness with the head noun. Nouns can also modify nouns in construct chains (סמיכות, smikhut).

### Examples
- חָבֵר אֲמִתִּי / ḥaver ʾamitī / "a true/genuine friend"
- עֵד מְהֵימָן / ʿed meheymen / "a credible witness"

### Notes
Postnominal adjective agrees with head noun in gender, number, and definiteness (e.g., הָעֵד הַמְּהֵימָן, "the credible witness"). Construct-state noun + noun (e.g., עֵדוּת שֶׁקֶר, ʿedut sheqer, "false testimony") is an alternative attributive pattern without adjectival agreement.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
לֹא (lo, "not") precedes the adjective in predicative position; in attributive position, a negative adjective or the negated predicate is used. Hebrew lacks a productive negative attributive prefix on adjectives; negation is typically predicative or expressed via a negative noun (e.g., חוֹסֶר, ḥoser, "lack of").

### Examples
- זֶה לֹא נָכוֹן / ze lo nakhon / "that is not correct/wrong"
- חוֹסֶר כַּנּוּת / ḥoser kannut / "lack of sincerity, insincerity"

### Notes
Predicative negation with לֹא is the primary pattern (לֹא נָכוֹן, לֹא מְהֵימָן, לֹא הָגוּן). The nominal construction חוֹסֶר + abstract noun serves as the standard attributive-equivalent negation for abstract qualities. The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) productively negates abstract nouns (אִי-אֲמִינוּת, ʾi-ʾaminut, "unreliability"; אִי-סְבִירוּת, ʾi-sevirut, "unreasonableness") but not adjectives directly.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
A prepositional phrase or relative clause modifies a noun. In the truth domain, the most productive pattern is noun + שֶׁ- (she-, "that/which") clause, or noun + prepositional phrase specifying scope or temporal validity.

### Examples
- נָכוֹן לְהַיּוֹם / nakhon le-hayom / "correct as of today" (adjective + prepositional scope phrase)
- גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת / garʿin shel ʾemet / "a kernel of truth" (noun + shel-phrase)

### Notes
The pattern nakhon le-[date/time] is a productive formula in administrative and journalistic registers specifying the temporal scope of a truth-claim. The shel-genitive (גַּרְעִין שֶׁל אֱמֶת, חֲצִי אֱמֶת) modifies the quantity or degree of truth attributed to a claim.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
The verb לִהְיוֹת (lihyot, "to be/become") in future or past tense, or the verb לְהַפֹּךְ לְ- (lehafokh le-, "to become"), combined with a truth-domain adjective or noun. Hebrew does not have a dedicated inchoative morphology; becoming is expressed periphrastically.

### Examples
- זֶה הָפַךְ לְאֱמֶת / ze hafakh le-ʾemet / "this became truth / this turned out to be true"
- הַחוֹזֶה הָפַךְ לְבָטֵל / ha-ḥoze hafakh le-batel / "the contract became void"

### Notes
The inchoative is expressed periphrastically with הָפַךְ לְ- (hafakh le-, "became") or נַעֲשָׂה (naʿasah, "became, turned into"). No dedicated inchoative morphological pattern exists for truth-domain adjectives. The construction is compositional and not lexicalized.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
The Hifʿil (הִפְעִיל) binyan functions as the primary morphological causative in Hebrew. In the truth domain, causative meanings ("to make true," "to cause to err," "to make valid") are expressed via Hifʿil forms of relevant roots.

### Examples
- לְהַטְעוֹת / lehatʿot / "to mislead, to cause to err" (Hifʿil of ט-ע-ה)
- לְהַצְדִּיק / lehatsdiq / "to justify, to vindicate" (Hifʿil of צ-ד-ק)

### Notes
The Hifʿil binyan productively derives causatives: הִטְעָה (hitʿah, "caused to err/misled"), הִצְדִּיק (hitsdiq, "justified/vindicated"), הֶאֱמִין (heʾemin, "caused to believe/believed"). A direct causative "to make [something] true" (e.g., *lehaʾemet) is not a productive or common form; the causative in this domain tends to operate on persons (causing someone to err, to believe, to be justified) rather than on propositions.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
The suffix -וּת (-ut) is the primary productive abstract nominalizer in modern Hebrew, attaching to adjectives and participial forms. The pattern Adj + -ut is highly regular across the truth domain.

### Examples
- אֲמִינוּת / ʾaminut / "reliability, credibility" (from אָמִין, ʾamin)
- סְבִירוּת / sevirut / "reasonableness" (from סָבִיר, savir)

### Notes
The -ut suffix is the dominant and productive pattern: מְהֵימָנוּת (meheymanut), עַקְבִּיּוּת (ʿaqbiyut), אֲמִתִּיּוּת (ʾamitiyut), תִּקִינוּת (taqinut), הֲגוּנוּת (hagunot), יְשָׁרוּת (yesharut). The prefix אִי- (ʾi-) combines productively with -ut nominalizations to form negated abstracts (אִי-אֲמִינוּת, אִי-סְבִירוּת, אִי-תִּקִינוּת). Verbal nouns (מַשְׁקַל patterns, e.g., זִיּוּף, דִּיּוּק, סְתִירָה) form a parallel nominalization system for action/process meanings.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
The suffix -ָן (-an) is the primary productive agentive nominalizer, denoting a person characterized by habitual performance of an action or possession of a quality. It attaches to roots rather than to adjectives directly.

### Examples
- שַׁקְרָן / shaqran / "liar, habitual liar" (from root ש-ק-ר)
- דַּיְקָן / dayqan / "a precise/meticulous person, stickler for accuracy" (from root ד-י-ק)

### Notes
The -an agentive is productive for negative or characterological evaluations: שַׁקְרָן (liar), בַּטְלָן (idler). A parallel agentive for "truth-teller" is not lexicalized with -an; the function is served periphrastically (מִי שֶׁאוֹמֵר אֱמֶת, "one who tells the truth") or by participial forms (נֶאֱמָן, neʾeman, "faithful/trustworthy one"). The -an agentives productively generate further -anut abstracts (שַׁקְרָנוּת, דַּיְקָנוּת).

## 8. PREPOSITIONAL ADVERBIAL (additional pattern)

### Pattern
Preposition בְּ- (be-, "in/with") + truth-domain noun forms a highly productive adverbial construction expressing manner or modality of action.

### Examples
- בֶּאֱמֶת / be-ʾemet / "truly, really, in truth"
- בְּכַנּוּת / be-kannut / "sincerely, honestly, frankly"

### Notes
This pattern is exceptionally productive in the truth domain: בְּדִיּוּק (be-diyyuq, "exactly"), בְּיֹשֶׁר (be-yosher, "with integrity"), בְּתֹקֶף (be-toqef, "by virtue of"), בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים (be-ʾemet u-ve-tamim, "in truth and integrity"). These prepositional adverbials function as sincerity markers, manner adverbials, and discourse particles. Several (especially בֶּאֱמֶת, בְּדִיּוּק) have high token frequency in everyday speech and have acquired pragmatic functions beyond their compositional meanings.

## 9. CONCESSIVE / EPISTEMIC FRAME CONSTRUCTION (additional pattern)

### Pattern
A truth-domain adjective or noun heads a subordinating frame construction that introduces a proposition, typically with the complementizer שֶׁ- (she-, "that"). The construction acknowledges a true or correct point, often before a qualification.

### Examples
- נָכוֹן שֶׁהוּא נִיסָּה, אֲבָל... / nakhon she-hu nissa, ʾaval... / "It is true that he tried, but..."
- סָבִיר שֶׁהַמַּצָּב יִשְׁתַּנֶּה / savir she-ha-matsav yishtanneh / "It is reasonable that the situation will change"

### Notes
The pattern [truth-adjective] + שֶׁ- + clause is productive with נָכוֹן (nakhon), סָבִיר (savir), נְכוֹן לְצַיֵּן שֶׁ- (nakhon letsayen she-, "it should be noted that"), and similar items. The נָכוֹן שֶׁ... construction functions specifically as a concessive frame, acknowledging a true premise before introducing a counterpoint; it is among the most frequent discourse patterns in spoken and written Hebrew argumentation.
