# G4 — Cross-Language Derivational and Morphological Strategies in the Truth Domain

---

## 1. Identification of Main Strategies

The following strategies are identified from the documented forms across the eight languages. Each is described in terms of the morphological operation involved, the semantic output, and the evidence base.

---

## 2. Cross-Language Comparison Table

| Strategy Type | Description | Languages Using It | Concrete Examples | Distribution / Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Abstract nominalization via suffix on adjectival/evaluative base** | A suffix attaches to a truth-domain adjective or evaluative root to yield an abstract noun naming the property (truthfulness, accuracy, reliability, etc.) as an entity. The *ʾi-* prefix in he is distinctive in applying productively to *-ut* nominalizations but not to the corresponding adjectives. |
| **Agentive nominalization of truth-domain verbal/adjectival bases** | A suffix or prefix derives a noun denoting a person characterized by a truth-domain property or action (liar, deceiver, verifier, etc.). | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *伪造* → *伪造者*; he *shaqran* (liar, *-an* suffix); sa *satya* → *satyavādin* (*-vādin* compound); el *ψεύτης* (liar, *-ης* suffix); en *fake* → *faker* (*-er*); tl *sinungaling* (liar, lexicalized); sw *uongo* → *mwongo* (*m-* prefix); qu *llulla* → *llullaq* (*-q* suffix) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages.** A consistent asymmetry is documented: negative-pole agents (liars, deceivers, forgers) are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-tellers). In zh, he, el, en, tl, sw, and qu, the concept of "truth-teller" requires either a compound form or a periphrastic construction, while "liar" is a single lexicalized word. This asymmetry is supported across unrelated language families. |
| **Causative derivation from truth-domain bases** | A causative morpheme derives a verb meaning "to make [X] true/correct/genuine" or "to cause [someone] to err/believe falsely." The resulting verb is used in verification, authentication, correction, and deception contexts. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *核实* (verify), *纠正* (correct); he *leʾammet* (to verify, Pi'el of *ʾemet*), *letaqqef* (to validate); sa *satyāpayati* (to verify, causative of *satya*), *pantachiy* (to mislead, qu); el *επαληθεύω* (to verify), *αποδεικνύω* (to prove); en *verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*, *falsify*; tl *patunayan* (*pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*); sw *kuthibitisha* (*-isha* causative); qu *cheqanchay* (*-ncha-* causative verbalizer), *cheqachiy* (*-chi-* causative) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages.** The morphological form of the causative varies considerably: zh uses dedicated lexical verbs; he uses the Pi'el binyan; sa uses the *-aya-* causative suffix; el uses dedicated derived verbs; en uses dedicated derived verbs (*-ate*, *-ify*); tl uses the *pa-…-an* paradigm; sw uses the *-sha/-isha* suffix; qu uses *-ncha-* (on adjectival roots) or *-chi-* (on verbal roots). The semantic output is consistent: an agent causes a truth-domain state to obtain or be established. |
| **Inchoative/stative derivation from truth-domain bases** | A morpheme derives a verb or construction meaning "to become true/genuine/correct" or "to come to be in a truth-domain state." The resulting form describes a change of state rather than a caused action. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *变成真的* (become real, periphrastic); he *hafakh le-ʾemet* (became true, periphrastic); sa *pramā jāyate* (valid cognition arises), *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate* (false knowledge ceases); el *αποδείχτηκε αληθής* (proved to be true); en *proved true*, *came true*; tl *naging totoo* (*naging* + adjective), *totoo na* (particle-marked); sw *ulidhihirika* (*-ika* stative suffix); qu *cheqakay* (*-ka-* intransitive verbalizer) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages**, but the morphological form varies considerably. Quechua and Swahili have dedicated derivational suffixes for the inchoative (*-ka-* and *-ika/-eka* respectively). Hebrew, Greek, and English use periphrastic constructions with a change-of-state verb. Tagalog uses the *naging* construction or the change-of-state particle *na*. Chinese uses periphrastic constructions. Sanskrit uses dedicated intransitive verbs (*jāyate*, *utpadyate*, *nivartate*) that are domain-specific. |
| **Compounding of truth-domain root with a nominal head** | A truth-domain evaluative root combines with a nominal head to yield a compound evaluating the head noun as true/false/genuine/etc. The compound functions as an attributive or predicative expression. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *真话* (true + word), *假话* (false + word), *真相* (true + appearance); he *ʿed ʾemet* (witness of truth), *ʿedut sheqer* (false testimony); sa *satyavādin* (truth-speaker), *asatyavādin* (false-speaker), *cheqa simi* (qu: true + word); el *αληθής αξία* (true value); en *truth-teller*, *half-truth*; tl *cheqa simi* (qu), *totoong kwento* (true story); sw *ukweli wa mambo* (truth of matters), *shahidi wa uongo* (false witness); qu *cheqa simi* (true word), *cheqa sonqo* (true heart), *llulla simi* (false word) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages.** The compounding strategy is productive in all languages, though the internal structure varies (head-final in zh and sa; head-initial in he, el, en, tl, sw; head-initial with linker in tl and sw). The semantic pattern is consistent: a truth-domain evaluative root modifies a nominal head to yield a compound evaluating that head as true, false, genuine, or deceptive. |
| **Reduplication for intensification of truth-domain predicates** | Full or partial reduplication of a truth-domain root or adjective yields an intensified form asserting that something is emphatically or absolutely true/genuine. | zh, tl, sw, qu | zh *真真假假* (true-true-false-false, mixed); tl *totoong-totoo* (absolutely true), *tunay na tunay* (absolutely genuine); sw *kweli kweli* (really truly), *sawa sawa* (exactly right); qu *cheqapuni* (absolutely true, via *-puni* emphatic suffix rather than reduplication proper) | **Moderately attested** (4 of 8 languages). Reduplication as an intensification strategy for truth-domain predicates is documented in zh, tl, and sw. In qu, the emphatic suffix *-puni* serves a parallel function without being reduplication proper. Not documented as a productive strategy in he, sa, el, or en for truth-domain items specifically. The strategy is most productive in tl and sw. |
| **Discourse-marker grammaticalization of truth nominals** | A truth-domain nominal (or a prepositional phrase containing one) grammaticalizes into a sentence-initial or parenthetical discourse marker signaling that the speaker is about to state what is actually the case. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *实际上*, *事实上*, *本质上* (in fact, essentially); he *ha-ʾemet hi she-* (the truth is that); sa *vastutaḥ*, *tattvataḥ* (*-taḥ* adverbials); el *η αλήθεια είναι ότι* (the truth is that); en *to tell the truth*, *truth be told*; tl *sa totoo lang*, *ang totoo* (to tell the truth, the truth is); sw *kwa kweli*, *ukweli ni kwamba* (truly, the truth is that); qu *cheqaqta rimay* (speak the truth, used as a framing device) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages.** The grammaticalization of truth nominals into discourse markers is a consistent cross-linguistic pattern. The degree of grammaticalization varies: in zh, the *-上* adverbials are fully grammaticalized as sentence-level operators; in sa, the *-taḥ* adverbials are conventionalized in commentarial prose; in tl, *sa totoo lang* is a fixed discourse particle; in sw, *kwa kweli* is a conventionalized sincerity frame. In all languages, these constructions signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment of the actual state of affairs. |
| **Pseudo-instance marking via a dedicated morpheme or element** | A morpheme or compound element marks something as resembling but not being a genuine instance of a category (pseudo-science, fallacious reason, apparent truth, etc.). | sa, el, en, zh (partial) | sa *ābhāsa* (semblance, pseudo-): *pramāṇābhāsa* (pseudo-valid means), *hetvābhāsa* (fallacious reason); el *ψευδο-*: *ψευδοεπιστήμη* (pseudoscience), *ψευδοδίλημμα* (false dilemma); en *pseudo-*: *pseudoscience*, *pseudo-intellectual*; zh *伪* (spurious): *伪科学* (pseudoscience), *伪装* (disguise) | **Moderately attested** (4 of 8 languages). The strategy of marking pseudo-instances via a dedicated morpheme is most systematically developed in Sanskrit (*ābhāsa* as a cross-domain marker) and Greek (*ψευδο-* as a productive prefix). English *pseudo-* is a borrowing from Greek. Chinese *伪* functions similarly in some compounds. Not documented as a productive strategy in he, tl, sw, or qu. |
| **Evidential morphology marking source of knowledge** | Obligatory or semi-obligatory morphological markers encode whether the speaker's knowledge is based on direct observation, reported information, or inference/conjecture. | qu (grammaticalized); tl (particle-based, partial) | qu *-m/-mi* (direct evidential), *-s/-si* (reportative), *-chá* (conjectural): *cheqam* (it is true, I know directly), *cheqas* (it is true, reportedly), *cheqachá* (it is probably true); tl *daw* (reportative particle, not morphologically integrated into the predicate) | **Single-language observation** for the grammaticalized system (qu only). The three-way distinction between direct knowledge, reported information, and conjecture is obligatorily marked on predicates in Quechua and has no parallel as a grammatical category in any other language in the corpus. Tagalog has a reportative particle (*daw*) but it is not morphologically integrated into the predicate and is not part of a grammaticalized three-way system. |
| **Reflexive/middle marking on truth-domain causatives** | A reflexive or middle morpheme added to a causative truth-domain verb shifts the action from externally directed to self-directed (verifying for oneself, correcting oneself, etc.). | qu, tl, sw | qu *cheqanchay* (to verify) → *cheqanchakuy* (*-ku-* reflexive: to verify for oneself); tl *patunayan* (to prove) → *pagpapatunayan* (nominalized, self-directed); sw *kuthibitisha* (to confirm) → *kujithibitishia* (to confirm for oneself, *ji-* reflexive) | **Moderately attested** (3 of 8 languages with clear documentation). The reflexive/middle marking on causative truth-domain verbs is documented in qu (*-ku-*), sw (*ji-*), and implied in tl. Not documented as a productive strategy in zh, he, sa, el, or en for truth-domain items specifically. |
| **Passive derivation of truth-domain causative verbs** | A passive morpheme derives a form meaning "to be verified/confirmed/deceived" from a causative truth-domain verb. The resulting form is used in legal, administrative, and narrative contexts. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *核实* → *被核实* (passive with *被*); he *leʾammet* → *meʾummat* (Pu'al passive participle: verified); sa *satyāpita* (past passive participle: verified); el *επαληθεύτηκε* (was verified, passive aorist); en *was verified*, *was authenticated*; tl *napatunayan* (was proven, *na-* completive passive); sw *kuthibitishwa* (*-wa* passive suffix); qu *cheqanchasqa* (*-sqa* participial/resultative) | **Widely attested across all 8 languages.** Passive derivation of causative truth-domain verbs is productive in all languages. The morphological form varies (zh uses the *被* passive construction; he uses the Pu'al binyan; sa uses the *-ita* past passive participle; el uses the passive aorist; en uses the *be* + past participle construction; tl uses *na-* completive; sw uses *-wa*; qu uses *-sqa* participial). The semantic output is consistent: the entity that was verified, confirmed, or deceived is foregrounded as the grammatical subject. |
| **Stative/inchoative suffix distinct from passive** | A stative or inchoative suffix derives a form meaning "to be in a state of" or "to come to be in a state of" a truth-domain property, without implying an external agent. Semantically distinct from the passive. | sw, qu | sw *kudanganyika* (*-ika* stative: to be deceived/to fall for it, no agent implied) vs. *kudanganywa* (*-wa* passive: to be deceived by an agent); qu *cheqakay* (*-ka-* intransitive: to become true) vs. *cheqanchay* (*-ncha-* causative: to make true) | **Single-language or two-language observation.** The morphological distinction between a stative/inchoative (no agent implied) and a passive (agent implied or available) is explicitly documented in sw (*-ika* vs. *-wa*) and qu (*-ka-* vs. *-ncha-*). In other languages, this distinction is expressed lexically or constructionally rather than through dedicated morphological markers. |
| **Templatic or binyan-based derivation** | The root is placed in a morphological template (binyan in Hebrew) that encodes a specific semantic relationship (causative, passive, reflexive, etc.) without a separable suffix. | he | he root *ʾ-m-t*: Qal *ʾemet* (truth), Pi'el *leʾammet* (to verify), Pu'al *meʾummat* (verified); root *ʾ-m-n*: Qal *ʾaman* (to be firm), Hif'il *lehaʾamin* (to believe), Nif'al *neʾeman* (faithful), Pi'el *leʾamen* (to train/confirm) | **Single-language observation** (he only in this corpus). The root-and-pattern (binyan) morphology of Hebrew produces a systematic set of derivational forms from each root, with the semantic relationship encoded in the template rather than in a separable affix. This is structurally distinct from the suffixal and prefixal strategies documented in the other seven languages. |
| **Circumfix nominalization** | A prefix and suffix together (circumfix) attach to a root to yield an abstract nominal. Neither the prefix nor the suffix alone produces the nominalization. | tl | tl *totoo* → *katotohanan* (*ka-* + *totoo* + *-an*); *tapat* → *katapatan*; *sinungaling* → *kasinungalingan*; *tunay* → *katunayan* | **Single-language observation** (tl only in this corpus). The *ka-…-an* circumfix is the primary abstract nominalization strategy in Tagalog and is not paralleled in the other seven languages. The circumfix as a unit produces the abstract nominal; neither *ka-* nor *-an* alone produces the same result. |
| **Discourse particle marking of epistemic stance on truth predicates** | Grammatical particles (not affixes on the predicate itself) mark the speaker's epistemic stance toward a truth claim: surprise, realization, concession, confirmation, etc. | tl | tl *totoo ba?* (*ba*: interrogative/doubt), *totoo na* (*na*: change of state), *totoo pala* (*pala*: realization/discovery), *totoo nga* (*nga*: confirmation/as expected), *totoo naman* (*naman*: concessive/responsive) | **Single-language observation** (tl only in this corpus). The system of discourse particles in Tagalog encodes a range of epistemic stances toward truth predicates that are expressed through dedicated grammatical particles rather than through affixes on the predicate or through separate lexical items. No other language in the corpus documents a comparable system of truth-domain-specific discourse particles. |
| **Emphatic suffix on truth-domain predicates** | A suffix attaches to a truth-domain predicate to assert that the truth or genuineness is absolute, unqualified, or beyond doubt. | qu, tl (partial) | qu *cheqa* → *cheqapuni* (*-puni*: absolutely, without exception), *cheqalla* (*-lla*: purely, only), *cheqallam* (*-lla* + *-m*: it is purely the truth); tl *totoong-totoo* (reduplication as emphatic, see above) | **Moderately attested** (2 of 8 languages with clear documentation). The *-puni* emphatic suffix in Quechua and the *-lla* limitative suffix serve to intensify or restrict truth claims in ways that are expressed through separate adverbs or degree modifiers in other languages (zh *千真万确*; he *bediyyuq*; el *αληθώς*; en *absolutely true*). |
| **Limitative suffix restricting truth claims** | A suffix attaches to a truth-domain predicate to assert that something is purely or exclusively true/genuine, without admixture of falsehood. | qu | qu *cheqa* → *cheqalla* (*-lla*: purely, only, nothing but), *cheqallam* (*-lla* + *-m*: it is purely the truth) | **Single-language observation** (qu only). The *-lla* limitative suffix in Quechua restricts the truth claim to exclude any admixture of falsehood, a function expressed through separate lexical items in other languages (zh *纯粹*, *只有*; en *nothing but the truth*; sw *ukweli mtupu*). |
| **Compound formation with a body-part or inner-self nominal** | A truth-domain evaluative root combines with a body-part or inner-self nominal to yield a compound evaluating a person's inner disposition as honest, sincere, or deceptive. | zh, tl, qu | zh *真心* (true + heart), *诚心* (sincere + heart); tl *tapat na loob* (honest + inner self), *katapatan ng loob* (honesty of inner self); qu *cheqa sonqo* (true + heart/inner self), *cheqa sonqoyoq* (one who has a true heart) | **Moderately attested** (3 of 8 languages with clear documentation). The compounding of a truth-domain root with a body-part or inner-self nominal to evaluate inner disposition is documented in zh (*心*), tl (*loob*), and qu (*sonqo*). In he, *yosher lev* (integrity of heart) is a fixed phrase with a similar structure. Not documented as a productive strategy in sa, el, en, or sw. |
| **Adverbial case-marking of truth-domain adjectives** | A case suffix (typically accusative or ablative) attaches to a truth-domain adjective to derive an adverb meaning "truly," "correctly," or "in truth." | qu, sa | qu *cheqaq* → *cheqaqta* (accusative/adverbial: truly, in truth); sa *satyam* (accusative used adverbially: truly, in truth), *vastutaḥ* (ablative: in reality), *tattvataḥ* (ablative: in truth/reality) | **Moderately attested** (2 of 8 languages with clear documentation). The use of case suffixes to derive adverbs from truth-domain adjectives is documented in qu (*-ta* accusative/adverbial) and sa (*-m* accusative adverbial, *-taḥ* ablative adverbial). In other languages, adverbs are derived through dedicated adverbial suffixes (*-ly* en; *-ώς* el; *-mente* implied in tl borrowings) or through prepositional phrases (*kwa kweli* sw; *beʾemet* he). |

The asymmetry between *-ncha-* (for positive adjectival roots) and *-chi-* (for negative verbal roots) in Quechua is explicitly documented in the data and has no parallel in the other seven languages, where causative formation is typically uniform across evaluative polarity. |
| **Negation of nominalization via a dedicated prefix distinct from adjectival negation** | A prefix negates the abstract nominalization of a truth-domain adjective, while a different strategy (syntactic negation or a different prefix) negates the adjective itself. | he | he: adjective *ʾamin* negated by *lo ʾamin* (syntactic); nominalization *ʾaminut* negated by *ʾi-ʾaminut* (*ʾi-* prefix). The *ʾi-* prefix in Hebrew applies productively to *-ut* nominalizations to yield negated abstract nouns, while the corresponding adjectives are negated syntactically with *lo* or with *bilti*. This creates an asymmetry between the adjectival and nominalized negation systems that is not paralleled in the other seven languages. |
| **Fixed four-character expressions encoding truth-domain evaluations** | A fixed four-morpheme sequence functions as a single evaluative unit, often with internal negation producing an antonym. | zh | zh *名副其实* (name matches reality) / *名不副实* (name does not match reality); *弄虚作假* (engage in fraud); *货真价实* (genuine goods at fair prices); *真金不怕火炼* (true gold fears no fire); *表里如一* (consistent inside and out) / *表里不一* | **Single-language observation** (zh only). | **Light-verb construction asymmetry for truth vs. falsehood** | Different light verbs are used with truth-domain nominals for the positive pole (truth-telling) vs. the negative pole (lying), creating a constructional asymmetry. | sw, zh | sw: *sema ukweli* (speak + truth, for truth-telling) vs. *piga uongo* (strike/do + falsehood, for lying); zh: *说真话* (say + true words) vs. *说假话* (say + false words) — symmetric in zh; *打假* (strike + fake, for anti-counterfeiting) — asymmetric | **Moderately attested** (2 of 8 languages with clear documentation). The light-verb asymmetry is most clearly documented in sw, where *sema* (speak) is used for truth-telling and *piga* (strike/do) is used for lying, with no *piga kweli* construction attested. In zh, the *说* + *真话/假话* construction is symmetric, but *打假* (combat fakes) uses a different verb. Not documented as a productive asymmetry in he, sa, el, en, tl, or qu. |

## 3. Narrative Analysis

### 3.1 Patterns recurring across unrelated language families

**Abstract nominalization of truth-domain adjectives** is the most consistently attested derivational strategy across the corpus. All eight languages — spanning Sino-Tibetan (zh), Semitic (he), Indo-European (sa, el, en), Austronesian (tl), Bantu (sw), and Quechuan (qu) — derive abstract property nouns from truth-domain evaluative bases. The morphological form of the nominalizing element varies considerably: a suffix in he (*-ut*), sa (*-tā/-tva*), el (*-ότητα*), en (*-ity/-ness*); a prefix in sw (*u-*); a circumfix in tl (*ka-…-an*); a copular nominalization in qu (*-kay*); and a suffix in zh (*-性*). Despite this formal variation, the semantic output is consistent: a nominal that names the abstract property (truthfulness, accuracy, reliability) as an entity that can be the subject or object of predication. The resulting nominals are used in formal, legal, and philosophical registers across all languages, suggesting that the communicative need for abstract property nouns in these registers is cross-linguistically robust.

**The agentive asymmetry** — negative-pole agents (liars, deceivers, forgers) more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-tellers) — is documented across all eight languages and across unrelated language families. In zh, the *-者* suffix yields *伪造者* (forger) and *欺骗者* (deceiver) but no parallel single-word truth-teller. In he, *shaqran* (liar) is a lexicalized agentive but no *-an* agentive for truth-teller is attested. In el, *ψεύτης* (liar) is a common word but no single-word truth-teller is lexicalized. In en, *faker*, *counterfeiter*, and *fabricator* are single words but *truth-teller* requires a compound. In tl, *sinungaling* (liar) is a lexicalized root but no parallel agentive for honest person is documented. In sw, *mwongo* (liar) and *mdanganyifu* (deceiver) are single words but *mtu wa kweli* (truth-teller) requires a periphrastic construction. In qu, *llullaq* (liar) is a productive agentive but *cheqaq* functions primarily as an adjective rather than a person-characterization. This asymmetry is supported across unrelated language families and is one of the most robust cross-linguistic patterns in the corpus.

**Discourse-marker grammaticalization of truth nominals** is attested in all eight languages. In every language, a truth-domain nominal or a phrase containing one has grammaticalized (to varying degrees) into a sentence-initial or parenthetical marker signaling that the speaker is about to state what is actually the case. The degree of grammaticalization varies from fully grammaticalized adverbials (zh *实际上*, *事实上*; sa *vastutaḥ*) to conventionalized fixed phrases (he *ha-ʾemet hi she-*; el *η αλήθεια είναι ότι*; en *to tell the truth*; tl *sa totoo lang*; sw *kwa kweli*; qu *cheqaqta rimay*). The cross-linguistic consistency of this pattern across unrelated language families suggests that the pragmatic function of signaling frank or corrective speech is a robust communicative need that is met through grammaticalization of truth vocabulary in all languages examined.

**Causative derivation for verification and authentication** is attested in all eight languages. The morphological form varies (zh uses dedicated lexical verbs; he uses the Pi'el binyan; sa uses the *-aya-* causative; el and en use dedicated derived verbs; tl uses the *pa-…-an* paradigm; sw uses *-sha/-isha*; qu uses *-ncha-* or *-chi-*), but the semantic output is consistent: an agent causes a truth-domain state to obtain or be established. This pattern is productive in all languages and is particularly associated with legal, administrative, and journalistic registers.

**Proverbial or semi-fixed expressions asserting that truth is painful, endures, or will be revealed** are attested in at least six of the eight languages (zh, he, el, en, tl, sw; not clearly documented in sa and qu). The cross-linguistic consistency of this pattern across unrelated language families suggests that the cultural encoding of truth as a value that is difficult but ultimately inescapable is widespread, though it is not universal in the corpus.

### 3.2 Striking asymmetries

**The agentive asymmetry** (negative-pole agents lexicalized, positive-pole agents requiring periphrasis) is the most striking asymmetry in the corpus. It is documented across all eight languages and across unrelated language families. The asymmetry is not explained by any single morphological factor: it appears in languages with agentive suffixes (zh *-者*, he *-an*, el *-ης*, en *-er*, qu *-q*), in languages with agentive prefixes (sw *m-*), and in languages with lexicalized agentive roots (tl *sinungaling*). The asymmetry is therefore not a morphological accident but a consistent semantic pattern: the concept of "one who lies" is more consistently lexicalized as a single word than the concept of "one who tells the truth."

**The causative pathway asymmetry in Quechua** is a striking single-language asymmetry: the *-ncha-* causative verbalizer applies to positive-pole adjectival roots (*cheqa* → *cheqanchay*, *allin* → *allinchay*, *sut'i* → *sut'inchay*) but not to the negative-pole root *llulla*, which instead uses *-chi-* directly (*llullachiy*). This asymmetry — different causative pathways for positive and negative evaluative roots — is documented in the data and has no parallel in the other seven languages.

**The *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry in Hebrew** is a striking morphological asymmetry: the *ʾi-* prefix negates *-ut* nominalizations (*ʾi-ʾaminut*, *ʾi-sevirut*, *ʾi-diyyuq*) but not the corresponding adjectives, which are negated syntactically with *lo* or with *bilti*. This creates an asymmetry between the adjectival and nominalized negation systems that is not paralleled in the other seven languages.

**The light-verb asymmetry in Swahili** (*sema ukweli* for truth-telling vs. *piga uongo* for lying) is a striking constructional asymmetry: the two poles of the truth/falsehood domain are expressed through different light verbs, with no *piga kweli* construction attested. This asymmetry is explicitly documented in the data and is not paralleled in the other seven languages.

### 3.3 One-way or blocked derivational pathways

**No BECOME form is attested for *llulla* in Quechua.** The *-ka-* intransitive verbalizer is productive for positive-pole roots (*cheqakay*, *allinkay*) but no *llullakay* (to become false/deceptive) is documented. This is a one-way pathway: the inchoative is available for the positive pole but blocked for the negative pole.

**No *-性* nominalization is attested for monosyllabic roots in Chinese.** The *-性* suffix is productive for disyllabic adjectival compounds (*真实性*, *可靠性*, *正确性*) but not for monosyllabic roots (*真*, *假*). This is a morphological restriction on the nominalization strategy that creates a gap in the derivational paradigm.

**No *-ncha-* causative is attested for *llulla* in Quechua** (see above). The *-ncha-* pathway is restricted to positive-pole adjectival roots; the negative-pole root uses a different causative strategy.

**No *piga kweli* construction is attested in Swahili.** The *piga* light verb is restricted to the negative pole (*piga uongo*, to lie); the positive pole uses a different light verb (*sema ukweli*, to tell the truth). This is a one-way constructional restriction.

**No agentive nominalization for "truth-teller" via simple suffixation is attested in most languages.** In zh, he, el, en, tl, sw, and qu, the concept of "one who tells the truth" requires either a compound form or a periphrastic construction, while "liar" is a single lexicalized word. This is a consistent one-way gap across unrelated language families.

### 3.4 Productivity, restriction, lexicalization, and unclear attestation

**Productive strategies** (freely generating new forms in the data):
- Abstract nominalization via suffix/prefix/circumfix: productive in all 8 languages
- Prefixal negation of truth-domain adjectives: productive in sa, el, en, he (on nominalizations), tl; syntactic in zh, sw, qu
- Causative derivation for verification/authentication: productive in all 8 languages
- Compounding of truth-domain root with nominal head: productive in all 8 languages
- *-ncha-* causative verbalizer on positive-pole adjectival roots: productive in qu
- *ψευδο-* prefix for pseudo-instances: productive in el, en
- *ābhāsa* element for pseudo-instances: productive in sa

**Restricted strategies** (attested but not freely productive):
- Reduplication for intensification: productive in tl and sw; restricted in zh (specific fixed forms); not attested in he, sa, el, en
- Reflexive/middle marking on causative truth-domain verbs: attested in qu, sw; not documented in zh, he, sa, el, en
- Adverbial case-marking of truth-domain adjectives: attested in qu and sa; not documented in zh, he, el, en, tl, sw
- Compound formation with body-part/inner-self nominal: attested in zh, tl, qu; restricted in he (fixed phrase *yosher lev*); not documented in sa, el, en, sw
- Emphatic suffix on truth-domain predicates (*-puni*, *-lla*): restricted to qu

**Lexicalized or fossilized patterns** (attested as fixed forms, not freely productive):
- Four-character expressions in zh (*名副其实*, *弄虚作假*, etc.): lexicalized fixed forms; internal negation is productive within the format but the base expressions are fixed
- *beʾemet* in he: grammaticalized sincerity adverbial; no longer fully compositional
- *bediyyuq* in he: grammaticalized precision adverb; has extended beyond the truth domain
- *kwa kweli* in sw: conventionalized sincerity frame; partially grammaticalized
- *sa totoo lang* in tl: conventionalized discourse particle; partially grammaticalized
- *cheqam* in qu: the direct evidential clitic on *cheqa* is a grammaticalized assertive formula
- *ama llulla, ama suwa, ama qella* in qu: a culturally fixed prohibitive triad; not freely productive

**Unclear or weakly attested patterns:**
- *cheqasqa* in qu (past participial directly on adjectival root): the morphological analysis is debated in the data; marked [UNCERTAIN]
- *cheqaqsiy* in qu (reflexive verbalizer on *cheqaq*): less frequently attested than *cheqaqchay*; marked [UNCERTAIN]
- *halisisha* in sw (causative of *halisi*): attested but less frequent; marked [UNCERTAIN]
- *uhalisi* and *uhalisia* in sw: the distinction between the two nominalizations is not fully established; both marked [MARGINAL]
- *chiqap* in qu: the relationship to *cheqa* and its precise distribution across varieties is not fully established; marked [UNCERTAIN]
- *paylla* in qu: the evaluative use in authenticity contexts is less clearly documented; marked [UNCERTAIN]
- The *D10* dimension (correspondence between label and actual nature) is tentatively supported but only clearly lexicalized as a distinct grouping in zh and sa; in most other languages it is covered by D1 or D2 vocabulary without a dedicated grouping
