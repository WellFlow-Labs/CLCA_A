## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION absorbed by BECOME periphrases

P4a and P4b document no structurally distinct REVELATION construction in English. The two periphrastic BECOME operators — *prove* + ADJ and *turn out to be* + ADJ — carry both the inchoative reading (entry into a truth-domain state) and the retrospective-discovery reading (disclosure that a state already obtained). The distinction between these two readings is encoded in the semantics of the individual verb rather than in a separate construction:

- *prove* + ADJ tends toward formal, written registers and encodes retrospective confirmation upon investigation.
- *turn out to be* + ADJ is register-neutral and encodes discovery, often with an implication of surprise.

Neither construction is structurally dedicated to REVELATION alone. REVELATION is therefore **SUBSTITUTED BY BECOME** in English, with the two periphrastic operators (*prove*, *turn out to be*) serving both functions simultaneously.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction type

P4a §5 and P4b §5 document that VERIFICATION (*verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*) and DISCONFIRMATION (*invalidate*, *falsify*, *delegitimize*) are expressed by the same construction type: a derived causative-evidential verb taking an NP object. No structural feature distinguishes a VERIFICATION construction from a DISCONFIRMATION construction; the distinction is carried entirely by the semantics of the individual verb. The same periphrastic fallback (*make/render* + NP + ADJ) is available for both, though it is [MARGINAL] in formal registers.

Additionally, VERIFICATION and CAUSATION (factual) share the same construction type. The verbs *verify*, *validate*, and *authenticate* can be read as either causing a state to be established (CAUSATION) or confirming a pre-existing state (VERIFICATION); the construction itself does not disambiguate.

### 2.3 NEGATION: analytic and morphological strategies coexist; neither is universal

P4a §2 and P4b §2 document three coexisting negation strategies:

- **Morphological (prefixal)**: *inaccurate*, *invalid*, *illegitimate*, *insincere*, *dishonest*, *implausible*, *untruthful*, *inauthentic*, *unreliable*. Available for most Latinate bases; formal and written register preference.
- **Analytic (sentential)**: *not* + adjective. Universally available for all items in the domain without exception; the default fallback.
- **Lexical (antonym substitution)**: *false*, *fake*, *bogus*, *counterfeit*, *spurious*, *fraudulent*. Available for selected items; these carry additional connotations (deliberate deception, material forgery, pretense of validity) not present in simple negation.

The three strategies are not fully interchangeable. *Disingenuous* and *spurious* carry connotations of strategic concealment and pretense respectively that *not genuine* and *not valid* do not replicate. *Fake* and *bogus* imply deliberate deception in a way that *not genuine* does not. The choice of strategy is conditioned by the morphological origin of the base (Latinate vs. Germanic), register, and the specific connotational profile required.

### 2.4 Positive/negative asymmetry in agentive nominalization

P4a §7 and P4b §7 document a consistent asymmetry: agent nouns for the negative/deceptive pole (*faker*, *counterfeiter*, *falsifier*, *fabricator*, *fraudster*) are formed by productive suffixation (*-er*, *-ster*) on the corresponding verb and are [CORE] in legal and journalistic registers. Agent nouns for the positive/honest pole are not formed by suffixation on truth-domain adjectives (forms such as *\*truer* and *\*truther* are non-standard); instead, the compound *truth-teller* serves this function and is rated [MARGINAL]. This asymmetry — richer morphological expression for the deceptive agent than for the honest agent — is consistent across P4a and P4b.

### 2.5 CAUSATION (character) has no dedicated construction

No construction in P4a or P4b is documented as directly causing a person to be characterized as honest or dishonest in the way that CAUSATION (factual) verbs (*validate*, *invalidate*) operate on propositions. The closest attested expressions involve agentive nominals used in predicative contexts (*expose someone as a faker*; *establish someone's credibility*) or ATTR constructions with person-denoting subjects (*his testimony was truthful*). These are circumlocutions rather than a dedicated CAUSATION (character) construction.

### 2.6 Partial-truth constructions occupy a distinct functional space

P4a §10 and P4b §10 document a set of constructions (*a grain of truth*, *a kernel of truth*, *a half-truth*, *stretch the truth*, *bend the truth*, *be economical with the truth*) that express partial or distorted truth. These constructions have no single-word equivalents and are not reducible to NEGATION (which expresses total absence of a truth-domain property) or to ATTR (which expresses full presence). They occupy the functional space between full truth and full falsity. No CLCA canonical operator directly covers this space; these constructions are documented here as a distinct functional cluster with no canonical mapping.

### 2.7 Discourse-marker constructions are syntactically distinct from all canonical operators

P4a §11 and P4b §11 document a set of sentence-initial and parenthetical constructions (*to be honest*, *truth be told*, *in truth*, *true enough*, *honestly*) that do not predicate a truth-domain adjective of a propositional subject. They function as pragmatic framing devices. These constructions do not map to any of the ten canonical CLCA operators, which all involve predication of a truth-domain property. They are noted here as a distinct construction type with no canonical mapping.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **CAUSATION (character): MISSING (structural gap)** | No construction in P4a or P4b directly causes a person to be characterized as honest or dishonest as a productive pattern. Circumlocution is required (e.g., *expose someone as a faker*). |
| **REVELATION (dedicated construction): MISSING (structural gap)** | No structurally distinct REVELATION construction exists. The function is absorbed by BECOME periphrases (*prove* + ADJ, *turn out to be* + ADJ). |
| **Morphological inchoative: MISSING (structural gap)** | No morphological inchoative exists in English for this domain. All inchoative meaning is expressed periphrastically. |
| **Morphological causative: MISSING (structural gap)** | No productive morphological causative suffix covers the full domain. Causative meaning is lexicalized in derived verbs or expressed periphrastically. |
| **Partial-truth operator: MISSING (structural gap)** | No canonical CLCA operator covers the partial-truth functional space. English expresses this through fixed idiomatic constructions (*a grain of truth*, *economical with the truth*) with no single-word equivalents. |

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **Causative verbs for some adjectives** | Derived causative verbs are not available for all truth-domain adjectives. *\*Genuinize*, *\*accuratize*, *\*plausibilize* are unattested; periphrasis (*make it genuine*, *render it accurate*) is required. |
| **Prefixal negation for some adjectives** | *\*Ungenuine* and *\*unauthentic* are non-standard; *not genuine* and *inauthentic* serve instead. Prefixal negation is unavailable for native Germanic items (*fake*, *ersatz*) and fixed expressions (*bona fide*). |
| **Agentive nominalization for positive pole** | No suffixed agent noun is derived directly from *true*, *honest*, *sincere*, *accurate*, *genuine*, or *authentic* in standard English. The compound *truth-teller* is the primary positive-pole agent nominal and is [MARGINAL]. |
| **Nominalizing suffixes for fixed expressions** | *Bona fide*, *ersatz*, and the bound prefix *pseudo-* do not take English nominalizing suffixes. |
| **Non-productive *-th* suffix** | The suffix *-th* in *truth* is synchronically non-productive; no new *-th* nominalizations are formed in this domain. |
| **Non-productive *-y* suffix** | *Honesty* is the only *-y* nominalization in this domain; the suffix is not productive beyond this item. |
| **Non-productive *-hood* suffix** | *Falsehood* is the only *-hood* nominalization in this domain; the suffix is not productive beyond this item. |

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **No dedicated lexical item for "technically accurate but misleading"** | P4a §8 notes that *technically accurate* asserts narrow truth while implying broader misleadingness, and that no dedicated lexical item exists for this concept. The meaning is expressed only through the degree-modification construction *technically* + *accurate*. |
| **No single-word equivalent for partial-truth expressions** | *A grain of truth*, *a half-truth*, *economical with the truth* have no single-word equivalents in the domain. |

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| **Positive-pole agentive nominalization is [MARGINAL]; negative-pole is [CORE]** | Agent nouns for the deceptive/fraud cluster (*faker*, *counterfeiter*, *falsifier*, *fraudster*) are [CORE] with productive suffixation. Agent nouns for the honest/truth-telling role (*truth-teller*) are [MARGINAL] and formed as compounds rather than by suffixation. *Liar* is the dominant negative-pole agent nominal but is an independent lexeme not derived from any P1 item. |
| **Prefixal negation available for Latinate bases, unavailable for Germanic bases** | Latinate adjectives (*accurate*, *valid*, *sincere*, *legitimate*, *plausible*) have productive prefixed negatives. Germanic and mixed items (*fake*, *ersatz*) and fixed expressions (*bona fide*) do not. |
| **Inchoative *come true* restricted to positive pole** | *Come true* is lexicalized and restricted to *true* with subjects denoting wishes, predictions, or fears. No parallel lexicalized inchoative exists for *false* or other negative-pole items. |
| **Competing nominalizations concentrated at negative pole** | *Falsity* / *falsehood* / *falseness* are three competing nominalizations for the negative pole, with register and semantic differentiation. The positive pole (*truth*) has a single dominant form. |
