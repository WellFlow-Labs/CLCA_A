# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 Natural Semantic Grouping
## Language: Korean (ko)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which items appear in the same collocations, which are contrasted with each other, which share the same verbal partners, which appear in the same institutional registers, and which participate in the same evaluative constructions. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect what the data supports.

---

## Group 1: Items Used to Assert or Deny That a Statement Corresponds to What Actually Happened

**Label:** Factual correspondence markers

**Definition:** These items are used primarily to evaluate whether a specific claim, report, or statement matches what actually occurred or exists. They appear in constructions that affirm, deny, or investigate correspondence between a proposition and a state of affairs. They are the items most commonly used in the construction 사실이다 / 사실이 아니다 and in collocations with 확인하다 (to verify), 왜곡하다 (to distort), and 직시하다 (to face squarely).

**Representative expressions:**
- 사실 (sasil) — "fact, actuality; in fact"
- 사실이다 (sasil-ida) — "to be a fact, to be the case"
- 사실이 아니다 (sasil-i anida) — "that is not the case"
- 사실무근 (sasil-mugeun) — "without factual basis, groundless"
- 사실로 드러나다 (sasil-ro deureonada) — "to turn out to be true"
- 사실을 확인하다 (sasil-eul hwagin-hada) — "to verify the facts"
- 사실을 직시하다 (sasil-eul jiksihada) — "to face the facts squarely"
- 사실을 왜곡하다 (sasil-eul waegokada) — "to distort the facts"
- 사실 여부를 따지다 (sasil yeobu-reul ttajida) — "to examine whether something is factual"
- 실제로 일어난 일 (siljero ileonan il) — "what actually happened"
- 허위사실 (heowi-sasil) — "false facts, fabricated claims presented as facts"

**Notes:** 사실 is the central item in this group. 실제 (silje) overlaps here when used in the construction 실제와 다르다 (to differ from reality), but 실제 also belongs to Group 2. 허위사실 belongs here because it specifically concerns false claims presented as factual, but 허위 more broadly belongs to Group 4.

---

## Group 2: Items Used to Contrast What Is Actually the Case with What Appears, Is Claimed, or Is Theoretical

**Label:** Actuality-versus-appearance markers

**Definition:** These items are used to draw a contrast between what is genuinely or actually the case and what is merely apparent, officially stated, formally designated, or theoretically posited. They appear in constructions like 실제로 (in reality), 실상을 파악하다 (to grasp the actual state of affairs), and 실제 상황과 괴리가 있다 (there is a gap between the actual situation and something). The contrast they mark is not primarily between true and false propositions but between surface and underlying reality.

**Representative expressions:**
- 실제 (silje) — "reality, actuality; actual, real in practice"
- 실제로 (siljero) — "actually, in reality, in practice"
- 실상 (silsang) — "the actual state of affairs, the reality"
- 실태 (siltae) — "actual conditions, the situation as it actually is"
- 실태 조사 (siltae josa) — "survey of actual conditions"
- 실체 (silche) — "substance, the real thing, actual identity"
- 실체를 드러내다 (silche-reul deureonada) — "to reveal the true substance or identity"
- 실제 상황과 괴리가 있다 (silje sanghwang-gwa goeri-ga itda) — "there is a gap between the actual situation and something"
- 실제와 다르다 (silje-wa dareuda) — "to differ from reality"
- 사실상 (sasilsang) — "in effect, de facto, for all intents and purposes"
- 진상 (jinsang) — "the true state of affairs, what actually happened"
- 진상 규명 (jinsang gyumyeong) — "clarification of the true facts"

**Notes:** 사실상 (sasilsang) belongs here rather than in Group 1 because its function is to assert de facto status despite formal or official appearances, not to verify a proposition. 진상 belongs here because it is used specifically in investigative contexts where the gap between official account and actual events is at issue. 실체 overlaps with Group 5 when used in philosophical contexts.

## Group 3: Items Used in Evaluating Whether an Object, Document, or Person Is the Genuine Article Rather Than an Imitation or Forgery

**Label:** Genuineness-versus-counterfeit markers

**Definition:** These items are used to evaluate whether a physical item, document, artwork, or identity is what it purports to be, as opposed to being a copy, forgery, or imitation. They appear in contexts of authentication, fraud investigation, and consumer protection. The contrast they mark is between 진짜 (genuine) and 가짜 (fake), or between 진품 (authentic item) and 위조품 (forged item). The evaluative question is one of identity and provenance rather than propositional truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진짜 (jinjja) — "real, genuine; really, truly"
- 가짜 (gajja) — "fake, counterfeit, imitation"
- 진짜배기 (jinjja-baegi) — "the real thing, the genuine article"
- 진품 (jinpum) — "genuine article, authentic item"
- 위조 (wijo) — "forgery, counterfeiting"
- 위조하다 (wijo-hada) — "to forge, to counterfeit"
- 위조품 (wijopum) — "forged item, counterfeit goods"
- 위조문서 (wijo-munseo) — "forged document"
- 위조지폐 (wijo-jipye) — "counterfeit banknote"
- 위작 (wijak) — "forgery of an artwork or literary work"
- 진본 (jinbon) — "authentic original"
- 진본 여부를 감정하다 (jinbon yeobu-reul gamjeong-hada) — "to appraise whether something is the authentic original"
- 진위 (jinwi) — "truth or falsity, authenticity"
- 진위를 가리다 (jinwi-reul garida) — "to determine the truth or falsity of something"
- 가짜를 진짜로 속이다 (gajja-reul jinjja-ro sogida) — "to pass off a fake as genuine"
- 가짜로 판명되다 (gajja-ro panmyeong-doeda) — "to be determined to be fake"
- 위조로 판명되다 (wijo-ro panmyeong-doeda) — "to be determined to be a forgery"
- 진짜인지 가짜인지 모르다 (jinjja-inji gajja-inji moreuda) — "unable to tell the genuine from the counterfeit"

**Notes:** 진위 (jinwi) is placed here because its primary collocational context in the data is authentication of objects and documents (진위를 가리다, 진위 여부). However, it can also frame propositional questions and overlaps with Group 1. 진짜 also functions as an intensifying adverb (진짜 좋다, "really good") that has no connection to this group; that use is not represented here.

## Group 4: Items Used in Formal and Legal Contexts to Characterize Statements, Reports, or Representations as False

**Label:** Formal falsity designators

**Definition:** These items appear primarily in legal, administrative, and journalistic registers to designate statements, documents, or representations as false. They are used in official contexts — criminal law, administrative proceedings, regulatory enforcement — where the falsity of a claim has legal consequences. They contrast with the everyday vocabulary of lying (거짓말) and tend to appear in fixed legal compounds.

**Representative expressions:**
- 허위 (heowi) — "falsehood, falsity, misrepresentation"
- 허위진술 (heowi-jinsul) — "false statement"
- 허위신고 (heowi-singo) — "false report, false declaration"
- 허위광고 (heowi-gwanggo) — "false advertising"
- 허위사실 유포 (heowi-sasil yupyo) — "spreading false information"
- 허위임이 밝혀지다 (heowi-im-i balkhyeojida) — "to be revealed as false"
- 위증 (wijeung) — "perjury, false testimony"
- 위증하다 (wijeung-hada) — "to commit perjury"
- 위증죄 (wijeungjoe) — "the crime of perjury"
- 허위로 꾸미다 (heowi-ro kkumida) — "to fabricate, to construct falsely"

**Notes:** 거짓 and 거짓말 are not placed in this group because their primary register is everyday speech rather than formal legal designation. 위조 overlaps with this group (forged documents are a legal matter) but is placed primarily in Group 3 because its core contrast is genuineness-versus-counterfeit rather than true-versus-false statement. 위증 is marked [MARGINAL] in P1 and P2 because of its domain specificity; it is included here as the clearest representative of legal falsity designation.

## Group 5: Items Used to Evaluate Whether a Person's Expressed Feelings, Commitments, or Actions Reflect Their Actual Inner State

**Label:** Inner-state authenticity markers

**Definition:** These items are used to evaluate whether a person's outward expressions — apologies, political commitments, artistic works, declarations of feeling — reflect genuine inner conviction or are performed, strategic, or hollow. They appear in constructions like 진정성 있다/없다 (to have/lack authenticity) and 진정성 있는 사과 (a sincere apology). The evaluative question is not whether a proposition is true but whether a person's expression is genuine.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진정성 (jinjeonseong) — "authenticity, genuineness, sincerity"
- 진정성 있다 (jinjeonseong itda) — "to be authentic, to have genuineness"
- 진정성 없다 (jinjeonseong eopda) — "to lack authenticity, to be inauthentic"
- 진정성 있는 사과 (jinjeonseong inneun saga) — "a sincere apology"
- 진정성을 의심받다 (jinjeonseong-eul uisim-batda) — "to have one's authenticity doubted"
- 진정성을 보여주다 (jinjeonseong-eul boyeojuda) — "to demonstrate authenticity"
- 진정성 논란에 휩싸이다 (jinjeonseong nollan-e hwipsaida) — "to be embroiled in a sincerity controversy"
- 진정한 (jinjeongan) — "true, genuine (attributive)"
- 진정으로 (jinjeongeuro) — "truly, genuinely, sincerely"
- 위선 (wiseon) — "hypocrisy, false virtue"
- 위선자 (wiseonja) — "hypocrite"
- 위선적 (wiseonjeok) — "hypocritical"
- 참되다 (cham-doeda) — "to be true, to be genuine"
- 진실하다 (jinsil-hada) — "to be truthful, to be sincere"
- 진실되다 (jinsil-doeda) — "to be truthful, to be genuine"

**Notes:** 위선 (hypocrisy) belongs here because its defining contrast is between professed virtue and actual inner character — the same inner-outer gap that 진정성 addresses, but from the negative side. 진실하다 and 진실되다 overlap with Group 6 (honesty in speech) but are placed here because their primary evaluative target in the data is a person's character or inner state rather than the truth-value of a specific statement. 참되다 appears primarily in literary and religious registers evaluating genuine character.

---

## Group 6: Items Used to Evaluate Whether a Person Speaks or Acts Without Deception

**Label:** Honesty-in-conduct markers

**Definition:** These items are used to evaluate persons or their conduct with respect to whether they tell the truth and refrain from deception. They appear in moral, educational, and interpersonal contexts and participate in constructions like 정직하게 말하다 (to speak honestly) and 정직이 최선의 방책이다 (honesty is the best policy). The evaluative target is a person's habitual disposition or a specific act of communication, not the truth-value of a proposition.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정직 (jeongjik) — "honesty, integrity, straightforwardness"
- 정직하다 (jeongjik-hada) — "to be honest"
- 정직하게 말하다 (jeongjik-age malhada) — "to speak honestly"
- 정직히 (jeongjiki) — "honestly, straightforwardly"
- 정직이 최선의 방책이다 (jeongjik-i choesonui bangchaek-ida) — "honesty is the best policy"
- 정직하고 성실하다 (jeongjik-ago seongsil-hada) — "to be honest and diligent"
- 거짓말 (geojitmal) — "lie, falsehood"
- 거짓말하다 (geojitmal-hada) — "to lie, to tell a lie"
- 거짓말쟁이 (geojitmal-jaengi) — "liar"
- 거짓말이 들통나다 (geojitmal-i deultongnaDA) — "the lie is found out"
- 거짓말을 밥 먹듯 하다 (geojitmal-eul bap meokdeut hada) — "to lie habitually"
- 새빨간 거짓말 (saeppalgan geojitmal) — "a blatant lie"
- 거짓말도 방편이다 (geojitmal-do bangpyeon-ida) — "even a lie can be a means to an end"
- 거짓 없이 말하다 (geojit eopsi malhada) — "to speak without falsehood"
- 진실을 말하다 (jinsil-eul malhada) — "to tell the truth"
- 참말 (chammal) — "true words, truthful speech"

**Notes:** 거짓말 is placed here rather than in Group 4 because its register is everyday speech and its evaluative target is a person's act of deception, not a formal legal designation. 진실을 말하다 overlaps with Group 7 (truth as something to be revealed or concealed) but is placed here because the collocation emphasizes the speaker's honest conduct. 정직 and 거짓말 are natural contrasts within this group, as the data shows them appearing in paired evaluative contexts.

---

## Group 7: Items Used in Contexts Where Truth Is Something That Can Be Revealed, Concealed, Distorted, or Investigated

**Label:** Truth-as-object-of-investigation markers

**Definition:** These items appear in constructions where truth is treated as something that exists independently and can be brought to light, hidden, distorted, or officially established through investigation. They are characteristic of journalistic, legal, and political discourse and appear with verbs like 밝히다 (to reveal), 숨기다 (to conceal), 왜곡하다 (to distort), 드러나다 (to emerge), and 규명하다 (to clarify through investigation). The focus is on the process of establishing or suppressing truth rather than on evaluating a specific proposition.

**Representative expressions:**
- 진실을 밝히다 (jinsil-eul balkhida) — "to reveal the truth"
- 진실을 숨기다 (jinsil-eul sumgida) — "to conceal the truth"
- 진실을 왜곡하다 (jinsil-eul waegokada) — "to distort the truth"
- 진실을 외면하다 (jinsil-eul oemyeonhada) — "to turn away from the truth"
- 진실이 밝혀지다 (jinsil-i balkhyeojida) — "the truth comes to light"
- 진실이 드러나다 (jinsil-i deureonada) — "the truth emerges"
- 진실과 거짓을 가리다 (jinsil-gwa geojit-eul garida) — "to distinguish truth from falsehood"
- 진상 규명 (jinsang gyumyeong) — "clarification of the true facts"
- 진상 조사 (jinsang josa) — "fact-finding investigation"
- 진실화해위원회 (jinsil-hwahae-wiwonhoe) — "Truth and Reconciliation Commission"
- 거짓이 드러나다 (geojit-i deureonada) — "the falsehood is exposed"
- 진위를 둘러싼 논란 (jinwi-reul dulleossan nollan) — "controversy surrounding the question of authenticity"
- 진실의 순간 (jinsil-ui sungan) — "moment of truth"

**Notes:** This group is defined by the verbal constructions that appear with 진실 as object or subject, not by 진실 itself. 진실 as a noun participates in multiple groups (Groups 6, 7, and potentially Group 1), but the constructions in this group are specifically those where truth is treated as a hidden or contested object to be uncovered. 진상 belongs here because it appears almost exclusively in investigative collocations (진상 규명, 진상 조사).

## Group 8: Items Used to Evaluate Whether a Claim Has Adequate Evidential or Rational Support

**Label:** Groundedness evaluators

**Definition:** These items are used to assess whether a claim, argument, or judgment is adequately supported by evidence or reasoning. They appear in constructions like 근거가 있다/없다 (to be grounded/ungrounded), 근거 없는 주장 (unsubstantiated claim), and 타당성을 검토하다 (to review the validity of). The evaluative question is not whether a claim is true but whether it is sufficiently supported to be taken seriously.

**Representative expressions:**
- 근거 (geungeo) — "basis, grounds, evidence"
- 근거가 있다 (geungeo itda) — "to be grounded, to have a basis"
- 근거가 없다 (geungeo eopda) — "to be groundless, to lack basis"
- 근거 없는 주장 (geungeo eomneun jujang) — "unsubstantiated claim"
- 근거를 제시하다 (geungeo-reul jesihada) — "to present evidence"
- 근거를 대다 (geungeo-reul daeda) — "to give grounds, to back up a claim"
- 충분한 근거가 있다 (chungbunhan geungeo-ga itda) — "there is sufficient evidence"
- ~에 근거한 판단 (~e geungeuhan pandan) — "a judgment based on ~"
- 타당 (tadang) — "validity, soundness, appropriateness"
- 타당하다 (tadang-hada) — "to be valid, to be sound"
- 타당성 (tadangseong) — "validity, soundness as a quality"
- 타당성을 검토하다 (tadangseong-eul geomtohada) — "to review the validity of"
- 타당한 근거를 제시하다 (tadangan geungeo-reul jesihada) — "to present valid grounds"
- 타당한 이유가 없다 (tadangan iyu-ga eopda) — "there is no valid reason"
- 신빙성 (sinbyeongseong) — "credibility, plausibility"
- 신빙성 있다 (sinbyeongseong itda) — "to be credible"
- 신빙성 없다 (sinbyeongseong eopda) — "to lack credibility"
- 신빙성을 의심하다 (sinbyeongseong-eul uisimhada) — "to doubt the credibility of"
- 모순 (mosun) — "contradiction, inconsistency"
- 모순되다 (mosun-doeda) — "to be contradictory"
- 모순적 (mosunjeok) — "contradictory, inconsistent"

**Notes:** 모순 (contradiction) is placed here because its primary function in the data is to identify logical or evidential failure within a set of claims — it is used to challenge the groundedness of an argument. 신빙성 is placed here rather than in a trust group because its collocations (신빙성을 의심하다, 신빙성이 높다/낮다) concern the credibility of claims and testimony, not the trustworthiness of persons or institutions. 타당 and 근거 are closely associated in the data (타당한 근거를 제시하다) and share the same evaluative domain.

## Group 9: Items Used to Evaluate Whether a Source, Person, or Institution Can Be Depended Upon

**Label:** Trustworthiness evaluators

**Definition:** These items are used to evaluate persons, institutions, or sources with respect to whether they can be relied upon over time. They appear in constructions involving the building, loss, betrayal, and restoration of trust: 신뢰를 쌓다 (to build trust), 신뢰를 잃다 (to lose trust), 신뢰를 저버리다 (to betray trust), 신뢰를 회복하다 (to restore trust). The evaluative target is a relationship or institution, not a specific claim.

**Representative expressions:**
- 신뢰 (sinloe) — "trust, reliability, credibility"
- 신뢰하다 (sinloe-hada) — "to trust, to rely on"
- 신뢰성 (sinloeseong) — "reliability, trustworthiness"
- 신뢰도 (sinloedo) — "degree of reliability, trust level"
- 신뢰를 쌓다 (sinloe-reul ssada) — "to build trust"
- 신뢰를 잃다 (sinloe-reul ilta) — "to lose trust"
- 신뢰를 저버리다 (sinloe-reul jeobeorida) — "to betray trust"
- 신뢰를 회복하다 (sinloe-reul hoebokada) — "to restore trust"
- 신뢰가 무너지다 (sinloe-ga muneoJida) — "trust collapses"
- 신뢰에 기반하다 (sinloe-e gibanhada) — "to be based on trust"
- 신뢰받다 (sinloe-batda) — "to be trusted, to receive trust"
- 신뢰할 수 있다 (sinloe-hal su itda) — "to be trustworthy, can be trusted"

**Notes:** 신뢰 and 신빙성 are distinguished in the data by their collocational patterns: 신뢰 appears with verbs of building, losing, betraying, and restoring (쌓다, 잃다, 저버리다, 회복하다), which are relational and temporal; 신빙성 appears with verbs of assessment and degree (높다, 낮다, 의심하다), which are evaluative and scalar. This distinction in verbal partners supports placing them in different groups despite their semantic proximity.

---

## Group 10: Items Used to Evaluate Whether Something Corresponds Precisely to a Standard or Description

**Label:** Precision and correctness markers

**Definition:** These items are used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, answer, or representation corresponds precisely to what it describes or to an applicable standard. They appear in constructions like 정확히 말하면 (to be precise, strictly speaking), 맞는 말이다 (that is a correct statement), and 정확한 정보를 제공하다 (to provide accurate information). The evaluative question concerns precision of correspondence, not moral character or evidential support.

**Representative expressions:**
- 정확 (jeonghwak) — "accuracy, precision, exactness"
- 정확하다 (jeonghwak-hada) — "to be accurate, to be precise"
- 정확히 (jeonghwaki) — "accurately, exactly, precisely"
- 정확히 말하면 (jeonghwaki malhamyeon) — "to be precise, strictly speaking"
- 정확도 (jeonghwakdo) — "degree of accuracy"
- 정확도가 높다/낮다 (jeonghwakdo-ga nopda/natda) — "to have high/low accuracy"
- 맞다 (matda) — "to be right, correct, to match"
- 맞는 말이다 (manneun mal-ida) — "that is a correct statement"
- 딱 맞다 (ttak matda) — "to be exactly right, to fit perfectly"
- 맞아떨어지다 (majatteoreojida) — "to fit together perfectly, to check out"
- 틀리다 (teullida) — "to be wrong, incorrect, mistaken"
- 맞는지 틀린지 모르다 (manneun-ji teullin-ji moreuda) — "unable to tell if it is correct"
- 올바르다 (olbareuda) — "to be correct, right, proper"
- 올바른 판단을 내리다 (olbareun pandan-eul naerida) — "to make a correct judgment"
- 올바르게 이해하다 (olbareuge ihaehada) — "to understand correctly"

**Notes:** 맞다 and 틀리다 are natural contrasts within this group and appear together in fixed paired constructions (맞는지 틀린지, 맞고 틀리고를 떠나서). 올바르다 overlaps with Group 6 (honesty-in-conduct) when applied to moral conduct, but is placed here because its primary collocational partners in the data (판단, 이해, 길) concern correctness of reasoning and action rather than honesty of speech. 정확 is more specifically about precision of measurement or statement; 맞다 is broader and more colloquial.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items show clear patterns of use in multiple groups and cannot be cleanly assigned to one:

---

**진실 (jinsil) — "truth, truthfulness"**

Participates in Group 6 (진실을 말하다 — honest speech), Group 7 (진실이 드러나다 — truth as object of investigation), and Group 5 (진실하다, 진실되다 — inner sincerity). The noun 진실 itself is not fixed to any single group; its group membership is determined by the construction it appears in.

---

**진짜 (jinjja) — "real, genuine; really, truly"**

Participates in Group 3 (genuineness of objects: 진짜배기, 진짜인지 가짜인지 모르다) and functions as an intensifying adverb (진짜 좋다, "really good") that does not belong to any group in this domain. The adverbial use is productive and common but falls outside the scope of the truth-evaluation domain documented in P1 and P2.

---

**진위 (jinwi) — "truth or falsity, authenticity"**

Participates in Group 3 (authentication of objects: 진위를 가리다, 진본 여부) and Group 1 (propositional truth or falsity: 진위 여부가 불분명하다). The item frames the binary question of genuine-versus-false and can apply to both objects and propositions depending on context.

---

**올바르다 (olbareuda) — "to be correct, right, proper, upright"**

Participates in Group 10 (correctness of reasoning and understanding: 올바른 판단, 올바르게 이해하다) and Group 6 (moral conduct: 올바른 길을 가다, 올바른 자세를 갖추다). The item applies to both epistemic correctness and moral uprightness; the data does not support treating these as separate lexical items.

---

**거짓 (geojit) — "falsehood, lie, untruth"**

Participates in Group 6 (거짓 없이 말하다 — honest speech; 거짓이 드러나다 — exposure of deception) and Group 7 (진실과 거짓을 가리다 — distinguishing truth from falsehood). As a modifier in noun phrases (거짓 정보, 거짓 희망, 거짓 평화), it extends into domains not covered by any group above.

**참 (cham) — "truth, the genuine/real; truly, really"**

Participates in Group 5 (참되다 — to be genuinely what one is) and Group 6 (참말 — truthful speech). In mathematical and logical contexts (참값, true value), it participates in Group 10. The item's range across registers (literary, philosophical, mathematical, everyday) means it does not settle into any single group.

---

**본질 (bonjil) — "essence, essential nature"**

Does not fit cleanly into any group above. Its collocations (본질을 파악하다, 본질을 흐리다, 본질에서 벗어나다) concern the identification and preservation of fundamental character, which is related to but distinct from the truth-evaluation patterns documented in the other groups. It appears in analytical and philosophical contexts where the question is what something fundamentally is, not whether a claim about it is true or false.

**실증 (siljeung) / 실증적 (siljeungjeok) — "empirical evidence; empirical, evidence-based"**

These items appear in academic and scientific contexts evaluating whether knowledge claims are grounded in observable evidence. They overlap with Group 8 (groundedness evaluators) but their register is specifically academic and their concern is with the method of knowledge production rather than the evaluation of specific claims. They are not placed in any group because the data does not show them participating in the same collocational patterns as the Group 8 items.

---

**위장 (wijang) — "disguise, camouflage; to disguise oneself"**

Participates in Group 3 (false identity: 위장 취업) but also appears in military and tactical contexts (camouflage) where the domain is concealment rather than authenticity evaluation. The item does not fit cleanly into any single group.

## Items That Do Not Fit Any Group

**유사 (yusa) — "similarity, resemblance"**

The data shows 유사 used in comparative evaluation (유사하다, 유사성) and in legal contexts (trademark similarity). Its function is to assess degree of resemblance between two things, which is related to but distinct from the truth-evaluation patterns in the groups above. No group is identified for this item.

**객관적 (gaekgwanjeok) / 주관적 (jugwanjeok) — "objective / subjective"**

These items appear in the data as meta-evaluative terms characterizing the standpoint from which an evaluation is made, rather than as evaluations of truth, authenticity, or honesty themselves. They do not participate in the same collocational patterns as the items in any group above. No group is identified for these items.

**공정 (gongjeon) — "fairness, impartiality"**

The data shows 공정 used to evaluate procedures and judgments as free from bias. This is related to but distinct from the truth-evaluation domain: a judgment can be fair without being accurate, and accurate without being fair. The collocational partners of 공정 (공정하게 평가하다, 공정성 논란) do not overlap with those of the groups above. No group is identified for this item.
