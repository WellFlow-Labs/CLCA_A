# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. The focus is on syntactic and periphrastic constructions — combinations of words that function as units in discourse but are not single morphological forms. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. The primary data source is the P1 and P1b output; no new forms are generated beyond what is supported by prior steps. Collocations already introduced in P1b as compound expressions (e.g., *cheqa simi*, *llulla qelqa*) are carried forward here only where they appear in syntactic constructions with additional material; bare collocations already documented are not re-listed.

Quechua is a head-final, agglutinative language with SOV word order. Multi-word constructions in Quechua typically involve: (a) nominal phrases with adjectival modifiers preceding the head noun; (b) verbal phrases with adverbial modifiers preceding the verb; (c) evidential and modal constructions involving clitics attached to the predicate; (d) prohibitive constructions with *ama*; (e) comparative constructions with *hina*. These structural patterns are the primary sources of multi-word constructions documented here.

Epistemic statuses are preserved from prior steps where forms were already assessed. New assessments follow the same criteria.

## P1 Item 1: cheqa

---

**C1.1**
- **Base Form:** cheqa
- **Example:** Cheqam kay.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqa-m kay.
- **English translation:** This is true. / It is true.
- **Usage notes:** A minimal predicate construction asserting truth, with the direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi* attached to the predicate nominal *cheqa*. This is the standard form for asserting that something is the case with direct evidential commitment. Widely attested in Southern Quechua discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.2**
- **Base Form:** cheqa
- **Example:** Cheqachu kay?
- **Transliteration:** Cheqa-chu kay?
- **English translation:** Is this true? / Is it true?
- **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction using the yes/no question clitic *-chu* attached to the predicate *cheqa*. The standard form for asking whether something is true. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.3**
- **Base Form:** cheqa
- **Example:** Mana cheqachu.
- **Transliteration:** Mana cheqa-chu.
- **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not the case.
- **Usage notes:** Negative predicate construction using *mana* (negator) with the negative polarity clitic *-chu* on the predicate. The standard form for denying the truth of a proposition in Southern Quechua. Widely attested.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C1.4**
- **Base Form:** cheqa
- **Example:** Cheqa kasqanmanta willakuyki.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqa-n-manta willakuy-ki.
- **English translation:** I will tell you about what turned out to be true. / I will tell you of its being true.
- **Usage notes:** A nominalized construction in which *cheqa* is embedded as a predicate within a nominalized clause (*kasqanmanta*, "about its having been") functioning as the complement of *willakuy* ("to tell"). Illustrates how *cheqa* participates in complex clause embedding in testimonial discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## P1 Item 2: cheqaq

---

**C2.1**
- **Base Form:** cheqaq
- **Example:** Cheqaqmi nin.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-mi ni-n.
- **English translation:** He/she says it is true. / He/she says truly.
- **Usage notes:** A reportative construction in which *cheqaq* with the direct evidential clitic *-mi* is combined with the verb *niy* ("to say") in third person. Used to report that someone has asserted something as true. Attested in Southern Quechua narrative and testimonial discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.2**
- **Base Form:** cheqaq
- **Example:** Cheqaqtam rimasqanki.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-ta-m rima-sqa-n-ki.
- **English translation:** You spoke the truth. / What you said was true.
- **Usage notes:** A construction combining the adverbial *cheqaqta* with the direct evidential clitic and the past participle of *rimay* ("to speak") in second person. Used in assessments of prior speech as truthful. Attested in Southern Quechua.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.3**
- **Base Form:** cheqaq
- **Example:** Cheqaqpunim chay.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-puni-m chay.
- **English translation:** That is absolutely true. / That is certainly the case.
- **Usage notes:** Emphatic predicate construction combining *cheqaqpuni* (absolutely true) with the direct evidential clitic and a demonstrative. Used for strong assertion of truth. Attested in Southern Quechua testimonial and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.4**
- **Base Form:** cheqaq
- **Example:** Cheqaqchus chay?
- **Transliteration:** Cheqaq-chus chay?
- **English translation:** Is that really true? / Can that really be the case?
- **Usage notes:** Dubitative interrogative construction combining *cheqaq* with the dubitative clitic *-chus* and a demonstrative. Used to express doubt or surprise about whether something is genuinely the case. Attested in Southern Quechua everyday speech and narrative.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C2.5**
- **Base Form:** cheqaq
- **Example:** Cheqaq runám pay.
- **Transliteration:** Cheqaq runa-m pay.
- **English translation:** He/she is an honest person. / He/she is a truthful person.
- **Usage notes:** Nominal predicate construction in which *cheqaq* modifies *runa* ("person, human being") with the direct evidential clitic. A standard form for characterizing a person as honest or truthful. Attested in Southern Quechua moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 6: llulla

---

**C6.1**
- **Base Form:** llulla
- **Example:** Llullaм chay.
- **Transliteration:** Llulla-m chay.
- **English translation:** That is a lie. / That is false.
- **Usage notes:** Minimal predicate construction asserting falsehood, with the direct evidential clitic *-m/-mi* attached to *llulla* as predicate. The standard form for asserting that something is false or a lie. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.2**
- **Base Form:** llulla
- **Example:** Mana llullachu.
- **Transliteration:** Mana llulla-chu.
- **English translation:** It is not a lie. / That is not false.
- **Usage notes:** Negative predicate construction denying that something is a lie or false. Uses the standard negation pattern *mana ... -chu*. Attested in Southern Quechua.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.3**
- **Base Form:** llulla
- **Example:** Llulla runám pay.
- **Transliteration:** Llulla runa-m pay.
- **English translation:** He/she is a liar. / He/she is a dishonest person.
- **Usage notes:** Nominal predicate construction in which *llulla* modifies *runa* ("person") with the direct evidential clitic. The standard form for characterizing a person as a liar or dishonest. Attested in Southern Quechua moral discourse as the antonym of *cheqaq runam pay*.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.4**
- **Base Form:** llulla
- **Example:** Ama llulla, ama suwa, ama qella.
- **Transliteration:** Ama llulla, ama suwa, ama qella.
- **English translation:** Do not lie, do not steal, do not be lazy.
- **Usage notes:** The traditional Andean moral triad, widely cited in Andean cultural, educational, and political discourse. This is a fixed formulaic construction and is documented here as a multi-word construction in its full triadic form. Widely attested across Quechua varieties and in Andean cultural contexts broadly.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C6.5**
- **Base Form:** llulla
- **Example:** Llullam nin.
- **Transliteration:** Llulla-m ni-n.
- **English translation:** He/she says it is a lie. / He/she calls it false.
- **Usage notes:** Reportative construction parallel to C2.1, combining *llulla* with the direct evidential clitic and the verb *niy* ("to say"). Used to report that someone has characterized something as false. Attested in Southern Quechua discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 7: llullakuy

---

**C7.1**
- **Base Form:** llullakuy
- **Example:** Ama llullakuychu.
- **Transliteration:** Ama llullakuy-chu.
- **English translation:** Do not lie. / Don't deceive.
- **Usage notes:** The standard prohibitive construction using *ama* (prohibitive particle) with the verb *llullakuy* and the prohibitive suffix *-chu*. This is the fuller verbal form of the moral injunction, as opposed to the nominal *ama llulla* (C6.4). Widely attested in Southern Quechua ethical and educational discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**C7.2**
- **Base Form:** llullakuy
- **Example:** Llullakusqanmantam p'enqakun.
- **Transliteration:** Llullakusqa-n-manta-m p'enqa-ku-n.
- **English translation:** He/she is ashamed of having lied. / He/she feels shame because of his/her lie.
- **Usage notes:** A nominalized clause construction in which *llullakusqanmanta* ("because of his/her having lied") functions as the cause complement of *p'enqakuy* ("to be ashamed"). Illustrates how *llullakuy* participates in complex clause embedding in moral discourse. Attested in Southern Quechua narrative and moral discourse.
- **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## Summary Note on Patterns and Absences

The following observations are directly supported by the data above and are reported without interpretation beyond what the data warrant.

**Predicate constructions with evidential clitics:** The most consistently attested construction type across P1 items is the simple predicate construction with the direct evidential clitic *-mi/-m* (e.g., *cheqaqmi*, *llullam*, *chiqanmi*, *sut'im*, *qelqasqam*). This pattern is attested for *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *llulla*, *chiqan*, *allin*, *sut'i*, *kikin*, and *qelqasqa*. It is not documented for *pura*, *paylla*, *recto/riqtu*, *cheqniy*, or *iñisqa* within the truth-evaluation domain.

**Interrogative constructions with *-chu*:** Interrogative predicate constructions using the yes/no clitic *-chu* are attested for *cheqa*, *cheqaq*, *allin*, *chiqan*, *sut'i*, and *kikin*. They are not documented for *llulla*, *pantay*, or *willakuy* within the truth-evaluation domain in available sources; this asymmetry is preserved without explanation.

**Prohibitive constructions with *ama*:** Prohibitive constructions are attested for *llulla* (*ama llulla*) and *llullakuy* (*ama llullakuychu*). No prohibitive construction is attested for *cheqa*, *chiqan*, or other P1 items within scope.

**Imperative constructions:** Imperative constructions are attested for *cheqaqta rimay*, *cheqaqta willaway*, and *sut'ichaway*. No imperative construction is attested for *llulla*, *chiqan*, *kikin*, or *pantay* within scope.

**Nominalized clause complements:** Constructions involving nominalized clause complements are attested for *iñiy* (C12.2), *yachay* (C11.1), and *willakuy* (C29.2), and tentatively for *cheqa* (C1.4) and *llullakuy* (C7.2). These are marked [UNCERTAIN] where attestation is limited.

**Items with no constructions identified:** The following P1 items have no common multi-word constructions identified within the truth-evaluation domain: *cheqaqsiy*, *iñisqa*, *recto/riqtu*, *pura*, *paylla*, *cheqniy*, *chiqap*. This is reported as absence in the data, not as evidence of impossibility.
