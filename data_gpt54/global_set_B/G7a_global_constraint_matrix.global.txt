## Main recurring semantic dimensions

The data supports the following recurring dimensions in the truth-related domain:

1. **Statement truth vs falsehood**  
   Whether a statement/report/proposition is true or false.

2. **Correctness / rightness of answer, judgment, or procedure**  
   Whether something is correct, right, proper, or mistaken.

3. **Precision / exactness / fine-grained accuracy**  
   Whether wording, measurement, description, or representation is exact.

4. **Credibility / trustworthiness / dependability**  
   Whether a source, witness, report, or person deserves belief or reliance.

5. **Honesty / sincerity / truthful character or speech**  
   Whether a person or utterance is candid, honest, or morally truthful.

6. **Genuineness / authenticity / real identity or object**  
   Whether an object, identity, relation, or experience is genuine rather than fake.

7. **Fake / counterfeit / forged / deceptive imitation**  
   Whether something is fake, forged, falsified, or deceptively non-genuine.

8. **Verification / checked or officially confirmed status**  
   Whether truth/authenticity is established through checking, investigation, or proof.

9. **Basis / support / evidential grounding**  
   Whether a claim/report/accusation has grounds or lacks support.

10. **Validity / formal standing / admissibility**  
    Whether something counts as valid, null, acceptable, or standing under rules.

11. **Lawfulness / legitimacy / rightful standing**  
    Whether an act, claim, authority, or status is lawful or legitimate.

12. **Justice / fairness / moral rightness**  
    Whether treatment, judgment, or outcome is fair or just.

13. **Reality / actuality / what is really the case**  
    Whether something is actual rather than apparent, fictional, or merely claimed.

14. **Essence / substance / underlying nature**  
    What something fundamentally is, beyond form or surface.

15. **Resemblance / correspondence / sameness to an original or model**  
    Whether something matches, resembles, corresponds to, or is identical with something else.

16. **Appearance-of-truth / plausibility / seeming real**  
    Whether something seems true, believable, or lifelike without being established as true.

These dimensions are not equally distinct in all languages. Some are clearly split in some languages and merged in others:
- **truth vs authenticity** is clearly split in zh/he/el/en/tl/sw, weaker in qu, partly merged in sa.
- **correctness vs precision** is strong in zh/he/el/en/tl, weaker in qu and partly sw.
- **credibility vs truth** is strong in zh/he/en/tl/sw, weaker in qu, partly absorbed into validity/proof in sa.
- **reality vs essence** is strongly distinct in zh/he/sa/el, weaker elsewhere.

## Main recurring constructional functions / realization types

From the data, the main recurring constructional functions are:

1. **Simple predicative evaluation**  
   “X is true/correct/reliable/fake/etc.”

2. **Negative predicative evaluation / rejection**  
   “X is not true / false / incorrect / baseless / fake.”

3. **Noun modification / classification**  
   “true story,” “fake document,” “reliable source,” etc.

4. **Speech/reporting action constructions**  
   “tell the truth,” “lie,” “answer truthfully,” “false statement.”

5. **Interrogative checking / querying**  
   “Is it true?”, “Is that correct?”, “Are you sure?”

6. **Change into a state / inchoative realization**  
   “come true,” “turn out true,” “become credible,” “prove false.”

7. **Causative / induced result**  
   “mislead,” “authenticate,” “validate,” “correct,” “falsify.”

8. **Abstract nominal evaluation**  
   “truth,” “accuracy,” “validity,” “authenticity,” “credibility.”

9. **Person-labeling / agentive characterization**  
   “liar,” “truthful person,” “trustworthy person,” “forger.”

10. **Discourse framing / stance marking**  
    “to tell the truth,” “in fact,” “in reality,” “in essence.”

11. **Correspondence / fidelity construction**  
    “matches the facts,” “faithful to the original,” “true to life.”

12. **Basis / support construction**  
    “based on evidence,” “has basis,” “baseless.”

13. **Verification / authentication procedure construction**  
    “verify X,” “verified X,” “confirmed true,” “authenticated.”

These are also unevenly distributed:
- **Simple predication**, **negative predication**, **noun modification**, **abstract nominalization** are strongest cross-linguistically.
- **Inchoative** and **causative** patterns are much less uniform.
- **Interrogative checking** is clearly foregrounded only in some languages in the construction tables, though present elsewhere.
- **Correspondence/fidelity** is strongly constructional in some languages, more lexical in others.

## Narrative analysis

### Strongly recurrent constraints

#### 1. Static evaluation is the most stable realization type
Across the languages, the strongest and most recurrent pattern is **simple predicative evaluation**:
- true / false
- correct / incorrect
- reliable / unreliable
- authentic / fake
- valid / invalid

This is strongly supported for:
- statement truth
- correctness
- precision
- credibility
- authenticity
- fake/counterfeit status
- validity
- lawfulness
- justice/fairness
- reality

So the broadest cross-language generalization is:  
**truth-related meanings are most robustly encoded as stable evaluative states.**

#### 2. Negative predication is also highly stable
A second strong recurrent pattern is **negative predicative evaluation**:
- not true
- false
- not correct
- baseless
- invalid
- unlawful
- fake

This is supported across nearly all languages, either by:
- analytic negation,
- lexical negatives,
- or specialized rejecting predicates.

So another strong constraint is:  
**if a semantic dimension supports simple predication, it usually also supports some form of negative predication.**

#### 3. Noun modification is broadly available
Many dimensions are naturally realized as noun modifiers:
- true story
- false statement
- correct answer
- accurate description
- reliable source
- authentic document
- fake passport
- lawful authority

This is especially strong for:
- statement truth/falsity
- correctness
- precision
- credibility
- authenticity
- fake/counterfeit
- lawfulness/legitimacy

So:  
**classification of entities by truth-related properties is a highly recurrent cross-language strategy.**

#### 4. Abstract nominalization is also highly recurrent
Many languages strongly support abstract nouns for:
- truth
- accuracy
- validity
- authenticity
- credibility
- fairness
- legitimacy
- reality

This is one of the strongest cross-language patterns after predication and noun modification.

---

### Likely prohibitions or avoidance patterns

#### 1. Broad inchoative “become true/false/authentic/correct” systems are generally weak
A major recurrent avoidance pattern is the lack of a **single general change-of-state system** across the whole domain.

Across many languages:
- “become true” is only available in restricted lexical families
- “become false” is often weak or absent
- “become authentic/genuine” is especially weak
- “become correct” is often less natural than “correct/fix/verify”

This is especially clear in:
- he
- sa
- el
- tl
- sw
- qu
and only partly overcome in:
- en
- zh
- tl (for one family only)

So a likely global constraint is:  
**truth-related domains broadly resist uniform inchoative realization; change-of-state is usually lexically restricted.**

#### 2. General causative “make X true/authentic/correct” is broadly avoided
Another strong avoidance pattern is the absence of a general causative construction meaning:
- make true
- make authentic
- make genuine
- make correct
across the whole domain.

Instead, languages prefer **specialized lexical verbs**:
- verify
- authenticate
- validate
- falsify
- forge
- correct
- mislead

So a likely prohibition is:  
**languages tend not to generalize one causative construction across truth-related meanings; they substitute domain-specific verbs.**

#### 3. Authenticity strongly resists ordinary inchoatives
This is one of the clearest subdomain-specific restrictions:
- “proved fake/forged” is common
- “became authentic/genuine” is often weak or absent

This pattern is visible in:
- he
- el
- en
- tl
- sw
- qu
and partly in zh

So:  
**the fake side is often easier to lexicalize dynamically than the genuine side.**

#### 4. Verification terms often resist ordinary “become” syntax
Some verification meanings are not treated like ordinary adjectives.
Most clearly:
- zh 属实 resists ordinary become/cause-to-become patterns

More broadly, many languages encode verification through:
- investigation
- proof
- authentication
- checking
rather than ordinary inchoative predication.

So:  
**verification status is often constructionally specialized and not freely integrated into ordinary change-of-state patterns.**

---

### Important divergences, substitutions, and asymmetries

#### 1. Different languages split the domain differently
The matrix should not be read as implying identical semantic partitions in all languages.

Some dimensions are strongly split in some languages and merged in others:
- **truth vs authenticity**
- **correctness vs precision**
- **credibility vs truth**
- **reality vs essence**
- **validity vs legitimacy vs justice**

This means some ALLOWED cells reflect broad cross-language recurrence, but not identical lexical boundaries.

#### 2. Dynamicity is often rerouted through other constructions
Instead of “become true,” languages often use:
- **verification/result** constructions
- **revelation** constructions
- **proof** constructions
- **correction** constructions
- **falsification** constructions

Typical substitutions:
- truth → prove true / verified / came out
- falsity → proved false / shown false
- authenticity → authenticated / proved forged
- correctness → corrected / got right
- validity → validated / invalidated
- credibility → judged credible / trusted / not credible

So the recurrent asymmetry is:
**state assignment is general; state transition is often indirect and domain-specific.**

#### 3. Negative domains are often more lexically compact
Across several languages:
- liar terms are stronger than truth-teller terms
- fake/forged terms are stronger than become-genuine terms
- falsification verbs are stronger than make-true verbs

This asymmetry is visible in:
- en
- el
- he
- qu
- sw
and partly elsewhere.

#### 4. Speech-action constructions are especially robust
Even where dynamic state-change is weak, many languages strongly support:
- tell the truth
- lie
- answer truthfully
- false statement

So one important substitution pattern is:
**speech-action constructions often replace more abstract truth-state dynamics.**

#### 5. Basis/support is uneven but important
Some languages strongly lexicalize support/grounding:
- sa
- tl
- zh/he/en partly
while others show it less clearly.

This means:
- support/basis is a real recurring dimension,
- but its constructional profile is less uniform than truth/correctness/authenticity.

#### 6. Correspondence/fidelity is often separate from truth proper
Several languages distinguish:
- being true
from
- matching an original
- being faithful to a source
- being identical
- seeming lifelike

This is especially strong in:
- he
- el
- en
- zh
and weaker elsewhere.

So:
**correspondence/fidelity should not be collapsed into statement truth.**

## Confidence assessment

### Strong evidence
Evidence is strongest for:
- **statement truth vs falsehood**
- **correctness**
- **precision**
- **credibility**
- **authenticity**
- **fake/counterfeit**
- **simple predicative evaluation**
- **negative predicative evaluation**
- **noun modification**
- **abstract nominalization**

These are supported across many languages with multiple examples and repeated contrasts.

### Moderate evidence
Evidence is moderate for:
- **verification / confirmed status**
- **basis / support / evidential grounding**
- **validity / formal standing**
- **lawfulness / legitimacy**
- **justice / fairness**
- **correspondence / fidelity**
- **discourse framing**
- **person-labeling**
- **change into state / inchoative**
- **causative / induced result**

These are clearly present, but less evenly distributed and often domain-specific.

### Weak or missing evidence
Evidence is weak or missing for:
- **interrogative checking** as a cross-language constructional type in the F4 tables
- **inchoatives for honesty/sincerity**
- **inchoatives for authenticity**
- **general causatives for truth/correctness/authenticity**
- **person-labeling for many positive domains**
- **essence/substance with dynamic constructions**
- **appearance-of-truth with support/verification constructions**
- **resemblance/sameness with speech-action constructions**

These cells are therefore marked **BLOCKED**, **VARIABLE**, or **INSUFFICIENT DATA** rather than generalized positively.

---

## Brief final assessment

The data supports a **multi-dimensional truth-related domain** rather than a single true/false system. The most stable cross-language realization types are **static predication**, **negative predication**, **noun modification**, and **abstract nominalization**. By contrast, **dynamic change** and **causation** are uneven, often blocked, and frequently replaced by **verification**, **proof**, **correction**, **falsification**, or **authentication** constructions. The strongest asymmetries are:
- negative/fake/lying terms are often more compact than positive counterparts,
- authenticity resists ordinary inchoatives,
- verification is often constructionally specialized,
- and many languages prefer subdomain-specific verbs over a general “make true” or “become true” system.
