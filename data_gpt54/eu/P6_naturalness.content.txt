## 1. BECOMING TRUE / ESTABLISHED AS TRUE
### Pattern
**X egia dela egiaztatu da / X egia dela egiaztatu dute**  
‘it has been verified that X is true’ / ‘they verified that X is true’

### Examples
- **Albistea egia dela egiaztatu dute.** → “They verified that the news is true.”
- **Hori egia dela egiaztatu da.** → “It has been verified that that is true.”

### Notes
From the supplied material, Basque does not show a dedicated simple inchoative pattern meaning “become true” with the listed vocabulary alone. What is clearly supported is a **procedural shift into confirmed truth-status** through **egiaztatu**. This encodes not spontaneous becoming, but confirmation or establishment as true.

---

## 2. BECOMING KNOWN AS TRUE / TRUTH EMERGING
### Pattern
**egia agerian jarri / egia azaleratu / egia aurkitu / egia jakin**
‘bring the truth to light’ / ‘reveal the truth’ / ‘find the truth’ / ‘learn the truth’

### Examples
- **Azkenean, egia agerian jarri dute.** → “In the end, they brought the truth to light.”
- **Denborarekin, egia azaleratu da.** → “With time, the truth came out.”
- **Egia aurkitu dute.** → “They found the truth.”
- **Orain egia jakin dugu.** → “Now we have learned the truth.”

### Notes
These are the clearest dynamic expressions in the dataset for movement from concealment or uncertainty toward truth being available, known, or public.  
They do **not** literally mean “X becomes true”; rather, they mean that **the truth becomes known, found, or revealed**.

## 4. BECOMING CORRECT / PUT RIGHT
### Pattern
**X zuzendu da / Y-k X zuzendu du**
‘X was corrected’ / ‘Y corrected X’

### Examples
- **Testua zuzendu da.** → “The text has been corrected.”
- **Irakasleak erantzuna zuzendu du.** → “The teacher corrected the answer.”

### Notes
For the **zuzen** family, the dynamic meaning is well supported through **zuzendu**. This is the main way to encode movement into a correct state.  
This is better supported than any simple “become correct” construction with bare **zuzen**.

---

## 5. BECOMING WRONG / MISTAKEN
### Pattern
**X okerra da / X oker idatzi da / X oker ahoskatu da**
‘X is wrong’ / ‘X was written incorrectly’ / ‘X was pronounced incorrectly’

### Examples
- **Erantzuna okerra da.** → “The answer is wrong.”
- **Hitza oker idatzi da.** → “The word was written incorrectly.”
- **Izena oker ahoskatu da.** → “The name was pronounced incorrectly.”

### Notes
The context supports **wrong-state attribution** and adverbial incorrect performance with **oker**.  
However, no dedicated inchoative “become wrong” construction is securely established. The dynamic reading is usually inferred from context or from the event verb phrase such as **oker idatzi** or **oker ahoskatu**.

## 6. CAUSING SOMETHING TO COUNT AS TRUE: VERIFICATION
### Pattern
**Y-k X egiaztatu du**
‘Y verified X’

### Examples
- **Datuak egiaztatu dituzte.** → “They verified the data.”
- **Agiria egiaztatu dute.** → “They verified the document.”

### Notes
This is the clearest causative-like dynamic in the truth domain from the supplied material.  
**egiaztatu** does not literally mean “make true,” but rather **check and establish as true/valid**. It is especially procedural, administrative, legal, or technical.

---

## 7. CAUSING SOMETHING TO BECOME CORRECT
### Pattern
**Y-k X zuzendu du**
‘Y corrected X’

### Examples
- **Irakasleak testua zuzendu du.** → “The teacher corrected the text.”
- **Zuzentzaileak akatsa zuzendu du.** → “The proofreader corrected the mistake.”

### Notes
This is the strongest supported caused-change pattern in the dataset for the correctness domain.  
It directly encodes a transition from an incorrect or defective form to a corrected one.

---

## 8. CAUSING TRUTH TO APPEAR OR EMERGE
### Pattern
**Y-k egia agerian jarri du / Y-k egia azaleratu du**
‘Y brought the truth to light’ / ‘Y revealed the truth’

### Examples
- **Kazetariek egia agerian jarri dute.** → “The journalists brought the truth to light.”
- **Testigantzak egia azaleratu du.** → “The testimony revealed the truth.”

### Notes
These constructions encode caused disclosure rather than caused truth itself.  
The truth is treated as something hidden that can be **made public**, **surfaced**, or **exposed**.

---

## 9. ENTERING A LYING ACTIVITY
### Pattern
**X gezurretan ari da / X gezurra esan du**
‘X is lying’ / ‘X told a lie’

### Examples
- **Gezurretan ari da.** → “He/she is lying.”
- **Gezurra esan du.** → “He/she told a lie.”

### Notes
These are dynamic in the sense of **engaging in deception**.  
They do not mean “become false,” but they do encode entry into or performance of a falsehood-related act.

---

## 10. STABILITY OF TRUTH STATUS
### Pattern
**X egia da / X ez da egia**
‘X is true’ / ‘X is not true’

### Examples
- **Hori egia da.** → “That is true.”
- **Hori ez da egia.** → “That is not true.”

### Notes
The most basic way Basque encodes stable truth status in the supplied material is simple attribution.  
No special stability marker is needed: the copular pattern itself presents the proposition as holding in a stable way.

---

## 11. STABILITY OF AUTHENTICITY / GENUINENESS
### Pattern
**X benetakoa da / X egiazkoa da / X ez da benetakoa**
‘X is real/genuine’ / ‘X is true/genuine’ / ‘X is not real/genuine’

### Examples
- **Albiste hori ez da benetakoa.** → “That news item is not genuine/real.”
- **Istorio hori egiazkoa da.** → “That story is true.”

### Notes
Again, the supplied material supports **state attribution** more clearly than change into that state.  
So for **benetako** and **egiazko**, stable classification is well supported, but a dedicated “become genuine/real” pattern is not securely established here.

---

## 12. STABILITY OF CORRECTNESS
### Pattern
**X zuzena da / X ez da zuzena**
‘X is correct’ / ‘X is not correct’

### Examples
- **Erantzun hori zuzena da.** → “That answer is correct.”
- **Idazkera hori ez da zuzena.** → “That writing is not correct.”

### Notes
As with truth and genuineness, the stable state is encoded by ordinary attribution.  
Dynamic change is more naturally expressed with **zuzendu** than with a bare “become correct” construction.

## 13. OTHER DYNAMIC EXPRESSIONS IN THE DATASET
### Pattern
**egia bilatu / egia aurkitu / egia jakin / sinetsi**
‘seek the truth’ / ‘find the truth’ / ‘learn the truth’ / ‘believe’

### Examples
- **Egia bilatu dute.** → “They sought the truth.”
- **Azkenean egia aurkitu du.** → “In the end he/she found the truth.”
- **Orain egia jakin dugu.** → “Now we know the truth.”
- **Ez dut sinetsi.** → “I did not believe it.”

### Notes
These expressions show important truth-related dynamics:
- **egia bilatu**: movement toward truth through search
- **egia aurkitu**: arrival at truth through discovery
- **egia jakin**: transition from not knowing to knowing
- **sinetsi**: acceptance of something as true

They are dynamic, but they concern **access to truth**, not truth changing in itself.

---

## 14. SUMMARY
### Strongly supported dynamic patterns
- **egiaztatu** → verify / establish as true or valid
- **zuzendu** → correct / make right
- **egia agerian jarri / egia azaleratu** → bring truth out / reveal truth
- **egia aurkitu / egia jakin / egia bilatu** → find / know / seek truth
- **gezurretan ari / gezurra esan** → engage in lying

### Weak or unsupported from the supplied material
- dedicated simple “become true”
- dedicated simple “become false”
- dedicated simple “become genuine”
- dedicated simple “become a lie”

### General note
In the supplied Basque material, truth-related dynamics are encoded more naturally through:
1. **verification**
2. **revelation**
3. **discovery**
4. **correction**
5. **lying activity**

rather than through a broad productive “become X” pattern for all truth-related adjectives and nouns.
