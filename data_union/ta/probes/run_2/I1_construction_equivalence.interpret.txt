## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for BECOME in truth-specific contexts

The data show two formally distinct inchoative constructions in Tamil. The general BECOME operator is expressed by *āki(viṭu)*, which is productive across domains. However, for the specific meaning of truth being revealed or coming to light, the data attest a fixed collocation *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* using the verb *veḷippaṭu* ("to come out / be revealed"). P4b explicitly states these two constructions are not interchangeable in this collocation. This means that in the truth domain, REVELATION is not simply a sub-case of BECOME but is expressed by a dedicated lexical collocation. The BECOME construction (*āki*) is attested for changes of state involving truth-domain nominals in other configurations (e.g., credibility becoming a question mark), but the specific REVELATION meaning (truth emerging from concealment) is carried by *veḷippaṭu* alone.

### 2.2 CAUSATION (factual) constructions carry VERIFICATION meaning

No dedicated VERIFICATION construction is attested as formally distinct from the causative pattern. The meanings "to confirm," "to certify," and "to prove" are expressed by specific lexical items (*uṟutippaṭuttu*, *nirūpikku*) that operate within the general causative morphosyntactic frame (*paṭuttu* or lexical verb + nominal object). VERIFICATION is therefore not a separate morphosyntactic operator in Tamil as documented; it is a semantic specialization within the CAUSATION (factual) construction, distinguished only by lexical choice.

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION is not attested as a construction

The data contain no construction specifically encoding the act of showing something to be false or refuting a claim. The closest attested patterns are: (a) negative attribution (*ātāramaṟṟa*, "lacking evidence"), which encodes absence of support rather than active refutation; and (b) the binary question construction (*uṇmaiyā illai poyyā*), which frames the true/false opposition as an interrogative but does not itself assert falsity. No morphological or syntactic pattern for "to disprove" or "to refute" is attested in the P4a/P4b data. This constitutes a structural gap in the documented inventory.

### 2.4 Asymmetry between positive and negative agentive forms

The data explicitly note that agentive nominalizations for negative truth-domain properties (*poyyan* "liar," *vañcakaṉ* "deceiver," *kapaṭi* "hypocrite") are [CORE] and attested across registers including colloquial speech. Agentive forms for positive truth-domain properties (*uṇmaiyāḷar* "person of truth," *nēṟmaiyāḷar* "person of integrity") are rated [MARGINAL] and restricted to formal or elevated registers. This asymmetry is explicitly flagged in both P4a and P4b. The CAUSATION (character) operator is therefore partially available: fully available for negative character attribution, marginally available for positive character attribution.

### 2.5 Asymmetry in the EPISTEMIC DISCOURSE MARKER

The epistemic discourse marker function (sentence-initial adverb marking correction or strong factual assertion) is attested only for *uṇmaiyil* / *uṇmaiyilēyē*, derived from *uṇmai* ("truth"). P4b explicitly states that no parallel forms from *poy* ("falsehood") or *nēṟmai* ("honesty/integrity") are attested in this discourse-marker function. This means the grammaticalized epistemic marker is lexically restricted to the *uṇmai* base. The EPISTEMIC DISCOURSE MARKER construction does not map directly onto any of the ten canonical CLCA operators listed in the task; it is noted here as an additional construction type attested in the data that falls outside the canonical inventory.

### 2.6 NEGATION is morphological, not analytic

In the truth domain, negation of attributive properties is expressed morphologically through two privative participial suffixes (*-aṟṟa*, *-illāta*) rather than through a separate analytic negation word modifying the positive form. The two forms are register-differentiated (*-aṟṟa* formal, *-illāta* cross-register) but semantically equivalent. The binary question construction provides an analytic *illai* element, but this functions as an interrogative coordinator ("or not"), not as a negation operator applied to a truth-domain predicate.

### 2.7 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION do not share the same construction

VERIFICATION is expressed through the causative frame with specific lexical items (*uṟutippaṭuttu*, *nirūpikku*). DISCONFIRMATION has no attested construction in the data. These two operators therefore do not share a construction; rather, one is partially filled (through causative substitution) and the other is absent.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps
- **DISCONFIRMATION**: No construction encoding the act of showing something to be false or refuting a claim is attested in the P4a/P4b data. Neither the negative attribution constructions nor the binary question construction fills this function. This is a structural gap.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps
- **VERIFICATION**: No dedicated morphosyntactic operator for verification is attested. The meaning is expressed through lexical specialization within the general CAUSATION (factual) construction (*uṟutippaṭuttu*, *nirūpikku*). The gap is lexical in the sense that VERIFICATION is not a distinct construction type but is carried by specific lexical items within an existing frame.
- **EPISTEMIC DISCOURSE MARKER from *poy* or *nēṟmai***: The grammaticalized sentence-initial epistemic marker is attested only for *uṇmai*. No parallel lexicalized forms from *poy* or *nēṟmai* are attested. This is a lexical gap within the epistemic marker paradigm.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps
- No conceptual gaps are identifiable from the data alone. All major truth-domain concepts (truth, falsehood, honesty, deception, evidence, credibility) are lexically represented. The gaps identified above are constructional or lexical, not conceptual.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps
- **CAUSATION (character) — positive agents**: Positive agentive nominalizations (*uṇmaiyāḷar*, *nēṟmaiyāḷar*) are [MARGINAL] and register-restricted (formal/elevated only). Negative agentive nominalizations (*poyyan*, *vañcakaṉ*, *kapaṭi*) are [CORE] and cross-register. The positive side of the CAUSATION (character) operator is therefore underrepresented relative to the negative side.
- **EPISTEMIC DISCOURSE MARKER — truth vs. falsehood**: The grammaticalized epistemic marker is attested only for *uṇmai* (truth-asserting direction). No parallel marker for the falsehood direction is attested.
- **Abstract nominalization — *-mai* vs. *taṉmai***: The *-mai* forms are fully productive and cross-register; the *taṉmai* compound forms are [MARGINAL] and formal-register-restricted. Both encode abstract nominalization, but the *taṉmai* forms are partially unavailable in spoken and informal registers.
