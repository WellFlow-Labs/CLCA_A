# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b COMPOSITIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: English (en)

## MULTI-WORD CONSTRUCTIONS

### BASE: true

#### 1. come true
**Example:** Her dreams finally came true.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** be realized, be fulfilled, become reality
**Usage:** Describes wishes, predictions, or dreams being actualized in reality. Common in narrative and everyday speech about hopes and expectations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. hold true
**Example:** This principle holds true across all cases.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** remain valid, continue to apply, be accurate
**Usage:** Evaluates statements, principles, or patterns as remaining accurate across contexts or time. Used in analytical and explanatory discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. ring true
**Example:** His explanation rings true.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** seem genuine, sound believable, feel authentic
**Usage:** Evaluates statements or accounts as seeming genuine or believable based on intuitive assessment. Common in evaluative discourse about credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 4. true to form
**Example:** True to form, he arrived late.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** as expected, characteristically, predictably
**Usage:** Evaluates behavior or events as consistent with established patterns or character. Common in narrative and descriptive contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 5. true to life
**Example:** The film is remarkably true to life.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** realistic, lifelike, authentic
**Usage:** Evaluates artistic representations, descriptions, or portrayals as accurately reflecting reality. Used in aesthetic and literary criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 6. true to one's word
**Example:** She was true to her word and returned the money.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** keeping promises, reliable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates persons as fulfilling their promises or commitments. Used in moral and practical evaluation of character.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 7. true to oneself
**Example:** He remained true to himself despite the pressure.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** authentic, maintaining one's principles, not compromising one's values
**Usage:** Evaluates persons as maintaining their authentic identity or principles. Common in moral and personal development discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 8. too good to be true
**Example:** The offer seemed too good to be true.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** suspiciously attractive, unlikely to be genuine
**Usage:** Expresses skepticism about claims or offers that seem unrealistically favorable. Common in everyday evaluative discourse.

#### 9. if truth be told
**Example:** If truth be told, I never liked him.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** to be honest, frankly speaking, actually
**Usage:** Discourse marker introducing a frank or honest admission, often contradicting previous statements or appearances. Somewhat formal or literary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 10. truth be told
**Example:** Truth be told, we made several mistakes.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** to be honest, frankly speaking, actually
**Usage:** Discourse marker introducing a frank or honest admission. More common than "if truth be told" in contemporary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 11. in truth
**Example:** In truth, the situation was far worse.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** actually, in fact, truly
**Usage:** Formal or literary expression emphasizing the truth of a statement. Used to introduce corrections or clarifications.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 12. the truth of the matter
**Example:** The truth of the matter is that we failed.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** the actual facts, what really happened, the reality
**Usage:** Phrase introducing a frank statement of facts, often after discussion or speculation. Common in explanatory discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 13. tell the truth
**Example:** I'm going to tell you the truth.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** speak honestly, be truthful, reveal facts
**Usage:** Refers to the act of speaking truthfully. Extremely common across all registers.

#### 14. speak the truth
**Example:** He always speaks the truth, no matter the cost.
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Translation:** be truthful, tell the truth, be honest

#### 15. moment of truth
**Example:** This is the moment of truth.

#### 16. the truth will out

#### 17. nothing could be further from the truth

#### 18. to tell (you) the truth

#### 19. the plain truth

#### 20. the honest truth

#### 21. the whole truth

#### 22. a grain of truth

#### 24. home truth

#### 25. gospel truth

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Distribution Patterns

1. **High productivity bases:** "true," "false," "right," and "wrong" show extensive multi-word construction patterns, reflecting their centrality in everyday epistemic and moral evaluation.

2. **Moderate productivity bases:** "real," "honest," "correct," "mistaken," and "sound" show several multi-word constructions, often in specific registers or contexts.

3. **Low productivity bases:** Many technical or formal terms ("accurate," "authentic," "reliable," "veridical," "veracious," "mendacious," "fallacious," "erroneous," "spurious," "apocryphal") show few or no multi-word constructions beyond their derivational forms.

4. **Fixed expressions:** Several bases appear primarily in fixed letter closings ("faithfully," "sincerely") or borrowed phrases ("bona fide").

### Functional Categories

1. **Discourse markers:** Many constructions function as discourse markers introducing frank statements: "to be honest," "to tell the truth," "truth be told," "if truth be told," "for real though."

2. **Idiomatic expressions:** Numerous idioms for authenticity ("the real deal," "the genuine article," "the real thing"), correctness ("on the right track," "get it right"), and error ("go wrong," "get wrong").

3. **Proverbial expressions:** Some constructions are proverbial: "two wrongs don't make a right," "the truth will out."

4. **Technical collocations:** Certain bases appear primarily in technical contexts: "false positive/negative," "spurious correlation," "in real time."

5. **Evaluative phrases:** Many constructions serve evaluative functions: "ring true/false," "legitimate question/concern," "safe and sound."

### Register Variation

1. **Informal/conversational:** "for real," "get real," "keep it real," "the real deal," "for real though"

2. **Formal/literary:** "in truth," "if truth be told," "of sound mind," "stand corrected"

3. **Legal/technical:** "false imprisonment," "false arrest," "of sound mind," "no longer valid"

4. **Conventional/formulaic:** "yours faithfully," "yours sincerely," "safe and sound"

### Asymmetries

1. "True" and "false" show parallel constructions in some cases ("ring true/false") but not others ("come true" has no *"come false").

2. "Right" and "wrong" show some parallelism ("in the right/wrong," "get it right/wrong") but many unique constructions for each.

3. Technical terms show minimal multi-word construction productivity compared to everyday terms.

4. Borrowed terms ("bona fide," "ersatz") and highly formal terms ("veridical," "mendacious") show no productive multi-word construction patterns.
