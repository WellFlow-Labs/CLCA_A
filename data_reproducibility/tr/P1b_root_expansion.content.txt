# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Turkish (tr)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **doğru** (doğru)
- **Participates in:** 1 P1 item directly, but serves as foundational root
- **Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, directness, correctness; conveys alignment with fact or proper direction

### Root 2: **hak** (hak)
- **Participates in:** 4 P1 items (hak, hakikat, haklı, haksız)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Right(ness), truth, justice; from Arabic *ḥaqq*, conveys what is due, true, or just

### Root 3: **gerçek** (gerçek)
- **Participates in:** 1 P1 item directly
- **Core meaning contribution:** Reality, actuality, genuineness; emphasizes what is real versus apparent or false

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: **doğru**

1. **doğruluk**
   - Transliteration: doğruluk
   - Literal breakdown: doğru (straight/true) + -luk (nominalizer)
   - Translation: truth, correctness, honesty, integrity
   - Context: Abstract noun used in philosophical and moral discourse to denote the quality of being true or honest. Common in statements like "doğruluk önemlidir" (truth/honesty is important).
   - [CORE]

2. **doğrulamak**
   - Transliteration: doğrulamak
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -la (verbalizer) + -mak (infinitive)
   - Translation: to verify, to confirm, to validate
   - Context: Used in scientific, legal, and administrative contexts to indicate the act of confirming or verifying information. Essential in research methodology and fact-checking.
   - [CORE]

3. **doğrulama**
   - Transliteration: doğrulama
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -la + -ma (verbal noun)
   - Translation: verification, confirmation, validation
   - Context: Nominal form used in technical and administrative contexts, particularly in computing (authentication), legal procedures, and quality control.
   - [CORE]

4. **doğrudan**
   - Transliteration: doğrudan
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -dan (ablative case marker, lexicalized)
   - Translation: directly, straightforwardly
   - Context: Adverb indicating direct action or communication without intermediaries. Used in both spatial ("doğrudan gitmek" - to go directly) and communicative contexts.
   - [CORE]

5. **doğrultmak**
   - Transliteration: doğrultmak
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -lt (causative) + -mak
   - Translation: to straighten, to direct, to aim
   - Context: Used for physically straightening objects or metaphorically directing efforts or attention toward something. Common in both concrete and abstract contexts.
   - [CORE]

6. **doğrultu**
   - Transliteration: doğrultu
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -ltu (direction nominalizer)
   - Translation: direction, line, course
   - Context: Technical and everyday term for direction or trajectory. Used in navigation, geometry, and metaphorically for policy direction ("bu doğrultuda" - in this direction/vein).
   - [CORE]

7. **doğru dürüst**
   - Transliteration: doğru dürüst
   - Literal breakdown: doğru (true) + dürüst (honest) [reduplicative compound]
   - Translation: proper, decent, respectable, adequate
   - Context: Colloquial expression meaning "proper" or "decent," often used to describe adequate quality or respectable behavior. Can be used negatively: "doğru dürüst bir iş yok" (there's no proper job).
   - [CORE]

8. **doğrucu**
   - Transliteration: doğrucu
   - Literal breakdown: doğru + -cu (agentive/adherent suffix)
   - Translation: truthful person, honest person
   - Context: Less common agentive form describing someone characterized by truthfulness. More literary or formal than everyday speech.
   - [MARGINAL]

9. **doğrulukçu**
   - Transliteration: doğrulukçu
   - Literal breakdown: doğruluk + -çu (adherent suffix)
   - Translation: advocate of truth, principled person
   - Context: Describes someone who strongly adheres to principles of truth and honesty, sometimes with connotation of rigidity. Used in character descriptions.
   - [MARGINAL]

10. **doğrusunu söylemek**
    - Transliteration: doğrusunu söylemek
    - Literal breakdown: doğru-su-nu (its truth-ACC) + söyle-mek (to say)
    - Translation: to tell the truth, to be honest
    - Context: Fixed phrase used to introduce honest statements or confessions. Very common in conversational Turkish: "Doğrusunu söylemek gerekirse..." (To tell the truth...).
    - [CORE]

11. **doğru mu?**
    - Transliteration: doğru mu
    - Literal breakdown: doğru + mu (question particle)
    - Translation: is it true? right? correct?
    - Context: Common question form used to seek confirmation or verification. Can be used as tag question or standalone inquiry.
    - [CORE]

12. **doğru söylemek**
    - Transliteration: doğru söylemek
    - Literal breakdown: doğru + söyle-mek (to say)
    - Translation: to tell the truth, to speak truthfully
    - Context: Phrase emphasizing truthful speech. Distinguished from "doğrusunu söylemek" by being more general rather than introducing specific confession.
    - [CORE]

13. **doğru yol**
    - Transliteration: doğru yol
    - Literal breakdown: doğru + yol (path/way)
    - Translation: right path, correct way, straight path
    - Context: Used literally for direct routes and metaphorically for moral or religious righteousness. Common in religious discourse and moral instruction.
    - [CORE]

14. **doğru düzgün**
    - Transliteration: doğru düzgün
    - Literal breakdown: doğru + düzgün (orderly, proper) [alliterative compound]
    - Translation: proper, decent, orderly, respectable
    - Context: Similar to "doğru dürüst" but emphasizes orderliness and propriety. Common in everyday speech to describe adequate or respectable quality.
    - [CORE]

15. **doğruca**
    - Transliteration: doğruca
    - Literal breakdown: doğru + -ca (manner suffix)
    - Translation: directly, straight away
    - Context: Adverbial form indicating direct manner of action. Slightly more emphatic than "doğrudan."
    - [CORE]

### ROOT 2: **hak**

1. **hakkında**
   - Transliteration: hakkında
   - Literal breakdown: hak-kı-n-da (right-POSS-GEN-LOC, lexicalized)
   - Translation: about, concerning, regarding
   - Context: Extremely common postposition used to indicate the topic or subject of discussion. Essential in all registers: "bu konu hakkında" (about this topic).
   - [CORE]

2. **hakkaniyet**
   - Transliteration: hakkaniyet
   - Literal breakdown: from Arabic *ḥaqqāniyya* (righteousness, fairness)
   - Translation: fairness, equity, justice
   - Context: Formal term used in legal and ethical discourse to denote fairness and equitable treatment. More elevated than simple "adalet."
   - [CORE]

3. **hakketmek**
   - Transliteration: hakketmek
   - Literal breakdown: hak + -et (light verb) + -mek
   - Translation: to deserve, to merit, to earn
   - Context: Common verb indicating deservingness or merit. Used in moral judgments and evaluations: "bunu hak etti" (he/she deserved this).
   - [CORE]

4. **hakkı olmak**
   - Transliteration: hakkı olmak
   - Literal breakdown: hak-kı (right-POSS) + ol-mak (to be)
   - Translation: to have the right, to be entitled
   - Context: Fixed expression indicating entitlement or rightful claim. Common in legal and everyday contexts: "senin hakkın var" (you have the right/you're entitled).
   - [CORE]

5. **hak vermek**
   - Transliteration: hak vermek
   - Literal breakdown: hak + ver-mek (to give)
   - Translation: to agree with, to side with, to acknowledge someone is right
   - Context: Common expression meaning to concede that someone is correct or justified. Used in arguments and discussions: "sana hak veriyorum" (I agree with you/you're right).
   - [CORE]

6. **hak yemek**
   - Transliteration: hak yemek
   - Literal breakdown: hak + ye-mek (to eat)
   - Translation: to violate someone's rights, to wrong someone
   - Context: Idiomatic expression meaning to unjustly take what belongs to another or violate their rights. Strong moral condemnation in Turkish culture.
   - [CORE]

7. **haksızlık**
   - Transliteration: haksızlık
   - Literal breakdown: hak-sız-lık (right-without-nominalizer)
   - Translation: injustice, unfairness, wrong
   - Context: Abstract noun denoting unjust action or state. Common in moral and legal discourse: "bu bir haksızlık" (this is an injustice).
   - [CORE]

8. **haksızlık etmek**
   - Transliteration: haksızlık etmek
   - Literal breakdown: haksızlık + et-mek (to do)
   - Translation: to wrong someone, to treat unjustly
   - Context: Verbal phrase indicating commission of injustice. Common in accusations and moral judgments.
   - [CORE]

9. **haklılık**
   - Transliteration: haklılık
   - Literal breakdown: hak-lı-lık (right-with-nominalizer)
   - Translation: rightness, justification, being in the right
   - Context: Abstract noun denoting the state of being right or justified. Used in arguments and legal contexts.
   - [CORE]

10. **hak sahibi**
    - Transliteration: hak sahibi
    - Literal breakdown: hak + sahib-i (owner-POSS, from Arabic)
    - Translation: rightful owner, rights holder, entitled person
    - Context: Legal and formal term for someone who holds rights to something. Common in property law and intellectual property contexts.
    - [CORE]

11. **hak iddia etmek**
    - Transliteration: hak iddia etmek
    - Literal breakdown: hak + iddia (claim, from Arabic) + et-mek
    - Translation: to claim a right, to assert entitlement
    - Context: Legal and formal expression for asserting one's rights or claims. Used in disputes and legal proceedings.
    - [CORE]

12. **hak aramak**
    - Transliteration: hak aramak
    - Literal breakdown: hak + ara-mak (to seek)
    - Translation: to seek justice, to pursue one's rights
    - Context: Expression for actively pursuing justice or asserting rights, often through legal channels. Common in discussions of legal recourse.
    - [CORE]

13. **hak ve adalet**
    - Transliteration: hak ve adalet
    - Literal breakdown: hak + ve (and) + adalet (justice)
    - Translation: right and justice, justice and fairness
    - Context: Fixed phrase common in legal and political discourse, often used rhetorically to invoke principles of justice.
    - [CORE]

14. **hakkını almak**
    - Transliteration: hakkını almak
    - Literal breakdown: hak-kı-nı (right-POSS-ACC) + al-mak (to take)
    - Translation: to get one's due, to obtain justice
    - Context: Expression meaning to receive what one deserves or is entitled to. Can be positive (getting deserved reward) or negative (getting deserved punishment).
    - [CORE]

15. **hakkını helal etmek**
    - Transliteration: hakkını helal etmek
    - Literal breakdown: hak-kı-nı + helal (permissible, from Arabic) + et-mek
    - Translation: to forgive someone's debt/wrong, to waive one's right
    - Context: Religious and cultural expression meaning to forgive someone who has wronged you or to waive a debt. Important in Islamic ethical framework.
    - [CORE]

16. **hakkını vermek**
    - Transliteration: hakkını vermek
    - Literal breakdown: hak-kı-nı + ver-mek (to give)
    - Translation: to give credit, to do justice to, to acknowledge merit
    - Context: Expression meaning to properly acknowledge someone's merit or to do something justice. Common in evaluations: "hakkını vermek lazım" (one must give credit where it's due).
    - [CORE]

17. **hakikatçi**
    - Transliteration: hakikatçi
    - Literal breakdown: hakikat + -çi (adherent suffix)
    - Translation: seeker of truth, realist
    - Context: Describes someone devoted to truth or reality. Can be used philosophically or to describe realistic, no-nonsense people.
    - [MARGINAL]

18. **hakikaten**
    - Transliteration: hakikaten
    - Literal breakdown: hakikat + -en (adverbial suffix)
    - Translation: really, truly, indeed, in reality
    - Context: Common adverb emphasizing truth or reality of a statement. Used across all registers: "hakikaten öyle" (it's really so).
    - [CORE]

19. **hakiki**
    - Transliteration: hakiki
    - Literal breakdown: from Arabic *ḥaqīqī* (real, true, genuine)
    - Translation: real, genuine, true, authentic
    - Context: Adjective emphasizing genuineness or authenticity. More formal than "gerçek," often used in literary or elevated discourse.
    - [CORE]

20. **hak ettiği değer**
    - Transliteration: hak ettiği değer
    - Literal breakdown: hak et-tiğ-i (deserve-REL-POSS) + değer (value)
    - Translation: deserved value, due recognition
    - Context: Expression referring to proper recognition or valuation. Common in discussions of merit and appreciation.
    - [CORE]

21. **Allah hakkı için**
    - Transliteration: Allah hakkı için
    - Literal breakdown: Allah (God) + hak-kı (right-POSS) + için (for)
    - Translation: for God's sake, in the name of God
    - Context: Religious oath or emphatic appeal invoking divine truth/right. Used to emphasize sincerity or make urgent requests.
    - [CORE]

22. **hak gözetmek**
    - Transliteration: hak gözetmek
    - Literal breakdown: hak + gözet-mek (to observe/watch)
    - Translation: to respect rights, to be mindful of justice
    - Context: Expression meaning to be careful to respect others' rights and act justly. Used in moral instruction.
    - [MARGINAL]

### ROOT 3: **gerçek**

1. **gerçeklik**
   - Transliteration: gerçeklik
   - Literal breakdown: gerçek + -lik (nominalizer)
   - Translation: reality, realness, actuality
   - Context: Abstract noun denoting the quality or state of being real. Common in philosophical discourse and discussions distinguishing reality from appearance or fiction.
   - [CORE]

2. **gerçekten**
   - Transliteration: gerçekten
   - Literal breakdown: gerçek + -ten (ablative, lexicalized as adverb)
   - Translation: really, truly, actually, genuinely
   - Context: Very common adverb used to emphasize truth or reality of statements. Essential in everyday speech: "gerçekten mi?" (really?).
   - [CORE]

3. **gerçekleşmek**
   - Transliteration: gerçekleşmek
   - Literal breakdown: gerçek + -leş (become) + -mek

   - Literal breakdown: gerçek + -leş + -tir (causative) + -mek

## Step 3 — Note Peripheral Extensions

### Extensions of **doğru**:

1. **Spatial/directional uses**: The root maintains strong connection to physical straightness and direction ("doğrultu," "doğrudan," "doğrultmak"), which appears to be the etymological source of the truth-related meanings. The spatial sense remains active alongside epistemic uses.

2. **Adequacy/propriety marking**: In compounds like "doğru dürüst" and "doğru düzgün," the root shifts toward marking general adequacy, propriety, or respectability rather than specifically truth or correctness. These expressions evaluate quality or social acceptability.

3. **Manner intensification**: Forms like "doğruca" function primarily as manner adverbs emphasizing directness of action, with reduced epistemic content.

### Extensions of **hak**:

1. **Topicalization function**: "Hakkında" has grammaticalized into a postposition meaning "about/concerning" with no remaining sense of right or truth—purely topical/relational function.

2. **Religious/divine reference**: "Allah hakkı için" and related expressions invoke divine truth/right in oath-like constructions, extending into religious performative speech acts.

3. **Deservingness/merit domain**: Many expressions ("hak etmek," "hakkını almak") focus on deservingness and merit rather than truth per se, though connected through notion of what is rightfully due.

4. **Forgiveness/waiving**: "Hakkını helal etmek" extends into religious-ethical domain of forgiveness and debt cancellation, moving beyond simple truth or justice evaluation.

### Extensions of **gerçek**:

1. **Technological terminology**: "Sanal gerçeklik" and "artırılmış gerçeklik" are technical terms in computing where "gerçeklik" functions as equivalent to English "reality" in compound technical terms.

2. **Artistic movement designation**: "Gerçekçilik" as term for Realism in art history functions as proper name for aesthetic movement, extending beyond simple evaluation of truth-likeness.

3. **Identity/essence marking**: Expressions like "gerçek kimlik," "gerçek yüz," "gerçek değer" use the root to mark authentic essence or true nature, extending into metaphysical/essential domains.

4. **Actualization/realization**: The highly productive "gerçekleşmek/gerçekleştirmek" family focuses on process of becoming real or making real, emphasizing ontological transition rather than epistemic evaluation.

5. **Surrealism terminology**: "Gerçeküstü" functions as technical term for Surrealism, where the root participates in naming an artistic movement defined by transcending reality.
