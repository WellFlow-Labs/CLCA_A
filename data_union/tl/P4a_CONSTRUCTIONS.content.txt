# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Predicate-initial clause: **[truth-domain adjective] + *ang* + [noun phrase]**

Tagalog is predicate-initial; the truth-evaluating adjective or nominal occupies the predicate position, and the subject follows marked by *ang* (topic marker) or a pronoun. The copula is absent; the adjective alone constitutes the predicate.

### Examples
- **Totoo ang balita.** / Totoo ang balita. / "The news is true."
- **Tama ang sagot mo.** / Tama ang sagot mo. / "Your answer is correct."
- *Totoo iyan.* → Totoo iyan → "That is true."
- *Mali ang sagot.* → Mali ang sagot → "The answer is wrong / incorrect."
- *Tama ang sagot niya.* → Tama ang sagot niya. → "His/her answer is correct."

### Notes
Tagalog is predicate-initial; truth-domain adjectives (*totoo*, *tama*, *mali*, *tunay*, *tapat*, *huwad*, *peke*, *kredible*) appear as verbal predicates before the *ang*-marked topic. No copula is used. The same slot accepts the full range of P1 items.

The topic marker *ang* introduces the subject NP. This is the unmarked structure for all attributive truth-predications in Tagalog.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**Hindi + [truth-domain adjective] + *ang* + [noun phrase]**

(*Hindi* is the standard negator for non-verbal predicates; *wala* is used for existential negation of nominal truth-terms)

### Examples
- **Hindi totoo ang alegasyon.** / Hindi totoo ang alegasyon. / "The allegation is not true."
- **Hindi tapat ang kanyang mga salita.** / Hindi tapat ang kanyang mga salita. / "His/her words are not honest."
- *Hindi totoo iyan.* → Hindi totoo iyan → "That is not true / That is false."
- *Hindi mali ang ginawa mo.* → Hindi mali ang ginawa mo → "What you did was not wrong."
- *Hindi totoo ang kanyang sinabi.* → Hindi totoo ang kanyang sinabi. → "What he/she said is not true."
- *Walang katotohanan ang akusasyon.* → Walang katotohanan ang akusasyon. → "The accusation has no truth to it."

### Notes
*Hindi* is the standard negator for predicative adjectives and verbs. The clitic form *di-* is used in more formal or written registers (*di-tunay*, *di-lehitimo*, *di-kredible*), typically hyphenated before the adjective.

*Hindi* negates adjectival predicates (*hindi totoo*, *hindi tama*, *hindi tunay*, *hindi tapat*). *Wala* + *-ng* linker negates existential constructions with abstract nominals (*walang katotohanan*, *walang katunayan*, *walang katapatan*); these two negation patterns are structurally distinct and not interchangeable.

The construction *hindi mali* frequently carries a hedging or defensive nuance beyond simple negation.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**[truth-domain adjective] + *na* (linker) + [noun]** — or — **[noun] + *na* + [truth-domain adjective]**

The linker *na* follows consonant-final forms; *-ng* is suffixed to vowel-final forms; the modifier precedes the head noun in the standard order. The ligature *-ng* (after vowel-final forms) or *na* (elsewhere) links modifier to head in either order; prenominal position is strongly preferred for truth-domain adjectives.

### Examples
- **Tunay na kaibigan** / Tunay na kaibigan / "A genuine friend"
- **Huwad na dokumento** / Huwad na dokumento / "A forged document"
- *totoong kwento* → totoong kwento → "true story / real story"
- *maling impormasyon* → maling impormasyon → "wrong information / misinformation"
- *Tunay na ginto* → Tunay na ginto → "Genuine gold"
- *Mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon* → Mapagkakatiwalaang pinagkukunan ng impormasyon → "Reliable source of information"

### Notes
The linker *na* / *-ng* is obligatory between modifier and head noun; it does not mark grammatical case. The pattern is fully productive across all truth-domain adjectives: *tunay na kaibigan* ("true friend"), *huwad na dokumento* ("forged document"), *kredibleng saksi* ("credible witness"), *tapat na tao*, *wastong paraan*, *kredible na saksi*.

This pattern is fully productive: *tunay na* + [any noun] asserts the genuine instance of that category.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern A
**Maging + [truth-domain adjective]** (+ *sa* + [goal/person])

*Maging* is the infinitive/imperative form of the copular-inchoative verb "to become/to be"; it introduces a change-of-state or injunctive reading.

### Pattern B
**[truth-adjective / nominal] + *na* (change-of-state particle)**

The discourse particle *na* attached after a predicate signals a change of state or the onset of a new situation. No separate inchoative verb is required.

### Examples
- **Maging tapat ka sa akin.** / Maging tapat ka sa akin. / "Be honest with me." (lit. "Become honest toward me.")
- **Maging totoo ka.** / Maging totoo ka. / "Be real / Be truthful."
- *Maging tapat sa iyong sarili.* → Maging tapat sa iyong sarili. → "Be honest with yourself."
- *Naging totoo ang kanyang pangarap.* → Naging totoo ang kanyang pangarap. → "His/her dream became real / came true."
- *Totoo na.* → Totoo na → "It's become real. / It's actually happening now."
- *Sigurado na ako.* → Sigurado na ako → "I'm certain now. / I've made sure of it."

### Notes
*Maging* (infinitive of *mag-* + *ging*) is the standard inchoative/equative verb. It expresses entry into a state rather than a stable property. The construction is most attested with *tapat* and *totoo*; it is less natural with *huwad* or *peke*, which describe objects rather than persons adopting a state.

*Naging* is the completed-aspect form ("became"). The construction is attested for *tapat* (moral injunction) and *totoo* (realization of a state); it is less conventionalized for *tama*, *tunay*, or borrowed adjectives in truth-evaluation contexts.

The form *nagkatotoo* ("came true," from *nagka-* + *totoo*) is an alternative inchoative specifically for the realization of predictions or wishes.

The particle-based inchoative (*na*) does not require a dedicated inchoative prefix. The same *na* marks completion or current relevance in other contexts; the inchoative reading arises from the combination with a stative predicate. No dedicated inchoative prefix for this semantic field is attested beyond the *maging* construction.

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**Pa- + [root] + -an verbal paradigm** (for *tunay*: *patunayan*); ***i-* + [correctness adjective]** (for *tama* → *itama*; *wasto* → *iwasto*)

### Examples
- **Patunayan mo ang iyong sinasabi.** / Patunayan mo ang iyong sinasabi. / "Prove what you are saying." (lit. "Cause your statement to be genuine/verified.")
- **Itama ang iyong mga pagkakamali.** / Itama ang iyong mga pagkakamali. / "Correct your mistakes." (lit. "Cause your mistakes to be right.")
- *Patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi.* → Patunayan mo ang iyong sinabi. → "Prove what you said." (cause it to be verified)
- *Itama ang pagkakamali.* → Itama ang pagkakamali → "Correct the mistake."
- *Iwasto ang mali.* → Iwasto ang mali → "Set the wrong right. / Correct the error."
- *Iniwasto na ang pagkakamali.* → Iniwasto na ang pagkakamali. → "The mistake has already been corrected."

### Notes
Tagalog does not have a single dedicated causative morpheme for truth-domain adjectives. Causative meaning is expressed through the *pa-* prefix (on *tunay* → *patunayan*, "to prove/verify") and the *i-* object-focus prefix (on *tama* → *itama*, *wastó* → *iwasto*, meaning "to correct/rectify"). These are lexically specific rather than a single productive causative pattern.

The *i-* causative pattern is productive for correctness adjectives (*tama* → *itama*; *wasto* → *iwasto*; *akma* → *iakma*) but is not attested for truth-predicate adjectives (*totoo*, *tunay*). For the verification/proof domain, the causative is formed differently: *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* → *patunayan* ("to cause to be proven; to prove to someone"), a distinct morphological pattern.

A productive causative verbal paradigm is attested only for *tunay* via *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* (*patunayan*, *pinatunayan*, *pagpapatunay*). No productive causative verbal paradigm is attested for *totoo* or *tapat* in the truth-evaluation domain; this is a notable asymmetry in the data.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
***Ka-*** + root + ***-an*** circumfix (primary pattern for native roots); ***pag-*** + root (action nominalization); ***-idad* suffix** (Spanish-borrowed adjectives)

### Examples
- **Ang katotohanan ay mahalaga.** / Ang katotohanan ay mahalaga. / "Truth is important." (*ka-* + *totoo* + *-an*)
- **Ang pagtapat niya ay nagulat sa lahat.** / Ang pagtapat niya ay nagulat sa lahat. / "His/her honest disclosure surprised everyone." (*pag-* + *tapat*)
- *katotohanan* → katotohanan → "truth / the truth / reality" (from *totoo*)
- *kasinungalingan* → kasinungalingan → "lie / falsehood / untruth" (from *sinungaling*)
- *Ang katotohanan ay mapait.* → Ang katotohanan ay mapait. → "The truth is bitter."
- *Ang kredibilidad ng saksi ay pinag-aralan ng hukuman.* → Ang kredibilidad ng saksi ay pinag-aralan ng hukuman. → "The credibility of the witness was examined by the court."

### Notes
The *ka-...-an* circumfix is the primary nominalizer for abstract states and domains: *katotohanan* (truth), *katunayan* (proof/genuineness), *katapatan* (honesty/loyalty), *kawastuhan* (correctness), *katamaan* (rightness), *kamalian* (error/mistake, from *mali*), *kasiguruhan* (certainty, from *siguro*), *katumpakan* (precision, from *tumpak*), *kaangkupan* (appropriateness, from *angkop*), *kadalisayan* (purity). The circumfix applies to both indigenous roots and integrated borrowings.

Spanish-borrowed adjectives take *-idad*: *kredibilidad*, *lehitimidad*.

The *pag-* prefix nominalizes the action: *pagtapat* (act of honest disclosure), *pagtotoo* (truth-telling), *pagpapatunay* (verification), *pagsisinungaling* ("the act of lying"), *pagwawasto* ("the act of correcting"), *pagkakamali* ("a specific instance of error"), *pagtatapat* (act of confessing), *paghahanap ng katotohanan* (truth-seeking). Both the *ka-...-an* and *pag-* patterns are productive across the P1 inventory.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**Root form used directly as noun** (for *sinungaling*); ***mang-/man-* + [root]** (for agentive deception terms); ***tagapag-* + [verbal base]** (for defender/advocate roles)

### Examples
- **Sinungaling ka!** / Sinungaling ka! / "You are a liar!"
- **Ang mamamahayag ay tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan.** / Ang mamamahayag ay tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan. / "The journalist is a defender of truth."
- *Sinungaling ka.* → Sinungaling ka → "You are a liar."
- *Huwag kang maging sinungaling.* → Huwag kang maging sinungaling → "Don't be a liar."
- *Tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan* → Tagapagtanggol ng katotohanan → "Defender of truth"

### Notes
There is no single productive agentive morpheme that derives "truth-teller," "deceiver," or "verifier" from the P1 roots as a regular pattern.

*Sinungaling* (liar) is a lexicalized root, not transparently derived from a truth-domain adjective. It functions as both noun ("liar") and adjective ("lying, false") without additional affixation.

*Manlinlang* (*man-* + *linlang*) is the agentive form for "one who deceives habitually."

*Tagapagtanggol* uses the *taga-* agentive prefix on a verbal base (*pagtanggol*, "defense") and is a compound construction rather than a simple derivation. The *tagapag-* prefix yields institutional/role agentives (*tagapagtanggol* — defender/advocate). These are not lexicalized as stable single-word items in this field.

*Mapanlinlang* (*mapa-* + *linlang*) is an adjectival agentive ("one prone to deceiving") but functions attributively rather than as a pure agentive nominal.

No parallel lexicalized agentive for "truth-teller" (e.g., *\*katotohanin*, *\*tagapagsabi ng katotohanan* as a stable single word) is attested in the data; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*ang nagsasabi ng katotohanan* — "one who tells the truth"). No common agentive nominal for "truth-teller" or "honest person" is formed by a dedicated agentive affix; instead, the adnominal construction *tapat na tao* ("an honest person") serves this function.

## 8. EMPHATIC INTENSIFICATION (REDUPLICATION)

### Pattern
**[truth-domain adjective] + *-ng*/*na* (linker) + [same adjective]** (full reduplication with linker)

### Examples
- **Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro.** / Totoong-totoo ito, hindi biro. / "This is absolutely real, not a joke."
- **Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal.** / Tunay na tunay ang kanyang pagmamahal. / "His/her love is absolutely genuine."

### Notes
Full reduplication with the linker (*-ng* after vowels, *na* elsewhere) is a productive emphatic pattern across the P1 inventory: *totoong-totoo*, *tunay na tunay*, *tapatang-tapat*, *maling-mali*, *wastong-wasto*, *huwad na huwad*. It consistently expresses the highest degree of the property, often in response to expressed doubt.

## 9. APPEARANCE / EVIDENTIAL FRAMING

### Pattern
**Mukhang** / **Parang** + [truth-domain adjective or noun phrase]

### Examples
- **Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala.** / Mukhang totoo ang produkto pero peke pala. / "The product looked real but turned out to be fake."
- **Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak.** / Parang totoo ang kanyang pagiyak. / "Her crying seemed real."

### Notes
*Mukha* (face/appearance) + *-ng* linker grammaticalizes as a visual-evidential marker ("looks like, appears to be"); *parang* ("like, as if") marks resemblance without assertion. Both are used specifically to evaluate surface appearance against a truth-domain standard without committing to the truth of the predicate. This construction type is absent from the standard attribution pattern and constitutes a distinct evidential slot in Tagalog truth-domain expression.

## 10. EXISTENTIAL CONSTRUCTION (PRESENCE / ABSENCE OF TRUTH OR PROOF)

### Pattern
***May* / *Wala* + *(-ng)* + [truth/proof nominal]**

The existential verb *may* ("there is/are; has") and its negative *wala* ("there is none; lacks") combine with truth and proof nominals to assert the presence or absence of truth, evidence, or trust.

### Examples
- *Walang pruweba.* → Walang pruweba → "There is no proof."
- *May katotohanan iyan.* → May katotohanan iyan → "There is truth in that. / That has some truth."

### Notes
This construction is highly productive across the proof/evidence cluster (*walang ebidensya*, *walang katunayan*, *may katunayan*, *may tiwala*) and the truth cluster (*walang katotohanan*, *may katotohanan*). The negative *wala* requires the linker *-ng* before a following noun (*walang pruweba*). This construction is the standard formula for asserting evidentiary sufficiency or insufficiency in legal and argumentative discourse.

## 11. DISCOURSE-FRAMING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Fixed sentence-initial phrases that frame the following proposition as frank, corrective, or factual: **Sa totoo lang**, **Ang totoo**, **Ang totoo nito**, **Sa katotohanan**, **Sa katunayan**

Both **Ang + [truth-nominal]** and **Sa + [truth-nominal]** function as sentence-initial pragmatic frames.

### Examples
- **Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam.** / Sa totoo lang, hindi ko alam. / "To tell the truth, I don't know."
- **Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.** / Ang totoo nito, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon. / "The truth of the matter is, the situation is more complicated."
- *Sa katotohanan, hindi ko alam.* → Sa katotohanan, hindi ko alam → "In truth, I don't know."
- *Sa katunayan, mas mataas ang bilang.* → Sa katunayan, mas mataas ang bilang → "In fact, the count is higher."
- *Ang totoo, hindi ko alam.* → Ang totoo, hindi ko alam. → "The truth is, I don't know."
- *Sa katotohanan, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon.* → Sa katotohanan, mas kumplikado ang sitwasyon. → "In truth, the situation is more complicated."

### Notes
These are conventionalized sentence-adverbial constructions in which a nominalized or topic-marked truth-domain expression scopes over the entire following clause, framing it as candid (*sa totoo lang*), corrective (*ang totoo*, *ang totoo nito*), or confirmatory (*sa katunayan* "in fact"). They are not predicative but function as discourse operators.

*Sa katotohanan* and *sa katunayan* are the two primary grammaticalized connectors in this field. A third pattern, *Ang totoo...* ("The truth is..."), uses the topic-marked nominalized form of *totoo* as a sentence-initial frame. These connectors function analogously to English "in truth," "in fact," and "the truth is," and their pragmatic force (correction, emphasis, concession) is not fully predictable from their compositional meanings.

No equivalent construction type is attested for *mali*, *huwad*, or *peke*.

This construction type is specific to truth-domain nominals and has no parallel in the correctness (*tama*) or authenticity (*tunay*) clusters at the same level of conventionalization.

## 12. PARTICLE-MARKED EPISTEMIC STANCE

### Pattern
**Totoo + [discourse particle]** (*ba*, *nga*, *pala*, *naman*, *pa rin*)

### Examples
- *Totoo nga pala.* → Totoo nga pala. → "So it really is true after all." (confirmed realization)
- *Totoo naman ang sinabi ko.* → Totoo naman ang sinabi ko. → "What I said is indeed true." (responsive affirmation against doubt)

### Notes
This construction type is specific to *totoo* among the truth-domain items; no parallel conventionalized particle-cluster constructions are attested for *tunay*, *tapat*, or *tama* at the same density. The particles encode distinct epistemic stances: *ba* (interrogative), *nga* (confirmation), *pala* (realization/discovery), *naman* (contrast/responsive), *pa rin* (persistence). These are not freely compositional in all combinations; *totoo nga pala* as a unit marks a specific epistemic update not reducible to the sum of its particles.
