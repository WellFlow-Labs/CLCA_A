# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Lexical Items

### 1
**Form:** אמת  
**Transliteration:** emet  
**Literal meaning:** truth, faithfulness  
**Typical English translations:** truth, true, truthfulness  
**Usage description:** The primary term for truth in Hebrew, used across all registers from biblical to modern contexts. Functions as both a noun and in adjectival constructions (be-emet "in truth, truly"). Appears in moral, legal, religious, and everyday evaluative contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 2
**Form:** שקר  
**Transliteration:** sheker  
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, deception  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception, false  
**Usage description:** The standard antonym of emet, denoting falsehood or deception. Used in moral condemnation, legal testimony contexts, and everyday speech to characterize statements or representations as false. Common in the phrase davar sheker "false statement."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 3
**Form:** נכון  
**Transliteration:** nakhon  
**Literal meaning:** correct, established, firm (from root k-w-n)  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, true, accurate  
**Usage description:** Widely used in modern Hebrew to affirm correctness of statements, answers, or assessments. Functions as both adjective and interjection ("Right!" "Correct!"). Less morally weighted than emet, more focused on factual accuracy.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4
**Form:** אמיתי  
**Transliteration:** amiti  
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (adjectival form derived from emet)  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic  
**Usage description:** Adjectival form used to characterize objects, persons, or statements as genuine or authentic. Common in phrases like sippur amiti "true story" or ohev amiti "true friend." Emphasizes genuineness rather than mere factual accuracy.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 5
**Form:** אמין  
**Transliteration:** amin  
**Literal meaning:** reliable, trustworthy (from root '-m-n)  
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible  
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, dependable  
**Usage description:** Describes persons, sources, or information as reliable or trustworthy. Commonly used in evaluating testimony, sources of information, or character. Related to the root of emet but focuses on reliability rather than truth per se.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6
**Form:** מהימן  
**Transliteration:** meheyман  
**Literal meaning:** trustworthy, reliable (hif'il participle from '-m-n)  
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible, authentic  
**Usage description:** Overlaps significantly with amin but often used in more formal or literary contexts. Frequently applied to sources, documents, or testimony in legal and academic settings. Can also describe persons of reliable character.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7
**Form:** צודק  
**Transliteration:** tsodek  
**Literal meaning:** right, just (from root ts-d-k)  
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, just  
**Usage description:** Used to affirm that someone is right in their statement or position, or that an action is just. Common in conversational Hebrew ("ata tsodek" = "you're right"). Carries connotations of both correctness and moral rightness.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8
**Form:** צדק  
**Transliteration:** tsedek  
**Literal meaning:** justice, righteousness  
**Typical English translations:** justice, righteousness, rightness  
**Usage description:** Abstract noun denoting justice or righteousness, used in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse. Appears in fixed expressions like mishpat tsedek "just judgment" and in evaluations of conduct and social arrangements.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 9
**Form:** דומה  
**Transliteration:** domeh  
**Literal meaning:** resembling, similar (from root d-m-h)  
**Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, like  
**Usage description:** Used to express resemblance or similarity between objects, persons, or situations. Functions as both verb and adjective. Common in comparative evaluations and descriptions of likeness.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 10
**Form:** מדויק  
**Transliteration:** meduyak  
**Literal meaning:** precise, exact (pu'al participle from d-y-k)  
**Typical English translations:** precise, exact, accurate  
**Usage description:** Emphasizes precision and exactness in statements, measurements, or representations. Commonly used in technical, scientific, and formal contexts where accuracy of detail is evaluated. Stronger than nakhon in implying exactitude.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 11
**Form:** מזויף  
**Transliteration:** mezuyaf  
**Literal meaning:** forged, falsified (pu'al participle from z-y-f)  
**Typical English translations:** fake, forged, counterfeit, falsified  
**Usage description:** Applied to objects, documents, or representations that are deliberately made to deceive. Common in legal contexts and in evaluating authenticity of artifacts, documents, or currency. Implies intentional deception.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 12
**Form:** מציאותי  
**Transliteration:** metsi'uti  
**Literal meaning:** real, realistic (adjectival form from metsi'ut "reality")  
**Typical English translations:** real, realistic, actual  
**Usage description:** Used to characterize something as real or realistic, as opposed to imaginary or idealistic. Common in philosophical discourse and everyday evaluation of plans, expectations, or representations. Can mean both "real" and "realistic/feasible."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 13
**Form:** באמת  
**Transliteration:** be-emet  
**Literal meaning:** in truth  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, in truth, actually  
**Usage description:** Fixed adverbial expression used to emphasize the truth or reality of a statement. Functions as an intensifier or to express genuine agreement or confirmation. Very common in spoken Hebrew.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 14
**Form:** כן  
**Transliteration:** ken  
**Literal meaning:** yes, so, thus  
**Typical English translations:** yes, right, correct, so  
**Usage description:** Basic affirmative particle used to confirm truth or correctness of statements. Can function as simple "yes" or as "that's right/correct." Extremely common in all registers of Hebrew.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 15
**Form:** לא נכון  
**Transliteration:** lo nakhon  
**Literal meaning:** not correct  
**Typical English translations:** incorrect, wrong, not true  
**Usage description:** Standard negation of nakhon, used to deny correctness or accuracy of statements. Common in corrections, disagreements, and evaluations. Less morally charged than sheker.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 16
**Form:** טעות  
**Transliteration:** ta'ut  
**Literal meaning:** error, mistake  
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, wrong  
**Usage description:** Denotes an error or mistake, whether in statement, judgment, or action. Used to characterize something as wrong without necessarily implying moral fault or deliberate deception. Common in legal contexts (ta'ut be-mekakh "error in transaction").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 17
**Form:** מוטעה  
**Transliteration:** mut'eh  
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, erroneous (huf'al participle from t-'-h)  
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, erroneous  
**Usage description:** Describes persons or beliefs as mistaken or in error. Often used to characterize someone's understanding or position as incorrect without harsh moral judgment. Common in argumentative and corrective contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 18
**Form:** אותנטי  
**Transliteration:** otenti  
**Literal meaning:** authentic (loanword from European languages)  
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine  
**Usage description:** Modern term borrowed into Hebrew, used especially in aesthetic, cultural, and commercial contexts to evaluate genuineness. Often applied to art, cultural expressions, or personal authenticity. Overlaps with amiti but more contemporary in register.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 19
**Form:** מוצדק  
**Transliteration:** mutsдak  
**Literal meaning:** justified (huf'al participle from ts-d-k)  
**Typical English translations:** justified, warranted, legitimate  
**Usage description:** Used to evaluate actions, claims, or beliefs as justified or warranted. Common in moral and legal reasoning to assess whether something is rightfully done or claimed. Implies having adequate grounds or reasons.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 20
**Form:** יושר  
**Transliteration:** yosher  
**Literal meaning:** straightness, uprightness, honesty  
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, uprightness  
**Usage description:** Denotes honesty and moral uprightness, particularly in conduct and character. Used in evaluating persons and actions for moral rectitude. Common in phrases like yosher lev "honesty of heart" and in business ethics contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 21
**Form:** כנות  
**Transliteration:** khenut  
**Literal meaning:** honesty, sincerity (from root k-n-n)  
**Typical English translations:** honesty, sincerity, frankness  
**Usage description:** Emphasizes sincerity and honesty in speech and conduct. Often used to evaluate whether someone is being genuine or forthright. Common in interpersonal contexts and in evaluating testimony or statements.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 22
**Form:** כנה  
**Transliteration:** khen  
**Literal meaning:** honest, sincere (adjective from k-n-n)  
**Typical English translations:** honest, sincere, frank, genuine  
**Usage description:** Adjectival form describing persons or statements as honest and sincere. Used to evaluate whether someone is being truthful and genuine in their expression. Common in both formal and informal contexts.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 23
**Form:** מציאות  
**Transliteration:** metsi'ut  
**Literal meaning:** reality, actuality  
**Typical English translations:** reality, actuality, fact  
**Usage description:** Abstract noun denoting reality or what actually exists. Used in philosophical discourse and everyday speech to distinguish real from imaginary or to invoke factual constraints. Common in phrases evaluating correspondence to reality.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 24
**Form:** עובדה  
**Transliteration:** uvdah  
**Literal meaning:** fact (from root '-b-d "work, do")  
**Typical English translations:** fact  
**Usage description:** Standard term for fact, used to characterize statements or situations as factual rather than opinion or speculation. Common in argumentative contexts to assert factual status. Often used emphatically ("ze uvdah" = "that's a fact").  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 25
**Form:** ודאי  
**Transliteration:** vadai  
**Literal meaning:** certain, sure  
**Typical English translations:** certain, sure, definite  
**Usage description:** Expresses certainty or definiteness about statements or situations. Functions as both adjective and adverb. Common in assertions of confidence about truth or occurrence of something.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 26
**Form:** בטוח  
**Transliteration:** batuakh  
**Literal meaning:** sure, certain, safe (from root b-t-kh)  
**Typical English translations:** sure, certain, confident  
**Usage description:** Expresses certainty or confidence, often about truth of statements or reliability of information. Can describe both epistemic confidence and safety/security. Very common in conversational Hebrew.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 27
**Form:** ספק  
**Transliteration:** safek  
**Literal meaning:** doubt, uncertainty  
**Typical English translations:** doubt, uncertainty, question  
**Usage description:** Denotes doubt or uncertainty about truth, correctness, or occurrence. Used in legal contexts (safek de-oraita "biblical doubt") and everyday speech to express or acknowledge uncertainty. Common in phrases like ein safek "no doubt."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 28
**Form:** מפוקפק  
**Transliteration:** mefukpak  
**Literal meaning:** doubtful, dubious (pu'al participle from f-k-f-k, reduplication of s-f-k)  
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, dubious, questionable  
**Usage description:** Characterizes statements, claims, or situations as doubtful or questionable. Implies skepticism about truth or reliability. Common in evaluative contexts where credibility is at issue.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 29
**Form:** מהות  
**Transliteration:** mahut  
**Literal meaning:** essence, nature, whatness  
**Typical English translations:** essence, nature, substance  
**Usage description:** Philosophical and formal term denoting the essential nature or whatness of something. Used in contexts evaluating what something fundamentally is. Common in academic and philosophical Hebrew.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 30
**Form:** זיוף  
**Transliteration:** ziyuf  
**Literal meaning:** forgery, falsification (noun from z-y-f)  
**Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting  
**Usage description:** Denotes the act or result of forging or falsifying. Used in legal contexts and in evaluating authenticity of documents, currency, or representations. Implies deliberate deception.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
