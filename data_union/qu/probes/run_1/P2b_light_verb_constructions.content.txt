# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving lexical items identified in P1. Analysis is restricted to Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties), consistent with prior steps. Constructions are drawn from documented grammatical, lexicographic, and textual sources; no constructions are invented or back-translated from English. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Where a construction type is not attested for a given base form, this is stated explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

A note on the boundary between derivation and composition: Quechua noun-noun and adjective-noun collocations documented in P1b (e.g., *cheqa simi*, *allin runa*) are compositional in structure. Where such collocations function as fixed or semi-fixed multi-word units with identifiable usage patterns, they are documented here as constructions. Where they are fully transparent and productive (i.e., any adjective + any noun), they are noted as such without exhaustive listing.

## BASE FORM 1: cheqa

---

**Construction 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam kay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqam kay
4. **English translation:** It is true. / That is the truth. / It is real.
5. **Usage notes:** The most basic predicative truth assertion in Southern Quechua, combining the adjectival root with the direct evidential enclitic *-m* and the copular *kay*. The copula may be omitted in rapid speech, yielding *cheqam* alone as a complete utterance. Used across all registers as a direct affirmation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqachus?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqachus?
4. **English translation:** Is it true? / Is that really so? / Can it be real?
5. **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction combining *cheqa* with the polar question enclitic *-chu*. Used to query the truth or genuineness of a claim or situation. Extremely common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqachus, manachus?
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqachus, manachus?
4. **English translation:** Is it true or not? / Is it real or isn't it?
5. **Usage notes:** A paired polar question construction using *-chu* on both the positive and negative alternatives. Used to request a definitive answer about the truth or genuineness of something. Common in everyday and legal discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqata rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqata rimay
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth. / Speak truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** Imperative construction with *cheqa* in the accusative/adverbial case (*-ta*) modifying the verb *rimay* ('to speak'). The standard collocation for commanding or requesting truthful speech. Used in legal, moral, and everyday contexts. Documented in P1b as a collocation; included here as an attested syntactic construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqata rimani
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqata rimani
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth. / I speak truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** First-person declarative form of the *cheqata rimay* construction. Used in testimony and everyday assertion to claim that one's own speech is truthful. Common in legal and moral contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Mana cheqachu
3. **Transliteration:** Mana cheqachu
4. **English translation:** It is not true. / That is not genuine. / That is false.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard negative predication of falsity, combining the negator *mana* with *cheqa* and the negative polar enclitic *-chu*. The *-chu* is required in negative clauses in Quechua. Used as a direct denial across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.7**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa hina rikukun
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa hina rikukun
4. **English translation:** It appears to be genuine. / It looks like the real thing. / It seems true.
5. **Usage notes:** Similative construction using *cheqa hina* ('like the genuine/true') with the reflexive perception verb *rikukun* ('it is seen, it appears'). Used to describe something that has the appearance of genuineness without asserting that it is genuine. Relevant in contexts of evaluating representations and imitations.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.8**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanta yachani
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqanta yachani
4. **English translation:** I know that it is true. / I know it to be genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** Nominalized subordinate clause construction: *cheqa kasqanta* ('its being true', accusative nominalization of the predicate) as the object of *yachani* ('I know'). Used to express propositional knowledge of a truth. Common in epistemic assertion contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.9**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa kasqanman hina
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa kasqanman hina
4. **English translation:** As if it were true. / In the manner of its being genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** A complex similative construction combining the nominalized predicate *cheqa kasqan* ('its being true') with the directional/similative postposition *-man hina* ('in the direction of, as if'). Used to describe conduct or representation that proceeds as though something were true, without asserting that it is. Appears in narrative and evaluative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Construction 1.10**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqam, Diospa sutimpi
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqam, Diospa sutimpi
4. **English translation:** It is true, in God's name. / Truly, I swear by God.
5. **Usage notes:** An oath or solemn affirmation construction combining the evidentially marked truth assertion *cheqam* with the prepositional phrase *Diospa sutimpi* ('in the name of God'). Attested in colonial and contemporary legal and religious Quechua texts as a formula for sworn testimony.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 1.11**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Example:** Cheqa nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqa nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** To speak saying it is true. / To assert truthfully.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using the quotative converb *nispa* ('saying, having said') to embed *cheqa* as a reported or performed assertion within a speech act description. The *nispa* construction is a productive syntactic pattern in Quechua for embedding direct speech or thought. Used in narrative and testimonial contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 2: cheqanchay

---

**Construction 2.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Qelqata cheqanchay
3. **Transliteration:** Qelqata cheqanchay
4. **English translation:** To verify a document. / To confirm the accuracy of a text.
5. **Usage notes:** The standard collocation of *cheqanchay* with a document object in the accusative case. Used in administrative and legal contexts for the act of checking a written record against reality. The accusative *-ta* marks the document as the object of verification.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchay atinichu
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanchay atinichu
4. **English translation:** I cannot verify it. / I am unable to confirm it.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction combining the infinitive *cheqanchay* with the negated ability verb *atinichu* ('I cannot'). Used to express inability to verify or confirm a claim. Common in administrative and testimonial contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2.3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqanchay
2. **Example:** Cheqanchasqam
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqanchasqam
4. **English translation:** It has been verified. / It is confirmed.
5. **Usage notes:** The participial form *cheqanchasqa* with the direct evidential enclitic *-m*, functioning as a complete predicative assertion of verified status. Used in administrative and legal contexts to declare that something has been officially confirmed. Documented in P2a as a derivational form; included here as an attested predicative construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 5: chiqap

---

**Construction 5.1**

1. **Base Form:** chiqap
2. **Example:** Chiqap cheqam
3. **Transliteration:** Chiqap cheqam
4. **English translation:** It is truly true. / It is genuinely real. / It is absolutely genuine.
5. **Usage notes:** Intensifying collocation of the adverb *chiqap* ('truly, really') with the evidentially marked predicate *cheqam* ('it is true'). Used for emphatic assertion of truth or genuineness. Attested in Ayacucho-Chanka Quechua; the combination reinforces the truth claim through lexical stacking.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.2**

1. **Base Form:** chiqap
2. **Example:** Chiqapmi
3. **Transliteration:** Chiqapmi
4. **English translation:** Truly. / Really. / Indeed.
5. **Usage notes:** The adverb *chiqap* with the direct evidential enclitic *-m*, functioning as a standalone affirmative or intensifying response. Used in everyday speech to confirm or emphasize the truth of a preceding statement. Attested in Ayacucho-Chanka Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5.3**

1. **Base Form:** chiqap
2. **Example:** Chiqap nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Chiqap nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** To speak truly. / To say it genuinely.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction using *chiqap* as an adverbial modifier within a *nispa* quotative frame with *rimay* ('to speak'). Used to characterize speech as genuinely and sincerely meant. Attested in Ayacucho-Chanka Quechua.
6. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## BASE FORM 6: rura

---

**Construction 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** rura
2. **Example:** Allin rurasqam
3. **Transliteration:** Allin rurasqam
4. **English translation:** It is well made. / It has been done properly.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction combining the evaluative adjective *allin* ('good, proper') with the participial *rurasqa* ('made, done') and the direct evidential enclitic *-m*. Used in aesthetic and practical evaluation of craftsmanship or completed work. The *rura* root here participates in a compound participial construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** rura
2. **Example:** Mana allin rurasqachu
3. **Transliteration:** Mana allin rurasqachu
4. **English translation:** It is not well made. / It was not done properly.
5. **Usage notes:** Negative counterpart to Construction 6.1, using *mana* and the negative polar *-chu*. Used in practical and aesthetic evaluation of defective or improper work.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on rura:** The root *rura* as a standalone nominal ('deed, act') does not generate a large set of fixed multi-word constructions beyond those involving the verbal base *ruray*. The constructions above involve the participial form *rurasqa* in combination with evaluative adjectives. No further fixed constructions specific to *rura* as a P1 item are identified.

## BASE FORM 7: allin

---

**Construction 7.1**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allinllachu?
3. **Transliteration:** Allinllachu?
4. **English translation:** Are you all right? / Is everything okay?
5. **Usage notes:** Greeting inquiry construction combining *allin* with the limitative enclitic *-lla* and the polar question enclitic *-chu*. Used in everyday social interaction to inquire about a person's well-being. The expected response is *allinllam* or *allinllan*.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.2**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allinllan / Allinllam
3. **Transliteration:** Allinllan / Allinllam
4. **English translation:** Just fine. / Quite well. / Everything is all right.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard response to well-being inquiries, with the limitative *-lla* and either the third-person enclitic *-n* or the direct evidential *-m*. Used in everyday social interaction. Documented in P1b; included here as an attested response construction in a fixed exchange.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.3**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allin p'unchaykim
3. **Transliteration:** Allin p'unchaykim
4. **English translation:** Good day (to you). / Have a good day.
5. **Usage notes:** Greeting construction with *allin p'unchay* ('good day') and the second-person possessive enclitic *-ki* plus the direct evidential *-m*, yielding a formulaic greeting directed at the interlocutor. Used in everyday social interaction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.4**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Mana allinkuchu
3. **Transliteration:** Mana allinkuchu
4. **English translation:** He/she is not well. / It is not going well.
5. **Usage notes:** Negative construction combining *mana* ('not'), the intransitive verbal form *allinku-* (third-person plural or reflexive form of *allinkay*), and the negative polar *-chu*. Used to report that a person is unwell or that a situation is not improving. Common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.5**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allin ruwayta yachay
3. **Transliteration:** Allin ruwayta yachay
4. **English translation:** To know how to do things well. / To have the skill to act properly.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction combining the adverbial *allin* with the infinitive *ruwayta* (accusative of *ruwayinfintive*; note: *ruwayinfintive* is the Cusco-Collao variant of *ruray*) as the object of *yachay* ('to know'). Used in practical and moral evaluation of competence and proper conduct.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.6**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allinmi kanqa
3. **Transliteration:** Allinmi kanqa
4. **English translation:** It will be all right. / It will be good. / Everything will be fine.
5. **Usage notes:** Future predicative construction with *allin*, the direct evidential *-mi*, and the future copula *kanqa* ('it will be'). Used as a reassurance or expression of optimism. Common in everyday social speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.7**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allin kachun
3. **Transliteration:** Allin kachun
4. **English translation:** May it be good. / Let it be well. / May all be right.
5. **Usage notes:** Optative/benedictive construction with *allin* and the third-person optative copula *kachun* ('let it be, may it be'). Used as a benediction or expression of good wishes. Common in social greetings, farewells, and ritual speech. Documented in P1b; included here as an attested syntactic construction.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7.8**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Example:** Allinmi, mana allinkuchu nispa
3. **Transliteration:** Allinmi, mana allinkuchu nispa
4. **English translation:** Saying it is good (when) it is not good. / Claiming something is fine when it is not.
5. **Usage notes:** A contrastive construction embedding both a positive and negative predication via the quotative *nispa*, used to characterize deceptive or mistaken evaluation. Appears in moral and narrative discourse to describe the act of misrepresenting a situation's quality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

## BASE FORM 9: mana cheqa

3. **Transliteration:** Mana cheqachu
4. **English translation:** It is not true. / It is false. / That is not genuine.

6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9.3**

1. **Base Form:** mana cheqa
2. **Example:** Mana cheqa kasqanta yachay
3. **Transliteration:** Mana cheqa kasqanta yachay
4. **English translation:** To know that it is not true. / To know it to be false.

## BASE FORM 11: llullakuy

4. **English translation:** Do not lie. / Do not deceive.

3. **Transliteration:** Llullakuyta munay

**Construction 11.3**

1. **Base Form:** llullakuy
2. **Example:** Llullakusqanmanta pantarqani
3. **Transliteration:** Llullakusqanmanta pantarqani
4. **English translation:** I was mistaken because of his/her lying. / I erred on account of the deception.
5. **Usage notes:** Causal construction using the ablative postposition *-manta* ('because of, from') on the nominalized past event *llullakusqan* ('his/her having lied'). Used to attribute one's own error to another's deception. Appears in legal and narrative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 13: pantasqa

---

**Construction 13.1**

1. **Base Form:** pantasqa
2. **Example:** Pantasqam

## BASE FORM 25: -m / -mi

---

**Note on -m/-mi:** The direct evidential enclitic is not itself a base form for multi-word constructions. Its function is documented throughout this analysis as a component of predicative and assertoric constructions. The following entries document constructions where the evidential contrast between *-m/-mi* and *-s/-si* is itself the primary communicative feature.

---

**Construction 25.1**

1. **Base Form:** -m / -mi
2. **Example:** Cheqam (vs. Cheqas)
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqam / Cheqas
4. **English translation:** It is true (I know directly) / It is true (I was told)
5. **Usage notes:** The contrast between the direct evidential *-m* and the reportative *-s* on the same predicate *cheqa* constitutes a grammatically encoded distinction between first-hand and reported knowledge of truth. This contrast is fundamental to the Quechua evidentiality system and is relevant to all truth-evaluation contexts documented in P1.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25.2**

1. **Base Form:** -m / -mi
2. **Example:** Cheqam nispa rimay
3. **Transliteration:** Cheqam nispa rimay
4. **English translation:** To say that it is true (asserting direct knowledge). / To claim it is true.
5. **Usage notes:** The *nispa* quotative construction embedding a *-m*-marked assertion. The direct evidential marking within the quoted speech signals that the original speaker claimed direct knowledge. Used in reported speech and narrative contexts to characterize the epistemic commitment of a quoted speaker.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
