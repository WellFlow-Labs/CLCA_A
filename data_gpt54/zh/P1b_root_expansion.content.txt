## Step 1 — Central roots

The most frequent building blocks across the P1 items are:

1. **真 — zhēn**
   - **Participates in:** 8 P1 items
   - **Core meaning contribution:** “real; true; genuine,” often contributing senses of authenticity, reality, or faithful resemblance.

2. **实 — shí**
   - **Participates in:** 10 P1 items
   - **Core meaning contribution:** “real; actual; factual; solid,” often contributing senses of factuality, substance, or accordance with reality.

3. **正 — zhèng**
   - **Participates in:** 2 P1 items
   - **Core meaning contribution:** “correct; proper; upright,” often contributing senses of correctness, legitimacy, or normative rightness.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

### Root 1: 真 — zhēn

Because **真** is highly productive, the list below focuses on the most common and well-attested forms; additional forms exist.

1. **真**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēn
   - **Literal breakdown:** “real; true”
   - **English:** true; real; genuine
   - **Context:** Common adjective for authenticity or truth, and also an adverbial intensifier in colloquial speech, as in 真好 “really good.” It is basic in contrasts like 真/假.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **真正**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 正 “proper, actual”
   - **English:** genuine; real; truly
   - **Context:** Used to distinguish what is actual or genuine from what is only apparent, nominal, or superficial. Very common in both speech and writing.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **真相**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 相 “appearance; aspect”
   - **English:** truth; the real facts
   - **Context:** Common for the actual facts behind an event, rumor, or controversy. Frequent in journalism, law, and public discourse.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **真理**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 理 “principle”
   - **English:** truth
   - **Context:** Used in philosophy, ideology, religion, and abstract discussion for truth as principle or doctrine. Often appears in elevated or theoretical language.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **真诚**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 诚 “sincere”
   - **English:** sincere; genuine
   - **Context:** Common in evaluating attitude, emotion, apology, or interpersonal conduct. It concerns genuineness of feeling rather than factual truth.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **真心**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 心 “heart”
   - **English:** sincere heart; genuinely
   - **Context:** Used for genuine intention or feeling, often in interpersonal contexts like 真心帮助别人 “help others sincerely.” Also used adverbially in speech.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **真切**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnqiè
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 切 “close; earnest”
   - **English:** vivid and real; genuine; sincere
   - **Context:** Used for feelings, memories, experiences, or expression that seem deeply real and immediate. Common in literary and reflective prose.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **真伪**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 伪 “false”
   - **English:** authenticity; true or false
   - **Context:** Common in legal, commercial, and forensic contexts for determining whether something is genuine. Often used with 鉴别 or 辨别.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **真假**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 假 “false”
   - **English:** true and false; authenticity
   - **Context:** Common in everyday speech for whether something is real or fake, true or not. Slightly more colloquial than 真伪.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **真品**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnpǐn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “genuine” + 品 “item; product”
    - **English:** genuine article; authentic item
    - **Context:** Used for artworks, branded goods, antiques, and collectibles. Common in commerce and appraisal.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **真迹**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnjì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “authentic” + 迹 “trace; writing”
    - **English:** authentic handwriting/work
    - **Context:** Used especially for calligraphy, painting, and manuscripts to indicate an original work by the actual creator. Common in art history and collecting.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **真容**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnróng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 容 “appearance”
    - **English:** true appearance; real face
    - **Context:** Used for revealing what something or someone really looks like, literally or figuratively. Common in media and descriptive writing.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **真身**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnshēn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 身 “body”
    - **English:** real body; true identity
    - **Context:** Used for actual identity behind disguise, online anonymity, or transformed appearance. Also appears in religious and mythological contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **真经**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnjīng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 经 “scripture; canon”
    - **English:** true scripture; genuine teaching
    - **Context:** Used in religious discourse and also figuratively for a “real secret” or “true method.” The figurative use is common in colloquial speech.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

15. **真知**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnzhī
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 知 “knowledge”
    - **English:** true knowledge
    - **Context:** Used in philosophical, educational, and reflective writing for genuine understanding as opposed to superficial opinion.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **真知灼见**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真知 “true knowledge” + 灼见 “penetrating view”
    - **English:** true knowledge and penetrating insight
    - **Context:** Fixed expression used in formal praise for insightful judgments or analyses. Common in essays, speeches, and evaluations of scholarship.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **真才实学**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn cái shí xué
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 才 “talent” + 实 “solid” + 学 “learning”
    - **English:** real talent and solid learning
    - **Context:** Used to praise someone as genuinely capable rather than merely impressive in appearance. Common in education, personnel evaluation, and public commentary.

18. **真情**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnqíng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “genuine” + 情 “feeling”
    - **English:** genuine feeling; true emotion
    - **Context:** Used for sincere emotion in relationships, literature, and performance. It emphasizes authenticity of sentiment.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **真情实感**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn qíng shí gǎn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真情 “true feeling” + 实感 “real feeling”
    - **English:** genuine feelings; sincere emotion
    - **Context:** Common in literary criticism, composition teaching, and interpersonal evaluation. It praises expression that comes from real feeling.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **真话**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnhuà
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 话 “speech”
    - **English:** truth; truthful words
    - **Context:** Common in everyday speech for words that correspond to fact or honest belief. Often contrasted with 假话.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **真意**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēnyì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 意 “meaning; intention”
    - **English:** true meaning; real intention
    - **Context:** Used in interpretation, conversation, and literary reading for the actual intended meaning behind words or actions.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **真名实姓**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn míng shí xìng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 名 “name” + 实 “actual” + 姓 “surname”
    - **English:** real name and surname; true identity
    - **Context:** Used for actual personal identity as opposed to pseudonym or alias. Common in administration, online discourse, and policing contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **真金不怕火炼**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn jīn bù pà huǒ liàn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真金 “real gold” + 不怕 “does not fear” + 火炼 “fire-refining”
    - **English:** real gold does not fear the test of fire
    - **Context:** Fixed saying meaning what is genuine can withstand testing. Used in moral, practical, and rhetorical contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **真本事**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn běnshi
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 本事 “ability”
    - **English:** real ability
    - **Context:** Common colloquial expression for genuine competence rather than bluff or show. Used in praise or challenge.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **真功夫**
    - **Transliteration:** zhēn gōngfu
    - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “real” + 功夫 “skill; effort”
    - **English:** real skill; genuine mastery
    - **Context:** Used for actual expertise acquired through practice. Common in performance, craftsmanship, sports, and figurative evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **天真**
    - **Transliteration:** tiānzhēn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 天 “natural; heaven” + 真 “genuine; innocent”
    - **English:** innocent; naive
    - **Context:** Very common adjective for childlike innocence or naivety. This is a major extension beyond factual truth.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

27. **纯真**
    - **Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 纯 “pure” + 真 “genuine”
    - **English:** pure and innocent; pure-hearted
    - **Context:** Used for personality, emotion, childhood, and style. Common in literary and evaluative contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

28. **率真**
    - **Transliteration:** shuàizhēn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 率 “frank; direct” + 真 “genuine”
    - **English:** frank and sincere
    - **Context:** Used to describe a person’s manner as straightforward and genuine. Common in character evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

29. **逼真**
   - **Transliteration:** bīzhēn
   - **Literal breakdown:** 逼 “close to” + 真 “real”
   - **English:** lifelike; realistic
   - **Context:** Used for depictions, acting, images, and models that closely resemble reality. It concerns convincing resemblance rather than actual authenticity.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

30. **仿真**
   - **Transliteration:** fǎngzhēn
   - **Literal breakdown:** 仿 “imitate” + 真 “real”
   - **English:** simulation; realistic imitation
   - **Context:** Used in engineering, design, manufacturing, and consumer description for simulation or lifelike imitation. Common in technical and commercial usage.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 2: 实 — shí

Because **实** is highly productive, the list below focuses on the most common and well-attested forms; additional forms exist.

1. **实**
   - **Transliteration:** shí
   - **Literal breakdown:** “real; solid; actual”
   - **English:** real; solid; factual
   - **Context:** Used as a bound and free morpheme in many compounds relating to factuality, substance, fullness, and solidity. In modern usage it is especially productive in formal compounds.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **真实**
   - **Transliteration:** zhēnshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 真 “true” + 实 “actual”
   - **English:** real; true; authentic
   - **Context:** Common for facts, situations, reports, and representations that correspond to reality. Very frequent in formal and everyday use.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **属实**
   - **Transliteration:** shǔshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 属 “be; belong to” + 实 “fact”
   - **English:** be true; be verified as true
   - **Context:** Common in official statements, investigations, and legal-administrative writing. Often appears in formulas like 经查属实.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **失实**
   - **Transliteration:** shīshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 失 “lose; miss” + 实 “fact”
   - **English:** inaccurate; not in accordance with facts
   - **Context:** Used for reports or descriptions that do not match the facts. Common in media criticism and official rebuttal.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **如实**
   - **Transliteration:** rúshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 如 “according to” + 实 “fact”
   - **English:** truthfully; according to the facts
   - **Context:** Common adverb in testimony, reporting, and administration. It means to state things as they actually are.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **不实**
   - **Transliteration:** bùshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 不 “not” + 实 “factual”
   - **English:** untrue; unfounded
   - **Context:** Frequent in formal written Chinese for allegations, reports, or claims lacking factual basis. Common in legal and media contexts.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **事实**
   - **Transliteration:** shìshí
   - **Literal breakdown:** 事 “matter” + 实 “fact”
   - **English:** fact
   - **Context:** One of the most common words for objective fact or actual situation. Used across all registers.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **实话**
   - **Transliteration:** shíhuà
   - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “true; real” + 话 “speech”
   - **English:** truth; honest words
   - **Context:** Common in everyday speech, often in phrases like 说实话 “to tell the truth.” It can emphasize frankness as well as factuality.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **说实话**
   - **Transliteration:** shuō shíhuà
   - **Literal breakdown:** 说 “say” + 实话 “truthful words”
   - **English:** honestly speaking; to tell the truth
   - **Context:** Very common discourse marker in conversation and writing. It introduces candid opinion or admission.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **老实**
    - **Transliteration:** lǎoshi
    - **Literal breakdown:** 老 “old; consistently” + 实 “honest; solid”
    - **English:** honest; well-behaved; naive
    - **Context:** Very common adjective for honesty, compliance, or plain straightforwardness. It is also used in commands like 老实点.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **诚实**
    - **Transliteration:** chéngshí
    - **Literal breakdown:** 诚 “sincere” + 实 “honest; true”
    - **English:** honest
    - **Context:** Standard term for honesty in moral and educational contexts. Used for persons, statements, and conduct.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **忠实**
    - **Transliteration:** zhōngshí
    - **Literal breakdown:** 忠 “loyal” + 实 “true; faithful”
    - **English:** faithful; loyal; true to
    - **Context:** Used for loyalty to a person, cause, or source text, and also for faithful reproduction. Common in political, literary, and technical contexts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **踏实**
    - **Transliteration:** tāshi
    - **Literal breakdown:** 踏 “step on” + 实 “solid”
    - **English:** steady; down-to-earth; reassured
    - **Context:** Used for work style, personality, or emotional state. It often concerns solidity or groundedness rather than factual truth.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **扎实**
    - **Transliteration:** zhāshi
    - **Literal breakdown:** 扎 “firmly” + 实 “solid”
    - **English:** solid; well-grounded
    - **Context:** Common for knowledge, work, skills, and foundations that are strong and substantial. Frequent in education and evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **实在**
    - **Transliteration:** shízài
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “real” + 在 “exist”
    - **English:** real; honest; indeed
    - **Context:** Used as adjective, adverb, and evaluative term. It can mean genuine/substantial, or colloquially “really.”
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **实用**
    - **Transliteration:** shíyòng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “practical; real” + 用 “use”
    - **English:** practical; useful
    - **Context:** Common for tools, methods, knowledge, and design. It extends from “real” toward practical applicability.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **实证**
    - **Transliteration:** shízhèng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual; empirical” + 证 “evidence; proof”
    - **English:** empirical; empirical evidence
    - **Context:** Used in philosophy of science, social science, medicine, and law for evidence-based or empirically grounded approaches.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **实测**
    - **Transliteration:** shícè
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual” + 测 “measure”
    - **English:** actual measurement; field-tested measurement
    - **Context:** Used in engineering, surveying, and science for measured data from real conditions rather than simulation or estimation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **实录**
    - **Transliteration:** shílù
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “true; actual” + 录 “record”
    - **English:** true record; documentary record
    - **Context:** Used for documentary-style records, memoir-like accounts, and historical compilations. It emphasizes recording actual events.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **实况**
    - **Transliteration:** shíkuàng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual” + 况 “condition”
    - **English:** actual situation; live condition
    - **Context:** Used for live broadcasts, on-site conditions, and actual circumstances. Common in media and reporting.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **实地**
    - **Transliteration:** shídì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual” + 地 “place”
    - **English:** on the spot; in the field
    - **Context:** Used in phrases like 实地调查 and 实地考察 for field investigation or on-site inspection. Common in administration, journalism, and research.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **实事求是**
    - **Transliteration:** shí shì qiú shì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实事 “actual matters” + 求 “seek” + 是 “what is right/so”
    - **English:** seek truth from facts; be practical and realistic
    - **Context:** Very common fixed expression in political, intellectual, and everyday evaluative language. It emphasizes proceeding from actual facts.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **名副其实**
    - **Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
    - **Literal breakdown:** 名 “name” + 副 “match” + 其 “its” + 实 “reality”
    - **English:** truly live up to the name
    - **Context:** Used when reputation, title, or description matches actual reality. Common in praise and evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **货真价实**
    - **Transliteration:** huò zhēn jià shí
    - **Literal breakdown:** 货 “goods” + 真 “genuine” + 价 “price” + 实 “solid; honest”
    - **English:** genuine and fairly priced; solid and authentic
    - **Context:** Used for goods and figuratively for qualifications or achievements that are genuine and substantial. Common in commerce and rhetoric.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **实质**
    - **Transliteration:** shízhì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual” + 质 “substance”
    - **English:** substance; actual nature
    - **Context:** Common in formal analysis for substantive content or actual nature behind appearances or procedures. Often contrasts with form.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **实情**
    - **Transliteration:** shíqíng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “real” + 情 “situation”
    - **English:** true situation; actual circumstances
    - **Context:** Used for the real state of affairs, often after doubt or concealment. Common in reporting and investigation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

27. **实效**
    - **Transliteration:** shíxiào
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “actual” + 效 “effect”
    - **English:** actual effect; practical results
    - **Context:** Common in policy, management, and evaluation for concrete effectiveness rather than nominal effort.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

28. **实惠**
    - **Transliteration:** shíhuì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “real; substantial” + 惠 “benefit”
    - **English:** tangible benefit; economical
    - **Context:** Used for prices, deals, and benefits that are genuinely advantageous. Common in consumer and policy language.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

29. **实心**
    - **Transliteration:** shíxīn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “solid” + 心 “core”
    - **English:** solid-core; wholehearted
    - **Context:** Used literally for solid objects and figuratively in some contexts for sincerity or wholeheartedness. The literal sense is especially common.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

30. **实数**
    - **Transliteration:** shíshù
    - **Literal breakdown:** 实 “real” + 数 “number”
    - **English:** real number
    - **Context:** Standard mathematical term. This is a technical extension of the root beyond ordinary factuality.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 3: 正 — zhèng

**正** is productive, but in the truth-evaluation domain its overlap with P1 is narrower than 真 and 实.

1. **正**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèng
   - **Literal breakdown:** “upright; correct; proper”
   - **English:** correct; proper; upright
   - **Context:** Basic morpheme in compounds of correctness, legitimacy, regularity, and moral uprightness. Also has many non-epistemic uses in Chinese.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **正确**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngquè
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “correct” + 确 “certain; accurate”
   - **English:** correct; right
   - **Context:** Standard term for correct answers, judgments, methods, and actions. Very common in education and formal evaluation.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **正当**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngdàng
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “proper” + 当 “appropriate”
   - **English:** proper; legitimate; justified
   - **Context:** Used in legal, moral, and procedural contexts for justified reasons, rights, or conduct. Common in phrases like 正当理由 and 正当防卫.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **正误**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngwù
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “correct” + 误 “error”
   - **English:** right and wrong; correctness and error
   - **Context:** Common in education, editing, and evaluation for distinguishing correct from incorrect forms or answers.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **正解**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngjiě
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “correct” + 解 “solution; interpretation”
   - **English:** correct solution; correct interpretation
   - **Context:** Common in educational and explanatory contexts, especially for the right answer or proper reading of a problem.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **正论**
   - **Transliteration:** zhènglùn
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “correct; orthodox” + 论 “argument; doctrine”
   - **English:** sound argument; orthodox doctrine
   - **Context:** Used in formal or literary contexts for proper doctrine or sound argument. It can also carry ideological or classical overtones.
   - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

7. **正名**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngmíng
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “rectify; make correct” + 名 “name”
   - **English:** rectify names; vindicate; correct naming
   - **Context:** Used in classical, philosophical, and modern public discourse for correcting names, titles, or reputations. It concerns proper designation rather than simple factual truth.
   - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

8. **正本清源**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèng běn qīng yuán
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “set right” + 本 “root” + 清 “clear” + 源 “source”
   - **English:** rectify at the root and clarify the source
   - **Context:** Fixed expression used in political, intellectual, and institutional discourse for correcting matters fundamentally. It often concerns doctrinal or procedural correctness.
   - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

9. **正视**
   - **Transliteration:** zhèngshì
   - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “straight; properly” + 视 “look at”
   - **English:** face squarely; confront directly
   - **Context:** Used for acknowledging and confronting problems, facts, or realities directly. Common in formal commentary and public discourse.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **正宗**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngzōng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “orthodox; authentic” + 宗 “lineage; school”
    - **English:** authentic; orthodox; genuine
    - **Context:** Common for food, style, lineage, doctrine, and products judged to be the authentic or original version. Very frequent in everyday and cultural evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **正牌**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngpái
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “genuine; proper” + 牌 “brand; label”
    - **English:** genuine brand; legitimate
    - **Context:** Used for authentic branded goods and sometimes figuratively for officially recognized status. Common in contrast with 冒牌.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **正品**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngpǐn
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “genuine; proper” + 品 “product”
    - **English:** genuine product
    - **Context:** Common in commerce and e-commerce for authentic goods rather than counterfeit ones. Very frequent in consumer language.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **正货**
    - **Transliteration:** zhènghuò
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “genuine” + 货 “goods”
    - **English:** genuine goods; authentic merchandise
    - **Context:** Used in retail and consumer contexts for authentic products. Also appears figuratively in colloquial speech.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **正色**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngsè
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “serious; proper” + 色 “expression; color”
    - **English:** stern expression; serious manner
    - **Context:** Common in literary and formal description for adopting a serious, proper expression. This is a clear extension away from truth evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

15. **正直**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngzhí
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “upright” + 直 “straight”
    - **English:** upright; honest
    - **Context:** Common moral term for integrity and upright character. It overlaps with honesty but is primarily ethical.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **公正**
    - **Transliteration:** gōngzhèng
    - **Literal breakdown:** 公 “public; impartial” + 正 “just; right”
    - **English:** fair; just; impartial
    - **Context:** Very common in legal, political, and moral contexts for fairness and justice. It concerns normative rightness rather than factual truth.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **正义**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngyì
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “just; right” + 义 “justice; righteousness”
    - **English:** justice; righteousness
    - **Context:** Common in moral, political, and legal discourse. It is a major extension from correctness into ethical legitimacy.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **正经**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngjing
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “proper” + 经 “canon; standard”
    - **English:** proper; serious; respectable
    - **Context:** Common colloquial adjective for seriousness or propriety, and historically for orthodox scripture. It often has little to do with factual truth.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **正路**
    - **Transliteration:** zhènglù
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “proper” + 路 “path”
    - **English:** proper path; right way
    - **Context:** Used literally and figuratively for the correct or legitimate course. Common in moral and social evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

20. **正道**
    - **Transliteration:** zhèngdào
    - **Literal breakdown:** 正 “proper; orthodox” + 道 “way”
    - **English:** the right path; orthodox way
    - **Context:** Used in moral, religious, and political discourse for the proper or legitimate way. It often carries ethical or doctrinal force.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral extensions

Some expressions show clear shifts away from straightforward truth/falsity or factual evaluation:

- **真**: 真诚, 真心, 真切, 真情, 真情实感, 天真, 纯真, 率真, 真经, 逼真, 仿真
- **实**: 老实, 踏实, 扎实, 实用, 实效, 实惠, 实心, 实数
- **正**: 正当, 正宗, 正牌, 正品, 正货, 正色, 正直, 公正, 正义, 正经, 正路, 正道, 正名, 正本清源

These are simply flagged as peripheral or shifted uses of the roots relative to core truth-evaluation meaning.
