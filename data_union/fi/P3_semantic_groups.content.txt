# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Finnish (fi)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in the P1 and P2 data: which terms co-occur, which are contrasted with which, which appear in the same discourse contexts, and which share functional roles in Finnish usage. No external theoretical framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries are determined by the data. Items that do not fit cleanly are noted at the end.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement holds / attribute truth or falsity to propositions and claims

**Label:** Propositional truth and falsity attribution; asserting or denying that a claim is the case

**Definition:** These expressions are used in Finnish to evaluate whether a proposition, statement, or claim corresponds to how things are. They appear in contexts where a speaker is affirming, denying, or questioning the truth of something said or believed. They are used predicatively (*se on tosi*, *väite on epätosi*), attributively (*väärä väite*, *epätosi väite*), or in constructions that introduce or frame a truth claim (*totuus on se, että...*). They appear in everyday speech, journalism, and formal writing. The core contrast in this group is between expressions that affirm truth and those that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tosi* — predicative and attributive use: *se on tosi* (that is true); used as the base of the cluster; also used as intensifier
- *totuus* — the nominal form; appears in *kertoa totuus*, *koko totuus ja vain totuus*, *totuus on se, että...*
- *epätosi* — the direct negation; used in formal and philosophical discourse; *väite on epätosi* (the claim is false), *epätosi väite* (a false claim)
- *epätotuus* — "untruth"; used in journalistic and formal contexts without necessarily implying deliberate deception; *puhua epätotuuksia* (to say untruths/falsehoods)
- *pitää paikkansa* — "to hold/be true"; the standard colloquial construction for asserting a claim is correct; near-synonym of *olla totta* in this function, more common in evaluative assessment of specific claims
- *ei pidä paikkaansa* — "it is not true/does not hold"; the standard colloquial negation
- *olla totta* — to be true; to be the case (partitive construction)
- *totta puhuen* — "to tell the truth / truthfully speaking"; parenthetical adverbial; discourse marker introducing a candid statement
- *puhua totta* — "to speak the truth"
- *sanoa totta* — to say something true
- *totuusarvo* — "truth value"; technical use in logic and formal semantics
- *totuudenmukainen* — "truthful; conforming to truth"; used to evaluate whether an account corresponds to the facts; more frequently in legal contexts for witness accounts
- *totuudenmukaisuus* — "truthfulness, fidelity to truth, accuracy"; used in journalistic evaluation of accounts; bridges truth-assertion and conformity-to-standard contexts
- *totuudenvastainen* — "contrary to truth; false" (of statements)
- *totuudenvastaisuus* — "untruthfulness; contrariness to truth"; formal and legal register
- *todella* — "really, truly, indeed"; adverb asserting that something is genuinely the case
- *todennäköinen* — "probable"; the probabilistic extension of the truth-assertion cluster; introduces degree rather than binary assertion
- *todennäköisyys* — "probability"
- *todennäköisesti* — "probably"
- *todenperäinen* — factual; based on truth; true (of accounts)
- *todenperäisyys* — factual basis; truth of an account
- *tosiseikka* — fact; factual circumstance (legal and formal)
- *tosiasia* — fact; embedded in *tosiasia on, että*
- *tosiasiallinen* — factual; actual; de facto
- *toden totta* — truly; really; in all truth (emphatic fixed phrase)
- *tosiaan* — really; indeed; actually (colloquial affirmative)
- *totta kai* — of course; certainly (colloquial affirmative)
- *tosipohjainen* — fact-based; grounded in fact
- *todenmukainen* — true-to-life; realistic; accurate (of representations); primary context is aesthetic and journalistic evaluation of how faithfully something represents reality
- *todenmukaisuus* — verisimilitude; true-to-lifeness
- *todenmukaisesti* — *kuvata tilanne todenmukaisesti* (to describe the situation accurately)
- *totuudenmukaisesti* — *kertoa totuudenmukaisesti* (to tell truthfully)
- *tosiasia on, että* — the fact is that (discourse marker)
- *totuus on, että* — the truth is that (discourse marker, slightly more rhetorical)
- *totuus tulee ilmi* — "the truth comes to light"
- *totuus paljastuu* — the truth comes to light
- *osoittaa epätodeksi* — to show to be false
- *todistaa vääräksi* — to prove false; to disprove
- *osoittautua vääräksi* — to turn out to be false
- *osoittautua oikeaksi* — to prove to be correct
- *väärä* (in propositional use) — *väärä väite* (a false claim); see also Ambiguous Items

**Observations:** The contrast between *tosi* and *epätosi* is more formal and explicitly propositional than the contrast between *oikea* and *väärä*. The construction *pitää paikkansa* / *ei pidä paikkaansa* is the most frequent everyday equivalent. *Todennäköinen* and its derivatives belong here because they are built on the same root and function in the same evaluative space, but they introduce degree rather than binary assertion. *Todenmukainen* and *todenmukaisuus* shade into Group 10 (accuracy evaluation), since their primary context is aesthetic and journalistic evaluation of how faithfully something represents reality. *Tosiaan* and *totta kai* are primarily affirmative particles in spoken Finnish and are only loosely connected to the epistemic core of this group. *Totuudenmukaisuus* bridges this group and Group 4 (conformity to standard): it concerns both truth and conformity to truth as a standard for statements and accounts.

## Group 2: Expressions used to evaluate correctness relative to a standard or norm

**Label:** Conformity to a standard, norm, or rule; expressions for evaluating whether something meets a standard or norm

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether an answer, action, procedure, or text conforms to what is required, expected, or proper. They are concentrated in administrative, legal, linguistic, and procedural registers. The standard or norm may be explicit (a rule, a qualification requirement, a prescribed procedure) or implicit (common usage, professional expectation). The key distinction from Group 1 is that these expressions do not primarily assert correspondence to reality but rather conformity to a rule or norm: *oikeellisuus* concerns conformity to a linguistic or procedural standard; *totuudenmukaisuus* concerns conformity to the truth as a standard for statements; *oikeakielisyys* concerns conformity to prescriptive language norms. The contrast between *oikeellisuus* (rule-conformity) and *totuus* (correspondence to fact) is explicitly noted in P1.

**Representative expressions:**

- *oikea* — "right, correct; proper"; used for answers, persons, words, times (*oikea vastaus*, *oikea sana*, *oikea henkilö*); highly polysemous — see Ambiguous Items
- *oikein* — "correctly"; adverb (*vastata oikein*, *ymmärtää oikein*, *tehdä oikein*, *oikein vai väärin*)
- *olla oikeassa* — "to be right/correct"; the standard periphrastic construction
- *oikeellisuus* — "correctness; accuracy; propriety"; formal and administrative evaluation of conformity to standard; *tietojen oikeellisuus* (accuracy of information), *kielellinen oikeellisuus* (linguistic correctness)
- *oikeakielisyys* — "linguistic correctness; prescriptive correctness"
- *oikeakielinen* — "linguistically correct"
- *oikeaoppinen* — "orthodox; doctrinally correct"; can carry ironic force; specifically about doctrinal conformity, somewhat peripheral to the administrative core of this group
- *oikeaoppisuus* — "orthodoxy; doctrinal correctness"
- *vääräoppinen* — "heretical; heterodox"
- *vääräoppisuus* — "heresy; heterodoxy"
- *oikaista* — "to correct; to set right"
- *oikaisu* — "correction"
- *väärä* — "wrong, incorrect"; the primary antonym (*väärä vastaus*, *olla väärässä*); see also Ambiguous Items
- *väärin* — "wrongly, incorrectly"; adverb (*vastata väärin*, *ymmärtää väärin*); see also Ambiguous Items
- *väärinkäsitys* — "misunderstanding; incorrect understanding"
- *väärintulkinta* — "misinterpretation"
- *väärintulkita* — "to misinterpret"
- *asianmukainen* — "proper, appropriate"; conformity to what the matter requires
- *asianmukaisuus* — "appropriateness; propriety"
- *asianmukaisesti* — "properly; appropriately"; procedural conformity
- *asianmukainen menettely* — "proper procedure"
- *asianmukainen dokumentaatio* — "proper documentation"
- *poliittinen oikeellisuus* — "political correctness"; a specific application of the correctness-to-norm pattern
- *tarkistaa oikeellisuus* — to check correctness
- *varmistaa tietojen oikeellisuus* — to verify the accuracy of the information
- *vastata tietojen oikeellisuudesta* — to be responsible for the accuracy of the information
- *perusteeton* — "unfounded; groundless; without basis"; evaluates whether a claim or accusation has adequate grounds; sits between Group 1 and this group — see Ambiguous Items
- *tarkka* — "accurate, precise, exact, careful"
- *tarkkuus* — "accuracy, precision, exactness"
- *tarkasti* — "accurately, precisely, exactly"
- *tarkasti ottaen* — "strictly speaking; to be precise"
- *totuudenmukaisuus* — see Group 1 and Ambiguous Items
- *totuudenmukainen* — see Group 1 and Ambiguous Items

**Observations:** This group overlaps with Group 1 in that *väärä* and *oikea* can also be used in propositional truth contexts. The distinguishing feature here is that the evaluation is against a norm, standard, or expectation rather than against a state of affairs in the world. *Asianmukainen* belongs here rather than in Group 1 because it evaluates procedural and normative conformity, not propositional truth. *Oikeaoppinen* and *vääräoppinen* are specifically about doctrinal conformity and are somewhat peripheral to the core of this group, which is more administrative and procedural.

## Group 3: Expressions used in evaluating genuineness or non-counterfeit status

**Label:** Evaluating genuineness and authenticity versus imitation

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether an object, document, person, or emotion is the real thing as opposed to an imitation, forgery, or performance. They appear in art authentication, consumer protection, legal proceedings, and everyday character evaluation. The central contrast is between *aito* / *alkuperäinen* (genuine/original) and *jäljitelmä* / *väärennös* / *epäaito* (imitation/forgery/inauthentic). The translative constructions *osoittautua aidoksi*, *osoittautua väärennökseksi*, and *todistaa aidoksi* are characteristic of this group.

**Representative expressions:**

- *aito* — "genuine, authentic; real"; the primary term (*aito nahka*, *aito tunne*, *aito suomalainen*, *aito allekirjoitus*, *aito ilo*)
- *aitous* — "authenticity, genuineness"; *todistaa aitous* (to prove authenticity), *epäillä aitoutta* (to doubt the authenticity)
- *aidosti* — "genuinely, authentically"; *aidosti kiinnostunut* (genuinely interested)
- *aito ja alkuperäinen* — the formulaic pairing asserting both authenticity and originality
- *epäaito* — "inauthentic; fake; spurious"; used more in philosophical and personal identity contexts (existential inauthenticity) than in object-authentication contexts
- *epäaitous* — "inauthenticity"
- *alkuperäinen* — "original"; asserts temporal and genealogical priority (*alkuperäinen teos*, *alkuperäinen sopimus*, *alkuperäinen asiakirja*)
- *alkuperäisyys* — "originality; authenticity of provenance"
- *alkuperäiskappale* — "original copy/document"
- *alkuperäisversio* — "original version"
- *jäljitelmä* — "imitation; replica; copy" (with implication of lesser status; does not imply fraudulent intent, distinguishing it from *väärennös*)
- *halpa jäljitelmä* — "cheap imitation"
- *pelkkä jäljitelmä* — "mere imitation"
- *jäljitellä* — to imitate, to copy
- *jäljittely* — imitation as a practice
- *jäljittelemätön* — inimitable
- *väärennös* — "forgery, counterfeit"; the product of deliberate falsification; *paljastaa väärennös* (to expose a forgery), *tunnistaa väärennös* (to identify a forgery), *osoittautua väärennökseksi* (to turn out to be a forgery); see also Group 7
- *väärennösepäily* — "suspicion of forgery"
- *epäillä väärennöstä* — "to suspect forgery"
- *todistaa aidoksi* — to certify as genuine
- *osoittautua aidoksi* — to prove to be genuine
- *kyseenalaistaa aitous* — to call into question the authenticity
- *ei ole aito* — is not genuine; is fake
- *aito vai väärennös* — genuine or a forgery
- *aitoustodistus* — certificate of authenticity
- *oikea* (in the sense of genuine) — *oikea nahka* (real leather), *oikea henkilö* (the right/genuine person); see also Ambiguous Items

**Observations:** *Aito* and *alkuperäinen* overlap but are not identical: *aito* asserts non-counterfeit status, while *alkuperäinen* asserts temporal priority (being the first version). The P2 data shows these are frequently paired (*aito ja alkuperäinen*) precisely because they cover adjacent but distinct concerns. *Väärennös* also belongs to Group 7 (deliberate falsification and fraud) given its legal dimension. *Jäljitelmä* does not imply fraudulent intent, distinguishing it from *väärennös*. *Epäaito* and *epäaitous* are used more in philosophical and personal identity contexts than in the object-authentication contexts where *väärennös* and *jäljitelmä* appear. *Kopio* (neutral copy) sits at the edge of this group: it does not inherently imply inauthenticity or fraud, unlike *jäljitelmä* or *väärennös*, and is therefore listed under items without a clear group.

## Group 4: Expressions used in legal and investigative contexts for establishing what happened

**Label:** Evidence, proof, and their procedural handling in legal and investigative discourse

**Definition:** These expressions cluster in legal, judicial, and investigative contexts where the task is to establish what actually occurred, who is responsible, and what evidence supports a conclusion. They appear in court proceedings, criminal investigations, and formal legal texts. The cluster is organized around the *todistaa* / *todistus* / *todiste* family and the associated procedural vocabulary. The cluster includes both the acts of proving and testifying, the products of those acts, the persons involved, and the procedural concepts governing how evidence is handled.

**Representative expressions:**

- *todistaa* — "to prove, to testify, to witness; to certify"; central to both legal testimony and logical proof; *todistaa oikeudessa* (to testify in court), *todistaa valan nojalla* (to testify under oath), *todistaa syyllisyys* (to prove guilt), *todistaa syyttömyys* (to prove innocence), *todistaa oikeaksi* (to certify as correct), *todistaa vääräksi* (to prove false; to disprove), *todistaa aidoksi* (to certify as genuine), *todistaa henkilöllisyys* (to prove identity)
- *todistaminen* — *syyllisyyden todistaminen* (proving guilt)
- *todistus* — "proof, certificate, testimony"; *antaa todistus* (to issue a certificate), *todistus oikeaksi* (certified as correct), *todistus syyllisyydestä* (proof of guilt)
- *todistaja* — "witness"; *todistaja oikeudessa* (witness in court)
- *todistajanlausunto* — witness statement
- *todiste* — "piece of evidence"; *esittää todiste* (to present evidence), *riittävä todiste* (sufficient evidence), *kiistaton todiste* (incontrovertible evidence), *pätevä todiste* (valid evidence), *pätemätön todiste* (inadmissible evidence)
- *todistusaineisto* — "body of evidence"
- *todistustaakka* — "burden of proof"
- *todistusvoima* — "probative force, evidentiary weight"
- *todistuskeino* — "means of proof"
- *todistuskappale* — "exhibit; piece of evidence"
- *todistusketju* — "chain of evidence"
- *todistusharkinta* — "evaluation of evidence"
- *todistusstandardi* — "standard of proof"
- *vapaa todistusharkinta* — "free evaluation of evidence" (procedural principle)
- *todistusharkinnan vapaus* — free evaluation of evidence
- *todistettava* — "provable; demonstrable"; *todistettava väite* (a provable claim)
- *todistettu* — *todistettu syyllisyys* (proved guilt)
- *todistamaton* — "unproved; unverified"; *todistamaton väite* (an unproved claim)
- *todistetusti* — "demonstrably, as has been proven"
- *todistettavasti* — "demonstrably; verifiably" (modal nuance)
- *todentaa* — "to verify; to authenticate; to confirm as real"; *todentaa henkilöllisyys* (to verify identity); see also Group 5
- *todentaminen* — "verification; authentication"; see also Group 5
- *syntymätodistus* — birth certificate
- *kuolemantodistus* — death certificate
- *lääkärintodistus* — medical certificate
- *koulutodistus* — school certificate
- *työtodistus* — employment certificate
- *aitoustodistus* — certificate of authenticity
- *tarkistaa* — "to check, to verify"; see also Group 5
- *tarkistus* — "check, verification"; see also Group 5
- *tarkistaja* — "checker, verifier, inspector"; see also Group 5
- *väärä todistus* — "false testimony; perjury"
- *väärä vala* — "false oath, perjury"
- *valheellinen* — "mendacious"; used in legal evaluation of testimony; see also Group 8 and Group 11
- *valheellisuus* — "mendacity"; see also Group 8 and Group 11
- *valheellisesti todistaa* — "to testify falsely"
- *perusteeton* — "unfounded, groundless"; used to evaluate accusations and claims in legal contexts; see also Ambiguous Items
- *perusteltu* — "justified, well-founded"; used to evaluate decisions and suspicions; *perusteltu epäilys* (a well-founded suspicion)
- *perusteltu epäilys* — "a well-founded suspicion"
- *oikeuden edessä* — "before the court"
- *viedä oikeuteen* — "to take to court"
- *väärennysrikos* — "forgery offence"
- *totuus tulee ilmi* — "the truth comes to light"

**Observations:** This group is large because Finnish legal discourse draws heavily on the *tode-* root for proof and evidence, the *oikeus* root for the institutional apparatus, and the *väärä* root for falsification offences. *Tarkistaa* and *todentaa* appear here because they function in verification roles within legal and administrative proceedings, though they also appear in non-legal contexts (see Group 5). *Valheellinen* belongs here because its primary register is legal and its primary referent is testimony, though it also appears in Group 8 (honesty evaluation of persons). The certificate compounds (*syntymätodistus*, *lääkärintodistus*, etc.) are administrative rather than strictly evidentiary, but they share the same base and the same function of certifying a fact. *Todistaa oikeaksi* and *todistaa aidoksi* also connect to Group 3 (authenticity evaluation).

## Group 5: Expressions used in administrative and procedural contexts for checking and confirming

**Label:** Checking and verification acts; administrative contexts for checking, verifying, and confirming

**Definition:** These expressions describe acts of checking, verifying, inspecting, or monitoring — whether of documents, data, persons, or situations. They appear in administrative, bureaucratic, technical, and scientific contexts where the task is to confirm that data, documents, credentials, or procedures meet required standards. They are used by institutions, officials, and systems rather than primarily in interpersonal or philosophical discourse. The distinction between *tarkistaa* (to check/verify, everyday) and *tarkastaa* (to inspect/audit, formal/official) is register-based and semantically significant: *tarkastaa* implies an authoritative or institutional inspection, while *tarkistaa* is used for routine checking. *Todentaa* (to verify/authenticate) is more technical and is increasingly associated with IT security contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- *tarkistaa* — "to check; to verify; to inspect; to review" (everyday); *tarkistaa tiedot* (to verify the information), *tarkistaa passi* (to check the passport)
- *tarkistus* — "check; verification; inspection" (everyday)
- *tarkistaja* — "checker, verifier, inspector"
- *tarkistaminen* — "the act of checking/verifying"
- *tarkastaa* — "to inspect; to audit; to examine" (formal/official)
- *tarkastus* — "inspection; audit; examination" (formal/official)
- *tarkastaja* — "inspector; auditor; examiner"
- *tarkkailla* — "to observe; to monitor; to watch carefully"
- *tarkkailu* — "observation; monitoring; surveillance"
- *tarkkailija* — "observer; monitor"
- *tarkkaavainen* — "attentive; observant; alert"
- *tarkkaavaisuus* — "attentiveness; attention"
- *tarkka silmä* — "a sharp eye; an eye for detail"
- *todentaa* — "to verify; to authenticate; to confirm as real"; *todentaa henkilöllisyys* (to verify identity); see also Group 4
- *todentaminen* — "verification; authentication"; see also Group 4
- *todennus* — "verification, authentication"
- *todentaa henkilöllisyys* — "to verify identity"
- *kaksivaiheinen todennus* — "two-factor authentication"
- *pätevä* — "valid, competent"; *pätevä hakija* (a qualified applicant); see also Group 7
- *pätevyys* — "validity, competence, qualification"
- *pätevyysvaatimus* — "qualification requirement"
- *pätevyystodistus* — "certificate of competence"
- *täyttää pätevyysvaatimukset* — "to meet the qualification requirements"
- *pätemätön* — "invalid, not applicable"
- *pätemättömyys* — "invalidity"
- *oikeellisuustarkistus* — "correctness check, validation"
- *tietojen oikeellisuus* — "the correctness of the information"
- *asianmukainen* — "proper, appropriate"; procedural conformity; see also Group 2
- *asianmukaisesti* — "properly"; procedural conformity
- *asianmukainen menettely* — "proper procedure"
- *asianmukainen dokumentaatio* — "proper documentation"
- *luotettavuusarvio* — "reliability assessment"
- *tarkkuusvaatimus* — "accuracy requirement"
- *mittaustarkkuus* — measurement accuracy
- *tarkistaa oikeellisuus* — to check correctness

**Observations:** This group overlaps with Group 4 (legal contexts) and Group 2 (norm-conformity) but is distinguished by its specifically administrative and procedural character: the evaluation is performed by an institution or system against formal requirements, and the outcome is typically a binary pass/fail determination. *Pätevyys* is central here because it covers both the validity of credentials and the competence of persons in formal administrative evaluation. *Todentaa* and *todentaminen* also belong to Group 4 (evidence and proof) in legal and administrative contexts. *Tarkkailu* shades into surveillance contexts that are not strictly about truth or accuracy. *Tarkkaavainen* and *tarkkaavaisuus* are more about attentiveness as a personal quality than about verification acts.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate the reliability or credibility of sources and persons

**Label:** Evaluating reliability and credibility of sources, persons, and claims

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers evaluate whether a source of information, a witness, an institution, or a claim is such that it can be relied upon or believed. They appear in journalistic, academic, legal, and everyday discourse. The cluster is organized around the contrast between *luotettava* (reliable, dependable) and *uskottava* (credible, believable), which are distinct in Finnish usage: *luotettava lähde* concerns whether a source can be depended upon over time, while *uskottava selitys* concerns whether a specific explanation is believable.

**Representative expressions:**

- *luotettava* — "reliable, trustworthy, dependable"; emphasizes that something can be depended upon; *luotettava lähde* (a reliable source), *luotettava todistaja* (a reliable witness), *luotettava kumppani* (a reliable partner)
- *luotettavuus* — "reliability, trustworthiness"; *lähteen luotettavuus* (the reliability of the source), *tutkimuksen luotettavuus* (the reliability of the research), *arvioida luotettavuutta* (to assess reliability)
- *luotettavasti* — "reliably, dependably"; *toimia luotettavasti* (to act reliably)
- *epäluotettava* — "unreliable; untrustworthy"; *epäluotettava lähde* (an unreliable source)
- *epäluotettavuus* — "unreliability"; *lähteen epäluotettavuus* (the unreliability of the source)
- *luotettava lähde* — "a reliable source"
- *luotettava todistaja* — "a reliable witness"
- *pitää luotettavana* — "to consider reliable"
- *pitää epäluotettavana* — to consider unreliable
- *osoittautua luotettavaksi* — "to prove to be reliable"
- *osoittautua epäluotettavaksi* — to prove to be unreliable
- *kyseenalaistaa luotettavuus* — "to call reliability into question"
- *luotettavuusongelma* — "a credibility/reliability problem"; particularly common in journalistic discourse about institutional credibility
- *luotettavuuden arviointi* — assessment of reliability
- *heikentää luotettavuutta* — to undermine reliability
- *ei kovin luotettava* — not very reliable (hedged evaluation)
- *uskottava* — "credible, believable, plausible; convincing"; emphasizes that something can or should be believed; *uskottava selitys* (a credible explanation), *uskottava uhka* (a credible threat), *uskottava pelote* (credible deterrent)
- *uskottavuus* — "credibility, believability, plausibility"
- *uskottavasti* — "credibly, convincingly"
- *epäuskottava* — "incredible, implausible; unconvincing"
- *epäuskottavuus* — "implausibility; lack of credibility"
- *uskottava selitys* — "a credible explanation"
- *uskottava uhka* — "a credible threat"
- *uskottava todistaja* — "a credible witness"
- *tehdä uskottavaksi* — "to make credible, to lend credibility to"
- *pitää uskottavana* — "to consider credible"
- *vaikuttaa uskottavalta* — to seem credible
- *horjuttaa uskottavuutta* — to undermine credibility
- *menettää uskottavuus* — to lose credibility
- *uskottavuuden arviointi* — assessment of credibility
- *nauttia uskottavuutta* — to command credibility
- *osoittautua luotettavaksi* — "to prove to be reliable"

**Observations:** The P1 and P2 data explicitly distinguish *luotettava* (reliability/dependability) from *uskottava* (believability/credibility), and Finnish usage maintains this distinction. Both terms appear with *todistaja* (witness) in legal contexts, but with different evaluative emphases: *luotettavuus* concerns whether the witness is dependable and consistent; *uskottavuus* concerns whether the witness's account is believable. This distinction is maintained in Finnish legal proceedings. *Luotettavuusongelma* is particularly common in journalistic discourse about institutional credibility.

## Group 7: Expressions used to characterize deliberate misrepresentation, distortion, or fraud

**Label:** Deliberate falsification, fraudulent conduct, and distortion of facts

**Definition:** These expressions are used when Finnish speakers characterize acts or products of deliberate falsification, distortion, or misrepresentation.
