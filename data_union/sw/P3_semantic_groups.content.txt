# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual distribution observed across P1 and P1b/P2a/P2b documentation. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where items appear in multiple groups, this is noted explicitly. Items that do not fit any observed grouping are listed at the end. Group sizes are uneven and reflect the data, not a desire for symmetry.

---

## GROUP 1: Expressions used to assert or affirm that a statement is true / Propositional truth assertion and denial

**Label:** Assertion-of-truth expressions / Reality-asserting expressions / Propositional truth assertion and denial

**Definition:** These expressions are used by a speaker to affirm that a proposition corresponds to fact, or to frame a statement as sincere and accurate. They appear in contexts where the speaker is either making a direct claim of truth or intensifying the credibility of what they are saying, including in response to claims, in argument, and in contexts where a speaker is committing to or challenging the factual status of a proposition. They contrast with expressions in the falsehood/deception group and are distinct from correctness expressions, which evaluate correctness of procedures or documents rather than propositional truth.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kweli* — used as a bare assertion ("truth / truly / really") or as a discourse particle inviting confirmation (*Kweli?*)
- *ukweli* — the nominalized form used when truth is the topic of discourse (*ukweli wa mambo*, "the truth of the matter")
- *kweli kweli* — reduplicated intensifier used in spoken Swahili to emphasize that something is genuinely true; also appears as a discourse marker of sincerity (see Group 2)
- *kwa kweli* — adverbial phrase framing a statement as sincere or factually grounded (*kwa kweli nakuambia*, "I tell you truly"); also appears as a sincerity marker (see Group 2)
- *kwa kweli kweli* — further intensified form of the above, more colloquial; also appears as a sincerity marker (see Group 2)
- *kweli kabisa* — "absolutely true"; uses *kabisa* as a postposed intensifier
- *kweli ni kweli* — tautological copular construction used rhetorically to assert that a fact cannot be denied
- *Hii ni kweli* — basic copular predication asserting that something is true
- *hakika* — used as a discourse particle or noun affirming certainty (*hakika kabisa*, "absolutely certain"); overlaps with certainty/reliability group
- *kwa hakika* — adverbial phrase asserting that something is definitely the case
- *bila shaka* — "without doubt"; fixed expression used as a strong affirmation of truth or certainty; overlaps with certainty/reliability group
- *dhahiri* — "evident, obvious"; used to assert that something is plainly and visibly true; overlaps with verification group
- *Nakuambia kweli* — "I am telling you the truth"; periphrastic assertion construction
- *ni kweli* — "it is true"
- *si kweli* — "it is not true / that is not true / that's false"
- *ni kweli kabisa kwamba...* — "it is absolutely true that..."
- *kweli au uongo* — "true or false"
- *Je, ni kweli?* — "Is it true? / Really?"
- *ukweli ni kwamba...* — "the truth is that..."
- *ukweli wa mambo ni kwamba...* — "the truth of the matter is that..."
- *ni sahihi* — "it is correct"
- *si sahihi* — "it is not correct / wrong"
- *sahihi kabisa* — "absolutely correct"
- *haki kabisa!* — "absolutely right!" (used as an affirmative response particle in spoken Swahili; overlaps with rights/justice group)
- *ukweli mtupu* — "the plain truth; nothing but the truth"
- *ukweli wote* — "the whole truth"
- *sahihi sahihi* — "exactly right; precisely correct"
- *sawa* — "correct; right; equal" (overlaps with moral fairness group and functions as a simple confirmation marker)
- *sawa sawa* — "exactly; precisely; correctly"
- *ni dhahiri kwamba…* — "it is evident that…"
- *kwa dhahiri* — "evidently; clearly"
- *hali halisi* — "the actual situation; the real state of affairs"
- *halisi* — "genuine; authentic; real" (overlaps with genuineness/counterfeit group)
- *haki* (in truth-asserting uses) — "right; truth" (overlaps with rights/justice and moral rightness groups)
- *haki ya Mungu* — oath formula asserting veracity
- *ukweli wa Mungu* — "God's truth"

**Notes:** *hakika* and *bila shaka* shade into the certainty/reliability group and are listed in both. *kweli* in its discourse-particle use (*Kweli?*) is more of an interactional device than a truth assertion, but it belongs to the same cluster of usage. Within this group, there is a further distinction in usage between expressions that assert truth of a *statement* (*kweli*, *sahihi*, *sawa*) and expressions that assert the genuineness of an *object or situation* (*halisi*, *hali halisi*). These two sub-patterns overlap but are not identical: *sahihi* is used of answers, documents, and calculations; *halisi* is used of gold, goods, and persons. *Dhahiri* is distinctive in that it asserts self-evidence rather than simple accuracy — it is used when the speaker treats the truth as requiring no further argument. *Kweli* and its reduplicated and prepositional forms are the most broadly distributed across all these sub-contexts. *Haki kabisa* is used as an affirmative response particle in spoken Swahili and overlaps with the rights/justice group, but its use as a truth-affirmation response is well attested.

## GROUP 2: Expressions used as sincerity or honesty markers in speech

**Label:** Sincerity framing in discourse

**Definition:** These expressions are used at the beginning of utterances, or parenthetically, to signal that the speaker is being honest or genuine — not to assert the truth of a specific proposition, but to frame the entire speech act as sincere. They function as discourse markers rather than as predicates.

**Representative expressions:**

- *kwa kweli* — "honestly, truly" (sentence-initial)
- *kwa kweli, sijui* — "honestly, I don't know"
- *kusema ukweli* — "to tell the truth" (parenthetical frame)
- *kusema ukweli, sikupenda hilo* — "to tell the truth, I didn't like that"
- *kweli kweli, hii ni ajabu* — "truly, this is amazing"
- *kwa kweli kweli* — "really and truly" (emphatic sincerity marker)

**Notes:** Several of these expressions also appear in Group 1 (*kwa kweli*, *kweli kweli*). The distinction observed in usage is that in Group 1 they modify a specific propositional claim, while here they frame the speaker's overall stance. *Kusema ukweli* as a parenthetical is particularly characteristic of this group and does not appear in Group 1 usage.

## GROUP 3: Expressions used to evaluate the degree of certainty or reliability of information

**Label:** Certainty and reliability expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a piece of information, a source, or a claim can be relied upon or is known with confidence. They appear in epistemic contexts where the speaker is assessing how sure they or others are, and they contrast with *shaka* (doubt) and *bila uhakika* (without certainty). They differ from Group 1 in that they focus on the epistemic status of the knower or source rather than on the propositional content of a claim.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uhakika* — "certainty, reliability, assurance"; the abstract nominal used when evaluating whether something is known for sure
- *hakika* — overlaps with Group 1 but also used specifically in epistemic evaluation of confidence level
- *bila uhakika* — "without certainty"; used to describe a state of not knowing for sure
- *kwa uhakika* — "with certainty, reliably"; adverbial phrase
- *hakika kabisa* — "absolutely certain"
- *Nina uhakika* — "I am sure / I have certainty"
- *Sina uhakika* — "I am not sure / I have no certainty"; extremely common in everyday epistemic discourse
- *shaka* — "doubt, uncertainty"; the primary expression of the opposite pole
- *bila shaka* — "without doubt"; overlaps with Group 1 but specifically addresses the absence of epistemic uncertainty
- *kuhakikisha* — "to make sure, to verify, to confirm"; the causative verb used when someone acts to establish certainty; overlaps with verification group
- *uhakikisho* — "assurance, guarantee, verification"; the nominal form of the above; overlaps with verification group
- *uaminifu wa chanzo* — "reliability of the source"; used in journalistic and academic contexts
- *uaminifu wa habari* — "reliability of information"

**Notes:** *kuhakikisha* and *uhakikisho* also appear in the verification and authentication group because they are used in procedural contexts of confirming facts or documents. *shaka* and *bila shaka* are the clearest markers of the contrast that defines this group. The everyday procedural use of *kuhakikisha* (*hakikisha mlango umefungwa*, "make sure the door is closed") does not involve truth evaluation at all and falls outside both this group and the verification group.

## GROUP 4: Expressions used to evaluate correctness or accuracy of statements, documents, and procedures

**Label:** Correctness and accuracy expressions / Accuracy and correctness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether a statement, calculation, document, answer, or procedure meets a standard of accuracy or correctness. They are particularly common in educational, administrative, legal, scientific, and technical contexts. They differ from Group 1 in that they evaluate against a standard or norm (correct/incorrect) rather than asserting correspondence to fact in a general sense. They are frequently applied to specific domains by the pattern *X sahihi* or *usahihi wa X*.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahihi* — "correct, accurate, right, exact"; the core evaluative adjective in this group
- *usahihi* — "correctness, accuracy, precision"; the abstract nominal form
- *Ni sahihi* — "It is correct"; copular affirmation of correctness
- *Si sahihi* — "It is incorrect"; negative copular evaluation
- *sahihi kabisa* — "absolutely correct"
- *jibu sahihi* — "correct answer"; extremely common in educational contexts
- *taarifa sahihi* — "accurate report"
- *nakala sahihi* — "certified true copy"
- *sahihi na kamili* — "accurate and complete"; paired expression used in legal declarations
- *tarehe sahihi* — "correct date"
- *kwa usahihi* — "accurately, precisely, with precision"
- *kwa usahihi zaidi* — "more precisely, to be more exact"
- *kwa usahihi zaidi, idadi ni elfu tatu, si mbili* — precision-correction frame
- *usahihi wa takwimu* — "accuracy of data"
- *usahihi wa lugha* — "linguistic accuracy, grammatical correctness"
- *usahihi wa habari* — "accuracy of information"
- *usahihi wa tafsiri* — "accuracy of translation"
- *usahihi wa taarifa* — "accuracy of a report"
- *usahihi wa kipimo* — "accuracy of measurement"
- *kusahihisha* — "to correct, to rectify, to mark (examinations), to proofread"
- *kusahihishwa* — "to be corrected; to be validated"
- *usahihishaji* — "correction, the process of marking or editing"
- *msahihishaji / wasahihishaji* — "corrector(s), examiner(s), marker(s)"
- *takwimu sahihi* — "accurate statistics"
- *matamshi sahihi* — "correct pronunciation"
- *sarufi sahihi* — "correct grammar"
- *tafsiri sahihi* — "accurate translation"
- *hesabu sahihi* — "correct calculation"
- *maelezo sahihi* — "accurate description"
- *lugha sahihi* — "correct language"
- *utambuzi sahihi* — "correct identification"
- *thibitisha usahihi* — "verify the accuracy"
- *kuhakikisha usahihi* — "to ensure accuracy"
- *jibu lako si sahihi — jaribu tena* — "your answer is not correct — try again"
- *taarifa hii imethibitishwa kuwa sahihi* — "this report has been verified to be accurate"
- *hakikisha kuwa taarifa ni sahihi kabla ya kuisambaza* — accuracy-before-sharing frame
- *sahihisha makosa* — "correct the errors"
- *hii si sahihi* — "this is not correct; this is wrong"
- *sahihi sahihi* — "exactly right; precisely correct"

**Notes:** *sahihi* also has a distinct use as "signature" (see Group 6), which is a separate usage cluster. The correctness/accuracy cluster is the dominant one in terms of frequency and range of contexts. The pattern *kwa usahihi zaidi* as a precision-correction frame is particularly characteristic. *Kusahihisha* (to correct) belongs here as the remedial response to error, though it also appears in the verification group when used of document validation.

## GROUP 5: Expressions used to identify or accuse something as false, deceptive, or fraudulent / Expressions used in characterizing acts or patterns of deception

**Label:** Falsehood, deception, and inauthenticity expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to identify a statement, object, or person as false, deceptive, or not genuine, and to characterize acts, persons, or patterns of conduct as involving deliberate deception, misleading, or fraud. They appear in contexts of accusation, moral condemnation, legal evaluation, commercial fraud detection, and narrative contexts where the intentional production of false impressions is at issue. Several of these expressions contrast directly with authenticity expressions in Group 7.

**Representative expressions:**

- *uongo* — "lie, falsehood, deception"; the primary nominal for false statements; antonym of *ukweli*; the broadest term, appearing across all registers
- *Hii ni uongo* — "This is a lie / This is false"
- *Piga uongo* — "Tell a lie / Lie"; the most colloquial periphrastic construction for the act of lying
- *Anapiga uongo* — "He/she is lying"
- *kusema uongo* — "to tell lies"
- *uongo mtupu* — "a complete lie, pure falsehood, nothing but lies"
- *uongo wa wazi* — "a blatant lie"
- *Uongo una miguu mifupi* — "A lie has short legs"; widely attested proverb
- *Uongo ni dhambi* — "Lying is a sin"; used in religious and moral instruction
- *kusema uongo ni dhambi* — "telling lies is a sin"
- *kueneza uongo* — "to spread lies"
- *amejaa uongo* — "he/she is full of lies"
- *uongo wake ulidhihirika* — "his/her lie was exposed"
- *kudanganya* — "to deceive, to lie, to mislead"
- *udanganyifu* — "deception, deceitfulness, dishonesty, fraudulence"
- *danganyifu* — "deceptive, deceitful"
- *mdanganyifu / wadanganyifu* — "deceiver(s), liar(s)"
- *kudanganywa* — "to be deceived"
- *kudanganyika* — "to be susceptible to deception, to fall for it"
- *alikudanganya kwa makusudi* — "he/she deceived you deliberately"
- *kudanganya kwa maneno na matendo* — "to deceive by words and deeds"
- *alidanganywa na macho yake mwenyewe* — "he/she was deceived by his/her own eyes"
- *udanganyifu wake ulifunuliwa hadharani* — "his/her deception was exposed publicly"
- *kujikinga na udanganyifu* — "to protect oneself from deception"
- *ghushi* — "fraud, forgery, adulteration"; carries a stronger connotation of deliberate criminal fraud than *bandia*; overlaps with Group 7
- *kughushi* — "to forge, to commit fraud"
- *mghushi / waghushi* — "forger(s), fraudster(s)"
- *Hii ni ghushi* — "This is fraudulent / This is a forgery"
- *Si kweli* — "It is not true / That is not true"
- *nusu ukweli* — "half-truth"; describes a statement that is technically accurate but misleading
- *ulaghai* — "fraud, swindling"
- *mlaghai* — "a fraudster"
- *kulaghai* — "to defraud, to swindle"
- *kesi ya ulaghai* — "a fraud case"
- *alishtakiwa kwa ulaghai* — "he/she was charged with fraud"
- *muongo* — "a liar"
- *waongo* — "liars"
- *shahidi wa uongo* — "a false witness; a perjurer"
- *imani potofu* — "false belief; misguided faith; erroneous conviction"
- *muongo wa hali ya juu* — "a consummate liar"

**Notes:** *bandia* overlaps with this group but is treated as the core of Group 7 because its primary usage is in identifying non-genuine objects rather than in moral condemnation of deceptive acts. *ghushi* and *kudanganya/udanganyifu* are more strongly associated with deliberate deception and moral/legal condemnation. *nusu ukweli* is a borderline item: it describes something that is not entirely false but is used in contexts of criticism and exposure. The construction *ukweli nusu ni uongo* (a half-truth is a lie) explicitly bridges this group and Group 1. *uongo* is primarily used of statements and verbal acts; *bandia* is primarily used of objects, documents, and physical goods. *udanganyifu* and *kudanganya* emphasize the deliberate, intentional character of the deception, whereas *uongo* can describe a false statement without necessarily implying intent. *muongo* and *mdanganyifu* are agentive forms that characterize persons rather than acts or objects. *ulaghai* is more specific than *udanganyifu*, carrying connotations of material fraud and appearing more in legal contexts. The distinction between *uongo* (falsehood as a state or product) and *kudanganya* (the act of deceiving) is consistently maintained in usage.

## GROUP 6: Expressions used in contexts of verifying, confirming, or proving something / Expressions used in formal verification, authentication, and document contexts

**Label:** Verification, confirmation, and proof expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker or institution acts to establish, confirm, or demonstrate that something is true, authentic, or correct. They appear in legal, administrative, academic, journalistic, and everyday procedural contexts. They differ from Group 1 (asserting truth) in that they describe a process or act of establishing truth rather than simply claiming it.

**Representative expressions:**

- *thibitisha* — "to confirm, to verify, to prove, to authenticate"
- *kuthibitisha* — full infinitival form of the above
- *uthibitisho* — "confirmation, verification, proof, authentication, attestation"; overlaps with document signature group
- *kuthibitishwa* — "to be confirmed, to be verified, to be authenticated"
- *mthibitisho* — "a confirmation, a proof, a certificate"; refers to a specific document or instance of proof
- *kuhakikisha* — "to make sure, to ensure, to verify, to confirm"; overlaps with Group 3
- *uhakikisho* — "assurance, guarantee, verification, confirmation"; overlaps with Group 3
- *hakikisha* — imperative: "make sure, ensure, verify"
- *kudhihirisha* — "to reveal, to demonstrate, to make evident, to show clearly"
- *udhihirisho* — "demonstration, manifestation, revelation, evidence, proof"
- *kudhihirika* — "to become evident, to be revealed, to become apparent"
- *dhahiri* — overlaps here in the sense of something being demonstrably evident; also in Group 1
- *ushahidi* — "evidence; testimony; proof"
- *ushahidi wa maandishi* — "documentary evidence; written testimony"
- *ushahidi wa kutosha* — "sufficient evidence"
- *ushahidi madhubuti* — "solid evidence; compelling testimony"
- *kukusanya ushahidi* — "to gather evidence"
- *kutoa ushahidi* — "to give evidence; to testify"
- *kupinga ushahidi* — "to challenge evidence; to contest testimony"
- *shahidi* — "witness; testifier"
- *shahidi wa jicho* — "an eyewitness"
- *kushuhudia* — "to witness; to testify; to attest"
- *uthibitisho wa maandishi* — "written confirmation; documentary proof"
- *uthibitisho wa kisayansi* — "scientific confirmation; empirical proof"
- *kutoa uthibitisho* — "to provide proof; to furnish confirmation"
- *kuthibitisha utambulisho* — "to verify identity"
- *kuthibitisha madai* — "to verify claims; to substantiate allegations"
- *thibitisha habari kabla ya kuisambaza* — "verify the news before spreading it"
- *habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi* — "the news has been confirmed by multiple sources"
- *uthibitisho wa hati* — "authentication of a document"
- *thibitisha utambulisho wako* — "verify your identity"
- *kuthibitisha kuwa halisi* — "to verify that (something) is genuine"
- *taarifa hii imethibitishwa kuwa sahihi* — "this report has been verified to be accurate"

**Notes:** *kuhakikisha* is used in both everyday procedural contexts (making sure a door is closed) and in epistemic verification contexts (confirming a fact). The procedural uses do not involve truth evaluation at all, which means this verb spans this group and contexts entirely outside the truth domain. *thibitisha* and its derivatives are more consistently used in contexts where truth or authenticity is specifically at issue. *thibitisha* and *ushahidi* are the central items in this group. *thibitisha* is oriented toward the act of verifying or confirming; *ushahidi* is oriented toward the material or testimony used as the basis for verification. *shahidi* is the person who provides *ushahidi*. *kudhihirisha* and *kudhihirika* overlap with this group in argumentative contexts where evidence is presented as making something self-evident, but they also appear in Group 1 contexts (asserting that something is clear). The construction *habari imethibitishwa na vyanzo vingi* is characteristic of journalistic verification discourse and shows the integration of *thibitisha* with source-evaluation language.

## GROUP 7: Expressions used in the context of signing or authenticating documents

**Label:** Document signature and authentication expressions

**Definition:** These expressions cluster around the use of *sahihi* in its extended sense of "signature," and are used in administrative, legal, and commercial contexts where a document requires a signature or formal authentication. This usage is distinct from the correctness/accuracy uses of *sahihi* documented in Group 4, though both derive from the same form.

**Representative expressions:**

- *sahihi* (signature meaning) — "signature"
- *sahihi ya mkono* — "handwritten signature, manual signature"
- *sahihi ya kidole* — "fingerprint signature, thumbprint used as signature"
- *sahihi ya dijiti* — "digital signature, electronic signature"
- *sahihi ya kidijitali* — "digital signature"
- *weka sahihi* — "sign here, put your signature, affix a signature"
- *weka sahihi hapa* — "sign here"
- *tia sahihi* — "sign, put a signature, affix a signature"; alternative to *weka sahihi*
- *kuhakikisha sahihi* — "to verify the signature"
- *sahihi yake haikubaliani na ile ya pasipoti* — signature mismatch construction
- *hati sahihi* — "valid document"
- *uthibitisho* — overlaps here in the sense of formal authentication of a document

**Notes:** This group is relatively small and tightly defined. It is included because the usage pattern is clearly distinct from other uses of *sahihi* and because the expressions form a coherent cluster in administrative and legal practice. *uthibitisho* overlaps with Group 6.

## GROUP 8: Expressions used to evaluate whether an object, person, or practice is genuine or counterfeit

**Label:** Genuineness and counterfeit expressions / Authenticity and genuineness evaluation

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether an item, person, or practice is the real thing or an imitation. They are particularly common in commercial, legal, and cultural contexts. The contrast between *halisi* (genuine) and *bandia* (fake) is the clearest organizing opposition in this group. These expressions differ from Group 5 in that they focus on the status of objects and things rather than on the acts or character of persons.

**Representative expressions:**

- *halisi* — "genuine, authentic, real, pure"; the primary positive evaluative adjective
- *uhalisi* — "authenticity, genuineness, reality"; the abstract nominal (also has distinct uses in literary/aesthetic discourse and in reference to actual conditions; see Groups 11 and 12)
- *dhahabu halisi* — "genuine gold"
- *bidhaa halisi* — "genuine goods"; contrasts directly with *bidhaa bandia*
- *Hii ni halisi* — "This is genuine"
- *Si halisi* — "It is not genuine / It is fake"
- *mtu halisi* — "a genuine person, a person of integrity"
- *upendo halisi* — "genuine love"
- *hisia halisi* — "genuine feelings"
- *picha halisi* — "genuine photograph"
- *halisi na ya kweli* — "genuine and true"
- *halisi kwa asilimia mia* — "one hundred percent genuine"
- *hii ni halisi, si bandia* — "this is genuine, not fake"
- *halisi au bandia — jinsi ya kutofautisha* — "genuine or fake — how to tell the difference"
- *nakala halisi* — "a certified true copy; an authenticated copy"
- *hati halisi* — "a genuine document; a valid deed"
- *bandia* — "fake, counterfeit, artificial, imitation"; the primary negative evaluative adjective
- *pesa bandia* — "counterfeit money"
- *bidhaa bandia* — "fake goods"
- *dawa bandia* — "fake medicine, counterfeit drugs"
- *hati bandia* — "forged document"
- *habari bandia* — "fake news"
- *picha bandia* — "fake image, manipulated photo"
- *akili bandia* — "artificial intelligence" [UNCERTAIN — see notes]
- *tabasamu la bandia* — "a fake smile"
- *Hii ni bandia* — "This is fake"
- *hii ni bandia — usiinunue* — "this is fake — don't buy it"
- *jinsi ya kutambua bidhaa bandia* — "how to identify counterfeit goods"
- *dawa bandia zinaweza kuua* — "counterfeit medicines can kill"
- *asili* — "origin, original, authentic, indigenous"; used to evaluate whether something is of the original kind; overlaps with Group 17
- *wa asili* — "original, native, natural, authentic"; fixed adjectival expression
- *ghushi* — overlaps here in the sense of fraudulent/adulterated goods (*bidhaa za ghushi*, *pesa za ghushi*); also in Group 5

**Notes:** *asili* and *wa asili* are included here because their primary evaluative use is in distinguishing original or native things from introduced or imitation ones, which aligns with the genuineness/counterfeit contrast. However, *asili* also has uses in cultural and philosophical discourse about origins that do not fit this group (see Group 17). *ghushi* overlaps with both this group and Group 5. *uhalisi* also has a distinct use in literary/aesthetic discourse (see Group 11) and in reference to actual conditions (see Group 12). *akili bandia* (artificial intelligence) belongs to this group in form (using *bandia*, the inauthenticity marker) but is used in technology discourse without the evaluative negativity characteristic of this group. *bandia* here carries the "artificial/not natural" sense rather than the "counterfeit/fraudulent" sense. This item does not fit cleanly into this group as defined by usage patterns in the truth-evaluation domain. *halisi* evaluates against a standard of genuineness (not counterfeit), while *asili* evaluates against a standard of origin (not modified or imported). *mtu halisi* and *hisia halisi* extend the authenticity evaluation to persons and emotional states, which overlaps with Group 10 (character evaluation).

## GROUP 9: Expressions used to evaluate the legitimacy or lawfulness of acts, claims, and entitlements

**Label:** Legitimacy and lawfulness expressions

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate whether an act, claim, document, or entitlement is legally or morally valid and recognized. They appear in legal, religious, and political discourse. They are oriented toward the question of whether something is rightfully permitted or recognized, rather than simply whether it is factually accurate.

**Representative expressions:**

- *halali* — "lawful, legitimate, permissible, rightful"; also used in Islamic jurisprudential discourse for dietary permissibility; in broader uses functions as a general moral evaluator
- *uhalali* — "legality, legitimacy, lawfulness"
- *uhalali wa kisheria* — "legal legitimacy; lawfulness"
- *uhalali wa kikatiba* — "constitutional legitimacy"
- *uhalali wa uchaguzi* — "the legitimacy of an election"
- *kuhalalisha* — "to legitimize, to legalize, to validate, to authorize"
- *kuhalalishwa* — "to be legitimized; to be made lawful"
- *uhalalishaji* — "legitimization, legalization"
- *kuthibitisha uhalali* — "to verify legitimacy; to validate"
- *kupinga uhalali* — "to challenge legitimacy; to contest validity"
- *halali ya kisheria* — "legally permissible"
- *halali ya kidini* — "religiously permissible"
- *chakula halali* — "halal food; lawfully permissible food"
- *mali halali* — "lawfully acquired property; legitimate wealth"
- *mrithi halali* — "rightful heir; legitimate inheritor"
- *serikali halali* — "legitimate government"
- *haki halali* — "a legitimate right; a lawful entitlement"
- *haki* (in entitlement uses) — "right; entitlement; due" (overlaps with Groups 1, 9, and 13)
- *haki za binadamu* — "human rights"
