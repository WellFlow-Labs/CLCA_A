# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P2: which items appear together in the same constructions, which items are contrasted with each other, which items share typical contexts of use (legal, moral instruction, everyday speech, formal/literary, administrative), and which items participate in the same productive patterns. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups, their sizes, and their boundaries reflect the data as documented.

---

---

## Group 1: Terms used to assert or deny that a statement or situation corresponds to what is actually the case

**Label:** Asserting and denying factual correspondence

**Definition:** These expressions are used when a speaker is making a claim about whether something is true, false, or actually the case — typically in contexts of correction, clarification, or challenge. They appear in constructions such as *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟāl...* (the truth is that...), *itu uṇmaiyā illaiyā?* (is this true or not?), *poy collātē* (don't lie), and *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail). The central contrast in this group is between *uṇmai* and *poy*, which function as direct antonyms across a wide range of registers.

**Representative expressions:**

- உண்மை / *uṇmai* — the primary nominal for truth/what is actually the case
- பொய் / *poy* — the primary nominal for falsehood/what is not the case
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — adjectival: true, genuine, real
- பொய்யான / *poyyāna* — adjectival: false, untrue
- உண்மையாக / *uṇmaiyāka* — adverbial: truly, genuinely
- பொய்யாக / *poyyāka* — adverbial: falsely, untruthfully
- உண்மையில் / *uṇmaiyil* — discourse marker: in truth, actually
- உண்மையிலேயே / *uṇmaiyilēyē* — emphatic: truly, really
- மெய் / *mey* — classical/literary term for truth/reality
- மெய்மை / *meymmai* — classical abstract nominal for truth/reality
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmaitaṉmai* — formal: truthfulness, authenticity
- பொய்மை / *poymai* — formal: the quality of being false
- உண்மையற்ற / *uṇmaiyaṟṟa* — privative: lacking truth, false
- பொய்யில்லாத / *poyyillāta* — privative: without falsehood, truthful

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail)
- *itu poyyā illai uṇmaiyā?* (is this a lie or the truth?)
- *uṇmai eṉṉaveṉṟāl...* (the truth is that...)
- *itil uṇmai illai* (there is no truth in this)
- *poy colli ēmāṟṟiṉāṉ* (he deceived by lying)
- *meyyē vellum* (truth alone will prevail — literary)

## Group 2: Terms used to evaluate persons or conduct as honest or dishonest

**Label:** Evaluating persons and conduct for honesty and its absence

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically to evaluate whether a person's character or conduct is straightforward and free of deception, as opposed to evaluating whether a particular statement is factually accurate. They appear most frequently in moral instruction, interpersonal evaluation, and political discourse. The root *nēr* (straight, direct) underlies the central terms in this group, and the typical contrast is between *nēṟmaiyāna* (honest, upright) and terms like *kaḷḷamāna* (deceitful) or *kapaṭamāna* (hypocritical).

**Representative expressions:**

- நேர்மை / *nēṟmai* — honesty, integrity, uprightness
- நேர்மையான / *nēṟmaiyāna* — honest, upright, sincere
- நேர்மையாக / *nēṟmaiyāka* — honestly, sincerely
- நேர்மையற்ற / *nēṟmaiyaṟṟa* — dishonest, lacking integrity
- நேர்மையுடன் / *nēṟmaiyuṭaṉ* — with honesty, with integrity
- நேர் / *nēr* — straight, direct, honest (base form)
- நேரடி / *nēraṭi* — direct, straightforward, unmediated
- நேரடியாக / *nēraṭiyāka* — directly, straightforwardly
- தூய்மை / *tūymai* — purity, moral integrity (in evaluative contexts)
- தூய்மையான / *tūymaiyāna* — pure, morally unblemished
- தூய்மையற்ற / *tūymaiyaṟṟa* — impure, morally tainted, corrupt

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *nēṟmaiyāka iru* (be honest)
- *nēṟmai tāṉ ciṟanta koḷkai* (honesty is the best policy)
- *nēṟmaiyāna vaḻiyil campāti* (earn by honest means)
- *nēṟmaiyāna atikāri kiṭaippatu aritu* (an honest official is hard to find)
- *maṉat tūymai mukkiyam* (purity of mind is important)
- *tūymai illāta arациyal* (politics without integrity)

**Note on overlap with Group 1:** *nēṟmai* and related terms can appear in contexts where the honesty of a statement (not just a person) is at issue, creating overlap with Group 1. However, the dominant usage pattern for *nēṟmai* is evaluation of persons and conduct, while *uṇmai/poy* more typically evaluate propositions and states of affairs. This distinction is supported by the different constructions each participates in.

## Group 3: Terms used to characterize persons or objects as deceptive, treacherous, or hypocritical

**Label:** Characterizing deception, treachery, and hypocrisy

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons or conduct as involving active deception, betrayal, or a discrepancy between outward appearance and inner reality. They appear in moral, literary, and legal contexts, and several of them (particularly *kaḷḷam* and *kapaṭam*) co-occur as a fixed binomial pair. The terms in this group differ from those in Group 2 in that they characterize the active presence of deceptive intent or conduct, rather than simply the absence of honesty.

**Representative expressions:**

- கள்ளம் / *kaḷḷam* — deceit, guile, duplicity
- கள்ளமான / *kaḷḷamāna* — deceitful, guileful
- கள்ளமில்லாத / *kaḷḷamillāta* — without deceit, guileless
- வஞ்சகம் / *vañcakam* — treachery, betrayal, fraud
- வஞ்சகமான / *vañcakamāna* — treacherous, fraudulent
- வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai* — deception, betrayal (action-oriented)
- வஞ்சகன் / *vañcakaṉ* — deceiver, traitor (masculine agentive)
- கபடம் / *kapaṭam* — hypocrisy, pretense, duplicity
- கபடமான / *kapaṭamāna* — hypocritical, pretentious
- கபடி / *kapaṭi* — hypocrite, pretender (agentive)

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta maṉitaṉ* (a person without deceit or hypocrisy — fixed binomial)
- *avaṉ maṉacul kaḷḷam illai* (there is no deceit in his heart)
- *vañcakam ceytāṉ* (he committed treachery)
- *vañcakattil iruntu kāppāṟṟu* (protect from treachery)
- *kapaṭam niṟaintavaṉ* (one who is full of hypocrisy)
- *kaḷḷattaṉamāka naṭantukkoṇṭāṉ* (he behaved deceitfully)

**Note on internal distinctions:** Within this group, usage patterns suggest a distinction between *kaḷḷam* (inner guile, concealed intent — often located in the heart: *maṉacul kaḷḷam*) and *vañcakam* (active betrayal or fraud, more action-oriented and formal/literary). *kapaṭam* specifically involves a discrepancy between outward appearance and inner reality (hypocrisy, pretense). These distinctions are supported by the different constructions each participates in, but all three share the core evaluative function of characterizing deceptive conduct.

## Group 4: Terms used in assessing whether something is genuine or a fake/copy

**Label:** Assessing genuineness versus fakeness or copying

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the question is whether an object, document, credential, or person is the real thing as opposed to a counterfeit, imitation, or copy. They appear most frequently in administrative, legal, consumer, and everyday contexts. The central contrast is between *acal* (original, genuine) and *pōli* (fake, counterfeit), with *nakal* (copy) occupying a distinct but related position as evaluatively neutral.

**Representative expressions:**

- அசல் / *acal* — original, genuine, authentic
- அசலான / *acalāna* — adjectival: original, genuine
- போலி / *pōli* — fake, counterfeit, imitation
- போலியான / *pōliyāna* — adjectival: fake, spurious
- போலித்தனம் / *pōlittaṉam* — fakeness, the quality of being counterfeit
- நகல் / *nakal* — copy, duplicate (evaluatively neutral)
- உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* — genuine, authentic (in this context)
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmaitaṉmai* — authenticity (in document/object evaluation)

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?)
- *itu acal illai, pōli* (this is not the original — it's a fake)
- *pōli eṉṟu kaṇṭupiṭikkappaṭṭatu* (it was found to be fake)
- *acal āvaṇam koṇṭu vā* (bring the original document)
- *acal eṉṟu nampi vāṅkiṉāṉ* (he bought it believing it to be genuine)
- *pōli āvaṇam vaittiruntāṉ* (he was in possession of a forged document)
- *inta āvaṇattiṉ uṇmaitaṉmai cantēkattirku uriyatu* (the authenticity of this document is open to doubt)

**Note on *nakal*:** *nakal* (copy) is included here because it participates in the *acal/nakal* contrast that structures this evaluative domain, but it is not itself negatively evaluative — a *nakal* is not a fake, merely a reproduction. Its inclusion reflects usage patterns rather than a judgment that it belongs to the same evaluative category as *pōli*.

## Group 5: Terms used in providing, demanding, or evaluating evidential support for claims

**Label:** Providing and evaluating evidential support

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether a claim, accusation, or position has adequate support — whether evidence exists, has been produced, or is sufficient. They appear most frequently in legal, journalistic, and argumentative contexts. The typical constructions involve demanding evidence (*ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē*), asserting its absence (*ātāramaṟṟa vatanti*), or establishing its presence (*cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu*).

**Representative expressions:**

- சான்று / *cāṉṟu* — evidence, proof, testimony, certificate
- சான்றிதழ் / *cāṉṟitaḻ* — certificate, credential
- சான்றாளர் / *cāṉṟāḷar* — witness, attester
- சான்றுமொழி / *cāṉṟumoḻi* — testimony, sworn statement
- சான்றற்ற / *cāṉṟaṟṟa* — without evidence, unattested
- ஆதாரம் / *ātāram* — evidence, basis, grounds
- ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna* — well-founded, evidence-based
- ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa* — without basis, unsubstantiated
- ஆதாரப்படுத்து / *ātārappaṭuttu* — to substantiate, to support with evidence

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *cāṉṟu illāmal nirūpikka muṭiyātu* (without evidence, it cannot be proved)
- *cāṉṟukaḷai maṟaittārkaḷ* (they concealed the evidence)
- *itarku eṉṉa cāṉṟu irukkiṟatu?* (what evidence is there for this?)
- *ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē* (don't make accusations without evidence)
- *itu ātāramaṟṟa vatanti* (this is an unsubstantiated rumor)
- *ātārattūṭaṉ nirūpi* (prove it with evidence)
- *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal collātē* (don't assert without reliable evidence)

**Note on internal distinction:** Usage patterns suggest that *cāṉṟu* is more frequently used in formal legal contexts (testimony, certification, witnessing) while *ātāram* is more common in everyday argumentative contexts (basis, grounds, support for a claim). Both appear in the same constructions, however, and the distinction is one of register and typical context rather than a sharp semantic boundary.

## Group 6: Terms used in verifying, confirming, or establishing accuracy

**Label:** Verifying and confirming accuracy

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the activity described is the checking, confirming, or establishing of whether something is correct or accurate. They appear in administrative, technical, journalistic, and everyday contexts. The typical constructions involve instructions to verify (*caripārtu col*), reports of confirmation (*uṟutippaṭuttal*), and the act of establishing facts (*uṇmai niṟuvu*).

**Representative expressions:**

- சரிபார் / *caripār* — to verify, to check, to validate
- சரிபார்ப்பு / *caripārppu* — verification, checking, review
- உறுதிப்படுத்து / *uṟutippaṭuttu* — to confirm, to verify, to certify
- உறுதிப்படுத்தல் / *uṟutippaṭuttal* — confirmation, verification
- உறுதியான / *uṟutiyāna* — firm, certain, confirmed
- உறுதிமொழி / *uṟutimoḻi* — oath, solemn pledge
- சரிசெய் / *caricey* — to correct, to fix, to set right
- சரியான / *cariyāna* — correct, proper, right
- சரியாக / *cariyāka* — correctly, properly, accurately

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *caripārtu col* (check and then say / verify before speaking)
- *cari paṇṇa muṭiyumā?* (can it be fixed/corrected?)
- *itu cariyā illaiyā?* (is this correct or not?)
- *uṇmai eṉṟu nirūpikkāṇum* (it must be proved to be true)

**Note on *uṟutimoḻi*:** The term *uṟutimoḻi* (oath, solemn pledge) is included here because it derives from the same base (*uṟuti*, firmness/certainty) and appears in legal contexts of formal confirmation, but its primary function is as a speech act of commitment rather than an epistemic verification. It sits at the edge of this group.

## Group 7: Terms used to evaluate the trustworthiness or credibility of persons, sources, or information

**Label:** Evaluating trustworthiness and credibility

**Definition:** These expressions are used when the question is whether a person, institution, source, or piece of information can be relied upon or believed. They appear in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts, and the typical constructions involve asserting or denying credibility (*nampakamāna takavalu*, *nampakattaṉmai kēḷvikkuṟiyākiviṭṭatu*) or describing the relationship of trust between a person and a source (*nampikkai vai*, *nampikkai iḻa*).

**Representative expressions:**

- நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna* — reliable, trustworthy, credible
- நம்பகத்தன்மை / *nampakattaṉmai* — credibility, reliability
- நம்பகமற்ற / *nampakamāṟṟa* — unreliable, not credible
- நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* — trust, faith, confidence
- நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiāna* — trustworthy, reliable
- நம்பிக்கையற்ற / *nampikkaiāṟṟa* — faithless, without trust
- நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka* — worthy of trust, credible
- நம்பமுடியாத / *nampamuṭiyāta* — unbelievable, not credible
- நம்பு / *nampu* — to trust, to believe

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *itu nampakamāna takavala?* (is this reliable information?)
- *avaratu nampakattaṉmai kēḷvikkuṟiyākiviṭṭatu* (his/her credibility has come into question)
- *nampakattaṉmaiyai nilainātṭāṇum* (credibility must be established)
- *nampikkai iḻa* (to lose trust)
- *nampikkai vai* (to place trust in)
- *nampikkai turōkam* (betrayal of trust)
- *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal collātē* (don't assert without reliable evidence)

**Note on overlap with Group 3:** *nampikkai turōkam* (betrayal of trust) and *nampikkai mōcaṭi* (breach of trust/fraud) connect this group to Group 3 (deception and treachery), since betrayal of trust is a form of deception. The overlap is supported by usage: *nampikkai* constructions appear in both trust-evaluation contexts and in descriptions of treacherous conduct.

## Group 8: Terms used in legal and administrative contexts for formal documentation and certification

**Label:** Formal documentation, certification, and official attestation

**Definition:** These expressions are used specifically in administrative and legal contexts where the formal status of documents, credentials, and official statements is at issue. They appear in fixed expressions such as *nakal cari* (certified copy), *acal āvaṇam* (original document), and *cāṉṟitaḻ* (certificate). Several of these terms are loanwords (Arabic *acal*, *nakal*) or Sanskrit-derived terms (*ātāram*, *cāṉṟu*) that have become fixed in administrative Tamil.

**Representative expressions:**

- அசல் / *acal* — original document
- நகல் / *nakal* — copy, duplicate
- சான்றிதழ் / *cāṉṟitaḻ* — certificate, credential
- சான்றுபெயர் / *cāṉṟupeyar* — to certify, to attest officially
- சான்றாளர் / *cāṉṟāḷar* — witness, official attester
- உண்மை ஆவணம் / *uṇmai āvaṇam* — authentic document
- போலி ஆவணம் / *pōli āvaṇam* — forged document
- பொய்சாட்சி / *poycāṭci* — false testimony, perjury
- உறுதிமொழி / *uṟutimoḻi* — oath, formal pledge

**Typical constructions from P2:**

- *acal āvaṇam koṇṭu vā* (bring the original document)
- *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?)
- *nakal cari ceyyappaṭṭatu* (the copy was certified)
- *nakal eṭuttu koṭu* (make a copy and give it)
- *cāṉṟu camarppikkāṇum* (evidence/documentation must be submitted)

**Note:** This group is not a semantic category in the same sense as the others — it is defined primarily by register and context of use (administrative/legal) rather than by a shared evaluative function. Several items here also appear in other groups (e.g., *acal/pōli* in Group 4, *cāṉṟu* in Group 5). The grouping is noted because the administrative context creates a distinct cluster of co-occurring terms with specific fixed expressions.

## Group 9: Terms used in moral instruction and proverbial discourse

**Label:** Terms that appear in moral instruction and proverbial assertions

**Definition:** Several expressions from P1 and P2 appear with particular frequency in moral instruction contexts — parental speech, proverbs, ethical discourse — and participate in a set of recurring construction types: *X tāṉ vellum* (X alone will prevail), *X illāta iṭattil Y illai* (where there is no X, there is no Y), *X tāṉ ciṟanta koḷkai* (X is the best policy). These constructions are not confined to a single semantic domain but cut across Groups 1, 2, and 7.

**Representative expressions and constructions:**

- *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* (truth alone will prevail)
- *niyāyam tāṉ vellum* (justice alone will prevail)
- *meyyē vellum* (truth/reality alone will prevail — literary)
- *nēṟmai tāṉ ciṟanta koḷkai* (honesty is the best policy)
- *nēṟmai illāta iṭattil nampikkai illai* (where there is no honesty, there is no trust)
- *uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ kaṣṭattil tāṉ teriyum* (a true friend is known only in hardship)
- *poy coluvatu tavaṟu* (telling lies is wrong)
- *kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta maṉitaṉ* (a person without deceit or hypocrisy)
- *nēṟmaiyāna atikāri kiṭaippatu aritu* (an honest official is hard to find)

**Note:** This is not a grouping by meaning but by construction type and discourse context. The same terms (*uṇmai*, *nēṟmai*, *niyāyam*, *poy*, *kaḷḷam*, *kapaṭam*) appear in other groups; this grouping captures a cross-cutting pattern of use in proverbial and moral-instruction registers.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 participate in multiple groups or do not fit cleanly into any single group:

---

**நியாயம் / *niyāyam***

This term appears in constructions that overlap with Group 1 (asserting what is right/correct), Group 2 (evaluating conduct as fair or unfair), and Group 9 (proverbial assertions: *niyāyam tāṉ vellum*). Its usage spans epistemic (logical soundness), moral (fairness, justice), and legal (justification) contexts in ways that do not map cleanly onto any single group. It is noted here rather than placed in one group.

---

**தூய்மை / *tūymai***

Placed in Group 2 (evaluating persons and conduct for honesty) because of its use in moral evaluation of persons and conduct (*tūymai illāta arациyal*, *maṉat tūymai*). However, it also appears in religious and aesthetic contexts (physical purity, ritual cleanliness) that are outside the truth-evaluation domain entirely. Its placement in Group 2 reflects only the subset of its usage documented in P1 and P2.

---

**நேர்த்தி / *nēṟtti***

This term derives from the same root as *nēṟmai* (Group 2) but its documented usage is primarily in aesthetic and craft-evaluation contexts (neatness, precision of form) rather than in moral evaluation of persons. It does not fit cleanly into Group 2 or any other group identified here. It is noted as an item that shares a root with Group 2 terms but whose usage pattern is distinct.

---

**உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* in emotional and relational contexts**

The adjectival *uṇmaiyāna* appears in collocations such as *uṇmaiyāna naṇpaṉ* (true friend), *uṇmaiyāna aṉpu* (true love), and *uṇmaiyāna mukam* (true face/real character). In these uses, the term evaluates the depth or authenticity of a relationship or character rather than the factual accuracy of a statement. This usage overlaps with Group 1 (genuineness) and Group 4 (genuine vs. fake) but is not identical to either. The P1b documentation flags this as a peripheral extension of the root.

---

**நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* in religious contexts**

As noted in P1b, *nampikkai* appears in religious faith contexts (*kaṭavuḷ mēl nampikkai*, faith in God) where the object of trust is not an empirical claim subject to verification. This usage overlaps with Group 7 (trustworthiness/credibility) but involves a different kind of reliance. No separate group is created for this usage as the data does not support a distinct cluster.

---

**மெய் / *mey* and மெய்மை / *meymmai***

These classical/literary terms for truth and reality overlap with Group 1 in meaning but are confined to formal, literary, and philosophical registers where *uṇmai* would be used in everyday speech. They participate in Group 9 constructions (*meyyē vellum*) and in the compound *meyññāṉam* (true knowledge). They are not placed in Group 1 as a primary member because their usage pattern is distinct (register-restricted, philosophical), but they are cross-referenced there.

---

**உண்மைக்கு புறம்பான / *uṇmaikkup puṟampāna***

This formal legal expression (contrary to truth, against the facts) is documented in P1 as a marginal item. It participates in the *uṇmai/poy* contrast of Group 1 but is confined to legal and administrative registers and does not participate in the productive construction patterns of Group 1. It is noted here as a register-restricted variant of Group 1 evaluation.

---

## Items That Do Not Fit Clearly into Any Group

**நேர்கோடு / *nērkōṭu*** (straight line) and **நேர்கோணம் / *nērkōṇam*** (right angle): These are technical geometric terms derived from the *nēr* root. They share a root with Group 2 terms but their usage is purely technical with no evaluative or epistemic content. They are not placed in any group.

**நேர்முகம் / *nērmukam*** and **நேர்முக நேர்கண்டல் / *nērmuka nērkkaṇṭal***: These terms (face-to-face, in-person interview) derive from *nēr* and carry the sense of directness, but their primary usage is in administrative and journalistic contexts describing a mode of interaction rather than evaluating honesty or truth. They are not placed in any group.

**நேர்மாறு / *nērmāṟu*** (opposite, inverse): A logical/mathematical term derived from *nēr*. No evaluative or epistemic content in documented usage. Not placed in any group.
