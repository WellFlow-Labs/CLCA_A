# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 Core Vocabulary Collection
## Language: Korean (ko)
### Transliteration Standard: Revised Romanization of Korean (RR)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 진실
**Transliteration:** jinsil
**Literal meaning:** 真實 — "true/genuine" + "substance/reality"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, sincerity, reality

Used to describe statements, persons, or situations that correspond to reality or are free from deception. Appears in both everyday and formal registers, including moral, legal, and journalistic contexts, often in opposition to 거짓 (geojit, falsehood). Participates in derivation: 진실하다 (to be truthful/sincere), 진실성 (truthfulness, authenticity/veracity as a quality).

**[CORE]**

---

**2.**
**Form:** 사실
**Transliteration:** sasil
**Literal meaning:** 事實 — "matter/affair" + "real/solid/substance"
**Typical English translations:** fact, actuality, truth (in the sense of what actually happened or factual accuracy)

Used to assert that something corresponds to actual events or states of affairs, as opposed to opinion, rumor, or fabrication. Widely used in legal, journalistic, and everyday speech to ground claims in verifiable reality. Also functions adverbially meaning "in fact" or "actually." Participates in derivation: 사실이다 (to be a fact), 사실상 (in effect, de facto, in fact), 사실적 (factual, realistic).

**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** 진짜
**Transliteration:** jinjja
**Literal meaning:** Colloquial; 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 짜 (suffix of uncertain origin, possibly from 것)
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, authentic; (adverbially) really, truly

Used to distinguish genuine items, persons, or states from imitations, pretenses, or exaggerations. Contrasts directly with 가짜 (fake, counterfeit). Functions as both adjective and adverb in informal speech. Extremely frequent in everyday speech across a wide range of contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**4.**
**Form:** 가짜
**Transliteration:** gajja
**Literal meaning:** 가 (假, false/temporary/fake) + 짜 (colloquial suffix, parallel to 진짜)
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, imitation, phony, false

Used to characterize objects, documents, persons, or claims that are not what they purport to be, whether an object, credential, or identity. Directly contrasts with 진짜 (jinjja). Appears in legal and commercial contexts involving fraud or misrepresentation, as well as consumer and everyday moral contexts.

**[CORE]**

---

**5.**
**Form:** 정확
**Transliteration:** jeonghwak
**Literal meaning:** 正確 — "correct/upright" + "certain/firm/confirmed"
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness; (adjectival form 정확한) accurate, precise, exact

Used to evaluate whether a statement, measurement, translation, or representation corresponds precisely to what it describes. Used in scientific, legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts. Participates in derivation: 정확하다 (to be accurate/precise/exact), 정확성 (accuracy as a property), 정확히 (accurately, exactly).

**[CORE]**

---

**6.**
**Form:** 올바르다
**Transliteration:** olbareuda
**Literal meaning:** 올 (right, proper, upright, native Korean root) + 바르다 (to be straight, upright, correct)
**Typical English translations:** to be correct, right, proper, upright

Applied to conduct, reasoning, values, and statements judged to conform to a standard of correctness or moral rightness. Used in ethical and educational contexts. The adjectival form 올바른 (olbareun) is very common in moral and civic discourse, modifying nouns directly. Nominalized as 올바름 (rightness, correctness).

**[CORE]**

---

**7.**
**Form:** 옳다
**Transliteration:** olta
**Literal meaning:** Native Korean root; to be right/correct
**Typical English translations:** to be right, correct, proper, just

Used to evaluate propositions, actions, or judgments as correct or morally right. Appears in both epistemic contexts (this answer is right) and moral contexts (this action is right). Contrasts with 그르다 (geureuda, to be wrong/incorrect). Participates in the fixed pair 옳고 그름 (right and wrong).

**[CORE]**

---

**8.**
**Form:** 그르다
**Transliteration:** geureuda
**Literal meaning:** Native Korean root; to be wrong/incorrect
**Typical English translations:** to be wrong, incorrect, improper, mistaken

The direct antonym of 옳다 (olta), used to evaluate propositions or conduct as incorrect or morally wrong. Somewhat more formal or literary in register than 틀리다 (teullida). Appears in moral and philosophical discourse. Appears in the fixed expression 옳고 그름.

**[CORE]**

---

**9.**
**Form:** 맞다
**Transliteration:** matda
**Literal meaning:** to fit, to match, to be correct
**Typical English translations:** to be right, correct, to match, to fit

Extremely common verb used to affirm that a statement, answer, or identification is correct or that two things correspond. Used across factual, moral, and practical evaluative contexts. Contrasts with 틀리다 (to be wrong, mistaken).

**[CORE]**

---

**10.**
**Form:** 틀리다
**Transliteration:** teullida
**Literal meaning:** to be off, to deviate (from a standard or correct form); native Korean root
**Typical English translations:** to be wrong, incorrect, mistaken; to differ

Used to evaluate statements, answers, or calculations as factually or logically incorrect. More commonly used in everyday speech than 그르다 (geureuda) for factual incorrectness. Distinct from 다르다 (to be different), though the two are sometimes conflated in colloquial speech; 틀리다 carries a normative sense of error. Participates in 틀림없다 (to be without error, certain).

**[CORE]**

---

**11.**
**Form:** 틀림없다
**Transliteration:** teullim-eopda
**Literal meaning:** 틀림 (error, deviation) + 없다 (to not exist/have)
**Typical English translations:** to be certain, undoubtedly correct, without error

Fixed expression used to assert strong confidence in the accuracy or truth of a claim. Functions epistemically to mark a statement as beyond reasonable doubt. Common in both spoken and written registers.

**[CORE]**

---

**12.**
**Form:** 정직
**Transliteration:** jeongjik
**Literal meaning:** 正直 — "correct/upright" + "straight/honest"
**Typical English translations:** honesty, integrity, straightforwardness

Used to evaluate persons or conduct with respect to truthfulness and the absence of deception. Appears prominently in moral, educational, legal, and professional contexts. Participates in derivation: 정직하다 (to be honest), 정직성 (honesty as a quality).

**[CORE]**

---

**13.**
**Form:** 거짓
**Transliteration:** geojit
**Literal meaning:** falsehood, lie (native Korean word; etymology debated)
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth, falsity, falseness

Used to characterize statements or representations that are not true, both in moral condemnation of lying and neutral description of inaccuracy. Contrasts with 진실 and 사실. Appears in compounds such as 거짓말 (lie, literally "false word/speech") and 거짓말하다 (to lie).

**[CORE]**

---

**14.**
**Form:** 거짓말
**Transliteration:** geojitmal
**Literal meaning:** 거짓 (geojit, falsehood) + 말 (mal, word, speech)
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood

A conventionalized compound specifically denoting a false utterance or deliberate deception. The standard everyday term for a spoken or communicated lie. Extremely common across all registers and contexts. Participates in further derivation: 거짓말하다 (to tell a lie/to lie), 거짓말쟁이 (liar).

**[CORE]**

---

**15.**
**Form:** 신뢰
**Transliteration:** sinloe
**Literal meaning:** 信賴 — "trust/faith" + "rely on/depend on"
**Typical English translations:** trust, reliability, credibility

Used to evaluate persons, institutions, or sources with respect to whether they can be depended upon to be accurate or honest. Used in interpersonal, institutional, and legal contexts. Participates in derivation: 신뢰하다 (to trust), 신뢰성 (reliability, trustworthiness), 신뢰도 (degree of reliability), 신뢰할 수 있다 (to be trustworthy).

**[CORE]**

---

**16.**
**Form:** 신뢰성
**Transliteration:** sinloeseong
**Literal meaning:** 신뢰 (sinloe, trust/reliability) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**Typical English translations:** reliability, trustworthiness, credibility

A nominalized form used to evaluate whether a source, testimony, or system can be relied upon for accurate information. Prominent in legal, academic, and journalistic discourse. Closely related to but distinct from 진정성 (jinjeongseong), which emphasizes sincerity over reliability.

**[CORE]**

---

**17.**
**Form:** 신빙성
**Transliteration:** sinbyeongseong
**Literal meaning:** 信憑性 — "trust" + "evidence/basis/rely on" + "-ness/-ity"
**Typical English translations:** credibility, plausibility, reliability (of a claim or source), trustworthiness (of testimony or evidence)

Used specifically to evaluate whether a claim, testimony, or account has sufficient basis to be believed. Particularly common in legal and investigative contexts. Somewhat more formal and domain-specific than 신뢰성 (sinloeseong). Often appears in 신빙성이 있다/없다 (to have/lack credibility).

**[CORE]**

---

**18.**
**Form:** 진위
**Transliteration:** jinwi
**Literal meaning:** 眞僞 — "true" + "false"
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, truth or falsity, genuineness; truth or falsehood

Used to frame the question of whether something is true or false, genuine or counterfeit, particularly for documents, artworks, or claims. Typically appears in the construction 진위를 가리다 (to determine the truth or falsity/authenticity of something). Common in legal, art-historical, journalistic, and investigative contexts. Primarily nominal in use.

**[CORE]**

---

**19.**
**Form:** 진본
**Transliteration:** jinbon
**Literal meaning:** 眞本 — "true/genuine" + "original/copy"
**Typical English translations:** authentic original, genuine copy, the real thing (of a document or artwork)

Used in archival, legal, and art-historical contexts to designate an item as the genuine original rather than a reproduction or forgery. Contrasts with 위본 (forged copy) and 복사본 (copy).

**20.**
**Form:** 위조
**Transliteration:** wijo
**Literal meaning:** 僞造 — "false/fake/counterfeit" + "to make/manufacture/create"
**Typical English translations:** forgery, counterfeiting, fabrication

Used in legal and criminal contexts to describe the act of producing false documents, currency, signatures, or other items intended to deceive. Participates in derivation: 위조하다 (to forge, to counterfeit), 위조품 (forged item, counterfeit goods), 위조문서 (forged document).

**[CORE]**

---

**21.**
**Form:** 허위
**Transliteration:** heowi
**Literal meaning:** 虛僞 — "empty/hollow/false" + "false/counterfeit/deceptive"
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, misrepresentation

Used in formal, legal, journalistic, and administrative registers to characterize statements or representations that are false or deliberately misleading. More formal than 거짓 (geojit). Appears in legal compounds such as 허위진술 (false statement), 허위신고 (false report), 허위광고 (false advertising).

**[CORE]**

---

**22.**
**Form:** 진정
**Transliteration:** jinjeong
**Literal meaning:** 진 (眞, true/genuine) + 정 (正, correct/upright) or 정 (情, feeling/sincerity, depending on context)
**Typical English translations:** genuine, true, real, sincere; (adverbially) truly, really

Used to evaluate whether something is authentic in character or feeling, often with an emphasis on sincerity rather than mere surface appearance. Appears in expressions like 진정한 사랑 (jinjeonghan sarang, true love) and 진정성 (jinjeongseong, authenticity/genuineness). Participates in productive adjectival and nominal derivation.

**[CORE]**

---

**23.**
**Form:** 진정성
**Transliteration:** jinjeongseong
**Literal meaning:** 眞正性 — "true" + "correct/proper" + "-ness/-ity"
**Typical English translations:** authenticity, genuineness, sincerity

A nominalized form evaluating whether a person's actions, expressions, or works reflect genuine inner conviction or character rather than performance or pretense. Prominent in contemporary public discourse on art, politics, personal relationships, and personal integrity. Often used to question or affirm the credibility of public figures or artistic works. Participates in 진정성 있다/없다 (to have/lack authenticity).

**[CORE]**

**24.**
**Form:** 본질
**Transliteration:** bonjil
**Literal meaning:** 本質 — "root/origin/fundamental" + "substance/nature/quality"
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, substance, what something fundamentally is

Used in philosophical, analytical, and everyday contexts to refer to the fundamental nature or defining character of something, as opposed to its surface appearance or accidental properties. Participates in derivation: 본질적 (essential, fundamental), 본질적으로 (essentially, fundamentally).

**[CORE]**

---

**25.**
**Form:** 실체
**Transliteration:** silche
**Literal meaning:** 實體 — "real/actual" + "body/substance/form"
**Typical English translations:** substance, true nature, real entity, actuality, reality

Refers to the actual or underlying reality of something, as opposed to appearance or representation. Used in philosophical, legal, and journalistic contexts to probe what something really is. Appears in expressions like 실체를 밝히다 (silchereul balkida, to reveal the true nature of something). Related to 실체적 (substantive, real).

**[CORE]**

---

**26.**
**Form:** 실제
**Transliteration:** silje
**Literal meaning:** 實際 — "real/actual" + "boundary/occasion"
**Typical English translations:** reality, actuality; (adjectival/adverbial) actual, real, in practice

Used to contrast what is actually the case with what is apparent, claimed, or theoretical. Participates in derivation: 실제로 (actually, in reality), 실제적 (practical, actual). Common across registers.

**[CORE]**

---

**27.**
**Form:** 타당
**Transliteration:** tadang
**Literal meaning:** 妥當 — "fitting/appropriate/settled" + "proper/right/fitting"
**Typical English translations:** validity, appropriateness, reasonableness, soundness; (adjectival form 타당한) valid, reasonable, sound

Used to evaluate arguments, decisions, or claims as well-founded or appropriate given relevant standards or evidence. Used in academic, legal, deliberative, and policy contexts. Participates in derivation: 타당하다 (to be valid/sound/reasonable), 타당성 (validity, soundness, reasonableness), 타당한 이유 (valid reason).

**[CORE]**

---

**28.**
**Form:** 타당성
**Transliteration:** tadangseong
**Literal meaning:** 타당 (tadang, valid/appropriate) + 성 (性, -ness/-ity)
**Typical English translations:** validity, soundness, reasonableness

A nominalized form used to evaluate the logical or normative soundness of an argument, policy, or claim. Prominent in academic, legal, and policy discourse. Often appears in the construction 타당성을 검토하다 (tadangseong-eul geomto-hada, to examine the validity of something).

**[CORE]**

---

**29.**
**Form:** 근거
**Transliteration:** geungeo
**Literal meaning:** 根據 — "root/basis" + "basis/foundation/to rely on"
**Typical English translations:** basis, grounds, evidence, foundation

Used to refer to the evidential or rational basis for a claim or judgment. Appears in 근거가 있다/없다 (to be grounded/ungrounded, to be well-founded/unfounded), 근거 없는 주장 (unsubstantiated claim). Central to evaluative discourse about the reliability of claims. Common across academic, legal, and everyday registers.

**[CORE]**

**30.**
**Form:** 공정
**Transliteration:** gongjeon
**Literal meaning:** 公正 — "public/impartial" + "correct/upright/just"
**Typical English translations:** fairness, impartiality, justice

Used to evaluate procedures, judgments, processes, or persons with respect to whether they are free from bias and conform to standards of equal treatment. Used in legal, institutional, journalistic, and everyday moral contexts. Participates in derivation: 공정하다 (to be fair/impartial), 공정성 (fairness, impartiality), 공정한 평가 (fair evaluation).

**[CORE]**

---

**31.**
**Form:** 객관적
**Transliteration:** gaekgwanjeok
**Literal meaning:** 客觀的 — 객관 (gaekgwan, 客觀, objective view) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix -ic/-al)
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial, unbiased

Used to characterize evaluations, descriptions, or methods as based on external, verifiable criteria rather than personal perspective. Used in academic, journalistic, and everyday evaluative discourse. Contrasts with 주관적 (jugwanjeok, subjective). Participates in derivation: 객관성 (gaekgwanseong, objectivity).

**[CORE]**

---

**32.**
**Form:** 주관적
**Transliteration:** jugwanjeok
**Literal meaning:** 主觀的 — 주관 (jugwan, 主觀, subjective view) + 적 (的, adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** subjective, personal, biased (in some contexts)

Used to characterize evaluations or perceptions as dependent on individual perspective rather than external standards. Used in academic, aesthetic, and everyday contexts, sometimes neutrally and sometimes critically. Contrasts with 객관적 (gaekgwanjeok, objective). Appears in discussions of aesthetic judgment, testimony, and the reliability of personal accounts.

**[CORE]**

**33.**
**Form:** 모순
**Transliteration:** mosun
**Literal meaning:** 矛盾 — "spear" + "shield" (classical Chinese allusion to logical contradiction)
**Typical English translations:** contradiction, inconsistency

Used to identify logical or factual inconsistency within a set of claims or between a claim and evidence. Participates in derivation: 모순되다 (to be contradictory), 모순이 있다 (to contain a contradiction). Common in argumentation and legal contexts.

**[CORE]**

**34.**
**Form:** 유사
**Transliteration:** yusa
**Literal meaning:** 類似 — "category/kind" + "resemble/to resemble"
**Typical English translations:** similarity, resemblance, analogy, likeness

Used to evaluate the degree to which two things resemble each other, relevant in contexts of identification, classification, legal comparison, and imitation. Participates in derivation: 유사하다 (to be similar), 유사성 (similarity, resemblance). Appears in legal contexts such as 유사품 (similar/imitation product), e.g., trademark similarity.

**[CORE]**

**35.**
**Form:** 위증
**Transliteration:** wijeung
**Literal meaning:** 僞證 — "false" + "testimony/evidence"
**Typical English translations:** perjury, false testimony

Legal term for the act of giving false testimony under oath. Participates in derivation: 위증하다 (to commit perjury), 위증죄 (the crime of perjury). Domain-specific but clearly attested and productive.

**36.**
**Form:** 참
**Transliteration:** cham
**Literal meaning:** Native Korean word; truth, genuineness, the real
**Typical English translations:** truth, the genuine/real; (adverbially) truly, really

Used as a noun meaning truth or reality (contrasting with 거짓), and as an adverb meaning truly or really. Appears in philosophical and literary registers as well as everyday speech. Participates in derivation: 참되다 (to be true, genuine), 참값 (true value, in mathematics and logic).

**[CORE]**

**37.**
**Form:** 모조
**Transliteration:** mojo
**Literal meaning:** 模造 — "to imitate/model" + "to make/manufacture/create"
**Typical English translations:** imitation, replica, copy, counterfeit

Denotes an object made to resemble another, not necessarily with intent to deceive but lacking the authenticity of the original. Used in consumer, legal, and art contexts. Appears in compounds such as 모조품 (mojopum, imitation goods/replica). Distinct from 위조 (wijo) in that 모조 does not always imply criminal intent.

**[CORE]**

**38.**
**Form:** 표절
**Transliteration:** pyojeol
**Literal meaning:** 剽竊 — "to steal/plagiarize" + "to steal secretly/to pilfer"
**Typical English translations:** plagiarism, copying without attribution, literary theft

Evaluates a work or act as involving the unacknowledged appropriation of another's intellectual or creative output, thereby misrepresenting its origin. Used in academic, literary, and legal contexts. Participates in derivation: 표절하다 (to plagiarize), 표절작 (plagiarized work).

**[CORE]**

**39.**
**Form:** 왜곡
**Transliteration:** waegok
**Literal meaning:** 歪曲 — "crooked/distorted" + "bent/curved/distorted"
**Typical English translations:** distortion, misrepresentation, falsification, twisting

Evaluates a representation, account, or interpretation as having been altered or skewed away from accurate correspondence to reality. Used in journalistic, historical, legal, and political contexts. Participates in derivation: 왜곡하다 (to distort/misrepresent), 왜곡된 (distorted), 역사 왜곡 (yeoksa waegok, historical distortion).

**40.**
**Form:** 유효
**Transliteration:** yuhyo
**Literal meaning:** 有效 — "to have" + "effect/validity"
**Typical English translations:** validity, effectiveness, being in force

Used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a document, contract, or action has legal force or is still operative. Participates in derivation: 유효하다 (to be valid), 유효성 (validity). Contrasts with 무효 (invalidity, nullity).

**41.**

**Transliteration:** muhyo
**Literal meaning:** 無效 — "without" + "effect/validity"
**Typical English translations:** invalidity, nullity, void

Used in legal and formal contexts to declare that something lacks legal force or effect. Directly contrasts with 유효. Appears in derivation: 무효화하다 (to invalidate, to nullify).

Used to evaluate whether an action, claim, or authority is rightful, justified, or proper. Participates in derivation: 정당하다 (to be legitimate/just), 정당성 (legitimacy, justification), 정당화하다 (to justify). Common in legal, political, and ethical discourse.

**Typical English translations:** legality, lawfulness

Used specifically to evaluate whether an action or state of affairs conforms to law. Participates in derivation: 합법적 (legal, lawful), 합법성 (legality). Contrasts with 불법 (illegality).

**Transliteration:** oryu
**Literal meaning:** 誤謬 — "mistake/error" + "error/fallacy"
**Typical English translations:** error, mistake, fallacy

Used to identify mistakes in reasoning, data, or representation. Common in academic, logical, and technical contexts. Participates in derivation: 오류를 범하다 (to commit an error/fallacy). Overlaps with but is more formal than 실수 (mistake).
