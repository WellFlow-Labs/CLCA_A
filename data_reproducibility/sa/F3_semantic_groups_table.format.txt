# F3 — SEMANTIC GROUPS TABLE

| Group Label | Definition | Representative Expressions | Overlapping / Ambiguous Items | Boundary Notes |
|------------|------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| GROUP 1: Terms for Factual Correspondence | This group comprises expressions used to indicate that statements, cognitions, or representations correspond to actual states of affairs. These terms appear in contexts where the primary concern is whether something matches reality or fact, rather than moral evaluation. They are commonly contrasted with terms denoting error, illusion, or misrepresentation, and frequently appear in epistemological discussions about the accuracy of knowledge claims. | सत्य / satya (when used for factual truth), तथ्य / tathya, यथार्थ / yathārtha, वस्तु / vastu, वास्तव / vāstava, वास्तविक / vāstavika, वस्तुतः / vastutaḥ (construction: "in reality, actually"), यथार्थं ज्ञा / yathārthaṃ jñā (construction: "know accurately"), तथ्यं वच् / tathyaṃ vac (construction: "speak the truth, state facts") | सत्य / satya: Also functions in moral/ethical contexts as "truthfulness" and in ontological contexts as "real, existing"; वस्तु / vastu: Can denote both "real object" and simply "thing, object" without truth-evaluation; रूप / rūpa: Sometimes used for actual form/nature but more commonly for appearance generally | Overlaps with GROUP 2 (epistemic validation) in contexts where factual accuracy is established through valid means |
| GROUP 2: Terms for Epistemic Validation | This group includes expressions specifically concerned with valid means of knowledge and the process of epistemic justification. These terms appear predominantly in philosophical texts discussing how knowledge is acquired, validated, or certified. They focus on the instruments, methods, or standards by which claims come to be accepted as knowledge rather than mere belief or error. The group is characterized by technical usage in Indian epistemological systems. | प्रमाण / pramāṇa, प्रमा / pramā, प्रामाण्य / prāmāṇya, प्रमाता / pramātā, प्रमेय / prameya, प्रामाणिक / prāmāṇika, प्रत्यक्षप्रमाण / pratyakṣapramāṇa, अनुमानप्रमाण / anumānapramāṇa, शब्दप्रमाण / śabdapramāṇa, प्रमाणेन सिध् / pramāṇena sidh (construction: "be established by valid means"), किं प्रमाणम् / kiṃ pramāṇam (construction: "what is the proof?"), प्रामाण्यस्य हेतुः / prāmāṇyasya hetuḥ (construction: "cause of validity") | उपपत्ति / upapatti: Overlaps with logical reasoning terms but specifically concerns epistemic justification; न्याय / nyāya: Functions both as logical method and as justice/fairness | Overlaps with GROUP 1 (factual correspondence) in contexts where factual accuracy is established through valid means |
| GROUP 3: Terms for Cognitive Error | This group consists of expressions denoting mistaken cognition, false perception, or erroneous understanding. These terms appear in contexts discussing the failure of cognitive processes to apprehend reality correctly. They are regularly contrasted with terms for valid cognition and often appear in discussions of illusion, misperception, or logical fallacy. The group includes both general error terms and more specific technical vocabulary for types of cognitive mistakes. | भ्रम / bhrama, भ्रान्ति / bhrānti, भ्रान्त / bhrānta, विपर्यय / viparyaya, मिथ्या / mithyā (in epistemological contexts), अप्रमा / apramā, आभास / ābhāsa (when denoting false appearance), प्रमाणाभास / pramāṇābhāsa, भ्रमं गम् / bhramaṃ gam (construction: "fall into error"), मिथ्या ज्ञा / mithyā jñā (construction: "know falsely"), विपर्ययं गम् / viparyayaṃ gam (construction: "fall into error") | मिथ्या / mithyā: Also used in metaphysical contexts for illusory appearance of reality (Vedānta); आभास / ābhāsa: Can denote mere appearance without necessarily implying error; प्रमाद / pramāda: Denotes negligent error rather than purely cognitive mistake | Overlaps with GROUP 4 (deliberate falsehood) in contexts where the distinction between mistake and lie is unclear |
| GROUP 4: Terms for Deliberate Falsehood | This group comprises expressions specifically indicating intentional deception, lying, or fraudulent misrepresentation. Unlike cognitive error terms, these expressions carry implications of agency and moral culpability. They appear in ethical, legal, and social contexts where the concern is with dishonest behavior rather than mere mistake. These terms often appear in prohibitions, moral condemnations, or legal discussions of fraud. | असत्य / asatya (when denoting lying), अनृत / anṛta (when denoting falsehood in speech), मृषा / mṛṣā, कूट / kūṭa, छल / chala, अलीक / alīka, मृषा वद् / mṛṣā vad (construction: "speak falsely, lie"), असत्यं मा वद् / asatyaṃ mā vad (construction: "do not speak untruth"), कूटं रच् / kūṭaṃ rac (construction: "fabricate fraud"), छलं कृ / chalaṃ kṛ (construction: "practice deception") | असत्य / asatya: Can denote both factual incorrectness and moral falsehood; छल / chala: Technical term in Nyāya logic for sophistical refutation, not always implying moral fault; मिथ्या / mithyā: Primarily denotes falsity/error rather than deliberate deception | Overlaps with GROUP 3 (cognitive error) in contexts where the distinction between mistake and lie is unclear |
| GROUP 5: Terms for Moral/Cosmic Order | This group includes expressions denoting rightness, propriety, or correctness understood as conformity to moral law, cosmic order, or proper conduct. These terms appear in contexts where evaluation concerns ethical behavior, religious duty, or alignment with natural/divine order rather than factual accuracy. They are characteristic of dharmaśāstra literature, religious texts, and ethical instruction, often appearing in prescriptions for proper conduct. | धर्म / dharma, ऋत / ṛta, साधु / sādhu (when denoting moral goodness), सत्य / satya (when denoting moral truthfulness), सत्यव्रत / satyavrata, सत्यधर्म / satyadharma, धर्मं चर् / dharmaṃ car (construction: "practice dharma"), धर्मात् न चलितव्यम् / dharmāt na chalitavyam (construction: "one should not deviate from dharma"), ऋतस्य पथा / ṛtasya pathā (construction: "by the path of ṛta"), सत्यं वद् धर्मं चर / satyaṃ vad dharmaṃ car (construction: "speak truth, practice dharma") | सत्य / satya: Functions across factual, moral, and ontological domains; न्याय / nyāya: Denotes both logical method and justice/fairness; साधु / sādhu: Can indicate both moral goodness and simple correctness/propriety | Overlaps with GROUP 6 (logical propriety) in contexts where rational and moral evaluation converge |
| GROUP 6: Terms for Logical/Rational Propriety | This group consists of expressions indicating what is logically sound, rationally justified, or methodologically proper. These terms appear in contexts of argumentation, logical analysis, and rational demonstration where the concern is with coherence, valid inference, or proper reasoning rather than moral evaluation or factual correspondence alone. They are common in philosophical debate and logical texts. | न्याय / nyāya (when denoting logic/reasoning), युक्त / yukta, युक्ति / yukti, उपपत्ति / upapatti, समुपपत्ति / samupapatti, युक्त्या / yuktyā (construction: "by reasoning"), उपपत्त्या / upapattyā (construction: "by logical reasoning"), युक्तं यत् / yuktaṃ yat (construction: "it is proper that"), न्यायेन / nyāyena (construction: "logically, according to reason") | न्याय / nyāya: Also denotes justice/fairness in legal contexts; साधु / sādhu: Can indicate logical correctness but more commonly moral goodness; सम्यक् / samyak: Indicates general correctness across multiple domains | Overlaps with GROUP 5 (moral order) in contexts where rational and moral evaluation converge |
| GROUP 7: Terms for Essential Nature/Reality | This group comprises expressions denoting the essential nature, true form, or ultimate reality of things as opposed to their appearance or accidental properties. These terms appear in metaphysical and philosophical contexts concerned with distinguishing essence from accident, reality from appearance, or substance from attribute. They are particularly prominent in Vedānta and other philosophical systems discussing levels of reality. | तत्त्व / tattva, स्वरूप / svarūpa, वस्तु / vastu (when denoting reality/substance), रूप / rūpa (when denoting essential form), सत्त्व / sattva (when denoting existence/essence), तात्त्विक / tāttvika, तत्त्वं ज्ञा / tattvaṃ jñā (construction: "know reality"), स्वरूपेण / svarūpeṇa (construction: "essentially, by true nature"), तत्त्वतः / tattvataḥ (construction: "in reality, essentially") | रूप / rūpa: Can denote both essential form and mere appearance; सत्त्व / sattva: In Sāṃkhya, denotes a specific quality (guṇa) rather than general essence; वस्तु / vastu: Functions both as "real object" and simply "thing" | Defined in opposition to GROUP 8 (appearance) but shares vocabulary (especially रूप) |
| GROUP 8: Terms for Appearance/Semblance | This group includes expressions denoting how things appear, seem, or present themselves, often with implications that appearance may differ from reality. These terms appear in contexts distinguishing between how things seem and how they are, or in discussions of similarity and resemblance. They are common in epistemological discussions of illusion and in metaphysical discussions of phenomenal appearance. | आभास / ābhāsa, रूप / rūpa (when denoting appearance), सदृश / sadṛśa, सादृश्य / sādṛśya, आभासिक / ābhāsika, आभासमात्रम् / ābhāsamātram (construction: "mere appearance"), रूपेण / rūpeṇa (construction: "in form, by appearance") | रूप / rūpa: Can denote both appearance and essential form; आभास / ābhāsa: Sometimes implies false appearance, sometimes neutral appearance; सदृश / sadṛśa: Primarily denotes similarity rather than appearance per se | Defined in opposition to GROUP 7 (essential nature) but shares vocabulary (especially रूप) |
| GROUP 9: Terms for Correctness/Completeness | This group consists of expressions indicating that something is correct, complete, proper, or done rightly across various domains without specific restriction to factual, moral, or logical correctness. These terms appear as general markers of approval or propriety in diverse contexts. They function as broad evaluative terms that can apply to speech, action, cognition, or conduct. | सम्यक् / samyak, साधु / sādhu, सत्कार / satkāra, सत्कृत / satkṛta, समुपपत्ति / samupapatti, सम्यग् ज्ञा / samyag jñā (construction: "know correctly"), साधु उक्तम् / sādhu uktam (construction: "well said"), सम्यक् कृ / samyak kṛ (construction: "do correctly") | साधु / sādhu: Can specifically denote moral goodness or general correctness; सम्यक् / samyak: Particularly important in Buddhist contexts with technical meanings; युक्त / yukta: More specifically denotes logical/rational propriety | General evaluative terms that cut across multiple domains |
| GROUP 10: Negation Terms (Impropriety/Wrongness) | This group comprises negative counterparts to terms of correctness, propriety, or rightness. These expressions denote what is improper, wrong, unfitting, or contrary to established norms across moral, logical, or practical domains. They appear in contexts of prohibition, criticism, or identification of deviation from standards. Most are formed through negative prefixation of positive terms. | अधर्म / adharma, अयुक्त / ayukta, असत्य / asatya, अनृत / anṛta, अप्रमाण / apramāṇa, अप्रमा / apramā, असत्कार / asatkāra, अयुक्तं यत् / ayuktaṃ yat (construction: "it is improper that"), न युक्तम् / na yuktam (construction: "it is not proper") | असत्य / asatya: Can denote factual incorrectness, moral falsehood, or ontological non-existence; अनृत / anṛta: Archaic term with limited classical usage; अप्रमा / apramā: Technical epistemological term rather than general wrongness | Positive evaluation terms show richer semantic differentiation and more productive compounding than their negative counterparts |
| GROUP 11: Terms for Positive Moral Qualities | This group includes expressions denoting positive moral character, virtuous conduct, or good qualities in persons and actions. These terms appear in ethical evaluation, character description, and moral instruction. They often appear in compounds with sat- (good) and are used to describe praiseworthy persons, actions, or qualities in social and religious contexts. | सत्पुरुष / satpuruṣa, सज्जन / sajjana, सद्गुण / sadguṇa, सद्वृत्त / sadvṛtta, सत्कर्म / satkarma, सत्संग / satsaṅga, साधु / sādhu (when denoting virtuous person), धार्मिक / dhārmika, सत्यवादिन् / satyavādin | साधु / sādhu: Functions both as general correctness marker and specific virtue term; धार्मिक / dhārmika: Can denote both moral righteousness and religious observance; सत्त्व / sattva: In Sāṃkhya, technical term for quality rather than moral virtue | Domain-specific terms for ethical evaluation and character description |
| GROUP 12: Vedic Cosmic Order Terms | This group consists of archaic Vedic expressions specifically denoting cosmic order, ritual propriety, and divine truth as understood in Vedic religion. These terms are characterized by limited productivity in classical Sanskrit and primary occurrence in Vedic texts. They represent an older conceptual framework where cosmic, ritual, and moral order are unified. Most derivatives and compounds are archaic. | ऋत / ṛta, अनृत / anṛta, ऋतज्ञ / ṛtajña, ऋतावन् / ṛtāvan, ऋतव्रध्न / ṛtavradhna, ऋतं सत्यं च / ṛtaṃ satyaṃ ca (construction: "cosmic order and truth"), ऋतेन ऋतम् आप् / ṛtena ṛtam āp (construction: "attain ṛta through ṛta") | ऋतु / ṛtu: Derivative denoting seasons, extended into temporal/natural domain; सत्य / satya: In Vedic contexts often paired with ṛta but has broader classical usage | Vedic terms form a distinct semantic cluster with limited integration into classical usage patterns, suggesting historical semantic shift |
| GROUP 13: Terms for Measurement/Standard | This group includes expressions related to measurement, quantification, and standards of comparison. These terms appear in contexts involving quantitative assessment, establishment of norms, or comparative evaluation. While some have epistemic functions (as standards of knowledge), others operate in purely quantitative or evaluative domains. | मान / māna, मानदण्ड / mānadaṇḍa, मानक / mānaka, परिमाण / parimāṇa, उपमान / upamāna, प्रमाण / pramāṇa (when denoting standard/measure) | प्रमाण / pramāṇa: Primarily epistemic but retains measurement semantics; मान / māna: Polysemous across measurement, honor, and pride; उपमान / upamāna: Technical epistemological term but based on comparison/measurement | Some terms have epistemic functions while others operate in purely quantitative domains |
| (Ungrouped Items) | Items with unclear or multiple group membership that do not form clear groups | सत्त्व / sattva (in general usage: existence/essence; in Sāṃkhya: specific technical term for one of three guṇas; in ethical contexts: goodness/purity), प्रमाद / pramāda (denotes negligent error but not pure cognitive error; carries moral culpability but not deliberate deception; sits between cognitive error and moral fault), ऋतु / ṛtu (derivative of ऋत but semantically distant; denotes temporal/seasonal order rather than moral/cosmic order; extended into biological domain), मिथ्या / mithyā (in epistemological contexts: cognitive error; in Vedānta metaphysics: illusory appearance of reality; in ethical contexts: falsehood; usage spans multiple domains), Terms with limited attestation: ऋतायु / ṛtāyu, ऋतचित् / ṛtacit, ऋतधीति / ṛtadhīti, सात्य / sātya, धार्म्य / dhārmya, साम्यक / sāmyaka, मैथ्य / maithya (marked UNCERTAIN in P2a), Terms for promise-keeping and commitment (सत्यसन्ध, सत्यप्रतिज्ञ) relate to truthfulness but form a distinct volitional domain, Terms for honor and respect (सत्कार, मान) relate to evaluation but in social rather than epistemic or moral domains, Seasonal/temporal terms (ऋतु and derivatives) form a natural order domain distinct from moral order | Highly polysemous terms: सत्य / satya (functions in factual correspondence, moral truthfulness, and ontological existence contexts without clear boundaries), न्याय / nyāya (functions as logical method/reasoning and as justice/fairness in legal contexts), रूप / rūpa (can denote essential form/nature, appearance/semblance, or simple form without truth-evaluation), साधु / sādhu (functions as general correctness marker, moral virtue term, and logical correctness marker) | Several marginal or uncertain terms show insufficient usage patterns for confident grouping; some usage patterns do not form clear groups; technical philosophical usage limits clear grouping for some terms |
