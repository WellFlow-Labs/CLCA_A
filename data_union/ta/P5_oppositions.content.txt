# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919

---

**Methodological note:** The following oppositions are extracted solely from patterns explicitly documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is inferred beyond what the data directly supports. Where the data describes a contrast, that contrast is reported. Where asymmetry exists between the two poles of a contrast, this is noted rather than normalized.

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** பொய் / *poy*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *poy*
4. **English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *poy* is "the primary antonym of *uṇmai*" and that *uṇmai* "contrasts with *poyymai* (falsehood)." P3 Group 1 identifies *poy* as "the primary negative counterpart" of *uṇmai*, used predicatively to assert that a claim does not correspond to reality. The pair appears in parallel constructions: *atu uṇmaitāṉ* (that is indeed true) vs. *atu poytāṉ* (that is indeed a lie), in the binary question construction *itu uṇmaiyā illai poyyā?* (is this true or false?), and in the construction *poyyai uṇmai eṉṟu nampināṉ* (he believed the lie to be true). P3 identifies this as "the central contrast" of Group 1, and in the proverbial structure where *uṇmai* prevails and *poy* is eventually exposed.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*
2. **Term B:** பொய்யான / *poyyāna*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyāna* / *poyyāna*
4. **English translation:** true, genuine, real / false, untrue, deceptive
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 item 4 explicitly states that *uṇmaiyāna* "contrasts with *poyyāna* (false) in parallel constructions." Both are *-āna* adjectival forms of their respective base nominals and function as direct antonyms in attributive and prenominal modifier position.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** கற்பனை / *kaṟpaṉai*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *kaṟpaṉai*
4. **English translation:** truth, fact / fiction, fabrication, imagination
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *kaṟpaṉai* as used "to characterize a claim or narrative as invented rather than factual." P2b Construction 18.2 documents *itu veṟum kaṟpaṉai* (this is mere fabrication) as a dismissal of a claim as invented. P2b Construction 18.3 documents the compound *kaṟpaṉaikkum eṭṭāta uṇmai* (a truth beyond imagination), which places the two terms in explicit relational contrast. The opposition is between what is real/factual and what is invented/imagined.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** நகல் / *nakal*
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *nakal*
4. **English translation:** original / copy, duplicate
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 item 14 explicitly states *acal* "directly contrasts with *nakal* (copy)." P2b Construction 8.2 (also documented as Construction 2 under *acal* in P2b) documents the disjunctive question *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?) as a fixed evaluative pair, attested also in P4b. P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad as the core of the genuineness-evaluation group. The contrast is between the genuine original and a reproduction, with *nakal* carrying neutral connotation in this pair. P3 notes *nakal* is "evaluatively neutral" while *acal* is positively evaluative.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** போலி / *pōli*
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *pōli*
4. **English translation:** original, genuine / fake, counterfeit, sham
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 item 14 states *acal* "directly contrasts with *pōli* (fake)." P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad and notes that *pōli* "is always negative" while *nakal* can be neutral. P1 describes *pōli* as applied to "objects, persons, or credentials that falsely claim to be genuine" and as carrying "a strongly negative evaluative charge." The pair co-occurs in the construction *itu acal illai, pōli* (this is not the original — it's a fake), documented in P2b Construction 3 under *pōli*. The construction *atu pōliyā, acalā?* (Is that fake or genuine?) is documented as a standard disjunctive contrast in P2b Construction 11.3. P3 Group 4 identifies this as the central contrast of the genuineness-versus-fakeness domain. The contrast between *acal* and *pōli* is between genuine authenticity and fraudulent imitation, with a moral/legal dimension absent from the *acal/nakal* pair.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** நகல் / *nakal* and போலி / *pōli* (combined negative pole)
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *nakal*, *pōli*
4. **English translation:** original / copy (neutral) and fake (negative)
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad and explicitly notes that "*nakal* is neutral in connotation (a certified copy of a document is a *nakal* without negative implication), while *pōli* is always negative." This documents a three-way structure in which *acal* contrasts with two distinct negative poles that differ in their evaluative charge: *nakal* (non-original but legitimate) and *pōli* (non-original and fraudulent).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna*
2. **Term B:** போலி / *pōli*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyāna* / *pōli*
4. **English translation:** genuine, authentic / fake, counterfeit

P2a item 12 (under *acal*) notes that *acalāna* "directly contrasts with *pōliyāna* (fake)," and *uṇmaiyāna* participates in the same evaluative frame.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Term B:** வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmai* / *vañcaṉai*
4. **English translation:** honesty, integrity / deceit, deception, fraud

The parallel adverbial constructions *nērmaiyāka naṭa* (behave honestly) and *vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa* (behave deceitfully) are documented in P2b as structurally parallel.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** நியாயம் / *niyāyam*
2. **Term B:** அநியாயம் / *aniyāyam*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyam* / *aniyāyam*
4. **English translation:** justice, fairness, rightness / injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *aniyāyam* as the "direct antonym of *niyāyam*." P2a notes that *aniyāyam* is formed by the Sanskrit privative prefix *a-* applied to the Sanskrit-origin base *nyāya* before borrowing into Tamil, making this "a lexical antonym pair rather than a productive Tamil-internal derivation." P2b documents the predicative use *itu aniyāyam* (this is unjust) as the negative counterpart to *itu niyāyamā?* (is this fair?).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** நியாயம் / *niyāyam*
2. **Term B:** அன்யாயம் / *anyāyam*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyam* / *anyāyam*
4. **English translation:** justice, fairness, rightness / injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *anyāyam* "functions as the direct antonym of *niyāyam* in evaluative contexts" (P1, item 16), and that *anyāyamāṉa* is the "direct antonym of *niyāyamāṉa*" (P2a, item 16.1). The opposition is etymologically encoded: *anyāyam* derives from Sanskrit *anyāya* (contrary to *nyāya*).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** சரியான / *cariyāna*
2. **Term B:** தவறான / *tavaṟāna*
3. **Transliteration:** *cariyāna* / *tavaṟāna*
4. **English translation:** correct, right, proper / wrong, erroneous, mistaken
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 items 5 and 6 document *cari* and *tavaṟu* as the primary terms for correctness and error/wrongness respectively. P2b Construction 4 under *poy* attests the construction *poy coluvatu tavaṟu* (telling lies is wrong), linking the two domains. The adjectival forms *cariyāna* and *tavaṟāna* appear in parallel evaluative constructions across spoken and written registers.

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna*
2. **Term B:** நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampakamāna* / *nampattakāta*
4. **English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, credible / untrustworthy, not credible, not worth trusting
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents *nampattakāta* as "the direct negative counterpart of *nampattakka*" and P1b documents both *nampattakka* and *nampattakāta* as a positive/negative pair for evaluating whether a person, source, or claim is worthy of trust. P2a notes the morphological relationship: both are formed from *nampu* + *-att-* + *takka* (worthy) vs. *takāta* (not worthy). The contrast is between credibility and its absence in source and testimony evaluation.

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka*
2. **Term B:** நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampattakka* / *nampattakāta*
4. **English translation:** worth trusting, credible / not worth trusting, not credible
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a explicitly documents these as a positive/negative participial adjective pair derived from the same base (*nampu* + *-att-*) by the contrast between *takka* (worthy of) and *takāta* (not worthy of, negative participial). P1b Root 2 items 13–14 presents these as a matched pair: *nampattakka* is "worthy of trust, credible" and *nampattakāta* is "its negative counterpart." P4b documents both as attested in journalistic and legal evaluation of sources and testimony. This is a morphologically formed minimal pair within the same derivational paradigm.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 31

1. **Term A:** ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna*
2. **Term B:** ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *ātāramāna* / *ātāramaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** well-founded, evidenced, substantiated / unfounded, unsubstantiated, baseless
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents both as derived from *ātāram* (evidence, basis): *ātāramāna* via the *-āna* adjectival suffix and *ātāramaṟṟa* via the *aṟṟa* privative participial suffix. P4b documents *ātāramaṟṟa kuṟṟaccāṭṭu* (a baseless accusation) as the attested example of the negative form. The pair is also attested in the fixed collocation *ātāramaṟṟa vatanti* (unsubstantiated rumor) documented in P2b and P4b, and in the construction *ātāram illāmal kuṟṟam cāṭṭātē* (don't accuse without evidence). The two negative forms *ātāramaṟṟa* and *ātāramillāta* are presented as alternants of each other, with *ātāramillāta* slightly more common in spoken registers. The contrast is between claims that have evidentiary support and those that do not.
