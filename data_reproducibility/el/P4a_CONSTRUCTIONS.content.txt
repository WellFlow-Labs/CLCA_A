# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X είναι TRUTH-TERM** (X is TRUTH-TERM)
Standard copular construction with truth-domain adjectives or nouns. Subject X is characterized by the truth-property.

### Examples
- **αυτό είναι αλήθεια** (aftó íne alítheia) "this is truth/true"
- **η ιστορία είναι αληθινή** (i istoría íne alithiní) "the story is true"

### Notes
Greek uses the copula είναι with both nominal predicates (αλήθεια) and adjectival predicates (αληθινός, σωστός, ψευδής). No special construction required; standard predication pattern.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
**X δεν είναι TRUTH-TERM** (X not is TRUTH-TERM)
Standard negation with δεν preceding the copula. Also: **X είναι NEGATIVE-TRUTH-TERM** using inherently negative terms.

### Examples
- **αυτό δεν είναι αλήθεια** (aftó den íne alítheia) "this is not true"
- **αυτό είναι ψέμα** (aftó íne pséma) "this is a lie"

### Notes
Greek employs both analytic negation (δεν + positive term) and lexical negation (inherently negative terms like ψέμα, λάθος, άδικος). Both patterns equally common.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-ADJ + NOUN** (attributive adjective precedes noun)
Truth-domain adjectives modify nouns directly in attributive position, agreeing in gender, number, and case.

### Examples
- **αληθινή ιστορία** (alithiní istoría) "true story"
- **ψευδής κατάθεση** (psevdís katáthesi) "false testimony"

### Notes
Standard Greek adjective-noun agreement. Truth adjectives (αληθινός, σωστός, ψευδής, πλαστός, etc.) function as regular attributive modifiers with full inflectional agreement.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
**γίνομαι + TRUTH-ADJ** (become + TRUTH-ADJ)
The verb γίνομαι "become" combines with truth adjectives to express change of state.

### Examples
- **γίνεται σωστό** (gínete sostó) "it becomes correct"
- **η κατάσταση γίνεται σαφής** (i katástasi gínete safís) "the situation becomes clear"

### Notes
Standard inchoative construction using γίνομαι. Less common with core truth terms (αλήθεια, ψέμα) which resist inchoative framing; more natural with correctness terms (σωστός) and clarity terms.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
**κάνω + X + TRUTH-ADJ** (make + X + TRUTH-ADJ)
The verb κάνω "make/do" can express causation with adjectival complements. Also: specialized causative verbs with -ώνω suffix.

### Examples
- **το κάνει σωστό** (to káni sostó) "it makes it correct"
- **ακυρώνω το έγγραφο** (akyróno to éngrafo) "I invalidate the document"

### Notes
Greek has both analytic causatives (κάνω + adjective) and morphological causatives (ακυρώνω "invalidate," βεβαιώνω "certify"). The morphological pattern is productive for legal/administrative terms but not for core truth vocabulary.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-ROOT + -ότητα/-εια/-ία** (abstract noun suffixes)
Productive derivational pattern creating abstract quality nouns from adjectives.

### Examples
- **ορθότητα** (orthótita) "correctness" (from ορθός)
- **αξιοπιστία** (axiopistía) "reliability" (from αξιόπιστος)

### Notes
Highly productive pattern documented in P2a. The suffix -ότητα is most common (ορθότητα, σωστότητα, αληθινότητα), followed by -εια/-ία (ακρίβεια, ειλικρίνεια, αδικία). Creates abstract quality nouns from truth-domain adjectives.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-ROOT + agent suffix** (-της, -τρα, -αράς, -εώνας)
Derivational pattern creating agent nouns for persons characterized by truth-related behavior.

### Examples
- **ψεύτης** (pséftis) "liar" (from ψεύδομαι)
- **απατεώνας** (apateónas) "swindler" (from απατώ)

### Notes
Productive for negative agents (ψεύτης, ψεύτρα, ψευταράς, απατεώνας) but limited for positive agents. No common agentive noun for "truth-teller" in everyday use (αληθολόγος exists but is marginal/literary).

---

## 8. TRUTH-TELLING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**λέω + την αλήθεια/ψέματα** (say + the truth/lies)
Light verb λέω "say/tell" combines with truth nouns to express truth-telling or lying.

### Examples
- **λέω την αλήθεια** (léo tin alítheia) "I tell the truth"
- **λέω ψέματα** (léo psémata) "I tell lies"

### Notes
This is the primary construction for expressing truth-telling and lying in Greek. The verb λέω is obligatory; no single-word verb equivalent to English "lie" exists (*ψεμίζω is not standard). The verb ψεύδομαι exists but is more formal.

---

## 9. ERROR-MAKING CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**κάνω + λάθος** (make/do + mistake)
Light verb κάνω combines with error noun λάθος to express making mistakes.

### Examples
- **κάνω λάθος** (káno láthos) "I make a mistake / I'm wrong"
- **έκανα ένα μεγάλο λάθος** (ékana éna megálo láthos) "I made a big mistake"

### Notes
This is the standard construction for expressing error in Greek. The verb κάνω is obligatory; λάθος does not function as a verb. The construction is extremely common and productive with various modifiers of λάθος.

---

## 10. PREPOSITIONAL TRUTH PHRASES

### Pattern
**στην + TRUTH-NOUN** (in/at + TRUTH-NOUN)
Prepositional phrases with truth nouns function as discourse markers or adverbials.

### Examples
- **στην αλήθεια** (stin alítheia) "in truth, actually"
- **στην πραγματικότητα** (stin pragmatikótita) "in reality, actually"

### Notes
These prepositional phrases function as discourse markers introducing corrections or frank statements. Very common in both spoken and written Greek. The pattern is productive with abstract truth nouns.

---

## 11. INTERROGATIVE TRUTH EXPRESSIONS

### Pattern
**TRUTH-NOUN + interrogative intonation**
Single truth nouns used as questions to express surprise or seek confirmation.

### Examples
- **αλήθεια;** (alítheia?) "really? is that true?"
- **σοβαρά;** (sovará?) "seriously?" (lit. "serious?")

### Notes
Greek allows bare truth nouns with interrogative intonation to function as complete questions. This pattern is extremely common in colloquial speech for expressing surprise or seeking confirmation.

---

## 12. EXCLAMATORY TRUTH EXPRESSIONS

### Pattern
**TRUTH-NOUN/ADJ + exclamatory intonation**
Truth vocabulary used as exclamations to express emphasis or denial.

### Examples
- **ψέματα!** (psémata!) "lies! no way!"
- **αλήθεια μου!** (alítheia mou!) "honestly! truly!"

### Notes
Greek productively uses truth vocabulary as exclamations. The plural ψέματα functions as a denial ("that's not true!"). The possessive construction αλήθεια μου emphasizes sincerity.

---

## 13. CATCHING IN FALSEHOOD

### Pattern
**πιάνω + X + στο ψέμα/λάθος** (catch + X + in-the lie/mistake)
Construction expressing discovery of someone in error or deception.

### Examples
- **τον έπιασα στο ψέμα** (ton épiasa sto pséma) "I caught him in a lie"
- **την έπιασαν στο λάθος** (tin épiasan sto láthos) "they caught her in the mistake"

### Notes
The verb πιάνω "catch" combines with prepositional phrase στο ψέμα/λάθος to express discovery of falsehood or error. Common construction in everyday speech.

---

## 14. TRUTH REVELATION

### Pattern
**βγαίνω/αποκαλύπτω + η αλήθεια** (come-out/reveal + the truth)
Constructions expressing truth being revealed or discovered.

### Examples
- **βγαίνει η αλήθεια στο φως** (vgéni i alítheia sto fos) "the truth comes to light"
- **αποκαλύπτω την αλήθεια** (apokalýpto tin alítheia) "I reveal the truth"

### Notes
Greek uses both intransitive (βγαίνω "come out") and transitive (αποκαλύπτω "reveal") verbs with αλήθεια to express truth revelation. The phrase στο φως "to the light" is a common metaphorical extension.

---

## 15. TRUTH CONCEALMENT

### Pattern
**κρύβω + την αλήθεια** (hide + the truth)
Construction expressing concealment of truth.

### Examples
- **κρύβει την αλήθεια** (krývi tin alítheia) "he/she hides the truth"
- **δεν μπορώ να κρύψω την αλήθεια** (den boró na krýpso tin alítheia) "I can't hide the truth"

### Notes
The verb κρύβω "hide" combines with αλήθεια to express truth concealment. Common in accusations and discussions of deception. Contrasts with revelation constructions.

---

## 16. LIVING IN FALSEHOOD

### Pattern
**ζω + στο ψέμα** (live + in-the lie)
Idiomatic construction expressing inauthentic living or maintained deception.

### Examples
- **ζει στο ψέμα** (zi sto pséma) "he/she lives a lie"
- **ζούσαμε στο ψέμα** (zoúsame sto pséma) "we were living a lie"

### Notes
Idiomatic construction using ζω "live" with prepositional phrase στο ψέμα. Expresses sustained inauthenticity or false pretenses. Common in discussions of authenticity and self-deception.

---

## 17. VERIFICATION/CONFIRMATION

### Pattern
**αληθεύομαι** (passive/middle: be-verified, prove-true)
Middle/passive voice construction expressing verification or confirmation of truth.

### Examples
- **αληθεύτηκε η πρόβλεψη** (alithéftike i próvlepsi) "the prediction proved true"
- **αληθεύονται οι φήμες** (alithévonde i fímes) "the rumors are being confirmed"

### Notes
The middle/passive form αληθεύομαι expresses that something is verified or proves to be true. Less common than active forms but productive for expressing confirmation of predictions or claims.

---

## 18. COLOR-MODIFIED LIES

### Pattern
**COLOR-ADJ + ψέμα** (color + lie)
Idiomatic construction using color adjectives to grade lie severity.

### Examples
- **άσπρο ψέμα** (áspro pséma) "white lie"
- **μαύρο ψέμα** (mávro pséma) "black lie, blatant lie"

### Notes
Greek uses color metaphors (άσπρο "white," μαύρο "black," κόκκινο "red") to create a severity scale for lies. This pattern is productive and well-established in colloquial usage.

---

## 19. DIMINUTIVE MITIGATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-NOUN + -άκι** (diminutive suffix)
Diminutive formation to minimize severity of errors or lies.

### Examples
- **ψεματάκι** (psematáki) "little lie, fib"
- **λαθάκι** (latháki) "small mistake"

### Notes
The diminutive suffix -άκι productively attaches to truth-domain nouns to minimize severity or express affection. Common in everyday speech for mitigating face-threatening acts.

---

## 20. PSEUDO-COMPOUNDS

### Pattern
**ψευδο- + NOUN** (pseudo- + noun)
Highly productive prefix pattern creating compounds indicating falsity or resemblance.

### Examples
- **ψευδοεπιστήμη** (psevdoepistími) "pseudoscience"
- **ψευδομαρτυρία** (psevdomartyría) "false testimony, perjury"

### Notes
The prefix ψευδο- is exceptionally productive in Greek, creating compounds across multiple domains (scientific, legal, religious). Documented extensively in P1b. Pattern remains productive for new coinages.

---

## 21. ORTHO-COMPOUNDS

### Pattern
**ορθο- + NOUN** (ortho- + noun)
Productive prefix pattern creating compounds indicating correctness or straightness.

### Examples
- **ορθογραφία** (orthografía) "orthography, correct spelling"
- **ορθοδοξία** (orthodoxía) "orthodoxy, correct belief"

### Notes
The prefix ορθο- is highly productive, particularly in technical, religious, and educational domains. Creates compounds indicating both physical straightness and abstract correctness. Documented in P1b.

---

## 22. INVALIDATION/CANCELLATION

### Pattern
**ακυρώνω + OBJECT** (invalidate + object)
Causative verb construction expressing official invalidation or cancellation.

### Examples
- **ακυρώνω το έγγραφο** (akyróno to éngrafo) "I invalidate the document"
- **ακυρώνω την απόφαση** (akyróno tin apófasi) "I cancel the decision"

### Notes
The causative verb ακυρώνω (from άκυρος "invalid") is the standard construction for expressing invalidation in legal and administrative contexts. Highly productive with institutional objects.

---

## 23. CERTIFICATION/CONFIRMATION

### Pattern
**βεβαιώνω + OBJECT/CLAUSE** (certify + object/clause)
Causative verb construction expressing official certification or confirmation.

### Examples
- **βεβαιώνω την ταυτότητα** (veveóno tin taftótita) "I certify the identity"
- **βεβαιώνω ότι είναι αληθές** (veveóno óti íne alithés) "I certify that it is true"

### Notes
The causative verb βεβαιώνω (from βέβαιος "certain") expresses official certification. Common in administrative and legal contexts. Can take both nominal objects and clausal complements.

---

## 24. ADVERBIAL AFFIRMATION

### Pattern
**TRUTH-ADV** (standalone adverb as affirmative response)
Truth-domain adverbs function as complete affirmative responses.

### Examples
- **ακριβώς!** (akrivós!) "exactly!"
- **βέβαια!** (vévea!) "certainly! of course!"

### Notes
Greek productively uses truth-domain adverbs (ακριβώς, βέβαια, σωστά) as standalone affirmative responses. These function as complete utterances expressing agreement or confirmation.

---

## 25. FABRICATION ACCUSATION

### Pattern
**κατασκευάζω + ACCUSATION-OBJECT** (fabricate + accusation-object)
Construction expressing creation of false evidence or charges.

### Examples
- **κατασκευάζει στοιχεία** (kataskevázi stichía) "he/she fabricates evidence"
- **κατασκεύασαν κατηγορίες** (kataskéfasan katigoríes) "they fabricated charges"

### Notes
The verb κατασκευάζω "construct/fabricate" is used specifically for creating false evidence or accusations. Common in legal contexts and accusations of frame-ups. The participle κατασκευασμένος functions as adjective.

---

## NOTES ON ABSENT CONSTRUCTIONS

**No common expression exists for:**

1. **Degree modification of core truth terms**: Greek does not commonly use degree modifiers (*"very true," *"somewhat false") with core truth vocabulary. Instead, it uses different lexical items (απόλυτη αλήθεια "absolute truth") or emphatic constructions.

2. **Comparative truth constructions**: No standard construction for comparing truth-values (*"more true than"). Greek uses alternative strategies (πιο σωστό "more correct" works for correctness but not for truth per se).

3. **Evidential marking on truth terms**: Greek does not grammatically mark evidentiality on truth vocabulary. Source of knowledge is expressed through separate constructions, not integrated into truth-marking.

4. **Habitual truth-telling constructions**: No specialized aspectual construction for habitual truth-telling distinct from general habitual aspect marking.

5. **Single-word causative for "make true"**: While ακυρώνω "invalidate" exists, there is no parallel *αληθώνω "make true" or *σωστώνω "make correct." Causation is expressed analytically (κάνω σωστό).
