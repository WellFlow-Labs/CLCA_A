# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a_CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Swahili (sw)

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Copular construction: **NP ni PRED** (NP is PRED) where PRED is a truth-domain adjective or noun. Negation uses **si** in place of **ni**.

### Examples
- Ni kweli → ni kweli → "It's true"
- Habari hii si sahihi → habari hii si sahihi → "This news is not correct"

### Notes
Standard copular pattern with invariant copula **ni** (affirmative) or **si** (negative). Truth predicates function as complements without agreement marking.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Same copular structure as affirmative but with negative copula **si**: **NP si PRED**

### Examples
- Si kweli kabisa → si kweli kabisa → "Not true at all"
- Jibu lako si sahihi → jibu lako si sahihi → "Your answer is not correct"

### Notes
Negation expressed through copula replacement, not through verbal negation morphology. Intensifiers like **kabisa** follow the predicate.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Two main patterns: (a) Associative construction **N -a TRUTH-NOUN** with agreement on associative marker; (b) Direct adjectival modification **N ADJ** with agreement.

### Examples
- Rafiki wa kweli → rafiki wa kweli → "true friend" (associative)
- Jibu sahihi → jibu sahihi → "correct answer" (direct)

### Notes
Associative pattern (-a) highly productive with abstract nouns (kweli, uongo, haki). Direct modification used with Arabic-derived adjectives (sahihi, halisi, kamili) which do not inflect for noun class.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Stative verb **kuwa** + truth predicate: **kuwa PRED**. For process of becoming corrupt/rotten, root verb forms exist.

### Examples
- Kuwa kweli → kuwa kweli → "to become true, to turn out to be true"
- Kubovu → kubovu → "to rot, to become corrupt"

### Notes
General inchoative uses **kuwa** + predicate. Specific roots like **-bovu** have inherent stative/inchoative semantics without requiring **kuwa**.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Causative suffix **-ish-** or **-iz-** added to roots: **ROOT-ish-a**. Highly productive for verification and validation actions.

### Examples
- Hakikisha → hakik-isha → "to make certain, to ensure, to verify"
- Thibitisha → thibitish-a → "to confirm, to verify, to validate"

### Notes
Causative derivation productive with certainty/verification roots. Forms like **hakikisha** (cause to be certain), **kamilisha** (cause to be complete), **batilisha** (cause to be invalid) are common.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Prefix **u-** (noun class 11/14) + root: **u-ROOT**. Highly productive pattern deriving abstract nouns from adjectives and some verbs.

### Examples
- Ukweli → u-kweli → "truth, reality, fact"
- Usahihi → u-sahihi → "correctness, accuracy, validity"

### Notes
Most productive derivational pattern in truth domain. Forms abstract nouns denoting qualities or states: **uongo** (falsehood), **uhalisi** (genuineness), **uadilifu** (honesty), **uhakika** (certainty), **ubovu** (corruption).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Prefix **m-** (noun class 1) + adjectival form in **-ifu**: **m-ROOT-ifu**. Limited productivity in truth domain.

### Examples
- Mdanganyifu → m-dangany-ifu → "deceiver, liar, fraud"
- Mwenye kweli → m-enye kweli → "truthful person, one who has truth"

### Notes
Direct agentive derivation limited. More common pattern uses possessive **mwenye** + truth noun. No productive "truth-teller" agentive form; expressed periphrastically as **mtu wa kweli** (person of truth) or **mwenye kweli** (one having truth).

---

## 8. COMPLEMENTIZER CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Copular predicate + complementizer **kwamba** + clause: **ni PRED kwamba S**

### Examples
- Ni kweli kwamba alifika → ni kweli kwamba a-li-fika → "It's true that he arrived"
- Ni dhahiri kwamba hawajui → ni dhahiri kwamba ha-wa-jui → "It's evident that they don't know"

### Notes
Standard pattern for propositional truth attribution. Complementizer **kwamba** introduces finite clause. Highly productive with truth predicates: **ni hakika kwamba** (it's certain that), **haki ni kwamba** (the truth is that).

---

## 9. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Prepositional phrase with **kwa** + abstract noun: **kwa N**. Functions as manner adverbial.

### Examples
- Kwa kweli → kwa kweli → "truly, really, in truth"
- Kwa hakika → kwa hakika → "certainly, surely, definitely"

### Notes
Highly productive pattern. Forms adverbials from abstract nouns: **kwa usahihi** (accurately), **kwa haki** (justly), **kwa uadilifu** (honestly). Very common in discourse for emphasizing manner or sincerity.

---

## 10. VERBAL OBJECT CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Verb + truth-domain noun as direct object: **V + TRUTH-NOUN**. Productive with speech verbs and epistemic verbs.

### Examples
- Kusema kweli → kusema kweli → "to tell the truth, to speak truthfully"
- Tafuta ukweli → tafuta ukweli → "to seek the truth"

### Notes
Highly productive with verbs of speech (**sema, ambia, ongea**), cognition (**jua, elewa, tambua**), and action (**tafuta, gundua, ficha, pinga**). Object nouns typically **kweli** or **ukweli** for truth, **uongo** for lies, **haki** for rights/justice.

---

## 11. INTENSIFICATION

### Pattern
Truth predicate + intensifier **kabisa**: **PRED kabisa**. Also reduplication for emphasis: **PRED PRED**.

### Examples
- Kweli kabisa → kweli kabisa → "absolutely true, completely true"
- Kweli kweli → kweli kweli → "really truly, very true"

### Notes
**Kabisa** (completely, absolutely) follows predicate for intensification. Reduplication of **kweli** and **sawa** common in informal speech for emphatic affirmation. Both patterns highly productive.

---

## 12. POSSESSIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Possessive construction with **-enye** (having): **N-enye TRUTH-NOUN** where N- agrees with subject class.

### Examples
- Mwenye haki → m-enye haki → "one who is right, rightful person"
- Wale wenye uongo → wale wenye uongo → "those who lie, liars"

### Notes
**-enye** takes noun class agreement prefix. Productive pattern for identifying persons characterized by truth-domain qualities. Common with **haki** (right/justice), **kweli** (truth), **uongo** (lies), **busara** (wisdom).

---

## 13. RIGHTS AND ENTITLEMENTS

### Pattern
**haki ya/za + NOUN/INFINITIVE**: possessive construction marking specific rights or entitlements.

### Examples
- Haki za binadamu → haki za binadamu → "human rights"
- Haki ya kura → haki ya kura → "right to vote, voting right"

### Notes
Highly productive domain-specific pattern. **Haki** takes plural **za** for general rights or singular **ya** for specific rights. Common with infinitives: **haki ya kumiliki** (right to own), **haki ya kujieleza** (right to express oneself).

---

## 14. EVIDENTIAL/OBVIOUSNESS MARKING

### Pattern
Relative clause construction marking evident truth: **ukweli u-lio wazi** (truth which-is open/clear).

### Examples
- Ukweli ulio wazi → ukweli u-lio wazi → "obvious truth, clear truth"
- Ukweli usio na shaka → ukweli u-sio na shaka → "undoubted truth, certain truth"

### Notes
Uses relative marker **-lio/-sio** with agreement. Pattern marks epistemic status of truth claims. **Wazi** (open/clear) indicates obviousness; **bila/si na shaka** (without doubt) indicates certainty.

---

## 15. DOCUMENT VALIDATION

### Pattern
**N sahihi** where N is document-related noun: direct adjectival modification marking official validity.

### Examples
- Nakala sahihi → nakala sahihi → "certified copy, authentic copy"
- Hati sahihi → hati sahihi → "valid document, authentic document"

### Notes
Specialized administrative/legal pattern. **Sahihi** modifies document nouns without class agreement (Arabic loan). Common with **saini** (signature), **chapa** (stamp), **uthibitisho** (verification), **nakala** (copy).

---

## 16. MODAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Modal auxiliary + copula + truth predicate: **MODAL ni PRED**

### Examples
- Lazima ni kweli → lazima ni kweli → "It must be true"
- Inawezekana ni kweli → i-na-wezekana ni kweli → "It's possible it's true, it may be true"

### Notes
General modal pattern not specific to truth domain. Common modals: **lazima** (must), **inawezekana** (it's possible), **labda** (perhaps). Modals precede copular construction.

---

## 17. PROVERBS AND FIXED EXPRESSIONS

### Pattern
Fixed formulaic expressions with truth vocabulary, often metaphorical.

### Examples
- Ukweli ni uchungu → ukweli ni uchungu → "Truth is bitter" (proverb)
- Ukweli hauna miguu → ukweli ha-u-na miguu → "Truth has no legs" (proverb: truth doesn't run away)

### Notes
Culturally specific fixed expressions. Common structure: **ukweli + copula/verb + metaphorical predicate**. Used in moral instruction and philosophical discourse. Not productive patterns.

---

## 18. CAUSAL/REASON CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**kwa sababu + truth predicate**: causal clause with truth as reason.

### Examples
- Kwa sababu ni kweli → kwa sababu ni kweli → "because it's true"
- Kwa sababu ya ukweli → kwa sababu ya ukweli → "because of the truth"

### Notes
General causal pattern using **kwa sababu** (because) or **kwa sababu ya** (because of). Truth predicates function as reasons in justifications and explanations.

---

## 19. CONCESSIVE CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
**ingawa/hata kama + truth predicate**: concessive clause acknowledging truth while contrasting.

### Examples
- Ingawa ni kweli → ingawa ni kweli → "although it's true"
- Hata kama ni kweli → hata kama ni kweli → "even if it's true"

### Notes
General concessive pattern not specific to truth domain. **Ingawa** (although) introduces factual concession; **hata kama** (even if) introduces hypothetical concession. Common in argumentative discourse.

---

## 20. RECIPROCAL CONSTRUCTIONS

### Pattern
Verb root + reciprocal suffix **-ana**: **ROOT-ana**. Limited productivity in truth domain.

### Examples
- Hojiana → hoji-ana → "to argue with each other, to debate"

### Notes
Reciprocal derivation limited to argument/debate vocabulary. **Hojiana** (from **hoja** "argument") common for mutual argumentation. No productive reciprocal forms for truth-telling or verification actions.
