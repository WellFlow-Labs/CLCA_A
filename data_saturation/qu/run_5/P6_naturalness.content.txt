# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
# Language: Quechua (Southern, Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents dynamic expressions — encoding change of state, causation of a state, and stability of a state — involving the truth-related vocabulary established in P1 through P5. All forms and constructions are drawn exclusively from the CONTEXT. Where a dynamic expression is not attested for a given root, this is stated explicitly. Roots are treated separately where their behavior differs. The asymmetry in the *cheqa* root's verbal morphology (noted in P2a, P4a, and P4b) is preserved and reported where relevant.

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / BECOMING CORRECT

### Pattern
[Root] + *-ya-* + *-y* — inchoative verbalizer *-ya-* derives a change-of-state verb from an adjectival base.

### Attested form
- *allinyay* → allin-ya-y → "to become good / to become right / to improve"

### Notes
The *-ya-* inchoative is attested for *allin* (P2a: D-allin-03; P4a: Construction 4; P4b). This form covers the change from a wrong or bad state to a correct or good one. It is rated [CORE] in P4b.

The *cheqa* root does **not** attest a direct *cheqayay* form. P4a (Construction 4) and P4b explicitly note: "the *cheqa* root does not attest a direct *cheqayay* inchoative form; inchoative meaning for that root is expressed through *cheqanchay* or periphrasis." No inchoative change-of-state form meaning "to become true" is attested for *cheqa* in the CONTEXT.

The *llulla* root attests the opposite direction (see §2 below). The *panta-* root does not attest a *-ya-* inchoative in the CONTEXT.

**If NO COMMON EXPRESSION EXISTS:** No common single-word expression meaning "to become true" built on the *cheqa* root is attested in the CONTEXT.

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / BECOMING DECEPTIVE

### Pattern
[Root] + *-ya-* + *-y* — inchoative verbalizer *-ya-* derives a change-of-state verb from an adjectival base.

### Attested form
- *llullayay* → llulla-ya-y → "to become false / to turn deceptive / to become a lie"

### Notes
Attested in P1b (Root 2, item 11) and P2a (D-llulla-04), rated [MARGINAL] in both. P1b describes the context of use as "a process by which something that was true or genuine becomes false or corrupted," including deterioration of testimony, documents, or character. The form is structurally parallel to *allinyay* but is less frequently attested, hence the [MARGINAL] rating.

No parallel *cheqayay* ("to become true") is attested, making the inchoative pattern asymmetric across the primary antonym pair: the change *toward* falsity (*llullayay*) is lexicalized; the change *toward* truth is not directly lexicalized on the *cheqa* root.

---

## 3. BECOMING MISTAKEN / GOING WRONG

### Pattern
[Verbal stem] + *-ri-* (inceptive/punctual aspect) + *-sqa* (past participle) — marks the onset of an error at a specific point.

### Attested form
- *pantarisqa* → panta-ri-sqa → "having suddenly erred / having gone wrong at a specific moment"

### Notes
Attested in P1b (Root 3, item 12) and P2a (D-panta-05), rated [MARGINAL] in both. P1b describes this as "aspectually marked, indicating that an error occurred at a specific point or suddenly," appearing in narrative contexts. The *-ri-* inceptive suffix marks the onset of the error state rather than a gradual change, distinguishing it from the *-ya-* inchoative pattern.

The *pantakuy* form (P2a: D-panta-04; rated [CORE]) — "to err, to go wrong (intransitive)" — also encodes the process of going wrong, though without the punctual aspectual marking of *pantarisqa*.

---

## 4. CAUSING SOMEONE TO ERR / MISLEADING

### Pattern
[Verbal stem] + *-chi-* + *-y* — causative suffix *-chi-* derives a transitive causative verb; the agent causes another person to enter an error state.

### Attested forms
- *pantachiy* → panta-chi-y → "to cause to err / to mislead / to lead astray"
- *pantachiwankim* → pantachi-wa-nki-m → "you are misleading me / you are causing me to err"

### Notes
*Pantachiy* is attested in P1b (Root 3, item 4), P2a (D-panta-03, rated [CORE]), P2b (C-pantachiy-01), P3 (Groups 7 and 10), and P4a (Construction 5). P4b rates it [CORE]. The inflected form *pantachiwankim* is attested in P2b (C-pantachiy-01) with the first-person object suffix *-wa-* and direct evidential *-m*, used to accuse an interlocutor of deliberate misleading.

P3 notes that *pantachiy* introduces "an element of agency and potential deliberateness, which brings it closer to the *llulla* domain," while remaining primarily in the error domain (Group 7). P3 also flags it as an overlapping item between Groups 7 and 10 (legal contexts).

---

## 5. CAUSING SOMEONE TO LIE / INDUCING FALSE TESTIMONY

### Pattern
[Root] + *-chi-* + *-y* — causative suffix *-chi-* derives a transitive causative verb; the agent causes another person to produce false speech.

### Attested forms
- *llullachiy* → llulla-chi-y → "to cause someone to lie / to induce deception / to make someone give false testimony"

### Notes
Attested in P1b (Root 2, item 14), P2a (D-llulla-03, rated [MARGINAL]), and P3 (Groups 6 and 10, Ambiguous Items). P1b describes its context as "legal contexts evaluating whether a witness has been induced to give false testimony." The [MARGINAL] rating in P2a reflects limited attestation rather than structural irregularity.

This form is structurally parallel to *pantachiy* (§4 above) but targets the *llulla* domain (deliberate deception) rather than the *panta-* domain (inadvertent error). P3 (Ambiguous Items) notes that *llullachiy* "evaluates the character of the person who induces lying (Group 6) and appears specifically in legal contexts of evaluating induced false testimony (Group 10)."

---

## 6. CAUSING ONESELF TO ERR / SELF-DIRECTED MISLEADING

### Pattern
[Causative stem] + *-ku-* + *-y* — reflexive/middle suffix *-ku-* applied to the causative stem, producing a self-directed causative.

### Attested form
- *pantachikuy* → pantachi-ku-y → "to mislead oneself / to cause oneself to err / to go wrong on one's own account"

### Notes
Attested in P1b (Root 3, item 5) and P2a (D-panta-07), rated [MARGINAL] in both. P1b describes it as "used when the subject causes themselves to err or when the misleading is self-directed." This form occupies a structural position between the causative (*pantachiy*, §4) and the simple reflexive/intransitive (*pantakuy*, §3), encoding a self-directed causative of error.

No parallel *llullachikuy* ("to cause oneself to lie") is attested in the CONTEXT.

---

## 7. CAUSING SOMETHING TO BE ESTABLISHED AS TRUE / VERIFYING

### Pattern
[Root] + *-ncha-* (causative/verbalizer) + *-y* — the *-ncha-* verbalizer derives a transitive verb from the *cheqa* root meaning "to make true / to verify / to establish as true."

### Attested forms
- *cheqanchay* → cheqa-ncha-y → "to verify / to confirm / to correct / to establish as true"
- *cheqanchakuy* → cheqancha-ku-y → "to verify for oneself / to confirm (reflexive/intransitive) / to make sure"

### Notes
*Cheqanchay* is attested in P1 (item 27), P1b (Root 1, item 8), P2a (D-cheqa-04, D-cheqanchay-01, D-cheqanchay-02), P2b (C-cheqanchay-01, C-cheqanchay-02), P3 (Groups 8 and 10), and P4a (Construction 5). It is rated [CORE] throughout.

P4a (Construction 5) and P4b explicitly note the asymmetry: "the *cheqa* root uses a distinct verbalizer *-ncha-* (*cheqanchay*) rather than bare *-chi-*, reflecting an asymmetry in how the root forms a verbal stem." This means that while *pantachiy* and *llullachiy* use the standard *-chi-* causative, the causative/verbalizing derivation from *cheqa* runs through *-ncha-* instead.

*Cheqanchakuy* (reflexive/middle of *cheqanchay*) is attested in P1b (Root 1, item 10) and P2a (D-cheqanchay-02), rated [CORE]. P1b describes it as used "when the subject verifies or confirms something for their own benefit or in a self-directed manner," parallel in structure to *pantachikuy* (§6) but in the truth-establishment domain.

P3 (Group 8) notes that *cheqanchay* and *allinchay* are "action-oriented (the act of correcting or verifying)" within the correction domain.

## 8. MAKING SOMETHING GOOD / CORRECTING

### Pattern
[Root] + *-cha-* (causative/verbalizer) + *-y* — the *-cha-* verbalizer derives a transitive verb from the *allin* root meaning "to make good / to correct / to put right."

### Attested forms
- *allinchay* → allin-cha-y → "to make good / to correct / to fix / to improve / to put right"
- *allinchasqa* → allincha-sqa → "corrected / fixed / made right / improved" (resultant state)

### Notes
*Allinchay* is attested in P2a (D-allin-04, rated [CORE]) and P3 (Group 8). *Allinchasqa* is attested in P2a (D-allin-05, rated [CORE]). P3 (Group 8) groups *allinchay* with *cheqanchay* as "action-oriented" correction expressions, distinguishing them from the discourse-level corrective marker *cheqaqa*.

The *-cha-* verbalizer on *allin* is structurally parallel to the *-ncha-* verbalizer on *cheqa*, both producing transitive verbs meaning "to cause [root state]." P4a (Construction 5) notes this parallel in the context of the *cheqa* root's asymmetric behavior.

---

## 9. RESULTING STATE: VERIFIED / CONFIRMED / CORRECTED

### Pattern
[Causative/verbalizing stem] + *-sqa* (past participle/resultative) — the resultative participle encodes the state resulting from the causative action.

### Attested forms
- *cheqanchasqa* → cheqancha-sqa → "verified / confirmed / corrected / established as true"
- *cheqanchasqam* → cheqancha-sqa-m → "it has been verified / it is confirmed" (predicative with direct evidential)
- *allinchasqa* → allincha-sqa → "corrected / fixed / made right"
- *pantasqa* → panta-sqa → "mistaken / wrong / erroneous / in error" (resultant state of having erred)
- *llullasqa* → llulla-sqa → "deceived / lied to / falsified / having been misled"
- *pantarisqa* → panta-ri-sqa → "having erred at a specific moment" (punctual resultant)

### Notes
The *-sqa* resultative participle is attested across all three primary roots (*cheqa-*, *panta-*, *llulla-*) and for *allin*, producing adjectival forms encoding the state that results from the relevant dynamic process. P2a documents all these forms. P4a (Construction 3) notes that *-sqa* participial forms function as adnominal modifiers in the same structural position as simple adjectives.

*Cheqanchasqa* and *cheqanchasqam* are specifically attested in legal and administrative contexts (P2b: C-cheqanchay-02; P3: Groups 8 and 10) where the resultant state of official verification is relevant.

*Llullasqa* (P2a: D-llullakuy-01) is notable in that it encodes the state of the *object* of deception (having been deceived or misled) rather than the state of the deceiver, distinguishing it from *llullaq* (the agent who deceives).

## 10. STABILITY: EMPHATIC ASSERTION OF UNCHANGING TRUTH

### Pattern
[Truth predicate] + *-puni* (emphatic/intensifying suffix, "definitely, always, without exception") — the *-puni* suffix asserts that the truth state holds without exception or doubt.

### Attested forms
- *cheqaqpuni* → cheqaq-puni → "absolutely true / definitely genuine / truly and certainly so"
- *allinpuni* → allin-puni → "definitely good / absolutely right / certainly proper"

### Notes
*Cheqaqpuni* is attested in P1b (Root 1, item 29), P2a (D-cheqaq-04, rated [CORE]), and P5 (Opposition 27). P1b describes it as used "in contexts of strong affirmation or in response to skepticism," and P5 documents it as the opposite pole from *cheqaqchus* (doubt) on the certainty scale. *Allinpuni* is attested in P2a (D-allin-06, rated [CORE]).

These forms encode not a change of state but the *resistance to change* — the *-puni* suffix rules out the possibility that the truth state could be otherwise. P5 (Opposition 27) documents the contrast between *cheqaqpuni* and *cheqaqchus* as a morphological opposition between certainty and doubt on the same predicate.

---

## 11. STABILITY: UNERRING / NOT SUBJECT TO ERROR

### Pattern
*mana* + [agentive error form] — negation of the agentive form produces a characterization of a person or source as not subject to error.

### Attested forms
- *mana pantaq* → mana panta-q → "unerring / one who does not make mistakes / infallible"
- *mana pantasqa* → mana panta-sqa → "not mistaken / without error / correct / unerring"

### Notes
Both forms are attested in P2a (D-pantaq-01, D-pantasqa-01, both rated [CORE]) and P3 (Groups 6 and 7). P2a notes that *mana pantaq* "appears in religious contexts applied to divine authority and in everyday evaluation of trustworthy persons." P5 (Oppositions 21 and 22) documents both as the negative poles of their respective oppositions with *pantaq* and *pantasqa*.

These forms encode a stable state of freedom from error — not a change of state but the characterization of an agent or source as constitutively not subject to the error process. P3 (Ambiguous Items) notes that *mana pantaq* in religious contexts functions as a claim of infallibility, which is a stronger stability claim than the everyday usage.

---

## 12. REDUPLICATION FOR EMPHATIC DYNAMIC ASSERTION

### Pattern
[Root] + [Root] (reduplication) — reduplicated form used for emphatic assertion of a state, implying thoroughness or completeness.

### Attested forms
- *cheqa cheqa* → cheqa cheqa → "truly true / absolutely genuine / really real / the very truth"
- *pantay pantay* → pantay pantay → "full of errors / very wrong / repeatedly mistaken / all wrong"

### Notes
*Cheqa cheqa* is attested in P1b (Root 1, item 30, rated [CORE]). P1b describes it as used "for emphatic affirmation that something is genuinely and completely true or real." *Pantay pantay* is attested in P1b (Root 3, item 8, rated [MARGINAL]). P1b describes it as used "for emphatic condemnation of errors."

These reduplicated forms do not encode change of state but rather assert the *completeness or thoroughness* of a state — the state holds fully, repeatedly, or without qualification. They are included here as dynamic in the sense that they intensify the assertion of a state beyond its simple predication.

No reduplicated form of *llulla* (*llulla llulla*) is attested in the CONTEXT.

## Summary of Dynamic Patterns Observed

The following observations are directly supported by the forms documented above. No interpretive claims are made beyond what the data support.

**1. Asymmetry in inchoative formation:** The *-ya-* inchoative is attested for *llulla* (*llullayay*, "to become false") and *allin* (*allinyay*, "to become good/right") but not for *cheqa*. The change *toward* falsity is lexicalized; the change *toward* truth on the *cheqa* root is not directly lexicalized through this pattern. This asymmetry is preserved from P4a and P4b.

**2. Asymmetry in causative formation:** The *-chi-* causative is attested for *panta-* (*pantachiy*) and *llulla-* (*llullachiy*) but not for *cheqa*, which uses *-ncha-* (*cheqanchay*) instead. The causative of truth-establishment runs through a different verbalizer than the causatives of error-induction and deception-induction. This asymmetry is preserved from P2a, P4a, and P4b.

**3. Reflexive/middle forms across causative and non-causative stems:** The *-ku-* reflexive/middle is attested for both simple verbal stems (*pantakuy*, *llullakuy*, *cheqanchakuy*) and causative stems (*pantachikuy*), producing self-directed variants of both the basic action and the causative action. This pattern is consistent across the domain.

**4. Resultative *-sqa* across all roots:** The *-sqa* resultative participle is attested across all primary roots (*cheqanchasqa*, *pantasqa*, *llullasqa*, *allinchasqa*), encoding the state resulting from the relevant dynamic process. This is the most consistent dynamic pattern across the domain.

**5. Stability encoded through *-puni* and *mana* + agentive:** Two distinct strategies encode stability of a truth-related state: (a) the emphatic suffix *-puni* on truth predicates (*cheqaqpuni*, *allinpuni*), asserting that the state holds without exception; (b) negation of the error-agentive (*mana pantaq*, *mana pantasqa*), characterizing a person or source as constitutively free from error.

**6. No dynamic expressions attested for *tiqsi*, *rura*, *kallpachakuy*, or *ima kay*:** Consistent with their [MARGINAL] or [UNCERTAIN] status throughout the CONTEXT, none of these items participate in the dynamic patterns documented here.
