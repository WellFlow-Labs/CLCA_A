# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope:** Quechua is a dialect continuum with significant lexical variation across varieties (Cusco-Collao, Ayacucho, Ecuadorian, etc.). Items below are drawn primarily from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka), the most extensively documented varieties. Variety-specific distribution is noted where relevant. Transliteration follows the standard three-vowel orthography used in academic Quechua linguistics (as in Cerrón-Palomino and related scholarship), with digraphs for ejectives (p', t', k', q', ch') and aspirates (ph, th, kh, qh, chh), consistent with the normalized orthography established by the Peruvian Ministry of Education for Southern Quechua.

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; no further decomposition established; "true, real, correct, straight"
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, correct, right

Used as an adjective or adverb to predicate truth or correctness of statements, and genuineness or authenticity of persons and things. Appears in constructions such as *cheqam* (it is true, with evidential suffix *-m/-mi*) and *cheqa runa* (a genuine/upright person). Used to predicate truth or genuineness of a statement, person, or thing. Functions as both adjective and predicate nominal. Participates in extensive derivation: *cheqakay* (to become true/real), *cheqanchay* / *cheqachiy* (to verify, to confirm). It is among the most frequently cited and documented lexical items in Quechua semantic studies, attested across Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties.

**2.**
**Form:** cheqam / cheqami
**Transliteration:** cheqam / cheqami
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + validating evidential clitic *-m/-mi*; "it is true (I assert this directly)"
**Typical English translations:** it is true, indeed, certainly, that is correct

A fixed assertive expression used to affirm the truth of a proposition with direct evidential commitment from the speaker. The evidential clitic *-mi* marks that the speaker has direct knowledge of what is asserted. Commonly used in discourse to confirm or validate a prior statement.

**3.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative/verbalizer) + -y (infinitive nominalizer)
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to establish as true, to correct

Used for the act of verifying or confirming the accuracy or correctness of something. Appears in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts where a claim or document is checked against reality.

**4.**
**Form:** cheqachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqachiy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y*; "to cause to be true/correct, to verify"
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct

Used in contexts of verification, confirmation, or correction of a statement or situation. Can refer to the act of establishing that something is accurate or to correcting an error. Attested in legal and administrative Quechua contexts as well as everyday speech.

**5.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + -q (agentive/adjectival nominalizer); "one who is true, that which is true/real"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real, authentic, honest

Functions as an attributive adjective meaning 'true' or 'genuine', and can nominalize to mean 'one who is truthful'. Used to describe persons of integrity and statements that are factually accurate. Frequently used in moral evaluation of persons and in assertions about the truth of propositions. Attested in both Cusco and Ayacucho varieties.

**[CORE]**

**6.**
**Form:** cheqaqta
**Transliteration:** cheqaqta
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqaq + *-ta* (accusative/adverbial case); "truly, in truth, genuinely"
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, honestly, in truth

*Cheqaqta* is the adverbial form used to assert that something is genuinely the case or to emphasize the sincerity or accuracy of a statement. It commonly appears in discourse as an evidential-like reinforcer of a speaker's commitment to the truth of what they are saying. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.

**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to prove true, to authenticate

Used in contexts of verification or confirmation — establishing that something is indeed the case or that a claim is accurate. It appears in legal and testimonial contexts as well as in everyday speech when a speaker confirms a prior assertion. Attested in Southern Quechua documentation.

Describes the process by which something or someone is verified or proves to be genuine. Used in contexts where a claim or person's character is tested and found to be authentic. Less frequently attested than *cheqaqchay* but documented in Southern Quechua sources.

**11.**

**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa-n-ta (truth-POSS-ACC) + rimay (to speak); "to speak the truth"
**Typical English translations:** to tell the truth, to speak truthfully

A conventionalized verbal expression used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to enjoin or describe truthful speech. Appears in testimony contexts and in moral instruction. Functions as a fixed collocation rather than a free compositional phrase.

**12.**
**Form:** chiqap
**Transliteration:** chiqap
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root or derived form; possibly related to *cheqa* by dialectal variation, or variant/related form of *chiqan*; "true, real, genuine, exact, correct, right, just"
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in truth, true, real, genuine, exact, correct, right, just

Attested in Ayacucho-Chanka Quechua and some Southern Quechua sources as a near-synonym of *cheqa* with possible emphasis on exactness or precision, and in some sources as a form used in evaluative contexts of correctness and exactness, sometimes in legal and moral discourse. Functions primarily as an adverb or intensifier meaning 'truly' or 'really', used to assert the genuine nature of something. The relationship between *chiqap* and *cheqa*, and between *chiqap* and *chiqan*, and its precise distribution across varieties, is not fully established in available documentation.

**15.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "false, lying, deceptive"
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, liar, deceptive, fake, untrue

The primary term for falsehood and deception, used as both noun ('a lie') and adjective ('false, deceptive') to characterize statements, persons, or representations as dishonest or counterfeit. Used of persons (a liar), statements (a false claim), and objects (a fake or counterfeit). Contrasts directly with *cheqa*. Productive: *llullakuy* (to lie, to deceive oneself or others), *llullaq* (liar). Widely attested across Southern Quechua varieties.

**16.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** llulla + -ku- / *-kuy* (reflexive/middle) + -y (infinitive); "to lie, to deceive"
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods, to be dishonest

The standard verb for the act of lying or deceiving. Used in moral condemnation of dishonest speech and in legal contexts where false statements are at issue. The reflexive suffix *-ku* marks the action as self-directed or internally motivated rather than purely causative. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.

**22.**
**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; "good, well, correct, proper"
**Typical English translations:** good, right, well, proper, fine, correct

Broadly used to evaluate persons, objects, conduct, and states as good, correct, or proper. One of the most productive roots in Quechua moral and evaluative vocabulary. Used in moral evaluation of conduct and persons, and in assessments of whether something is done correctly or is in proper order. Participates in derivation: *allinchay* 'to improve, to fix, to make right', *allinkay* 'to become good/well'. One of the most frequently attested lexical items in Quechua.

Used for the act of correcting or setting something right — a statement, an object, a situation, or conduct. Used in contexts of correcting errors, repairing objects, and rectifying conduct or situations.
