# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Turkish (tr)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving the P1 lexical items. The focus is on constructions that are not purely derivational (handled in P2a) but involve syntactic combination, periphrasis, or idiomatization. Many such constructions were already identified in P1b and P2a as collocations; where they were documented there, they are noted as cross-references rather than re-documented in full, unless the constructional pattern itself warrants separate treatment.

Constructions are organized by base form. Where a base form generates no attested multi-word constructions beyond those already documented, this is stated explicitly.

## CONSTRUCTIONS BY BASE FORM

---

### Base: **doğru**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğruyu söylemek gerekirse...
3. **Transliteration:** Doğruyu söylemek gerekirse...
4. **English translation:** To tell the truth... / If I'm being honest...
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed discourse-initial formula used to introduce a frank or potentially unwelcome statement. Functions as a hedging or sincerity marker rather than a propositional claim. Extremely common across spoken and written registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğru mu yanlış mı?
3. **Transliteration:** Doğru mu yanlış mı?
4. **English translation:** True or false? / Right or wrong?
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed interrogative construction used in educational, evaluative, and rhetorical contexts to pose a binary epistemic or moral question. The paired structure is formulaic and appears in test formats, debates, and everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Ne kadar doğru?
3. **Transliteration:** Ne kadar doğru?
4. **English translation:** How accurate is it? / How true is that?
5. **Usage notes:** A common interrogative construction used to question the degree of accuracy or truth of a claim. Used in journalistic, analytical, and everyday contexts to introduce scrutiny of a statement.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Bu doğru değil.
3. **Transliteration:** Bu doğru değil.
4. **English translation:** This is not correct. / This is not true.
5. **Usage notes:** The standard negation construction for *doğru*, using the copular negation *değil*. One of the most basic and frequent constructions in the domain. Used across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğru söylüyorsun ama...
3. **Transliteration:** Doğru söylüyorsun ama...
4. **English translation:** You're right, but... / What you're saying is true, but...
5. **Usage notes:** A concessive construction used to acknowledge the truth of an interlocutor's statement while introducing a qualification or counterpoint. The *ama* (but) marks the concessive structure. Extremely common in conversational and argumentative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğrusu şu ki...
3. **Transliteration:** Doğrusu şu ki...
4. **English translation:** The truth of the matter is that... / To be honest...
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse marker construction using the possessive form *doğrusu* (its truth / the truth of it) followed by the demonstrative-complementizer sequence *şu ki*. Used to introduce a frank or corrective statement. Common in formal and informal speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğrusu, bilmiyorum.
3. **Transliteration:** Doğrusu, bilmiyorum.
4. **English translation:** Honestly, I don't know. / To tell the truth, I don't know.
5. **Usage notes:** *Doğrusu* used as a sentence-initial adverbial meaning "honestly" or "to tell the truth," followed by a statement of uncertainty or admission. Functions as a sincerity hedge. Very common in spoken Turkish.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Tam olarak doğru değil.
3. **Transliteration:** Tam olarak doğru değil.
4. **English translation:** Not exactly correct. / Not entirely true.
5. **Usage notes:** A hedged negation construction using *tam olarak* (exactly, precisely) to soften the denial. Used when a claim is partially but not fully accurate. Common in analytical and conversational registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğru yolda olmak
3. **Transliteration:** Doğru yolda olmak
4. **English translation:** To be on the right path / To be on track
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed idiomatic construction using *doğru* in its moral/directional sense. *Doğru yol* (the right/straight path) carries both literal and figurative meanings, with strong moral and religious resonances in Turkish cultural context. Used in moral, religious, and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** doğru
2. **Example:** Doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek
3. **Transliteration:** Doğruyu yanlıştan ayırt etmek
4. **English translation:** To distinguish truth from falsehood / To tell right from wrong
5. **Usage notes:** A standard periphrastic construction for the epistemic or moral capacity to discriminate between correct and incorrect. *Ayırt etmek* (to distinguish, to discern) is the operative verb. Used in educational, philosophical, and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **yanlış**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlış anlaşılmasın.
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlış anlaşılmasın.
4. **English translation:** Don't get me wrong. / Let there be no misunderstanding.
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed formulaic construction using the negative optative of *yanlış anlaşılmak* (to be misunderstood). Used as a preemptive disclaimer before a potentially sensitive statement. Extremely common in spoken and written Turkish.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlış bir izlenim vermek
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlış bir izlenim vermek
4. **English translation:** To give a false impression / To create a misleading impression
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction describing the act of creating an inaccurate impression in another person's mind. *İzlenim vermek* (to give an impression) is the operative phrase. Used in legal, journalistic, and everyday evaluative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlışta ısrar etmek
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlışta ısrar etmek
4. **English translation:** To persist in error / To insist on being wrong
5. **Usage notes:** A construction describing the refusal to correct an acknowledged error. *Israr etmek* (to insist, to persist) combined with *yanlışta* (in the wrong/error) creates a morally charged expression. Used in moral and argumentative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlışı kabul etmek
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlışı kabul etmek
4. **English translation:** To admit one's mistake / To acknowledge the error
5. **Usage notes:** A standard construction for the act of acknowledging that one was wrong. *Kabul etmek* (to accept, to acknowledge) is the operative verb. Used in moral, interpersonal, and legal contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlış yerde olmak
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlış yerde olmak
4. **English translation:** To be in the wrong place / To be in the wrong
5. **Usage notes:** Used both literally (being in the wrong physical location) and figuratively (being on the wrong side of an issue or situation). The figurative use is common in everyday and journalistic speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** yanlış
2. **Example:** Yanlış hesap Bağdat'tan döner.
3. **Transliteration:** Yanlış hesap Bağdat'tan döner.
4. **English translation:** A wrong calculation comes back from Baghdad. (Proverb: miscalculations have consequences; you can't escape the results of your errors.)
5. **Usage notes:** A well-known Turkish proverb warning that errors in planning or judgment will eventually catch up with one. Used in everyday and proverbial discourse to caution against overconfidence or poor planning.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **gerçek**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçek şu ki...
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçek şu ki...
4. **English translation:** The truth is that... / The fact is that...
5. **Usage notes:** Already documented in P1b as a discourse marker. A very common construction used to introduce a frank or corrective statement. Not re-documented in full here.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE] *(see P1b)*

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeği kabul etmek
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeği kabul etmek
4. **English translation:** To accept the truth / To face reality
5. **Usage notes:** A standard periphrastic construction for the act of acknowledging an unwelcome truth or reality. *Kabul etmek* (to accept) is the operative verb. Used in psychological, moral, and everyday contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeğin ta kendisi
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeğin ta kendisi
4. **English translation:** The very truth itself / The absolute reality
5. **Usage notes:** A reinforced nominal construction using *ta* (very, right) and *kendisi* (itself) to emphasize that something is the genuine article or the unvarnished truth. Used for emphasis in spoken and written Turkish.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçekten uzak olmak
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçekten uzak olmak
4. **English translation:** To be far from the truth / To be divorced from reality
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *uzak olmak* (to be distant/far from) to characterize a claim or position as significantly inaccurate or unrealistic. Used in analytical and evaluative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeğe en yakın
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeğe en yakın
4. **English translation:** Closest to the truth / Nearest to reality
5. **Usage notes:** A superlative approximation construction used to identify the most accurate available account or estimate. Used in investigative, scientific, and everyday evaluative contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Acı gerçek
3. **Transliteration:** Acı gerçek
4. **English translation:** Bitter truth / Harsh reality
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed nominal collocation using *acı* (bitter, painful) to characterize a truth that is unpleasant to acknowledge. *Acı gerçeklerle yüzleşmek* (to confront bitter truths) is a common extension. Used across all registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçek dışı bir senaryo
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçek dışı bir senaryo
4. **English translation:** An unrealistic scenario / A fictitious scenario
5. **Usage notes:** A compositional construction using *gerçek dışı* as a prenominal modifier. Used in analytical, legal, and everyday contexts to characterize scenarios or claims that do not correspond to reality.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeği çarpıtmak
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeği çarpıtmak
4. **English translation:** To distort the truth / To misrepresent reality
5. **Usage notes:** Already documented in P1b. A standard construction for deliberate misrepresentation. Not re-documented in full here.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE] *(see P1b)*

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeği gün yüzüne çıkarmak
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeği gün yüzüne çıkarmak
4. **English translation:** To bring the truth to light / To expose the truth
5. **Usage notes:** An idiomatic construction using *gün yüzüne çıkarmak* (to bring to the surface of the day, i.e., to bring to light). Used in journalistic, investigative, and moral discourse to describe the revelation of hidden truths.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçek ortaya çıkmak
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçek ortaya çıkmak
4. **English translation:** The truth comes out / The truth emerges
5. **Usage notes:** A standard construction using *ortaya çıkmak* (to emerge, to come to the surface) with *gerçek* as subject. Used to describe the eventual revelation of facts. Very common in journalistic and narrative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 11**

1. **Base Form:** gerçek
2. **Example:** Gerçeği söylemek
3. **Transliteration:** Gerçeği söylemek
4. **English translation:** To tell the truth / To state the reality
5. **Usage notes:** A basic periphrastic construction parallel to *doğruyu söylemek*. While both are common, *gerçeği söylemek* tends to emphasize the factual/ontological dimension, whereas *doğruyu söylemek* may carry a stronger moral charge of truthfulness.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **sahte**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** sahte
2. **Example:** Sahte çıkmak
3. **Transliteration:** Sahte çıkmak
4. **English translation:** To turn out to be fake / To prove counterfeit
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using *çıkmak* (to come out/emerge) to describe the outcome where something is revealed to be counterfeit or false. *Ürün sahte çıktı* (the product turned out to be fake). Common in consumer, legal, and journalistic contexts.

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** sahte
2. **Example:** Sahte olduğu anlaşılmak
3. **Transliteration:** Sahte olduğu anlaşılmak
4. **English translation:** To be understood/discovered to be fake
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *olduğu anlaşılmak* (to be understood to be) to describe the discovery that something is counterfeit. More formal and explicit than *sahte çıkmak*. Used in legal and journalistic registers.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** sahte
2. **Example:** Sahte ile gerçeği ayırt etmek
3. **Transliteration:** Sahte ile gerçeği ayırt etmek
4. **English translation:** To distinguish the fake from the genuine
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction for the act of discriminating between authentic and counterfeit items. Used in consumer, legal, and analytical contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **yalan**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar.
3. **Transliteration:** Yalancının mumu yatsıya kadar yanar.
4. **English translation:** A liar's candle burns only until the evening prayer. (Proverb: lies are eventually exposed.)
5. **Usage notes:** One of the most widely known Turkish proverbs about lying. Used to warn that deception cannot be sustained indefinitely. Appears in moral, educational, and everyday discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan söylemek günah
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan söylemek günah
4. **English translation:** Lying is a sin / It is sinful to lie
5. **Usage notes:** A formulaic moral statement combining *yalan söylemek* (to lie) with *günah* (sin). Used in moral and religious instruction, particularly in addressing children. Reflects the moral charge carried by *yalan* in Turkish cultural context.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan üstüne yalan
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan üstüne yalan
4. **English translation:** Lie upon lie / One lie on top of another
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed intensifying construction using *üstüne* (on top of) to describe compounding deception. *Yalan üstüne yalan söylemek* (to tell lie upon lie). Used in moral and everyday discourse to characterize habitual or escalating dishonesty.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Beyaz yalan
3. **Transliteration:** Beyaz yalan
4. **English translation:** White lie
5. **Usage notes:** A calque from English (or parallel development) using *beyaz* (white) to describe a harmless or well-intentioned lie. Used in everyday moral discourse. The construction is widely understood and used, though some speakers consider it a borrowing of the English concept.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan dünya
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan dünya
4. **English translation:** The false/illusory world / This transient world (of lies)
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed nominal construction with philosophical and religious resonance, characterizing the material world as illusory or deceptive. Used in literary, religious, and reflective discourse. *Bu yalan dünyada* (in this false world) is a common extension.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan mı gerçek mi?
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan mı gerçek mi?
4. **English translation:** Lie or truth? / False or real?
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed interrogative construction posing the binary question of whether something is a lie or the truth. Used in everyday, journalistic, and narrative contexts to frame an epistemic question about a claim's veracity.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan söylemeden duramıyor.
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan söylemeden duramıyor.
4. **English translation:** He/she can't stop lying. / He/she is compelled to lie.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using *-meden duramamak* (to be unable to stop doing something) with *yalan söylemek* to describe compulsive lying. Used in everyday moral characterization of persons.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** yalan
2. **Example:** Yalan söylediği belli olmak
3. **Transliteration:** Yalan söylediği belli olmak
4. **English translation:** It becomes obvious that he/she is lying / His/her lie becomes apparent
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction using *belli olmak* (to become evident/obvious) to describe the detection of a lie. Used in everyday and narrative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Base: **asılsız**

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** asılsız
2. **Example:** Asılsız olduğu kanıtlanmak
3. **Transliteration:** Asılsız olduğu kanıtlanmak
4. **English translation:** To be proven to be unfounded / To be demonstrated to be baseless
5. **Usage notes:** A periphrastic construction used in legal and journalistic contexts to describe the formal demonstration that a claim lacks any factual basis. *İddia asılsız olduğu kanıtlandı* (the allegation was proven to be unfounded).
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** asılsız
2. **Example:** Asılsız olduğunu öne sürmek
3. **Transliteration:** Asılsız olduğunu öne sürmek
4. **English translation:** To claim that (something) is unfounded / To allege groundlessness
5. **Usage notes:** A construction using *öne sürmek* (to put forward, to allege) to describe the act of asserting that a claim is baseless. Used in legal and argumentative discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: asılsız does not appear to participate in a wide range of multi-word constructions beyond its primary collocation asılsız iddia (documented in P2a) and the periphrastic constructions above. Its constructional range is narrower than that of doğru, yanlış, or gerçek.*

### Base: **asıl**

**Construction 3**

3. **Transliteration:** Asıl olan şu...
4. **English translation:** What really matters is... / The essential thing is...

**Construction 1**

4. **English translation:** Its originality is debatable / Whether it is original is disputed

---

**Construction 2**

2. **Example:** Özgün bir ses olmak
3. **Transliteration:** Özgün bir ses olmak

*Note: özgün participates in fewer multi-word constructions than the truth-evaluation terms. Its primary constructional environment is as a prenominal modifier (özgün eser, özgün yaklaşım, etc.) rather than in periphrastic or idiomatic constructions.*

3. **Transliteration:** Geçerli bir neden olmak
