## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Sanskrit shows clear direct constructions for attribution, adnominal modification, negation, and nominalization. These are the most stable parts of the truth-domain inventory in the supplied material.

For **BECOME**, the language does not use one dedicated inchoative marker. Instead, the operator is distributed across light verbs and semantically compatible predicates:
- **सत्यतां गच्छति**
- **प्रमा जायते**
- also noted in P4a: **bhavati / jāyate / gacchati**

So the “become” meaning is available, but it is carried by general verbs such as **gacchati**, **bhavati**, and **jāyate**, not by a single specialized truth-domain construction.

For **REVELATION**, no construction is provided in P4a or P4b. On the supplied evidence, there is no attested revelation operator here, and there is no basis for saying that revelation replaces becoming. Therefore:
- REVELATION: **MISSING (structural gap)**
- REVELATION does **not** demonstrably substitute for BECOME in the provided data.

For **CAUSATION (factual)**, P4a explicitly states that “no single common productive causative pattern meaning ‘make true’ or ‘make valid’ emerges.” Instead, Sanskrit uses lexicalized or technical forms in the **pramāṇa** family:
- **प्रमाणीकृतम्**
- **प्रमाणीकरणम्**

These behave more like technical “validated/authenticated” expressions than a broad productive causative operator. So factual causation is not absent lexically, but it lacks a common general construction.

For **CAUSATION (character)**, nothing in P4a/P4b gives a construction meaning “make someone truthful / make someone a liar” or similar. Agentive nouns exist:
- **सत्यवादिन्**
- **असत्यवादिन्**
but these are nominal character labels, not causative constructions. So this category is structurally missing in the supplied material.

For **VERIFICATION**, Sanskrit uses evidential and validation-oriented expressions rather than a dedicated verification operator:
- **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्** “established by valid proof”
- technical/lexicalized **प्रमाणीकृतम्**, **प्रमाणीकरणम्**

Thus verification is functionally present, but mainly through proof/grounds and technical validation vocabulary.

For **DISCONFIRMATION**, no dedicated construction is listed. The available negative patterns are:
- **प्रमाणं नास्ति**
- **न युक्तम्**
and lexical negatives such as **asatya, ayathārtha, apramāṇika**

These negate truth, proof, or propriety, but they do not constitute a separately identified disconfirmation construction in the supplied data. So disconfirmation is expressed by negative attribution or absence of proof rather than by a distinct operator.

On **NEGATION**, the language uses both:
- **analytic negation**: **na / nāsti**
- **morphological negation**: **a- / an-** stems

The notes explicitly say analytic negation is usual, while lexical negatives are also common. So negation is not a gap; it is available in two formats, with analytic negation primary in the examples.

On whether **VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION share the same construction**: the data do not show a shared dedicated construction. Verification is expressed through proof/validation forms such as **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**, while disconfirmation is not separately attested and is instead replaced by negation or absence-of-proof expressions.

There is also a positive/negative asymmetry:
- positive truth, validity, and attribution are richly represented;
- negative attribution is also well represented;
- but dedicated disconfirmation and causative operators are not.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **REVELATION** — **MISSING (structural gap)**
- **CAUSATION (character)** — **MISSING (structural gap)**
- **DISCONFIRMATION** — no dedicated construction attested; **SUBSTITUTED BY NEGATION**
- **CAUSATION (factual)** — no common productive construction; **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**

### Lexical Gaps
- No lexical gap for basic truth attribution:
  - **सत्यम् एतत्**
  - **युक्तं भवति**
- No lexical gap for negative attribution:
  - **प्रमाणं नास्ति**
  - **न युक्तम्**
- No lexical gap for abstract truth/validity nouns:
  - **सत्यता**
  - **यथार्थता**
  - **प्रामाण्यम्**
- No lexical gap for truth/falsity character labels:
  - **सत्यवादिन्**
  - **असत्यवादिन्**

The main issue is not missing words, but missing or weakly developed construction types for some operators.

### Conceptual Gaps
No conceptual gap is explicitly evidenced in P4a/P4b. The data show that truth, validity, proof, propriety, and falsehood are all expressible. The gaps are constructional rather than conceptual.

### Asymmetric Gaps
- **Positive vs. negative asymmetry**:
  - Positive attribution and negative attribution are both available.
  - Verification has partial expression through proof/validation.
  - Disconfirmation lacks a dedicated construction and is replaced by negation.
- **Truth vs. character causation asymmetry**:
  - Character labels exist (**सत्यवादिन्**, **असत्यवादिन्**).
  - Character-changing causation is not attested.
- **BECOME vs. REVELATION asymmetry**:
  - BECOME is available through light-verb constructions.
  - REVELATION is unattested.

### Normalization of multi-word expressions
- **सत्यम् एतत्**: predicative adjective/noun construction; neuter predicate functioning attributively.
- **युक्तं भवति**: predicate adjective + light/copular verb **bhavati**.
- **सत्यतां गच्छति**: abstract noun + light verb **gacchati**; the light verb carries inchoative/resultative force.
- **प्रमा जायते**: noun + light verb **jāyate**; the verb carries arising/becoming force.
- **प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्**: instrumental noun marking means/grounds + result predicate; functions as verification/evidential support.
- **वाक्यस्य सत्यता**, **ज्ञानस्य प्रामाण्यम्**: genitive + abstract noun; nominal evaluative construction.
- **मिथ्या वदति**: adverbial falsity form + speech verb.
- **सत्यं वद**: object-like neuter complement + speech verb.
