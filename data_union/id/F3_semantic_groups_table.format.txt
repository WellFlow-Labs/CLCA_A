| Factual correctness evaluation | These terms are used to assess whether a proposition, report, or claim corresponds to what is actually the case. They appear in contexts of verification, denial, correction, and acknowledgment — in journalism, legal proceedings, everyday conversation, and formal discourse. The central contrast in this group is between terms that affirm correspondence with reality and terms that deny it. | *benar*, *tidak benar*, *salah*, *keliru*, *bohong*, *dusta*, *kebenaran*, *kebohongan*, *kekeliruan*, *sebenarnya*, *sebenar-benarnya*, *memang benar bahwa*, *ada benarnya*, *benar, tapi*, *tidak terbukti*, *informasi yang keliru*, *berita palsu*, *benar adanya*, *kebenaran ilmiah*, *kebenaran formal*, *kebenaran materiil*, *akurat*, *tepat*, *nyata*, *kenyataan*, *diakui kebenarannya* | *bohong* and *dusta* (also Group 3); *keliru* and *salah* (also Group 2); *nyata* (shades into Group 3 when emphasizing perceptibility); *kebenaran* in philosophical/religious collocations (*kebenaran hakiki*, *kebenaran sejati*, *kebenaran mutlak*) shades into Group 7; *akurat* and *tepat* overlap substantially | Within this group, *bohong* and *dusta* specifically imply deliberate deception, while *keliru* and *salah* do not necessarily carry this implication. *Benar* and *tidak benar* are the most neutral and widely distributed; *dusta* is restricted to formal, literary, and religious registers where *bohong* would be used in everyday speech. *benar* and *keliru* are frequently contrasted in educational and analytical contexts. *akurat* and *tepat* overlap substantially but *akurat* is more restricted to formal and technical registers. *nyata* shades into Group 3 (genuineness) when it emphasizes perceptibility rather than factual correctness. |
| Characterizing error and mistaken belief | These terms are used to indicate that a belief, judgment, statement, or action is incorrect — typically without implying deliberate deception. They appear in contexts of correction, diagnosis of misunderstanding, and acknowledgment of fault. They contrast with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 1 (factual correctness). | *salah*, *keliru*, *kesalahan*, *salah paham*, *salah kaprah*, *salah tafsir*, *salah sangka*, *kekeliruan*, *tidak benar*, *salah baca*, *salah dengar*, *salah tulis*, *salah ucap*, *salah cetak* | *salah* and *keliru* (also Group 1); *tidak benar* (also Group 1) | The distinction between *salah* and *keliru* in practice is one of register and implied agency: *keliru* more often describes innocent error or misunderstanding, while *salah* covers a wider range including moral fault. *salah kaprah* is distinctive in that it describes error that has become socially normalized. The *salah* + [verb/noun] compounding pattern is highly productive and generates new forms as needed. |
| Characterizing deliberate falsehood and lying | These terms are used to describe statements or persons where the untruth is understood to be intentional — where the speaker knows the statement is false and makes it anyway. They appear in moral, interpersonal, legal, and journalistic contexts. They contrast with Group 2 (innocent error) and are often paired with terms from Group 6 (deception and manipulation). | *bohong*, *dusta*, *kebohongan*, *tidak benar*, *bohong demi kebaikan*, *sekali bohong, selamanya tidak dipercaya*, *ketahuan bohong*, *sengaja menyesatkan*, *berbohong*, *pembohong*, *membohongi*, *berdusta*, *pendusta*, *kedustaan* | *bohong* and *dusta* (also Group 1); *sengaja menyesatkan* bridges this group and Group 6; *palsu* (Group 4) can describe false statements but is more typically applied to objects and documents | *bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms distinguished primarily by register; *bohong* is the everyday term, *dusta* is formal and literary. Both contrast with *keliru* (Group 2) in that they imply intent. |
| Formal and institutional validity | These terms appear in contexts where an act, document, decision, or status is evaluated as meeting or failing to meet formally required conditions — in court decisions, contracts, official documents, administrative procedures, and legislative processes. The central question in this group is not whether something is factually true but whether it is recognized as binding, legitimate, or procedurally correct by an authoritative institution. | *sah*, *tidak sah*, *absah*, *mengesahkan*, *disahkan*, *pengesahan*, *keabsahan*, *sah dan mengikat*, *sah secara hukum*, *sah menurut agama*, *sah menurut agama tetapi tidak menurut negara*, *dinyatakan sah*, *alasan yang sah*, *tidak absah menurut hukum*, *terbukti secara sah dan meyakinkan*, *sah sebagai*, *anak sah* / *anak tidak sah*, *sah secara agama*, *asli dan sah*, *valid*, *validitas*, *data yang valid*, *dinyatakan valid*, *memvalidasi*, *validasi*, *divalidasi*, *sahih*, *kesahihan*, *kebenaran formal* / *kebenaran materiil*, *diakui kebenarannya* | *sahih* (also Group 5); *sah menurut agama* (also Group 5); *asli dan sah* (also Group 6) | *sah*, *sahih*, and *valid* occupy distinct register positions: *sah* is the general-purpose term across legal, administrative, and religious contexts; *sahih* is restricted to Islamic scholarship and academic methodology; *valid* is restricted to academic and scientific registers. The distinction between *sah* (legal validity) and *asli* (genuineness) is maintained in practice: a document can be *asli* (genuine) but *tidak sah* (invalid) if it lacks proper authorization, and vice versa. |
| Terms with primary or strong Islamic religious usage | These terms appear specifically in Islamic religious and scholarly discourse to evaluate hadith (prophetic traditions) and religious rulings as meeting or failing to meet criteria of authenticity, chain-of-transmission reliability, and doctrinal soundness. Their distribution is largely restricted to this domain in Indonesian usage. Several also appear in secular philosophical or formal literary contexts. | *sahih*, *hadis sahih*, *kesahihan*, *hadis sahih riwayat [narrator]*, *mendustakan ayat-ayat Allah*, *para nabi didustakan oleh kaumnya*, *berdusta*, *pendusta*, *dilarang berdusta*, *bukti sahih*, *sah* (for validity of religious acts), *ikhlas*, *dusta*, *hakiki*, *hakikat*, *niat yang benar*, *menegakkan kebenaran*, *tulus ikhlas*, *jauh dari dusta dan tipu daya* | *sahih* (also Group 4); *sah* (also Group 4); *ikhlas* (also Group 7); *dusta*, *berdusta*, *pendusta* (also Groups 1 and 3); *hakiki*, *hakikat* (also Group 7) | *Sahih* shares its Arabic root (*ṣaḥīḥ*) with *sah* and *absah* but is not interchangeable with them in Indonesian usage. *Sah* and *absah* operate in legal-administrative contexts; *sahih* operates in Islamic scholarly contexts. The overlap is etymological rather than functional. *Dusta*, *berdusta*, and *pendusta* appear in both this group and Groups 1/3, but their frequency and characteristic collocations in Islamic religious discourse are sufficiently distinct to note here. This group is defined by context of use rather than by etymology alone. |
| Genuineness and originality | These terms appear in contexts where the question is whether an object, document, credential, or person is what it claims or appears to be — as opposed to being a copy, forgery, imitation, or assumed identity. They are used in commercial, legal, artistic, forensic, and identity contexts. The central contrast is between the original or genuine and the copy or fake. | *asli*, *palsu*, *tiruan*, *otentik*, *otentisitas*, *otentikasi*, *mengotentikasi*, *keaslian*, *pemalsuan*, *pemalsu*, *memalsukan*, *dipalsukan*, *kepalsuan*, *meniru*, *peniru*, *peniruan*, *asli atau palsu*, *dijamin asli*, *barang tiruan*, *barang palsu*, *uang palsu*, *identitas palsu*, *nama palsu*, *ijazah palsu*, *versi aslinya*, *sertifikat asli*, *naskah asli*, *foto asli*, *karya asli*, *terbukti palsu*, *mengaku asli padahal palsu*, *bukan asli*, *asli dan sah*, *sulit dibedakan dari yang asli* | *kepalsuan* (extends into Group 7 in collocations such as *kepalsuan hidup modern*); *berita palsu* (also Group 3 and Group 9); *asli dan sah* (also Group 4); *sejati* (overlaps with *asli* in some uses but operates primarily in Group 7) | The three-way contrast *asli* / *tiruan* / *palsu* is well established in Indonesian commercial and legal discourse: *asli* is the genuine original, *tiruan* is an imitation that may be openly labeled as such, and *palsu* is a fraudulent substitute. *Palsu* implies deliberate deception and is used in legal contexts for fraud and forgery; *tiruan* describes a copy that may be openly acknowledged as such and does not necessarily imply fraud. *Asli* and *otentik* overlap in meaning but differ in register: *asli* is the everyday term, *otentik* is restricted to formal and written registers. *Sejati* (see Group 7) overlaps with *asli* in some uses but operates primarily in moral and relational contexts rather than object-authenticity contexts. *berita palsu* (fake news) is a notable extension of the *[noun] + palsu* pattern into the domain of information rather than physical objects, which creates overlap with Group 3 (deliberate falsehood) and Group 9 (distortion of information). The *[noun] + palsu* compounding pattern is fully open and productive. |
| Honesty and sincerity in personal conduct; genuine qualities of persons | These terms appear in contexts where the question is not whether an object is a forgery but whether a person, quality, or relationship is deep, complete, and genuine rather than superficial, performed, or partial, or whether a person's statements, actions, or motivations are free from deception, concealment, or hidden agenda. They are used in moral, literary, philosophical, and interpersonal discourse. The evaluative concern is with depth and completeness of a quality, or with freedom from deception in conduct, rather than with the distinction between original and copy. | *sejati*, *jujur*, *kejujuran*, *ketidakjujuran*, *jujur dan amanah*, *jujur saja*, *untuk jujur*, *berkata jujur*, *berkata benar*, *bersikap jujur*, *terpercaya*, *tidak terpercaya*, *kepercayaan*, *mempercayai*, *tulus*, *ikhlas*, *sungguh*, *sungguh-sungguh*, *bersungguh-sungguh*, *kesungguhan*, *dengan sungguh-sungguh*, *sungguh-sungguh dari hati*, *senyum palsu*, *harapan palsu*, *tampil apa adanya* | *terpercaya* (also Group 8); *ikhlas* (also Group 5); *sungguh* shades into Group 1 when used to assert the reality of a claim rather than the sincerity of a person; *senyum palsu* and *harapan palsu* represent extensions of *palsu* from Group 6; *kebenaran* in collocations (*kebenaran hakiki*, *kebenaran sejati*, *kebenaran mutlak*) shades into this group from Group 1 | *Sejati* and *asli* overlap in the sense of "genuine" but *sejati* is used for persons, qualities, and relationships where depth and completeness are at issue, while *asli* is used for objects, documents, and identity in the sense of "not a copy." *Jujur* specifically concerns freedom from deception in communication and conduct; it is not used for objects. *Tulus* and *ikhlas* are frequently paired (*tulus ikhlas*) and overlap substantially, but *ikhlas* carries a specifically religious connotation of selfless acceptance that *tulus* does not. *Sungguh* is primarily an intensifier of sincerity and commitment rather than a term for honesty per se; it shades into Group 1 when used to assert the reality of a claim rather than the sincerity of a person. *Senyum palsu* and *harapan palsu* represent extensions of *palsu* from Group 6 into this domain, where the concern is with performed or unfounded states rather than forged objects. *Terpercaya* and *kredibel* (see Group 8) are near-synonyms in journalistic and institutional contexts; *terpercaya* is the native form and *kredibel* is the loanword. |
