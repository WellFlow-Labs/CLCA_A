## Step 1 — Central roots

### 1. **-kweli** — kweli / -kweli
- **Root form:** kweli / -kweli
- **Participates in P1 items:** **2**
  - *kweli*
  - *-kweli*
- **Core meaning contribution:** truth, genuineness, actuality; marks that something is true, real, or authentic.

### 2. **-amini-** — aminifu / uaminifu / kuaminika / aminika
- **Root form:** -amini-
- **Participates in P1 items:** **4**
  - *aminifu*
  - *uaminifu*
  - *kuaminika*
  - *aminika*
- **Core meaning contribution:** trust, belief, credibility, reliability; marks confidence in a person, statement, source, or institution.

### 3. **sahihi**
- **Root form:** sahihi / -sahihi
- **Participates in P1 items:** **3**
  - *sahihi*
  - *usahihi*
  - *-sahihi*
- **Core meaning contribution:** correctness, accuracy, exactness, validity; marks conformity to a correct answer, proper form, or precise standard.

## Step 2 — Root expansion

### Root: **kweli / -kweli**

This root is productive, but the most common regular expressions are limited. Fewer than 20 very common fixed expressions are readily identifiable.

1. **ni kweli**
   - **Transliteration:** ni kweli
   - **Breakdown:** *ni* “is” + *kweli* “true”
   - **Translation:** it is true
   - **Context:** Very common in everyday speech for confirming that a statement is accurate. Also used in discussion and argument to concede a point before adding a qualification.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **kweli kabisa**
   - **Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
   - **Breakdown:** *kweli* “truly/true” + *kabisa* “completely”
   - **Translation:** absolutely true; really; truly
   - **Context:** Used for emphatic confirmation in conversation and writing. It can strengthen agreement or insist that something is genuinely the case.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **kweli ni kwamba**
   - **Transliteration:** kweli ni kwamba
   - **Breakdown:** *kweli* “truth” + *ni* “is” + *kwamba* “that”
   - **Translation:** the truth is that
   - **Context:** Common in explanatory or argumentative discourse when introducing what the speaker presents as the real situation. It appears in both speech and formal prose.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **kwa kweli**
   - **Transliteration:** kwa kweli
   - **Breakdown:** *kwa* “by/in” + *kweli* “truth”
   - **Translation:** truly; really; in truth; actually
   - **Context:** Extremely common as a discourse marker in everyday speech and writing. It may intensify sincerity, mark surprise, or introduce a frank assessment.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **ukweli**
   - **Transliteration:** ukweli
   - **Breakdown:** *u-* abstract noun + *-kweli* “true”
   - **Translation:** truth
   - **Context:** Central noun in ordinary, journalistic, philosophical, religious, and legal discourse. It refers to truth as a property of statements or to the actual facts of a matter.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **ukweli mtupu**
   - **Transliteration:** ukweli mtupu
   - **Breakdown:** *ukweli* “truth” + *mtupu* “bare, mere, pure”
   - **Translation:** pure truth; nothing but the truth
   - **Context:** Used to insist that a statement is entirely true without embellishment. It appears in emphatic everyday speech and rhetorical writing.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **ukweli wa mambo**
   - **Transliteration:** ukweli wa mambo
   - **Breakdown:** *ukweli* “truth” + *wa* “of” + *mambo* “matters/things”
   - **Translation:** the truth of the matter; the real state of affairs
   - **Context:** Used in discussion, journalism, and public commentary when distinguishing appearances from actual circumstances. It often introduces a corrective account.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **ukweli ni kwamba**
   - **Transliteration:** ukweli ni kwamba
   - **Breakdown:** *ukweli* “truth” + *ni* “is” + *kwamba* “that”
   - **Translation:** the truth is that
   - **Context:** A very common fixed framing expression in speech and prose. It is used to present what the speaker treats as the decisive or underlying fact.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **sema ukweli**
   - **Transliteration:** sema ukweli
   - **Breakdown:** *sema* “say/speak” + *ukweli* “truth”
   - **Translation:** tell the truth
   - **Context:** Common in everyday interaction, parenting, moral instruction, and interrogation. It directly urges honesty in speech.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **kusema ukweli**
    - **Transliteration:** kusema ukweli
    - **Breakdown:** *ku-sema* “to speak” + *ukweli* “truth”
    - **Translation:** to tell the truth; truthfully speaking
    - **Context:** Used both literally and as a discourse formula equivalent to “to tell the truth.” It is frequent in conversation and reflective writing.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **kweli tupu**
    - **Transliteration:** kweli tupu
    - **Breakdown:** *kweli* “true/truth” + *tupu* “mere, bare”
    - **Translation:** sheer truth; absolutely true
    - **Context:** Emphatic expression in speech and writing. It overlaps with *ukweli mtupu* but is somewhat more colloquial in feel.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **wa kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** wa kweli
    - **Breakdown:** agreement form + *-kweli* “true, genuine”
    - **Translation:** genuine; real; true
    - **Context:** Used of persons or plural-class nouns, especially in contrasts between genuine and fake, sincere and insincere, or authentic and superficial. Common in religious and moral language as well.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **ya kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** ya kweli
    - **Breakdown:** agreement form + *-kweli* “true, genuine”
    - **Translation:** true; real; genuine
    - **Context:** Used with appropriate noun classes for statements, stories, goods, or situations. It is common in everyday evaluation of authenticity or factuality.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **cha kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** cha kweli
    - **Breakdown:** agreement form + *-kweli* “true, genuine”
    - **Translation:** genuine; real; true
    - **Context:** Used with class-agreeing nouns in ordinary descriptive speech. It often appears in contrasts with imitation, pretense, or exaggeration.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **kweli ya mambo**
    - **Transliteration:** kweli ya mambo
    - **Breakdown:** *kweli* “truth” + *ya* “of” + *mambo* “matters”
    - **Translation:** the truth of the matter
    - **Context:** Used in public commentary and everyday explanation. It is less fixed than *ukweli wa mambo* but still attested in ordinary usage.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

16. **mkweli**
    - **Transliteration:** mkweli
    - **Breakdown:** *m-* person noun + *-kweli* “truth/true”
    - **Translation:** truthful person; honest person
    - **Context:** Used for a person characterized by truthfulness. It appears in moral, religious, and everyday evaluation of character.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **ukweli**
    - **Transliteration:** ukweli
    - **Breakdown:** *u-* abstract noun + *-kweli*
    - **Translation:** truth
    - **Context:** Same lexical item as above; included here as the core nominal derivative from the root. It is the main abstract noun built from *-kweli*.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **kweli au si kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** kweli au si kweli
    - **Breakdown:** *kweli* “true” + *au* “or” + *si kweli* “not true”
    - **Translation:** true or not true
    - **Context:** Used in questioning, debate, and evidential discussion. It frames an issue explicitly in terms of factual verification.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

19. **si kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** si kweli
    - **Breakdown:** *si* “is not/not” + *kweli* “true”
    - **Translation:** not true; false
    - **Context:** Very common in direct contradiction of a claim. It is used in everyday denial, argument, and public rebuttal.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **kweli kweli**
    - **Transliteration:** kweli kweli
    - **Breakdown:** reduplication of *kweli*
    - **Translation:** really truly; genuinely
    - **Context:** Colloquial emphatic expression in speech. It intensifies sincerity, surprise, or insistence that something is genuinely so.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root: **-amini-**

This root is highly productive. The list below gives the most common regular forms and expressions; additional forms exist.

1. **kuamini**
   - **Transliteration:** kuamini
   - **Breakdown:** *ku-* infinitive + *-amini* “believe, trust”
   - **Translation:** to believe; to trust
   - **Context:** Central verb in everyday, religious, and intellectual discourse. It is used both for believing propositions and trusting persons or institutions.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **amini**
   - **Transliteration:** amini
   - **Breakdown:** imperative/root form from *-amini*
   - **Translation:** believe; trust
   - **Context:** Used in direct exhortation or persuasion. It is common in speech, preaching, and advertising-style appeals.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **naamini**
   - **Transliteration:** naamini
   - **Breakdown:** *na-* 1sg + *-amini*
   - **Translation:** I believe
   - **Context:** Very common in speech and writing for stating opinion, conviction, or faith. As an inflected form, it is not a separate lexical item, so it is noted here only as a very frequent fixed-use expression.
   - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

4. **kuaminika**
   - **Transliteration:** kuaminika
   - **Breakdown:** *ku-* infinitive + *-amini* + *-ika* stative
   - **Translation:** to be believable; to be trustworthy; to be credible
   - **Context:** Used for sources, testimony, institutions, and persons. It is common in journalism, law, and public evaluation of reliability.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **aminika**
   - **Transliteration:** aminika
   - **Breakdown:** *-amini* + *-ika*
   - **Translation:** credible; trustworthy; reliable
   - **Context:** Used adjectivally or participially for evidence, reports, witnesses, and institutions. It is especially common in formal and media language.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **aminifu**
   - **Transliteration:** aminifu
   - **Breakdown:** *-amini* + adjectival formation
   - **Translation:** trustworthy; faithful; honest
   - **Context:** Used mainly for persons and character. It often carries moral and relational force beyond mere factual reliability.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **uaminifu**
   - **Transliteration:** uaminifu
   - **Breakdown:** *u-* abstract noun + *aminifu*
   - **Translation:** honesty; trustworthiness; faithfulness
   - **Context:** Common in moral, religious, and social evaluation. It refers to dependable and sincere conduct in speech and action.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **imani**
   - **Transliteration:** imani
   - **Breakdown:** nominal derivative from root of belief/trust
   - **Translation:** faith; belief; confidence
   - **Context:** Very common in religious discourse, but also used more broadly for confidence or trust. It can refer to doctrinal faith, personal conviction, or confidence in someone.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **kuwa na imani**
   - **Transliteration:** kuwa na imani
   - **Breakdown:** *kuwa na* “to have” + *imani* “faith/confidence”
   - **Translation:** to have faith; to have confidence
   - **Context:** Used in religion, motivation, and everyday encouragement. It may refer to trust in God, in oneself, or in another person or process.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **imani katika**
    - **Transliteration:** imani katika
    - **Breakdown:** *imani* “faith/belief” + *katika* “in”
    - **Translation:** belief in; faith in
    - **Context:** Common in religious, philosophical, and political language. It introduces the object of belief or confidence.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **kuamini katika**
    - **Transliteration:** kuamini katika
    - **Breakdown:** *kuamini* “to believe” + *katika* “in”
    - **Translation:** to believe in
    - **Context:** Used for ideological, religious, and moral commitments, not only for factual belief. It is common in speeches and reflective prose.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **kuamini kwamba**
    - **Transliteration:** kuamini kwamba
    - **Breakdown:** *kuamini* “to believe” + *kwamba* “that”
    - **Translation:** to believe that
    - **Context:** Standard complement-taking pattern for propositional belief. It is common in all registers.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **kuamini mtu**
    - **Transliteration:** kuamini mtu
    - **Breakdown:** *kuamini* “to trust/believe” + *mtu* “person”
    - **Translation:** to trust someone; to believe someone
    - **Context:** Used in interpersonal and institutional contexts. It can mean either accepting what someone says or placing trust in them more generally.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **kujiamini**
    - **Transliteration:** kujiamini
    - **Breakdown:** *ku-* + reflexive *ji-* + *-amini*
    - **Translation:** to be self-confident
    - **Context:** Very common in everyday and educational language. It refers to confidence in oneself rather than belief in truth directly.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **kujiamini kupita kiasi**
    - **Transliteration:** kujiamini kupita kiasi
    - **Breakdown:** *kujiamini* “to be self-confident” + *kupita kiasi* “excessively”
    - **Translation:** to be overconfident
    - **Context:** Used in personal evaluation, education, and commentary. It marks excessive self-confidence.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

16. **kuaminiana**
    - **Transliteration:** kuaminiana
    - **Breakdown:** *ku-* + *-amini* + reciprocal *-ana*
    - **Translation:** to trust each other
    - **Context:** Common in family, social, workplace, and political contexts. It refers to mutual trust between persons or groups.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

17. **uaminifu kwa**
    - **Transliteration:** uaminifu kwa
    - **Breakdown:** *uaminifu* “faithfulness/trustworthiness” + *kwa* “to/toward”
    - **Translation:** faithfulness to; loyalty toward
    - **Context:** Used in moral, marital, religious, and institutional contexts. It often emphasizes loyalty and constancy.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

18. **mwaminifu**
    - **Transliteration:** mwaminifu
    - **Breakdown:** *m-* person noun + *aminifu*
    - **Translation:** faithful person; trustworthy person; believer
    - **Context:** Used for a person marked by faithfulness or trustworthiness. In religious contexts it can also mean “believer” or “the faithful.”
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

19. **waaminifu**
    - **Transliteration:** waaminifu
    - **Breakdown:** plural person agreement + *aminifu*
    - **Translation:** faithful people; trustworthy people; believers
    - **Context:** Common in religious and moral discourse for groups of believers or trustworthy persons. Also used in institutional praise or evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

20. **asiyeaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** asiyeaminika
    - **Breakdown:** negative relative + *-amini* + *-ika*
    - **Translation:** untrustworthy; not credible
    - **Context:** Common in public evaluation of witnesses, rumors, and persons. It is especially natural in journalism and legal commentary.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

21. **chanzo kinachoaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** chanzo kinachoaminika
    - **Breakdown:** *chanzo* “source” + relative + *kuaminika* “be credible”
    - **Translation:** a credible source
    - **Context:** Very common in journalism, research, and public-information settings. It evaluates the reliability of information origin.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

22. **ushahidi unaoaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** ushahidi unaoaminika
    - **Breakdown:** *ushahidi* “evidence/testimony” + relative + *kuaminika*
    - **Translation:** credible evidence
    - **Context:** Used in legal, investigative, and journalistic contexts. It marks evidence judged worthy of belief.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

23. **taarifa inayoaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** taarifa inayoaminika
    - **Breakdown:** *taarifa* “report/information” + relative + *kuaminika*
    - **Translation:** reliable information; credible report
    - **Context:** Common in media, administration, and public communication. It distinguishes dependable information from rumor or speculation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

24. **mashahidi waaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** mashahidi waaminika
    - **Breakdown:** *mashahidi* “witnesses” + *waaminika* “credible”
    - **Translation:** credible witnesses
    - **Context:** Used in legal and journalistic contexts. It evaluates whether testimony can be trusted.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **kuaminiwa**
    - **Transliteration:** kuaminiwa
    - **Breakdown:** *ku-* + *-amini* + passive *-wa*
    - **Translation:** to be trusted; to be believed
    - **Context:** Used of persons, institutions, and statements that receive trust from others. It is common in social and political evaluation.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

26. **anaaminiwa**
    - **Transliteration:** anaaminiwa
    - **Breakdown:** inflected passive of *kuamini*
    - **Translation:** he/she is trusted; he/she is believed
    - **Context:** Frequent in reporting and public commentary. As an inflected form it is not an independent lexical item, but it is a common fixed evaluative expression.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

27. **kutokuamini**
    - **Transliteration:** kutokuamini
    - **Breakdown:** *kuto-* negative infinitive + *-amini*
    - **Translation:** not to believe; disbelief
    - **Context:** Used in philosophical, religious, and everyday contexts. It refers either to refusal to believe or absence of belief.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

28. **kutoaminika**
    - **Transliteration:** kutoaminika
    - **Breakdown:** *kuto-* negative infinitive + *-amini* + *-ika*
    - **Translation:** lack of credibility; being untrustworthy
    - **Context:** Used in formal discussion of institutions, testimony, and public figures. It names the condition of not being credible.
    - **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

29. **kutia imani**
    - **Transliteration:** kutia imani
    - **Breakdown:** *kutia* “to instill/put” + *imani* “faith/confidence”
    - **Translation:** to inspire confidence; to instill faith
    - **Context:** Used in leadership, religion, and motivational discourse. It refers to causing others to trust or feel assured.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

30. **kukosa imani**
    - **Transliteration:** kukosa imani
    - **Breakdown:** *kukosa* “to lack” + *imani* “faith/confidence”
    - **Translation:** to lack faith; to lack confidence
    - **Context:** Used in religion, personal psychology, and social trust contexts. It may refer to doubt, discouragement, or mistrust.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root: **sahihi / -sahihi**

This root is moderately productive. Common expressions are concentrated in educational, administrative, technical, and procedural usage.

1. **sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** basic lexical item
   - **Translation:** correct; accurate; right; valid
   - **Context:** Used widely for answers, forms, details, and procedures. It is one of the main words for correctness in modern Swahili.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

2. **usahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** usahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *u-* abstract noun + *sahihi*
   - **Translation:** correctness; accuracy; precision
   - **Context:** Common in formal evaluation of writing, reporting, measurement, and data. It is especially natural where exactness is being assessed.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

3. **-sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** -sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** adjectival stem
   - **Translation:** correct; proper; accurate
   - **Context:** Used with noun-class agreement in descriptive phrases. It is common in school and bureaucratic language.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

4. **jibu sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** jibu sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *jibu* “answer” + *sahihi* “correct”
   - **Translation:** correct answer
   - **Context:** Extremely common in educational settings, tests, and exercises. It is also used metaphorically in problem-solving discussion.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

5. **majibu sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** majibu sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *majibu* “answers” + *sahihi* “correct”
   - **Translation:** correct answers
   - **Context:** Common in teaching materials, marking schemes, and classroom instruction. It refers to answers judged right by a standard.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

6. **taarifa sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** taarifa sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *taarifa* “information/report” + *sahihi* “accurate/correct”
   - **Translation:** accurate information; correct information
   - **Context:** Common in media, administration, health communication, and public notices. It emphasizes factual reliability and exactness.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

7. **maelezo sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** maelezo sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *maelezo* “explanation/details” + *sahihi* “correct/accurate”
   - **Translation:** correct details; accurate explanation
   - **Context:** Used in instruction, reporting, and official communication. It can refer either to exact details or to a proper explanation.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

8. **majina sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** majina sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *majina* “names” + *sahihi* “correct”
   - **Translation:** correct names
   - **Context:** Used in schoolwork, records, and identification contexts. It stresses proper naming or accurate entry of names.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

9. **tahajia sahihi**
   - **Transliteration:** tahajia sahihi
   - **Breakdown:** *tahajia* “spelling” + *sahihi* “correct”
   - **Translation:** correct spelling
   - **Context:** Common in education and editing. It evaluates whether a written form conforms to standard orthography.
   - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

10. **matumizi sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** matumizi sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *matumizi* “use/usage” + *sahihi* “proper/correct”
    - **Translation:** proper use; correct usage
    - **Context:** Used in language teaching, technical instructions, medicine, and product guidance. It refers to use according to accepted rules or instructions.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

11. **utaratibu sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** utaratibu sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *utaratibu* “procedure” + *sahihi* “proper/correct”
    - **Translation:** proper procedure; correct procedure
    - **Context:** Common in administration, law-enforcement commentary, medicine, and technical operations. It evaluates whether a process follows the right steps.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

12. **njia sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** njia sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *njia* “way/method” + *sahihi* “right/correct”
    - **Translation:** the right way; correct method
    - **Context:** Used in instruction, advice, and technical explanation. It can refer to practical method, moral course, or procedural correctness.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

13. **wakati sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** wakati sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *wakati* “time” + *sahihi* “right/correct”
    - **Translation:** the right time
    - **Context:** Common in planning, medicine, agriculture, and everyday advice. It refers to appropriateness or exact timing rather than truth directly.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

14. **mahali sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** mahali sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *mahali* “place” + *sahihi* “right/correct”
    - **Translation:** the right place; correct location
    - **Context:** Used in directions, filing, technical setup, and logistics. It marks proper placement or correct destination.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

15. **namba sahihi**
    - **Transliteration:** namba sahihi
    - **Breakdown:** *namba* “number” + *sahihi* “correct”
    - **Translation:** correct number
    - **Context:** Used in records, phone contacts, forms, and calculations. It refers to exact numerical correctness.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

16. **jina sahihi**

24. **usahihi wa vipimo**
    - **Transliteration:** usahihi wa vipimo
    - **Breakdown:** *usahihi* “accuracy” + *wa* “of” + *vipimo* “measurements”
    - **Translation:** measurement accuracy
    - **Context:** Common in science, engineering, and laboratory work. It refers to precision and correctness of measured values.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

25. **usahihi wa taarifa za habari**
    - **Transliteration:** usahihi wa taarifa za habari
    - **Breakdown:** *usahihi* “accuracy” + *wa* “of” + *taarifa za habari* “news reports/information”
    - **Translation:** accuracy of news reporting
    - **Context:** Used in media criticism, journalism training, and public-information ethics. It evaluates whether reporting is factually correct.
    - **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral extensions

The following expressions show the root being used in ways that extend beyond narrow truth/falsity evaluation:

- **kwa kweli** — often functions as a discourse marker or intensifier, not only as a literal truth claim.
- **kweli kweli / kweli kabisa** — can function mainly as emphasis.
- **wa kweli / ya kweli / cha kweli** — often refer to genuineness or authenticity of persons or things, not only propositional truth.
- **mkweli** — shifts toward moral character evaluation.
- **imani / kuwa na imani / kutia imani / kukosa imani** — often concern religious faith, confidence, or psychological assurance.
- **kujiamini / kujiamini kupita kiasi** — concern self-confidence rather than truth or credibility.
- **mwaminifu / waaminifu / uaminifu kwa** — often concern loyalty, faithfulness, and moral constancy.
- **wakati sahihi / mahali sahihi / njia sahihi** — often concern appropriateness, suitability, or practical correctness rather than truth.
- **matumizi sahihi / utaratibu sahihi** — often concern proper procedure or norm-conformity.
- **tafsiri sahihi** — extends into interpretive and textual fidelity.
- **usahihi wa kisarufi** — extends into grammatical norm evaluation.
