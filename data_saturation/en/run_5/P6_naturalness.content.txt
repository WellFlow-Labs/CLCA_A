# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
**Language: English (en)**

---

**Note on transliteration:** English uses the Latin script natively; transliteration is not applicable for any item below.

**Scope note:** This step documents how English encodes change of state, causation of state, and stability of state for truth-related vocabulary drawn from the CONTEXT (P1–P5). All expressions are drawn from attested patterns in the CONTEXT. Where no common expression exists for a given term or construction type, this is stated explicitly. Terms are treated separately where their behavior differs.

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1.1 Inchoative constructions: overview

As documented in P4a and P4b, English has no dedicated inchoative morpheme for the truth domain. Change of state is expressed periphrastically. The following periphrastic strategies are attested across the P1 vocabulary:

- **prove** + adjective (implies testing or demonstration preceded the change)
- **turn out to be** + adjective (implies the state was revealed by events)
- **become** + adjective or noun (neutral inchoative; implies gradual or unmarked change)
- **come true** (fixed idiom; restricted to hopes, predictions, and dreams)
- **emerge as** + noun phrase (implies the state was established through a process)

These strategies are not interchangeable across all P1 items. Behavior is documented term by term below.

## 2. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 2.1 Overview

As documented in P4a and P4b, English encodes causation of truth-related states through two strategies:

- **Periphrastic causatives:** *make*, *render*, *prove* + object + adjective
- **Lexical causatives:** *verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*, *falsify*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*, *discredit*, *correct*

These strategies are not interchangeable across all P1 items. Behavior is documented term by term below.

---

### 2.2 Causing something to be true / verified

| Expression | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *The laboratory verified the results.* | The laboratory verified the results. | Lexical causative; documented in P4a and P4b. *Verify* means to cause a claim to be confirmed as true through checking. Most natural in scientific, administrative, and journalistic registers. |
| *The evidence confirmed that the account was true.* | The evidence confirmed that the account was true. | *Confirm* + *that*-clause; *confirm* is not a P1 item but is the most common near-synonym of *verify* in this construction. Included because it is the natural paraphrase of the lexical causative. |
| *Independent testing proved the claim true.* | Independent testing proved the claim true. | *Prove* + object + adjective; documented in P4a. |

---

### 2.3 Causing something to be false / falsified

| Expression | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *He falsified the financial records.* | He falsified the financial records. | Lexical causative (legal sense); documented in P1b Root 3 and P2a. Implies deliberate alteration with intent to deceive. |

The following table summarizes the stability constructions attested across the P1 vocabulary. All are compositional except *hold true*, which is a fixed verbal idiom.

| Expression | Translation | Notes |

## 4. CROSS-TERM OBSERVATIONS

### 4.1 Lexical causative availability is uneven

The P1 vocabulary divides into two groups with respect to lexical causative availability:

**Items with dedicated lexical causatives:**
- *true* / *verified* → *verify*
- *correct* → *correct* (verbal)
- *valid* → *validate*; *invalid* → *invalidate*
- *legitimate* → *legitimize*; *illegitimate* → *delegitimize*
- *authentic* → *authenticate*
- *credible* (loss of) → *discredit*
- *false* (legal) → *falsify*
- *false* (methodological) → *falsify* (Popperian sense)

**Items without dedicated lexical causatives (periphrastic only):**
- *accurate* / *inaccurate*
- *reliable* / *unreliable*
- *plausible* / *implausible*
- *sincere* / *insincere*
- *honest* / *dishonest*
- *sound* / *unsound*
- *verifiable* / *unverifiable*
- *falsifiable* / *unfalsifiable*
- *genuine* / *counterfeit* / *spurious*

This asymmetry is not random. Items with lexical causatives tend to be those used in institutional, legal, and administrative contexts (*validate*, *authenticate*, *legitimize*, *invalidate*, *delegitimize*) or in formal methodological contexts (*verify*, *falsify*). Items without lexical causatives tend to be those used in dispositional, epistemic, or evaluative contexts (*reliable*, *sincere*, *honest*, *plausible*).

### 4.2 *prove* + adjective is the most productive inchoative across the P1 inventory

*Prove* + adjective is attested as a natural inchoative construction for *true*, *false*, *accurate*, *inaccurate*, *correct*, *incorrect*, *genuine*, *counterfeit*, *spurious*, *credible*, *sound*, and *valid*. It is the closest thing to a general-purpose inchoative in the truth domain, and it carries the additional implication that testing or demonstration preceded the change of state. It is not attested as natural for *sincere*, *honest*, *reliable*, *plausible*, *legitimate*, or *authentic* in the same way; for these items, *turn out to be* or *become* is preferred.

### 4.3 *hold true* is the only lexically specific stability construction

All stability constructions except *hold true* are fully compositional (*remain* + adjective, *still* + adjective, *retain* + nominal). *Hold true* is restricted to *true* and does not extend to other P1 adjectives. No parallel fixed stability idioms exist for *accurate*, *valid*, *credible*, or other P1 items.

### 4.4 Gradability affects inchoative behavior

Items that admit of degrees (*accurate*, *credible*, *reliable*, *plausible*, *authentic*) naturally appear in inchoative constructions with comparative adjectives (*become more accurate*, *become increasingly credible*). Items that are treated as non-gradable in formal contexts (*true*, *valid* in logic) do not naturally appear in comparative inchoative constructions in those registers, though they may do so in everyday speech (*become more true to life*, *become more valid as an argument*).

### 4.5 *falsify* encodes two distinct causative meanings

As documented in P2a, P3, and P5, *falsify* encodes two distinct causative meanings that are not interchangeable:

- **Legal sense:** to cause a document or record to be false by deliberate alteration (*He falsified the records.*)
- **Popperian methodological sense:** to cause a hypothesis to be shown false by evidence (*The data falsified the theory.*)

In the legal sense, the agent is a person acting with intent to deceive; in the methodological sense, the agent is evidence or a test. This distinction is the only case in the P1 data where a single lexical causative encodes two semantically distinct causative relations.
