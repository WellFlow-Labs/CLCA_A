# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
**Language: English (en)**

---

**Note on transliteration:** English uses the Latin script natively; transliteration is not applicable for any item below.

**Scope note:** All oppositions below are drawn directly from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is posited that is not supported by co-occurrence, explicit contrast, or documented usage in those steps. Where the data supports asymmetry, asymmetry is preserved.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *false*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true / false
**Description of contrast:** These are the broadest and most fundamental proposition-evaluation terms in the P1 inventory, explicitly contrasted across logical, legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts. They anchor the positive and negative poles of the truth-evaluation axis documented in P3 Group 1, and their derivational families (*truth*, *truthful*, *falsehood*, *falsity*, etc.) are the most productive in the domain.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *untrue*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true / not true
**Description of contrast:** *Untrue* is the prefixed morphological negation of *true*, documented in P2a as slightly more formal than *false* in some contexts and also carrying the additional sense of disloyalty or unfaithfulness to a person or commitment. The contrast is morphologically transparent but semantically not fully symmetrical with Opposition 1.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** *truth*
**Term B:** *untruth*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** truth / untruth (a false statement; a lie)
**Description of contrast:** *Untruth* is the prefixed nominalization used as a formal or euphemistic alternative to *lie* or *falsehood*, documented in P1b and P2a. The contrast is morphological but the two nominals are not fully symmetrical: *truth* refers to the property of being true or to a true statement, while *untruth* refers specifically to a false statement rather than to the abstract property of falsity.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 32

**Term A:** *falsify* (Popperian: to test a hypothesis by attempting to show it false)
**Term B:** *verify* (to confirm a claim against evidence)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** to falsify (in the methodological sense) / to verify
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b Root 3 item 7 and P2a under *false* and *verifiable*. ## Opposition 36

**Term A:** *true positive* / *true negative* (implied)
**Term B:** *false positive* / *false negative*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** correct test result / incorrect test result
**Description of contrast:** The *false positive* / *false negative* pair documented in P1b and P2b implies a contrasting pair of *true positive* and *true negative* (correct test results). These implied forms are not explicitly documented as P1 or P2 items but are the direct constructional counterparts within the same technical vocabulary. ## Opposition 41

**Term A:** *verifiability* (the property of being capable of confirmation)
**Term B:** *falsifiability* (the property of being capable of disconfirmation)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** verifiability / falsifiability
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1b, P2a, and P3 Group 5. Both terms are central to philosophy of science but represent different methodological orientations: *verifiability* (associated with logical positivism) requires that a meaningful claim be capable of confirmation by evidence; *falsifiability* (associated with Popper) requires that a scientific claim be capable of being shown false. The contrast is between two criteria for the epistemic standing of claims, not between truth and falsity as properties.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 46

**Term A:** *sound argument* (valid with true premises)
**Term B:** *valid argument* (conclusion follows from premises, regardless of truth of premises)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** sound argument / valid argument (in the technical logical sense)
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P1 items 6 and 13, and P3 Group 5. In formal logic, *valid* and *sound* are explicitly distinguished: a valid argument has a conclusion that follows necessarily from its premises, but the premises may be false; a sound argument is valid and additionally has true premises. The contrast is between a formal structural property (*valid*) and a combined formal-and-factual property (*sound*).

## Opposition 47

**Term A:** *attribution construction* (*The statement is true.*)
**Term B:** *predicate complement with epistemic verb* (*The court found the testimony credible.*)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** simple attribution / attributed evaluation
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P4a and P4b. The simple attribution construction predicates a truth-domain property of a subject without specifying an epistemic agent. The predicate complement with epistemic verb construction additionally encodes the identity of the agent performing the evaluation (*the court*, *investigators*, *experts*). The contrast is constructional: the same truth-domain adjective may appear in either frame, but the epistemic-verb frame adds an agent and implies a process of assessment.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 48

**Term A:** *analytic negation* (*The claim is not accurate.*)
**Term B:** *synthetic negation* (*The claim is inaccurate.*)
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** not accurate / inaccurate
**Description of contrast:** Documented in P4a and P4b. Both constructions negate the same truth-domain adjective, but analytic negation (*not* + adjective) is the default in spoken and informal registers, while synthetic negation (prefixed adjective) is more common in formal, academic, and legal registers. P4b notes that the two strategies are largely interchangeable in propositional content but differ in register and emphasis.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
