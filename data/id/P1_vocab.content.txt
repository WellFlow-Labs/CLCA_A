# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Indonesian (id)

## Lexical Items

### 1. benar
**Form:** benar  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** true, correct  
**Typical English translations:** true, correct, right, accurate

Used to evaluate statements as factually accurate or actions as morally/procedurally correct. Appears in both epistemic contexts ("itu benar" = "that's true") and normative contexts ("cara yang benar" = "the right way"). Common in everyday speech across all registers.

**[CORE]**

### 2. salah
**Form:** salah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** wrong, mistaken  
**Typical English translations:** wrong, incorrect, mistaken, false

Evaluates statements as inaccurate or actions as improper. Functions as direct antonym to "benar" in most contexts. Used for factual errors ("jawaban salah" = "wrong answer") and moral/procedural mistakes ("perbuatan salah" = "wrongdoing").

**[CORE]**

### 3. asli
**Form:** asli  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** original, native  
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, original, real

Evaluates objects or representations as genuine rather than counterfeit or copied. Commonly used for documents ("dokumen asli" = "original document"), products ("barang asli" = "genuine goods"), and identity ("orang asli" = "native person"). Contrasts with "palsu" (fake).

**[CORE]**

### 4. palsu
**Form:** palsu  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** false, fake  
**Typical English translations:** fake, counterfeit, false, forged

Evaluates objects, documents, or representations as inauthentic or deliberately deceptive. Used for counterfeit goods ("uang palsu" = "counterfeit money"), forged documents, and false identities. Carries negative connotation of intentional deception.

**[CORE]**

### 5. bohong
**Form:** bohong  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** lie, false  
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, untrue, dishonest

Evaluates statements as deliberately false or deceptive. Functions as both noun ("itu bohong" = "that's a lie") and verb ("dia bohong" = "he/she is lying"). Implies intentional deception rather than mere error.

**[CORE]**

### 6. jujur
**Form:** jujur  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** honest, truthful  
**Typical English translations:** honest, truthful, sincere, frank

Evaluates persons or statements as truthful and without deception. Used to characterize people ("orang jujur" = "honest person") and speech acts ("berkata jujur" = "speak honestly"). Positive moral evaluation of truthfulness.

**[CORE]**

### 7. tepat
**Form:** tepat  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** precise, exact  
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, appropriate

Evaluates statements, measurements, or actions as precisely correct or appropriate to context. Used for accuracy ("jawaban tepat" = "precise answer"), timing ("tepat waktu" = "on time"), and appropriateness ("tepat sasaran" = "on target").

**[CORE]**

### 8. akurat
**Form:** akurat  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** accurate (from Dutch/English "accurate")  
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise

Evaluates information, measurements, or representations as factually accurate. More technical/formal register than "tepat," often used in scientific, journalistic, or professional contexts ("data akurat" = "accurate data").

**[CORE]**

### 9. nyata
**Form:** nyata  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** real, actual  
**Typical English translations:** real, actual, tangible, evident

Evaluates existence or reality of something as actual rather than imaginary or theoretical. Used for concrete reality ("bukti nyata" = "tangible evidence") and contrasts with "khayalan" (imaginary). Also means "evident" or "clear."

**[CORE]**

### 10. sungguh
**Form:** sungguh  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** truly, really  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, seriously

Functions as intensifier affirming truth or genuineness of statements. Can stand alone ("sungguh?" = "really?") or modify adjectives/verbs ("sungguh benar" = "truly true"). Expresses sincerity or emphasis on truthfulness.

**[CORE]**

### 11. betul
**Form:** betul  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** correct, true  
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, true

Near-synonym of "benar," used interchangeably in many contexts. Slightly more colloquial in some regions. Common in confirmations ("betul sekali" = "exactly right") and evaluations of correctness.

**[CORE]**

### 12. keliru
**Form:** keliru  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, erroneous  
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, erroneous, confused

Evaluates statements or beliefs as mistaken, often implying confusion rather than deliberate falsehood. More formal than "salah" and suggests error in judgment or understanding ("pendapat keliru" = "mistaken opinion").

**[CORE]**

### 13. sah
**Form:** sah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** valid, legitimate  
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, legal, authorized

Evaluates documents, procedures, or status as legally or officially valid. Common in legal and administrative contexts ("dokumen sah" = "valid document," "pernikahan sah" = "legitimate marriage"). Implies conformity with proper authority or procedure.

**[CORE]**

### 14. valid
**Form:** valid  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** valid (from English/Dutch)  
**Typical English translations:** valid

Evaluates arguments, data, or documents as logically or procedurally valid. More technical/academic register than "sah," often used in research, logic, or formal analysis contexts ("argumen valid" = "valid argument").

**[CORE]**

### 15. sejati
**Form:** sejati  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (se- + jati "essence")  
**Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic

Evaluates essence or fundamental nature as genuine. Often used for identity ("cinta sejati" = "true love," "teman sejati" = "true friend"). Emphasizes authenticity of essential character rather than surface appearance.

**[CORE]**

### 16. hakiki
**Form:** hakiki  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** essential, fundamental (from Arabic ḥaqīqī)  
**Typical English translations:** true, essential, fundamental, real

Evaluates something as representing fundamental truth or essence. More philosophical/formal register, often used in religious or philosophical discourse ("kebenaran hakiki" = "fundamental truth"). Emphasizes deep or ultimate reality.

**[CORE]**

### 17. dusta
**Form:** dusta  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (from Sanskrit)  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deceit

Evaluates statements as false or deceptive. More formal/literary than "bohong," often used in religious, literary, or formal contexts. Carries strong negative moral connotation ("perkataan dusta" = "false words").

**[CORE]**

### 18. kebenaran
**Form:** kebenaran  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** truth (ke- + benar + -an, nominalization)  
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, veracity

Nominalized form of "benar," refers to truth as abstract concept or quality. Used in philosophical, religious, and everyday contexts ("mencari kebenaran" = "seek truth"). Can refer to both factual and moral truth.

**[CORE]**

### 19. kebohongan
**Form:** kebohongan  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (ke- + bohong + -an, nominalization)  
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, deception

Nominalized form of "bohong," refers to lies or deception as concept or instance. Used for deliberate falsehoods ("penuh kebohongan" = "full of lies"). Emphasizes deceptive quality.

**[CORE]**

### 20. mirip
**Form:** mirip  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** similar, resembling  
**Typical English translations:** similar, resembling, alike, look-alike

Evaluates resemblance between objects, persons, or representations. Used for physical similarity ("mirip sekali" = "very similar") and can imply authenticity questions when something resembles but may not be genuine ("mirip asli" = "looks genuine").

**[CORE]**

### 21. serupa
**Form:** serupa  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** similar, alike (se- + rupa "form/appearance")  
**Typical English translations:** similar, alike, resembling

Evaluates similarity in form or appearance. Slightly more formal than "mirip," often used in comparisons ("serupa tapi tak sama" = "similar but not the same"). Emphasizes likeness in appearance or form.

**[CORE]**

### 22. sama
**Form:** sama  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** same, equal  
**Typical English translations:** same, equal, identical

Evaluates identity or equality between things. Used for exact sameness ("sama persis" = "exactly the same") or equality ("sama rata" = "equal"). Fundamental term for identity relations.

**[CORE]**

### 23. beda
**Form:** beda  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** different, distinct  
**Typical English translations:** different, distinct, other

Evaluates difference or distinction between things. Direct antonym of "sama," used in comparisons and distinctions ("beda sekali" = "very different"). Common in everyday speech for marking differences.

**[CORE]**

### 24. tulen
**Form:** tulen  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** pure, genuine  
**Typical English translations:** genuine, pure, authentic, unadulterated

Evaluates purity or genuineness, especially of materials or character. Used for pure substances ("emas tulen" = "pure gold") and authentic character ("orang tulen" = "genuine person"). Emphasizes unmixed or unadulterated quality.

**[CORE]**

### 25. meyakinkan
**Form:** meyakinkan  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** convincing (meN- + yakin + -kan, causative)  
**Typical English translations:** convincing, persuasive, credible

Evaluates statements, evidence, or arguments as capable of producing conviction or belief. Used for credibility assessment ("bukti meyakinkan" = "convincing evidence"). Focuses on persuasive power rather than truth per se.

**[CORE]**

### 26. dapat dipercaya
**Form:** dapat dipercaya  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** can be trusted (dapat "can" + di- + percaya "trust")  
**Typical English translations:** trustworthy, reliable, credible

Fixed expression evaluating reliability of persons, sources, or information. Used for trustworthiness assessment ("sumber dapat dipercaya" = "reliable source"). Emphasizes reliability and credibility.

**[CORE]**

### 27. absah
**Form:** absah  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** valid, legitimate (from Arabic)  
**Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, legal

Evaluates legal or religious validity, similar to "sah" but more formal/religious register. Common in Islamic legal contexts and formal administrative language ("nikah absah" = "valid marriage in Islamic law").

**[CORE]**

### 28. fiktif
**Form:** fiktif  
**Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)  
**Literal meaning:** fictitious (from Dutch/English)  
**Typical English translations:** fictitious, fictional, fabricated

Evaluates something as invented or not corresponding to reality. Used in formal/technical contexts for fabricated data, fictional accounts, or non-existent entities ("data fiktif" = "fabricated data"). Implies deliberate construction rather than reality.

**[CORE]**
