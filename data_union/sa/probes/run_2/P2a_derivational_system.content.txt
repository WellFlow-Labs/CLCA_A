# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST, consistent with P1 and P1b.

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope

This document records attested derivational morphology for the 30 base lexical items identified in P1. The inventory is drawn from those items directly; P1b expansions are not treated as new bases unless they independently appear in P1. Derivations are reported only where usage is attested or strongly supported in the literature. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved without normalization.

---

## Item 1 — सत्य / *satya*

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यता
3. Transliteration: satyatā
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the state of being true"
5. Natural English Translation: truthfulness, the property of being true
6. Usage: Used in philosophical and ethical literature to denote the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from any particular true statement. Appears in discussions of the nature of truth as a property predicated of statements or persons.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यत्व
3. Transliteration: satyatva
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + abstract suffix *-tva*; "truth-ness"
5. Natural English Translation: the property of being true, truthhood
6. Usage: A more technical philosophical variant of *satyatā*, appearing in Navya-Nyāya logical literature to refer to the property of truth as a formal category subject to definition and analysis.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: असत्य
3. Transliteration: asatya
4. Literal Breakdown: *a-* (negation) + *satya*; "not true"
5. Natural English Translation: untruth, falsehood
6. Usage: Used across ethical, legal, and philosophical literature as the direct negation of *satya*, denoting a false statement or the quality of being untrue. Common in moral prescriptions against lying.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यवादिन्
3. Transliteration: satyavādin
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*); "one who speaks truth"
5. Natural English Translation: truth-speaker, one who speaks truthfully
6. Usage: Used in epic, Purāṇic, and legal literature to characterize a person who habitually speaks the truth; a standard epithet of virtuous characters in narrative literature.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यसंध
3. Transliteration: satyasaṃdha
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *saṃdha* (bound by, pledged to); "bound to truth"
5. Natural English Translation: true to one's word, faithful to one's pledge
6. Usage: Used in epic literature as an epithet for heroes who keep their oaths and promises; evaluates a person's reliability and integrity in fulfilling commitments.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्याग्रह
3. Transliteration: satyāgraha
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *āgraha* (firm holding, insistence); "holding firmly to truth"
5. Natural English Translation: insistence on truth, truth-force
6. Usage: Systematized by Mohandas Gandhi to denote nonviolent resistance grounded in the force of truth; used in political, ethical, and historical discourse.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यापन
3. Transliteration: satyāpana
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *āpana* (causing to obtain, from causative of root *āp-*); "causing truth to be established"
5. Natural English Translation: verification, confirmation, attestation
6. Usage: Used in legal and administrative contexts to denote the act of verifying or confirming the truth of a statement or document.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यापित
3. Transliteration: satyāpita
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *āpita* (past passive participle of causative of *āp-*); "caused to be verified"
5. Natural English Translation: verified, confirmed, attested
6. Usage: The past passive participle form corresponding to *satyāpana*; appears on official documents to indicate that a statement has been verified.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**

1. Base Form: सत्य (*satya*)
2. Derived Form: सत्यशोधन
3. Transliteration: satyaśodhana
4. Literal Breakdown: *satya* + *śodhana* (purification, investigation, from root *śudh-*); "investigation of truth"
5. Natural English Translation: truth-finding, inquiry into truth
6. Usage: Used in modern Sanskrit-influenced discourse, including Gandhi's autobiography (*Satya-śodhana* / *My Experiments with Truth*), to denote the systematic pursuit of truth.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** The base *satya* is highly productive in compounding. The forms above represent the most clearly derivational (rather than purely compositional) patterns. Additional compounds (*satyaśīla*, *satyavrata*, *satyaparāyaṇa*) exist but are not separately documented here as they do not introduce additional derivational morphology beyond compounding.

## Item 2 — ऋत / *ṛta*

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. Derived Form: ऋतवान्
3. Transliteration: ṛtavān
4. Literal Breakdown: *ṛta* + possessive suffix *-vat* (masculine nominative *-vān*); "one who possesses cosmic order"
5. Natural English Translation: one who embodies right order, righteous
6. Usage: Appears in Vedic literature as a characterization of persons or deities who uphold or embody *ṛta*; the form is confined to archaic Vedic contexts.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. Derived Form: ऋतधीति
3. Transliteration: ṛtadhīti
4. Literal Breakdown: *ṛta* + *dhīti* (thought, insight, from root *dhī-*); "thought/insight in accordance with cosmic order"
5. Natural English Translation: right-minded thought, insight aligned with cosmic order
6. Usage: A Vedic compound appearing in the Ṛgveda to characterize inspired or correct thought; confined to Vedic poetic contexts.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on productivity:** The base *ṛta* generates relatively few derivations outside Vedic literature. Its most productive derivational relationship is with *anṛta* (item 9 in P1), which is documented under that item. The *ṛta*-base does not participate in the same productive derivational patterns as *satya* in post-Vedic literature.

## Item 3 — यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

**Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Derived Form: यथार्थता
3. Transliteration: yathārthatā
4. Literal Breakdown: *yathārtha* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the state of being according to the object"
5. Natural English Translation: accuracy, correspondence to reality, literalness
6. Usage: Used in philosophical and literary contexts to denote the property of accurately corresponding to one's referent or object; evaluates whether a description, name, or cognition is accurate.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Derived Form: यथार्थत्व
3. Transliteration: yathārthatva
4. Literal Breakdown: *yathārtha* + abstract suffix *-tva*; "the property of being according to the object"
5. Natural English Translation: the property of accuracy, correspondence-to-object as a formal property
6. Usage: A more technical variant of *yathārthatā* appearing in Navya-Nyāya and epistemological commentary literature; less common than *yathārthatā*.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Derived Form: यथार्थज्ञान
3. Transliteration: yathārthajñāna
4. Literal Breakdown: *yathārtha* + *jñāna* (knowledge); "knowledge according to the object"
5. Natural English Translation: accurate knowledge, knowledge that corresponds to its object
6. Usage: Used in epistemological literature to denote a cognition that accurately apprehends its object as it is; functionally equivalent to *pramā* in many contexts.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. Base Form: यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. Derived Form: अयथार्थ
3. Transliteration: ayathārtha
4. Literal Breakdown: *a-* (negation) + *yathārtha*; "not according to the object"
5. Natural English Translation: inaccurate, not corresponding to reality, incorrect
6. Usage: Used in philosophical and grammatical literature as the negation of *yathārtha*, characterizing a description, name, or cognition that fails to correspond to its referent.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

## Item 4 — प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: प्रामाण्य
3. Transliteration: prāmāṇya
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramāṇa* + secondary nominal suffix *-ya* (with *vṛddhi* of initial syllable); "the property of being a valid means of cognition"
5. Natural English Translation: validity, epistemic authority, the quality of being a *pramāṇa*
6. Usage: A central technical term in Sanskrit epistemology denoting the property of validity or authority; debates about whether *prāmāṇya* is intrinsic (*svataḥ*) or extrinsic (*parataḥ*) are a major topic in Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: प्रामाणिक
3. Transliteration: prāmāṇika
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramāṇa* + adjectival suffix *-ika* (with *vṛddhi*); "pertaining to or having the character of a *pramāṇa*"
5. Natural English Translation: authoritative, reliable, valid, authentic
6. Usage: Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday contexts to characterize a source, person, or document as authoritative and reliable; in modern usage commonly means "authentic" or "genuine."
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: अप्रामाणिक
3. Transliteration: aprāmāṇika
4. Literal Breakdown: *a-* (negation) + *prāmāṇika*; "not authoritative, lacking validity"
5. Natural English Translation: inauthentic, lacking authority, unreliable, invalid
6. Usage: Used in philosophical, legal, and textual-critical contexts to characterize a source, argument, or document that lacks the authority or validity of a genuine *pramāṇa*.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: अप्रमाण
3. Transliteration: apramāṇa
4. Literal Breakdown: *a-* (negation) + *pramāṇa*; "not a valid means of cognition"
5. Natural English Translation: invalid means of cognition, non-authority
6. Usage: Used in epistemological literature to characterize a source or means of cognition that fails to yield valid knowledge; also used in legal contexts to denote evidence lacking probative force.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: प्रमाणता
3. Transliteration: pramāṇatā
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramāṇa* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "the state of being a *pramāṇa*"
5. Natural English Translation: the status of being a valid means of cognition, evidential force
6. Usage: Used in epistemological commentary literature to denote the property of being a *pramāṇa*; appears in discussions of what conditions must be met for a source to count as a valid means of knowledge.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: प्रमाणीकरण
3. Transliteration: pramāṇīkaraṇa
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramāṇa* + *-īkaraṇa* (making into, from causative of *kṛ-*); "making into a *pramāṇa*, authentication"
5. Natural English Translation: authentication, validation, certification
6. Usage: Used in legal and administrative contexts to denote the act of authenticating or validating a document, statement, or claim; common in modern Sanskrit-influenced legal language.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. Base Form: प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. Derived Form: प्रमाणभूत
3. Transliteration: pramāṇabhūta
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramāṇa* + *bhūta* (become, past participle of *bhū-*); "having become a *pramāṇa*"
5. Natural English Translation: one who has become an authority, a reliable source
6. Usage: Used in Buddhist epistemological literature to characterize a person or source that has attained the status of a valid authority; Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika* opens with the characterization of the Buddha as *pramāṇabhūta*.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

## Item 5 — प्रमा / *pramā*

**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. Derived Form: अप्रमा
3. Transliteration: apramā
4. Literal Breakdown: *a-* (negation) + *pramā*; "not valid cognition"
5. Natural English Translation: erroneous cognition, invalid apprehension
6. Usage: A technical epistemological term denoting a cognition that fails to accurately apprehend its object; contrasted with *pramā* and includes *bhrama* (error), *saṃśaya* (doubt), and related categories in some classifications.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. Derived Form: प्रमात्व
3. Transliteration: pramātva
4. Literal Breakdown: *pramā* + abstract suffix *-tva*; "the property of being valid cognition"
5. Natural English Translation: the property of being valid cognition
6. Usage: Appears in Navya-Nyāya technical literature as a formal term for the property that distinguishes valid from invalid cognition; less common than *prāmāṇya* but attested in epistemological commentary.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on productivity:** *Pramā* is primarily a technical term; its derivational productivity is limited compared to *pramāṇa*. The agent noun *pramātṛ* (knower, one who has valid cognition) is closely related but derives directly from the root *mā-* with prefix *pra-* and agent suffix *-tṛ*, rather than from the nominal *pramā* itself.

## Item 6 — सत् / *sat*

**Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सत्ता
3. Transliteration: sattā
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + abstract suffix *-tā*; "being-ness"
5. Natural English Translation: existence, being, reality
6. Usage: Used in philosophical literature (Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Vedānta) to denote the property of existence as such; appears in debates about whether existence is a universal property or a specific relation.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सत्त्व
3. Transliteration: sattva
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + abstract suffix *-tva*; "the state of being"
5. Natural English Translation: being, existence, essence; the quality of clarity/goodness (in Sāṃkhya)
6. Usage: In Sāṃkhya philosophy, *sattva* is the first of the three *guṇas*, associated with clarity, purity, and illumination; in general philosophical usage it also denotes genuine existence or substantiality.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सदसत्
3. Transliteration: sadasat
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + *asat* (dvandva compound); "being and non-being"
5. Natural English Translation: the real and the unreal, being and non-being
6. Usage: Used in philosophical literature to denote the totality of existence or to pose the question of the ontological status of the world; appears in Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic discussions of the nature of reality.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सच्चिदानन्द
3. Transliteration: saccidānanda
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* (being) + *cit* (consciousness) + *ānanda* (bliss); "being-consciousness-bliss"
5. Natural English Translation: being-consciousness-bliss (characterization of ultimate reality)
6. Usage: A central formula in Advaita Vedānta characterizing the nature of *Brahman* as the unity of pure being, pure consciousness, and pure bliss; used in philosophical and devotional contexts.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सत्पुरुष
3. Transliteration: satpuruṣa
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + *puruṣa* (man, person); "a genuine/good man"
5. Natural English Translation: a good man, a genuine person, a man of integrity
6. Usage: Used in ethical and narrative literature to characterize a person of genuine virtue and integrity; the *sat-* prefix marks authenticity and moral worth rather than mere appearance.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सत्कार
3. Transliteration: satkāra
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + *kāra* (doing, making, from root *kṛ-*); "doing what is good/right"
5. Natural English Translation: hospitality, honor, respectful treatment
6. Usage: Used in social and ethical contexts to denote the proper and respectful treatment of a guest, elder, or honored person; common in narrative and prescriptive literature.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सत्संग
3. Transliteration: satsaṃga
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* + *saṃga* (company, association); "company of the good/real"
5. Natural English Translation: association with the good, company of the virtuous, spiritual fellowship
6. Usage: Used in devotional and religious literature to denote the practice of associating with virtuous or spiritually realized persons; a central concept in bhakti traditions.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**

1. Base Form: सत् (*sat*)
2. Derived Form: सन्मार्ग
3. Transliteration: sanmārga
4. Literal Breakdown: *sat* (sandhi form *san-*) + *mārga* (path, way); "the right/true path"
5. Natural English Translation: the right path, the path of virtue
6. Usage: Used in ethical and devotional literature to denote the path of correct conduct and virtue; contrasted with *kumārga* (wrong path).
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

## Item 7 — असत् / *asat*

**Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: असत् (*asat*)
2. Derived Form: असत्कल्पना
3. Transliteration: asatkalpanā
4. Literal Breakdown: *asat* + *kalpanā* (imagination, construction, from root *kḷp-*); "construction of the unreal"
5. Natural English Translation: fabrication of the non-existent, false imagination
6. Usage: Used in philosophical literature to characterize the cognitive act of constructing or imagining something that has no real basis; appears in Vedāntic and Buddhist discussions of erroneous cognition.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: असत् (*asat*)
2. Derived Form: असत्ख्याति
3. Transliteration: asatkhyāti
4. Literal Breakdown: *asat* + *khyāti* (cognition, appearance, from root *khyā-*); "appearance of the non-existent"
5. Natural English Translation: the theory that erroneous cognition presents the non-existent as real
6. Usage: A technical term in Sanskrit epistemological debate about the nature of error (*khyātivāda*); the *asatkhyāti* theory holds that in erroneous cognition a non-existent object is presented as real, associated with the Mādhyamika Buddhist position.
7. Epistemic Status: [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: असत् (*asat*)
2. Derived Form: असत्कर्मन्
3. Transliteration: asatkarman
4. Literal Breakdown: *asat* + *karman* (action, deed); "wrong/bad action"
5. Natural English Translation: wrong action, evil deed
6. Usage: Used in ethical and religious literature as the negation of *sat-karman*, denoting actions that are morally incorrect or contrary to *dharma*.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Note on productivity:** *Asat* participates in compounding but generates fewer independent derivations than *sat*. Its most significant derivational relationship is with *asatya* (documented under item 1) and with the *khyātivāda* technical vocabulary.

## Item 10 — मिथ्या / *mithyā*

**Base Form:** मिथ्या (*mithyā*)

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Derived Form: मिथ्याज्ञान
3. Transliteration: mithyājñāna
4. Literal Breakdown: *mithyā* + *jñāna* (knowledge); "false knowledge"
5. Natural English Translation: false knowledge, erroneous cognition
6. Usage: Used in Advaita Vedānta and other philosophical literature to denote a cognition that apprehends its object incorrectly or presents the illusory as real; a standard term in discussions of *avidyā* (ignorance).
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Derived Form: मिथ्यावाद
3. Transliteration: mithyāvāda
4. Literal Breakdown: *mithyā* + *vāda* (doctrine, statement, from root *vad-*); "false doctrine"
5. Natural English Translation: false doctrine, erroneous teaching
6. Usage: Used in philosophical and polemical literature to characterize a philosophical position or teaching as false or mistaken; common in inter-school polemics.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Derived Form: मिथ्यात्व
3. Transliteration: mithyātva
4. Literal Breakdown: *mithyā* + abstract suffix *-tva*; "the property of being false/illusory"
5. Natural English Translation: falsity, illusoriness, the property of being *mithyā*
6. Usage: A technical term in Advaita Vedānta denoting the property of being neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal; used in formal philosophical definitions of the status of the phenomenal world.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Derived Form: मिथ्यादृष्टि

4. Literal Breakdown: *mithyā* + *dṛṣṭi* (view, seeing, from root *dṛś-*); "false view"
5. Natural English Translation: false view, wrong view, erroneous perspective
6. Usage: Used in Buddhist and Hindu philosophical literature to characterize a fundamentally mistaken view of reality; in Buddhist contexts it often refers specifically to the denial of karma or rebirth.
7. Epistemic Status: [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**

1. Base Form: मिथ्या (*mithyā*)
2. Derived Form: मिथ्याभिमान
3. Transliteration: mithyābhimāna
4. Literal Breakdown: *mithyā* + *abhimāna* (self-conceit, false identification, from *abhi-* + root *man-*); "false self-identification"
