# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

## Lexical Items

### 1. chiqan
**Form:** chiqan  
**Literal meaning:** true, real, genuine  
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic, correct

Used to describe statements that correspond to reality or things that are authentic and not false. Commonly appears in both everyday speech and formal contexts to affirm accuracy or genuineness of information, objects, or situations.

**[CORE]**

### 2. chiqap
**Form:** chiqap  
**Literal meaning:** truly, really (adverbial form)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, indeed, in truth

Adverbial form used to emphasize the truth or reality of a statement. Functions as an intensifier to confirm the veracity of what is being said or to express certainty about a claim.

**[CORE]**

### 3. llulla
**Form:** llulla  
**Literal meaning:** false, lie, deceptive  
**Typical English translations:** false, lie, untrue, deceptive

Describes statements that are not true or people who deceive. Used both as a noun (a lie) and as an adjective (false, lying), commonly employed in everyday contexts to identify falsehood or dishonesty.

**[CORE]**

### 4. llullakuy
**Form:** llullakuy  
**Literal meaning:** to lie, to deceive (verb with reflexive suffix -ku-)  
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods

Verbal form indicating the action of lying or deceiving. The reflexive suffix suggests an action that affects the subject's own moral standing or involves self-directed behavior in the act of deception.

**[CORE]**

### 5. allin
**Form:** allin  
**Literal meaning:** good, well, correct  
**Typical English translations:** good, well, correct, right, proper

Widely used term for positive evaluation across moral, practical, and aesthetic domains. Applied to conduct, objects, situations, and statements to indicate they meet standards of correctness, quality, or appropriateness.

**[CORE]**

### 6. mana allin
**Form:** mana allin  
**Literal meaning:** not good  
**Typical English translations:** bad, not good, wrong, improper

Negated form of allin used to express negative evaluation. Commonly employed to describe improper conduct, incorrect actions, or things that fail to meet expected standards.

**[CORE]**

### 7. chanin
**Form:** chanin  
**Literal meaning:** valuable, worthy, legitimate  
**Typical English translations:** valuable, worthy, legitimate, valid, authentic

Indicates something has proper value, legitimacy, or authenticity. Used in contexts evaluating the worth or validity of objects, claims, or persons, particularly in formal or legal contexts.

**[CORE]**

### 8. yachay
**Form:** yachay  
**Literal meaning:** to know, knowledge  
**Typical English translations:** to know, knowledge, to learn

Fundamental epistemic term referring to knowledge or the act of knowing. Used to distinguish between what is known with certainty versus what is merely believed or supposed.

**[CORE]**

### 9. yuyay
**Form:** yuyay  
**Literal meaning:** to think, to remember, thought  
**Typical English translations:** to think, to remember, thought, memory, opinion

Refers to mental processes including thinking, remembering, and forming opinions. Distinguished from yachay in that it indicates subjective mental activity rather than established knowledge.

**[CORE]**

### 10. cheqan
**Form:** cheqan  
**Literal meaning:** straight, direct, correct  
**Typical English translations:** straight, direct, correct, right, proper

Describes both physical straightness and metaphorical correctness. Applied to paths, conduct, and statements to indicate they are direct, proper, or morally upright.

**[CORE]**

### 11. pantay
**Form:** pantay  
**Literal meaning:** to err, to make a mistake  
**Typical English translations:** to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong, error

Verbal form indicating error or mistake. Used when someone makes an incorrect judgment, takes a wrong action, or holds a mistaken belief.

**[CORE]**

### 12. pantasqa
**Form:** pantasqa  
**Literal meaning:** mistaken, erroneous (past participle)  
**Typical English translations:** mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error

Participial form describing something that contains error or someone who is mistaken. Commonly used to characterize incorrect statements or misguided actions.

**[CORE]**

### 13. suti
**Form:** suti  
**Literal meaning:** name, clear, evident  
**Typical English translations:** name, clear, evident, obvious, manifest

Indicates clarity and evidential quality of something. Used to describe situations or facts that are clear, obvious, and not subject to doubt or misinterpretation.

**[CORE]**

### 14. rikch'ay
**Form:** rikch'ay  
**Literal meaning:** to resemble, to appear, to seem  
**Typical English translations:** to resemble, to appear, to seem, to look like

Describes resemblance or appearance. Used when evaluating whether something appears to be what it claims or resembles something else, often with implications about authenticity.

**[CORE]**

### 15. rikch'akuy
**Form:** rikch'akuy  
**Literal meaning:** to seem, to appear (with reflexive)  
**Typical English translations:** to seem, to appear, to give the impression

Reflexive form emphasizing subjective appearance or seeming. Used when something gives an impression that may or may not correspond to reality.

**[CORE]**

### 16. ch'ulla
**Form:** ch'ulla  
**Literal meaning:** single, odd (not paired), incomplete  
**Typical English translations:** single, odd, incomplete, lacking its pair

Describes something that is incomplete or lacking its proper complement. Can be used metaphorically to indicate something is not whole or genuine in its current state.

**[CORE]**

### 17. hunt'a
**Form:** hunt'a  
**Literal meaning:** full, complete, whole  
**Typical English translations:** full, complete, whole, entire, genuine

Indicates completeness and wholeness. Applied to objects, statements, or situations to indicate they are complete and lack nothing essential.

**[CORE]**

### 18. k'ari
**Form:** k'ari  
**Literal meaning:** truly, really, indeed (emphatic particle)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, indeed, certainly

Emphatic particle used to strengthen assertions and confirm truth. Functions as a discourse marker to emphasize the speaker's commitment to the veracity of a statement.

**[MARGINAL]**

### 19. qhapaq
**Form:** qhapaq  
**Literal meaning:** rich, noble, genuine, powerful  
**Typical English translations:** rich, noble, genuine, authentic, powerful

Historically significant term indicating nobility and authenticity. Used to describe things of genuine quality or high status, particularly in traditional or formal contexts.

**[CORE]**

### 20. ch'usaq
**Form:** ch'usaq  
**Literal meaning:** empty, hollow, vain  
**Typical English translations:** empty, hollow, vain, meaningless, false

Describes emptiness both physical and metaphorical. Applied to statements or promises that lack substance or truth, indicating they are hollow or meaningless.

**[CORE]**

### 21. kaq
**Form:** kaq  
**Literal meaning:** being, existing, that which is (agentive/substantive suffix)  
**Typical English translations:** being, existing, real, actual, that which is

Substantivizing suffix that indicates existence or being. Used to mark what actually exists or is real, as opposed to what is merely imagined or false.

**[CORE]**

### 22. mana kaq
**Form:** mana kaq  
**Literal meaning:** not being, non-existent  
**Typical English translations:** non-existent, unreal, false, not actual

Negated form indicating non-existence or unreality. Used to characterize things that do not actually exist or statements that have no basis in reality.

**[CORE]**

### 23. takyay
**Form:** takyay  
**Literal meaning:** to verify, to test, to prove  
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to test, to prove, to examine

Indicates the action of testing or verifying something to establish its truth or quality. Used in contexts where evidence or proof is being sought or established.

**[CORE]**

### 24. yachanayay
**Form:** yachanayay  
**Literal meaning:** to be knowable, to be able to be known  
**Typical English translations:** to be knowable, to be ascertainable, to be verifiable

Derived form indicating the possibility of something being known or verified. Used when discussing whether truth or knowledge about something can be established.

**[MARGINAL]**

### 25. imayna
**Form:** imayna  
**Literal meaning:** how, what kind, what manner  
**Typical English translations:** how, what kind, in what way, what manner

Interrogative term used to inquire about the nature, manner, or quality of something. Essential in contexts evaluating what something fundamentally is or how it truly appears.

**[CORE]**

### 26. chiqamanta
**Form:** chiqamanta  
**Literal meaning:** from truth, truly (ablative form)  
**Typical English translations:** truly, from truth, in truth, genuinely

Ablative form emphasizing origin in truth. Used to indicate that something proceeds from or is grounded in truth rather than falsehood or deception.

**[CORE]**

### 27. musyay
**Form:** musyay  
**Literal meaning:** to perceive, to sense, to realize  
**Typical English translations:** to perceive, to sense, to realize, to become aware

Indicates perceptual or intuitive awareness. Used when someone comes to recognize or realize the truth of something through direct experience or insight.

**[CORE]**

### 28. riqsiy
**Form:** riqsiy  
**Literal meaning:** to know (by acquaintance), to recognize  
**Typical English translations:** to know, to recognize, to be acquainted with, to identify

Indicates knowledge through direct acquaintance or recognition. Distinguished from yachay by emphasizing personal familiarity or ability to identify something as what it truly is.

**[CORE]**
