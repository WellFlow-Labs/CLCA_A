# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

The following documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Forms already introduced in P1 or P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full. Derivations are listed per base form; where a derived form itself appears as a base in P1 or P1b, this is noted. The inventory does not assume paradigm completeness. Gaps are preserved and noted where they are informative.

---

## 1. Base Form: **egia**

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Derived Form:** egiazko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zko (relational/adjectival suffix) — "of truth"
- **Natural English Translation:** true, genuine, real, authentic
- **Usage:** Adjectival form used to characterize statements, persons, objects, or situations as genuinely corresponding to reality. Documented in P1 (item 2) and extensively in P1b.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Derived Form:** egiaztatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zta- (verbalizing infix) + -tu (perfective suffix) — "to make true / to verify"
- **Natural English Translation:** to verify, to confirm, to authenticate, to certify
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used in legal, administrative, and journalistic contexts to denote the act of confirming or authenticating something. Documented in P1 (item 3).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base Form:** egia
- **Derived Form:** egiazkotasun
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zko (relational suffix) + -tasun (abstract nominal suffix) — "the quality of being true/genuine"
- **Natural English Translation:** truthfulness, veracity, genuineness, authenticity
- **Usage:** Abstract noun used in philosophical and formal written discourse to denote the property of being true or genuine. Documented in P1 (item 20). Less frequent in everyday speech.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base Form:** egia (via egiaztatu)
- **Derived Form:** egiaztatze
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zta- (verbalizing infix) + -tze (process nominalizer) — "the act/process of verifying"
- **Natural English Translation:** verification, authentication, the process of confirming
- **Usage:** Nominal form of *egiaztatu* referring to the ongoing process of verification. Used in administrative, legal, and technical procedural language. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 7).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base Form:** egia (via egiaztatu)
- **Derived Form:** egiaztapen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zta- (verbalizing infix) + -pen (deverbal result nominal suffix) — "result/act of verifying"
- **Natural English Translation:** verification, confirmation, attestation
- **Usage:** Deverbal noun referring to a completed act of verification or a document attesting to it. Slightly more formal than *egiaztatze*; used in administrative and legal registers. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 8).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base Form:** egia (via egiaztatu)
- **Derived Form:** egiaztagarri
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zta- (verbalizing infix) + -garri (adjectival suffix, "capable of / worthy of") — "capable of being verified"
- **Natural English Translation:** verifiable, capable of verification
- **Usage:** Adjective used in scientific, legal, and philosophical contexts to describe claims or facts that can in principle be verified. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 9).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7**
- **Base Form:** egia (via egiaztatu)
- **Derived Form:** egiaztaezin
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** egia (truth) + -zta- (verbalizing infix) + -ezin (negative potential suffix) — "impossible to verify"
- **Natural English Translation:** unverifiable, impossible to confirm
- **Usage:** Used in philosophical, scientific, and legal contexts to describe claims that cannot be confirmed or verified. Documented in P1b (Root 1, item 10).
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on egia derivational productivity:** The root *egia* generates derivations primarily through the intermediate verbalizing sequence *-zta-*, which then serves as a further base for nominal (*-tze*, *-pen*) and adjectival (*-garri*, *-ezin*) suffixation. Direct suffixation on *egia* itself is limited to *-zko* (yielding *egiazko*) and the *-zko* + *-tasun* chain (yielding *egiazkotasun*). No agentive (*-tzaile*, *-dari*) or other derivational forms on the bare root *egia* have been identified.

## 4. Base Form: **gezur**

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Derived Form:** gezurra
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie, falsehood) + -a (definite article suffix) — "the lie"
- **Natural English Translation:** the lie, the falsehood
- **Usage:** The definite singular form of *gezur*, functioning as the standard citation form in many contexts. This is inflectional rather than derivational, but is noted here because the bare root *gezur* and the definite form *gezurra* are both in active use and the distinction is relevant to morphological description.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Derived Form:** gezurrezko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie) + -rezko (relational/adjectival suffix, variant of *-zko* after certain stems) — "of falsehood"
- **Natural English Translation:** false, lying, counterfeit, fake
- **Usage:** Adjectival form used to characterize statements, documents, or representations as false or deceptive. Documented in P1 (item 5). Used in legal and moral evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Derived Form:** gezurtatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie) + -ta- (verbalizing infix) + -tu (perfective suffix) — "to give the lie to; to falsify"
- **Natural English Translation:** to give the lie to, to contradict, to falsify, to prove false
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used to describe the act of demonstrating that something is false or of contradicting a claim. Used in argumentative, journalistic, and legal contexts. Mentioned in P1 (item 4 usage note).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Derived Form:** gezurti
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie) + -ti (adjectival/nominal suffix indicating habitual tendency or characteristic) — "one given to lying"
- **Natural English Translation:** liar, lying (adjective), untruthful
- **Usage:** Adjective and noun used to characterize a person as habitually dishonest or given to telling lies. Common in everyday moral evaluation, particularly in interpersonal and educational contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base Form:** gezur
- **Derived Form:** gezurkeria
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezur (lie) + -keria (nominal suffix indicating a reprehensible practice or quality) — "the practice/habit of lying"
- **Natural English Translation:** lying, deceitfulness, dishonesty (as a practice or quality)
- **Usage:** Abstract noun used to denote habitual or systematic dishonesty as a moral failing. Carries a pejorative connotation. Used in moral and interpersonal evaluative contexts.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base Form:** gezur (via gezurtatu)
- **Derived Form:** gezurtatze
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** gezurtatu (to give the lie to) with *-tu* replaced by *-tze* (process nominalizer)
- **Natural English Translation:** the act of contradicting, refutation, falsification
- **Usage:** Process nominal of *gezurtatu*, used in argumentative and legal contexts to refer to the act of demonstrating that a claim is false.
- **Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on gezur derivational productivity:** The root *gezur* generates a moderately productive paradigm. The agentive/characterizing suffix *-ti* (yielding *gezurti*) and the pejorative practice suffix *-keria* (yielding *gezurkeria*) are both attested and in common use. The verbalizing sequence *-ta-tu* (yielding *gezurtatu*) parallels the pattern seen in *egiaztatu*. No *-tasun* abstract nominal directly on *gezur* has been identified as broadly attested.

## 8. Base Form: **ziur**

---

**Derived Form 1**
- **Base Form:** ziur
- **Derived Form:** ziurtatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -ta- (verbalizing infix) + -tu (perfective suffix) — "to make certain"
- **Natural English Translation:** to certify, to assure, to make sure, to confirm
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts to denote the act of making something certain or officially confirmed. Documented in P1 (item 9).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
- **Base Form:** ziur
- **Derived Form:** ziurtasun
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -tasun (abstract nominal suffix) — "the quality of being certain"
- **Natural English Translation:** certainty, sureness, assurance, reliability
- **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the state or quality of being certain or reliable. Documented in P1 (item 10). Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday discourse.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
- **Base Form:** ziur
- **Derived Form:** ziurrenik
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -enik (superlative/adverbializing suffix)
- **Natural English Translation:** certainly, most likely, probably, in all likelihood
- **Usage:** Epistemic adverb expressing high probability or near-certainty. One of the most common epistemic adverbs in Basque across all registers. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 4). Note that in actual use it frequently marks hedged probability rather than full certainty.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
- **Base Form:** ziur (via ziurtatu)
- **Derived Form:** ziurtapen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -ta- (verbalizing infix) + -pen (result nominal suffix) — "act/result of certifying"
- **Natural English Translation:** certification, assurance, confirmation
- **Usage:** Deverbal noun used in administrative and legal registers to refer to a formal act of certification or the document resulting from it. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 7).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
- **Base Form:** ziur (via ziurtatu)
- **Derived Form:** ziurtagiri
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziur (certain) + -ta- (verbalizing infix) + -giri (nominal suffix indicating document or instrument)
- **Natural English Translation:** certificate, certification document
- **Usage:** The standard Basque term for a certificate or official document attesting to a fact, qualification, or status. Widely used in administrative, educational, and legal contexts. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 8). One of the most frequently encountered derivatives of *ziur* in everyday administrative Basque.
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6**
- **Base Form:** ziur (via ziurtatu)
- **Derived Form:** ziurtatze
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal Breakdown:** ziurtatu (to certify) with *-tu* replaced by *-tze* (process nominalizer)
- **Natural English Translation:** certifying, the process of certification
- **Usage:** Process nominal of *ziurtatu*, used in administrative and procedural contexts. Documented in P1b (Root 2, item 18).
- **Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on ziur derivational productivity:** The root *ziur* generates a moderately productive paradigm. Its most institutionally embedded derivative is *ziurtagiri* (certificate), which has extended into technical domains (digital certificates, medical certificates, academic certificates). The *-tasun* abstract nominal (*ziurtasun*) and the adverb *ziurrenik* are both highly frequent. No adjectival *-garri* or negative *-ezin* forms directly on the *ziur* stem have been identified as broadly attested, in contrast to the *egiaztatu* paradigm.
