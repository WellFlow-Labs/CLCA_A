# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)
# Transliteration standard: IAST (consistent with P1 and P1b)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This document records attested derivational morphology for the P1 lexical items. The inventory draws on forms documented in classical Sanskrit literature, philosophical treatises, grammatical texts, legal literature, and (where noted) modern Sanskrit-medium usage. Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than repeated in full. The constraint against paradigm completion is observed throughout: only forms with genuine attestation or strong philological support are included. Gaps are preserved and noted explicitly.

The organization follows the P1 item list. For each P1 item, derivations are listed as attested. Where no common derivation beyond what P1b already documents is identified, this is stated explicitly.

## P1 Item 1: सत्य / *satya*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यता
3. **Transliteration:** satyatā
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *-tā* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state or property of being true"
5. **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, truth as an abstract property
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical and ethical discourse to name the abstract property of being true or truthful, as distinct from particular true statements or acts. Appears in characterizations of persons and propositions.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 1, item 1.*

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यत्व
3. **Transliteration:** satyatva
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *-tva* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being true"
5. **Natural English translation:** truthfulness, the property of being true
6. **Usage:** Parallel to *satyatā* but formed with the *-tva* suffix; used in philosophical prose to name the abstract property of truth. Both *satyatā* and *satyatva* are attested, with *satyatā* somewhat more common in ethical contexts and *satyatva* more common in technical philosophical argument.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सात्य
3. **Transliteration:** sātya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + secondary adjectival derivation (vrddhi of initial syllable); "relating to or characterized by truth"
5. **Natural English translation:** relating to truth, of the nature of truth
6. **Usage:** Attested as a secondary adjectival form in some philosophical and grammatical contexts, though less common than *satya* itself used adjectivally. The form is morphologically regular but its independent attestation is limited.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
2. **Derived Form:** सत्यापन
3. **Transliteration:** satyāpana
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *satya* + *āpana* (causative action noun from root *āp-*, to reach/attain); "causing truth to be established, verification"
5. **Natural English translation:** verification, attestation, confirmation
6. **Usage:** Used in legal and administrative contexts for the act of verifying or attesting to the truth of a statement or document. Standard in modern Sanskrit-medium official usage.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 1, item 7.*

## P1 Item 2: ऋत / *ṛta*

---

2. **Derived Form:** ऋतधामन्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtadhāman

5. **Natural English translation:** one whose dwelling is cosmic order; an epithet of Varuṇa and other Vedic deities
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities associated with cosmic order, particularly Varuṇa; evaluates divine nature in terms of conformity to *ṛta*.

5. **Natural English translation:** one who possesses or upholds cosmic order
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic literature as an epithet of deities and persons who uphold or embody *ṛta*; evaluates conformity to cosmic and moral order.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b supplementary expansion, item 2.*

5. **Natural English translation:** one who knows cosmic order, knower of the right
6. **Usage:** Used in Vedic literature for one who has knowledge of *ṛta*; evaluates knowledge in terms of conformity to the right order of things.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

*Note: Also documented in P1b supplementary expansion, item 3.*

6. **Usage:** A fixed phrase in Vedic hymns (Ṛgveda) designating the way of cosmic order along which the gods and the righteous travel; used in ritual and cosmological contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b supplementary expansion, item 4.*

---

**Derived Form 5**

2. **Derived Form:** ऋतचित्
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtacit
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *cit* (perceiving, knowing, from root *cit-*); "perceiving cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who perceives or knows cosmic order
6. **Usage:** Attested in Vedic hymns as an epithet of deities and seers who have insight into *ṛta*; the form is Vedic and does not extend into classical Sanskrit.

---

**Derived Form 6**

1. **Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
2. **Derived Form:** ऋतप्रजात
3. **Transliteration:** ṛtaprajāta
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *ṛta* + *prajāta* (born, produced, from *pra-* + root *jan-*); "born of cosmic order"
5. **Natural English translation:** born of right order, produced by cosmic truth
6. **Usage:** Attested in Vedic hymns as a characterization of divine beings or sacred fires understood as arising from *ṛta*; confined to Vedic usage.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note on productivity:** The root *ṛta* is derivationally productive primarily within Vedic Sanskrit. Its compound formation in post-Vedic classical Sanskrit is limited. The forms listed above represent the main attested expressions; no further common derivations are identified.

## P1 Item 3: यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Derived Form:** यथार्थता
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthatā
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *-tā* (abstract nominal suffix); "the state of being as the object is, accuracy"
5. **Natural English translation:** accuracy, correctness, fidelity to the object
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological and aesthetic contexts to name the abstract property of accurately representing or corresponding to an object; appears in discussions of the accuracy of descriptions, images, and cognitions.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Derived Form:** यथार्थत्व
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthatva
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *-tva* (abstract nominal suffix); "the property of being as the object is"
5. **Natural English translation:** accuracy, the property of corresponding to the object
6. **Usage:** Parallel to *yathārthatā*; used in philosophical prose to name the property of accurate correspondence. Both forms are attested; *yathārthatva* appears more frequently in technical epistemological argument.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Derived Form:** यथार्थज्ञान
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthajñāna
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *jñāna* (knowledge, from root *jñā-*); "knowledge that is as the object is"
5. **Natural English translation:** accurate knowledge, knowledge that corresponds to its object
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological literature to characterize cognition that correctly apprehends its object; closely related to *pramā* in function but emphasizing the correspondence relation explicitly.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**

1. **Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
2. **Derived Form:** यथार्थवादिन्
3. **Transliteration:** yathārthavādin
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *yathārtha* + *vādin* (one who speaks, from root *vad-*); "one who speaks as the object is"
5. **Natural English translation:** one who speaks accurately, a realist (in philosophical usage)
6. **Usage:** Used in philosophical contexts to characterize a speaker or position that asserts things as they actually are; in some contexts used to characterize philosophical realism as a position.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Note:** No verbal derivation from *yathārtha* is commonly attested. The form functions primarily as an adjective and adverb, with nominal abstracts (*-tā*, *-tva*) as its main derivational products.

## P1 Item 4: प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रामाण्य
3. **Transliteration:** prāmāṇya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pramāṇa* + *-ya* (abstract adjectival suffix, with vṛddhi of initial syllable); "the property of being a valid means of knowledge"
5. **Natural English translation:** validity, epistemic authority, the property of being a genuine means of knowledge
6. **Usage:** A central technical term in Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya epistemology for the property that makes a cognition or means of knowledge valid; debates about whether *prāmāṇya* is intrinsic or extrinsic are among the most important in Sanskrit epistemology.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 2, item 3.*

**Derived Form 2**

2. **Derived Form:** अप्रामाण्य

4. **Literal Breakdown:** *a-* (negation) + *prāmāṇya*; "invalidity, lack of epistemic authority"
5. **Natural English translation:** invalidity, lack of epistemic authority
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological literature for the property of a cognition or means of knowledge that fails to be valid; appears in discussions of how invalidity is recognized and what causes it.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 2, item 6.*

---

**Derived Form 3**

## P1 Item 5: प्रमा / *pramā*

---

**Derived Form 1**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमातृ
3. **Transliteration:** pramātṛ
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pra-* + *mātṛ* (agent noun from root *mā-*); "the one who measures correctly, the knower"
5. **Natural English translation:** the knower, the knowing subject, the epistemic agent
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological literature for the subject of valid cognition — the agent who employs a *pramāṇa* to arrive at *pramā*; one of the three elements of the epistemological triad.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 2, item 1.*

---

**Derived Form 2**

1. **Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
2. **Derived Form:** प्रमेय
3. **Transliteration:** prameya
4. **Literal Breakdown:** *pra-* + *meya* (gerundive of root *mā-*); "that which is to be measured or known"
5. **Natural English translation:** the object of valid cognition, the knowable
6. **Usage:** Used in epistemological literature for the object that valid cognition apprehends; in Nyāya, *prameya* is one of the sixteen categories subject to philosophical investigation.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: Also documented in P1b Root 2, item 2.*

---

**Note on further derivation:** Beyond *pramātṛ* and *prameya*, which are the standard members of the epistemological triad with *pramāṇa*, no additional common derivations from *pramā* itself (as distinct from *pramāṇa*) are identified. The form *pramā* is primarily a technical noun; its derivational productivity is absorbed into the *pramāṇa* cluster.

6. **Usage:** Used in Sāṃkhya philosophy as one of the three *guṇas* (constituents of *prakṛti*), characterized by clarity, lightness, and goodness; also used more broadly for the quality of genuine being or goodness in a person or thing.
