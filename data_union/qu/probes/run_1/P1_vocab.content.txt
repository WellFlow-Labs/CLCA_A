# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope:** Quechua is a dialect continuum with significant lexical variation across varieties (Cusco-Collao, Ayacucho, Ecuadorian, etc.). Items below are drawn primarily from Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao / Ayacucho-Chanka), the most extensively documented varieties. Variety-specific distribution is noted where relevant. Transliteration follows the standard three-vowel orthography used in academic Quechua linguistics (as in Cerrón-Palomino and related scholarship), with digraphs for ejectives (p', t', k', q', ch') and aspirates (ph, th, kh, qh, chh).

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; no further decomposition established
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, correct, right

Used to predicate truth or genuineness of a statement, person, or thing. Functions as both adjective and predicate nominal (e.g., *cheqam* 'it is true/real', with validational enclitic *-m/-mi*). Participates in productive derivation: *cheqakaय़* 'to become true/real', *cheqanchaय़* 'to verify, to confirm'.
**[CORE]**

**2.**
**Form:** cheqanchay
**Transliteration:** cheqanchay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative/verbalizer) + -y (infinitive nominalizer)
**Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to establish as true, to correct

Used for the act of verifying or confirming the accuracy or correctness of something. Appears in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts where a claim or document is checked against reality.
**[CORE]**

---

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + -q (agentive/adjectival nominalizer)
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real (as attributive adjective or nominalized agent)

Functions as an attributive adjective meaning 'true' or 'genuine', and can nominalize to mean 'one who is truthful'. Closely related to *cheqa* but the *-q* form is more commonly used in attributive position before nouns.
**[CORE]**

**4.**
**Form:** cheqasqa
**Transliteration:** cheqasqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** cheqa + -sqa (past participial suffix, here adjectivalized)
**Typical English translations:** verified, confirmed, established as true

Used to describe something that has been confirmed or verified, often in contexts of official or social validation. The participial form implies a completed process of verification.

**5.**
**Form:** chiqap
**Transliteration:** chiqap
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root (possibly related to *cheqa* by dialectal variation); genitive/adverbial form
**Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in truth

Functions primarily as an adverb or intensifier meaning 'truly' or 'really', used to assert the genuine nature of something. Attested in Ayacucho-Chanka Quechua; the relationship to *cheqa* is debated in the literature.

Used in contexts where the authenticity or quality of a made object or action is evaluated. Relevant in aesthetic and moral evaluation of craftsmanship or conduct.

**7.**
**Form:** allin
**Transliteration:** allin
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root
**Typical English translations:** good, right, well, proper, fine

Broadly used to evaluate persons, objects, conduct, and states as good, correct, or proper. One of the most productive roots in Quechua moral and evaluative vocabulary; participates in derivation: *allinchaय़* 'to improve, to fix, to make right', *allinkaय़* 'to become good/well'.
**[CORE]**

**8.**
**Form:** allinchaय़
**Transliteration:** allinchay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** allin + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
**Typical English translations:** to fix, to correct, to improve, to make right, to repair

Used for the act of correcting or setting something right — a statement, an object, a situation, or conduct. Relevant in contexts of moral correction and practical repair alike.
**[CORE]**

**9.**
**Form:** mana cheqa
**Transliteration:** mana cheqa
**Literal meaning/morphology:** mana (negator) + cheqa (true/real)
**Typical English translations:** untrue, false, not genuine, incorrect

The standard negated form used to predicate falsity or inauthenticity. Functions as a fixed evaluative expression in contexts of assessing statements and representations.
**[CORE]**

**10.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root
**Typical English translations:** lie, false, liar, deceptive

Used as both noun ('a lie') and adjective ('false, deceptive') to characterize statements, persons, or representations as dishonest or counterfeit. Contrasts directly with *cheqa*. Productive: *llullakuय़* 'to lie (habitually)', *llullaq* 'liar'.
**[CORE]**

**11.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Literal meaning/morphology:** llulla + -ku- (reflexive/middle) + -y (infinitive)
**Typical English translations:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods

The standard verb for the act of lying or deceiving. Used in moral and legal contexts to characterize conduct as dishonest.
**[CORE]**

**12.**
**Form:** pantay
**Transliteration:** pantay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root + -y (infinitive); root *panta-* 'to err, to be mistaken'
**Typical English translations:** to err, to be mistaken, to be wrong, to make an error

Used to describe being in error or making a mistake, applicable to statements, judgments, and conduct. Relevant in evaluative contexts where correctness is at issue. Productive: *pantasqa* 'mistaken, erroneous', *pantachiy* 'to mislead, to cause to err'.

Used attributively or predicatively to describe a statement, belief, or person as mistaken or incorrect. Common in everyday and pedagogical contexts.

**Typical English translations:** to mislead, to cause to err, to deceive, to lead astray

Used in contexts where an agent deliberately or inadvertently causes another to hold a false belief or make an error. Relevant in moral and legal evaluation of conduct.

**24.**
**Form:** kay
**Transliteration:** kay
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Root; 'to be, being, this'
**Typical English translations:** to be, being, existence, this (thing)

The copular root, used in predications of identity and essential nature ('X is Y'). Fundamental to all evaluative predication in Quechua. Also functions as a demonstrative ('this').
**[CORE]**

**25.**
**Form:** -m / -mi
**Transliteration:** -m / -mi
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Enclitic; direct evidential / validational marker
**Typical English translations:** (it is) indeed, (I assert that), (I know directly that)

Attached to predicates or focused constituents to assert that the speaker has direct evidence for the truth of a claim. Central to the Quechua evidentiality system and to the marking of epistemic commitment to accuracy.
**[CORE]**

**26.**
**Form:** -s / -si
**Transliteration:** -s / -si
**Literal meaning/morphology:** Enclitic; reportative evidential marker
**Typical English translations:** (it is said that), (reportedly), (I heard that)

Marks a statement as based on reported rather than direct evidence. Contrasts with *-m/-mi* and is relevant to the evaluation of the reliability and source of information.
**[CORE]**
