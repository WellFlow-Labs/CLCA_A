# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Swahili (sw)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: -kweli**

- **Root form:** kweli
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*kweli*, *ukweli*, *kweli kweli*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Assertion of factual correspondence; the quality of a statement or situation being true. Serves as the primary indigenous (non-Arabic-derived) root for truth as propositional accuracy.

---

**Root 2: ḥaqq / hakika family (Arabic ح-ق-ق)**

- **Root form:** haki / hakika / uhakika
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (*haki*, *hakika*, *uhakika*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, entitlement, and certainty understood as grounded in reality. This Arabic root cluster covers truth as moral and legal right, as factual certainty, and as the underlying nature of things. It is the most semantically wide-ranging root in the domain.

---

**Root 3: -sahihi (Arabic ص-ح-ح)**

- **Root form:** sahihi / usahihi
- **P1 items participating:** 2 (*sahihi*, *usahihi*)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Correctness and precision as evaluable properties of statements, documents, calculations, and procedures. This root anchors the domain of technical and procedural accuracy.

---

**Note on scope:** The Arabic-derived roots *amini/uaminifu*, *halisi/uhalisi*, and *thibitisha/uthibitisho* are also productive in this domain but participate in fewer P1 items and are treated as secondary. The *-kweli*, *haki/hakika*, and *sahihi* roots are selected as the three central roots for expansion.

---

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### ROOT 1: -kweli

---

**1.**
**Form:** kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** Root *-kweli*; bare form functioning as noun or adverb
**Natural English translation:** truth; truly; really
**Context of use:** Used in everyday assertions to affirm that a statement is factually accurate. Also used as a discourse particle to invite or confirm agreement: *kweli?* ("really?").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ukweli
**Transliteration:** ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (class 11/14 abstract prefix) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truth; truthfulness; veracity
**Context of use:** The standard abstract nominal form used in moral, legal, and philosophical discourse when referring to truth as a property or value. Already listed in P1; included here as the primary nominal derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** kweli kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** Reduplication of *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** really truly; genuinely; for real; in actual fact
**Context of use:** Intensified form used in spoken Swahili to emphasize sincerity or factual accuracy. Already listed in P1; included here as the primary intensified derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** kwa kweli
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* (prepositional particle: by, with, in) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truly; in truth; honestly; in earnest
**Context of use:** Adverbial phrase used to frame a statement as sincere or factually grounded. Common in both spoken and written registers, including religious and moral discourse: *kwa kweli nakuambia* ("I tell you truly").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** kwa kweli kweli
**Transliteration:** kwa kweli kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kwa* + reduplicated *kweli kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truly and sincerely; in all honesty; absolutely for real
**Context of use:** Further intensified adverbial phrase used in emphatic spoken assertions. More colloquial than *kwa kweli*; used when a speaker wishes to stress complete sincerity or factual certainty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** kweli kabisa
**Transliteration:** kweli kabisa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* + *kabisa* (completely, absolutely; from Arabic *كبيسة*)
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true; completely true; the absolute truth
**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to assert that something is entirely and without qualification true. *Kabisa* functions as an intensifier across many Swahili evaluative expressions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ukweli wa mambo
**Transliteration:** ukweli wa mambo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *wa* (associative particle) + *mambo* (things, matters, affairs; plural of *jambo*)
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter; the facts of the case; how things really are
**Context of use:** Used in discourse to refer to the actual state of affairs, as opposed to appearances or claims. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ukweli mtupu
**Transliteration:** ukweli mtupu
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *mtupu* (pure, mere, nothing but; class 11 agreement)
**Natural English translation:** the plain truth; the bare truth; nothing but the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to assert that a statement contains no admixture of falsehood or embellishment. Equivalent in function to English "the plain truth."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** sema ukweli
**Transliteration:** sema ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *sema* (imperative of *kusema*, to speak, to say) + *ukweli*
**Natural English translation:** tell the truth; speak the truth
**Context of use:** Imperative construction used in moral instruction, legal proceedings, and everyday confrontations. Equivalent to "tell the truth" as a directive.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** kusema ukweli
**Transliteration:** kusema ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ku-* (infinitive prefix) + *sema* + *ukweli*
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth; to speak truthfully
**Context of use:** Infinitival phrase used as a subject or object in moral and legal discourse: *kusema ukweli ni wajibu* ("telling the truth is a duty").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** ukweli unauma
**Transliteration:** ukweli unauma
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *u-na-uma* (class 11 subject prefix + present tense + *uma*, to hurt/sting)
**Natural English translation:** the truth hurts
**Context of use:** A widely used proverbial or semi-proverbial expression acknowledging that truthful statements can be painful to hear. Used in everyday moral and interpersonal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** kweli ni kweli
**Transliteration:** kweli ni kweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* + *ni* (copula) + *kweli*
**Natural English translation:** truth is truth; the truth is the truth
**Context of use:** Used as an emphatic assertion that a fact cannot be denied or altered regardless of circumstances. Functions as a rhetorical affirmation in argument and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ukweli na uwazi
**Transliteration:** ukweli na uwazi
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *na* (and) + *uwazi* (openness, transparency; *u-* + *wazi*, open)
**Natural English translation:** truth and transparency; honesty and openness
**Context of use:** A paired expression used in governance, journalism, and civil society discourse to describe the values of honest and open communication. Common in institutional mission statements and political rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ukweli na haki
**Transliteration:** ukweli na haki
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *na* + *haki* (right, justice, truth)
**Natural English translation:** truth and justice; truth and rights
**Context of use:** A paired expression used in legal, political, and moral discourse. Common in the names of advocacy organizations and in discourse about accountability and justice.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** tafuta ukweli
**Transliteration:** tafuta ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *tafuta* (imperative of *kutafuta*, to search for, to seek) + *ukweli*
**Natural English translation:** seek the truth; search for the truth
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, philosophical, and religious discourse to describe the pursuit of factual or moral truth. Also appears as a motto or slogan in educational and media contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ukweli wa kihistoria
**Transliteration:** ukweli wa kihistoria
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *wa* + *ki-* (adjectival class prefix) + *historia* (history, from English/Portuguese)
**Natural English translation:** historical truth; historical accuracy
**Context of use:** Used in academic, journalistic, and political discourse when evaluating whether accounts of past events are factually accurate. Common in discussions of historiography and collective memory.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** ukweli wa kisayansi
**Transliteration:** ukweli wa kisayansi
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *wa* + *ki-* + *sayansi* (science, from English)
**Natural English translation:** scientific truth; empirical truth
**Context of use:** Used in academic and educational discourse to refer to truths established through scientific method. Contrasts with *ukweli wa kidini* (religious truth) in philosophical and public discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** ukweli wa kidini
**Transliteration:** ukweli wa kidini
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *wa* + *ki-* + *dini* (religion, from Arabic *دين*)
**Natural English translation:** religious truth; doctrinal truth
**Context of use:** Used in theological and interfaith discourse to refer to truths held on the basis of religious authority or revelation. Contrasts with empirical or scientific truth in philosophical discussion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** mwambie ukweli
**Transliteration:** mwambie ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *m-* (object prefix, class 1) + *ambie* (subjunctive of *kuambia*, to tell) + *ukweli*
**Natural English translation:** tell him/her the truth
**Context of use:** A common directive construction used in everyday interpersonal contexts, instructing someone to be honest with a third party.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ukweli uliofichwa
**Transliteration:** ukweli uliofichwa
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *u-lio-fichwa* (relative clause: class 11 subject + relative marker + *fichwa*, passive of *ficha*, to hide)
**Natural English translation:** hidden truth; concealed truth; suppressed truth
**Context of use:** Used in investigative journalism, political discourse, and everyday speech to refer to facts that have been deliberately concealed. Common in contexts of exposé or revelation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** nusu ukweli
**Transliteration:** nusu ukweli
**Morpheme breakdown:** *nusu* (half) + *ukweli*
**Natural English translation:** half-truth; partial truth
**Context of use:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse to describe a statement that is technically accurate but misleading because it omits relevant information. Common in media criticism and political commentary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** ukweli mzima
**Transliteration:** ukweli mzima
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *mzima* (whole, complete, full; class 11 agreement)
**Natural English translation:** the whole truth; the full truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral contexts to insist on complete disclosure without omission. Parallels the legal formula "the whole truth and nothing but the truth."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** kweli au uongo
**Transliteration:** kweli au uongo
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* + *au* (or) + *uongo* (falsehood)
**Natural English translation:** true or false; truth or lie
**Context of use:** Used in educational contexts (true/false questions), in everyday challenges to a statement's accuracy, and in philosophical discussion of bivalence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** ukweli unaosimama
**Transliteration:** ukweli unaosimama
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ukweli* + *u-nao-simama* (class 11 subject + present relative + *simama*, to stand, to hold firm)
**Natural English translation:** truth that stands; enduring truth; truth that holds up
**Context of use:** Used in religious, moral, and rhetorical discourse to describe truths considered permanent or unassailable. Common in sermon and oratory registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**25.**
**Form:** kweli hii
**Transliteration:** kweli hii
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kweli* + *hii* (this; demonstrative, class 9)
**Natural English translation:** this truth; this fact
**Context of use:** Demonstrative nominal phrase used in discourse to refer back to a specific truth or fact just stated or about to be stated. Common in both spoken and written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note:** The *-kweli* root is moderately productive. Its primary expansion is through nominal modification (*ukweli wa X*), adverbial phrases (*kwa kweli*), and imperative constructions (*sema ukweli*). It does not generate a large number of lexicalized compound forms beyond those listed above.

### ROOT 2: haki / hakika (Arabic ح-ق-ق)

---

**1.**
**Form:** haki
**Transliteration:** haki
**Morpheme breakdown:** From Arabic *حق* (ḥaqq); bare form
**Natural English translation:** right; justice; truth; entitlement; due
**Context of use:** Core evaluative term covering moral rightness, legal entitlement, and factual truth. Already listed in P1; included here as the primary form of this root cluster.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** hakika
**Transliteration:** hakika
**Morpheme breakdown:** From Arabic *حقيقة* (ḥaqīqa); bare form
**Natural English translation:** certainty; for sure; definitely
**Context of use:** Used as a noun and discourse particle affirming certainty. Already listed in P1; included here as a primary form of this root cluster.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** uhakika
**Transliteration:** uhakika
**Morpheme breakdown:** *u-* (abstract prefix) + *hakika*
**Natural English translation:** certainty; reliability; assurance; sureness
**Context of use:** Abstract noun referring to the state of being certain or reliable. Already listed in P1; included here as a primary nominal derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**

**Transliteration:** haki za binadamu

**Natural English translation:** human rights

**Note:** This root cluster (*haki/hakika*) is highly productive, particularly *haki* in the domain of rights discourse and *kuhakikisha* in everyday procedural language. The forms listed above represent the 30 most common.

**Note:** The *-sahihi* root is moderately productive. Its most notable semantic extension is into the domain of signatures and document authentication (*sahihi ya mkono*, *weka sahihi*, etc.), where *sahihi* functions as the standard Swahili word for "signature." This is a significant semantic shift from the core meaning of "correct/accurate." Additional attested forms include *sahihi ya kampuni* (company seal/signature), *sahihi ya daktari* (doctor's signature), and further *usahihi wa X* constructions in technical domains.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three central roots in contexts where the contribution of the root appears to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From -kweli:**

- *kweli kweli* is used not only to assert factual truth but also to express emotional sincerity and intensity in contexts that are not primarily about propositional accuracy (e.g., *nakupenda kweli kweli*, "I truly love you").

- *ukweli* appears in the expression *ukweli wa sanaa* (the truth of art / artistic truth), where it is applied to aesthetic evaluation rather than factual correspondence.

- *kwa kweli* appears in religious contexts as a marker of sincere devotion or prayer, where the relevant domain is sincerity of feeling rather than factual accuracy.

---

**From haki/hakika:**

- *haki* is used in expressions of moral desert and punishment (*alipata haki yake*, "he got what he deserved"), where the relevant domain is retributive justice rather than truth or factual rightness.

- *kuhakikisha* is used in everyday procedural contexts entirely unrelated to truth evaluation, such as *hakikisha mlango umefungwa* ("make sure the door is closed"), where the relevant domain is task completion and reliability rather than epistemic certainty.

- *haki* appears in the expression *haki ya mwandishi* (author's right / copyright), where the relevant domain is intellectual property rather than truth or justice in the moral sense.

- *uhakika* appears in *uhakika wa chakula* (food security) and *uhakika wa usalama* (security assurance), where the relevant domain is material reliability and stability rather than epistemic certainty.

- *haki* appears in *haki ya njia* (right of way), a legal and traffic term where the relevant domain is spatial entitlement rather than moral or epistemic rightness.

---

**From -sahihi:**

- *sahihi* has developed the meaning "signature" (*sahihi ya mkono*, *weka sahihi*), functioning as the standard Swahili term for a handwritten or official signature on a document. In this use, the root's contribution is authentication and authorization rather than propositional correctness.

- *kusahihisha* is used in the context of correcting student work (*kusahihisha mitihani*, "to mark/correct examinations"), where the relevant domain is pedagogical evaluation and grading rather than truth-determination in a philosophical sense.

- *sahihi* appears in *sahihi ya kampuni* (company seal / company signature), where the relevant domain is institutional authorization rather than factual accuracy.

- *usahihi* appears in *usahihi wa tafsiri* (accuracy of translation), where the relevant domain is fidelity of representation between two texts rather than correspondence to external facts.
