# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from observed patterns in P1 and P2: which terms appear in the same contexts, which are contrasted with each other, which share collocational partners, and which participate in the same discourse registers. No theoretical framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns are ambiguous or overlapping, this is noted explicitly. Items that do not fit any observed grouping are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to evaluate statements and claims as true or false

**Label:** Propositional truth and falsehood evaluation

**Definition:** These terms are used primarily to evaluate the truth-value of statements, propositions, testimonies, and declarations — i.e., whether what is said corresponds to what is the case. They appear characteristically in legal testimony contexts (ψευδής κατάθεση, λέω την αλήθεια), philosophical discourse about truth, and everyday contexts where someone asserts or denies that a claim is accurate. The core contrast in this group is between terms that affirm correspondence with reality and terms that deny it.

**Representative expressions:**

- αληθής (*alithís*) — used of statements and propositions
- αλήθεια (*alíthia*) — the primary noun for truth in propositional contexts
- αληθινός (*alithinós*) — colloquial adjective for true/real
- αληθεύω (*alithévo*) — to speak the truth
- αναλήθεια (*analíthia*) — formal noun for untruth
- αναληθής (*analithís*) — formal adjective for untrue
- ψευδής (*psevdís*) — formal adjective for false (of statements)
- ψεύδος (*psévdhos*) — the primary noun for falsehood
- ψέμα (*pséma*) — colloquial noun for a lie
- ψεύτης (*psévtis*) — the person who makes false statements
- λέω την αλήθεια (*léo tin alíthia*) — to tell the truth
- λέω ψέματα (*léo pséma­ta*) — to tell lies
- ψευδής κατάθεση (*psevdhís katáthesi*) — false testimony
- ψευδής δήλωση (*psevdhís dhílosi*) — false declaration
- ψευδής ισχυρισμός (*psevdhís ischirismós*) — false claim
- αλήθεια ή ψέμα (*alíthia í pséma*) — truth or lie (binary framing)
- Αυτό είναι ψέμα (*Aftó íne pséma*) — That is a lie
- η αλήθεια είναι ότι (*i alíthia íne óti*) — the truth is that…
- ψεύδομαι (*psévdhome*) — to lie (formal verb)
- ψευδώς (*psevdhós*) — falsely (formal adverb)

**Notes:** αληθής and ψευδής are the formal/philosophical pair; αληθινός and ψέμα/ψεύτικος are the colloquial equivalents. The legal register draws heavily on ψευδής (ψευδής κατάθεση, ψευδής καταγγελία, ψευδής δήλωση). The everyday register uses λέω ψέματα and λέω την αλήθεια as the canonical expressions for the same acts.

## Group 2: Terms used in evaluating objects, documents, and works as genuine or not genuine

**Label:** Genuineness and its absence in objects and documents

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a physical object, document, artwork, or text is what it purports to be — i.e., whether it is the real thing or a fabrication, copy, or imitation. They appear characteristically in legal, commercial, archival, and art-market contexts. The central contrast is between items that are what they claim to be (γνήσιος, αυθεντικός, πρωτότυπο) and items that are not (πλαστός, νόθος, κίβδηλος, ψεύτικος in material senses).

**Representative expressions:**

- γνήσιος (*gníssios*) — genuine, of legitimate origin
- γνησιότητα (*gnissiótita*) — genuineness, authenticity
- γνήσιο αντίγραφο (*gnísio andígrafo*) — certified true copy
- γνήσιο προϊόν (*gnísio proión*) — genuine product
- αυθεντικός (*afthentikós*) — authentic, original
- αυθεντικότητα (*afthentikótita*) — authenticity
- αυθεντικό έργο τέχνης (*afthentikó érgo téchnis*) — authentic work of art
- πρωτότυπο (*protótipo*) — the original (as opposed to a copy)
- πρωτότυπο και αντίγραφο (*protótipo ke andígrafo*) — original and copy
- στο πρωτότυπο (*sto protótipo*) — in the original
- αντίγραφο (*andígrafo*) — copy, reproduction
- επικυρωμένο αντίγραφο (*epikirоméno andígrafo*) — certified copy
- ακριβές αντίγραφο εκ του πρωτοτύπου — true copy from the original (legal formula)
- πλαστός (*plastós*) — forged, fabricated
- πλαστή ταυτότητα (*plastí taftótita*) — forged identity document
- πλαστό διαβατήριο (*plastó dhiavatírio*) — forged passport
- πλαστή υπογραφή (*plastí ipografí*) — forged signature
- πλαστά έγγραφα (*plastá éngrafa*) — forged documents
- πλαστογραφία (*plastografía*) — document forgery
- νόθος (*nóthos*) — spurious, of doubtful authenticity
- νόθο κείμενο (*nótho kímeno*) — spurious text
- νοθεία (*nothía*) — adulteration, falsification
- νοθεύω (*nothévo*) — to adulterate, to falsify
- κίβδηλος (*kívdhilos*) — counterfeit, debased (more literary)
- ψεύτικο δέρμα (*psévtiko dhérma*) — fake leather (material sense)
- παραποίηση (*parapiísi*) — falsification, tampering
- πιστοποίηση γνησιότητας (*pistopiísi gnissiótitas*) — certification of authenticity

**Notes:** γνήσιος and αυθεντικός overlap considerably in this group but are not identical: γνήσιος emphasizes legitimate origin (contrasting with νόθος), while αυθεντικός emphasizes that something is the original rather than a copy or imitation (contrasting with πλαστός and αντίγραφο). πλαστός is the standard legal term for deliberate forgery; νόθος is used more in philological and historical contexts for texts of uncertain attribution; κίβδηλος is literary and rare in everyday speech. νοθεία covers adulteration of substances (food, fuel) as well as document falsification, giving it a broader range than πλαστογραφία.

## Group 3: Terms used in formal processes of checking, certifying, and establishing facts

**Label:** Verification, certification, and establishment of facts

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where a claim, document, or fact is subjected to a formal or procedural process of checking or confirmation — in scientific, legal, administrative, and journalistic registers. They describe both the act of checking (επαληθεύω, διαπιστώνω) and the formal instruments that result from such checking (πιστοποιητικό, απόδειξη, τεκμήριο). The shared feature is that truth or validity is not simply asserted but established through a recognized procedure.

**Representative expressions:**

- επαληθεύω (*epalithévo*) — to verify, to confirm as true
- επαλήθευση (*epalíthefsi*) — verification, the act of confirming
- επαληθεύσιμος (*epalithéfsimos*) — verifiable
- ανεπαλήθευτος (*anepalítheftos*) — unverified
- ανεπαλήθευτες πληροφορίες — unverified information
- διαπιστώνω (*dhiapistóno*) — to ascertain, to establish (a fact)
- διαπίστωση (*dhiapístoси*) — finding, ascertainment
- πιστοποιώ (*pistopiό*) — to certify, to attest
- πιστοποίηση (*pistopiísi*) — certification, attestation
- πιστοποιητικό (*pistopiitikó*) — certificate
- πιστοποίηση ταυτότητας (*pistopiísi taftótitas*) — identity verification
- τεκμήριο (*tekmírio*) — evidence, indicator (typically indirect)
- τεκμηριώνω (*tekmirióno*) — to substantiate, to document
- τεκμηρίωση (*tekmiriósi*) — documentation, evidence base
- τεκμηριωμένος (*tekmiriomenos*) — substantiated, evidence-based
- απόδειξη (*apódhiksi*) — proof, demonstration; also receipt
- έγκυρος (*éngiros*) — valid, carrying recognized authority
- εγκυρότητα (*engirotita*) — validity
- έγκυρη πηγή (*éngiri pigí*) — reliable/authoritative source
- έγκυρο έγγραφο (*éngiro éngrafo*) — valid document
- άκυρος (*ákiros*) — invalid, void
- ακυρώνω (*akiróno*) — to cancel, to annul
- ακύρωση (*akírosi*) — cancellation, annulment

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 by its procedural character: the terms here describe not just whether something is true but whether it has been checked, certified, or formally established. επαληθεύω and διαπιστώνω describe the act of checking; πιστοποιητικό and απόδειξη describe the formal output of such checking. έγκυρος and άκυρος describe the status of documents and decisions with respect to formal requirements — a document can be έγκυρος (formally valid) without anyone having recently verified its content. τεκμήριο is specifically associated with indirect evidence from which a conclusion is inferred, distinguishing it from απόδειξη (direct proof or demonstration).

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate the reliability and trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Label:** Credibility and trustworthiness of persons and sources

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a person, institution, or source of information can be relied upon — i.e., whether their testimony, reports, or behavior merit confidence. They appear characteristically in legal contexts (evaluating witnesses), journalistic contexts (evaluating sources), and interpersonal contexts (evaluating friends and partners). The central question is not whether a specific statement is true but whether the person or source is the kind of entity whose statements can generally be trusted.

**Representative expressions:**

- αξιόπιστος (*aksiópistos*) — reliable, trustworthy, credible
- αξιοπιστία (*aksiopistía*) — credibility, reliability
- αναξιόπιστος (*anaksiópistos*) — unreliable, not credible
- αναξιοπιστία (*anaksiopistía*) — lack of credibility
- αξιόπιστη πηγή (*aksiópistos pigí*) — reliable source
- αξιόπιστος μάρτυρας (*aksiópistos mártyras*) — credible witness
- αξιόπιστη μαρτυρία (*aksiópistos martyría*) — credible testimony
- αξιόπιστος συνομιλητής (*aksiópistos sinomilitís*) — credible interlocutor
- Δεν είναι αξιόπιστος (*Dhen íne aksiópistos*) — He/she is not credible
- εμπιστοσύνη (*embistosíni*) — trust, confidence
- εμπιστεύομαι (*embistévome*) — to trust, to confide in
- εμπιστευτικός (*embisteftikós*) — confidential
- πιστός (*pistós*) — faithful, loyal, reliable
- πιστή μετάφραση (*pistí metáfrasi*) — faithful translation
- καλή πίστη (*kalí písti*) — good faith
- κακή πίστη (*kakí písti*) — bad faith
- απιστία (*apistía*) — infidelity, disloyalty
- άπιστος (*ápistos*) — unfaithful, disloyal

**Notes:** αξιόπιστος is the primary term for source credibility in journalistic and legal registers; εμπιστοσύνη and εμπιστεύομαι are the primary terms for interpersonal trust. πιστός covers both personal loyalty (πιστός φίλος) and fidelity of representation (πιστή μετάφραση), which places it at the boundary between this group and Group 2. καλή πίστη / κακή πίστη are legal and moral terms for the intent behind an action rather than the reliability of a person per se, but they participate in the same evaluative register.

## Group 5: Terms used in discourse to signal sincerity, frankness, or the introduction of a candid statement

**Label:** Sincerity-marking and truth-framing in discourse

**Definition:** These expressions function as discourse devices — they signal that what follows is a frank, sincere, or candid statement, often one that the speaker might otherwise hesitate to make. They appear at the beginning of turns or clauses, and their primary function is pragmatic rather than propositional: they frame the speaker's stance rather than evaluate the truth-value of a specific claim. Many are fixed phrases or formulaic frames.

**Representative expressions:**

- Αλήθεια; (*Alíthia?*) — Really? (surprise/confirmation marker)
- Αλήθεια, ήθελα να σε ρωτήσω… — By the way, I wanted to ask you… (topic-shift marker)
- στ' αλήθεια (*st' alíthia*) — truly, honestly (sincerity marker)
- Η αλήθεια είναι ότι… (*I alíthia íne óti…*) — The truth is that… (frank-statement frame)
- Για να λέμε την αλήθεια… (*Ya na léme tin alíthia…*) — To tell the truth… (candor frame)
- Αν θέλεις την αλήθεια… (*An thélis tin alíthia…*) — If you want the truth… (candor frame)
- Πες μου την αλήθεια (*Pes mu tin alíthia*) — Tell me the truth (demand for candor)
- αληθινά (*alithiná*) — truly, really (intensifier/sincerity marker)
- αληθώς (*alithós*) — truly, verily (formal/liturgical sincerity marker)
- Αληθώς ανέστη (*Alithós anésti*) — Truly He is risen (liturgical formula)
- κατά βάθος η αλήθεια είναι ότι… — Deep down, the truth is that… (depth-framing device)
- Αυτή είναι η αλήθεια (*Aftí íne i alíthia*) — That is the truth (emphatic closure)
- αλήθεια του Θεού (*alíthia tu Theú*) — God's truth (oath/emphatic assertion)
- με την αλήθεια στο στόμα (*me tin alíthia sto stóma*) — with the truth on one's lips

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 1 by function: the expressions here do not primarily evaluate a proposition as true or false but signal the speaker's commitment to candor. The same word αλήθεια participates in both groups — as a propositional noun (Group 1) and as a discourse particle (this group). The bleaching of propositional content is most complete in the topic-shift use ("Αλήθεια, ήθελα να σε ρωτήσω…") and the surprise-marker use ("Αλήθεια;").

## Group 6: Terms used in proverbial and fixed moral assertions about truth and lying

**Label:** Proverbial and fixed moral formulas about truth and deception

**Definition:** These are fixed expressions — proverbs, fixed collocations, and formulaic moral assertions — that encode culturally shared evaluations of truth-telling and lying. They function as complete utterances or near-complete utterances and are not typically modified or varied. They appear in everyday moral discourse, parental instruction, and rhetorical contexts.

**Representative expressions:**

- Η αλήθεια πονάει (*I alíthia ponái*) — The truth hurts
- Η αλήθεια βγαίνει πάντα στο φως (*I alíthia vgéni pánda sto fos*) — The truth always comes to light
- Το ψέμα έχει κοντά ποδάρια (*To pséma échi kondá podhária*) — Lies have short legs
- Μην λες ψέματα (*Min les pséma­ta*) — Don't tell lies
- πικρή αλήθεια (*pikrí alíthia*) — bitter truth
- γυμνή αλήθεια (*gimní alíthia*) — naked truth
- μισή αλήθεια (*misí alíthia*) — half-truth
- άσπρο ψέμα (*áspro pséma*) — white lie
- ψέμα με τα μούτρα (*pséma me ta mútra*) — barefaced lie
- ψέμα και απάτη (*pséma ke apáti*) — lies and deceit
- αλήθεια και δικαιοσύνη (*alíthia ke dhikiosíni*) — truth and justice
- αλήθεια και ψέμα (*alíthia ke pséma*) — truth and falsehood (binary moral frame)
- Πιάστηκε στο ψέμα (*Piástike sto pséma*) — He/she was caught in a lie

**Notes:** Several of these expressions (πικρή αλήθεια, γυμνή αλήθεια, μισή αλήθεια) are fixed collocations that modify αλήθεια with an evaluative adjective, encoding a culturally recognized type of truth. άσπρο ψέμα and ψέμα με τα μούτρα similarly encode culturally recognized types of lie. The proverbs (Η αλήθεια πονάει, Το ψέμα έχει κοντά ποδάρια) are complete fixed utterances. This group overlaps with Group 1 in that all items involve propositional truth and falsehood, but the fixed, formulaic, and morally evaluative character distinguishes them from the more neutral evaluative terms in Group 1.

## Group 7: Terms used to describe things that have the appearance of something without being that thing

**Label:** Appearance without reality — simulation, imitation, and false semblance

**Definition:** These terms are used to describe entities — persons, institutions, disciplines, arguments, or perceptions — that present the appearance or form of something without possessing its substance. They appear in contexts of critical evaluation, where the gap between appearance and reality is the central concern. The ψευδο- prefix is the primary morphological marker of this group, but other terms participate through their usage patterns.

**Representative expressions:**

- ψευδοεπιστήμη (*psevdhoepistími*) — pseudoscience
- ψευδοεπιστημονικός (*psevdhoepistimoniкós*) — pseudoscientific
- ψευδοεπιστήμονας (*psevdhoepistímonas*) — pseudoscientist
- ψευδοδιανοούμενος (*psevdhodhianoúmenos*) — pseudo-intellectual
- ψευδοπροφήτης (*psevdhoprofítis*) — false prophet
- ψευδοδημοκρατία (*psevdhodhimokratía*) — pseudo-democracy, sham democracy
- ψευδοδίλημμα (*psevdhodhílima*) — false dilemma
- ψευδαίσθηση (*psevdhésthisi*) — illusion, hallucination, false impression
- ψευδαίσθηση ελέγχου (*psevdhésthisi elénchu*) — illusion of control
- εικονικός (*ikonikós*) — simulated, nominal, fictitious
- εικονική πραγματικότητα — virtual reality
- εικονική σύμβαση — fictitious/sham contract
- αληθοφανής (*alithofanís*) — plausible, truth-seeming
- αληθοφάνεια (*alithofánia*) — verisimilitude, appearance of truth
- ψεύτικο χαμόγελο (*psévtiko chamóyelo*) — fake smile
- ψεύτικα δάκρυα (*psévtika dhákria*) — crocodile tears
- ψεύτικες υποσχέσεις (*psévtikes iposchésis*) — false promises

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 2 (genuineness of objects) by its focus on the gap between form and substance in categories, disciplines, institutions, and perceptions rather than in physical objects or documents. ψευδο- compounds are the clearest members. εικονικός belongs here through its usage in legal (εικονική σύμβαση = sham transaction) and technical (εικονική πραγματικότητα = virtual reality) contexts, where the shared feature is that something has the form but not the substance of what it appears to be. αληθοφανής and αληθοφάνεια are distinctive in that they describe the appearance of truth as a positive quality (in rhetoric and literature) rather than a defect — the appearance of truth is something a narrative can achieve deliberately. ψεύτικα δάκρυα and ψεύτικο χαμόγελο belong here through their focus on performed or simulated affect rather than deliberate deception.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate correctness of procedure, action, or expression against a standard

**Label:** Correctness against a norm or standard

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether an action, statement, procedure, or expression conforms to an applicable standard, rule, or norm. They appear in educational, legal, administrative, and professional contexts where there is a recognized criterion of correctness. The contrast is between what conforms to the standard (ορθός, σωστός, ακριβής) and what deviates from it (ανακριβής, λανθασμένος).

**Representative expressions:**

- ορθός (*orthós*) — correct, proper, conforming to a standard
- ορθότητα (*orthótita*) — correctness
- ορθά (*orthá*) — correctly
- ορθή χρήση (*orthí chrísi*) — correct use
- ορθή εφαρμογή (*orthí efarmoyí*) — correct application
- ορθή κατανόηση (*orthí katanóisi*) — correct understanding
- ορθογραφία (*orthografía*) — correct spelling
- σωστός (*sostós*) — correct, right, proper
- σωστά (*sostá*) — correctly; also as response particle: "Correct!"
- σωστή απάντηση (*sostí apándisi*) — correct answer
- σωστός άνθρωπος (*sostós ánthropos*) — a decent/proper person
- Κάνεις σωστά (*Kánis sostá*) — You're doing the right thing
- Δεν είναι σωστό αυτό (*Dhen íne sostó aftó*) — That's not right
- ακριβής (*akrivís*) — accurate, precise, exact
- ακρίβεια (*akrívia*) — accuracy, precision
- ακριβώς (*akrivós*) — exactly, precisely; also response particle: "Exactly!"
- ανακριβής (*anakrivís*) — inaccurate, imprecise
- ανακρίβεια (*anakrívia*) — inaccuracy
- Για να είμαι ακριβής… (*Ya na íme akrivís…*) — To be precise…
- Αν κατάλαβα ορθά… (*An katálava orthá…*) — If I understood correctly…

**Notes:** ορθός and σωστός overlap substantially in meaning but differ in register: ορθός is more formal and appears in compounds (ορθογραφία, ορθόδοξος, ορθολογισμός), while σωστός is the dominant colloquial term. ακριβής emphasizes precision and exactness (closeness of fit to a standard) rather than mere conformity to a rule, which gives it a slightly different emphasis from ορθός and σωστός. The response-particle uses of σωστά and ακριβώς (both meaning "Exactly!" or "Correct!" in conversation) are a distinctive feature of this group in spoken Greek. This group overlaps with Group 1 in that a statement can be evaluated as ακριβής (accurate) or ανακριβής (inaccurate), but the primary orientation of this group is toward conformity with a standard rather than correspondence with reality.

## Group 9: Terms used in contexts of dispute, challenge, and the questioning of claims

**Label:** Disputing, challenging, and expressing doubt about claims

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where a claim, right, or fact is being actively questioned, challenged, or held in doubt. They cover both the act of challenging (αμφισβητώ) and the state of being in doubt (αμφίβολος, αμφιβολία), as well as the assertion that something is beyond challenge (αναμφισβήτητος, αναμφίβολος). They appear in legal proceedings, academic argumentation, and everyday discourse about uncertain or contested matters.

**Representative expressions:**

- αμφισβητώ (*amfisviтó*) — to dispute, to contest, to challenge
- αμφισβήτηση (*amfisviтisi*) — dispute, challenge, contestation
- αμφισβητήσιμος (*amfisvitiísimos*) — disputable, contestable
- αναμφισβήτητος (*anamfisviтitos*) — undisputed, incontestable
- αναμφισβήτητα (*anamfisviтita*) — undisputedly
- αναμφισβήτητο γεγονός — undisputed fact
- αμφίβολος (*amfívolos*) — doubtful, dubious, questionable
- αμφιβολία (*amfivolía*) — doubt, uncertainty
- χωρίς αμφιβολία (*chorís amfivolía*) — without doubt
- Έχω αμφιβολίες (*Écho amfivolíes*) — I have doubts
- αναμφίβολος (*anamfívolos*) — undoubted, indubitable
- αναμφίβολα (*anamfívola*) — undoubtedly
- αμφίβολης ποιότητας (*amfívolis piótitas*) — of dubious quality
- αμφίβολης φήμης (*amfívolis fímis*) — of dubious reputation
- Είναι αμφίβολο αν… (*Íne amfívolo an…*) — It is doubtful whether…
- αμφισβητώ μια κατάθεση — to challenge a testimony
- Δεν αμφισβητώ ότι… (*Dhen amfisviтó óti…*) — I do not dispute that…
- αμφισβητήσιμο δικαίωμα — contestable right

**Notes:** αμφίβολος and αμφισβητώ are related in etymology but differ in usage: αμφίβολος describes a state of doubt or dubiousness (something is questionable), while αμφισβητώ describes an active act of challenging (someone contests something). The privative forms αναμφίβολος and αναμφισβήτητος are used to assert that something is beyond doubt or beyond challenge respectively — they participate in this group by negating its central concern. αμφίβολης ποιότητας and αμφίβολης φήμης are fixed genitive constructions that use αμφίβολος to evaluate persons or things as of suspect quality or reputation, extending the doubt-marking function into evaluative discourse.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items show usage patterns that place them at the intersection of multiple groups, or that do not fit cleanly into any single group identified above.

---

**αξιόλογος (*aksiólogos*) and its derivatives (αξιολόγηση, αξιολογώ, αξιολογητής, αξιολογικός)**

These terms are related etymologically to the truth-evaluation domain (ἄξιος + λόγος = worthy of account) but in contemporary usage they function primarily as evaluation and assessment terms — "noteworthy," "to evaluate," "evaluation" — rather than as truth-evaluation terms. αξιολόγηση (evaluation, assessment) is the dominant contemporary form and appears in educational, professional, and administrative contexts where the question is not truth or falsity but merit and quality. These terms sit at the boundary between the truth-evaluation domain and a broader evaluative domain. They are noted here without being assigned to a group, as their primary usage pattern in contemporary Greek is not centered on truth and falsity.

---

**ομοιότητα (*omióтita*)**

This term (resemblance, similarity) appears in P1 in connection with authenticity evaluation (resemblance of a portrait to its subject, resemblance of a trademark to another). However, its usage is not primarily in the truth-evaluation domain — it describes a relationship between two things rather than evaluating either as true, genuine, or correct. It participates in authenticity discourse (Group 2) only indirectly, as a criterion used in such evaluations. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above.

---

**πίστη (*písti*) and πιστεύω (*pistévo*)**

These terms cover a wide range of uses: religious faith, interpersonal trust, and the act of believing a proposition. πιστεύω ότι… (I believe that…) participates in Group 1 as a propositional attitude verb. πίστη in the sense of religious faith is outside the truth-evaluation domain as defined by usage patterns here. πιστεύω (nominalized as "creed") is also outside this domain. The same root generates both the credibility cluster (Group 4: αξιόπιστος, εμπιστοσύνη) and the financial credit cluster (πίστωση, πιστωτής, πιστωτική κάρτα), which is entirely outside the truth-evaluation domain. These financial uses are noted in P1b as peripheral extensions and are not assigned to any group here.

---

**ψεύτικα νέα (*psévtika néa*) — fake news**

This collocation belongs to Group 1 (false statements) and Group 7 (appearance without reality) simultaneously. It describes news content that is false (Group 1) but also content that presents the appearance of legitimate journalism without its substance (Group 7). It is listed under Group 1 (ψεύτικος) but its usage pattern also connects it to Group 7.

---

**ψευδεπίγραφος (*psevdhepígrafos*)**

This philological term (falsely attributed text) belongs to Group 2 (genuineness of documents and texts) in that it concerns the authenticity of attribution, but it also belongs to Group 7 (appearance without reality) in that a ψευδεπίγραφος text presents itself as the work of an author it is not. It is listed under Group 2 but participates in both.

---

**η δική μου αλήθεια (*i dhikí mu alíthia*) — my truth**

This construction (documented in P2b) does not fit cleanly into any group. It is used in contemporary psychological and social media registers to assert a personal or subjective truth, which involves a different use of αλήθεια from the propositional truth-evaluation of Group 1 and the sincerity-marking of Group 5. It reflects a usage pattern where αλήθεια is applied to personal experience or perspective rather than to propositions about external states of affairs. It is noted here as an item whose usage pattern is distinct from the other groups but does not constitute a group on its own within the data collected.

---

**Δεν υπάρχει μία αλήθεια (*Dhen ipárchi mía alíthia*) — There is no single truth**

Similarly, this fixed philosophical assertion (documented in P2b) uses αλήθεια in a way that is distinct from Group 1 (propositional truth evaluation) and Group 5 (sincerity marking). It functions as a recognized argumentative formula in relativist discourse. Like η δική μου αλήθεια, it reflects a usage pattern where αλήθεια is the subject of a meta-level claim about the nature of truth rather than a term used to evaluate specific propositions. These two items share a usage pattern but are insufficient in number to constitute a separate group.

---

**αληθής αξία (*alithís aksía*) — true value**

This financial and philosophical collocation uses αληθής in a context where the question is not propositional truth but intrinsic or real value as opposed to nominal or market value. It participates in Group 1 etymologically but its usage pattern in financial discourse is distinct. It is noted here as a peripheral use of αληθής outside the core propositional truth-evaluation function.

## Items Without a Clear Group Assignment

The following items from P1 and P2 do not show usage patterns that place them clearly within any of the groups identified above. They are listed without assignment.

- **ακριβολόγος / ακριβολογία** — precision of language; marginal in contemporary usage and not clearly integrated into any of the groups above
- **γνησίως** — formal adverb of γνήσιος; marginal and primarily a formal variant of γνήσιος rather than a distinct usage pattern
- **αληθοφιλής** — truth-loving; rare and literary, not integrated into any observed usage cluster
- **αμφισβητίας** — habitual disputant; marginal and primarily historical/political in usage
- **ψευδοαρχαιολογία, ψευδοκλασικός, ψευδοεπιστολή** — marginal ψευδο- compounds in specialized academic registers; they participate in Group 7 structurally but are too specialized to be representative members
- **ολιγοπιστία / ολιγόπιστος** — primarily religious and metaphorical; participates in the πιστ- root family but does not fit clearly into Group 4 (credibility) or any other group identified here
