# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**
**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 participation:** 10 items — 真 (item 1), 真实 (3), 真正 (9), 名副其实 (10, via 真), 货真价实 (11), 真伪 (13), 辨别真伪 (14), 逼真 (25), 失真 (26), 原真性 (29)
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts correspondence between a thing and its genuine, unaltered, or actual nature. Functions as the positive pole of the authentic/inauthentic axis across nearly all sub-domains of the truth-evaluation field.

---

**Root 2**
**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 participation:** 11 items — 真实 (3), 名副其实 (10), 货真价实 (11), 真伪 (13, via compound partner), 失实 (15), 属实 (16), 如实 (17), 本质 (18, via 质 but 实 is the operative root in the cluster), 实质 (19), 名不副实 (20), 诚实 (27)
**Core meaning contribution:** Denotes solidity, factual grounding, and correspondence to concrete reality. Where 真 asserts genuineness, 实 asserts factual substance — what is actually the case, as opposed to what is nominal, empty, or merely formal.

---

**Root 3**
**Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**P1 participation:** 5 items — 假 (item 2), 虚假 (4), 真伪 (13, as the negative pole), 辨别真伪 (14), 伪造 (12, via semantic field overlap; note: 伪 is a distinct morpheme but 假 and 伪 function as near-synonyms in this domain)
**Core meaning contribution:** Marks the negative pole of the authenticity axis — that which is fabricated, counterfeit, borrowed, or not what it presents itself as being. Also extends into grammatical and temporal uses (see Step 3).

*Note on 伪 (wěi):* 伪 appears in 伪造 (12) and 真伪 (13) and is a productive root in its own right. However, its independent productivity in everyday compounds is lower than that of 真, 实, or 假, and it overlaps substantially with 假 in the falsity domain. It is treated as a secondary root and included in the expansion of 假 where relevant, but not assigned a separate Step 2 expansion.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: 真 (zhēn)

*Note: 真 is highly productive. The 25 most common compounds and expressions are listed below; additional technical and dialectal forms exist beyond this set.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal breakdown:** true + principle/reason
**Natural English translation:** truth (as an abstract principle), Truth
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, political, and religious discourse to refer to fundamental or universal truth — propositions or principles that are objectively and permanently valid. Common in Marxist-Leninist discourse ("真理标准" — the criterion of truth) and in general philosophical writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal breakdown:** true + appearance/face/aspect
**Natural English translation:** the truth (of a matter), the real situation, the facts
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of affairs behind appearances or official accounts, often in contexts of investigation, revelation, or cover-up. Extremely common in journalism, legal discourse, and everyday speech ("揭露真相" — to expose the truth).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true + sincere
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person's attitude, expression, or conduct as genuinely felt rather than performed or strategic. Used in interpersonal, diplomatic, and moral contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真心
**Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
**Literal breakdown:** true + heart/mind
**Natural English translation:** sincere, wholehearted, genuine (in feeling or intention)
**Context of use:** Emphasizes that an emotion, intention, or commitment is genuinely felt rather than feigned. Common in everyday speech and in descriptions of personal relationships ("真心话" — words from the heart, honest words).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真话
**Transliteration:** zhēnhuà
**Literal breakdown:** true + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, truthful words, honest speech
**Context of use:** Refers to statements that reflect what the speaker actually believes or knows, as opposed to polite fictions or deliberate falsehoods. Common in everyday speech ("说真话" — to tell the truth, to speak honestly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** true + false
**Natural English translation:** true or false, genuine or fake, authenticity
**Context of use:** Frames the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit. Used in everyday and commercial contexts, often as a compound noun ("分不清真假" — unable to tell what is real from what is fake).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 真实性
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/actual + -ness (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, factual accuracy, veracity
**Context of use:** The nominalized form of 真实, used in formal, legal, journalistic, and academic contexts to refer to the property of being truthful or factually accurate ("核实其真实性" — to verify its authenticity/accuracy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 真凭实据
**Transliteration:** zhēn píng shí jù
**Literal breakdown:** true + evidence/proof + solid/real + evidence/basis
**Natural English translation:** solid evidence, concrete proof, hard facts
**Context of use:** A four-character expression used in legal and argumentative contexts to assert that a claim is backed by genuine and substantial evidence. Often used in the phrase "拿出真凭实据" — to produce solid proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 千真万确
**Transliteration:** qiān zhēn wàn què
**Literal breakdown:** thousand + true + ten-thousand + certain/definite
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, beyond all doubt, one hundred percent certain
**Context of use:** A four-character expression used in everyday speech to emphatically assert that something is completely and undeniably true. Often used to counter skepticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 半真半假
**Transliteration:** bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** half + true + half + false
**Natural English translation:** half true and half false, a mixture of truth and fiction
**Context of use:** Describes statements or accounts that blend accurate and inaccurate elements, often in contexts of evaluating testimony, rumors, or narratives.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 以假乱真
**Transliteration:** yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn
**Literal breakdown:** use + false + confuse/disorder + true
**Natural English translation:** to pass off the fake as genuine, to use counterfeits to confuse the real
**Context of use:** A four-character expression describing the act of substituting or mixing fakes with genuine items so as to deceive. Used in commercial, art authentication, and general evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 弄假成真
**Transliteration:** nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn
**Literal breakdown:** make/play + false + become + true
**Natural English translation:** what started as a pretense becomes reality; fiction becomes fact
**Context of use:** A four-character expression describing a situation where something originally intended as a pretense, joke, or fiction ends up becoming genuinely real. Used in narrative, interpersonal, and social commentary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 真知灼见
**Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge + clear/penetrating + insight/view
**Natural English translation:** genuine insight, penetrating and well-founded views
**Context of use:** A four-character expression used in intellectual and evaluative contexts to commend views or analyses that are both accurate and perceptive, as opposed to superficial or mistaken opinions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 真善美
**Transliteration:** zhēn shàn měi
**Literal breakdown:** true + good/virtuous + beautiful
**Natural English translation:** the true, the good, and the beautiful; truth, goodness, and beauty
**Context of use:** A philosophical and aesthetic triad used in discussions of values, aesthetics, and moral philosophy. Common in educational, literary, and cultural discourse as a summary of ideal human values.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 认真
**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious; to take seriously
**Context of use:** Describes an attitude of genuine engagement and care in performing a task or considering a matter. Extremely common in everyday, educational, and professional contexts ("认真工作" — to work conscientiously; "别太认真" — don't take it too seriously).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 当真
**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** treat as + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to take seriously, to take as real; really, truly (colloquial)
**Context of use:** Used in everyday speech to indicate that someone is treating something as genuinely true or serious, often in contexts where this is surprising or inadvisable ("你当真了？" — Are you taking this seriously?). Also used as an adverb meaning "really."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 较真
**Transliteration:** jiàozhēn
**Literal breakdown:** compare/be particular about + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to be a stickler, to be pedantic about details, to take things too literally
**Context of use:** Colloquial expression describing someone who insists on strict accuracy or takes things too literally, sometimes to an excessive degree. Used in everyday speech, often with a mildly critical or amused tone.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 纯真
**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** pure + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** pure and genuine, innocent, unspoiled
**Context of use:** Describes a quality of innocence and unaffected genuineness, typically applied to children, emotions, or artistic expression. Used in literary, aesthetic, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 天真
**Transliteration:** tiānzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** heaven/natural + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** naive, innocent, artless; (of children) naturally genuine
**Context of use:** Describes a quality of natural, unaffected innocence, applied to children positively and to adults with a range from affectionate to critical depending on context ("太天真了" — too naive).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 写真
**Transliteration:** xiězhēn
**Literal breakdown:** depict/write + true/real
**Natural English translation:** portrait, photographic portrait, photo shoot; (historical) realistic depiction
**Context of use:** In contemporary usage, refers primarily to portrait photography or photo shoots, especially in the context of celebrity or personal photography. In classical usage, referred to realistic portraiture in painting.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 传真
**Transliteration:** chuánzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** transmit + true/real
**Natural English translation:** fax, facsimile
**Context of use:** The standard term for facsimile transmission (fax). The compound reflects the original concept of transmitting a true copy of a document. Used in administrative and business contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 写实
**Transliteration:** xiěshí
**Literal breakdown:** depict/write + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** realistic (in art or literature), realism
**Context of use:** Used in aesthetic and literary criticism to describe works that aim to depict reality as it is, without idealization. Common in art criticism, literary theory, and everyday evaluations of artistic style. *(Note: this item contains 实 rather than 真 but is included here as a near-parallel compound in the same semantic cluster.)*
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 真人
**Transliteration:** zhēnrén
**Literal breakdown:** true + person
**Natural English translation:** real person; (Daoist) perfected person, realized being
**Context of use:** In everyday contexts, refers to a real, actual person as opposed to a fictional character or avatar ("真人秀" — reality show; "真人版" — live-action version). In Daoist religious discourse, refers to a spiritually perfected individual.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 真菌
**Transliteration:** zhēnjūn
**Literal breakdown:** true + fungus/bacterium
**Natural English translation:** true fungi, Fungi (taxonomic kingdom)
**Context of use:** The standard scientific term for the kingdom Fungi in biological taxonomy. Used in scientific, educational, and technical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 真空
**Transliteration:** zhēnkōng
**Literal breakdown:** true + empty/void
**Natural English translation:** vacuum (physics); true void
**Context of use:** The standard scientific and technical term for a vacuum — a space devoid of matter. Used in physics, engineering, and everyday contexts ("真空包装" — vacuum packaging). Also used metaphorically to describe an absence of something ("权力真空" — power vacuum).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 2: 实 (shí)

*Note: 实 is highly productive across multiple semantic domains. The 25 most common compounds and expressions are listed below; additional forms exist.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 事实
**Transliteration:** shìshí
**Literal breakdown:** matter/affair + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** fact, the facts, factual reality
**Context of use:** The standard term for a fact or the factual state of affairs. Extremely common across all registers — legal, journalistic, everyday, and academic ("事实上" — in fact, as a matter of fact; "尊重事实" — to respect the facts).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 现实
**Transliteration:** xiànshí
**Literal breakdown:** present/manifest + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** reality, the real world; realistic, practical
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of the world as it currently exists, often contrasted with ideals, wishes, or fiction. Used in philosophical, political, and everyday contexts ("面对现实" — to face reality; "现实主义" — realism).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 实际
**Transliteration:** shíjì
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + border/boundary/occasion
**Natural English translation:** actual, practical, real; in practice, in reality
**Context of use:** Emphasizes what is actually the case or operationally effective, as opposed to theoretical or nominal. Extremely common in everyday, administrative, and analytical discourse ("实际情况" — the actual situation; "脱离实际" — divorced from reality).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 实在
**Transliteration:** shízài
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + exist/be present
**Natural English translation:** real, genuine, solid; really, truly (adverb); (philosophy) reality, being
**Context of use:** In everyday speech, used as an adverb meaning "really" or "truly" ("实在没办法" — there's really no way), and as an adjective meaning solid or genuine. In philosophical discourse, refers to ontological reality or being.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 实话
**Transliteration:** shíhuà
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, honest words, straight talk
**Context of use:** Refers to statements that reflect the actual facts, as opposed to polite fictions or evasions. Common in everyday speech ("说实话" — to tell the truth, to be honest; "实话实说" — to say it straight, to tell it like it is).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 实话实说
**Transliteration:** shíhuà shíshuō
**Literal breakdown:** real words + real + say/speak
**Natural English translation:** to tell it like it is, to speak frankly, to say it straight
**Context of use:** A fixed expression emphasizing candid, unvarnished communication of the facts. Used in everyday speech and became widely known as the title of a popular television talk show.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 实证
**Transliteration:** shízhèng
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + evidence/proof/verify
**Natural English translation:** empirical evidence; to verify empirically; positivism (in philosophy)
**Context of use:** Used in scientific and academic contexts to refer to evidence-based verification. "实证主义" is the standard translation of positivism. Also used in legal contexts to mean concrete proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 落实
**Transliteration:** luòshí
**Literal breakdown:** fall/land + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** to implement, to put into practice, to follow through on; to verify, to confirm
**Context of use:** Extremely common in administrative and political discourse to mean carrying out a policy, decision, or plan in concrete terms ("落实政策" — to implement policy). Also used to mean verifying or pinning down a fact.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 核实
**Transliteration:** héshí
**Literal breakdown:** check/verify + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to check the facts
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, administrative, and legal contexts to describe the process of checking whether a claim or piece of information is accurate ("核实信息" — to verify information).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 证实
**Transliteration:** zhèngshí
**Literal breakdown:** prove/evidence + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to verify, to prove to be true
**Context of use:** Used across journalistic, legal, and scientific contexts to indicate that a claim has been confirmed by evidence or investigation ("经调查证实" — confirmed by investigation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 查实
**Transliteration:** cháshí
**Literal breakdown:** investigate/check + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** to investigate and confirm, to verify through inquiry
**Context of use:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to describe the process of investigating and confirming the truth of a matter. More formal and procedural than 核实.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 坐实
**Transliteration:** zuòshí
**Literal breakdown:** sit/settle + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to nail down (a fact or accusation), to establish as true
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic and everyday contexts to indicate that a rumor, suspicion, or allegation has been definitively confirmed ("坐实了传言" — the rumor has been confirmed). Relatively colloquial.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 务实
**Transliteration:** wùshí
**Literal breakdown:** engage in/attend to + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth
**Context of use:** Describes an approach or attitude that focuses on practical realities rather than ideology or theory. Common in political, administrative, and business discourse ("务实的态度" — a pragmatic attitude).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 踏实
**Transliteration:** tāshí
**Literal breakdown:** tread/step + real/solid
**Natural English translation:** steady, grounded, reliable; at ease, settled (of feelings)
**Context of use:** Describes both a quality of solid, dependable work or character ("踏实肯干" — steady and willing to work hard) and a feeling of being settled or at ease ("心里踏实" — feeling settled in one's heart).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 扎实
**Transliteration:** zhāshí
**Literal breakdown:** plant/drive in + real/solid
**Natural English translation:** solid, thorough, well-grounded (of skills, knowledge, or work)
**Context of use:** Describes knowledge, skills, or work as having a solid, well-established foundation. Common in educational and professional contexts ("基础扎实" — solid foundations; "扎实推进" — to advance steadily and thoroughly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 充实
**Transliteration:** chōngshí
**Literal breakdown:** fill/full + real/solid
**Natural English translation:** to enrich, to fill out, to make more substantial; rich, full, substantive
**Context of use:** Describes the process of making something more substantial or complete, or the state of being full and meaningful ("充实生活" — to enrich one's life; "内容充实" — substantive content).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 朴实
**Transliteration:** pǔshí
**Literal breakdown:** plain/simple + real/solid
**Natural English translation:** plain, simple, unpretentious, down-to-earth
**Context of use:** Describes a style, person, or manner as simple and unaffected, without pretension or ornamentation. Used in character evaluations and aesthetic judgments ("朴实无华" — plain and unadorned).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 老实
**Transliteration:** lǎoshí
**Literal breakdown:** old/experienced + real/solid
**Natural English translation:** honest, straightforward, well-behaved; simple-minded (sometimes)
**Context of use:** Describes a person as honest and straightforward, sometimes with a connotation of being overly compliant or simple. Very common in everyday speech ("老实说" — to be honest; "老实人" — an honest, straightforward person).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 实力
**Transliteration:** shílì
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + strength/power
**Natural English translation:** actual strength, real capability, substantive power
**Context of use:** Refers to genuine, demonstrated capability or power, as opposed to nominal or claimed strength. Common in political, military, sports, and business contexts ("实力派" — those with real ability; "展示实力" — to demonstrate real strength).

---

**20.**
**Form:** 实用
**Transliteration:** shíyòng
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + use/function
**Natural English translation:** practical, functional, utilitarian
**Context of use:** Evaluates something as having genuine practical utility. Used in everyday, design, and philosophical contexts ("实用主义" — pragmatism/utilitarianism; "实用性强" — highly practical).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 名实
**Transliteration:** míngshí
**Literal breakdown:** name/title + real/actual
**Natural English translation:** name and reality, nominal and actual
**Context of use:** A classical and philosophical compound referring to the relationship between names or labels and the realities they purport to designate. Central to the School of Names (名家) in classical Chinese philosophy ("名实相符" — name and reality correspond).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 实录
**Transliteration:** shílù
**Literal breakdown:** real/actual + record/document
**Natural English translation:** veritable records, faithful record, documentary account
**Context of use:** Refers to historical records or accounts that faithfully document what actually occurred. Used in historiographical contexts ("实录体" — the veritable records genre in Chinese historiography) and in documentary filmmaking.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 写实主义
**Transliteration:** xiěshí zhǔyì
**Literal breakdown:** depict + real + -ism
**Natural English translation:** realism (as an artistic or literary movement)
**Context of use:** The standard term for realism as an aesthetic doctrine in art, literature, and theater. Used in art criticism, literary theory, and cultural discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 实事求是
**Transliteration:** shíshì qiúshì
**Literal breakdown:** real + matters/affairs + seek + is/correct
**Natural English translation:** to seek truth from facts; to be realistic and empirical
**Context of use:** A classical phrase adopted as a key political and epistemological slogan, particularly associated with Deng Xiaoping's reform-era ideology. Means approaching problems empirically and practically rather than dogmatically. Extremely common in political, administrative, and everyday discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 言过其实
**Transliteration:** yán guò qí shí
**Literal breakdown:** words + exceed/surpass + its + reality/substance
**Natural English translation:** to exaggerate, to overstate, to say more than the facts warrant
**Context of use:** A four-character expression used to characterize statements that go beyond what the facts support. Used in evaluative and critical contexts across everyday and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### ROOT 3: 假 (jiǎ)

*Note: 假 has two distinct pronunciations — jiǎ (false, fake) and jià (vacation, leave). Both are included below as they derive from the same written character, with the pronunciation noted for each entry.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 假冒
**Transliteration:** jiǎmào
**Literal breakdown:** false + counterfeit/impersonate
**Natural English translation:** counterfeit, fake, to pass off as genuine
**Context of use:** Used in commercial and legal contexts to describe products or persons falsely presented as genuine or as someone else ("假冒伪劣" — counterfeit and substandard goods; "假冒商标" — counterfeit trademark).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假冒伪劣
**Transliteration:** jiǎmào wěiliè
**Literal breakdown:** false + counterfeit + fake/spurious + inferior/poor quality
**Natural English translation:** counterfeit and substandard (goods), fake and shoddy products
**Context of use:** A fixed four-character compound used in commercial, regulatory, and consumer protection contexts to describe goods that are both inauthentic and of poor quality. Extremely common in Chinese consumer and legal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 假话
**Transliteration:** jiǎhuà
**Literal breakdown:** false + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** lies, false statements, untruths
**Context of use:** Refers to statements that are not true, whether through deliberate deception or otherwise. Common in everyday speech ("说假话" — to tell lies; "不说假话" — not to tell lies).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 假象
**Transliteration:** jiǎxiàng
**Literal breakdown:** false + appearance/image/phenomenon
**Natural English translation:** false appearance, illusion, misleading phenomenon
**Context of use:** Describes a surface appearance or phenomenon that gives a misleading impression of the underlying reality. Used in analytical, political, and everyday contexts ("不要被假象迷惑" — don't be misled by false appearances).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 假设
**Transliteration:** jiǎshè
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + set up/establish
**Natural English translation:** hypothesis, assumption; to hypothesize, to assume
**Context of use:** The standard term for a hypothesis or assumption in scientific, logical, and everyday reasoning. Used in academic, technical, and everyday contexts ("提出假设" — to put forward a hypothesis; "假设条件" — hypothetical conditions).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 假如
**Transliteration:** jiǎrú
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + as if/like
**Natural English translation:** if, supposing that, in the event that
**Context of use:** A common conditional conjunction used in everyday and written Chinese to introduce hypothetical conditions. Equivalent to "if" or "supposing that."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 假若
**Transliteration:** jiǎruò
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + as if/like
**Natural English translation:** if, supposing that (slightly more literary than 假如)
**Context of use:** A conditional conjunction equivalent to 假如, slightly more literary in register. Used in written and formal spoken Chinese.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 假定
**Transliteration:** jiǎdìng
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + fix/determine
**Natural English translation:** to assume, to suppose; assumption, hypothesis
**Context of use:** Used in logical, scientific, and everyday reasoning to introduce an assumption or stipulation. Slightly more formal than 假如 and more explicitly marks the content as a stipulated assumption rather than a real condition.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 假装
**Transliteration:** jiǎzhuāng

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots 真, 实, or 假 in contexts where the contribution of the root appears to shift away from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**真 (zhēn) — peripheral extensions:**

- **认真** (rènzhēn): The root 真 here appears to contribute a sense of earnestness or intensity of engagement rather than a truth-value judgment about propositions or objects.

- **天真** (tiānzhēn): The root 真 appears in a context of natural innocence or naivety, where the evaluative dimension concerns a quality of character or developmental state rather than correspondence to fact.

- **真空** (zhēnkōng): The root 真 modifies a physical/scientific concept (vacuum), where "true emptiness" functions as a technical qualifier rather than an epistemic or authenticity judgment.

- **真菌** (zhēnjūn): The root 真 functions as a taxonomic classifier ("true fungi"), distinguishing a biological kingdom rather than making an epistemic claim.

- **真善美** (zhēn shàn měi): 真 appears as one term in a philosophical triad alongside goodness and beauty, suggesting a domain of value theory that extends beyond factual truth.

- **真人** (zhēnrén) in Daoist usage: The root 真 designates a spiritually perfected or realized person, where the evaluative dimension concerns spiritual attainment rather than factual accuracy.

- **写真** (xiězhēn): The root 真 appears in a compound that has shifted in contemporary usage to refer primarily to portrait photography, where the original fidelity-of-representation meaning has become lexicalized into a genre label.

- **千真万确** (qiān zhēn wàn què): 真 functions here as an intensifier within a fixed expression, where the rhetorical force is emphatic assertion rather than a specific claim about the nature of genuineness.

---

**实 (shí) — peripheral extensions:**

- **踏实** (tāshí): The root 实 appears in a compound describing a psychological state (feeling settled or at ease) and a character quality (steadiness), where the connection to factual reality is indirect.

- **充实** (chōngshí): The root 实 appears in a context of enrichment or fullness — making something more substantial — where the evaluative dimension concerns completeness or richness rather than truth-correspondence.

- **实力** (shílì): The root 实 modifies a concept of power or capability, where "real strength" functions as a contrast to nominal or claimed strength rather than as an epistemic claim about propositions.

- **实用** (shíyòng): The root 实 appears in a context of practical utility, where the evaluative dimension concerns functional effectiveness rather than factual accuracy.

- **扎实** (zhāshí) and **朴实** (pǔshí): The root 实 appears in evaluations of character, style, or quality (solid, plain, unpretentious) where the connection to factual truth is metaphorical or indirect.

- **实事求是** (shíshì qiúshì): While this expression is centrally about empirical truth-seeking, the root 实 here also carries a strong normative and political valence, functioning as a prescription for correct methodology rather than a description of a truth-value.

---

**假 (jiǎ/jià) — peripheral extensions:**

- **假设** (jiǎshè), **假如** (jiǎrú), **假若** (jiǎruò), **假定** (jiǎdìng): The root 假 in these conditional and hypothetical expressions does not assert that something is false or counterfeit, but rather introduces a stipulated or imagined condition. The root functions as a marker of hypotheticality or conditionality rather than of falsity.

- **假借** (jiǎjiè) in the linguistic sense: The root 假 in the classical Chinese linguistic category of phonetic loan characters (六书) refers to borrowing rather than falsity, marking a functional extension into the domain of writing system analysis.

- **假期** (jiàqī), **请假** (qǐngjià), **放假** (fàngjià): These items use the pronunciation jià and the meaning of leave or vacation — a semantic domain entirely distinct from falsity or inauthenticity. The relationship between the jiǎ (false) and jià (leave) senses of the character is etymologically complex and the two meanings are functionally separate in contemporary usage.

- **假公济私** (jiǎ gōng jì sī): The root 假 here functions as a marker of instrumental use or pretext ("using X as a cover for Y") rather than straightforwardly asserting that something is counterfeit or untrue.
