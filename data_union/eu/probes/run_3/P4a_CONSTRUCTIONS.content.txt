# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Predicative: NOUN/ADJECTIVE + copula *da* (3sg of *izan*)
The truth-domain adjective or nominal appears as predicate complement with the copula *da*.

### Examples
- *Egia da* → N/A → "It is true"
- *Zilegi da* → N/A → "It is lawful / It is permissible"

### Notes
Basque predicative attribution uses *da* (existential/equative copula *izan*) for inherent properties; stative *egon* is used for contingent states (see §4 below). The predicate nominal or adjective appears without case marking in this position.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Negation particle *ez* precedes the auxiliary: NOUN/ADJECTIVE + *ez da*
Alternatively, *ez-* prefixes the adjective itself to form a lexicalized negative: *ez-* + ADJECTIVE.

### Examples
- *Ez da egia* → N/A → "It is not true"
- *Ez-fidagarria da* → N/A → "It is unreliable / It is not trustworthy"

### Notes
Two distinct strategies are attested: syntactic negation (*ez da*) is productive and applies to any predicate; lexical negation (*ez-* prefix) is more restricted and yields semi-lexicalized forms such as *ez-fidagarria*, *ez-sinesgarria*, *ez-zilegia*. Both strategies are documented in P2a.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Indefinite attributive form (typically *-zko*, *-tako*, *-ezko*, or *-ko* suffix, without definite article *-a*) precedes the head noun directly.

### Examples
- *Egiazko informazioa* → N/A → "True information / Genuine information"
- *Gezurrezko lekukotasuna* → N/A → "False testimony / Perjury"

### Notes
Basque is head-final; modifiers precede the noun. The indefinite attributive form (e.g., *egiazko*, *gezurrezko*, *benetako*, *ustezko*) is obligatory in prenominal position; the definite form (*egiazkoa*, *gezurrezkoa*) appears only in predicative or standalone nominal positions. This is a systematic morphological alternation, not lexical suppletion.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Root adjective/noun + *-tu* verbal suffix, used intransitively, or root + stative *egon* for resulting state.
Inchoative meaning ("to become X") is expressed by the *-tu* verb; the resulting state is expressed by ADJECTIVE + *egon*.

### Examples
- *Gauzak okertu dira* → N/A → "Things have gone wrong / Things have become crooked"
- *Oker nago* → N/A → "I am wrong / I am mistaken" (resultant state with *egon*)

### Notes
The *-tu* suffix on root adjectives (*okertu*, *usteldu*, *zuzendu*) yields verbs that can be read as inchoative ("become X") or causative ("make X") depending on argument structure (see §5). The *egon* construction marks the resultant state as contingent and reversible, contrasting with *izan* for inherent properties.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
The same *-tu* verbal suffix used for inchoatives (§4) also yields causative/transitive readings when a causee argument is present. No dedicated morphological causative exists; transitivity is determined by argument structure.

### Examples
- *Akatsak zuzendu* → N/A → "To correct the errors" (causative: agent acts on object)
- *Epaia baliogabetu* → N/A → "To nullify the judgment" (causative: agent causes invalidity)

### Notes
Basque does not have a dedicated causative morpheme in this domain. The *-tu* verbs (*zuzendu*, *okertu*, *usteldu*, *baliogabetu*, *egokitu*) are polyfunctional: transitive use with an explicit object yields causative meaning; intransitive use yields inchoative meaning. The distinction is syntactic, not morphological.

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Root + *-tasun* (abstract property suffix) + *-a* (definite article).
A parallel pattern uses *-pen* or *-keta* for result/action nominals derived from verbal stems.

### Examples
- *Egiatasuna* → N/A → "Truthfulness / The quality of being true"
- *Egiaztapen* → N/A → "Verification / Attestation (as result or document)"

### Notes
The *-tasun* suffix is the primary abstract property nominalizer across the entire domain: *egiazkotasuna*, *gezurtasuna*, *zuzentasuna*, *zehaztasuna*, *fidagarritasuna*, *sinesgarritasuna*, *legitimotasuna*, *zilegitasuna*, *benetakotasuna*, *autentikotasuna*, *zintzotasuna*, *leialatasuna*. The *-pen* suffix (*egiaztapen*, *zehaztapen*) nominalizes verbal stems to yield result nominals rather than property nominals.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Two productive suffixes: *-tzaile* (general agent) and *-gile* (maker/doer agent), both followed by definite *-a*.
The characterological suffix *-ti* (habitual tendency) + *-a* yields a distinct subtype marking habitual agents.

### Examples
- *Gezurtia* → N/A → "Liar (habitual)" (*gezur-* + *-ti* + *-a*)
- *Iruzurgilea* → N/A → "Fraudster / Swindler" (*iruzur* + *-gile* + *-a*)

### Notes
Three agentive patterns are attested in this domain: (i) *-tzaile* for professional/role agents (*zuzentzailea*, "corrector/proofreader"); (ii) *-gile* for maker/doer agents (*iruzurgilea*, "fraudster"; *itxuragilea*, "pretender/impostor"); (iii) *-ti* for characterological habitual agents (*gezurtia*, "habitual liar"; *iruzurti*, "habitually deceptive"). The *-ti* type implies a stable character trait rather than a role or act, and is the primary pattern for moral character evaluation in this domain.

---

## 8. PROCESS NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Verbal stem + *-tze* (process/action nominalization suffix) + optional case suffixes.

### Examples
- *Egiaztatze-prozesua* → N/A → "The verification process"
- *Egokitze* → N/A → "Adaptation / The process of adapting"

### Notes
The *-tze* suffix nominalizes a verbal stem to yield a process nominal (the ongoing activity), contrasting with *-pen* (result nominal, §6) and *-keta* (action/result nominal, as in *zuzenketa*). All three nominalization types are attested in this domain and are not interchangeable: *egiaztatze* (process), *egiaztapen* (result/document), *egiaztagiri* (the physical certificate).

---

## 9. PEJORATIVE ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Root noun or adjective + *-keria* (pejorative abstract suffix).

### Examples
- *Ustelkeria* → N/A → "Corruption / Moral decay" (*ustel* + *-keria*)
- *Itxurakeria* → N/A → "Pretense / Superficiality" (*itxura* + *-keria*)

### Notes
The *-keria* suffix is a distinct nominalizer from *-tasun* (§6): where *-tasun* yields a neutral abstract property nominal, *-keria* yields a pejorative nominal implying a pattern of blameworthy conduct. Attested forms in this domain include *ustelkeria*, *itxurakeria*, *okerkeria*, *faltsukeria*, *bidegabekeria*, *ustekeria*, *egiazkeria*. This pattern is productive and semantically consistent across all instances.

---

## 10. EPISTEMIC HEDGE ADVERBIALS

### Pattern
Base nominal + instrumental suffix *-z* → adverbial hedge.
Two primary forms: *itxuraz* (appearance-based hedge) and *ustez* (belief-based hedge).

### Examples
- *Itxuraz ondo dago* → N/A → "Apparently it is fine / It seems to be fine"
- *Ustez erruduna da* → N/A → "Supposedly he/she is guilty / Allegedly guilty"

### Notes
Both *itxuraz* and *ustez* are formed by the instrumental *-z* suffix on their base nominals (*itxura*, "appearance"; *uste*, "belief/supposition"). They function as sentence-level epistemic hedges, marking the speaker's reduced commitment to the proposition. A parallel attributive form exists for each: *itxurazko* and *ustezko* (adnominal, §3). This is a productive pattern: the instrumental *-z* on epistemic nominals yields adverbial hedges.

---

## 11. LIGHT-VERB CONSTRUCTIONS WITH *EGIN* AND *ESAN*

### Pattern
Truth-domain nominal + light verb *egin* ("to do/make") or *esan* ("to say/tell").

### Examples
- *Gezurra esan* → N/A → "To tell a lie" (*gezurra* + *esan*)
- *Iruzurra egin* → N/A → "To commit fraud / To cheat" (*iruzurra* + *egin*)

### Notes
Light-verb constructions with *esan* are used for speech-act expressions (*egia esan*, *gezurra esan*, *zintzoki esan*); those with *egin* are used for action expressions (*iruzurra egin*, *itxura egin*). This is the primary verbal strategy for truth-domain nominals that lack a dedicated *-tu* verb. The nominal bears absolutive case and the light verb carries all tense/agreement morphology.
