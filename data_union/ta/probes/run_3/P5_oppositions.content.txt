# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919 (applied consistently throughout)

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** பொய் / *poy*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* vs. *poy*
4. **English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** The data explicitly identifies *poy* as "the primary antonym of *uṇmai*" (P1, item 2). They appear in direct contrast in evaluative constructions, as predicates (*atu uṇmai* vs. *atu poy*), and as objects of *col* and *pēcu*. The P3 grouping places both in Group 1 as the primary positive and negative terms for propositional truth.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāṉa*
2. **Term B:** பொய்யான / *poyyāṉa*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyāṉa* vs. *poyyāṉa*
4. **English translation:** true, genuine, real / false, untrue, deceptive
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *uṇmaiyāṉa* "contrasts with *poyyāṉa* (false) in parallel constructions" (P1, item 4). Both are *-āṉa* adjectival forms of their respective base nominals and function as direct antonyms in attributive position.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** நியாயம் / *niyāyam*
2. **Term B:** அன்யாயம் / *anyāyam*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyam* vs. *anyāyam*
4. **English translation:** justice, fairness, rightness / injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *anyāyam* "functions as the direct antonym of *niyāyam* in evaluative contexts" (P1, item 16), and that *anyāyamāṉa* is the "direct antonym of *niyāyamāṉa*" (P2a, item 16.1). The opposition is etymologically encoded: *anyāyam* derives from Sanskrit *anyāya* (contrary to *nyāya*).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** நியாயமான / *niyāyamāṉa*
2. **Term B:** அன்யாயமான / *anyāyamāṉa*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyamāṉa* vs. *anyāyamāṉa*
4. **English translation:** just, fair, reasonable / unjust, unfair, wrongful
5. **Description of contrast:** The data explicitly identifies these as direct antonyms in evaluative constructions (P1, item 15; P2a, item 16.1). Both are *-āṉa* adjectival forms and appear in parallel attributive and predicative positions.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** போலி / *pōli*
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* vs. *pōli*
4. **English translation:** original, genuine / fake, counterfeit
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *acal* "contrasts directly with *pōli* (fake, counterfeit) and *nakal* (copy)" (P1, item 30), and the construction *atu pōliyā, acalā?* ("Is that fake or genuine?") is documented as a standard disjunctive contrast in P2b (Construction 11.3). The opposition is used in legal, commercial, and everyday contexts to distinguish authentic from inauthentic objects and documents.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** நேர்மையான / *nērmayāṉa*
2. **Term B:** நேர்மையற்ற / *nērmaiyaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmayāṉa* vs. *nērmaiyaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** honest, upright, principled / dishonest, lacking integrity, unprincipled
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *nērmaiyaṟṟa* "contrasts directly with *nērmayāṉa* in parallel evaluative constructions" (P1, item 28). The privative suffix *-aṟṟa* (from *aṟu* — to be severed/lacking) produces the negative counterpart of the *-āṉa* adjectival.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** நம்பகமான / *nampakamāṉa*
2. **Term B:** நம்பகமற்ற / *nampakam aṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampakamāṉa* vs. *nampakam aṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, credible / unreliable, untrustworthy, not credible
5. **Description of contrast:** The data identifies *nampakam aṟṟa* as "the antonym of *nampakamāṉa*" used in legal, journalistic, and everyday evaluative contexts (P2a, item 8.2). The privative *-aṟṟa* suffix produces the negative counterpart of the *-āṉa* adjectival on the same base.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka*
2. **Term B:** நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampattakka* vs. *nampattakāta*
4. **English translation:** worthy of trust, credible / unworthy of trust, not credible
5. **Description of contrast:** The data presents these as a matched pair: *nampattakka* is "worthy of trust, credible" and *nampattakāta* is "its negative counterpart" (P1b, Root 2, items 13–14). Both are formed from the same dative infinitive base (*nampu* + *-at*) with *takka* (worthy of) versus *takāta* (not worthy of).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** நம்பிக்கையான / *nampikkaiyāṉa*
2. **Term B:** நம்பிக்கையற்ற / *nampikkaiyaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampikkaiyāṉa* vs. *nampikkaiyaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** trustworthy, reliable, faithful / untrustworthy, faithless, unreliable
5. **Description of contrast:** The data presents these as a matched adjectival pair built on *nampikkai*, with the *-āṉa* positive form and the *-aṟṟa* privative negative form (P1b, Root 2, items 6–7). Both are used in moral evaluation of persons and conduct.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

1. **Term A:** தரமான / *taramāṉa*
2. **Term B:** தரமற்ற / *taram aṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *taramāṉa* vs. *taram aṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** of good quality, up to standard / substandard, of poor quality
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *taramāṉa* "contrasts with *taram kuṟainta* or *taramillāta* (substandard, of poor quality)" (P1, item 26), and *taram aṟṟa* is documented as its privative antonym (P2a, item 25.2). The opposition is used in commercial and everyday evaluative contexts.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** சரியான / *cariyāṉa*
2. **Term B:** தவறான / *tavaṟāṉa*
3. **Transliteration:** *cariyāṉa* vs. *tavaṟāṉa*
4. **English translation:** correct, right, proper / wrong, erroneous, incorrect
5. **Description of contrast:** The data places *cari* and *tavaṟu* in the same semantic group (P3, Group 8) as the primary positive and negative terms for practical correctness. The adjectival forms *cariyāṉa* and *tavaṟāṉa* are their respective *-āṉa* derivatives and appear in parallel evaluative constructions across spoken and written registers.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது / *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu*
2. **Term B:** உண்மை மறைக்கப்பட்டது / *uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* vs. *uṇmai maṟaikkap-paṭṭatu*
4. **English translation:** The truth came to light / The truth was concealed
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents both constructions in P2b (Constructions 1.3 and 1.4) and groups them together in P3 (Group 2) as expressions of the disclosure versus concealment of truth. They are structurally parallel — both use *uṇmai* as subject — but with opposing verbs: *veḷippaṭu* (to emerge, become visible) versus *maṟai* (to conceal, hide) in the passive.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

1. **Term A:** சரிபார்க்கப்பட்ட / *caripārkkap-paṭṭa*
2. **Term B:** சரிபார்க்கப்படாத / *caripārkkap-paṭāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *caripārkkap-paṭṭa* vs. *caripārkkap-paṭāta*
4. **English translation:** verified, checked, confirmed / unverified, unchecked, not confirmed
5. **Description of contrast:** The data presents these as a matched passive participial pair built on the same verb *caripār* (P2a, items 23.1–23.2). The past passive participial (*-paṭṭa*) indicates completed verification; the negative passive participial (*-paṭāta*) indicates its absence. Both are used in administrative and journalistic contexts.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** நிரூபிக்கப்பட்ட / *nirūpikkap-paṭṭa* (implied by the data)
2. **Term B:** நிரூபிக்கப்படாத / *nirūpikkap-paṭāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nirūpikkap-paṭṭa* vs. *nirūpikkap-paṭāta*
4. **English translation:** proven, demonstrated / unproven, not demonstrated
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents *nirūpikkap-paṭāta* (unproven) as a passive negative participial (P2a, item 22.2) and references *nirūpitta uṇmai* (a proven truth) as an attested form (P2a, item 22.1). The opposition between proven and unproven status is explicitly used in legal discourse (*nirūpikkap-paṭāta kuṟṟam* — an unproven charge).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

1. **Term A:** ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāṉa*
2. **Term B:** ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāram aṟṟa* (also: ஆதாரமில்லாத / *ātāramillāta*)
3. **Transliteration:** *ātāramāṉa* vs. *ātāram aṟṟa* / *ātāramillāta*
4. **English translation:** well-founded, evidenced / unfounded, without basis, baseless
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents *ātāramāṉa* (well-founded, evidenced) and its privative counterparts *ātāram aṟṟa* and *ātāramillāta* (P2a, items 21.1–21.3). The two negative forms are presented as alternants of each other, with *ātāramillāta* slightly more common in spoken registers.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** உண்மை சொல்லு / *uṇmai collu*
2. **Term B:** பொய் சொல்லாதே / *poy collāte*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai collu* vs. *poy collāte*
4. **English translation:** Tell the truth / Don't tell lies
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents both as imperative constructions using the same light verb *col* (P2b, Constructions 1.1 and 2.1). They are structurally parallel — nominal object + *col* — but with opposing nominals (*uṇmai* vs. *poy*) and opposing polarity (positive imperative vs. negative imperative). Both are described as extremely common in moral instruction and everyday speech.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** கள்ளம் கபடம் இல்லாத / *kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta*
2. **Term B:** மனதில் கள்ளம் வைத்திருக்கிறான் / *maṉatil kaḷḷam vaittirukkirāṉ*
3. **Transliteration:** *kaḷḷam kapaṭam illāta* vs. *maṉatil kaḷḷam vaittirukkirāṉ*
4. **English translation:** without deceit or guile (guileless) / he harbors deceit in his heart
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents both constructions in P2b (Constructions 12.1 and 12.3) as opposing characterizations of a person with respect to *kaḷḷam*. The first uses the privative *illāta* to assert the complete absence of deceit; the second uses the locative-durative construction *maṉatil X vaittiru* to assert its internalized presence.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** உண்மைக்கு மாறான / *uṇmaikkum māṟāṉa*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* vs. *uṇmaikkum māṟāṉa*
4. **English translation:** truth / contrary to truth, at variance with the facts
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents *uṇmaikkum māṟāṉa* as a formal and legal expression meaning "contrary to truth" (P1, item 29; P1b, Root 1, item 10), formed from *uṇmai* + dative + *māṟu* (contrary, opposite) + *-āṉa*. The expression encodes opposition to *uṇmai* within its own morphological structure, making *uṇmai* itself one pole of the contrast.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Term B:** நேர்மையின்மை / *nērmayiṉmai*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmai* vs. *nērmayiṉmai*
4. **English translation:** honesty, integrity / dishonesty, absence of honesty
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents *nērmayiṉmai* as an abstract nominal formed from *nērmai* + *iṉmai* (absence, non-existence), used in formal and written Tamil to denote the property of lacking honesty (P1b, Root 3, item 13; P2a, item 3.4). The *iṉmai* suffix encodes the absence of the quality named by the base.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** நியாயம் கிடைக்கவில்லை / *niyāyam kiṭaikkavil̲l̲ai*
2. **Term B:** நியாயம் வெல்லும் / *niyāyam vellum*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyam kiṭaikkavil̲l̲ai* vs. *niyāyam vellum*
4. **English translation:** Justice was not obtained / Justice will prevail
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents both constructions in P2b (Constructions 14.3 and 14.4). They represent opposing outcomes for justice: failure to obtain it (*kiṭai* in the negative past) versus its ultimate triumph (*vel* — to win/prevail — in the future). Both are described as common in legal, political, and moral discourse.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** பொய்மை / *poymai*
2. **Term B:** உண்மை / *uṇmai* (or உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmai-t-taṉmai*)
3. **Transliteration:** *poymai* vs. *uṇmai* / *uṇmai-t-taṉmai*
4. **English translation:** falsehood, falseness (as abstract quality) / truth; truthfulness, authenticity (as abstract quality)
5. **Description of contrast:** The data states that *poymai* appears "in literary and philosophical contexts contrasting *uṇmai* (truth) with *poymai* (falsehood) as opposing qualities" (P2a, item 2.3). The more formally parallel opposition at the abstract nominal level is *uṇmai-t-taṉmai* (truthfulness, authenticity) versus *poymai* (falsehood, falseness), both being abstract nominals in the same register.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 22

1. **Term A:** அது உண்மைதான் / *atu uṇmaitāṉ*
2. **Term B:** அது பொய்யில்லை / *atu poyyillai*
3. **Transliteration:** *atu uṇmaitāṉ* vs. *atu poyyillai*
4. **English translation:** That is indeed the truth / That is not a lie
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents both as affirmative responses or assertions about the same state of affairs (P2b, Constructions 1.11 and 2.7), explicitly noting that *atu poyyillai* "functions as the negative counterpart of *atu uṇmaitāṉ*." They are structurally distinct — one is a positive emphatic predication, the other a negative existential — but both assert the truth of a claim.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
