## Methodological note

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contextual restriction, contrastive use, register distribution, collocational behavior, and domain restriction as documented across P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. No external semantic framework has been applied. Where usage patterns support multiple affiliations, items are listed under each relevant group and flagged in the overlapping items section. Items for which no clear grouping is supported by the documented evidence are listed explicitly at the end.

## Group 1: Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings involving documents, credentials, and transactions

**Label:** Document-and-transaction falsity vocabulary

**Definition:** These expressions appear predominantly in legal, criminal, notarial, and administrative contexts where the central question is whether a physical document, credential, signature, financial instrument, or formal transaction is genuine or has been altered, fabricated, or misrepresented. They are used by courts, prosecutors, forensic experts, and administrative bodies. They do not typically appear in philosophical or interpersonal discourse about honesty.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1b and P2b data show that q̇albi combines productively with a large set of document- and object-denoting nouns: q̇albi dokumenti, q̇albi puli, q̇albi banknotˈi, q̇albi xelmoq̇era, q̇albi moq̇moba, q̇albi angarišiˈ. piqtiuri appears in a parallel set of legal collocations: piqtiuri garigeba, piqtiuri kompania, piqtiuri qorwineba, piqtiuri dasaqmeba, piqtiuri misamarti. gaq̇albeba is the standard verbal noun for the act of falsification. These expressions do not appear in the everyday moral or interpersonal contexts where sicrue or mt'q̇uari appear.

**Representative expressions:**

- სიყალბე / siq̇albe — the abstract noun for the quality of being forged or counterfeit
- გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba — the act of forging or falsifying
- გაყალბებული / gaq̇albebuli — participial adjective for items that have been falsified
- გამაყალბებელი / gamaq̇albebeli — forger, falsifier
- ყალბობა / q̇alboba — forgery, fraud, falsification
- ყალბობის ფაქტი / q̇albobis paqti — the fact of forgery

**Notes on internal distinctions:**
- Within this group, q̇albi and gaq̇albebuli are the primary terms for the negative finding (the item is forged), while namdvili/namdviloba and avtentiuri/avtentiuroba are the primary terms for the positive finding (the item is genuine).
- Avtentiuri is restricted to formal and archival registers; namdvili covers the same ground in less formal usage.
- q̇albi is the native root and applies broadly across documents, currency, persons, and statements. piqtiuri is a borrowing with more restricted use, appearing primarily in commercial and administrative law contexts (transactions, companies, marriages, employment, addresses) rather than in the physical forgery contexts where q̇albi is most at home. The two overlap in legal discourse but are not interchangeable: piqtiuri garigeba is the standard phrase for a sham transaction, while q̇albi dokumenti is the standard phrase for a forged document.
- Kanonieri and ukanono appear in this group when the question is whether ownership or a claim is legally valid, rather than whether a physical object is genuine.

## Group 2: Expressions used in legal proceedings to evaluate testimony and witnesses

**Label:** Expressions used to assess the truthfulness and reliability of witnesses and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions appear in legal proceedings — trials, investigations, depositions — when the question is whether a witness is telling the truth and whether their testimony can be relied upon. They are used by courts, lawyers, and investigators to classify witnesses and statements as credible or false. This group is distinct from Group 1 in that it concerns persons and verbal acts rather than physical objects.

**Representative expressions:**

- სარწმუნო მტკიცებულება / sarwmuno mtkicebuleba — credible evidence
- სარწმუნო პირი / sarwmuno piri — reliable person
- სარწმუნო წყარო / sarwmuno wq̇aro — reliable source
- სარწმუნოდ ითვლება / sarwmunod itvleba — is considered reliable
- სანდო / sando — trustworthy, reliable
- სანდო წყარო / sando wq̇aro — trustworthy source
- სანდოობა / sandooba — trustworthiness
- არასანდო / arasando — untrustworthy
- დამაჯერებელი მტკიცებულება / damajerebeliˈ mtkicebuleba — convincing evidence
- დამაჯერებელი / damajerebeliˈ — convincing, persuasive
- დამაჯერებელი არ არის / damajerebeliˈ ar aris — is not convincing
- უტყუარი მტკიცებულება / utyuari mtkicebuleba — irrefutable evidence
- უტყუარი ფაქტი / utyuari paqti — indubitable fact
- უტყუარად დადგინდა / utyuarad dadginda — was established beyond doubt
- სიმართლის თქმა / simartlis tqma — truth-telling (in oath contexts)
- გამართლება / gamartleba — legal acquittal, vindication

**Notes on internal distinctions:**
- Sarwmuno and sando are near-synonyms; sando may carry a stronger interpersonal connotation.
- Damajerebeliˈ is distinguished from sarwmuno by emphasizing the effect on the hearer (persuasive force) rather than an intrinsic property of the source.
- Utyuari occupies the strongest position in this group, marking evidence or facts that are beyond any challenge.
- The group overlaps with Group 1 where the same terms (e.g., gaq̇albebuli mtkicebuleba) apply to both physical evidence and testimonial evidence.

## Group 3: Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings to refer to evidence and proof

**Label:** Expressions used in legal and administrative proceedings to refer to evidence and proof

**Definition:** These expressions appear specifically in legal procedural contexts — court proceedings, criminal investigations, administrative decisions — to refer to the material or acts by which claims are established or refuted. They are used in fixed legal collocations and do not typically appear in everyday conversational speech.

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that mtkicebuleba and mtkiceba appear in a dense cluster of legal collocations: mtkicebis tvirti ("burden of proof"), mtkicebis standart'i ("standard of proof"), mtkicebulebata šepaseba ("evaluation of evidence"), mtkicebulebata dasašveboba ("admissibility of evidence"), mtkicebulebata c̣ardgena ("submission of evidence"). These forms do not appear in the everyday constructions documented for martali, simartle, or namdvili.

**Representative expressions:**

- მტკიცებულება / mtkicebuleba — evidence, proof (legal)
- მტკიცება / mtkiceba — assertion, act of proving (legal/philosophical)
- მტკიცების ტვირთი / mtkicebis tvirti — burden of proof
- მტკიცების სტანდარტი / mtkicebis standart'i — standard of proof
- მტკიცებულებათა შეფასება / mtkicebulebata šepaseba — evaluation of evidence
- მტკიცებულებათა დასაშვებობა / mtkicebulebata dasašveboba — admissibility of evidence
- მტკიცებულებათა წარდგენა / mtkicebulebata c̣ardgena — submission of evidence
- დამტკიცებული / damtkicebuli — proven, ratified
- დადასტურება / dadastureba — confirmation, verification
- დადასტურებული / dadasturebuli — confirmed, verified
- დაუდასტურებელი / daudasturebeli — unconfirmed

**Notes on internal distinctions:**
- Mtkicebuleba refers to the material of proof (evidence as a thing), while mtkiceba refers to the act of asserting or proving.
- Dadastureba appears in both legal and journalistic contexts (see also Group 6), making it a candidate for multiple groups.
- Damtkiceba appears in both legal ratification contexts and argumentative proof contexts, giving it a broader range than mtkicebuleba.

## Group 4: Expressions used to evaluate the factual correctness of statements, answers, and claims

**Label:** Factual correctness vocabulary

**Definition:** These expressions are used when evaluating whether a specific statement, answer, calculation, or claim corresponds to the facts or is logically sound. They appear in educational, journalistic, scientific, and everyday contexts where the question is whether something said or written is right or wrong. They contrast with expressions focused on documentary forgery (Group 1) and with expressions focused on personal sincerity (Group 9).

**Basis for grouping:** The P2b data shows that martali, sc̣ori, and zusti all collocate with pasuxi ("answer") and informacia ("information"), and all appear in constructions where a speaker confirms or endorses a claim. The adverbial forms martla, martlac, zustad, and sc̣orad function as discourse-level confirmations in spoken Georgian. The predicative constructions martali xar, martali var, zustad asea, and sc̣oria all serve the same interactional function of confirming correctness.

**Representative expressions:**

- სწორი / sc̣ori — correct, right, accurate (sc̣ori pasuxiˈ, "correct answer")
- სწორია თუ არა? / sc̣oria tu ara? — is it correct or not?
- სწორად / sc̣orad — correctly, rightly
- სწორად გავიგე / sc̣orad gavige — I understood correctly
- სისწორე / sisc̣ore — correctness, accuracy (as an abstract property)
- გასწორება / gasc̣oreba — correction, rectification
- გასწორებული / gasc̣orebuli — corrected
- არასწორი / arasc̣ori — incorrect, wrong
- არასწორად / arasc̣orad — incorrectly, wrongly
- მართალი / martali — right, correct (applied to statements and claims)
- მართალია / martalia — is right, is correct (predicative)
- მართალი ხარ / martali xar — you are right
- მართალი გამოდგა / martali gamodga — turned out to be right
- მართალია, მაგრამ... / martalia, magram... — concessive acknowledgment of truth
- მართლა / martla — really, truly (truth-asserting adverb)
- მართლაც / martlac — indeed, truly (emphatic)
- მართლაც რომ / martlac rom — it is indeed the case that
- მართლა? / martla? — really? (challenge/question)
- მცდარი / mcdari — erroneous, mistaken, wrong (honest error, not deliberate deception)
- მცდარი შეხედულება / mcdari šexeduleba — a mistaken view
- მცდარი გამოდგა / mcdari gamodga — turned out to be wrong
- მცდარი წარმოდგენა / mcdari c̣armogdena — a misconception
- მცდარი გზით / mcdari gzit — by the wrong path or method
- მცდარად / mcdarad — erroneously, mistakenly
- სიმცდარე / simcdare — erroneousness, incorrectness
- მცრუ / mcru — false, deceptive (formal/literary)
- ზუსტი / zusti — exact, precise, accurate
- ზუსტი თარგმანი / zusti targmani — accurate translation
- ზუსტი მონაცემები / zusti monacemebi — precise data
- ზუსტად ასეა / zustad asea — that is exactly right
- ზუსტი პასუხი არ არის / zusti pasuxiˈ ar aris — there is no precise answer
- სიზუსტე / sizuste — accuracy, precision
- უზუსტო / uzusto — inaccurate, imprecise
- უზუსტო ინფორმაცია / uzusto inpormacia — inaccurate information
- უზუსტო მონაცემები / uzusto monacemebi — inaccurate data
- უზუსტობა / uzustoba — inaccuracy, imprecision
- დაზუსტება / dazusteba — specification, clarification, making precise
- დაზუსტებული / dazustebuli — specified, clarified
- მართებული / martebuli — correct, appropriate, well-founded
- მართებულად / martebuladˈ — correctly, appropriately
- მართებულობა / martebuloba — correctness, soundness, validity
- სიმართლე ისაა, რომ... / simartle isaa, rom... — the fact is that..., the truth is that...

**Notes on internal distinctions:**
- Zusti and sc̣ori both appear with pasuxi but zusti emphasizes precision of correspondence (e.g., to a measurement or translation), while sc̣ori emphasizes correctness of an answer or decision.
- Martali appears more freely in predicative constructions about persons ("you are right") and carries a broader range including moral uprightness (see Group 7).
- The adverbial forms martla and martlac function as discourse particles in spoken Georgian in ways that zustad and sc̣orad do not.
- Mcdari is important within this group because it marks error without implying deliberate deception, distinguishing it from q̇albi (which implies intent to deceive).
- Martebuli is used specifically for well-foundedness of arguments and decisions — it implies that something has been correctly reasoned or appropriately applied, not merely that it is factually accurate.

## Group 5: Expressions used in contexts of establishing, seeking, or asserting truth in investigative, legal, and moral discourse

**Label:** Truth-establishment and truth-assertion vocabulary

**Definition:** These expressions appear when the central activity is determining what actually happened, asserting that a claim is true, or describing the process of uncovering truth. They are used in court proceedings, journalistic investigations, moral argument, and everyday speech when someone is asserting their own truthfulness or demanding that of another. They differ from Group 4 in that they focus on the act or process of truth-telling and truth-finding rather than on the correctness of a specific answer or measurement.

**Representative expressions:**

- სიმართლე / simartle — truth (the primary everyday word for truth)

- სიმართლეს ვამბობ / simartles vambob — I am telling the truth
- სიმართლე გითხრა / simartle gitxra — let me tell you the truth (informal); to tell you the truth (sincerity marker)
- სიმართლე მოგახსენო / simartle mogaxseno — let me tell you the truth (formal/polite)
- სიმართლე ისაა, რომ / simartle isaa, rom — the truth is that
- სიმართლეს თუ ვიტყვი / simartles tu vitq̇vi — to be honest; if I am to tell the truth
- სიმართლე გამოვიდა / simartle gamovida — the truth came out
- სიმართლე ეთქვა / simartle etqva — the truth was told to him/her

## Group 6: Expressions used in philosophical, theological, and literary discourse about truth as an abstract or essential property

**Label:** Abstract and essential truth vocabulary

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth is treated as a property of fundamental importance — as an abstract quality, a theological attribute, or the essential nature of something — rather than as a feature of a specific statement or document. They are characteristic of religious texts, philosophical writing, and literary criticism. They tend to co-occur with nouns denoting God, faith, knowledge, nature, and value rather than with nouns denoting documents, answers, or data.

**Basis for grouping:** The P1b and P2b data show that č̣ešmariteba and č̣ešmariti appear in a cluster of philosophical and theological constructions not shared by simartle or martali: č̣ešmaritebis dzieba ("the search for truth"), č̣ešmaritebis kriteriumiˈ ("criterion of truth"), č̣ešmaritebis teoria ("theory of truth"), č̣ešmariti ɣmerti ("the true God"), č̣ešmariti sarcmunoeba ("the true faith"), č̣ešmariteba ertia ("truth is one"), č̣ešmariteba maradiulia ("truth is eternal"). The adverbial form č̣ešmaritad is described as appearing in classical Georgian texts and formal prose.
