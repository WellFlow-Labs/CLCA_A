# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[Adjective] + [Noun] — prenominal attributive; adjective precedes noun and agrees in case.

### Examples
- ჭეშმარიტი ამბავი / č̣ešmariti ambavi / "a true account; a factual story"
- ყალბი დოკუმენტი / q̇albi dokumenti / "a forged document"

### Notes
Georgian adjectives precede the noun and take the same case suffix as the noun they modify. No copula is involved. This is the unmarked attributive pattern across all truth-domain adjectives (č̣ešmariti, martali, sc̣ori, zusti, q̇albi, namdvili, etc.).

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
[ara- + Adjective] + [Noun] — privative prefix ara- attached to adjective; or [Adjective] + [Noun] + [ar aris] for predicative negation.

### Examples
- არასწორი პასუხი / arasc̣ori pasuxiˈ / "an incorrect answer"
- არაგულწრფელი ბოდიში / aragulc̣rpeli bodišiˈ / "an insincere apology"

### Notes
The prefix ara- is the productive negation strategy for native adjectives in this domain (arasc̣ori, arasc̣muno, aragulc̣rpeli). The privative circumfix u-...-o is attested for one item (uzusto) but is less productive across the set. Predicative negation uses the separate negative particle ar with the copula.

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION (genitive)

### Pattern
[Noun-GEN] + [Head Noun] — genitive modifier (suffix -is / -is) precedes head noun.

### Examples
- სიმართლის დადგენა / simarṭlis dadgena / "establishment of the truth; determination of the facts"
- სიყალბის ნიშნები / siq̇albis nišnebi / "signs of forgery; indicators of falsity"

### Notes
The genitive suffix -is attaches to the modifying noun, which precedes the head. This pattern generates the majority of multi-word truth-domain nominals documented in P1b and P2b (simarṭlis tqma, č̣ešmaritebis dzieba, namdvilobis šemoc̣meba, etc.). The construction is fully productive.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
[Noun/Adjective] + gamodga / aġmočnda — "turned out to be X; proved to be X"; or gamovida — "came out, emerged" (for truth becoming known).

### Examples
- სიმართლე გამოვიდა / simarṭle gamovida / "the truth came out; the truth emerged"
- მართალი გამოდგა / martali gamodga / "turned out to be right; proved correct"

### Notes
Georgian lacks a single inchoative copula equivalent to "become." The verbs gamodga ("turned out to be") and aġmočnda ("proved to be, was found to be") fill this function for truth-domain predicates, expressing the discovery or confirmation of a property. Both are attested with truth adjectives (martali, mcdari, q̇albi, namdvili) and with the noun simarṭle.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
[da-/ga- preverb] + [root] + [-eba nominalization] / finite verb — preverb + root constructions express caused change of state; no dedicated causative morpheme isolated to this domain.

### Examples
- გაყალბება / gaq̇albeba / "forgery; the act of making [something] false" (from q̇alb-)
- დაზუსტება / dazusteba / "making precise; specifying exactly" (from zust-)

### Notes
Georgian does not have a single productive causative suffix equivalent to English "make X true/false." Caused-state meanings in this domain are expressed through preverb + root + -eba verbal nouns (gamarṭleba "making right/justifying," gasc̣oreba "correcting," gaq̇albeba "forging"). The preverb ga- is most frequent in this set; da- also appears (dazusteba, dadastureba). These forms are lexicalized rather than generated by a transparent causative rule.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Two productive patterns:
(a) si-...-e circumfix on root: simartle, siq̇albe, sicrue, sizuste, sisc̣ore
(b) Adjective + -eba suffix: č̣ešmariteba, namdviloba, gulc̣rpelobaˈ, marṭebuloba, avtentiuroba

### Examples
- სიმართლე / simartle / "truth; correctness" (si- + marṭ- + -e)
- ჭეშმარიტება / č̣ešmariteba / "truth; truthfulness; veracity" (č̣ešmariti + -eba)

### Notes
The circumfix si-...-e operates on bare roots and yields abstract quality nouns; it is the older native pattern. The suffix -eba attaches to adjectival bases and is more productive with borrowed and compound adjectives. Both patterns are well-attested in this domain. The suffix -oba (namdviloba, gulc̣rpelobaˈ, avtentiuroba) is a third nominalizing strategy, typically yielding nouns denoting a state or quality associated with the adjective base.

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
(a) [Noun-GEN] + moq̇vare / madziebeliˈ — "lover/seeker of [noun]": genitive + agentive noun
(b) [Verb root] + -veli / -eli suffix: mimbadzveliˈ, tvaltmaqci

### Examples
- სიმართლის მაძიებელი / simarṭlis madziebeliˈ / "seeker of truth; truth-seeker"
- მიმბაძველი / mimbadzveliˈ / "imitator; one who copies"

### Notes
Georgian has no single agentive suffix dedicated to truth-domain roles. "Truth-teller" and "liar" as single-word agentive nouns are not prominently attested in the P1–P3 data; the documented expressions use genitive + agentive noun compounds (simarṭlis moq̇vare, simarṭlis madziebeliˈ) or the verbal noun construction (simarṭlis tqma, sicruis tqma) rather than a single derived agentive form. The agentive suffix -veli is attested in mimbadzveliˈ; the compound tvaltmaqci functions as an agentive for "deceiver/hypocrite."

## 8. PREDICATIVE TRUTH-ASSERTION (additional pattern)

### Pattern
[simarṭle / č̣ešmariteba] + [copula -a / itqva / isaa rom] — truth-noun as subject of copular or passive construction; or [simarṭle] + [tkva / vambob / gitxra] — truth-noun as object of speech verb.

### Examples
- სიმართლე ისაა, რომ / simarṭle isaa, rom / "the truth is that…" (framing construction)
- სიმართლეს ვამბობ / simarṭles vambob / "I am telling the truth" (first-person assertion)

### Notes
This pattern is highly productive in Georgian and has no single-word equivalent. The truth noun (simarṭle or č̣ešmariteba) appears as subject or object in a clause whose main verb is a speech verb (tkva, ambobs, axseneba) or the copula. The dative object construction (simarṭles vambob, sicrues ver etq̇vi) is structurally distinctive: the truth/lie noun takes dative case as the object of the speech verb. These constructions function as the primary everyday mechanism for asserting or denying one's own truthfulness.

## 9. OSTENSIBLE / APPARENT PREDICATION (additional pattern)

### Pattern
[močvenebiti / piqtiuri] + [Noun] + [aqvs / aris] — adjective in attributive or predicative position asserting that a property is apparent rather than real; or [piqtiurad / q̇albad] + [passive verb] — adverbial form with passive verb asserting nominal or fictitious status.

### Examples
- მოჩვენებითი ხასიათი აქვს / močvenebiti xasiati aqvs / "it has an ostensible/illusory character; it is merely apparent in nature"
- ფიქტიურად ირიცხება / piqtiurad iricxeba / "is fictitiously registered; is nominally enrolled"

### Notes
This pattern is specific to legal and analytical discourse and expresses the distinction between appearance and reality. The adjectival form (močvenebiti, piqtiuri) in attributive position asserts that a property is surface-level only; the adverbial form (piqtiurad, q̇albad) with a passive verb asserts that an action or status is nominal rather than genuine. Both constructions are documented primarily in legal registers.
