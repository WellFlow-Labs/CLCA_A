# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

This step examines how Yoruba encodes dynamic states — becoming, causing, and remaining — with respect to the truth-related vocabulary documented in P1–P5. The analysis draws exclusively on vocabulary, constructions, and patterns attested in the preceding steps. Where a given term does not participate in a particular dynamic pattern, this is stated explicitly. Terms are treated separately where their behavior differs.

Three general observations from the prior data are relevant here:

1. Yoruba has no dedicated inchoative or causative morpheme in this domain. Change-of-state and causation are expressed through lexical verbs, serial verb constructions, and periphrastic structures (P4a, Construction 4 and 5).
2. The most productive change-of-state verb in the epistemic domain is *farahàn* (to become visible, to be revealed, to become clear), which takes *pé* + propositional complement (P2b, Construction 4).
3. The most productive causative structure is the serial verb *fi* X *hàn* (to cause X to be seen/revealed) and *fi* X *gbà* Y (to use X to cause receipt of Y), alongside the permissive *jẹ́ kí* (P4a, Construction 5).

---

## SECTION 1: X BECOMES A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

---

### 1.1 òtítọ́ — truth, factual correctness

**Pattern:** *farahàn pé* + propositional complement

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni*
> It became clear that it is the truth / It turned out to be true

**Notes:** The verb *farahàn* (to become visible, to appear, to be revealed) is the primary lexical vehicle for expressing the emergence of truth into clarity. The construction does not assert that something *becomes* true in an ontological sense but that its truth-status becomes apparent or established. The subject of *farahàn* is typically an impersonal *ó* (it) with the propositional content introduced by *pé*.

No construction expressing "X becomes truth" in the sense of a proposition changing from false to true is attested in the data. The available constructions express revelation of pre-existing truth rather than ontological change.

---

**Pattern:** *hàn gbangba* (to become clearly visible)

**Attested example (P2b, under ìwà):**
> *Ìwà rẹ̀ hàn gbangba*
> His/her true character is clearly visible / His/her true nature has become evident

**Notes:** *hàn* (to appear, become visible) with the adverb *gbangba* (openly, clearly) expresses the becoming-apparent of a previously concealed or unrecognized state. In the truth domain this applies to character (*ìwà*) and, by extension, to the truth of a matter becoming publicly evident. The subject is the thing revealed, not the proposition about it.

---

### 1.2 irọ́ — lie, falsehood

**Pattern:** *A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni* (it becomes known that it is a lie)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *A mọ̀ pé irọ́ ni*
> We know that it is a lie / It is known to be a lie

**Notes:** This construction does not express becoming in a morphological sense but expresses the epistemic transition from not-knowing to knowing that something is false. The verb *mọ̀* (to know) in its perfective reading implies that knowledge has been acquired. No dedicated "becomes a lie" construction is attested; the closest available expression is the revelation construction with *farahàn* applied to falsehood:

> *Ó farahàn pé irọ́ ni*
> It became clear that it is a lie / It turned out to be false

This construction is not explicitly attested in P2b for *irọ́* but follows directly from the attested pattern for *òtítọ́* and the parallel syntactic behavior of the two terms documented throughout P1–P5.

---

### 1.3 ìgbàgbọ́ — belief, faith

**Pattern:** *yí padà* (serial verb: to turn around, to change)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà*
> His/her belief changed / His/her faith shifted

**Notes:** The serial verb *yí padà* (lit. turn-return) is the attested expression for change in the state of belief. It applies to the nominal *ìgbàgbọ́* as subject and expresses a change of orientation or commitment. The direction of change (from belief to unbelief, or from one belief to another) is not specified by the construction itself; context determines this.

---

**Pattern:** *dúró ṣinṣin nínú* (to stand firm in) — stability, not change

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó dúró ṣinṣin nínú ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀*
> He/she stood firm in his/her faith / He/she remained steadfast in his/her belief

**Notes:** This construction expresses resistance to change — the maintenance of a belief state under pressure. It is included here as the stability counterpart to *yí padà*. The verb *dúró* (to stand, remain) with the adverb *ṣinṣin* (firmly, steadfastly) and the locative *nínú* (in) produces a construction expressing that a belief state has not changed despite circumstances that might cause it to.

---

### 1.4 ìwà — character, nature

**Pattern:** *hàn gbangba* (to become clearly visible)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ìwà rẹ̀ hàn gbangba*
> His/her true character is clearly visible / His/her true nature has become evident

**Notes:** As noted under 1.1, *hàn gbangba* expresses the becoming-apparent of character. Character itself (*ìwà*) is not described in the data as changing; the constructions in P2b evaluate it as good or bad, as visible or concealed, but not as undergoing transformation. The absence of a "character becomes good/bad" construction in the data is consistent with the P1 description of *ìwà* as referring to fundamental nature rather than isolated acts.

---

### 1.5 tọ́ — correctness, rightness

**Pattern:** *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* as evaluative predicates

**Notes:** No "becomes correct" or "becomes incorrect" construction is attested in the data for *tọ́* as a stative predicate. The predicate *ó tọ́* evaluates a current state; change toward correctness is expressed through the correction constructions *ìtọ́sí* (rectification) and *tọ́ sí* (to correct toward), documented in P1b and P2a. These are causative in orientation (see Section 2 below) rather than inchoative.

---

### 1.6 gidi — genuine, authentic

**Pattern:** *ẹ ṣàyẹ̀wò bóyá ó jẹ́ gidi* (examine whether it is genuine)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ẹ ṣàyẹ̀wò bóyá ó jẹ́ gidi*
> Examine it to see whether it is genuine / Check whether it is authentic

**Notes:** This construction frames genuineness as a state to be verified rather than one that comes into being. No "becomes genuine" construction is attested in the data. The copular construction *ó jẹ́ gidi* (it is genuine) is stative; *gidi* does not participate in inchoative constructions in the attested data.

---

### 1.7 Summary of inchoative patterns

The following inchoative patterns are attested in the data:

| Expression | Applies to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *farahàn pé* + complement | *òtítọ́*, *irọ́*, propositions generally | truth/falsity of a matter becomes apparent |
| *hàn gbangba* | *ìwà*, character states | a state becomes publicly visible |
| *yí padà* | *ìgbàgbọ́* | a belief or faith changes |
| *mọ̀ pé* (perfective) | *irọ́*, *òtítọ́* | it becomes known that something is true/false |

No inchoative construction is attested for *tọ́* (correctness), *gidi* (genuineness), *ẹ̀rí* (evidence), or *ìjẹ́rìísí* (certification) as states that come into being. These are treated in the data as states that are evaluated or verified, not as states that emerge.

## SECTION 2: Y CAUSES X TO BE A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

---

### 2.1 Causing truth to be revealed: *fi...hàn*

**Pattern:** *fi* + [truth-related nominal] + *hàn* (serial verb: to use/take...to show/reveal)

**Attested examples (P2b):**
> *Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn*
> He/she revealed the truth / He/she showed the truth

> *Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀*
> He/she presented evidence before the court / He/she caused evidence to be seen before the tribunal

**Notes:** The serial verb construction *fi* X *hàn* is the primary causative structure for causing truth or evidence to become visible or known. The agent is the subject; the truth-related nominal occupies the object slot of *fi*; *hàn* (to show, reveal, make visible) is the result verb. The locative *lójú* (before the face of, in the presence of) optionally specifies the recipient audience.

This construction applies productively to *òtítọ́* (truth), *ẹ̀rí* (evidence), and *ìmọ̀* (knowledge):

> *Ó fi ìmọ̀ rẹ̀ hàn* (P2b)
> He/she demonstrated his/her knowledge / He/she caused his/her knowledge to be seen

---

### 2.2 Causing someone to believe: *dánilójú*

**Pattern:** *dánilójú* (to convince, to assure, to establish in someone's eyes)

**Attested examples (P2a):**
> *dánilójú* — to convince, to assure, to provide proof to, to satisfy

**Notes:** The verb *dánilójú* (from *dá* — to establish + *ni* — for/to someone + *lójú* — in the eye/face of) is the primary causative verb for causing a person to arrive at a state of conviction or assurance. Its nominalization *ìdánilójú* (assurance, convincing proof) and its negation *àìdánilójú* (failure to convince) are documented in P2a. The construction expresses that an agent causes a recipient to hold a belief or be convinced of a truth.

No full sentence example with *dánilójú* is attested in P2b, but the verbal base is documented in P2a with the meaning "to establish in someone's eyes." The construction would take the form:

> *Ó dánilójú pé òtítọ́ ni*
> He/she convinced [them] that it is the truth / He/she established in their eyes that it is true

This follows directly from the morphological description and the attested nominalization *ìdánilójú*.

---

### 2.3 Causing falsehood to be used as an instrument: *fi irọ́ gbà*

**Pattern:** *fi irọ́ gbà* + [result] (serial verb: to use lies to obtain)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó*
> He/she obtained money through deception / He/she used lies to cause receipt of money

**Notes:** This construction treats *irọ́* (falsehood) as an instrument in a causative chain: the agent uses falsehood to cause a result (here, obtaining money). The construction is documented in P2b in the context of fraud. It is the primary attested pattern for expressing causation through deception.

---

### 2.4 Causing concealment of truth: *pa...mọ́* and *bo...pamọ́*

**Pattern:** *pa* + [truth-related nominal] + *mọ́* (serial verb: to keep/conceal)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́*
> He/she is concealing the truth / He/she is hiding the truth

**Pattern:** *bo* + [truth-related nominal] + *pamọ́* (serial verb: to cover/conceal)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó ń gbìyànjú láti bo irọ́ rẹ̀ pamọ́*
> He/she is trying to conceal his/her lie / He/she is attempting to hide his/her falsehood

**Notes:** These two serial verb constructions express causation of concealment. *pa...mọ́* applies to *òtítọ́* (truth); *bo...pamọ́* applies to *irọ́* (a lie). Both involve an agent causing a truth-related state to remain hidden. The progressive marker *ń* in both attested examples indicates ongoing action. These constructions are the dynamic counterparts to the revelation construction *fi...hàn*: where *fi...hàn* causes something to become visible, *pa...mọ́* and *bo...pamọ́* cause it to remain concealed.

---

### 2.5 Causing correction: *tọ́ sí* and *ìtọ́sí*

**Pattern:** *tọ́* + [object] + *sí* (to correct toward, to direct properly)

**Attested examples (P1b, P2b):**
> *Ẹ tọ́ ọ sí ọ̀nà tó tọ́*
> Direct him/her to the correct path / Guide him/her to the right way

**Notes:** The verbal phrase *tọ́...sí* (to correct toward, to direct properly) expresses causation of correctness: an agent causes a person or thing to move toward the correct state or path. The nominalization *ìtọ́sí* (correction, rectification) refers to this act as a completed or named process. This is the primary causative construction for the *tọ́* domain.

---

### 2.6 Causing acceptance of truth: *gbà...gbọ́*

**Pattern:** *gbà* + [truth-related nominal] + *gbọ́* (serial verb: to accept/receive...to hear/believe)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó gbà òtítọ́ gbọ́*
> He/she accepted the truth / He/she received the truth

**Notes:** This serial verb construction expresses the act of a person causing themselves to accept a truth — or, in a causative reading, an agent causing a recipient to receive and accept a truth. The construction combines *gbà* (to accept, receive) and *gbọ́* (to hear, believe), the two components of *gbàgbọ́* (to believe), in a serial structure that emphasizes the act of reception. It is documented in P2b as a construction for accepting a true statement or teaching.

---

### 2.7 Causing someone to testify: *jẹ́rìí* constructions

**Pattern:** *kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí* (refusal to testify — blocking causation)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó kọ̀ láti jẹ́rìí*
> He/she refused to testify / He/she declined to bear witness

**Notes:** This construction expresses the blocking of testimony — a person causing the act of *jẹ́rìí* (testifying) not to occur. No construction expressing "Y causes X to testify" is attested in the data beyond the imperative *Ẹ jẹ́rìí sí ohun tí ẹ rí* (testify to what you saw), which is a directive rather than a causative in the strict sense.

---

### 2.8 Permissive causation: *jẹ́ kí*

**Pattern:** *jẹ́ kí* + subjunctive (let/allow that)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́*
> Let us speak the truth / Let us tell the truth

**Notes:** The construction *jẹ́ kí* (let, allow that) expresses permissive causation — causing a state of affairs to come about by allowing or enabling it. In the truth domain it appears in hortative and exhortative contexts calling for truthful speech. It is documented in P4a as a distinct causative sub-type from the *fi*-serial verb causative.

---

### 2.9 Summary of causative patterns

The following causative patterns are attested in the data:

| Construction | Agent causes... | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| *fi* X *hàn* | X to become visible/revealed | *òtítọ́*, *ẹ̀rí*, *ìmọ̀* |
| *dánilójú* | recipient to be convinced | *òtítọ́*, propositions generally |
| *fi irọ́ gbà* + result | result through deception | *irọ́* as instrument |
| *pa* X *mọ́* | X to remain concealed | *òtítọ́* |
| *bo* X *pamọ́* | X to remain hidden | *irọ́* |
| *tọ́...sí* / *ìtọ́sí* | correction toward correctness | *tọ́* domain |
| *gbà* X *gbọ́* | acceptance/reception of X | *òtítọ́* |
| *jẹ́ kí* + subjunctive | permissive: enabling truthful speech | *sọ òtítọ́* |

## SECTION 3: STABILITY — RESISTANCE TO CHANGE

---

### 3.1 Proverbial stability of truth

**Pattern:** *kò ní kú* (will not die — prospective negative)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Òtítọ́ kò ní kú*
> Truth will not die / Truth is indestructible

**Notes:** This proverbial construction uses the prospective negative *kò ní* (will not) with the verb *kú* (to die) to assert the permanent, unchanging nature of truth. It is documented in P2b as widely attested in moral discourse and everyday speech. The construction expresses stability as resistance to the most extreme form of change (cessation of existence).

---

### 3.2 Stability of belief under pressure

**Pattern:** *dúró ṣinṣin nínú* (to stand firm in)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ó dúró ṣinṣin nínú ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀*
> He/she stood firm in his/her faith / He/she remained steadfast in his/her belief

**Notes:** As noted in Section 1.3, this construction expresses the maintenance of a belief state against potential change. The verb *dúró* (to stand, remain) with *ṣinṣin* (firmly) and the locative *nínú* (in) produces a stability construction that is the direct counterpart to *yí padà* (to change). Both apply to *ìgbàgbọ́* as subject.

---

### 3.3 Stability of justice

**Pattern:** *yóò ṣẹ* (will be fulfilled — prospective)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Òdodo yóò ṣẹ*
> Justice will prevail / Righteousness will be fulfilled

**Notes:** This construction uses the prospective marker *yóò* (will) with the verb *ṣẹ* (to be fulfilled, to come to pass) to assert the ultimate realization of justice. It expresses a different kind of stability from *kò ní kú*: not resistance to destruction but inevitable fulfillment. The construction is documented in P2b as appearing in religious and moral discourse.

---

### 3.4 Stability of character

**Pattern:** *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (character is what makes a person — proverbial)

**Attested example (P2b):**
> *Ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn*
> It is character that makes a person / Character is what defines a person

**Notes:** This proverbial cleft construction asserts the primacy and permanence of character as the defining quality of a person. It does not express stability in the same syntactic sense as *dúró ṣinṣin* or *kò ní kú*, but it encodes a conceptual claim about the unchanging, fundamental nature of *ìwà* that is consistent with the P1 description of *ìwà* as referring to what a person fundamentally is rather than what they do in isolated acts.

---

### 3.5 Summary of stability patterns

The following stability patterns are attested in the data:

| Construction | Applies to | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *kò ní kú* | *òtítọ́* | truth will not die; truth is permanent |
| *dúró ṣinṣin nínú* | *ìgbàgbọ́* | belief/faith remains firm, does not change |
| *yóò ṣẹ* | *òdodo* | justice will inevitably be fulfilled |
| *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* (proverbial) | *ìwà* | character is the permanent defining quality of a person |

---

## SECTION 4: PATTERNS NOT ATTESTED

The following dynamic expressions are not attested in the data and cannot be reported:

- "X becomes correct/right" (*tọ́* as an inchoative state): no construction expressing a transition into correctness is attested; correction is expressed causatively (*tọ́ sí*, *ìtọ́sí*) rather than inchoatively.
- "X becomes genuine" (*gidi* as an inchoative state): no construction attested; *gidi* appears only in stative and verificational constructions.
- "X becomes evidence" (*ẹ̀rí* as an inchoative state): no construction attested; evidence is gathered (*gba ẹ̀rí jọ*) and presented (*fi ẹ̀rí hàn*) but not described as coming into being.
- "Y causes X to have good character" (*ìwà rere* as a caused state): no construction attested; character is evaluated and described but not caused by an external agent in the attested data.
- "Y causes X to be certified" (*ìjẹ́rìísí* as a caused state): no causative construction with *ìjẹ́rìísí* as result is attested; certification is verified (*gba ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ wò*) and evaluated (*ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo / irọ́*) but not expressed as caused.
