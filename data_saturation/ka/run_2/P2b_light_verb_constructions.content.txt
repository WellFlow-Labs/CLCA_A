# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Methodological note:** This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 lexical items. Derivational forms already documented in P2a are excluded. Constructions already fully documented in P1b (fixed phrases, collocations, and idiomatic expressions identified during root expansion) are cross-referenced rather than re-described in full, with a brief note confirming their status. New constructions not previously documented are described in full. Where no common construction exists for a given item or pattern, this is stated explicitly. Register variation is noted where relevant.

A construction is included only where there is positive reason to believe it is natural and attested in Georgian. Constructions are not generated to fill expected semantic slots.

---

## 1. Base Form: ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი ღმერთი
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti ɣmerti
4. **English translation:** the true God
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. A fixed theological phrase used in Christian liturgical and doctrinal texts, including the Nicene Creed in Georgian. Distinguishes the God of Christian faith from false gods.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი სიყვარული
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti siq̇varuli
4. **English translation:** true love, genuine love
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. Used in literary, religious, and everyday contexts to distinguish authentic love from superficial or counterfeit forms. A common collocation in Georgian poetry and prose.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი მეგობარი
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti megobari
4. **English translation:** a true friend, a genuine friend
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. Used in moral and everyday discourse to distinguish a genuinely loyal friend from a superficial or false one. Frequent in proverbs and moral literature.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი ცოდნა
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti codna
4. **English translation:** true knowledge, genuine knowledge
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. Used in philosophical and theological contexts to distinguish genuine knowledge from mere opinion or false belief. Appears in epistemological discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი ბუნება
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti buneba
4. **English translation:** true nature, genuine nature, real nature
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. Used in philosophical and theological contexts to refer to the essential or authentic nature of a thing or person, as opposed to its appearance or accidental properties.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი მოწმე
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti mowme
4. **English translation:** a truthful witness, a true witness
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. Used in legal and religious contexts; in theological usage also connects to the concept of martyrdom (Greek martys, "witness").

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** ჭეშმარიტი (č̣ešmariti)
2. **Example:** ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვნება
3. **Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti xelovneba
4. **English translation:** true art, genuine art
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b as [MARGINAL]. Used in aesthetic and critical discourse to distinguish authentic artistic expression from imitation or commercial production.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

*Note on č̣ešmariti constructions: The adjective č̣ešmariti functions primarily as a prenominal modifier in noun phrases of the pattern č̣ešmariti + N, where it asserts the genuine or authentic character of the referent. No periphrastic or verbal constructions with č̣ešmariti as a predicate have been identified beyond the standard copular pattern (X č̣ešmaritia, "X is true/genuine"), which is a regular inflectional pattern rather than a fixed construction. No idiomatic constructions specific to č̣ešmariti (as opposed to its nominal derivative č̣ešmariteba) have been identified.*

## 3. Base Form: სიმართლე (simartle)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლის თქმა
3. **Transliteration:** simartlis t̕q̇ma
4. **English translation:** truth-telling, the telling of the truth
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b and P2a. A fixed nominal phrase for the act of stating what is true. Highly frequent in legal oath contexts and moral discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე გამოვიდა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle gamovida
4. **English translation:** the truth came out, the truth emerged
5. **Usage notes:** Documented in P1b. A fixed idiomatic expression used in narrative and everyday speech to describe the revelation of a previously concealed truth. Extremely common in storytelling and journalistic contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე თვალში გიყრის
3. **Transliteration:** simartle t̕valši giq̇ris
4. **English translation:** the truth stares you in the face
5. **Usage notes:** Colloquial variant of the č̣ešmariteba construction above. More common in everyday speech than the č̣ešmariteba version. Used to assert that a truth is self-evident or undeniable.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლეს ვამბობ
3. **Transliteration:** simartle s vambob
4. **English translation:** I am telling the truth, I speak the truth
5. **Usage notes:** A first-person assertion of truthfulness used in everyday speech and in legal or quasi-legal contexts. The construction uses the verb amboba (to say, to tell) with simartle as direct object. Functions as a performative assertion of honesty.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე გითხრა?
3. **Transliteration:** simartle git̕xra?
4. **English translation:** Shall I tell you the truth? / To tell you the truth...
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse-initial formula used in spoken Georgian to preface a candid or potentially unwelcome statement. Functions as a hedging device that signals the speaker is about to say something frank. The subjunctive form git̕xra (shall I tell you) is characteristic.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე მოგახსენო
3. **Transliteration:** simartle mogaxseno
4. **English translation:** to tell you the truth (polite register)
5. **Usage notes:** A polite or formal variant of the preceding construction, using the honorific verb form mogaxseno (to inform you, to tell you respectfully). Used in formal speech and writing when addressing a superior or in a deferential register.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე ისაა, რომ...
3. **Transliteration:** simartle isaa, rom...
4. **English translation:** the truth is that..., the fact is that...
5. **Usage notes:** A cleft-like construction used in spoken and written Georgian to introduce a factual assertion, often one that corrects a misconception or provides a frank assessment. Isaa is the copula (is) with the demonstrative pronoun is (that/it). Extremely common in discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე რომ ვთქვათ
3. **Transliteration:** simartle rom vt̕q̇vat̕
4. **English translation:** to tell the truth, if we're being honest, frankly speaking
5. **Usage notes:** A parenthetical construction used in spoken and written Georgian to signal that the speaker is about to make a frank or candid statement. The subjunctive vt̕q̇vat̕ (let us say) gives the construction a deliberative flavor. Functionally equivalent to English "to be honest" or "frankly."
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლე ნახევარი სიმართლეა
3. **Transliteration:** simartle naxevari simartlea
4. **English translation:** a half-truth is (still) a truth / a half-truth is not the whole truth
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction used in moral and rhetorical discourse to comment on the deceptive potential of partial truths. The exact form varies; the underlying pattern (naxevari simartle) is well-established.
6. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Construction 10**

1. **Base Form:** სიმართლე (simartle)
2. **Example:** სიმართლეს ვერ გაექცევი
3. **Transliteration:** simartles ver gaekceviˈ
4. **English translation:** you cannot escape the truth, there is no escaping the truth
5. **Usage notes:** An idiomatic expression used in moral and everyday discourse to assert the inescapability of truth. The construction uses the verb gaekcevaˈ (to escape, to flee from) with simartle in the dative case, following the standard Georgian pattern for verbs of avoidance.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Construction 3**

2. **Example:** მართალი ხარ
3. **Transliteration:** martali xar

**Construction 4**

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** მართალი (martali)
2. **Example:** მართალი სიტყვა
3. **Transliteration:** martali sit̕q̇va
4. **English translation:** a true word, a truthful statement

6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

**Construction 7**

3. **Transliteration:** ɣmerti martalia

## 5. Base Form: სიყალბე (siq̇albe)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბე გამოაშკარავდა
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albe gamoaškaravda
4. **English translation:** the falseness was exposed, the fraud was revealed
5. **Usage notes:** Used in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts to describe the exposure of something that had been falsely presented. The verb gamoaškaraveba (to expose, to bring to light) is a standard collocate of siq̇albe in contexts of unmasking deception.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბეში დაიჭირეს
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albešiˈ daič̣ires
4. **English translation:** they were caught in a falsehood, they were caught out in fraud
5. **Usage notes:** Used in legal and everyday contexts to describe the act of catching someone in a deception or fraudulent act. The postposition -ši (in) with siq̇albe creates a locative construction meaning "in the act of falseness."
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** სიყალბე (siq̇albe)
2. **Example:** სიყალბის ნიშნები
3. **Transliteration:** siq̇albis nišnebi
4. **English translation:** signs of falseness, indicators of fraud
5. **Usage notes:** Used in legal, forensic, and administrative contexts to describe the observable markers that indicate a document, object, or statement is false or counterfeit. A standard phrase in document verification and fraud detection discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: siq̇albe participates in fewer idiomatic constructions than simartle, reflecting its more restricted register (primarily legal and formal). Its most productive constructions are genitive phrases (siq̇albis + N) and verb phrases with verbs of exposure and detection.*
