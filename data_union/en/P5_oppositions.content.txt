# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: English (en)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Each opposition below is extracted directly from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is posited unless it is explicitly supported by the data in those steps — through direct contrast statements, antonym citations, derivational negation pairs, or documented constructional alternations. Where the data supports asymmetry, asymmetry is preserved. Where the data does not support a clean opposition, this is noted.

Transliteration is not applicable for English (Latin script native); that column is marked N/A throughout.

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *false*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true / false
**Description of contrast:** The foundational binary opposition of the domain; *true* and *false* are the two classical truth values and the primary positive and negative poles of propositional truth-evaluation in English. The opposition is documented in P1 (items 1 and 10), P1b (*truth value*, *truth table*), P3 Groups 1 and 6, and P4a (§8, §9). P4a documents *it is true that* and *it is false that* as parallel assertive frames, and *prove true* / *prove false* as parallel inchoative constructions. P3 Group 1 treats them as the most general and syntactically flexible members of the statement-evaluation group.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *true*
**Term B:** *untrue*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** true / untrue
**Description of contrast:** *Untrue* is the morphological negation of *true* via the prefix *un-*, documented in P1b (1.4) and P2a (1.4). P2a documents *untrue* as a prefixally negated form of *true* functioning as a softer alternative to *false* in general speech. P3 Group 1 notes this register distinction explicitly: *untrue* is described as "a somewhat softer alternative to *false* or *wrong*." Unlike the lexical opposition *true* / *false*, this pair is formed within a single root family; *untrue* carries an additional sense of unfaithfulness or disloyalty not present in *false*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** *truth*
**Term B:** *untruth*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** truth / untruth
**Description of contrast:** *Untruth* is the morphological negation of *truth* via *un-*, documented in P1b (1.5), P2a (1.5), and P1b (Root 2, item 2.5). P2a notes that *untruth* functions as a euphemism for *lie* in formal and political contexts, a pragmatic asymmetry not present in the base form. The data notes that *untruth* carries a softer connotation than *lie* or *falsehood*, suggesting that the falsity may be partial or unintentional, which distinguishes it from the lexical opposition *truth* / *falsehood*.
**Contrast type:** morphological
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

**Term A:** *valid*
**Term B:** *invalid*
**Transliteration:** N/A

## Opposition 10

**Term A:** *validate*
**Term B:** *invalidate*
**Transliteration:** N/A

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** *valid*
**Term B:** *sound*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** valid / sound
**Description of contrast:** A constructional contrast documented in P1 (items 6 and 25) and P3 (Group 8). In formal logic, *valid* describes an argument whose conclusion follows necessarily from its premises regardless of whether the premises are true; *sound* describes an argument that is both valid and has true premises. *Sound* is therefore a stronger condition than *valid* within the logical register. P3 notes that both are used with looser meanings in general registers. P2b (§6.2) notes that the technical and everyday senses of *valid argument* are distinct.
**Contrast type:** constructional
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** *genuine*
**Term B:** *fake*
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English translation:** genuine / fake
**Description of contrast:** A lexical opposition documented in P1 (items 4 and 9) and P3 (Groups 2 and 3). *Genuine* asserts that something is what it purports to be; *fake* asserts that it is not. P1 establishes *fake* as contrasting with *genuine* (and *counterfeit* and *spurious*) in the authenticity domain. P2b documents *pass off as genuine* as the standard verbal construction for the act of presenting a fake as genuine, directly linking the two terms. The data notes that *fake* implies deliberate misrepresentation and carries additional connotations of deliberate deception absent from simple negation, while *genuine* is neutral with respect to the intentions of any party.
**Contrast type:** lexical
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
