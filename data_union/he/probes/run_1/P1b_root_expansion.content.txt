# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)**

**Native script:** א-מ-נ
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-n
**P1 items participating:** 4 — *ʾamin* (2), *ʾaminut* (8), *mehiman* (9), and the root family shared with *ʾemet* (1) and *ʾamiti* (7) via the closely related א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t) stem
**Core meaning contribution:** Reliability, trustworthiness, firmness of support. This root generates the primary vocabulary for evaluating whether a person, source, or claim can be trusted or depended upon. It underlies both the interpersonal dimension (is this person reliable?) and the epistemic dimension (is this source credible?).

---

**Root 2: א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t)**

**Native script:** א-מ-ת
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-t
**P1 items participating:** 3 — *ʾemet* (1), *ʾamiti* (7), and the derivational base for several fixed phrases noted in P1
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth, factual and moral correspondence to reality. This root generates the core vocabulary for asserting that something is true, real, or genuine — both in propositional contexts (a true statement) and in ontological/moral contexts (a genuine person, a real feeling). It is the most philosophically and morally weighted root in the domain.

---

**Root 3: צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q)**

**Native script:** צ-ד-ק
**Transliteration:** ts-d-q
**P1 items participating:** 2 — *tsedeq* (14), *tsaddiq* (15)
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, justice, conformity to a moral or legal standard. This root generates vocabulary for evaluating whether conduct, persons, or judgments are right in a normative sense — morally, legally, and socially. It is the primary root for the justice/righteousness dimension of truth-adjacent evaluation.

---

**Note on scope:** The roots א-מ-נ and א-מ-ת are historically and morphologically related (sharing the consonants א-מ and a common semantic field of firmness/reliability), and their derivatives overlap in usage. They are treated as distinct roots here because they generate distinct lexical families in attested Hebrew. The root י-ש-ר (y-sh-r), underlying *yashar* (16) and *yosher* (17), was considered for inclusion as a third root but yields fewer compounds than צ-ד-ק and is treated in peripheral notes where relevant.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

---

**1.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה
**Transliteration:** ʾemuna
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern *ʾeCuCa*
**Natural English translation:** faith, belief, faithfulness, trust
**Context of use:** One of the most central terms in Hebrew religious and moral discourse, referring to the quality of trusting in God, a person, or a principle, and to the state of being faithful. Used in theological contexts (*ʾemuna be-ʾelohim*, faith in God), in moral evaluation (*ʾish ʾemuna*, a man of faith/faithfulness), and in the fixed phrase *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* (truth and faithfulness). Also used in modern Hebrew for religious belief more broadly.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** אֱמוּן
**Transliteration:** ʾemun
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern *ʾeCuC*
**Natural English translation:** trust, confidence, reliance
**Context of use:** Used to describe the trust placed in a person, institution, or system. Common in political and social discourse (*ʾemun ha-tsibur*, public trust; *ʾemun ba-memshala*, trust in the government). Also appears in the phrase *ʾish ʾemun* (a trusted person, a confidant). Slightly more abstract and institutional than *ʾemuna*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Nif'al participle
**Natural English translation:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy, reliable
**Context of use:** Used to describe persons who are loyal and can be trusted — a faithful friend, a loyal employee, a reliable witness. Common in both everyday and formal Hebrew. Appears in fixed phrases such as *neʾeman le-* (faithful/loyal to) and *neʾeman la-ʾemet* (faithful to the truth). Also used as a title or form of address in some religious contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָנוּת
**Transliteration:** neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Nif'al nominal form + *-ut* suffix
**Natural English translation:** loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity, trustworthiness
**Context of use:** The abstract noun denoting the quality of being loyal or faithful. Used in moral, political, and legal discourse to evaluate whether a person or institution has maintained fidelity to a commitment, person, or principle. Common in discussions of political loyalty, marital fidelity, and professional integrity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** הֶאֱמִין
**Transliteration:** heʾemin
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il perfect 3ms
**Natural English translation:** believed, trusted (verb: to believe, to trust)
**Context of use:** The standard verb for "to believe" in Hebrew, used in both religious contexts (believing in God) and everyday epistemic contexts (believing a person, believing a claim). The infinitive *lehaʾamin* is extremely common. Takes the preposition *be-* (*lehaʾamin be-*, to believe in) or *le-* (*lehaʾamin le-*, to believe someone).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אֵמוּן
**Transliteration:** ʾemun
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern; note: this is a distinct lexical item from *ʾemun* (trust) above, though homophonous
**Natural English translation:** training, apprenticeship, upbringing
**Context of use:** Used to describe the process of training or raising someone — an apprentice, a ward, or a trainee. Appears in the phrase *ben ʾemun* (a ward, a foster child) and in contexts of professional training. This is a distinct semantic branch of the root from the trust/belief cluster.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** אוֹמֵן
**Transliteration:** ʾomen
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Qal active participle
**Natural English translation:** trainer, tutor, foster father, guardian
**Context of use:** Used in biblical and literary Hebrew to describe a person who raises or trains another — a guardian, tutor, or foster parent. Appears in the biblical narrative of Mordecai as the *ʾomen* of Esther. Less common in everyday modern Hebrew but retained in literary and religious registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
**Form:** אוֹמֶנֶת
**Transliteration:** ʾomenet
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Qal active participle, feminine form
**Natural English translation:** nurse, nanny, foster mother, guardian
**Context of use:** The feminine counterpart of *ʾomen*, used in biblical and literary Hebrew for a woman who raises or nurses a child. Appears in biblical narrative (e.g., the nurse of the infant Joash). Retained in literary and religious registers; less common in everyday modern Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**9.**
**Form:** מַאֲמִין
**Transliteration:** maʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il active participle
**Natural English translation:** believer, one who believes
**Context of use:** Used to describe a person who holds religious belief or who believes a particular claim. In religious discourse, *maʾamin* denotes a believer in God or in a religious doctrine. In everyday discourse, it can describe someone who believes a particular person or claim. The plural *maʾaminim* is common in religious contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** אֲמָנָה
**Transliteration:** ʾamana
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern *ʾaCaCa*
**Natural English translation:** agreement, covenant, charter, compact
**Context of use:** Used in legal and political discourse to describe a formal agreement or charter between parties. In modern Hebrew, *ʾamana* is used for international agreements, organizational charters, and formal compacts. The term implies a binding commitment grounded in mutual trust.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** אָמֵן
**Transliteration:** ʾamen
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, frozen liturgical form
**Natural English translation:** amen (affirmation of truth/reliability of a statement or prayer)
**Context of use:** Used in religious liturgy and prayer as an affirmation that what has been said is true or that the prayer is endorsed. Borrowed into many languages from Hebrew. In modern Hebrew, also used colloquially to express strong agreement or hope that something will come true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** בִּטְחוֹן
**Transliteration:** bitaḥon
**Morpheme breakdown:** root ב-ט-ח (b-ṭ-ḥ) + nominal pattern — **Note:** this item is from a different root and is included here only to note its frequent co-occurrence and near-synonymy with *ʾemun* in the trust cluster; it is not a derivative of א-מ-נ
**Natural English translation:** security, confidence, trust
**Context of use:** Noted here for exclusion from the א-מ-נ expansion; it belongs to a separate root family. Not listed as an expansion item.
**Epistemic status:** [excluded — different root]

---

**13.**
**Form:** מְהֵימָנוּת
**Transliteration:** mehimanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Pu'al or Hif'il nominal form + *-ut* suffix
**Natural English translation:** trustworthiness, credibility, reliability
**Context of use:** The abstract noun corresponding to *mehiman* (P1 item 9). Used in legal and formal discourse to describe the property of being credible or trustworthy, especially of witnesses and sources. Appears in legal judgments in the phrase *behinat ha-mehimanut* (examination of credibility).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-ʾemuna
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemet* (truth, root א-מ-ת) + *ve-* (and) + *ʾemuna* (faithfulness, root א-מ-נ)
**Natural English translation:** truth and faithfulness; in truth and in faith
**Context of use:** A fixed liturgical and literary phrase used in Jewish prayer (notably in the *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* blessing following the *Shema*) and in formal declarations to affirm that something is both factually true and sincerely meant. Also used in legal and contractual language to indicate good faith.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** לֹא נֶאֱמָן
**Transliteration:** lo neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *neʾeman* (faithful, trustworthy)
**Natural English translation:** untrustworthy, unreliable, disloyal
**Context of use:** Used in everyday and legal contexts to describe a person or source that cannot be trusted. In legal discourse, a witness described as *lo neʾeman* is one whose testimony is not to be relied upon. Also used in interpersonal contexts to describe disloyalty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** עֵד נֶאֱמָן
**Transliteration:** ʿed neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʿed* (witness) + *neʾeman* (faithful, trustworthy)
**Natural English translation:** reliable witness, faithful witness
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in legal and religious discourse to describe a witness whose testimony is considered credible and trustworthy. Appears in biblical texts and in modern legal Hebrew. Also used metaphorically to describe a reliable record or document.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָן לְ-
**Transliteration:** neʾeman le-
**Morpheme breakdown:** *neʾeman* (faithful) + *le-* (to, toward)
**Natural English translation:** faithful to, loyal to, true to
**Context of use:** A productive collocation used to describe fidelity to a person, principle, or commitment. Common in political discourse (*neʾeman la-miflag*, loyal to the party), moral discourse (*neʾeman la-ʾemet*, faithful to the truth), and professional contexts (*neʾeman la-ʾavodato*, faithful to his work).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** הַאֲמָנָה
**Transliteration:** haʾamana
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il verbal noun (infinitive construct pattern)
**Natural English translation:** accreditation, authorization, credentialing
**Context of use:** Used in diplomatic and institutional contexts to describe the formal accreditation of an ambassador or the authorization of a representative. *Kitvey haʾamana* (letters of accreditation) is the standard phrase for diplomatic credentials presented by an ambassador to a head of state.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** כִּתְבֵי הַאֲמָנָה
**Transliteration:** kitvey haʾamana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kitvey* (letters/documents of, construct plural of *ketav*) + *haʾamana* (accreditation)
**Natural English translation:** letters of credence, letters of accreditation
**Context of use:** The standard diplomatic term for the formal documents by which an ambassador is accredited to a foreign government. Used in official diplomatic and governmental contexts in modern Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** מוּסָד מְהֵימָן
**Transliteration:** musad mehiman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *musad* (institution) + *mehiman* (trustworthy, reliable)
**Natural English translation:** reliable institution, credible institution
**Context of use:** A collocation used in public and journalistic discourse to describe an institution — a news organization, a research body, a court — that is considered credible and trustworthy. Evaluates institutional rather than personal credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן
**Transliteration:** maqor mehiman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *maqor* (source) + *mehiman* (trustworthy, reliable)
**Natural English translation:** reliable source, credible source
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in journalistic, legal, and intelligence contexts to describe a source of information that is considered credible. Appears frequently in news reporting (*le-fi maqor mehiman*, "according to a reliable source").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנִים
**Transliteration:** ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern + masculine plural suffix
**Natural English translation:** faithfulness, loyalty (plural/intensive form); also: the faithful (as a group)
**Context of use:** Used in biblical and literary Hebrew, often in poetic or elevated registers, to describe the quality of faithfulness or the community of the faithful. Appears in Psalms and other biblical poetry. Less common in everyday modern Hebrew but retained in religious and literary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**23.**
**Form:** אֲמִינוּת מֵידָע
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut meyda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾaminut* (reliability) + *meyda* (information)
**Natural English translation:** information reliability, data reliability
**Context of use:** Used in technical, journalistic, and intelligence contexts to evaluate the reliability of a body of information or data. Common in discussions of media literacy, intelligence assessment, and scientific methodology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** בּוֹחֵן אֱמוּנִים
**Transliteration:** boḥen ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** *boḥen* (tester, examiner, Qal participle of ב-ח-נ) + *ʾemunim* (faithfulness, loyalties)
**Natural English translation:** tester of loyalties, examiner of faithfulness
**Context of use:** A literary and biblical phrase used to describe God or a situation that tests the faithfulness or loyalty of a person. Appears in biblical and liturgical poetry. Used metaphorically in modern Hebrew to describe a situation that reveals whether someone is truly loyal.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**25.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנַת שָׁוְא
**Transliteration:** ʾemunat shaʾv
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith, belief) + *shaʾv* (vain, empty, false)
**Natural English translation:** false belief, vain faith, misplaced trust
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and religious discourse to describe belief or trust that is misplaced or unfounded — faith in something that does not merit it. Contrasts with *ʾemuna ʾamitit* (genuine faith). Used in critiques of superstition or ideological credulity.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**26.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה עִוֶּרֶת
**Transliteration:** ʾemuna ʿiveret
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith, belief) + *ʿiveret* (blind, feminine adjective)
**Natural English translation:** blind faith
**Context of use:** Used in critical discourse — philosophical, journalistic, and everyday — to describe faith or trust that is held without examination or evidence. A common collocation in modern Hebrew discussions of religion, ideology, and authority. Carries a negative evaluative connotation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה דָּתִית
**Transliteration:** ʾemuna datit
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith) + *datit* (religious, adjective from *dat*, religion/law)
**Natural English translation:** religious faith
**Context of use:** The standard collocation for religious faith or belief in modern Hebrew discourse. Used in sociological, philosophical, and everyday contexts to describe the domain of religious belief as distinct from other forms of trust or confidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן
**Transliteration:** mashber ʾemun
**Morpheme breakdown:** *mashber* (crisis) + *ʾemun* (trust, confidence)
**Natural English translation:** crisis of trust, confidence crisis
**Context of use:** Used in political and social discourse to describe a situation in which public trust in an institution, leader, or system has collapsed or is severely damaged. Common in journalistic and political commentary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** הַצְהָרַת אֱמוּנִים
**Transliteration:** hatshaʾrat ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** *hatshaʾrat* (declaration of, construct form of *hatshaʾra*) + *ʾemunim* (loyalties, faithfulness)
**Natural English translation:** oath of allegiance, declaration of loyalty
**Context of use:** The standard term for an oath of allegiance or loyalty declaration, used in Israeli civic and military contexts. New citizens, soldiers, and public officials take a *hatshaʾrat ʾemunim* to the state. A formal institutional use of the root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר
**Transliteration:** ʾemun ha-tsibbur
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemun* (trust) + *ha-tsibbur* (the public)
**Natural English translation:** public trust, public confidence
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in political, legal, and journalistic discourse to describe the trust that the public places in institutions, officials, or systems. Erosion of *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* is a standard topic in Israeli public discourse about governance and media.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: The root א-מ-נ is highly productive. Additional attested forms include verbal conjugations of *lehaʾamin* across all binyanim, further nominal derivatives such as *ʾamanah* (in some dialects/registers), and compound phrases in legal and diplomatic Hebrew. The 30 items above represent the most common and lexically distinct expressions.*

### Root א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t)

---

**1.**
**Form:** אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + nominal pattern *ʾeCeC*
**Natural English translation:** truth
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 1. Included here as the base form for the expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** אֲמִתִּי
**Transliteration:** ʾamiti
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת + adjectival suffix *-i*
**Natural English translation:** true, real, genuine
**Context of use:** Already listed as P1 item 7. Included here as a core derivative.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** לֶאֱמֹת
**Transliteration:** leʾemot
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Qal infinitive construct
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to authenticate
**Context of use:** Used in formal and administrative contexts to mean verifying or confirming the truth or authenticity of something — a document, a claim, a signature. Common in legal and bureaucratic Hebrew. The Pi'el form *leʾammet* is more common in modern usage (see below).
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**4.**
**Form:** לְאַמֵּת
**Transliteration:** leʾammet
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el infinitive
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to authenticate, to confirm, to validate
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew verb for verifying or authenticating something — a document, a claim, an identity, a digital signature. Used in legal, administrative, technical, and everyday contexts. *Imet* (Pi'el perfect 3ms) and *meʾammet* (Pi'el participle) are common forms.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** אִמּוּת
**Transliteration:** immut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pi'el verbal noun
**Natural English translation:** verification, authentication, validation, confirmation
**Context of use:** The nominal form corresponding to *leʾammet*. Used in legal, administrative, and technical contexts to describe the process of verifying or authenticating something. Common in phrases such as *immut zehut* (identity verification), *immut mismaḥ* (document authentication), and *immut ḥatima* (signature verification).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אִמּוּת זֶהוּת
**Transliteration:** immut zehut
**Morpheme breakdown:** *immut* (verification) + *zehut* (identity)
**Natural English translation:** identity verification, identity authentication
**Context of use:** A standard technical and administrative phrase used in digital security, banking, and governmental contexts to describe the process of confirming that a person is who they claim to be. Extremely common in modern Israeli administrative and technological discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** מְאֻמָּת
**Transliteration:** meʾummat
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-ת, Pu'al passive participle
**Natural English translation:** verified, authenticated, confirmed
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective to describe something that has been officially verified or authenticated — a verified document, an authenticated copy, a confirmed identity. Common in legal, administrative, and digital contexts. *Eʾummat* (Pu'al perfect) also occurs.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** עֵד אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ʿed ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʿed* (witness) + *ʾemet* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** truthful witness, witness to the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and religious discourse to describe a witness who testifies truthfully. Appears in biblical and rabbinic texts and in modern legal Hebrew. Contrasts with *ʿed sheqer* (false witness).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** עֵד שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** ʿed sheqer
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʿed* (witness) + *sheqer* (falsehood, lie)
**Natural English translation:** false witness, perjurer
**Context of use:** The standard phrase for a false witness in biblical, rabbinic, and modern legal Hebrew. Appears in the biblical prohibition against bearing false witness (*lo taʿane be-reʿakha ʿed sheqer*). Used in modern legal discourse to describe perjury or false testimony.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** דּוֹבֵר אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** dover ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** *dover* (speaker, Qal participle of ד-ב-ר) + *ʾemet* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** truth-teller, one who speaks the truth
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse to describe a person who habitually tells the truth. Appears in biblical wisdom literature and in modern Hebrew moral evaluation. Contrasts with *dover sheqer* (one who speaks falsehood).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** דּוֹבֵר שֶׁקֶר
**Transliteration:** dover sheqer
**Morpheme breakdown:** *dover* (speaker) + *sheqer* (falsehood)
**Natural English translation:** liar, one who speaks falsehood
**Context of use:** Used in moral and religious discourse as the negative counterpart of *dover ʾemet*. Appears in biblical texts (e.g., Proverbs) and in modern Hebrew moral evaluation. A characterization of a person rather than a single act of lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** אֱמֶת לְאַמִּתָּהּ
**Transliteration:** ʾemet leʾammittah
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemet* (truth) + *le-* (to, according to) + *ʾammittah* (its truth, its true nature; suffixed form of *ʾemet*)
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter, the real truth, truth in its true sense
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in rabbinic and legal discourse to indicate that something is being examined or stated in its most precise and genuine sense — not approximately or superficially, but truly and exactly. Common in Talmudic argumentation and in modern legal reasoning.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** בֶּאֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** beʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** *be-* (in, with) + *ʾemet* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth, honestly
**Context of use:** An extremely common adverbial phrase used in everyday Hebrew to emphasize that something is genuinely the case or to express sincerity. Functions as an intensifier (*beʾemet, hu ḥoshev she-*, "he really thinks that") and as a marker of sincerity (*beʾemet, lo yadaʿti*, "honestly, I didn't know").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** לֶאֱמִתּוֹ שֶׁל דָּבָר
**Transliteration:** leʾamitto shel davar
**Morpheme breakdown:** *le-* (to, according to) + *ʾamitto* (its truth, suffixed form) + *shel* (of) + *davar* (thing, matter)
**Natural English translation:** in truth, in actual fact, to tell the truth about the matter
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in formal, legal, and literary Hebrew to introduce a statement that corrects a misapprehension or states the genuine facts of a matter. Common in legal judgments, journalistic analysis, and formal argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** חוֹתָם שֶׁל אֱמֶת
**Transliteration:** ḥotam shel ʾemet
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ḥotam* (seal, stamp) + *shel* (of) + *ʾemet* (truth)
**Natural English translation:** seal of truth, stamp of truth
**Context of use:** A literary and religious phrase used to describe something that bears the mark or quality of genuine truth. In rabbinic literature, *ḥotamo shel ha-qadosh baruḥ hu ʾemet* ("the seal of the Holy One is truth") is a famous dictum. Used metaphorically in modern Hebrew to describe something that has the unmistakable quality of being true.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following are expressions where the root's contribution appears to shift meaning or function compared to its core truth-domain use. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n):**

- *ʾomen* / *ʾomenet* (trainer, foster parent): the root appears in a context of raising or training, where the connection to trust or reliability is not the primary semantic content.
- *ʾemun* (training, apprenticeship): the root generates a term for a process of formation or upbringing, distinct from the trust/belief cluster.
- *ʾamen* (liturgical affirmation): the root appears as a frozen liturgical form functioning as a performative speech act rather than a descriptive or evaluative term.
- *ʾemuna ʿiveret* (blind faith): the root appears in a collocation that evaluates the quality of belief negatively, where the root's contribution is to the object of criticism rather than to a positive epistemic standard.
- *haʾamana* / *kitvey haʾamana* (accreditation, letters of credence): the root appears in a diplomatic/institutional context where the primary function is formal authorization rather than personal trustworthiness.
- *bitsḥon* (security, confidence): noted as a near-synonym from a different root that frequently co-occurs with א-מ-נ derivatives in the trust cluster.

---

**Root א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t):**
