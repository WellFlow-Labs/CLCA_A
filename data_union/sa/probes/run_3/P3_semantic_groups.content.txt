# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual association documented in P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b. Where a term appears in multiple distinct usage contexts that align with different groups, this is noted explicitly. The number of groups is not predetermined. Items that do not fit any observed grouping are listed at the end.

---

## Group 1: Terms used in evaluating speech acts as true or false

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate utterances as truthful or false

**Definition:** These terms appear characteristically in contexts where a specific speech act — a statement, testimony, vow, or report — is being assessed as corresponding or failing to correspond to the facts. They are applied to utterances, speakers, and the act of speaking itself. They appear in *Dharmaśāstra* witness evaluation, in ethical injunctions about speech, and in legal discussions of false testimony. The primary contrast operative in this group is between speech that accords with what is the case and speech that does not.

**Representative expressions:**

- सत्य / *satya* (#1) — applied to utterances and to persons characterized by truthful speech
- असत्य / *asatya* (#4) — the standard negation; applied to false statements and disqualified witnesses
- अनृत / *anṛta* (#3) — applied to false speech and fraudulent representation in legal and Vedic contexts
- सत्यवादिन् / *satyavādin* (P2a #2) — "one who speaks truth"; a characterization of a reliable witness or person
- असत्यभाषण / *asatya-bhāṣaṇa* (P2a #16) — the act of speaking falsehood; appears in lists of prohibited conduct
- अनृतभाषिन् / *anṛta-bhāṣin* (P2a #13) — "one who speaks falsehood"; used in witness disqualification
- अनृतवचन / *anṛta-vacana* (P2a #14) — a false utterance; appears in *Dharmaśāstra* lists of sins
- सत्यवाक्य / *satya-vākya* (P2a #6) — a true statement; used in discussions of testimony
- सत्यं वद / *satyaṃ vada* (P2b #1) — formulaic imperative: "Speak the truth"
- सत्यं ब्रूयात् / *satyaṃ brūyāt* (P2b #2) — normative optative: "One should speak the truth"
- अनृतं न वदेत् / *anṛtaṃ na vadet* (P2b #11) — prohibitive optative: "One should not speak falsehood"
- असत्यं वदति / *asatyaṃ vadati* (P2b #13) — "he speaks falsehood"; the conventional periphrastic expression for lying
- मिथ्या न वदेत् / *mithyā na vadet* (P2b #66) — "one should not speak falsely"; parallel prohibitive formula

**Notes:** ऋत / *ṛta* (#2) also appears in speech-evaluation contexts (particularly in Vedic literature) but its primary usage domain is broader (see Group 2). मिथ्या / *mithyā* (#26) appears here in its ethical-speech usage but has a distinct technical usage in Vedāntic ontology (see Group 5).

## Group 2: Terms associated with cosmic, moral, and ritual order

**Label:** Terms used in contexts of cosmic and moral ordering

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the concern is not primarily with individual speech acts but with a larger ordering — of the cosmos, of ritual, of social conduct, of the seasons — that is treated as normative and as underlying both natural regularity and moral rightness. They are characteristic of Vedic hymnic literature, of *Dharmaśāstra* discussions of duty, and of epic ethical discourse. The contrast operative here is between what fits within or upholds this order and what violates or disrupts it.

**Representative expressions:**

- ऋत / *ṛta* (#2) — the primary Vedic term for cosmic and ritual order; contrasted with *anṛta*
- अनृत / *anṛta* (#3) — disorder, falsehood as violation of order; the antonym of *ṛta*
- धर्म / *dharma* (#9) — right conduct, duty, moral law; what sustains proper order
- अधर्म / *adharma* (#10) — violation of duty, moral disorder; the antonym of *dharma*
- ऋतावन् / *ṛtāvan* (P2a #9) — "possessing *ṛta*"; epithet of deities and persons who uphold order
- ऋतधामन् / *ṛta-dhāman* (P2a #11) — "whose abode is *ṛta*"; Vedic epithet of Varuṇa
- ऋतु / *ṛtu* (P1b Root 3, #5) — the seasons as properly ordered divisions of time
- ऋतुकाल / *ṛtu-kāla* (P1b Root 3, #6) — the proper time; used in ritual and legal specification of correct timing
- ऋजु / *ṛju* (P1b Root 3, #7) — straight, honest; etymologically related to *ṛta* through the root *ṛ-*
- ऋजुता / *ṛjutā* (P1b Root 3, #8) — straightforwardness, honesty as a virtue
- ऋतं च सत्यं च / *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca* (P2b #7) — the formulaic Vedic pairing of cosmic order and truth
- ऋतस्य पन्थाः / *ṛtasya panthāḥ* (P2b #8) — "the path of *ṛta*"; conventional expression for right conduct
- धर्मं चर / *dharmaṃ cara* (P2b #24) — "walk in *dharma*"; formulaic imperative
- धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः / *dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ* (P2b #25) — proverbial: "*dharma* protects the one who protects it"
- धर्मे स्थितः / *dharme sthitaḥ* (P2b #26) — "established in *dharma*"; conventional expression of moral steadfastness
- धर्मात् न चलति / *dharmāt na calati* (P2b #27) — "does not deviate from *dharma*"
- यतो धर्मस्ततो जयः / *yato dharmas tato jayaḥ* (P2b #28) — proverbial: "where there is *dharma*, there is victory"
- सत्यं नास्ति परो धर्मः / *satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ* (P2b #3) — "there is no higher *dharma* than truth"
- सन्मार्ग / *san-mārga* (P1b Root 1, #26) — "the right path"; moral or spiritual correctness

**Notes:** The overlap between this group and Group 1 is real and attested: *anṛta* and *asatya* are used in overlapping contexts, and the Vedic pairing *ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca* treats the two as complementary. The distinction between the groups reflects a difference in the primary context of use — individual speech acts versus larger normative ordering — not a sharp semantic boundary. *Dharma* is included here rather than in Group 1 because its primary evaluative domain is conduct and duty rather than speech specifically, though it does appear in speech contexts.

## Group 3: Terms used in epistemological literature to classify cognitions and their sources

**Label:** Terms used to classify cognitions and their sources as valid or invalid

**Definition:** These terms appear characteristically in the technical epistemological literature (*Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*, Buddhist logic) where the central concern is whether a cognition or its source meets the criteria for yielding genuine knowledge. They are applied to cognitive events, to the means that produce them, and to the subjects who undergo them. The primary contrast is between what counts as a genuine instance of knowledge-yielding cognition and what merely resembles it or falls short.

**Representative expressions:**

- प्रमाण / *pramāṇa* (#5) — valid means of knowledge; the central organizing term of this group
- प्रमा / *pramā* (#6) — valid cognition; the result of a successful *pramāṇa*
- यथार्थ / *yathārtha* (#7) — accurate, as the object is; used to characterize veridical cognitions
- अयथार्थ / *ayathārtha* (#8) — inaccurate, not corresponding to the object
- प्रमाणता / *pramāṇatā* (P2a #19) — the property of being a valid means of knowledge
- प्रामाण्य / *prāmāṇya* (P2a #18) — validity; the abstract property that makes something a *pramāṇa*
- अप्रमाण / *apramāṇa* (P2a #20) — invalid means of knowledge; lacking epistemic warrant
- अप्रमा / *apramā* (P2a #27) — erroneous cognition; the general term for failed cognition
- प्रमातृ / *pramātṛ* (P2a #28) — the knowing subject; the epistemic agent
- प्रमेय / *prameya* (P2a #29) — the object of valid cognition; what is to be known
- पramāṇābhāsa / *pramāṇābhāsa* (P2a #22) — pseudo-valid means of knowledge; resembles but is not genuine
- प्रमाणसिद्ध / *pramāṇa-siddha* (P2a #23) — established by valid means; epistemically warranted
- स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥ-prāmāṇya* (P2a #25) — intrinsic validity (*Mīmāṃsā* position)
- परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥ-prāmāṇya* (P2a #26) — extrinsic validity (*Nyāya* position)
- अनुमान / *anumāna* (P1b Root 2, #14) — inference; a primary *pramāṇa*
- अनुमिति / *anumiti* (P1b Root 2, #15) — inferential cognition; the result of inference
- उपमान / *upamāna* (P1b Root 2, #16) — comparison; a *pramāṇa* recognized in *Nyāya*
- यथार्थता / *yathārthatā* (P2a #30) — accuracy; the property of corresponding to the object
- यथार्थज्ञान / *yathārtha-jñāna* (P2a #31) — accurate knowledge; cognition that corresponds to its object
- प्रमाणं किम्? / *pramāṇaṃ kim?* (P2b #15) — "What is the valid means of knowledge here?"; formulaic challenge in debate
- प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / *pramāṇena siddham* (P2b #16) — "established by valid means"; standard formula in argumentation
- न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति / *na tatra pramāṇam asti* (P2b #17) — "there is no valid means of knowledge for that"
- प्रमायाः करणं प्रमाणम् / *pramāyāḥ karaṇaṃ pramāṇam* (P2b #21) — definitional formula: "the instrument of valid cognition is the *pramāṇa*"
- बाधित / *bādhita* (P1b Root 2, #23) — contradicted, sublated; a cognition overridden by stronger evidence
- बाधा / *bādhā* (P1b Root 2, #24) — sublation; the process of a cognition being overridden

**Notes:** This group is the most internally differentiated of all groups identified, reflecting the extensive technical elaboration of the epistemological domain in Sanskrit philosophical literature. The *pramāṇa* framework generates its own internal vocabulary for the subject, means, object, result, and failure of cognition. Several terms from other groups (e.g., *vastu-tantra*, *sākṣāt*) also appear in epistemological contexts and are noted under those groups.

## Group 4: Terms used to characterize cognitive error and illusion

**Label:** Terms used to characterize cognitions that fail to represent their objects correctly

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where a cognition is being identified as erroneous, illusory, or inverted — where what is cognized does not correspond to what is actually there. They are used in epistemological literature to analyze the structure and varieties of cognitive failure, and in Vedāntic literature to discuss the nature of perceptual illusion. The canonical example associated with this group is the rope-snake illusion (*rajju-sarpa-bhrānti*).

**Representative expressions:**

- भ्रान्ति / *bhrānti* (#22) — cognitive error, illusion; the standard term for erroneous cognition
- विपर्यय / *viparyaya* (#23) — inverted or reversed cognition; one of the five *vṛttis* in *Sāṃkhya-Yoga*
- प्रतिभास / *pratibhāsa* (#21) — mere appearance; how something seems as opposed to what it is
- आभास / *ābhāsa* (#24) — semblance, pseudo-instance; something that resembles the genuine article without being it
- मिथ्या / *mithyā* (#26) — false, illusory; used in epistemological contexts for false cognition
- भ्रान्त / *bhrānta* (P2a #74) — erroneous, confused; the adjectival form
- भ्रम / *bhrama* (P2a #76) — error, confusion; near-synonym of *bhrānti*
- भ्रान्तिज्ञान / *bhrānti-jñāna* (P2a #75) — erroneous cognition
- विपर्यस्त / *viparyasta* (P2a #78) — inverted, reversed; the adjectival form of *viparyaya*
- हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa* (P2a #80) — fallacious reason; a pseudo-*hetu* in logical inference
- प्रतिभासिक / *pratibhāsika* (P2a #72) — pertaining to mere appearance; a level of reality in Advaita Vedānta
- मिथ्याज्ञान / *mithyā-jñāna* (P2a #88) — false knowledge, erroneous cognition
- मिथ्यादृष्टि / *mithyā-dṛṣṭi* (P2a #90) — false view, wrong view
- रज्जौ सर्पभ्रान्तिः / *rajjau sarpa-bhrāntiḥ* (P2b #57) — the canonical example: mistaking a rope for a snake
- भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते / *bhrāntiḥ nivartate* (P2b #58) — "the illusion ceases"; describes the removal of error
- बाधेन भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते / *bādhena bhrāntiḥ nivartate* (P2b #59) — "by sublation, the illusion ceases"
- विपर्ययो मिथ्याज्ञानम् / *viparyayo mithyājñānam* (P2b #60) — definitional formula from *Yogasūtra* 1.8
- प्रतिभासमात्रम् इदम् / *pratibhāsamātram idam* (P2b #55) — "this is mere appearance"
- हेत्वाभासो न हेतुः / *hetvābhāso na hetuḥ* (P2b #62) — "a fallacious reason is not a reason"
- आभासमात्रम् न वस्तु / *ābhāsamātram na vastu* (P2b #63) — "a mere semblance is not a real thing"
- मिथ्याज्ञानं निवर्तते सम्यग्ज्ञानेन / *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate samyag-jñānena* (P2b #68) — "false knowledge is removed by correct knowledge"

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 3 in that the terms for cognitive error are defined in contrast to the terms for valid cognition. *Bādhita* and *bādhā* (documented under Group 3) are also operative here as the mechanism by which error is removed. *Mithyā* appears in both this group and Group 5 (Vedāntic ontological usage), reflecting its dual usage.

## Group 5: Terms used in discussions of what is genuinely real versus merely apparent

**Label:** Terms used to distinguish genuine reality from appearance or convention

**Definition:** These terms appear in philosophical contexts — primarily Vedāntic, but also *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* and Buddhist — where the question is not whether a cognition is accurate but what ontological status the object itself has. The concern is with the difference between what genuinely exists or is real and what is merely apparent, conventional, or of indeterminate status. These terms are applied to objects, states of affairs, and levels of reality rather than primarily to cognitions or speech acts.

**Representative expressions:**

- सत् / *sat* (#11) — being, real, genuine; the ontological ground of the truth-domain
- असत् / *asat* (#12) — non-being, unreal; the ontological antonym of *sat*
- वास्तव / *vāstava* (#13) — real, pertaining to the actual object
- वस्तु / *vastu* (#14) — real thing, actual entity; what something genuinely is
- तत्त्व / *tattva* (#16) — true nature, real essence; what something fundamentally is
- मिथ्या / *mithyā* (#26) — in Advaita Vedānta: neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal
- सत्ता / *sattā* (P2a #42) — existence, being as a property; used in *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika* ontology
- सत्त्व / *sattva* (P2a #41) — being-ness; also the *Sāṃkhya guṇa* associated with clarity
- मिथ्यात्व / *mithyātva* (P2a #89) — the ontological status of being neither real nor unreal; central Advaita term
- वास्तविक / *vāstavika* (P2a #47) — real, actual, genuine; used in philosophical and general prose
- वस्तुतः / *vastutaḥ* (P2a #49) — "in reality, actually"; adverb asserting factual grounding
- वस्तुस्थिति / *vastu-sthiti* (P2a #50) — the actual state of affairs, the facts of the matter
- वस्तुतन्त्र / *vastu-tantra* (P2a #51) — object-dependent; governed by the real thing rather than by the subject
- वस्तुसत् / *vastu-sat* (P2a #52) — genuinely real as an actual entity; contrasted with *saṃvṛti-sat*
- सदसत् / *sad-asat* (P1b Root 1, #25) — being and non-being; the pair used in Vedāntic discussions of ultimate reality
- सच्चिदानन्द / *sac-cid-ānanda* (P2a #44) — being-consciousness-bliss; the triple nature of *Brahman*
- सत्कार्यवाद / *sat-kārya-vāda* (P1b Root 1, #30) — the doctrine that effects pre-exist in causes
- असत्कार्यवाद / *asat-kārya-vāda* (P2a #46) — the doctrine that effects are genuinely new
- तत्त्वतः / *tattvataḥ* (P2a #56) — "in truth, according to the real nature"; adverb
- तत्त्वज्ञान / *tattva-jñāna* (P2a #57) — knowledge of the real nature; metaphysical knowledge
- सदेव सोम्येदम् अग्र आसीत् / *sad eva somyedam agra āsīt* (P2b #31) — "in the beginning, only *sat* existed"; *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* formula
- असतो मा सद्गमय / *asato mā sad gamaya* (P2b #33/34) — "lead me from the unreal to the real"; *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* prayer
- असत् न विद्यते / *asat na vidyate* (P2b #35) — "the unreal does not exist"; *Bhagavad Gītā* formula
- मिथ्येति निश्चितम् / *mithyeti niścitam* (P2b #67) — "it is determined to be *mithyā*"
- प्रतिभासिक / *pratibhāsika* (P2a #72) — the level of mere appearance in Advaita ontology

**Notes:** This group overlaps with Group 4 in that the same terms (particularly *mithyā*, *pratibhāsa*, *asat*) appear in both cognitive-error contexts and ontological contexts. The distinction between the groups is one of primary context of use: Group 4 terms are applied to cognitions and their failures; Group 5 terms are applied to the ontological status of objects and states of affairs. *Sat* and *asat* are the most fundamental terms in this group and generate the largest derivational families in the entire P1 set.

## Group 6: Terms used in grammatical literature to evaluate linguistic forms

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate linguistic forms as correct or incorrect

**Definition:** These terms appear in the Pāṇinian grammatical tradition and related literature to characterize a linguistic form as either well-formed (conforming to grammatical rules) or ill-formed (violating them). The primary contrast is between *sādhu* (correct form) and *asādhu* (incorrect form). This usage is distinct from the ethical usage of the same terms, though the P1 documentation notes that both senses derive from the same evaluative structure.

**Representative expressions:**

- साधु / *sādhu* (#19) — a grammatically correct or well-formed expression
- असाधु / *asādhu* (#20) — a grammatically incorrect or ill-formed expression
- साधुता / *sādhutā* (P2a #68) — grammatical correctness; the property of being well-formed
- असाधुता / *asādhutā* (P2a #71) — grammatical incorrectness; the property of being ill-formed
- शुद्ध / *śuddha* (#25) — pure, correct, unadulterated; used in grammatical contexts for correct forms
- शुद्धि / *śuddhi* (P2a #83) — correction, purification; in grammar, the correction of an error
- अशुद्ध / *aśuddha* (P2a #85) — impure, incorrect; the antonym of *śuddha* in grammatical usage
- अशुद्धि / *aśuddhi* (P2a #86) — incorrectness, a grammatical error
- साधुः शब्दः / *sādhuḥ śabdaḥ* (P2b #53) — "a correct linguistic form"; the standard formula for asserting grammatical correctness
- असाधुः शब्दः / *asādhuḥ śabdaḥ* (P2b #54) — "an incorrect linguistic form"; the standard formula for marking ill-formedness

**Notes:** *Sādhu* and *asādhu* also appear in ethical contexts (characterizing persons and actions as good or bad), and *śuddha* appears in ritual and philosophical contexts. The grammatical usage is a distinct and well-defined domain of application, not a metaphorical extension. The overlap between grammatical correctness and moral goodness in the same terms is a feature of the Sanskrit evaluative vocabulary noted in P1.

## Group 7: Terms used to assert or evaluate fittingness and appropriateness

**Label:** Terms used to evaluate conduct, expression, or action as fitting or appropriate to context

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the evaluation is not primarily about truth or falsity, or about ontological status, but about whether something is appropriate, fitting, or suitable to its context, role, or circumstances. They are characteristic of Sanskrit aesthetic literature (*alaṃkāraśāstra*) and of ethical literature where conduct is evaluated against contextual norms rather than universal rules.

**Representative expressions:**

- औचित्य / *aucitya* (#17) — fittingness, propriety; the central term in Sanskrit aesthetic evaluation
- अनौचित्य / *anaucitya* (P2a #62) — impropriety, unfittingness; the antonym of *aucitya*
- उचित / *ucita* (P2a #61) — fitting, appropriate, suitable; the adjectival base of *aucitya*
- न्याय / *nyāya* (#18) — in its usage as "correct method" or "rule"; what is procedurally appropriate
- औचित्यं काव्यस्य जीवितम् / *aucityaṃ kāvyasya jīvitam* (P2b #47) — "*aucitya* is the life of poetry"; the central aesthetic formula
- औचित्यं रक्षितव्यम् / *aucityaṃ rakṣitavyam* (P2b #46) — "propriety is to be preserved"
- यथावत् / *yathāvat* (#30) — as it is, in the proper manner; used to characterize actions performed correctly
- सत्कार / *sat-kāra* (P1b Root 1, #2) — proper treatment, hospitality; doing what is fitting
- सत्कर्मन् / *sat-karman* (P1b Root 1, #3) — right action, genuinely meritorious deed

**Notes:** This group is less tightly unified than Groups 1–6. *Nyāya* in its sense of "correct method" or "maxim" overlaps with this group, but its primary technical usage is in the epistemological and logical domain (Group 3). *Yathāvat* appears both here (as "in the proper manner") and in Group 3 (as "accurately, as the object is"). The distinction between fittingness-evaluation and truth-evaluation is real in the usage data but the boundary is not always sharp.

## Group 8: Terms used to characterize what is direct or immediate versus mediated

**Label:** Terms used to characterize cognition or presence as direct and unmediated

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the claim being made is that something is known, seen, or present without the mediation of inference, report, or representation. They appear in legal contexts (direct eyewitness knowledge), in epistemological contexts (perception as the most direct *pramāṇa*), and in Vedāntic and devotional contexts (direct vision of the deity or of *Brahman*).

**Representative expressions:**

- साक्षात् / *sākṣāt* (#29) — directly, face to face, with one's own eyes
- साक्षात् पश्यति / *sākṣāt paśyati* (P2b #70) — "sees directly"; direct unmediated perception
- साक्षात् अनुभवः / *sākṣāt anubhavaḥ* (P2b #71) — "direct experience"; immediate apprehension
- साक्षात्कारः / *sākṣāt-kāraḥ* (P2b #72) — direct realization; immediate apprehension of a truth or deity
- तत्त्वदर्शिन् / *tattva-darśin* (P2a #58) — "one who sees the truth"; one who has direct insight
- तत्त्वदर्शनेन मोक्षः / *tattva-darśanena mokṣaḥ* (P2b #45) — "liberation through the vision of reality"
- सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः / *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ* (P2b #5) — "established in truth"; grounded in direct contact with truth
- ऋतंभरा / *ṛtaṃbharā* (P2a #10) — "truth-bearing"; the highest wisdom that directly bears truth (*Yogasūtra* 1.48)

**Notes:** This group is smaller and less internally differentiated than Groups 1–5. The terms here share a usage pattern of asserting immediacy and unmediated contact, but they do so across different domains (legal, epistemological, soteriological). *Sākṣāt* is the clearest member of this group; the others participate in it through their association with direct insight or vision rather than inferential or testimonial knowledge.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items appear in multiple groups and cannot be assigned to a single group without distortion:

**मिथ्या / *mithyā* (#26):** Appears in Group 4 (cognitive error: false cognition, wrong view) and Group 5 (Vedāntic ontology: neither real nor unreal). The two usages are distinct in context and in the philosophical tradition, but the same form is used for both.

**साधु / *sādhu* (#19) and असाधु / *asādhu* (#20):** Appear in Group 6 (grammatical correctness) and also in ethical contexts that align with Group 2 (moral ordering) and Group 7 (fittingness). The grammatical and ethical senses are documented as distinct in P1 but share the same evaluative structure.

**शुद्ध / *śuddha* (#25):** Appears in Group 6 (grammatical correctness), in ritual purity contexts (which do not map cleanly onto any group above), and in philosophical contexts (pure consciousness, *śuddha-caitanya*) that align with Group 5.

**यथावत् / *yathāvat* (#30):** Appears in Group 3 (epistemological accuracy: *yathāvat jñānam*) and Group 7 (proper manner of action: performing something as it should be done). The two usages are contextually distinct.

**न्याय / *nyāya* (#18):** Appears in Group 3 (as the name of the epistemological school and as "correct logical procedure") and in Group 7 (as "correct method" or "maxim" in legal and procedural contexts). The term also appears in legal contexts as "justice" or "right adjudication," which does not map cleanly onto either group.

**सत्य / *satya* (#1):** Appears primarily in Group 1 (speech-act evaluation) but also in Group 2 (as the highest *dharma*: *satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ*) and in Group 5 (as the ontological ground of reality, related to *sat*). The term's usage spans all three domains.

**प्रतिभास / *pratibhāsa* (#21):** Appears in Group 4 (cognitive error: mere appearance as opposed to reality) and Group 5 (ontological status: the level of *pratibhāsika* reality in Advaita Vedānta).

**स्वरूप / *svarūpa* (#15):** Appears in Group 5 (what something genuinely is, its intrinsic nature) and in Group 3 (in the *Yogasūtra* formula *svarūpe 'vasthānam*, the seer abiding in its own nature). It also appears in *Dharmaśāstra* contexts of definitional inquiry (*svarūpaṃ kim?*) that align with Group 7.

## Items That Do Not Fit Cleanly into Any Group

**कृत्रिम / *kṛtrima* (#27) and अकृत्रिम / *akṛtrima* (#28):** These terms are used to distinguish artificial or manufactured things from natural or genuine ones. This usage pattern — evaluating objects as produced versus natural — does not align closely with any of the groups identified above. The terms appear in *Āyurvedic*, legal, and aesthetic contexts but do not form a cluster with other P1 items. They are noted as isolated in the P1 documentation (both marked [MARGINAL]) and remain ungrouped here.

**सत्संग / *sat-saṅga* (P1b Root 1, #4):** Used in devotional and religious contexts to mean "company of the spiritually genuine." The *sat-* component marks spiritual authenticity, but the primary usage context (devotional practice, religious community) does not align with any of the groups identified above.

**सत्यनारायण / *satya-nārāyaṇa* (P1b Root 1, #15) and सत्यलोक / *satya-loka* (P1b Root 1, #16):** These are devotional and cosmological compounds where *satya* functions as a theological or cosmological qualifier rather than as a truth-predicate. They do not fit cleanly into Group 1 (speech evaluation), Group 2 (moral ordering), or Group 5 (ontological reality), though they have affinities with all three.

**सत्याग्रह / *satyāgraha* (P1b Root 1, #14):** A modern coinage with a specific political usage (Gandhian nonviolent resistance) that does not correspond to any usage pattern in the classical literature documented in P1 and P2. It participates in the *satya* family morphologically but its usage context is sui generis within this dataset.

**सद्भाव / *sad-bhāva* (P2a #43):** Used to mean both "genuine feeling / good faith / sincerity" (ethical) and "real existence" (ontological). The ethical sense (sincerity, good faith) does not map cleanly onto any of the groups above; it is adjacent to Group 7 (fittingness) and Group 1 (truthful speech) but is not a close fit with either.

**प्रमाणपत्र / *pramāṇa-patra* (P1b Root 2, #20) and प्रमाणीकरण / *pramāṇīkaraṇa* (P2a #24):** These terms appear in administrative and legal contexts where *pramāṇa* means "official document" or "certificate" rather than "valid means of knowledge." This usage is noted in P1b as a peripheral extension and does not fit within Group 3 (epistemological classification) as defined by the technical philosophical literature.
