## Short narrative

A few derivational and morphological patterns recur across clearly unrelated language families.

First, one of the strongest recurring patterns is **abstract quality formation**: languages repeatedly derive nouns meaning ‘truth’, ‘correctness’, ‘accuracy’, ‘authenticity’, ‘validity’, or ‘reliability’ from adjectival or lexical bases. This is very clear in Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean, and functionally present in Yoruba too. The morphology differs, but the strategy itself is **widely attested**.

Second, many languages build **adverbs** from truth/reality bases: ‘truly’, ‘really’, ‘actually’, ‘accurately’, ‘genuinely’. This is also **widely attested**, though some forms are fully productive while others are more lexicalized or discourse-marking.

Third, many languages rely heavily on **compounding** or tightly bound multi-part lexical formations. This is especially visible in:
- legal/institutional vocabulary,
- technical truth vocabulary,
- authenticity vocabulary,
- and essence/underlying-nature vocabulary.

Examples include Finnish **totuusarvo**, Georgian **მართლმსაჯულება**, Basque **egiaztatagiri**, Tamil **மெய்ப்பொருள்**, Turkish **hakikat sonrası**, Korean **허위 사실**, and Indonesian fixed compound-like expressions such as **tepat sasaran**.

A particularly strong cross-language pattern is not purely derivational but still morphological-structural: **truth as object of a light verb**. Many languages prefer constructions like “tell the truth,” “hide the truth,” “reveal the truth,” “seek the truth,” “confirm the facts,” rather than a single simple verb meaning “truth-tell” or “make true.” This is **widely attested** across Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Indonesian, Turkish, and Korean.

Another recurring pattern is **revelation-based dynamics**. Across several unrelated languages, truth is often encoded as something that:
- comes out,
- is revealed,
- is brought to light,
- is confirmed,
- is checked,
rather than something that simply “becomes true.” This is especially strong in Georgian, Basque, Tamil, Turkish, Korean, and also visible in Finnish and Indonesian. This pattern is **widely attested**.

## Striking asymmetries

One striking asymmetry is between **negative and positive person-denoting forms**. Several languages have a clear lexicalized noun for “liar” or a related negative role, but no equally central positive noun for “truth-teller.” This is visible in Basque (**gezurti**), Korean (**거짓말쟁이**), and in the broader notes for Georgian and Finnish, where positive counterparts are often descriptive phrases instead.

Another asymmetry is that many languages have strong morphology for:
- **verification**,
- **correction**,
- **invalidation**,
- **falsification**,
but not for a general causative meaning **“make true.”**
This is especially explicit in Basque, Tamil, Korean, Georgian, and Yoruba. Indonesian and Turkish also show strong lexical causatives, but these are root-specific: “validate,” “correct,” “falsify,” “confirm,” not a single general “truth-causative.”

A further asymmetry is that **negative derivation** is unevenly distributed. Finnish and Turkish show especially clear morphological opposites:
- Finnish **tosi / epätosi**, **aito / epäaito**
- Turkish **geçerli / geçersiz**, **haklı / haksız**, **meşru / gayrimeşru**, **asıl / asılsız**
But other languages rely more on analytic negation or separate lexical items.

## One-way or blocked pathways

Some derivational pathways appear **one-way** or at least much stronger in one direction than the other.

- In Indonesian, there is strong derivation around **validity** and **falsification** (*validasi, mengesahkan, memalsukan*), but no equally well-supported common verb meaning “make accurate” from **akurat**, and no common ordinary “become genuine” from **asli**.
- In Korean, there are strong patterns for **revealing truth** and **invalidating status** (*드러나다, 밝히다, 무효가 되다, 무효로 하다*), but no clearly supported common direct “become true” or “make true” pattern from the core truth lexemes.
- In Basque, **egiaztatu** and **zuzendu** are strong lexical verbs, but a general inchoative “become true/false” is explicitly weak or unsupported.
- In Tamil, **சரி ஆகு** is strong, but a parallel common dynamic pattern with **தவறு** is not clearly identified.
- In Yoruba, the data strongly supports predication, evidence, and truth-object constructions, but not a dedicated general change-of-state morphology for truth/falsity/authenticity.

## Productive vs restricted vs lexicalized

### Productive
Patterns that look broadly productive in the supplied data include:
- abstract noun formation from evaluative bases,
- adverb formation,
- analytic negation,
- noun + light-verb constructions,
- compounding with truth/fact/right/validity elements,
- and in some languages, general “become” constructions.

This is especially clear in Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, Georgian, and Korean.

### Restricted
Restricted patterns include:
- special inchoatives or causatives tied to only a few roots,
- emphatic extensions like Korean **-배기**,
- Turkish **-leş- / -leştir-** truth-adjacent forms such as **sahteleşmek, özgünleşmek, dürüstleşmek**,
- Basque and Korean lexical revelation verbs that are central but not generalizable to every truth adjective,
- Tamil dynamic patterns that are strong for **சரி** but not equally for all neighboring roots.

### Lexicalized / fossilized
Several forms are clearly lexicalized or fossilized:
- Yoruba **kebetulan** has drifted away from the truth/correctness domain.
- Turkish technical compounds like **doğru akım**, **gerçek sayı** are synchronically outside ordinary truth evaluation.
- Tamil **மெய்** derivatives in grammar and technical vocabulary are part of the root family but semantically peripheral.
- Georgian **მართ-** governance forms are morphologically related but semantically shifted.

### Unclear or weakly attested
Weak or unclear areas include:
- a general “make true” derivation in several languages,
- a common positive agent noun parallel to “liar,”
- some low-frequency or marked derivatives such as Korean **진실로**, Basque **egiazki**, Turkish **hakikatli**, Finnish **totuudellinen**, and Indonesian **mengakuratkan**.

## Overall

The strongest recurring strategies across unrelated languages are:
1. **abstract quality derivation**,
2. **adverb derivation**,
3. **compound formation**,
4. **light-verb constructions with truth nouns**,
5. **analytic negation**,
6. and **revelation/verification-based dynamic encoding**.

What is less universal is a neat derivational pathway for “become true” or “make true.” In the supplied data, many languages instead prefer to say that truth is **revealed, checked, confirmed, established, accepted, or recognized**.
