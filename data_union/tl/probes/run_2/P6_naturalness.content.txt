# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

---

## Preliminary Note

This step examines how Tagalog encodes dynamic truth-related meanings — becoming, causing, and stability — using only vocabulary attested in P1–P5. The analysis proceeds term by term where behavior differs. Evidence is drawn from attested forms and constructions; gaps are stated explicitly rather than filled by inference.

The primary morphological and constructional resources relevant here are:

- The particle ***na*** (change of state / current relevance), documented in P4a as the inchoative construction
- The ***i-*** causative prefix, documented in P4a as productive for correctness adjectives
- The ***pa-…-an*** causative pattern, documented in P4a as the verification/proof causative
- The ***maging*** ("to become/be") periphrastic construction, documented in P2a for *tapat*
- Aspect morphology on verbal stems (*nagkamali*, *napatunayan*, *pinatunayan*), documented in P2a and P1b

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1a. *totoo* — becoming true / becoming real

**Attested construction:** ***[predicate] + na***

The particle *na* attached after *totoo* signals change of state, as documented in P4a (Construction 4) and P2b (Construction 2).

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Totoo na.* | Totoo na | "It's become real. / It's actually happening now." | Attested in P2b (Construction 2) and P4a. The inchoative reading arises from *na* with a stative predicate. |
| *Totoo na pala.* | Totoo na pala | "So it's actually true now. / It turns out it's real." | The particle *pala* (realization/discovery) combines with *totoo na* to mark that the speaker has just discovered the truth of something. Attested pattern from *na* + *pala* combination; *pala* is a function word. |

**No dedicated inchoative verb** from the root *totoo* is attested. P4a explicitly states: "No dedicated inchoative prefix for this semantic field is attested."

---

### 1b. *sigurado* — becoming certain

**Attested construction:** ***sigurado + na***

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Sigurado na ako.* | Sigurado na ako | "I'm certain now. / I've made sure of it." | Attested in P2b (Construction 77) and P4a. The *na* marks a shift from uncertainty to certainty, or the completion of verification. |

---

### 1c. *tama* — becoming correct / reaching sufficiency

**Attested construction:** ***tama + na***

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Tama na.* | Tama na | "That's enough. / Stop." | Attested in P2b (Construction 31) and P5 (Opposition 15). P4a and P3 both note this construction is pragmatically specialized: its meaning (sufficiency/cessation) is not fully predictable from *tama* ("correct") + *na*. It does not straightforwardly encode "becoming correct." |

**Note:** The *tama + na* construction encodes reaching a threshold of sufficiency rather than a change into a state of correctness. No attested construction encodes "X becomes correct" with *tama* as the predicate in a straightforward inchoative sense.

---

### 1d. *mali* — becoming wrong / making an error

**Attested verbal form:** ***nagkamali***

The completed-aspect form *nagkamali* ("made a mistake / erred") is attested in P2a (Derived Form 6.5) and P1b.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Nagkamali ako.* | Nagkamali ako | "I made a mistake. / I erred." | Attested in P2a and P1b. The *nag-* perfective marks the completed act of falling into error. This is the closest attested form to "became wrong" for the *mali* root. |

---

### 1e. *sinungaling* — becoming a liar

**Attested periphrastic construction:** ***maging sinungaling***

P2a documents *maging tapat* as the standard verbal construction for the *tapat* root (Derived Form 8.3), noting that *maging* is "the standard verbal construction for expressing the state or act of being" the root quality. The same *maging* construction is available for *sinungaling* by the same pattern.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Huwag kang maging sinungaling.* | Huwag kang maging sinungaling | "Don't be / become a liar." | Attested in P4a (Construction 7, second example). *Maging* here encodes both the state of being and the process of becoming. |

---

### 1f. *tapat* — becoming honest / faithful

**Attested periphrastic construction:** ***maging tapat***

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Maging tapat ka sa akin.* | Maging tapat ka sa akin | "Be honest with me." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 8.3) and P2b (Construction 47). P2a notes this is "the primary verbal expression of the root's meaning." |

---

### 1g. *tunay*, *huwad*, *peke*, *wasto*, *tumpak*, *akma*, *angkop*, *lehitimo*, *kredible*, *mapagkakatiwalaan*

**No attested inchoative or becoming-construction** for these terms is documented in P1–P5. The *na* particle is theoretically combinable with any stative predicate in Tagalog, but no specific attested examples with these roots in an inchoative function are reported in the context data. This gap is preserved.

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH / OPPOSED STATE

### 2a. *hindi totoo* — becoming untrue / being revealed as false

No attested construction for "X becomes untrue" is documented in the context data. The negation *hindi totoo* is attested as a static predication (P2b, Construction 4; P4a, Construction 2) but not in a dynamic becoming-construction.

---

### 2b. *kawalan ng tiwala* — loss of trust

**Attested nominal construction:** ***kawalan ng tiwala***

P2a (Derived Form 9.4) and P2b (Construction 54) document *kawalan ng tiwala* as the nominalized form for "distrust / lack of trust / **loss** of confidence." The word *kawalan* (from *wala*, "absence/lack") encodes the transition from presence to absence of trust.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *kawalan ng tiwala* | kawalan ng tiwala | "loss of trust, distrust, erosion of confidence" | Attested in P2a and P2b. The nominal *kawalan* encodes the dynamic of losing or lacking trust rather than a static state. |

---

### 2c. *pagkakamali* — falling into error

**Attested nominal and verbal forms**

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Nagkamali siya.* | Nagkamali siya | "He/she made a mistake. / He/she fell into error." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 6.5). The *nag-* perfective encodes the completed transition into a state of error. |
| *Aminin ang pagkakamali.* | Aminin ang pagkakamali | "Admit the mistake." | Attested in P2b (Construction 40). *Pagkakamali* nominalizes the event of having erred. |

---

### 2d. *pagsisinungaling* — the act of lying (transition into deceptive speech)

**Attested nominal:** ***pagsisinungaling***

P1 and P2a document *pagsisinungaling* as the verbal noun for "the act of lying." It encodes the transition into deceptive speech as an event.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *pagsisinungaling* | pagsisinungaling | "the act of lying, telling a lie" | Attested in P1 and P2a. The *pag-* verbal noun nominalizes the act as an event, implying a transition from non-lying to lying. |

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 3a. *itama* / *iwasto* — causing correctness (correcting)

**Attested causative construction:** ***i-* + correctness adjective**

P4a (Construction 5) documents this as the productive causative for correctness adjectives.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Itama ang pagkakamali.* | Itama ang pagkakamali | "Correct the mistake." | Attested in P2a, P2b (Construction 41), and P4a. *Itama* = *i-* (causative) + *tama*: "cause [it] to be correct." |
| *Iwasto ang mali.* | Iwasto ang mali | "Set the wrong right. / Correct the error." | Attested in P2a, P2b (Construction 41), and P4a. *Iwasto* = *i-* (causative) + *wasto*: "cause [it] to be proper/correct." |

**Constraint documented in P4a:** The *i-* causative is **not attested** for truth-predicate adjectives *totoo* and *tunay*. P4a states this explicitly.

---

### 3b. *iakma* / *iaangkop* — causing fit / causing appropriateness

**Attested causative constructions**

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Iakma ang paglalarawan.* | Iakma ang paglalarawan | "Make the description fit / accurate." | *Iakma* attested in P2a (Derived Form 17.1). *i-* + *akma*: "cause [it] to fit/correspond." |
| *Iaangkop sa konteksto.* | Iaangkop sa konteksto | "Adapt it to the context. / Make it appropriate to the context." | *Iaangkop* attested in P2a (Derived Form 18.1). *i-* + *angkop*: "cause [it] to be appropriate." |

---

### 3c. *patunayan* — causing something to be proven / verified

**Attested causative construction:** ***pa-* + *tunay* + *-an***

P4a (Construction 5, note) and P2a (Derived Form 3.2) document this as the causative for the verification/proof domain, explicitly distinguished from the *i-* causative.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Patunayan mo.* | Patunayan mo | "Prove it. / Demonstrate it." | Attested in P2a and P2b (Construction 5 in P1b root expansion). *pa-* (causative) + *tunay* + *-an*: "cause [it] to be verified/proven." |
| *Hindi mapatunayan.* | Hindi mapatunayan | "It cannot be proven. / It is unprovable." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 3.4) and P5 (Opposition 13). *ma-* (potential) + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* under negation. |
| *Napatunayan na.* | Napatunayan na | "It has already been proven." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 3.5). *na-* (perfective) + *pa-* + *tunay* + *-an* + particle *na*: completed verification. |
| *Pinatunayan niya.* | Pinatunayan niya | "He/she proved it." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 3.6). Completed-aspect actor-focus form. |

---

### 3d. *siguraduhin* — causing certainty / ensuring

**Attested causative construction:** ***siguradu-* + *-hin***

P2a (Derived Form 16.2) documents *siguraduhin* as the object-focus verb "to make sure of something; to ensure."

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Siguraduhin mo.* | Siguraduhin mo | "Make sure of it. / Ensure it." | Attested in P2a. *siguradu-* + *-hin* (object-focus): "cause [it] to be certain/sure." |

---

### 3e. *pagpapatunay* — the act of causing verification

**Attested action nominal**

P2a (Derived Form 3.3) and P1 document *pagpapatunay* as the verbal noun for the act of verifying or authenticating.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *pagpapatunay ng dokumento* | pagpapatunay ng dokumento | "document authentication / the act of verifying a document" | Attested in P2a and P2b (Construction 27). Nominalizes the causative act of verification. |
| *pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan* | pagpapatunay ng pagkakakilanlan | "identity verification" | Attested in P2a and P2b (Construction 26). |

---

### 3f. *pagtatago ng katotohanan* — causing truth to be hidden

**Attested nominal construction**

P2b (Construction 14) and P1b document *pagtatago ng katotohanan* as "the act of withholding or suppressing true information."

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *pagtatago ng katotohanan* | pagtatago ng katotohanan | "concealment of the truth / hiding the truth" | Attested in P2b. *pagtatago* (verbal noun of *magtago*, "to hide") + *ng katotohanan*: causing truth to be absent from discourse. This is a causative-of-absence construction rather than a causative-of-presence. |

---

### 3g. Causing trust: *tiwalahin* / *magtiwala*

**Attested constructions**

P2a (Derived Form 9.3) documents *tiwalahin* and P2b (Construction 56) documents *magtiwala*.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Magtiwala ka sa kanya.* | Magtiwala ka sa kanya | "Trust him/her. / Place your trust in him/her." | Attested in P2b (Construction 56 pattern). *mag-* (actor-focus) + *tiwala*: the actor initiates the state of trust. |
| *Tiwalahin mo siya.* | Tiwalahin mo siya | "Trust him/her." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 9.3). *tiwala* + *-hin* (object-focus): the trusted person is foregrounded. |

**Note:** These constructions encode the initiation of a trust relationship rather than causing an external state to be true or verified. They are causative in the sense that the subject brings about a relational state.

---

### 3h. No attested causative for *totoo*, *tunay*, *huwad*, *peke*, *lehitimo*, *kredible*

P4a explicitly states that the *i-* causative is **not attested** for *totoo* and *tunay*. No causative construction for *huwad*, *peke*, *lehitimo*, or *kredible* is documented anywhere in P1–P5. This gap is preserved.

## 4. STABILITY EXPRESSIONS

### 4a. Permanence of truth

**Attested construction:** ***Ang katotohanan ay mananatiling katotohanan.***

P2b (Construction 17) documents this as a fixed rhetorical formula asserting the permanence of truth.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Ang katotohanan ay mananatiling katotohanan.* | Ang katotohanan ay mananatiling katotohanan | "The truth will remain the truth. / Truth will always be truth." | Attested in P2b (Construction 17), marked [MARGINAL]. Described as "a fixed rhetorical formula used to assert the permanence or inviolability of truth." The verb *mananatili* ("will remain") encodes stability over time. |

---

### 4b. Established proof: *napatunayan*

The completed-aspect form *napatunayan* ("has been proven") encodes a state that has been achieved and now holds.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Napatunayan na.* | Napatunayan na | "It has already been proven. / It stands proven." | Attested in P2a (Derived Form 3.5). The combination of perfective aspect and the particle *na* encodes a stable achieved state. |

---

### 4c. Persistent trust: *mapagkakatiwalaan*

P1 and P2a document *mapagkakatiwalaan* as a stable dispositional adjective ("trustworthy, reliable, dependable") rather than a dynamic expression. It encodes a stable property of a person or source rather than a change or event.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Mapagkakatiwalaan siya.* | Mapagkakatiwalaan siya | "He/she is trustworthy / reliable." | Attested in P1 and P2a. The *ma-* prefix encodes a stable potential or dispositional quality. |

---

### 4d. Persistent error: *malimit magkamali*

P1b documents *malimit magkamali* ("prone to error / frequently mistaken") as a construction encoding habitual or persistent error.

| Example | Transliteration | Translation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Malimit siyang magkamali.* | Malimit siyang magkamali | "He/she is frequently wrong / prone to error." | Attested in P1b. *malimit* (often/frequently) + *magkamali* (to make a mistake): encodes error as a stable dispositional tendency rather than a single event. |

## 5. SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

The following patterns are directly supported by the data above:

1. **The particle *na*** is the primary mechanism for encoding change of state in this semantic field, attested with *totoo*, *sigurado*, and *tama*. It is not a dedicated inchoative morpheme but generates inchoative readings with stative predicates. No dedicated inchoative prefix for truth-related vocabulary is attested.

2. **The *i-* causative** is productive for correctness adjectives (*tama* → *itama*; *wasto* → *iwasto*; *akma* → *iakma*; *angkop* → *iaangkop*) and is explicitly documented as **not attested** for truth-predicate adjectives (*totoo*, *tunay*).

3. **The *pa-…-an* causative** is the attested causative for the verification/proof domain (*tunay* → *patunayan*), forming a distinct morphological pattern from the *i-* causative. Its aspect-marked forms (*napatunayan*, *pinatunayan*, *mapatunayan*) encode achieved, actor-initiated, and potential proof respectively.

4. **The *maging* periphrastic construction** encodes becoming a state for *tapat* and *sinungaling*, where no morphologically derived inchoative verb is available.

5. **Action nominals with *pag-*** (*pagpapatunay*, *pagsisinungaling*, *pagkakamali*, *pagtatago ng katotohanan*) encode truth-related events as nominalized processes, implying a transition into or out of a state.

6. **The nominal *kawalan ng tiwala*** encodes the dynamic of losing trust through the *kawalan* ("absence/loss") nominal, representing the only clearly lexicalized nominal for a transition away from a truth-related state in the data.

7. **Stability** is encoded periphrastically (*mananatiling katotohanan*) or through dispositional adjectives (*mapagkakatiwalaan*) and habitual constructions (*malimit magkamali*) rather than through dedicated stability morphology.

8. **Asymmetry is preserved:** No causative, inchoative, or stability construction is attested for *huwad*, *peke*, *lehitimo*, *kredible*, *tumpak*, or *orihinal* in the context data. These gaps are reported as gaps.
