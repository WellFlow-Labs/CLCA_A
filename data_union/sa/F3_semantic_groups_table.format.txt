| Cognition-evaluating terms in epistemological argument | These terms are used in Sanskrit philosophical literature — primarily Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Buddhist epistemology — to assess whether a given cognitive episode accurately apprehends the object it is directed at. They appear in technical discussions of the structure of knowledge, the causes of error, and the distinction between reliable and unreliable cognitive episodes. They are regularly contrasted with one another as positive and negative poles of the same evaluative dimension. | प्रमा / *pramā*; अप्रमा / *apramā*; यथार्थज्ञान / *yathārthajñāna*; भ्रम / *bhrama*; विपर्यय / *viparyaya*; भ्रमज्ञान / *bhramajñāna*; मिथ्याज्ञान / *mithyājñāna*; भ्रमो न प्रमा / *bhramo na pramā*; विपर्ययो न प्रमा / *viparyayo na pramā*; भ्रान्ति / *bhrānti*; भ्रान्त / *bhrānta*; भ्रान्तिज्ञान / *bhrānti-jñāna*; भ्रामक / *bhrāmaka*; विपर्यस्त / *viparyasta*; मिथ्यादृष्टि / *mithyā-dṛṣṭi*; अविसंवादिन् / *avisaṃvādin*; असत्ख्याति / *asatkhyāti*; विपर्ययो मिथ्याज्ञानम् / *viparyayo mithyājñānam*; मिथ्याज्ञानं निवर्तते सम्यग्ज्ञानेन / *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate samyag-jñānena*; रज्जौ सर्पभ्रान्तिः / *rajjau sarpa-bhrāntiḥ*; भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते / *bhrāntiḥ nivartate*; बाधेन भ्रान्तिः निवर्तते / *bādhena bhrāntiḥ nivartate*; बाधित / *bādhita*; बाधा / *bādhā* | मिथ्याज्ञान / *mithyājñāna* (also Group 5, carries Advaita Vedānta ontological connotations); भ्रम / *bhrama* and विपर्यय / *viparyaya* (near-synonyms across different school vocabularies); भ्रान्ति / *bhrānti* (near-synonym of *bhrama*) | *Bhrama* and *viparyaya* are near-synonyms in function but belong to different school vocabularies; *bhrama* is the standard Nyāya term, *viparyaya* the standard Sāṃkhya-Yoga term. *Bhrānti* is a near-synonym of *bhrama* emphasizing the state of being in error. *Mithyājñāna* carries additional Advaita Vedānta connotations about the ontological status of the object, not only the accuracy of the cognition. *Apramā* is the most technically neutral negation. *Avisaṃvādin* is a Buddhist *pramāṇa*-theory term. |
| Source-evaluating terms in epistemological argument | These terms are used to assess not the cognition itself but the means, source, or procedure by which it is produced. They appear in debates across all major Sanskrit philosophical schools about which sources of knowledge count as authoritative and why. The central question is not whether a particular cognition is accurate but whether the *means* by which it was arrived at is reliable. The central term *pramāṇa* generates a large cluster of derived expressions that evaluate sources as genuine, spurious, absent, or contested. | प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*; प्रामाण्य / *prāmāṇya*; अप्रामाण्य / *aprāmāṇya*; स्वतःप्रामाण्य / *svataḥprāmāṇya*; परतःप्रामाण्य / *parataḥprāmāṇya*; प्रमाणाभास / *pramāṇābhāsa*; हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa*; प्रमाणेन सिद्धम् / *pramāṇena siddham*; प्रमाणाभावात् / *pramāṇābhāvāt*; प्रमाणं नास्ति / *pramāṇaṃ nāsti*; प्रमाणविरुद्ध / *pramāṇaviruddha*; प्रमाणसिद्ध / *pramāṇasiddha*; अनुमान / *anumāna*; अनुमिति / *anumiti*; उपमान / *upamāna*; प्रामाणिक / *prāmāṇika*; अप्रमाण / *apramāṇa*; अप्रामाणिक / *aprāmāṇika*; प्रमाणभूत / *pramāṇabhūta*; प्रमाणता / *pramāṇatā*; प्रमातृ / *pramātṛ*; प्रमेय / *prameya*; वेदः प्रमाणम् / *vedaḥ pramāṇam*; प्रमाणं किम्? / *pramāṇaṃ kim?*; न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति / *na tatra pramāṇam asti*; प्रमायाः करणं प्रमाणम् / *pramāyāḥ karaṇaṃ pramāṇam*; हेत्वाभासो न हेतुः / *hetvābhāso na hetuḥ* | प्रमाण / *pramāṇa* (also Group 1, as the source that produces *pramā*); हेत्वाभास / *hetvābhāsa* (a specific application of the *ābhāsa* pattern to inferential reasoning) | The *svataḥ/parataḥ* pair marks a specific intra-school debate about the locus of validity-conferring conditions. *Hetvābhāsa* is a specialized application of the *ābhāsa* (semblance) pattern to the specific case of inferential reasoning. *Pramāṇābhāsa* is the more general term for spurious epistemic sources. This group is closely related to Group 1 but is distinct: Group 1 terms evaluate individual cognitions or statements, while Group 2 terms evaluate the *sources and methods* that generate cognitions. The two groups interact: a cognition is *pramā* (Group 1) when it results from a *pramāṇa* (Group 2). |
| Speech-evaluating terms in ethical and legal contexts | These terms are used in Dharmaśāstra, epic literature, and legal contexts to evaluate utterances, testimony, and speech acts as truthful, false, or wrongful. They appear in prescriptive injunctions about the duty of truthful speech, in legal evaluations of testimony, and in characterizations of persons as reliable or unreliable speakers. The evaluative dimension here is primarily moral and legal rather than epistemological. | सत्य / *satya*; असत्य / *asatya*; अनृत / *anṛta*; तथ्य / *tathya*; सत्यवादिन् / *satyavādin*; असत्यवादिन् / *asatyavādin*; मिथ्यावादिन् / *mithyāvādin*; सत्यवचन / *satyavacana*; असत्यवचन / *asatyavacana*; अनृतवचन / *anṛtavacana*; असत्यभाषण / *asatya-bhāṣaṇa*; अनृतभाषिन् / *anṛta-bhāṣin*; सत्यवाक्य / *satya-vākya*; सत्यसंध / *satyasaṃdha*; सत्यं वद / *satyaṃ vada*; सत्यं ब्रूयात् / *satyaṃ brūyāt*; असत्यं न वदेत् / *asatyaṃ na vadet*; अनृतं न वदेत् / *anṛtaṃ na vadet*; तथ्यं वदति / *tathyaṃ vadati*; असत्यं पापम् / *asatyaṃ pāpam*; असत्यं वदति / *asatyaṃ vadati*; मिथ्या न वदेत् / *mithyā na vadet*; सत्यं नास्ति परो धर्मः / *satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ*; सत्यं परं धर्मः / *satyaṃ paraṃ dharmaḥ* | सत्य / *satya* (also Groups 4, 5, 6); अनृत / *anṛta* (older Vedic term with cosmological connotations, overlapping with Group 5); तथ्य / *tathya* (also Group 4); मिथ्यावादिन् / *mithyāvādin* (overlapping with Group 1) | *Satya* and *asatya* are the most widely used pair in post-Vedic prose. *Anṛta* is the older Vedic term and carries cosmological connotations that *asatya* does not. *Tathya* is more restricted to factual accuracy in legal and narrative contexts and lacks the moral weight of *satya*. The prescriptive constructions (*brūyāt*, *na vadet*) are the characteristic syntactic form of this group. |
| Reality-correspondence terms in philosophical and general prose | These terms are used to assess whether a description, property, or characterization pertains to how things actually are, as opposed to how they appear, are conventionally described, or are merely named. They appear in philosophical argument when distinguishing genuine from apparent or conventional properties, and in general prose when asserting that an account is accurate to the facts. They are regularly contrasted with terms for appearance, convention, or mere verbal designation. | वास्तव / *vāstava*; वस्तु / *vastu*; यथार्थ / *yathārtha*; तथ्य / *tathya*; वस्तुतः / *vastutaḥ*; वास्तवतः / *vāstavataḥ*; वस्तुस्थिति / *vastusthiti*; वस्तुनि यथावत् ज्ञानम् / *vastuni yathāvat jñānam*; वस्तु न शब्दाधीनम् / *vastu na śabdādhīnam*; वस्तुबलात् / *vastubalaāt*; वास्तविक / *vāstavika*; वस्तुस्वभाव / *vastusvabhāva*; वस्तुतन्त्र / *vastu-tantra*; वस्तुसत् / *vastu-sat*; यथार्थता / *yathārthatā*; आभासमात्रम् न वस्तु / *ābhāsamātram na vastu* | वस्तु / *vastu* (also Group 5); यथार्थ / *yathārtha* (also Group 1); तथ्य / *tathya* (also Group 3) | *Vastu* and *vāstava* are closely related, with *vastu* being the noun (the real thing) and *vāstava* the adjective (pertaining to the real thing). *Yathārtha* emphasizes the correspondence relation between a description and its object, while *vastu*/*vāstava* emphasize the ontological status of the object itself. *Tathya* overlaps with this group but is more restricted to factual accuracy in testimony and narrative. |
| Being-status terms in metaphysical and cosmological argument | These terms are used to assess whether something genuinely exists or has genuine being, as opposed to being unreal, illusory, or of indeterminate ontological status. They appear in Vedāntic, Sāṃkhya, and Vaiśeṣika philosophical argument about the nature of reality, in cosmological discussions of what exists before and after creation, and in debates about causation. The causation debate (*satkāryavāda/asatkāryavāda*) is a specific application of the *sat/asat* distinction to the problem of the ontological status of effects. *Pratibhāsa* and *pratibhāsika* mark the level of mere appearance in Advaita ontology. |
