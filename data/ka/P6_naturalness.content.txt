# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / ACQUIRING TRUTH STATUS

### 1.1 Truth Revelation (Intransitive)

**Pattern:** Truth noun as subject + intransitive verb with preverb გამო- (gamo-) "out from"

**Examples:**
- სიმართლე გამოვლინდა (simart'le gamovlinda) → "the truth was revealed / came to light"
- ჭეშმარიტება გამოჩნდა (č'ešmarit'eba gamoč'nda) → "the truth appeared / became evident"

**Notes:** This is the most natural and frequent construction for truth "becoming known." The preverb გამო- metaphorically represents emergence from concealment. The construction emphasizes truth revealing itself rather than being made true.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 Verification / Confirmation (Passive)

**Pattern:** Statement/claim as subject + passive verb დადასტურდა (dadast'urda) "was confirmed"

**Examples:**
- ეს დადასტურდა (es dadast'urda) → "this was confirmed / verified"
- ინფორმაცია დადასტურდა (inp'ormacia dadast'urda) → "the information was confirmed"

**Notes:** This construction emphasizes the process of verification rather than inherent truth. The verb is passive, focusing on the claim undergoing confirmation rather than becoming true per se.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.3 Becoming Clear / Evident

**Pattern:** Truth/fact as subject + verb გაირკვა (gairkva) "became clear"

**Examples:**
- სიმართლე გაირკვა (simart'le gairkva) → "the truth became clear"
- ფაქტი გაირკვა (p'ak't'i gairkva) → "the fact became clear"

**Notes:** This construction emphasizes epistemic accessibility—truth becoming known or clear—rather than ontological change. Common in contexts of discovery or clarification.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.4 Becoming Correct (Rare)

**Pattern:** Statement/judgment + verb გახდა (gaxda) "became" + instrumental case of adjective

**Examples:**
- მართალი გახდა (mart'ali gaxda) → "became right/correct" (rare, marked)
- სწორი გახდა (sc'ori gaxda) → "became correct" (rare, marked)

**Notes:** This construction is grammatically possible but pragmatically marked. Georgian speakers prefer constructions emphasizing revelation or verification rather than statements "becoming true." This reflects conceptualization of truth as discovered rather than created.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 1.5 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "becoming true" as state change

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** Georgian lacks a common, natural expression for propositions or statements "becoming true" in the sense of acquiring truth value they previously lacked. The language strongly prefers constructions where truth is revealed, discovered, or verified rather than created or acquired. This suggests a conceptualization of truth as pre-existing and discovered rather than as a property that can be acquired.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / LOSING TRUTH STATUS

### 2.1 Refutation / Disproof

**Pattern:** Claim as subject + passive verb გაიმტყუნა (gaimṭq'una) "was refuted" or უარყო (uarq'o) "was denied"

**Examples:**
- ეს გაიმტყუნა (es gaimṭq'una) → "this was refuted / disproved"
- თეორია გაიმტყუნა (teoria gaimṭq'una) → "the theory was refuted"
- ინფორმაცია უარყო (inp'ormacia uarq'o) → "the information was denied / refuted"

**Notes:** These constructions emphasize the process of refutation rather than truth "becoming false." The verbs focus on rejection or disproof of claims.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Exposure as False

**Pattern:** Claim/person as subject + verb გამოიშიშვლა (gamoišišvla) "was exposed" or გამოჩნდა (gamoč'nda) "appeared/turned out" + predicate

**Examples:**
- ეს ტყუილად გამოჩნდა (es ṭq'uilad gamoč'nda) → "this turned out to be a lie"
- ის მატყუარად გამოჩნდა (is maṭq'uarad gamoč'nda) → "he/she turned out to be a liar"

**Notes:** These constructions emphasize revelation of pre-existing falsity rather than truth changing to falsehood. The preverb გამო- again indicates emergence or revelation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Becoming Outdated / No Longer True

**Pattern:** Statement/fact + verb მოძველდა (moʒvelda) "became outdated" or აღარ არის (aɣar aris) "is no longer"

**Examples:**
- ეს ინფორმაცია მოძველდა (es inp'ormacia moʒvelda) → "this information became outdated"
- ეს აღარ არის სიმართლე (es aɣar aris simart'le) → "this is no longer the truth"

**Notes:** These constructions address temporal change where previously true information becomes outdated or no longer applicable. The verb მოძველდა specifically means "became old/outdated."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for truth "becoming false"

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** Georgian lacks a natural expression for truth actively "becoming false" or "turning into falsehood." The language prefers constructions where falsity is revealed (was false all along) or where truth becomes outdated/inapplicable. This asymmetry mirrors the pattern in "becoming true"—Georgian conceptualizes truth/falsity as discovered rather than as states that change.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 3. CAUSING TRUTH / MAKING TRUE

### 3.1 Revealing Truth (Causative)

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + transitive verb გამოაჩინა (gamoač'ina) "revealed" + truth as object

**Examples:**
- მან სიმართლე გამოაჩინა (man simart'le gamoač'ina) → "he/she revealed the truth"
- გამოძიებამ ფაქტები გამოაჩინა (gamoʒiebam p'ak't'ebi gamoač'ina) → "the investigation revealed the facts"

**Notes:** This is the most natural causative construction in the truth domain, but it emphasizes revealing pre-existing truth rather than making something true. The agent causes truth to become known, not to become true.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Confirming / Verifying (Causative)

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + transitive verb დაადასტურა (daadast'ura) "confirmed" + claim as object

**Examples:**
- მან ეს დაადასტურა (man es daadast'ura) → "he/she confirmed this"
- ექსპერტმა ინფორმაცია დაადასტურა (ek'sp'ert'ma inp'ormacia daadast'ura) → "the expert confirmed the information"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent causing verification or confirmation, but again emphasizes establishing truth rather than creating it. The agent validates pre-existing truth.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Making Correct / Fixing

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გაასწორა (gaasc'ora) "corrected/fixed" + object

**Examples:**
- მან შეცდომა გაასწორა (man šecdoma gaasc'ora) → "he/she corrected the mistake"
- ტექსტი გაასწორა (ṭek'st'i gaasc'ora) → "corrected the text"

**Notes:** This construction involves making something correct by fixing errors, but applies to correcting mistakes rather than making statements true. It's about repair rather than truth-creation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Proving / Demonstrating

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb დაამტკიცა (daamṭk'ica) "proved" + claim as object

**Examples:**
- მან ეს დაამტკიცა (man es daamṭk'ica) → "he/she proved this"
- მეცნიერმა თეორია დაამტკიცა (mecnierrma teoria daamṭk'ica) → "the scientist proved the theory"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent demonstrating or proving truth, but again emphasizes establishing or showing truth rather than creating it. The focus is on demonstration.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "making true" as causative

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** Georgian lacks a natural causative construction meaning "to make X true" in the sense of causing a proposition to acquire truth value. All attested causative constructions involve revealing, confirming, proving, or correcting—actions that presuppose pre-existing truth rather than creating it. This pattern is consistent across the documented vocabulary.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 4. CAUSING FALSEHOOD / MAKING FALSE

### 4.1 Falsifying / Forging

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გააყალბა (gaaq'alba) "falsified/forged" + object

**Examples:**
- მან დოკუმენტი გააყალბა (man dok'ument'i gaaq'alba) → "he/she forged the document"
- ხელმოწერა გააყალბა (xelmoc'era gaaq'alba) → "forged the signature"

**Notes:** This construction involves creating false objects or representations. The verb გააყალბა specifically means to forge or falsify, creating counterfeit items.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.2 Refuting / Disproving

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გაამტყუნა (gaamṭq'una) "refuted" + claim as object

**Examples:**
- მან თეორია გაამტყუნა (man teoria gaamṭq'una) → "he/she refuted the theory"
- არგუმენტი გაამტყუნა (argument'i gaamṭq'una) → "refuted the argument"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent disproving or refuting claims, showing them to be false. The focus is on demonstration of falsity rather than creation of falsity.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Distorting / Misrepresenting

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb დაამახინჯა (daamaxinja) "distorted" + object

**Examples:**
- მან ფაქტები დაამახინჯა (man p'ak't'ebi daamaxinja) → "he/she distorted the facts"
- სიმართლე დაამახინჯა (simart'le daamaxinja) → "distorted the truth"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent distorting or misrepresenting truth, creating false representations. The verb emphasizes distortion rather than outright falsification.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.4 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "making false" as direct causative

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** Georgian lacks a direct causative construction meaning "to make X false" in the sense of causing a true proposition to become false. Attested constructions involve forging (creating false objects), refuting (showing falsity), or distorting (misrepresenting truth), but not causing truth to become falsehood.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 5. MAINTAINING TRUTH / STABILITY

### 5.1 Preserving Truth

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb შეინარჩუნა (šeinarčuna) "preserved/maintained" + truth as object

**Examples:**
- მან სიმართლე შეინარჩუნა (man simart'le šeinarčuna) → "he/she preserved the truth"
- ისტორიული სიმართლე შეინარჩუნა (ist'oriuli simart'le šeinarčuna) → "preserved the historical truth"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent maintaining or preserving truth against threats of distortion or loss. The verb შეინარჩუნა means "preserved/maintained."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.2 Defending Truth

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb დაიცვა (daicva) "defended/protected" + truth as object

**Examples:**
- მან სიმართლე დაიცვა (man simart'le daicva) → "he/she defended the truth"
- ჭეშმარიტება დაიცვა (č'ešmarit'eba daicva) → "defended the truth"

**Notes:** This construction involves an agent protecting or defending truth, typically against attack or denial. The verb დაიცვა means "defended/protected."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.3 Remaining True / Staying True

**Pattern:** Truth/statement as subject + verb დარჩა (darča) "remained" + predicate

**Examples:**
- ეს სიმართლე დარჩა (es simart'le darča) → "this remained the truth"
- მისი სიტყვები მართალი დარჩა (misi sit'q'vebi mart'ali darča) → "his/her words remained true"

**Notes:** This construction emphasizes stability or persistence of truth over time. The verb დარჩა means "remained/stayed."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.4 Unchanging Truth

**Pattern:** Truth + adjective უცვლელი (ucvleli) "unchanging/immutable"

**Examples:**
- უცვლელი სიმართლე (ucvleli simart'le) → "unchanging truth / immutable truth"
- მარადიული ჭეშმარიტება (maradiuli č'ešmarit'eba) → "eternal truth"

**Notes:** These are adjectival constructions characterizing truth as stable or unchanging rather than describing processes of maintenance. Common in philosophical and religious discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5.5 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for active "keeping true"

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** While Georgian has constructions for preserving and defending truth, there is no common expression for actively "keeping something true" in the sense of maintaining truth value. The language prefers constructions emphasizing protection of truth from distortion rather than maintenance of truth status.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 6. DECEPTION DYNAMICS

### 6.1 Deceiving (Causing False Belief)

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb მოატყუა (moaṭq'ua) "deceived" + person as object

**Examples:**
- მან ის მოატყუა (man is moaṭq'ua) → "he/she deceived him/her"
- ხალხი მოატყუა (xalxi moaṭq'ua) → "deceived the people"

**Notes:** This is the standard causative construction for deception—causing someone to hold false beliefs. The verb მოატყუა is transitive with the deceived person as direct object.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.2 Being Deceived (Passive)

**Pattern:** Person as subject + passive verb მოატყუეს (moaṭq'ues) "was deceived" or მოტყუებული იყო (moṭq'uebuli iq'o) "was deceived"

**Examples:**
- ის მოატყუეს (is moaṭq'ues) → "he/she was deceived"
- მოტყუებული ვიყავი (moṭq'uebuli viq'avi) → "I was deceived"

**Notes:** Passive construction with person as subject undergoing deception. The participial form მოტყუებული can combine with copula for stative meaning.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.3 Falling into Deception

**Pattern:** Person as subject + verb ჩავარდა (č'avarda) "fell" + postposition -ში (-shi) "into" + deception noun

**Examples:**
- მოტყუებაში ჩავარდა (moṭq'uebashi č'avarda) → "fell into deception / was deceived"
- თვითმოტყუებაში ჩავარდა (tviṭmoṭq'uebashi č'avarda) → "fell into self-deception"

**Notes:** This construction uses spatial metaphor of "falling into" deception, emphasizing the person's entry into a state of being deceived. Common with both other-deception and self-deception.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.4 Undeceiving / Disabusing

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გააცნობიერებინა (gaacnobierebina) "made realize" + person as object + truth

**Examples:**
- მან მას სიმართლე გააცნობიერებინა (man mas simart'le gaacnobierebina) → "he/she made him/her realize the truth"

**Notes:** This construction involves causing someone to recognize truth, effectively ending their deception. The verb is causative of "realize/become aware."

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 6.5 Realizing Deception

**Pattern:** Person as subject + verb მიხვდა (mixvda) "realized/understood" + clause or noun

**Examples:**
- მან მიხვდა, რომ მოატყუეს (man mixvda, rom moaṭq'ues) → "he/she realized that he/she had been deceived"
- მოტყუებას მიხვდა (moṭq'uebas mixvda) → "realized the deception"

**Notes:** This construction describes the person's realization of having been deceived, marking transition from deceived state to awareness. The verb მიხვდა means "realized/understood."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 7. AUTHENTICITY DYNAMICS

### 7.1 Becoming Genuine / Authentic

**Pattern:** Object/person as subject + verb გახდა (gaxda) "became" + ნამდვილი (namdvili) "genuine"

**Examples:**
- ნამდვილი მეგობარი გახდა (namdvili megobari gaxda) → "became a true friend"
- ნამდვილი ადამიანი გახდა (namdvili adamiani gaxdა) → "became a genuine person"

**Notes:** This construction describes development of authentic character or relationships. More natural than "becoming true" for propositions, as it applies to persons and relationships rather than statements.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.2 Revealing True Nature

**Pattern:** Person/thing as subject + verb გამოაჩინა (gamoač'ina) "revealed" + ნამდვილი სახე (namdvili saxe) "true face"

**Examples:**
- მან ნამდვილი სახე გამოაჩინა (man namdvili saxe gamoač'ina) → "he/she showed his/her true face"
- ნამდვილი სახე გამოჩნდა (namdvili saxe gamoč'nda) → "the true face appeared"

**Notes:** Idiomatic construction for revealing true character or nature. Can be transitive (agent reveals) or intransitive (nature reveals itself).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.3 Losing Authenticity

**Pattern:** Object/person as subject + verb დაკარგა (dak'arga) "lost" + authenticity noun

**Examples:**
- ნამდვილობა დაკარგა (namdviloba dak'arga) → "lost genuineness/authenticity"
- ავთენტურობა დაკარგა (avt'ent'uroba dak'arga) → "lost authenticity"

**Notes:** This construction describes loss of authentic character or quality. The verb დაკარგა means "lost."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 7.4 NO COMMON EXPRESSION for "making authentic"

**Pattern:** (no common expression identified)

**Notes:** While Georgian has constructions for becoming authentic (personal development) and revealing authenticity, there is no common causative construction for "making something authentic" in the sense of causing acquisition of genuine status. The language prefers constructions emphasizing revelation or development.

**Epistemic status:** [GAP]

---

## 8. CORRECTNESS DYNAMICS

### 8.1 Correcting Errors

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გაასწორა (gaasc'ora) "corrected" + error/text as object

**Examples:**
- შეცდომა გაასწორა (šecdoma gaasc'ora) → "corrected the mistake"
- ტექსტი გაასწორა (ṭek'st'i gaasc'ora) → "corrected the text"

**Notes:** This is the standard construction for fixing errors or making corrections. The verb გაასწორა derives from სწორი "correct" and means "made correct/fixed."

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 8.2 Making Mistakes / Becoming Wrong

**Pattern:** Person as subject + verb შეეშალა (šeešala) "made a mistake" or დაშვა (dašva) "made/committed" + შეცდომა (šecdoma) "mistake"

**Examples:**
- შეეშალა (šeešala) → "made a mistake / was wrong"
- შეცდომა დაშვა (šecdoma dašva) → "made a mistake / committed an error"

**Notes:** These constructions describe the act of making errors. The verb შეეშალა is intransitive meaning "made a mistake," while დაშვა + შეცდომა is a light verb construction.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 8.3 Becoming Incorrect (Passive)

**Pattern:** Statement/answer as subject + verb გახდა (gaxda) "became" + მცდარი (mcdari) "incorrect"

**Examples:**
- პასუხი მცდარი გახდა (p'asuxi mcdari gaxda) → "the answer became incorrect"

**Notes:** This construction is grammatically possible but pragmatically marked. More natural to say information became outdated (მოძველდა) than to say it "became incorrect."

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

### 8.4 Fixing / Setting Right

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb გამოასწორა (gamoasc'ora) "fixed/corrected" + situation/problem as object

**Examples:**
- სიტუაცია გამოასწორა (sit'uacia gamoasc'ora) → "fixed the situation"
- პრობლემა გამოასწორა (p'roblema gamoasc'ora) → "corrected the problem"

**Notes:** Extended use of correction verb for fixing situations or problems, not just textual errors. The preverb გამო- adds completive aspect.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 9. VERIFICATION DYNAMICS

### 9.1 Verifying / Confirming (Active)

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb დაამოწმა (daamoc'ma) "verified" or დაადასტურა (daadast'ura) "confirmed" + claim as object

**Examples:**
- ინფორმაცია დაამოწმა (inp'ormacia daamoc'ma) → "verified the information"
- ფაქტები დაადასტურა (p'ak't'ebi daadast'ura) → "confirmed the facts"

**Notes:** These are standard transitive constructions for verification and confirmation. Both verbs are common in formal and evidentiary contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 9.2 Being Verified (Passive)

**Pattern:** Claim as subject + passive verb დადასტურდა (dadast'urda) "was confirmed" or დამოწმდა (damoc'mda) "was verified"

**Examples:**
- ინფორმაცია დადასტურდა (inp'ormacia dadast'urda) → "the information was confirmed"
- ფაქტი დამოწმდა (p'ak't'i damoc'mda) → "the fact was verified"

**Notes:** Passive constructions with claim as subject undergoing verification. Very common in formal and journalistic discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 9.3 Checking / Testing

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb შეამოწმა (šeamoc'ma) "checked/tested" + object

**Examples:**
- ინფორმაცია შეამოწმა (inp'ormacia šeamoc'ma) → "checked the information"
- მონაცემები შეამოწმა (monacemebi šeamoc'ma) → "checked the data"

**Notes:** This construction emphasizes the process of checking or testing rather than final confirmation. The preverb შე- indicates thoroughness.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 9.4 Failing Verification

**Pattern:** Claim as subject + negative verb არ დადასტურდა (ar dadast'urda) "was not confirmed"

**Examples:**
- ინფორმაცია არ დადასტურდა (inp'ormacia ar dadast'urda) → "the information was not confirmed"
- ფაქტი ვერ დამოწმდა (p'ak't'i ver damoc'mda) → "the fact could not be verified"

**Notes:** Negative constructions indicating failure of verification. The particle ვერ (ver) indicates inability rather than simple negation.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 10. PRECISION DYNAMICS

### 10.1 Making Precise / Specifying

**Pattern:** Agent as subject + verb დააზუსტა (daazusta) "made precise/specified" + statement as object

**Examples:**
- ინფორმაცია დააზუსტა (inp'ormacia daazusta) → "made the information precise / specified"
- მონაცემები დააზუსტა (monacemebi daazusta) → "specified the data"

## SUMMARY OF PATTERNS

### Attested Dynamic Constructions

**Highly productive:**
1. Truth revelation (intransitive): Truth emerges or becomes known
2. Verification (passive): Claims undergo confirmation
3. Deception (causative): Agents cause false beliefs
4. Correction (causative): Agents fix errors
5. Preservation (causative): Agents maintain truth
6. Authenticity revelation: True nature becomes visible

**Moderately productive:**
7. Becoming reliable/authentic: Development of character
8. Justice establishment/restoration: Institutional dynamics
9. Precision specification: Making statements more exact
10. Refutation: Showing falsity

**Marginal:**
11. Generic "becoming true/false": Grammatically possible but pragmatically marked
12. Objectivity dynamics: Limited natural usage
13. Causative "making true": Rare or absent

### Systematic Gaps

**No common expressions for:**
1. Propositions "becoming true" (acquiring truth value)
2. Truth "becoming false" (losing truth value)
3. Direct causative "making X true" (causing truth acquisition)
4. Direct causative "making X false" (causing truth loss)
5. Active "keeping true" (maintaining truth value)
6. "Making authentic" (causing authenticity)

### Conceptual Patterns

**Georgian strongly prefers:**
- **Revelation over creation**: Truth is discovered/revealed rather than made true
- **Verification over validation**: Focus on confirming pre-existing truth
- **Preservation over maintenance**: Protecting truth from distortion
- **Character development over state change**: Becoming authentic (persons) more natural than becoming true (propositions)

**Georgian avoids:**
- Constructions suggesting truth is created or manufactured
- Direct causatives for truth value changes
- Constructions implying truth/falsity as acquired properties

This pattern suggests Georgian conceptualizes truth as **discovered** rather than **created**, and as **revealed** rather than **made**. Dynamic constructions focus on epistemic access (revelation, verification) rather than ontological change (becoming true/false).
