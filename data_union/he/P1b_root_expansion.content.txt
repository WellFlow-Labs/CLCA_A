# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)**

**Native script:** א-מ-נ
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-n
**P1 items participating:** Multiple — *ʾamin*, *ʾaminut*, *mehiman*/*meheymen*, *neʾeman*, *ʾemuna*, and the root family shared with *ʾemet* (in the majority lexicographic analysis treating ʾemet as a nominal derivative of א-מ-נ with final-nun weakening; the alternative analysis assigns ʾemet to א-מ-ת; both analyses are attested)
**Core meaning contribution:** Firmness, reliability, trust, and faithfulness. This root generates the largest cluster of terms in the truth-evaluation domain, covering trustworthiness of persons, credibility of testimony, and the quality of belief or faith. It underlies both epistemic evaluation (is this source credible?) and moral-relational evaluation (is this person faithful?).

---

**Root 2: א-מ-ת (ʾ-m-t)**

**Native script:** א-מ-ת
**Transliteration:** ʾ-m-t
**P1 items participating:** *ʾemet* (in the analysis treating it as a derivative of this root), *ʾamiti*, and the derivational base for several fixed phrases
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as a stable, grounded reality. This root provides the primary lexical anchor for the concept of truth itself in Hebrew, generating both the noun and its adjectival derivative. It is etymologically related to א-מ-נ (sharing the ʾ-m sequence) and contributes the sense of something that holds firm and corresponds to reality. It is the most philosophically and morally weighted root in the domain.

---

**Root 3: צ-ד-ק (ts-d-q)**

**Native script:** צ-ד-ק
**Transliteration:** ts-d-q
**P1 items participating:** *tsedeq*, *tsaddiq*
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, justice, conformity to a moral or legal standard. This root generates vocabulary for evaluating whether conduct, persons, or judgments are right in a normative sense — morally, legally, and socially. It is the primary root for the justice/righteousness dimension of truth-adjacent evaluation.

---

**Root 4: י-ש-ר (y-sh-r)**

**Native script:** י-ש-ר
**Transliteration:** y-sh-r
**P1 items participating:** *yashar*, *yosher*
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, uprightness, and moral directness. This root contributes the spatial-moral metaphor of a straight path or line as the basis for evaluating honesty and integrity. It generates terms used in both moral character evaluation and procedural/legal contexts where conduct is assessed as correct or deviant. Productive across moral, legal, and spatial domains.

---

**Root 5: כ-ו-נ (k-w-n)**

**Native script:** כ-ו-נ
**Transliteration:** k-w-n
**P1 items participating:** *nakhon*, and by extension *kannut* (sincerity) in some analyses
**Core meaning contribution:** Being established, set, fixed, correct, or prepared. Contributes the sense of correctness as a state of being properly set or aligned with reality. Highly productive in modern Hebrew across epistemic, procedural, and directional domains.

---

**Note on scope:** The roots א-מ-נ and א-מ-ת are historically and morphologically related (sharing the consonants א-מ and a common semantic field of firmness/reliability), and their derivatives overlap in usage. They are treated as distinct roots here because they generate distinct lexical families in attested Hebrew. The root י-ש-ר is selected because it participates in multiple P1 items and generates a substantial family of moral-epistemic expressions. The root צ-ד-ק is included because it participates in P1 items and generates a large family of justice/righteousness vocabulary. The root כ-ו-נ is included because *nakhon* is among the highest-frequency truth-evaluation terms in modern Hebrew and the root generates a large family of related expressions.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root א-מ-נ (ʾ-m-n)

---

**1.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה / אמונה
**Transliteration:** ʾemunah
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern *ʾeCuCa* + feminine suffix -ah
**Natural English translation:** faith, belief, faithfulness, trust
**Context of use:** One of the most central terms in Hebrew religious and moral discourse, referring to the quality of trusting in God, a person, or a principle, and to the state of being faithful. Used in theological contexts (*ʾemuna be-ʾelohim*, faith in God), in moral evaluation (*ʾish ʾemuna*, a man of faith/faithfulness), and in the fixed phrase *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* (truth and faithfulness). Also used in modern Hebrew for religious belief more broadly. Used across biblical, rabbinic, and modern contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** אֱמוּן / אמון
**Transliteration:** ʾemun / ʾimun
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern; also analyzed as Pi'el verbal noun pattern
**Natural English translation:** trust, confidence, reliance; also training, practice
**Context of use:** Used in two distinct senses: (a) interpersonal or institutional trust, e.g., *ʾimun ha-tsibur* ("public trust/confidence"), *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* ("public trust"); and (b) professional or physical training, e.g., *ʾimun tsvaʾi* ("military training"). Context disambiguates. Common in political and social discourse. Also appears in the phrase *ʾish ʾemun* (a trusted person, a confidant). Slightly more abstract and institutional than *ʾemuna*.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָן / נאמן
**Transliteration:** neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Nif'al participle
**Natural English translation:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy, reliable; also trustee, fiduciary (in legal contexts)
**Context of use:** Used to describe persons who are loyal and can be trusted — a faithful friend, a loyal employee, a reliable witness. Common in both everyday and formal Hebrew. Appears in fixed phrases such as *neʾeman le-* (faithful/loyal to) and *neʾeman la-ʾemet* (faithful to the truth). Also used as a title or form of address in some religious contexts. In legal and financial contexts, *neʾeman* denotes a trustee or fiduciary — a person entrusted with managing assets or interests on behalf of another. Standard legal terminology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָנוּת / נאמנות
**Transliteration:** neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Nif'al nominal form + *-ut* suffix
**Natural English translation:** loyalty, faithfulness, fidelity, trustworthiness; also trust (legal), fiduciary relationship, trusteeship
**Context of use:** The abstract noun denoting the quality of being loyal or faithful. Used in moral, political, and legal discourse to evaluate whether a person or institution has maintained fidelity to a commitment, person, or principle. Common in discussions of political loyalty, marital fidelity, and professional integrity. In legal contexts, *neʾemanut* denotes a trust or fiduciary arrangement. Standard in Israeli property and financial law.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** הֶאֱמִין / האמין
**Transliteration:** heʾemin
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il perfect 3ms
**Natural English translation:** believed, trusted (verb: to believe, to trust)
**Context of use:** The standard verb for "to believe" in Hebrew, used in both religious contexts (believing in God) and everyday epistemic contexts (believing a person, believing a claim). The infinitive *lehaʾamin* is extremely common. Takes the preposition *be-* (*lehaʾamin be-*, to believe in) or *le-* (*lehaʾamin le-*, to believe someone). "אני מאמין לך" ("I believe you") is a common everyday expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** אֵמוּן / אמון
**Transliteration:** ʾemun (training sense)
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern; note: this is a distinct lexical item from *ʾemun* (trust) above, though homophonous in some analyses
**Natural English translation:** training, apprenticeship, upbringing
**Context of use:** Used to describe the process of training or raising someone — an apprentice, a ward, or a trainee. Appears in the phrase *ben ʾemun* (a ward, a foster child) and in contexts of professional training. This is a distinct semantic branch of the root from the trust/belief cluster.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** אוֹמֵן / אומן
**Transliteration:** ʾomen
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Qal active participle
**Natural English translation:** trainer, tutor, foster father, guardian
**Context of use:** Used in biblical and literary Hebrew to describe a person who raises or trains another — a guardian, tutor, or foster parent. Appears in the biblical narrative of Mordecai as the *ʾomen* of Esther. Less common in everyday modern Hebrew but retained in literary and religious registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**8.**
**Form:** אוֹמֶנֶת / אומנת
**Transliteration:** ʾomenet
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Qal active participle, feminine form
**Natural English translation:** nurse, nanny, foster mother, guardian
**Context of use:** The feminine counterpart of *ʾomen*, used in biblical and literary Hebrew for a woman who raises or nurses a child. Appears in biblical narrative (e.g., the nurse of the infant Joash). Retained in literary and religious registers; less common in everyday modern Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**9.**
**Form:** מַאֲמִין / מאמין
**Transliteration:** maʾamin
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il active participle
**Natural English translation:** believer, one who believes; trusting
**Context of use:** Used to describe a person who holds religious belief or who believes a particular claim. In religious discourse, *maʾamin* denotes a believer in God or in a religious doctrine. In everyday discourse, it can describe someone who believes a particular person or claim, e.g., *ʾani maʾamin she-hu tsodeq* ("I believe he is right"). The plural *maʾaminim* is common in religious contexts. Used as both a verbal participle ("I believe") and a noun ("a believer").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** אֲמָנָה / אמנה
**Transliteration:** ʾamana / ʾamanaʾh
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern *ʾaCaCa* + feminine suffix
**Natural English translation:** agreement, covenant, charter, compact
**Context of use:** Used in legal, political, and international contexts to describe a formal agreement or charter between parties. In modern Hebrew, *ʾamana* is used for international agreements, organizational charters, and formal compacts. The term implies a binding commitment grounded in mutual trust. E.g., *ʾamanaʾh leʾumit* ("national charter"), *ʾamanaʾh benleʾumit* ("international convention/charter").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** אָמֵן / אמן
**Transliteration:** ʾamen
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, frozen liturgical form, "so be it, it is firm/true"
**Natural English translation:** amen; so be it; affirmation of truth/reliability of a statement or prayer
**Context of use:** Used in religious liturgy and prayer as an affirmation that what has been said is true or that the prayer is endorsed. Borrowed into many languages from Hebrew. In modern Hebrew, also used colloquially to express strong agreement or hope that something will come true.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** מְהֵימָנוּת / מהימנות
**Transliteration:** mehimanut / meheymanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Pu'al or Hif'il nominal form + *-ut* suffix
**Natural English translation:** trustworthiness, credibility, reliability
**Context of use:** The abstract noun corresponding to *mehiman*/*meheymen*. Used in legal and formal discourse to describe the property of being credible or trustworthy, especially of witnesses and sources. Appears in legal judgments in the phrase *behinat ha-mehimanut* (examination of credibility). Standard in legal, journalistic, and scientific contexts when evaluating sources, witnesses, or data.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה / אמת ואמונה
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ve-ʾemuna
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemet* (truth, root א-מ-ת) + *ve-* (and) + *ʾemuna* (faithfulness, root א-מ-נ)
**Natural English translation:** truth and faithfulness; in truth and in faith
**Context of use:** A fixed liturgical and literary phrase used in Jewish prayer (notably in the *ʾemet ve-ʾemuna* blessing following the *Shema*) and in formal declarations to affirm that something is both factually true and sincerely meant. Also used in legal and contractual language to indicate good faith.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** לֹא נֶאֱמָן / לא נאמן
**Transliteration:** lo neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *lo* (not) + *neʾeman* (faithful, trustworthy)
**Natural English translation:** untrustworthy, unreliable, disloyal
**Context of use:** Used in everyday and legal contexts to describe a person or source that cannot be trusted. In legal discourse, a witness described as *lo neʾeman* is one whose testimony is not to be relied upon. Also used in interpersonal contexts to describe disloyalty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** עֵד נֶאֱמָן / עד נאמן
**Transliteration:** ʿed neʾeman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʿed* (witness) + *neʾeman* (faithful, trustworthy)
**Natural English translation:** reliable witness, faithful witness
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in legal and religious discourse to describe a witness whose testimony is considered credible and trustworthy. Appears in biblical texts and in modern legal Hebrew. Also used metaphorically to describe a reliable record or document.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** נֶאֱמָן לְ- / נאמן ל-
**Transliteration:** neʾeman le-
**Morpheme breakdown:** *neʾeman* (faithful) + *le-* (to, toward)
**Natural English translation:** faithful to, loyal to, true to
**Context of use:** A productive collocation used to describe fidelity to a person, principle, or commitment. Common in political discourse (*neʾeman la-miflag*, loyal to the party), moral discourse (*neʾeman la-ʾemet*, faithful to the truth), and professional contexts (*neʾeman la-ʾavodato*, faithful to his work).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** הַאֲמָנָה / האמנה
**Transliteration:** haʾamana
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ, Hif'il verbal noun (infinitive construct pattern)
**Natural English translation:** accreditation, authorization, credentialing
**Context of use:** Used in diplomatic and institutional contexts to describe the formal accreditation of an ambassador or the authorization of a representative. *Kitvey haʾamana* (letters of accreditation) is the standard phrase for diplomatic credentials presented by an ambassador to a head of state.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** כִּתְבֵי הַאֲמָנָה / כתבי האמנה
**Transliteration:** kitvey haʾamana
**Morpheme breakdown:** *kitvey* (letters/documents of, construct plural of *ketav*) + *haʾamana* (accreditation)
**Natural English translation:** letters of credence, letters of accreditation
**Context of use:** The standard diplomatic term for the formal documents by which an ambassador is accredited to a foreign government. Used in official diplomatic and governmental contexts in modern Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** מוּסָד מְהֵימָן / מוסד מהימן
**Transliteration:** musad mehiman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *musad* (institution) + *mehiman* (trustworthy, reliable)
**Natural English translation:** reliable institution, credible institution
**Context of use:** A collocation used in public and journalistic discourse to describe an institution — a news organization, a research body, a court — that is considered credible and trustworthy. Evaluates institutional rather than personal credibility.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** מָקוֹר מְהֵימָן / מקור מהימן
**Transliteration:** maqor mehiman
**Morpheme breakdown:** *maqor* (source) + *mehiman* (trustworthy, reliable)
**Natural English translation:** reliable source, credible source
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in journalistic, legal, and intelligence contexts to describe a source of information that is considered credible. Appears frequently in news reporting (*le-fi maqor mehiman*, "according to a reliable source").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנִים / אמונים
**Transliteration:** ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ + nominal pattern + masculine plural suffix
**Natural English translation:** faithfulness, loyalty (plural/intensive form); also: the faithful (as a group)
**Context of use:** Used in biblical and literary Hebrew, often in poetic or elevated registers, to describe the quality of faithfulness or the community of the faithful. Appears in Psalms and other biblical poetry. Less common in everyday modern Hebrew but retained in religious and literary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** אֲמִינוּת מֵידָע / אמינות מידע
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut meyda
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾaminut* (reliability) + *meyda* (information)
**Natural English translation:** information reliability, data reliability
**Context of use:** Used in technical, journalistic, and intelligence contexts to evaluate the reliability of a body of information or data. Common in discussions of media literacy, intelligence assessment, and scientific methodology.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** בּוֹחֵן אֱמוּנִים / בוחן אמונים
**Transliteration:** boḥen ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** *boḥen* (tester, examiner, Qal participle of ב-ח-נ) + *ʾemunim* (faithfulness, loyalties)
**Natural English translation:** tester of loyalties, examiner of faithfulness
**Context of use:** A literary and biblical phrase used to describe God or a situation that tests the faithfulness or loyalty of a person. Appears in biblical and liturgical poetry. Used metaphorically in modern Hebrew to describe a situation that reveals whether someone is truly loyal.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**24.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנַת שָׁוְא / אמונת שווא
**Transliteration:** ʾemunat shaʾv
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith, belief) + *shaʾv* (vain, empty, false)
**Natural English translation:** false belief, vain faith, misplaced trust
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and religious discourse to describe belief or trust that is misplaced or unfounded — faith in something that does not merit it. Contrasts with *ʾemuna ʾamitit* (genuine faith). Used in critiques of superstition or ideological credulity.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**25.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה עִוֶּרֶת / אמונה עיוורת
**Transliteration:** ʾemuna ʿiveret
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith, belief) + *ʿiveret* (blind, feminine adjective)
**Natural English translation:** blind faith
**Context of use:** Used in critical discourse — philosophical, journalistic, and everyday — to describe faith or trust that is held without examination or evidence. A common collocation in modern Hebrew discussions of religion, ideology, and authority. Carries a negative evaluative connotation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** אֱמוּנָה דָּתִית / אמונה דתית
**Transliteration:** ʾemuna datit
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemuna* (faith) + *datit* (religious, adjective from *dat*, religion/law)
**Natural English translation:** religious faith
**Context of use:** The standard collocation for religious faith or belief in modern Hebrew discourse. Used in sociological, philosophical, and everyday contexts to describe the domain of religious belief as distinct from other forms of trust or confidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** מַשְׁבֵּר אֱמוּן / משבר אמון
**Transliteration:** mashber ʾemun
**Morpheme breakdown:** *mashber* (crisis) + *ʾemun* (trust, confidence)
**Natural English translation:** crisis of trust, confidence crisis
**Context of use:** Used in political and social discourse to describe a situation in which public trust in an institution, leader, or system has collapsed or is severely damaged. Common in journalistic and political commentary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** הַצְהָרַת אֱמוּנִים / הצהרת אמונים
**Transliteration:** hatshaʾrat ʾemunim
**Morpheme breakdown:** *hatshaʾrat* (declaration of, construct form of *hatshaʾra*) + *ʾemunim* (loyalties, faithfulness)
**Natural English translation:** oath of allegiance, declaration of loyalty
**Context of use:** The standard term for an oath of allegiance or loyalty declaration, used in Israeli civic and military contexts. New citizens, soldiers, and public officials take a *hatshaʾrat ʾemunim* to the state. A formal institutional use of the root.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** אֱמוּן הַצִּבּוּר / אמון הציבור
**Transliteration:** ʾemun ha-tsibbur
**Morpheme breakdown:** *ʾemun* (trust) + *ha-tsibbur* (the public)
**Natural English translation:** public trust, public confidence
**Context of use:** A fixed phrase used in political, legal, and journalistic discourse to describe the trust that the public places in institutions, officials, or systems. Erosion of *ʾemun ha-tsibbur* is a standard topic in Israeli public discourse about governance and media.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**30.**
**Form:** אמון ציבורי
**Transliteration:** ʾimun tsiburi
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾimun (root א-מ-נ, "trust") + tsiburi ("public")
**Natural English translation:** public trust; public confidence
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in political, journalistic, and institutional discourse to evaluate whether citizens trust government bodies, public figures, or institutions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**31.**
**Form:** אמון בית משפט
**Transliteration:** ʾimun beyt mishpat
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾimun (root א-מ-נ, "trust") + beyt mishpat ("court of law")
**Natural English translation:** the court's trust; judicial confidence
**Context of use:** Used in legal discourse to describe the degree to which a court places confidence in a witness, expert, or piece of evidence, e.g., *ha-ʿed zakaʾh be-ʾimun beyt ha-mishpat* ("the witness earned the court's trust").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**32.**
**Form:** שבועת נאמנות
**Transliteration:** shevaʿat neʾemanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** shevaʿah ("oath") + neʾemanut (root א-מ-נ, "faithfulness")
**Natural English translation:** oath of allegiance; loyalty oath
**Context of use:** A fixed legal and civic expression for the formal oath sworn by public officials, new citizens, or military personnel affirming their loyalty to the state or institution.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**33.**
**Form:** נאמן בית

**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman (root א-מ-נ, "trusted one") + bayit ("house")
**Natural English translation:** trusted household steward; majordomo; trusted aide
**Context of use:** A biblical and literary expression for a trusted household manager or personal aide; also used in modern Hebrew for a trusted personal assistant or steward in institutional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**34.**
**Form:** נאמן לעדות
**Transliteration:** neʾeman le-ʿedut
**Morpheme breakdown:** neʾeman (root א-מ-נ, "reliable") + le-ʿedut ("for testimony")
**Natural English translation:** competent to testify; admissible as a witness
**Context of use:** A legal term in both halakhic and Israeli civil law indicating that a person meets the criteria to serve as a valid witness, e.g., in matters of contract, marriage, or criminal proceedings.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**35.**
**Form:** אמינות מקורות

**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾaminut (root א-מ-נ, "credibility") + meqorot ("sources")
**Natural English translation:** source credibility; reliability of sources
**Context of use:** Used in journalistic, academic, and intelligence contexts to evaluate whether the sources cited in a report or study are trustworthy and dependable.

**36.**
**Form:** אמינות עד
**Transliteration:** ʾaminut ʿed
**Morpheme breakdown:** ʾaminut (root א-מ-נ, "credibility") + ʿed ("witness")
**Natural English translation:** witness credibility
**Context of use:** A standard legal term used in Israeli court proceedings and legal scholarship to refer to the assessment of whether a witness's testimony is believable and reliable.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**37.**
**Form:** בר-אמן
**Transliteration:** bar-ʾaman
**Morpheme breakdown:** bar ("son of, one who has a quality," Aramaic) + ʾaman (root א-מ-נ, "trust")
**Natural English translation:** trustworthy person; reliable individual
**Context of use:** Used in rabbinic and halakhic literature to designate a person who is trusted in a specific domain, e.g., *bar-ʾaman be-ʿisur ve-heter* ("trusted in matters of religious law"). Occasionally appears in modern legal Hebrew.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

**38.**
**Form:** אמן / אומן
**Transliteration:** ʾoman / ʾomman
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ; noun pattern, "craftsman, artisan, artist"; also intensive noun pattern (Aramaic influence), "master craftsman, expert"
**Natural English translation:** artisan; craftsman; artist; master craftsman; expert practitioner
**Context of use:** Used to describe a skilled practitioner of a craft or art, e.g., *ʾoman tsayar* ("a painter-artist"). The connection to the root is through the sense of mastery and reliable skill. The intensive form *ʾumman* is used in rabbinic literature and occasionally in modern Hebrew to designate a master of a craft or trade, with emphasis on expert skill.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**39.**

**Transliteration:** ʾomanut
**Morpheme breakdown:** root א-מ-נ; abstract noun from *ʾoman*, "art, craft, artistry"
**Natural English translation:** art; artistry; craft
**Context of use:** The standard modern Hebrew word for art in all its forms — visual art, performing arts, literary arts — and also for skilled craftsmanship. Used in aesthetic, cultural, and educational discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**40.**

**41.**
