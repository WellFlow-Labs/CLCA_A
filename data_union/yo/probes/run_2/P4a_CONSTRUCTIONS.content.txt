# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Subject + stative predicate (*tọ́*, *dára*, *jẹ́* + nominal)

### Examples
- Ó tọ́ → *ó tọ́* → It is correct / It is right / It is proper
- Ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo → *ìjẹ́rìísí rẹ̀ jẹ́ òdodo* → His/her certificate is genuine/valid

### Notes
The root *tọ́* functions directly as a stative predicate without a copula; for nominal predicates (e.g., *irọ́*, *òdodo*, *gidi*), the copula *jẹ́* is required. Both patterns are productive across factual, moral, and administrative domains.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
*kò* + stative predicate; *kì í ṣe* + nominal predicate

### Examples
- Kò tọ́ → *kò tọ́* → It is not correct / It is wrong / It is improper
- Kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi → *kì í ṣe nǹkan gidi* → It is not the real thing / It is not genuine

### Notes
*kò* negates stative and verbal predicates; *kì í ṣe* is the negative copula used with nominal predicates. The two forms are not interchangeable: *kò tọ́* (stative negation) vs. *kì í ṣe irọ́* (nominal negation).

---

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Head noun + modifier (noun or adjective follows the head it modifies)

### Examples
- ẹ̀rí ojú → *ẹ̀rí ojú* → eyewitness evidence (lit. eye-evidence)
- ìdáhùn tọ́ → *ìdáhùn tọ́* → correct answer (lit. answer [that is] correct)

### Notes
Yoruba is head-initial in nominal modification: the modifying noun or adjective follows the head noun directly, without an overt linker in most cases. Adjectival predicates (*tọ́*, *rere*, *búburú*) double as postnominal modifiers in this pattern.

---

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
*ó farahàn pé* + propositional complement; *yí padà* (serial verb: to turn/change)

### Examples
- Ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni → *ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* → It became clear that it is the truth / It turned out to be true
- Ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà → *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ yí padà* → His/her belief changed / His/her faith shifted

### Notes
Yoruba lacks a dedicated inchoative morpheme. Change-of-state is expressed lexically: *farahàn* (to become visible/clear) for epistemic revelation, and the serial verb *yí padà* (lit. turn-return) for change in a state or orientation.

---

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
Serial verb construction: *fi* X *hàn* (use X to show/reveal); *fi* X *gbà* Y (use X to obtain Y); *jẹ́ kí* + subjunctive

### Examples
- Ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀ → *ó fi ẹ̀rí hàn lójú ìgbìmọ̀* → He/she presented evidence before the court (caused evidence to be seen)
- Ó fi irọ́ gbà owó → *ó fi irọ́ gbà owó* → He/she obtained money through deception (used lies to cause receipt of money)

### Notes
Yoruba has no dedicated causative morpheme in this domain. Causation is expressed through serial verb constructions, most productively *fi* (to use/take) + object + result verb. The construction *jẹ́ kí* (let/allow that) expresses permissive causation in normative contexts (*Ẹ jẹ́ kí a sọ òtítọ́* — let us speak the truth).

---

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*ì-* + verbal root; *ò-* + root; *ẹ̀-* + root; *à-* + root

### Examples
- ìgbàgbọ́ → *ìgbàgbọ́* → belief, faith (from *gbàgbọ́*, to believe)
- òtítọ́ → *òtítọ́* → truth, correctness (from root *tọ́*, correct/straight)

### Notes
Abstract nominalization is the most productive derivational process in this domain. The prefix *ì-* is the most frequent nominalizer of verbal roots; *ò-* and *ẹ̀-* produce distinct nominals from the same root with different semantic ranges (e.g., *òtítọ́* [truth] vs. *ẹ̀tọ́* [right/entitlement], both from *tọ́*). Negated abstract nominals are formed with *àì-* (*àìmọ̀* — ignorance, *àìgbàgbọ́* — unbelief).

---

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
*olù-* + verbal root (agentive); *ẹni tó ní* + nominal (possessive relative, sometimes contracted); *al-* / *oní-* + nominal

### Examples
- olùgbàgbọ́ → *olùgbàgbọ́* → believer, one who believes (from *gbàgbọ́*)
- ẹlẹ́rìí → *ẹlẹ́rìí* → witness (contracted from *ẹni tó ní ẹ̀rí*, one who has/bears evidence)

### Notes
Two agentive strategies are attested: the prefix *olù-* attaches to verbal roots to produce agent nouns (*olùgbàgbọ́*, *olùmọ̀*, *olùjẹ́rìí*); the possessive relative construction *ẹni tó ní* + noun contracts to produce agent nouns from nominal bases (*ẹlẹ́rìí* from *ẹ̀rí*). The pattern *oní-* / *olú-* also produces agentives from nominal bases (*olóirọ́* — liar, *olòtítọ́* — truthful person). Negative agentives are formed with *aláì-* (*aláìgbàgbọ́* — unbeliever, *aláìmọ̀* — ignorant person).

---

## 8. PROPOSITIONAL COMPLEMENT CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Epistemic/speech verb + *pé* + complement clause

### Examples
- Ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni → *ó gbàgbọ́ pé òtítọ́ ni* → He/she believes that it is the truth
- Ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀ → *ó jẹ́rìí pé ó wà níbẹ̀* → He/she testified that he/she was there

### Notes
The complementizer *pé* (that) is the standard introducer of propositional complements after epistemic verbs (*mọ̀*, *gbàgbọ́*), speech verbs (*sọ*, *jẹ́rìí*), and perception verbs. This construction is the primary syntactic frame for expressing propositional truth, belief, and testimony in Yoruba.

---

## 9. CLEFT / FOCUS CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Focused constituent + *ni* (focus particle) + remainder of clause

### Examples
- Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ → *òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* → It is the truth that he/she spoke / What he/she spoke is the truth
- Ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn → *ìwà ló ń ṣe ènìyàn* → It is character that makes a person

### Notes
The focus particle *ni* (and its fused form *ló* = *ni* + *ó* before a following subject pronoun) places contrastive or identificational focus on the fronted constituent. This construction is highly productive in truth-domain contexts for asserting or contesting the truth-value of a claim, and in proverbial statements asserting the primacy of a concept.

---

## 10. NEGATIVE EXISTENTIAL (ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE / TRUTH)

### Pattern
*kò sí* + noun phrase (+ locative/relative complement)

### Examples
- Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀ → *kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* → There is no truth in his/her words
- Kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi → *kò sí ẹ̀rí tó tó láti fi dá a lẹ́bi* → There is no sufficient evidence to convict him/her

### Notes
*kò sí* (there is not / there does not exist) is the standard negative existential construction. In truth-domain usage it denies the presence of truth, falsehood, justice, or evidence in a situation. The construction readily takes relative clause complements (*tó tó* — that is sufficient) and purpose clauses (*láti fi...* — in order to use...), making it productive in complex legal argument.
