# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P6 CHANGE, CAUSATION, AND STABILITY
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## 1. BECOMING TRUE / CORRECT

### Pattern
**di òtítọ́** (become truth) — change-of-state verb **di**
**di tọ́** (become correct/true) — **di** with stative predicate

### Examples

**Ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́**
Ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́
"That matter became truth" / "That statement turned out to be true"
[CORE]

**Ó di tọ́**
Ó di tọ́
"It became correct/true" / "It turned out to be true"
[CORE]

**Ìrò̩run rẹ̀ di òtítọ́**
Ìrò̩run rẹ̀ di òtítọ́
"His/her dream became truth" / "His/her dream came true"
[CORE]

### Notes
The verb **di** 'become' is the standard change-of-state marker. It is used when something previously uncertain, doubted, or believed false is revealed to be true. Common in narrative contexts describing verification or vindication. The construction **di òtítọ́** can mean both "become true" (for propositions that were false) and "turn out to be true" (for propositions whose truth-value was unknown).

---

## 2. BECOMING FALSE / INCORRECT

### Pattern
**di irọ́** (become lie/falsehood) — less common than **di òtítọ́**
**di àìtọ́** (become incorrect) — using negative nominal

### Examples

**Ọ̀rọ̀ tí ó ti tọ́ di irọ́**
Ọ̀rọ̀ tí ó ti tọ́ di irọ́
"Statement that was correct became lie" / "What was true became false"
[MARGINAL]

**Ó di àìtọ́**
Ó di àìtọ́
"It became incorrect" / "It turned out to be wrong"
[CORE]

### Notes
The construction **di irọ́** is less common than **di òtítọ́** because statements don't typically "become lies" — they are either lies from the start or remain true. However, the construction can be used when circumstances change such that a previously true statement becomes false, or when something believed true is revealed to be false. **Di àìtọ́** is more common for describing something that turns out to be incorrect.

---

## 3. CEASING TO BE TRUE

### Pattern
**kò tọ́ mọ́** (not correct anymore) — negation plus **mọ́** 'anymore'
**dẹ́kun láti jẹ́ òtítọ́** (cease to be truth) — less common

### Examples

**Kò tọ́ mọ́**
Kò tọ́ mọ́
"It is not correct anymore" / "It is no longer true"
[CORE]

**Ọ̀rọ̀ náà kò jẹ́ òtítọ́ mọ́**
Ọ̀rọ̀ náà kò jẹ́ òtítọ́ mọ́
"That statement is not truth anymore" / "That is no longer true"
[CORE]

### Notes
Yoruba typically expresses cessation of truth-states through negation plus the particle **mọ́** 'anymore/again'. The verb **dẹ́kun** 'cease' is less commonly used with truth predicates. The construction emphasizes that something was previously true but is no longer the case.

---

## 4. BEGINNING TO BELIEVE

### Pattern
**bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́** (begin to believe) — inchoative with **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí**

### Examples

**Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́**
Mo bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́
"I began to believe" / "I started to believe"
[CORE]

**Ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́**
Ó bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
"He/She began to believe that it is true"
[CORE]

**Àwọn ènìyàn bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀**
Àwọn ènìyàn bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí gbàgbọ́ ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
"People began to believe his/her words"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí** 'begin to' is the standard inchoative marker. Very common in contexts describing gradual acceptance of claims or growing conviction. Can be used with all epistemic verbs.

## 5. CEASING TO BELIEVE

### Pattern
**dẹ́kun gbàgbọ́** (cease believing) — **dẹ́kun** 'cease' plus verb
**kò gbàgbọ́ mọ́** (not believe anymore) — negation plus **mọ́**

### Examples

**Mo dẹ́kun gbàgbọ́**
Mo dẹ́kun gbàgbọ́
"I ceased believing" / "I stopped believing"
[CORE]

**Kò gbàgbọ́ mọ́**
Kò gbàgbọ́ mọ́
"He/She doesn't believe anymore" / "He/She no longer believes"
[CORE]

**Mo ti dẹ́kun gbàgbọ́ irọ́ rẹ̀**
Mo ti dẹ́kun gbàgbọ́ irọ́ rẹ̀
"I have ceased believing his/her lies"
[CORE]

### Notes
Both **dẹ́kun** and **kò...mọ́** are productive for expressing cessation of belief. **Dẹ́kun** emphasizes the deliberate act of stopping, while **kò...mọ́** simply states the current non-belief state. Common in contexts of disillusionment or discovery of deception.

## 6. BECOMING CERTAIN

### Pattern
**di dájú** (become certain) — **di** plus certainty predicate
**bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí dájú** (begin to be certain) — inchoative

### Examples

**Mo di dájú**
Mo di dájú
"I became certain" / "I became sure"
[CORE]

**Ó di dájú pé ó tọ́**
Ó di dájú pé ó tọ́
"It became certain that it is true"
[CORE]

**Lẹ́yìn ìwádìí, ó di dájú**
Lẹ́yìn ìwádìí, ó di dájú
"After investigation, it became certain"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Di dájú** describes the transition from uncertainty to certainty. Very common in investigative and epistemic contexts. Can describe both personal certainty (with personal subject) and objective certainty (with impersonal subject).

## 7. BECOMING UNCERTAIN / LOSING CERTAINTY

### Pattern
**kò dájú mọ́** (not certain anymore) — negation plus **mọ́**
**di aláìdájú** (become uncertain) — less common

### Examples

**Mo kò dájú mọ́**
Mo kò dájú mọ́
"I am not certain anymore" / "I am no longer sure"
[CORE]

**Ó kò dájú mọ́ pé ó tọ́**
Ó kò dájú mọ́ pé ó tọ́
"It is no longer certain that it is true"
[CORE]

### Notes
Loss of certainty is typically expressed through negation plus **mọ́** rather than through **di** 'become'. This reflects the fact that uncertainty is often conceptualized as absence of certainty rather than as a positive state.

## 8. CAUSING SOMEONE TO BELIEVE

### Pattern
**mú X gbàgbọ́** (make X believe) — causative **mú**
**jẹ́ kí X gbàgbọ́** (let/allow X believe) — permissive causative

### Examples

**Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́**
Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́
"He/She made me believe" / "He/She convinced me"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ mú mi gbàgbọ́**
Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ mú mi gbàgbọ́
"His/her evidence made me believe"
[CORE]

**Ó mú wọn gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́**
Ó mú wọn gbàgbọ́ pé ó tọ́
"He/She made them believe that it is true"
[CORE]

**Jẹ́ kí wọ́n gbàgbọ́**
Jẹ́ kí wọ́n gbàgbọ́
"Let them believe" / "Allow them to believe"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Mú...gbàgbọ́** 'make believe' is the standard causative construction for inducing belief. Very common in contexts of persuasion, evidence, and argumentation. **Jẹ́ kí...gbàgbọ́** is permissive rather than directly causative.

## 9. CAUSING SOMEONE TO KNOW / REALIZE THE TRUTH

### Pattern
**mú X mọ̀ òtítọ́** (make X know truth) — causative with **mọ̀** 'know'
**fi òtítọ́ hàn fún X** (show truth to X) — 'reveal truth to'

### Examples

**Ó mú mi mọ̀ òtítọ́**
Ó mú mi mọ̀ òtítọ́
"He/She made me know the truth"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀rí náà mú wọn mọ̀ òtítọ́**
Ẹ̀rí náà mú wọn mọ̀ òtítọ́
"That evidence made them know the truth"
[CORE]

**Fi òtítọ́ hàn fún mi**
Fi òtítọ́ hàn fún mi
"Show the truth to me" / "Reveal the truth to me"
[CORE]

**Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn fún àwọn ènìyàn**
Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn fún àwọn ènìyàn
"He/She revealed the truth to the people"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Mú...mọ̀** 'make know' is direct causation of knowledge. **Fi...hàn** 'use...show' is the standard construction for revelation or disclosure. Both are very common in truth-revelation contexts.

## 10. CAUSING DECEPTION / MAKING SOMEONE BELIEVE A LIE

### Pattern
**tan X** (deceive X) — transitive verb **tan**
**mú X gbàgbọ́ irọ́** (make X believe lie) — causative plus **irọ́**

### Examples

**Ó tan mi**
Ó tan mi
"He/She deceived me" / "He/She tricked me"
[CORE]

**Ó tan àwọn ènìyàn**
Ó tan àwọn ènìyàn
"He/She deceived the people"
[CORE]

**Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ irọ́ rẹ̀**
Ó mú mi gbàgbọ́ irọ́ rẹ̀
"He/She made me believe his/her lie"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀tàn rẹ̀ mú wọn gbàgbọ́ irọ́**
Ẹ̀tàn rẹ̀ mú wọn gbàgbọ́ irọ́
"His/her deception made them believe the lie"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Tan** 'deceive' is a simple transitive verb that directly expresses causing someone to believe what is false. **Mú...gbàgbọ́ irọ́** 'make believe lie' is more explicit about the false content. Both are very common in contexts of deception and fraud.

## 11. MAKING SOMETHING TRUE / ESTABLISHING TRUTH

### Pattern
**mú X di òtítọ́** (make X become truth) — causative plus **di**
**ṣe X di òtítọ́** (do/make X become truth) — alternative causative
**gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀** (establish truth) — fixed expression

### Examples

**Ó mú ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́**
Ó mú ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́
"He/She made that statement become true" / "He/She made that come true"
[CORE]

**Ìṣe rẹ̀ ṣe ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́**
Ìṣe rẹ̀ ṣe ọ̀rọ̀ náà di òtítọ́
"His/her action made that statement become true"
[CORE]

**Gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀**
Gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀
"Establish the truth" / "Set down the truth"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀**
Ẹ̀rí rẹ̀ gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀
"His/her evidence established the truth"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Mú...di** and **ṣe...di** are causative constructions that can mean either "make become true" (causing a false statement to become true through action) or "establish as true" (proving something true). **Gbé...kalẹ̀** 'set down' is a fixed expression specifically for establishing or proving truth. Context determines which interpretation is intended.

## 12. CONFIRMING / VERIFYING (CAUSING CERTAINTY)

### Pattern
**fẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀** (confirm, verify) — verification verb
**ṣe ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀** (do confirmation) — verbal construction
**mú X dájú** (make X certain) — causative with **dájú**

### Examples

**Fẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀ pé ó tọ́**
Fẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀ pé ó tọ́
"Confirm that it is true" / "Verify that it is correct"
[CORE]

**Mo ṣe ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀ ọ̀rọ̀ náà**
Mo ṣe ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀ ọ̀rọ̀ náà
"I confirmed that matter" / "I verified that statement"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀rí náà mú mi dájú**
Ẹ̀rí náà mú mi dájú
"That evidence made me certain"
[CORE]

**Ó mú wọn dájú pé ó tọ́**
Ó mú wọn dájú pé ó tọ́
"It made them certain that it is true"
[CORE]

### Notes
**Fẹ̀sẹ̀múlẹ̀** and **ṣe ìfẹ́sẹ̀múlẹ̀** are the standard terms for verification and confirmation. **Mú...dájú** 'make certain' is the causative construction for inducing certainty. All are very common in investigative and legal contexts.

## 13. REVEALING / EXPOSING TRUTH

### Pattern
**fi òtítọ́ hàn** (show truth) — 'reveal truth'
**sọ òtítọ́ jáde** (tell truth come.out) — serial verb 'reveal'
**mú òtítọ́ jáde** (bring truth out) — causative serial

### Examples

**Fi òtítọ́ hàn**
Fi òtítọ́ hàn
"Show the truth" / "Reveal the truth"
[CORE]

**Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn nípa ọ̀rọ̀ náà**
Ó fi òtítọ́ hàn nípa ọ̀rọ̀ náà
"He/She revealed the truth about that matter"
[CORE]

**Sọ òtítọ́ jáde**
Sọ òtítọ́ jáde
"Tell the truth out" / "Reveal the truth"
[CORE]

**Ó mú òtítọ́ jáde**
Ó mú òtítọ́ jáde
"He/She brought the truth out" / "He/She exposed the truth"
[CORE]

### Notes
All three constructions express revelation or disclosure of truth. **Fi...hàn** 'use...show' is most general. **Sọ...jáde** 'tell...come.out' emphasizes verbal disclosure. **Mú...jáde** 'bring...come.out' emphasizes active extraction or exposure. All are very common.

## 14. EXPOSING LIES / REVEALING FALSEHOOD

### Pattern
**fi irọ́ hàn** (show lie) — 'expose lie'
**mú irọ́** (catch lie) — 'catch in a lie'
**mú irọ́ jáde** (bring lie out) — 'expose lie'

### Examples

**Fi irọ́ rẹ̀ hàn**
Fi irọ́ rẹ̀ hàn
"Show his/her lie" / "Expose his/her lie"
[CORE]

**Mo mú irọ́ rẹ̀**
Mo mú irọ́ rẹ̀
"I caught his/her lie" / "I exposed his/her falsehood"
[CORE]

**Wọ́n mú irọ́ náà jáde**
Wọ́n mú irọ́ náà jáde
"They brought that lie out" / "They exposed that lie"
[CORE]

**Ẹ̀rí náà fi irọ́ rẹ̀ hàn**
Ẹ̀rí náà fi irọ́ rẹ̀ hàn
"That evidence exposed his/her lie"
[CORE]

### Notes
These constructions parallel the truth-revelation patterns but with **irọ́** 'lie'. **Mú irọ́** 'catch lie' is particularly common for detecting or exposing deception. All are frequent in investigative and accusatory contexts.

**tún àṣìṣe ṣe** (redo error) — 'correct the error'
**ṣe àtúnṣe** (do correction) — 'make correction'
**tún X ṣe** (redo X) — general correction pattern

### Examples

**Tún àṣìṣe náà ṣe**
Tún àṣìṣe náà ṣe
"Redo that error" / "Correct that mistake"
[CORE]

**Mo tún ọ̀rọ̀ mi ṣe**
Mo tún ọ̀rọ̀ mi ṣe
"I redid my statement" / "I corrected my statement"
[CORE]

**Ṣe àtúnṣe**
Ṣe àtúnṣe
"Do correction" / "Make corrections"
[CORE]

**Ó ṣe àtúnṣe ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀**
Ó ṣe àtúnṣe ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀

[CORE]

### Notes
**Tún...ṣe** 'redo' is the standard pattern for correction, literally meaning to do something again (correctly). **Ṣe àtúnṣe** 'do correction' is the nominal construction. Both are very common in editorial and corrective contexts.

## 20. STABILITY OF TRUTH (UNCHANGING TRUTH)

### Pattern
**òtítọ́ kò yí padà** (truth NEG change) — 'truth doesn't change'
**òtítọ́ wà títí** (truth exist always) — 'truth exists forever'
**òtítọ́ dúró ṣinṣin** (truth stand firm) — 'truth stands firm'

### Examples

**Òtítọ́ kò yí padà**
Òtítọ́ kò yí padà
"Truth doesn't change" / "Truth is unchanging"
[CORE]

**Òtítọ́ wà títí láéláé**
Òtítọ́ wà títí láéláé
"Truth exists forever and ever"
[CORE]

**Òtítọ́ dúró ṣinṣin**
Òtítọ́ dúró ṣinṣin
"Truth stands firm" / "Truth is stable"
[CORE]

### Notes
These expressions characterize truth as stable, unchanging, or eternal. Common in philosophical and religious discourse. **Kò yí padà** 'doesn't change' is the most explicit negation of change. **Wà títí** 'exists always' emphasizes permanence. **Dúró ṣinṣin** 'stand firm' emphasizes stability.

### Examples

**Òtítọ́ ń hàn díẹ̀díẹ̀**
Òtítọ́ ń hàn díẹ̀díẹ̀
"Truth is showing little by little" / "Truth is gradually being revealed"
[CORE]

**Òtítọ́ ń jáde**
Òtítọ́ ń jáde
"Truth is coming out" / "Truth is emerging"
[CORE]

**Òtítọ́ ń tú jáde**
Òtítọ́ ń tú jáde
"Truth is spreading out" / "Truth is being disclosed"
[CORE]

### Notes
Progressive aspect marker **ń** with verbs of emergence or revelation expresses gradual disclosure of truth. **Díẹ̀díẹ̀** 'little by little' emphasizes the gradual nature. Common in investigative and narrative contexts.

## 23. GROWING CERTAINTY

### Pattern
**ìdájú ń pọ̀ sí i** (certainty PROG increase) — increasing certainty
**mo ń dájú sí i** (1SG PROG certain toward it) — becoming more certain

### Examples

**Ìdájú mi ń pọ̀ sí i**
Ìdájú mi ń pọ̀ sí i
"My certainty is increasing" / "I am becoming more certain"
[CORE]

**Mo ń dájú sí i púpọ̀ sí i**
Mo ń dájú sí i púpọ̀ sí i
"I am becoming more and more certain about it"
[CORE]

### Notes
Progressive aspect with verbs of increase (**pọ̀** 'increase') or directional markers (**sí i** 'toward it') expresses gradual growth of certainty. Common in contexts of accumulating evidence or growing conviction.

[CORE]

**Mú...sọ òtítọ́** 'make tell truth' is the general causative. **Fi ipá** 'use force' can be added to specify coercion. Common in interrogation and legal contexts.

## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

### Highly Productive Patterns

1. **Change of state with di**: The verb **di** 'become' is extremely productive for expressing transitions in truth-related states (di òtítọ́, di dájú, di olótítọ́, di apurọ́, di abàjẹ́).

2. **Causative with mú**: The causative verb **mú** 'make/cause' is highly productive for causing belief, knowledge, certainty, and character change (mú...gbàgbọ́, mú...mọ̀, mú...dájú, mú...bàjẹ́).

3. **Inchoative with bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí**: The inchoative marker **bẹ̀rẹ̀ sí** 'begin to' is productive with all truth-domain verbs and predicates.

4. **Cessation with kò...mọ́**: The pattern of negation plus **mọ́** 'anymore' is the standard way to express cessation of states (kò tọ́ mọ́, kò gbàgbọ́ mọ́, kò dájú mọ́).

5. **Revelation with jáde**: The directional verb **jáde** 'come out' in serial constructions is very productive for expressing emergence or revelation of truth (òtítọ́ jáde, sọ...jáde, mú...jáde).

### Asymmetries

1. **Becoming true vs. becoming false**: **Di òtítọ́** 'become true' is much more common than **di irọ́** 'become false', reflecting the conceptualization that truth is revealed rather than created, while falsehood is typically present from the start.

2. **Moral corruption**: The intransitive verb **bàjẹ́** 'become corrupt' has no direct positive counterpart. Becoming righteous is expressed through **di olódodo** (become + agentive) rather than a simple intransitive verb.

3. **Gradual vs. sudden change**: Truth revelation can be marked for tempo (gradual with **díẹ̀díẹ̀**, sudden with **lójijì**), but other truth-domain changes are less commonly marked for tempo.

### Fixed Expressions

Several constructions appear as relatively fixed expressions:
- **Gbé òtítọ́ kalẹ̀** 'establish the truth'
- **Fi òtítọ́ hàn** 'reveal the truth'
- **Dúró ní òtítọ́** 'stand in truth'
- **Òtítọ́ kò yí padà** 'truth doesn't change'

### No Expressions Found

No common expressions were identified for:
- Making something false (as opposed to exposing falsehood)
- Preventing truth (as opposed to concealing it)
- Forcing someone to lie (coerced lying is conceptually possible but no fixed expression was found)

All documented patterns are marked **[CORE]** except where noted as **[MARGINAL]**.
