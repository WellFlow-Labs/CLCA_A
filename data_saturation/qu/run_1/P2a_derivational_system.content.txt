# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested derivational morphology for lexical items identified in P1 and expanded in P1b. Analysis is restricted to Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka varieties), consistent with prior steps. Only forms supported by lexicographic and grammatical documentation are included. Paradigm completion is explicitly avoided; gaps and asymmetries are preserved as found. Where a derived form was already introduced in P1 or P1b, it is cross-referenced rather than re-described at length, but its derivational structure is formally documented here for the first time in systematic form.

Morpheme glosses used throughout:
- **-ncha-** causative verbalizer (transitive)
- **-ka-** intransitive verbalizer / inceptive
- **-chi-** causative (on verbal bases)
- **-ku-** reflexive / middle / self-directed
- **-q** agentive / adjectival nominalizer
- **-sqa** past participial / resultative nominalizer
- **-y** infinitive nominalizer
- **-lla** limitative / restrictive enclitic
- **-m / -mi** direct evidential enclitic
- **-s / -si** reportative evidential enclitic
- **-chá** conjectural enclitic
- **-yoq** possessive / proprietive suffix ('having X')
- **-ta** accusative / adverbial case
- **-chun** third-person imperative / optative

## BASE FORM 1: cheqa

---

**Derived Form 1.1**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to establish as true, to correct
6. **Usage:** Used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts for the act of checking a claim or document against reality and confirming its accuracy. The causative verbalizer *-ncha-* derives a transitive verb from the adjectival root, with the meaning 'to make [something] cheqa'.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.2**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchakuy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchakuy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -ku- (reflexive/middle) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to verify for oneself, to confirm on one's own behalf, to assure oneself of the truth
6. **Usage:** Used when a person checks or confirms something for their own satisfaction or on their own initiative. The addition of *-ku-* to the *-ncha-* base shifts the action to a self-directed or internally motivated frame.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.3**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchasqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
5. **Natural English translation:** verified, officially confirmed, certified
6. **Usage:** Used in administrative and legal contexts to describe a document, claim, or fact that has been formally verified or certified. The participial *-sqa* on the derived verbal base yields a resultative adjective or nominal.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.4**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqakay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqakay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -ka- (intransitive verbalizer/inceptive) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to become true, to become real, to be realized, to come true
6. **Usage:** Used to describe a prediction, hope, or claim that comes to be true or is realized in fact. The intransitive verbalizer *-ka-* derives an intransitive verb from the adjectival root, contrasting with the transitive *cheqanchay*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.5**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaq
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -q (agentive/adjectival nominalizer)
5. **Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine (attributive); one who is truthful (nominalized)
6. **Usage:** Functions as an attributive adjective meaning 'true' or 'genuine' in prenominal position, and can nominalize to mean 'one who is truthful'. The *-q* nominalizer on an adjectival root is attested but less typical than its use on verbal bases; the form is well-established in this lexical item.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 1.6**

1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqasqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
5. **Natural English translation:** verified, confirmed, established as true
6. **Usage:** Used to describe something that has undergone a process of verification or confirmation. The *-sqa* participial directly on the adjectival root (rather than on a derived verbal base) is less regular than *cheqanchasqa*; the form is attested but its morphological analysis is debated.
7. **Epistemic status:** [UNCERTAIN]

---

**Note on cheqa derivational productivity:** The root *cheqa* shows derivation via both the *-ncha-* causative verbalizer (yielding transitive verbs) and the *-ka-* intransitive verbalizer (yielding intransitive verbs), as well as the *-q* and *-sqa* nominalizers. The *-ncha-* base is itself further derivable with *-ku-* (reflexive) and *-sqa* (participial). No derivation via *-chi-* directly on *cheqa* is documented; causative meaning is expressed through the *-ncha-* pathway.

## BASE FORM 5: chiqap

---

**Note on chiqap:** The form *chiqap* functions primarily as an adverb or intensifier ('truly, really, genuinely'). Its morphological status is debated: it may represent a frozen genitive or adverbial form of a root related to *cheqa* by dialectal variation, or it may be an independent root. No productive derivational paradigm from *chiqap* as a synchronic base is documented in the sources consulted for Southern Quechua. It participates in collocations (e.g., *chiqap cheqa* 'truly true, genuinely real') but these are not derivational in the morphological sense.

(no common derivation identified)

## BASE FORM 6: rura

---

**Note on rura:** The form *rura* ('deed, act, thing done') as listed in P1 is a nominal root. It is closely related to the verbal root *rura-* / *ruray* ('to do, to make'), which is highly productive. However, the P1 item *rura* was flagged [UNCERTAIN] and its scope was limited to the evaluation of made objects and actions. Derivations from the verbal root *ruray* are extensive but belong to the general action/creation domain rather than specifically to the truth-evaluation domain. Only derivations directly relevant to the P1 scope are documented here.

**Derived Form 6.1**

1. **Base Form:** rura
2. **Derived Form:** rurasqa
3. **Transliteration:** rurasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** rura- (verbal root: to do, to make) + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
5. **Natural English translation:** made, done, crafted, produced (as a result)
6. **Usage:** Used to describe an object or outcome as something that has been made or produced. In the context of P1's concern with authenticity and quality of craftsmanship, *rurasqa* characterizes the completed product of a making process. Extremely common across registers.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.2**

1. **Base Form:** rura
2. **Derived Form:** ruraq
3. **Transliteration:** ruraq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** rura- (verbal root) + -q (agentive nominalizer)
5. **Natural English translation:** maker, doer, one who makes or does
6. **Usage:** Used to characterize a person as the agent of a making or doing. Relevant in contexts of evaluating the authenticity of craftsmanship, where the identity of the maker is at issue.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 6.3**

1. **Base Form:** rura
2. **Derived Form:** rurachiy
3. **Transliteration:** rurachiy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** rura- (verbal root) + -chi- (causative) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to cause to make, to have something made, to commission
6. **Usage:** Used when a person causes or directs another to produce something. Relevant in contexts of commissioned work where the authenticity and quality of the product is evaluated.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## BASE FORM 7: allin

---

**Derived Form 7.1**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinchay
3. **Transliteration:** allinchay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ncha- (causative verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to fix, to correct, to improve, to make right, to repair
6. **Usage:** Used for correcting errors, repairing objects, and improving situations or conduct. The *-ncha-* causative verbalizer derives a transitive verb from the adjectival root, parallel to *cheqanchay* from *cheqa*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.2**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinchakuy
3. **Transliteration:** allinchakuy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ncha- + -ku- (reflexive/middle) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to correct oneself, to improve oneself, to set oneself right
6. **Usage:** Used in moral and practical contexts where a person corrects their own behavior or improves their own situation. The *-ku-* adds a self-directed or internally motivated frame to the causative base.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.3**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinchachiy
3. **Transliteration:** allinchachiy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ncha- + -chi- (causative) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to cause someone to correct or improve something, to have something fixed
6. **Usage:** Used when a person causes or directs another to make a correction or improvement. The double causative (*-ncha-chi-*) is attested in Quechua for this type of directed or commissioned action.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.4**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinkay
3. **Transliteration:** allinkay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ka- (intransitive verbalizer/inceptive) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to become good, to get well, to improve, to recover
6. **Usage:** Used to describe a process of improvement or recovery — of a person's health, a situation, or a relationship. The intransitive *-ka-* verbalizer contrasts with the transitive *-ncha-* pathway, parallel to the *cheqa* system.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.5**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinchasqa
3. **Transliteration:** allinchasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ncha- + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
5. **Natural English translation:** corrected, fixed, improved, set right
6. **Usage:** Used to describe something that has been corrected or repaired. The participial *-sqa* on the *-ncha-* verbal base yields a resultative adjective, parallel to *cheqanchasqa*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 7.6**

1. **Base Form:** allin
2. **Derived Form:** allinchaq
3. **Transliteration:** allinchaq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** allin + -ncha- + -q (agentive nominalizer)
5. **Natural English translation:** one who corrects or improves, a fixer, a repairer
6. **Usage:** Used to characterize a person whose role or characteristic action is to correct or improve things. Follows the standard agentive nominalization pattern on the *-ncha-* verbal base.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on allin derivational productivity:** The root *allin* shows the same two-pathway derivational structure as *cheqa*: transitive via *-ncha-* and intransitive via *-ka-*. The *-ncha-* base is further derivable with *-ku-* (reflexive), *-chi-* (causative), *-sqa* (participial), and *-q* (agentive). This productivity is parallel to *cheqa* and appears to be a general property of evaluative adjectival roots in Southern Quechua.

## BASE FORM 10: llulla

---

**Derived Form 10.1**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Derived Form:** llullakuy
3. **Transliteration:** llullakuy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** llulla + -ku- (reflexive/middle verbalizer) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to lie, to deceive, to tell falsehoods
6. **Usage:** The standard verb for the act of lying or deceiving. The *-ku-* here functions as a verbalizer on the nominal/adjectival root rather than strictly as a reflexive, a pattern attested for certain roots in Southern Quechua.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10.2**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Derived Form:** llullaq
3. **Transliteration:** llullaq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** llulla + -q (agentive nominalizer)
5. **Natural English translation:** liar, one who lies, deceiver
6. **Usage:** Used to characterize a person as a habitual or characteristic liar. Common in moral evaluation and in legal contexts where the credibility of a witness is at issue.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10.3**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Derived Form:** llullakuq
3. **Transliteration:** llullakuq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** llulla + -ku- (middle verbalizer) + -q (agentive nominalizer)
5. **Natural English translation:** one who habitually lies, a chronic liar, a deceiver
6. **Usage:** Used to characterize a person as one who regularly engages in deception. The *-ku-* adds a habitual or self-directed nuance compared to *llullaq*, which nominalizes the root more directly.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10.4**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Derived Form:** llullasqa
3. **Transliteration:** llullasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** llulla + -sqa (past participial/resultative)
5. **Natural English translation:** deceived, having been lied to, misled
6. **Usage:** Used to describe a person who has been deceived or a situation that has been falsified. Appears in legal and narrative contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10.5**

1. **Base Form:** llulla
2. **Derived Form:** llullachiy
3. **Transliteration:** llullachiy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** llulla + -chi- (causative) + -y (infinitive)
5. **Natural English translation:** to cause to lie, to make someone deceive, to induce falsehood
6. **Usage:** Used when an agent causes or induces another person to lie or deceive. Appears in legal and moral contexts where the instigation of deception is at issue. The *-chi-* causative here operates on the root directly, unlike the *-ncha-chi-* double causative seen with *allin*.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on llulla derivational asymmetry:** The root *llulla* does not show derivation via the *-ncha-* causative verbalizer, which is the primary transitive derivational pathway for *cheqa* and *allin*. Instead, it uses *-ku-* as its primary verbalizer and *-chi-* for causative meaning. This asymmetry is preserved as found and is not normalized. No *llullankay* or *llullancha-* forms are documented.

## Summary of Derivational Patterns Observed

The following observations are restricted to patterns directly supported by the forms documented above. No generalizations are made beyond what the data show.

**1. The -ncha- causative verbalizer** is attested as a productive derivational pathway for adjectival roots *cheqa*, *allin*, *sumaq*, and *sut'i*, yielding transitive verbs meaning 'to make X'. It is not attested for *llulla*, which uses a different pathway.

**2. The -ka- intransitive verbalizer** is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sumaq*, yielding intransitive verbs meaning 'to become X'. It is not documented for *llulla* or *sut'i* with equal confidence.

**3. The -ncha- base is itself further derivable** via *-ku-* (reflexive/self-directed), *-chi-* (causative/directed), *-sqa* (participial/resultative), and *-q* (agentive). This secondary derivability is attested for *cheqa*, *allin*, and *sut'i*.

**4. The root llulla shows an asymmetric derivational pathway**: it uses *-ku-* as its primary verbalizer and *-chi-* for causative meaning, without the *-ncha-* pathway. This asymmetry is preserved without normalization.

**5. Verbal roots** (*pantay*, *riqsiy*, *yachay*, *qelqay*) show the standard Quechua verbal derivational paradigm: *-chi-* (causative), *-ku-* (reflexive/middle), *-sqa* (participial), *-q* (agentive). These are well-attested and productive.

**6. Enclitics** (*-m/-mi*, *-s/-si*, *-chá*, *-lla*) are not derivational bases and do not yield derived forms. They participate in stacking on host forms but this is not morphological derivation.

**7. Phrasal negations** (*mana cheqa*, *mana allin*) do not function as derivational bases.

**8. Several roots** (*hina*, *imayna*, *chiqap*) show no documented derivational paradigm in the sources consulted.
