# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Tagalog (tl)

## STEP 1 — IDENTIFY CENTRAL ROOTS

### Root 1: **totoo**
- **Form:** totoo
- **P1 participation:** 5 items (totoo, katotohanan, walang-katotohanan, totóo nga, di-totoo)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes factual reality, actual existence, or correspondence to reality. Primary root for truth as factuality.

### Root 2: **tapat**
- **Form:** tapat
- **P1 participation:** 2 items (tapat, matapat)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes honesty, sincerity, and faithfulness in speech and conduct. Primary root for truthfulness as moral quality.

### Root 3: **tama**
- **Form:** tama
- **P1 participation:** 1 item (tama)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Denotes correctness, rightness, or accuracy in judgments, statements, and actions. Primary root for evaluative correctness.

## STEP 2 — EXPAND EACH ROOT

### ROOT 1: **totoo** EXPANSION

1. **katotohanan**
   - Literal breakdown: ka- + totoo + -an (nominalizer)
   - Translation: truth, reality, fact
   - Context: Abstract noun for truth as concept or body of facts. Used in formal discourse, philosophical discussion, and when seeking or revealing truth.
   - [CORE]

2. **makatotohanan**
   - Literal breakdown: maka- + totoo + -an (adjective-forming)
   - Translation: realistic, true-to-life, believable
   - Context: Describes narratives, depictions, or representations that accurately reflect reality. Common in literary and artistic criticism ("makatotohanang kuwento" = "realistic story").
   - [CORE]

3. **pagkatotoo**
   - Literal breakdown: pagka- + totoo (state/quality nominalizer)
   - Translation: truthfulness, reality, state of being true
   - Context: Refers to the quality or state of being true or real. Used in abstract discussions about the nature of truth or reality.
   - [CORE]

4. **totoo nga**
   - Literal breakdown: totoo + nga (emphatic particle)
   - Translation: truly, indeed, really
   - Context: Emphatic confirmation of truth. Very common in conversation to strongly affirm statements.
   - [CORE]

5. **di-totoo**
   - Literal breakdown: di- (not) + totoo
   - Translation: untrue, false, not real
   - Context: Simple negation of truth or reality. Common in everyday denial of claims or statements.
   - [CORE]

6. **hindi totoo**
   - Literal breakdown: hindi (not) + totoo
   - Translation: not true, false, unreal
   - Context: Full negation form, more emphatic than "di-totoo." Standard way to deny truth of statements.
   - [CORE]

7. **walang-katotohanan**
   - Literal breakdown: walang (without) + katotohanan (truth)
   - Translation: without truth, baseless, false
   - Context: Describes claims or accusations lacking factual basis. Used to dismiss unfounded statements.
   - [CORE]

8. **totohanan**
   - Literal breakdown: totoo + -han (verbal suffix indicating reciprocal/mutual action)
   - Translation: to be honest with each other, to speak truthfully
   - Context: Verb form indicating mutual honesty or serious truthful exchange. Used when calling for frank discussion ("Totohanan na tayo" = "Let's be honest with each other").
   - [CORE]

9. **magkatotoo**
   - Literal breakdown: mag- + ka- + totoo (verb: to become true)
   - Translation: to come true, to be realized, to become reality
   - Context: Describes predictions, dreams, or wishes becoming reality. Common in discussing fulfillment of expectations ("Nagkatotoo ang pangarap ko" = "My dream came true").
   - [CORE]

10. **katotohanang**
    - Literal breakdown: katotohanan + -ng (linker)
    - Translation: truth that, the truth which
    - Context: Linker form used before modifying clauses. Common in formal statements identifying specific truths.
    - [CORE]

11. **sa totoo lang**
    - Literal breakdown: sa (in/at) + totoo + lang (only/just)
    - Translation: to be honest, truthfully, actually
    - Context: Fixed conversational phrase introducing frank or honest statements. Extremely common in everyday speech.
    - [CORE]

12. **sa katotohanan**
    - Literal breakdown: sa (in/at) + katotohanan
    - Translation: in truth, in reality, actually
    - Context: Formal or emphatic way to introduce true state of affairs. Used when contrasting appearance with reality.
    - [CORE]

13. **totoo ba**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + ba (question particle)
    - Translation: is it true?, really?
    - Context: Standard question form asking for confirmation of truth. Very common in conversation.
    - [CORE]

14. **totoo naman**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + naman (concessive particle)
    - Translation: it's true though, actually it is true
    - Context: Concessive acknowledgment of truth, often before introducing contrasting point. Common in argumentative discourse.
    - [CORE]

15. **pagtotoo**
    - Literal breakdown: pag- + totoo (nominalized action)
    - Translation: act of being truthful, truthfulness
    - Context: Refers to the act or practice of being truthful. Less common than "katotohanan" but used in moral discourse.
    - [MARGINAL]

16. **totoo rin**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + rin (also/too)
    - Translation: also true, true as well
    - Context: Acknowledges additional truth alongside previously stated truth. Common in adding confirmatory information.
    - [CORE]

17. **kung totoo**
    - Literal breakdown: kung (if) + totoo
    - Translation: if true, if it's real
    - Context: Conditional construction expressing hypothetical truth. Common in expressing doubt or considering possibilities.
    - [CORE]

18. **totoo pa**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + pa (still/even)
    - Translation: still true, even true, moreover
    - Context: Emphasizes continued or additional truth. Used to add emphasis or introduce further confirmation.
    - [CORE]

19. **napakatotoo**
    - Literal breakdown: napaka- (very/extremely) + totoo
    - Translation: very true, extremely real
    - Context: Intensified form expressing high degree of truth or reality. Common in emphatic statements.
    - [CORE]

20. **pakatotoo**
    - Literal breakdown: paka- (as much as possible) + totoo
    - Translation: as true as possible, as real as can be
    - Context: Expresses maximum degree of truth or reality. Used when emphasizing authenticity or genuineness.
    - [MARGINAL]

21. **totoo kaya**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + kaya (wonder/doubt particle)
    - Translation: I wonder if it's true, could it be true?
    - Context: Expresses speculation or doubt about truth. Common in expressing uncertainty while considering possibilities.
    - [CORE]

22. **magtotoo**
    - Literal breakdown: mag- + totoo (future verb form)
    - Translation: will become true, will be realized
    - Context: Future tense of "magkatotoo." Used in predictions about things coming true.
    - [CORE]

23. **ikatotoo**
    - Literal breakdown: ika- + totoo (causative)
    - Translation: to cause to be true, to make real
    - Context: Causative form indicating making something true or real. Less common, used in formal or literary contexts.
    - [MARGINAL]

24. **totoo lamang**
    - Literal breakdown: totoo + lamang (only/merely)
    - Translation: only true, merely true, just the truth
    - Context: Emphasizes that something is simply or merely true without exaggeration. Formal or literary usage.
    - [MARGINAL]

25. **paninindigan ng katotohanan**
    - Literal breakdown: paninindigan (standing firm) + ng (of) + katotohanan
    - Translation: standing for truth, upholding truth
    - Context: Fixed expression about defending or maintaining truth. Used in moral and political discourse.
    - [CORE]

### ROOT 2: **tapat** EXPANSION

1. **matapat**
   - Literal breakdown: ma- + tapat (adjective-forming)
   - Translation: honest, sincere, truthful
   - Context: Adjectival form describing honest persons or sincere conduct. Common in character evaluation.
   - [CORE]

2. **katapatan**
   - Literal breakdown: ka- + tapat + -an (nominalizer)
   - Translation: honesty, sincerity, loyalty, faithfulness
   - Context: Abstract noun for the quality of being honest or faithful. Used in moral discourse and character assessment.
   - [CORE]

3. **pagkatapat**
   - Literal breakdown: pagka- + tapat (state nominalizer)
   - Translation: honesty, state of being honest/faithful
   - Context: Refers to the state or quality of honesty. Similar to "katapatan" but emphasizes the condition or state.
   - [CORE]

4. **magtapat**
   - Literal breakdown: mag- + tapat (verb)
   - Translation: to confess, to be honest, to tell the truth

5. **nagtapat**

6. **magtapatan**

7. **pagtapat**

8. **tapatang**

11. **walang-katapatan**

    - [CORE]

12. **tapat na tapat**

13. **katapatan sa**

14. **magpakatapat**

15. **tapatang loob**

### ROOT 3: **tama** EXPANSION

1. **katamaan**
   - Literal breakdown: ka- + tama + -an (nominalizer)
   - Translation: correctness, rightness, accuracy
   - Context: Abstract noun for the quality of being correct or right. Used in evaluative and instructional contexts.
   - [CORE]

2. **tamaan**
   - Literal breakdown: tama + -an (locative/recipient verb)
   - Translation: to be hit, to be struck, to be affected
   - Context: Verb meaning to be hit or struck by something. Also used metaphorically for being affected by circumstances. **[Note: Semantic shift from correctness to physical impact]**
   - [CORE]

3. **tumama**
   - Literal breakdown: -um- + tama (actor-focus verb)
   - Translation: to hit, to strike, to be correct
   - Context: Verb with dual meaning: (1) to hit a target physically, (2) to guess or answer correctly. Common in both physical and evaluative contexts.
   - [CORE]

4. **pagtama**
   - Literal breakdown: pag- + tama (nominalized action)
   - Translation: act of being correct, hitting the mark
   - Context: Refers to the act of being correct or hitting a target. Used in both literal and figurative senses.
   - [CORE]

5. **tama na**
   - Literal breakdown: tama + na (already/now)
   - Translation: that's enough, stop now, enough already
   - Context: Fixed expression signaling sufficiency or calling for cessation. Very common in everyday speech. **[Note: Semantic shift from correctness to sufficiency]**
   - [CORE]

6. **tama ka**
   - Literal breakdown: tama + ka (you)
   - Translation: you're right, you're correct
   - Context: Standard way to affirm someone's correctness. Extremely common in conversation and agreement.
   - [CORE]

7. **hindi tama**
   - Literal breakdown: hindi (not) + tama

## STEP 3 — PERIPHERAL EXTENSIONS

### ROOT 1: **totoo** — Peripheral Extensions

**Flagged expressions:**

1. **magkatotoo** (to come true, to be realized)
   - The root shifts from describing factual truth to describing fulfillment or realization of predictions, dreams, or wishes. The focus moves from correspondence to reality to becoming reality.

2. **makatotohanan** (realistic, true-to-life)
   - The root extends into aesthetic evaluation, describing artistic or narrative verisimilitude rather than factual truth. Used for evaluating representations and depictions.

3. **pakatotoo** (as true/real as possible)
   - The root functions as degree or intensity marker, expressing maximum extent rather than binary truth value.

### ROOT 2: **tapat** — Peripheral Extensions

**Flagged expressions:**

1. **tapat sa tungkulin** (faithful to duty)
   - The root extends from truthfulness in speech to faithfulness in conduct and duty fulfillment. Shifts from epistemic honesty to moral loyalty and dedication.

2. **tapatang loob** (honest heart, sincere intentions)
   - The root extends into evaluation of internal states and intentions rather than external truthful speech.

3. **tapat sa salita** (true to one's word)
   - The root extends from honesty to reliability and promise-keeping, emphasizing consistency between word and deed rather than truth-telling per se.

### ROOT 3: **tama** — Peripheral Extensions

**Flagged expressions:**

1. **tamaan/tumama** (to be hit, to strike)
   - Major semantic shift from correctness to physical impact or striking. The root functions in completely different domain of physical contact and hitting targets.

2. **tama na** (that's enough, stop now)
   - The root shifts from correctness to sufficiency or adequacy. Functions as discourse marker for cessation rather than evaluation of truth or correctness.

3. **tamang panahon** (right time, proper season)
   - The root extends to temporal appropriateness and opportune timing, moving beyond correctness of statements to appropriateness of timing.

4. **tama ang daan** (on the right path)
   - The root extends to spatial and metaphorical direction, evaluating paths and life choices rather than truth or correctness of statements.

5. **itama** (to correct, to fix)
   - The root functions causatively to describe making corrections or repairs, extending beyond evaluation to action of rectification.

---

**Note on productivity:** All three roots are highly productive. The **tama** root shows particularly extensive use beyond the truth domain, especially in physical (hitting/striking) and sufficiency contexts. Additional compounds exist beyond those listed, particularly in specialized technical and administrative registers.
