# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
**Language: Indonesian (id)**

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: benar**
**Form:** benar
**Script:** Latin (no separate native script)
**Transliteration:** N/A

**P1 participation:** 3 items directly (*benar* item 1, *kebenaran* item 24, *sebenarnya* item 25); additionally the root underlies *membenarkan* mentioned in item 1's derivational note.

**Core meaning contribution:** The primary root for correctness, rightness, and truth as correspondence between a claim or state and reality. Serves as the base for the most productive derivational family in the truth-evaluation domain in Indonesian.

---

**Root 2: sah / sahih**
**Form:** sah; sahih
**Script:** Latin
**Transliteration:** N/A (both from Arabic *ṣaḥḥa* / *ṣaḥīḥ*)

**P1 participation:** 4 items (*sah* item 10, *sahih* item 9, *absah* item 11, *mengesahkan* mentioned in derivational notes of items 9 and 10).

**Core meaning contribution:** Validity, legitimacy, and formal correctness — the sense that something meets the required conditions to be recognized as binding, sound, or authoritative. Operates primarily in legal, religious, and institutional contexts.

---

**Root 3: asli**
**Form:** asli
**Script:** Latin
**Transliteration:** N/A

**P1 participation:** 2 items directly (*asli* item 3, *otentik* item 19 as a near-synonym in the same semantic space); the root also contrasts structurally with *palsu* (item 4) and *tiruan* (item 18), anchoring the authenticity/genuineness pole of the domain.

**Core meaning contribution:** Originality, genuineness, and non-derivedness — the sense that something is the source rather than a copy, and that it has not been altered, substituted, or fabricated. Highly productive in legal, commercial, cultural, and identity contexts.

---

*Note on selection rationale:* The root *benar* was selected over *jujur* or *palsu* because it is demonstrably the most derivationally productive root in the domain, generating the largest number of distinct lexical items in regular use. *Sah/sahih* was selected because it anchors a distinct sub-domain (institutional validity) with its own productive family. *Asli* was selected because it anchors the authenticity/genuineness sub-domain and generates a substantial set of compounds and collocations not yet listed in P1.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

### ROOT 1: benar

---

**1.**
**Form:** benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** base form
**Natural English translation:** correct, right, true
**Context of use:** Used as a predicate or modifier to affirm that a statement, fact, or judgment corresponds to reality. Appears across all registers from casual conversation to formal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** kebenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *ke-benar-an* (nominalizer *ke-...-an* + *benar*)
**Natural English translation:** truth, correctness, rightness
**Context of use:** Refers to truth as an abstract quality or to the correctness of a specific claim. Used in moral, legal, philosophical, and everyday contexts. Already listed in P1 (item 24); included here as the central nominal of the family.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** sebenarnya
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *se-benar-nya* (*se-* 'in the manner of' + *benar* + *-nya* 3sg/definiteness clitic)
**Natural English translation:** actually, in truth, in fact, really
**Context of use:** Discourse marker used to introduce a correction, clarification, or disclosure of what is actually the case. Extremely frequent in both spoken and written Indonesian. Already listed in P1 (item 25).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** membenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *me(m)-benar-kan* (active transitive prefix *me(m)-* + *benar* + causative suffix *-kan*)
**Natural English translation:** to confirm, to justify, to validate, to correct (someone)
**Context of use:** Used to mean both "to confirm that something is true" and "to justify or excuse an action." Appears in journalistic, legal, and everyday contexts. The dual sense (epistemic confirmation vs. moral justification) is well established.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** dibenarkan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *di-benar-kan* (passive prefix *di-* + *benar* + *-kan*)
**Natural English translation:** to be confirmed, to be justified, to be permitted
**Context of use:** Passive form of *membenarkan*. Frequently used in legal and regulatory language: *tidak dibenarkan* ("not permitted/not justified") is a standard formula in official documents and rules.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** pembenaran
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *pe(m)-benar-an* (agentive/process nominalizer *pe(m)-...-an* + *benar*)
**Natural English translation:** justification, confirmation, validation, rationalization
**Context of use:** Used in moral, psychological, and legal discourse to refer to the act or process of justifying something. *Pembenaran diri* (self-justification) is a common collocation in psychological and moral commentary.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** pembenaran diri
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** *pe(m)-benar-an* + *diri* (self)
**Natural English translation:** self-justification, rationalization
**Context of use:** Used in psychological, moral, and journalistic discourse to describe the tendency to justify one's own actions or beliefs, often without adequate grounds. A stable collocation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**

**Transliteration:** N/A

**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely, absolutely
**Context of use:** Intensified form used to emphasize the reality or sincerity of a state or action. Extremely common in spoken and written Indonesian across all registers. *Dia benar-benar tidak tahu* ("He really didn't know").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the three roots identified above but appear in contexts where the root's contribution shifts from its core truth-domain function. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From root benar:**

- *benar-benar* used as a pure degree intensifier in contexts with no truth-evaluative content: *dia benar-benar cantik* ("she is truly beautiful") — the root contributes degree/intensity rather than epistemic evaluation.

- *membenarkan* in the sense of "to permit" or "to allow": *peraturan ini tidak membenarkan hal tersebut* ("this regulation does not permit that") — the root appears in a deontic/normative context rather than an epistemic one.

- *jalan yang benar* in religious contexts where "correct path" refers to moral or spiritual rectitude rather than factual accuracy.

- *niat yang benar* — the root evaluates inner intention rather than the truth-value of a proposition.

- *pembenaran* in the psychological sense of "rationalization" — the root appears in a context of motivated reasoning rather than straightforward truth-assessment.

- *kebenaran* in the expression *kebenaran sejati* (true/ultimate truth) in mystical and Sufi discourse, where it refers to a metaphysical or spiritual reality rather than propositional correctness.

---

**From root sah/sahih:**

- *sah* in *pernikahan yang sah* — the root evaluates the formal/ritual completeness of an act rather than the truth of a proposition.

- *anak sah* — the root functions as a marker of social and legal status (legitimacy of birth) rather than epistemic validity.

- *sah dan mengikat* — the root appears in a context of contractual obligation and binding force rather than truth-evaluation.

- *sahih* applied to a chain of transmission (*sanad*) in hadith science — the root evaluates the reliability of a transmission chain, which involves historical and biographical judgment rather than direct propositional truth.

- *mengesahkan* in the sense of "to ratify a law" — the root appears in a context of legislative procedure and political authority rather than truth-assessment.

---

**From root asli:**

- *asli* in *warna asli* and *rasa asli* — the root describes unaltered physical properties (color, flavor) rather than the truth-value of a claim.

- *asli* in *bahasa asli* and *tanah asli* — the root marks origin and belonging in identity and territorial contexts rather than authenticity of a claim.

- *asli* in *rasa asli* used figuratively for "authentic feeling" — the root appears in an affective/experiential context.

- *penduduk asli* and *pribumi* — the root participates in ethnic and political identity categorization, where "original" marks ancestral belonging rather than epistemic genuineness.

- *budaya asli* — the root evaluates cultural practices as original or indigenous, a judgment that involves historical and political criteria beyond simple authenticity of an object.

- *asli Indonesia* in commercial branding — the root functions as a marker of national origin and commercial provenance rather than a direct truth-evaluation.
