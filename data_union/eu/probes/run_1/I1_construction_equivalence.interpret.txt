## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION absent; BECOME present

No construction in P4a or P4b corresponds to the REVELATION operator (the framing of a truth as coming to light or being uncovered as a distinct event type). The BECOME operator is robustly attested via the *-tu* inchoative (*zintzotu*, *okertu*) and the periphrastic *bihurtu* alternate. There is no evidence that BECOME substitutes for REVELATION or vice versa; rather, REVELATION appears to be simply absent from the attested construction inventory. No circumlocution for REVELATION is noted in P4a or P4b.

### 2.2 DISCONFIRMATION substituted by NEGATION

No dedicated DISCONFIRMATION construction (a construction specifically encoding the act of showing something to be false, as distinct from asserting that it is not true) is identified in P4a or P4b. The function of DISCONFIRMATION is covered by the NEGATION construction (*ez da egia*, "it is not true"), which is analytic and preverbal. The data do not attest a dedicated verb or nominal meaning "to disprove" or "to disconfirm" as a lexicalized item in the truth domain. This constitutes a lexical gap: the morphosyntactic resources for negation are present, but no dedicated lexical item or construction is identified for the specific act of disconfirmation.

### 2.3 CAUSATION (factual) and CAUSATION (character) share constructions

P4a identifies a single morphological causative mechanism (*-arazi* productive; *-tu* transitive lexicalized) that covers both factual causation (*gezurra sinetsarazi*, "to make someone believe a lie") and character causation (*zintzotu* transitive, "to make someone honest"). No construction is exclusive to character causation. The *-tu* transitive form is the shared carrier for both readings, disambiguated by argument structure and context as noted in P4a §4 and §5. This is not a gap but a case of constructional overlap.

### 2.4 VERIFICATION covered by two distinct constructions

VERIFICATION is served by two constructions that differ in register and in the nature of the verification act. The lexicalized transitive *egiaztatu* ("to verify, to confirm") covers procedural or epistemic verification and is common in administrative, legal, and journalistic registers. The translative construction *egiazkotzat jo* ("to consider as true, to take as genuine") covers institutional or judgmental attribution of verified status and is concentrated in legal and formal written registers. These two constructions are structurally distinct: *egiaztatu* is a single verb form; *egiazkotzat jo* is a multi-word expression in which *-tzat* marks the translative case and *jo* functions as a light verb carrying the evaluative meaning.

### 2.5 Asymmetry between truth-pole and falsity-pole agentive nominals

P4a §7 notes that *gezurti* ("liar") is a fully lexicalized agentive nominal with the *-ti* characterizing suffix, while no parallel *-tzaile* or *-ti* form meaning "truth-teller" is broadly attested. The truth-pole agentive is expressed periphrastically (*egia esaten duena*, "one who tells the truth"). This constitutes an asymmetric lexical gap: the falsity-pole agentive is lexicalized; the truth-pole agentive is not.

### 2.6 Asymmetry in pejorative nominalization

The *-keria* suffix (P4a §10) is attested only for negative or morally failing practices (*gezurkeria*, *itxurakeria*, *faltsukeria*, *okerkeria*). No *-keria* form encoding a positive habitual truth-related practice is identified. This is consistent with the inherently pejorative semantics of the suffix as described in P4a, and constitutes a conceptual asymmetry rather than a gap: the suffix is not available for positive-pole encoding by its own semantic constraints.

### 2.7 Negation is analytic, not morphological

The NEGATION construction in Basque as attested in P4a §2 is analytic: the particle *ez* precedes the finite verb and is described as obligatory and not subject to variation in this function. There is no morphological negation (no negative prefix or suffix on the predicate adjective or nominal). The reduced form *ziurra ez* is noted as elliptical and restricted to informal contexts where the copula is recoverable, and does not represent a morphological negation pattern.

### 2.8 NOMINALIZATION is the most internally differentiated operator

The NOMINALIZATION operator is served by five morphologically distinct suffixes: *-tasun* (abstract quality), *-tzaile* (agentive actor), *-ti* (characterizing/habitual agent), *-keria* (pejorative habitual practice), *-pen* (result nominal), and *-tze* (process nominal). Each suffix occupies a distinct semantic and functional niche. No single suffix covers the full range of nominalization functions; the operator is distributed across a paradigm of suffixes.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

- **REVELATION**: No construction encoding the coming-to-light or uncovering of a truth as a distinct event type is attested in P4a or P4b. No circumlocution for this function is noted in the data. This is a structural gap in the sense that no construction — morphological, periphrastic, or lexical — is identified for this operator.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

- **DISCONFIRMATION**: No dedicated verb, nominal, or construction meaning "to disprove" or "to disconfirm" is identified. The function is covered by the NEGATION construction (*ez da egia*). The morphosyntactic resources exist; the dedicated lexical item does not.
- **Truth-pole agentive nominal**: No lexicalized *-tzaile* or *-ti* form meaning "truth-teller" is attested. The concept is expressed periphrastically (*egia esaten duena*). This is a lexical gap on the positive pole of the agentive nominalization paradigm.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

- **Positive-pole *-keria* nominalization**: The *-keria* suffix is semantically constrained to pejorative and morally failing practices. No positive-pole habitual practice nominal using this suffix is possible given the suffix's inherent semantics as described in P4a. This is not a gap in the language's expressive capacity but a conceptual constraint internal to the suffix.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

- **Agentive nominalization (truth vs. falsity pole)**: *Gezurti* ("liar") is lexicalized; the truth-pole equivalent is not. The falsity pole is lexically filled; the truth pole has only a periphrastic substitute. This is an asymmetric lexical gap favoring the falsity pole.
- **Pejorative nominalization (positive vs. negative practice)**: *-keria* forms are attested only for negative practices (*gezurkeria*, *itxurakeria*). No positive-practice *-keria* form is attested or expected given the suffix semantics. This is an asymmetric conceptual constraint favoring the negative pole.
- **Word-order variation in NEGATION**: The postposed-predicate variant (*ziurra ez da*) is rated [MARGINAL] while the canonical order (*ez da ziurra*) is [CORE]. This is an asymmetry in frequency and naturalness within the NEGATION construction, not a gap.
