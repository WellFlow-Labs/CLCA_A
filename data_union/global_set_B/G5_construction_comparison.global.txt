# G5 — Cross-Language Construction Comparison

---

## 1. Identification of Main Recurring Constructional Functions

The following constructional functions are identified from the F4 data across the eight languages. Each function is defined by what it does in discourse, not by a theoretical label. The identification proceeds from the data upward.

**Function A: Attributing a truth-domain property to a subject**
A speaker predicates that a subject (a statement, object, person, document, or situation) has a truth-domain property (true, false, genuine, correct, reliable, etc.). This is the most basic and consistently attested function across all eight languages.

**Function B: Negating a truth-domain attribution**
A speaker denies that a subject has a truth-domain property, or asserts the opposite property. This is the negative counterpart of Function A and is attested in all eight languages.

**Function C: Modifying a noun with a truth-domain evaluative expression**
A truth-domain adjective, participle, or nominal expression modifies a head noun within a noun phrase, yielding a modified nominal (e.g., "a genuine document," "false testimony," "a reliable source").

**Function D: Describing a change into or out of a truth-domain state**
A speaker describes a subject as coming to be in a truth-domain state (becoming true, turning out to be genuine, being revealed as false) or ceasing to be in one. This is the inchoative or change-of-state function.

**Function E: Causing a truth-domain state to obtain**
A speaker describes an agent as bringing about a truth-domain state in an object or person — verifying, confirming, authenticating, correcting, falsifying, or deceiving. This is the causative function.

**Function F: Deriving an abstract nominal naming a truth-domain property**
A speaker uses a nominal form that names the abstract property itself (truthfulness, accuracy, reliability, legitimacy) rather than predicating it of a specific subject. This is the abstract nominalization function.

**Function G: Naming an agent characterized by a truth-domain property**
A speaker uses a nominal form that names a person characterized by a truth-domain property (a liar, a deceiver, a forger, a verifier). This is the agentive nominalization function.

**Function H: Framing a statement as frank, corrective, or sincere**
A speaker uses a fixed or semi-fixed expression at the beginning or margin of an utterance to signal that what follows is their genuine assessment of the actual state of affairs, often in contrast to a prior claim or expectation. This is the discourse-framing function.

**Function I: Embedding a truth-domain evaluation in a subordinate clause**
A speaker embeds a truth-domain predication as the complement of a verb of knowing, believing, proving, or saying. This is the propositional-complement function.

**Function J: Expressing obligation or prohibition regarding truth-domain conduct**
A speaker uses a construction to enjoin truthful speech, prohibit lying, or assert that verification is required. This is the deontic function.

**Function K: Marking the degree or intensity of a truth-domain predication**
A speaker uses a degree modifier, intensifier, or emphatic element to assert that something is absolutely, completely, or only partially true, genuine, or correct. This is the degree-modification function.

**Function L: Marking the source or certainty of a truth-domain claim**
A speaker marks whether a truth-domain claim is based on direct knowledge, reported information, inference, or conjecture. This is the evidential or epistemic-source function.

**Function M: Expressing resemblance to truth or genuineness without asserting it**
A speaker describes something as appearing to be true or genuine, or as resembling the genuine article, without asserting that it actually is. This is the similative or verisimilitude function.

| **C. Modifying a noun with a truth-domain expression (continued)** | Participial or resultative modifier: a past participle or resultative form of a truth-domain verb modifies a head noun. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *经过核实的信息* (verified information); he *meʾummat* (verified, Pu'al participle); sa *satyāpita* (verified, past passive participle); el *τεκμηριωμένος* (documented, evidenced); en *verified*, *authenticated*, *forged*; tl *napatunayan* (proven); sw *iliyothibitishwa* (that which has been confirmed); qu *cheqanchasqa* (verified, participial). **Attested in all 8 languages.** The participial modifier is particularly associated with legal and administrative registers across all languages. |
| **D. Describing a change into or out of a truth-domain state** | Periphrastic change-of-state construction: a change-of-state verb (*become*, *turn out to be*, *prove*, *come to light*) combines with a truth-domain predicate. | zh, he, el, en, tl, sw | zh *变成真的*, *弄假成真*; he *hafakh le-ʾemet* (became true), *hitgalaʾh ke-ʾotenti* (was revealed as authentic); el *αποδείχτηκε αληθής* (proved to be true), *βγήκε αληθινό* (came out true); en *proved true*, *turned out to be false*, *came true*; tl *naging totoo* (*naging* + adjective); sw *ulidhihirika* (became evident, intransitive). **Widely attested** (6 of 8 languages). In zh, the periphrastic *变成* is the general strategy; lexicalized four-character expressions (*弄假成真*, *假戏真做*) encode specific inchoative meanings. In he, *hafakh le-* is the general periphrastic inchoative; *hitgalaʾh ke-* adds a disclosure component. In el, *αποδείχτηκε* is the primary and most natural inchoative for truth-value transitions; *γίνομαι* + adjective is grammatical but noted as less natural for this domain specifically. In en, *prove* + adjective is the most formal; *turn out to be* is register-neutral; *come true* is lexicalized and restricted to *true* with subjects denoting wishes or predictions. In tl, *naging* + adjective is the standard construction; the change-of-state particle *na* (*totoo na*) is a distinctive additional strategy. |
| **D. Describing a change into or out of a truth-domain state (continued)** | Morphological inchoative: a dedicated suffix or verbalizer derives an intransitive verb meaning "to become [truth-domain state]" from the evaluative base. | sw, qu | sw *-ika/-eka* suffix: *kudanganyika* (to be taken in/become deceived, no agent implied), *ulidhihirika* (became evident); qu *-ka-* intransitive verbalizer: *cheqakay* (to become true), *allinkay* (to become good/correct). **Two-language observation.** The morphological inchoative is documented in sw and qu. In sw, the *-ika/-eka* suffix is semantically distinct from the *-wa* passive: *kudanganyika* presents the subject as entering a state without an external agent, while *kudanganywa* foregrounds an external agent. In qu, the *-ka-* intransitive verbalizer contrasts systematically with the *-ncha-* causative verbalizer: *cheqakay* (to become true, intransitive) vs. *cheqanchay* (to make true, transitive). No morphological inchoative is documented for the negative-pole root *llulla* in qu; this is an attested gap. |
| **D. Describing a change into or out of a truth-domain state (continued)** | Lexicalized inchoative: a fixed expression encodes a specific change-of-state meaning that is not compositionally derived. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw | zh *弄假成真* (pretense became real), *真相大白* (truth came to light); he *hitgalaʾh ke-* (was revealed as); sa *pramā jāyate* (valid cognition arises), *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate* (false knowledge ceases); el *η αλήθεια βγαίνει στο φως* (truth comes to light); en *come true*, *ring true*, *the truth will out*; tl *totoo na* (it's become real); sw *ukweli ulidhihirika* (the truth became evident). **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages). In sa, the inchoative constructions are domain-specific: *jāyate* and *utpadyate* are used for the arising of cognitive states, while *nivartate* is used for the cessation of erroneous cognition. These are not freely interchangeable with general change-of-state verbs. |
| **E. Causing a truth-domain state to obtain** | Morphological causative: a causative suffix or morphological process derives a transitive verb meaning "to make [X] true/correct/genuine" or "to cause [someone] to err/believe falsely." | he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | he Pi'el *leʾammet* (to verify), *letaqqef* (to validate); sa *satyāpayati* (to verify, *-aya-* causative); el *επαληθεύω* (to verify), *αποδεικνύω* (to prove); en *verify*, *authenticate*, *validate*, *falsify*, *invalidate*; tl *patunayan* (*pa-* + *tunay* + *-an*), *itama* (*i-* + *tama*); sw *kuthibitisha* (*-isha* causative), *kusahihisha* (*-isha* causative), *kuhakikisha* (*-isha* causative); qu *cheqanchay* (*-ncha-* causative verbalizer), *cheqachiy* (*-chi-* causative), *pantachiy* (*-chi-* causative). **Widely attested** (7 of 8 languages). In zh, causative meaning is expressed through dedicated lexical verbs (*核实*, *鉴定*, *纠正*) rather than through a productive causative morpheme; zh is the only language in the corpus where the causative function in this domain is expressed exclusively through lexical means. In qu, the *-ncha-* causative applies to positive-pole adjectival roots (*cheqa*, *allin*, *sut'i*) but not to the negative-pole root *llulla*, which uses *-chi-* directly; this asymmetry is documented and has no parallel in the other languages. |
| **E. Causing a truth-domain state to obtain (continued)** | Periphrastic causative: a general causative verb (*make*, *render*, *cause to be*) combines with a truth-domain predicate to express causation. | zh, he, el, en, sa | zh *使报道失真* (cause the report to become distorted); he *lehatʿot* (to mislead, Hif'il causative); el *αποδεικνύω ότι* (I prove that); en *make it valid*, *render invalid*; sa *doṣam āpādayati* (brings about a fault). **Moderately attested** (5 of 8 languages). In most languages, the periphrastic causative is less idiomatic than the morphological causative for this domain; dedicated derived verbs are preferred in formal registers. In zh, the *使/让* + NP + adjective construction is the primary causative strategy, as no productive causative morpheme is available. |
| **E. Causing a truth-domain state to obtain (continued)** | Reflexive causative: a reflexive or middle morpheme added to a causative truth-domain verb shifts the action from externally directed to self-directed. | qu, sw, tl | qu *cheqanchakuy* (*-ku-* reflexive on *cheqanchay*: to verify for oneself); sw *kujithibitishia* (*ji-* reflexive: to confirm for oneself); tl *pagpapatunayan* (self-directed verification). **Three-language observation.** The reflexive causative is documented in qu, sw, and tl. Not documented in zh, he, sa, el, or en for truth-domain items specifically. |
| **F. Deriving an abstract nominal naming a truth-domain property** | Suffix nominalization: a suffix attaches to a truth-domain adjective or evaluative root to yield an abstract noun naming the property. | zh, he, sa, el, en | zh *真实* → *真实性* (*-性*); he *ʾamin* → *ʾaminut* (*-ut*); sa *satya* → *satyatā* (*-tā*) / *satyatva* (*-tva*); el *αυθεντικός* → *αυθεντικότητα* (*-ότητα*); en *authentic* → *authenticity* (*-ity*), *truthful* → *truthfulness* (*-ness*). **Attested in 5 of 8 languages** via suffix. In zh, the *-性* suffix is restricted to disyllabic adjectival compounds; monosyllabic roots (真, 假) do not take *-性* productively. In he, the *-ut* suffix is the primary abstract nominalizer; the *ʾi-* prefix applies to *-ut* forms to yield negated abstract nouns (*ʾi-ʾaminut*), a strategy not available for the corresponding adjectives. In sa, both *-tā* and *-tva* are productive with no consistent semantic distinction between them. In el, *-ότητα* is the primary suffix for quality nouns from adjectives. In en, *-ity/-cy* is preferred for Latinate bases and *-ness* for Germanic bases. |
| **F. Deriving an abstract nominal naming a truth-domain property (continued)** | Prefix nominalization: a prefix attaches to a truth-domain root to yield an abstract noun. | sw, tl | sw *u-* prefix: *kweli* → *ukweli*, *amini* → *uaminifu*; tl *ka-…-an* circumfix: *totoo* → *katotohanan*, *tapat* → *katapatan*. **Two-language observation.** In sw, the *u-* prefix is the primary abstract nominalization strategy; it applies to both native roots and Arabic loanwords. In tl, the *ka-…-an* circumfix is the primary strategy; neither *ka-* nor *-an* alone produces the nominalization. |
| **F. Deriving an abstract nominal naming a truth-domain property (continued)** | Copular nominalization: the copular verb or a nominalized copular construction yields an abstract nominal. | qu, sa | qu *cheqa kay* (truth, the state of being true: *cheqa* + *kay* "to be"); sa *satya* itself functions as both adjective and abstract noun; *cheqa kaynin* (its true nature: *cheqa* + *kay* + possessive). **Two-language observation.** In qu, the copular nominalization is the primary strategy for abstract nominals in this domain; no dedicated abstract nominalization suffix parallel to *-性* or *-ut* is documented. In sa, the *-tā/-tva* suffixes are the primary strategy, but the root *satya* itself already functions as an abstract noun without further derivation. |
| **G. Naming an agent characterized by a truth-domain property** | Agentive suffix on verbal or adjectival base: a suffix derives a noun denoting a person characterized by a truth-domain property. | zh, he, sa, el, en, qu | zh *伪造* → *伪造者* (*-者*); he *shaqran* (liar, *-an* suffix); sa *satya* → *satyavādin* (*-vādin* compound), *asatyavādin*; el *ψεύτης* (liar, *-ης*), *πλαστογράφος* (forger, *-γράφος*); en *faker* (*-er*), *counterfeiter* (*-er*); qu *llulla* → *llullaq* (*-q* agentive). **Attested in 6 of 8 languages** via suffix or compound. A consistent asymmetry is documented across all 8 languages: negative-pole agents (liars, deceivers, forgers) are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-tellers). In zh, *伪造者* and *欺骗者* are attested but no *-者* form for truth-teller is documented. In he, *shaqran* (liar) is lexicalized but no *-an* agentive for truth-teller is attested. In el, *ψεύτης* is common but no single-word truth-teller is lexicalized. In en, *faker* and *counterfeiter* are single words but *truth-teller* requires a compound. In qu, *llullaq* is productive but *cheqaq* functions primarily as an adjective. |
| **G. Naming an agent characterized by a truth-domain property (continued)** | Agentive prefix on nominal base: a prefix derives a noun denoting a person characterized by a truth-domain property. | sw, tl | sw *m-/wa-* prefix: *uongo* → *mwongo* (liar), *danganya* → *mdanganyifu* (deceiver); tl *sinungaling* (liar, lexicalized root functioning as agentive nominal). **Two-language observation.** In sw, the *m-/wa-* class 1/2 prefix is the standard agentive strategy; it applies to both native roots and Arabic loanwords. In tl, *sinungaling* is a lexicalized root that functions directly as an agentive nominal without additional affixation. |
| **G. Naming an agent characterized by a truth-domain property (continued)** | Periphrastic agentive: a general noun for "person" combines with a truth-domain expression to yield an agentive nominal. | zh, he, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *说真话的人* (person who tells the truth); he *dover ʾemet* (one who speaks truth, participial construction), *mtu wa kweli* (sw: person of truth); el *αυτός που λέει την αλήθεια* (one who tells the truth); en *truth-teller* (compound), *one who tells the truth*; tl *taong tapat* (honest person); sw *mtu wa kweli* (person of truth); qu *cheqaq runa* (truthful person, compound). **Attested in all 8 languages** for the positive pole. The periphrastic agentive is the primary strategy for "truth-teller" in most languages, while "liar" is expressed through a single lexicalized word. This asymmetry is the most consistent cross-linguistic pattern in the agentive nominalization domain. |
| **H. Framing a statement as frank, corrective, or sincere** | Sentence-initial adverbial frame: a fixed or semi-fixed adverbial expression at the beginning of an utterance signals that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *实际上*, *事实上*, *本质上*; he *ha-ʾemet hi she-* (the truth is that), *beʾemet* (truly, in truth); sa *vastutaḥ*, *tattvataḥ* (*-taḥ* adverbials); el *η αλήθεια είναι ότι* (the truth is that), *για να λέμε την αλήθεια* (to tell the truth); en *to tell the truth*, *truth be told*, *to be honest*; tl *sa totoo lang* (to tell the truth), *ang totoo* (the truth is); sw *kwa kweli* (truly, in truth), *ukweli ni kwamba* (the truth is that); qu *cheqaqta rimay* (speak the truth, used as a framing device). **Attested in all 8 languages.** The degree of grammaticalization varies: zh *实际上* and *事实上* are fully grammaticalized sentence-level adverbials; sa *vastutaḥ* is conventionalized in commentarial prose; tl *sa totoo lang* is a fixed discourse particle; sw *kwa kweli* is a conventionalized sincerity frame. In all languages, these constructions signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment, often in contrast to a prior claim. |
| **H. Framing a statement as frank, corrective, or sincere (continued)** | Concessive frame: a truth-domain expression acknowledges the partial validity of a prior claim before introducing a qualification or counterpoint. | he, sa, el, en, qu | he *nakhon she-… aval* (it is true that… but); sa *satyaṃ tu* (true, but), *satyaṃ tathāpi* (true, yet even so); el *αληθώς… αλλά* (truly… but), *ναι, αλλά* (yes, but); en *it is true that… but*, *true enough, but*; qu *cheqam, ichaqa* (it is true, but). **Moderately attested** (5 of 8 languages). The concessive frame is most explicitly documented in he, sa, el, and en. Not documented as a distinct construction type in zh, tl, or sw in the F4 data, though functionally equivalent expressions exist in those languages. |
| **I. Embedding a truth-domain evaluation in a subordinate clause** | Nominalized subordinate clause: a truth-domain predication is nominalized and used as the complement of a verb of knowing, believing, proving, or saying. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *知道这是真的* (know that this is true); he *lehaʾamin she-* (to believe that); sa *cheqa kasqanta yachani* (qu: I know that it is true, nominalized subordinate clause); el *ξέρω ότι είναι αληθινό* (I know that it is true); en *I know that it is true*, *proved that the claim was false*; tl *naniniwala akong totoo ito* (I believe this is true); sw *najua kwamba ni kweli* (I know that it is true); qu *cheqa kasqanta yachani* (I know that it is true, *kasqanta* nominalization). **Attested in all 8 languages.** The nominalization strategy for the embedded clause varies: qu uses the *-sqa* + possessive nominalization (*cheqa kasqanta*); sa uses a similar nominalized clause structure; he, el, en, tl, and sw use a complementizer (*she-*, *ότι*, *that*, *na*, *kwamba*) to introduce the embedded clause without nominalization. In zh, the complementizer *是* or *说* introduces the embedded clause. |
| **J. Expressing obligation or prohibition regarding truth-domain conduct** | Deontic periphrasis: a modal or deontic verb/construction combines with a truth-domain verbal expression to assert that truthful speech or verification is required or prohibited. | zh, he, sa, el, en, tl, sw, qu | zh *严禁弄虚作假* (strictly forbidden to falsify), *不得造假* (fabrication is not permitted); he *tsarikh ledaber ʾemet* (one must tell the truth); sa *satyaṃ brūyāt* (one should speak the truth, optative), *satyāt na pramaditavyam* (one must not deviate from truth, gerundive); el *πρέπει να λέει την αλήθεια* (must tell the truth); en *must tell the truth*, *it is prohibited to deceive*; tl *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan* (qu: you must tell the truth, deontic periphrasis), *kailangan mong sabihin ang katotohanan* (tl: you need to tell the truth); sw *kusema ukweli ni wajibu* (telling the truth is a duty); qu *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan* (you must tell the truth, *-na* + possessive + *tiyan*). **Attested in all 8 languages.** The morphological form of the deontic construction varies considerably: sa uses the optative (*brūyāt*) and gerundive (*pramaditavyam*); qu uses the *-na* + possessive + *tiyan* construction; zh uses the regulatory prohibitive (*严禁*, *不得*); he, el, en, tl, and sw use modal verbs or deontic expressions. ## 3. Narrative Analysis

### 3.1 Recurring cross-language constructional patterns

**Attribution via copular sentence (Function A)** is the most consistently attested constructional function across all eight languages. Every language uses a copular or zero-copula construction to predicate a truth-domain property of a subject. The variation is in the copula itself: overt in zh (*是*), el (*είναι*), en (*be*), tl (*ay*), sw (*ni*); absent by default in he, sa, and qu. In qu, the absence of a copula is compensated by the obligatory evidential enclitic (*-m/-mi*), which marks the speaker's direct epistemic commitment to the predication. This substitution — evidential morphology replacing the copula as the primary marker of assertive force — is unique to qu in the corpus.

**Negation of truth-domain attributions (Function B)** is attested in all eight languages, but the strategies differ in their degree of morphological integration. At one end, qu uses a syntactic negator (*mana*) combined with an obligatory negative polar suffix (*-chu*) on the predicate, making the negation morphologically integrated into the predicate itself. At the other end, sw uses a suppletive negative copula (*si*) that replaces the positive copula (*ni*) entirely. In between, he, sa, el, and en use prefixal negation that is morphologically integrated into the adjective (*asatya*, *αναξιόπιστος*, *inaccurate*), while zh and tl use syntactic negators (*不*, *hindi*) that precede the predicate without morphological integration. The he system is distinctive in using different negation strategies for adjectives (*lo*) and their nominalizations (*ʾi-*), creating an asymmetry not paralleled in the other languages.

**Discourse-framing constructions (Function H)** are attested in all eight languages, suggesting that the pragmatic function of signaling frank or corrective speech is a robust communicative need that is met through grammaticalization of truth vocabulary across unrelated language families. The degree of grammaticalization varies from fully grammaticalized adverbials (zh *实际上*; sa *vastutaḥ*) to conventionalized fixed phrases (he *ha-ʾemet hi she-*; el *η αλήθεια είναι ότι*; en *to tell the truth*; tl *sa totoo lang*; sw *kwa kweli*; qu *cheqaqta rimay*). In all languages, these constructions occupy sentence-initial or parenthetical position and signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment, often in contrast to a prior claim or expectation.

**The agentive asymmetry (Function G)** is the most consistent cross-linguistic pattern in the corpus: negative-pole agents (liars, deceivers, forgers) are more consistently lexicalized as single words than positive-pole agents (truth-tellers). This asymmetry is documented across all eight languages and across unrelated language families (Sino-Tibetan, Semitic, Indo-European, Austronesian, Bantu, Quechuan). The concept of "one who lies" is expressed through a single lexicalized word in all eight languages (zh *骗子*; he *shaqran*; sa *asatyavādin*; el *ψεύτης*; en *liar*; tl *sinungaling*; sw *mwongo*; qu *llullaq*), while the concept of "one who tells the truth" requires either a compound form or a periphrastic construction in all eight languages. This asymmetry is not explained by any single morphological factor and appears to reflect a consistent semantic pattern across the corpus.

**Abstract nominalization (Function F)** is productive in all eight languages, but the morphological strategy varies: suffix in zh, he, sa, el, en; prefix in sw; circumfix in tl; copular nominalization in qu. Despite this formal variation, the semantic output is consistent: a nominal naming the abstract property as an entity. The resulting nominals are used in formal, legal, and philosophical registers across all languages, and they are the primary forms used when the property itself (rather than a specific instance of it) is the topic of discourse.

**Causative derivation for verification and authentication (Function E)** is attested in all eight languages, but zh is distinctive in expressing this function exclusively through dedicated lexical verbs rather than through a productive causative morpheme. In all other languages, a productive causative morpheme or morphological process derives the verification verb from the truth-domain base. In qu, the causative pathway is asymmetric: the *-ncha-* verbalizer applies to positive-pole adjectival roots but not to the negative-pole root *llulla*, which uses *-chi-* directly. This asymmetry is documented and has no parallel in the other languages.

### 3.2 Important asymmetries, substitutions, and restrictions

**The qu evidential system substitutes for several functions** that are expressed through separate constructions in other languages. The direct evidential *-m/-mi* simultaneously marks the speaker's epistemic commitment (Function L), reinforces the assertive force of the predication (Function A), and signals that the speaker has direct knowledge rather than reported information. In other languages, these three functions are expressed through separate constructions: a copula (Function A), a certainty adverb (Function L), and a sincerity frame (Function H). The qu evidential system thus compresses into a single morphological marker what other languages express through multiple distinct constructions.

**The sw *-ika/-eka* stative suffix substitutes for a periphrastic change-of-state construction** (Function D) in contexts where no external agent is implied. In other languages, the absence of an agent in a change-of-state construction is expressed through a passive or through a periphrastic construction with an intransitive change-of-state verb (en *turned out to be*, el *αποδείχτηκε*). In sw, the *-ika/-eka* suffix derives an intransitive form that is semantically distinct from the *-wa* passive: *kudanganyika* (to be taken in, no agent implied) vs. *kudanganywa* (to be deceived by an agent). This morphological distinction between agentless inchoative and agentive passive is documented in sw and qu (*-ka-* vs. *-ncha-*) but not in the other six languages.

**The tl discourse particle system substitutes for several constructional functions** that are expressed through separate lexical items or constructions in other languages. The particles *ba* (interrogative/doubt), *na* (change of state), *pala* (realization/discovery), *nga* (confirmation), and *naman* (concessive/responsive) encode epistemic stances toward truth predicates that are expressed through separate adverbs, modal verbs, or discourse markers in other languages. The *totoo na* construction (Function D) is particularly distinctive: it uses a grammatical particle to mark a change into a truth-domain state, while other languages use a change-of-state verb (*proved true*, *hafakh le-ʾemet*) or a morphological inchoative (*cheqakay*, *ulidhihirika*).

**The zh four-character expression format** is a constructional strategy with no parallel in the other seven languages. Fixed four-character expressions (*名副其实*, *弄虚作假*, *真金不怕火炼*) function as single evaluative units, and internal negation (inserting *不* or *非* into the fixed sequence) is a productive strategy for producing antonyms within this format (*名副其实* → *名不副实*). This format encodes evaluative meanings that in other languages would require a full clause or a periphrastic construction.

**The he *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry** creates a constructional gap: the *ʾi-* prefix negates *-ut* nominalizations (*ʾi-ʾaminut*, *ʾi-sevirut*) but not the corresponding adjectives, which are negated syntactically with *lo* or with *bilti*. This means that the negated abstract nominal (*ʾi-ʾaminut*, unreliability) and the negated adjective (*lo ʾamin*, unreliable) are formed through different strategies, creating an asymmetry between the adjectival and nominalized negation systems. In all other languages, the negation strategy is more uniform across word classes.

**The sw light-verb asymmetry** (*sema ukweli* for truth-telling vs. *piga uongo* for lying) creates a constructional asymmetry within the same domain: the two poles of the truth/falsehood domain are expressed through different light verbs, with no *piga kweli* construction attested. In zh, the *说* + *真话/假话* construction is symmetric (the same verb is used for both poles), making the sw asymmetry distinctive.

### 3.3 Cases where one constructional strategy appears to replace another

**In zh, dedicated lexical verbs replace morphological causatives** for the verification and authentication function (Function E). While all other languages use a productive causative morpheme or morphological process (*-isha* sw; *-ncha-* qu; Pi'el he; *-aya-* sa; *-ate/-ify* en; *pa-…-an* tl), zh expresses this function through dedicated lexical verbs (*核实*, *鉴定*, *纠正*) without a productive causative morpheme. This substitution is consistent across the zh data and is not a gap but a systematic alternative strategy.

**In qu, the *-puni* emphatic suffix and *-lla* limitative suffix replace degree adverbs** for the degree-modification function (Function K). In other languages, degree modification is expressed through separate adverbs (*absolutely*, *completely*, *purely*) or through reduplication (tl, sw). In qu, the emphatic suffix *-puni* (*cheqapuni*, absolutely true) and the limitative suffix *-lla* (*cheqalla*, purely genuine) are morphologically integrated into the predicate, replacing the separate adverb strategy used in other languages.

**In tl, the *ka-…-an* circumfix replaces both suffix and prefix nominalization** for the abstract nominalization function (Function F). While other languages use either a suffix (zh *-性*, he *-ut*, sa *-tā/-tva*, el *-ότητα*, en *-ity/-ness*) or a prefix (sw *u-*), tl uses a circumfix that requires both a prefix and a suffix simultaneously. Neither element alone produces the nominalization; the circumfix as a unit is the strategy.

**In sa, the *-taḥ* adverbial replaces a clausal discourse frame** for the discourse-framing function (Function H). While other languages use a full clausal frame (*ha-ʾemet hi she-* he; *η αλήθεια είναι ότι* el; *ukweli ni kwamba* sw) or a fixed phrase (*to tell the truth* en; *sa totoo lang* tl), sa uses a morphologically derived adverbial (*vastutaḥ*, *tattvataḥ*, *paramārthataḥ*) that is a single word functioning as a sentence-level operator. This adverbial strategy is more morphologically compact than the clausal frame strategy used in most other languages.

### 3.4 Functions that are weakly expressed, constructionally indirect, or absent

**Function D (change into or out of a truth-domain state) is weakly expressed in sa** relative to the other languages. While sa has domain-specific inchoative constructions (*pramā jāyate*, *mithyājñānaṃ nivartate*), these are restricted to the arising and cessation of cognitive states in epistemological discourse. A general periphrastic inchoative for truth-value transitions (parallel to en *proved true*, el *αποδείχτηκε αληθής*, he *hafakh le-ʾemet*) is not documented in the F4 data for sa. The sa data focuses on the arising and cessation of cognitive states rather than on the discovery or revelation of truth in the world.

**Function M (resemblance to truth without assertion) is not documented in sw** in the F4 data. While sw has vocabulary for genuineness (*halisi*) and its absence (*bandia*), no construction for describing something as appearing to be genuine without asserting that it is is documented in the F4 data. The *parang totoo* (tl), *cheqa hina* (qu), *αληθοφανής* (el), and *ābhāsa* (sa) constructions have no documented parallel in sw.

**Function L (marking the source of knowledge) is absent as a grammatical category in all languages except qu.** In all other languages, the source of knowledge is expressed lexically (en *reportedly*, *allegedly*; sw *inasemekana*; tl *daw*) or through specific constructions, but not as an obligatory grammatical category. The qu evidential system is the only instance in the corpus of grammaticalized source-of-knowledge marking as an obligatory morphological feature.

**Function J (deontic constructions) is restricted in zh** to the deliberate-falsification sub-domain. The regulatory prohibitive constructions (*严禁弄虚作假*, *不得造假*) apply to falsification verbs but not to truth-evaluation adjectives or authenticity nouns. In other languages, deontic constructions apply more broadly across the truth domain (sa *satyaṃ brūyāt*; qu *cheqanta rimanayki tiyan*; sw *kusema ukweli ni wajibu*).

**The partial-truth construction (Function K sub-type) is not documented in qu** in the F4 data. While zh, he, el, en, tl, and sw all have a dedicated expression for a statement that is only partially true (*半真半假*, *ḥatsi ʾemet*, *μισή αλήθεια*, *half-truth*, *kalahating katotohanan*, *nusu ukweli*), no parallel construction is documented in qu. The concept may be expressible in qu through compositional means, but no fixed or conventionalized expression is attested in the data.

**The concessive frame (Function H sub-type) is not documented in zh, tl, or sw** in the F4 data as a distinct construction type. While he (*nakhon she-… aval*), sa (*satyaṃ tu*), el (*αληθώς… αλλά*), en (*it is true that… but*), and qu (*cheqam, ichaqa*) all have documented concessive frames that acknowledge a prior claim before introducing a qualification, no parallel construction is documented in zh, tl, or sw in the F4 data. This may reflect a gap in the data rather than an absence in the languages.
