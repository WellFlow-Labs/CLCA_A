# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on scope and method:** This step documents attested derivational morphology associated with the P1 lexical items. The primary data source is the P1 and P1b output, cross-referenced against the grammars and dictionaries cited therein (Cerrón-Palomino, Cusihuamán, Hornberger & Hornberger, Parker, Academia Mayor). Forms already fully documented in P1b are cross-referenced rather than re-described at full length. The organization follows P1 item order. Derivations are listed as they are attested; no paradigm completion has been performed. Gaps and asymmetries are preserved without comment beyond what the data supports.

A note on what counts as derivation here: Quechua morphology involves both derivational suffixes (which change lexical category or substantially alter meaning) and inflectional/clitic elements (which mark grammatical relations, tense, evidentiality, etc.). This step documents both types where they produce forms that are lexically or semantically significant for the truth-evaluation domain, consistent with the treatment in P1 and P1b. Purely inflectional paradigm forms (e.g., full person-number conjugations) are not listed.

## P1 Item 1: cheqa

**Base Form:** cheqa

---

**Derived Form 1**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaq
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaq
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (nominalizing/agentive suffix); "that which is true / one who is true"
5. **Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine (adjectival/nominal)
6. **Usage description:** Functions as an adjective or substantivized nominal. Used in moral and testimonial contexts to describe a truthful person or genuine state of affairs; interchangeable with *cheqa* in many contexts but carries a more nominal character.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 2**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqan
3. **Transliteration:** cheqan
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-n* (third-person possessive/nominalizing suffix); "its truth / the true one"
5. **Natural English translation:** the truth, what is correct, what is right
6. **Usage description:** Used to refer to the truth of a matter or the correct state of affairs, particularly in contexts of disclosure or rectification; appears in phrases meaning "the truth is that…"
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 3**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchay
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha-* (causative/verbalizing suffix) + *-y* (infinitive); "to make true / to verify"
5. **Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to correct, to rectify
6. **Usage description:** Verbal form used to establish or confirm the truth of something, or to correct an error; appears in legal, testimonial, and everyday correction contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 4**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchasqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchasqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha-* (causative/verbalizing) + *-sqa* (past participle/resultative); "verified, confirmed"
5. **Natural English translation:** verified, confirmed, established as true, corrected
6. **Usage description:** Participial form of *cheqanchay*; used to describe something that has been verified or confirmed, appearing in legal, administrative, and testimonial contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 5**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqanchariy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchariy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ncha-* (causative) + *-ri-* (inceptive/directional suffix) + *-y* (infinitive); "to begin to make true / to set right"
5. **Natural English translation:** to set right, to go about correcting, to begin to rectify
6. **Usage description:** Used to describe the process of beginning to correct or rectify a situation; the inceptive suffix adds a sense of initiating the action of correction, appearing in narrative and procedural contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 6**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaniy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaniy
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ni-* (verbalizing suffix) + *-y* (infinitive); "to be true / to be correct"
5. **Natural English translation:** to be true, to be correct, to be genuine
6. **Usage description:** A verbal form of *cheqa* used to predicate truth or genuineness of a subject; less frequent than the adjectival use of *cheqa* and appears in assertive and evaluative contexts.
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**Derived Form 7**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqata
3. **Transliteration:** cheqata
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-ta* (accusative/adverbial case); "truly, correctly" (adverbial)
5. **Natural English translation:** truly, correctly, rightly, accurately
6. **Usage description:** Adverbial case form of *cheqa* used to modify verbs of speaking, acting, or representing; appears in moral exhortations and evaluative descriptions such as *cheqata rimay* ("speak truly").
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 8**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqaqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqaqa
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-qa* (topic marker); "as for the truth / the truth is…"
5. **Natural English translation:** the truth is…, as for what is true…, in fact…
6. **Usage description:** Topic-marking construction used to introduce a correction or disclosure of the true state of affairs, often in contrast to a previously stated or implied falsehood; appears in narrative and conversational discourse.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 9**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqapuni
3. **Transliteration:** cheqapuni
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-puni* (emphatic/absolute suffix); "absolutely true / certainly genuine"
5. **Natural English translation:** absolutely true, certainly genuine, definitely correct
6. **Usage description:** Used for emphatic assertion of truth or genuineness; *-puni* adds a sense of certainty or absoluteness and appears in everyday speech and narrative when a speaker asserts something as unquestionably true.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 10**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqam
3. **Transliteration:** cheqam
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-m* (direct evidential clitic, reduced form of *-mi*); "it is true (I assert from direct knowledge)"
5. **Natural English translation:** it is true, it is real, that is correct (asserted from direct evidence)
6. **Usage description:** Combines the truth adjective with the direct evidential clitic, producing a strongly committed affirmation; appears in testimonial, legal, and everyday assertive contexts when a speaker affirms truth from personal knowledge.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 11**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqas
3. **Transliteration:** cheqas
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-s* (reportative evidential clitic, reduced form of *-si*); "it is true (reportedly)"
5. **Natural English translation:** it is apparently true, it is said to be true, reportedly true
6. **Usage description:** Used when a speaker affirms the truth of a proposition on the basis of reported rather than direct evidence; appears in narrative and testimonial contexts where the speaker relays information from another source.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Derived Form 12**
1. **Base Form:** cheqa
2. **Derived Form:** cheqacha
3. **Transliteration:** cheqacha
4. **Literal Breakdown:** cheqa + *-chá* (conjectural evidential clitic); "it is probably true / I suppose it is true"
5. **Natural English translation:** it is probably true, it seems to be true, I suppose it is correct
6. **Usage description:** Used when a speaker hedges a truth assertion, indicating inference or conjecture rather than direct knowledge; appears in contexts of uncertainty or tentative affirmation.
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Summary Note on Productivity

The data above support the following observations, each directly grounded in the attested forms:

**cheqa** is the most productive base in this domain, generating derivations via *-q*, *-n*, *-ncha-y*, *-ncha-sqa*, *-ncha-ri-y*, *-ni-y*, *-ta*, *-qa*, *-puni*, *-m*, *-s*, and *-chá*, as well as participating in numerous nominal compounds. This breadth of derivation is consistent with its status as the primary truth-evaluation root.

**llulla** generates a parallel but smaller set of derivations, with verbal forms via *-ku-y*, *-cha-y*, and *-cha-ku-y*, agentive and participial nominalizations, and clitic-marked forms. It does not generate as many nominal compounds as *cheqa* in the philosophical and abstract registers.

**sut'i** generates derivations primarily via *-cha-y* and its further extensions (*-sqa*, *-ku-y*), plus the adverbial *-ta* and clitic-marked forms. Its derivational range is more concentrated than *cheqa*.

**pantay**, **yachay**, **iñiy**, **qaway**, and **ruray** each generate agentive (*-q*), participial (*-sqa*), and causative (*-chi-y*) forms in patterns consistent with standard Quechua verbal morphology. **pantay** additionally shows a reflexive-middle form (*pantakuy*) and a causative (*pantachiy*) that are both attested and semantically significant for the error/misleading domain.

Several bases — *cheqaqpaq*, *cheqan*, *iñisqa*, *pantasqa*, *mana allin*, *hinam*, *cheqa simi*, *llulla simi*, *k'uychi*, and the evidential clitics — do not generate further derivations as independent bases in the attested record.
