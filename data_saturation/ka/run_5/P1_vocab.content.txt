# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

**Transliteration standard:** National System of Georgia (2002), as used in Georgian passports and official romanization, supplemented by standard academic conventions for Georgian studies. Applied consistently throughout.

---

**1.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტი
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariti
**Literal meaning:** root č̣ešmar- (truth, genuine quality) + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine, real
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements and propositions, as well as genuineness of persons or things. Appears in philosophical, theological, and everyday contexts. Participates in derivation: č̣ešmariteba (truth, the abstract noun).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** ჭეშმარიტება
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmariteba
**Literal meaning:** č̣ešmariti + nominalizing suffix -eba
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, veracity
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for truth in Georgian, used in philosophical, religious, legal, and everyday discourse. Frequently appears in constructions asserting or questioning the truth of a claim or state of affairs.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** სიმართლე
**Transliteration:** simartle
**Literal meaning:** si- (nominal prefix) + martal- (right, correct, just) + -e (nominal suffix)
**Typical English translations:** truth, correctness, rightness, justice
**Usage:** Near-synonym of č̣ešmariteba but with a stronger association with moral and legal rightness, as well as factual correctness. Commonly used in everyday speech when asserting that something said is accurate or that a person is in the right.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართალი
**Transliteration:** martali
**Literal meaning:** root martal- + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** right, correct, true, just, honest
**Usage:** Applied to persons (an honest or just person), statements (a correct or true claim), and situations (a rightful state of affairs). One of the most productive roots in this semantic domain, generating numerous derivatives.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** სიყალბე
**Transliteration:** siq̇albe
**Literal meaning:** si- + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -e
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, falsity, forgery, counterfeit quality
**Usage:** Denotes the abstract quality of being false or counterfeit, applicable to documents, currency, statements, and representations. Used in legal contexts for forgery and in everyday contexts for deception.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ყალბი
**Transliteration:** q̇albi
**Literal meaning:** q̇alb- + adjectival suffix -i; the root is borrowed from Persian/Arabic via Turkish (qalb, false)
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, forged
**Usage:** The standard adjective for counterfeit or forged items (documents, money, signatures) and for false or insincere statements or persons. Widely used in legal and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** ნამდვილი
**Transliteration:** namdvili
**Literal meaning:** na- (participial/resultative prefix) + mdvil- (to place, establish) + -i; semantically: that which has been established/settled
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, actual, true
**Usage:** The primary adjective for authenticity and genuineness of objects, persons, and states of affairs. Contrasts with q̇albi (counterfeit) and used to assert that something is what it purports to be.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ნამდვილობა
**Transliteration:** namdviloba
**Literal meaning:** namdvili + nominalizing suffix -oba
**Typical English translations:** genuineness, authenticity, reality
**Usage:** Abstract noun derived from namdvili, used in legal, philosophical, and administrative contexts to refer to the property of being genuine or authentic. Appears in discussions of document verification and ontological status.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიცრუე
**Transliteration:** sicrue
**Literal meaning:** si- + cru- (lie, falsehood) + -e
**Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
**Usage:** The standard noun for a lie or falsehood in everyday and moral discourse. Used to characterize statements that are deliberately false, and appears in moral evaluations of persons and conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** მცდარი
**Transliteration:** mcdari
**Literal meaning:** m- (participial prefix) + cdar- (to err, to be mistaken) + -i
**Typical English translations:** erroneous, mistaken, wrong, incorrect
**Usage:** Applied to statements, beliefs, and judgments that are factually or logically incorrect, without necessarily implying deliberate deception. Commonly used in academic, scientific, and evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** სწორი
**Transliteration:** sc̣ori
**Literal meaning:** sc̣or- (straight, level, correct) + -i
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate, straight
**Usage:** Used to evaluate statements, calculations, behavior, and physical alignment as correct or accurate. One of the most common evaluative adjectives in everyday Georgian, applicable across factual and normative domains.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** არასწორი
**Transliteration:** arasc̣ori
**Literal meaning:** ara- (negation prefix) + sc̣ori
**Typical English translations:** incorrect, wrong, inaccurate
**Usage:** The direct negation of sc̣ori, used to evaluate statements, actions, or representations as incorrect or improper. Productive in both factual and normative evaluations.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** სარწმუნო
**Transliteration:** sarc̣muno
**Literal meaning:** sa- (potential/relational prefix) + rc̣mun- (to believe, to trust) + -o
**Typical English translations:** credible, trustworthy, reliable, believable
**Usage:** Evaluates sources, witnesses, documents, and persons as worthy of belief or trust. Frequently used in legal testimony contexts and in assessments of information reliability.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** დაუჯერებელი
**Transliteration:** daujerebelilit
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + u- (negation) + jer- (to believe) + -ebeli (potential suffix) + -i
**Typical English translations:** unbelievable, incredible, implausible
**Usage:** Used to characterize claims or events that strain credibility, either because they are false or because they are extraordinarily surprising. Carries both epistemic and evaluative force.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** სიზუსტე
**Transliteration:** sizuste
**Literal meaning:** si- + zust- (exact, precise; from Persian/via borrowing) + -e
**Typical English translations:** accuracy, precision, exactness
**Usage:** Refers to the quality of being exact or precise in measurements, statements, translations, and representations. Used in scientific, technical, and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** ზუსტი
**Transliteration:** zusti
**Literal meaning:** zust- + adjectival suffix -i
**Typical English translations:** exact, precise, accurate
**Usage:** The adjectival form corresponding to sizuste. Applied to measurements, translations, reproductions, and statements to indicate that they correspond closely to a standard or reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** გულწრფელი
**Transliteration:** gulc̣rpeli
**Literal meaning:** gul- (heart) + c̣rpel- (sincere, straightforward) + -i
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt, candid
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, expressions, and conduct as sincere or genuine in their inner motivation. Used in moral and interpersonal contexts to distinguish authentic feeling or intent from performance or pretense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** თვალთმაქცობა
**Transliteration:** tvaltmaqcoba
**Literal meaning:** tval- (eye) + t- (connective) + maqc- (to deceive, to trick) + -oba (nominalization)
**Typical English translations:** deception, trickery, illusion, sleight of hand
**Usage:** Refers to deliberate deception through appearances or misleading representations. Used in moral and legal contexts to characterize conduct that creates false impressions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** მოჩვენებითი
**Transliteration:** močvenebiti
**Literal meaning:** mo- (preverb) + čven- (to show, to appear) + -ebiti (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, illusory, ostensible
**Usage:** Describes something that appears to be the case but is not genuinely so. Used in philosophical, legal, and rhetorical contexts to mark a gap between appearance and reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** ავთენტური
**Transliteration:** avtent̕uri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek/Latin authenticus via international vocabulary + adjectival suffix -uri
**Typical English translations:** authentic
**Usage:** A more recent borrowing used in legal, archival, and academic contexts, particularly for documents and cultural artifacts. Tends to appear in formal registers alongside or instead of namdvili.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** ლეგიტიმური
**Transliteration:** legitimuri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin legitimus via international vocabulary + -uri
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, lawful, valid
**Usage:** Used in legal and political discourse to evaluate whether authority, claims, or actions are lawful and properly grounded. Distinct from mere legality in that it carries normative force about rightful status.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** მიბაძვა
**Transliteration:** mibadzva
**Literal meaning:** mi- (directional preverb) + badz- (to imitate) + -va (verbal noun suffix)
**Typical English translations:** imitation, mimicry, copying
**Usage:** Refers to the act of imitating or copying something, used in aesthetic contexts (imitation of a style or work) and in evaluative contexts where imitation is contrasted with originality or authenticity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** გაყალბება
**Transliteration:** gaq̇albeba
**Literal meaning:** ga- (preverb) + q̇alb- (false, counterfeit) + -eba (verbal noun)
**Typical English translations:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting
**Usage:** The verbal noun for the act of forging or falsifying documents, evidence, or other representations. Widely used in legal contexts and in discussions of fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** შეუსაბამო
**Transliteration:** šeusabamo
**Literal meaning:** še- (preverb) + u- (negation) + sabam- (correspondence, conformity) + -o
**Typical English translations:** inconsistent, incongruent, incompatible, non-conforming
**Usage:** Used to evaluate a mismatch between a claim, representation, or action and a standard, rule, or reality. Appears in legal, administrative, and logical evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**25.**
**Form:** დამაჯერებელი
**Transliteration:** damajerebelilit
**Literal meaning:** da- (preverb) + majer- (to cause to believe) + -ebeli (potential/agentive suffix) + -i
**Typical English translations:** convincing, persuasive, credible, plausible
**Usage:** Evaluates arguments, evidence, persons, or representations as having the quality of being believable or persuasive. Used in legal, rhetorical, and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**26.**
**Form:** ობიექტური
**Transliteration:** obieqt̕uri
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Latin/international objectivus + -uri
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial, unbiased
**Usage:** Used in academic, journalistic, and legal contexts to evaluate assessments, reports, or persons as free from subjective bias. A relatively recent borrowing that has become standard in formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** მიკერძოებული
**Transliteration:** mikerdzoebulilit
**Literal meaning:** mi- (preverb) + kerdzo- (partial, one-sided) + -ebuli (resultative participle) + -i
**Typical English translations:** biased, partial, prejudiced
**Usage:** Evaluates persons, judgments, or representations as skewed toward one side or interest. Used in legal, journalistic, and moral contexts to challenge the reliability or fairness of an assessment.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** არსებითი
**Transliteration:** arsebiti
**Literal meaning:** arseb- (existence, being; from arsebi, existing) + -iti (adjectival suffix)
**Typical English translations:** essential, substantial, fundamental, material
**Usage:** Used to identify what is fundamental or constitutive about something, as opposed to accidental or superficial features. Appears in philosophical, legal (material facts), and general evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**29.**
**Form:** ფსევდო-
**Transliteration:** psevdo-
**Literal meaning:** borrowed from Greek pseudō- (false, spurious) + productive prefix in Georgian
**Typical English translations:** pseudo-, false, spurious, sham
**Usage:** Functions as a productive prefix in Georgian to mark something as falsely or spuriously claiming a status or identity (e.g., psevdomecnieri, pseudo-scientist). Used in academic and critical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**30.**
**Form:** საეჭვო
**Transliteration:** saečvo
**Literal meaning:** sa- (relational prefix) + ečv- (doubt) + -o
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, suspicious, questionable, dubious
**Usage:** Evaluates claims, persons, documents, or conduct as warranting doubt or suspicion regarding their genuineness, reliability, or legitimacy. Used in legal, investigative, and everyday evaluative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
