# G7a — Data-Derived Global Constraint Matrix

---

## Preliminary Notes on Scope and Method

The dimensions and constructional functions below are derived from patterns documented in G1–G6 and the underlying F-phase tables. No external theoretical framework is applied. Dimensions are named from recurring semantic groupings identified in F3 and G3. Constructional functions are named from recurring patterns identified in F4 and G5. Where a dimension or function was identified in only one or two languages, this is noted explicitly.

The matrix covers 13 semantic dimensions and 20 constructional functions, yielding 260 cells. Each cell is classified as ALLOWED, BLOCKED, VARIABLE, or INSUFFICIENT DATA, with brief supporting notes. The narrative analysis follows.

---

## Semantic Dimensions (rows)

**D1 — Propositional Correspondence:** Terms evaluating whether statements or claims match actual states of affairs (ka სიმართლე/სწორი, yo tọ́/òtítọ́, fi tosi/totuus, eu egia/egiazkoa, ta uṇmai/cari, id benar/salah, tr doğru/yanlış, ko 진실/사실/맞다).

**D2 — Deliberate Deception:** Terms marking intentional false representation or lying, as distinct from unintentional error (ka ტყუილი/მოტყუება, yo irọ́/purọ́, fi valhe/valehdella, eu gezurra/iruzurra, ta poy, id bohong, tr yalan, ko 거짓말).

**D3 — Unintentional Error:** Terms marking accidental mistake or incorrect belief without deceptive intent (ka მცდარი/შეცდომა, yo àṣìṣe, fi virheellinen/väärinkäsitys, eu okerra, ta tappu/tavaṟu, id keliru/kesalahan, tr hatalı/yanılmak, ko 틀리다/실수).

**D4 — Authenticity of Objects:** Terms evaluating whether physical objects, documents, or identities are genuine originals versus fakes or counterfeits (ka ნამდვილი/ყალბი, yo gidi, fi aito/väärennös, eu benetakoa/faltsua, ta acal/pōli, id asli/palsu, tr gerçek/sahte, ko 진짜/가짜).

**D5 — Honesty and Moral Character:** Terms evaluating truthfulness and integrity as stable character traits of persons (ka გულწრფელი/ლიცემერი, yo ìwà rere/ọkàn tọ́, fi rehellinen/vilpitön, eu zintzoa, ta nērmai, id jujur, tr dürüst, ko 정직/솔직/진심).

**D6 — Moral Rightness and Justice:** Terms evaluating actions, decisions, or situations according to moral or legal standards of rightness and fairness (ka სამართლიანი/მართლმსაჯულება, yo òdodo, fi oikeudenmukaisuus, eu zuzentasuna/zuzenbide, ta niyāyam/aṟam, id adil/pantas, tr hak/haklı/haksız/adil, ko 정당하다/타당하다).

**D7 — Reliability and Credibility of Sources:** Terms evaluating whether sources, persons, or information can be trusted, prior to or independently of verification (ka სანდო/საეჭვო, yo dájú/gbàgbọ́, fi luotettava/uskottava, eu fidagarria/zalantzagarria, ta nampakam/cantēkam, tr güvenilir/inandırıcı, ko 신빙성/그럴듯하다).

**D8 — Epistemic Certainty and Doubt:** Terms expressing the speaker's degree of confidence or certainty regarding a claim (ka ეჭვი/უტყუარი, yo ìdájú/dájú, fi todennäköinen, eu ziurra/ziurtasuna, ta uṟuti/cantēkam/teḷivu, tr kesin/şüpheli, ko 확실하다/불확실성).

**D9 — Appearance vs. Underlying Reality:** Terms distinguishing surface appearances or seemings from underlying actual states (ka მოჩვენებითი/ზედაპირული/არსებითი, fi harhaanjohtava/petollinen, eu itxurazko/ageria/funtsean, ta māyai/uṇmai mukam, id sebenarnya/hakikat, tr gerçek yüz/gerçeğe aykırı, ko 진면목/실체/본질).

**D10 — Validity and Legitimacy:** Terms evaluating whether actions, documents, or arguments conform to established rules, legal requirements, or accepted standards (ka მართებული/სამართლებრივი, yo yẹ, fi oikeutettu/oikeuttaa, eu zuzenbide/egiaztagiri, ta niyāyam, id sah/absah/batal, tr geçerli/meşru, ko 정당하다/타당하다).

**D11 — Similarity and Correspondence:** Terms evaluating degrees of resemblance or matching between entities or between representations and their referents (ka მსგავსი/ადეკვატური, yo bá mu/ìbámu/jọ, eu antzekoa/berdina, ta oppu, id sama/mirip).

**D12 — Practical Reality vs. Theory:** Terms distinguishing what actually exists or occurs in practice from what is planned, theoretical, or nominal (fi tosielämä/tosiasiallinen, eu benetako/egiazko egoera, ta uṇmai vāḻkkai, id nyata/kenyataan, tr gerçek hayat, ko 실제/실질/실천/실현).

**D13 — Verification and Proof Processes:** Terms describing the processes of establishing, confirming, or demonstrating truth through evidence or systematic inquiry (ka დაამტკიცა/გადაამოწმა, yo ṣe ìwádìí/ṣe àyẹ̀wò, fi todistaa/todiste/todentaa, eu egiaztatu/egiaztapen, ta uṟutipaṭuttu/cāṭci, id membenarkan/mengesahkan, tr doğrulamak/yanlışlamak, ko 진실을 밝히다/사실을 확인하다).

## Constructional Functions (columns)

**CF1 — Predicative Attribution:** Asserting that a subject has a truth-domain property (copula + predicate).

**CF2 — Predicative Negation:** Denying that a subject has a truth-domain property.

**CF3 — Adnominal Modification:** Using a truth-domain adjective to modify a noun attributively.

**CF4 — Inchoative (positive direction):** Expressing change of state toward truth, correctness, or genuineness.

**CF5 — Inchoative (negative direction):** Expressing change of state away from truth, correctness, or genuineness.

**CF6 — Causative (causing truth/correctness):** Expressing that an agent causes a truth state to obtain.

**CF7 — Causative (causing falsity/deception):** Expressing that an agent causes a false or deceptive state.

**CF8 — Abstract Nominalization:** Deriving an abstract noun denoting the quality or property.

**CF9 — Agentive Nominalization (negative agent):** Deriving a noun denoting a person characterized by lying or deception.

**CF10 — Agentive Nominalization (positive agent):** Deriving a noun denoting a person characterized by truthfulness or honesty.

**CF11 — Degree/Scalar Modification:** Modifying a truth-domain predicate with degree adverbs or scalar intensifiers.

**CF12 — Evidential/Epistemic Marking:** Marking the speaker's epistemic stance toward a truth claim (certainty, inference, hearsay, surprise).

**CF13 — Discourse Marker Grammaticalization:** Using a truth-domain form as a grammaticalized discourse marker (introducing corrections, frank statements, factual clarifications).

**CF14 — Metalinguistic Truth Predicate:** Fixed constructions describing the speech act of truth-telling or lying.

**CF15 — Appearance/Seeming Construction:** Expressing that something appears or seems true/false without commitment to actual truth value.

**CF16 — Verification/Confirmation Construction:** Expressing the act of proving, confirming, or verifying truth through evidence.

**CF17 — Doubt/Questioning Construction:** Expressing doubt about or questioning the truth of a claim.

**CF18 — Contrastive/Corrective Frame:** Explicitly rejecting one alternative and asserting another.

**CF19 — Concessive Construction:** Acknowledging truth of a claim while introducing a contrasting consideration.

**CF20 — Compound Formation ([FALSE/FAKE] + domain noun):** Generating domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit goods, etc.

## Cell-Level Notes (selected cells requiring elaboration)

### D1 (Propositional Correspondence)

**D1 × CF4 (Inchoative +): VARIABLE.** Productive in ka (მართალი გახდა [CORE]), fi (toteutua [CORE]), tr (gerçekleşmek [CORE]), id (menjadi benar [CORE]), ta (uṇmai āku [NATURAL]). Marginal in eu (egia bihurtu da [MARGINAL]) and ko (소문이 사실이 되다 [MARGINAL]). Absent in yo (truth treated as static). The variation is not random: languages that treat truth as a static property to be discovered (yo, ko) resist this construction; languages that treat truth as a state that can emerge or be realized (fi, tr) allow it freely.

**D1 × CF5 (Inchoative −): VARIABLE.** No language has a simple, productive inchoative meaning "become false/untrue." All languages express this through discovery-of-falsity constructions (fi osoittautua vääräksi [CORE], tr yanlış çıkmak [CORE], ko 거짓으로 판명되다 [CORE], id ternyata palsu [CORE]). Finnish explicitly lacks a common expression for this direction (F6: [UNCERTAIN]). Yoruba has no attested construction. The direction is consistently less productive than CF4 across all languages.

**D1 × CF6 (Causative truth): VARIABLE.** No language has a productive morphological causative of a truth adjective meaning "make true" in everyday use. The function is expressed through verification verbs (prove, confirm, verify) in all languages. Direct causatives are marginal in yo (reinterpreted as verification) and absent in ko. In ka, fi, eu, ta, id, tr, lexical verification verbs substitute for morphological causatives.

**D1 × CF7 (Causative falsity): VARIABLE.** More productive than CF6 but still variable. Distortion and falsification causatives are natural in ka (გააყალბა [CORE]), fi (vääristää [CORE]), eu (faltsutu [CORE]), ta (poy kaṭṭu [NATURAL]), id (memalsukan [CORE]), tr (yalanlamak [CORE]). In yo, causing someone to lie is natural (wọ́n mú kí ó pa irọ́ [CORE]) but causing a proposition to be false is marginal. In ko, falsification of evidence is natural (조작하다 [CORE]) but direct causative of propositional falsity is absent.

**D1 × CF10 (Agentive positive): BLOCKED.** No language productively derives an agentive noun for "truth-teller" through the same morphological strategy used for "liar." Where a form exists, it is marginal (ka მართლმოსაუბრე [MARGINAL], tr doğrucu [MARGINAL]) or requires a compound or periphrastic construction (yo ọmọ òtítọ́, ko 진실을 말하는 사람). This is the most consistent cross-language blocking in the dataset.

**D1 × CF11 (Degree/Scalar): VARIABLE.** Degree modification of core propositional truth predicates is resisted in yo (tọ́ jù ú lọ [MARGINAL]), ko (no attested degree modification), fi (truth-becoming not gradual), eu (no degree modification attested), ta (muḻu uṇmai [MARGINAL]). Allowed in ka (ძალიან მართალი [CORE]), id (sangat benar [CORE]), tr (çok doğru [CORE]). The variation correlates with whether the language treats truth as binary or scalar.

### D2 (Deliberate Deception)

**D2 × CF4 (Inchoative +): VARIABLE.** "Becoming a liar" or "starting to lie" is natural for character change (fi mulai berbohong [CORE], ko 거짓말하기 시작하다 [CORE]) but "a lie becoming true" is conceptually incoherent and not attested. The inchoative applies to the agent's character, not to the truth-value of the lie.

**D2 × CF5 (Inchoative −): VARIABLE.** "A lie being exposed" is natural across all languages (ka ტყუილი გამოვლინდა, yo irọ́ ni [CORE], fi paljastua valheeksi [CORE], eu gezurra agertu da [CORE], ta poy ampalamāku [NATURAL], id ternyata bohong [CORE], tr yalan çıkmak [CORE], ko 거짓말이 들통나다 [CORE]). This is a discovery construction, not a true inchoative of truth-value change.

**D2 × CF9 (Agentive negative): ALLOWED.** Every language has a dedicated agentive form for "liar": ka მატყუარა, yo apurọ́, fi valehtelija, eu gezurlari, ta poyyan/poyyar, id pembohong, tr yalancı, ko 거짓말쟁이. This is the most consistently ALLOWED agentive pattern in the dataset.

**D2 × CF10 (Agentive positive): BLOCKED.** No language productively derives an agentive for "truth-teller" parallel to the "liar" agentive. See D1 × CF10 note.

**D2 × CF20 (Compound [FALSE+N]): ALLOWED.** All eight languages productively generate domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit money, fake news, etc. through [deception modifier + domain noun]: ka ყალბი ფული/ყალბი დოკუმენტი, yo irọ́ compounds, fi väärä hälytys/väärä todistus, eu gezurrezko agiri/gezurrezko lekukotza, ta poy cāṭci/poy āvaṇam, id uang palsu/dokumen palsu, tr sahte para/sahte belge, ko 거짓증언/거짓정보. This is the most uniformly ALLOWED compound pattern in the dataset.

### D3 (Unintentional Error)

**D3 × CF9 (Agentive negative): VARIABLE.** Some languages have agentive forms for "one who errs" but these are less consistently lexicalized than "liar" forms. In fi, no dedicated agentive for "one who makes errors" is documented. In id, penyalah exists but is more "abuser/misuser" than "one who errs." In tr, hatalı is adjectival rather than agentive. The pattern is less consistent than D2 × CF9.

**D3 × CF12 (Evidential/Epistemic): VARIABLE.** Epistemic marking of unintentional error is less systematically documented than for propositional truth. Some languages mark the discovery of error evidentially (fi osoittautua vääräksi, id ternyata salah) but dedicated epistemic particles for error are not documented.

### D4 (Authenticity of Objects)

**D4 × CF4 (Inchoative +): VARIABLE.** "Turning out to be genuine" is natural in most languages (fi terbukti asli [CORE], id ternyata asli [CORE], tr gerçek çıkmak [CORE], ko 진품으로 판명되다 [CORE]) but is expressed as discovery rather than change of state. "Becoming genuine" in the sense of a fake becoming real is pragmatically constrained across languages.

**D4 × CF9 (Agentive negative): VARIABLE.** Forger/counterfeiter agentives exist in several languages (fi pemalsu [CORE], id pemalsu [CORE], tr sahtekâr [CORE], eu gezurlari [CORE]) but are not as universally documented as "liar" agentives. The pattern is present but less consistent.

**D4 × CF11 (Degree/Scalar): VARIABLE.** Authenticity is treated as more gradable than propositional truth in some languages (fi benar-benar asli "genuinely authentic" [CORE], id sangat asli [CORE]) but as binary in others. Less resistance to degree modification than D1.

### D5 (Honesty and Moral Character)

**D5 × CF4 (Inchoative +): ALLOWED.** Character change toward honesty is natural across all languages with F6 data: fi hän muuttui rehellisemmäksi [CORE], eu geu saram-i jeongjikhaejyeotda [CORE], ta (character change constructions natural), id menjadi jujur [CORE], tr dürüst olmak [CORE], ko 정직해졌다 [CORE]. This contrasts with D1 × CF4, which is VARIABLE. Character is treated as changeable; propositional truth is not (in several languages).

**D5 × CF5 (Inchoative −): VARIABLE.** "Becoming dishonest" or "losing integrity" is attested in some languages (fi muuttua epärehelliseksi, tr dürüstlüğünü kaybetmek) but less systematically documented than the positive direction.

**D5 × CF6 (Causative truth): VARIABLE.** "Making someone honest" is pragmatically constrained in several languages. In ko, 사람을 정직하게 만들다 is [MARGINAL]; preferred alternatives are 정직하게 키우다 (raise honestly) or 정직함을 가르치다 (teach honesty). In id, no productive causative for "make honest" is documented. The constraint is less absolute than for D1 × CF6 but present.

**D5 × CF9 (Agentive negative): ALLOWED.** Hypocrite/insincere person agentives are documented: ka ლიცემერი [CORE], yo (ìwà búburú characterizations), fi valehtelija [CORE], eu gezurlari [CORE], ta poyyar [CORE], id pembohong [CORE], tr yalancı [CORE], ko 거짓말쟁이 [CORE]. These overlap with D2 × CF9.

**D5 × CF10 (Agentive positive): BLOCKED.** No language productively derives an agentive for "honest person" through the same morphological strategy used for "liar/hypocrite." Where forms exist (ka გულწრფელი as adjective, tr dürüst as adjective), they are adjectival rather than agentive. The blocking parallels D1 × CF10 and D2 × CF10.

### D6 (Moral Rightness and Justice)

**D6 × CF9 (Agentive negative): INSUFFICIENT DATA.** Agentive forms for "unjust person" or "wrongdoer" are not systematically documented in the F-phase tables as part of the truth domain. Some forms exist (tr haksız as adjective, id penyalah as "abuser") but the pattern is not consistently documented.

**D6 × CF10 (Agentive positive): INSUFFICIENT DATA.** Agentive forms for "just person" or "righteous person" are not systematically documented. Some forms exist (yo ẹni òtítọ́ "person of truth," tr adil as adjective) but the pattern is not consistently documented.

**D6 × CF11 (Degree/Scalar): VARIABLE.** Justice and fairness terms appear more gradable than propositional truth terms. Comparative forms are attested (tr daha adil "more just," ko 더 정당하다 "more legitimate") but not systematically documented across all languages.

### D7 (Reliability and Credibility)

**D7 × CF4 (Inchoative +): ALLOWED.** Gaining credibility or becoming reliable is natural across languages: ka სანდო გახდა [CORE], fi luotettavuus kasvaa [CORE], eu fidagarria bihurtu [CORE], ta nampakam āku [NATURAL], id menjadi terpercaya [CORE], tr güvenilir hale gelmek [CORE], ko 신빙성이 높아지다 [CORE]. Reliability is treated as a gradable, changeable property.

**D7 × CF5 (Inchoative −): ALLOWED.** Losing credibility is equally natural: ka სანდოობა დაკარგა [CORE], fi luotettavuus heikkenee [CORE], eu zalantzagarria bihurtu [CORE], id kehilangan kepercayaan [CORE], tr güvenilirliğini kaybetmek [CORE], ko 신빙성이 떨어지다 [CORE]. Both directions are productive, contrasting with D1 where the negative direction is consistently weaker.

**D7 × CF10 (Agentive positive): VARIABLE.** "Reliable person" or "credible witness" agentives are partially documented (fi luotettava henkilö as adjectival phrase, tr güvenilir kişi as adjectival phrase) but not as morphologically derived agentives. The pattern is present but less consistently lexicalized than D2 × CF9.

**D7 × CF11 (Degree/Scalar): ALLOWED.** Reliability and credibility are freely gradable across all languages: ka უფრო სანდო [CORE], yo dájúdájú [CORE], fi erittäin luotettava [CORE], eu oso fidagarria [CORE], ta nampakamāka [NATURAL], id sangat terpercaya [CORE], tr çok güvenilir [CORE], ko 신빙성이 높다 [CORE]. This contrasts with D1 × CF11 (VARIABLE) and reflects the consistent cross-language pattern that reliability is scalar while propositional truth is treated as binary in several languages.

### D8 (Epistemic Certainty and Doubt)

**D8 × CF4 (Inchoative +): ALLOWED.** Becoming certain or gaining certainty is natural: ka ეჭვი გაქრა [CORE], yo ìdájú pọ̀ sí i [CORE], fi varmuus kasvaa [CORE], eu ziurtasuna handitu [CORE], ta uṟuti āku [NATURAL], id menjadi yakin [CORE], tr kesinleşmek [CORE], ko 확실해지다 [CORE].

**D8 × CF5 (Inchoative −): ALLOWED.** Becoming uncertain or losing certainty is equally natural: ka ეჭვი გაჩნდა [CORE], fi epävarmuus kasvaa [CORE], eu zalantza sortu [CORE], ta cantēkam ēṟpaṭu [NATURAL], id menjadi ragu [CORE], tr şüphelenmek [CORE], ko 불확실해지다 [CORE]. Both directions are productive, unlike D1.

**D8 × CF11 (Degree/Scalar): ALLOWED.** Certainty and doubt are freely gradable: yo dájúdájú [CORE], fi hyvin todennäköinen [CORE], eu oso ziurra [CORE], ta uṟutiyāka [NATURAL], id sangat yakin [CORE], tr kesinlikle [CORE], ko 매우 확실하다 [CORE].

### D9 (Appearance vs. Underlying Reality)

**D9 × CF15 (Appearance/Seeming): ALLOWED.** Appearance constructions are natural for this dimension across all languages: ka მოჩვენებითი სიმართლე [CORE], yo ó hàn bí òtítọ́ [CORE], fi näyttää todelta [CORE], eu egiazko itxura du [CORE], ta uṇmai eṉṟu terikiṟatu [NATURAL], id kelihatannya benar [CORE], tr doğru gibi görünmek [CORE], ko 사실인 것 같다 [CORE]. This is the most natural constructional function for D9.

**D9 × CF8 (Abstract Nominalization): VARIABLE.** Abstract nouns for "appearance" or "seeming" are documented in some languages (ka მოჩვენება [CORE], eu itxura [CORE], ta māyai [CORE]) but not systematically across all eight.

### D10 (Validity and Legitimacy)

## Narrative Analysis

### Strongly Recurrent Constraints

**1. The agentive asymmetry (D1/D2/D5 × CF10 BLOCKED) is the most consistent cross-language constraint in the dataset.**

Across all eight languages, the morphological pathway that creates "liar" agentives (D2 × CF9: ALLOWED) does not productively create "truth-teller" agentives (D1/D2/D5 × CF10: BLOCKED). Every language has a dedicated agentive form for "liar" (ka მატყუარა, yo apurọ́, fi valehtelija, eu gezurlari, ta poyyan/poyyar, id pembohong, tr yalancı, ko 거짓말쟁이). No language productively derives a parallel form for "truth-teller" through the same morphological strategy. Where positive-agent forms exist, they are marginal (ka მართლმოსაუბრე [MARGINAL], tr doğrucu [MARGINAL]) or require compounds or periphrastic constructions (yo ọmọ òtítọ́, ko 진실을 말하는 사람). This asymmetry is not explained by morphological constraints: the same suffixes that create "liar" forms could in principle create "truth-teller" forms. It appears to reflect a cross-linguistic tendency to lexicalize the negative agent more readily than the positive agent in the deception domain.

**2. The inchoative asymmetry (D1 × CF4 VARIABLE vs. D1 × CF5 VARIABLE, with CF5 consistently weaker) is cross-linguistically robust.**

Across all eight languages, constructions expressing "becoming true" or "coming true" (CF4) are more productive and more naturally expressed than constructions expressing "becoming false" or "turning out to be a lie" (CF5). The positive direction is realized through dedicated inchoative verbs in fi (toteutua), tr (gerçekleşmek), and eu (egiaztatu da), and through discovery/revelation verbs in ko (드러나다) and yo (absent). The negative direction is consistently expressed through discovery-of-falsity constructions rather than true inchoatives: something is "revealed as false," "proven wrong," or "exposed as a lie" rather than simply "becoming false." Finnish explicitly lacks a common expression for the negative direction (F6: [UNCERTAIN]). Yoruba lacks attested constructions for both directions. This asymmetry is not a gap in the data but a consistent cross-language pattern.

**3. The causative asymmetry (D1 × CF6 VARIABLE vs. D1 × CF7 VARIABLE, with CF7 consistently more productive) is cross-linguistically robust.**

Causing deception (CF7) is consistently more morphologically productive than causing truth (CF6) across languages. In Yoruba and Korean, causing propositional truth states is pragmatically constrained or absent as a productive construction, while causing deception is natural. In all other languages, causing truth is expressed through verification and confirmation verbs (prove, confirm, verify) rather than through direct causatives of truth adjectives. This suggests that across languages, truth is conceptualized as something discovered or established through evidence rather than something directly caused by an agent.

**4. The compound formation pattern (D2 × CF20 ALLOWED) is the most uniformly attested constructional pattern in the dataset.**

All eight languages productively generate domain-specific terms for false documents, false testimony, counterfeit money, and fake news through the structural pattern [deception modifier + domain noun]. The specific modifier varies (ka ყალბი, yo irọ́, fi väärä, eu gezurrezko, ta poy, id palsu, tr sahte, ko 거짓), but the compounding strategy is consistent. This is the most formally uniform cross-language pattern in the dataset.

**5. Discourse marker grammaticalization (D1 × CF13 ALLOWED) is universal.**

In all eight languages, at least one truth-domain form has grammaticalized into a discourse marker that introduces corrections, frank statements, or factual clarifications. The forms range from fully grammaticalized (fi totta kai, id ternyata) to semi-transparent (ka მართლა?, ko 사실은). This pattern appears across unrelated language families and is not explained by contact.

**6. Reliability and similarity are freely gradable (D7 × CF11 ALLOWED, D11 × CF11 ALLOWED) while propositional truth is not (D1 × CF11 VARIABLE).**

The contrast between D7 and D11 (both ALLOWED for degree modification) and D1 (VARIABLE, with several languages showing resistance) is consistent across the dataset. Languages that treat propositional truth as binary (yo, ko, fi, eu, ta) freely allow degree modification of reliability and similarity terms. This suggests that the resistance to degree modification is specific to propositional truth rather than a general property of the truth domain.

**7. Character change (D5 × CF4 ALLOWED) is more productive than propositional truth change (D1 × CF4 VARIABLE).**

Across all languages with F6 data, inchoative constructions for character change toward honesty are natural (fi hän muuttui rehellisemmäksi, ko 정직해졌다, id menjadi jujur, tr dürüst olmak). This contrasts with D1 × CF4, which is VARIABLE and resisted in yo and ko. The distinction suggests that languages treat character as a changeable property while treating propositional truth as a static property to be discovered.

**8. Validity and legitimacy causatives (D10 × CF6 ALLOWED, D10 × CF7 ALLOWED) are more productive than propositional truth causatives (D1 × CF6 VARIABLE).**

Causing something to become valid or invalid is natural across languages (id mengesahkan/membatalkan, tr meşrulaştırmak/geçersiz kılmak, fi oikeuttaa/mitätöidä). This contrasts with D1 × CF6, where causing propositional truth is constrained. The distinction suggests that validity and legitimacy are treated as institutional or procedural properties that can be conferred or revoked by agents, while propositional truth is not.

### Likely Prohibitions or Avoidance Patterns

**Prohibition 1: Productive agentive nominalization for "truth-teller" (CF10 for D1, D2, D5).**
The evidence across all eight languages supports treating this as a genuine cross-linguistic avoidance pattern rather than a gap in the data. The morphological resources exist in every language; the forms are simply not produced. This is the strongest candidate for a cross-linguistic prohibition in the dataset.

**Prohibition 2: Simple inchoative "become false/untrue" for propositions (D1 × CF5).**
No language has a simple, productive inchoative verb meaning "become false/untrue" for propositions. The function is consistently expressed through discovery-of-falsity constructions. Finnish explicitly documents this as a gap. Yoruba lacks any construction. This is a strong avoidance pattern, though it falls short of a complete prohibition since some languages (tr, id) have constructions that approach this meaning through resultative discovery verbs.

**Prohibition 3: Direct causative "make true" for propositions (D1 × CF6).**
No language has a productive morphological causative of a truth adjective meaning "make true" in everyday use. The function is consistently expressed through verification verbs. Yoruba and Korean show the strongest resistance; other languages show the same substitution pattern. This is a strong avoidance pattern.

**Avoidance pattern: Degree modification of core propositional truth predicates (D1 × CF11).**
Five of eight languages show documented resistance to scalar degree modification of core truth predicates (yo, ko, fi, eu, ta). Three languages allow it (ka, id, tr). This is an avoidance pattern rather than a prohibition, since it is not universal.

### Important Divergences, Substitutions, and Asymmetries

**Divergence 1: Yoruba and Korean vs. other languages on inchoative and causative constructions for D1.**
Yoruba and Korean show the strongest resistance to inchoative and causative constructions for propositional truth. Both languages treat truth as a static property to be discovered rather than a state that changes or is caused. The other six languages show more productive inchoative and causative patterns, though all show the asymmetry between positive and negative directions and between truth-causation and falsity-causation.

**Divergence 2: Indonesian vs. other languages on morphological negation strategy.**
Indonesian uses analytic negation (tidak + adjective) for all truth-domain negations rather than a prefix or suffix. This means Indonesian lacks the morphological antonym pairs found in tr (-lı/-sız), ta (-āṉa/-aṟṟa), fi (epä-), ko (부-/불-), and eu (-garri on contrasting bases). Indonesian's oppositions are consistently constructional rather than morphological in the truth domain.

**Divergence 3: Basque pejorative nominalization asymmetry.**
Basque uses the neutral -tasun suffix for positive moral qualities (zintzotasuna "honesty") and the pejorative -keria suffix for negative moral qualities (gezurkeria "habitual lying," faltsukeria "falseness"). This formal asymmetry encoding a moral asymmetry is not found in other languages, where both positive and negative nominalizations use the same suffix pattern.

**Divergence 4: Tamil register stratification.**
Tamil is the only language with a three-way register stratification of truth vocabulary: uṇmai (general), mey (literary/classical/spiritual), nijam (colloquial/borrowed). The mey root generates an extensive cluster of philosophical and spiritual compounds (meyññāṉam, meyyanpu, mey teyvam) that are absent for uṇmai. No other language shows this degree of register-based lexical differentiation within the truth domain.

**Divergence 5: Georgian ტყუილი extension to "futile/in vain."**
The semantic extension of the "lie/false" root to cover "futile" or "in vain" is documented only in Georgian (ტყუილად "in vain," ტყუილუბრალოდ "pointlessly"). This is a single-language observation with no parallel in the other seven languages.

**Substitution 1: Discovery/revelation verbs substitute for inchoative in the truth domain.**
Across yo, ko, fi, and eu, the inchoative construction ("become true") is replaced by or reinterpreted as a discovery construction ("come to light," "be revealed," "be brought to light"). This substitution reflects a conceptual framing: truth does not change its value; it is uncovered.

**Substitution 2: Verification verbs substitute for causative "make true."**
In all eight languages, the preferred way to express causing something to be true is through lexical verification verbs (prove, confirm, verify, reveal) rather than through morphological causatives of truth adjectives. This substitution is most complete in yo and ko.

**Substitution 3: Periphrastic constructions substitute for agentive "truth-teller."**
In all eight languages, the morphological agentive that creates "liar" forms does not productively create "truth-teller" forms. Periphrastic constructions (compounds, relative clauses, noun phrases) substitute in all languages.

**Asymmetry: Metalinguistic truth predicates (D1/D2 × CF14).**
In at least four languages (yo, fi, ta, ko), truth-telling is expressed through a verb + object construction while lying has a dedicated verb or compound verb. In yo, the verb used with "lie" (pa "kill/strike") is different from the verb used with "truth" (sọ "speak"), and the pairing is conventionalized. In ta, truth takes the accusative case marker while lie appears without case marking. In ko, lying is expressed as a compound verb (거짓말하다) while truth-telling requires a separate object (진실을 말하다). These asymmetries consistently show that the deception side is more morphologically condensed than the truth side.
