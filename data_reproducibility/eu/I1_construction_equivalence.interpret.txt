## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 BECOME → Specialized Lexicalized Verbs

The generic inchoative construction with *bihurtu* (become) is rated **[MARGINAL]** for truth-domain concepts. The language strongly prefers specialized lexicalized verbs:
- *egiaztu* (to be verified/confirmed) instead of *egia bihurtu*
- *okertu* (to go wrong) instead of *oker bihurtu*

This represents a systematic preference for derivational morphology over periphrastic constructions for change-of-state in the truth domain.

### 2.2 REVELATION as Distinct Operator

Basque maintains REVELATION as a distinct construction type using the locative *agerian* (in the open) combined with position/motion verbs (*jarri* "put", *utzi* "leave", *egon* "be"). This does NOT substitute for BECOME but rather expresses a separate semantic operation: making manifest or exposing what was hidden. Examples:
- *Egia agerian jarri dut* (I exposed the truth)
- *Gezurra agerian dago* (The lie is exposed)

This construction is **[CORE]** and productive, not a substitute for inchoative meaning.

### 2.3 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION: Shared Morphological Pattern

Both operators use the same derivational pattern (*-tu/-du* causative suffix on roots):
- *egiaztatu* (verify) - from *egiaz-* root
- *gezurtatu* (falsify) - from *gezur-* root

However, they are distinct lexicalized verbs, not a single construction with different arguments. The morphological parallelism is productive but each verb is independently lexicalized.

### 2.4 CAUSATION: Factual vs. Character Asymmetry

**Factual causation** (making something true/false/correct) is robustly expressed through lexicalized causative verbs:
- *egiaztatu* (verify/make true)
- *zuzendu* (correct/make right)
- *gezurtatu* (falsify/make false)
- *okertu* (make wrong)

**Character causation** (making someone truthful/deceptive) shows a **structural gap**. No common expression exists for "make someone a liar" or "make someone truthful." The P4a data notes: "For 'truth-teller,' no single lexicalized form exists; periphrastic *egia esaten duena* (one who tells truth) is used."

Circumlocution would require constructions like:
- *gezurlari bihurtu* (become a liar) - using generic BECOME
- *egia esatera behartu* (force to tell truth) - using force/compulsion verbs

This represents a **conceptual gap** where the language does not readily encode causation of character traits in the truth domain.

### 2.5 NEGATION: Analytic, Not Morphological

Negation is consistently analytic, using the particle *ez* before the finite verb:
- *Hau ez da egia* (This is not true)
- *Zure esana ez da zuzena* (Your statement is not correct)

No morphological negation (negative prefix/suffix on truth-domain roots) is attested in the data. The position of *ez* is fixed (immediately pre-verbal), and the predicate remains unchanged.

### 2.6 Light Verb Constructions as Semantic Carriers

Basque extensively uses NOUN + VERB collocations where the noun carries semantic weight:
- *egia esan* (tell truth) - literally "truth say"
- *gezurra esan* (tell lie) - literally "lie say"

These function as semi-lexicalized units but are syntactically compositional. The verb *esan* (say) serves as the grammatical carrier while *egia/gezurra* provides the truth-domain semantics. This pattern is **[CORE]** and highly productive.

### 2.7 Doubt/Questioning as Parallel to Revelation

The construction *zalantzan jarri/egon* (call into question / be in doubt) parallels the REVELATION construction structurally:
- Locative case (*zalantzan* "in doubt") + position verb
- *Haren zintzotasuna zalantzan jarri dut* (I called his/her honesty into question)

This represents a distinct epistemic operator (expressing uncertainty) rather than a substitute for DISCONFIRMATION. It is **[CORE]** and productive.

### 2.8 Positive vs. Negative Asymmetries

No systematic asymmetries appear between positive (truth/correctness) and negative (falsehood/error) forms. Both sides of the truth domain show:
- Predicative adjectives: *egia/gezurra*, *zuzen/oker*
- Attributive forms: *egiazko/gezurrezko*
- Causative verbs: *egiaztatu/gezurtatu*, *zuzendu/okertu*
- Abstract nominalizations: *egiazkotasuna/gezurtasuna*
- Agent nominalizations: *gezurlari* (liar) - though "truth-teller" lacks a single lexeme

The only notable gap is the absence of a single-word agent noun for "truth-teller," requiring periphrastic *egia esaten duena*.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

**CAUSATION (character)**: No common construction exists for causing someone to become truthful or deceptive. The language lacks a productive pattern for "make X a liar" or "make X truthful." Circumlocution requires generic BECOME (*gezurlari bihurtu*) or compulsion verbs (*egia esatera behartu*).

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

**Agent noun for "truth-teller"**: No single lexicalized form exists. The language uses periphrastic *egia esaten duena* (one who tells truth). This contrasts with *gezurlari* (liar), which is fully lexicalized.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

**Character causation** represents a conceptual gap where the language does not readily encode the causation of truth-related character traits. This is distinct from factual causation (making statements/documents true/false), which is robustly expressed.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Agent nominalization asymmetry**: 
- Negative agent (*gezurlari* "liar") is lexicalized with *-lari* suffix
- Positive agent ("truth-teller") requires periphrastic expression
- This is the only clear positive/negative asymmetry in the data

### 3.5 Non-Gaps (Clarifications)

**Generic BECOME is not missing**: The construction *X bihurtu da* exists but is **[MARGINAL]** for truth-domain concepts because specialized lexicalized verbs (*egiaztu*, *okertu*) are strongly preferred. This is a preference pattern, not a structural gap.

**DISCONFIRMATION is not missing**: *Gezurtatu* (falsify) directly expresses this operator, though it may be less frequent than *egiaztatu* (verify) in actual usage.

## 6. Summary Statement

Basque shows a **highly productive derivational morphology** for truth-domain concepts, with robust support for most canonical CLCA operators. The primary gaps are:

1. **Character causation** (structural/conceptual gap)
2. **Single-word "truth-teller"** (lexical gap)
3. **Generic BECOME** (functional substitution by specialized verbs)

The language strongly prefers **lexicalized causative verbs** over periphrastic constructions and maintains **distinct constructions** for REVELATION (exposure) vs. BECOME (change of state). Negation is consistently **analytic** (particle *ez*), and the language shows **no systematic positive/negative asymmetries** except in agent nominalization.
