# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
## Language: English (en)

The following groupings are derived solely from observed usage patterns documented in the P1, P1b, P2a, and P2b outputs — collocations, contrasts, register notes, and construction behavior. Groups overlap; several items appear in more than one; some items remain ungrouped.

---

### GROUP 1 — Statements matching the facts

**Definition:** Terms applied to assertions, claims, and accounts that do or do not correspond to fact. In usage these cluster around evaluating *what is said* ("that is true," "an inaccurate report"), and they are routinely contrasted as positive/negative pairs. Common in factual, journalistic, and everyday evaluative discourse.

**Representative expressions:**
- true / untrue
- false
- accurate / inaccurate
- correct / incorrect
- exact ("the exact figure," "to be exact")
- right / wrong ("get it right," "that's right")

**Notes:** *right* and *correct* straddle this group and Group 6 (conformity to standards); *exact* leans toward Group 2 in technical measurement contexts.

---

### GROUP 2 — Precision and close correspondence

**Definition:** Terms used to assess how closely a representation, measurement, or copy matches a target, with attention to degree of deviation. Usage is concentrated in technical, journalistic, and measurement contexts ("an exact copy," "accurate measurement," "trueness" in metrology).

**Representative expressions:**
- accurate (accuracy, accurately)
- exact (exactness, exactitude)
- correct (in the "free from error" sense)
- trueness / true up / out of true (mechanical alignment senses)

**Notes:** The alignment senses of *true* are explicitly flagged as peripheral in P1b/P2b; they share the "deviation from a standard" usage but not the truth-of-statements usage.

---

### GROUP 3 — Genuine vs. imitation objects

**Definition:** Terms used to contrast a real or original item against a copy, fake, or imitation, frequently in commercial, material, and authentication contexts ("genuine leather," "a fake designer bag," "counterfeit money"). Usage clusters around objects and their provenance.

**Representative expressions:**
- genuine (the genuine article)
- real (the real thing, the real deal, the real McCoy)
- authentic (authenticate, authentication)
- fake (fake out, fake designer bag)
- counterfeit (counterfeiter, counterfeiting)
- forged (forgery, forger)
- bona fide / bona fides

**Notes:** This group is held together strongly by the authentication-idiom cluster noted in P2b (*the genuine article*, *the real thing*, *the real deal*, *the real McCoy*). *fake*, *counterfeit*, and *forged* form the negative pole; *forged* and *counterfeit* additionally carry the legal connotation linking them to Group 8.

---

### GROUP 4 — Honesty and sincerity of persons

**Definition:** Terms evaluating persons and their expressions as free from pretense or deceit, concentrated in moral and interpersonal contexts ("a sincere apology," "an honest answer"). Usage frequently appears in candor-marking discourse phrases and letter-closing formulas.

**Representative expressions:**
- honest / dishonest (to be honest, honest to God, honest mistake)
- sincere / insincere (Yours sincerely)
- truthful (truthfulness)
- genuine (in the "genuine concern," "genuine person" sense)
- veracious (formal/literary)

**Notes:** *genuine* overlaps with Group 3 (objects) and Group 4 (sincerity of persons) — usage splits between material authenticity and moral sincerity. The candor discourse markers (*to be honest*, *truth be told*, *to tell the truth*) cluster across this group and Group 5.

---

### GROUP 5 — Loyalty and fidelity

**Definition:** Terms used for steadfastness of persons or faithfulness of representations to their source, spanning interpersonal loyalty and representational fidelity ("a faithful spouse," "a faithful translation," "true to one's word"). Usage is distinct from factual truth and is flagged in P1b as a peripheral extension of *true*.

**Representative expressions:**
- faithful / unfaithful (the faithful, Yours faithfully)
- true (true to one's word, true colors, true-blue, tried and true)
- trustworthy / reliable (in the "dependable person" sense)

**Notes:** *faithful* bridges this group and Group 6 via the "faithful translation" (accurate representation) sense. The *true*-idioms here exploit the loyalty sense rather than the factual sense.

---

### GROUP 6 — Conformity to rule, standard, or propriety

**Definition:** Terms marking conduct, procedure, or usage as conforming to an accepted standard, rule, or norm, used in normative, etiquette, legal, and procedural contexts ("proper procedure," "the correct answer," "a just verdict," "the right thing to do"). Usage shades from factual correctness into moral and social acceptability.

**Representative expressions:**
- correct (correctly, politically correct)
- proper (properly, right and proper, propriety, improper)
- right (rightful, righteous, the right thing, by rights)
- just / unjust (justice, justify, justifiable)
- legitimate / illegitimate (legitimacy)
- valid / invalid

**Notes:** This group is internally diverse: *just*, *right*, *righteous* lean moral; *proper* leans etiquette; *valid*, *legitimate* lean legal/administrative. *correct* and *right* overlap with Group 1 (factual correctness).

---

### GROUP 7 — Soundness of arguments and claims

**Definition:** Terms used to evaluate reasoning, claims, and propositions for logical soundness or legal/administrative force, in argumentative, logical, and scholarly contexts ("a valid argument," "a spurious argument," "truth-value"). Usage targets the structure or warrant of a claim rather than its bare factual content.

**Representative expressions:**
- valid / invalid (validity, validate)
- spurious (spuriously)
- legitimate ("a legitimate concern")
- truth-value / truth-functional (logic)
- falsifiable (philosophy of science)

**Notes:** *valid* and *legitimate* overlap with Group 6; *spurious* overlaps with Group 3 (falsely-presented objects/claims). The logic-specific *truth-* compounds are a small specialized sub-cluster.

---

### GROUP 8 — Believability and trustworthiness of sources and evidence

**Definition:** Terms applied to sources, witnesses, and claims judged worthy of belief or reliance, concentrated in evidential, journalistic, and legal contexts ("a reliable witness," "a credible threat," "a plausible explanation"). Usage assesses whether something *deserves* belief, without necessarily asserting its truth.

**Representative expressions:**
- reliable / unreliable (reliability)
- trustworthy (trustworthiness)
- credible / incredible (credibility) — note *credible threat*
- plausible / implausible (plausibility, plausible deniability)
- veracious (a veracious witness)

**Notes:** P2b explicitly distinguishes *plausible* and *credible* as marking belief-worthiness "without asserting truth," setting this group apart from Group 1. *reliable* and *trustworthy* overlap with Group 5 (dependable persons); *incredible* has a dominant intensifier sense that falls outside the group.

---

### GROUP 9 — Resemblance and representational realism

**Definition:** Terms used to assess how convincingly an image, depiction, or artwork resembles its subject, concentrated in aesthetic, literary, and critical registers ("a lifelike portrait," literary verisimilitude). Usage targets depictions rather than statements or objects.

**Representative expressions:**
- lifelike
- verisimilitude / verisimilar
- mimetic / mimesis
- realistic / realism (from *real*)
- faithful ("a faithful representation")

**Notes:** This group is largely marginal/scholarly (*verisimilitude*, *mimetic* flagged [MARGINAL]) except *lifelike* and *realistic*. *faithful* and *accurate* feed this group through the "accurate representation" sense.

---

### GROUP 10 — Candor-marking discourse phrases

**Definition:** Fixed conversational phrases that preface a frank or qualified statement, functioning at clause level rather than describing an object or person. Identified in P2b as a distinct cluster of hedges around honesty and precision.

**Representative expressions:**
- truth be told
- to tell (you) the truth
- to be honest
- to be exact
- correct me if I'm wrong

**Notes:** These draw lexically on Groups 1, 4, and 2 but behave distinctly as discourse markers, not predicates. Grouped here on usage (sentential, conversational) rather than meaning.

---

### Cross-cutting observations (usage-based, from the source data)

- **Native monosyllabic core vs. Latinate periphery:** *true, false, real, right, fake, honest* dominate idiom and construction formation (Groups 1, 3, 4, 5, and 10), while Latinate items (*accurate, authentic, valid, legitimate, counterfeit, spurious, veracious, verisimilitude, mimetic, truthful, forged, trustworthy*) yield few or no fixed constructions. This is a strong usage asymmetry noted in P2b, cutting across several groups.
- **Positive/negative antonym pairing** is most systematic in Groups 1, 4, 6, 7, 8 (true/untrue, honest/dishonest, valid/invalid, etc.), but the negating prefix is lexically unpredictable (*un-*, *in-*, *im-*, *il-*, *dis-*).
- **Antonym asymmetry:** *false* generates fewer idioms than *true*, and some positive items (*accurate, valid, legitimate*) have no idiomatic counterpart at all — so groups should not be read as balanced.

---

### Items that resist clean grouping

- **very / verily / veritable** — etymologically in the *ver-* "true" family but used overwhelmingly as **intensifiers**; flagged in P1b as a peripheral extension away from the truth sense. Do not fit the evaluative groups.
- **verdict, aver** — *ver-* family items used for **legal judgment / speech-act assertion**; outside the descriptive-evaluation usage of the groups.
- **rectangle, rectilinear, erect, direct, rectify (chemistry/electronics)** — share the *rect-* root but used for **physical straightness/orientation**, explicitly flagged as outside the domain.
- **right (directional / entitlement senses)** — *turn right*, *human rights*, *right of way* — spatial and legal-entitlement uses unrelated to the evaluative senses in Groups 1 and 6.
- **come true / a dream come true** — uses *true* in the **realization/actualization** sense; does not fit the statement-truth or genuineness groups.
- **moment of truth, truth serum, post-truth, home truths, gospel truth** — *truth* compounds with domain-specific or idiomatic meanings (decisive test, pharmacology, political commentary) that diverge from the core evaluative usage.
- **realize / realization** — dominant "become aware" sense (noted in P2a) sits outside all groups.
- **incredible / incredibly** — dominant intensifier sense ("amazing," "very") falls outside Group 8.

These items are noted but **not forced** into any group.
