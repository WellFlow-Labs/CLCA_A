## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Swahili shows clear direct support for **attribution**, **adnominal modification**, **negation**, and **nominalization** in the truth domain.

For **BECOME**, the attested construction is narrow. The only clearly identified pattern is **-aminika** ‘be credible, be trustworthy’, as in **Taarifa hii inaaminika.** and **Chanzo hicho hakiaminiki.** P4a explicitly states that no equally common inchoative was identified for **kweli**, **uongo**, or **sahihi**. So BECOME is only partially available and is lexically restricted.

No **REVELATION** construction is listed in P4a or P4b. On the supplied evidence, this category is structurally missing. There is therefore no basis here for saying that revelation replaces become.

For **CAUSATION (factual)**, P4a explicitly says **(no common expression identified)**. The only nearby form is **kutia imani**, illustrated by **Hotuba yake ilitia imani.** and **Kiongozi huyo anatia imani.** P4b marks this as marginal and restricts it to causing confidence, not making something true or correct. So factual causation is not directly expressed by an attested productive construction.

For **CAUSATION (character)**, **kutia imani** functions as a periphrastic causation-like expression. Syntactically, it is a verb phrase with a light verb carrier (**-tia**) plus the noun **imani**. It does not directly encode truth or correctness, but rather the causing of confidence/trust.

**VERIFICATION** is not represented by a distinct construction in the supplied material. Instead, truth confirmation is expressed through ordinary attribution or evidential predication: **Ni kweli.**, **Hii ni sahihi.**, **Ni dhahiri kwamba ni uongo.** Descriptively, verification is therefore substituted by **ATTR**.

**DISCONFIRMATION** is likewise not given as a separate operator. Swahili uses either analytic negation (**Si kweli.**, **Jibu hili si sahihi.**) or stronger negative-value attribution (**ni uongo**, **ni batili**). Descriptively, disconfirmation is expressed through **NEGATION** and through ordinary attribution with a negative lexical predicate.

There is a clear asymmetry between positive and negative forms. Plain rejection is strongly represented by **si + predicate**, and stronger rejection can be expressed by **ni uongo / ni batili**. By contrast, positive inchoative or causative formations for “become true/correct” or “make true/correct” are not identified.

Negation is clearly **analytic**, not morphological, in the core predicative pattern: **si + predicate**. In the BECOME examples, polarity also appears analytically in the clause, as in **hakiaminiki**, but P4a/P4b do not present this as a general truth-domain negation operator; the core negation pattern identified is **si + predicate**.

**VERIFICATION** and **DISCONFIRMATION** do not share one dedicated construction. Both are instead distributed across attributional patterns: positive attribution for verification-like meanings, and negated or negative-value attribution for disconfirmation-like meanings.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **REVELATION**: **MISSING (structural gap)**
- No distinct attested construction for **VERIFICATION** as a separate operator: **SUBSTITUTED BY ATTR**
- No distinct attested construction for **DISCONFIRMATION** as a separate operator: **SUBSTITUTED BY NEGATION / ATTR**

### Lexical Gaps
- No equally common inchoative lexical/constructional expression identified for **kweli**, **uongo**, or **sahihi** under BECOME
- No dedicated causative lexical expression identified for “make true/correct”

### Conceptual Gaps
- No conceptual gap is explicitly established in P4a/P4b; the missingness described is constructional or lexical, not stated as inability to express the meanings altogether

### Asymmetric Gaps
- **BECOME** is available only for credibility/trustworthiness via **-aminika**, not broadly across truth/correctness predicates
- **CAUSATION** is only marginally represented by **kutia imani**, which targets confidence/trust rather than truth/correctness
- Negative evaluation is better represented than positive inchoative/causative derivation: **si kweli**, **si sahihi**, **ni uongo**, **ni batili** are attested, while no parallel productive “become true/correct” or “make true/correct” construction is identified

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully available
- **ATTR**
- **ADNOM**
- **NEGATION**
- **NOMINALIZATION**

### Available with substitution
- **VERIFICATION** — **SUBSTITUTED BY ATTR**
- **DISCONFIRMATION** — **SUBSTITUTED BY NEGATION / ATTR**

### Partially available
- **BECOME** — available only in the restricted credibility/trustworthiness pattern **-aminika**
- **CAUSATION (character)** — only via marginal periphrastic **kutia imani**

### Missing entirely
- **REVELATION**
- **CAUSATION (factual)**

### Normalized notes on multi-word expressions
- **ni kweli / ni sahihi / ni dhahiri / ni uongo / ni batili**: predicative attribution constructions
- **si kweli / si sahihi**: analytic negation of predication
- **taarifa sahihi / dawa bandia**: postnominal adjectival/descriptive modification
- **habari za uongo / bidhaa ya asili**: genitive adnominal qualification
- **sema ukweli / alisema uongo**: light-verb construction, with **sema/kusema** as the verbal carrier and **ukweli/uongo** as the semantic object
- **kutia imani**: periphrastic light-verb construction, with **-tia** as the verbal carrier and **imani** as the semantic object
- **kwamba**-clauses: propositional complement construction used with belief/evidential predicates, not a separate truth-operator construction in the supplied data
