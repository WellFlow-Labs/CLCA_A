# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

Yoruba derivational morphology operates primarily through:
1. Nominalization prefixes (ò-, ì-, à-, a-)
2. Gerundive/participial prefixes (tí-, ṣí-)
3. Compounding (noun + noun, verb + noun, etc.)
4. Reduplication (partial or full)
5. Verb serialization (creating complex predicates)

The analysis below documents only attested forms with clear derivational relationships to P1 base items.

---

## DERIVATIONS BY BASE FORM

### BASE: **tọ́** (to be straight, correct, true)

#### Derivation 1: Nominalization
**Base Form:** tọ́  
**Derived Form:** òtítọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** ò- (nominalizer) + tí (relativizer) + tọ́ (be straight/correct)  
**Translation:** truth, truthfulness, honesty  
**Usage:** Primary abstract noun for truth. Used across all registers from everyday speech to formal/religious discourse.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derivation 2: Gerundive nominalization
**Base Form:** tọ́  
**Derived Form:** títọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** tí- (gerund prefix) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** being straight/correct, the state of being true  
**Usage:** Gerundive form used in philosophical and analytical discourse to refer to the process or state of being correct.  
**Status:** [MARGINAL]

#### Derivation 3: Abstract nominalization (alternative)
**Base Form:** tọ́  
**Derived Form:** atọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** a- (nominalizer) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** straightness, correctness  
**Usage:** Less common abstract noun than òtítọ́. Appears in formal discourse but rarely in everyday speech.  
**Status:** [MARGINAL]

#### Derivation 4: Adverbial form through reduplication
**Base Form:** tọ́  
**Derived Form:** tòótọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** tọ́ (reduplicated with tone change)  
**Translation:** truly, truthfully  
**Usage:** Adverbial form typically appearing in the construction "ní tòótọ́" (in truth, truly). Common in emphatic assertions.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derivation 5: Negative nominalization
**Base Form:** tọ́  
**Derived Form:** àìtọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** àì- (negative nominalizer) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** incorrectness, untruth, wrongness  
**Usage:** Nominal form for the state of being incorrect or untrue. Used in evaluative and analytical contexts.  
**Status:** [CORE]

### BASE: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

#### Derivation 1: Verbal base
**Base Form:** irọ́  
**Derived Form:** purọ́  
**Literal Breakdown:** pu (verb root) + rọ́ (extension)  
**Translation:** to lie, to tell falsehood  
**Usage:** The verbal form from which the noun irọ́ derives. Note: This represents the reverse derivational direction—irọ́ is derived from purọ́, not vice versa.  
**Status:** [CORE]

**Note:** No other productive derivations from irọ́ identified. The noun irọ́ itself is derived from the verb purọ́ through i- nominalization.

**Translation:** wrongdoing, offense, misdeed  
**Usage:** Nominal form for wrong actions or offenses. Used in moral and legal contexts.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derivation 4: Gerundive nominalization
**Base Form:** ṣe  
**Derived Form:** ṣíṣe  
**Literal Breakdown:** ṣí- (gerund prefix) + ṣe (to do)  
**Translation:** doing, making, action  
**Usage:** General gerundive form for the act of doing or making. Used broadly across contexts.  
**Status:** [CORE]

#### Derivation 5: Agentive nominalization
**Base Form:** ṣe  
**Derived Form:** aṣe  
**Literal Breakdown:** a- (agentive/nominalizer) + ṣe (to do)

**Note:** The root ṣe is highly productive and appears in numerous compounds (ṣe déédé, ṣe pàtàkì, ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀, etc.), but these are better analyzed as verb serialization or auxiliary constructions rather than derivational morphology of ṣe itself.

## DERIVATIONAL PATTERNS SUMMARY

### Productive Nominalization Patterns

1. **ì- nominalization** (most productive)
   - Creates abstract nouns from verbs
   - Examples: ìgbàgbọ́ (belief), ìbámu (correspondence), ìdájú (certainty), ìjọ (resemblance)

2. **ò- nominalization** (with relativizer tí)
   - Creates abstract nouns, often with moral/evaluative content
   - Example: òtítọ́ (truth)

3. **à- nominalization**
   - Creates abstract nouns, often with negative or error connotations
   - Examples: àṣìṣe (error), àìṣe (wrongdoing), àìbámu (non-correspondence)

4. **àì- negative nominalization** (highly productive)
   - Creates nouns expressing absence or negation of a quality
   - Examples: àìtọ́ (incorrectness), àìmọ̀ (ignorance), àìpé (incompleteness), àìyẹ (inappropriateness)

### Productive Agentive Patterns

1. **a- agentive prefix**
   - Creates agent nouns from verbs
   - Examples: apurọ́ (liar), agbàgbọ́ (believer)
   - Note: Less productive than nominalization patterns

### Productive Gerundive Patterns

1. **CV- reduplication with high tone** (gerund prefix)
   - Creates gerundive/participial forms
   - Examples: títọ́ (being correct), pípé (being complete), gbígbàgbọ́ (believing), pípurọ́ (lying), sísọ (speaking), ṣíṣe (doing)
   - Pattern: First consonant + í + base verb

### Reduplication Patterns

1. **Full reduplication for emphasis**
   - Example: dájúdájú (very certain)
   - Note: Not highly productive; appears with specific lexical items

2. **Tonal reduplication for adverbial forms**
   - Example: tòótọ́ (from tọ́)
   - Note: Limited productivity; creates adverbial/emphatic forms

### Non-Productive or Absent Patterns

1. **Causative derivation**: No systematic causative morphology identified
2. **Applicative derivation**: No systematic applicative morphology identified  
3. **Reciprocal derivation**: Limited to specific verbs (e.g., jọra from jọ)
4. **Diminutive/augmentative**: No systematic patterns identified

### Gaps and Asymmetries

1. Not all verbs show agentive derivation (e.g., tọ́ has no *atọ́ "truth-teller")
2. Some nouns lack transparent verbal bases (e.g., òdodo)
3. Gerundive formation is regular but not universal
4. Ideophonic/adverbial elements (déédé, gidi) do not participate in derivational morphology

## NOTES ON METHODOLOGY

This analysis documents only attested derivational relationships. Forms were included based on:
1. Clear morphological relationship to P1 base items
2. Evidence of productive or semi-productive patterns
3. Semantic transparency and regular meaning contribution

Forms were excluded if:
1. The derivational relationship was unclear or disputed
2. The form appeared to be a separate lexical item without transparent morphology
3. The form was a compound or serial verb construction rather than a derivation

The analysis preserves asymmetries and gaps in the derivational system, reflecting the actual patterns of Yoruba morphology rather than imposing theoretical completeness.
