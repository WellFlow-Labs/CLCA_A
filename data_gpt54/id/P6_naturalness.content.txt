## 1. BECOMING TRUE / CORRECT / ACCEPTED AS TRUE

### Pattern
**X menjadi benar** / **X terbukti benar** / **X ternyata benar**

### Examples
- *Dugaan itu ternyata benar.* → “That suspicion turned out to be true.”
- *Kabar itu terbukti benar.* → “That report proved to be true.”

### Notes
For ordinary truth of statements, Indonesian commonly uses **ternyata benar** and **terbukti benar** when something is later shown to be true. **Menjadi benar** is possible in a literal sense, but it is less natural for many cases than result-discovery expressions like **ternyata** or **terbukti**.  
A related dynamic expression from the same family is **membenarkan kabar / berita**, where an authority confirms that something is true rather than the proposition itself “becoming” true.

---

## 2. BECOMING NOT TRUE / INCORRECT

### Pattern
**X menjadi tidak benar** / **X terbukti tidak benar**

### Examples
- *Pernyataan itu terbukti tidak benar.* → “That statement proved to be untrue.”
- *Kabar itu ternyata tidak benar.* → “That report turned out not to be true.”

### Notes
For reversal into falsity or failed truth, **terbukti tidak benar** and **ternyata tidak benar** are the most natural patterns in the provided vocabulary set. **Menjadi tidak benar** is structurally possible, but often sounds less idiomatic unless there is a clear change in circumstances or status.

---

## 3. CAUSING SOMETHING TO COUNT AS TRUE / CONFIRMED / JUSTIFIED

### Pattern
**Y membenarkan X** / **Y membenarkan anggapan bahwa ...** / **Y membenarkan kabar / berita**

### Examples
- *Pejabat itu membenarkan kabar tersebut.* → “The official confirmed that report.”
- *Temuan itu membenarkan anggapan bahwa data sebelumnya kurang akurat.* → “That finding confirmed the assumption that the earlier data were not very accurate.”

### Notes
The main dynamic verb here is **membenarkan**. It does not simply mean “cause to become true”; rather, it commonly means **confirm as true** or **justify**. This is an important distinction.  
Also attested is **membenarkan diri**, which shifts from truth-confirmation to self-justification.

---

## 4. CORRECTING INTO A RIGHT FORM

### Pattern
**Y membetulkan X** / **Y melakukan pembetulan**

### Examples
- *Petugas itu membetulkan data yang tidak benar.* → “The officer corrected the incorrect data.”
- *Sudah ada pembetulan pada dokumen itu.* → “There has already been a correction to that document.”

### Notes
**Membetulkan** is the clearest verb for causing something to move from an incorrect state toward a correct one. It is used for speech, writing, names, figures, and documents.  
It can also extend beyond truth-related meaning into physical fixing or straightening, so its dynamic meaning is broader than pure truth evaluation.

---

## 5. BECOMING ACCURATE / PRECISE

### Pattern
**X menjadi tepat** / **X menjadi akurat** / **X lebih tepat** / **X lebih akurat**

### Examples
- *Istilah itu menjadi lebih tepat setelah penyesuaian.* → “That term became more precise after adjustment.”
- *Data itu menjadi lebih akurat setelah pembetulan.* → “The data became more accurate after correction.”

### Notes
For **tepat** and **akurat**, change is commonly expressed with **menjadi** plus the predicate, often with **lebih**.  
No strongly established simple causative derivative from **akurat** is supported here; the P2a material explicitly marks **mengakuratkan** as uncertain. So if one wants to express causing greater accuracy, the natural strategy in this dataset is periphrastic, e.g. **membetulkan data** so that it **menjadi lebih akurat**.

---

## 6. BECOMING INACCURATE / LESS PRECISE

### Pattern
**X menjadi tidak tepat** / **X menjadi kurang tepat** / **X menjadi tidak akurat** / **X menjadi kurang akurat**

### Examples
- *Jawaban itu menjadi kurang tepat setelah istilahnya diubah.* → “That answer became less precise after its terminology was changed.”
- *Data itu menjadi tidak akurat setelah diubah.* → “The data became inaccurate after being altered.”

### Notes
This is a productive use of **menjadi** with the negative or softened negative forms already established in the context: **tidak tepat, kurang tepat, tidak akurat, kurang akurat**.

---

## 7. BECOMING WELL-TARGETED / MISTARGETED

### Pattern
**X menjadi tepat sasaran** / **X menjadi tidak tepat sasaran** / **X belum tepat sasaran**

### Examples
- *Program itu menjadi lebih tepat sasaran setelah penyesuaian.* → “The program became better targeted after adjustment.”
- *Bantuan itu menjadi tidak tepat sasaran setelah data diubah.* → “The aid became mistargeted after the data were changed.”

### Notes
Within the **tepat** family, **tepat sasaran** is especially natural for dynamic evaluation of policy or distribution.  
The context also supports non-result states such as **belum tepat sasaran**, which is useful for unstable or incomplete targeting rather than completed change.

---

## 8. BECOMING IN ACCORDANCE / OUT OF ACCORDANCE

### Pattern
**X menjadi sesuai dengan ...** / **X menjadi tidak sesuai dengan ...** / **X menyesuaikan X dengan ...**

### Examples
- *Isi dokumen itu menjadi sesuai dengan fakta setelah pembetulan.* → “The contents of the document became consistent with the facts after correction.”
- *Data itu menjadi tidak sesuai dengan laporan awal.* → “The data became inconsistent with the initial report.”

### Notes
The **sesuai** family has a clear dynamic verb: **menyesuaikan** ‘to adjust; to make conform’. This is one of the strongest causative patterns in the dataset for conformity meanings.  
Related noun: **penyesuaian**, which often names the process that leads something to become **sesuai**.

---

## 9. CAUSING CONFORMITY / ADJUSTMENT

### Pattern
**Y menyesuaikan X dengan ...** / **ada penyesuaian**

### Examples
- *Mereka menyesuaikan data dengan fakta.* → “They adjusted the data to the facts.”
- *Ada penyesuaian agar program itu tepat sasaran.* → “There was an adjustment so that the program would be well-targeted.”

### Notes
This is a central dynamic construction for the conformity domain. It does not mean “make true” in a narrow sense, but “make match” or “bring into accordance.”

---

## 10. CAUSING A MATCH / ALIGNMENT

### Pattern
**Y mencocokkan X dengan Y** / **ada pencocokan**

### Examples
- *Petugas mencocokkan data dengan dokumen sah.* → “The officer matched the data against the valid documents.”
- *Pencocokan itu menunjukkan kecocokan yang tinggi.* → “That matching showed a high degree of compatibility/match.”

### Notes
**Mencocokkan** is dynamic and strongly attested. It expresses causing or testing a match between two things.  
This is especially relevant in verification contexts, though it is about matching rather than truth in the narrow propositional sense.

---

## 11. BECOMING VALID / LEGITIMATE

### Pattern
**X menjadi sah** / **X menjadi absah** / **X menjadi valid** / **X dinyatakan sah** / **X dianggap valid**

### Examples
- *Dokumen itu menjadi sah setelah pengesahan.* → “That document became valid after ratification.”
- *Data itu dianggap valid setelah validasi.* → “The data were considered valid after validation.”

### Notes
For validity meanings, Indonesian often uses either:
- **menjadi + predicate** for change of state, or
- evaluative-passive expressions such as **dinyatakan sah**, **dianggap valid**.

These are not identical. **Menjadi sah/valid** presents a change of status; **dinyatakan sah** presents an authoritative declaration; **dianggap valid** presents institutional acceptance.

---

## 12. BECOMING INVALID

### Pattern
**X menjadi tidak sah** / **X menjadi tidak absah** / **X menjadi tidak valid** / **X belum valid**

### Examples
- *Dokumen itu menjadi tidak sah setelah diubah.* → “That document became invalid after being altered.”
- *Data itu menjadi tidak valid setelah diubah.* → “That data became invalid after being changed.”

### Notes
This pattern is clearly supported for **sah / absah / valid**.  
The context also supports **belum valid**, which is useful when validity has not yet been achieved rather than lost.

---

## 13. CAUSING VALIDITY / FORMAL ACCEPTANCE

### Pattern
**Y mengesahkan X** / **Y mengabsahkan X** / **Y memvalidasi X** / **ada pengesahan / pengabsahan / validasi**

### Examples
- *Pemerintah mengesahkan undang-undang itu.* → “The government ratified that law.”
- *Petugas memvalidasi data itu.* → “The officer validated that data.”

### Notes
This is one of the clearest causative areas in the dataset:
- **mengesahkan**: make formally valid, ratify
- **mengabsahkan**: validate, legitimize
- **memvalidasi**: validate, especially technical/administrative
- **pengesahan / pengabsahan / validasi**: the process or act

These verbs are domain-specific and should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated “make true” pattern.

---

## 14. BECOMING GENUINE / BEING SHOWN GENUINE

### Pattern
**X dipastikan asli** / **X masih asli**

### Examples
- *Dokumen itu dipastikan asli.* → “That document was confirmed to be genuine.”
- *Bagian itu masih asli.* → “That part is still original/genuine.”

### Notes
No common expression in the provided material clearly means “become asli” in the ordinary authenticity sense. Authenticity is usually treated as something checked or confirmed, not something newly acquired.  
So for this domain, **dipastikan asli** is more natural than **menjadi asli**.

---

## 15. BECOMING FAKE / BEING SHOWN FAKE

### Pattern
**X menjadi palsu** / **X diduga palsu** / **X terbukti palsu**

### Examples
- *Dokumen itu terbukti palsu.* → “That document proved to be fake.”
- *Tanda tangan itu diduga palsu.* → “That signature was suspected to be fake.”

### Notes
For authenticity failure, **terbukti palsu** is especially natural. **Menjadi palsu** is possible when something is altered or falsified, but in many real contexts Indonesian prefers the causative verb **memalsukan** for the act and **terbukti palsu** for the result.

---

## 16. CAUSING FALSITY / FORGERY

### Pattern
**Y memalsukan X** / **ada pemalsuan**

### Examples
- *Dia memalsukan tanda tangan itu.* → “He forged that signature.”
- *Kasus itu melibatkan pemalsuan dokumen sah.* → “That case involved the falsification of valid documents.”

### Notes
**Memalsukan** is the main causative verb for moving something into the **palsu** domain. This is a strong, lexicalized dynamic pattern.

---

## 17. STABILITY: REMAINING TRUE, VALID, ACCURATE, GENUINE

### Pattern
**X masih benar / masih sah / masih valid / masih asli**  
**X tetap benar / tetap sah / tetap valid**  

### Examples
- *Keterangan itu masih benar.* → “That statement is still true.”
- *Dokumen itu masih sah.* → “That document is still valid.”

### Notes
With function words, Indonesian naturally marks stability through **masih** and **tetap**.  
Among the listed items, **masih asli** is explicitly supported in the context. Parallel uses such as **masih benar**, **masih sah**, and **masih valid** are natural extensions with the same predicate pattern.

---

## 18. STABILITY: REMAINING IMPRECISE, INVALID, OR FAKE

### Pattern
**X masih tidak tepat / masih tidak valid / masih palsu**  
**X tetap tidak benar / tetap tidak sah**

### Examples
- *Data itu masih tidak valid.* → “That data are still invalid.”
- *Dokumen itu tetap tidak sah.* → “That document remains invalid.”

### Notes
These are ordinary stability patterns built from the attested negative predicates. They are natural when a correction, validation, or verification process has not changed the status.

---

## 19. RELIABILITY AS A DYNAMIC PROPERTY

### Pattern
**X menjadi andal** / **X menjadi tidak andal** / **Y mengandalkan X**

### Examples
- *Sistem itu menjadi lebih andal setelah penyesuaian.* → “That system became more reliable after adjustment.”
- *Mereka mengandalkan sumber data yang andal.* → “They rely on a reliable data source.”

### Notes
For **andal**, the main dynamic verb in the dataset is not “make reliable” but **mengandalkan** ‘rely on’.  
A simple causative meaning “cause to be andal” has **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED** in the provided material. Change of reliability is therefore best expressed periphrastically with **menjadi (lebih) andal / tidak andal**.

---

## 20. RESEMBLANCE AND ESSENCE: LIMITED DYNAMIC BEHAVIOR

### Pattern
**X menjadi mirip dengan ...** / **Y memiripkan X dengan ...**

### Examples
- *Gambar itu menjadi mirip dengan aslinya.* → “That image became similar to the original.”
- *Mereka memiripkan bentuk itu dengan aslinya.* → “They made that form resemble the original.”

### Notes
For **mirip**, dynamic use is possible, but this is about resemblance, not truth or validity.  
For **hakikat**, **jati diri**, and **sejati**, the context mainly supports reflective or stative expressions. A clear productive “become” or “cause to become” pattern is not strongly established here from the provided material, except ordinary periphrastic uses with **menjadi** if needed. No more specific common dynamic construction is identified in the dataset.

---

## 21. PROPRIETY: BECOMING OR JUDGING AS PROPER

### Pattern
**X menjadi patut / tidak patut** / **Y mematutkan X**

### Examples
- *Tindakan itu menjadi tidak patut dalam keadaan itu.* → “That action became improper in that situation.”
- *Bentuk itu dimatutkan dalam penilaian formal.* → “That form was deemed proper in formal evaluation.”

### Notes
The dataset supports **patut / tidak patut / kurang patut** clearly as evaluative states.  
A causative derivative **mematutkan** is attested but marked as limited and formal. So dynamic uses exist, but they are less central than in the **sah/valid** or **sesuai** families.

---

## 22. SUMMARY OF STRONGEST DYNAMIC PATTERNS

### Most natural “become” patterns
- **menjadi tidak valid**
- **menjadi sah**
- **menjadi lebih tepat**
- **menjadi lebih akurat**
- **menjadi sesuai dengan ...**
- **menjadi tepat sasaran**

### Most natural result-discovery patterns
- **ternyata benar / tidak benar**
- **terbukti benar / tidak benar**
- **terbukti palsu**
- **dipastikan asli**
- **dinyatakan sah**
- **dianggap valid**

### Most natural causative patterns
- **membenarkan** = confirm / justify
- **membetulkan** = correct
- **menyesuaikan** = make conform / adjust
- **mencocokkan** = match / compare
- **mengesahkan / mengabsahkan / memvalidasi** = make valid
- **memalsukan** = falsify / forge

### Explicit gaps
- A common simple causative “make accurate” from **akurat**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED**
- A common ordinary expression “become genuine” from **asli** in the authenticity sense: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED**
- A common simple causative “make reliable” from **andal**: **NO COMMON EXPRESSION IDENTIFIED**
