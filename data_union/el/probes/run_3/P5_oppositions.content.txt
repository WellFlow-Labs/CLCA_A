# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 843, consistent with P1–P4.

**Scope note:** Each opposition below is drawn directly from attested data in P1–P4. Where the data explicitly documents a contrast, that contrast is recorded. Where the data notes asymmetry, gap, or partial overlap, this is preserved. No opposition is inferred beyond what the data supports.

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** αληθής / *alithís*
2. **Term B:** ψευδής / *psevdís*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *alithís* — B: *psevdís*
4. **English translation:** A: "true" — B: "false"
5. **Description of contrast:** The two base adjectives for the truth-value poles of a proposition. They appear together in the fixed binary construction αληθής ή ψευδής ("true or false"), used in logic, philosophy, and educational testing. Both are used in formal and written registers; both apply primarily to statements, claims, and documents.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** αλήθεια / *alíthia*
2. **Term B:** ψεύδος / *psévdos*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *alíthia* — B: *psévdos*
4. **English translation:** A: "truth" — B: "lie, falsehood"
5. **Description of contrast:** The primary abstract nouns for the two poles of the truth domain. Both appear in formal and written registers; ψεύδος is the formal noun for a lie or falsehood, while αλήθεια is the primary noun for truth across all registers.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** αλήθεια / *alíthia*
2. **Term B:** ψέμα / *pséma*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *alíthia* — B: *pséma*
4. **English translation:** A: "truth" — B: "lie"
5. **Description of contrast:** The colloquial register pairing of the truth/lie opposition. ψέμα is the most frequent everyday noun for a lie; αλήθεια is register-neutral. The contrast is most visible in the paired constructions λέω την αλήθεια ("I tell the truth") and λέω ψέματα ("I tell lies"), which are documented as the primary verbal expressions for truth-telling and lying in everyday speech.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** έγκυρος / *éngiros*
2. **Term B:** άκυρος / *ákiros*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *éngiros* — B: *ákiros*
4. **English translation:** A: "valid, having legal force" — B: "invalid, null, void"
5. **Description of contrast:** The data documents these as co-derivatives from the shared base κῦρος (authority, validity), with έγκυρος formed by the prefix εν- and άκυρος by the privative α-. ## Opposition 26

1. **Term A:** δήθεν / *díthеn*
2. **Term B:** φερόμενος ως / *ferómenos os*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *díthеn* — B: *ferómenos os*
4. **English translation:** A: "supposedly, so-called" (with ironic/skeptical force) — B: "purported, alleged" (neutral hedging)

The data identifies this as "the only case where two sub-patterns within a single construction type carry observably different epistemic force" (P4b). Both are [CORE].

7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 38

1. **Term A:** νοθεύω / *nothévo*
2. **Term B:** επαληθεύω / *epalithévo*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *nothévo* — B: *epalithévo*
4. **English translation:** A: "to adulterate, to falsify, to corrupt" — B: "to verify, to confirm as true"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are documented as [CORE] causative verbs in the truth domain, but with opposite directionality: νοθεύω causes something to become adulterated or falsified; επαληθεύω causes something to be confirmed as genuine or true. The data places them in adjacent but distinct semantic groups (Group 4 and Group 1 in P3).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 39

1. **Term A:** εικονικός (in legal/financial sense) / *eikonikós*
2. **Term B:** αυθεντικός / *afthentikós*
3. **Transliteration:** A: *eikonikós* — B: *afthentikós*
4. **English translation:** A: "fictitious, nominal, sham" — B: "authentic, genuine"
5. **Description of contrast:** In legal and financial contexts, εικονικός (as in εικονική εταιρεία, εικονικό τιμολόγιο) describes something that exists in appearance only, while αυθεντικός describes something that is genuinely what it claims to be. The data places these in adjacent semantic groups (Group 5 and Group 3 in P3) and notes that εικονικός in legal contexts carries "strong negative evaluative force."
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
