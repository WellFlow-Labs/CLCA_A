# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

---

## Preliminary Note

All oppositions below are drawn directly from patterns documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, and P4. No opposition has been inferred or constructed beyond what the data explicitly support. Where the data describe a pair as "directly opposed," "antonym," "negative counterpart," "contrasts with," or "parallel construction," this is treated as evidence of an opposition. Where overlap exists between oppositions, this is noted rather than resolved.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** *òtítọ́*
**Term B:** *irọ́*
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ / irọ́
**English Translation:** truth, truthfulness / lie, falsehood, untruth
**Description of Contrast:** P1 explicitly identifies *irọ́* as "the direct antonym of *òtítọ́*" and "the standard antonym of *òtítọ́*." The two terms appear together in proverbs (*irọ́ àti òtítọ́ kò lè papọ̀ gbé*, "lie and truth cannot coexist"), in parallel verbal collocations (*sọ òtítọ́* / *sọ irọ́*), in parallel proverbs (*òtítọ́ kò ní kú* / *irọ́ kò ní dúró*), and in parallel negative future assertions about their respective fates. They appear in directly parallel syntactic frames: *Òtítọ́ ni ó sọ* vs. *Irọ́ ni ó sọ*; *Kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀* vs. *Kò sí irọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*. P3 identifies them as "the most consistently paired terms in the entire dataset." The contrast is between a statement that corresponds to reality and one that does not, used across moral, legal, and everyday discourse.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** *sọ òtítọ́*
**Term B:** *sọ irọ́*
**Transliteration:** sọ òtítọ́ / sọ irọ́
**English Translation:** speak the truth, tell the truth / tell a lie, lie
**Description of Contrast:** Both constructions share the same verb (*sọ*, to say/speak) and differ only in the object noun. P2b documents them as parallel constructions and P3 identifies them as a "matched pair across moral, legal, and everyday discourse." Both are verbal collocations built on the same verb *sọ* with contrasting nominal objects; P2b documents them as the standard antonymic pair for truth-telling versus lying across all registers. The constructional parallelism is exact: same verb, contrasting object. *pa irọ́* is documented in P2b as a common alternative to *sọ irọ́* with the same meaning.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

**Term A:** *òtítọ́ kò ní kú*
**Term B:** *irọ́ kò ní dúró*
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ kò ní kú / irọ́ kò ní dúró
**English Translation:** truth will not die / a lie will not stand
**Description of Contrast:** Two proverbial constructions documented in P2b that assert the permanence of truth and the impermanence of falsehood respectively. Both use the negative future construction *kò ní* but with different predicates (*kú* "die" vs. *dúró* "stand/endure"), producing a structural parallel with opposing semantic outcomes.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 4

**Term A:** *ó tọ́*
**Term B:** *kò tọ́*
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́ / kò tọ́
**English Translation:** it is correct / it is not correct; it is right / it is wrong; it is proper / it is improper
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as the affirmative and negative predicative forms of the root *tọ́*, described as "fully parallel" and "extremely common in everyday and formal discourse." P4a and P4b identify them as the core attributive and negative attributive construction pair in the truth domain. They appear in directly parallel constructions: *Ó tọ́ pé kí a sọ òtítọ́* vs. *Kò tọ́ pé kí o sọ irọ́*. The pair is documented as a highly productive evaluative construction used across factual, moral, procedural, and social domains.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 5

**Term A:** *ó tọ́ pé*
**Term B:** *kò tọ́ pé*
**Transliteration:** ó tọ́ pé / kò tọ́ pé
**English Translation:** it is right that / it is not right that
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as the propositional-complement versions of the *ó tọ́* / *kò tọ́* pair, extending the correctness evaluation to embedded clauses. The contrast is structurally parallel to Opposition 4 but operates at the level of propositional content rather than simple predication.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

**Term A:** *òtítọ́ ni*
**Term B:** *kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀*
**Transliteration:** òtítọ́ ni / kò sí òtítọ́ nínú ọ̀rọ̀ rẹ̀
**English Translation:** it is the truth; that is true / there is no truth in his/her words
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as fixed assertive constructions used in legal testimony and everyday dispute; the first affirms the truth of a statement, the second denies it. The contrast is between existential affirmation and existential denial of truth within a specific speech act or account.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** *ìmọ̀*
**Term B:** *àìmọ̀*
**Transliteration:** ìmọ̀ / àìmọ̀
**English Translation:** knowledge, understanding, awareness / ignorance, lack of knowledge, unawareness
**Description of Contrast:** P1 identifies *àìmọ̀* as formed by the negation prefix *àì-* on the root *mọ̀* and describes it as contrasting with *ìmọ̀* (ignorance). P2a documents *àìmọ̀* as "the standard negated nominal form of *ìmọ̀*," derived by the productive *àì-* prefix. P3 assigns both to the same group as antonyms and identifies the two as "the core opposition" in Group 4. The morphological negation is transparent and productive. The pair appears in directly parallel constructions: *Ó ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀* vs. *Kò ní ìmọ̀ nípa rẹ̀*. Both forms are used in legal and evaluative contexts to assess whether a person's testimony or conduct is epistemically grounded.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

**English Translation:** knowledgeable person, one who knows / ignorant person, one who lacks knowledge
**Description of Contrast:** These are the agentive nominals derived from *mọ̀* (to know) and its negation, respectively, documented in P2a. The contrast is between a person characterized by relevant knowledge and one characterized by its absence, used in evaluations of credibility and reliability.
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

**Term A:** *ìwà rere*
**Term B:** *ìwà burúkú*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / ìwà burúkú
**English Translation:** good character, virtue, moral goodness / bad character, wickedness, moral corruption
**Description of Contrast:** P1 identifies *ìwà burúkú* as "the antonym of *ìwà rere*" and "the standard antonym of *ìwà rere*." Both are documented as P1 base items and as the "core opposition" in P3 Group 6. They share the same head noun (*ìwà*) and differ only in the evaluative modifier (*rere* "good" vs. *burúkú* "bad/evil"). P3 places them at opposite poles of Group 4. They appear in parallel evaluative constructions: *Ó ní ìwà rere* vs. *Kò ní ìwà rere*; *Ìwà rere ló ń gbé ènìyàn ga* vs. *Ìwà búburú rẹ̀ ló pa á*. The contrast is between a person's fundamental nature being judged morally good versus morally deficient.

*(Note: the modifier appears as both *burúkú* and *búburú* across probes; both forms are preserved as attested.)*
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

**Term A:** *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́*
**Term B:** *ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́ / ìwà burúkú ń pa ènìyàn
**English Translation:** good character saves/helps a person / bad character kills/destroys a person
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents these as parallel proverbial constructions with the same subject-predicate structure but opposing subjects (*ìwà rere* vs. *ìwà burúkú*) and opposing outcomes (saving vs. destroying). Both use the habitual marker *ń*.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

**Term A:** *ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́*
**Term B:** *ìwà burúkú ló pa á*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere ló ń gbani lọ́wọ́ / ìwà burúkú ló pa á
**English Translation:** it is good character that saves one / it is bad character that destroyed him/her
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as focus constructions (*ló* = focus marker) attested in moral and proverbial discourse; they are structurally parallel (same focus construction, same verb slot pattern) but with contrasting subjects (*ìwà rere* / *ìwà burúkú*) and contrasting outcomes (salvation / destruction). The contrast is between the protective consequence of good character and the destructive consequence of bad character, expressed through parallel focus constructions.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** ó ní ìwà rere / kò ní ìwà rere
**English Translation:** he/she has good character / he/she does not have good character; he/she lacks virtue
**Description of Contrast:** These are directly parallel possessive constructions documented in P2b, differing only in the negator *kò*. They are the standard constructions for asserting or denying that a person possesses the fundamental moral quality of good character.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

**Term A:** *ìwà rere*
**Term B:** *àbùkù*
**Transliteration:** ìwà rere / àbùkù
**English Translation:** good character, virtue, moral excellence / blemish, defect, disgrace, fault
**Description of Contrast:** These two items are not explicitly paired as antonyms in the data, but P1 describes *àbùkù* as marking a person or thing that falls short of what it should genuinely be, and places it in the same semantic group as *ìwà rere* and *ìwà búburú*. The contrast is between possessing the quality one should have and being marked by a deficiency or flaw. The opposition is not fully symmetrical: *ìwà rere* is a positive characterization of a person's fundamental nature, while *àbùkù* marks a specific deficiency that may apply to persons or objects.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 16

**Term A:** *ìgbàgbọ́*
**Term B:** *àìgbàgbọ́*
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ / àìgbàgbọ́
**English Translation:** belief, faith, trust, conviction / unbelief, disbelief, lack of faith
**Description of Contrast:** *àìgbàgbọ́* is the morphological negation of *ìgbàgbọ́*, formed with the negating prefix *àì-*. P2a documents it as "the antonym of *ìgbàgbọ́* in religious and everyday discourse." The contrast is between the state of holding a belief or faith and the state of not holding it. Both forms are documented as productive in religious and philosophical discourse, with corresponding agentive forms *olùgbàgbọ́* (believer) and *aláìgbàgbọ́* (unbeliever).
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

**Transliteration:** olùgbàgbọ́ / aláìgbàgbọ́

P2a describes them as contrasting identity categories in religious communities and as antonyms used in religious discourse. The contrast is between a person characterized by belief and one characterized by its absence.

## Opposition 18

**Term A:** *gbàgbọ́ pé*
**Term B:** *kò gbàgbọ́*
**Transliteration:** gbàgbọ́ pé... / kò gbàgbọ́...
**English Translation:** to believe that... / to not believe; to disbelieve; to doubt; to reject
**Description of Contrast:** These are the affirmative and negated forms of the propositional belief construction documented in P2b. *gbàgbọ́ pé* introduces a believed proposition; *kò gbàgbọ́* expresses rejection of a claim or testimony. The contrast is between epistemic acceptance and epistemic rejection of a proposition or account.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

**Term A:** *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára*
**Term B:** *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀*
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ lágbára / ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ kò ní ìpìlẹ̀
**English Translation:** his/her faith is strong / his/her belief has no foundation; his/her faith lacks a basis
**Description of Contrast:** These are parallel evaluative constructions documented in P2b that assess the epistemic quality of a belief from opposite directions: *lágbára* (to be strong, firm) evaluates a belief positively, while *kò ní ìpìlẹ̀* (has no foundation) evaluates it negatively. The contrast is between a well-grounded, firm belief and an unfounded one.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

**Term A:** *ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú*
**Term B:** (implicit: faith with works, which is living)
**Transliteration:** ìgbàgbọ́ láìsí ìṣe jẹ́ òkú
**English Translation:** faith without works is dead
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents this construction as asserting that *ìgbàgbọ́* (faith) without *ìṣe* (works/action) is *òkú* (dead). The construction encodes an internal opposition between faith as mere belief-state and faith as expressed in action, with *òkú* (dead) implying the existence of a living counterpart. No single lexical term for the opposing pole (faith with works) is documented as a fixed expression.

*(no single opposing construction identified as a fixed form in the data)*
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

**Term A:** *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́*
**Term B:** *ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó*
**Transliteration:** ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé rẹ̀ ti fọ́ / ìgbàgbọ́ rẹ̀ ti wó
**English Translation:** trust in him/her has broken / his/her faith/belief has collapsed
**Description of Contrast:** P2b documents both as structurally parallel constructions describing the destruction of trust or belief, using different verbs of collapse (*fọ́* "to break/shatter" for *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé*; *wó* "to collapse/fall" for *ìgbàgbọ́*) and notes they "appear in similar contexts of betrayal or disillusionment." This is not a semantic opposition between the two constructions but a structural parallel that distinguishes the two concepts (*ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* versus *ìgbàgbọ́*) through different collapse metaphors. The contrast is between the breaking of interpersonal reliance and the collapse of internal conviction, expressed through non-identical but parallel constructions.
**Contrast Type:** constructional
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** *ẹlẹ́rìí ojú*
**Term B:** *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́*
**Transliteration:** ẹlẹ́rìí ojú / ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́
**English Translation:** eyewitness, direct witness / false witness, perjurer
**Description of Contrast:** P2a documents both as compounds formed on the base *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness), with *ojú* (eye) marking the most credible witness type and *irọ́* (lie) marking the negation of the witness role's truth-function. P3 notes that *ẹlẹ́rìí irọ́* "combines a term from Group 3 with a term from Group 1, marking the violation of the witness role's truth-function." Both compounds share the head noun *ẹlẹ́rìí* (witness) and differ in the modifying noun: *ojú* (eye) marks a witness whose testimony is based on direct observation, while *irọ́* (lie) marks a witness whose testimony is deliberately false.
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

The contrast is between performing the act of testimony and withholding it.
