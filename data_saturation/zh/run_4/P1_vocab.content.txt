# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真
**Transliteration:** zhēn
**Literal meaning:** true, genuine (single morpheme; functions as root in many compounds)
**Typical English translations:** true, real, genuine, authentic
**Usage:** Used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers to assert that something corresponds to fact or is not counterfeit. Appears productively in compounds such as 真相, 真实, 真正. Can modify nouns (真金 "genuine gold") or function predicatively.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假
**Transliteration:** jiǎ
**Literal meaning:** false, fake (morpheme contrasting with 真)
**Typical English translations:** fake, false, counterfeit, artificial
**Usage:** Direct antonym of 真 in most contexts; used to describe counterfeit goods, false statements, and deceptive appearances. Appears in compounds such as 假冒, 假话, 虚假. Also used in logical and conditional constructions (假设 "suppose/assume").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真实
**Transliteration:** zhēnshí
**Literal meaning:** true + solid/real
**Typical English translations:** truthful, authentic, real, factual
**Usage:** Commonly used to evaluate whether accounts, representations, or depictions correspond to reality. Applied to testimony, historical records, and artistic portrayals. Slightly more formal than bare 真 in many contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 虚假
**Transliteration:** xūjiǎ
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false
**Typical English translations:** false, fictitious, fraudulent, deceptive
**Usage:** Used in legal and regulatory language (e.g., 虚假广告 "false advertising," 虚假陈述 "false representation") and in moral evaluation of dishonest conduct. More formal and negative in connotation than bare 假.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 正确
**Transliteration:** zhèngquè
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, accurate
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an answer, method, or action conforms to a standard or is free from error. Widely used in educational, logical, and procedural contexts. Contrasts with 错误 "wrong/erroneous."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 错误
**Transliteration:** cuòwù
**Literal meaning:** wrong/mistaken + miss/err
**Typical English translations:** wrong, erroneous, mistaken, error
**Usage:** Applied to factual claims, moral judgments, and procedural steps that deviate from what is correct. Functions as both noun ("an error") and attributive adjective ("an erroneous view"). Frequently paired with 正确 in contrastive contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 准确
**Transliteration:** zhǔnquè
**Literal meaning:** standard/level + certain/precise
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact
**Usage:** Emphasizes precision and exactness, often in measurement, translation, or description. Slightly narrower than 正确 in that it foregrounds degree of correspondence rather than mere correctness. Common in scientific, journalistic, and technical registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 真相
**Transliteration:** zhēnxiàng
**Literal meaning:** true + appearance/form
**Typical English translations:** the truth, the true facts, the real situation
**Usage:** Refers to the actual state of affairs behind appearances or concealment; frequently used in investigative, legal, and journalistic contexts (e.g., 揭露真相 "reveal the truth"). Implies that a surface appearance may differ from underlying reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 本质
**Transliteration:** běnzhì
**Literal meaning:** root/origin + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** essence, essential nature, fundamental character
**Usage:** Used in philosophical, political, and analytical discourse to refer to what something fundamentally is, as opposed to its surface features. Appears in constructions like 本质上 "essentially/fundamentally" and 揭示本质 "reveal the essence."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 实质
**Transliteration:** shízhì
**Literal meaning:** solid/real + substance/nature
**Typical English translations:** substance, essence, real nature
**Usage:** Often used in legal and formal analytical contexts to distinguish the substantive reality of something from its formal or nominal appearance (e.g., 实质上 "in substance"). Overlaps with 本质 but tends to foreground practical or legal substance over philosophical essence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal meaning:** true + upright/proper
**Typical English translations:** genuine, real, true, authentic
**Usage:** Emphasizes that something is the genuine article or truly what it claims to be, often in contrast to something superficial or nominal. Used attributively (真正的朋友 "a true friend") and adverbially (真正理解 "truly understand").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal meaning:** true + sincere/honest
**Typical English translations:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Usage:** Evaluates the sincerity and authenticity of a person's intentions, expressions, or conduct. Applied to apologies, promises, and interpersonal behavior. Contrasts with 虚伪 "hypocritical/insincere."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 虚伪
**Transliteration:** xūwěi
**Literal meaning:** empty/hollow + false/hypocritical
**Typical English translations:** hypocritical, insincere, false, two-faced
**Usage:** Moral evaluation of a person or behavior as outwardly presenting one thing while inwardly being another. Stronger in negative moral connotation than 假 alone. Commonly used in character assessments and ethical criticism.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 可靠
**Transliteration:** kěkào
**Literal meaning:** can + lean on/rely on
**Typical English translations:** reliable, dependable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Evaluates whether a source, person, method, or piece of information can be depended upon. Used in assessments of evidence quality, character, and technical systems. Contrasts with 不可靠 "unreliable."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 可信
**Transliteration:** kěxìn
**Literal meaning:** can + believe/trust
**Typical English translations:** credible, believable, trustworthy
**Usage:** Applied specifically to the believability or credibility of statements, witnesses, and sources. Overlaps with 可靠 but focuses more narrowly on whether something merits belief. Common in legal and journalistic evaluation of testimony and evidence.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 伪造
**Transliteration:** wěizào
**Literal meaning:** false/counterfeit + make/fabricate
**Typical English translations:** forge, fabricate, counterfeit (verb/noun)
**Usage:** Refers to the deliberate creation of false documents, signatures, or objects intended to deceive. Primarily used in legal and criminal contexts. Closely related to 伪装 (disguise/camouflage) but specifically implies fabrication of a false artifact.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 真伪
**Transliteration:** zhēnwěi
**Literal meaning:** true + false/counterfeit
**Typical English translations:** authenticity vs. forgery, genuine vs. fake, true or false
**Usage:** A fixed binomial used to frame the question of whether something is genuine or counterfeit, especially in authentication of artworks, documents, and historical artifacts (e.g., 辨别真伪 "distinguish the genuine from the fake"). Functions as a compound noun.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 相符
**Transliteration:** xiāngfú
**Literal meaning:** mutually + conform/match
**Typical English translations:** conform to, match, correspond with, tally with
**Usage:** Evaluates whether two things — a claim and a fact, a copy and an original, a description and a reality — are in agreement or correspondence. Used in legal, administrative, and evidentiary contexts (e.g., 与事实相符 "consistent with the facts").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 名副其实
**Transliteration:** míng fù qí shí
**Literal meaning:** name + live up to + its + reality
**Typical English translations:** worthy of the name, living up to its reputation, genuinely what it claims to be
**Usage:** A fixed four-character expression (成语) evaluating whether something's name or reputation corresponds to its actual nature. Used in assessments of persons, institutions, and products. Its negation 名不副实 ("the name does not match the reality") is equally common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 如实
**Transliteration:** rúshí
**Literal meaning:** as + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** truthfully, as things actually are, accurately
**Usage:** Adverbial expression meaning "in accordance with the facts." Frequently appears in legal and testimonial contexts (如实陈述 "state truthfully," 如实反映 "reflect accurately"). Implies faithful correspondence between report and reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 失真
**Transliteration:** shīzhēn
**Literal meaning:** lose + truth/fidelity
**Typical English translations:** distortion, loss of fidelity, inaccurate representation
**Usage:** Used in technical contexts (audio/visual reproduction, data transmission) to mean distortion or loss of accuracy, and in evaluative contexts to mean that a representation has deviated from the original or the truth. Can apply to media reports and historical accounts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 客观
**Transliteration:** kèguān
**Literal meaning:** guest/external + observe/view
**Typical English translations:** objective, impartial, unbiased
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an assessment, account, or judgment is based on external facts rather than personal bias. Widely used in journalism, scholarship, and legal evaluation. Contrasts with 主观 "subjective."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 公正
**Transliteration:** gōngzhèng
**Literal meaning:** public/impartial + upright/correct
**Typical English translations:** just, fair, impartial
**Usage:** Evaluates conduct, decisions, or persons as conforming to standards of fairness and impartiality. Common in legal, moral, and political discourse. Overlaps with 公平 but often carries a stronger moral or institutional weight.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 合法
**Transliteration:** héfǎ
**Literal meaning:** conform to + law
**Typical English translations:** lawful, legal, legitimate
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an action, document, or status conforms to legal requirements. Widely used in administrative and legal contexts. Contrasts with 非法 "illegal/unlawful" and overlaps partially with 合规 "compliant."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 正当
**Transliteration:** zhèngdàng
**Literal meaning:** upright/correct + proper/fitting
**Typical English translations:** legitimate, proper, justified, rightful
**Usage:** Evaluates whether an action or claim is morally or legally justified and appropriate. Used in both legal contexts (正当防卫 "legitimate self-defense") and moral evaluation of conduct. Slightly broader than 合法 in that it encompasses moral as well as legal legitimacy.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** 原版
**Transliteration:** yuánbǎn
**Literal meaning:** original + edition/version
**Typical English translations:** original version, authentic edition
**Usage:** Refers to the original, unaltered version of a text, recording, or artifact, as opposed to a copy, adaptation, or counterfeit. Used in publishing, film, and authentication contexts. Implies genuineness through priority and unaltered status.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**27.**
**Form:** 仿冒
**Transliteration:** fǎngmào
**Literal meaning:** imitate + counterfeit/pass off as
**Typical English translations:** counterfeit, imitation (fraudulent), pass off as genuine
**Usage:** Used in commercial and legal contexts to describe products or marks that fraudulently imitate genuine ones. Slightly more specific than 假冒 in emphasizing the imitative mechanism of the fraud. Common in intellectual property and consumer protection discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** 名实
**Transliteration:** míngshí
**Literal meaning:** name + reality/substance
**Typical English translations:** name and reality, nominal vs. actual
**Usage:** A classical binomial referring to the relationship between a name or designation and the reality it purports to denote. Appears in philosophical discourse (associated with the School of Names, 名家) and in modern usage when questioning whether a label corresponds to actual substance. More formal and literary in register.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** 辨伪
**Transliteration:** biànwěi
**Literal meaning:** distinguish/discriminate + false/counterfeit
**Typical English translations:** detect forgeries, distinguish the genuine from the false, textual criticism (of authenticity)
**Usage:** Used in scholarly and archival contexts to refer to the critical examination of texts or artifacts to determine whether they are genuine or forged. Associated with traditional Chinese philological practice (辨伪学). More domain-specific than general authentication vocabulary.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** 失实
**Transliteration:** shīshí
**Literal meaning:** lose/deviate from + reality/fact
**Typical English translations:** inaccurate, contrary to fact, misrepresentation
**Usage:** Used in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts to evaluate whether a report or statement deviates from the facts (e.g., 报道失实 "inaccurate reporting"). More formal than colloquial expressions of inaccuracy and implies a failure of correspondence between claim and reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
