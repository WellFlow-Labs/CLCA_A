# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Hebrew (he)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: א-מ-ת ('alef-mem-tav)
**Transliteration:** '-m-t  
**P1 items:** 4 (emet, amiti, amin, meheyман [via '-m-n variant])  
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth, faithfulness, reliability, firmness. This root conveys both factual truth and trustworthiness/faithfulness. It is the primary semantic anchor of the truth domain in Hebrew.

### Root 2: צ-ד-ק (tsadi-dalet-kuf)
**Transliteration:** ts-d-k  
**P1 items:** 3 (tsodek, tsedek, mutsдak)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Rightness, justice, correctness. This root combines epistemic correctness with moral rightness, linking truth evaluation to justice and proper conduct.

### Root 3: כ-ו-ן (kaf-vav-nun)
**Transliteration:** k-w-n  
**P1 items:** 2 (nakhon, lo nakhon)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Correctness, establishment, firmness, being set right. This root emphasizes factual accuracy and proper arrangement/establishment.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: א-מ-ת / א-מ-ן ('-m-t / '-m-n)

#### 1
**Form:** אמונה  
**Transliteration:** emunah  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** '-m-n + -ah (feminine noun pattern)  
**Natural English translation:** faith, belief, trust, faithfulness  
**Context of use:** Used extensively in religious contexts to denote faith in God, but also in secular contexts for trust, belief, or faithfulness. Common in phrases like emunah datit "religious faith" or emunah be- "faith/trust in."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2
**Form:** אמונים  
**Transliteration:** emunim  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** '-m-n + -im (masculine plural)  
**Natural English translation:** faithful ones, believers, loyalists  
**Context of use:** Refers to faithful or loyal persons, often in religious contexts (believers) or political contexts (loyalists). Can describe those who maintain trust or allegiance.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3
**Form:** נאמן  
**Transliteration:** ne'eman  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** ni- (nif'al pattern) + '-m-n  
**Natural English translation:** faithful, loyal, trustworthy  
**Context of use:** Describes persons as faithful or loyal, commonly used for trusted officials, loyal friends, or reliable sources. Often appears in titles like ne'eman al "trustee of."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 4
**Form:** נאמנות  
**Transliteration:** ne'emanut  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** ne'eman + -ut (abstract noun suffix)  
**Natural English translation:** faithfulness, loyalty, reliability  
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being faithful or loyal. Used in evaluating character, relationships, and institutional reliability. Common in phrases like ne'emanut le- "loyalty to."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 5
**Form:** אמן  
**Transliteration:** amen  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** '-m-n (verbal form or interjection)  
**Natural English translation:** amen, so be it, truly  
**Context of use:** Liturgical and conversational interjection affirming truth or expressing agreement with what has been said. Used at end of prayers and blessings, or to express strong agreement.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 6
**Form:** האמין  
**Transliteration:** he'emin  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** hi- (hif'il causative) + '-m-n  
**Natural English translation:** believed, trusted, had faith  
**Context of use:** Verb meaning to believe or trust, used for both epistemic belief (believing a statement) and trust in persons or institutions. Common construction: he'emin le- "believed in/trusted."  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 7
**Form:** מאמין  
**Transliteration:** ma'amin  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** ma- (hif'il participle) + '-m-n  
**Natural English translation:** believer, one who believes/trusts  
**Context of use:** Describes someone who believes or has faith, often in religious contexts (ma'amin be-elohim "believer in God") but also for anyone who believes a claim or trusts in something.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 8
**Form:** אמת מידה  
**Transliteration:** amat midah  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** emet + midah "measure"  
**Natural English translation:** criterion, standard, yardstick  
**Context of use:** Fixed expression meaning a standard or criterion for evaluation. Used in formal and informal contexts to refer to the measure by which something is judged.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 9
**Form:** לאמיתו של דבר

**Literal morpheme breakdown:** le- "to" + amito "its truth" + shel "of" + davar "thing/matter"  
**Natural English translation:** in truth, actually, to tell the truth  
**Context of use:** Fixed idiomatic expression used to introduce what the speaker considers the real truth of a matter, often correcting or clarifying a previous statement.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 10
**Form:** אמיתות  
**Transliteration:** amitiyut  
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** amiti + -ut (abstract noun suffix)  
**Natural English translation:** authenticity, genuineness, truthfulness  
**Context of use:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or authentic. Used in aesthetic, philosophical, and everyday contexts to evaluate authenticity.  
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 11

**Context of use:** Adverbial expression describing action done faithfully or in good faith. Common in legal contexts (ba'al emunah "in good faith") and in describing loyal service.

#### 13

#### 14

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Natural English translation:** craftsman, artisan, expert

#### 19

#### 20

**Natural English translation:** indeed, it is true that, admittedly

## Step 3 — Note Peripheral Extensions

### Root '-m-t / '-m-n Extensions:

**Items 18-19 (oman, omanut):** The root appears in words for craftsman/artisan and art/craft. The semantic connection to truth/faithfulness operates through the notion of faithful execution of craft or mastery, but these terms function primarily in aesthetic and technical domains rather than truth-evaluation per se.

**Item 1 (emunah):** While core to truth-evaluation in its sense of trust/reliability, this term extends significantly into religious faith contexts where it denotes belief/faith in divine matters, which may involve commitment beyond or distinct from epistemic evaluation.

### Root ts-d-k Extensions:

**Items 2, 14 (tsedakah, tsedakot):** While etymologically connected to justice/righteousness, these terms function primarily in the domain of charitable giving and social welfare, with the connection to truth-evaluation being indirect through moral rightness.

**Items 9-10 (tsidkani, tsidkanut):** These pejorative terms for self-righteousness represent a negative evaluation of excessive moral claims, extending the root into character criticism.

**Item 17 (tsidkat ha-din):** This religious expression of accepting divine judgment extends the root into theological acceptance and submission, which may involve different epistemic commitments than ordinary truth-evaluation.

### Root k-w-n Extensions:

**Items 1-7, 19-20 (kivun, kiven, kavanah, etc.):** The root extends extensively into spatial direction and intentionality/purpose, domains that relate to truth-evaluation through the notion of proper aim or correct direction but function largely independently in navigation, planning, and intentionality contexts.

**Items 12-14 (tikun, tiken, metukan):** These repair/correction terms connect to truth-evaluation through the notion of fixing errors, but function broadly in technical and physical repair contexts beyond epistemic correction.

**Items 15-16 (konen, mekhonen):** These establishment/founding terms emphasize the firmness and stability aspect of the root, extending into institutional and foundational contexts.
