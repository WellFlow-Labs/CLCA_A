# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P4a CONSTRUCTIONS
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Transliteration standard:** ISO 15919

---

## 1. ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
NP + *uṇmaiyāna* / *nērmaiyāna* / *pōliyāna* / *tavaṟāna* (adjectival form: NOUN + *-āna*) functioning as predicate nominal or attributive phrase. The copula is typically null in present tense; past/future use the verb *iru-*. Alternatively, nominal predicate + *-āka* + copular verb *iru* for predicative attribution.

### Examples
- அது உண்மைதான் / *atu uṇmaitāṉ* / "That is indeed true" (emphatic predicative; *-tāṉ* = emphatic particle)
- அவர் நேர்மையான ஆள் / *avar nērmaiyāna āḷ* / "He/she is an honest person"
- உண்மையான செய்தி → *uṇmaiyāna ceytī* → "a true/genuine news report"
- நம்பகமான ஆதாரம் → *nampakamāna ātāram* → "a reliable source/evidence"
- அது உண்மை — *atu uṇmai* — "That is the truth / That is true"
- இது அன்யாயம் — *itu anyāyam* — "This is unjust / This is an injustice"

### Notes
The *-āna* suffix (copular participial) is the primary adjectival derivation across this domain, applying uniformly to *uṇmai*, *poy*, *nērmai*, *tavaṟu*, *pōli*, *niyāyam*, *ātāram*, *poruttam*, and others. Predicate attribution uses the bare nominal (*atu uṇmai*) or emphatic form (*atu uṇmaitāṉ*); attributive modification places the *-āna* form before the head noun.

Tamil attributive modification is prenominal. The suffix *-āna* attaches to the nominal base (e.g., *uṇmai*, *nampakam*, *nēṟmai*, *poy*, *pōli*) to produce an adjectival modifier. This pattern is fully productive across all truth-domain nominals documented in P1–P2a.

The predicate nominal stands bare or with the emphatic particle *tāṉ* (*atu uṇmaitāṉ* — "that is indeed true"). Interrogative attribution uses the question particle *-ā* suffixed to the nominal (*atu uṇmaiyā?* — "Is that true?"). No overt copula is required in the present tense.

## 2. NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION

### Pattern
Two distinct strategies: (a) *NOUN + illāta* (negative existential participial, "lacking X") and (b) *NOUN + aṟṟa* (privative participial, "cut off from X"). These are not interchangeable; *illāta* is broader; *aṟṟa* tends to attach to quality/attribute nouns. A third strategy uses *NOUN + illai* (negative existential) for full negative predication rather than attributive modification.

### Examples
- நேர்மையில்லாத மனிதன் / *nērmaiyillāta maṉitaṉ* / "A person lacking honesty, a dishonest person" (*illāta* strategy)
- ஆதாரமற்ற குற்றச்சாட்டு / *ātāramaṟṟa kuṟṟaccāṭṭu* / "A baseless accusation, an unsubstantiated charge" (*aṟṟa* strategy)
- ஆதாரமற்ற வதந்தி → *ātāramaṟṟa vatanti* → "an unsubstantiated rumor"
- கள்ளமில்லாத மனிதன் → *kaḷḷamillāta maṉitaṉ* → "a person without guile"
- அது பொய்யில்லை — *atu poyyillai* — "That is not a lie / That is not false"
- அது என் தவறு இல்லை — *atu eṉ tavaṟu illai* — "That is not my mistake / That is not my fault"

### Notes
Both *-aṟṟa* and *-illāta* strategies are productive across the domain. *aṟṟa* is attested with *takuti* (→ *takutiyaṟṟa*, unqualified), *ātāram* (→ *ātāramaṟṟa*, baseless), and *poruttam* (→ *poruttamaṟṟa*, inappropriate). *illāta* is attested with *nērmai* and *nampikkai* (→ *nampikkaiinmai*, lack of trust). No single privative suffix covers the entire domain.

Two privative forms are attested: *-aṟṟa* (participial of *aṟu*, "to be severed/lacking") and *-illāta* (participial of *illai*, "there is not"). Both function as prenominal modifiers. *-aṟṟa* tends toward formal registers (*ātāramaṟṟa*, *nēṟmaiyaṟṟa*); *-illāta* is common across registers (*kaḷḷamillāta*, *poyyillāta*). Both are productive across truth-domain nominals.

*illai* is the standard negative existential used for nominal predicates across Tamil. The privative participial suffix *-aṟṟa* (e.g., *nērmaiyaṟṟa*, *nampakam aṟṟa*) performs a related but distinct function: it forms a negative attributive modifier rather than a full negative predication.

## 3. ADNOMINAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
Bare nominal (uninflected) or *-āna* adjectival form placed directly before a head noun. Tamil is head-final; modifiers precede the noun they modify. No agreement morphology on the modifier. A second pattern uses the genitive/compound construction: Noun₁ (+ genitive *-iṉ* or juxtaposition) + Noun₂ → "the X of Y" or "X-Y compound."

### Examples
- உண்மையான தகவல் / *uṇmaiyāna taka'l* / "True/accurate information" (*-āna* adjectival modifier)
- போலி ஆவணம் / *pōli āvaṇam* / "Forged/fake document" (bare nominal modifier, no *-āna*)
- ஆவணத்தின் உண்மைத்தன்மை → *āvaṇattiṉ uṇmaitaṉmai* → "the authenticity of the document"
- மனத் தூய்மை → *maṉat tūymai* → "purity of mind / mental integrity"
- நம்பகமான ஆதாரம் — *nampakamāṉa ātāram* — "a reliable source / credible evidence"
- நியாயமான கோரிக்கை — *niyāyamāṉa kōrikkai* — "a reasonable/just demand"

### Notes
Both strategies coexist: *pōli*, *acal*, *nakal*, *kaṟpaṉai*, and *poy* commonly appear as bare prenominal modifiers without *-āna* (e.g., *poy cāṭci*, false testimony; *acal āvaṇam*, original document). The *-āna* form is more common with abstract quality nouns (*uṇmai*, *nērmai*, *niyāyam*). The choice between bare and *-āna* forms is partly lexically conditioned and partly register-sensitive.

Tamil uses the genitive suffix *-iṉ* (or its phonological variants) for "of" relationships, or simple juxtaposition in tighter compounds. Both patterns are attested in the truth domain. The genitive construction is more common with abstract nominals (*uṇmaitaṉmai*, *nampakattaṉmai*); juxtaposition is more common in fixed compounds (*maṉat tūymai*, *uṇmai nilai*).

The *-āṉa* suffix is fully productive across all truth-domain nominals: *uṇmaiyāṉa*, *poyyāṉa*, *nērmayāṉa*, *tavaṟāṉa*, *cariyāṉa*, *niyāyamāṉa*, *anyāyamāṉa*, *pōliyāṉa*, *kaḷḷamāṉa*, *vañcakamāṉa*. The privative *-aṟṟa* participial (e.g., *nampakam aṟṟa takaval* — "information lacking credibility") functions as an alternative negative adnominal modifier.

## 4. BECOME / INCHOATIVE

### Pattern
Periphrastic constructions using *āku* (to become) or *teri* (to become known/visible) + *varu* (to come). No morphological inchoative suffix is attested in this domain. The construction *X āku* expresses a change of state; *X teriya varu* expresses the coming-to-light of a hidden truth. A distinct pattern uses *-paṭu* (to undergo/become) in compound verbs. The inchoative is also formed with the adverbial suffix *-āka* on the nominal plus the verb *āku* (to become), often combined with the completive auxiliary *viṭu*.

### Examples
- உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது / *uṇmai teriya vantatu* / "The truth became known / came to light" (*teriya varu* construction)
- அது உண்மையாகிவிட்டது / *atu uṇmaiyākiviṭṭatu* / "That has turned out to be true / has become true" (*uṇmai* + *āki* + completive *viṭu*)
- நம்பகத்தன்மை கேள்விக்குறியாகிவிட்டது → *nampakattaṉmai kēḷvikkuṟiyākiviṭṭatu* → "His credibility has become a question mark"
- உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது → *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* → "The truth came out / became revealed"
- உண்மை வெளிப்பட்டது — *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* — "The truth came to light / The truth became revealed"
- உண்மை தெரிய வந்தது — *uṇmai teriya vantatu* — "The truth became known / The truth came out"

### Notes
The *teriya varu* construction is specific to the revelation-of-hidden-truth pattern and treats truth as an entity moving from concealment to visibility. The *āku* construction is more general and expresses a change of state. The completive auxiliary *viṭu* adds a sense of finality or irreversibility. No single morphological inchoative exists.

The construction *uṇmai veḷippaṭu* (truth + "come out/be revealed") is a distinct inchoative collocation using the passive-inchoative verb *veḷippaṭu* rather than *āku*.

Tamil does not have a single dedicated inchoative morpheme. The truth-domain inchoative is most naturally expressed through motion-and-perception verbs (*veḷippaṭu* — to become visible/emerge; *teriya va* — to come to be known) rather than through *-āka āku*, which is available but less idiomatic for this semantic field. The construction *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* is the most conventionalized form for "truth coming to light."

## 5. CAUSATIVE

### Pattern
One morphologically derived causative is attested: *mey* + causative *-ppi-* → *meyppiкka* / *meyppittal* ("to prove, to cause to be real/true"). A second morphological pattern uses the productive causative suffix *-paṭuttu-* (from *paṭu* — to undergo) attached to the nominal base. All other causative-like expressions in this domain are periphrastic, using *paṭuttu* (causative verb, "to cause/make") or light verbs such as *niṟuvu* (to establish) or *nirūpikku* (to prove).

### Examples
- அதை மெய்ப்பிக்க முடியவில்லை / *atai meyppiкka muṭiyavillai* / "It was not possible to prove that / could not be demonstrated" (morphological causative)
- உண்மையை நிலைநாட்டினார் / *uṇmaiyai nilaināṭṭiṉār* / "He/she established the truth / proved the truth definitively" (periphrastic: *nilaināṭṭu* = to make stand firm)
- உண்மை நிறுவு → *uṇmai niṟuvu* → "to establish the truth"
- ஆதாரப்படுத்து → *ātārappaṭuttu* → "to substantiate / to support with evidence"
- உண்மைப்படுத்துதல் — *uṇmaippaṭuttutal* — "authentication, verification, substantiation" (the act of causing something to be established as true)
- ஆதாரத்தை உண்மைப்படுத்து — *ātāratai uṇmaippaṭuttu* — "authenticate / substantiate the evidence"

### Notes
*meyppiкka* is the only morphologically derived verbal form via the causative suffix *-ppi-* in the entire truth domain; it is formed on the classical root *mey*. No parallel causative from *uṇmai* via *-ppi-* is attested. Periphrastic alternatives (*nilaināṭṭu*, *nirūpi*, *meyppiкka*) carry the proving/establishing function across registers. This asymmetry — causative derivation via *-ppi-* available only from *mey*, not from *uṇmai* — is a structural gap in the system.

The causative construction *paṭuttu* attaches productively to truth-domain nominals: *uṟutippaṭuttu* (to confirm/certify), *ātārappaṭuttu* (to substantiate), *niyāyappaṭuttu* (to justify), *tūymaippaṭuttu* (to purify). A second causative pattern uses *niṟuvu* (to establish) or *nirūpikku* (to prove) as the main verb with the nominal as object; this is a lexical rather than morphological causative.

The *-paṭuttu* causative on *uṇmai* is attested and used in official Tamil for document authentication. However, this pattern is **not productive across the full truth-domain vocabulary**: no common *poyppaṭuttu*, *nērmaipaṭuttu*, or *niyāyappaṭuttu* has been identified. The causative for most truth-domain meanings is expressed periphrastically via light verb constructions (*niyāyam cey* — to do justice; *tavaṟu tiruttu* — to correct a mistake) rather than through morphological causativization.

## 6. ABSTRACT NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
The suffix *-mai* is the primary abstract nominalizer in this domain, deriving quality nouns from roots or adjectives. Several items in the inventory are themselves *-mai* nominals. Secondary nominalization uses *taṉmai* (nature, quality) as a compounding element.

### Examples
- பொய்மை / *poymai* / "Falsehood, the quality of being false" (*poy* + *-mai*)
- உண்மைத்தன்மை / *uṇmait-taṉmai* / "Truthfulness, the property of being true" (*uṇmai* + *taṉmai*)
- நேர் + *-mai* → நேர்மை (*nēṟmai*) → "honesty, integrity"
- உண்மை + *taṉmai* → உண்மைத்தன்மை (*uṇmaitaṉmai*) → "truthfulness, authenticity"
- பொய்மை — *poymai* — "falsehood, falseness" (*poy* + *-mai*; literary and philosophical)
- நம்பகத்தன்மை — *nampakat-taṉmai* — "reliability, credibility, trustworthiness" (*nampakam* + *taṉmai*)

### Notes
*uṇmai* and *nērmai* are themselves *-mai* nominals (from *uḷ* and *nēr* respectively) and do not reduplicate the suffix. *poymai* and *meymmai* (*mey* + euphonic *-m-* + *-mai*) are the clearest secondary *-mai* nominalizations in the domain. The *taṉmai* compound (*uṇmait-taṉmai*) is a lexicalized form used in formal and philosophical registers to refer to the abstract property of truthfulness. *nampikkai* (*nampu* + *-ikkai*) and *nampakam* (*nampu* + *-akam*) represent alternative nominalizing suffixes on the same root.

*-mai* is the primary abstract nominalizing suffix in Tamil and attaches directly to roots (*nēr* → *nēṟmai*, *tūy* → *tūymai*, *poy* → *poymai*, *mey* → *meymmai*). The compound *taṉmai* (quality, nature) attaches to already-nominal bases to produce more formal, philosophical abstracts (*uṇmaitaṉmai*, *nampakattaṉmai*, *poyttaṉmai*). The *-taṉmai* forms are consistently more formal and less frequent than the *-mai* forms.

The *-mai* suffix is the primary nominalizer: *uṇmai* (truth), *nērmai* (honesty), *poymai* (falsehood), *nērmayiṉmai* (dishonesty, via *iṉmai* — absence). The *taṉmai* compound (*uṇmai-t-taṉmai*, *nampakat-taṉmai*, *nēr-taṉmai*) produces more formal, philosophical abstract nominals and is more restricted in register. Both patterns are productive but the *taṉmai* forms are marked [MARGINAL] in everyday use.

## 7. AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION

### Pattern
Agentive nominals are formed periphrastically or via compounding with *-vāti* (advocate/proponent), *āḷ/āḷar* (person/agent), or *piriyar* (devotee/lover). No dedicated agentive suffix for truth-domain agents is attested that covers the full domain. Agentive nominals are also formed by attaching person/agentive suffixes (*-aṉ*, *-aḷ*, *-ar*, *-āḷar*, *-āḷaṉ*) to the truth-domain base. Number and gender distinctions are encoded in the suffix. The most common strategy for "liar" is the participial relative clause construction.

### Examples
- பொய் சொல்பவன் / *poy colpavaṉ* / "One who tells lies, a liar" (participial relative clause: *col-* + *-pavaṉ*, one who does)
- நம்பிக்கையாளர் / *nampikkaiāḷar* / "Believers, persons of faith" (*nampikkai* + *āḷar*, agent/person suffix)
- பொய் + *-aṉ* → பொய்யன் (*poyyan*) → "liar (masculine)"
- வஞ்சகம் + *-aṉ* → வஞ்சகன் (*vañcakaṉ*) → "deceiver, traitor (masculine)"
- பொய்யன் — *poyyaṉ* — "liar" (masculine singular; colloquial and literary)
- நேர்மையாளர் — *nērmayāḷar* — "person(s) of integrity / honest person(s)" (honorific/plural; formal and political)

### Notes
Tamil lacks a dedicated agentive suffix for truth-domain roles parallel to English *-er* (liar, truth-teller). The participial relative clause (*poy colpavaṉ*, *uṇmai colpavaṉ*) is the productive strategy for forming agent nominals from verbal phrases. The *-vāti* suffix (*uṇmaivāti*, truth-advocate; *realist*) and *piriyar* (*uṇmaip piriyar*, lover of truth) are attested but marginal, confined to philosophical and literary registers. *nampikkaiāḷar* (believers) uses the *āḷar* agent suffix on a nominal base and is specific to religious contexts.

Agentive forms are well-attested for *poy* (*poyyaṉ* masc. sg., *poyyar* pl./honorific, *poyyāḷaṉ* more formal masc.) and *nērmai* (*nērmayāḷar*). The form *vañcakaṉ* (traitor, deceiver; from *vañcakam*) is attested in literary Tamil. No common agentive nominal meaning "truth-teller" built on *uṇmai* has been identified as a lexicalized item; the concept is expressed periphrastically (*uṇmai pēcukiṟavaṉ* — one who speaks the truth).

Agentive nominals are attested for several truth-domain bases: *poyyan/poyyaḷ* (liar m./f.), *vañcakaṉ/vañcaki* (deceiver m./f.), *kapaṭi* (hypocrite), *cāṉṟāḷar* (witness/attester, honorific). The honorific/plural form *-āḷar* is used for socially neutral or positive agents (*uṇmaiyāḷar*, *nēṟmaiyāḷar*, *cāṉṟāḷar*). Agentive forms for positive truth-domain properties (*uṇmaiyāḷar*, *nēṟmaiyāḷar*) are attested but less frequent than those for negative properties (*poyyan*, *vañcakaṉ*, *kapaṭi*).

## 8. EVIDENTIAL / SOURCE-MARKING CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Periphrastic construction: *nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu teriya vantatu* (lit. "it came to be known from reliable circles"). Source is marked by the ablative *-iliruntu* (from); reliability of source is evaluated by *nampakamāna* (reliable) or *nampattakka* (worth trusting).

### Examples
- நம்பகமான வட்டாரங்களிலிருந்து தெரிய வந்தது / *nampakamāna vaṭṭāraṅkaḷiliruntu teriya vantatu* / "It became known from reliable circles / credible sources"
- நம்பகமான ஆதாரம் இல்லாமல் எதுவும் சொல்ல வேண்டாம் / *nampakamāna ātāram illāmal etuvum colla vēṇṭām* / "Nothing should be said without reliable evidence"

### Notes
This construction type emerges from the data as a distinct pattern not reducible to attribution or nominalization. It is characteristic of journalistic and legal Tamil, where the credibility of a source (*nampakamāna*) and the existence of evidentiary basis (*ātāram*) are evaluated together. The ablative *-iliruntu* marks the source; the negative conditional *illāmal* (without) marks the absence of required evidence. This pattern is anchored in the domain of establishing and evidencing claims.

## 9. CONCEALMENT / REVELATION VERBAL CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Two-pole periphrastic pattern: (a) *uṇmaiyai maṟai* (hide the truth; transitive, accusative object) for concealment; (b) *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu* / *uṇmai teriya vantatu* (the truth came out; intransitive, truth as subject) for revelation. Truth is construed as a moveable entity.

### Examples
- உண்மையை மறைக்கிறார்கள் / *uṇmaiyai maṟaikkiṟārkaḷ* / "They are hiding the truth / concealing the facts"
- உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது / *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu* / "The truth came out / the truth emerged"

### Notes
The transitive concealment construction requires accusative marking on *uṇmai* (*uṇmaiyai*); the intransitive revelation construction treats *uṇmai* as subject with no case marking. The two poles are structurally and semantically complementary. The proverbial *poy oru nāḷ veḷiyē varum* (a lie will come out one day) uses the same intransitive revelation structure with *poy* as subject.

## 10. DEONTIC CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Verbal noun (*V-vatu* or infinitive) + obligative suffix *-āṇum* / *-vēṇṭum* → "must/should do X."

### Examples
- உண்மை சொல்லணும் → *uṇmai collāṇum* → "(One) must tell the truth"
- தவறை ஒப்புக்கொள்ளணும் → *tavaṟai oppukkoḷḷāṇum* → "(One) must admit the mistake"

### Notes
Deontic constructions are highly productive in the truth domain and appear across multiple base forms: *uṇmai collāṇum*, *cāṉṟu camarppikkāṇum*, *uṇmai niṟuvāṇum*, *nampakattaṉmaiyai nilainātṭāṇum*. The suffix *-āṇum* is the spoken/colloquial form; *-vēṇṭum* is the formal written equivalent. Both are attested.

## 11. EPISTEMIC DISCOURSE MARKER

### Pattern
*uṇmaiyil* / *uṇmaiyilēyē* (sentence-initial adverbial) + proposition → "In truth / Actually, [proposition]."

### Examples
- உண்மையில் அவன் அங்கே இல்லை → *uṇmaiyil avaṉ aṅkē illai* → "In truth, he was not there"
- உண்மையிலேயே இப்படி நடந்துச்சா? → *uṇmaiyilēyē ippaṭi naṭantuccā?* → "Did this really happen like this?"

### Notes
*uṇmaiyil* (locative: "in truth") and its emphatic form *uṇmaiyilēyē* function as sentence-initial epistemic adverbs marking correction of a mistaken belief or strong assertion of factual reality. These are grammaticalized from the locative case of *uṇmai* and are not compositionally interpreted in discourse; they function as fixed markers. No parallel forms from *poy* or *nēṟmai* are attested in this discourse-marker function.

---

## 12. BINARY QUESTION CONSTRUCTION

### Pattern
Noun/adjective₁ + *-ā* + *illai* + Noun/adjective₂ + *-ā* → "Is it X or Y?"

### Examples
- இது உண்மையா இல்லை பொய்யா? → *itu uṇmaiyā illai poyyā?* → "Is this true or false?"
- இது அசலா நகலா? → *itu acalā nakalā?* → "Is this the original or a copy?"

### Notes
The *X-ā illai Y-ā* pattern is a productive Tamil interrogative construction for binary alternatives. In the truth domain, the most frequent pairs are *uṇmai/poy* (true/false), *acal/nakal* (original/copy), *cariyā/illaiyā* (correct or not), and *niyāyamā/illaiyā* (fair or not). The construction is attested across all registers.

## 13. PROVERBIAL / SCALAR ASSERTION

### Pattern
Nominal + *tāṉ* (emphatic/exclusive particle) + *vellum* (will prevail) → "X alone will prevail."

### Examples
- உண்மை தான் வெல்லும் → *uṇmai tāṉ vellum* → "Truth alone will prevail"
- நியாயம் தான் வெல்லும் → *niyāyam tāṉ vellum* → "Justice alone will prevail"

### Notes
This construction is a fixed proverbial pattern in Tamil moral discourse. The particle *tāṉ* adds exclusive/contrastive emphasis ("it is precisely X that..."). The pattern is attested with *uṇmai*, *niyāyam*, and the literary *mey* (*meyyē vellum*). It functions as a moral assertion about the ultimate triumph of truth or justice and appears in speeches, proverbs, and popular culture.

## 14. ADVERBIAL MODIFICATION

### Pattern
**Truth-domain nominal + *-āka*** (adverbial/predicative suffix)

The suffix *-āka* (converbal form of *āku*) attaches to truth-domain nominals to produce adverbials qualifying manner of action, or predicative expressions asserting a state.

### Examples
- நேர்மையாக பேசு — *nērmayāka pēcu* — "Speak honestly / Speak with integrity"
- தவறாக புரிந்துகொண்டேன் — *tavaṟāka purinthukōṇṭēṉ* — "I understood it wrongly / I misunderstood it"

### Notes
The *-āka* suffix is fully productive across the truth-domain vocabulary: *uṇmaiyāka*, *poyyāka*, *nērmayāka*, *cariyāka*, *tavaṟāka*, *niyāyamāka*, *anyāyamāka*, *pōliyāka*. It serves both as a manner adverbial (qualifying a verb) and as a predicative complement (*itu uṇmaiyāka irukku* — "this is genuinely the case"). It is distinct from the *-āṉa* adjectival (§3) in that it modifies verbs and predicates rather than nouns.
