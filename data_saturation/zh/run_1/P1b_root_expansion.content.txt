# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** 真 (zhēn)
**P1 participation:** 8 items — 真 (#1), 真实 (#3), 真相 (#9), 真伪 (#11), 失真 (#17), 名副其实 (#14, as counterpart), 表里如一 (#22, implicitly), 货真价实 (#24)
**Core meaning contribution:** Asserts that something corresponds to reality, is not counterfeit, and is what it claims to be. Functions as the primary positive pole of the truth/authenticity axis in Chinese.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** 实 (shí)
**P1 participation:** 9 items — 真实 (#3), 事实 (#10), 名副其实 (#14), 名不副实 (#15), 如实 (#16), 失实 (#29), 实质 (#21), 货真价实 (#24), 表里如一 (#22, via 一 but 实 appears in the set broadly)
**Core meaning contribution:** Denotes solidity, actuality, and factual grounding — what is concretely the case as opposed to what is nominal, empty, or merely claimed. Pairs with 真 to form the core truth-evaluation vocabulary and independently anchors the "fact" and "substance" clusters.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** 假 (jiǎ)
**P1 participation:** 5 items — 假 (#2), 虚假 (#4), 真伪 (#11, via its antonym 伪), 冒充 (#13, semantically), 弄虚作假 (#23)
**Note:** 假 and 伪 function as near-synonymous negative-pole roots and are treated together in the expansion below, since they co-occur in compounds and are difficult to separate in the truth-evaluation domain. Each is expanded individually.

---

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

---

### Root 1: 真 (zhēn)

*This root is highly productive. The 25 most common compounds and expressions are listed below; additional forms exist in technical and dialectal usage.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 真正
**Transliteration:** zhēnzhèng
**Literal breakdown:** true + upright/correct
**Natural English translation:** truly, genuinely, real, authentic
**Context of use:** Used as an adverb or attributive adjective to intensify the claim that something is genuinely what it appears to be, not merely nominal or superficial. Extremely common across all registers: 真正的朋友 (a true friend), 真正理解 (to truly understand).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 真诚
**Transliteration:** zhēnchéng
**Literal breakdown:** true + sincere/honest
**Natural English translation:** sincere, genuine, heartfelt
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person's attitude, expression, or intention as genuinely felt rather than performed or strategic. Common in interpersonal, diplomatic, and moral discourse: 真诚的道歉 (a sincere apology), 真诚待人 (to treat people sincerely).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 真心
**Transliteration:** zhēnxīn
**Literal breakdown:** true + heart/mind
**Natural English translation:** wholehearted, sincere, genuinely
**Context of use:** Asserts that an emotion, intention, or commitment is genuinely felt rather than feigned. Used in everyday speech and literary contexts: 真心话 (words from the heart), 真心喜欢 (to genuinely like).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真假
**Transliteration:** zhēnjiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** true + false
**Natural English translation:** genuine or fake, authenticity, true or false
**Context of use:** Names the binary question of whether something is authentic or counterfeit. Appears in collocations such as 辨别真假 (to distinguish the genuine from the fake) and 真假难辨 (hard to tell real from fake). Common in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 真理
**Transliteration:** zhēnlǐ
**Literal breakdown:** true + principle/reason
**Natural English translation:** truth (as a principle), Truth (philosophical)
**Context of use:** Refers to fundamental or universal truth, especially in philosophical, ideological, and scientific discourse. Appears in expressions like 追求真理 (to pursue truth) and 真理越辩越明 (truth becomes clearer through debate). More abstract than 真相.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 真实性
**Transliteration:** zhēnshíxìng
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + -ness (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truthfulness, authenticity, veracity, fidelity
**Context of use:** The nominalized form of 真实, used to evaluate the degree to which a document, account, artwork, or claim corresponds to reality. Common in legal, journalistic, and literary-critical discourse: 新闻的真实性 (the truthfulness of news reporting).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 真凭实据
**Transliteration:** zhēn píng shí jù
**Literal breakdown:** true + evidence/proof + solid/real + evidence/basis
**Natural English translation:** solid evidence, concrete proof, hard facts
**Context of use:** A fixed four-character expression asserting that a claim is backed by genuine and substantial evidence. Used in legal argumentation and everyday disputes to demand or affirm evidentiary grounding.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 真知灼见
**Transliteration:** zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + knowledge + clear/penetrating + view/insight
**Natural English translation:** genuine insight, penetrating understanding, well-founded opinion
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to praise a judgment or analysis as both accurate and perceptive. Common in academic, literary, and intellectual discourse as a compliment to a speaker or writer's views.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 真才实学
**Transliteration:** zhēn cái shí xué
**Literal breakdown:** true + talent + solid/real + learning
**Natural English translation:** genuine talent and solid learning, real ability
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person as possessing authentic skills and knowledge rather than merely a reputation or credentials. Used in educational and professional contexts to distinguish substantive competence from superficial qualification.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 真金不怕火炼
**Transliteration:** zhēn jīn bù pà huǒ liàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + gold + not + fear + fire + smelt/test
**Natural English translation:** True gold fears no fire; genuine quality withstands scrutiny.
**Context of use:** A proverb asserting that what is genuinely good or authentic will prove itself under testing conditions. Used in moral and evaluative contexts to encourage confidence in genuine quality or integrity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 逼真
**Transliteration:** bīzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to press/approach + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** lifelike, realistic, true to life, vivid
**Context of use:** Evaluates a representation — painting, performance, imitation, description — as so close to the original that it seems real. Common in aesthetic and artistic criticism: 画得逼真 (painted so realistically), 模仿得逼真 (imitated convincingly).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 认真
**Transliteration:** rènzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to recognize/take + true/seriously
**Natural English translation:** serious, earnest, conscientious, to take seriously
**Context of use:** Describes an attitude of genuine engagement and care in performing a task or holding a position. Extremely common in everyday, educational, and professional contexts: 认真工作 (to work conscientiously), 别太认真 (don't take it too seriously).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 当真
**Transliteration:** dàngzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to treat as + true/real
**Natural English translation:** to take seriously, to take as real, to mean it
**Context of use:** Used to ask or assert whether something is being treated as genuinely true or meant. Common in conversational contexts: 你当真吗？(Are you serious?) and 别当真 (Don't take it literally/seriously).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 纯真
**Transliteration:** chúnzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** pure + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** pure, innocent, guileless, genuine
**Context of use:** Describes a person — especially a child — or an emotion as unaffected and without artifice. Common in literary, emotional, and moral evaluations: 纯真的童年 (an innocent childhood), 纯真的感情 (genuine, unaffected feelings).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 天真
**Transliteration:** tiānzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** heaven/natural + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** naive, innocent, artless, ingenuous
**Context of use:** Describes a person or belief as naturally unaffected and without guile, sometimes with the implication of being unrealistically simple. Used both as a compliment (of children) and as mild criticism (of adults): 天真烂漫 (innocent and carefree), 太天真了 (too naive).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 真面目
**Transliteration:** zhēn miànmù
**Literal breakdown:** true + face + appearance
**Natural English translation:** true face, true nature, real identity
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual nature or identity of a person or thing, especially when it has been concealed. Appears in the fixed phrase 庐山真面目 (the true face of Mount Lu; one's true nature, from a Su Shi poem) and in everyday usage: 露出真面目 (to reveal one's true colors).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 真实感
**Transliteration:** zhēnshígǎn
**Literal breakdown:** true + solid/real + sense/feeling
**Natural English translation:** sense of reality, feeling of authenticity, verisimilitude
**Context of use:** Used in literary, cinematic, and artistic criticism to evaluate whether a work produces a convincing impression of reality. Also used in psychological contexts to describe a person's sense of connection to reality.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 真伪莫辨
**Transliteration:** zhēn wěi mò biàn
**Literal breakdown:** true + false/spurious + cannot + distinguish
**Natural English translation:** impossible to tell genuine from fake, indistinguishable
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to describe a situation in which authentic and counterfeit items or claims are so similar that differentiation is impossible. Common in legal, archival, and investigative contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 半真半假
**Transliteration:** bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ
**Literal breakdown:** half + true + half + false
**Natural English translation:** half true, half false; partly genuine
**Context of use:** Describes a statement or situation that mixes truth and falsehood. Used in everyday and journalistic contexts to characterize claims that are not wholly reliable: 他说的话半真半假 (what he said was half true, half false).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 真善美
**Transliteration:** zhēn shàn měi
**Literal breakdown:** true + good + beautiful
**Natural English translation:** truth, goodness, and beauty; the True, the Good, and the Beautiful
**Context of use:** A philosophical and aesthetic triad used in educational, literary, and moral discourse to name the three highest values. Appears in discussions of art, ethics, and education: 追求真善美 (to pursue truth, goodness, and beauty).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 写真
**Transliteration:** xiězhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to write/depict + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** portrait, realistic depiction, photo (in some contexts)
**Context of use:** In classical and art-historical contexts, refers to realistic portraiture or life-drawing. In modern usage, especially in Taiwan and influenced by Japanese, refers to a photographic portrait or photo shoot. Used in both artistic and commercial contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 传真
**Transliteration:** chuánzhēn
**Literal breakdown:** to transmit + true/genuine
**Natural English translation:** fax, facsimile; also: to transmit a true likeness
**Context of use:** In modern usage, the standard term for facsimile (fax) transmission. The compound originally referred to transmitting a true likeness; the technical meaning is now dominant. Common in administrative and business contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 真空
**Transliteration:** zhēnkōng
**Literal breakdown:** true + empty/void
**Natural English translation:** vacuum (physical); also: a genuine void or absence
**Context of use:** The standard scientific and technical term for a vacuum (absence of matter). Also used metaphorically for a power vacuum or an absence of something expected: 权力真空 (power vacuum). Common in both technical and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 真菌
**Transliteration:** zhēnjūn
**Literal breakdown:** true + fungus/bacterium
**Natural English translation:** true fungi, Fungi (taxonomic kingdom)
**Context of use:** The standard biological and taxonomic term for the kingdom Fungi, distinguishing true fungi from other fungus-like organisms. Used in scientific, medical, and educational contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 千真万确
**Transliteration:** qiān zhēn wàn què
**Literal breakdown:** thousand + true + ten-thousand + certain/precise
**Natural English translation:** absolutely true, beyond any doubt, one hundred percent certain
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to assert with maximum emphasis that something is genuinely and certainly true. Common in everyday speech and informal writing when a speaker wishes to insist on the truth of a claim: 这件事千真万确 (this matter is absolutely true).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 2: 实 (shí)

*This root is highly productive. The 25 most common compounds and expressions are listed below; additional forms exist.*

---

**1.**
**Form:** 实际
**Transliteration:** shíjì
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + border/boundary
**Natural English translation:** actual, practical, in practice, reality
**Context of use:** Contrasts what is actually the case with what is theoretical, ideal, or claimed. Extremely common across all registers: 实际情况 (the actual situation), 脱离实际 (divorced from reality), 实际上 (in practice, actually).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 实际上
**Transliteration:** shíjìshàng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + border + on/above
**Natural English translation:** in fact, actually, in practice, in reality
**Context of use:** An adverbial phrase used to introduce a correction or clarification, asserting what is actually the case as opposed to what appears or is claimed. One of the most common discourse markers in Chinese across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 现实
**Transliteration:** xiànshí
**Literal breakdown:** present/manifest + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** reality, the real world; realistic, pragmatic
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual state of the world as it currently exists, often contrasted with ideals, wishes, or fiction. Also used as an adjective meaning pragmatic or realistic: 面对现实 (to face reality), 现实主义 (realism).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 实现
**Transliteration:** shíxiàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to appear/manifest
**Natural English translation:** to realize, to achieve, to bring about, to actualize
**Context of use:** Describes the process of making something actual or concrete that was previously only potential or planned. Common in goal-oriented, political, and everyday discourse: 实现梦想 (to realize a dream), 实现目标 (to achieve a goal).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 实施
**Transliteration:** shíshī
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to carry out/implement
**Natural English translation:** to implement, to put into effect, to enforce
**Context of use:** Used in legal, administrative, and policy contexts to describe the putting into practice of a law, plan, or measure: 实施法律 (to implement a law), 实施方案 (to carry out a plan).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 实践
**Transliteration:** shíjiàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to tread/practice
**Natural English translation:** practice, to practice, to put into practice
**Context of use:** Refers to the application of knowledge or principles in action, as opposed to theory. Central to Marxist epistemology in Chinese political discourse (实践是检验真理的唯一标准, practice is the sole criterion for testing truth) and to everyday usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 实验
**Transliteration:** shíyàn
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + to test/verify
**Natural English translation:** experiment, to experiment, experimental
**Context of use:** The standard term for scientific experiment or experimental procedure. Also used more broadly for any trial or test of a hypothesis or method: 做实验 (to conduct an experiment), 实验室 (laboratory).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 实证
**Transliteration:** shízhèng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + evidence/proof
**Natural English translation:** empirical evidence, empirical proof; positivism (in philosophy)
**Context of use:** Used in scientific and philosophical discourse to refer to evidence grounded in observation and fact. 实证主义 is the standard translation of positivism. Also used in legal contexts for concrete proof.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** 实情
**Transliteration:** shíqíng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + situation/circumstances
**Natural English translation:** the true situation, the actual facts, the real state of affairs
**Context of use:** Refers to the actual circumstances of a matter, especially when these may differ from what has been reported or claimed. Common in legal, investigative, and everyday contexts: 了解实情 (to understand the true situation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** 实话
**Transliteration:** shíhuà
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, truthful words, honest statement
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that accurately reflects what the speaker knows or believes. Appears in the common expression 实话实说 (to tell it like it is, to speak the truth plainly) and 说实话 (to tell the truth).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** 实话实说
**Transliteration:** shí huà shí shuō
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + speech + solid/real + to speak
**Natural English translation:** to tell it like it is, to speak plainly and honestly
**Context of use:** A fixed expression used to introduce or request a frank, unvarnished account. Common in everyday conversation and also the name of a well-known Chinese television talk show, which extended its currency.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** 实力
**Transliteration:** shílì
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + strength/power
**Natural English translation:** actual strength, real capability, substantive power
**Context of use:** Refers to the genuine, demonstrated capacity of a person, team, or state, as opposed to nominal rank or reputation. Common in sports, military, political, and business discourse: 实力派 (a person of genuine ability), 综合实力 (overall strength).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** 务实
**Transliteration:** wùshí
**Literal breakdown:** to attend to/engage with + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** pragmatic, practical, down-to-earth, results-oriented
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person, policy, or approach as focused on concrete realities and achievable outcomes rather than ideology or abstraction. Common in political and administrative discourse: 务实的态度 (a pragmatic attitude), 务实外交 (pragmatic diplomacy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** 扎实
**Transliteration:** zhāshi
**Literal breakdown:** to plant/root + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** solid, thorough, well-grounded, substantial
**Context of use:** Evaluates knowledge, skills, or work as genuinely thorough and well-founded rather than superficial. Common in educational and professional contexts: 基础扎实 (a solid foundation), 工作扎实 (thorough, solid work).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** 充实
**Transliteration:** chōngshí
**Literal breakdown:** to fill/full + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** to enrich, to fill out, substantial, fulfilling
**Context of use:** Describes making something more substantial or complete, or evaluates a life, schedule, or content as rich and full. Common in everyday and literary contexts: 充实生活 (to enrich one's life), 内容充实 (substantial content).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** 落实
**Transliteration:** luòshí
**Literal breakdown:** to fall/land + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** to implement, to put into effect, to follow through, to pin down
**Context of use:** Describes the process of ensuring that a plan, policy, or commitment is actually carried out in concrete terms. Extremely common in administrative and political discourse: 落实政策 (to implement policy), 落实责任 (to pin down responsibility).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** 踏实
**Transliteration:** tāshi
**Literal breakdown:** to tread/step + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** steady, down-to-earth, reliable, at ease
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person as grounded and reliable, or describes a feeling of security and settledness. Common in character evaluation and everyday speech: 踏实的人 (a steady, reliable person), 心里踏实 (to feel at ease, settled).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** 诚实
**Transliteration:** chéngshí
**Literal breakdown:** sincere/honest + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** honest, truthful, sincere
**Context of use:** Evaluates a person's character or a statement as genuinely honest and not deceptive. One of the most common moral character terms in Chinese: 诚实的人 (an honest person), 诚实守信 (honest and trustworthy).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** 老实
**Transliteration:** lǎoshi
**Literal breakdown:** old/experienced + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** honest, straightforward, well-behaved, simple-minded
**Context of use:** Describes a person as honest and uncomplicated, sometimes with the implication of being overly simple or easily taken advantage of. Common in everyday character evaluation: 老实人 (an honest, straightforward person), 老实说 (to be honest, frankly speaking).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** 老实说
**Transliteration:** lǎoshi shuō
**Literal breakdown:** old/experienced + solid/real + to speak
**Natural English translation:** to be honest, frankly speaking, to tell the truth
**Context of use:** A discourse marker used to introduce a frank or candid statement, often one that the speaker might otherwise hesitate to make. Common in conversational and informal written registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** 坦实
**Transliteration:** tǎnshí
**Literal breakdown:** frank/open + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** frank and honest, candid
**Context of use:** Less common than 诚实 or 老实; used to describe a person or manner of speaking as both open and grounded in fact. Appears in formal and literary contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**22.**
**Form:** 实名
**Transliteration:** shímíng
**Literal breakdown:** solid/real + name
**Natural English translation:** real name, verified identity
**Context of use:** Used in administrative, legal, and digital contexts to refer to registration or participation under one's actual identity, as opposed to anonymity or pseudonymity: 实名制 (real-name registration system), 实名认证 (real-name verification).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** 属实
**Transliteration:** shǔshí
**Literal breakdown:** to belong to/be + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** to be true, to be confirmed as fact, to be verified
**Context of use:** Used in formal, legal, and administrative contexts to confirm that a reported matter corresponds to the facts: 情况属实 (the situation is confirmed as true), 经查属实 (verified upon investigation).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**24.**
**Form:** 写实
**Transliteration:** xiěshí
**Literal breakdown:** to write/depict + solid/real
**Natural English translation:** realistic depiction, realism (in art and literature)
**Context of use:** Used in literary and artistic criticism to describe a style that aims to represent reality accurately and without idealization. Contrasts with 浪漫 (romantic) or 抽象 (abstract): 写实主义 (realism), 写实风格 (realistic style).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** 言过其实
**Transliteration:** yán guò qí shí
**Literal breakdown:** speech + exceeds + its + reality/fact
**Natural English translation:** to exaggerate, to overstate, to say more than the facts warrant
**Context of use:** A fixed four-character expression used to criticize a statement as going beyond what the facts support. Common in evaluative and critical discourse: 他的话言过其实 (his words were an exaggeration).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Root 3a: 假 (jiǎ)

---

**1.**
**Form:** 假冒
**Transliteration:** jiǎmào
**Literal breakdown:** false + to pretend/pass off as
**Natural English translation:** counterfeit, to pass off as genuine, fake
**Context of use:** Describes goods or persons falsely presented as genuine or authorized. Common in commercial and legal contexts: 假冒商品 (counterfeit goods), 假冒伪劣 (fake and inferior products).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** 假冒伪劣
**Transliteration:** jiǎ mào wěi liè
**Literal breakdown:** false + pretend + spurious + inferior
**Natural English translation:** fake and substandard, counterfeit and inferior goods
**Context of use:** A fixed four-character expression used in regulatory, commercial, and consumer-protection contexts to describe products that are both inauthentic and of poor quality. A standard phrase in Chinese market regulation discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** 假话
**Transliteration:** jiǎhuà
**Literal breakdown:** false + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** lie, false statement, untruth
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that is not true. Common in everyday moral and interpersonal discourse: 说假话 (to tell a lie), 不说假话 (not to tell lies). Contrasts with 实话 and 真话.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** 真话
**Transliteration:** zhēnhuà
**Literal breakdown:** true + speech/words
**Natural English translation:** the truth, a true statement, honest words
**Context of use:** Refers to a statement that accurately reflects reality. Appears in the contrast 真话假话 (true words and false words) and in expressions like 说真话 (to tell the truth). Common in everyday and moral discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** 假设
**Transliteration:** jiǎshè
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + to set up/establish
**Natural English translation:** hypothesis, assumption, to hypothesize, to assume
**Context of use:** Used in scientific, logical, and everyday discourse to introduce a supposition or working assumption: 假设条件 (hypothetical conditions), 做出假设 (to make an assumption). Also functions as a conditional conjunction meaning "supposing that."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** 假如
**Transliteration:** jiǎrú
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + as if/like
**Natural English translation:** if, supposing that, in the event that
**Context of use:** A common conditional conjunction used to introduce hypothetical or counterfactual conditions. Extremely common in everyday speech and writing across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** 假若
**Transliteration:** jiǎruò
**Literal breakdown:** false/hypothetical + as if/like
**Natural English translation:** if, supposing, were it the case that
**Context of use:** A slightly more formal or literary variant of 假如, used to introduce hypothetical conditions. Common in written and formal spoken registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** 假装
**Transliteration:** jiǎzhuāng
**Literal breakdown:** false + to dress/pretend
**Natural English translation:** to pretend, to feign, to fake
**Context of use:** Describes the act of behaving as if something is the case when it is not. Common in everyday speech: 假装睡觉 (to pretend to be asleep), 假装不知道 (to feign ignorance).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**9.**

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the roots 真, 实, 假, or 伪 in contexts where their contribution appears to shift away from the core truth/authenticity domain. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

- **认真** (zhēn, #12 above): The compound is used to describe intensity of effort and seriousness of engagement in performing a task, where the question of correspondence to reality is not directly at issue.

- **天真** (zhēn, #15 above): The root contributes a sense of natural, unaffected simplicity that shades into naivety; the evaluative dimension concerns character and social sophistication rather than factual accuracy.

- **真空** (zhēn, #23 above): In its primary scientific usage, 真 contributes a sense of completeness or absoluteness (a "true" void) rather than correspondence to a claim or representation.

- **真菌** (zhēn, #24 above): The root functions as a taxonomic classifier distinguishing a category of organisms, with no direct evaluative or epistemic dimension.

- **传真** (zhēn, #21 above): In its dominant modern usage as "fax/facsimile," the truth-related etymology is opaque to most users; the term functions as a technical label.

- **写真** (zhēn, #21 above): In its modern photographic usage (especially in Taiwan and influenced by Japanese), the term refers to a portrait or photo shoot, where 真 marks fidelity to appearance rather than propositional truth.

- **逼真** (zhēn, #11 above): The root evaluates the closeness of a representation to its original in aesthetic and perceptual terms, extending the truth domain into artistic verisimilitude.

- **真善美** (zhēn, #20 above): 真 appears as one term in a philosophical triad alongside goodness and beauty, where it functions as a value category rather than a predicate about specific claims.

- **实力** (shí, #12 above): The root contributes a sense of genuine, demonstrated capacity; the evaluative dimension concerns power and competence rather than factual accuracy of a statement.

- **充实** (shí, #15 above): The root contributes a sense of fullness and substantiality applied to life, content, or schedule, where the contrast is with emptiness rather than with falsity.

- **踏实** (shí, #17 above): The root contributes a sense of groundedness and settledness applied to character and emotional states, where the contrast is with instability or anxiety rather than with falsehood.

- **落实** (shí, #16 above): The root contributes a sense of concretization and follow-through in administrative and policy contexts, where the contrast is with plans remaining unexecuted rather than with false claims.

- **假设 / 假如 / 假若** (jiǎ, #5–7 above): In these conditional and hypothetical uses, 假 introduces a counterfactual or suppositional frame without implying deception or moral fault; the root functions as a logical operator.

- **假借** (jiǎ, #10 above): In its linguistic usage as one of the six classical categories of Chinese character formation, 假 refers to phonetic borrowing of a character's sound, a technical classification with no evaluative dimension.

- **伪装** (wěi, #1 above): In military usage (camouflage), the root describes concealment for tactical purposes, where the moral dimension of deception may be absent or differently weighted.

- **伪命题** (wěi, #7 above): The root is used in philosophical and logical discourse to label a proposition as ill-formed or based on false premises, extending beyond the domain of forgery or impersonation into formal reasoning.
