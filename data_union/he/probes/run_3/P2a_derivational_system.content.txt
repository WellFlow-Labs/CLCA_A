# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a Derivational System
# Language: Hebrew (he)

---

**Methodological note:** The following documents attested derivational morphology for each P1 base form. Where P1b root expansion has already identified and described a derived form, that form is cross-referenced rather than re-described in full. Forms are listed per base item; no attempt is made to complete paradigms or impose symmetry across items. Gaps are preserved. Inflectional forms (conjugated verbs, plural nouns, construct forms) are excluded unless they have acquired independent lexical status. The distinction between derivation and inflection is applied conservatively; borderline cases are noted.

---

## Item 1 — אֱמֶת (ʾemet)

---

**1.1**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּי (ʾamitī)
**Transliteration:** ʾamitī
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾemet + adjectival suffix -ī
**Natural English translation:** true, genuine, real, authentic
**Usage:** The standard modern Hebrew adjective derived from ʾemet, used attributively and predicatively across all registers to evaluate statements, objects, feelings, and identities as genuine or real. Listed as P1 item 2.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.2**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Derived Form:** אֲמִתִּיּוּת (ʾamitiyut)
**Transliteration:** ʾamitiyut
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾamitī + abstract noun suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** genuineness, authenticity, realness
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being genuine or real. Used in philosophical, psychological, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something possesses true or authentic character. Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 12).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.3**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Derived Form:** בֶּאֱמֶת (be-ʾemet)
**Transliteration:** be-ʾemet
**Literal Breakdown:** be- (preposition "in/with") + ʾemet
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, in truth, honestly
**Usage:** Extremely common adverbial phrase functioning as a sincerity marker or intensifier in everyday speech. Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.4**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Derived Form:** אֱמֶת הַמִּדָּה (ʾemet ha-middah)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet ha-middah
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾemet + ha-middah ("the measure/standard")
**Natural English translation:** the standard, the benchmark, the true measure
**Usage:** Used in evaluative and critical discourse to refer to the genuine standard or criterion by which something is judged. Common in journalistic and intellectual writing. Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 7).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**1.5**
**Base Form:** אֱמֶת (ʾemet)
**Derived Form:** אֱמֶת לַאֲמִתָּהּ (ʾemet le-ʾamittah)
**Transliteration:** ʾemet le-ʾamittah
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾemet + le- ("to/of") + ʾamittah (feminine possessive form, "its truth")
**Natural English translation:** the truth of the matter, the real truth, strictly speaking
**Usage:** A fixed phrase used in rabbinic and philosophical discourse to distinguish genuine or essential truth from a superficial or approximate version; also used in modern Hebrew to mean "strictly speaking." Documented in P1b root expansion (א-מ-נ, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on ʾemet:** The prepositional phrase be-ʾemet and the fixed phrase ʾemet le-ʾamittah have acquired independent adverbial and discourse-marker status beyond simple compositional use of the noun. The form ʾamitī and its abstract ʾamitiyut represent the primary adjectival and abstract nominal derivations. No verbal derivation directly from ʾemet is attested in modern Hebrew; the verbal function is served by forms of the root א-מ-נ (heʾemin, etc.).

## Item 3 — נָכוֹן (nakhon)

---

**3.1**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Derived Form:** נְכוֹנוּת (nekhonot)
**Transliteration:** nekhonot
**Literal Breakdown:** nakhon + abstract noun suffix -ut (with vowel shift)
**Natural English translation:** correctness, accuracy, rightness
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being correct or accurate. Used in educational, scientific, and legal contexts to evaluate the degree to which a statement or procedure is correct. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 2).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.2**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Derived Form:** לֹא נָכוֹן (lo nakhon)
**Transliteration:** lo nakhon
**Literal Breakdown:** lo ("not") + nakhon
**Natural English translation:** incorrect, wrong, not right
**Usage:** The standard negation of nakhon, used to evaluate statements, answers, or actions as incorrect. Extremely common across all registers. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.3**
**Base Form:** נָכוֹן (nakhon)
**Derived Form:** נָכוֹן לְהַיּוֹם (nakhon le-hayom)
**Transliteration:** nakhon le-hayom
**Literal Breakdown:** nakhon + le- + hayom ("today")
**Natural English translation:** as of today, correct as of the present date
**Usage:** A fixed administrative and journalistic formula specifying that information is accurate as of the current date. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 5).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on nakhon:** The primary derivation is the abstract noun nekhonot. The negated form lo nakhon and the temporal formula nakhon le-hayom have acquired fixed status. The broader root כ-ו-נ generates many additional forms (kavvanah, mukhan, kinnun, etc.) documented in P1b; those are not re-listed here as derivations of nakhon specifically, since they derive from the root rather than from the nakhon form itself.

## Item 4 — מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)

---

**4.1**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** דִּיּוּק (diyyuq)
**Transliteration:** diyyuq
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel verbal noun of root ד-י-ק
**Natural English translation:** precision, exactness, accuracy; punctiliousness
**Usage:** The standard abstract noun for precision or exactness, used in scientific, legal, and everyday contexts to evaluate the degree of accuracy in a statement, measurement, or action. Also used in rabbinic contexts to denote careful textual analysis or punctilious observance.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.2**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** בְּדִיּוּק (be-diyyuq)
**Transliteration:** be-diyyuq
**Literal Breakdown:** be- + diyyuq
**Natural English translation:** exactly, precisely, accurately
**Usage:** Extremely common adverbial phrase used to affirm that a statement or description is exactly correct. Used across all registers in everyday speech and writing ("זה בדיוק מה שאמרתי" — "That is exactly what I said").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.3**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** לְדַיֵּק (ledayyeq)
**Transliteration:** ledayyeq
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel infinitive of root ד-י-ק
**Natural English translation:** to be precise, to be exact, to specify precisely
**Usage:** Used in everyday and professional speech to mean being careful and precise in one's statements or actions. Common in contexts where a speaker is correcting an imprecise formulation or insisting on exactness ("צריך לדייק בנושא הזה" — "One needs to be precise on this matter").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.4**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** דַּיְקָן (dayqan)
**Transliteration:** dayqan
**Literal Breakdown:** Root ד-י-ק + agentive suffix -an
**Natural English translation:** a precise person, a stickler for accuracy, a punctilious person
**Usage:** Denotes a person who is characteristically precise, meticulous, or punctilious. Can be used approvingly (a careful, accurate person) or with mild irony (an overly pedantic person). Used in everyday and professional contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.5**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** דַּיְקָנוּת (dayqanut)
**Transliteration:** dayqanut
**Literal Breakdown:** dayqan + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** precision, meticulousness, punctiliousness
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being precise or meticulous. Used in everyday and professional contexts to evaluate a person's or institution's degree of care and exactness. Can carry both positive and mildly negative (overly pedantic) connotations depending on context.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.6**
**Base Form:** מְדֻיָּק (meduyaq)
**Derived Form:** אִי-דִּיּוּק (ʾi-diyyuq)
**Transliteration:** ʾi-diyyuq
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾi- (negation prefix) + diyyuq
**Natural English translation:** inaccuracy, imprecision, lack of precision
**Usage:** Used to evaluate a statement, measurement, or description as lacking the required degree of precision. Common in scientific, journalistic, and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on meduyaq:** The root ד-י-ק is moderately productive. The central derivational cluster consists of the verbal noun diyyuq, the adverbial be-diyyuq, the verb ledayyeq, the agentive dayqan, and the abstract dayqanut. The negated ʾi-diyyuq is also well attested. No adverbial form directly from meduyaq itself (as distinct from diyyuq) is in common use.

## Item 5 — שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)

---

**5.1**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Derived Form:** שַׁקְרָן (shaqran)
**Transliteration:** shaqran
**Literal Breakdown:** Root ש-ק-ר + agentive suffix -an
**Natural English translation:** liar, habitual liar
**Usage:** Denotes a person who characteristically lies or deceives. Used in everyday speech and in moral evaluation. The term implies habitual or characteristic dishonesty rather than a single instance of lying.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.2**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Derived Form:** שַׁקְרָנוּת (shaqranut)
**Transliteration:** shaqranut
**Literal Breakdown:** shaqran + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** habitual lying, mendacity, dishonesty
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality or practice of habitual lying. Used in moral and everyday contexts to evaluate a person's or institution's characteristic dishonesty.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.3**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Derived Form:** שַׁקְרָנִי (shaqranī)
**Transliteration:** shaqranī
**Literal Breakdown:** shaqran + adjectival suffix -ī
**Natural English translation:** mendacious, dishonest, characteristic of a liar
**Usage:** Adjective used to evaluate statements, behavior, or persons as characteristically dishonest or mendacious. Used in everyday and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.4**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Derived Form:** לְשַׁקֵּר (leshaqer)
**Transliteration:** leshaqer
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel infinitive of root ש-ק-ר
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to tell a lie
**Usage:** The standard modern Hebrew verb for "to lie" or "to tell a falsehood." Used across all registers. "הוא שיקר לי" ("He lied to me") is a common everyday expression.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.5**
**Base Form:** שֶׁקֶר (sheqer)
**Derived Form:** עֵד שֶׁקֶר (ʿed sheqer)
**Transliteration:** ʿed sheqer
**Literal Breakdown:** ʿed ("witness") + sheqer ("falsehood")
**Natural English translation:** false witness, perjurer
**Usage:** A fixed biblical and legal expression denoting a person who gives false testimony. Appears in the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20:13) and in legal discourse. Functions as a conventionalized compound.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on sheqer:** The root ש-ק-ר generates a productive agentive cluster (shaqran, shaqranut, shaqranī) and the standard verb leshaqer. The compound ʿed sheqer is a fixed legal and biblical expression. No abstract noun directly parallel to ʾemet (i.e., *shiqrut or similar) is in common use; sheqer itself functions as both the concrete noun ("a lie") and the abstract noun ("falsehood, lying").

## Item 6 — אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)

---

**6.1**
**Base Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִי (ʾotentī)
**Derived Form:** אוֹתֶנְטִיּוּת (ʾotentiyut)
**Transliteration:** ʾotentiyut
**Literal Breakdown:** ʾotentī + abstract noun suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** authenticity
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being authentic. Used in cultural, philosophical, artistic, and journalistic contexts to evaluate whether something is genuine rather than derivative or imitative. The standard modern Hebrew rendering of "authenticity" in educated discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on ʾotentī:** As a loanword, ʾotentī has limited derivational productivity in Hebrew. The abstract noun ʾotentiyut is the primary and essentially only attested derivation. No verbal form, agentive noun, or adverbial form derived from ʾotentī is in common use. The adverbial function is typically served by the phrase בצורה אותנטית (be-tsura ʾotentit, "in an authentic manner") rather than a dedicated adverbial derivation.

## Item 7 — מְקוֹרִי (meqorī)

---

**7.1**
**Base Form:** מְקוֹרִי (meqorī)
**Derived Form:** מְקוֹרִיּוּת (meqoriyut)
**Transliteration:** meqoriyut
**Literal Breakdown:** meqorī + abstract noun suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** originality, authenticity, genuineness
**Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the quality of being original or authentic. Used in aesthetic, intellectual property, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether something is a genuine original rather than a copy or imitation. The standard modern Hebrew term for "originality."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on meqorī:** The primary derivation is the abstract noun meqoriyut. No verbal form or agentive noun derived directly from meqorī is in common use. The base noun מקור (maqor, "source, origin") from which meqorī is itself derived generates additional forms (e.g., in the sense of "source" in journalism and scholarship), but those are derivations of maqor rather than of meqorī and are not listed here.

## Item 8 — מְזֻיָּף (mezuyaf)

---

**8.1**
**Base Form:** מְזֻיָּף (mezuyaf)
**Derived Form:** זִיּוּף (ziyyuf)
**Transliteration:** ziyyuf
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel verbal noun of root ז-י-פ
**Natural English translation:** forgery, falsification, counterfeiting
**Usage:** The standard noun for the act or product of forgery or falsification. Used in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts to evaluate whether a document, artwork, or identity has been deliberately falsified. Common in criminal law (עבירת זיוף, ʿavirat ziyyuf, "forgery offense").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.2**
**Base Form:** מְזֻיָּף (mezuyaf)
**Derived Form:** לְזַיֵּף (lezayyef)
**Transliteration:** lezayyef
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel infinitive of root ז-י-פ
**Natural English translation:** to forge, to falsify, to counterfeit
**Usage:** The standard verb for the act of forging or falsifying. Used in legal and everyday contexts. "זייף את החתימה" ("He forged the signature") is a typical usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.3**
**Base Form:** מְזֻיָּף (mezuyaf)
**Derived Form:** מְזַיֵּף (mezayyef)
**Transliteration:** mezayyef
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel active participle of root ז-י-פ
**Natural English translation:** forger, one who falsifies; (as adjective) falsifying
**Usage:** Used both as an agentive noun ("a forger") and as an active participial adjective ("falsifying"). Common in legal and journalistic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on mezuyaf:** The root ז-י-פ generates a productive cluster: the verbal noun ziyyuf, the verb lezayyef, and the agentive/participial mezayyef. No abstract noun of the type *ziyyufut is in common use; ziyyuf itself serves the abstract nominal function. The root is less productive than ש-ק-ר in generating personal character evaluations (no *zayyafan parallel to shaqran is in common use).

## Item 9 — כַּנּוּת (kannut)

---

**9.1**
**Base Form:** כַּנּוּת (kannut)
**Derived Form:** כֵּן (ken)
**Transliteration:** ken
**Literal Breakdown:** Adjectival base form from which kannut is derived (via root כ-נ-נ or related to כ-ו-נ)
**Natural English translation:** sincere, honest; (also) yes
**Usage:** The adjective from which kannut is derived. Used to evaluate a person or statement as sincere and honest. Also functions as the standard Hebrew word for "yes." The evaluative adjectival use is somewhat more formal or literary than the everyday use as "yes."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.2**
**Base Form:** כַּנּוּת (kannut)
**Derived Form:** בְּכַנּוּת (be-kannut)
**Transliteration:** be-kannut
**Literal Breakdown:** be- + kannut
**Natural English translation:** sincerely, honestly, frankly
**Usage:** Standard adverbial phrase used to introduce a sincere or frank statement. Common in everyday speech and writing. Documented in P1b root expansion (כ-ו-נ, item 23).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on kannut:** The primary derivational relationship runs from the adjective ken to the abstract noun kannut, and from kannut to the adverbial be-kannut. No verbal form derived directly from kannut or ken (in the sense of "sincerity") is in common use. The root's productivity in the truth-evaluation domain is largely exhausted by these three forms.

## Item 10 — יָשָׁר (yashar)

**Natural English translation:** integrity, uprightness, honesty, fairness

**Transliteration:** yesharut
**Literal Breakdown:** yashar + abstract suffix -ut
**Natural English translation:** straightforwardness, honesty, directness
**Usage:** Abstract noun somewhat more colloquial than yosher, emphasizing directness of manner. Documented in P1b root expansion (י-ש-ר, item 3).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.3**
**Base Form:** יָשָׁר (yashar)
**Derived Form:** בִּישָׁרוּת (be-yesharut)
**Transliteration:** be-yesharut
**Literal Breakdown:** be- + yesharut
**Natural English translation:** straightforwardly, honestly, directly
**Usage:** Adverbial phrase used to characterize speech or action as direct and honest. Common in everyday speech. Documented in P1b root expansion (י-ש-ר, item 4).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.4**
**Base Form:** יָשָׁר (yashar)
**Derived Form:** לְיַשֵּׁר (leyasher)
**Transliteration:** leyasher
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel infinitive of root י-ש-ר
**Natural English translation:** to straighten, to align, to rectify
**Usage:** The Piʿel verbal form meaning to straighten or align something. Used both literally (straightening a physical object) and figuratively (aligning positions, rectifying distortions). ---

**10.5**
**Base Form:** יָשָׁר (yashar)
**Derived Form:** יִישׁוּר קַו (yishshur qav)
**Transliteration:** yishshur qav
**Literal Breakdown:** Piʿel verbal noun of י-ש-ר + qav ("line")
**Natural English translation:** alignment, getting on the same page
**Usage:** Nominal form of the expression leyasher qav. Used in organizational and political contexts to describe the process of bringing positions or procedures into alignment. Documented in P1b root expansion (י-ש-ר, item 11).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.6**
**Base Form:** יָשָׁר (yashar)
**Derived Form:** יִישַׁר כֹּחַ (yishar koaḥ)
**Transliteration:** yishar koaḥ
**Literal Breakdown:** yishar (Piʿel jussive, "may it be straight/strong") + koaḥ ("strength")
**Natural English translation:** well done, congratulations
**Usage:** A very common conventional expression of congratulation or appreciation, used across all registers. Documented in P1b root expansion (י-ש-ר, item 9).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.7**
**Base Form:** יָשָׁר (yashar)
**Derived Form:** מִידַּת הַיֹּשֶׁר (middat ha-yosher)
**Transliteration:** middat ha-yosher
**Literal Breakdown:** middah ("measure, standard") + ha-yosher
**Natural English translation:** the standard of fairness, the measure of equity
**Usage:** A legal and ethical expression used in rabbinic and Israeli law to denote the standard of equitable conduct. Documented in P1b root expansion (י-ש-ר, item 7).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Item 12 — צֶדֶק (tsedeq)

---

**12.1**
**Base Form:** צֶדֶק (tsedeq)
**Derived Form:** צַדִּיק (tsaddiq)
**Transliteration:** tsaddiq
**Literal Breakdown:** Qal adjectival/participial form of root צ-ד-ק with dagesh forte
**Natural English translation:** righteous, just; a righteous person
**Usage:** Used as both an adjective and a noun to evaluate a person as morally righteous or just. Extremely common in biblical, rabbinic, and modern Hebrew across religious and secular contexts. The plural צדיקים (tsaddiqim) denotes righteous persons collectively.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.2**
**Base Form:** צֶדֶק (tsedeq)
**Derived Form:** צְדָקָה (tsedaqah)
**Transliteration:** tsedaqah
**Literal Breakdown:** Nominal form of root צ-ד-ק with feminine suffix -ah
**Natural English translation:** charity, righteousness, almsgiving
**Usage:** In biblical Hebrew denotes righteousness broadly; in rabbinic and modern Hebrew has narrowed primarily to the sense of charitable giving or almsgiving, understood as a moral obligation rather than voluntary generosity. One of the most common terms in Jewish ethical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.3**
**Base Form:** צֶדֶק (tsedeq)
**Derived Form:** צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט (tsedaqah u-mishpat)
**Transliteration:** tsedaqah u-mishpat
**Literal Breakdown:** tsedaqah + u- ("and") + mishpat ("justice, judgment")
**Natural English translation:** righteousness and justice
**Usage:** A fixed biblical paired expression (Genesis 18:19, Isaiah 1:27, etc.) denoting the dual foundations of a just social order. Widely cited in religious, legal, and political discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.4**
**Base Form:** צֶדֶק (tsedeq)
**Derived Form:** לִצְדֹּק (litsdog)
**Transliteration:** litsdog
**Literal Breakdown:** Qal infinitive of root צ-ד-ק
**Natural English translation:** to be right, to be correct, to be justified
**Usage:** The verbal form meaning "to be right" or "to be correct." Used in everyday speech ("הוא צדק" — "He was right") and in legal and moral discourse to evaluate whether a position or claim is justified.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.5**
**Base Form:** צֶדֶק (tsedeq)

**Usage:** A fixed legal and administrative expression denoting a clerical or typographical error in a document, as distinct from a substantive error. Used in legal and administrative contexts to evaluate whether an error in a document is merely formal and correctable.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Note on mutʿe:** The Hofʿal participial form mutʿe ("misled, mistaken") is itself a derivation of the root ט-ע-י/ט-ע-ה. The primary derivational cluster from this root includes the noun taʿut and the verb litʿot, both of which are more basic and frequent than mutʿe itself. The fixed compounds taʿut be-shiqul ha-daʿat and taʿut sofer have acquired independent legal status.
