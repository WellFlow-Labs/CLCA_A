# G3 — Emergent Cross-Language Semantic Dimensions

## Methodology Note

The dimensions below are derived from the F3 semantic grouping tables, F1 vocabulary inventories, F2 derivational and constructional data, and the G1–G2 comparative analyses already in context. Each dimension is defined by co-occurrence patterns, grouping decisions, and explicit contrasts documented in the source data. No dimension is asserted unless it is supported by evidence from at least two languages. Single-language observations are flagged as such. Where prior G-phase steps have already identified a pattern, that identification is confirmed or refined here rather than repeated in full.

---

## Proposed Semantic Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Propositional Correspondence

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a specific statement, claim, or report matches what is actually the case in the world. The evaluative target is a proposition or utterance, not a person or object. Terms in this dimension appear in contexts of assertion, denial, correction, and verification of factual claims.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *simarṭle* (truth, everyday), *č̣ešmariteba* (truth, philosophical), *mcdari* (erroneous), *sicrue* (falsehood)
- yo: *òtítọ́* (truth), *irọ́* (lie/falsehood), *ó tọ́* (it is correct)
- fi: *tosi* (true), *totuus* (truth), *epätosi* (untrue), *pitää paikkansa* (to hold/be true)
- eu: *egia* (truth), *gezurra* (lie/falsehood), *egia da* (it is true)
- ta: *uṇmai* (truth), *poy* (falsehood), *cari* (correct), *tavaṟu* (wrong/error)
- id: *benar* (correct/true), *salah* (wrong), *kebenaran* (truth)
- tr: *doğru* (correct/true), *yanlış* (wrong/false), *doğruluk* (truthfulness/accuracy)
- ko: *사실* (fact/truth), *허위* (falsehood), *맞다* (to be correct), *틀리다* (to be wrong)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. This is the most consistently attested dimension in the dataset. Every language maintains at least one dedicated nominal for truth and one for falsehood in the propositional sense, and these are the most productive bases for further derivation. Several languages split this dimension by register: ka maintains *č̣ešmariteba* (philosophical/theological) vs. *simarṭle* (everyday/legal); fi maintains *totuus* (nominal, formal) vs. *tosi* (predicative, colloquial); ko maintains *진리* (philosophical/universal) vs. *진실* (everyday truthfulness) vs. *사실* (factual accuracy). yo and ta show less register differentiation within this dimension.

### Dimension 2: Personal Honesty and Integrity in Conduct

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a person's habitual conduct, character, or disposition is free from deception and consistent with truthful expression. The evaluative target is a person or their pattern of behavior, not a specific proposition. Terms in this dimension evaluate character rather than the truth-value of a claim.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *gulc̣rpeli* (sincere/straight-hearted), *tvaltmaqcoba* (hypocrisy/eye-deceiving)
- yo: *ìwà rere* (good character), *ìwà burúkú* (bad character), *olòtítọ́* (truthful person)
- fi: *rehellinen* (honest), *rehellisyys* (honesty), *vilpitön* (sincere/in good faith), *vilpillinen* (fraudulent/deceitful)
- eu: *benetakoa* (genuine/sincere), *benetan* (truly/in earnest), *itxurakeria* (pretense/hypocrisy)
- ta: *nērmai* (honesty/integrity), *kaḷḷam* (deceit/guile), *vañcaṉai* (deception)
- id: *jujur* (honest), *kejujuran* (honesty), *tulus* (sincere), *ikhlas* (selflessly sincere)
- tr: *dürüst* (upright/honest), *dürüstlük* (honesty/integrity), *özü sözü bir* (one whose word matches their character)
- ko: *정직* (honesty/integrity), *정직하다* (to be honest), *거짓말* (lie), *거짓말하다* (to lie)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. The evaluative target (person/character vs. proposition) is the defining criterion distinguishing this from D1. The absence of a lexicalized single-word agentive for "truth-teller" is consistent across all eight languages, while agentives for "liar" are lexicalized in all eight. yo elaborates this dimension most extensively through the *ìwà* (character/manner of being) concept, which organizes a large cluster of moral evaluation vocabulary with no close structural parallel in other languages. id distinguishes *tulus* (sincere inner motivation) from *ikhlas* (selflessly sincere, with specifically religious connotations of acceptance without expectation of reward) — a sub-distinction not documented in other languages.

### Dimension 3: Source Trustworthiness vs. Content Credibility

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a person, institution, or source of information can be depended upon to provide accurate information (source trustworthiness), and whether a specific claim or account is such that it merits belief (content credibility). These two evaluative targets — the source and the content — are distinguished in all eight languages, though the degree of lexical differentiation varies.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *sarc̣muno* (credible/trustworthy, inherent property of source) vs. *damajerebeliˈ* (convincing/persuasive, effect on hearer); *sando* (reliable/dependable, interpersonal)
- yo: *fidagarri* (trustworthy source) vs. *sinesgarri* (believable content); *ìgbẹ́kẹ̀lé* (reliance/dependability)
- fi: *luotettava* (reliable/dependable over time) vs. *uskottava* (believable for a specific claim)
- eu: *fidagarria* (trustworthy source) vs. *sinesgarria* (believable content)
- ta: *nampakamāna* (reliable) vs. *nampattakka* (worth trusting for a specific claim); *nampikkai* (trust/faith)
- id: *terpercaya* (trusted/reliable) vs. *kredibel* (credible); *kepercayaan* (trust)
- tr: *güvenilir* (reliable/trustworthy) vs. *inanılır* (believable)
- ko: *신뢰성* (reliability of a source) vs. *신빙성* (credibility of a specific claim); *신뢰도* (degree of reliability)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. The source/content distinction is explicitly noted in F3 boundary notes for ka, yo, fi, and eu. ko shows the most internal differentiation within the trustworthiness dimension, maintaining distinct terms for *신뢰* (trust as a relationship), *신뢰성* (reliability as a property), *신뢰도* (degree of reliability), and *신빙성* (credibility of a specific claim).

### Dimension 4: Genuineness of Objects vs. Fraudulent Imitation

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a physical object, document, artwork, currency, or credential is what it purports to be (genuine/original) or is a fraudulent substitute (counterfeit/forged). The evaluative target is a physical item or document, not a proposition or person. Terms in this dimension appear in legal, commercial, and art-authentication contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *namdvili* (genuine/real), *q̇albi* (fake/counterfeit), *gaq̇albeba* (forgery/falsification), *avtentiuri* (authentic, formal)
- yo: *gidi* (genuine/real); less elaborated than other languages
- fi: *aito* (genuine/authentic), *alkuperäinen* (original), *väärennös* (forgery), *jäljitelmä* (imitation, neutral)
- eu: *benetakoa* (genuine/real), *jatorrizkoa* (original), *faltsua* (fake), *faltsifikatu* (to forge)
- ta: *acal* (original/genuine), *nakal* (copy, neutral), *pōli* (fake/counterfeit)
- id: *asli* (genuine/original), *tiruan* (imitation, neutral), *palsu* (fake/fraudulent), *pemalsuan* (forgery)
- tr: *orijinal* (original), *özgün* (authentic/original, creative), *sahte* (counterfeit/fake), *taklit* (imitation)
- ko: *진짜* (genuine/real), *가짜* (fake/counterfeit), *진품* (genuine article), *위조* (forgery), *모조품* (imitation goods)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, though the degree of elaboration varies. At least six languages (fi, eu, ta, id, tr, ko) explicitly distinguish a neutral-copy pole (imitation without fraudulent intent) from a fraudulent-fake pole (counterfeit with intent to deceive). ka and yo show less elaboration of this three-way contrast: ka has *namdvili* vs. *q̇albi* as a binary, and yo lacks a dedicated forgery vocabulary.

### Dimension 5: Legal/Procedural Validity vs. Moral/Substantive Legitimacy

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether an action, document, contract, or authority meets the formal conditions required for legal recognition (procedural validity) and whether it is rightful or justified in a broader normative sense that may extend beyond strict legality (legitimacy). All eight languages maintain some distinction between these two evaluative targets, though the degree of lexical differentiation varies.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *kanonieri* (lawful, tied to positive law) vs. *legitimuri* (legitimate, with possible normative dimension); *sasamartlo* (court), *marṭlmsajuleba* (justice/administration of justice)
- yo: *ẹ̀tọ́* (right/entitlement/correctness), *ìdájọ́* (judgment/adjudication), *òdodo* (righteousness/justice); less lexically differentiated
- fi: *pätevä* (valid/competent) vs. *oikeudenmukainen* (just/fair)
- eu: *baliozkoa* (valid) vs. *zilegi* (lawful/permissible) vs. *zuzen* (correct/just); *baliogabea* (invalid)
- ta: *caṭṭappaṭi* (according to law) vs. *niyāyam* (justice/rightness); *takuti* (qualification/eligibility)
- id: *sah* (legally valid) vs. *adil* (fair/just)
- tr: *yasal* (legal, conforming to written law) vs. *meşru* (legitimate, extending beyond legality); *geçerli* (valid/in force) vs. *geçersiz* (invalid/void)
- ko: *합법* (lawful) vs. *정당* (legitimate/justified); *유효* (valid/in force) vs. *무효* (invalid/void)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages. The distinction between strict legality and broader legitimacy is explicitly documented in F3 grouping tables for ka, fi, eu, id, tr, and ko. The distinction between operative validity (in force) and invalidity (void) is documented in all eight languages. tr shows the most explicit lexical differentiation, with *yasal* (legal), *meşru* (legitimate), and *geçerli* (valid/in force) as three distinct terms. ko similarly distinguishes *합법* (lawful), *정당* (legitimate/justified), and *유효* (valid/in force). yo shows the least elaboration of this dimension.

### Dimension 6: Precision and Exactness of Correspondence

**Definition.** This dimension concerns the degree to which a measurement, description, translation, or representation corresponds closely and exactly to its referent or standard. Unlike D1 (propositional correspondence, which is binary), this dimension is scalar and concerns the closeness of fit between a representation and what it represents. Terms in this dimension appear in scientific, technical, journalistic, and administrative contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *zusti* (exact/precise), *sizuste* (accuracy/precision), *zusti targmani* (accurate translation)
- yo: *tọ́ pátápátá* (completely correct); less elaborated as a distinct dimension
- fi: *tarkka* (precise/accurate), *tarkkuus* (accuracy/precision), *tarkka mittaus* (accurate measurement)
- eu: *zehatza* (precise/exact/accurate), *zehaztasuna* (precision/accuracy)
- ta: *uṇmaiyāna* (true/genuine/accurate), *caripārtttal* (verification/checking); less elaborated as a distinct dimension
- id: *tepat* (precise/exact/fitting), *akurat* (accurate), *cermat* (careful/meticulous/precise)
- tr: *doğru* (correct/accurate), *doğruluk* (accuracy/truthfulness); less elaborated as distinct from correctness
- ko: *정확* (accuracy/precision), *정확성* (accuracy as a property), *정확히* (accurately/exactly), *정확도* (degree of accuracy)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in at least six languages (ka, fi, eu, id, ko, and partially tr). In yo and ta, the dimension is less elaborated as a distinct lexical category; the concept is expressed through the general correctness vocabulary rather than through dedicated precision terms. ko shows the most elaboration, with *정확* generating a productive derivational family including *정확성*, *정확도*, *정확히*, and *부정확*. fi shows elaboration through *tarkka*, *tarkkuus*, *tarkistaa* (to check/verify), and *tarkastaa* (to inspect/audit), with the *tarkistaa*/*tarkastaa* register distinction documented only in fi.

### Dimension 7: Authenticity of Inner States and Expressed Feelings

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a person's expressed feelings, intentions, apologies, or commitments reflect genuine inner conviction rather than performance, calculation, or pretense. The evaluative target is the relationship between inner state and outward expression, not the truth-value of a proposition or the genuineness of a physical object. Terms in this dimension appear in interpersonal, political, and artistic evaluation contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *gulc̣rpeli* (sincere/straight-hearted, literally "straight-hearted"), *tvaltmaqcoba* (hypocrisy/eye-deceiving)
- yo: *ìwà rere* (good character), *àgàbàgebè* (pretense/hypocrisy/sham behavior)
- fi: *aito tunne* (genuine feeling), *epäaito* (inauthentic), *vilpitön* (sincere/in good faith)
- eu: *benetakoa* (genuine/sincere), *benetan* (truly/in earnest), *itxurakeria* (pretense/hypocrisy)
- ta: *uṇmai aṉpu* (true love/genuine affection), *nērmai* (honesty/integrity); less elaborated as a distinct dimension
- id: *tulus* (sincere), *ikhlas* (selflessly sincere), *sungguh-sungguh* (in earnest/genuinely)
- tr: *sahici* (genuine/authentic, colloquial), *özü sözü bir* (one whose word matches their character)
- ko: *진정성* (authenticity of inner state), *진심* (sincere heart/genuine feeling), *진심으로* (sincerely/from the heart)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages, but the degree of lexical elaboration varies considerably. ko shows the most elaboration, with *진정성* functioning as a highly developed public evaluative category with a rich collocation network (*진정성 있다/없다*, *진정성 논란*, *진정성을 의심받다*). eu and fi show moderate elaboration. ta and tr show less elaboration of this as a distinct dimension. yo's *ìwà* (character/manner of being) concept organizes this dimension differently, treating it as a matter of fundamental character rather than the authenticity of specific expressions.

### Dimension 8: Appearance vs. Underlying Reality

**Definition.** This dimension concerns the contrast between how something appears on the surface and what it actually is. Terms in this dimension are used to signal that a surface appearance may be misleading, that something presents itself as other than it is, or that the true nature of something differs from its apparent nature. This dimension is distinct from D4 (genuineness of objects) in that it applies to situations, arguments, and qualities rather than primarily to physical items.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *moč̣venebiti* (apparent/seeming/ostensible), *garegnuli* (external/outward/superficial), *šinaˈarsi* (content/substance/inner meaning)
- yo: *ìfarahàn* (appearance/manifestation), *àgàbàgebè* (pretense/sham); less elaborated as a distinct dimension
- fi: *näennäinen* (apparent/seeming/ostensible), *todellinen* (real/actual), *todellisuus* (reality)
- eu: *itxura* (appearance/semblance), *itxurazko* (apparent/seeming/superficial), *itxurakeria* (pretense/hypocrisy), *itxuraz* (apparently/seemingly)
- ta: *uṇmai nilavaram* (true state of affairs), *uṇmai nilai* (the actual situation); less elaborated as a distinct dimension
- id: *sebenarnya* (actually/in truth), *pada kenyataannya* (in reality), *nyata* (real/manifest)
- tr: *gerçek dışı* (unreal/contrary to fact), *gerçeğe aykırı* (contrary to reality), *aslında* (actually/in fact)
- ko: *실체* (true nature/underlying reality), *실제* (actuality/reality), *실제로* (actually/in reality)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages, but the degree of lexical elaboration varies. eu shows the most elaboration, with *itxura* generating a productive derivational family (*itxurazko*, *itxurakeria*, *itxuragilea*, *itxuraz*). ka shows elaboration through *moč̣venebiti* (ostensible) and the *šinaˈarsi*/*garegnuli* (inner content/outer appearance) contrast. fi shows elaboration through *näennäinen* (apparent/seeming). yo and ta show less elaboration of this as a distinct lexical dimension; the contrast is expressed through constructions and discourse markers rather than dedicated adjectives. The ka *moč̣venebiti* is explicitly distinguished from *q̇albi* (fake, implying deliberate deception) and from *namdvili* (genuine) in the F5 opposition tables — a three-way distinction not paralleled in other languages.

### Dimension 9: Evidential Grounding and Proof

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a claim is supported by adequate evidence or grounds, and the processes by which evidence is produced, evaluated, and used to establish the truth of a claim. Terms in this dimension appear in legal, scientific, journalistic, and argumentative contexts. The evaluative question is not merely whether a claim is true but whether it can be demonstrated to be true.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *mt'k'icebuleba* (proof/evidence), *mt'k'iceba* (assertion/act of proving), *dadastureba* (confirmation/verification), *utyuari* (irrefutable/beyond doubt)
- yo: *ẹ̀rí* (evidence/proof/witness), *ìjẹ́rìísí* (testimony/attestation/certification), *ìdánwò* (test/trial/verification)
- fi: *todiste* (piece of evidence), *todistus* (proof/testimony/certificate), *todistustaakka* (burden of proof), *todistusvoima* (probative force)
- eu: *froga* (proof/evidence), *frogatu* (to prove/demonstrate), *egiaztagarria* (verifiable), *egiaztaezina* (unverifiable)
- ta: *ātāram* (evidence/basis/grounds), *cāṉṟu* (evidence/proof/attestation), *nirūpaṇam* (proof/demonstration)
- id: *bukti* (evidence/proof), *pembuktian* (proof/verification), *tidak berdasar* (unfounded/baseless)
- tr: *kanıt* (evidence/proof), *kanıtlamak* (to prove), *ispat* (proof, formal), *asılsız* (unfounded/baseless)
- ko: *근거* (basis/grounds/evidence), *신빙성* (credibility of a claim), *타당성* (validity/soundness), *위증* (perjury/false testimony)

**Distribution:** Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. The distinction between the act of proving and the product of proof is documented in at least five languages (ka *mt'k'iceba* vs. *mt'k'icebuleba*; fi *todistaa* vs. *todiste*; eu *frogatu* vs. *froga*; id *membuktikan* vs. *bukti*; tr *kanıtlamak* vs. *kanıt*). eu shows the most elaboration of the verifiability sub-dimension, with the three-way *egiaztagarria* (verifiable) / *egiaztagaitza* (difficult to verify) / *egiaztaezina* (impossible to verify) distinction unique in the dataset. tr shows the unique *doğrulanabilirlik*/*yanlışlanabilirlik* (verifiability/falsifiability) morphological opposition.

### Dimension 10: Truth Revelation and Concealment as Processes

**Definition.** This dimension concerns the processes by which truth is brought to light, concealed, sought, or suppressed. Terms in this dimension treat truth as an entity that can be revealed, hidden, distorted, or discovered, and they appear in investigative, journalistic, narrative, and legal contexts. This dimension is distinct from D1 (propositional correspondence) in that it concerns the dynamics of truth-disclosure rather than the static evaluation of a proposition.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *simarṭle gamovida* (the truth came out), *simarṭle damalva* (concealment of truth), *simarṭle gamovlena* (revelation of truth)
- yo: *ó farahàn pé òtítọ́ ni* (it became clear that it is the truth), *ó ń pa òtítọ́ mọ́* (he/she is concealing the truth)
- fi: *totuus tulee ilmi* (the truth comes to light), *totuus paljastuu* (the truth is revealed)
- eu: *egia argitara atera* (to bring truth to light), *egia ezkutatu* (to hide the truth), *egiaren bila ibili* (to go about seeking the truth)
- ta: *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* (the truth came to light), *uṇmai maṟaittal* (concealment of truth), *uṇmai tēṭutal* (seeking the truth)
- id: *mengungkap kebenaran* (to reveal the truth), *menyembunyikan kebenaran* (to conceal the truth), *kebenaran akan terungkap* (the truth will come out)
- tr: *gerçeği ortaya çıkarmak* (to bring the truth to light), *gerçeği gizlemek* (to hide the truth), *hakikati aramak* (to seek the truth)
- ko: *진실이 드러나다* (the truth emerges), *진실을 밝히다* (to reveal the truth), *진실을 숨기다* (to conceal the truth), *진상 규명* (clarification of the true facts)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages. The intransitive/passive framing of truth-revelation (truth as grammatical subject of an emergence verb) is documented in all eight languages. The transitive framing (agent reveals/conceals truth) is also documented in all eight languages. The proverbial assertion that truth ultimately prevails is documented in all eight languages. ko shows the most elaboration of the investigative sub-dimension, with *진상 규명* (clarification of the true facts) and *진실화해위원회* (Truth and Reconciliation Commission) as institutionalized expressions. The spatial metaphor of truth "coming to light" or "coming to the surface" is consistent across all eight languages.

### Dimension 11: Correctness of Reasoning, Judgment, and Understanding

**Definition.** This dimension concerns whether a judgment, inference, interpretation, or understanding is correct — whether it follows properly from evidence or reasoning. It is distinct from D1 (propositional correspondence) in that the evaluative target is a cognitive process or its output rather than a simple factual claim. Terms in this dimension appear in educational, philosophical, and argumentative contexts.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *martebuli* (correct/appropriate/well-founded), *martebuloba* (correctness/soundness), *gamarṭleba* (justification/vindication)
- yo: *ó tọ́ pé* (it is right that), *àṣìṣe* (error/mistake/wrongdoing)
- fi: *perusteltu* (justified/well-founded), *perusteeton* (unfounded/groundless), *pätevä* (valid/sound)
- eu: *zuzena* (correct/right/just), *okerra* (wrong/incorrect/crooked), *zuzentasun* (correctness/justice)
- ta: *niyāyamāna* (just/fair/reasonable), *cariyāna* (correct/proper), *tavaṟāna* (wrong/erroneous)
- id: *tepat* (precise/fitting/appropriate), *wajar* (reasonable/natural/proper), *pantas* (fitting/appropriate/deserved)
- tr: *haklı* (justified/in the right), *haksız* (unjust/wrongful), *tarafsız* (impartial/neutral)
- ko: *타당* (valid/reasonable/sound), *타당성* (validity/soundness), *올바르다* (to be correct/right/proper), *옳다* (to be right/correct)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages, but the degree of differentiation from D1 (propositional correspondence) varies. In some languages (yo, ta), the same root covers both propositional correctness and correctness of reasoning. In others (ka, fi, ko), distinct vocabulary is used for these two functions. ka shows the most elaboration of the justification sub-dimension, with *gamarṭleba* (justification/vindication/acquittal) generating a productive derivational family. tr shows elaboration through the *haklı*/*haksız* pair (justified/unjustified), which covers both epistemic and moral justification. ko shows elaboration through the *타당성* (validity/soundness) cluster, specifically associated with argumentative and policy contexts.

### Dimension 12: Deliberate Deception vs. Innocent Error

**Definition.** This dimension concerns the distinction between statements or representations that are false because of deliberate intent to deceive and those that are false because of honest mistake or misunderstanding. Terms in this dimension appear in moral, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts where the agent's intent is relevant to the evaluation. This dimension is a sub-distinction within the broader domain of falsehood.

**Representative terms:**
- ka: *sicrue*/*ẗq̇uili* (deliberate lie) vs. *mcdari* (erroneous/mistaken, without implying intent); *q̇albi* (fake, implying deliberate deception) vs. *mcdari* (erroneous)
- yo: *irọ́* (deliberate lie) vs. *àṣìṣe* (error/mistake, without strong implication of intent); *ìtànjẹ́* (deliberate deception/fraud)
- fi: *valhe* (lie, deliberate) vs. *virhe* (error, neutral); *väärentää* (to forge, implying intent) vs. *vääristymä* (distortion, possibly unintentional)
- eu: *gezurra* (lie, deliberate) vs. *oker* (wrong/mistaken, without implying intent); *faltsifikatu* (to forge, implying intent)
- ta: *poy* (lie, deliberate) vs. *tavaṟu* (mistake/error, without implying intent); *vañcaṉai* (deliberate deception)
- id: *bohong* (deliberate lie) vs. *keliru* (innocent error/misunderstanding); *salah* (wrong, neutral to intent)
- tr: *yalan* (deliberate lie) vs. *yanlış* (wrong/incorrect, neutral to intent); *aldatıcı* (deceptive, implying intent) vs. *yanıltıcı* (misleading, possibly unintentional)
- ko: *거짓말* (deliberate lie) vs. *실수* (mistake, innocent); *허위* (falsehood, formal) vs. *오류* (error/fallacy, neutral)

**Distribution:** Present in all eight languages. Explicitly documented in F3 grouping tables for at least six languages (ka, fi, eu, id, tr, ko). id shows the most explicit lexical differentiation, with *bohong* (deliberate lie) vs. *keliru* (innocent error) explicitly distinguished in the F3 grouping tables. tr shows elaboration through the *aldatıcı* (deceptive, implying intent) vs. *yanıltıcı* (misleading, possibly unintentional) distinction. fi shows elaboration through the *väärentää* (to forge, implying intent) vs. *vääristymä* (distortion, possibly unintentional) distinction.

## Evaluation of Cross-Linguistic Support

### Strongly Supported Dimensions

The following dimensions are clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, appear in the F3 semantic grouping tables of all eight languages, and are supported by explicit contrasts in the F5 opposition tables of multiple languages:

- **D1 (Propositional Correspondence)** — The most consistently attested dimension. Every language has dedicated nominals for truth and falsehood in the propositional sense, and these are the most productive derivational bases in all eight languages.
- **D2 (Personal Honesty and Integrity)** — Universally present. The evaluative target (person/character) consistently distinguishes this from D1 across all languages. The absence of a single-word agentive for "truth-teller" is consistent across all eight languages.
- **D3 (Source Trustworthiness vs. Content Credibility)** — Explicitly documented in all eight languages, with the source/content distinction noted in F3 boundary notes for multiple languages.
- **D5 (Legal/Procedural Validity vs. Moral/Substantive Legitimacy)** — Present in all eight languages. The operative validity vs. invalidity sub-distinction is documented in all eight languages.
- **D9 (Evidential Grounding and Proof)** — Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages. The act-of-proving vs. product-of-proof distinction is documented in at least five languages.
- **D10 (Truth Revelation and Concealment as Processes)** — Present in all eight languages. The intransitive/passive framing of truth-revelation and the proverbial assertion that truth ultimately prevails are both documented in all eight languages.
- **D12 (Deliberate Deception vs. Innocent Error)** — Present in all eight languages. Explicitly documented in F3 grouping tables for at least six languages.

### Moderately Supported Dimensions

The following dimensions are present in most languages but show variable degrees of lexical elaboration:

- **D4 (Genuineness of Objects vs. Fraudulent Imitation)** — Clearly lexicalized in all eight languages, but the neutral-copy vs. fraudulent-fake sub-distinction is documented in only six languages. yo shows the least elaboration.
- **D6 (Precision and Exactness of Correspondence)** — Clearly lexicalized in at least six languages. Less elaborated in yo and ta as a distinct dimension separate from D1.
- **D7 (Authenticity of Inner States and Expressed Feelings)** — Present in all eight languages, but degree of elaboration varies considerably. ko shows the most elaboration; ta and tr show less.
- **D8 (Appearance vs. Underlying Reality)** — Present in at least six languages with explicit documentation. eu shows the most elaboration. yo and ta show less elaboration as a distinct lexical dimension.
- **D11 (Correctness of Reasoning, Judgment, and Understanding)** — Present in all eight languages, but degree of differentiation from D1 varies. Some languages use the same root for both propositional correctness and correctness of reasoning.

### Dimensions That Split, Merge, or Vary Across Specific Languages

**D1 and D11 merge in yo and ta.** The *tọ́* root in yo covers both propositional correctness (*ó tọ́*, "it is correct") and procedural/moral correctness (*ó tọ́ pé*, "it is right that") without lexical differentiation. Similarly, ta *cari* covers both factual correctness and procedural correctness. ka, fi, and ko maintain more distinct vocabulary for these two functions.

**D2 and D7 partially merge in eu and id.** eu *benetakoa* covers both "genuine (not fake)" and "sincere (not performed)," merging D4 and D7 partially. id *tulus* and *ikhlas* cover both personal honesty (D2) and authenticity of inner states (D7) without a sharp lexical boundary.

**D4 and D7 partially merge in eu.** *Benetakoa* applies to both genuine objects (D4) and genuine feelings/intentions (D7), while fi and ko maintain more distinct vocabulary for these two functions.

**D5 splits into three sub-dimensions in tr and ko.** tr distinguishes *yasal* (legal), *meşru* (legitimate), and *geçerli* (valid/in force) as three distinct terms. ko similarly distinguishes *합법* (lawful), *정당* (legitimate/justified), and *유효* (valid/in force). Other languages show less lexical differentiation within D5.

**D6 is weakly attested in yo and ta.** The precision/exactness dimension is not elaborated as a distinct lexical category in these languages; the concept is expressed through the general correctness vocabulary of D1.

**D8 is weakly attested in yo and ta.** The appearance-vs.-reality dimension is not elaborated as a distinct lexical category in these languages; the concept is expressed through more general vocabulary and discourse markers.

**D9 shows unique elaboration in eu.** The three-way *egiaztagarria* (verifiable) / *egiaztagaitza* (difficult to verify) / *egiaztaezina* (impossible to verify) distinction within the verifiability sub-dimension has no parallel in any other language in the dataset.

**D9 shows unique morphological elaboration in tr.** The *doğrulamak*/*yanlışlamak* productive morphological pair encoding the verification/falsification distinction, with parallel *doğrulanabilirlik*/*yanlışlanabilirlik* nominalizations, is unique in the dataset.

**D10 shows unique institutionalization in ko.** The *진상 규명* (clarification of the true facts) and *진실화해위원회* (Truth and Reconciliation Commission) expressions represent an institutionalized elaboration of this dimension not paralleled in other languages.

**D7 shows unique elaboration in ko.** The *진정성* (authenticity of inner state) cluster is more elaborated as a public evaluative category in ko than in any other language in the dataset, with a rich collocation network (*진정성 있다/없다*, *진정성 논란*, *진정성을 의심받다*) that has no close parallel elsewhere.

**D1 shows a unique register bifurcation in ka.** The *sarwmunoeba* form bifurcates into "credibility" (secular) and "faith/religion" (theological) — a semantic split within a single form that is documented only in ka, with only a partial parallel in yo *ìgbàgbọ́* (covering both belief and faith).
