# CLCA Protocol v3.3 — P6 Change, Causation, and Stability
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Scope note:** Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka). All vocabulary drawn exclusively from P1–P5. Orthography follows the normalized three-vowel system. Where no common expression is attested in the source data, this is stated explicitly. Each truth-related term is treated separately where the data indicate different behavior.

---

## 1. BECOMING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE

### 1.1 Becoming correct / good (*allin*)

**Pattern:** allin + inchoative suffix *-ya* + infinitive *-y* → *allinyay*

**Example:** Allinyay
**Transliteration:** allin-ya-y
**English translation:** to become correct, to improve, to get right, to become good

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 16.2) as the inchoative verb derived from *allin* by the productive suffix *-ya*. Described as appearing in moral, practical, and health-related contexts without register restriction. This is the most straightforwardly attested inchoative form in the truth-evaluation domain.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.2 Becoming true / real (*cheqa*)

**Pattern:** cheqa + stative *-ka* + infinitive *-y* → *cheqakay* (base); nominalized accusative form *cheqakayta* is the attested surface form

**Example:** Cheqakayta
**Transliteration:** cheqa-ka-y-ta
**English translation:** the being true, the becoming real (as object of an epistemic verb)

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 1.5) and P4a (section 4). The *-ka* stative suffix on *cheqa* is noted as specific to this root rather than the general productive inchoative *-ya* used on *allin*. The attested form appears in object position of epistemic verbs (e.g., "to know that something is true"). P4a rates this as [MARGINAL] in the inchoative function, noting contextual restriction to that position. The base form *cheqakay* is implied but the accusative nominalization is the attested surface form.

**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL] *(contextually restricted to object position of epistemic verbs in attested data)*

---

### 1.3 Becoming false / deceptive (*llulla*)

**(no common expression identified)**

**Note:** P4a explicitly states that "no inchoative form of *llulla* is attested as a common expression in the sources consulted" (P4a, section 4). No form parallel to *allinyay* or *cheqakay* is documented for *llulla* across P1–P5.

---

### 1.4 Becoming clear (*sut'i*)

**(no common inchoative expression identified)**

**Note:** No inchoative form of *sut'i* (e.g., *sut'iyay*) is documented in P1–P5. The causative-reflexive *sut'ichakuy* ("to become clear, to clarify for oneself," P1b item 8 / P2a Derived Form 26.5) describes a process of becoming clear but is analyzed in the source data as a causative-reflexive rather than a bare inchoative. It is documented separately under section 3 below.

---

### 1.5 Becoming mistaken / in error (*pantay*)

**Pattern:** *pantay* is itself the verb for being/going into error; no separate inchoative derivation is documented

**Example:** Pantay
**Transliteration:** pantay
**English translation:** to err, to make a mistake, to be wrong, to go astray

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1 (item 24) as "the standard verb for being in error or making a mistake." The verb itself encodes entry into an error state. No separate inchoative derivation from *pantay* is documented in P1–P5.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.6 Becoming a believer / coming to believe (*iñiy*)

**Pattern:** iñiy + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *iñikuy*

**Example:** Iñikuy
**Transliteration:** iñi-ku-y
**English translation:** to believe for oneself, to put one's trust in, to commit to belief

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 13.3) as describing "the act of personally committing to belief or trust," with the reflexive *-ku* marking the belief as self-directed and internally motivated. This form encodes the transition into a state of belief rather than the static state of believing. Appears in religious and interpersonal contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 1.7 Becoming known / coming to be recognized (*riqsiy*)

**Pattern:** riqsiy + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *riqsikuy*

**Example:** Riqsikuy
**Transliteration:** riqsi-ku-y
**English translation:** to know oneself, to introduce oneself, to identify oneself

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 11.4) as used in contexts of self-identification and self-knowledge. The reflexive *-ku* marks the action as self-directed. This form encodes a transition into a state of being known or recognized (from the perspective of the agent presenting themselves). Appears in social and legal contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 2. BECOMING A NON-TRUTH / OPPOSED STATE

### 2.1 Going into error (*pantay* / *pantakuy*)

**Pattern A:** pantay (base verb — entering error state)

**Example:** Pantay
**Transliteration:** pantay
**English translation:** to err, to make a mistake, to go astray

**Usage notes:** As noted in 1.5 above, *pantay* itself encodes entry into error. Documented in P1 (item 24).

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Pattern B:** pantay + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *pantakuy*

**Example:** Pantakuy
**Transliteration:** panta-ku-y
**English translation:** to make a mistake on one's own, to err (reflexively), to go astray

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 24.4) as describing "the act of making an error that is self-directed or internally motivated, as opposed to being caused by another." Appears in contexts of self-assessment and moral reflection. The reflexive *-ku* distinguishes self-generated error from error caused by another agent.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.2 Becoming deceived (*llullasqa*)

**Pattern:** llulla + past participle *-sqa* → *llullasqa* (resultant state of having been deceived)

**Example:** Llullasqa
**Transliteration:** llulla-sqa
**English translation:** deceived, having been lied to, taken in by a falsehood

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 7.5) as used "to characterize a person who has been the victim of deception." The past participle encodes the resultant state of having undergone deception. Appears in narrative and legal contexts when describing the outcome of dishonest acts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.3 Becoming self-deceived (*llullachikuy*)

**Pattern:** llulla + causative *-chi* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *llullachikuy*

**Example:** Llullachikuy
**Transliteration:** llulla-chi-ku-y
**English translation:** to deceive oneself, to be self-deceived, to let oneself be misled

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 7.4) and P1b as describing "self-deception or the state of allowing oneself to be deceived." The causative-reflexive combination encodes a transition into a state of self-deception. Appears in moral and psychological discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.4 Pretending to be something one is not (*llulla tukuy*)

**Pattern:** llulla + tukuy (to become, to pretend to be)

**Example:** Llulla tukuy
**Transliteration:** llulla tukuy
**English translation:** to pretend, to feign, to act falsely, to put on a false appearance

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 15 under llulla) and P3 (Group 7) as describing "the act of pretending or feigning — presenting a false appearance of being something one is not." The verb *tukuy* (to become, to pretend to be) encodes a transition into a false state or appearance. P3 notes that this expression also appears in narrative contexts describing theatrical or ritual performance where the pretending is not morally condemned.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 2.5 Becoming realized / actualized (*cheqa tukuy* — the actualization sense)

**Pattern:** cheqa + tukuy (to become real)

**Example:** Cheqa tukuy
**Transliteration:** cheqa tukuy
**English translation:** to become real, to be realized (actualization sense)

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1b (item 25) and P3 (overlapping items) as having two attested senses: (1) the actualization of something into reality, and (2) the pretense of being genuine when one is not. The actualization sense encodes a transition from a non-real to a real state. P3 notes that the two senses are present in the same form and the data do not support assigning it to one group over the other.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## 3. CAUSING A TRUTH-RELATED STATE (CAUSATIVES)

### 3.1 Causing something to be true / verified (*cheqachiy*)

**Pattern:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *cheqachiy*

**Example:** Cheqachiy
**Transliteration:** cheqa-chi-y
**English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to prove, to correct

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1 (item 4), P2a (Derived Form 1.3), and P4a (section 5) as the primary causative of *cheqa*. Described as used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts for "the act of establishing accuracy or correcting error." The causative suffix *-chi* encodes an agent causing a state of truth or correctness to obtain. Rated [CORE] throughout.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.2 Causing someone to be deceived / causing a false state (*llullachiy*)

**Pattern:** llulla + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *llullachiy*

**Example:** Llullachiy
**Transliteration:** llulla-chi-y
**English translation:** to deceive someone, to cause to lie, to mislead

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 7.3) and P4a (section 5) as the causative of *llulla*. Described as used "in contexts where one person causes another to be deceived or induces another to lie," with the causative construction shifting agency to the person who engineers the deception. Appears in legal and moral discourse.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.3 Causing something to become clear (*sut'ichay*)

**Pattern:** sut'i + causative *-cha* + infinitive *-y* → *sut'ichay*

**Example:** Sut'ichay
**Transliteration:** sut'i-cha-y
**English translation:** to clarify, to explain, to make plain, to specify

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1 (item 27), P2a (Derived Form 26.1), and P4a (section 5) as the causative of *sut'i*. Described as used in contexts of clarification, specification, and disambiguation. The causative suffix *-cha* encodes an agent causing a state of clarity to obtain.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.4 Causing something to be corrected / made right (*allinchay*)

**Pattern:** allin + causative *-cha* + infinitive *-y* → *allinchay*

**Example:** Allinchay
**Transliteration:** allin-cha-y
**English translation:** to correct, to fix, to repair, to make right, to improve

**Usage notes:** Documented in P1 (item 17), P2a (Derived Form 16.1), and P4a (section 5) as the causative of *allin*. Described as used in contexts of correcting errors, repairing objects, and rectifying conduct. The causative encodes an agent bringing something into conformity with what is right or correct.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.5 Causing someone to err / misleading (*pantachiy*)

**Pattern:** pantay + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *pantachiy*

**Example:** Pantachiy
**Transliteration:** panta-chi-y
**English translation:** to mislead, to cause to err, to lead astray, to confuse

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 24.3) as the causative of *pantay*. Described as used "in contexts where one person causes another to make an error or go astray," appearing in moral discourse about misleading others and in legal contexts involving deliberate misdirection.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.6 Causing someone to believe / convincing (*iñichiy*)

**Pattern:** iñiy + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *iñichiy*

**Example:** Iñichiy
**Transliteration:** iñi-chi-y
**English translation:** to convince, to persuade, to cause to believe, to make someone trust

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 13.2) as the causative of *iñiy*. Described as used "in contexts of persuasion and the establishment of credibility," appearing in legal, religious, and everyday contexts where one person attempts to bring another to belief or trust.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.7 Causing someone to know / teaching (*yachachiy*)

**Pattern:** yachay + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *yachachiy*

**Example:** Yachachiy
**Transliteration:** yacha-chi-y
**English translation:** to teach, to instruct, to cause to learn

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 12.2) as the causative of *yachay*. Described as "the standard verb for teaching and instruction" and "one of the most commonly attested causative forms in Quechua." The causative encodes an agent causing a state of knowledge to come about in another person.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.8 Causing someone to know / recognize (*riqsichiy*)

**Pattern:** riqsiy + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *riqsichiy*

**Example:** Riqsichiy
**Transliteration:** riqsi-chi-y
**English translation:** to introduce, to make known, to present, to identify to someone

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 11.3) as the causative of *riqsiy*. Described as used "in contexts of introduction and identification — making a person or thing known to another," appearing in social, legal, and administrative contexts where formal identification is required.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3.9 Causing something to become real / actualizing (*cheqakaychiy*)

**Pattern:** cheqa + stative *-ka* + causative *-chi* + infinitive *-y* → *cheqakaychiy*

**Example:** Cheqakaychiy
**Transliteration:** cheqa-ka-y-chi-y
**English translation:** to realize, to actualize, to make real, to bring about

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 1.6) and P3 (overlapping items) as used "in contexts where a plan, promise, or intention is brought into reality," appearing in administrative, political, and religious contexts (fulfilling a vow or prophecy). P3 notes that its connection to the truth-evaluation domain is through the root *cheqa*, but its primary function in the attested contexts is actualization rather than truth-evaluation per se.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 4. SELF-DIRECTED CAUSATIVE (CAUSATIVE + REFLEXIVE) CONSTRUCTIONS

### 4.1 Verifying for oneself / proving oneself genuine (*cheqachikuy*)

**Pattern:** cheqa + causative *-chi* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *cheqachikuy*

**Example:** Cheqachikuy
**Transliteration:** cheqa-chi-ku-y
**English translation:** to verify for oneself, to confirm, to prove oneself genuine

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 1.7) as used "in contexts where a person establishes their own credibility or verifies something personally," with the reflexive *-ku* marking the action as self-directed. Appears in legal and interpersonal contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.2 Deceiving oneself (*llullachikuy*)

**Pattern:** llulla + causative *-chi* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *llullachikuy*

**Example:** Llullachikuy
**Transliteration:** llulla-chi-ku-y
**English translation:** to deceive oneself, to be self-deceived, to let oneself be misled

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 7.4) as describing self-deception. Already noted under section 2.3 above; included here to complete the parallel set of causative-reflexive forms across the domain.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.3 Correcting oneself (*allinchakuy*)

**Pattern:** allin + causative *-cha* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *allinchakuy*

**Example:** Allinchakuy
**Transliteration:** allin-cha-ku-y
**English translation:** to correct oneself, to improve oneself, to reform

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 16.6) as describing "the process of self-correction or self-improvement," appearing in moral and educational discourse when a person rectifies their own errors or conduct.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 4.4 Clarifying for oneself / becoming clear (*sut'ichakuy*)

**Pattern:** sut'i + causative *-cha* + reflexive *-ku* + infinitive *-y* → *sut'ichakuy*

**Example:** Sut'ichakuy
**Transliteration:** sut'i-cha-ku-y
**English translation:** to become clear, to clarify for oneself, to clear up

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 26.5) as describing "the process of something becoming clear or of a person achieving clarity of understanding," appearing in epistemic and deliberative contexts.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## 5. RESULTANT STATES (PAST PARTICIPLE FORMS)

The past participle suffix *-sqa* encodes the resultant state following a change or causation event. The following forms are attested across the domain.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| cheqachisqa | cheqa-chi-sqa | verified, confirmed, proven, corrected | P2a Derived Form 4.1 | [CORE] |
| llullasqa | llulla-sqa | deceived, having been lied to | P2a Derived Form 7.5 | [CORE] |
| llullakusqa | llulla-ku-sqa | having lied, having deceived | P2a Derived Form 7.7 | [CORE] |
| allinchasqa | allin-cha-sqa | corrected, fixed, repaired, made right | P2a Derived Form 17.1 | [CORE] |
| sut'ichasqa | sut'i-cha-sqa | clarified, made clear, specified, explained | P2a Derived Form 26.6 | [CORE] |
| pantasqa | panta-sqa | mistaken, wrong, erroneous, in error | P1 item 25 | [CORE] |
| riqsisqa | riqsi-sqa | known, recognized, identified | P2a Derived Form 11.1 | [CORE] |
| yachasqa | yacha-sqa | known, learned, that which is known | P2a Derived Form 12.4 | [CORE] |
| iñisqa | iñi-sqa | believed, trusted, that which is believed | P2a Derived Form 13.4 | [CORE] |

**Notes:** The past participle *-sqa* is productive across all roots in this domain. It encodes the resultant state of a completed process of change or causation. In the attested data, these forms function as predicate adjectives or nominal modifiers characterizing the state that obtains after the relevant change or causation event has occurred.

## 6. STABILITY AND RESISTANCE TO CHANGE

### 6.1 Absolute / incontrovertible truth (*cheqapuni*)

**Pattern:** cheqa + emphatic *-puni* (definitely, absolutely, without exception) → *cheqapuni*

**Example:** Cheqapuni
**Transliteration:** cheqa-pu-ni
**English translation:** absolutely true, certainly real, definitely correct

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 1.8) and P1b (item 19) as used "to assert truth or genuineness with strong emphasis and without qualification," with *-puni* adding "a sense of incontrovertibility." The emphatic suffix encodes resistance to doubt or qualification — a state of truth that is not subject to revision. Appears in emphatic assertions in everyday and formal speech.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.2 Absolute correctness (*allinpuni*)

**Pattern:** allin + emphatic *-puni* → *allinpuni*

**Example:** Allinpuni
**Transliteration:** allin-pu-ni
**English translation:** absolutely good, definitely correct, certainly right

**Usage notes:** Documented in P2a (Derived Form 16.4) as used "to assert goodness or correctness with strong emphasis," appearing in emphatic affirmations in everyday and formal speech. Parallel in structure to *cheqapuni*.

**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 6.3 Asymmetry: no emphatic form attested for *llulla*

**(no common expression identified)**

**Note:** P2a (Summary observation 2) and P5 (Oppositions 31–32) explicitly document that "the emphatic *-puni* and contrastive *-taq* are attested on *cheqa* but not on *llulla*." No form *llullapuni* is attested in the sources consulted. This asymmetry is reported in the data and is not resolved here.

## 7. SUMMARY OF PATTERNS OBSERVED

The following observations are drawn directly from the attested forms documented above and are not interpretive claims beyond what the data support.

**1. Causative productivity:** The causative suffixes *-chi* and *-cha* are attested on all major roots in the domain (*cheqa*, *llulla*, *sut'i*, *allin*, *pantay*, *iñiy*, *yachay*, *riqsiy*). This is the most productive dynamic pattern in the domain.

**2. Causative-reflexive combination:** The combination causative + reflexive *-ku* is attested on *cheqa* (*cheqachikuy*), *llulla* (*llullachikuy*), *allin* (*allinchakuy*), and *sut'i* (*sut'ichakuy*), yielding self-directed versions of the causative meaning across all four major roots.

**3. Inchoative asymmetry:** The inchoative *-ya* is attested on *allin* (*allinyay*) but not on *llulla*, *sut'i*, or *cheqa* (which uses *-ka* instead). No inchoative form of *llulla* is attested. This asymmetry is explicitly noted in the source data.

**4. Emphatic stability asymmetry:** The emphatic *-puni* encoding incontrovertible or stable truth is attested on *cheqa* and *allin* but not on *llulla*. This asymmetry is explicitly documented in P2a and P5.

**5. Resultant state productivity:** The past participle *-sqa* encoding resultant states is attested on all roots in the domain without exception, making it the most uniformly productive suffix for encoding the outcome of change or causation events.

**6. *Pantay* as inherently dynamic:** Unlike the adjectival roots *cheqa*, *llulla*, *sut'i*, and *allin*, the root *pantay* is itself a verb encoding entry into an error state. It does not require an additional inchoative suffix to express becoming-in-error; the verb itself encodes this transition.

**7. *Tukuy* as a change verb:** The verb *tukuy* (to become, to pretend to be) combines with both *cheqa* and *llulla* to encode transitions into real or false states (*cheqa tukuy*, *llulla tukuy*). This is the only change verb from outside the truth-evaluation roots that is attested in combination with those roots in the source data.
