# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns of co-occurrence, contrast, and contextual restriction observed in the P1 and P2 documentation. No external semantic framework has been imposed. Where usage patterns support multiple groupings for a single item, this is noted explicitly. Where no clear grouping is supported, items are listed in the ungrouped section with a brief note.

---

## Group 1: Expressions used to assert or deny that a statement is true

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where a speaker is evaluating whether a proposition, claim, or statement corresponds to reality. They are used in direct assertion, denial, or interrogation of factual accuracy. The core contrast in this group is between *egia da* and *gezurra da*, which function as the primary poles of propositional evaluation in everyday Basque speech. Items in this group appear in argumentative discourse, testimony, and everyday factual exchange.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egia da* — "it is true; that is true" (predicative assertion of propositional truth)
- *gezurra da* — "it is a lie; it is false" (predicative denial of propositional truth)
- *egia da ala gezurra?* — "is it true or false?" (binary interrogative framing)
- *egia da, baina...* — "it is true, but..." (concessive construction acknowledging truth while introducing qualification)
- *egia esan* — "to tell the truth" (verbal collocation asserting honest disclosure)
- *gezurra esan* — "to tell a lie" (verbal collocation asserting dishonest disclosure)
- *oker egon* — "to be wrong; to be mistaken" (predicative assertion of incorrectness)
- *zuzen egon* — "to be right; to be correct" (predicative assertion of correctness)
- *oker nago* — "I am wrong; I am mistaken" (first-person acknowledgment of error)
- *ziur nago* — "I am sure; I am certain" (first-person assertion of epistemic confidence)
- *ziur ez egon* — "to not be sure; to be uncertain" (negative epistemic state)
- *oker ez banago* — "if I am not mistaken; unless I am wrong" (epistemic hedge)

**Notes:** *Zuzen* and *oker* appear here in their propositional-correctness uses, but both terms have additional uses in other contexts (see Groups 3 and 5). *Ziur nago* and *ziur ez egon* shade into Group 2 (epistemic certainty expressions) and are listed in both.

---

## Group 2: Expressions used to indicate degree of epistemic certainty or uncertainty

**Definition:** These expressions are used to calibrate how confident a speaker is in a proposition, ranging from full certainty to acknowledged uncertainty. They appear as hedges, intensifiers, or explicit markers of epistemic stance in both spoken and written Basque. A notable feature of this group is that *ziurrenik*, derived from the root meaning "certain," functions in practice as a probability hedge ("probably," "most likely") rather than as an assertion of full certainty.

**Representative expressions:**
- *ziur egon* — "to be sure; to be certain" (predicative certainty)
- *ziur nago* — "I am sure; I am certain" (first-person certainty)
- *ziur asko* — "most certainly; very likely; almost certainly" (intensified certainty or high probability)
- *ziurrenik* — "certainly; most likely; probably" (epistemic adverb, frequently used as probability hedge)
- *ziur-ziur* — "absolutely certain; for sure; definitely" (reduplicated emphatic certainty)
- *ziur samar egon* — "to be fairly sure; to be reasonably certain" (hedged certainty)
- *ziur al zaude?* — "are you sure?" (interrogative of certainty)
- *ziur ez dakit* — "I don't know for sure; I'm not certain" (acknowledged uncertainty)
- *ziurtasun osoz* — "with complete certainty; with full assurance" (adverbial full certainty)
- *ziurtasun eza* — "uncertainty; lack of certainty" (nominal absence of certainty)
- *ziurtasun falta* — "lack of certainty; deficiency of assurance" (nominal absence of certainty)
- *antza du* — "it seems; it appears; it looks like" (hedged epistemic assertion)
- *antza denez* — "apparently; it seems; as it appears" (parenthetical epistemic hedge)

**Notes:** *Antza du* and *antza denez* are derived from the *antze* (resemblance) base and appear here because their primary function in usage is as epistemic hedges rather than as expressions of resemblance. They are also relevant to Group 7 (appearance vs. reality). *Ziurrenik* is notable for straddling certainty and probability; its placement here reflects its actual usage range as documented in P1b and P2a.

## Group 3: Expressions used in administrative and legal certification contexts

**Definition:** These expressions appear in official, administrative, and legal contexts where the formal confirmation, authentication, or validation of documents, facts, qualifications, or identities is at issue. They are associated with institutional procedures rather than with personal epistemic states or interpersonal moral evaluation. The most distinctive item in this group is *ziurtagiri* (certificate), which has become the standard term for official certification documents across multiple domains.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egiaztatu* — "to verify; to confirm; to authenticate; to certify" (transitive verb of verification)
- *egiaztapen* — "verification; confirmation; attestation" (result nominal of verification)
- *egiaztatze* — "verification; the process of confirming" (process nominal of verification)
- *egiaztagarri* — "verifiable; capable of verification" (adjectival form)
- *egiaztaezin* — "unverifiable; impossible to confirm" (negative potential adjectival form)
- *ziurtatu* — "to certify; to assure; to make sure; to confirm" (transitive verb of certification)
- *ziurtapen* — "certification; assurance; confirmation" (result nominal of certification)
- *ziurtatze* — "certifying; the process of certification" (process nominal)
- *ziurtagiri* — "certificate; certification document" (document noun)
- *ziurtagiri digitala* — "digital certificate" (technical domain extension)
- *ziurtagiri medikua* — "medical certificate" (administrative domain)
- *ziurtagiri akademikoa* — "academic certificate" (educational domain)
- *ziurtatu gabeko albistea* — "unverified news; unconfirmed report" (journalistic use)
- *ziurtatu gabeko* — "uncertified; unconfirmed; unverified" (participial construction)
- *ofizialki ziurtatu* — "to officially certify; to formally confirm" (adverbially specified certification)
- *baliozko agiria* — "a valid document; a legitimate document" (nominal collocation)
- *baliozkotu* — "to validate; to ratify; to authenticate" (verb of validation)
- *baliogabe* — "invalid; null; void; worthless" (negative validity adjective)
- *baliogabetu* — "to invalidate; to annul; to void; to nullify" (verb of invalidation)
- *baliozkotzat jo* — "to consider as valid; to regard as legitimate" (translative construction)
- *egiazko kopiak ziurtatu* — "to certify true copies; to authenticate copies" (combined certification collocation)
- *egiazkotzat jo* — "to consider as true; to take as genuine" (translative construction)
- *egiazkotzat onartu* — "to accept as true; to acknowledge as genuine" (formal acceptance construction)
- *behar bezala egiaztatu* — "to verify properly; to verify as required" (procedural specification)
- *ziurtasun juridikoa* — "legal certainty; juridical certainty" (legal principle)
- *ziurtasun juridikoa bermatu* — "to guarantee legal certainty" (legal obligation construction)
- *faltsifikatu* — "to falsify; to forge; to counterfeit" (verb of document falsification)
- *agiriak faltsifikatu* — "to falsify documents; to forge papers" (most common collocational context)
- *faltsifikatze* / *faltsifikazio* — "falsification; forgery" (nominals of falsification)
- *ziurtatze prozesua* — "certification process" (procedural nominal)

**Notes:** *Egiaztatu* and *ziurtatu* are documented in P2a as having overlapping but distinct emphases: *egiaztatu* focuses on confirming factual content, while *ziurtatu* emphasizes assurance and formal confirmation. In practice, both appear in administrative certification contexts and are grouped together here. *Faltsifikatu* and its nominals appear here as the legal counterpart (falsification of documents) rather than in the moral-evaluation groups.

## Group 4: Expressions used in moral evaluation of persons

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate persons as honest, dishonest, trustworthy, loyal, or their opposites, in interpersonal and moral contexts. They focus on character and conduct rather than on the truth-value of specific propositions or the validity of documents. Several items in this group are used particularly in evaluating children or in educational contexts, and several carry a pejorative connotation when applied to habitual patterns of behavior.

**Representative expressions:**
- *zintzo* — "honest; upright; faithful; sincere; well-behaved" (character adjective)
- *zintzotasun* — "honesty; uprightness; faithfulness; sincerity" (abstract noun of character)
- *zintzoki* — "honestly; uprightly; faithfully; sincerely" (adverb of honest conduct)
- *zintzotu* — "to become honest; to reform; to straighten out" (verb of moral change)
- *leial* — "loyal; faithful; true" (loyalty adjective)
- *lealtasun* — "loyalty; faithfulness; fidelity" (abstract noun of loyalty)
- *gezurti* — "liar; lying; untruthful" (characterizing adjective/noun for habitual liar)
- *gezurkeria* — "lying; deceitfulness; dishonesty as a practice" (pejorative practice noun)
- *fidagarri* — "reliable; trustworthy; dependable; credible" (trustworthiness adjective)
- *fidagarritasun* — "reliability; trustworthiness; dependability" (abstract noun)
- *fidaezin* — "untrustworthy; unreliable" (negative potential form)
- *fidagaitz* — "untrustworthy; hard to trust; unreliable" (difficulty-of-trust form)
- *zintzo jokatu* — "to act honestly; to behave uprightly; to play fair" (conduct collocation)
- *zintzo ibili* — "to behave honestly; to conduct oneself uprightly" (conduct collocation)
- *faltsu jokatu* — "to act falsely; to behave insincerely; to play false" (negative conduct collocation)
- *faltsukeria* — "falseness; insincerity; duplicity as a practice" (pejorative practice noun)
- *okerkeria* — "wrongdoing; misconduct; error as a practice or pattern" (pejorative practice noun)
- *itxurakeria* — "pretense; affectation; keeping up appearances; hypocrisy" (pejorative practice noun)

**Notes:** The *-keria* suffix appears across multiple items in this group (*gezurkeria*, *faltsukeria*, *okerkeria*, *itxurakeria*), consistently marking habitual or reprehensible practices. This suffix pattern is a notable feature of the moral-evaluation domain in Basque. *Faltsu* and *faltsu jokatu* also appear in Group 5 (false appearance), but their use in evaluating persons' sincerity places them here as well. *Fidagarri* overlaps with Group 6 (source credibility) and is listed in both.

## Group 5: Expressions used to characterize something as false, fake, or spurious

**Definition:** These expressions are used to evaluate objects, documents, statements, or representations as not being what they claim to be — as counterfeit, fabricated, or spuriously presented. They appear in legal, journalistic, and everyday contexts where the distinction between genuine and fake is at issue. This group is distinct from Group 4 (moral evaluation of persons) in that the focus is on the thing or claim rather than on the person making it, though there is overlap.

**Representative expressions:**
- *faltsu* — "false; fake; counterfeit; insincere" (general falseness adjective)
- *faltsutasun* — "falseness; falsity; inauthenticity" (abstract noun)
- *faltsuki* — "falsely; insincerely; deceptively" (adverb)
- *gezurrezko* — "false; lying; counterfeit; fake" (relational adjective of falsehood)
- *gezurrezko agiriak erabili* — "to use false documents; to use forged papers" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko lekukotasuna eman* — "to give false testimony; to provide false witness" (legal collocation)
- *gezurrezko itxura eman* — "to give a false appearance; to create a false impression" (deception collocation)
- *sasi-* — "pseudo-; false; spurious; sham" (productive prefix)
- *sasi-jakintsu* — "pseudo-scholar; charlatan; fake expert" (person compound)
- *sasi-zientzia* — "pseudoscience" (discipline compound)
- *sasi-demokrazia* — "pseudo-democracy; sham democracy" (institution compound)
- *sasi-mediku* — "fake doctor; quack" (person compound)
- *sasi-lege* — "pseudo-law; spurious legislation" (legal compound)
- *faltsutzat jo* — "to consider as false; to regard as fake" (translative construction)
- *faltsu agertu* — "to appear false; to turn out to be fake" (revelation construction)
- *gezurtatu* — "to give the lie to; to contradict; to prove false" (verb of refutation)
- *egiazko izena* — "real name; true name" (implicitly contrasting with false name)
- *egiazko sinadura* — "genuine signature; authentic signature" (implicitly contrasting with forged)

**Notes:** *Sasi-* is distinctive in this group for being a prefix rather than a free form, and for its productivity across nominal categories. *Egiazko izena* and *egiazko sinadura* are included here because their primary function in usage is to distinguish genuine from false identity markers, even though they are formally positive (*egiazko*) rather than negative. *Faltsifikatu* and its nominals appear in Group 3 (administrative/legal certification) rather than here, because their usage is more specifically tied to document forgery procedures.

## Group 6: Expressions used to evaluate the credibility of sources and testimony

**Definition:** These expressions appear in journalistic, legal, and analytical contexts where the question is whether a source, witness, or account deserves to be believed or relied upon. They are used to assess the reliability of information and its origins rather than to evaluate the truth of a specific proposition directly. The contrast between *fidagarri* (trustworthy source) and *sinesgarri* (believable content) is documented in P1 as a distinction in usage, though the two overlap.

**Representative expressions:**
- *fidagarri* — "reliable; trustworthy; dependable; credible" (source trustworthiness adjective)
- *fidagarritasun* — "reliability; trustworthiness; dependability" (abstract noun)
- *fidaezin* — "untrustworthy; unreliable" (negative form)
- *fidagaitz* — "untrustworthy; hard to trust" (difficulty form)
- *iturri fidagarria* — "a reliable source; a trustworthy source" (standard journalistic collocation)
- *iturri fidagarrietatik jaso* — "to receive from reliable sources" (sourcing collocation)
- *fidagarria ez den informazioa* — "information that is not reliable; unreliable information" (relative clause evaluation)
- *sinesgarri* — "believable; credible; plausible; convincing" (content believability adjective)
- *sinesgarritasun* — "credibility; believability; plausibility" (abstract noun)
- *sinesgaitz* — "unbelievable; hard to believe; implausible" (difficulty form)
- *lekuko sinesgarria* — "a credible witness; a believable witness" (legal collocation)
- *sinesgarritasuna galdu* — "to lose credibility" (credibility-loss collocation)
- *sinesgarritasuna irabazi* — "to gain credibility; to earn believability" (credibility-gain collocation)
- *ez da batere sinesgarria* — "it is not at all credible; it is completely unbelievable" (emphatic negative)
- *egiazko lekukotasuna eman* — "to give true testimony; to provide genuine witness" (legal collocation)
- *egiaztatu ezin den informazioa* — "information that cannot be verified; unverifiable information" (journalistic evaluation)
- *egiaztatu gabe utzi* — "to leave unverified; to leave without verification" (journalistic failure collocation)

**Notes:** *Fidagarri* and *sinesgarri* are documented in P1 as having a usage distinction: *fidagarri* evaluates the source, *sinesgarri* evaluates the content or claim. Both appear in this group because in practice they co-occur in the same evaluative contexts (legal testimony, journalistic sourcing). *Egiazko lekukotasuna eman* appears here because its primary context is legal testimony evaluation, even though *egiazko* is formally a truth-adjective.

## Group 7: Expressions used to contrast appearance with underlying reality

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where the outward appearance of something is distinguished from what is actually the case. They appear in moral, analytical, literary, and everyday contexts where the speaker is signaling that surface impressions may be misleading or that something presents itself as other than it is. The base *itxura* (appearance, semblance) is central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *itxura* — "appearance; semblance; look; form; pretense" (base noun)
- *itxurazko* — "apparent; seeming; superficial; ostensible" (relational adjective)
- *itxurazko adostasuna* — "apparent agreement; superficial consensus" (analytical collocation)
- *itxurazko lasaitasuna* — "apparent calm; seeming tranquility" (literary collocation)
- *itxura eman* — "to give the appearance of; to make it seem; to create the impression of" (deception/impression verb)
- *itxura ona eman* — "to make a good impression; to give a good appearance" (social impression verb)
- *itxurak gorde* — "to keep up appearances; to maintain the facade" (social pretense collocation)
- *itxurak salbu* — "appearances aside; setting aside appearances" (analytical framing)
- *itxura batean* — "at first glance; on the surface; apparently" (adverbial framing)
- *itxura guztien arabera* — "by all appearances; to all appearances" (evidential framing)
- *itxurak engainatzen du* — "appearances deceive; looks are deceiving" (proverbial)
- *itxuratu* — "to disguise; to simulate; to make appear; to feign" (verb of appearance-creation)
- *itxuragabe* — "shapeless; shameless; brazen" (absence-of-appearance form)
- *itxurakeria* — "pretense; affectation; keeping up appearances; hypocrisy" (pejorative practice noun)
- *antza du* — "it seems; it appears; it looks like" (hedged appearance assertion)
- *antza denez* — "apparently; it seems; as it appears" (parenthetical hedge)
- *egiazko arazoa* — "the real problem; the genuine issue" (implicitly contrasting with apparent problem)
- *benetako arrazoia ezkutatu* — "to hide the real reason; to conceal the genuine motive" (concealment of reality)
- *egia gordea* — "the hidden truth; the concealed truth" (concealed reality noun)
- *egia argitara atera* — "to bring the truth to light; to reveal the truth" (revelation of hidden reality)

**Notes:** *Antza du* and *antza denez* appear in both Group 2 (epistemic certainty) and here, because their function as epistemic hedges is related to but distinct from their function as appearance-markers. *Egiazko arazoa* and *benetako arrazoia ezkutatu* appear here because their usage specifically involves the contrast between surface and underlying reality, even though *egiazko* and *benetako* are formally positive authenticity adjectives. *Itxurakeria* also appears in Group 4 (moral evaluation of persons).

## Group 8: Expressions used to assert genuineness or authenticity of persons, relationships, or feelings

**Definition:** These expressions are used to characterize persons, relationships, feelings, or cultural expressions as sincere, non-artificial, and genuinely what they present themselves as being. They are distinct from Group 5 (false/fake characterizations) in being the positive pole, and distinct from Group 3 (administrative certification) in that they operate in interpersonal, affective, and cultural domains rather than in institutional ones. The *benetako* / *benetan* cluster is central to this group.

**Representative expressions:**
- *benetako* — "genuine; real; authentic; true" (genuineness adjective)
- *benetakotasun* — "authenticity; genuineness; realness" (abstract noun)
- *benetan* — "truly; really; genuinely; in earnest; seriously" (sincerity adverb)
- *benetan esan* — "to say in earnest; to mean it; to say sincerely" (sincerity assertion)
- *benetan uste izan* — "to truly believe; to genuinely think" (sincere belief assertion)
- *benetan ala txantxetan?* — "seriously or jokingly? in earnest or in jest?" (sincerity interrogative)
- *benetan harritu* — "to be truly surprised; to be genuinely astonished" (authentic emotion assertion)
- *benetan nahi izan* — "to truly want; to genuinely desire" (sincere desire assertion)
- *benetako laguna* — "a real friend; a genuine friend" (interpersonal genuineness)
- *benetako arrazoia* — "the real reason; the genuine motive" (underlying reality)
- *benetakotzat hartu* — "to take as genuine; to regard as real" (acceptance as genuine)
- *benetakotasuna bilatu* — "to seek authenticity" (cultural/philosophical pursuit)
- *benetakotasun falta* — "lack of authenticity" (absence of genuineness)
- *egiazko maitasuna* — "true love; genuine love" (affective genuineness)
- *egiazko adiskidea* — "true friend; genuine friend" (interpersonal genuineness)
- *egiazko balioa* — "true value; real value; genuine worth" (intrinsic value)

**Notes:** *Egiazko maitasuna*, *egiazko adiskidea*, and *egiazko balioa* appear here rather than in Group 1 (propositional truth) because their usage, as noted in P1b, involves evaluation of sincerity and intrinsic quality rather than factual correspondence. The *benetan* cluster is the most distinctively interpersonal and affective subgroup within this group. *Benetakotasun* is documented in P1 as more common in literary, philosophical, and cultural discourse than in legal contexts, which distinguishes it from *egiazkotasun*.

## Group 9: Expressions used in the domain of correction and rectification

**Definition:** These expressions are used in contexts where an error, wrong, or deviation from a standard is identified and addressed. They appear in educational, editorial, legal, and moral contexts. The root *zuzen* generates the most productive paradigm in this group, with derivations covering the act of correcting, the agent who corrects, the result of correction, and the process of correction.

**Representative expressions:**
- *zuzendu* — "to correct; to rectify; to direct; to edit; to address" (core correction verb)
- *zuzenketa* — "correction; amendment; rectification" (result/process nominal)
- *zuzentzaile* — "corrector; editor; proofreader; referee" (agent nominal)
- *zuzentzapen* — "correction; rectification; amendment" (formal variant nominal)
- *zuzentze lanak* — "correction work; editing work; proofreading tasks" (activity collocation)
- *zuzenketa proposamena* — "amendment proposal; proposed correction" (legislative collocation)
- *oker zuzendu* — "to correct a mistake; to right a wrong" (combined correction collocation)
- *okertu* — "to go wrong; to make a mistake; to distort; to bend" (verb of going wrong)
- *okertze* — "going wrong; distortion; the process of erring" (process nominal)
- *oker ulertu* — "to misunderstand; to understand incorrectly" (misunderstanding collocation)
- *oker esan* — "to say incorrectly; to misspeak" (misspeaking collocation)
- *oker joan* — "to go wrong; to go astray; to turn out badly" (going-wrong collocation)
- *oker dagoela onartu* — "to admit that one is wrong; to acknowledge one's error" (error acknowledgment)
- *zuzen idatzi* — "to write correctly; to spell correctly" (normative writing collocation)
- *zuzen hitz egin* — "to speak correctly; to speak properly" (normative speech collocation)
- *zuzen ulertu* — "to understand correctly; to interpret rightly" (correct interpretation collocation)
- *zuzen konpondu* — "to fix correctly; to resolve properly" (correct resolution collocation)
- *zuzentasun linguistikoa* — "linguistic correctness; grammatical correctness" (normative domain)

**Notes:** This group is dominated by the *zuzen* / *oker* antonym pair and their derivations. *Zuzendu* is notably polysemous, covering correction of texts, rectification of wrongs, and direction of persons or organizations; the editorial and organizational senses are included here only where the correction sense is primary. The organizational senses of *zuzendu* (yielding *zuzendari*, *zuzendaritza*) are not included in this group as they do not involve correction or rectification.

## Group 10: Expressions used in discourse about truth-telling as an obligation or practice

**Definition:** These expressions appear in contexts where truth-telling is framed as a moral obligation, a practice to be upheld or violated, or a value to be pursued. They are used in moral, rhetorical, and legal discourse to characterize the act of telling the truth as something owed, required, or worthy of commitment. Several items in this group function as discourse markers that frame what follows as candid or honest disclosure.

**Representative expressions:**
- *egia esan behar* — "to tell the truth (as obligation); truth be told" (obligative discourse marker)
- *egia esateko* — "to tell the truth; frankly; to be honest" (purposive discourse marker)
- *zuzen esanda* — "to be fair; frankly speaking; to put it correctly" (participial discourse marker)
- *egiari eutsi* — "to hold to the truth; to maintain the truth" (adherence to truth collocation)
- *egiaren bila ibili* — "to go about seeking the truth; to be in search of the truth" (pursuit of truth collocation)
- *egiaren aldeko* — "in favor of truth; on the side of truth" (alignment with truth)
- *egiari zor* — "owed to truth; in debt to truth" (obligation-to-truth expression)
- *egia jakiteko eskubidea* — "the right to know the truth" (rights-based framing)
- *egia aitortu* — "to admit the truth; to confess the truth; to acknowledge the truth" (confession collocation)
- *egia ukatu* — "to deny the truth; to refuse to acknowledge the truth" (denial collocation)
- *egia ezkutatu* — "to hide the truth; to conceal the truth" (concealment collocation)
- *egia argitara atera* — "to bring the truth to light; to reveal the truth" (revelation collocation)
- *egia beti ateratzen da azalera* — "the truth always comes to the surface; truth will out" (proverbial)
- *egia osoa eta ezer ez besterik* — "the whole truth and nothing but the truth" (legal oath formula)
- *egiari begira* — "looking at the truth; facing the truth" (rhetorical framing)
- *zintzoki* — "honestly; uprightly; faithfully; sincerely" (adverb of honest conduct)
- *gardenki* — "transparently; openly; candidly" (adverb of transparent conduct)
- *gardentasun* — "transparency; openness; candor" (institutional transparency noun)
- *gardentasuna bermatu* — implied by *zuzentasun soziala bermatu* pattern — (guarantee of transparency)

**Notes:** *Egia argitara atera* appears in both this group and Group 7 (appearance vs. reality), because it involves both the revelation of hidden truth and the moral act of disclosure. *Gardentasun* and *gardenki* are included here because their primary usage in administrative and political discourse frames transparency as an obligation or value, not merely as a descriptive property.

## Ambiguous or overlapping items

The following items have been assigned to groups above but present notable overlap or ambiguity worth flagging explicitly:

- **egiazko** — appears in Group 1 (propositional truth), Group 5 (false/fake contrast), Group 6 (testimony credibility), and Group 8 (genuineness of persons/feelings), because the adjective is used across all these domains with somewhat different emphases depending on the noun it modifies.

- **zuzen** — appears in Group 1 (propositional correctness), Group 9 (correction/rectification), and in P1b's peripheral extensions (spatial directness, broadcasting, political structure). The organizational and directional senses (*zuzendari*, *zuzeneko emankizuna*) are not assigned to any group in this inventory because they do not clearly participate in the truth-evaluation domain as documented.

- **antza du / antza denez** — assigned to both Group 2 (epistemic certainty/uncertainty) and Group 7 (appearance vs. reality). The two functions are related but distinct in usage: as an epistemic hedge, *antza du* marks speaker uncertainty; as an appearance marker, it signals that something presents a certain face.

- **itxurakeria** — assigned to both Group 4 (moral evaluation of persons) and Group 7 (appearance vs. reality), because it involves both the moral failing of pretense and the appearance/reality contrast.

- **fidagarri** — assigned to both Group 4 (moral evaluation of persons) and Group 6 (source credibility), because it is used to evaluate both persons' trustworthiness and the reliability of information sources.

- **egia gordea / egia argitara atera** — assigned to both Group 7 (appearance vs. reality) and Group 10 (truth-telling as obligation/practice), because they involve both the concealment/revelation dynamic and the moral framing of truth disclosure.

## Items not assigned to any group

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups identified above, or their usage patterns as documented do not provide sufficient basis for grouping:

- **egia absolutua** / **egia erlatiboa** — These appear in philosophical discourse about the nature of truth (absolute vs. relative), but no broader usage pattern connecting them to other items in the inventory has been documented. They are collocations of *egia* with loanword adjectives and appear to be restricted to academic and theological registers without generating further productive patterns.

- **egia biluzia** — "the naked truth; the bare truth." Documented as a rhetorical/literary collocation, but its usage pattern does not clearly connect it to any of the groups above beyond the general *egia* domain.

- **egiazkotasun** / **benetakotasun** — Both are documented as abstract nominals used in philosophical and formal discourse, but their frequency is low and their usage patterns are not sufficiently distinct from their base adjectives (*egiazko*, *benetako*) to warrant separate grouping. They are terminal derived forms with limited collocational productivity.

- **antzekotasun** — "similarity; resemblance; likeness." Documented as an abstract noun, but its usage in the truth-evaluation domain is peripheral. It appears in aesthetic and legal contexts involving resemblance assessment, but no clear grouping with other items is supported by the documented patterns.

- **berdintsu** / **berdintsuki** — These express approximate equality or similarity. Their connection to the truth-evaluation domain is indirect (comparison and resemblance), and their usage patterns do not clearly align with any of the groups identified. They are noted here as ungrouped.

- **zuzendaritza** / **zuzendari** / **zuzendaritza kontseilua** — These organizational and administrative terms derive from *zuzen* but operate in a domain (institutional management and direction) that is not connected to truth-evaluation in the documented usage patterns. They are not assigned to any group.

- **zuzeneko emankizuna** / **zuzeneko demokrazia** / **zuzeneko elkarrizketa** — These uses of *zuzeneko* (direct, live) in broadcasting and political contexts are documented in P1b as peripheral extensions of the *zuzen* root. Their usage does not connect them to any of the truth-evaluation groups identified.

- **leialki** — Documented as an adverb of loyal conduct, but attested primarily in written Basque with limited collocational productivity. Its usage pattern does not add to what is already covered by *leial* and *lealtasun* in Group 4.

- **sinesgaizki** — Documented as an adverb ("unbelievably; implausibly") but marked as marginal in P2a. Insufficient usage data to assign to a group independently of *sinesgaitz*.

- **zuzentasun prozedurala** — "procedural justice; procedural correctness." Documented as a legal/administrative term but marked as marginal. Its usage pattern is too restricted to support grouping.
