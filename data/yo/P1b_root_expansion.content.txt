# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Yoruba (yo)

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

### Root 1: **tọ́**
**Form:** tọ́  
**Participates in:** 8 P1 items (òtítọ́, tọ́, àìtọ́, tọ̀nà, ṣòótọ́, òtítọ́-inú, òtítọ́ ni)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Straightness, correctness, alignment with proper standards or reality. The fundamental root for truth-as-correspondence and accuracy.

### Root 2: **irọ́**
**Form:** irọ́  
**Participates in:** 2 P1 items (irọ́, purọ́)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Falsehood, deception, misrepresentation. The primary root for lies and false statements.

### Root 3: **ṣe**
**Form:** ṣe  
**Participates in:** 6 P1 items (ṣe déédé, àṣìṣe, ṣe bẹ́ẹ̀, ṣe pàtàkì, àìṣedéédé, ṣìṣe)  
**Core meaning contribution:** Doing, making, performing, being-the-case. A highly productive verb root that appears in evaluative constructions assessing whether something is done correctly or is actually the case.

## Step 2 — Expand Each Root

### ROOT 1: **tọ́** (to be straight, correct, true)

#### 1. tọ́ sí
**Form:** tọ́ sí  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight/correct) + sí (to/toward)  
**Translation:** to be directed toward correctly, to be right about  
**Context:** Used when evaluating whether someone's statement or action is correctly directed toward or applies to something. Common in everyday discourse when assessing the accuracy of claims about specific matters.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. tọ́jú
**Form:** tọ́jú  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ojú (eye/face)  
**Translation:** to care for, to tend to, to look after properly  
**Context:** Used to describe proper care or attention given to persons, animals, or things. While not directly about truth, it involves doing what is right/proper in caregiving contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. tọ́ ọwọ́
**Form:** tọ́ ọwọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ọwọ́ (hand)  
**Translation:** to be skillful, to be dexterous, to do correctly with one's hands  
**Context:** Used to evaluate manual skill or the proper execution of tasks requiring hand coordination. Common in craft and practical work contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. tọ́ka sí
**Form:** tọ́ka sí  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ọwọ́ (hand, contracted to -ka) + sí (to)  
**Translation:** to point to, to indicate, to refer to  
**Context:** Used when directing attention to something or when statements refer to specific referents. Common in explanatory and instructional discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. kò tọ́
**Form:** kò tọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** kò (negative) + tọ́ (be straight/correct)  
**Translation:** it is not correct, it is wrong, it is not true  
**Context:** Standard negation of tọ́, used to deny the truth or correctness of statements or the propriety of actions. Very common in everyday disagreement and correction.  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. tọ́ lọ
**Form:** tọ́ lọ  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + lọ (go)  
**Translation:** to go straight, to proceed correctly  
**Context:** Used for physical movement in a straight direction or for proceeding correctly in a course of action. Common in giving directions and procedural instructions.  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. tọ́ wá
**Form:** tọ́ wá  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + wá (come)  
**Translation:** to come straight, to come directly  
**Context:** Used when someone comes directly without deviation or when approaching something in the proper manner. Common in spatial and procedural contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. ìtọ́
**Form:** ìtọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ì- (nominalizer) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** straightness, correctness, the act of being straight  
**Context:** Nominal form referring to the quality or state of being straight or correct. Less common than òtítọ́ but used in more abstract or philosophical discussions.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 9. tọ́tọ́
**Form:** tọ́tọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + tọ́ (reduplicated)  
**Translation:** very straight, completely correct, absolutely true  
**Context:** Reduplication for emphasis, indicating something is thoroughly or completely straight/correct. Used when emphasizing the degree of accuracy or straightness.  
**[CORE]**

#### 10. tọ́ ẹnu
**Form:** tọ́ ẹnu  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ẹnu (mouth)  
**Translation:** to speak correctly, to say the right thing  
**Context:** Used to evaluate whether someone has spoken appropriately or accurately. Common in contexts assessing the propriety or truth of speech.  
**[CORE]**

#### 11. tọ́ ọkàn
**Form:** tọ́ ọkàn  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ọkàn (heart)  
**Translation:** to be sincere, to have a straight heart, to be honest in intention  
**Context:** Used to evaluate the sincerity or honesty of someone's inner disposition. Common in moral evaluation of character and motivation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 12. ọ̀nà tọ́
**Form:** ọ̀nà tọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ọ̀nà (way/path) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** straight path, correct way, right method  
**Context:** Used to describe the proper method or approach to something. Common in moral, procedural, and instructional contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 13. ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́
**Form:** ọ̀rọ̀ tọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ọ̀rọ̀ (word/matter) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** true statement, correct matter, right word  
**Context:** Used to characterize statements as accurate or matters as properly handled. Very common in discourse evaluation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 14. ètò tọ́
**Form:** ètò tọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ètò (system/arrangement) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** correct system, proper arrangement, right order  
**Context:** Used to evaluate whether systems, procedures, or arrangements are properly organized. Common in administrative and organizational contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 15. tọ́ sílẹ̀
**Form:** tọ́ sílẹ̀  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + sí (to) + ilẹ̀ (ground)  
**Translation:** to be grounded in truth, to be well-founded  
**Context:** Used when evaluating whether claims or beliefs have solid foundation. Common in epistemic and argumentative contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 16. àtọ́
**Form:** àtọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** à- (nominalizer) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** correction, the act of making straight/correct  
**Context:** Refers to the process of correcting or making something right. Used in educational and editorial contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 17. atọ́
**Form:** atọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** a- (agent prefix) + tọ́ (be straight)  
**Translation:** one who corrects, corrector  
**Context:** Refers to a person who corrects or makes things right. Less common as a standalone term.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 18. tọ́ bọ̀
**Form:** tọ́ bọ̀  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + bọ̀ (return)  
**Translation:** to return straight, to come back correctly  
**Context:** Used when someone returns directly or when something reverts to its proper state. Common in spatial and restorative contexts.  
**[CORE]**

#### 19. tọ́ ìwà
**Form:** tọ́ ìwà  
**Literal breakdown:** tọ́ (be straight) + ìwà (character)  
**Translation:** to have upright character, to behave correctly  
**Context:** Used to evaluate moral character and proper conduct. Common in character assessment and moral discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 20. ìtọ́sọ́nà
**Form:** ìtọ́sọ́nà  
**Literal breakdown:** ì- (nominalizer) + tọ́ (be straight) + sí (to) + ọ̀nà (way)  
**Translation:** guidance, direction, showing the right way  
**Context:** Refers to the act of providing proper guidance or direction. Common in instructional and mentoring contexts.  
**[CORE]**

### ROOT 2: **irọ́** (lie, falsehood)

#### 1. pa irọ́
**Form:** pa irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** pa (kill/do) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** to tell a lie, to lie (same as purọ́)  
**Context:** Alternative form of purọ́, making the composition more transparent. Used interchangeably with purọ́ in everyday speech.  
**[CORE]**

#### 2. irọ́ ẹ̀tàn
**Form:** irọ́ ẹ̀tàn  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + ẹ̀tàn (deception)  
**Translation:** deceitful lie, fraudulent falsehood  
**Context:** Emphasizes the manipulative or fraudulent nature of a lie. Used when the falsehood is part of a deliberate scheme to deceive.  
**[CORE]**

#### 3. irọ́ púpọ̀
**Form:** irọ́ púpọ̀  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + púpọ̀ (many/much)  
**Translation:** many lies, much falsehood  
**Context:** Used to characterize someone who tells many lies or a situation involving extensive deception. Common in moral condemnation.  
**[CORE]**

#### 4. irọ́ kékeré
**Form:** irọ́ kékeré  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + kékeré (small)  
**Translation:** small lie, white lie, minor falsehood  
**Context:** Used to characterize relatively harmless or minor lies, though still recognized as falsehoods. Common in everyday moral reasoning about degrees of dishonesty.  
**[CORE]**

#### 5. irọ́ ńlá
**Form:** irọ́ ńlá  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + ńlá (big)  
**Translation:** big lie, serious falsehood  
**Context:** Used to characterize major or consequential lies. Common when emphasizing the gravity of deception.  
**[CORE]**

#### 6. ọmọ irọ́
**Form:** ọmọ irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ọmọ (child/offspring) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** liar (literally "child of lies"), habitual liar  
**Context:** Derogatory term for someone who habitually tells lies. Common in moral condemnation and character assessment.  
**[CORE]**

#### 7. irọ́ tí ó dùn
**Form:** irọ́ tí ó dùn  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + tí (that) + ó (it) + dùn (be sweet)  
**Translation:** pleasant lie, attractive falsehood  
**Context:** Used to describe lies that are appealing or tempting to believe. Common in discussions of flattery or self-deception.  
**[CORE]**

#### 8. irọ́ àti òtítọ́
**Form:** irọ́ àti òtítọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + àti (and) + òtítọ́ (truth)  
**Translation:** lies and truth, falsehood and truth  
**Context:** Fixed expression contrasting the two opposites. Common in moral and epistemic discourse about distinguishing truth from falsehood.  
**[CORE]**

#### 9. kò sí irọ́
**Form:** kò sí irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** kò (not) + sí (exist) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** there is no lie, it's not a lie, truly  
**Context:** Used to affirm the truth of a statement by denying that it contains falsehood. Common in emphatic assertions.  
**[CORE]**

#### 10. irọ́ ẹnu
**Form:** irọ́ ẹnu  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + ẹnu (mouth)  
**Translation:** verbal lie, spoken falsehood  
**Context:** Emphasizes that the lie is spoken rather than enacted in other ways. Common in distinguishing types of deception.  
**[CORE]**

#### 11. irọ́ ìṣe
**Form:** irọ́ ìṣe  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + ìṣe (action/deed)  
**Translation:** lie in action, deceptive deed  
**Context:** Refers to deception through actions rather than words. Used when someone's behavior is misleading.  
**[CORE]**

#### 12. ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́
**Form:** ẹni tí ó ń pa irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** ẹni (person) + tí (who) + ó (he/she) + ń (progressive) + pa (do) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** one who tells lies, liar  
**Context:** Descriptive phrase for a liar. Common in character assessment and moral discourse.  
**[CORE]**

#### 13. irọ́ inú
**Form:** irọ́ inú  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + inú (inside/heart)  
**Translation:** inner falsehood, insincerity  
**Context:** Refers to internal dishonesty or lack of sincerity, even when outward speech may seem truthful. Used in evaluating authenticity of character.  
**[CORE]**

#### 14. irọ́ ayé
**Form:** irọ́ ayé  
**Literal breakdown:** irọ́ (lie) + ayé (world)  
**Translation:** worldly lies, deceptions of the world  
**Context:** Used in religious or philosophical contexts to describe the illusory or deceptive nature of worldly appearances or claims.  
**[MARGINAL]**

#### 15. jẹ́ irọ́
**Form:** jẹ́ irọ́  
**Literal breakdown:** jẹ́ (be/affirm) + irọ́ (lie)  
**Translation:** to be a lie, to be false  
**Context:** Used to characterize something as false or to deny the truth of a claim. Common in everyday discourse.  
**[CORE]**

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

### Extensions of **tọ́**:

- **tọ́jú** (to care for): The root's contribution shifts from "being straight/correct" to "doing what is proper/right" in caregiving, extending into the domain of moral duty and proper conduct rather than truth-correspondence.

- **tọ́ ọwọ́** (to be skillful): The root extends from correctness-of-statement to correctness-of-execution, evaluating manual skill and practical competence rather than truth.

- **tọ́ka sí** (to point to): While involving directional straightness, this extends into reference and indication, which relates to truth only indirectly through the relationship between signs and referents.

- **tọ́ lọ / tọ́ wá** (to go/come straight): These primarily involve spatial straightness rather than truth, though the metaphorical connection to "direct" or "proper" approach remains.

### Extensions of **irọ́**:

- **irọ́ ayé** (worldly lies): This extends into philosophical/religious territory, characterizing the world itself as illusory or deceptive, moving beyond individual false statements to metaphysical claims about appearance versus reality.

- **irọ́ tí ó dùn** (pleasant lie): While still about falsehood, this introduces aesthetic or affective evaluation ("sweet/pleasant") alongside truth-evaluation.

### Extensions of **ṣe**:

- **ṣe pàtàkì / ṣe kókó** (to be important/essential): These shift from "being the case" to evaluating significance or importance, which is a different dimension of evaluation than truth/falsity.

- **ṣe àṣírí** (to keep secret): This involves concealment rather than truth-value per se, though it relates to disclosure/non-disclosure of true information.

- **ṣe àmúlò** (to use/apply): This extends into practical application rather than truth-evaluation, though it may involve applying true knowledge.

- **ṣe ìrántí** (to remember): This extends into memory and recollection, which relates to truth only insofar as accurate memory is concerned.

- **ṣe àkíyèsí** (to notice/observe): This extends into perceptual attention, which is preliminary to truth-evaluation but not itself an evaluation of truth.
