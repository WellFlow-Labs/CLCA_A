# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P5 OPPOSITIONS
# Language: Indonesian (id)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Oppositions are extracted only where the CONTEXT explicitly documents a contrast, names both terms as contrasting, or provides collocational evidence (e.g., *A atau B*, *A tetapi B*, *A maupun B*) that the two items function as poles of a contrast in attested use. Where the CONTEXT notes a relationship without providing sufficient evidence of contrastive use, this is stated. Oppositions are listed individually; no grouping or categorization is imposed.

---

## Opposition 1

**Term A:** benar
**Term B:** salah
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** true / correct — wrong / incorrect
**Description of Contrast:** *Benar* affirms that a statement, fact, or action corresponds to reality or a correct standard; *salah* indicates deviation from correctness, fact, or proper conduct. The pair is explicitly documented as a fixed binary evaluative frame (*benar atau salah*, "true or false / right or wrong"; *benar-salah*, "right and wrong") used in educational, logical, and moral contexts. Both terms also extend into moral evaluation, where *benar* means "right" and *salah* means "wrong" or "at fault." The two roots are treated as anchoring opposite poles of the correctness axis, and the pair appears in test formats (*soal benar atau salah*).
**Contrast Type:** lexical
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

**Term A:** benar
**Term B:** tidak benar
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** true / correct — not true / incorrect
**Description of Contrast:** *Tidak benar* is the standard morphological negation of *benar*, formed by inserting the general negator *tidak* before the predicate adjective. The CONTEXT documents this as the standard formula in official denials, corrections, and rebuttals, and notes it as distinct from *salah* in that it denies confirmation rather than asserting wrongness. The example *Hal itu tidak benar* ("That is not true") and *informasi itu tidak benar* are attested. It is described as "common in official denials and corrections."
**Contrast Type:** morphological
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 3

The CONTEXT documents this as a politeness strategy common in conversational and journalistic registers, functionally distinct from the categorical negation *tidak benar*.

## Opposition 7

**Term A:** asli
**Term B:** tiruan
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** genuine / original — imitation / copy
**Description of Contrast:** *Asli* indicates the original or genuine article; *tiruan* indicates a replica or imitation that does not necessarily involve fraudulent intent. The CONTEXT explicitly contrasts the two (*ini asli, bukan tiruan*; *hanya tiruan, bukan yang asli*) and notes that *tiruan* occupies an intermediate position between *asli* and *palsu*: it describes resemblance without necessarily implying fraud and "may be acknowledged openly." The pair *barang asli* vs. *barang tiruan* is attested in commercial and legal discourse. The CONTEXT distinguishes *tiruan* from *palsu* within this contrast: *tiruan* is "less strongly negative than *palsu* (which implies deliberate deception)."

## Opposition 29

**Term A:** bohong
**Term B:** dusta
**Transliteration:** N/A
**English Translation:** lie (everyday) — lie / falsehood (formal/literary)
**Description of Contrast:** *Bohong* and *dusta* are near-synonyms both denoting deliberate untruth, but they are distinguished by register: *bohong* is the everyday term used across spoken and informal written contexts, while *dusta* is more formal, literary, and common in religious (especially Islamic) discourse. The multi-word constructions available to *dusta* are a subset of those available to *bohong*, and constructions common with *bohong* (e.g., *ketahuan bohong*, *jangan bohong*) do not have equally common *dusta* equivalents. *Dusta*, *berdusta*, and *pendusta* have "characteristic collocations in Islamic religious discourse" that distinguish their distribution from *bohong* and its derivatives.
