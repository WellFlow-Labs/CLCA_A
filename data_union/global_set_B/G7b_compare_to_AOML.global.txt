# G7b — Empirical vs AOML Constraint Comparison

---

## Preliminary Notes

The empirical matrix (G7a) covers 11 semantic dimensions (D1–D11) and 13 constructional functions (F-A through F-M) across 8 languages. The AOML matrix covers 7 primary axes (VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, ESS, VERIF, LIFE), 2 meta-axes (DEG, TEMP), 7 core operators (ATTR, CAUSE, BECOME, RESULT, ABSTR, STRAT, CONTR), 4 levels, and 6 structural rules (R1–R6). The comparison proceeds dimension by dimension and construction by construction. Where no mapping exists, this is stated without forcing one.

## Summary

### Confirmed Patterns

**1. ATTR universally licensed across all axes and dimensions.**
The empirical finding that copular attribution (F-A) is ALLOWED for all 11 dimensions in all 8 languages directly supports AOML's default licensing of ATTR for all primary axes. No exceptions are documented.

**2. CONTR universally licensed.**
Negation of truth-domain attributions (F-B) is ALLOWED in all 8 languages for all dimensions. AOML's universal licensing of CONTR is supported. The variation in negation strategy (prefixal, syntactic, suppletive copula) is not modeled by AOML but does not conflict with it.

**3. Rule R1 (Dispositional Causation Block) strongly supported.**
The empirical finding that no language lexicalizes a causative for "making someone sincere" or "making someone honest" (D5/F-E) directly supports Rule R1 (CAUSE × DISPOSITIONAL → universally blocked). The causatives that are attested (*llullachiy*, *pantachiy* in qu) cause specific acts of lying or erring, not the dispositional trait itself. This distinction is consistent with AOML's formulation.

**4. Rule R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry) supported.**
The empirical finding that authenticity cannot be manufactured (CAUSE(+) × AUTH not attested as natural) but can be destroyed or forged (CAUSE(−) × AUTH: *伪造*, *kughushi*, *πλαστογραφώ* are [CORE]) directly supports Rule R3. The F6 finding that the inchoative is [UNCERTAIN] for the negative pole of AUTH (becoming forged) while the causative is preferred further supports the rule.

**5. Rule R4 (Positive Emergence Bias) supported.**
The empirical F6 data across zh, he, el, en, tl, sw, and qu consistently shows that positive emergence constructions (*proved true*, *turned out to be genuine*, *naging totoo*) are more natural than negative emergence constructions (*became false*, *became a liar*). This directly supports Rule R4.

**6. Provisional REVEAL operator supported.**
The empirical preference for revelation/discovery constructions over spontaneous BECOME constructions (F6 data: *αποδείχτηκε* [CORE] vs. *γίνομαι* + adjective [Non-idiomatic] in el; *proved true* [CORE] vs. *became true* [MARGINAL] in en; *ulidhihirika* [CORE] in sw; *hitgalaʾh ke-* [CORE] in he) is one of the most consistent findings in the corpus. This supports AOML's provisional REVEAL operator. The key diagnostic (LIFE blocks REVEAL) is also supported: no revelation construction for verisimilitude claims is attested in the empirical data.

**7. ABSTR broadly licensed for D1–D7.**
Abstract nominalization (F-F) is ALLOWED for all 8 languages across the core dimensions (D1–D7). This supports AOML's default licensing of ABSTR for VER, AUTH, SINC, NORM, VERIF.

**8. DEG meta-axis licensing supported.**
Degree modification (F-K) is ALLOWED for all 8 languages across all dimensions. This supports AOML's DEG meta-axis inheriting operator licensing from its host axis.

### Partial or Conditional Matches

**1. D3 (Conformity to a standard) maps partially onto NORM.**
D3 covers both normative correctness (moral rightness) and descriptive correctness (correct answer). AOML's NORM axis is primarily deontic (ought-claims). The descriptive-correctness sub-domain of D3 is not cleanly captured by NORM. The causative constructions for correction (*itama*, *kusahihisha*, *chiqanchay*) are [CORE] in all 8 languages, which may be more permissive than AOML's CAUSE × NORM restriction suggests.

**2. D4 (Reliability) and D5 (Sincerity) both map onto SINC, but are empirically distinct.**
AOML's SINC axis conflates reliability of sources (D4) and inner sincerity of persons (D5) under a single axis. The empirical data maintains these as distinct dimensions: D4 applies to institutions and information sources over time; D5 applies to the inner alignment of persons. The conflation in AOML loses this distinction.

**3. D7 (Legal validity) maps partially onto NORM.**
D7 covers formal institutional validity (whether a document has legal force). AOML's NORM covers moral, legal, and procedural rightness more broadly. The causatives for legal validation (*kuhalalisha*, *letaqqef*, *ακυρώνω*, *validate/invalidate*) are [CORE] in most languages, which may be more permissive than AOML's CAUSE × NORM restriction suggests for the institutional sub-domain.

**4. D8 (Epistemic certainty and source of knowledge) maps partially onto VERIF.**
AOML's VERIF axis covers the process of establishing evidence (claim→world). D8 also covers the epistemic state of the knower (world→claim) and the grammaticalized source-of-knowledge system in qu. The mapping is partial: VERIF captures the verification process but not the evidential source-marking function.

**5. D9 (Appearance of truth) maps partially onto LIFE.**
AOML's LIFE axis covers constructed resemblance to reality. The empirical D9 is broader, covering both aesthetic verisimilitude and epistemic appearance (appearing true without asserting truth). The mapping is partial: LIFE captures the aesthetic sub-domain but not the epistemic sub-domain of D9.

**6. D10 (Label-nature correspondence) maps partially onto ESS.**
AOML's ESS axis covers inherent nature and definitional properties. D10 covers whether a label accurately corresponds to the actual nature of a thing. The mapping is partial: ESS captures the nature side but not the label-accuracy side of D10.

**7. D11 (Ontological status) maps partially onto ESS.**
AOML's ESS axis covers inherent nature. D11 covers ontological status of being (real vs. unreal vs. indeterminate). The mapping is partial: ESS captures some of D11 but not the multi-level ontological framework developed in sa (*mithyā*, *paramārtha*, *vyāvahārika*).

**8. F-J (Deontic) involves cross-axis interaction not modeled in AOML.**
Deontic constructions enjoining truthful speech involve both VER (the content of the obligation) and NORM (the obligatory force). AOML treats these as separate axes without modeling their interaction. The empirical data shows this interaction is productive in all 8 languages.

**9. F-L (Evidential) partially maps onto AOML's validation habitats.**
AOML's validation habitats specify admissible methods for each axis, which is structurally related to the evidential function. But the grammaticalization of source-marking as an obligatory morphological category (qu) is not modeled. The three-way qu distinction (direct/reported/conjectural) maps approximately onto AOML's method categories but not one-to-one.

### Conflicts

**1. ABSTR × LIFE: empirical data does not support universal block.**
AOML claims ABSTR × LIFE is universally blocked (14:2 ratio across 16 languages). The empirical data from 8 languages shows abstract nominalizations for verisimilitude attested in 5 of 8 languages: el *αληθοφάνεια* [CORE], zh 真实感 [CORE], sa *ābhāsa* (functions as abstract nominal), en *verisimilitude* [MARGINAL], sw *uhalisia* [MARGINAL]. The conflict may reflect sampling differences (AOML's 16-language basis vs. 8 languages here), but within the available data the universal block is not confirmed.

**2. *mithyā* third ontological category conflicts with CONTR binary assumption.**
AOML's CONTR operator assumes binary opposition. The sa *mithyā* category introduces a three-valued ontological system (real / neither-real-nor-unreal / unreal) that cannot be captured by AOML's binary CONTR. This is a structural conflict: AOML's architecture does not accommodate non-binary ontological contrasts.

**3. *asato mā sad gamaya* (sa) appears to conflict with CAUSE × ESS block.**
The sa construction *asato mā sad gamaya* (lead me from the unreal to the real) is a causative construction applied to an ontological state, which appears to conflict with Rule R2 (CAUSE × ESS blocked). However, this conflict is uncertain: the construction may be operating on VER or NORM rather than ESS in the AOML sense. The mapping is ambiguous and the conflict may not be genuine.

---

### Unmodeled Empirical Findings

**1. The agentive asymmetry (F-G polarity asymmetry).**
The most robust empirical finding in the corpus — negative-pole agents (liar, deceiver, forger) consistently lexicalized as single words in all 8 languages; positive-pole agents (truth-teller) requiring compound or periphrasis in all 8 — has no AOML counterpart. AOML licenses ABSTR for all relevant axes without modeling the polarity asymmetry. This finding is supported across all 8 languages and all relevant dimensions (D1, D5, D6) and across unrelated language families.

**2. Discourse-framing constructions (F-H).**
The grammaticalization of truth nominals into discourse-framing constructions (*kwa kweli*, *sa totoo lang*, *beʾemet*, *to be honest*, *η αλήθεια είναι ότι*, *实际上*, *vastutaḥ*) is one of the most consistently documented cross-linguistic patterns in the empirical data. AOML has no operator, rule, or structural element corresponding to this function.

**3. Evidential grammaticalization (qu-specific).**
The grammaticalization of source-of-knowledge marking as an obligatory morphological category in qu (*-m/-mi*, *-s/-si*, *-chá*) is not modeled in AOML. AOML's validation habitats specify admissible methods but do not model grammaticalized evidentiality as a morphological system.

**4. Proverbial truth-endurance expressions.**
Cross-linguistic proverbial expressions asserting that truth is painful, endures, or will eventually be revealed (*真金不怕火炼*, *ukweli haiozi*, *η αλήθεια βγαίνει πάντα στο φως*, *the truth will out*, *ang katotohanan ay lalabas din*) are attested in 6 of 8 languages. AOML has no structural element corresponding to this culturally encoded pattern.

**5. D4/D5 distinction within SINC.**
The empirical data maintains a clear distinction between reliability of sources and institutions over time (D4) and inner sincerity of persons (D5). AOML conflates both under SINC. The loss of this distinction is not a conflict but is an unmodeled empirical finding.

**6. D3 descriptive-correctness sub-domain.**
The descriptive-correctness sub-domain of D3 (correct answer, accurate measurement) is not cleanly captured by AOML's NORM axis, which is primarily deontic. The empirical data shows productive causatives for correction (*itama*, *kusahihisha*, *chiqanchay*) that are [CORE] in all 8 languages, suggesting the descriptive-correctness sub-domain may require a separate treatment.

**7. D6 cross-axis structure.**
Deliberate deception (D6) spans AOML's VER, AUTH, and SINC axes depending on the specific sub-type (false statement, forged document, dishonest intent). The empirical data treats D6 as a unified evaluative category. AOML does not model this cross-axis unification.

**8. Language-specific constructional asymmetries.**
The qu causative pathway asymmetry (*-ncha-* vs. *-chi-*), the sw light-verb asymmetry (*sema* vs. *piga*), the he *ʾi-* prefix asymmetry, the zh four-character expression format, and the tl discourse particle system are all unmodeled in AOML. These are language-specific findings that AOML, as a cross-linguistic architecture, does not attempt to model.

**9. Partial-truth constructions as a distinct sub-type.**
Partial-truth constructions (*半真半假*, *ḥatsi ʾemet*, *half-truth*, *nusu ukweli*) are attested in 7 of 8 languages as a distinct evaluative category. AOML's DEG meta-axis licenses degree modification of VER claims, but the specific cultural and moral salience of the partial-truth category — and its consistent negative evaluation across all 8 languages — is not modeled.

### Unattested AOML Structures

**1. CAUSE × ESS blocked (Rule R2) — insufficient empirical data.**
Rule R2 (CAUSE × ESS blocked; BECOME × ESS blocked) is not clearly tested by the empirical data. D10 and D11 are the relevant dimensions, but the empirical data for these dimensions is insufficient (D10 is only clearly lexicalized in zh and sa; D11 is primarily sa). The rule may be correct but cannot be confirmed or disconfirmed from the available data.

**2. STRAT × AUTH restricted — not clearly tested.**
AOML marks STRAT × AUTH as restricted. The empirical data does not provide clear evidence for or against this restriction. The internal stratification of authenticity claims (inner vs. outer authenticity) is not a prominent pattern in the empirical data.

**3. STRAT × SINC restricted — not clearly tested.**
AOML marks STRAT × SINC as restricted. The empirical data shows some evidence for surface/deep sincerity distinctions (zh 表里如一/表里不一; tl *tapat na loob*; qu *cheqa sonqo*), but these are not systematically tested against the STRAT × SINC restriction.

**4. TEMP meta-axis (provisional) — not tested.**
AOML's provisional TEMP meta-axis is not clearly tested by the empirical data. The empirical data does not include systematic testing of temporal position claims in the truth domain.

**5. LIFE × CAUSE restricted — not clearly tested.**
AOML marks CAUSE × LIFE as restricted. The empirical data shows some causatives for creating lifelike representations (zh *使…逼真*; en *make it realistic*), but these are not systematically tested against the CAUSE × LIFE restriction.

**6. RESULT × ESS restricted — not clearly tested.**
AOML marks RESULT × ESS as restricted. The empirical data does not provide clear evidence for or against this restriction.

## Theoretical Discussion

### Where AOML appears strongly supported

**Rule R1 (Dispositional Causation Block)** receives the strongest empirical support in this comparison. The universal absence of causatives for "making someone sincere" or "making someone honest" across all 8 languages — while causatives for specific acts of lying and erring are [CORE] — is precisely what Rule R1 predicts. The distinction between causing a dispositional trait (blocked) and causing a specific act (licensed) is maintained consistently across unrelated language families. This finding is robust.

**Rule R3 (Authenticity Polarity Asymmetry)** is well supported by the empirical data. The consistent finding that authenticity cannot be manufactured (CAUSE(+) × AUTH not attested as natural) but can be destroyed or forged (CAUSE(−) × AUTH: forgery causatives are [CORE] in all 8 languages) directly supports the rule. The F6 finding that the inchoative is [UNCERTAIN] for the negative pole of AUTH (becoming forged) while the causative is preferred provides additional support.

**Rule R4 (Positive Emergence Bias)** is well supported by the F6 naturalness data across 7 of 8 languages. The consistent preference for revelation/discovery constructions over spontaneous BECOME constructions for truth-domain predicates is one of the most consistent patterns in the corpus.

**The provisional REVEAL operator** receives strong empirical support. The preference for *proved true* / *turned out to be* / *αποδείχτηκε* / *ulidhihirika* / *hitgalaʾh ke-* over spontaneous BECOME constructions is documented across 7 of 8 languages. The key diagnostic (LIFE blocks REVEAL) is also supported: no revelation construction for verisimilitude claims is attested in the empirical data. This finding strengthens the case for elevating REVEAL from provisional to confirmed primitive status.

**The target-type ontology** (FACTUAL / EPISTEMIC / DISPOSITIONAL) receives indirect support from the empirical data. The consistent distinction between causatives for factual states (licensed), causatives for epistemic states (restricted), and causatives for dispositional traits (blocked) across unrelated language families is consistent with the hypothesis that target types are structurally prior to operator licensing.

### Where AOML may require refinement

**1. The SINC axis conflates D4 and D5.**
The empirical data maintains a clear distinction between reliability of sources and institutions over time (D4) and inner sincerity of persons (D5). AOML's SINC axis covers both under a single axis with subregions (reliability, faithfulness). This conflation loses an empirically supported distinction. A refinement might separate SINC into two sub-axes: SINC-INNER (inner alignment of persons, D5) and SINC-RELATIONAL (reliability of sources and institutions over time, D4). Rule R1 would apply to SINC-INNER but might be relaxed for SINC-RELATIONAL, where trust-building through action is empirically attested.

**2. The NORM axis may be too broad for D3 and D7.**
AOML's NORM axis covers moral, legal, and procedural rightness (ought-claims). The empirical data shows that D3 (descriptive correctness) and D7 (institutional validity) have distinct properties: causatives for correction and validation are [CORE] in all 8 languages, which may be more permissive than AOML's CAUSE × NORM restriction suggests. A refinement might distinguish NORM-DEONTIC (moral and procedural ought-claims) from NORM-INSTITUTIONAL (formal validity of documents and procedures), with different causative licensing for each.

**3. The ABSTR × LIFE block requires re-examination.**
AOML claims ABSTR × LIFE is universally blocked (14:2 ratio across 16 languages). The empirical data from 8 languages shows abstract nominalizations for verisimilitude attested in 5 of 8 languages. While the conflict may reflect sampling differences, the empirical data suggests the block may be a strong tendency rather than a universal. A refinement might reclassify ABSTR × LIFE as "restricted" rather than "blocked," with the restriction being stronger for aesthetic verisimilitude (LIFE in the narrow sense) than for epistemic appearance (the broader D9 cluster).

**4. The CONTR operator assumes binary opposition.**
The sa *mithyā* category introduces a three-valued ontological system that cannot be captured by AOML's binary CONTR. A refinement might allow CONTR to operate on multi-valued scales (not just binary oppositions) for the ESS and D11 domains, or might introduce a separate operator for graded ontological distinctions.

**5. The D9/LIFE mapping is structurally ambiguous.**
AOML treats LIFE as a standalone primary axis (a claim type). The empirical data treats verisimilitude as a constructional function applied to D1 or D2 claims (F-M). This structural difference is significant: in AOML, LIFE is a primary axis with its own validation habitat (CONSENSUS); in the empirical data, verisimilitude is a modifier on other axes. A refinement might reconceptualize LIFE as a meta-axis (like DEG and TEMP) rather than a primary axis, since it modifies the epistemic status of claims on other axes rather than constituting an independent claim type.

### Where empirical findings suggest new dimensions, new constructional constraints, or revised constraint strength

**1. New dimension: The agentive polarity asymmetry.**
The consistent finding that negative-pole agents are more lexicalized than positive-pole agents across all 8 languages and all relevant dimensions is not modeled by AOML. This finding may reflect a deeper constraint: in the truth domain, the negative pole (deception, falsification) is conceptualized as an active, agentive process, while the positive pole (truth-telling, authenticity) is conceptualized as a default or baseline state. This asymmetry could be modeled as a constraint on ABSTR: ABSTR(negative pole) is more productive than ABSTR(positive pole) for dispositional and agentive nominalizations in the truth domain.

**2. New constructional constraint: Discourse-framing grammaticalization.**
The consistent grammaticalization of truth nominals into discourse-framing constructions across all 8 languages suggests a cross-linguistic constructional constraint: truth-domain nominals are preferentially recruited for discourse-framing functions (signaling frank or corrective speech) across unrelated language families. This is not modeled in AOML and may reflect a pragmatic universal that interacts with the semantic architecture.

**3. Revised constraint strength: CAUSE × NORM.**
The empirical data suggests that CAUSE × NORM may be less restricted than AOML predicts for the institutional sub-domain (D7). Causatives for legal validation and invalidation (*kuhalalisha*, *letaqqef*, *ακυρώνω*, *validate/invalidate*) are [CORE] in most languages. The restriction may apply primarily to the deontic sub-domain of NORM (causing someone to be morally right) rather than to the institutional sub-domain (causing a document to be legally valid).

**4. New dimension: The evidential source-marking system.**
The grammaticalized evidential system in qu (*-m/-mi*, *-s/-si*, *-chá*) represents a dimension of the truth domain that is not modeled in AOML. The three-way distinction between direct knowledge, reported information, and conjecture is a cross-linguistically attested semantic distinction (present lexically in all 8 languages, grammaticalized in qu) that interacts with all primary axes. A refinement might add an EVID meta-axis (parallel to DEG and TEMP) that modulates primary-axis claims by source of knowledge.

**5. Revised constraint strength: BECOME × SINC.**
The empirical data shows that BECOME × SINC is licensed in at least some languages (tl *naging tapat* [CORE]; he trust-erosion constructions [CORE]). AOML marks BECOME × SINC as restricted, which is consistent with the empirical data. However, the empirical data suggests that the restriction is weaker than for BECOME × ESS (which is blocked) and that character development through action is a natural and productive construction in most languages. The restriction should be maintained but its strength relative to other BECOME restrictions should be noted.

**6. New constructional constraint: The partial-truth construction.**
The consistent negative evaluation of partial-truth constructions (*half-truth*, *nusu ukweli*, *ḥatsi ʾemet*) across 7 of 8 languages suggests a cross-linguistic constraint: a claim that is only partially true is evaluated as a form of deception rather than as a weaker form of truth. This is consistent with AOML's Rule R5 (DEG[A] cannot substitute for A in inference) but goes beyond it: the empirical data shows that partial truth is not merely a weaker form of truth but is actively evaluated as a form of dishonesty in most languages. This constraint could be modeled as an interaction between DEG[VER] and SINC: a claim that uses DEG[VER] to simulate VER while withholding full VER constitutes a SINC violation.
