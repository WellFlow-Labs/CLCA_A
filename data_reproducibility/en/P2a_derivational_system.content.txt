# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2a DERIVATIONAL SYSTEM
# Language: English (en)

## DERIVATIONAL ANALYSIS

### BASE: true

#### 1. untrue
**Base form:** true
**Derived form:** untrue
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** un- (negation prefix) + true
**Translation:** not true, false, incorrect
**Usage:** Negates truth claims; used to evaluate statements as not corresponding to reality. Common in both formal and informal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. truth
**Base form:** true
**Derived form:** truth
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** true + -th (abstract noun suffix, Old English -þu/-þo)
**Translation:** truth, the quality of being true, true statement(s)
**Usage:** Nominal form referring to the property of being true or to true propositions themselves. Fundamental across all registers and contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. untruth
**Base form:** true (via truth)
**Derived form:** untruth
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** un- (negation) + truth
**Translation:** falsehood, lie, false statement
**Usage:** Nominal form for false statements. More formal or literary than "lie"; less common than "falsehood" in contemporary usage.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 4. truly
**Base form:** true
**Derived form:** truly
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** true + -ly (adverbial suffix)
**Translation:** in a true manner, genuinely, really
**Usage:** Adverbial form used to emphasize truth, genuineness, or sincerity. Extremely common across all registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 5. truism
**Base form:** true
**Derived form:** truism
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** true + -ism (abstract noun suffix)
**Translation:** obvious truth, self-evident statement, platitude
**Usage:** Refers to statements so obviously true as to be trite or uninteresting. Often used dismissively in analytical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** "truthful" and "truthfulness" are derived from "truth" rather than directly from "true" and are documented under BASE: truthful below.

### BASE: false

#### 1. falsehood
**Base form:** false
**Derived form:** falsehood
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** false + -hood (state/condition suffix, Old English -hād)
**Translation:** lie, untruth, false statement, the quality of being false
**Usage:** Nominal form for false statements or the condition of being false. More formal than "lie"; common in moral and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 2. falsely
**Base form:** false
**Derived form:** falsely
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** false + -ly (adverbial suffix)
**Translation:** in a false manner, incorrectly, untruthfully
**Usage:** Adverbial form indicating manner of being false. Common in legal contexts ("falsely accused") and formal descriptions.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 3. falsify
**Base form:** false
**Derived form:** falsify
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** false + -ify (verb-forming suffix from Latin -ficare)
**Translation:** to make false, to forge, to alter fraudulently, to disprove
**Usage:** Verb meaning to alter something to make it false or to prove something false. Used in legal, scientific, and philosophical contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 4. falsification
**Base form:** false (via falsify)
**Derived form:** falsification
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** falsify + -ation (noun suffix)
**Translation:** act of falsifying, forgery, the process of disproving
**Usage:** Nominal form for the act or result of falsifying. Technical term in philosophy of science (Popperian falsification); also used in legal contexts for fraud.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

#### 5. falsifiable
**Base form:** false (via falsify)
**Derived form:** falsifiable
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** falsify + -able (capable of being)
**Translation:** capable of being proven false, testable
**Usage:** Technical term in philosophy of science for hypotheses that can be empirically tested and potentially disproven. Primarily academic usage.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 6. falsifiability
**Base form:** false (via falsifiable)
**Derived form:** falsifiability
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** falsifiable + -ity (quality suffix)
**Translation:** the quality of being falsifiable, testability
**Usage:** Technical term in philosophy of science for the property of being empirically testable. Used in epistemological and scientific methodology discussions.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

#### 7. falsifier
**Base form:** false (via falsify)
**Derived form:** falsifier
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** falsify + -er (agent noun suffix)
**Translation:** one who falsifies, forger
**Usage:** Refers to persons who falsify documents or data. Less common than "forger" in everyday usage; appears in legal and academic contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**Note:** "falseness" exists but is rare compared to "falsehood" and "falsity."

#### 8. falsity
**Base form:** false
**Derived form:** falsity
**Transliteration:** N/A
**Literal breakdown:** false + -ity (quality suffix, via Latin falsitas)
**Translation:** the quality of being false, a false statement
**Usage:** Formal term for the quality of being false or for false statements. More formal than "falsehood"; used in philosophical and formal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]
