# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Basque (eu)

---

**1. egia**
- **Form:** egia
- **Transliteration:** N/A (Latin script)
- **Literal meaning:** truth; morphologically opaque root with nominal suffix *-a* (definite article)
- **Typical English translations:** truth, true thing
- **Usage:** The primary Basque noun for truth, used across everyday, philosophical, and legal registers. Appears in constructions such as *egia esan* ("to tell the truth") and *egia da* ("it is true"). Participates in productive derivation: *egiazko*, *egiaztatu*, *egiari*.
- **[CORE]**

---

**2. egiazko**
- **Form:** egiazko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egia* (truth) + *-zko* (adjectival/relational suffix) — "of truth," "pertaining to truth"
- **Typical English translations:** true, genuine, real, authentic
- **Usage:** Adjectival form derived from *egia*, used to describe statements, objects, or persons as genuinely corresponding to reality or being what they claim to be. Contrasts with *faltsuko* or *gezurrezko* in evaluative contexts. Common in both spoken and written Basque.
- **[CORE]**

---

**3. egiaztatu**
- **Form:** egiaztatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *egia* (truth) + *-zta-* (verbalizing infix) + *-tu* (perfective suffix) — "to make true," "to verify"
- **Typical English translations:** to verify, to confirm, to authenticate, to certify
- **Usage:** A transitive verb used in legal, administrative, and everyday contexts to denote the act of checking or confirming the accuracy or authenticity of something. Frequently appears in official documents and journalistic language. The nominal form *egiaztatze* refers to the process of verification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**4. gezur**
- **Form:** gezur
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** lie, falsehood (root form; definite: *gezurra*)
- **Typical English translations:** lie, falsehood, untruth
- **Usage:** The primary Basque term for a lie or false statement, functioning as the direct semantic opposite of *egia*. Used in moral, interpersonal, and legal contexts to evaluate statements as deliberately or factually false. Participates in compounds such as *gezurrezko* ("false, lying") and *gezurtatu* ("to falsify, to give the lie to").
- **[CORE]**

---

**5. gezurrezko**
- **Form:** gezurrezko
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *gezur* (lie) + *-rezko* (adjectival/relational suffix) — "of falsehood"
- **Typical English translations:** false, lying, counterfeit, fake
- **Usage:** Adjectival form used to characterize statements, documents, or representations as false or deceptive. Appears in legal and moral evaluative contexts, often in contrast with *egiazko*. Productive in nominal modification.
- **[CORE]**

---

**6. benetako**
- **Form:** benetako
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *benetan* (truly, really, in earnest) + *-ko* (adjectival suffix) — "of the real, genuine kind"
- **Typical English translations:** genuine, real, authentic, true
- **Usage:** Widely used adjective expressing authenticity or genuineness of persons, objects, feelings, or situations. Differs from *egiazko* in that it more strongly emphasizes sincerity and non-artificiality rather than factual correspondence. Common in everyday speech and literary language.
- **[CORE]**

---

**7. benetan**
- **Form:** benetan
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *bene-* (root, possibly related to sincerity/earnestness) + *-tan* (locative/adverbial suffix) — "in truth," "in earnest"
- **Typical English translations:** truly, really, genuinely, in earnest, seriously
- **Usage:** Adverb used to assert the sincerity or reality of a claim, action, or state. Functions as an intensifier of truthfulness in both spoken and written registers. Often used to distinguish genuine intent or fact from pretense.
- **[CORE]**

**8. ziur**
- **Form:** ziur
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** certain, sure (adjective; probable loanword from Latin *securus* via Romance)
- **Typical English translations:** certain, sure, definite
- **Usage:** Used to express epistemic certainty about the truth or reliability of a proposition or state of affairs. Appears in constructions such as *ziur egon* ("to be sure/certain") and *ziur nago* ("I am sure"). Also used to describe reliable persons or things.
- **[CORE]**

---

**9. ziurtatu**
- **Form:** ziurtatu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *ziur* (certain) + *-ta-* (verbalizing infix) + *-tu* (perfective suffix) — "to make certain"
- **Typical English translations:** to certify, to assure, to make sure, to confirm
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used in administrative, legal, and everyday contexts to denote the act of making something certain or officially confirmed. Related to *egiaztatu* but with a stronger emphasis on assurance rather than verification of factual content. The nominal *ziurtasun* ("certainty") is also widely used.
- **[CORE]**

**10. ziurtasun**
- **Form:** ziurtasun
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *ziur* (certain) + *-tasun* (abstract nominal suffix) — "the quality of being certain"
- **Typical English translations:** certainty, sureness, assurance, reliability
- **Usage:** Abstract noun denoting the state or quality of being certain or reliable. Used in philosophical, legal, and everyday discourse to evaluate the epistemic status of claims or the trustworthiness of persons and institutions.
- **[CORE]**

**11. fidagarri**
- **Form:** fidagarri
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *fida-* (to trust, from Romance *fiar*) + *-garri* (adjectival suffix indicating capacity or worthiness) — "worthy of trust"
- **Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, dependable, credible
- **Usage:** Adjective used to evaluate persons, sources, institutions, or information as deserving of trust. Common in journalistic, legal, and everyday evaluative contexts. The negative *fidaezin* or *fidagaitz* expresses unreliability.
- **[CORE]**

**12. sinesgarri**
- **Form:** sinesgarri
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *sines-* (to believe, *sinets-*) + *-garri* (adjectival suffix) — "worthy of belief"
- **Typical English translations:** believable, credible, plausible, convincing
- **Usage:** Adjective used to evaluate whether a statement, person, or account merits belief. Distinct from *fidagarri* in that it focuses on the believability of content rather than the trustworthiness of a source, though the two overlap. Used in legal testimony contexts and everyday speech.
- **[CORE]**

**13. baliozko**

- **Typical English translations:** valid, legitimate, of value
- **Usage:** Adjective used in legal and administrative contexts to describe documents, acts, or claims as legally or formally valid. Also used more broadly to evaluate whether something has genuine worth or standing. Related to *baliozkotu* ("to validate").

**14. baliozkotu**

- **Literal meaning:** *baliozko* (valid) + *-tu* (perfective/verbalizing suffix) — "to make valid"
- **Typical English translations:** to validate, to ratify, to authenticate
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used in legal and administrative registers to denote the formal act of conferring validity on a document, decision, or act. Less common in everyday speech than in official or written language.

**15. zuzen**
- **Form:** zuzen
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** straight, direct, correct, right (adjective/adverb; also noun meaning "right, justice")
- **Typical English translations:** correct, right, just, straight, fair
- **Usage:** Highly polysemous term covering correctness of statements or actions, moral rightness, and legal justice. Used in evaluative contexts ranging from grammatical correctness (*zuzen idatzi*, "to write correctly") to moral and legal rightness (*zuzen jokatu*, "to act rightly"). Also functions as a noun meaning "right" or "justice" in legal contexts.
- **[CORE]**

**17. oker**

- **Usage:** The primary antonym of *zuzen*, used to evaluate statements, actions, or judgments as incorrect or morally wrong. Appears in constructions such as *oker egon* ("to be wrong/mistaken") and *oker ibili* ("to go wrong, to be on the wrong track"). Common across all registers.

**18. faltsu**
- **Form:** faltsu
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** false, fake (loanword from Romance *falso*)
- **Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, insincere
- **Usage:** Adjective used to evaluate objects, documents, persons, or statements as false, counterfeit, or insincere. Overlaps with *gezurrezko* for statements and with *faltsifikatu* in the domain of forgery. Common in everyday and legal registers.
- **[CORE]**

**19. faltsifikatu**

- **Typical English translations:** to falsify, to forge, to counterfeit
- **Usage:** Transitive verb used primarily in legal and administrative contexts to denote the deliberate falsification of documents, data, or representations. Less common in everyday speech; predominantly a written and formal register term.

**25. berdintsu**

**30. sasi-**
- **Form:** sasi-
- **Transliteration:** N/A
- **Literal meaning:** *sasi* (bramble, thicket; also "false, pseudo, spurious" as prefix) — "false, pseudo, spurious"
- **Typical English translations:** pseudo-, false, spurious, sham (as prefix)

Used in critical and evaluative contexts to expose misrepresentation of identity or quality. Attested across written and spoken registers.
