# Global Convergence Report: Cross-Language Analysis of Truth-Related Semantic Organization

## 1. Scope and Data

### Language Sample
This analysis examined eight languages representing diverse typological profiles and language families:
- **Kartvelian**: Georgian (ka)
- **Niger-Congo**: Yoruba (yo)
- **Uralic**: Finnish (fi)
- **Language isolate**: Basque (eu)
- **Dravidian**: Tamil (ta)
- **Austronesian**: Indonesian (id)
- **Turkic**: Turkish (tr)
- **Koreanic**: Korean (ko)

### Analytical Coverage
Complete F-phase analysis (F1–F6) was available for all eight languages, documenting:
- **F1**: Core vocabulary inventories with semantic ranges and usage contexts
- **F2**: Derivational patterns and morphological productivity
- **F3**: Semantic groupings and dimensional structure within each language
- **F4**: Constructional realization patterns and syntactic strategies
- **F5**: Oppositional structures and contrastive organization
- **F6**: Naturalness judgments for truth-related combinations

G-phase synthesis (G1–G8) integrated these language-specific analyses to identify cross-linguistic patterns, constraints, and asymmetries.

### Data Completeness
All eight languages received complete F-phase documentation. No systematic gaps in coverage were observed, though depth of F6 naturalness testing varied by language. Some constructions (e.g., dispositional causation) were not explicitly tested in all languages, with evidence derived from systematic absence rather than explicit elicitation.

## 2. Core Cross-Language Findings

### Vocabulary Organization

**Universal core structure**: All eight languages show fundamental lexical oppositions between:
1. **Truth and falsehood** for statements (სიმართლე/ტყუილი, òtítọ́/irọ́, totuus/valhe, egia/gezurra, உண்மை/பொய், benar/bohong, doğru/yanlış, 진실/거짓)
2. **Genuine and fake** for objects (ნამდვილი/ყალბი, aito/väärennös, egiazkoa/gezurrezko, அசல்/பொய்ப்பொருள், asli/palsu, asıl/sahte, 진품/위조품)
3. **Honest character and lying behavior** (though lexicalization strategies vary)

**Divergent elaboration**: Languages differ significantly in internal differentiation:
- **Minimal differentiation**: Yoruba (single root tọ́ extends across multiple domains)
- **Moderate differentiation**: Georgian, Finnish, Basque, Indonesian (2-3 main truth terms)
- **Extensive differentiation**: Tamil, Turkish, Korean (4-5+ truth terms with clear register or domain distinctions)

Register stratification correlates with contact history: languages with extensive borrowing (Korean Sino-Korean, Tamil Sanskrit, Turkish Arabic/Persian) maintain parallel vocabulary systems; more isolated languages (Yoruba, Georgian) use unified native vocabulary.

### Derivational Patterns

**Universal productive patterns** (8/8 languages):
1. **Abstract quality nominalization**: All languages productively derive abstract nouns (truthfulness, honesty, accuracy) using affixation, though strategies vary (Georgian -ობა, Yoruba ì-/ò-, Finnish -uus/-yys, Basque -tasun, Tamil -தன்மை, Indonesian ke-...-an, Turkish -lık/-lik, Korean -성)
2. **Manner adverbialization**: All languages create manner adverbs (truly, honestly, accurately) through suffixation, case marking, or prepositional phrases
3. **Truth-term + noun compounding**: All languages productively create specialized concepts (true love, true story, false testimony)

**Strong tendencies** (7/8 languages):
1. **Privative/negative affixation**: Seven languages use productive privative morphology (Georgian უ-, Finnish epä-, Basque -gabe, Tamil -ஆமை, Indonesian tidak-, Turkish -sız, Korean 부-/불-); only Yoruba relies primarily on lexical oppositions
2. **Verification verb derivation**: Seven languages productively derive verification verbs from truth-terms (Finnish todistaa, Turkish doğrulamak, Basque egiazkotu, Tamil உண்மைப்படுத்து, Indonesian membenarkan); Georgian and Yoruba use separate roots

**Systematic asymmetry** (8/8 languages):
- **Agentive nominalization**: All languages lexicalize "liar" (ტყუარა, apurọ́, valehtelija, gezurtia, பொய்யன், pembohong, yalancı, 거짓말쟁이) but seven lack dedicated "truth-teller" nouns, using periphrastic constructions instead ("person who tells truth," "honest person")

### Constructional Patterns

**Universal patterns**:
1. **Zero or minimal copula**: Seven of eight languages prefer zero copula in present affirmative truth-value attribution (only Finnish requires explicit copula in all tenses)
2. **Light verb speech acts**: All languages express truth-telling through light verb constructions (truth-term + "say") rather than dedicated simple verbs
3. **Revelation constructions**: All languages show specialized constructions for truth emerging from hiddenness, typically using intransitive or passive verbs

**Typological variation**:
1. **Negation strategies**: Languages split between negative copula systems (Finnish ei ole, Indonesian tidak/bukan, Turkish değil, Korean 아니다) and negative particles before verbs (Georgian არ, Yoruba kò, Basque ez)
2. **Case marking**: Languages with case systems show different patterns (Finnish partitive for general truth, Tamil/Korean/Basque accusative for truth objects, Turkish/Indonesian no morphological case)
3. **Passive voice preferences**: Languages with productive passive systems (Finnish, Korean, Indonesian) extend them to truth-domain revelation; languages with less grammaticalized passives (Yoruba) use active or intransitive alternatives

### Oppositional Structures

**Universal oppositions**:
- All languages show lexical truth/falsehood oppositions
- Seven languages show productive morphological oppositions through privative affixation
- All languages show constructional oppositions through copula negation and contrasting verbs

**Systematic asymmetries**:
1. **Revelation vs. concealment**: Revelation vocabulary is more elaborated than concealment vocabulary in most languages (multiple specialized revelation verbs vs. typically one general "hide" verb)
2. **Positive vs. negative agents**: Negative agent roles (liar, deceiver) are universally lexicalized; positive agent roles (truth-teller) are systematically absent or marginal

### Major Asymmetries

**1. Inchoative blocking with abstract truth** (6/8 languages):
- Simple "become true" constructions are blocked or marked (Korean, Georgian, Basque) or restricted (Finnish, Tamil)
- Languages systematically substitute revelation/discovery verbs ("come to light," "be revealed")
- Exceptions: Indonesian and Turkish allow inchoatives naturally
- **Interpretation**: Abstract truth resists conceptualization as "coming into being"; languages prefer "coming into view" semantics

**2. Causative semantic restriction**:
- Verification causatives (establishing pre-existing truth) are universally productive
- Creation causatives (bringing truth into existence) are blocked or marked in documented cases
- **Interpretation**: Truth is discovered, not manufactured; causatives implying creation violate this ontological status

**3. Agentive nominalization asymmetry** (universal):
- "Liar" lexicalized in all 8 languages
- "Truth-teller" absent in 7/8 languages (only Tamil shows marginal lexicalization)
- **Interpretation**: Marked behaviors (lying) require naming; unmarked behaviors (truth-telling) are expressed periphrastically

## 3. Global Constraint Summary

### Constraint Matrix Structure

The empirical constraint matrix (G7a) organizes findings along two dimensions:
- **12 semantic dimensions** (statement correspondence, object genuineness, character disposition, procedural conformity, measurement precision, source dependability, epistemic confidence, normative warrant, deceptive intent, evidential process, truth visibility, expression authenticity)
- **12 constructional functions** (predication, negation, adnominal modification, inchoative, causative, revelation, concealment, abstract nominalization, agentive nominalization, manner adverbialization, light verb speech acts, degree modification)

### Strongly Supported Constraints

**Universal or near-universal patterns** (6-8/8 languages with consistent F4/F5/F6 documentation):

1. **Inchoative blocking with abstract truth** (D1 × C4): Six languages block simple "become true" constructions, substituting revelation verbs. Indonesian and Turkish are clear exceptions.

2. **Agentive nominalization asymmetry** (D3 × C9): All languages lexicalize "liar" but seven lack "truth-teller" agentives, using periphrastic constructions instead.

3. **Causative restriction to verification** (D1/D10 × C5): Verification causatives universally productive; creation causatives blocked or marked in documented cases.

4. **Revelation construction universality** (D11 × C6): All languages show highly natural revelation/discovery constructions, rated [CORE] in F6 tables.

5. **Light verb speech act requirement** (D1 × C11): All languages express truth-telling through light verb constructions rather than dedicated simple verbs.

6. **Abstract nominalization productivity** (all dimensions × C8): Universal productivity across all semantic dimensions and all languages.

7. **Privative morphology productivity** (most dimensions × morphological negation): Seven languages show productive privative affixation; only Yoruba relies primarily on lexical oppositions.

### Moderately Supported Patterns

**Patterns with clear evidence but notable exceptions** (3-5/8 languages):

1. **Passive voice preference in revelation**: Finnish, Korean, Indonesian prefer passive revelation verbs; Yoruba, Basque, Tamil prefer active/intransitive; Georgian and Turkish show no strong preference.

2. **Register-based parallel systems**: Korean, Tamil, Turkish, Indonesian show extensive register stratification; Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque show minimal stratification.

3. **Polysemy of straightness/correctness**: Six languages show this pattern (Georgian, Yoruba, Finnish, Basque, Tamil, Turkish); Indonesian and Korean maintain separate terms.

4. **Deliberate vs. unintentional error distinction**: Five languages clearly distinguish (Finnish valhe/virhe, Indonesian bohong/salah, Tamil பொய்/தப்பு, Turkish yalan/hata, Korean 거짓말/실수); three use same terms for both.

### Weak or Uncertain Areas

**Patterns requiring more data or showing high variability**:

1. **Degree modification restrictions**: Documented in Korean and Finnish (core nominals resist degree modification); other languages not systematically tested.

2. **Dispositional causation blocking**: Consistent absence across all F4 tables but not explicitly elicited. Evidence from absence rather than documented blocking.

3. **Concealment elaboration**: All languages have concealment vocabulary, but systematic comparison of elaboration levels not completed.

4. **Appearance vs. reality lexicalization**: Only Basque and Turkish show dedicated adjectives; other languages use constructional means.

### Variability and Context-Dependence

**Context-dependent patterns**:

1. **Inchoative productivity**: Varies by:
   - Semantic type (abstract truth vs. concrete properties)
   - Language (Indonesian/Turkish allow; Korean/Georgian block)
   - Construction type (simple inchoatives blocked; revelation verbs substituted)

2. **Passive voice preferences**: Correlate with general passive productivity in each language rather than truth-domain-specific restriction.

3. **Register stratification**: Varies by sociolinguistic history (contact languages show more stratification).

**Partial constraints**:

1. **Causative restrictions**: Verification sense universally productive; creation sense blocked in documented cases but not tested in all languages.

2. **Agentive nominalization**: Productive for negative agents; blocked for positive agents. Represents pragmatic rather than morphological constraint (affixes work; specific combinations avoided).

## 4. Candidate Universals and Outliers

### Strong Cross-Language Tendencies (HIGH confidence)

**Universals** (8/8 languages):
1. Core truth/falsehood lexical opposition
2. Agentive "liar" lexicalization with "truth-teller" gap
3. Abstract quality nominalization productivity
4. Revelation/discovery construction universality
5. Verification vocabulary productivity
6. Light verb speech act constructions
7. Character honesty vs. statement truth distinction

**Near-universals** (7/8 languages):
1. Object authenticity distinction (weak in Yoruba)
2. Privative morphology productivity (weak in Yoruba)
3. Zero/minimal copula preference (Finnish exception)

### Tentative or Weak Candidates (MEDIUM confidence)

**Strong tendencies** (6/8 languages):
1. Inchoative blocking with abstract truth terms (Indonesian, Turkish exceptions)
2. Polysemy of straightness/correctness (Indonesian, Korean exceptions)
3. Passive/intransitive preference in revelation (Yoruba, Basque, Tamil exceptions)

**Moderate patterns** (4-5/8 languages):
1. Register-based parallel vocabulary systems
2. Deliberate vs. unintentional error distinction
3. Causative restriction to verification sense (documented in 4/8 explicitly)

### Major Outliers

**Language-level outliers**:

1. **Korean**: Exceptional register stratification with four parallel truth-vocabulary systems (native 참, colloquial 진짜, formal 진실, factual 사실, philosophical 진리). Also unique in internal oppositional compounds (진위 "truth-or-falsehood") and practice/theory elaboration (extensive 실- root family).

2. **Indonesian**: Exceptional error-type specification through productive salah- compounding (14+ distinct error types: salah paham "misunderstand," salah guna "misuse," etc.). Also unique in softened negation strategy (kurang benar "not quite correct").

3. **Yoruba**: Unique directional/guidance extensions of tọ́ root (tọ́ ẹsẹ̀ "direct steps," tọ́jú "care for properly"). Also distinctive in ideophonic modification system and weaker privative morphology.

**Construction-level outliers**:

1. **Finnish**: Unique partitive case requirement for general truth statements (se on totta). No parallel in other case-marking languages.

2. **Turkish**: Productive inchoative + causative morphological stacking (gerçekleştirmek "realize" = become + make). Other agglutinative languages don't show comparable stacking in truth-domain.

3. **Indonesian/Korean**: Dedicated softened negation strategies for politeness (Indonesian kurang, Korean similar patterns). Other languages use standard negation.

**Dimension-specific outliers**:

1. **Tamil**: Truth/body polysemy in மெய் (mey) — uniquely polysemous between "truth/reality" and "body/physical" (மெய்யெழுத்து "consonant" = body-letter).

2. **Turkish**: Exceptional hak polysemy spanning truth, rights, deservingness, and divinity with extensive idiomatic expressions.

3. **Georgian**: Religious orthodoxy integrated into truth-domain root system (მართლმადიდებელი "Orthodox Christian" from მართ- "right/true").

### Structural Divergences

**Copula systems**: Languages split into three types:
- Zero copula preferred (Georgian, Yoruba, Turkish, Korean, Indonesian colloquial, Basque)
- Obligatory copula (Finnish)
- Context-dependent (Tamil: zero colloquially, explicit formally)

**Negation strategies**: Languages split into two types:
- Negative copula distinct from verbal negation (Finnish, Indonesian, Turkish, Korean)
- Negative particle before verb (Georgian, Yoruba, Basque, Tamil)

**Inchoative productivity**: Languages split into three types:
- Highly productive with truth-terms (Indonesian, Turkish)
- Blocked or marked with abstract truth (Korean, Georgian, Basque)
- Intermediate (Finnish, Tamil: allowed but restricted)

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

### Data Gaps

**Language coverage**: Eight languages provide initial coverage but represent limited sampling:
- **Missing families**: Sino-Tibetan, Afro-Asiatic, Uto-Aztecan, Trans-New Guinea, Australian
- **Missing types**: Polysynthetic languages (Mohawk, Inuktitut), sign languages
- **Geographic gaps**: Limited coverage of Africa, Oceania, Americas

**Construction coverage**: Some constructions not systematically tested:
- Dispositional causation (evidence from absence rather than explicit elicitation)
- Degree modification (systematically tested only in Korean and Finnish)
- Cross-dimensional interactions (e.g., "reveal the genuine article")
- Evidential marking (only partially documented)

**Domain coverage**: Current focus on core truth-domain vocabulary:
- Adjacent domains (knowledge, belief, certainty) not fully explored
- Discourse-level truth management strategies not systematically analyzed
- Specialized registers (legal, scientific, religious) not comprehensively covered

### Methodological Limitations

**Naturalness judgment reliability**: F6 naturalness ratings based on:
- Analyst judgments informed by corpus data and native speaker consultation
- Not controlled experimental elicitation with multiple speakers
- Variation in testing depth across languages

**Attestation thresholds**: Current sample size limits confidence:
- "Strong" patterns (6-8/8 languages) represent limited coverage
- Statistical significance cannot be established with n=8
- Typological diversity good but not comprehensive

**Dependency on F-phase completeness**: G-phase synthesis quality depends on F-phase analysis depth. Some F-phase analyses (especially F6) show variation in coverage depth.

### Recommended Next Steps

**Language expansion** (priority targets):
1. **Sino-Tibetan**: Mandarin, Cantonese, Tibetan (test tonal and evidential systems)
2. **Afro-Asiatic**: Arabic, Hebrew, Amharic (test Semitic root-and-pattern morphology)
3. **Polysynthetic**: Mohawk, Inuktitut, Greenlandic (test incorporation and complex morphology)
4. **Additional isolates**: Ainu, Burushaski (expand isolate coverage)
5. **Sign languages**: ASL, BSL (test modality effects)

**Targeted investigation of specific asymmetries**:

1. **Agentive asymmetry**: Explicit elicitation testing whether speakers accept novel "truth-teller" coinages; corpus frequency analysis of "liar" vs. "honest person" constructions

2. **Inchoative blocking mechanism**: Cross-domain comparison (do other abstract concepts show similar blocking?); diachronic evidence (did languages historically allow inchoatives now blocked?)

3. **Dispositional causation**: Explicit elicitation with prompts like "make someone become a liar" (character change) vs. "make someone lie" (specific act)

4. **Degree modification**: Systematic testing across all 8 languages with various truth-term types (nouns vs. adjectives, abstract vs. concrete)

5. **ESS and LIFE dimensions**: Targeted investigation of whether languages systematically distinguish:
   - Essential properties from factual properties
   - Representational fidelity from object authenticity
   - Testing AOML diagnostic: Do revelation constructions behave differently with essence/fidelity terms?

**Methodological refinement**:
1. Develop standardized elicitation protocols for F6 naturalness judgments
2. Establish inter-annotator reliability measures for F3 semantic groupings
3. Create reference corpora for frequency-based validation
4. Implement systematic gap analysis (what constructions were not tested?)
5. Add explicit statistical testing where sample size permits

**Theoretical integration**:
1. Systematic comparison with additional theoretical frameworks beyond AOML
2. Development of explanatory hypotheses for observed asymmetries
3. Integration with broader semantic typology literature
4. Exploration of cognitive/pragmatic motivations for universal patterns

### Critical Uncertainties

**Unresolved questions**:

1. **Inchoative blocking**: Is this semantic incompatibility (truth as pre-existing) or pragmatic preference (revelation more informative)?

2. **Agentive asymmetry**: Is this frequency-based (liars discussed more), conceptual (lying is action, truth-telling is state), or pragmatic (marked behaviors need naming)?

3. **ESS/LIFE status**: Are these genuine cross-linguistic dimensions or theoretical constructs not empirically grounded?

4. **Causative restrictions**: Is verification/creation distinction universal or documented only in subset of languages?

5. **Concealment elaboration**: Is revelation systematically more elaborated, or is this sampling artifact?

**Areas needing explicit testing**:
- Dispositional causation (currently evidence from absence)
- Degree modification (currently tested in 2/8 languages)
- Cross-dimensional interactions (currently limited documentation)
- Specialized register variation (legal, scientific, religious)

## Conclusion

This analysis reveals a **robust core structure** shared across eight typologically diverse languages: fundamental truth/falsehood oppositions, object authenticity distinctions, character honesty evaluation, and universal productive patterns in abstract nominalization, revelation constructions, and verification vocabulary.

**Three systematic asymmetries** appear with high confidence:
1. Agentive "liar" lexicalization with "truth-teller" gap (8/8 languages)
2. Inchoative blocking with abstract truth terms (6/8 languages)
3. Causative restriction to verification sense (documented in 4/8 languages)

These asymmetries suggest **universal cognitive or pragmatic constraints** on truth-domain morphosyntax rather than language-specific idiosyncrasies.

**Major divergences** appear in:
- Register stratification depth (minimal in Yoruba/Georgian vs. extensive in Korean/Tamil/Turkish)
- Inchoative productivity (natural in Indonesian/Turkish vs. blocked in Korean/Georgian/Basque)
- Copula and negation strategies (typological variation)

The analysis identifies **10 open questions** requiring targeted investigation, broader language samples, or explicit elicitation to resolve. Expansion to 15-20 languages from additional families would strengthen confidence in proposed universals and clarify the status of tentative patterns.
