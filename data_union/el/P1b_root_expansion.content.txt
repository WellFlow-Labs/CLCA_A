# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** αληθ- / ΑΛΗΘ-
**Transliteration:** alith-
**P1 items participating:** 5 (αληθής, αλήθεια, αληθινός, επαληθεύω, επαλήθευση)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes the primary concept of truth as unconcealment or correspondence with reality. It is the foundational morpheme for propositional truth, factual accuracy, and genuine states of affairs across all registers. It is the primary positive pole of the truth domain in Greek, generating the central noun, adjective, and verb for truth across all registers.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ψευδ- / ψευτ- / ΨΕΥΔ-
**Transliteration:** psevd- / psevt-
**P1 items participating:** 4 (ψεύδος, ψευδής, ψεύτικος, ψεύτης underlying ψεύτικος)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes falsehood, deception, and misrepresentation. It covers both the propositional dimension (a false statement) and the agentive dimension (a liar, a deceiver), and is highly productive in compounds and prefixed forms. It generates the primary vocabulary of lying, deception, and falsification across all registers.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** πιστ- / -ΠΙΣΤ-
**Transliteration:** pist-
**P1 items participating:** 3 (αξιόπιστος, αναξιόπιστος, διαπίστωση)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes trust, belief, and credibility — the relational dimension of truth-evaluation whereby a claim or person is deemed worthy of confidence. It underlies a large family of terms concerning reliability, faith, certification, and the epistemic relation between a person and a claim or source.

---

*Note on Root 3:* The root πιστ- (from πίστις, faith/trust) is highly productive across Greek but contributes 2–3 items directly to P1. The root αξι- (from ἄξιος, worthy) is similarly productive but contributes only 2 items. πιστ- is selected as the third root on the grounds that it underlies the credibility/trust sub-domain and generates a large attested family including compound stems εμπιστ-, πιστοποι-, and πιστωτ-.

---

*Note on εικ-/εικον- root:* The root εικ-/εικον- (from εἰκών, image/likeness) participates in P1 items εικονικός, εικαζόμενος, and εικαστικός, and generates a productive family of terms concerning representation, simulation, and conjecture. Its expansion is included below as Root 4.

---

**Root 4**

**Form:** εικ- / εικον-
**Transliteration:** eik- / eikon-
**P1 items participating:** 3 (εικονικός, εικαζόμενος, εικαστικός)
**Core meaning contribution:** The root encodes the concept of likeness, image, and resemblance. It contributes to vocabulary concerning representation, simulation, and conjecture — covering the space between genuine reality and its copies or approximations.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### ROOT 1: αληθ- (*alith-*)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εια (abstract noun suffix); "the state of being unconcealed"
**Natural English translation:** truth, truthfulness
**Context of use:** The primary noun for truth in all registers. Already listed in P1; included here as the base form for the expansion.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ής (adjectival suffix)
**Natural English translation:** true, truthful
**Context of use:** Base adjective for truth-value; already in P1. Included as anchor form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ινός (adjectival suffix indicating nature or material)
**Natural English translation:** real, genuine, true
**Context of use:** Already in P1. Included as anchor form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** αληθεύω
**Transliteration:** alithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix); "to be truthful, to tell the truth"
**Natural English translation:** to speak the truth, to be truthful, to be accurate
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, literary, and formal registers to describe the act of telling the truth or being in a state of truthfulness. Less common in everyday speech than λέω την αλήθεια. *Αληθεύει ότι…* means "it is true that…" in formal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** επαληθεύω
**Transliteration:** epalithévo
**Morpheme breakdown:** επί (upon, further) + αληθ- (truth) + -εύω (verbal suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to verify, to confirm, to validate
**Context of use:** Already in P1. Included here as a derived verbal form.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** επαλήθευση
**Transliteration:** epalíthefsi
**Morpheme breakdown:** επί + αληθ- + -ευση (verbal noun suffix)
**Natural English translation:** verification, confirmation, validation
**Context of use:** The noun corresponding to επαληθεύω. Used in scientific, administrative, journalistic, and legal contexts to refer to the process or act of verifying a claim, result, or document.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** επαληθεύσιμος
**Transliteration:** epalithéfsimos
**Morpheme breakdown:** επαληθεύω (to verify) + -σιμος (adjectival suffix indicating possibility); "capable of being verified"
**Natural English translation:** verifiable
**Context of use:** Used in scientific, philosophical, and journalistic contexts to describe claims or hypotheses that can in principle be checked against evidence. Related to the concept of falsifiability in philosophy of science.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** αληθοφανής
**Transliteration:** alithofanís
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθο- (truth-) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -ής (adjectival suffix); "appearing true"
**Natural English translation:** verisimilar, plausible, lifelike, credible, truth-seeming
**Context of use:** Applied to narratives, arguments, and representations that have the appearance of truth or reality without necessarily being true. Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophical discussion of probability, and in everyday evaluation of stories or claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια
**Transliteration:** alithofánia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθο- (truth-) + φαίνομαι (to appear) + -εια (abstract noun suffix); "the quality of appearing true"
**Natural English translation:** verisimilitude, plausibility, appearance of truth
**Context of use:** Used in literary criticism, rhetoric, and philosophy to describe the quality of appearing true or lifelike, without necessarily being true. Applied to narrative, argument, and representation. Corresponds to the classical concept of verisimilitude. *Η αληθοφάνεια ενός μυθιστορήματος* means "the verisimilitude of a novel."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** αληθοφάνεια αφήγησης
**Transliteration:** alithofánia afígisis
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθοφάνεια (verisimilitude) + αφήγηση (narration)
**Natural English translation:** narrative verisimilitude
**Context of use:** A fixed collocation used in literary theory and narratology to refer to the quality of a narrative that makes it seem true or realistic. Common in academic literary criticism and rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** αληθοφανής αφήγηση
**Transliteration:** alithofanís afígisi
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθοφανής (verisimilar) + αφήγηση (narration)
**Natural English translation:** verisimilar narrative, plausible account
**Context of use:** Used in literary theory and narratology to describe a narrative that achieves the effect of reality or truth. Common in academic literary criticism and rhetoric.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** αληθινά
**Transliteration:** alithiná
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθινός (true, genuine) + -α (adverbial suffix); "truly, genuinely"
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, genuinely
**Context of use:** Common adverbial form used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm the truth of a statement. Also used as a discourse marker of sincerity. "Αληθινά δεν το ήξερα" = "I truly didn't know."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** αληθώς
**Transliteration:** alithós
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθ- (truth) + -ώς (adverbial suffix)
**Natural English translation:** truly, verily, in truth
**Context of use:** A formal or archaic adverb used in religious, liturgical, and elevated literary contexts. Appears in the Orthodox liturgical response Αληθώς ανέστη ("He is truly risen").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** Αληθώς ανέστη
**Transliteration:** Alithós anésti
**Morpheme breakdown:** αληθώς (truly) + ανέστη (he rose, aorist of ανίσταμαι)
**Natural English translation:** He is truly risen
**Context of use:** The liturgical response in the Greek Orthodox Easter service, spoken in reply to Χριστός ανέστη ("Christ is risen"). A fixed religious formula.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** αλήθεια (exclamative)
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** αλήθεια (truth); used as a discourse particle
**Natural English translation:** Really? / Truly? / By the way
**Context of use:** Used as a conversational interjection to express surprise ("Αλήθεια;" = "Really?") or to introduce a new topic ("Αλήθεια, ήθελα να σε ρωτήσω…" = "By the way, I wanted to ask you…"). Extremely frequent in spoken Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** στ' αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** st' alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** εις (to/in) + τη (the) + αλήθεια (truth); "in truth"
**Natural English translation:** truly, in truth, honestly, really
**Context of use:** Fixed adverbial phrase used in everyday speech to intensify or affirm a statement, or to ask whether something is genuinely the case. "Στ' αλήθεια, δεν ξέρω τι να πω" = "Honestly, I don't know what to say."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** η αλήθεια είναι ότι
**Transliteration:** i alíthia íne óti
**Morpheme breakdown:** η αλήθεια (the truth) + είναι (is) + ότι (that)
**Natural English translation:** the truth is that…
**Context of use:** A fixed clausal frame used to introduce a frank or corrective statement, often conceding something the speaker might prefer not to admit. Functions to signal that what follows is the speaker's genuine assessment, often contrasting with a previous claim or expectation. Extremely common in both spoken and written Greek.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** λέω την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** léo tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** λέω (I say) + την αλήθεια (the truth)
**Natural English translation:** to tell the truth
**Context of use:** The standard colloquial expression for telling the truth. Used in everyday speech, legal contexts (witness testimony), and moral discourse. Functions as both a sincere assertion and a rhetorical marker.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** για την αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** ya tin alíthia
**Morpheme breakdown:** για (for) + την αλήθεια (the truth)
**Natural English translation:** for the sake of truth, to be honest, truthfully
**Context of use:** A fixed prepositional phrase used as a discourse marker to signal that the speaker is about to say something frank or corrective. Common in everyday speech and informal writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
