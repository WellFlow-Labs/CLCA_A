| 1. Basic statement-truth and fact-matching language | These expressions are used most directly for saying that a statement, belief, report, or proposition matches what is the case. | 2. Answer-checking, correction, and getting-things-right language | These expressions are common where people evaluate answers, interpretations, procedures, or judgments as right or wrong. | 3. | 4. | 5. Fake, forged, and deceptive-imitation language | These expressions are used when something is presented as genuine but is not. | 6. Reliability, trust, and believability of sources or accounts | These expressions are used when evaluating whether a person, source, witness, method, or account can be depended on or believed. | 7. Honesty and candid-speaking language | These expressions are used for speaking sincerely, not deceiving, or openly admitting what one really thinks. | 8. Faithfulness to an original, source, or lived reality | These expressions are used for translations, reproductions, portraits, performances, and adaptations that preserve or resemble an original closely. | 9. Appearance-of-truth, realism, and seeming-convincing language | These expressions are used when something seems true, lifelike, or believable without necessarily being directly verified. | 10. Validity, soundness, and accepted force under rules or reasoning | These expressions are used where something counts as acceptable, effective, or well-founded under logical, legal, procedural, or methodological standards. | 11. Properness, appropriateness, and conformity to expected form | These expressions are used when something is judged correct because it fits a norm, convention, role, or required procedure. | 12. Lawfulness, legitimacy, and rightful standing | These expressions are used for authority, claims, ownership, actions, or status recognized as lawful, justified, or properly grounded. | 13.
