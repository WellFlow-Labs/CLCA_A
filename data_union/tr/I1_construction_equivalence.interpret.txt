## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substituted by PREDICATE OUTCOME

No dedicated REVELATION construction is attested in P4a or P4b. The closest functional equivalent is the PREDICATE OUTCOME construction (P4a §16): *[truth-domain adjective/noun] + çıkmak* ("to turn out to be"). This construction (*Tahmini doğru çıktı* — "His/her prediction turned out to be correct"; *Bu yalan çıktı* — "This turned out to be a lie") covers the retrospective discovery that something has a particular truth-domain property, following a process of confirmation or exposure. However, it is semantically restricted to retrospective evaluation: it encodes that a truth-domain judgment is the result of discovery, not that a fact is being disclosed or made known to an audience. A prospective or communicative sense of revelation — "it has come to light that X," "it emerged that X" — is not covered by this construction. No single lexicalized operator for that prospective sense is attested in P4a. The SINCERITY/CORRECTION DISCOURSE MARKER construction (P4a §10: *aslında*, *doğrusu*, *gerçek şu ki*) provides a corrective or contrastive frame ("actually, in fact") that partially overlaps with the pragmatic function of revelation in discourse, but these are grammaticalized discourse markers, not propositional operators.

### 2.2 VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION: shared construction type, distinct lexemes

VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION are expressed through the same morphosyntactic construction type — the *-la-* factitive verbalizer applied to a truth-domain base — but through distinct lexemes: *doğrulamak* (to verify/confirm) vs. *yalanlamak* (to deny/refute) and *yanlışlamak* (to falsify). They do not share a single construction; the construction type is shared, but the lexical items are not interchangeable. The two sides of the domain are thus formally parallel but lexically separate. *Yanlışlamak* and *yalanlamak* are not synonymous: *yalanlamak* denotes active denial or refutation (typically of a claim by a person), while *yanlışlamak* denotes logical or evidential falsification (typically of a hypothesis or proposition). This distinction is register-sensitive: *yanlışlamak* is [MARGINAL] outside academic/philosophical registers, while *yalanlamak* is [CORE] in journalistic and everyday use.

### 2.3 NEGATION: analytic operator (*değil*) vs. morphological lexeme-formation (*-sız/-siz*)

Turkish maintains a strict functional distinction between two negation strategies. The analytic particle *değil* is the productive syntactic negation operator for adjectival predicates and applies compositionally to any truth-domain adjective (*doğru değil*, *gerçek değil*, *geçerli değil*). The privative suffix *-sız/-siz* does not function as a productive syntactic negation operator; it produces new lexical items (*asılsız*, *geçersiz*, *kanıtsız*) that are then used in the same attributive and predicative constructions as any other adjective. The two strategies are not interchangeable in the predicate slot: *asılsız* and *doğru değil* are not syntactic alternates of the same construction, though they may overlap semantically in some contexts. P4b explicitly notes this distinction and confirms that *değil* is the sole syntactic negation strategy for adjectival predicates.

### 2.4 Asymmetry in AGENTIVE NOMINALIZATION: deceptive vs. truth-telling roles

P4a §7 and P4b document a consistent asymmetry in the agentive nominalization domain. Lexicalized agentive nouns are attested on the deceptive/negative side: *yalancı* (liar), *sahteci* (forger), *taklitçi* (counterfeiter/imitator), *sahtekâr* (fraud/impostor). No lexicalized agentive noun for "truth-teller" or "verifier" as a personal role title is attested in common use. The concept is expressed periphrastically through participial relative clauses (*doğruyu söyleyen kişi* — "a person who tells the truth") or through character-evaluation adjectives in attributive position (*dürüst biri* — "an honest person"). *Doğrulayıcı* exists as a technical/adjectival form but is not a standard personal noun. This asymmetry is noted in P4a as a documented feature of the domain.

### 2.5 CAUSATION (character): no dedicated construction; substituted by attributive and idiomatic forms

No construction dedicated to "causing someone to have a truth-related character property" is attested. The closest available forms are: (a) character-evaluation adjectives in attributive position (*dürüst biri*, *güvenilir biri*), which describe rather than cause a character state; (b) the rights-fulfillment idiom *hakkını vermek* ("to do justice to / to give its due"), which expresses fulfillment of an obligation or standard rather than character causation in the strict sense. CAUSATION (factual) — causing a proposition or document to become true, valid, or legitimate — is well-attested through multiple constructions (*meşrulaştırmak*, *geçersiz kılmak*, *doğrulamak*). The character-causation sense is not structurally supported by a dedicated construction.

### 2.6 BECOME: morphological and periphrastic strategies in complementary distribution

The morphological *-laş-/-leş-* inchoative and the periphrastic *hale gelmek* / *olmak* strategies are in partial complementary distribution. *-laş-* is available only where it is already conventionalized on a given base (*meşrulaşmak*, *gerçekleşmek*); for bases where it is not conventionalized, the periphrastic forms are the default (*geçersiz hale gelmek*, *geçerli olmak*). The periphrastic forms are universally available. *Gerçekleşmek* has undergone semantic extension beyond the strict inchoative reading ("to become real") to the general meaning "to occur/materialize/take place," so its use as a BECOME operator in the truth domain is context-dependent.

### 2.7 CONCESSIVE PARTIAL-TRUTH as a distinct pragmatic operator

The construction *[locative NP] + bir doğruluk payı var* (P4a §17) functions as a pragmatically specialized operator for acknowledging partial truth in a claim. It does not map directly onto any of the ten canonical CLCA operators but is closest to a hedged or qualified ATTR. It is noted here because it represents a productive and frequent construction in argumentative and journalistic registers that has no single-word equivalent and is not reducible to standard ATTR or NEGATION.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

No structural gaps are identified. All ten canonical CLCA operator types have at least one attested construction in Turkish. The morphosyntactic resources (copular predication, inchoative and causative morphology, factitive verbalization, analytic negation, multiple nominalization strategies) are sufficient to express all operator types.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

| Gap | Description |
|---|---|
| No lexicalized agentive noun for "truth-teller" | The concept "one who tells the truth" as a personal role title has no common lexicalized form; expressed periphrastically (*doğruyu söyleyen kişi*) or through character adjectives (*dürüst biri*). *Doğrulayıcı* exists as a technical/adjectival form but not as a standard personal noun. |
| No single lexeme for prospective REVELATION | The sense "it has come to light / it emerged that X is true" has no dedicated single lexeme. *Çıkmak* covers retrospective discovery; discourse markers (*aslında*, *gerçek şu ki*) cover corrective framing; neither covers prospective revelation as a propositional operator. |
| *Yanlışlamak* restricted to academic register | The Popperian falsification sense of *yanlışlamak* is not available in general registers; *yalanlamak* covers denial/refutation in everyday use but is not a full semantic equivalent. |

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps are identified from the P4a/P4b data. All ten operator types are conceptually expressible in Turkish; the gaps identified above are lexical or register-based, not conceptual.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

| Asymmetry | Description |
|---|---|
| Agentive nominalization: deceptive side lexicalized, truth-telling side not | *Yalancı*, *sahteci*, *taklitçi*, *sahtekâr* are fully lexicalized common nouns; no equivalent lexicalized noun for "truth-teller," "verifier," or "honest agent" as a personal role title is attested. |
| DISCONFIRMATION: general-register form (*yalanlamak*) vs. academic form (*yanlışlamak*) | The positive side (VERIFICATION: *doğrulamak*, *teyit etmek*) has multiple fully [CORE] forms across registers; the disconfirmation side has a register split between the everyday denial verb (*yalanlamak*) and the philosophical falsification verb (*yanlışlamak*), with no single [CORE] form covering both senses. |
| Ability/potential nominalization: attested only for VERIFICATION and DISCONFIRMATION bases | *Doğrulanabilirlik* and *yanlışlanabilirlik* are attested; no parallel ability/potential nominalizations are documented for other truth-domain bases (e.g., *meşrulaşabilirlik*, *geçerlileşebilirlik*) in P4a. |
| CAUSATION (factual) well-attested; CAUSATION (character) not attested as a dedicated construction | Multiple constructions for causing propositions/documents to become true, valid, or legitimate; no dedicated construction for causing a person to have a truth-related character property. |
