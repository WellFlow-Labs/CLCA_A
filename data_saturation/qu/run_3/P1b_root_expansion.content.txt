# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note:** This expansion follows the same scope as P1 — primarily Southern Quechua (Cusco-Collao and Ayacucho-Chanka) with variety-specific notes where relevant. Attestation is drawn from available lexicographic, grammatical, and corpus documentation. Where a form's attestation is limited or its distribution uncertain, this is flagged in the epistemic status. The orthography follows the standard established in P1.

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1: cheqa**

- **Root form:** cheqa
- **P1 items participating:** 4 (cheqa, cheqaq, cheqaqta, cheqaqchay, cheqaqsiy — counting the base and all derivatives)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding truth, correctness, genuineness, and authenticity. It is the foundational morpheme for propositional truth, moral honesty, and the genuine nature of persons and things. It is the most productive root in the truth-evaluation domain in Southern Quechua.

---

**Root 2: llulla**

- **Root form:** llulla
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (llulla, llullakuy, llullaq)
- **Core meaning contribution:** The primary root encoding falsehood, deception, and dishonesty. It is the direct semantic antonym of cheqa in the truth-evaluation domain and is productive in moral and legal discourse.

---

**Root 3: chiqan / chiqap**

- **Root form:** chiqan (with related form chiqap)
- **P1 items participating:** 3 (chiqan, chiqanchay, chiqap)
- **Core meaning contribution:** Encodes straightness, correctness, exactness, and justice. It contributes a meaning of alignment with what is right or proper — spatial, moral, and procedural — and is productive in legal and ethical discourse.

**Note on selection:** These three roots are selected because they are the most morphologically productive in the P1 data and most central to the truth-evaluation domain. Other P1 items (yachay, iñiy, pantay, allin) are important but appear in P1 primarily as roots with fewer documented derivatives specifically within the truth-evaluation domain, or their derivational productivity is more general. They are not excluded from consideration but do not meet the threshold for root expansion here.

### ROOT 1: cheqa

---

**1.**
**Form:** cheqa
**Transliteration:** cheqa
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root; unanalyzable
**Natural English translation:** truth, true, real, genuine
**Context of use:** Used as a standalone nominal or adjectival predicate to assert that something is true or genuine. Appears in everyday speech, legal testimony, and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**2.**
**Form:** cheqaq
**Transliteration:** cheqaq
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqa + *-q* (nominalizing/adjectivizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** true, truthful, genuine, honest
**Context of use:** Adjectival and nominal form used to describe persons as honest or statements as true. Widely used in moral evaluation and in assertions about the accuracy of claims.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**3.**
**Form:** cheqaqta
**Transliteration:** cheqaqta
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** cheqaq + *-ta* (accusative/adverbial case)
**Natural English translation:** truly, really, honestly, in truth

**Context of use:** The adverbial form with the direct evidential clitic attached, used to assert that the speaker has direct knowledge that something is true. A common construction in Southern Quechua testimony and narrative.

### ROOT 2: llulla

---

**1.**
**Form:** llulla
**Transliteration:** llulla
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** Root; unanalyzable
**Natural English translation:** lie, false, liar, deceptive
**Context of use:** Used as a noun ("a lie"), adjective ("false, deceptive"), and in moral evaluation of persons and statements. Widely attested in Southern Quechua.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** llullakuy
**Transliteration:** llullakuy
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** llulla + *-kuy* (reflexive verbalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** to lie, to deceive, to be dishonest
**Context of use:** The primary verbal form for the act of lying or deceiving. Used in moral condemnation and in legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** llullaq
**Transliteration:** llullaq
**Literal morpheme breakdown:** llulla + *-q* (agentive suffix)
**Natural English translation:** liar, deceiver, dishonest person
**Context of use:** Agentive nominal characterizing a person as a habitual liar. Used in moral condemnation and in proverbs.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** llullakuq
**Transliteration:** llullakuq

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where their contribution appears to shift from the core truth-evaluation domain. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**cheqa kawsay** — The collocation of cheqa with kawsay ("life, living") extends the root into discourse about modes of existence and ways of living, not only about the truth-value of propositions or the honesty of persons.

**cheqa hatun** — The collocation of cheqa with hatun ("great, large") appears to use cheqa as an intensifier of degree or magnitude rather than as an evaluator of truth or genuineness.

**cheqa ñan / chiqan ñan** — Both cheqa and chiqan collocate with ñan ("path, road") in contexts that are spatial as well as moral, where the spatial meaning of straightness or directness is not clearly separable from the moral meaning of correctness.

**chiqan** — The root's primary spatial meaning ("straight") is not separable from its moral and procedural meanings in all contexts; the same form is used for a straight road and a just person.

**pura** (P1 item 17) — Although not a root expanded here, the P1 item pura shows a pattern where a term for purity or unmixedness is used in contexts of genuineness, suggesting a conceptual link between purity and authenticity that is distinct from propositional truth.

**cheqa Dios** — The collocation of cheqa with the Spanish loanword Dios places the root in specifically theological discourse, where it functions to assert ontological uniqueness or exclusive divine status rather than propositional truth.

**cheqa sonqo / llulla sonqo** — Both cheqa and llulla collocate with sonqo ("heart, inner self") in ways that locate truth and falsehood in the inner disposition of a person rather than in propositions or external conduct.

**ama llulla** — The root llulla appears in the traditional Andean moral triad (*ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qella*) as one of three foundational prohibitions, placing it in a domain of general moral duty rather than specifically epistemic or truth-evaluative discourse.

**llulla kawsay** — Parallel to cheqa kawsay, this collocation extends llulla into discourse about modes of existence and ways of living.

**chiqan kay** — The collocation of chiqan with kay ("to be, being") appears to nominalize the quality of correctness or justice as an abstract state, moving from adjectival evaluation to ontological or philosophical discourse.

**cheqaqpuni** — The attachment of the emphatic suffix *-puni* to cheqaq appears to use the truth root as a vehicle for degree or intensity marking, where the emphasis is on absolute certainty rather than on truth per se.
