## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

Greek shows a clear direct match for **ATTR**, **ADNOM**, **NEGATION**, and **NOMINALIZATION**.

For **BECOME**, the listed material shows that Greek often does not rely on a single dedicated inchoative operator across the whole truth domain. Instead, it commonly uses:
- **γίνομαι + ADJ/N**
- **αποδεικνύομαι/αποδείχθηκε + ADJ**
- lexical **αληθεύω**

Descriptively, this means that **BECOME** is frequently carried by forms that also express evidential establishment or later confirmation. In the attested examples, **Η υπογραφή αποδείχθηκε πλαστή.** functions both as a becoming/result reading and as a revelation/verification-type reading. So **REVELATION** does not appear as a separate dedicated construction in the provided material; it is effectively **substituted by BECOME/VERIFICATION-type predicates**, especially **αποδείχθηκε**.

For **VERIFICATION** and **DISCONFIRMATION**, the same constructional area is used:
- **αποδείχθηκε πλαστή**
- **αποδεικνύεται ψευδής**
- **αληθεύει**

So verification and disconfirmation are not separated by distinct canonical constructions in the data. They are expressed through a shared evidential/resultative pattern, with polarity determined lexically by the predicate adjective or verb:
- positive/confirmatory: **αληθεύει**
- negative/disconfirming: **ψευδής**, **πλαστή**

For **CAUSATION (factual)**, the notes explicitly state that there is **no general productive causative meaning** across the domain. Instead, Greek uses restricted lexical verbs for subtypes:
- **παραποιώ** for falsifying/tampering
- **πλαστογραφώ** for forging

So factual causation is only partially available and only through lexicalized subdomain verbs, not through a general construction.

For **CAUSATION (character)**, no corresponding construction is identified in the provided material. This is a stronger gap than the factual case, because even subtype-specific examples are not given for this operator.

There is also an asymmetry in agentive lexicalization noted in P4a/P4b:
- **ψεύτης** exists as a common lexical noun
- no common everyday single-word equivalent for “truth-teller” was identified

This is not one of the canonical operator categories, but it is a relevant asymmetry in the truth domain.

Negation is described as **analytic**, especially with **δεν είναι + ADJ/N**. No morphological negation pattern is listed for the canonical truth-domain constructions.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### Structural Gaps
- **CAUSATION (character)** — **MISSING (structural gap)**
- **REVELATION** as a separate dedicated construction — **SUBSTITUTED BY BECOME / VERIFICATION**

### Lexical Gaps
- No common everyday single-word equivalent identified for “truth-teller” — **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required**
- No general lexical causative covering the whole truth domain (“make true / make false / make authentic”) — only subtype-specific verbs are attested

### Conceptual Gaps
- No evidence in the provided material for a single general causative operator spanning truth, falsity, validity, authenticity, and sincerity
- No separate dedicated revelation operator distinct from evidential/resultative becoming/verification forms

### Asymmetric Gaps
- Strong asymmetry in agentive nominalization:
  - **ψεύτης** is available
  - positive counterpart “truth-teller” lacks a common single-word everyday equivalent
- Causation is asymmetrical:
  - negative/falsificatory subtypes are lexically available (**παραποιώ**, **πλαστογραφώ**)
  - no equally general positive causative counterpart is listed
- Verification/disconfirmation are asymmetrical only lexically, not constructionally:
  - same evidential/resultative constructional area is used
  - polarity comes from the lexical predicate (**αληθεύει** vs. **ψευδής / πλαστή**)

## 4. Canonicalized Operator Inventory

### Fully available
- **ATTR**
- **ADNOM**
- **NEGATION**
- **NOMINALIZATION**

### Available with substitution
- **BECOME** — often realized through **αποδεικνύομαι/αποδείχθηκε** and **αληθεύω**, not only simple inchoative **γίνομαι**
- **REVELATION** — **SUBSTITUTED BY BECOME / VERIFICATION**
- **VERIFICATION** — expressed through evidential/resultative predicates
- **DISCONFIRMATION** — expressed through the same evidential/resultative predicates and also by analytic negation

### Partially available
- **CAUSATION (factual)** — only through restricted lexical verbs such as **παραποιώ** and **πλαστογραφώ**; **NO COMMON EXPRESSION - circumlocution required** for a general operator

### Missing entirely
- **CAUSATION (character)** — **MISSING (structural gap)**

### Normalized operator behavior
- **Adjectival operators**: **αληθές, έγκυρη, σωστό, ψευδής, πλαστή**
- **Verbal operators**: **αληθεύει, αποδεικνύεται, αποδείχθηκε, παραποιώ, πλαστογραφώ**
- **Nominal operators**: **εγκυρότητα, ειλικρίνεια**
- **Adverbial/discourse operators**: **Στην πραγματικότητα**
- **Light/copular carrier**: **είναι** in attribution and discourse-framing
- **Analytic negation carrier**: **δεν** with **είναι**

Overall, Greek supports the canonical truth-domain inventory unevenly: core attributional, adnominal, negative, and nominal patterns are directly available; evidential/resultative predicates cover much of the space of becoming, revelation, verification, and disconfirmation; and causation is the main area of missingness or lexical restriction.
