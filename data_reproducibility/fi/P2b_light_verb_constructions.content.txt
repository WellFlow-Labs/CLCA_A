# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Finnish (fi)

## Methodology Note

This analysis documents **attested multi-word constructions** involving P1 lexical items. Finnish exhibits both fixed multi-word expressions and productive syntactic patterns. I focus on constructions that are conventionalized or show restricted compositionality, excluding freely compositional phrases and derivational morphology (covered in P2a).

---

## CONSTRUCTIONS WITH: tosi

### Construction 1: tosi juttu
**Base form:** tosi
**Example:** Se on tosi juttu.
**Transliteration:** se on tosi juttu
**Translation:** That's a true story / That's for real
**Usage:** Colloquial expression confirming something is genuine or actually happened. Common in informal speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 2: ihan tosi
**Base form:** tosi
**Example:** Se on ihan tosi.
**Transliteration:** se on ihan tosi
**Translation:** It's completely true / It's totally real
**Usage:** Emphatic construction using intensifier *ihan* "completely" with *tosi*. Very common in spoken Finnish.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 3: ei ole totta
**Base form:** tosi (via totuus → totta partitive)
**Example:** Se ei ole totta.
**Transliteration:** se ei ole totta
**Translation:** That's not true
**Usage:** Standard negation of truth claims using partitive case of *totuus*. Extremely common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 4: onko se totta
**Base form:** tosi (via totuus → totta)
**Example:** Onko se totta?
**Transliteration:** onko se totta
**Translation:** Is that true?
**Usage:** Standard question form for verifying truth. Very common.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 5: totta kai
**Base form:** tosi (via totuus → totta)
**Example:** Totta kai tulen.
**Transliteration:** totta kai tulen
**Translation:** Of course I'll come / Certainly
**Usage:** Fixed expression meaning "of course" or "certainly." Extremely common in affirmative responses.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 6: totta puhuen
**Base form:** tosi (via totuus → totta)
**Example:** Totta puhuen, en tiedä.
**Transliteration:** totta puhuen en tiedä
**Translation:** To tell the truth, I don't know / Truthfully
**Usage:** Fixed adverbial expression introducing honest admission. Common in both spoken and written Finnish.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 7: totta tosiaan
**Base form:** tosi (via totuus → totta + tosiaan)
**Example:** Totta tosiaan, unohdin sen.
**Transliteration:** totta tosiaan unohdin sen
**Translation:** Indeed, I forgot it / As a matter of fact
**Usage:** Emphatic fixed expression confirming or emphasizing truth. Somewhat formal or emphatic.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### Construction 8: ei pidä paikkaansa
**Base form:** pitää paikkansa (P1 item)
**Example:** Väite ei pidä paikkaansa.
**Transliteration:** väite ei pidä paikkaansa
**Translation:** The claim is not true / doesn't hold
**Usage:** Formal negation construction for rejecting claims. Common in formal discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## CROSS-CUTTING OBSERVATIONS

### High-frequency construction patterns:

**1. Inessive case constructions for "being right/wrong":**
- *olla oikeassa* "to be right"
- *olla väärässä* "to be wrong"
- These represent grammaticalized spatial metaphors (being "in" correctness/incorrectness)

**2. Complement clause constructions with epistemic predicates:**
- *on todennäköistä että* "it is probable that"
- *on tosiasia että* "it is a fact that"
- *väittää että* "to claim that"
- *on todettava että* "it must be noted that"

**3. Truth-object collocations:**
- *puhua totuutta/valhetta* "speak truth/lies"
- *vääristää totuutta* "distort truth"
- *paljastaa totuus/valhe* "reveal truth/lie"

**4. Information quality collocations:**
- *luotettava/virheellinen/harhaanjohtava/paikkansapitävä tieto* "reliable/erroneous/misleading/accurate information"
- *luotettava lähde* "reliable source"

**5. Emphatic truth constructions:**
- *ihan tosi* "completely true"
- *kiistaton tosiasia/todiste* "indisputable fact/evidence"
- *puhdas totuus* "pure truth"
- *koko totuus* "whole truth"

### Asymmetries:

**1. Productivity differences:**
- *tosi* and *totuus* generate many fixed expressions (*totta kai*, *totta puhuen*, *totuuden hetki*)
- *oikeellinen* and *todenmukaisuus* appear primarily in simple collocations
- *epätosi* lacks conventionalized multi-word constructions

**2. Register stratification:**
- Informal: *tosi juttu*, *ihan tosi*
- Formal: *paikkansapitävä tieto*, *virheellinen käsitys*, *voidaan todeta*
- Both: *olla oikeassa/väärässä*, *väittää että*

**3. Semantic specialization:**
- *väärä käsitys* (wrong impression) vs. *virheellinen käsitys* (erroneous understanding) - register difference
- *puhua valhetta* (tell lie) vs. *valehdella* (to lie) - construction vs. lexical verb
- *todistaa oikeaksi/vääräksi* (prove correct/false) - translative case construction

### Grammaticalization patterns:

**1. Case forms as adverbs:**
- *tosiasiassa* (inessive) "in fact"
- *tosissaan* (inessive + possessive) "seriously"
- *todisteiden perusteella* (genitive + postposition) "based on evidence"

**2. Participles in fixed expressions:**
- *totta puhuen* "truthfully speaking"
- *rehellisesti sanottuna* "honestly speaking"
- *tosiasioihin perustuva* "fact-based"

**3. Impersonal constructions:**
- *on todettava että* "it must be noted that"
- *voidaan todeta* "it can be stated"
- *on todennäköistä että* "it is probable that"

## NOTES ON METHODOLOGY

This analysis documents only attested multi-word constructions that show some degree of conventionalization or restricted compositionality. Excluded were:

- Freely compositional phrases (e.g., *tosi väite* "true claim" - simple modification)
- Derivational forms already covered in P2a
- Theoretical possibilities not attested in actual usage
- Highly specialized technical or legal constructions outside general discourse

The analysis reveals that Finnish truth-domain constructions cluster around:
1. Fixed idiomatic expressions (particularly with *tosi/totuus*)
2. Case-marked constructions (especially inessive for "being right/wrong")
3. Verb-object collocations (especially with information and truth nouns)
4. Complement clause constructions (with epistemic predicates)
5. Quality-noun collocations (especially with *tieto* "information")

The *tosi/totuus* root shows the greatest range of conventionalized constructions, consistent with its central role in Finnish truth-evaluation discourse.
