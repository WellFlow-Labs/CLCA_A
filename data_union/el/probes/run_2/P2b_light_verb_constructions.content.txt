# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Greek (el)

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions involving P1 items. The focus is on constructions that are not reducible to simple adjective-noun collocations already documented in P1b, and not derivational forms already documented in P2a. The boundary between a "collocation" (documented in P1b) and a "construction" (documented here) is drawn as follows: P1b documented fixed expressions and collocations built directly around the root words; P2b documents syntactically more complex or functionally distinct patterns, including predicative constructions, comparative constructions, negation patterns, modal constructions, and idiomatic multi-word units.

Where a construction was already documented in P1b, it is cross-referenced rather than re-documented in full.

Absence is recorded explicitly where no common construction was identified for a given P1 item or construction type.

## P1 Items — Compositional Constructions

---

### 1. αληθής (alithís)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** Η κατάθεσή του κρίθηκε αληθής.
3. **Transliteration:** I katáthesí tu krítike alithís.
4. **English translation:** His testimony was judged to be true.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative use of αληθής in a passive judicial construction. This pattern — [noun phrase] + κρίνεται/κρίθηκε + αληθής — is characteristic of legal and formal registers where a claim or statement is evaluated for truth-value by an authority.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** Ο ισχυρισμός αποδείχθηκε αληθής.
3. **Transliteration:** O ishirismós apodíhthike alithís.
4. **English translation:** The claim proved to be true.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction combining αληθής with αποδείχθηκε (was proven). Common in legal, journalistic, and scientific registers to indicate that a previously contested claim has been established as true.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** αληθής
2. **Example:** Δεν είναι αληθής η πληροφορία.
3. **Transliteration:** Den íne alithís i pliroforia.
4. **English translation:** The information is not true.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard negated predicative construction. Used in formal and journalistic registers to deny the truth of a piece of information. More formal than the equivalent construction with ψεύτικος or αληθινός.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

*Note: αληθής is primarily a predicative adjective in contemporary Greek. Attributive use (e.g., *αληθής ισχυρισμός*) is less common than predicative use and is largely confined to formal and legal registers. The more common attributive form in everyday speech is αληθινός.*

### 2. αλήθεια (alíthia)

*Core collocations and fixed expressions involving αλήθεια are extensively documented in P1b Root 2 #6–25. The constructions below are syntactically distinct patterns not covered there.*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Η αλήθεια βγαίνει πάντα στο φως.
3. **Transliteration:** I alíthia vgéni pánda sto fos.
4. **English translation:** The truth always comes to light.
5. **Usage notes:** A proverbial construction treating αλήθεια as a subject with agentive properties. Used in everyday moral discourse and journalism to assert the inevitability of truth being revealed. Functions as a cliché or proverb.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Κρύβει την αλήθεια.
3. **Transliteration:** Krívi tin alíthia.
4. **English translation:** He/she is hiding the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** Transitive construction with αλήθεια as direct object of κρύβω (to hide). Very common in everyday speech, journalism, and legal contexts to accuse someone of concealing true information.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Αποκάλυψε την αλήθεια.
3. **Transliteration:** Apokálipse tin alíthia.
4. **English translation:** He/she revealed the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** Transitive construction with αλήθεια as direct object of αποκαλύπτω (to reveal, uncover). Common across all registers. The verb αποκαλύπτω etymologically echoes the Greek concept of truth as unconcealment (αλήθεια), making this a semantically resonant pairing.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Δεν αντέχει την αλήθεια.
3. **Transliteration:** Den andéhi tin alíthia.
4. **English translation:** He/she cannot bear the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction treating αλήθεια as an object of emotional endurance. Used in everyday and literary registers to describe a person's psychological resistance to confronting true but unpleasant facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Η αλήθεια είναι πάντα σχετική.
3. **Transliteration:** I alíthia íne pánda shetikí.
4. **English translation:** Truth is always relative.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction used in philosophical, cultural, and everyday discourse to assert relativism about truth. Common in informal philosophical discussion and journalistic commentary.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Δεν υπάρχει μία και μόνη αλήθεια.
3. **Transliteration:** Den ipárhi mía ke móni alíthia.
4. **English translation:** There is no single truth. / There is not one and only one truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction used in philosophical, political, and cultural discourse to deny the existence of a single authoritative truth. Common in discussions of pluralism, relativism, and post-truth discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 7**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Έχει το θάρρος της αλήθειας.
3. **Transliteration:** Éhi to tháros tis alíthias.
4. **English translation:** He/she has the courage of truth. / He/she has the courage to tell the truth.
5. **Usage notes:** A construction pairing αλήθεια with θάρρος (courage) in a genitive relationship. Used in moral and journalistic discourse to praise someone who speaks truth despite personal risk or social pressure.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

1. **Base Form:** αλήθεια
2. **Example:** Στο όνομα της αλήθειας
3. **Transliteration:** Sto ónoma tis alíthias
4. **English translation:** In the name of truth
5. **Usage notes:** A prepositional construction used in rhetorical and moral discourse to invoke truth as a justifying principle for an action or statement. Common in journalism, political speech, and formal argumentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 3. αληθινός (alithinós)

*Core collocations involving αληθινός are documented in P1b Root 2 #13–16.*

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** αληθινός
2. **Example:** Αυτό είναι αληθινό.
3. **Transliteration:** Aftó íne alithinó.
4. **English translation:** This is true. / This is real.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard predicative construction. The most common everyday way of asserting that something is true or real, more colloquial than the equivalent with αληθής. Used across all registers and age groups.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** αληθινός
2. **Example:** Είναι πιο αληθινός από ποτέ.
3. **Transliteration:** Íne pio alithinós apó poté.
4. **English translation:** He is more genuine than ever.
5. **Usage notes:** Comparative construction with αληθινός. Used in everyday and journalistic registers to evaluate a person's authenticity or sincerity at a particular moment. The comparative degree is formed periphrastically with πιο.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** αληθινός
2. **Example:** Τίποτα δεν είναι αληθινό σε αυτή την ιστορία.
3. **Transliteration:** Típota den íne alithinó se aftí tin istoría.
4. **English translation:** Nothing in this story is true.
5. **Usage notes:** Negated predicative construction with universal quantifier. Used in everyday and journalistic registers to deny the truth of an entire narrative or account.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 4. γνήσιος (gnísios)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** γνήσιος
2. **Example:** Πρόκειται για γνήσιο έργο του Παρθένη.
3. **Transliteration:** Prókite ya gnísio érgo tu Parthéni.
4. **English translation:** This is a genuine work by Parthenis.
5. **Usage notes:** Attributive construction used in art authentication and philological contexts to assert the genuine origin of a work. The construction πρόκειται για + γνήσιο + [work] + [genitive of author] is standard in art criticism and auction contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** γνήσιος
2. **Example:** Αμφισβητείται η γνησιότητά του.
3. **Transliteration:** Amfisvitiíte i gnisiótitá tu.
4. **English translation:** Its authenticity is disputed.
5. **Usage notes:** Passive predicative construction combining γνησιότητα (derived from γνήσιος) with αμφισβητείται. Standard in art, philological, and legal contexts when the genuine origin of an object or document is under challenge.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** γνήσιος
2. **Example:** Δεν είναι γνήσιο — είναι απομίμηση.
3. **Transliteration:** Den íne gnísio — íne apomímisi.
4. **English translation:** It is not genuine — it is an imitation.
5. **Usage notes:** Contrastive construction opposing γνήσιος to απομίμηση (imitation). Common in commercial, legal, and everyday contexts when distinguishing authentic products from counterfeits or copies.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** γνήσιος
2. **Example:** Πιστοποιείται ως γνήσιο αντίγραφο.
3. **Transliteration:** Pistopoiíte o̱s gnísio andígrafo.
4. **English translation:** It is certified as a true copy.
5. **Usage notes:** A standard administrative and legal construction. *Γνήσιο αντίγραφο* is the fixed term for a certified true copy of a document in Greek administrative practice. The construction πιστοποιείται ως γνήσιο αντίγραφο appears on official certifications.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

### 5. αυθεντικός (afthentikós)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** αυθεντικός
2. **Example:** Αμφισβητείται η αυθεντικότητα της υπογραφής.
3. **Transliteration:** Amfisvitiíte i afthentikótita tis ipografís.
4. **English translation:** The authenticity of the signature is disputed.
5. **Usage notes:** Passive predicative construction combining αυθεντικότητα with αμφισβητείται. Standard in legal and forensic contexts when the genuine origin of a signature or document is under challenge.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** αυθεντικός
2. **Example:** Είναι αυθεντικός στις εκφράσεις του.
3. **Transliteration:** Íne afthentikós stis ekfráseis tu.
4. **English translation:** He is authentic in his expressions.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction with a prepositional complement specifying the domain of authenticity. Used in psychological, journalistic, and everyday registers to evaluate a person's sincerity or lack of pretense in a specific domain.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** αυθεντικός
2. **Example:** Το έργο πιστοποιήθηκε ως αυθεντικό.
3. **Transliteration:** To érgo pistopoiíthike o̱s afthentikó.
4. **English translation:** The work was certified as authentic.
5. **Usage notes:** Passive construction combining αυθεντικός with πιστοποιήθηκε (was certified). Standard in art authentication, archival, and legal contexts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** αυθεντικός
2. **Example:** Προσπαθεί να είναι αυθεντικός.
3. **Transliteration:** Prospathí na íne afthentikós.
4. **English translation:** He tries to be authentic.
5. **Usage notes:** Modal construction with the subjunctive να + είναι. Used in psychological and everyday discourse about personal identity and self-presentation. Common in discussions of authenticity as a personal value.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 6. ακριβής (akrivís)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Example:** Να είμαστε ακριβείς: δεν υπάρχουν αποδείξεις.
3. **Transliteration:** Na ímaste akriveís: den ipárhun apodíksis.
4. **English translation:** To be precise: there is no evidence.
5. **Usage notes:** A discourse-framing construction using the subjunctive να + είμαστε ακριβείς to introduce a corrective or clarifying statement. Functions as a precision marker in formal, journalistic, and academic discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Example:** Η περιγραφή δεν είναι ακριβής.
3. **Transliteration:** I perigrafi den íne akrivís.
4. **English translation:** The description is not accurate.
5. **Usage notes:** Standard negated predicative construction. Used across all registers to indicate that a description, account, or measurement does not correspond closely to the facts.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Example:** Κατά το δυνατόν ακριβής
3. **Transliteration:** Katá to dinatón akrivís
4. **English translation:** As accurate as possible
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed adverbial construction used in formal, legal, and technical registers to qualify a claim of accuracy. Appears in translation briefs, scientific reports, and legal documents.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ακριβής
2. **Example:** Για να είμαι ακριβής…
3. **Transliteration:** Ya na íme akrivís…
4. **English translation:** To be precise… / To be exact…
5. **Usage notes:** A first-person discourse marker used to introduce a more precise or corrected formulation. Common in formal speech, academic writing, and journalism. Functions as a hedging or precision-signaling device.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 7. σωστός (sostós)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** σωστός
2. **Example:** Έχεις σωστό.
3. **Transliteration:** Éhis sostó.
4. **English translation:** You are right. / You have it right.
5. **Usage notes:** A colloquial construction using σωστό as a nominal complement of έχω. Equivalent to έχεις δίκιο in some registers. Used in informal everyday speech to affirm that someone's statement or position is correct.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** σωστός
2. **Example:** Δεν είναι σωστό αυτό που λες.
3. **Transliteration:** Den íne sostó aftó pu les.
4. **English translation:** What you are saying is not correct.
5. **Usage notes:** Negated predicative construction with a relative clause complement. Very common in everyday speech to dispute a claim or statement. More colloquial than the equivalent with ακριβής or ορθός.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** σωστός
2. **Example:** Αν κατάλαβα σωστά…
3. **Transliteration:** An katálava sostá…
4. **English translation:** If I understood correctly…
5. **Usage notes:** A conditional construction using the adverb σωστά as a hedging device. Very common in everyday conversation and formal discourse to check comprehension or introduce a paraphrase. Functions as a discourse management device.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** σωστός
2. **Example:** Κάνω σωστά;
3. **Transliteration:** Káno̱ sostá?
4. **English translation:** Am I doing it right? / Is this correct?
5. **Usage notes:** Interrogative construction using the adverb σωστά. Extremely common in everyday speech to request confirmation that an action or procedure is being performed correctly.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

1. **Base Form:** σωστός
2. **Example:** Είναι σωστός άνθρωπος.
3. **Transliteration:** Íne sostós ánthropos.
4. **English translation:** He is a decent/upright person.
5. **Usage notes:** Attributive construction where σωστός is applied to a person to indicate moral uprightness or reliability rather than factual correctness. This is a distinct semantic use of σωστός in the domain of character evaluation. Common in everyday speech.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

### 8. ορθός (orthós)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** ορθός
2. **Example:** Κατά την ορθή ερμηνεία του νόμου…
3. **Transliteration:** Katá tin orthí ermineía tu nómu…
4. **English translation:** According to the correct interpretation of the law…
5. **Usage notes:** A prepositional construction used in legal discourse to invoke the correct or authoritative interpretation of a legal text. The phrase κατά την ορθή ερμηνεία is a standard formula in legal argumentation and judicial reasoning.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** ορθός
2. **Example:** Ορθώς εκτιμά την κατάσταση.
3. **Transliteration:** Orthós ektimá tin katástasi.
4. **English translation:** He/she correctly assesses the situation.
5. **Usage notes:** Adverbial construction using ορθώς to modify a verb of judgment or assessment. Used in formal, legal, and journalistic registers to affirm the correctness of an evaluation. More formal than the equivalent with σωστά.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** ορθός
2. **Example:** Δεν είναι ορθό να…
3. **Transliteration:** Den íne orthó na…
4. **English translation:** It is not correct to… / It is not right to…
5. **Usage notes:** Impersonal negated construction with subjunctive complement. Used in formal and normative registers to indicate that a procedure, action, or claim does not conform to correct standards. Common in legal, administrative, and ethical discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

1. **Base Form:** ορθός
2. **Example:** Υπό την ορθή έννοια του όρου
3. **Transliteration:** Ipó tin orthí énnoia tu óru
4. **English translation:** In the correct sense of the term
5. **Usage notes:** A prepositional construction used in philosophical, legal, and academic discourse to specify that a term is being used in its proper or correct sense. Functions as a precision marker in formal argumentation.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος

3. **Transliteration:** I psífos krítike éngiri.
4. **English translation:** The vote was ruled valid.
5. **Usage notes:** Passive predicative construction with κρίθηκε (was ruled/judged). Standard in electoral and legal contexts when an authority determines the validity of a vote, ballot, or procedural act.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** έγκυρος

3. **Transliteration:** Den theoríte pléon éngiro.
4. **English translation:** It is no longer considered valid.

6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

2. **Example:** Έγκυρο έως…
3. **Transliteration:** Éngiro éo̱s…
4. **English translation:** Valid until…
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed administrative formula appearing on documents, identity cards, and certificates to indicate the expiry date of validity. Extremely common in official documents.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Construction 3**

3. **Transliteration:** Den íne nómimon na…
4. **English translation:** It is not legal to…
5. **Usage notes:** Impersonal negated construction with subjunctive complement. Used across all registers to indicate that an action is prohibited by law. Very common in everyday speech, journalism, and legal discourse.

**Construction 4**

### 11. νόθος (nóthos)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** νόθος
2. **Example:** Κυκλοφορούν νόθα προϊόντα στην αγορά.
3. **Transliteration:** Kikloforún nótha proïónda stin agorá.
4. **English translation:** Adulterated/counterfeit products are circulating in the market.
5. **Usage notes:** Construction with νόθος in attributive position combined with a verb of circulation. Common in consumer protection journalism and regulatory discourse to warn about adulterated or counterfeit goods.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** νόθος
2. **Example:** Αποδείχθηκε ότι το κείμενο είναι νόθο.
3. **Transliteration:** Apodíhthike óti to kímeno íne nótho.
4. **English translation:** It was proven that the text is spurious.
5. **Usage notes:** Predicative construction used in philological and historical scholarship to assert that a text is not of genuine authorship. The construction αποδείχθηκε ότι + [text] + είναι νόθο is standard in academic philological discourse.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** νόθος
2. **Example:** Νόθευση εκλογικών αποτελεσμάτων
3. **Transliteration:** Nóthefsi eklogikón apotelesmáton
4. **English translation:** Falsification of election results
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed collocation using the derived noun νόθευση. Already noted in P2a under νόθος Derived Form 3. Cross-reference: P2a #11 Derived Form 3. Included here as the most common multi-word construction in the νόθος domain.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

*Note: The use of νόθος to refer to illegitimate birth (νόθο τέκνο, illegitimate child) is attested but archaic in contemporary usage, having been largely displaced by legal and social changes. It is not documented here as a productive contemporary construction.*

### 12. πλαστός (plastós)

---

**Construction 1**

1. **Base Form:** πλαστός
2. **Example:** Συνελήφθη με πλαστά έγγραφα.
3. **Transliteration:** Sinelífthike me plastá éngrafa.
4. **English translation:** He/she was arrested with forged documents.
5. **Usage notes:** Attributive construction in a prepositional phrase. The collocation πλαστά έγγραφα (forged documents) is the most common use of πλαστός in journalistic and legal reporting. The construction με πλαστά έγγραφα is a standard phrase in crime reporting.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

1. **Base Form:** πλαστός
2. **Example:** Κατηγορείται για χρήση πλαστών εγγράφων.
3. **Transliteration:** Katigoriíte ya hrísi plastón engrávon.
4. **English translation:** He/she is charged with use of forged documents.
5. **Usage notes:** A legal construction specifying the criminal charge of using forged documents. χρήση πλαστών εγγράφων is a standard legal phrase in Greek criminal law, naming a specific offense.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

1. **Base Form:** πλαστός
2. **Example:** Πλαστή ταυτότητα
3. **Transliteration:** Plastí taftótita
4. **English translation:** Forged identity card / false identity
5. **Usage notes:** A fixed attributive collocation. Used in legal and journalistic contexts to refer to a forged identity document or a false identity assumed by a person. Very common in crime reporting.
6. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**
