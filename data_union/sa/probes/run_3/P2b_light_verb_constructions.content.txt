# CLCA Protocol v3.4 — P2b Compositional Constructions
# Language: Sanskrit (sa)

Transliteration standard: IAST, consistent with P1, P1b, and P2a.

---

## Preliminary Note on Scope and Method

This step documents attested multi-word constructions — periphrastic, idiomatic, and syntactically-driven — that involve or extend the P1 lexical items. The following distinctions govern inclusion:

**Included:** Constructions where the P1 item participates in a fixed or semi-fixed multi-word expression that cannot be fully predicted from the sum of its parts, or where a periphrastic pattern is conventionally associated with the item's semantic domain.

**Excluded:** Derivational forms already documented in P2a; free compositional phrases that are simply the P1 item used in a sentence with no special constructional status.

**On the nature of Sanskrit multi-word constructions:** Sanskrit is a highly synthetic language with extensive compounding (*samāsa*) and derivational morphology. Many semantic functions that other languages express periphrastically are handled in Sanskrit through compounding or suffixation. This means that the inventory of genuinely periphrastic or idiomatic multi-word constructions is smaller relative to the derivational inventory than would be the case in, for example, an analytic language. Absence of constructions for certain items reflects this structural fact and is recorded explicitly.

**Asymmetry is preserved throughout.** Some items generate several constructions; others generate none or very few. No constructions have been generated to fill expected semantic roles.

## P1 Item 1 — सत्य / *satya*

---

**Construction 1**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्यं वद
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ vada
**English Translation:** Speak the truth.
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic imperative construction, widely attested in *Dharmaśāstra* and didactic literature as a normative injunction. The phrase appears in the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* (1.11.1) as part of the *śikṣāvallī*'s instructions to the graduating student: *satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara* ("Speak truth; walk in *dharma*"). It functions as a fixed ethical formula rather than a free compositional phrase.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 2**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्यं ब्रूयात्
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ brūyāt
**English Translation:** One should speak the truth.
**Usage Notes:** An optative construction expressing normative obligation, attested in *Manusmṛti* (4.138) and related *Dharmaśāstra* literature. The full verse (*satyaṃ brūyāt priyaṃ brūyāt, na brūyāt satyam apriyam*) is a well-known ethical maxim: "One should speak the truth; one should speak what is pleasant; one should not speak the truth if it is unpleasant." The construction is formulaic and widely cited.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 3**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्यं नास्ति परो धर्मः
**Transliteration:** satyaṃ nāsti paro dharmaḥ
**English Translation:** There is no higher *dharma* than truth.
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic maxim attested in the *Mahābhārata* and widely cited in ethical literature. Functions as a fixed proverbial expression asserting the supremacy of truth among moral values. The construction *nāsti paro X* ("there is no higher X") is itself a formulaic pattern in Sanskrit ethical literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 4**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्यमेव जयते
**Transliteration:** satyam eva jayate
**English Translation:** Truth alone conquers.
**Usage Notes:** Attested in the *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* (3.1.6); adopted as the national motto of the Republic of India. The *eva* (emphatic particle, "alone, indeed") is integral to the construction and cannot be omitted without changing the meaning. Documented in P1b as a fixed expression; included here because it is a multi-word construction rather than a derivational form.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 5**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्ये प्रतिष्ठितः
**Transliteration:** satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ
**English Translation:** Established in truth; grounded in truth.
**Usage Notes:** A participial construction (*pratiṣṭhita*, past passive participle of *prati-* + root *sthā-*, "to stand") with the locative of *satya*, used in philosophical and devotional literature to characterize a person or state as firmly grounded in truth. Appears in Vedāntic and epic literature as a characterization of the sage or the virtuous person.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 6**

**Base Form:** सत्य (*satya*)
**Example:** सत्यात् न प्रमदितव्यम्
**Transliteration:** satyāt na pramaditavyam
**English Translation:** One must not deviate from truth.
**Usage Notes:** A gerundive construction expressing prohibition, attested in the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* (1.11.1) as part of the same passage as *satyaṃ vada*. The gerundive *pramaditavyam* (from root *mad-* with prefix *pra-*, "to be negligent, to deviate") with the ablative of *satya* and the negation *na* forms a fixed injunctive formula.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 2 — ऋत / *ṛta*

---

**Construction 7**

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
**Example:** ऋतं च सत्यं च
**Transliteration:** ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca
**English Translation:** Both *ṛta* and *satya*; cosmic order and truth.
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic dvandva-like coordination attested in the *Ṛgveda* and *Atharvaveda*, pairing the two primary Vedic truth-terms. The coordination is not merely additive but marks a conventional pairing that appears repeatedly in Vedic literature; the two terms are treated as complementary aspects of a single domain. Appears in *Ṛgveda* 10.190.1 (*ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāt tapaso 'dhyajāyata*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 8**

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
**Example:** ऋतस्य पन्थाः
**Transliteration:** ṛtasya panthāḥ
**English Translation:** The path of *ṛta*; the way of cosmic order.
**Usage Notes:** A genitive construction attested in the *Ṛgveda* to designate the path or way that accords with cosmic order, often in the context of ritual correctness or moral conduct. The phrase functions as a conventional expression for the right way of acting or proceeding.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 9**

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
**Example:** ऋतेन ऋतम् अपिहितम्
**Transliteration:** ṛtena ṛtam apihitam
**English Translation:** By *ṛta* is *ṛta* covered/concealed.
**Usage Notes:** Attested in the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (5.15.1) and *Īśā Upaniṣad* (15), where the sun's disk is described as covered by *ṛta*. The construction uses the instrumental of *ṛta* with the past passive participle *apihita* (covered, from *api-* + root *dhā-*). It is a fixed liturgical and philosophical formula rather than a free compositional phrase.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 10**

**Base Form:** ऋत (*ṛta*)
**Example:** ऋतस्य योनिः
**Transliteration:** ṛtasya yoniḥ
**English Translation:** The womb/source of *ṛta*; the origin of cosmic order.
**Usage Notes:** A genitive construction attested in Vedic literature to designate the source or origin of cosmic order, typically applied to a deity (especially Varuṇa or Mitra) or to the sacrificial fire. Functions as a conventional epithet-formula in Vedic hymns.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## P1 Item 3 — अनृत / *anṛta*

---

**Construction 11**

**Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)
**Example:** अनृतं न वदेत्
**Transliteration:** anṛtaṃ na vadet
**English Translation:** One should not speak falsehood.
**Usage Notes:** An optative construction expressing prohibition, attested in *Dharmaśāstra* literature as a normative injunction against false speech. The construction is parallel to *satyaṃ brūyāt* (Construction 2) and appears in similar ethical contexts. The optative *vadet* (from root *vad-*) with negation *na* is the standard form for such prohibitions.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 12**

**Base Form:** अनृत (*anṛta*)
**Example:** अनृतेन परिवृत्तः
**Transliteration:** anṛtena parivṛttaḥ
**English Translation:** Surrounded by / enveloped in falsehood.
**Usage Notes:** A participial construction using the instrumental of *anṛta* with *parivṛtta* (past passive participle of *pari-* + root *vṛt-*, "to surround, to envelop"), used in Vedic and post-Vedic literature to characterize a person or state as encompassed by falsehood or disorder. Appears in contexts describing the condition of one who has departed from *ṛta*.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## P1 Item 4 — असत्य / *asatya*

---

**Construction 13**

**Base Form:** असत्य (*asatya*)
**Example:** असत्यं वदति
**Transliteration:** asatyaṃ vadati
**English Translation:** He/she speaks falsehood; he/she lies.
**Usage Notes:** A standard periphrastic construction for the act of lying, using the accusative of *asatya* with the verb *vad-* (to speak). While compositionally transparent, this construction is the conventional way of expressing the act of lying in *Dharmaśāstra* and legal literature, where it appears in discussions of witness disqualification and the sin of false speech.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 14**

**Base Form:** असत्य (*asatya*)
**Example:** असत्ये स्थितः
**Transliteration:** asatye sthitaḥ
**English Translation:** Established in falsehood; standing in untruth.
**Usage Notes:** A participial construction using the locative of *asatya* with *sthita* (past passive participle of root *sthā-*, "to stand"), used in ethical literature to characterize a person as habitually committed to falsehood. Appears as the negative counterpart of *satye pratiṣṭhitaḥ* (Construction 5).
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## P1 Item 5 — प्रमाण / *pramāṇa*

---

**Construction 15**

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Example:** प्रमाणं किम्?
**Transliteration:** pramāṇaṃ kim?
**English Translation:** What is the valid means of knowledge (here)? / What is the evidence?
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic interrogative construction used in philosophical debate and commentary to challenge an interlocutor to specify the epistemic basis for a claim. Widely attested in *Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*, and Buddhist philosophical literature as a standard move in dialectical exchange (*vāda*).
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 16**

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Example:** प्रमाणेन सिद्धम्
**Transliteration:** pramāṇena siddham
**English Translation:** Established by valid means of knowledge; proven by evidence.
**Usage Notes:** A participial construction using the instrumental of *pramāṇa* with *siddha* (past passive participle of root *sidh-*, "to be accomplished, to be established"), used in philosophical and legal literature to assert that a claim has been established through a recognized valid means of knowledge. A standard formula in philosophical argumentation.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 17**

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Example:** न तत्र प्रमाणम् अस्ति
**Transliteration:** na tatra pramāṇam asti
**English Translation:** There is no valid means of knowledge for that; there is no evidence for that.
**Usage Notes:** A standard negative construction used in philosophical debate to deny that a claim has any epistemic support. The construction *na ... pramāṇam asti* is a formulaic way of rejecting an opponent's position as epistemically unwarranted; widely attested in commentarial literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 18**

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Example:** प्रमाणतः सिद्धम्
**Transliteration:** pramāṇataḥ siddham
**English Translation:** Established from/by the valid means of knowledge.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the ablative/adverbial form *pramāṇataḥ* with *siddha*, used in philosophical literature to assert that something has been established through proper epistemic procedure. The ablative *-taḥ* form here functions adverbially, indicating the source or basis of the establishment.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 19**

**Base Form:** प्रमाण (*pramāṇa*)
**Example:** अस्माकं वेदः प्रमाणम्
**Transliteration:** asmākaṃ vedaḥ pramāṇam
**English Translation:** For us, the Veda is the valid means of knowledge / the authority.
**Usage Notes:** A predicative construction using *pramāṇa* as a predicate nominal, attested in *Mīmāṃsā* and *Dharmaśāstra* literature to assert the authoritative status of a source. The construction *X pramāṇam* (X is the *pramāṇa*) is a standard way of asserting epistemic or normative authority.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## P1 Item 6 — प्रमा / *pramā*

---

**Construction 20**

**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
**Example:** प्रमा जायते
**Transliteration:** pramā jāyate
**English Translation:** Valid cognition arises / is produced.
**Usage Notes:** A standard construction in *Nyāya* epistemological literature describing the production of valid cognition as the result of a successful *pramāṇa*. The verb *jāyate* (from root *jan-*, "to be born, to arise") is the conventional verb used for the arising of a cognition in this literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 21**

**Base Form:** प्रमा (*pramā*)
**Example:** प्रमायाः करणं प्रमाणम्
**Transliteration:** pramāyāḥ karaṇaṃ pramāṇam
**English Translation:** The instrument of valid cognition is the *pramāṇa*.
**Usage Notes:** A definitional construction attested in *Nyāya* literature (cf. *Nyāyasūtra* 1.1.1 and commentaries) that defines *pramāṇa* as the instrument (*karaṇa*) that produces *pramā*. The genitive *pramāyāḥ* with *karaṇam* is a standard technical formula in the epistemological literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 7 — यथार्थ / *yathārtha*

---

**Construction 22**

**Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
**Example:** यथार्थं ज्ञानम्
**Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ jñānam
**English Translation:** Knowledge that is as the object is; accurate cognition.
**Usage Notes:** A standard attributive construction in *Nyāya* epistemological literature, where *yathārtha* functions as an adjective modifying *jñāna* (cognition/knowledge). This is the conventional way of characterizing a cognition as veridical or accurate in the *Nyāya* tradition; the construction appears in definitional contexts.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 23**

**Base Form:** यथार्थ (*yathārtha*)
**Example:** यथार्थं वक्तुम्
**Transliteration:** yathārthaṃ vaktum
**English Translation:** To speak accurately; to state things as they are.
**Usage Notes:** An infinitive construction using *yathārtha* as an adverb with the infinitive of *vac-* (to speak), used in philosophical and legal contexts to characterize the obligation or intention to describe things accurately. Appears in discussions of testimony and witness obligation.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## P1 Item 9 — धर्म / *dharma*

---

**Construction 24**

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Example:** धर्मं चर
**Transliteration:** dharmaṃ cara
**English Translation:** Walk in *dharma*; practice *dharma*.
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic imperative construction attested in the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* (1.11.1) as part of the same passage as *satyaṃ vada* (Construction 1): *satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara*. The verb *car-* (to walk, to practice, to follow) with the accusative of *dharma* is the conventional expression for the practice or observance of *dharma*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 25**

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Example:** धर्मो रक्षति रक्षितः
**Transliteration:** dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ
**English Translation:** *Dharma* protects the one who protects it.
**Usage Notes:** A well-known maxim from *Manusmṛti* (8.15), widely cited in *Dharmaśāstra* and popular ethical literature. The construction uses the present tense of *rakṣ-* (to protect) with the past passive participle *rakṣita* (protected) in a chiastic structure. It functions as a fixed proverbial expression.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 26**

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Example:** धर्मे स्थितः
**Transliteration:** dharme sthitaḥ
**English Translation:** Established in *dharma*; standing firm in duty.
**Usage Notes:** A participial construction using the locative of *dharma* with *sthita* (past passive participle of root *sthā-*), used in epic and *Dharmaśāstra* literature to characterize a person as firmly committed to *dharma*. A conventional expression of moral steadfastness.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 27**

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Example:** धर्मात् न चलति
**Transliteration:** dharmāt na calati
**English Translation:** He/she does not deviate from *dharma*.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the ablative of *dharma* with the negated verb *cal-* (to move, to deviate), used in epic and ethical literature to characterize a person as unwavering in their adherence to *dharma*. The ablative of separation with *cal-* is the conventional way of expressing deviation from a norm.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 28**

**Base Form:** धर्म (*dharma*)
**Example:** यतो धर्मस्ततो जयः
**Transliteration:** yato dharmas tato jayaḥ
**English Translation:** Where there is *dharma*, there is victory.
**Usage Notes:** A well-known maxim from the *Mahābhārata*, used as a proverbial expression asserting the connection between righteousness and success. The correlative construction *yato ... tato* ("where ... there") is a standard Sanskrit formula for proverbial statements; the phrase functions as a fixed expression.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 10 — अधर्म / *adharma*

---

**Construction 29**

**Base Form:** अधर्म (*adharma*)
**Example:** अधर्मेण वर्धते
**Transliteration:** adharmeṇa vardhate
**English Translation:** He/she prospers through *adharma* (wrongdoing).
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the instrumental of *adharma* with the verb *vṛdh-* (to grow, to prosper), attested in *Manusmṛti* (4.174) in the context of a longer passage about the apparent but ultimately illusory prosperity of the unrighteous. The instrumental of *adharma* with *vṛdh-* is a conventional expression in this ethical context.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 30**

**Base Form:** अधर्म (*adharma*)
**Example:** अधर्माद् भयम्
**Transliteration:** adharmād bhayam
**English Translation:** Fear from *adharma*; danger arising from unrighteousness.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the ablative of *adharma* with *bhaya* (fear, danger), used in *Dharmaśāstra* and epic literature to express the consequence of unrighteous conduct. The ablative of source with *bhaya* is a standard Sanskrit construction for expressing the origin of fear or danger.
**Epistemic Status:** [MARGINAL]

## P1 Item 11 — सत् / *sat*

---

**Construction 31**

**Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
**Example:** सदेव सोम्येदम् अग्र आसीत्
**Transliteration:** sad eva somyedam agra āsīt
**English Translation:** In the beginning, dear one, this was being alone / only *sat* existed.
**Usage Notes:** The opening of *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 6.2.1, one of the most cited passages in Vedāntic literature. The construction *sad eva ... āsīt* ("only *sat* was") is a fixed philosophical formula asserting the primacy of being; it is cited as a fixed expression in Vedāntic commentary and debate.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 32**

**Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
**Example:** सतः किम् उत्पद्यते?
**Transliteration:** sataḥ kim utpadyate?
**English Translation:** What arises from being? / What is produced from what exists?
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic interrogative construction used in Vedāntic and *Sāṃkhya* philosophical debate to raise the question of causation and the relationship between being and its products. The genitive/ablative *sataḥ* with *utpadyate* (from *ut-* + root *pad-*, "to arise") is a standard formula in discussions of *sat-kārya-vāda*.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 33**

**Base Form:** सत् (*sat*)
**Example:** असतो मा सद्गमय
**Transliteration:** asato mā sad gamaya
**English Translation:** Lead me from the unreal to the real.
**Usage Notes:** From the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (1.3.28), one of the three great prayers (*śānti-mantra*). The construction uses the ablative of *asat* and the accusative of *sat* with the causative imperative *gamaya* (from root *gam-*, "to go"; causative "to lead"). It is a fixed liturgical formula, widely cited in Vedāntic literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

## P1 Item 12 — असत् / *asat*

---

**Construction 34**

**Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)
**Example:** असतो मा सद्गमय
**Transliteration:** asato mā sad gamaya
**English Translation:** Lead me from the unreal to the real.
**Usage Notes:** The same construction as Construction 33, documented here from the perspective of *asat* as the base form. The ablative *asataḥ* (contracted to *asato* before *mā*) is the starting point of the movement expressed in the prayer; the construction is inseparable from its *sat/asat* pairing.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 35**

**Base Form:** असत् (*asat*)
**Example:** असत् न विद्यते
**Transliteration:** asat na vidyate
**English Translation:** The unreal does not exist; non-being is not found.
**Usage Notes:** A construction attested in the *Bhagavad Gītā* (2.16: *nāsato vidyate bhāvo*) asserting the non-existence of the unreal. The negated passive *na vidyate* (from root *vid-*, "to find, to exist") is the conventional philosophical formula for asserting non-existence in Sanskrit.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 14 — वस्तु / *vastu*

---

**Construction 37**

**Base Form:** वस्तु (*vastu*)
**Example:** वस्तुतो दृष्ट्वा
**Transliteration:** vastuto dṛṣṭvā
**English Translation:** Having seen in reality; having looked at the actual state of affairs.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the adverb *vastutaḥ* (documented in P2a) with the absolutive *dṛṣṭvā* (from root *dṛś-*, "to see"), used in philosophical and legal literature to characterize an examination or judgment as based on the actual facts rather than on appearances or allegations. The adverb *vastutaḥ* with perceptual or cognitive verbs is a conventional formula for asserting factual grounding.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 38**

**Base Form:** वस्तु (*vastu*)
**Example:** वस्तुनि यथावत् प्रवृत्तिः
**Transliteration:** vastuni yathāvat pravṛttiḥ
**English Translation:** Activity/engagement with the object as it actually is.
**Usage Notes:** A construction using the locative of *vastu* with the adverb *yathāvat* (P1 item 30) and the noun *pravṛtti* (activity, engagement, from *pra-* + root *vṛt-*), used in *Nyāya* and *Mīmāṃsā* epistemological literature to characterize successful practical engagement with an object as it really is. The combination of *vastu* + *yathāvat* is a conventional formula for veridical practical cognition.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

---

**Construction 39**

**Base Form:** वस्तु (*vastu*)
**Example:** वस्तु पुरुषतन्त्रं न भवति
**Transliteration:** vastu puruṣatantraṃ na bhavati
**English Translation:** A real thing is not subject-dependent; reality is not governed by the person.
**Usage Notes:** A formulaic assertion in *Mīmāṃsā* epistemology, contrasting *vastu-tantra* (object-dependent) cognition with *puruṣa-tantra* (subject-dependent) cognition. The construction asserts the independence of real objects from subjective will or convention; attested in *Śābarabhāṣya* and related *Mīmāṃsā* literature.
**Epistemic Status:** [CORE]

## P1 Item 15 — स्वरूप / *svarūpa*

---

**Construction 40**

**Base Form:** स्वरूप (*svarūpa*)
**Example:** स्वरूपेण अवतिष्ठते
**Transliteration:** svarūpeṇa avatiṣṭhate
**English Translation:** It stands/abides in its own nature; it exists as it truly is.

The following P1 items generated no common multi-word constructions beyond free compositional use or derivational forms already documented in P2a:

- **अयथार्थ / *ayathārtha*** (P1 item 8): Functions primarily as an attributive adjective; no fixed constructions identified.

These absences reflect the structural tendency of Sanskrit to handle semantic distinctions through compounding and derivation rather than through periphrastic constructions, and the more restricted distribution of certain P1 items (particularly the *marginal* items) in the attested literature.
