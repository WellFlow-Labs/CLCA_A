## SUMMARY OBSERVATIONS

- All ten constructions identified in P4a receive a **[CORE]** rating with the exception of the postposed-predicate variant in Construction 2 (*ziurra ez da*), which is rated **[MARGINAL]** due to its lower frequency relative to canonical word order.
- No construction identified in P4a has been rated **[UNCERTAIN]**; all have sufficient attestation to support a usage characterization.
- Register variation is the primary axis of differentiation: the translative construction (8) and relational adnominal modification (3) are concentrated in formal and legal registers, while attribution (1), negative attribution (2), and pejorative nominalization (10) are unrestricted across registers.
- No new constructions have been introduced in this step; all alternates listed above are drawn from P4a notes.
- The suffixed *-tu* inchoative pattern (Construction 4) is lexically restricted and cannot be treated as a parallel productive pattern across the full domain; the periphrastic *bihurtu* is the productive general strategy.
- The *-arazi* morphological causative (Construction 5) is grammatically productive in Basque generally but relatively low-frequency for truth-domain verbs; periphrastic *eragin* and *behartu* + infinitive are the preferred causative strategies in this domain.
- The *-pen* result nominals (*egiaztapen*, *frogapen*, *zehaztapen*) are distinct in meaning (result/outcome) from *-tasun* (abstract property) and *-tze* (process) nominals and are not interchangeable with them.
- The *-gabe* and *ezin* privative strategies are not interchangeable: *-gabe* applies to nominal and verbal roots for absence; *ezin* applies to verbal roots for impossibility. The *-gaitz* / *-kaitz* pattern is the consistent direct negative counterpart to *-garri* across all attested data.
- The *egin* vs. *esan* distribution in light-verb constructions (Construction 11) follows a speech-act vs. action distinction consistently across attested examples.
- Character adjectives (*zintzoa*, *jatorra*) are attested as an alternative way to characterize a truth-telling person, but these are property attributions rather than agentive nominals and are not structural alternates of the *-ti* pattern.
- Adverbial epistemic hedge forms (*itxuraz*, *ustez*) and their adnominal counterparts (*itxurazko*, *ustezko*) are both attested and productive; the adverbial form (instrumental *-z*) modifies the sentence while the adnominal form (*-zko*) modifies a head noun.
