# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Tamil (ta)

---

**Methodological note:** The following oppositions are extracted solely from patterns explicitly documented in P1, P1b, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, and P4b. No opposition is inferred beyond what the data directly supports. Where the data describes a contrast, that contrast is reported. Where asymmetry exists between the two poles of a contrast, this is noted rather than normalized.

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** பொய் / *poy*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *poy*
4. **English translation:** truth / lie, falsehood
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *uṇmai* "contrasts with *poyymai* (falsehood)" and P3 Group 1 identifies *poy* as "the primary negative counterpart" of *uṇmai*, used predicatively to assert that a claim does not correspond to reality. The pair appears in parallel constructions: *atu uṇmaitāṉ* (that is indeed true) vs. *atu poytāṉ* (that is indeed a lie), and in the proverbial structure where *uṇmai* prevails and *poy* is eventually exposed.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai*
2. **Term B:** கற்பனை / *kaṟpaṉai*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *kaṟpaṉai*
4. **English translation:** truth, fact / fiction, fabrication, imagination
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *kaṟpaṉai* as used "to characterize a claim or narrative as invented rather than factual," and P2b Construction 18.2 documents *itu veṟum kaṟpaṉai* (this is mere fabrication) as a dismissal of a claim as invented. P2b Construction 18.3 documents the compound *kaṟpaṉaikkum eṭṭāta uṇmai* (a truth beyond imagination), which places the two terms in explicit relational contrast. The opposition is between what is real/factual and what is invented/imagined.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** நகல் / *nakal*
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *nakal*
4. **English translation:** original / copy, duplicate
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *acal* "contrasts with *nakal* (copy, duplicate)." P2b Construction 8.2 documents the disjunctive question *itu acalā nakalā?* (is this the original or a copy?) as a fixed evaluative pair. P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad as the core of the genuineness-evaluation group. The contrast is between the genuine original and a reproduction, with *nakal* carrying neutral connotation in this pair.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** போலி / *pōli*
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *pōli*
4. **English translation:** original, genuine / fake, counterfeit, sham
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad and notes that *pōli* "is always negative" while *nakal* can be neutral. P1 describes *pōli* as applied to "objects, persons, or credentials that falsely claim to be genuine" and as carrying "a strongly negative evaluative charge." The contrast between *acal* and *pōli* is between genuine authenticity and fraudulent imitation, with a moral/legal dimension absent from the *acal/nakal* pair.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** நேர்மை / *nērmai*
2. **Term B:** வஞ்சனை / *vañcaṉai*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmai* / *vañcaṉai*
4. **English translation:** honesty, integrity / deceit, deception, fraud
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *nērmai* "contrasts with *vaஞcaṉai* (deceit)." P3 Group 3 identifies both as the clearest members of the character-evaluation group, with *nērmai* characterizing honest conduct and *vañcaṉai* characterizing deliberate deception. The parallel adverbial constructions *nērmaiyāka naṭa* (behave honestly) and *vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa* (behave deceitfully) are documented in P2b as structurally parallel.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** நியாயம் / *niyāyam*
2. **Term B:** அநியாயம் / *aniyāyam*
3. **Transliteration:** *niyāyam* / *aniyāyam*
4. **English translation:** justice, fairness, rightness / injustice, unfairness, wrongfulness
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 describes *aniyāyam* as the "direct antonym of *niyāyam*." P2a notes that *aniyāyam* is formed by the Sanskrit privative prefix *a-* applied to the Sanskrit-origin base *nyāya* before borrowing into Tamil, making this "a lexical antonym pair rather than a productive Tamil-internal derivation." P2b documents the predicative use *itu aniyāyam* (this is unjust) as the negative counterpart to *itu niyāyamā?* (is this fair?).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** தகுதி / *takuti*
2. **Term B:** தகுதியற்ற / *takutiyaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *takuti* / *takutiyaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** qualification, eligibility, fitness / unqualified, ineligible, unfit
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 documents both items, with *takutiyaṟṟa* described as the evaluative term for persons or things that "fail to meet a required standard." P2a documents the *aṟṟa* privative participial suffix as the derivational strategy producing the negative pole from the positive nominal base. The contrast is between meeting and failing to meet a formal standard.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** நம்பகமான / *nampakamāna*
2. **Term B:** நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampakamāna* / *nampattakāta*
4. **English translation:** reliable, trustworthy, credible / untrustworthy, not credible, not worth trusting
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents *nampattakāta* as "the direct negative counterpart of *nampattakka*" and P1b documents both *nampattakka* and *nampattakāta* as a positive/negative pair for evaluating whether a person, source, or claim is worthy of trust. P2a notes the morphological relationship: both are formed from *nampu* + *-att-* + *takka* (worthy) vs. *takāta* (not worthy). The contrast is between credibility and its absence in source and testimony evaluation.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** நம்பத்தக்க / *nampattakka*
2. **Term B:** நம்பத்தகாத / *nampattakāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampattakka* / *nampattakāta*
4. **English translation:** worth trusting, credible / not worth trusting, not credible
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a explicitly documents these as a positive/negative participial adjective pair derived from the same base (*nampu* + *-att-*) by the contrast between *takka* (worthy of) and *takāta* (not worthy of, negative participial). P4b documents both as attested in journalistic and legal evaluation of sources and testimony. This is a morphologically formed minimal pair within the same derivational paradigm.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 10

1. **Term A:** நேர்மையான / *nērmaiyāna*
2. **Term B:** நேர்மையில்லாத / *nērmaiyillāta*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmaiyāna* / *nērmaiyillāta*
4. **English translation:** honest, of integrity / dishonest, lacking integrity
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents *nērmaiyillāta* as the negative form of *nērmai* formed by the *illāta* negative existential participial construction. P4b documents both as attested, with *nērmaiyillāta maṉitaṉ* (a person lacking honesty) as the example. The contrast is between the presence and absence of the quality of honesty in a person.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 11

1. **Term A:** ஆதாரமான / *ātāramāna*
2. **Term B:** ஆதாரமற்ற / *ātāramaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *ātāramāna* / *ātāramaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** well-founded, evidenced, substantiated / unfounded, unsubstantiated, baseless
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents both as derived from *ātāram* (evidence, basis): *ātāramāna* via the *-āna* adjectival suffix and *ātāramaṟṟa* via the *aṟṟa* privative participial suffix. P4b documents *ātāramaṟṟa kuṟṟaccāṭṭu* (a baseless accusation) as the attested example of the negative form. The contrast is between claims that have evidentiary support and those that do not.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 12

1. **Term A:** பொருத்தமான / *poruttamāna*
2. **Term B:** பொருத்தமற்ற / *poruttamaṟṟa*
3. **Transliteration:** *poruttamāna* / *poruttamaṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** appropriate, fitting, suitable / inappropriate, unsuitable, ill-fitting
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents both as derived from *poruttam* (appropriateness, fit): *poruttamāna* via *-āna* and *poruttamaṟṟa* via the *aṟṟa* privative. P3 Group 9 identifies both as members of the fit-and-conformity evaluation group. The contrast is between conformity to and deviation from a relevant standard or context.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 13

1. **Term A:** நம்பிக்கை / *nampikkai* (presence of trust)
2. **Term B:** நம்பிக்கையின்மை / *nampikkaiinmai*
3. **Transliteration:** *nampikkai* / *nampikkaiinmai*
4. **English translation:** trust, faith, confidence / lack of trust, distrust, absence of faith
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b documents *nampikkaiyiṉmai* as a compound formed by *nampikkai* + *iṉmai* (absence, lack), and P2a documents it as formed by the productive *iṉmai* privative nominal construction. P3 Group 5 lists *nampikkaiinmai* as a member of the trustworthiness group. The contrast is between the state of trusting and the absence of that state.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 14

1. **Term A:** உண்மை சொல் / *uṇmai col*
2. **Term B:** பொய் சொல் / *poy col*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai col* / *poy col*
4. **English translation:** tell the truth / tell a lie
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as parallel verbal constructions with the same verb *col* (to say/tell), describing them as "parallel in structure." P3 Group 8 includes *uṇmai col* as a moral imperative and P2b Construction 2.1 notes that *poy col* is "parallel in structure to *uṇmai col*." The constructions are structurally identical, differing only in the nominal complement (*uṇmai* vs. *poy*).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

2. **Term B:** பொய் பேசு / *poy pēcu*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai pēcu* / *poy pēcu*
4. **English translation:** speak the truth / speak falsely
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both constructions with the verb *pēcu* (to speak) and explicitly notes that the distinction between *uṇmai col* / *poy col* and *uṇmai pēcu* / *poy pēcu* "mirrors" across both pairs. The constructions are structurally parallel, differing only in the nominal complement.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 16

1. **Term A:** நேர்மையாக நட / *nērmaiyāka naṭa*
2. **Term B:** வஞ்சனையாக நட / *vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa*
3. **Transliteration:** *nērmaiyāka naṭa* / *vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa*
4. **English translation:** behave honestly / behave deceitfully
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both constructions and explicitly states that *vañcaṉaiyāka naṭa* is "parallel in structure to *nērmaiyāka naṭa*." Both use the *-āka* adverbial suffix on a nominal base with the verb *naṭa* (to conduct oneself). The constructions are structurally identical, differing only in the nominal base (*nērmai* vs. *vañcaṉai*).
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** உண்மையை மறை / *uṇmaiyai maṟai* (transitive, accusative)
2. **Term B:** உண்மை வெளியே வந்தது / *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu* (intransitive, subject)
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyai maṟai* / *uṇmai veḷiyē vantatu*
4. **English translation:** hide the truth (transitive) / the truth came out (intransitive)
5. **Description of contrast:** P4a Construction 9 and P4b explicitly document these as "structurally and semantically complementary" poles of the concealment/revelation pattern. In the concealment construction, *uṇmai* bears accusative case (*uṇmaiyai*) as a direct object; in the revelation construction, *uṇmai* is the subject with no case marking. P3 Group 7 identifies this as a distinct narrative pattern treating truth as a moveable entity.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai* (classical/everyday register)
2. **Term B:** மெய் / *mey* (classical/literary register)
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *mey*
4. **English translation:** truth, reality / truth, reality, body
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 Group 1 explicitly states that "*uṇmai* and *mey* are near-synonyms in this function but differ in register: *uṇmai* is the default across all registers; *mey* is confined to literary, philosophical, and formal contexts." P1 describes *mey* as "less common in everyday spoken Tamil than *uṇmai*." This is a register-based contrast between two items that share the same core meaning but differ in distribution.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** உண்மை / *uṇmai* (truth-as-correspondence to reality)
2. **Term B:** மெய் / *mey* (truth-as-bodily-reality, physical sense)
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai* / *mey* (body sense)
4. **English translation:** truth, fact / body, physical substance
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b documents that *mey* "participates in two distinct semantic streams — 'truth/reality' and 'body/physical'" and P2a notes this as "a structural fact about the root." The body-sense uses (*meypāṭu*, bodily expression of emotion; *meykāppu*, bodyguard; *mey cilirttal*, the body thrilling) are documented as falling "outside the truth-domain scope." This is an internal polysemy within *mey* that has no parallel in *uṇmai*.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** அசல் / *acal*
2. **Term B:** நகல் / *nakal* and போலி / *pōli* (combined negative pole)
3. **Transliteration:** *acal* / *nakal*, *pōli*
4. **English translation:** original / copy (neutral) and fake (negative)
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 Group 2 identifies the *acal/nakal/pōli* triad and explicitly notes that "*nakal* is neutral in connotation (a certified copy of a document is a *nakal* without negative implication), while *pōli* is always negative." This documents a three-way structure in which *acal* contrasts with two distinct negative poles that differ in their evaluative charge: *nakal* (non-original but legitimate) and *pōli* (non-original and fraudulent).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** மெய்ப்பிக்க / *meyppiкka* (morphological causative from *mey*)
2. **Term B:** (no parallel causative from *uṇmai*)
3. **Transliteration:** *meyppiкka* / —
4. **English translation:** to prove, to establish the truth of / (no equivalent derived form)
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a explicitly documents this as a "structural gap": "No morphological causative from *uṇmai* is attested; this asymmetry is noted in P4a as a structural gap." P4a states that *meyppiкka* is "the only morphologically derived verbal form in the entire truth domain" and that "no parallel causative from *uṇmai* is attested." The contrast is between the morphological productivity of *mey* (which yields a causative verbal form) and the absence of such productivity in *uṇmai* (which relies on periphrastic alternatives).
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 22

1. **Term A:** உண்மை கசக்கும் / *uṇmai kacakkum*
2. **Term B:** உண்மை எப்போதும் வெல்லும் / *uṇmai eppōtum vellum*
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmai kacakkum* / *uṇmai eppōtum vellum*
4. **English translation:** the truth is bitter / truth always prevails
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as proverbial constructions in P3 Group 8. Both use *uṇmai* as subject but predicate contrasting properties: *kacakkum* (is bitter, is unpleasant) vs. *vellum* (wins, prevails). The two proverbs encode different evaluative orientations toward truth — one emphasizing its cost to the hearer, the other its ultimate triumph — and co-exist in the same moral-instruction register without contradiction.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

1. **Term A:** சரி / *cari* (correct, right)
2. **Term B:** தவறு / *tavaṟu* (mistake, error, wrong)
3. **Transliteration:** *cari* / *tavaṟu*
4. **English translation:** correct, right / mistake, error, wrong
5. **Description of contrast:** P1 explicitly states that *tavaṟu* "contrasts with *cari* (correct) in evaluative contexts." P3 Group 9 identifies both as the most common members of the fit-and-conformity evaluation group in everyday speech. The contrast operates in both epistemic (factual correctness) and moral (right vs. wrong conduct) registers, as documented in P1 for both items.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 24

1. **Term A:** நம்பிக்கை வை / *nampikkai vai* (place trust)
2. **Term B:** நம்பிக்கை இழ / *nampikkai iḻa* (lose trust)
3. **Transliteration:** *nampikkai vai* / *nampikkai iḻa*
4. **English translation:** place trust, have faith / lose trust, lose faith
5. **Description of contrast:** P2b documents both as verbal constructions with *nampikkai* as the nominal base, using different verbal complements: *vai* (to place) for the acquisition of trust and *iḻa* (to lose) for its loss. P3 Group 5 lists both *nampikkai vaittal* and *nampikkai iḻattal* as members of the trustworthiness group. The constructions represent the entry and exit poles of the trust relationship.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

1. **Term A:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* (applied to propositions: true)
2. **Term B:** உண்மையான / *uṇmaiyāna* (applied to objects/persons: genuine)
3. **Transliteration:** *uṇmaiyāna* / *uṇmaiyāna*
4. **English translation:** true (of a statement) / genuine (of a thing or person)
5. **Description of contrast:** P3 documents *uṇmaiyāna* as participating in Group 1 (true propositions: *uṇmaiyāna taka'l*, true information), Group 2 (genuine objects: *uṇmaiyāna poruḷ*, genuine goods), and Group 3 (persons of integrity: *uṇmaiyāna maṉitaṉ*, a genuine person), noting that "the grouping depends entirely on the noun being modified." This is an internal semantic contrast within a single form, where the same adjectival derivation covers both propositional truth and object/person genuineness.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 26

1. **Term A:** நம்பிக்கை (epistemic trust, interpersonal/institutional)
2. **Term B:** நம்பிக்கை (religious faith, commitment beyond evidence)
3. **Transliteration:** *nampikkai* / *nampikkai*
4. **English translation:** trust, confidence (evidential) / faith, belief (religious)
5. **Description of contrast:** P1b documents this as a "peripheral extension" where *nampikkai* "shifts from epistemic trust based on evidence or track record to religious faith, which may involve commitment beyond or independent of evidential grounds." P3 notes the religious use (*nampikkaiāḷar*, believers; *nampikkai aṟikkai*, creed) as a usage pattern that "does not fit cleanly into any of the groups" and is "left ungrouped." This is an internal semantic contrast within a single form between two distinct orientations of the same root.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 27

1. **Term A:** *-āna* adjectival suffix (positive attribution)
2. **Term B:** *aṟṟa* privative participial suffix (negative attribution)
3. **Transliteration:** *-āna* / *aṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** (suffix marking presence of quality) / (suffix marking absence of quality)
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a documents both as productive derivational strategies in this domain and explicitly notes their co-occurrence: *takutiyāna* (qualified) vs. *takutiyaṟṟa* (unqualified); *ātāramāna* (well-founded) vs. *ātāramaṟṟa* (baseless); *poruttamāna* (appropriate) vs. *poruttamaṟṟa* (inappropriate). P4b confirms both as [CORE] and notes that *aṟṟa* "tends to attach to quality/attribute nouns" and is "less common in casual speech" than the *illāta* strategy.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 28

1. **Term A:** *illāta* negative existential participial (lacking X)
2. **Term B:** *aṟṟa* privative participial (cut off from X)
3. **Transliteration:** *illāta* / *aṟṟa*
4. **English translation:** lacking (X), without (X) / deprived of (X), cut off from (X)
5. **Description of contrast:** P2a explicitly states that "the *aṟṟa* privative participial suffix and the *illāta* negative existential construction represent two distinct privative strategies" and that "these are not interchangeable." P4b confirms both as [CORE] and notes that *illāta* "is broader" while *aṟṟa* "tends to attach to quality/attribute nouns." Both are attested in this domain but with different distributions: *illāta* with *nērmai* and *nampikkai*; *aṟṟa* with *takuti*, *ātāram*, and *poruttam*.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
