# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1b ROOT EXPANSION
# Language: Georgian (ka)

---

## Step 1 — Identify Central Roots

---

**Root 1**

**Form:** მართ- / marṭ-
**Transliteration:** marṭ-
**P1 participation:** 4 items — martali (#4), simartle (#3), gamarṭleba (#30), and the derivational base underlying mcdari (#10) via the contrasting cdar- root. Directly: martali, simartle, gamarṭleba, and the adverbial/prepositional form marṭo (see below). The root marṭ- (right, correct, straight, just) is the most productive native root in the truth-evaluation domain.
**Core meaning contribution:** Correctness, rightness, and justice — covering factual accuracy of statements, moral uprightness of persons, and legal vindication. The root spans epistemic, moral, and legal registers with high productivity.

---

**Root 2**

**Form:** ყალბ- / q̇alb-
**Transliteration:** q̇alb-
**P1 participation:** 4 items — q̇albi (#6), siq̇albe (#5), gaq̇albeba (#23), siq̇albis dadastureba (#21).
**Core meaning contribution:** Falseness, forgery, and counterfeit — covering the production and attribution of false or inauthentic items, documents, currency, and representations. The root is borrowed but fully integrated and highly productive in legal and everyday registers.

---

**Root 3**

**Form:** ჭეშმარ- / č̣ešmar-
**Transliteration:** č̣ešmar-
**P1 participation:** 2 items — č̣ešmariti (#1), č̣ešmariteba (#2). Fewer items than the other roots, but the root is the primary philosophical and theological vehicle for truth as an abstract property, and its derivational family is well-attested across registers.
**Core meaning contribution:** Truth as an inherent, substantive property of propositions, persons, and states of affairs — with stronger philosophical and theological weight than simartle or martali.

## Step 2 — Root Expansion

---

### Root 1: marṭ- (მართ-)

---

**1.**
**Form:** მართალია
**Transliteration:** martalia
**Literal breakdown:** martali + copula -a; "is right/correct"
**Natural English translation:** he/she/it is right; that is correct; you are right
**Context of use:** Extremely frequent in everyday conversation as a response particle or predicate, used to concede that an interlocutor's claim is correct or that a state of affairs is as described. Functions almost as a discourse marker in informal speech.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** მართალი ხარ
**Transliteration:** martali xar
**Literal breakdown:** martali (right/correct) + xar (2sg copula "you are")
**Natural English translation:** you are right
**Context of use:** Standard everyday expression for conceding correctness to an interlocutor. Ubiquitous in informal and semi-formal conversation across all registers and age groups.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** მართლა
**Transliteration:** marṭla
**Literal breakdown:** marṭ- + adverbial suffix -la; "truly, really, in truth"
**Natural English translation:** really, truly, indeed, in fact
**Context of use:** High-frequency adverb used in everyday speech to assert or emphasize that something is genuinely the case. marṭla moxda ("it really happened"), marṭla ar vici ("I truly don't know"). Functions as an intensifier and truth-asserting particle.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** მართლაც
**Transliteration:** marṭlac
**Literal breakdown:** marṭla (truly) + emphatic particle -c; "truly indeed"
**Natural English translation:** indeed, truly, in fact, as a matter of fact
**Context of use:** Emphatic form of marṭla, used to confirm or underscore the truth of a statement, often in response to a question or in conceding a point. Very common in both spoken and written Georgian.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** მართლაც რომ
**Transliteration:** marṭlac rom
**Literal breakdown:** marṭlac (indeed) + rom (subordinating conjunction); "it being truly the case that"
**Natural English translation:** it is indeed the case that; truly, the fact is that
**Context of use:** Used in written and formal spoken Georgian to introduce a concession or confirmation of a fact. Common in journalistic and analytical prose.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** სიმართლის თქმა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis tqma
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + tqma (saying, verbal noun of tkva); "the saying of truth"
**Natural English translation:** telling the truth, truth-telling
**Context of use:** Used in moral, legal, and everyday contexts to describe the act of speaking truthfully. simarṭlis tqma valdebulebaa ("telling the truth is an obligation") is a typical moral formulation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დადგენა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis dadgena
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + dadgena (establishment, determination); "determination of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** establishing the truth, determination of the facts
**Context of use:** Standard legal and investigative expression for the process of determining what actually occurred. Appears in court proceedings, journalistic investigations, and official inquiries.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** სიმართლის დამალვა
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis damalva
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + damalva (concealment, hiding); "concealment of the truth"
**Natural English translation:** concealment of the truth, hiding the truth
**Context of use:** Used in legal and moral discourse to describe deliberate suppression of true information. Appears in criminal law contexts and in moral condemnation of dishonest behavior.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** სიმართლის მოყვარე
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis moq̇vare
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + moq̇vare (lover, one who loves); "lover of truth"
**Natural English translation:** truth-lover, lover of truth, truth-seeking
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical, religious, and moral discourse to characterize a person who is devoted to truth. Appears in classical Georgian texts and in contemporary moral writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** სიმართლის მაძიებელი
**Transliteration:** simarṭlis madziebeliˈ
**Literal breakdown:** simarṭle (truth, genitive) + madziebeliˈ (seeker, one who seeks); "seeker of truth"
**Natural English translation:** seeker of truth, truth-seeker
**Context of use:** Used in philosophical and intellectual discourse to describe someone engaged in the pursuit of truth. More formal and literary than simarṭlis moq̇vare.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულება
**Transliteration:** marṭlmsajuleba
**Literal breakdown:** marṭl- (right, just) + msajul- (judge, judging) + -eba (nominalizing suffix); "right-judging, just judgment"
**Natural English translation:** justice, administration of justice, judiciary
**Context of use:** The standard term for the justice system and the administration of justice in legal and constitutional discourse. marṭlmsajulebis sistema ("justice system"), marṭlmsajulebis ganxorcieleba ("administration of justice"). Appears in the Georgian Constitution and legal codes.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** მართლმსაჯულების სისტემა
**Transliteration:** marṭlmsajulebis sistema
**Literal breakdown:** marṭlmsajuleba (justice, genitive) + sistema (system); "system of justice"
**Natural English translation:** justice system, judicial system
**Context of use:** Fixed administrative and constitutional phrase referring to the institutional framework of courts, prosecutors, and related bodies. Standard in legal and policy discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** მართლწერა
**Transliteration:** marṭlc̣era
**Literal breakdown:** marṭl- (correct, right) + c̣era (writing); "correct writing"
**Natural English translation:** orthography, correct spelling
**Context of use:** The standard term for orthography and correct spelling in Georgian. Used in educational, editorial, and linguistic contexts. marṭlc̣eris წესები ("rules of orthography") is a standard phrase in grammar instruction.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** მართლწერის წესები
**Transliteration:** marṭlc̣eris c̣esebi
**Literal breakdown:** marṭlc̣era (orthography, genitive) + c̣esebi (rules, plural); "rules of correct writing"
**Natural English translation:** rules of orthography, spelling rules
**Context of use:** Standard phrase in Georgian language education and editorial style guides. Appears in grammar textbooks and official language regulation documents.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებელი
**Transliteration:** marṭlmadidebeliˈ
**Literal breakdown:** marṭl- (right, correct, orthodox) + madideb- (glorifying, praising) + -el- + -i; "right-glorifying, orthodox"
**Natural English translation:** Orthodox (Christian), Eastern Orthodox
**Context of use:** The standard Georgian term for Eastern Orthodox Christianity and its adherents. marṭlmadidebeliˈ eklesia ("the Orthodox Church"). Ubiquitous in religious, historical, and cultural discourse in Georgia.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** მართლმადიდებლობა
**Transliteration:** marṭlmadideblobˈa
**Literal breakdown:** marṭlmadidebeliˈ + -oba (nominalizing suffix); "the state/quality of being Orthodox"
**Natural English translation:** Orthodoxy, Eastern Orthodox Christianity
**Context of use:** Abstract noun for the Orthodox Christian faith and tradition. Used in theological, historical, and cultural discourse about Georgian religious identity.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** გამართლება
**Transliteration:** gamarṭleba
**Literal breakdown:** ga- (preverb) + marṭl- (right, just) + -eba (nominalizing suffix)
**Natural English translation:** justification, vindication, acquittal, excuse
**Context of use:** Already listed in P1 (#30). Included here to anchor the derivational family. Used in legal contexts for acquittal and in moral/argumentative contexts for justification of conduct.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** გამართლება ვერ მოხდა
**Transliteration:** gamarṭleba ver moxda
**Literal breakdown:** gamarṭleba (justification/acquittal) + ver (negation of ability) + moxda (happened, occurred); "justification did not occur"
**Natural English translation:** no justification was found; acquittal did not occur; the action could not be justified
**Context of use:** Fixed legal and moral phrase used to state that an action or claim could not be vindicated or that an acquittal was not granted. Common in court reporting and moral evaluation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** გამართლებული
**Transliteration:** gamarṭlebuli
**Literal breakdown:** gamarṭleba (justification) + participial suffix -uli; "that which has been justified/acquitted"
**Natural English translation:** justified, vindicated, acquitted, warranted
**Context of use:** Used as an adjective to describe actions, decisions, or persons that have been vindicated or shown to be correct. gamarṭlebuli gadawyvetileba ("justified decision"), gamarṭlebuli pirovneba ("acquitted person").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** გაუმართლებელი
**Transliteration:** gaumarṭlebeliˈ
**Literal breakdown:** ga- + u- (privative) + marṭl- + -ebel- + -i; "incapable of being justified, unjustifiable"
**Natural English translation:** unjustifiable, unwarranted, inexcusable
**Context of use:** Applied to actions, decisions, or conduct that cannot be morally or legally justified. gaumarṭlebeliˈძალადობა ("unjustifiable violence"). Common in moral condemnation and legal argumentation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** მართებული
**Transliteration:** marṭebuli
**Literal breakdown:** marṭ- + -ebul- + -i; "that which is right/correct/appropriate"
**Natural English translation:** correct, appropriate, proper, well-founded
**Context of use:** Used to describe statements, decisions, or approaches that are correct or appropriate in context. marṭebuli daskvna ("well-founded conclusion"), marṭebuli midgoma ("correct approach"). Common in analytical and evaluative discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** მართებულად
**Transliteration:** marṭebuladˈ
**Literal breakdown:** marṭebuli + adverbial suffix -ad; "correctly, appropriately"
**Natural English translation:** correctly, appropriately, rightly, properly
**Context of use:** Adverbial form of marṭebuli, used to characterize the manner in which something is done or said as correct or appropriate. Common in academic, legal, and journalistic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**23.**
**Form:** მართებულობა

---

**24.**
**Form:** სიმართლე ითქვა

**Context of use:** Used in narrative and evaluative contexts to assert that what was said was true. Appears in literary, journalistic, and everyday discourse as a formulaic assertion of truthfulness.

**25.**

---

> **Note:** The marṭ- root is exceptionally productive in Georgian. Additional forms exist beyond those listed here, including further compounds in legal terminology (e.g., marṭlsawinaaġmdego, "unlawful, contrary to law"), religious vocabulary, and adverbial constructions. The 30 items above represent the most common and well-attested expressions.

## Step 3 — Peripheral Extensions

The following expressions involve the identified roots in contexts where the core truth-domain contribution appears to shift or extend into other semantic territories. These are flagged without interpretation or categorization.

---

**From marṭ-:**

- **მართლმადიდებელი / marṭlmadidebeliˈ** — The root marṭl- here contributes to a compound whose primary reference is religious denomination (Eastern Orthodox Christianity) rather than factual correctness or truthfulness of propositions.

- **მართლწერა / marṭlc̣era** — The root marṭl- here applies to the domain of spelling and orthographic convention, where "correctness" is defined by normative rule rather than correspondence to fact.

- **მართლი გზა / marṭli gza** — The root applies to moral conduct and life-path, where "rightness" is a normative-ethical rather than epistemic property.

- **მართლა / marṭla** and **მართლაც / marṭlac** — These adverbial forms function as intensifiers and discourse particles in everyday speech, where the truth-asserting function has partially grammaticalized into a general emphatic or confirmatory marker.

- **გამართლება / gamarṭleba** — In legal contexts, this refers to acquittal (a procedural outcome) rather than to the truth of a proposition; in everyday contexts, it can mean "to work out" or "to pay off" (e.g., of an investment or effort), extending into domains of practical success.

- **მართებული / marṭebuli** — Applied to decisions, approaches, and methods, where "correctness" refers to appropriateness or suitability rather than factual truth.

---

**From q̇alb-:**

- **ყალბი ღიმილი / q̇albi ġimili**, **ყალბი გრძნობები / q̇albi grdznobebi**, **ყალბი პატრიოტიზმი / q̇albi patriotizmi** — The root extends from documentary forgery and factual falsity into the domain of interpersonal sincerity, emotional authenticity, and political performance, where "falseness" is a property of persons and their expressed states rather than of propositions or documents.

- **ყალბი ღირებულებები / q̇albi ġirebulebebi** — The root applies to abstract values and cultural norms, where "falseness" is a critical or evaluative judgment about the authenticity of a cultural system rather than a factual claim about a specific item.

- **ყალბი ნაწარმოები / q̇albi nac̣armoˈebi** — The root applies to aesthetic and authorial authenticity, where "forgery" involves questions of artistic originality and attribution rather than factual truth.

---

**From č̣ešmar-:**

- **ჭეშმარიტი ხელოვანი / č̣ešmariti xelovani** — The root applies to aesthetic evaluation of artistic authenticity and genuine talent, where "true" is a judgment of quality and originality rather than factual correspondence.

- **ჭეშმარიტი ღირებულება / č̣ešmariti ġirebuleba** — The root applies to intrinsic or essential value, where "true" functions as a marker of depth or fundamentality rather than of propositional truth.

- **ჭეშმარიტი სარწმუნოება / č̣ešmariti sarc̣munoebaˈ** — The root applies to religious denomination and doctrinal correctness, where "true" is a normative theological judgment rather than an empirical claim.

- **ჭეშმარიტი სახე / č̣ešmariti saxe** — The root applies to the domain of appearance and identity, where "true" refers to an unmasked or essential form rather than to the truth-value of a proposition.
