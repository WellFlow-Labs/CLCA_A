## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 BECOME → REVELATION Substitution

The construction **Subject + di + òtítọ́** exists grammatically but does not express change in truth-value. Instead, it expresses **discovery or revelation** that something was true all along. P4b explicitly states: "Used for discovery or revelation rather than change in truth-value. Not common for statements 'becoming true' as truth is treated as static property."

This reflects a conceptual constraint: Yoruba treats truth as an inherent, static property rather than an emergent or changeable state. The language prefers evidential constructions like **A rí i pé ó tọ́** ("we saw that it is correct") over inchoative constructions.

**Pattern:** Where English might say "it became true," Yoruba says "it turned out to be true" or "it was revealed as true."

---

### 2.2 CAUSATION (factual) → VERIFICATION Substitution

The periphrastic causative **Subject + mú + kí + Clause** exists but is pragmatically marked for truth predicates. P4b notes: "Pragmatically marked; implies verification or making something correspond to truth rather than creating truth. Uncommon in everyday speech."

The construction is reinterpreted from "make X true" to "verify X" or "make X correspond to truth." This reflects another conceptual constraint: one cannot create or cause truth itself, only verify or demonstrate it.

**Pattern:** The same construction that expresses causation in other domains (e.g., **Ó mú kí omi gbóná** "He made the water hot") shifts to verification meaning with truth predicates.

---

### 2.3 REVELATION as Primary Strategy

Yoruba strongly prefers **revelation/demonstration** constructions over causative or inchoative ones. The serial verb pattern **fi + Object + hàn** ("use X show" = reveal X) is marked [CORE] and described as "highly productive" and "very common."

This pattern serves multiple functions:
- Revealing truth: **Mo fi òtítọ́ hàn** ("I revealed the truth")
- Demonstrating truth: overlaps with verification function
- Making truth visible: overlaps with what other languages might express as "making true"

---

### 2.4 Permissive vs. Factitive Causation

For character-related causation, Yoruba uses **jẹ́ kí** (allow/let) rather than **mú kí** (make/force):
- **Ó jẹ́ kí n sọ òtítọ́** ("He/she allowed me to tell the truth") — [CORE], natural
- **Ó mú kí n sọ òtítọ́** ("He/she made me tell the truth") — possible but less natural for truth-telling

This reflects a preference for permissive over factitive causation in the truth domain.

---

### 2.5 Asymmetries: Truth vs. Falsity

The language shows lexical asymmetry:
- **Truth:** Uses stative verb **tọ́** or nominal **òtítọ́**
- **Falsity:** Uses nominal **irọ́** (lie) or verb **purọ́** (to lie)

For agentive nominalization:
- **Liar:** **apurọ́** (productive **a-** prefix)
- **Truth-teller:** No equivalent *atọ́; must use **ọmọ òtítọ́** (child of truth)

This asymmetry suggests that lying is conceptualized as an active behavior (hence agentive nominalization), while truth-telling is conceptualized as a character trait (hence **ọmọ** "child of" construction).

---

### 2.6 Degree Modification Resistance

Truth predicates resist degree modification. P4b states: "Truth predicates like **tọ́** rarely take degree modification—truth is typically binary rather than scalar."

While constructions like **Predicate + púpọ̀** (greatly) and **Predicate + jù** (exceed, more than) exist and are [CORE] for other predicates, they are [MARGINAL] for truth predicates:
- **Ó tọ́ jù ú lọ** ("It is more correct than it") — grammatical but pragmatically odd

This reflects conceptualization of truth as binary rather than gradable.

---

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

**DISCONFIRMATION:** No dedicated construction for disconfirming or proving false. Must use circumlocution:
- Negation + verification: **kò fi hàn pé ó tọ́** ("didn't show that it is true")
- Negation + revelation: **kò hàn bí òtítọ́** ("didn't appear as truth")
- Simple negation: **kò tọ́** ("it is not correct")

The language lacks a single-word or dedicated construction equivalent to English "disprove" or "falsify."

---

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

**Agentive nominalization for truth-teller:** The productive **a-** prefix that creates **apurọ́** (liar) does not apply to **tọ́**. The form *atọ́ is not attested.

**Workaround:** Use **ọmọ òtítọ́** (child of truth) or **ẹni òtítọ́** (person of truth).

This is a lexical gap rather than a structural gap—the language has agentive nominalization patterns, but they don't apply to this particular root.

---

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

**BECOME (inchoative):** The construction **di + òtítọ́** exists but cannot express change in truth-value. P4b: "Yoruba conceptualizes truth as inherent rather than emergent."

**CAUSATION (factual):** The construction **mú kí + Clause** exists but is reinterpreted as verification. P4b: "One cannot 'make' truth."

These are conceptual constraints rather than structural impossibilities. The morphosyntax exists but is blocked or reinterpreted by semantic/pragmatic factors.

---

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Truth-teller vs. Liar:**
- Liar: **apurọ́** (morphological derivation)
- Truth-teller: **ọmọ òtítọ́** (periphrastic construction)

**Degree modification:**
- Other predicates: freely take **púpọ̀**, **jù**, **díẹ̀**
- Truth predicates: resist degree modification (binary conceptualization)

## Summary

Yoruba provides robust morphosyntactic resources for truth-domain expressions but shows systematic conceptual constraints:

1. **Truth is static:** BECOME constructions are reinterpreted as REVELATION
2. **Truth cannot be caused:** CAUSATION constructions are reinterpreted as VERIFICATION
3. **Truth is binary:** Degree modification is grammatical but pragmatically odd
4. **Revelation is central:** The **fi...hàn** pattern serves multiple functions (reveal, demonstrate, verify)
5. **Disconfirmation is absent:** No dedicated construction; must use negation strategies

The language shows one clear structural gap (DISCONFIRMATION), one lexical gap (agentive *atọ́), and several conceptual constraints that trigger substitution patterns rather than true gaps.
