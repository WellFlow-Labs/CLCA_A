## 1. Main recurring constructional functions across the languages

Below are the main constructional functions that recur in the F4 materials, with a brief language-by-language summary of how each language realizes them.

---

### A. Simple attribution: “X is true / correct / real / valid / reliable”
This is the most widely attested function.

- **ka (Georgian)**: copular/predicative evaluation with adjectives or nouns, e.g. **ეს მართალია** “this is true/right”; also domain-specific predicates like **სწორი**, **ნამდვილი**, **ზუსტი**.
- **yo (Yoruba)**: two main predicative patterns, **òtítọ́ ni** and **ó jẹ́ òtítọ́** “it is true”; also **ó tọ́** “it is right/correct,” **ó dájú** “it is certain.”
- **fi (Finnish)**: copular attribution, e.g. **Se on totta**, **Väite on epätosi**; many domain-specific predicates also fit this frame.
- **eu (Basque)**: copular attribution, e.g. **Hori egia da**, **Erantzuna okerra da**.
- **ta (Tamil)**: bare predication without overt present copula, e.g. **இது உண்மை**, **அது சரி**.
- **id (Indonesian)**: bare predicate attribution, e.g. **Informasi itu benar**, **Dokumen ini sah**.
- **tr (Turkish)**: zero-copula predication, e.g. **Bu bilgi doğru**, **Bu belge geçerli**.
- **ko (Korean)**: copular attribution, e.g. **그것은 사실이다**, **네 말이 옳다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested.

---

### B. Negative attribution: “X is not true / not correct / invalid / fake”
Also very widely attested, but realized in different ways.

- **ka**: analytic negation or negative correspondence, e.g. **ეს სიმართლეს არ შეესაბამება**, **ეს სწორი არ არის**, **ეს დოკუმენტი ყალბია**.
- **yo**: **kì í ṣe òtítọ́**, **irọ́ ni**, **kò tọ́**.
- **fi**: **Se ei ole tosi**, **Väite ei pidä paikkaansa**.
- **eu**: **Hori ez da egia**, **Albiste hori ez da benetakoa**.
- **ta**: **இது உண்மையில்லை**, **அது சரியல்ல**.
- **id**: **tidak + predicate**, also softened **kurang + predicate**, pending **belum + predicate**.
- **tr**: **doğru değil**, **geçerli değil**, plus lexical negatives like **asılsız**, **sahte**.
- **ko**: **사실이 아니다**, **사실과 다르다**, and lexical/formal negatives like **허위**.

**Assessment:** widely attested.

---

### C. Noun modification: “true story,” “accurate information,” “fake document,” “reliable source”
This is another very common function.

- **ka**: adjective + noun, e.g. **სწორი პასუხი**, **ნამდვილი დოკუმენტი**, **ყალბი დოკუმენტი**, **სარწმუნო წყარო**.
- **yo**: attested but less richly represented in F4; examples include **ọ̀nà títọ́**, **ẹlẹ́rìí ojú**.
- **fi**: adjective + noun, e.g. **aito nahka**, **virheellinen tieto**.
- **eu**: modifier + noun, e.g. **egiazko istorioa**, **gezurrezko agiria**.
- **ta**: adjectival forms in **-ஆன**, e.g. **உண்மையான தகவல்**, **பொய்யான குற்றச்சாட்டு**.
- **id**: **N yang ADJ** and also **N + ADJ**, e.g. **jawaban yang tepat**, **bukti yang sah**.
- **tr**: adjective + noun, e.g. **güvenilir kaynak**, **sahte belge**.
- **ko**: prenominal modifier forms, e.g. **진정한 사랑**, **정확한 정보**.

**Assessment:** widely attested, though Yoruba is less richly represented here in F4 than the others.

---

### D. Truth/fact as object of a verb: “tell the truth,” “hide the truth,” “seek the truth,” “know the truth”
This is one of the strongest recurring patterns.

- **ka**: **სიმართლის თქმა**, discourse variants like **სიმართლე გითხრათ**.
- **yo**: **sọ òtítọ́**, **mọ̀ òtítọ́**, **fẹ́ òtítọ́**, **fi òtítọ́ hàn**.
- **fi**: **puhua totta**, **kertoa totuus**, **etsiä totuutta**, **paljastaa totuus**.
- **eu**: **egia esan**, **egia bilatu**, **egia jakin**, **egia aurkitu**; also **gezurra esan**.
- **ta**: **உண்மையைச் சொல்**, **உண்மை பேசு**, **உண்மையை மறை**, **உண்மையை ஏற்று கொள்**.
- **id**: **mencari kebenaran**, **mengungkap kebenaran**, **membuktikan kebenaran**.
- **tr**: **doğruyu söylemek**, **gerçeği söylemek**, **hakikati söylemek**, **gerçeği çarpıtmak**, **hakikati gizlemek**.
- **ko**: **진실을 말하다**, **진실을 숨기다**, **사실을 확인하다**, **진실을 밝히다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested.

---

### E. Clause-level truth assessment: “It is true that … / It is not true that …”
This is common, but not equally explicit in all languages.

- **ka**: attested through correspondence and evaluative clause patterns, though F4 emphasizes predicate and correspondence constructions more than a dedicated “it is true that…” frame.
- **yo**: predicative clause-level truth with **òtítọ́ ni**, **ó jẹ́ òtítọ́**, **kì í ṣe òtítọ́**.
- **fi**: **On totta, että ...**, **Pitää paikkansa, että ...**.
- **eu**: **Egia da ...**, **egia ote den**.
- **ta**: quoted-clause + **உண்மை** / **சரி** is noted in F4.
- **id**: **benar bahwa ...**, **tidak benar bahwa ...**.
- **tr**: clausal nominalization + **... 사실이다**? No—that is Korean. In Turkish: F4 does not give a dedicated clausal complement label, but truth of propositions is expressed through predication and related constructions; this function is less explicitly singled out in Turkish F4 than in Finnish, Indonesian, Korean, Basque.
- **ko**: **~는 것은 사실이다**, **부인할 수 없는 사실이다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested, but more explicit in Finnish, Indonesian, Korean, Basque, Yoruba than in some others.

---

### F. Verification / checking / confirmation
This is a major recurring function, especially in formal, procedural, legal, journalistic, and technical contexts.

- **ka**: **სიმართლის დადგენა**, **ნამდვილობის დადგენა**, **სისწორის/სიზუსტის შემოწმება**.
- **yo**: **fi ẹ̀rí hàn**, **rí i dájú**, **ní ẹ̀rí / kò sí ẹ̀rí**.
- **fi**: proof/verification verbs and constructions are central, though F4 highlights **todistaa**, **pitää paikkansa**, and related evaluative patterns more than a single “check” construction.
- **eu**: **egiaztatu**, **Datuak egiaztatu dituzte**.
- **ta**: **சரிபார்**, **உறுதி செய்**, **உறுதிப்படுத்து**.
- **id**: evaluative passives and lexical verbs: **membenarkan**, **memvalidasi**, **memeriksa keaslian**, **diduga / dipastikan / terbukti**.
- **tr**: **doğrulamak**, **doğrulanmak**, **doğruluğunu kontrol etmek**.
- **ko**: **사실을 확인하다**, **사실 여부를 확인하다**, **정품 여부를 확인하다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested.

---

### G. Revelation / exposure: “the truth came out,” “the real nature was revealed”
This is especially prominent cross-linguistically.

- **ka**: **სიმართლე გაირკვა**, **სიმართლე დადგინდა**.
- **yo**: **fi òtítọ́ hàn**, **fi ẹ̀rí hàn**.
- **fi**: **totuus tulee ilmi**, **paljastaa totuus**.
- **eu**: **egia agerian jarri**, **egia azaleratu**.
- **ta**: **உண்மை வெளிவரு**, **உண்மை தெரியவரு**.
- **id**: **terbukti benar / tidak benar / palsu**, **dipastikan asli**.
- **tr**: **gerçek ortaya çıkmak**, **gerçeği yansıtmak**, **gerçeği çarpıtmak**; also revelation of hidden reality.
- **ko**: **진실이 드러나다**, **실체가 드러나다**, **거짓으로 드러나다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested.

---

### H. Change of status with “become” or equivalent
This is not equally strong in all languages.

- **ka**: weak as a general pattern; F4 explicitly says no simple dedicated “become true/correct” pattern was identified.
- **yo**: weak/uncertain; F4 explicitly says no common inchoative pattern identified.
- **fi**: strong in specific lexicalized patterns: **tulla todeksi**, **osoittautua epätodeksi**.
- **eu**: weak/uncertain; no common expression identified.
- **ta**: strong for some items, especially **சரி ஆகு**, and also **பொய்யாகி போ**.
- **id**: productive with **menjadi + predicate**, e.g. **menjadi tidak valid**.
- **tr**: productive with **olmak**, plus some restricted **-leş-** forms.
- **ko**: strong in specific institutional/status cases, especially **무효가 되다**.

**Assessment:** moderately attested overall; strong in some languages, weak or absent in others.

---

### I. Causing a status/result: “verify,” “invalidate,” “correct,” “make believable”
This function is common, but usually root-specific rather than uniform.

- **ka**: **სიმართლის დადგენა**, **გამართლება**, but no broad general “make true” pattern.
- **yo**: **fi òtítọ́ hàn**, **fi ẹ̀rí hàn**, **rí i dájú**.
- **fi**: **tehdä jokin uskottavaksi**, **todistaa jokin**, **oikeuttaa jokin**.
- **eu**: **egiaztatu**, **zuzendu**.
- **ta**: **சரிசெய்**, **உறுதிப்படுத்து**.
- **id**: **membenarkan**, **membetulkan**, **menyesuaikan**, **mencocokkan**, **mengesahkan**, **mengabsahkan**, **memvalidasi**, **memalsukan**.
- **tr**: **doğrulamak**; limited **-leştir-** causatives.
- **ko**: **밝히다**, **확인하다**, **무효로 하다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested as a function, but the realization is highly language-specific and often lexical rather than schematic.

---

### J. Appearance vs reality: “looks real,” “seems fake,” “turned out to be false”
This is a recurring constructional area.

- **ka**: less explicitly labeled in F4, but present through truth-revelation and authenticity contrasts.
- **yo**: less explicit in F4 as a separate construction type.
- **fi**: resultative/revelatory patterns like **paljastua väärennökseksi**.
- **eu**: less explicit in F4, though the lexical system supports it.
- **ta**: truth emergence and concealment patterns imply this contrast.
- **id**: **diduga palsu**, **terbukti palsu**, **dipastikan asli**.
- **tr**: **gerçeğe uygun**, **hakikatten uzak**.
- **ko**: especially strong: **진짜처럼 보인다**, **가짜처럼 보이다**, **실체가 드러나다**.

**Assessment:** moderately to widely attested; especially explicit in Korean and Indonesian.

---

### K. Abstract quality statements: “accuracy is important,” “reliability is low”
This is common in formal and evaluative discourse.

- **ka**: abstract nouns like **სიმართლე**, **ნამდვილობა**, **სიზუსტე**, **მართებულობა**.
- **yo**: lexical abstract nouns exist, but F4 gives fewer explicit abstract-quality statement examples.
- **fi**: **Totuus tulee ilmi**, **Lähteen luotettavuus on tärkeä**.
- **eu**: **egiazkotasuna**, **zuzentasuna**.
- **ta**: **உண்மைத்தன்மை முக்கியம்**, **நம்பகத்தன்மை குறைந்தது**.
- **id**: **kebenaran**, **keaslian dokumen**, etc.
- **tr**: **Raporun doğruluğu tartışılıyor**, **Ölçümün güvenilirliği düşük**.
- **ko**: **정확성이 중요하다**, **진실성이 느껴진다**.

**Assessment:** widely attested, though more explicit in some languages than others.

---

### L. Agent/person expressions: liar, honest person, witness, seeker of truth
This is less uniform.

- **ka**: no clear dedicated truth-teller; descriptive phrases like **მართალი კაცი**.
- **yo**: **ẹlẹ́rìí** ‘witness’ is strong; no parallel truth-teller/liar system in F4.
- **fi**: **totuudenetsijä**, **todistaja**; no strong everyday truth-teller noun.
- **eu**: **gezurti** ‘liar’; truth-teller expressed periphrastically.
- **ta**: descriptive phrases **பொய் சொல்பவன்**, **உண்மை பேசுபவர்**.
- **id**: no common expression identified in F4.
- **tr**: **yalancı** and descriptive **dürüst biri**.
- **ko**: **거짓말쟁이**, descriptive **정직한 사람**.

**Assessment:** moderately attested, but highly asymmetric and often periphrastic on the positive side.

## 3. Narrative comparison

### Recurring cross-language constructional patterns
The strongest recurring patterns in the F4 materials are:

1. **simple attribution**
   - Nearly every language has a basic way to say “X is true/correct/valid/real.”

2. **negative attribution**
   - Nearly every language also has a straightforward negated counterpart, often with ordinary negation rather than a special antonym.

3. **truth/fact as object of a verb**
   - This is one of the most stable cross-language patterns: “tell the truth,” “hide the truth,” “reveal the truth,” “seek the truth,” “check the facts.”

4. **verification and revelation**
   - Many languages strongly prefer constructions meaning “verify,” “confirm,” “bring to light,” or “be revealed” rather than a simple general “become true.”

5. **noun modification**
   - “true story,” “accurate information,” “fake document,” “reliable source” is a very common constructional zone across the languages.

6. **abstract quality statements**
   - Many languages readily use abstract nouns for truthfulness, accuracy, reliability, validity, or authenticity in evaluative statements.

These are the most clearly recurring constructional functions in the observed F4 data.

---

### Important asymmetries, substitutions, and restrictions

A major asymmetry is between **state attribution** and **change-of-state**.

- **Georgian, Yoruba, and Basque** do not show a strong general construction equivalent to “become true.”
- Instead, they rely more on:
  - truth being **revealed**
  - truth being **established**
  - facts being **checked**
  - something being **shown** or **confirmed**

By contrast:
- **Finnish** has clear lexicalized resultative patterns like **tulla todeksi** and **osoittautua epätodeksi**.
- **Tamil** has a strong everyday dynamic pattern with **சரி ஆகு** and a resultative falsity pattern **பொய்யாகி போ**.
- **Indonesian** productively uses **menjadi + predicate**.
- **Turkish** productively uses **olmak**.
- **Korean** has a strong status-change pattern in institutional domains, especially **무효가 되다**, but not a broad everyday “become true” pattern.

So one recurring substitution is:

- **revelation/verification constructions replacing general inchoative truth constructions**.

Another asymmetry concerns **causation**.

No language in the F4 materials shows one broad, uniform construction meaning “make X true” across the whole semantic field. Instead, languages use **domain-specific lexical verbs**:

- verify
- confirm
- correct
- invalidate
- validate
- reveal
- falsify
- make believable

This is especially clear in:
- **Basque**: **egiaztatu**, **zuzendu**
- **Tamil**: **சரிசெய்**, **உறுதிப்படுத்து**
- **Indonesian**: **membenarkan, membetulkan, menyesuaikan, memvalidasi, memalsukan**
- **Turkish**: **doğrulamak**
- **Korean**: **밝히다, 확인하다, 무효로 하다**

So another recurring substitution is:

- **lexical verbs replacing a general causative truth construction**.

---

### Cases where one strategy replaces another

Several languages show clear replacement patterns:

- **Georgian**: instead of “become true,” uses “truth became clear / was established.”
- **Yoruba**: instead of “become true,” uses predication plus evidence/showing constructions.
- **Basque**: instead of “become true,” uses verification and correction verbs.
- **Tamil**: instead of “make true,” uses confirmation or revelation; instead of “become wrong,” often uses negative correctness or failure expressions.
- **Korean**: instead of “become true,” uses **드러나다**, **밝히다**, **확인하다**; instead of “make true,” uses procedural or revelatory verbs.
- **Indonesian**: often uses evaluative passives like **terbukti**, **dipastikan**, **dinyatakan**, **dianggap** where another language might use a simpler predicate.
- **Turkish**: often uses lexical truth-object constructions or **olmak / çıkmak / doğrulamak** rather than a single generalized truth-change pattern.

---

### Weakly expressed, indirect, or absent functions

Some functions are weak or absent in the observed F4 data:

1. **A general “become true” construction**
   - weak/absent in **ka, yo, eu, ko** as a broad everyday pattern
   - only strong in some languages and often lexically restricted

2. **A general “make true” construction**
   - not strongly supported in any language as a broad constructional template
   - replaced by verification, confirmation, revelation, or justification verbs

3. **A balanced agentive pair for liar vs truth-teller**
   - many languages have a clear “liar” term or construction
   - but the positive side is often descriptive:
     - **ka** descriptive only
     - **fi** specialized or periphrastic
     - **ta** descriptive
     - **ko** descriptive
     - **id** absent in F4
   - **eu** has **gezurti** but no equally central simple positive counterpart in F4

4. **Appearance-vs-reality as a dedicated construction type**
   - very explicit in **ko**
   - fairly explicit in **id** and **fi**
   - more indirect or distributed across other constructions in several other languages

---

## Overall
From the F4 data alone, the most robust cross-language constructional picture is this:

- Languages strongly support **attribution**, **negated attribution**, **truth/fact as verb object**, **verification**, **revelation**, and **noun modification**.
- Many languages prefer **revealing/checking/confirming truth** over directly saying that truth “becomes” or is “made” true.
- Change-of-state and causative patterns exist, but they are often **restricted, lexical, or domain-specific**, not broad and uniform.
- Positive person-denoting constructions are often weaker and more descriptive than negative ones.
- Formal domains such as validity, legality, and authenticity often use specialized constructions that are distinct from ordinary truth attribution.
