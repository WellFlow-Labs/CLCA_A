# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P3 NATURAL SEMANTIC GROUPING
# Language: Chinese (zh)

---

## Preliminary Note on Method

Groupings below are derived from patterns observed in P1 and P1b/P2a/P2b: which terms co-occur in the same contexts, which terms are contrasted with each other, which terms appear in the same regulatory or discourse registers, and which terms share characteristic verb collocates or construction types. No external framework has been imposed. The number of groups and their sizes reflect the data. Items that do not fit cleanly are listed at the end with explicit notes.

---

## Group 1: Terms used to evaluate whether a statement or account corresponds to the facts

**Label:** Factual accuracy of statements and reports

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether a specific statement, report, testimony, or account accurately reflects what actually happened or what is actually the case. They appear characteristically in journalistic, legal, and administrative contexts where the fidelity of a representation to an underlying reality is at issue. The key contrast in this cluster is between a representation and the reality it purports to describe.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真实 (zhēnshí) — used to evaluate accounts, depictions, and testimony as faithfully reflecting reality; collocates with 反映, 还原, 追求
- 真实性 (zhēnshíxìng) — the nominalized property, used when the degree or presence of factual fidelity is itself the object of evaluation; 新闻报道的真实性
- 如实 (rúshí) — adverbial; specifies that a communicative act (陈述, 反映, 回答) is performed without distortion; strongly associated with legal testimony
- 失实 (shīshí) — evaluates a report or account as failing to correspond to the facts, without necessarily implying intent; 报道失实, 内容失实, 严重失实
- 失真 (shīzhēn) — evaluates a representation as having deviated from its original; overlaps with 失实 in media criticism contexts but extends into technical signal-distortion usage
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — evaluates claims and representations as without factual basis; stronger negative connotation than 失实; 虚假信息, 虚假广告
- 虚假性 (xūjiǎxìng) — nominalized form; 陈述的虚假性
- 失真度 (shīzhēndù) — technical nominalization measuring the degree of deviation from an original signal or representation

**Characteristic verbs and constructions in this group:**
- 还原真实 / 还原事件真相 (to restore/reconstruct the true account)
- 反映真实情况 (to reflect the true situation)
- 如实陈述 / 如实反映 / 如实回答
- 报道失实 / 内容失实
- 发布虚假信息
- 揭穿虚假

**Notes:** 真实 and 失实 are near-antonyms in journalistic and legal usage. 失真 overlaps with 失实 in media contexts but has a distinct technical register. 虚假 implies a stronger evaluative judgment (often deliberate deception) than 失实 (which may be inadvertent).

## Group 2: Terms used in the detection, verification, and establishment of facts

**Label:** Establishing and verifying what is actually the case

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the task is to determine, confirm, or establish what the facts of a matter are — particularly when facts are contested, hidden, or uncertain. They are associated with investigative, legal, and evidentiary processes. The characteristic verbs in this group are 查明, 揭露, 掩盖, 还原, 尊重, 歪曲, 以…为依据.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真相 (zhēnxiàng) — refers to the actual facts of a matter, especially when concealed or contested; the primary noun in this group; 揭露真相, 查明真相, 掩盖真相, 真相大白
- 事实 (shìshí) — denotes an established datum or actual state of affairs; 尊重事实, 歪曲事实, 以事实为依据, 铁的事实, 既成事实
- 事实上 (shìshíshàng) — discourse marker introducing a correction or contrast between appearance and reality
- 实情 (shíqíng) — the actual circumstances of a matter; 了解实情
- 真凭实据 (zhēn píng shí jù) — solid, genuine evidence backing a claim
- 实证 (shízhèng) — empirical evidence; evidence grounded in observation
- 属实 (shǔshí) — formal/legal confirmation that a reported matter corresponds to the facts; 情况属实, 经查属实
- 可靠 (kěkào) — evaluates sources, information, or evidence as worthy of being acted upon; 来源可靠, 可靠的证据
- 可靠性 (kěkàoxìng) — the nominalized property; 数据的可靠性

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 揭露真相 / 查明真相 / 掩盖真相 / 真相大白
- 尊重事实 / 歪曲事实 / 以事实为依据
- 铁的事实 / 既成事实 / 有目共睹的事实
- 经查属实
- 来源可靠 / 可靠的证据 / 找不到可靠的证据

**Notes:** 真相 and 事实 are closely related but differ in typical usage: 真相 tends to appear when facts are hidden or disputed and need to be uncovered; 事实 tends to appear when facts are being cited as the basis for a judgment or as evidence. 实情 is a near-synonym of 真相 in some contexts but is less common and less associated with investigative exposure. 可靠 evaluates the epistemic status of a source or piece of evidence rather than the facts themselves.

## Group 3: Terms used to evaluate the authenticity or genuineness of objects, persons, and products

**Label:** Authenticity and genuineness of things and people

**Definition:** These terms are used to evaluate whether an object, product, person, or cultural practice is the genuine article rather than a copy, imitation, or impostor. They appear characteristically in commercial, legal, art-authentication, and cultural contexts. The central question is whether something is what it presents itself as being, not whether a statement is true.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真 (zhēn) — the basic positive pole; 这是真的; contrasts with 假
- 假 (jiǎ) — the basic negative pole; 这是假的; contrasts with 真
- 真假 (zhēnjiǎ) — names the binary question of authenticity; 辨别真假, 真假难辨
- 真伪 (zhēnwěi) — frames the question of genuine vs. spurious, especially for documents, artworks, and texts; 辨别真伪, 鉴别真伪, 难辨真伪
- 伪造 (wěizào) — the act of making a false copy; 伪造文件, 伪造签名; criminal law term
- 伪证 (wěizhèng) — fabricated evidence or false testimony; legal term
- 伪劣 (wěiliè) — fake and inferior; 伪劣产品
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — passing off as genuine or authorized; 假冒商品
- 假冒伪劣 (jiǎ mào wěi liè) — standard regulatory phrase for counterfeit and substandard goods
- 冒充 (màochōng) — impersonating a person or passing off an object as something it is not; 冒充警察
- 鉴定 (jiàndìng) — expert evaluation to determine authenticity; 进行鉴定, 经专家鉴定, 送去鉴定
- 鉴定书 (jiàndìngshū) — the document produced by an authentication process
- 辨伪 (biànwěi) — scholarly or expert detection of forgeries
- 辨别真伪 (biànbié zhēnwěi) — the act of distinguishing genuine from fake
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) — affirms that goods are authentic and prices honest; extended to mean genuinely what it claims to be
- 道地 (dàodì) — colloquial affirmation of genuine regional or cultural authenticity; 道地药材, 道地的北京话
- 原真性 (yuánzhēnxìng) — specialized term for authenticity in heritage conservation contexts
- 打假 (dǎ jiǎ) — to combat counterfeits; anti-counterfeiting efforts
- 造假 (zào jiǎ) — to manufacture fakes or falsify
- 掺假 (chān jiǎ) — to adulterate; to mix in fake material
- 作假 (zuò jiǎ) — to falsify; to cheat
- 以假乱真 (yǐ jiǎ luàn zhēn) — using fakes to confuse the genuine
- 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn) — eliminating the false and retaining the genuine
- 真伪莫辨 (zhēn wěi mò biàn) — impossible to tell genuine from fake

**Notes:** This group is the largest and most internally differentiated. Within it, 真伪 and 辨伪 are more associated with documents, texts, and artworks (archival and connoisseurship contexts); 假冒 and 假冒伪劣 are more associated with commercial goods and regulatory language; 冒充 is more associated with persons and identity; 道地 is more colloquial and associated with cultural and regional products; 原真性 is restricted to heritage conservation discourse. 鉴定 is the term for the expert process that resolves questions raised by the other terms in this group.

## Group 4: Terms used to evaluate precision and correctness against a standard

**Label:** Correctness and precision relative to a standard

**Definition:** These terms evaluate whether something — an answer, a method, a measurement, a translation — conforms to a relevant standard or is free from error. They appear in educational, technical, and scientific contexts. The contrast is between conforming to a standard and deviating from it, rather than between genuine and fake, or between a representation and reality.

**Representative expressions:**

- 正确 (zhèngquè) — evaluates answers, methods, conduct as conforming to a standard; contrasts with 错误; 正确的道路, 保证正确
- 正确性 (zhèngquèxìng) — the nominalized property; 判断的正确性, 验证其正确性
- 不正确 (bùzhèngquè) — the standard negation
- 错误 (cuòwù) — deviation from what is correct; functions as both noun and adjective; 犯错误, 纠正错误, 承认错误, 严重的错误
- 准确 (zhǔnquè) — precision and exactness, especially in measurement, translation, and description; implies a tighter fit than 正确; 准确无误, 准确判断, 准确表达
- 准确性 (zhǔnquèxìng) — the nominalized property; 测量的准确性, 翻译的准确性
- 不准确 (bùzhǔnquè) — the standard negation

**Characteristic constructions:**
- 犯错误 / 纠正错误 / 承认错误
- 验证其正确性
- 准确无误
- 准确判断 / 准确预测 / 准确描述
- 无法准确判断

**Notes:** 正确 and 准确 are distinguished in usage: 正确 is used more broadly for conformity to a standard (including moral and procedural standards), while 准确 is used more specifically for precision of fit to a target (measurements, translations, predictions). 错误 is the primary antonym of 正确 in this group. This group does not overlap substantially with Group 1 (factual accuracy of statements), though there is some overlap when 正确 is used to evaluate factual claims.

## Group 5: Terms used to evaluate whether a person's character, conduct, or expression is genuine rather than performed

**Label:** Sincerity and genuineness of persons and their expressions

**Definition:** These terms evaluate whether a person's feelings, intentions, conduct, or character are genuinely what they appear to be, rather than performed, strategic, or hypocritical. They appear in interpersonal, moral, and literary contexts. The contrast is between inner reality and outward presentation in persons, rather than between genuine and counterfeit objects.

**Representative expressions:**

- 真诚 (zhēnchéng) — evaluates attitude, expression, or intention as genuinely felt; 真诚的道歉, 真诚待人
- 真心 (zhēnxīn) — asserts that an emotion or commitment is genuinely felt; 真心话, 真心喜欢
- 诚实 (chéngshí) — evaluates a person's character as genuinely honest; 诚实的人, 诚实守信
- 老实 (lǎoshi) — honest and uncomplicated; sometimes implies being easily taken advantage of; 老实人, 老实说
- 老实说 (lǎoshi shuō) — discourse marker for frank statement
- 说真的 (shuō zhēn de) — discourse marker signaling a sincere or frank statement
- 表里如一 (biǎo lǐ rú yī) — no discrepancy between outward appearance and inner reality; moral compliment; 做到表里如一
- 表里不一 (biǎo lǐ bù yī) — discrepancy between outward and inner; criticism
- 伪善 (wěishàn) — false virtue; presenting a moral appearance that does not correspond to actual character; 伪善者
- 伪君子 (wěi jūnzǐ) — a person who presents a virtuous exterior while being morally corrupt
- 虚情假意 (xū qíng jiǎ yì) — expressions of feeling or goodwill that are not genuine
- 假意 (jiǎyì) — feigned intention; 假意推辞
- 假装 (jiǎzhuāng) — to pretend, to feign; 假装睡觉, 假装不知道
- 装假 (zhuāng jiǎ) — to put on a false front

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 3 (authenticity of objects) by its focus on persons and their inner states. 表里如一 / 表里不一 are the most explicit terms for the inside/outside discrepancy in persons. 伪善 and 伪君子 are specifically moral-critical terms for hypocrisy. 真诚 and 真心 are positive evaluations; 假装, 虚情假意, and 伪善 are negative. 诚实 and 老实 overlap with this group but also appear in Group 4 contexts (honest reporting of facts).

## Group 6: Terms used to evaluate whether something is what it is called or represented to be

**Label:** Correspondence between label, reputation, or name and actual qualities

**Definition:** These terms evaluate whether a designation, title, reputation, or name accurately corresponds to the actual qualities of the person or thing so designated. They appear in evaluative contexts where the question is whether a label is deserved or misleading. The contrast is between nominal identity and actual character.

**Representative expressions:**

- 名副其实 (míng fù qí shí) — the label accurately corresponds to the actual qualities; 名副其实的专家
- 名不副实 (míng bù fù shí) — the label does not correspond to the actual qualities; 这个称号名不副实
- 真正 (zhēnzhèng) — intensifies the claim that something is genuinely what it appears to be; 真正的朋友, 真正理解
- 货真价实 (huò zhēn jià shí) — genuinely what it claims to be; extended from commercial to general use
- 真才实学 (zhēn cái shí xué) — genuine talent and solid learning, as opposed to mere credentials or reputation
- 言过其实 (yán guò qí shí) — a statement that exceeds what the facts warrant; exaggeration
- 实力 (shílì) — genuine, demonstrated capacity as opposed to nominal rank or reputation

**Notes:** This group is distinguished from Group 3 (authenticity of objects) by its focus on the correspondence between a label or reputation and actual qualities, rather than on whether an object is a genuine specimen of its kind. 名副其实 / 名不副实 are the most explicit terms for this correspondence. 真正 overlaps with Group 3 and Group 5 but is included here because it frequently appears in contexts evaluating whether something deserves its designation. 言过其实 is the only term in this group that evaluates a statement rather than a person or thing, but the statement in question is specifically one that overclaims about qualities.

## Group 7: Terms used in contexts of deliberate falsification and fraud

**Label:** Deliberate falsification and fraudulent conduct

**Definition:** These terms appear specifically in contexts where deception is deliberate and systematic, rather than inadvertent. They are associated with legal, regulatory, anti-corruption, and criminal discourse. The characteristic construction type is prohibitive (严禁, 不得) or criminal-charge framing (涉嫌).

**Representative expressions:**

- 弄虚作假 (nòng xū zuò jiǎ) — deliberate falsification or fraudulent conduct, especially in official, academic, or commercial contexts; 严禁弄虚作假, 不得弄虚作假
- 伪造 (wěizào) — deliberate manufacture of a false document, signature, or object; 伪造文件, 涉嫌伪造
- 造假 (zào jiǎ) — to manufacture fakes or falsify data; 数据造假, 学术造假
- 作假 (zuò jiǎ) — to falsify; to cheat; 考试作假, 账目作假
- 假冒 (jiǎmào) — passing off as genuine or authorized; 假冒商品
- 假公济私 (jiǎ gōng jì sī) — using public office for private gain under the guise of public interest; anti-corruption discourse
- 虚假 (xūjiǎ) — fraudulent representations; 虚假广告; legal and regulatory term
- 伪证 (wěizhèng) — fabricated evidence or perjury; 作伪证

**Notes:** Several terms in this group also appear in Group 3 (authenticity of objects): 伪造, 假冒, 虚假. The distinction is that Group 3 focuses on the question of whether something is genuine, while this group focuses on the act of deliberate deception. The prohibitive construction pattern (严禁/不得 + term) is specific to this group and is not attested for terms in other groups. 弄虚作假 is the most general term in this group and the one most associated with regulatory and anti-corruption language.

## Group 8: Terms used to evaluate objectivity and the role of personal perspective in judgment

**Label:** Objectivity and subjectivity in evaluation and judgment

**Definition:** These terms evaluate whether a judgment, account, or stance is based on external facts or on personal perspective and interest. They appear in academic, journalistic, and philosophical discourse. The central contrast is between 客观 and 主观.

**Representative expressions:**

- 客观 (kèguān) — based on external facts rather than personal perspective; 客观评价, 客观分析, 保持客观, 客观公正
- 客观性 (kèguānxìng) — the nominalized property; 新闻报道的客观性
- 主观 (zhǔguān) — shaped by personal perspective or interest; 过于主观
- 主观性 (zhǔguānxìng) — the nominalized property; 判断的主观性
- 主观臆断 (zhǔguān yìduàn) — arbitrary judgment based on personal impression rather than evidence
- 不能主观臆测 — normative prohibition on subjective conjecture

**Notes:** This group is relatively self-contained. 客观 and 主观 form a clear contrastive pair. The group does not overlap substantially with other groups, though 客观 collocates with 公正 (impartial) in some contexts, linking it to evaluative discourse more broadly.

---

## Group 9: Terms used to evaluate the underlying or essential nature of something

**Label:** Essence and underlying nature

**Definition:** These terms are used to assert what something fundamentally is, as opposed to what it appears to be on the surface or what it is called. They appear in philosophical, political, and analytical discourse. The contrast is between surface appearance or nominal character and underlying reality.

**Representative expressions:**

- 本质 (běnzhì) — the intrinsic or defining nature of something; 本质上, 揭示本质, 抓住本质, 看清本质, 从本质上说
- 实质 (shízhì) — the actual content or underlying reality, often in contrast to formal or nominal character; 实质上, 实质性, 触及实质, 流于形式缺乏实质
- 本质上 (běnzhìshàng) — adverbial: essentially, fundamentally
- 实质上 (shízhìshàng) — adverbial: in substance, in reality
- 实质性 (shízhìxìng) — attributive: substantive; 实质性进展, 实质性问题

**Notes:** 本质 and 实质 are closely related and sometimes interchangeable, but usage patterns distinguish them: 本质 tends to appear in philosophical and moral discourse about what something inherently is; 实质 tends to appear in legal and administrative discourse about what something amounts to in practical or legal terms. Both generate productive adverbial forms (本质上, 实质上) that function as discourse markers.

## Group 10: Terms used in moral and cognitive evaluation of the ability to distinguish right from wrong or true from false

**Label:** Distinguishing right from wrong and true from false

**Definition:** These terms appear in contexts where the cognitive or moral capacity to differentiate between correct and incorrect, genuine and false, or right and wrong is at issue. They are associated with educational, moral, and evaluative discourse. The characteristic construction is 辨别/明辨 + binary evaluative noun.

**Representative expressions:**

- 辨别 (biànbié) — the cognitive act of differentiating; 辨别真假, 辨别是非, 辨别好坏, 辨别能力
- 是非 (shìfēi) — the distinction between right and wrong, or true and false; 辨别是非, 明辨是非, 颠倒是非, 混淆是非, 是非曲直
- 明辨是非 (míng biàn shìfēi) — to clearly distinguish right from wrong
- 颠倒是非 (diāndǎo shìfēi) — to invert right and wrong deliberately
- 混淆是非 (hùnxiáo shìfēi) — to confuse or blur the distinction between right and wrong
- 去伪存真 (qù wěi cún zhēn) — the process of eliminating the false and retaining the genuine
- 真知灼见 (zhēn zhī zhuó jiàn) — genuine and penetrating insight; accurate and perceptive judgment

**Notes:** 是非 is the central noun in this group, naming the distinction that is being made or violated. 辨别 is the central verb. 颠倒是非 and 混淆是非 describe violations of the capacity to distinguish; 明辨是非 describes its proper exercise. 去伪存真 belongs here as well as in Group 3, since it describes a process of critical evaluation rather than simply the detection of fakes.

## Ambiguous and Overlapping Items

The following items from P1 and P2 could plausibly belong to more than one group, or their placement is uncertain. These are noted without forcing a resolution.

---

**真实 (zhēnshí)**
Belongs primarily to **Group 1** (factual accuracy of statements and reports) but also appears in **Group 3** contexts (authentic representation in art and literature: 逼真, 真实感, 写实). The term spans both the propositional and the representational/aesthetic dimensions of truth.

---

**虚假 (xūjiǎ)**
Belongs to both **Group 1** (false information, inaccurate representations) and **Group 7** (deliberate fraud: 虚假广告 as a legal term). The term's placement depends on whether the context emphasizes factual inaccuracy or deliberate deception.

---

**假冒 (jiǎmào)**
Belongs to both **Group 3** (authenticity of objects: counterfeit goods) and **Group 7** (deliberate falsification: the act of passing off). The term names both the state (a counterfeit object) and the act (deliberate misrepresentation).

---

**诚实 (chéngshí) / 老实 (lǎoshi)**
Belong primarily to **Group 5** (sincerity and genuineness of persons) but also appear in **Group 1** contexts when used to evaluate the honesty of a statement or report (老实说, 说实话). The terms straddle personal character and communicative honesty.

---

**真正 (zhēnzhèng)**
Belongs primarily to **Group 6** (correspondence between label and actual qualities) but also appears in **Group 3** (genuineness of objects: 真正的朋友 as a genuine friend rather than a nominal one) and **Group 5** (genuine feelings or commitments). It is a general intensifier of genuineness that does not belong exclusively to any one group.

---

**逼真 (bīzhēn)**
Belongs to **Group 3** in the sense of evaluating a representation as close to the genuine article, but its primary domain is aesthetic and artistic (a painting that looks real, an imitation that is convincing). It does not fit cleanly into the commercial/legal authenticity cluster of Group 3 or the factual accuracy cluster of Group 1. It is noted here as a borderline item.

---

**真理 (zhēnlǐ)**
Does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above. Its usage is primarily in philosophical, ideological, and scientific discourse to refer to fundamental or universal truth as a value or principle (追求真理), rather than to evaluate specific statements, objects, or persons. It is not contrasted with 假 or 伪 in the same way as other 真- compounds. It is left ungrouped.

---

**真善美 (zhēn shàn měi)**
Does not fit cleanly into any group. It functions as a philosophical and aesthetic triad in which 真 names one of three highest values alongside goodness and beauty. Its usage is evaluative in a broad sense but does not participate in the specific contrasts (genuine/fake, accurate/inaccurate, sincere/hypocritical) that define the groups above. It is left ungrouped.

---

**弄假成真 (nòng jiǎ chéng zhēn) / 假戏真做 (jiǎ xì zhēn zuò)**
These expressions describe a process in which the boundary between genuine and pretended dissolves. They do not evaluate something as genuine or fake but describe a dynamic in which the two categories become confused or merged. They do not fit cleanly into Group 3 (authenticity) or Group 5 (sincerity) and are left ungrouped.

---

**半真半假 (bàn zhēn bàn jiǎ)**
Describes a mixed state rather than evaluating something as clearly genuine or false. It does not fit cleanly into any group and is left ungrouped.

---

**天真 (tiānzhēn)**
The 真 root contributes a sense of natural, unaffected simplicity that shades into naivety. Its usage is primarily in character evaluation (of children, or of adults who are unrealistically simple), and the contrast is with social sophistication or worldliness rather than with falsity or inaccuracy. It does not fit cleanly into Group 5 (sincerity) or any other group and is left ungrouped.

---

**认真 (rènzhēn)**
Describes intensity of engagement and conscientiousness in performing a task. The contrast is with carelessness or superficiality rather than with falsity or inaccuracy. It does not fit cleanly into any of the groups above and is left ungrouped.

---

**真空 (zhēnkōng) / 真菌 (zhēnjūn) / 传真 (chuánzhēn)**
These terms use 真 in technical or taxonomic functions where the truth/authenticity dimension is either absent or opaque to most users. They are not grouped.

---

## Items Left Ungrouped

The following items from P1 and P2 do not fit clearly into any of the groups identified above, for the reasons noted:

| Item | Reason |
|---|---|
| 真理 (zhēnlǐ) | Philosophical/ideological value term; does not participate in the genuine/fake or accurate/inaccurate contrasts |
| 真善美 (zhēn shàn měi) | Philosophical triad; 真 functions as a value category, not an evaluative predicate |
| 天真 (tiānzhēn) | Character evaluation contrasting with worldliness, not with falsity |
| 认真 (rènzhēn) | Evaluates effort and engagement, not truth or authenticity |
| 弄假成真 / 假戏真做 | Describe dissolution of genuine/fake boundary rather than evaluating one side of it |
| 半真半假 | Describes a mixed state; does not evaluate |
| 真空 / 真菌 / 传真 | Technical/taxonomic uses; truth dimension absent or opaque |
| 写真 (xiězhēn) | In modern photographic usage, functions as a label for a portrait or photo shoot; truth dimension is etymological rather than active |
| 假设 / 假如 / 假若 | Conditional/hypothetical conjunctions; 假 functions as a logical operator without evaluative or deceptive dimension |
| 假借 (jiǎjiè) — linguistic usage | Technical classification in character formation; no evaluative dimension |
| 伪装 (wěizhuāng) — military usage | In camouflage contexts, the deceptive dimension may be absent or differently weighted; does not fit cleanly into Group 7 |
| 伪命题 (wěi mìngtí) | Philosophical/logical term for an ill-formed proposition; extends 伪 into formal reasoning in a way that does not fit Groups 3 or 7 |
