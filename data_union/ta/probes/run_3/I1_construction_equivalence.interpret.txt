## 2. Substitution Patterns (Narrative)

### 2.1 REVELATION substitutes for BECOME

The CLCA BECOME operator — a neutral change-of-state predication — is not expressed in Tamil through a dedicated inchoative morpheme or a neutral *-āka āku* construction for the truth domain. Instead, the inchoative meaning is systematically expressed through motion-and-perception verbs: *veḷippaṭu* (to become visible, to emerge) and *teriya va* (to come to be known). Both verbs encode not merely a change of state but specifically a change involving perceptibility or discoverability. The P4a and P4b data describe *uṇmai veḷippaṭṭatu* as the most conventionalized form for "truth coming to light," and *uṇmai teriya vantatu* as a fully natural alternant. The *-āka āku* pattern is noted as available but marked [MARGINAL] for this semantic field. The substitution is systematic and domain-specific: BECOME in the truth domain is realized as REVELATION.

### 2.2 LIGHT VERB CONSTRUCTION substitutes for morphological verbal derivation

Tamil does not productively derive verbs from truth-domain nominals through morphological processes (with the narrow exception of *uṇmaippaṭuttu* in formal registers). The functional slot occupied by verbal derivation in other languages is filled entirely by light verb constructions: *uṇmai collu* (tell the truth), *poy pēcu* (speak falsely/lie), *tavaṟu cey* (do wrong/make a mistake), *niyāyam kēṭku* (demand justice), *nērmai kāṭṭu* (show integrity), *vañcakam cey* (commit deception). This substitution applies across CAUSATION (factual), CAUSATION (character), and the expression of truth-domain verbal meanings generally. The light verb carries tense, aspect, and agreement; the nominal carries the semantic content of the operator.

### 2.3 DISCONFIRMATION is expressed through NEGATION, not a distinct operator

No dedicated disconfirmation construction — a construction specifically asserting that a claim is false or that evidence undermines a proposition — is attested in the P4a or P4b data. The closest available forms are negative attribution (*atu poyyillai* — "that is not false/a lie") and privative modification (*ātāramillāta kuṟṟaccāṭṭu* — "baseless accusation"). These are NEGATION constructions applied to truth-domain nominals, not a structurally distinct DISCONFIRMATION operator. DISCONFIRMATION is therefore expressed through NEGATION in Tamil as described in the data.

### 2.4 VERIFICATION is restricted and partially substituted

The morphological causative *-paṭuttu* on *uṇmai* (*uṇmaippaṭuttu* — "authenticate, substantiate") is the closest attested form to a VERIFICATION operator. However, P4a and P4b both note that this form is restricted to *uṇmai* in formal and administrative registers, and is not productive across the truth-domain vocabulary. No *poyppaṭuttu*, *nērmaipaṭuttu*, or *niyāyappaṭuttu* is attested. For other truth-domain nominals, verification-type meanings are expressed periphrastically through light verb constructions. VERIFICATION is therefore partially available through a restricted morphological form and partially substituted by light verb constructions.

### 2.5 Asymmetry between positive and negative agentive nominals

The agentive nominalization pattern (§7) shows an asymmetry: *poyyaṉ* ("liar") and *vañcakaṉ* ("deceiver") are lexicalized agentive nominals, while no equivalent lexicalized agentive for "truth-teller" built on *uṇmai* is attested. The concept is expressed periphrastically as *uṇmai pēcukiṟavaṉ* ("one who speaks the truth"). The negative-domain agentive (liar, deceiver) is lexically available; the positive-domain agentive (truth-teller) is a lexical gap requiring circumlocution.

### 2.6 Two surface forms for ATTR

The ATTR operator is expressed by two surface constructions: the zero-copula nominal predicate (*atu uṇmai*) and the *-āka irukku* predicative (*itu uṇmaiyāka irukku*). P4b notes these are available without change in meaning, with *irukku* appearing optionally in spoken registers. Both map to the same ATTR operator; the distinction is register-conditioned rather than semantic.

### 2.7 Analytic and morphological NEGATION coexist

Tamil expresses truth-domain negation through both analytic and morphological means. The analytic forms are *illai* (negative existential for full predication) and *illāta* (negative participial for attributive modification). The morphological form is the privative participial *-aṟṟa*. P4b notes that *illāta* is marginally more common in spoken registers while *-aṟṟa* is slightly more formal or literary. Both are [CORE] and fully productive across truth-domain nominals. The two forms are in free variation for attributive modification; only *illai* is available for full predication.

## 3. Missingness Summary

### 3.1 Structural Gaps

No structural gaps are identified. All ten CLCA operators can be expressed in Tamil, either through a direct construction or through a systematic substitution. The language does not lack a construction type for any operator; it may lack a dedicated construction for some operators, but a structural means of expression is always available.

### 3.2 Lexical Gaps

**Identified lexical gap:**
- No lexicalized agentive nominal meaning "truth-teller" built on *uṇmai* is attested. The concept requires the periphrastic expression *uṇmai pēcukiṟavaṉ* ("one who speaks the truth"). This is a lexical gap within the agentive nominalization pattern: the pattern itself is productive, but the specific lexical item is absent.

**Identified lexical restriction:**
- The morphological causative *-paṭuttu* is lexically restricted to *uṇmai* within the truth domain. No *poyppaṭuttu*, *nērmaipaṭuttu*, or *niyāyappaṭuttu* is attested. This is a lexical gap in the causative paradigm: the morphological pattern exists but is not lexically extended across the domain.

### 3.3 Conceptual Gaps

No conceptual gaps are identified from the data. All truth-domain operations — attribution, modification, becoming, revelation, causation, verification, disconfirmation, negation, and nominalization — are expressible in Tamil. No operation appears to be blocked or unavailable as a conceptual category.

### 3.4 Asymmetric Gaps

**Agentive nominalization — positive/negative asymmetry:**
- Negative-domain agentives are lexicalized: *poyyaṉ* ("liar"), *vañcakaṉ* ("deceiver").
- Positive-domain agentive for "truth-teller" on *uṇmai* is not lexicalized; circumlocution is required.
- The *-āḷar* honorific agentive *nērmayāḷar* ("person of integrity") is lexicalized on the positive side for *nērmai*, but not for *uṇmai*.

**Abstract nominalization — register asymmetry:**
- *-mai* and *taṉmai* abstract nominals are available but marked [MARGINAL] in everyday use; they are not absent but are register-restricted.
- No equivalent register restriction is noted for agentive nominals or light verb constructions, which are [CORE].

**Causative — lexical asymmetry:**
- The morphological causative *-paṭuttu* is attested only for *uṇmai* (truth/authentication); it is absent for *poy* (falsehood), *nērmai* (honesty), *niyāyam* (justice), and other truth-domain nominals.
