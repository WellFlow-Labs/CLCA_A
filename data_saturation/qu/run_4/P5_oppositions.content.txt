# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P5 Oppositions
# Language: Quechua (qu)

---

**Preliminary note on method:** Each opposition below is extracted directly from patterns documented in P1–P4. No opposition has been constructed by analogy or inferred from English semantic categories. Where the data support asymmetry between the two terms (e.g., one is more productive, one lacks an attested form), that asymmetry is preserved and noted. Overlapping oppositions are listed separately rather than collapsed.

---

## Opposition 1

1. **Term A:** cheqa
2. **Term B:** llulla
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa / llulla
4. **English translation:** true, real, genuine / false, lying, deceptive, fake
5. **Description of contrast:** These are the primary adjectival roots for truth and falsehood in Southern Quechua and are explicitly described in P1 as direct semantic counterparts. They participate in parallel derivational patterns (*cheqanchay* / *llullachay*; *cheqa runa* / *llulla runa*; *cheqa simi* / *llulla simi*; *cheqa sunqu* / *llulla sunqu*) and appear in direct opposition in testimonial and moral discourse.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 2

1. **Term A:** cheqam
2. **Term B:** llullam
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m / llulla-m
4. **English translation:** "It is true (I assert from direct knowledge)" / "It is false (I assert from direct knowledge)"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both forms combine a truth-evaluation predicate with the direct evidential clitic *-m*, producing opposed assertive predications from the same epistemic source. The opposition is between the content of the assertion (truth vs. falsity) while the evidential commitment is held constant.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 3

1. **Term A:** cheqas
2. **Term B:** llullas
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-s / llulla-s
4. **English translation:** "It is apparently true (I was told)" / "It is reportedly false"
5. **Description of contrast:** Parallel to Opposition 2 but with the reportative evidential clitic *-s* held constant; the opposition is again between the truth-value content while the evidential source (hearsay/report) is the same for both.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 4

1. **Term A:** cheqam
2. **Term B:** cheqas
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m / cheqa-s
4. **English translation:** "It is true (I know directly)" / "It is apparently true (I was told)"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both forms predicate truth of the same proposition but differ in the speaker's evidential source: direct personal knowledge (*-m*) versus reported/hearsay information (*-s*). This contrast is grammaticalized in Quechua and is described in P1 and P4a as encoding distinct epistemic sources rather than optional stylistic variation.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 5

1. **Term A:** cheqam
2. **Term B:** cheqacha
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m / cheqa-chá
4. **English translation:** "It is true (I know directly)" / "It is probably true (I conjecture)"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both forms predicate truth but differ in the speaker's epistemic commitment: direct evidence (*-m*) versus inference or conjecture (*-chá*). The contrast marks the difference between a fully committed assertion and a hedged one, within the same truth-value predication.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 6

1. **Term A:** cheqas
2. **Term B:** cheqacha
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-s / cheqa-chá
4. **English translation:** "It is apparently true (I was told)" / "It is probably true (I conjecture)"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both forms hedge the truth assertion but differ in the type of non-direct evidence: reported/hearsay source (*-s*) versus inference or conjecture (*-chá*). The contrast is between two distinct non-first-hand epistemic sources.
6. **Contrast type:** morphological
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 7

1. **Term A:** cheqam
2. **Term B:** mana cheqachu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-m / mana cheqa-chu
4. **English translation:** "It is true (I assert from direct knowledge)" / "It is not true."
5. **Description of contrast:** The affirmative direct-evidential predication is opposed to the standard negated form. The negation uses the discontinuous frame *mana* … *-chu* and, as noted in P4a, the evidential clitic is absent in the basic negated form, producing an asymmetry: the affirmative grammatically encodes the speaker's epistemic source while the basic negation does not.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 8

1. **Term A:** llullam
2. **Term B:** mana llullachu
3. **Transliteration:** llulla-m / mana llulla-chu
4. **English translation:** "It is false (I assert from direct knowledge)" / "It is not false."
5. **Description of contrast:** The same affirmative/negation asymmetry as Opposition 7, applied to *llulla*. The affirmative carries the direct evidential clitic; the basic negation does not. Both are documented as attested constructions in P2b and P4a.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 9

1. **Term A:** cheqa simi
2. **Term B:** llulla simi
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa simi / llulla simi
4. **English translation:** "true word / true statement / honest testimony" / "false word / false statement / lie"
5. **Description of contrast:** These are explicitly described in P1 as direct counterparts and are listed as a contrastive pair in P3 Group 6. Both are adnominal compounds with the same head noun *simi* ("word/statement/mouth") and opposed modifiers; they appear together in legal and moral discourse evaluating the truthfulness of testimony.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 15

1. **Term A:** cheqa rimay / cheqata rimay
2. **Term B:** llulla rimay
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa rimay / cheqata rimay / llulla rimay
4. **English translation:** "truthful speech / to speak truthfully" / "false speech / to speak falsely"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are collocations of a truth-evaluation modifier with *rimay* ("speech/to speak"), documented in P1b and P3 Group 6 as counterparts used in moral instruction and legal discourse about the quality of speech. The *cheqa* side has two attested forms (*cheqa rimay* as a nominal/verbal collocation and *cheqata rimay* as an adverbial construction); the *llulla* side has one (*llulla rimay*).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 17

1. **Term A:** yachay
2. **Term B:** iñiy
3. **Transliteration:** yachay / iñiy
4. **English translation:** "to know" / "to believe, to trust"
5. **Description of contrast:** These two verbs are explicitly contrasted in the construction *Iñinim, mana yachanichu* ("I believe it, but I do not know it"), documented in P2b and P3 Group 4. *Yachay* encodes propositional or practical knowledge; *iñiy* encodes belief or trust without direct knowledge. The contrast is described as epistemically significant in Quechua discourse.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 18

1. **Term A:** Cheqa kasqanta yachani.
2. **Term B:** Cheqa kasqanta iñini.
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa kasqa-n-ta yacha-ni / cheqa kasqa-n-ta iñi-ni
4. **English translation:** "I know that it is true." / "I believe it to be true."
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are nominalized complement constructions with the same embedded predicate (*cheqa kasqanta*, "its being true") but different matrix verbs (*yachay* vs. *iñiy*). The contrast instantiates the knowledge/belief distinction at the constructional level; it is documented in P2b and P4b as explicitly attested and epistemically significant.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 19

1. **Term A:** Iñinim.
2. **Term B:** Mana yachanichu.
3. **Transliteration:** iñi-ni-m / mana yacha-ni-chu
4. **English translation:** "I believe it (I assert)." / "I do not know it."
5. **Description of contrast:** This is the explicit contrastive construction documented in P2b (*Iñinim, mana yachanichu*), in which the speaker asserts belief with the direct evidential clitic while simultaneously disclaiming knowledge. The direct evidential on *iñinim* marks that the speaker has direct access to their own belief state, while the negated *yachanichu* denies direct access to the proposition itself.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 20

1. **Term A:** cheqa
2. **Term B:** pantasqa
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa / pantasqa
4. **English translation:** "true, correct" / "mistaken, wrong, in error"
5. **Description of contrast:** *Cheqa* predicates truth or correctness; *pantasqa* (the participial form of *pantay*, "to err") predicates error or incorrectness. Both appear as predicates in evaluative constructions (*Cheqam chay rimay* / *Pantasqam chay rimay*) and are documented in P3 as belonging to distinct semantic groups (Group 1/5 vs. Group 9). The contrast is between a correct state of affairs and an erroneous one.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

## Opposition 21

1. **Term A:** cheqanchay
2. **Term B:** pantachiy
3. **Transliteration:** cheqanchay / pantachiy
4. **English translation:** "to verify, to correct, to rectify" / "to cause to err, to mislead"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are causative verbal forms but with opposed directionality: *cheqanchay* moves a state of affairs toward correctness or truth; *pantachiy* moves a person toward error. Both are documented in P2a and P3 (Groups 7 and 9 respectively) as causatives in the truth-evaluation domain.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 23

1. **Term A:** cheqa
2. **Term B:** mana allin
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa / mana allin
4. **English translation:** "true, correct, right" / "not good, wrong, improper"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are used in evaluative predication of statements, actions, and conduct, but they differ in their semantic basis: *cheqa* evaluates against a standard of truth or genuineness, while *mana allin* evaluates against a standard of goodness or propriety. They overlap in practical and moral evaluative contexts but are not synonymous; the contrast is documented in P3 where they are assigned to different groups (Groups 1/5 vs. Group 9).
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 25

1. **Term A:** cheqa simiwan rimay
2. **Term B:** llulla simiwan ama rimaychu
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa simi-wan rimay / llulla simi-wan ama rima-y-chu
4. **English translation:** "Speak with true words." / "Do not speak with false words."
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are directive constructions using the instrumental/comitative case *-wan* with *simi* ("word/speech"), but one is an affirmative directive toward truthful speech and the other is a prohibitive construction against false speech. Both are documented in P2b as attested in legal and moral instructional contexts.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]

## Opposition 26

## Opposition 29

1. **Term A:** cheqa cheqa
2. **Term B:** llullapuni
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa cheqa / llulla-puni
4. **English translation:** "truly true / absolutely genuine" (reduplication) / "absolutely false / certainly a lie"
5. **Description of contrast:** Both are intensified forms used in emphatic assertion, but they use different intensification strategies: *cheqa cheqa* uses reduplication while *llullapuni* uses the emphatic suffix *-puni*. The asymmetry in intensification strategy between the two roots is a feature of the data as documented in P1b; no reduplicated *llulla llulla* is attested, and no *cheqapuni* / *llullapuni* pair with reduplication on the *llulla* side is documented.
6. **Contrast type:** lexical
7. **Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

## Opposition 30

1. **Term A:** cheqaqa (topic-marked)
2. **Term B:** (implied prior false or incorrect statement)
3. **Transliteration:** cheqa-qa
4. **English translation:** "The truth is… / As for what is true…"
5. **Description of contrast:** The topic-marking construction *cheqaqa* is documented in P1b as specifically used to introduce a correction or disclosure in contrast to a previously stated or implied falsehood. The opposition is between the topic-marked truth disclosure and the prior false or incorrect state of affairs it corrects; the contrasting term is not a fixed lexical item but a discourse-level implied prior.
6. **Contrast type:** constructional
7. **Epistemic status:** [CORE]
