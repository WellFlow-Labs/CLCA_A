# CLCA Protocol v3.2 — P1 CORE VOCABULARY COLLECTION
# Language: Greek (el)

---

**1.**
**Form:** αληθής
**Transliteration:** alithís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (privative) + λήθη (concealment, forgetting); "un-concealed"
**Typical English translations:** true, truthful, genuine
**Usage:** Used to predicate truth of statements, claims, or descriptions. Appears in both everyday and philosophical registers. Participates in a productive derivational family (αλήθεια, αληθινός, αληθεύω).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**2.**
**Form:** αλήθεια
**Transliteration:** alíthia
**Literal meaning:** nominal form of αληθής; "the state of being un-concealed"
**Typical English translations:** truth, truthfulness, reality
**Usage:** The primary noun for truth in Greek, used across everyday, legal, philosophical, and religious contexts. Frequently appears in fixed expressions such as *λέω την αλήθεια* ("I tell the truth").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**3.**
**Form:** αληθινός
**Transliteration:** alithinós
**Literal meaning:** adjectival derivative of αλήθεια; "of or belonging to truth"
**Typical English translations:** real, genuine, true, authentic
**Usage:** Applied to persons, objects, feelings, and states of affairs to indicate that they are what they purport to be. Contrasts with imitation or pretense, e.g., *αληθινός φίλος* ("a true friend").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**4.**
**Form:** γνήσιος
**Transliteration:** gnísios
**Literal meaning:** from γένος (birth, origin); "of legitimate birth, native"
**Typical English translations:** genuine, authentic, legitimate, pure
**Usage:** Used to assert that something or someone is of legitimate or original origin, not counterfeit or adulterated. Applied to products, documents, relationships, and character, e.g., *γνήσιο προϊόν* ("genuine product").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**5.**
**Form:** αυθεντικός
**Transliteration:** afthentikós
**Literal meaning:** from αὐθέντης (one who acts on their own authority); "authoritative, original"
**Typical English translations:** authentic, genuine, original
**Usage:** Used in aesthetic, legal, and everyday contexts to indicate that something is original and not a copy or forgery. Also applied to persons to indicate sincerity or lack of pretense.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**6.**
**Form:** ακριβής
**Transliteration:** akrivís
**Literal meaning:** from ἀ- (intensive) + κρίβανος or related to κρίνω; commonly glossed as "exact, precise"
**Typical English translations:** accurate, precise, exact, correct
**Usage:** Applied to measurements, descriptions, translations, and accounts to indicate that they correspond closely to the facts or standard. *Ακριβής μετάφραση* means "accurate translation."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**7.**
**Form:** σωστός
**Transliteration:** sostós
**Literal meaning:** from σώζω (to save, preserve); "preserved intact, sound"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, accurate
**Usage:** One of the most common everyday terms for correctness, applied to answers, behavior, procedures, and judgments. *Η σωστή απάντηση* means "the correct answer."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**8.**
**Form:** ορθός
**Transliteration:** orthós
**Literal meaning:** "straight, upright, direct"
**Typical English translations:** correct, right, proper, orthodox
**Usage:** Used in more formal, technical, and philosophical registers to indicate correctness or conformity to a standard. Appears in compounds such as *ορθογραφία* (correct writing) and *ορθόδοξος* (of correct opinion).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**9.**
**Form:** έγκυρος
**Transliteration:** éngiros
**Literal meaning:** from ἐν- + κῦρος (authority, validity); "having authority"
**Typical English translations:** valid, authoritative, legitimate
**Usage:** Used primarily in legal, administrative, and formal contexts to indicate that a document, decision, or claim has proper standing and force. *Έγκυρο συμβόλαιο* means "valid contract."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**10.**
**Form:** νόμιμος
**Transliteration:** nómimos
**Literal meaning:** from νόμος (law); "according to law"
**Typical English translations:** legal, lawful, legitimate
**Usage:** Indicates conformity with legal norms or established rules. Applied to persons, actions, and documents. Contrasts with *παράνομος* (illegal).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**11.**
**Form:** νόθος
**Transliteration:** nóthos
**Literal meaning:** "of illegitimate birth, bastard, spurious"
**Typical English translations:** spurious, counterfeit, illegitimate, fake
**Usage:** Used to indicate that something is not of genuine or legitimate origin — applied historically to persons of illegitimate birth and extended to texts, documents, or objects of doubtful authenticity. Appears in philological and legal contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**12.**
**Form:** πλαστός
**Transliteration:** plastós
**Literal meaning:** from πλάσσω (to mold, fabricate); "molded, fabricated"
**Typical English translations:** forged, counterfeit, fake, fabricated
**Usage:** Applied to documents, signatures, currency, and identities to indicate deliberate falsification. *Πλαστό διαβατήριο* means "forged passport."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**13.**
**Form:** ψεύτικος
**Transliteration:** pséftikos
**Literal meaning:** from ψεύδος (falsehood, lie); "of or belonging to falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** false, fake, counterfeit, lying
**Usage:** A common everyday adjective applied to objects, persons, and statements to indicate that they are not what they appear to be. Less formal than *πλαστός* and broader in application.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**14.**
**Form:** ψεύδος
**Transliteration:** psévdos
**Literal meaning:** "falsehood, lie"
**Typical English translations:** falsehood, lie, untruth
**Usage:** The primary noun for falsehood or a lie, used across philosophical, legal, and everyday registers. Participates in a large derivational family: *ψεύτης* (liar), *ψεύδομαι* (to lie), *ψευδής* (false).
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**15.**
**Form:** ψευδής
**Transliteration:** psevdís
**Literal meaning:** adjectival form of ψεύδος; "of the nature of falsehood"
**Typical English translations:** false, untrue, mendacious
**Usage:** Applied to statements, testimony, and claims to indicate that they do not correspond to reality. Common in legal contexts, e.g., *ψευδής κατάθεση* ("false testimony").
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**16.**
**Form:** αξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** aksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** from ἄξιος (worthy) + πίστις (trust, faith); "worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** reliable, trustworthy, credible
**Usage:** Applied to persons, sources, institutions, and information to indicate that they can be trusted or believed. *Αξιόπιστη πηγή* means "reliable source."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**17.**
**Form:** αναξιόπιστος
**Transliteration:** anaksiópistos
**Literal meaning:** privative ἀν- + αξιόπιστος; "not worthy of trust"
**Typical English translations:** unreliable, untrustworthy, incredible
**Usage:** The direct negation of *αξιόπιστος*, applied to persons, sources, and claims. Used in evaluative contexts where credibility is at issue.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**18.**
**Form:** αμφίβολος
**Transliteration:** amfívolos
**Literal meaning:** from ἀμφί (on both sides) + βάλλω (to throw); "thrown both ways, doubtful"
**Typical English translations:** doubtful, dubious, ambiguous, questionable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, situations, and persons whose status or truth is uncertain or open to question. *Αμφίβολη υπόθεση* means "a doubtful case."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**19.**
**Form:** αμφισβητήσιμος
**Transliteration:** amfisvitiísimos
**Literal meaning:** from ἀμφισβητέω (to dispute, contest); "disputable, contestable"
**Typical English translations:** disputable, contestable, questionable
**Usage:** Applied to claims, rights, and facts that are open to challenge or dispute. More formal than *αμφίβολος* and more specifically tied to the possibility of active contestation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**20.**
**Form:** αποδεδειγμένος
**Transliteration:** apodedeignménos
**Literal meaning:** perfect passive participle of ἀποδείκνυμι (to demonstrate, prove); "having been demonstrated"
**Typical English translations:** proven, demonstrated, established
**Usage:** Applied to facts, claims, and propositions that have been shown to be true through evidence or argument. Used in legal, scientific, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**21.**
**Form:** τεκμηριωμένος
**Transliteration:** tekmirio̱ménos
**Literal meaning:** from τεκμήριον (evidence, proof); "supported by evidence"
**Typical English translations:** documented, evidenced, substantiated
**Usage:** Applied to claims, arguments, and positions that are backed by concrete evidence or documentation. Common in journalistic, academic, and legal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**22.**
**Form:** αδιαμφισβήτητος
**Transliteration:** adiamfisvitiitos
**Literal meaning:** ἀ- (privative) + διαμφισβητέω (to dispute thoroughly); "not disputable"
**Typical English translations:** indisputable, unquestionable, undeniable
**Usage:** Applied to facts, rights, and truths that are held to be beyond reasonable challenge. Used for emphasis in legal, rhetorical, and everyday contexts.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**23.**
**Form:** εικονικός
**Transliteration:** eikonikós
**Literal meaning:** from εἰκών (image, likeness); "of or pertaining to an image"
**Typical English translations:** virtual, simulated, nominal, figurative
**Usage:** In contemporary usage, applied to things that have the form or appearance of something without being the thing itself — e.g., *εικονική πραγματικότητα* ("virtual reality"). Also used in older contexts for resemblance or representation.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**24.**
**Form:** φαινομενικός
**Transliteration:** fenomenikós
**Literal meaning:** from φαινόμενον (that which appears); "of or pertaining to appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparent, seeming, ostensible
**Usage:** Applied to qualities or states that appear to be the case but may not correspond to underlying reality. *Φαινομενική επιτυχία* means "apparent success."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**25.**
**Form:** ουσιαστικός
**Transliteration:** usiástikos
**Literal meaning:** from οὐσία (essence, substance, being); "of or pertaining to essence"
**Typical English translations:** essential, substantive, real, fundamental
**Usage:** Applied to distinguish what is genuinely or fundamentally the case from what is merely formal or superficial. *Ουσιαστική διαφορά* means "a substantive difference."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**26.**
**Form:** τυπικός
**Transliteration:** tipikós
**Literal meaning:** from τύπος (type, form, stamp); "of or pertaining to form or type"
**Typical English translations:** formal, nominal, typical, perfunctory
**Usage:** Often used in contrast to *ουσιαστικός* to indicate that something satisfies formal requirements without genuine substance. *Τυπική συμμόρφωση* means "formal compliance."
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

**27.**
**Form:** κατ' ουσίαν
**Transliteration:** kat' usían
**Literal meaning:** κατά (according to, in respect of) + οὐσία (essence); "in respect of essence"
**Typical English translations:** essentially, substantially, in substance
**Usage:** A fixed prepositional expression used to indicate that something is true or the case at the level of substance or essence, as opposed to mere form. Common in legal and philosophical discourse.
**Epistemic status:** [CORE]

---

**28.**
**Form:** κατά φαινόμενον
**Transliteration:** katá fenómenon
**Literal meaning:** κατά (according to) + φαινόμενον (appearance); "according to appearance"
**Typical English translations:** apparently, ostensibly, in appearance
**Usage:** A fixed prepositional expression contrasting with *κατ' ουσίαν*, used to indicate that something holds only at the level of appearance. Found in philosophical and formal registers.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**29.**
**Form:** εικαζόμενος
**Transliteration:** ikazómenos
**Literal meaning:** present passive participle of εἰκάζω (to conjecture, liken); "conjectured, supposed"
**Typical English translations:** alleged, supposed, presumed
**Usage:** Applied to persons or facts whose status is asserted but not established, particularly in legal and journalistic contexts. *Ο εικαζόμενος δράστης* means "the alleged perpetrator."
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]

---

**30.**
**Form:** εικός
**Transliteration:** ikós
**Literal meaning:** from ἔοικα (to seem, resemble); "that which seems likely, probable"
**Typical English translations:** probable, likely, plausible, verisimilar
**Usage:** Found primarily in classical and formal philosophical registers to indicate what is probable or plausible as opposed to what is certain or demonstrated. Less common in contemporary everyday Greek but retained in set expressions and academic writing.
**Epistemic status:** [MARGINAL]
